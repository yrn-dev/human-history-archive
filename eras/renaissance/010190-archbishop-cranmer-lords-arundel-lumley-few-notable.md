# Archbishop Cranmer and Lords Arundel and Lumley

## Book Ownership and Collection
In England, Archbishop Cranmer, Lord Arundel, and Lord Lumley were among those who assembled significant collections of the best books of their era across all departments of learned literature. These individuals were not book-collectors in the modern antiquarian sense—which focuses on the externals of books—but rather sought to acquire the most distinguished works of their time. To preserve and present these volumes, they had them bound in handsome leather jackets and decorated them with their heraldic badges and coats of arms.

## Archbishop Cranmer
Archbishop Cranmer (d. 1556) was a prominent figure in the English church and state. He was involved in the legal and ecclesiastical proceedings regarding the marriage of King Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn. On May 16, Anne informed Cranmer of a supposed impediment to her marriage with the king, which some accounts suggested was a previous marriage to Northumberland. After consideration by Cranmer and a committee of ecclesiastical lawyers, this impediment was pronounced sufficient to invalidate the marriage on May 17.

Cranmer also played a central role in the distribution of the "Great Bible," a splendid folio volume translated from Hebrew and Greek texts. The title-page of this Bible depicts King Henry VIII giving the "Word of God" to Cranmer and Cromwell, who then distributed it to clerics and laymen. A subsequent edition of this Bible, published in 1540, became known as "Cranmer's Bible" because it featured a long Preface written by the Archbishop. In portraits at Lambeth, Cranmer is depicted wearing a square cap.

## Lord Lumley
Lord Lumley is noted in the archive for his possession of a manuscript of the narrative of the traveller William de Rubruk (also referred to as Rubruquis). This imperfect manuscript served as the basis for the first publication of the narrative by Hakluyt. While the original manuscript belonging to Lord Lumley is not currently known, it provided the primary source for the early English dissemination of Rubruk's travels.

## Lord Arundel
The archive contains references to multiple individuals associated with the name Arundel. 

One Thomas Arundel (1353–1414) served as the Archbishop of Canterbury and Lord Chancellor. A member of an influential family, he was the son of Richard Fitzalan, earl of Arundel and Warenne. His career included serving as the bishop of Ely (consecrated April 1374) and the archbishop of York (April 1388). During his tenure as archbishop of Canterbury, which began in September 1396, he asserted his rights vigorously and conducted a thorough visitation of his province. However, he later fell from favor during King Richard II's attack on the lords appellant in 1397. Arundel was impeached by the Commons and sentenced to banishment. He had previously been involved in a conspiracy at Arundel Castle to murder the king's council lords and dethrone Richard II. Additionally, as Archbishop, he presided over a court in St Paul's, London, which condemned the Lollard martyr John Badby to be burned at Smithfield for denying transubstantiation.
