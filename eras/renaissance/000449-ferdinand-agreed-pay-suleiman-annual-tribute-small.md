# Ferdinand and the Tribute to Suleiman

## The Habsburg Succession and Division
Following the death of Emperor Maximilian in 1519, the Habsburg dominions in eastern Germany comprised the county of Tirol and the duchies of Carniola, Carinthia, Styria, and Upper and Lower Austria. Maximilian was succeeded as emperor and archduke of Austria by his grandson, Charles V (also known as Charles of Spain). Charles resigned all his claims on Hungary and Bohemia, as well as all his Austrian lands, to his brother Ferdinand. This arrangement resulted in the division of Spain and Austria, two entities that were never reunited despite the efforts of Charles during the Spanish Succession War.

## Conflict with the Ottoman Empire
Ferdinand spent the majority of his life attempting to secure Hungary and combating the Turks. A pivotal moment in this struggle occurred on August 28, 1526, at the battle of Mohacs. Suleiman the Magnificent defeated and killed Louis, the king of Hungary and Bohemia. Louis's sister, Anne, had been married to Ferdinand. As a result of this victory, the Turks conquered and maintained control over the greater part of Hungary until the peace of Karlowitz in 1699.

Ferdinand faced an active rival in John Zapolya, who received support from Suleiman. The conflict saw the Turks make several invasions of Austria and Hungary, including the siege of Vienna in 1530.

## The Annual Tribute
To address the ongoing conflict, Ferdinand eventually agreed to pay Suleiman an annual tribute. This payment was specifically for the small portion of Hungary that Ferdinand held, which measured approximately 12,228 square miles. This practice of paying tribute to the Turk continued not only under Ferdinand but also under his two successors.

## Political and Religious Context
While Ferdinand was entangled in Hungarian affairs and the Turkish threat, he maintained a neutral attitude during the struggles of Charles V with the German Protestants. This neutrality helped provide Germany with a brief period of internal peace. Although Ferdinand did not take a leading role in foreign or religious politics within Germany, the era was defined by two unifying themes: the claims of the House of Austria and the Reformation.

The Austrian line remained obstinately Catholic. Because they held their patrimony primarily on the eastern frontiers, they were deeply involved in Hungarian issues and the payment of tribute. This focus meant the Austrian line retained no grip on the north Germans, who were indifferent to the Turkish danger and inclined toward Protestantism and Baltic or westward affinities.

## Transition of Power
By 1547, Charles V had reached a temporary settlement and made final efforts to establish peace. In 1552, following the treaty of Passau, a further unstable equilibrium was reached. Weary of the empire and suffering from gout, Charles V abdicated. He resigned his sovereign rights in Germany to his brother Ferdinand, while his son Philip took over the Netherlands and Spain.
