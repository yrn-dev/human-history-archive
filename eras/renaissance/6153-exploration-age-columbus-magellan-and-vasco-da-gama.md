# Horizons of the Renaissance: The Age of Discovery

## Overview
The Age of Discovery, coinciding with the European Renaissance, marked a pivotal shift in global geopolitics, trade, and science. Driven by a desire to bypass Ottoman-controlled land routes to the spice markets of Asia, European powers—primarily Portugal and Spain—funded daring maritime expeditions. This era was characterized by the synthesis of rediscovered classical geography and new navigational technologies, such as the astrolabe and the development of the caravel. While these voyages expanded the known world for Europeans, they also initiated centuries of colonization, exploitation, and the devastating "Columbian Exchange" of plants, animals, and diseases.

## Timeline of Key Voyages

| Year | Explorer | Achievement | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1492 | Christopher Columbus | Reached the Bahamas | Opened the Americas to European colonization. |

## Key Facts

### Christopher Columbus
Sailing for the Spanish Crown (Castile), Columbus hypothesized that he could reach the East Indies by sailing west. While he landed in the Caribbean in 1492, he died believing he had reached Asia. Historians continue to debate the extent of his "discovery"; while he opened the door for European empire, indigenous civilizations had flourished in the Americas for millennia, and Norse explorers had reached Newfoundland centuries earlier.

### Vasco da Gama
Unlike Columbus, da Gama’s objective was the direct maritime route to India via the southern tip of Africa. In 1498, he successfully reached Calicut. This achievement shifted the economic center of gravity from the Mediterranean to the Atlantic, severely undermining the monopoly of Venetian and Ottoman traders.

### Ferdinand Magellan
Magellan’s expedition (1519–1522) was the first to sail from the Atlantic to the Pacific Ocean via the strait that now bears his name. Although Magellan himself was killed in a conflict in the Philippines in 1521, Juan Sebastián Elcano completed the voyage. This expedition provided the first empirical evidence of the Earth's circumference.

## Did You Know?
* **The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** To prevent conflict, Spain and Portugal signed a treaty dividing the newly "discovered" lands outside Europe along a meridian 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands.
* **The Spice Value:** During this era, certain spices like nutmeg and cloves were so valuable—due to their rarity and perceived medicinal properties—that they were often traded for their weight in gold.
* **Navigational Shifts:** The Renaissance saw a transition from "portolan charts" (which relied on coastal landmarks) to celestial navigation, using the stars to determine latitude.
