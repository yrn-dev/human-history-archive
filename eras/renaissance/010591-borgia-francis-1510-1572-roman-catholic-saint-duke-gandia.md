# BORGIA, FRANCIS (1510-1572)

Francis Borgia was a Roman Catholic saint, the duke of Gandia, and the general of the order of Jesuits. Born on October 10, 1510, in Gandia, Valencia, he was noted for his piety from a young age. His education began at the age of twelve in Saragossa, where he was under the charge of his uncle, the archbishop. During this period, Borgia began to exhibit a strong inclination toward a monastic life.

## Court Life and Military Service
In 1528, Borgia's father sent him to the court of Charles V. He distinguished himself during his time there and married Eleanor de Castro, a high-ranking Portuguese lady. Following his marriage, he was appointed master of the horse to the empress and created marquis of Lombay. 

Borgia's service to Charles V included accompanying him on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and into Provence in 1536. In 1539, following the death of the empress, Borgia was tasked with the duty of convoying her body to her burial-place in Granada. This experience reinforced his determination to embrace monastic life, provided he survived his consort.

## Political Career and Transition
Upon returning to Toledo, Borgia was granted several honors against his own will, including being named commander of the order of St James and viceroy of Catalonia. While serving as governor in Barcelona, he maintained a life of great austerity while efficiently and energetically discharging his official duties.

In 1543, Borgia succeeded his father in the dukedom of Gandia. This transition allowed him to obtain permission to resign his viceroyalty and retire to a life more congenial to his desires at Gandia. During this time, he maintained correspondence with Ignatius Loyola and provided powerful encouragement to the recently founded order of Jesus. One of his primary initiatives upon returning to Gandia was the construction of a Jesuit college.

## Entry into the Society of Jesus
Following the death of Eleanor in 1546, Borgia resolved to become a member of the Society of Jesus. Due to family and political circumstances, he obtained a papal dispensation that permitted him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children.

In 1550, Borgia visited Rome, where he was received with distinction and provided the funding necessary for the construction of the Collegium Romanum. He returned to Spain the following year and formally resigned his estate and rank.

## Contributions and Works
While serving as the duke of Gandia, Francis Borgia petitioned Pope Paul III to have *The Book of the Spiritual Exercises* examined and approved. The pope appointed censors for the Latin and Spanish translations, who reported that the work was wholesome, highly useful, and replete with holiness. Consequently, Paul III confirmed the work on July 31, 1548, via the breve *Pastoralis officii cura*.

St. Francis Borgia authored several works, most notably a treatise titled *Rhetorica Concionandi* and a series of *Exercises* similar to the *Exercitia Spiritualia* of Loyola. His *Opera Omnia* were published in Brussels in 1675. His life was documented by his confessor, Pedro de Ribadeneira.
