# Barthold Heinrich Brockes

Barthold Heinrich Brockes (1680–1747) was a German poet and public official. Born in Hamburg on September 22, 1680, he pursued studies in jurisprudence at Halle. Following a period of extensive travel through Holland, France, and Italy, Brockes returned to his native town of Hamburg in 1704.

## Public Career
Brockes held several significant administrative and political roles throughout his life. In 1720, he was appointed as a member of the Hamburg senate, a position that brought with it various important offices. Later, between 1735 and 1741, he served as a magistrate (_Amtmann_) at Ritzebtütel. He remained in Hamburg until his death on January 16, 1747.

## Poetic Works and Translations
Brockes is best known for his poetic output, most notably a series of nine volumes published between 1721 and 1748 under the title *Irdisches Vergnügen in Gott*. In addition to his original poetry, he was an active translator of foreign works. His translations include:
* *La Strage degli innocenti* by Marini (1715)
* *Essay on Man* by Pope (1740)
* *Seasons* by Thomson (1745)

## Literary Influence and Style
The work of Brockes is regarded as symptomatic of the shifts occurring in German literature at the start of the 18th century. Early in his career, Brockes contributed to the tradition of bombastic imitations of the Italian poet Marini. However, he eventually became one of the first German poets to move away from this style, substituting it with a diction that was simple and clear.

Brockes acted as a pioneer in introducing his countrymen to the new nature poetry originating in England. While his verses are sometimes described as crude or artificial, they introduced a religious interpretation of natural phenomena and a reverential attitude toward nature. This approach was a novelty in German poetry and served to prepare the way for the later poet Gottlieb Friedrich Klopstock.
