# DOLCE

## Ludovico Dolce
Ludovico Dolce, also known as Luigi Dolce (1508–1568 or 1569), was an Italian writer from Venice. Born into a family characterized by an honourable tradition but a decadent fortune, he received a good education and began supporting himself through his writing early in his life. 

Dolce was a prolific translator, producing over seventy works that included translations of Latin and Greek histories, satires, plays, epics, and treatises concerning art and language. He is also recognized as a poet of the romantic epic tradition in Italy.

## Dramatic and Epic Works
In the realm of drama, Dolce is primarily remembered for the tragedy *Marianna*, which focuses on the life of Herod; this work was later recast in French by Voltaire and Tristan and has maintained a presence on the stage. His other dramatic contributions include seven tragedies by Seneca and four comedies described as licentious: *Il Ragazzo* (1541), *Il Capitano* (1545), *Il Marito* (1560), and *Il Ruffiano* (1560).

His epic poetry includes several notable narratives:
* A work that united the stories of the *Aeneid* and the *Iliad* into a single narrative, reduced into *ottava rima*.
* A poem consisting of thirty-nine cantos regarding Primaleone, the son of Palmerius.
* A work celebrating the first exploits of Count Orlando.
* A poem concerning the Paladin Sacripante.

## Historical and Minor Treatises
Dolce produced chief historical accounts of two emperors: Ferdinand I (published in 1566) and Charles V (published in 1560). His minor treatises include *Dialogo nel quale si ragiona del modo di accrescar la memoria* (1552), *Osservazioni sulla lingua volgare* (1550), and *Dialogo della pittura* (1557).

## Influence on Dante's Works
Ludovico Dolce is noted for his role in the publication history of Dante's *Commedia*. While the poet himself was referred to as "divina" as early as 1512, the epithet *divina* did not appear on a title-page of the poem until 1555. This occurred in the edition produced by Ludovico Dolce and printed by Giolito.

## Carlo Dolce
Separate from the writer Ludovico, Carlo Dolce is mentioned in the context of the arts. The Italian engraver Francesco Bartolozzi (1725–1815), who spent nearly forty years residing in London, produced an engraving of the Virgin and Child based on the work of Carlo Dolce, which is cited as one of Bartolozzi's best works.
