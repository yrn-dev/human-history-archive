## Literary and Artistic Figures

Francois le Metel de Boisrobert (1592–1662) was a French poet and a member of the Academy, a plan for which he had suggested to Cardinal Richelieu. Born in Caen and trained in law at the bar at Rouen, Boisrobert later established himself at court in Paris. He was known as a talented raconteur and was one of five poets who implemented Richelieu's dramatic ideas. Throughout his career, he served both Richelieu and later Mazarin, the latter of whom he served faithfully during the Fronde. Boisrobert wrote several volumes of verse and a number of comedies, including *La Belle Plaideuse*, which is said to have influenced Moliere's *L'Avare*. He is also often credited with the licentious *Contes*, though these were published under the name of his brother, D'Ouville. Boisrobert died on March 30, 1662.

In the realm of French theater, the year 1662 marked a significant move for the dramatist Corneille, who relocated his residence from Rouen to Paris. During this period, Corneille produced the work *Sertorius*.
