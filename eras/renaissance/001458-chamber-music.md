# Chamber Music

Chamber music encompasses a wide variety of compositions intended for small ensembles or solo instruments, ranging from the early influence of Italian violinists to the diverse instrumental experiments of the 18th and 19th centuries.

## Early Italian Influence and the Violin
Arcangelo Corelli (1653–1713), an Italian violin-player and composer, produced compositions for the violin that marked an epoch in the history of chamber music. His influence extended beyond Italy, affecting composers such as Sebastian Bach. Corelli's style of execution was of vital importance to the development of violin-playing, though he utilized a limited compass of the instrument, with his violin parts never exceeding D on the first string.

Other Italian composers contributed significantly to the chamber-cantata, a form of chamber music. Emanuele d'Astorga (1681–1736) composed numerous chamber-cantatas for one or two voices; he is regarded as likely the last composer to maintain the traditions of this form as they had been perfected by Alessandro Scarlatti. Similarly, the composer Marcello wrote chamber-cantatas for his mistress, which are held in the library of the Brussels conservatoire.

## Works of J.S. Bach
The chamber music of Sebastian Bach includes a diverse array of sonatas, suites, and trios:
* **Clavier combinations:** Three sonatas for flute and clavier, and six sonatas and a suite for violin and clavier. He also composed three sonatas for clavier and viola da gamba.
* **Wind and String works:** Two flute-sonatas and a violin suite, both featuring figured bass, as well as two trios with figured bass.
* **Solo works:** Six suites for violoncello alone and six sonatas for violin alone (consisting of three sonatas and three partitas).

## The Basset Horn in Chamber Music
The basset horn was a favorite solo instrument in Germany at the end of the 18th century, utilized by instrumentalists such as Lotz, Springer, David, and Czerny. Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was a primary proponent of the instrument in chamber music, composing a short adagio for bassoon and two basset horns, as well as another piece for three basset horns and two clarinets. Later, Mendelssohn composed two concerted pieces (Opp. 113 and 114) for basset horn and clarinet with pianoforte accompaniment, dedicated to Carl and Heinrich Baermann.

## 19th Century Composers
Several 19th-century composers integrated chamber music into their broader catalogs:
* **Antonin Dvorak:** Dvorak produced an abundance of chamber music, much of which maintains a permanent place in concert programs. Early in his career, around 1864, he composed some chamber music that remained unheard in his desk and was later burnt.
* **Rudolph Kreutzer (1766–1831):** A prolific French violinist of German extraction, Kreutzer composed chamber music in addition to nineteen violin concertos and twenty-nine operas. In 1803, Beethoven dedicated his famous violin sonata, Op. 47 (the "Kreutzer"), to him.
* **Francois Adrien Boieldieu (1775–1834):** A French composer known primarily for comic opera, Boieldieu began composing chamber music and songs at a very early age.
