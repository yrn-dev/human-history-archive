# The Poetical Works of Breton (1604-1605)

The early seventeenth century saw the publication of several notable poetical works by Breton, including *The Passionate Shepheard* (1604), *The Soules Immortall Crowne* (1605), and *The Honour of Valour* (1605). These titles are part of a broader body of work produced by the author, which spans several decades and includes both poetry and prose.

## Literary Context and Poetical Works
Breton's poetic output began as early as 1577 with *The Workes of a Young Wit* and *A Floorish upon Fancie*. Over the following years, his work evolved to include a variety of themes and forms. His earlier contributions include *Bryton's Bowre of Delights* (1591), though this was an unauthorized publication containing poems Breton disclaimed, and *The Pilgrimage to Paradise* (1592). 

In the 1590s, Breton produced *Marie Magdalen's Love: a Solemne Passion of the Soules Love* (1595), a work consisting of a prose treatise (likely written by another hand) and a poem written in six-lined stanzas, which is certainly by Breton. He also contributed to miscellanies of verse, such as *England's Helicon*.

At the turn of the century, Breton published several works under the "Pasquil" moniker in 1600, including *Pasquil's Fooles cappe* (entered at Stationers' Hall), *Pasquil's Mistresse*, and *Pasquil's Passe and Passeth Not*. That same year, he wrote *Melancholike Humours*.

## Religious and Spiritual Poetry
A significant portion of Breton's work focuses on spiritual and divine themes, particularly around 1601 and 1602. His publications from 1601 include *A Divine Poem*—which contains "The Blessed Weeper" and "The Ravisht Soul"—as well as *The Soules Heavenly Exercise* and *An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart*. 

In 1602, he continued this exploration of the soul and morality with *The Soules Harmony*, *A True Description of Unthankfulnesse*, and *The Mother's Blessing*. This spiritual trajectory leads into the 1605 publication of *The Soules Immortall Crowne*.

## Other Works and Attributions
Beyond his spiritual and romantic poetry, Breton wrote *Olde Madcappe newe Gaily mawfrey* (1602) and later *An Invective against Treason; I would and I would not* (1614). He also had a partial hand in *The Arbor of Amorous Devises*, which was entered at Stationers' Hall in 1594.

In addition to his poetry, Breton authored twenty-two prose tracts. Notable examples include *Wit's Trenchmour* (1597), *The Wil of Wit* (1599), and *A Poste with a Packet of Mad Letters* (1603).

The archive notes that certain works are erroneously attributed to Breton. These include *The Passion of a Discontented Mind* (1601), *Mary Magdalen's Lamentations* (1604), and *Sir Philip Sidney's Ourania by N.B.* (1606).
