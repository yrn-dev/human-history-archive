# Baber and the Foundation of the Mogul Dynasty

## Early Life and Accession
Baber, also known as Zahir ud-din-Mahomet, was born on February 14, 1483. He was a descendant of Timur and the son of Omar Sheik, who served as the king of Ferghana, a district located in what is now Russian Turkestan. Upon the death of his father in 1495, Baber succeeded to the throne at the age of twelve. Although his uncles attempted to dislodge him from power, these efforts were unsuccessful. Once he was firmly established as the sovereign, Baber began to seek the extension of his dominions.

## Territorial Struggles and Losses
In 1497, Baber attacked and took possession of Samarkand, a city to which he believed he held a natural and hereditary right. However, his hold on his territories was unstable. A rebellion among his nobles resulted in the loss of his native kingdom. While he was marching to recover that kingdom, his troops deserted him, leading to the subsequent loss of Samarkand as well.

Although Baber managed to regain both Samarkand and his native kingdom after several reverses, he faced a formidable opponent in Shaibani (Sheibani) Khan, the ruler of the Uzbegs. In 1501, Shaibani Khan defeated Baber in a great engagement and drove him out of Samarkand.

## Period of Wandering and Recovery
Following his defeat by Shaibani Khan, Baber spent three years wandering in a vain attempt to recover his lost possessions. This period of instability ended in 1504 when he gathered a group of troops and crossed the snowy Hindu Kush. He besieged and captured the strong city of Kabul, a dexterous stroke that provided him with a wealthy new kingdom and completely re-established his fortunes.

In the year following the capture of Kabul, Baber united with Hussain Mirza of Herat to act against Shaibani. This expedition was halted by the death of Hussain, though Baber remained at the capital of Herat for one year.

## Later Challenges and Persistence
Upon returning to Kabul, Baber was forced to quell a formidable rebellion. Two years later, he was driven from his city again due to a revolt among some of the leading Moguls. He was compelled to flee with very few companions. However, Baber's daring and personal courage dismayed the army of his opponents, leading them to return to their allegiance and allowing Baber to regain his kingdom.

In 1510, following the death of Shaibani, Baber once again attempted to obtain possession of his native country. He received considerable aid from Shah Ismael of Persia and made a triumphal entry in 1511. Baber is remembered as a famous conqueror of India and the founder of the Mogul dynasty; his surname, Baber, means "the tiger."
