# Richard Barnfield

## Early Life and Family
Richard Barnfield (1574–1627) was an English poet born in Norbury, Staffordshire. He was the son of Maria Skrymsher and Richard Barnfield (alternatively spelled Barnefield), who were married in April 1572. The couple resided in the parish of Norbury, located in Staffordshire on the borders of Salop. Barnfield was baptized on June 13, 1574.

Family tragedy occurred early in 1581 when his mother died while giving birth to a daughter. Following this event, Elizabeth Skrymsher, the unmarried sister of Maria, appears to have dedicated herself to the care of the children.

## Education and Academic Career
In November 1589, Barnfield matriculated at Brasenose College, Oxford. He completed his degree in February 1592 and performed the exercise required for his master's gown. Despite this progress, he seems to have left the university abruptly and did not proceed to obtain his M.A.

## Literary Career and Influences
It is conjectured that Barnfield arrived in London in 1593. During this period, he became acquainted with Drayton, Watson, and potentially Spenser. Although Sir Philip Sidney had died while Barnfield was still a school-boy, the poet's imagination was strongly affected by Sidney, which served as an inspiration for some of his earliest verses.

In November 1594, at the age of twenty-one, Barnfield published his first work, *The Affectionate Shepherd*. This work was published anonymously and dedicated with familiar devotion to Penelope, Lady Rich. Written in the manner of Shakespeare and Lodge, the work is a florid romance consisting of two books of six-line stanza. It focuses on "the complaint of Daphnis for the love of Ganymede." Barnfield later admitted that the work was a paraphrase or expansion of the second eclogue of Virgil.

## Historical Context and Research
For a long period, the facts of Barnfield's career were unknown, and some had even doubted his existence. His relationship with Shakespeare, though close, remained obscure, though it has long been of interest to students. More recent attention has been drawn to his life due to important discoveries made by the late Dr. A. B. Grosart, who uncovered the details regarding his parentage and birth.
