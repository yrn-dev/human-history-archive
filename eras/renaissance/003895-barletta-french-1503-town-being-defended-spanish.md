# Barletta and the Spanish Defense of 1503

Barletta is a seaport town and episcopal see located in the province of Bari in Apulia, Italy, situated on the east-southeast coast. Historically, the town's importance dates back to the time of the Hohenstaufen. Its architectural landmarks include a Romanesque cathedral and the Gothic church of S. Sepolcro, both begun at the close of the 12th century. A bronze statue of the emperor Heraclius, standing 14 feet high, is located in front of the former church, while the castle behind the cathedral dates from 1537.

## The Siege of 1503

During 1503, Barletta became a focal point of conflict between French and Spanish forces. The town was defended by the Spanish army against a siege by the French. This period of conflict followed a quarrel between the French and Spaniards over booty after the king of Naples, Frederick (brother of Ferdinand), had been despoiled.

The Spanish forces were led by the general known as the Great Captain (Gonzalo de Cordoba). During the end of 1502 and the early part of 1503, the Spanish army, which was much outnumbered by the French, maintained a position in an entrenched camp at Barletta, located near the Ofanto on the shores of the Adriatic.

## Military Strategy of the Great Captain

While stationed at Barletta, the Great Captain adopted a resolute defensive strategy. He refused to be drawn into battle despite the discontent of his own soldiers and taunts from the French. Instead, he utilized flying expeditions of his own men and Aragonese partisans within the country to harass the communications of the French enemy.

The Great Captain's methods at Barletta have been noted for their resemblance to the later Torres Vedras campaign conducted by the duke of Wellington. Once the Spanish were reinforced and the French forces became overly scattered in their efforts to secure supplies, the Great Captain shifted to the offensive. He attacked the French depot of provisions at Cerignola, where he established a strong position using hasty field works strengthened by a type of wire entanglement. This resulted in a headlong front attack by the French, who were repulsed, assailed in the flank, and routed.

## The Disfida di Barletta

In the neighborhood of Barletta, specifically between Corato and Andria, a notable combat occurred during the 1503 siege. This engagement involved thirteen picked knights from France and Italy. The combat resulted in a victory for the Italian knights. This event has been celebrated by Massimo d' Azeglio in his work titled *Disfida di Barletta*.

## Aftermath and Legacy

The operations following the defense of Barletta and the victory at Cerignola continued on the Garigliano, eventually leading to the total expulsion of the French from Naples. Gonzalo de Cordoba remained the governor of Naples until 1507. Despite his success, he aroused the jealousy of King Ferdinand the Catholic, who recalled him. The Great Captain died on December 2, 1515.

The Great Captain is regarded by some as the first of modern generals. He is credited with founding the Spanish infantry of the 17th and 16th centuries and establishing a school of officers that trained the best generals of Charles V.
