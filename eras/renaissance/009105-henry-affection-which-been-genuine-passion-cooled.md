# Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn

## Early Life and Background of Anne Boleyn
Anne Boleyn (born approximately 1507, though some writers suggest 1501 or 1502) was the daughter of Elizabeth and Sir Thomas Boleyn. Her father later became the Earl of Ormonde and the Earl of Wiltshire, while her mother was the daughter of Thomas Howard, who served as the Earl of Surrey and later the Duke of Norfolk. 

In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France, serving as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I. Upon her return to England in 1521 or 1522, Anne attracted numerous suitors and admirers. Among these was the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt. Another suitor, Henry Percy, the heir of the Earl of Northumberland, was prevented from marrying her by the king, who instead provided a match in the person of Sir James Butler. Despite this, Anne remained unmarried.

## Relations with Henry VIII
Between 1522 and 1525, Henry bestowed a series of favours and grants upon Anne's father; some have interpreted these as symptoms of the king's affections, though this is viewed doubtfully. Unlike her sister Mary, who had been a victim of Henry's solicitations, Anne did not intend to become the king's mistress. Instead, she sought to be his queen, and her behavior appeared to be governed entirely by motives of ambition.

The precise start of the relationship between Henry and Anne is unknown. While some surmise it began as early as 1523, there is no proof that Henry's passion existed prior to May 1527, when proceedings for divorce were initiated; the love letters associated with the pair are undated. Anne's name first appeared openly as a potential wife in the event of Catherine's divorce in a letter dated August 16, 1527, written by the imperial ambassador Mendoza to Charles V.
