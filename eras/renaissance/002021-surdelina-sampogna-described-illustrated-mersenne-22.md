# The Surdelina and Sampogna

The *surdelina* (also referred to as the *sourdeline*) and the *sampogna* (or *zampogna*) are complex reed instruments belonging to the bag-pipe family. In the historical classification of such instruments, they are grouped alongside the musette, the Lowland bag-pipe, the union pipes of Ireland, and the Northumbrian or border bag-pipe. In Italy, the *cornemusa* or *piva* are considered to bear the same relationship to the *sampogna* or *surdelina* as the old Irish bag-pipe does to the union-pipes.

## Description and Construction
The *surdelina* is described and illustrated by Marin Mersenne, who identifies it as the *musette de Naples*. Mersenne characterizes the construction of the instrument as very complicated. Its structural components include:

*   **Pipes:** The instrument features four pipes fixed within a single stock. These consist of two chaunters and two drones.
*   **Chaunters:** The two chaunters possess narrow cylindrical bores and are equipped with both keys and finger-holes.
*   **Drones:** Each of the two drones is equipped with ten keys.
*   **Reeds and Inflation:** Double reeds are utilized throughout the instrument. Unlike bag-pipes inflated by the mouth, the bag of the *surdelina* is inflated by means of bellows.

## Origins and Development
Mersenne attributes the invention of the instrument to Dom Julio, Vincenze, and Jean Baptiste Riva, the latter of whom was living in Paris in 1620. However, the archive notes that Mersenne appears to have made his own alterations to the original instrument, though the specific nature of these changes is not clearly explained in the records.

## Related Instruments and Classifications
The *surdelina* is linked to other complex bellows-blown instruments. Mersenne classifies the instrument designed by Afranio—which consisted of two *bassons* interconnected by tubes and blown by bellows—as another kind of fagotto that belongs in the same class as the Neapolitan *sourdeline*. A defining characteristic of Afranio's instrument, similar to the *sourdeline*, is that the pipes only speak when the springs (keys) are open.

In a broader musical context, the *sampogna* was known in Venice, where Passenti published a collection of melodies for the instrument in 1628 titled *Canora Zampogna*.

## General Bag-pipe Mechanics
As a member of the bag-pipe family, the *surdelina* and *sampogna* utilize the fundamental principle of a wind-supply reservoir (the leather bag) to regulate sound-producing pressure via the performer's arm. The general anatomy of such instruments involves an air-tight bag with apertures containing stocks that serve as sockets for the pipes and air-chambers for the reeds. The pipes typically fall into three categories: the insufflation tube (blow-pipe or bellows), the melody-pipe (chaunter), and the drones, which produce a single fixed note.
