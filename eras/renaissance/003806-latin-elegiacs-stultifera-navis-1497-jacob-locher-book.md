# Latin Elegiacs of the Stultifera Navis (1497)

The Latin elegiacs of the *Stultifera Navis*, authored by Jacob Locher and published in 1497, served as a critical vehicle for the dissemination of the *Ship of Fools* concept across Europe. This work is a Latin version of the *Narrenschiff* originally written by Sebastian Brant, which first appeared in 1494. While Brant's original conception and treatment were essentially German, the themes of his hundred and thirteen types of fools possessed a universal interest that allowed the work to be read throughout the continent via Locher's Latin translation.

## Literary Context and Influence
The *Stultifera Navis* represents a significant transition in literary style, moving away from the general allegorical satires of previous centuries toward a blend of narrative, morality, and drama. Unlike earlier works that relied on abstractions, this text utilizes concrete examples to illustrate human folly. These figures include:
* The bibliophile who collects books without learning from them.
* The evil judge who accepts bribes to favor the guilty.
* The old fool whose folly is only strengthened by the passage of time.
* Individuals eager to follow fashions.
* Priests who occupy their time in church telling "gestes" of Robin Hood.

Although the work focuses on "fools," the underlying censure is directed more toward sins and vices than simple follies. This didactic nature influenced later writers, such as Barclay.

## Translations and Versions
The Latin elegiacs provided by Jacob Locher were highly influential and served as a primary source for subsequent translations. Barclay's *The Shyp of Folys of the Worlde*, printed by Richard Pynson in 1509, was translated from Dutch, French, and Latin, though Barclay appeared to be most familiar with the Latin version. Barclay took significant liberties with the text, adding or removing material as he saw fit, adding local color to the fools, and appending his own *envoi* to the end of nearly every section to reinforce the moral.

Additionally, a prose version of Locher's *Stultifera Navis* was produced by Henry Watson and printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1518.

## Illustrations and Iconography
The original 1494 German edition featured woodcuts, possibly devised and partly executed by Sebastian Brant. These illustrations introduced a sense of humor that was absent from the text and provided an impulse for the production of "emblems." These images were later copied in the English version. 

In a later edition of Jacob Locher's *Navis Stultifera* published in Basil in 1506, specifically in titulus 7, there is an illustration depicting a small lute and harp accompanied by the legend *nec cytharum tangit nec barbiton*.
