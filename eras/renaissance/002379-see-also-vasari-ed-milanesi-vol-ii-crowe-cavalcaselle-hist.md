# Italian Renaissance Painters and Art Historians

The study of Italian Renaissance art involves a complex network of artists, their pupils, and the historians who documented their lives and techniques. Key figures such as Titian and Giorgione exerted significant influence over subsequent painters, while historians like Vasari and modern critics like Bernhard Berenson have shaped the academic understanding of these masters.

## Paris Bordone
Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. In 1509, he entered the *bottega* of Titian. According to Vasari, who provides nearly all the known facts regarding Bordone's life, the artist did not spend many years with Titian and instead sought to imitate the style of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, the Giorgionesque elements in Bordone's early works are actually derived from Titian; this imitation was so precise that some of his paintings are still attributed to Titian today.

Bordone's body of work is described as being of unequal merit. His strengths include a nobility of style, a realistic approach to the human figure, and a dignified quality in his portraiture, as well as a "golden harmony of colour" learned from Titian. Conversely, his nudes are noted as being somewhat coarse in form, and his figures often exhibit unnatural or affected action.

As a Renaissance artist, Bordone produced religious pictures, allegories, nymphs, cupids, mythological scenes, and subjects based on the fables of Ovid, though he was most successful as a portraitist. His surviving works include:
*   "Fisherman and Doge" (Venice Academy), considered his principal surviving work.
*   "Daphnis and Chloe" and a portrait of a lady (National Gallery, London).
*   "Holy Family" (Bridgwater House).
*   A "Madonna" in the Tadini collection at Lovere.
*   Paintings in the Duomo of Treviso.
*   Two mythological works located in the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese.

Bordone also executed many important mural paintings in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, though all have perished. In 1538, Francis I invited him to France, where he painted numerous portraits; while no trace of these remains in French collections, two portraits were later acquired by the Louvre. He also completed significant works for the Fugger palace at Augsburg during his return journey, but these have since been lost.

## Lorenzo Lotto
Lorenzo Lotto (c. 1480–1556) was an Italian painter whose birthplace was variously cited as Treviso, Venice, or Bergamo. However, a document published by Dr. Bampo confirms he was born in Venice, and his will suggests 1480 as the likely year of birth.

For a period, Lotto was neglected by art historians, overshadowed by contemporaries such as Palma, Giorgione, and Titian. This changed when Bernhard Berenson wrote an "essay in constructive art criticism" that restored Lotto's position among the great Renaissance masters and clarified his artistic lineage. While earlier authorities attributed his training to Previtali (Crowe and Cavalcaselle), Giovanni Bellini (Morelli), or Leonardo da Vinci (Lomazzo), Berenson proved that Lotto was actually a pupil of Alvise Vivarini. The influence of Vivarini's asceticism and religious severity remained a dominant feature of Lotto's work, even as he later adopted the rich colors associated with Titian and Giorgione.

Lotto is distinguished from his peers by his personal vision, unconventionality, and psychological insight into character.

## Art Historical Sources and Bibliography
The documentation of Italian art relies on a variety of historical and critical texts. Giorgio Vasari is a primary source, with specific references found in the Milanesi edition (volumes ii and iii) and his *Vite di Gentile da Fabriano e Vittor Pisanello* (edited by Venturi). Other significant historical accounts include Ridolfi's *Le Maraviglie* and Francesco Sansovino's *Venezia Descritta*.

For the study of the Bellini family, sources include:
*   *Pittura Veneziana* by Zanetti.
*   *Elagio Storico di Jacopo e Giovanni Bellini* by F. Aghietti.
*   *Cenni intorna la vita e le opere di Jacopo Bellini* by G. Bernasconi.
*   *Giovanni Bellini e pittori contemporanei* by Moschini.
*   Works by Corrado Ricci, including *Jacopo Bellini e i suoi Libri di Disegni*.
*   *Les Dessins de Jacopo Bellini* by Victor Goloubeff, which reproduces the London and Paris sketch-books.

Additional critical perspectives are provided by Crowe and Cavalcaselle in *History of Painting in Italy* (vol. ii) and *History of Painting in North Italy* (vol. i), as well as Bernhard Berenson's *Study and Criticism of Italian Art* (2nd series).
