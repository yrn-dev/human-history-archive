# Ferdinand of Spain and the Peace of Italy

## Military Operations in Naples
During the early 16th century, the presence of Spanish forces in Italy was marked by significant conflict and shifting alliances. Under the leadership of the "Great Captain," Spanish forces were tasked with driving the Turks from Cephalonia and subsequently assisting in the despoiling of Frederick, the king of Naples and brother to Ferdinand. Following the removal of the king of Naples, a quarrel erupted between the Spanish and the French over the resulting booty.

The conflict entered two similar phases. From late 1502 through early 1503, the Spanish army, though outnumbered, maintained a defensive position in an entrenched camp at Barletta near the Ofanto on the Adriatic shores. The Great Captain avoided direct battle, instead utilizing flying expeditions and Aragonese partisans within the country to harass French communications. Once reinforced, the Spanish took the offensive, seizing the French provisions depot at Cerignola. By establishing a strong position with field works and wire entanglements, the Spanish routed a headlong French attack. Subsequent operations on the Garigliano resulted in the total expulsion of the French from Naples.

## Political Relations and Governance
Following the expulsion of the French, Gonzalo (the Great Captain) served as the governor of Naples until 1507. However, his rising influence aroused the jealousy of Ferdinand the Catholic, a typical king of the Renaissance. The death of the queen in 1504 removed a friend of Gonzalo's from the court. While Ferdinand provided the governor with fine words and various titles, he also recalled him as soon as possible, leaving him unemployed until his death in 1515.

## Regional Instability and the Italian Peace
The peace of Italy remained fragile throughout this era. By 1508, the league of Cambray had disturbed the peace of the region, leaving Florence in a precarious position between the powers of France and Spain. This period of instability was followed by the Holy League and the battle of Ravenna, which eventually led to the retirement of the French from Italy. 

The influence of Spain continued to penetrate the peninsula; for instance, the cardinal Giovanni de' Medici brought a Spanish army into Tuscany, leading to the sacking of Prato in August 1512. This event caused extreme terror in Florence, resulting in the deposition of the gonfalonier and the opening of the city gates to the princes of the house of Medici.

## Other Activities of Ferdinand of Aragon
Beyond his Italian interests, Ferdinand of Aragon engaged in maritime exploration and international diplomacy. In 1512, Henry VIII sent an English army to aid Ferdinand against the French. During this time, Ferdinand and his daughter Joanna contemplated an expedition from Santander to explore Newfoundland. In September 1512, Ferdinand summoned Sebastian Cabot to Logroño, appointing him a captain in the navy on October 30 with an annual salary of 50,000 maravedis. Cabot returned to the court in March 1514 to discuss the Newfoundland expedition, but the project was terminated by the death of the king in January 1516.
