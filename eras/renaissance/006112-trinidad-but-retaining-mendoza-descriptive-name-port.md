# Trinidad

The term Trinidad appears in the archive's records in several distinct contexts, referring to a colonial settlement in South America, a Crown colony, a geographical region, and a source of mineral asphalt.

## Colonial History in the La Plata Region
In 1580, Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay, led an expedition down the Paraná river. The primary objective of this mission was to establish a port near the sea to facilitate the provisioning of ships and the receipt of supplies from Spain, a need that had been keenly felt by Spanish colonists residing at Asunción. 

Garay laid out a town in the prescribed Spanish style and constructed a fort above a settlement previously abandoned by Don Pedro de Mendoza. He gave this new town the name "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad." However, in appreciation of the invigorating and agreeable atmosphere of the locality, Garay retained the descriptive name used by Mendoza for the port: Buenos Aires.

## Political and Administrative Status
In the context of British overseas empires as of January 1914, Trinidad is categorized as a Crown colony. In such colonies, the rule of the British Home Government, exercised through the Colonial Office, was described as verging on autocracy.

## Geography and Natural Resources
