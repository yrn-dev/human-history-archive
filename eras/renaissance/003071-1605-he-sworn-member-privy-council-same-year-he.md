# The Privy Council and Related Administrative Bodies

The Privy Council is a high-level administrative and advisory body found in various governmental systems, most notably within the English political structure and the Japanese state. Its functions range from deliberating on important matters of state to providing a framework for the executive cabinet and judicial appeals.

## The English Privy Council and the Cabinet
In English political institutions, the "Cabinet" is a conventional, though not legal, term used to describe the members of the Privy Council who hold the highest executive offices in the state. These individuals direct the government through concerted policy and are responsible for all acts of the crown. A modern cabinet typically consists of sixteen to twenty members. 

Core members of the cabinet include:
* The first lord of the treasury
* The lord chancellor of England
* The lord president of the council
* The lord privy seal
* The five secretaries of state
* The chancellor of the exchequer
* The first lord of the admiralty

Other officials, such as the president of the board of education, the president of the board of trade, the chief secretary for Ireland, and the lord chancellor of Ireland, are usually members of the cabinet, though not necessarily so. In recent times, the cabinet has evolved into an executive committee for the party in power and a consultative committee for the prime minister.

## The Lord President of the Council
The Lord President of the Council is a great officer of state, a member of the ministry, and a member of both the cabinet and the House of Lords. Appointed by a declaration made in council by the sovereign, the Lord President's office became permanent in 1679. Prior to this, formal direction of the Privy Council was handled by the lord keeper of the seal, the lord chancellor, or specific court officials.

Historically, the Lord President held significant responsibilities. Until 1855, the office was responsible for the education department. It also managed duties regarding agriculture (now with the Board of Agriculture and Fisheries) and public health (now with the Local Government Board). Additionally, committees of the lords of the council previously performed work now handled by the Board of Trade and the secretary of state for foreign affairs. Currently, the duties of the Lord President involve drawing up council minutes on subjects not belonging to other state departments and presiding over the council's infrequent meetings.

## Judicial Functions and Legal Reporting
The Privy Council maintains a judicial committee that handles specific legal matters. For example, the juridical committee of the privy council of the United Kingdom hears appeals regarding the legality of acts from the provincial legislatures of Canada, as defined by the British North America Act of 1867.

In the legal field, the Council of Law Reporting (formed in 1863) provides practitioners and students with reports from the House of Lords and superior courts. This includes a king's printer's copy of statutes and weekly notes on new rules made under other acts of parliament and the Judicature Acts, issued by the judicial committee of the privy council.

## The Privy Council in Japan
Under the Japanese system, the Privy Council is organized to deliberate upon important matters of State when consulted by the Emperor. This is distinct from the Ministers of State, who provide advice to the Emperor and are responsible for it. All imperial rescripts, public ordinances, and laws relating to state affairs require the counter-signature of a Minister of State. While judicial powers are exercised by courts of law in the name of the Emperor, the Privy Council remains a separate deliberative body.

## Notable Members and Appointments
Various historical figures have been sworn into different Privy Councils:
* **Henry Balnaves:** A Scottish politician and reformer who was sworn into the privy council of James V before 1540.
* **Baron Kingsdown (Thomas Pemberton Leigh):** A chancery barrister who accepted a privy councillorship and the chancellorship of the duchy of Cornwall, subsequently serving on the judicial committee of the privy council for nearly twenty years.
* **The Earl of Dalhousie:** Sworn into the privy council in May 1843 upon becoming vice-president of the board of trade.
* **Feversham:** Became a member of the privy council under James II and was later given chief command against the rebels under Monmouth in 1685.
