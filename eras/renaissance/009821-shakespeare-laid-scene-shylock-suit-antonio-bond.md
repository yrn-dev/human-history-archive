# Shylock's Suit and the Venetian Court

## Legal Context of the Bond
In the works of William Shakespeare, specifically in the play *The Merchant of Venice*, the plot involves a legal suit brought by Shylock based on a bond involving the character Antonio. The choice of setting for this legal conflict was intentional; Shakespeare laid the scene of Shylock's suit on Antonio's bond in a Venetian court. This decision was made with design, as such a bond would have received "short shrift" had the proceedings taken place in an English court.

## General Principles of Bonds
Within the broader legal framework of the archive's records, certain types of bonds are considered void if they are given to secure actions that run contrary to the policy of the law. Examples of such void bonds include those given to a woman for future cohabitation or "marriage brocage bonds," which are intended to procure a marriage between parties.

Other specific types of bonds include:
*   **Post Obit Bonds:** These are bonds provided by a legatee or expectant heir, which become payable on or after the death of the person from whom the obligor has expectations. These may be set aside if the obligee has demanded unconscionable terms.
*   **Bottomry Bonds:** These are contracts of hypothecation used by a ship's master or owner to borrow money for ship emergencies, such as necessary repairs. The ship (or its keel/bottom) serves as security. If the voyage is completed safely, the obligee is repaid with agreed interest; however, if the ship is totally lost, the obligee loses the money.

## Shakespeare's Dramatic Development
The inclusion of *The Merchant of Venice* occurs during a period of significant artistic growth for Shakespeare. After an early period where he was influenced by the "gore and rant" of Kyd and the style of Marlowe—including potential contributions to the three chronicle dramas titled *Henry VI* and the intensity shown in *Richard III*—Shakespeare moved into his own "vaster and unreturning curve."

He learned from the works of Greene and Lyly, who had displaced "uncouther comedy," allowing Shakespeare to develop a comedy characterized by good-tempered high spirits and poetic, chivalrous fancy. This evolution led to the perfection of his youthful blank verse, which reached a state of full beauty and cordiality in plays such as *A Midsummer Night's Dream*, *Romeo and Juliet*, and *The Merchant of Venice*.

## Other Shakespearean Settings
While the suit between Shylock and Antonio is set in Venice, Shakespeare utilized other locations for his dramas. For instance, the scene of his comedy *Twelfth Night* is laid in Illyria, a name that had disappeared from history but was preserved through literature.
