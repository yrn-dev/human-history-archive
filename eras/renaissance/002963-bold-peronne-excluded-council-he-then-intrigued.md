# Jean Balue and the Interview at Péronne

## Early Life and Rise to Power
Jean Balue (c. 1421–1491) was a French cardinal and minister who served under King Louis XI. Born into humble parentage in Angle, Poitou, Balue's early career was supported by the bishop of Poitiers. By 1461, he had risen to the position of vicar-general for the bishop of Angers. His mastery of intrigue, cunning, and activity brought him to the attention of Louis XI, who appointed him as his almoner. 

Balue's influence grew rapidly within the royal court. In 1465, he was granted the bishopric of Evreux and was named *le premier du grant conseil*. Despite leading a life described as dissolute, he was obtained a cardinalate in 1468.

## The Humiliation at Péronne
The trajectory of Balue's career was altered by the events of October 1468. Louis XI, preferring diplomacy over warfare, sought an interview with Charles the Bold, the Duke of Burgundy. To secure this meeting, Louis provided Charles with a gift of 60,000 golden crowns and obtained a safe conduct. The two met on October 9, 1468, at Péronne.

The interview turned disastrous on October 11 when false news reached Charles that the people of Liége had massacred their ducal governor and bishop at the instigation of the French king. Furious at this perceived duplicity, Charles the Bold took Louis XI prisoner. The king was only released three days later after signing a treaty. The terms required Louis to accompany Charles to the siege of Liége and to grant Flanders freedom from the interference of the parlement of Paris.

## Fall and Imprisonment
While Louis XI attempted to downplay the incident in his correspondence, the event was the greatest humiliation of his life. The king sought a scapegoat for the disaster and accused Cardinal Jean Balue of having plotted the treason at Péronne. Consequently, Balue was excluded from the council.

In retaliation, Balue entered into an intrigue with Charles the Bold against his master. He joined the bishop of Verdun, Guillaume de Harancourt, in a conspiracy to persuade Charles of France to demand the territories of Brie and Champagne—which the king had promised to Charles the Bold—rather than the distant Guienne where the king intended to place him. 

This secret correspondence was intercepted, and on April 23, 1469, Balue was thrown into prison. He remained incarcerated for eleven years. Although some allegations suggested he was kept in an iron cage, records indicate his prison quarters were comfortable enough.

## Later Years and Death
Balue was set at liberty in 1480 through the intervention of Pope Sixtus IV. Following his release, he lived in high favor at the court of Rome, where he received the bishoprics of Albano and later Palestrina. In 1484, he returned to France serving as a legate *a latere*. Jean Balue died at Ancona in 1491.
