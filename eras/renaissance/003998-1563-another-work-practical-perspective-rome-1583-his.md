# Giacomo Barocchio da Vignola

## Biography and Career
Giacomo Barocchio, also known as Barozzi or da Vignola, was an Italian architect born on October 1, 1507, in Vignola, located within the Modenese territory. His professional beginnings were marked by work conducted in several cities, including Perugia, Assisi, Piacenza, and Bologna. Eventually, he was summoned to Rome to serve as the papal architect under Pope Julius III.

In 1564, Barocchio succeeded Michelangelo as the architect of St Peter's. During his tenure there, he executed various portions of the fabric of the building and completed a variety of other works throughout Rome. Beyond his Italian projects, he also supplied the designs for the Escorial. Barocchio died in Rome on July 7, 1573. He was remembered by those who knew him for his noble generosity, amenity of manners, and exquisite taste.

## Architectural Style and Influence
Barocchio was an eminent upholder of the classic style. He maintained this commitment during a period when the architecture of Italy was being corrupted by the style known as baroque. 

There is a historical curiosity regarding the term "baroque." While the term was frequently used to describe a debased form of architecture encouraged by the Jesuits—whose church in Rome was built by Barocchio—the word itself is not derived from the architect's name. Instead, "baroque" originates from the Spanish words *berrueco* or *barrueco*, which refer to a pearl that is imperfectly round.

## Written Works
Barocchio contributed to the theoretical understanding of his craft through two significant publications:
*   ***Five Orders of Architecture***: Published in Rome in 1563, this is described as an excellent work.
*   ***Practical Perspective***: Published in Rome in 1583.
