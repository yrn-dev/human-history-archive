# John Ashburnham, 1st Baron Ashburnham

John Ashburnham (1656–1710) was an English nobleman who was created Baron Ashburnham in 1689. He was the son of William Ashburnham, who was the eldest of eight children born to an earlier generation of the Ashburnham family.

## Lineage and Succession
John Ashburnham was succeeded upon his death in 1710 by his second son, also named John (1687–1737). This second John became the 3rd Baron Ashburnham and was later elevated in rank in 1730, when he was created earl of Ashburnham and Viscount St Asaph. The family line continued through direct descendants, including the 5th earl (born 1840).

## Family History and Literary Records
The Ashburnham family history includes several notable figures and publications. An ancestor, John Ashburnham (c. 1603–1671), was a prominent English Royalist and a trusted adviser to the king. He served as the treasurer of the royal army during the Civil War and was a commissioner at Uxbridge in 1644. He was also a member of parliament for Hastings, representing the borough in 1628 and during the Long Parliament of 1640.

Due to his role in the king's flight from Oxford in 1646 and his subsequent advice regarding the king's refuge in the Isle of Wight, the elder John Ashburnham faced accusations of treachery and disloyalty. While Clarendon criticized his excessive self-confidence and folly, he absolved him of criminal intent, and Ashburnham was acquitted by both Charles I and Charles II.

Several documents related to this period of family history were published in later years:
*   *A Letter from Mr Ashburnham to a Friend* (1648), which defended John Ashburnham's conduct regarding the king.
*   *A Letter to W. Lenthall* (1647), which repudiated claims that the king had violated his parole.
*   A longer *Narrative*, published in 1830 by George, the 3rd earl of Ashburnham, though this publication included an unconvincing and uncritical championship of the ancestor.

## The Ashburnham Library
The family is also associated with the famous Ashburnham library. This collection was amassed by Bertram Ashburnham (1797–1878), the 4th earl, and was subsequently dispersed in 1883 and 1884.
