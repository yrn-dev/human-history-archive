# Works of Boiardo and Related Literary Collections

The literary output of Boiardo includes a variety of comedies, poetic collections, and translations. His recorded works include the comedy *Il Timone*, published in Scandiano in 1500 as a quarto (4to). His poetic contributions are represented by *Sonnetti e Canzoni*, published in Reggio in 1499 (4to), and *Carmen Bucolicon*, published in Reggio in 1500 (4to).

Boiardo also produced works in *terza rima*, specifically *Cinque Capitoli in terza rima*, which was published in Venice in either 1523 or 1533. His translation work is evidenced by *Apulejo dell' Asino d'Oro*, published in Venice in 1516 and 1518, and *Asino d'Oro de Luciano tradolto in volgare*, published in Venice in 1523 as an octavo (8vo). Additionally, he is associated with *Erodoto Alicarnasseo istorico, tradotto di Greco in Lingua Italiana*, published in Venice in 1533 and 1538 (8vo), and *Rerum Italicarum Scriptores*. Further details on Boiardo are contained in a nine-volume work by Panizzi published between 1830 and 1831.

## Other Notable Poetic Collections

Separate from the works of Boiardo, the archive contains records of the *Canzoniere* of Dante. This collection consists of canzoni, ballate, and sonnetti. While many are genuine, others are identified as spurious. Some poems attributed to Dante are actually the work of Dante de Maiano, who utilized a harsher style, while others attributed to Aldighiero are referable to Dante's grandsons or his sons, Pietro and Jacopo. Other poems may be attributed to predecessors and contemporaries such as Cino da Pistoia.

The genuine poems of Dante are considered to be scarcely inferior to those of Petrarch. A significant number of these were printed in *Sonetti e canzoni* by Giunta in 1527. The edition of the *Canzoniere* published by Barbera in Florence, edited by Fraticelli, is noted as the best edition; it includes seventy-eight genuine poems, fifty-four spurious poems, and eight that are doubtful. This collection also features an Italian paraphrase in *terza rima* of the seven penitential psalms, as well as paraphrases of the ten commandments, the seven sacraments, the Credo, the Ave Maria, and the Lord's Prayer.

## Historical and Literary Compilations

The archive also notes the contributions of Ludovico Antonio Muratori (1672–1750), a writer who spent nearly 60 years in the libraries of Modena and Milan. Muratori produced several significant works on Italian history, including:

*   *Rerum Italicarum Scriptores* (A.D. 500—1500), a 28-volume folio work published in Milan between 1723 and 1751.
*   *Antiquitates Italiæ Medii Ævi*, a six-volume folio published in Milan (1738–1743) containing 75 dissertations on the government, religion, and manners of Italians during the "darker ages."
*   *Dissertazioni sopra le Antiquita Italiane*, a three-volume quarto published in Milan in 1751, serving as a free version of the Latin text of the *Antiquities*.
*   *Annali d’ Italia*, an 18-volume octavo published in Milan (1753–1756) that provides an abridgment of Italian history from the birth of Christ to the middle of the 18th century.
*   *Dell’ Antichita Estense ed Italiane*, a two-volume folio published in Modena in 1717 and 1740.
