# The Lucas-Passion and Forkel's Reports

## The Reports of J. N. Forkel
J. N. Forkel provided several statements regarding the output of the composer Bach. One such claim was that Bach wrote five *Jahrgange* of church cantatas, which would imply the existence of enough works to provide one for every holy day and Sunday over a five-year period. This statement suggests that approximately 80 cantatas have been lost, though evidence from text-books, fragments, and other sources indicates that no more than seven or six cantatas are actually missing, suggesting Forkel's figure was a great exaggeration.

Additionally, Forkel reported that Bach composed five Passions.

## The Authenticity of the Lucas-Passion
The question of whether a genuine Lucas-Passion exists is central to explaining Forkel's report of five Passions. In addition to the well-known John and Matthew Passions, there is a work according to St Luke that exists in an indisputable Bach autograph. However, this specific work is considered so worthless that responsible critics argue the only reason Bach would have made a copy of it was due to a personal interest.

The existence of a genuine Lucas-Passion would provide an explanation for Forkel's claim that there were five Passions in total.

## Other Related Works
Regarding the other Passions mentioned in the context of Bach's work, there was a Passion according to St Mark that is now lost. Based on movements preserved in the *Trauer-Ode*, it is judged that the St Mark Passion was larger in scale than the Passion according to St John.

The archive also notes that some portions of the Christmas oratorio contain sources that are not definitely known; these include several lost secular works. These portions appear as fair copies in the autograph, similar to other duplicated numbers.
