# William Lilly

## Association with Elias Ashmole
William Lilly was an astrologer active in London during the mid-17th century. In 1646, he was among a group of astrologers whom Elias Ashmole met in London. Ashmole, an English antiquarian and founder of the Ashmolean Museum, had an interest in astrology that was aroused in part by Lilly and in part by Captain (later Sir) George Wharton. While Ashmole's interest in the field was stimulated by these encounters, it appears to have subsided in the following years as he shifted his focus toward antiquarian research and heraldry.

## Context of Astrology in History
Lilly practiced astrology during a period when the field had a long and varied history. Astrology is defined as the ancient science or art of divining the future and fate of human beings based on the positions of the planets, moon, and sun. Historically, a distinction was made between "natural astrology," which predicts eclipses and the motions of heavenly bodies, and "judicial astrology," which focuses on the influence of stars on human destiny.

The practice originated in a developed form among the ancient Babylonians and spread to other nations. It reached Greece around the middle of the 4th century B.C. and Rome before the Christian era. It was later developed by the Arabs between the 7th and 13th centuries. By the 14th and 15th centuries in Europe, astrologers served as dominating influences at court.

## Transition from Astronomy
For many ages, astrology (sometimes called astromancy) and astronomy were identified as the same. The separation of the two disciplines began to occur in the 16th century. This shift was aided by the system of Copernicus, which established the conviction that the earth is itself one of the heavenly bodies. Earlier, figures such as Isidore of Seville (who died in 636) were among the first to distinguish between the two fields.
