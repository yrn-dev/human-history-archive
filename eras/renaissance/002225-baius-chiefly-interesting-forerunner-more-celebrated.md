# Michael Baius

Michael Baius, also known as De Bay, was a Belgian theologian who lived from 1513 to 1589. Born in Melun in Hainault, he received his education at Louvain University, where he achieved distinguished success in the study of theology and philosophy. This academic success led to a series of appointments, including his 1552 appointment by Charles V as a professor of scriptural interpretation at the university.

## Academic Career and Influence
Baius established a significant reputation at Louvain as a leader within the 16th-century anti-scholastic reaction. The proponents of this movement operated under the banner of St Augustine. Baius's own Augustinian predilections eventually brought him into conflict with Rome regarding matters of free-will and grace.

In 1563, Baius was nominated as one of the Belgian representatives for the council of Trent; however, he arrived too late to play an important role in the council's deliberations. Despite his theological conflicts, he maintained his professional standing at Louvain, becoming the university's chancellor in 1575. He held both the chancellorship and his professorship until his death in 1589.

## Conflicts with Rome
The theological positions of Baius led to official condemnation by the papacy. In 1567, Pope Pius V issued the Bull *Ex omnibus afflictionibus*, which condemned seventy-nine propositions found in the writings of Baius. While Baius submitted to this condemnation, a renewal of the condemnation occurred in 1579 under Gregory XIII, prompted by indiscreet utterances from Baius and his supporters.

## Theological Characteristics and Legacy
Baius is regarded as chiefly interesting as a forerunner of Cornelius Jansen, a more celebrated figure. The scholar Harnack described the writings of Baius as a curious mixture of unconscious tendencies toward Protestantism and Catholic orthodoxy. The most noticeable aspect of his work is the great importance placed upon the fact of sin, encompassing both actual and original sin.

## Published Works
The principal works of Michael Baius were published in a collected form in Cologne in 1696, consisting of one 4to volume divided into two parts. Some of his larger treatises remain unpublished. A detailed study of Baius and his books was published in 1867 by Linsenmann, titled *Michael Baius, und die Grundlegung des Jansenismus*, in Tuebingen.
