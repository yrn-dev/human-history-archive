# Aldus Manutius

Aldus Manutius was a Roman printer who established his press in Venice around the year 1494. He is recognized as a significant figure in the history of printing, particularly for his contributions to the preservation and dissemination of Greek literature.

## Printing and Literary Contributions
The press of Aldus Manutius produced over sixty substantial works of Greek literature, the vast majority of which were printed for the first time. His output included several editions of works by the same authors, with some authors receiving two, three, or four separate editions. In 1536, Manutius printed the commentaries on books 1-4, 7 (partially), and 8 of the *Nicomachean Ethics* by Aspasius, alongside those of Eustratius and others.

In 1502, Manutius introduced a new "italic" type, which was likely derived from the handwriting of his friend Bembo. Using this type, he produced the first "pocket" edition of the *Commedia* (Dante), with a second edition following in 1515.

## Influence and Intellectual Context
Manutius's work was situated within a broader movement of Greek studies in Italy. During this period, the progress of these studies was marked by the replacement of Latin translations with editions of original Greek texts. However, the dissemination of this knowledge was sometimes met with resistance; some Italians sought to maintain a monopoly on Greek learning. This was evident when Aldus prepared to publish the Greek scholiasts on Euripides and Sophocles, prompting warnings that such an act would allow "Barbarians" to remain at home and reduce the number of people traveling to Italy.

Beyond his printing press, Manutius served as a teacher. Alberto Pio of Carpi, who was responsible for the fortifications, colonnades, and various Renaissance buildings in Carpi (including a palace and the nave of S. Niccolo), was a pupil of Aldus Manutius.

## Book Bindings and Aesthetics
Books associated with Aldus Manutius are noted for their specific aesthetic qualities. Several of his books were bound in dark morocco, featuring small dolphins and "Aldine" knot leaves executed in both gold and blind tooling. This style was similar to that used by the Florentine printer Giunta, although Giunta's bindings did not include the dolphins.

## Market Impact and Piracy
The success and specific formats of the Aldine press led to significant imitation and piracy. Manutius produced "octavo" editions of the classics, which were later targeted by printers in Lyons who created "glaring piracies" known as Lyonnese counterfeits. In the second quarter of the 16th century, French printers in Lyons and Paris further reduced the size of these Aldine octavos to create small sextodecimos.

Additionally, the prestige and privileges associated with the Aldine press led some competitors to use fictitious imprints to evade legal restrictions. For example, in 1499, a printer in Brescia used the spurious imprint "Florentiae" for an edition of Politian to bypass the privilege granted to Aldus.
