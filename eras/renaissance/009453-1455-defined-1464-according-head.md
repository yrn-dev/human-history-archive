# Events and Figures of the Mid-15th Century

The mid-15th century was marked by significant legal disputes in Germany, political instability in Japan, and regional conflicts in the borderlands of Britain.

## Legal and Commercial Disputes in Mainz
In Mainz, the early German printer Johann Fust, a member of a wealthy burgher family known to have flourished from 1423, was involved in a notable legal conflict with Gutenberg. While some accounts characterize Fust as the inventor of printing or Gutenberg's patron, others describe him as a speculator. 

The Helmasperger document dated November 6, 1455, records that Fust had advanced money to Gutenberg—specifically 800 guilders in 1450 and another 800 in 1452—to support his work. In 1455, Fust brought a suit against Gutenberg to recover a total of 2026 guilders for principal and interest. Gutenberg claimed that Fust had not paid 300 guilders annually for wages and expenses as agreed, and further asserted that Fust had stated he had no intention of claiming interest. The suit was decided in Fust's favor on November 6, 1455, in the refectory of the Barefooted Friars of Mainz, following an oath by Fust that he had borrowed 1550 guilders to give to Gutenberg.

## Political Conflict in Japan
In Japan, the year 1455 marked a transition in the house of Hatakeyama following the death of Tokuhon (also known as Mochikuni). The shogun recognized Masanaga as the head of the house, a decision that was not accepted by Yoshinari. This led to a military confrontation in 1456, where Yoshinari marched a Kawachi army against Masanaga; a deadly struggle was only avoided through the shogun's intervention.

The Hatakeyama subsequently split into two families. While Masanaga's branch was more powerful, Yoshinari gained favor at Muromachi and was named *kwanryo*. However, after the shogun's goodwill was estranged, Yoshinari fled Kyoto while being pursued by Masanaga, who held a commission from Muromachi to kill him. A conflict lasting seven years (1460–1467) took place in Yamato and Kawachi. Yoshinari, noted for his skill as a strategist, eventually gained the open support of Yamana Sozen. Consequently, Masanaga, who had been named *kwanryo* in 1464, was forced to give way to Shiba Yoshikado, the nominee of Sozen.

## Regional Strife and Governance in Europe
In the border regions between England and Scotland, the mid-15th century was a period of lawlessness and family feuds. One of the most serious encounters during this time occurred at Arkenholme (Langholm) in 1455. This region was characterized by frequent raids and fights involving families such as the Maxwells, Johnstones, Grahams, Eliots, and Armstrongs.

In the Dutch city of Bolsward, the medieval constitution featured a government consisting of four councillors with administrative functions and eight *scabini* with judicial functions. The family of Jongema held hereditary rights in the administration. While these rights were not mentioned in the town charter of 1455, they were defined in the charter of 1464. This later charter established that the head of the Jongema family would serve two years with the *scabini* and the third year with the councillors, maintaining the right to administer an oath to one member of each body. Additionally, the clergy held an influential position in civic administration, and in certain cases, appeals from the judgment of the *scabini* could be made to the clergy in conjunction with the councillors.
