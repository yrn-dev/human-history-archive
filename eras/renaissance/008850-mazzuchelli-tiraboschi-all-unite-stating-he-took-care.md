# Matteo Maria Boiardo

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1434–1494) was an Italian poet born at Scandiano, a seignorial estate of his family located near Reggio di Modena. He came from an illustrious and noble house that was established at Ferrara, although the family originally hailed from Reggio. There is some discrepancy regarding his birth date; Tiraboschi places it around the year 1434, while Mazzuchelli dates it to 1420.

## Education and Public Career
Boiardo entered the university of Ferrara at an early age. During his studies, he acquired a strong proficiency in Latin and Greek, as well as knowledge of Oriental languages. He eventually earned doctorates in both law and philosophy.

His professional life was closely tied to the court of Ferrara, where he enjoyed the favor of Duke Borso d'Este and the subsequent duke, Hercules. Boiardo was appointed to several honorable positions, including the governorship of Reggio, a role he held in 1478. In 1481, he was elected captain of Modena and was later reappointed as the governor of the citadel and town of Reggio. He died in 1494, though the specific month of his death is not recorded.

## Orlando Inamorato
Boiardo is best known for his great poem, *Orlando Inamorato*. While the work was composed for the amusement of the court of Duke Hercules, Boiardo typically did not write within the court's precincts. Instead, he retired to his estates, such as Scandiano, to focus on his compositions.

According to the combined accounts of Tiraboschi, Mazzuchelli, Vallisnieri, and Castelvetro, Boiardo took care to include descriptions of the agreeable environs of his chateau within the poem. Furthermore, these sources state that he drew the names of several of his heroes—including Agramant, Sacripant, Gradasse, and Mandricardo—from the names of his own peasants. He chose these names because their "uncouthness" made them appear appropriate for Saracen warriors.

## Literary Influence and Structure
The *Orlando Inamorato* is regarded as one of the most significant works of Italian literature and served as the first example of a romantic epic that could act as a model, subsequently influencing Ariosto's *Orlando Furioso*. 

Regarding the poem's inspiration, Mazzuchell and Gravina stated that Boiardo used Homer's *Iliad* as his model. In this comparison, Angelica occupies the role of Helen, and Paris is besieged in a manner similar to the city of Troy, making the poem a "reflex image" of the *Iliad*. However, the actual subject matter of the work is derived from the *Fabulous Chronicle* of the pseudo-Turpin. Aside from the presence of principal warriors such as Oliver, Roland, and Charlemagne, there is little resemblance between the detailed plot of the *Fabulous Chronicle* and Boiardo's poem.

## Publication History
Boiardo did not live to finish the poem. It was printed in Scandiano the year following his death under the supervision of his son, Count Camillo. Although the book itself is without a date, a Latin letter from Antonia Caraffa di Reggio prefixed to the poem is dated the kalends of June 1495. A second edition appeared in Venice before the year 1500, and the work was reprinted in Venice twice more during the following twenty years.
