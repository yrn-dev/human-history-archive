# Elias Ashmole

Elias Ashmole (1617–1692) was an English antiquarian and the founder of the Ashmolean Museum at Oxford. Born on May 23, 1617, in Lichfield, he was the son of a saddler.

## Early Career and Military Service
Ashmole began his professional life as a solicitor in 1638 and was appointed as a commissioner of excise in 1644. This appointment brought him to Oxford during the sitting of the Royalist Parliament. While there, he formed a friendship with Captain George Wharton (later Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole received a commission from the king to serve as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse.

## Personal Life and Interests
In 1646, Ashmole became the first amateur or gentleman to be "accepted" as a Freemason. During this same year, he met William Lilly and other astrologers in London; his interest in astrology was further encouraged by Wharton. However, in subsequent years, this interest subsided as he turned his attention toward antiquarian research and heraldry.

Ashmole's domestic life was marked by two marriages. He married Lady Mainwaring in 1649; she was a relative of his first wife, who had died eight years prior, and was approximately twenty years his senior. This marriage provided Ashmole with the affluence necessary to dedicate his full time to his studies. Although his wife petitioned for a separation in 1657, she failed to win her case and returned to live with him until her death in 1668. In that same year, Ashmole married the daughter of Sir William Dugdale.

## Court Appointments and Public Office
Between the crisis of his wife's separation petition and her death in 1668, Ashmole enjoyed high favor at court. During this period, he held a succession of offices, including:
* Windsor herald
* Commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

Despite his standing, Ashmole refused the office of Garter king-at-arms, allowing it to go to Sir William Dugdale.

## Antiquarian Research and the Ashmolean Museum
Ashmole was a diligent researcher of heraldry and the orders of knighthood. In 1672, he published *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*, an exhaustive work resulting from years of patient study. In this work, Ashmole discussed the nature of knighthood, contending that it ennobles the recipient; he argued that when a king grants this dignity to an ignoble person to recompense merit, the king is understood to confer everything required to complete the status, meaning a knight is necessarily a gentleman. He also wrote on the Knights of the Bath, suggesting that Henry IV did not institute a new order but restored an ancient manner of making knights, meaning Knights of the Bath were essentially knights bachelors created with specific ceremonies. Additionally, Ashmole identified the principal ensigns of knighthood, both ancient and modern, as including a gold chain or collar, gilt spurs, a belt and sword, a shield and lance, a gold ring, and a horse.

In 1677, Ashmole presented the university of Oxford with the Ashmolean Museum, the first public museum of curiosities in the kingdom. The majority of the collection had been inherited from his friend, John Tradescant. Ashmole stipulated that a suitable building must be erected to house the collection, and the museum was not finally installed until 1683. He later provided a further gift of his library to the university.
