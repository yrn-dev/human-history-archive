# David Rizzio and the Events of 1566

## Role and Influence
David Rizzio was an Italian who served as a close associate of Mary, Queen of Scots. His relationship with the Queen was viewed with suspicion by her husband, Henry, Lord Darnley, who believed that Mary's relations with Rizzio were detrimental to him as a husband. By 1565, Rizzio was reported to be the Queen's greatest favorite, second only to Sir James Balfour.

## The Murder of Rizzio
The tension surrounding Rizzio's influence led Lord Darnley to be easily persuaded to agree to the Italian's murder. Darnley himself took part in the commission of this crime. Following the murder, Queen Mary flattered and cajoled Darnley, who subsequently betrayed his fellow conspirators to her. This betrayal assisted the Queen in her escape from Holyrood to Dunbar. As a result of these revelations, Darnley was distrusted and deserted by the companions who had helped him kill Rizzio, and he eventually lost the favor of the Queen.

Other figures were also involved in the plot; for instance, Sir William Kirkcaldy of Grange is noted as having been accessory to the murder of Rizzio. In the aftermath of the event, Mary sought shelter at Dunbar Castle.

## Related Conspiracies of 1566
The year 1566 was marked by further political instability and plots. Sir James Balfour, a Scottish judge, politician, and secretary to Mary, was knighted and appointed lord-clerk-register in 1566. According to the Queen, a plot to murder Balfour was intended to take place in 1566 alongside the murder of Rizzio.

Later in the year, tensions between Mary and Darnley escalated. In December 1566, the couple had a confrontation involving bitter words at Stirling, where the baptism of their son, James, was being celebrated. Darnley remained apart from the festivities during this time. Simultaneously, a band of nobles met at Craigmillar to plot the liberation of Mary from her husband. While the initial intent may have been to secure a divorce, the conspiracy shifted toward the decision that Darnley must be killed.

## Aftermath and the Role of Bothwell
The political turmoil of 1566 led into the events of 1567. Sir James Balfour became an adherent of Bothwell and was deeply implicated in the murder of Darnley, although he was not present when the crime was committed. Balfour is believed to have drawn up the bond for the murder at Craigmillar and the marriage contract between Mary and Bothwell. He also facilitated Darnley's lodging at Kirk o' Field, his brothers' house, where the murder eventually occurred on February 9, 1567.
