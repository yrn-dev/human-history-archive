# The Philosophical Works of the Instauratio Magna

The philosophical writings of Francis Bacon are categorized into three distinct groups based on their relationship to his comprehensive scheme known as the *Instauratio*. The first group consists of writings originally intended for the *Instauratio* but later superseded or discarded. The second group comprises works connected to the scheme but not directly included in its plan. The third group consists of the writings that actually formed part of the *Instauratio Magna*.

## Structure of the Instauratio Magna

The *Instauratio Magna* was designed as a reorganization of the sciences intended to restore man's command over nature, a power lost following the fall. In its final form, this great work consisted of six divisions. 

The first division, *Partitiones Scientiarum*, serves as a survey of the sciences, acting as an inventory of the possessions of the human mind. This survey includes sciences that existed at the time as well as those requiring new construction. The classification used for this survey is based on an analysis of the objects and faculties of human knowledge and is represented by the *De Augmentis Scientiarum*.

The second division, *Interpretatio Naturae*, focuses on the new method used to train and direct the human mind toward the renovation of science. This follows the initial survey of existing inventions and discoveries. This division is represented, albeit imperfectly, by the *Novum Organum*, specifically book ii.

## The Role of Natural History and the Organum

Bacon's devised method was characterized by mechanical simplicity and absolute certainty. However, he recognized that this method could only produce fruit and be applicable if the phenomena of the universe were completely arranged and tabulated. This necessity led to his emphasis on a complete natural history.

Bacon viewed the *Organum* and the *Natural History* as correlative and equally necessary components of a true philosophy; their union produced the new philosophy. In the preface to the works forming the third part of the *Instauratio*, Bacon stated that his *Organum*, even if completed, would not significantly advance the *Instauration of the Sciences* without the *Natural History*. Conversely, he believed the *Natural History* without the *Organum* would advance it "not a little."

Regarding the difficulty of collecting such a vast amount of data, Bacon argued in a letter to Jean Antoine Baranzano that a store of instances must be procured or the business abandoned. He asserted that the true natural history should consist of nothing except canons, observations, connections, and instances, even if the description of these instances were to fill six times as many volumes as the history written by Pliny.

## Related and Superseded Works

Outside of the formal *Instauratio Magna*, Bacon produced several other significant philosophical tracts. Among the works connected to the scheme but not included in it is the *New Atlantis*, a philosophic romance describing an ideal state where state guidance and political machinery implement the principles of the new philosophy. Bacon intended to add a second part regarding the laws of a model commonwealth, but this was never written. Other connected works include the *De Principiis atque Originibus secundum Fabulas Cupidinis et Caeli*, which discusses the nature of primitive matter, the origin of things, and the limits of science; the *Cogitationes de Natura Rerum* (likely written in 1605); and the *De Fluxu et Refluxu Maris* (likely written around 1616).

There are also important tracts that were superseded by more elaborate works. These include:
*   *The Advancement of Learning*: This was completely worked into the *De Augmentis* and serves as the first part of the *Instauratio*.
*   *Valerius Terminus*: Composed around 1603, it provides an obscure outline of the first two parts of the *Instauratio*.
*   *Temporis Partus Masculus*: A fragment noted for its arrogant style; the term *masculus* refers to the fruit-bearing power of the new philosophy.
*   *Redargutio Philosophiarum*: An oration from 1608 or 1609 that details much of what later appears in the *Novum Organum* book i regarding the *Idola Theatri*.
*   *Cogitata et Visa*: Dating from 1607, this work contains the substance of the first book of the *Organum*.
*   *Descriptio Globi Intellectualis*: A work intermediate between the *Advancement* and the *De Augmentis* that details the general classification of the sciences.
