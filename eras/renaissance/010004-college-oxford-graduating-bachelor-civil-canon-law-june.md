# Oxford University Academic Degrees and Colleges

The University of Oxford is a historic educational institution featuring various residential colleges and a structured system of academic degrees. The university's academic framework includes the awarding of bachelor's and master's degrees across several disciplines, including law, divinity, and the arts.

## Academic Degrees and Qualifications

Degrees at Oxford are granted based on specific academic achievements and timeframes. In the field of law, the university has granted degrees such as the bachelor of civil and canon law. For example, Edmund Bonner graduated with a bachelor of civil and canon law in June 1519, later becoming a Doctor of Civil Law (D.C.L.) in 1525.

Other academic paths include the Master of Arts (M.A.). John Bampton graduated M.A. in 1712, and Robert Lowth graduated M.A. in 1737. In some instances, students have been granted bachelor's degrees before the statutory time due to exceptional academic work. Benjamin Kennicott, who distinguished himself in divinity and Hebrew, achieved this honor after publishing two dissertations: *On the Oblations of Cain and Abel* and *On the Tree of Life in Paradise, with some Observations on the Fall of Man*.

## Residential Colleges and Halls

The university is composed of various colleges and halls that provide education and residence for students.

### Trinity College and Durham College
The history of Trinity College is linked to the ecclesiastical foundation at Durham. Prior Richard de Hoton (1290-1308) originally established a hall in Oxford for students from Durham. In 1380, Bishop Hatfield refounded this establishment as Durham College. Following the surrender of the abbey of Durham's possessions in 1540, Durham College existed as a secular foundation for a few years before becoming Trinity College on a new foundation in 1555.

## University Appointments and Lectures

Oxford maintains various professorships and specialized lecture series. Robert Lowth was appointed professor of poetry in 1741, while Canon Robert Payne Smith served as the regius professor of divinity. Edward King held the position of regius professor of pastoral theology. Additionally, Thomas Chenery served as a professor of Arabic.

The university also hosts the Bampton lectures, established by the will of John Bampton. These annual lectures are delivered in the University Church on Sunday mornings during full term. The lecturer must be a Master of Arts from either Oxford or Cambridge and is chosen by the heads of colleges on the fourth Tuesday of Easter term. The lectures focus on establishing the Christian faith, the divinity of the Holy Ghost, the divinity of Jesus Christ, the authority of the Holy Scriptures, and the writings of the primitive fathers. Each lecturer is provided an endowment of L120 and must publish the lectures within two months of delivery.
