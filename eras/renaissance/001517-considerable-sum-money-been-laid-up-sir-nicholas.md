# Financial Provisions of Sir Nicholas Bacon

## Family and Early Life of Francis Bacon
Francis Bacon (1561–1626) was an English essayist, statesman, and philosopher born on January 22, 1560/1, at York House in the Strand, London. He was the youngest son of Sir Nicholas Bacon and his second wife, a daughter of Sir Anthony Cooke. His mother was a warm adherent of the Puritan or Reformed Church and a woman of considerable culture skilled in classical studies. Due to his delicate health, Francis likely received much of his early instruction at home before entering Trinity College, Cambridge, in April 1573. 

During his three years at Cambridge, where he resided with his brother Anthony, Francis studied the sciences. He concluded that the results and methods of the time were erroneous and came to despise current Aristotelian philosophy, viewing it as fruitless and useful only for disputation. He left the university with the conception that a new method must be devised to teach philosophy its true purpose.

## The Estate Fund and Financial Misfortune
Sir Nicholas Bacon had laid up a considerable sum of money specifically intended for the purchase of an estate for his youngest son, Francis, who was the only one of the children otherwise unprovided for. However, Sir Nicholas died suddenly in February 1578/9. Because of the sudden nature of his death, the intention to purchase the estate was not carried out.

As a result, Francis received only a fifth of the money that had been set aside. This event is described as one of the gravest misfortunes of his life. Starting with insufficient means, Francis developed a habit of borrowing and remained in debt for the rest of his life. This financial instability necessitated that he adopt a profession, leading him to select the law and take up residence at Gray's Inn in 1579.

## Professional and Political Development
Prior to the death of his father, Francis had begun his political and legal education. On June 27, 1576, he and his brother Anthony were entered *de societate magistrorum* at Gray's Inn. Shortly thereafter, he was sent to Paris with the English ambassador, Sir Amyas Paulet. The disturbed state of French society and government during this period provided him with valuable political instruction. While it was previously believed that his *Notes on the State of Christendom* resulted from these observations, Spedding has shown there is no reason to ascribe these notes to Francis; they are more probably attributed to a correspondent of his brother Anthony.

## Legacy of Sir Nicholas Bacon
Sir Nicholas Bacon served as the lord keeper. His influence extended beyond his immediate family; for example, Sir Thomas Bromley, who was born in Staffordshire in 1530 and educated at Oxford, progressed quickly in his legal profession through family influence and the patronage of Sir Nicholas. Following the death of Sir Nicholas Bacon in 1579, Sir Thomas Bromley was appointed lord chancellor.
