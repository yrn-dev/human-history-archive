# Horizons of the Renaissance: The Age of Discovery

## Overview
The Age of Discovery, coinciding with the height of the Renaissance, marked a pivotal shift in global geopolitics, trade, and human understanding. Driven by the desire to bypass Ottoman control of overland silk and spice routes, European powers—primarily Portugal and Spain—funded maritime expeditions to find direct sea routes to Asia. This era was characterized by a synthesis of Renaissance humanism, which encouraged empirical observation, and advancements in cartography and navigation (such as the use of the astrolabe and the development of the caravel). While these voyages expanded the known world, they also initiated centuries of colonialism and the devastating impact of Old World diseases on indigenous populations.

## Key Facts

### Christopher Columbus
Sailing under the Spanish Crown, Columbus sought a westward route to the "Indies." While he is often credited with "discovering" America, historians emphasize that the Americas were already inhabited by millions of people and had been visited by Norse explorers centuries earlier. A point of historical debate remains the exact nature of Columbus's intent and his administration of Hispaniola, which is now widely scrutinized for its brutality toward the Taíno people.

### Vasco da Gama
Unlike Columbus, da Gama successfully reached the intended destination: Calicut, India. His voyage established the *Carreira da Índia*, a maritime route that allowed Portugal to monopolize the spice trade. This shift fundamentally weakened the economic power of Venice and the Ottoman Empire, shifting the center of European commerce from the Mediterranean to the Atlantic.

### Ferdinand Magellan
Magellan’s expedition was the first to prove empirically that the Earth was round and far larger than previously estimated. Although Magellan himself was killed in the Philippines in 1521, his crew (led by Juan Sebastián Elcano) completed the circumnavigation. This voyage highlighted the vastness of the Pacific Ocean and the interconnectedness of the world's seas.

## Did You Know?
* **The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** To prevent conflict, Spain and Portugal signed a treaty mediated by the Pope, effectively dividing the non-European world into two spheres of influence along a meridian in the Atlantic.
* **The "Indies" Confusion:** Columbus died believing he had reached Asia, which is why he referred to the indigenous peoples of the Americas as "Indians."
* **The Spice Value:** During this era, certain spices like nutmeg and cloves were so valuable—due to their rarity and perceived medicinal properties—that they were often traded for their weight in gold.
