# Global Events and Figures (1554–1558)

The mid-16th century was marked by significant political transitions in Europe, maritime expansion and conflict in Asia, and the production of influential scholarly and religious works.

## Political and Religious Developments in Europe
The period saw major shifts in leadership and ecclesiastical administration. In 1556, Charles V abdicated, and he subsequently died in 1558. In the Mogul Empire, Akbar became the Great Mogul in 1556, a reign that continued until 1605.

In England, the relationship between the state and the church was characterized by state domination, leading to religious persecutions that functioned as state persecutions. Bonner served as an ecclesiastical sheriff in the realm's most refractory district. While some records, including those of Foxe, indicate instances where Bonner failed to persecute, and it is noted that many of his victims were forced upon him by the council—which occasionally viewed him as insufficiently severe—he was known for his brutality toward fallen foes. This was specifically evident in his jeers toward Cranmer during the degradation of his former chief. Despite his prominence, Bonner was never admitted to the privy council by Mary or Henry VIII, appearing to be viewed by his own party as a useful instrument for disagreeable work rather than a colleague.

Within the Catholic Church, the Society of Jesus continued to grow. Ignatius of Loyola, who had previously attempted to resign the generalship in 1547 and 1550, faced a trial in 1556 with the election of his old opponent, Caraffa, as pope. Caraffa intended to reform points of the Society that Ignatius considered vital. In early 1556, Ignatius grew weak and transferred active government to Natal, Madrid, and Polanco. He died suddenly on July 31, 1556, without asking for or receiving the last sacraments.

## Maritime Activity and Exploration
Maritime ventures during this era ranged from commercial exploration to piracy. In England, Master Cabot served as the life governor of the company of Merchant Adventurers, incorporated on December 18, 1551. In May 1553, three ships were sent to seek a north-east passage to the East; two were lost to ice near Arzina, but Chancellor's vessel reached the White Sea. The captain returned to England in the summer of 1554, facilitating a considerable trade with Russia. Further vessels were dispatched to Russia in 1555 and 1556. In May 1556, the "Searchthrift" departed, an event celebrated by Cabot with alms for the poor and a banquet at the sign of the Christopher. Cabot's pension was stopped on May 26, 1557, upon the arrival of King Philip II, though Mary renewed it three days later. Cabot is believed to have died within that year.

In East Asia, Japanese pirate vessels engaged in significant raiding. On April 5, 1554, a ship with a crew of 562 attacked a castle at Lungwang-tang using conches as trumpets and flying banners. In September 1555, a two-masted ship and eight five-masted ships looted Kinshan-hai. By February 23, 1556, pirate ships with masts described as a "dense forest of bamboo" arrived at the entrance to Kinshan-hai. In 1556, these pirates entered, burned, and looted the city of Yang-chou.

## Intellectual and Literary Works
The mid-1550s saw the publication of various academic, religious, and cartographic works.

### Scholarly and Medical Texts
Several works were produced by authors associated with the University of Cambridge and other institutions:
*   **1554:** *De Mendeti Methodo* (Basel).
*   **1556:** *De Ratione Victus* (Lov.), *De Mendeti Methodo* (London), and *De Ephemera Britannica* (an account of the sweating sickness in England).
*   **Other works:** The *Annals of the College from 1555 to 1572* and translations of Galen.

### Religious and Political Writings
John Knox produced several key texts during this period, including *On Prayer* (1554), *Epistles* and *Admonition* to English Brethren (1554), and *On Affliction* (1556). He later wrote *The First Blast of the Trumpet against the Monstrous Regiment of Women* in 1558.

In France, the work *Discours sur la roupture de la Trefve en l'an 1556* was published in Paris in 1556.

### Cartography
The period was also notable for the collection of maps. Anton Lafreri compiled the *Geografica tavole moderne* in Rome, which contained reprints of 142 maps of the world originally published between 1556 and 1572.
