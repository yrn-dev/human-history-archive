# Records of the Reformation and Harleian Sources

The study of the English Reformation and related historical figures relies upon a diverse array of primary documents, manuscripts, and published chronicles. Among these, the *Records of the Reformation* by N. Pocock (1870) and various Harleian manuscripts serve as critical bibliographic authorities for historians.

## Records of the Reformation and Tudor History
The *Records of the Reformation*, published by N. Pocock in 1870, is cited as an authority in the study of the Tudor period, specifically regarding the history of Henry VIII. It exists alongside a wide range of other sources used to document the reign of Henry VIII, including the *State Papers Henry VIII* and the *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* compiled by Brewer and Gardiner. 

Other complementary records for this era include the *Spanish Chronicle of Henry VIII* (translated by M.A.S. Hume), the *Chronicle* of C. Wriothesley, and E. Hall's *Chronicle*. Personal accounts and specific legal or social documents, such as the *State Trials* and the *Noble Tryumphaunt Coronacyon of Queen Anne* (1533), provide further detail on the political and religious shifts of the time.

## The Harleian Collections
The Harleian manuscripts, housed in the British Museum, are extensive repositories of historical data used across multiple areas of research.

### Harleian Miscellany and Personal Records
The *Harleian Miscellany* (1808) contains specific personal documents, such as love letters (iii. 47). In the study of Bishop Burnet, the Harleian MS. is noted as a fragmentary manuscript that, along with the Bodleian MS. and Sir Thomas Burnet's edition, helps clarify the relationship between different versions of Burnet's *History of His Own Time*.

### Harleian MSS. and State Correspondence
The Harleian catalogues are essential for researching the life of Burghley, as they contain a vast mass of correspondence. These are utilized in conjunction with the Cotton, Royal, Sloane, Egerton, and Additional MSS. Similarly, the Harleian MSS. (specifically Brit. Mus. 1580, art. 31-48) provide numerous references for the study of Bristol, alongside the *Cal. of State Papers, Dom. Series*.

### Legal and Noble Records
Harleian MS. 6063 is cited as a source regarding the history of nobility and titles of honor, appearing alongside the Hargrave MS. 325 in discussions of English law and the gentry.

## Related Reformation Works
Beyond Pocock's records, other significant works on the Reformation include Bishop Burnet's *History of the Reformation of the Church of England*. Burnet's work is analyzed through various editions, including those from the Clarendon Press and the Bodleian MS., which reveal differences in his perspective on the Anglican clergy and personal matters.
