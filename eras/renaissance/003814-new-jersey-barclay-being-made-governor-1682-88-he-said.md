# Robert Barclay and the Governance of East New Jersey

## Early Life and Education
Robert Barclay was born in 1648 at Gordonstown in Morayshire. His father had served under Gustavus Adolphus and experienced a tortuous path during the civil war. Barclay's education was completed in Paris, where he was at one time inclined toward the Roman Catholic faith. However, in 1667, he followed his father's example and joined the recently-formed Society of Friends, also known as Quakers. In 1670, he married Christian Mollison, a Quaker lady from Aberdeen.

## Theological Contributions
Barclay became a leading apologist for the Quaker doctrine, utilizing his mental powers and warm feelings to advance the faith. He first gained prominence during a controversy with William Mitchell. In 1676, he published fifteen *Theses Theologiae*, which sparked a public discussion in Aberdeen. The most significant of these theses concerned immediate revelation, asserting that the "inner light" is superior to both scripture and reason.

Following the *Theses*, Barclay published his most substantial work, *An Apology for the True Christian Divinity*, in Latin at Amsterdam in 1676. This work provided an elaborate statement of the fundamental positions established in his *Theses*. He later translated the *Apology* into English in 1678. The text is regarded as one of the most impressive theological writings of the century and remains perhaps the most important manifesto of the Quaker Society, characterized by a spirit of large tolerance.

## Political Influence and New Jersey
Barclay's commitment to the Society of Friends led to several periods of imprisonment due to the persecutions inflicted upon the society. He traveled extensively throughout Europe, accompanying George Fox and Penn on one occasion, and held several interviews with Elizabeth, princess palatine.

In his later years, Barclay exerted significant influence over James II. During his time as duke of York, James II granted a patent for the province of East New Jersey to twelve members of the Society of Friends. Robert Barclay was appointed governor of this province, serving from 1682 to 1688.

As the arrival of William of Orange became imminent, Barclay is said to have visited James II for the purpose of attempting to make terms of accommodation. Robert Barclay died on October 3rd.

## Regional Context of New Jersey
The province of New Jersey had roots in earlier royal grants. In 1650, King Charles granted Sir George Carteret "a certain island and adjacent islets near Virginia, in America," to be called New Jersey, though no settlement was initially made upon this grant. 

By 1677, a colony of English Quakers had settled Burlington. Originally known as New Beverly, the settlement was renamed after Bridlington (Burlington), the Yorkshire home of many of the settlers. In 1682, the assembly of West Jersey granted "Matinicunk Island" to Burlington to maintain a school for the education of youth; the trust fund from this grant remains one of the oldest for educational purposes in the United States.
