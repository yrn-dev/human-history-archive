# Daniel van Papenbroeck and the Bollandists

## The Bollandists and the Acta Sanctorum
The Bollandists are Belgian Jesuits responsible for the publication of the *Acta Sanctorum*. This extensive collection consists of biographies and legends of the saints, which are arranged according to the calendar by day. The project was originally conceived by a Jesuit father named Heribert Rosweyde, who issued a prospectus for the work in 1607 titled *Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis Bibliothecis manuscriptae*. Rosweyde intended to compile the lives of the saints from manuscripts across eighteen volumes, accompanied by sober notes. Although he collected a significant amount of material, he died in 1629 before the work could begin.

Following Rosweyde's death, Father John Bolland was appointed to continue the project and was sent to Antwerp. Bolland expanded the scope of the work and continued to gather material. The first two volumes, covering January, were published in 1643. Subsequent volumes followed: three volumes for February in 1658, three for March in 1668, and three for April in 1675.

## Collaboration of Daniel van Papenbroeck
The development of the *Acta Sanctorum* involved several collaborators. Godfried Henschen (Henschenius) was associated with Bolland starting in 1635 and worked on the project until 1681. Daniel van Papenbroeck, known as Papebroch, collaborated on the work from 1659 to 1714. This period is regarded as the most brilliant in the history of the *Acta Sanctorum*. However, Papebroch's use of free criticism earned him many enemies, and he frequently had to defend himself against their attacks.

## Later History and Continuation
The work continued in the same spirit, despite some inequalities, until the Society of Jesus was suppressed in 1773. The final volume of this initial period was volume iii of October, published in 1770. Following the dispersion of the Jesuits, the Bollandists received authorization to continue their work in Antwerp until 1778, at which point they transferred to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. In this location, they published volume iv of October in 1780 and volume v of October in 1886, before the monastery of Coudenberg was suppressed.

The work of the Bollandists ceased in 1788. The Premonstratensians of Tongerloo acquired the remains of the library and attempted to maintain the project, publishing volume vi of October in 1794.

## Modern Revival
The project was resumed in 1837 after the Society of Jesus was re-established in Belgium. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels. A new series began in 1845 with volume vii of October, eventually reaching volume xiii of October by 1883.

Several Jesuit fathers collaborated during this new series, including:
* Joseph van der Moere
* Joseph van Hecke
* Benjamin Bossue
* Victor de Buck
* Remi de Buck
* Ant. Tinnebroeck
* Edu. Carpentier
* Henr. Matagne

Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan was responsible for editing the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which appeared at the beginning of volume xi of October in 1864.
