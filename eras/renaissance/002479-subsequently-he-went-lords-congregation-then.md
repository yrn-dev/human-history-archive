# Sir James Balfour

Sir James Balfour of Pittendreich (d. 1583 or 1584) was a Scottish politician and judge whose career was marked by frequent shifts in allegiance during the religious and political turmoil of 16th-century Scotland.

## Early Life and Legal Career
Balfour was the son of Sir Michael Balfour of Montquhanny. He received an education tailored for the legal branch of the Church of Scotland. His early career was interrupted in June 1547 when he, along with John Knox and others, was captured at St Andrews and condemned to serve in the French galleys. He was released in 1549, at which point he abjured the reformers and entered the service of Mary of Guise. This transition led to his appointment to several significant legal positions.

## Political Allegiances and the Lords of the Congregation
Balfour's political loyalties were volatile. At one point, he went over to the lords of the Congregation, though he subsequently betrayed their plans. Following the arrival of Mary Stuart in Scotland, Balfour became one of her secretaries and was reported in 1565 to be her greatest favorite, second only to Rizzio.

During this period, he accumulated various titles and offices:
* In 1561, he obtained the parsonage of Flisk in Fife.
* He was nominated as a lord of session.
* In 1563, he was appointed as one of the commissaries of the court that replaced the former ecclesiastical tribunal.
* He was made a privy-councillor in 1565 and knighted.
* In 1566, he was appointed lord-clerk-register.

## Involvement in the Murder of Lord Darnley
Balfour was an adherent of Bothwell and became deeply implicated in the murder of Lord Darnley. Although he was not present when the crime was committed, he is credited with lodging Darnley at Kirk o' Field, which was the house of his brothers. It is further supposed that Balfour drew up the bond at Craigmillar for the murder, a document which he signed. 

Under Bothwell, Balfour served as the deputy-governor of Edinburgh Castle and is said to have drafted the marriage contract between Bothwell and Mary.

## Fall from Power and Later Years
As the fall of Bothwell became imminent, Balfour rapidly changed sides. He surrendered Edinburgh Castle to Murray, stipulating that in exchange he receive a pardon for the murder of Darnley, the retention of the priory of Pittenweem, and pecuniary rewards. Following the resignation of his office as lord-clerk-register, he was appointed president of the court of session.

In 1568, Balfour was present at the battle of Langside alongside the regent. However, he faced accusations of betraying the casket letters to enemies and advising Mary to leave Dunbar, an action described as leading to her ruin. Due to renewed intrigues with the faction of Mary, he was dismissed in 1568. The following year, he was imprisoned on charges of complicity in the murder of Darnley, though he eventually succeeded in effecting his escape.
