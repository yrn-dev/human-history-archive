# The Bag-pipe and the Coventry Mysteries

## History and Distribution of the Bag-pipe
The bag-pipe is a musical instrument that spread from England to Ireland and Caledonia. In these regions, the instrument took root and became identified with the life of the people, serving as a military instrument that was held in great esteem by the Celtic races. 

The bag-pipe was utilized across a variety of social contexts, including festivals, funerals, and weddings. During the 18th century, it was also used to lighten the burden of physical labor. An example of this occurred in 1786 in Skye, where each party of labourers engaged in roadmaking was accompanied by its own bag-piper. In 1534, the instrument was used in old mysteries at Coventry.

## The Coventry Mystery Plays
The city of Coventry is associated with mystery plays, which served as the basis for certain traditional legends. One such example is the famous "Cherry-tree Carol," also known as "Joseph was an old man," which is based on an old legend related within the Coventry mystery plays.

## Regional Context of Coventry
Coventry is located in England and is connected via a tramway to Bedworth, a manufacturing town in the Nuneaton parliamentary division of Warwickshire. Bedworth is situated on the Nuneaton-Coventry branch of the London & North Western railway, approximately 100 miles north-west of London. The Coventry canal passes through Bedworth and also connects to the Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal, which runs south from the springs at Moira in Leicestershire.
