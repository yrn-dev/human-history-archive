# Hypnerotomachia Poliphili

The *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* is a notable example of early Italian illustrative art, printed in Venice in 1499. It was produced by the printer Aldus during the final year of the 15th century.

## Artistic Characteristics and Value
The work is recognized for its fine outline illustrations. In the market for illustrated books, the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* is categorized among those whose early celebrity has prevented them from becoming exceptionally rare. Consequently, while rarer books may be priced in the hundreds, the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* seldom fetches more than L120. This distinguishes it from other early works, such as the *Nuremburg Chronicle* (1493), which seldom fetches more than L30.

## Context of Early Illustration
The *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* emerged during a period where illustration was coincident with the invention of printing. The development of wood and metal engraving during the Renaissance encouraged many painters to apply their skills to book illustration. This movement led to a gradual enlargement of illustrative art, featuring the line-work of artists such as Titian, Michelangelo, Mantegna, and Pollajuolo. While the Italian school produced examples like the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili*, the German school of the 16th century—represented by artists such as Holbein, Altdorfer, Burgmair, and Dürer—committed itself even more vigorously to the medium.
