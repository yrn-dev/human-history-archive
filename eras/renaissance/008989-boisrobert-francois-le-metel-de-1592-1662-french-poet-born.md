# BOISROBERT, FRANCOIS LE METEL DE (1592-1662)

Francois le Metel de Boisrobert was a French poet and priest who lived from 1592 to 1662. Born in Caen in 1592, he initially pursued a career in law, practicing for a period at the bar in Rouen.

## Court Life and Political Connections
Boisrobert moved to Paris around 1622. By the following year, he had secured a position at court, evidenced by his participation in the *Bacchanales* ballet performed at the Louvre in February 1623. In that same year, he was introduced to Cardinal Richelieu. Boisrobert's talent as a raconteur and his sense of humor made him an indispensable figure to the cardinal. 

His diplomatic and social travels included accompanying an embassy to England in 1625 and visiting Rome in 1630, where his wit earned him the favor of Urban VIII. Following the death of Richelieu, Boisrobert transitioned his loyalty to Mazarin, serving him faithfully throughout the period of the Fronde.

## Literary and Academic Contributions
Boisrobert was a significant figure in the literary circles of his time. He was one of five poets tasked with implementing the dramatic ideas of Cardinal Richelieu. Furthermore, it was Boisrobert who suggested the creation of the Academy to Richelieu; he subsequently became one of the institution's most active and earliest members.

His written works include several volumes of verse and a number of comedies. One of these comedies, *La Belle Plaideuse*, is said to have influenced Moliere's *L'Avare*. Additionally, the licentious *Contes*, which were published under the name of his brother D'Ouville, are frequently attributed to Boisrobert.

## Ecclesiastical Career and Personal Life
Boisrobert took holy orders and served as a canon of Rouen. Despite his religious standing, he was known for a passion for play and maintained a friendship with Ninon de l'Enclos. His enemies often cited the "undisguised looseness of his life" as a means to attack him. While he suffered disgrace on more than one occasion, these periods were generally brief, though in his later years he was required to devote more attention to his priestly duties.

Through the various benefices granted to him by his patron, Boisrobert became wealthy, and he was known to be liberal in his support of other men of letters.

## Death
Francois le Metel de Boisrobert died on March 30, 1662.
