# 20th of January 1609

The date of 20th of January 1609 is recorded in the archives as a significant day in the history of Bohemia, as well as a date of academic matriculation for notable English figures.

## The Bohemian Diet of 1609
The diet that convened on the 20th of January 1609 is regarded as one of the most important in the history of Bohemia. This meeting occurred following a temporary settlement, after which the estates of Bohemia once again presented their demands to their king, Rudolph. 

The proceedings were heavily influenced by the personal enmities of the 17th century. King Rudolph had previously declined to discuss religious matters while his brother's troops occupied portions of Bohemia; furthermore, Rudolph had not forgiven his brother's treachery. While appearing as a champion of Catholicism, Rudolph was secretly negotiating with Christian of Anhalt, the leader of the German Protestants. Because the Bohemians and the court of Spain were aware that the king could not rely on Spanish aid, the estates were not intimidated by Rudolph's sudden truculent attitude.

The conflict centered on a document consisting of twenty-five "Articles" drawn up by the estates in March of the previous year. These articles formulated demands regarding religion, including:
* The recognition of the *Confessio Bohemica* as one of the kingdom's fundamental laws.
* The granting of complete religious liberty to all classes.
* Equal rights for Roman Catholics and Protestants (a term then used to collectively describe Lutherans, the Bohemian Brethren, and Utraquists) to hold all offices of state.
* Limitations on the ability of the Jesuits to acquire land.
* Redress for grievances resulting from Rudolph's misrule.

King Rudolph considered this document illegal and demanded its delivery for destruction. However, the document remained with Budova, who refused to surrender it. Consequently, the estates appointed twelve negotiators, including Count Henry Matthias Thurn, to discuss the matter with the king and his councillors. These protracted discussions eventually led the king to state on the 31st of March that he could grant no concessions regarding religious matters.

## Academic Matriculations in England
On the same date, 20th of January 1609, Henry King and his younger brother John matriculated at Christ Church, Oxford. They had both previously attended Westminster School. 

Henry King (1591-1669) went on to enter the church and was appointed bishop of Chichester in 1642, receiving the living of Petworth, Sussex. During the conflict with the Parliamentary army, Chichester surrendered on the 29th of December 1642, and King was taken prisoner. Following his release, he found asylum with Lady Salter and his brother-in-law, Sir Richard Hobart. King was a close friend of Dr. Brian Duppa and was personally acquainted with Charles I; in a poem from 1649, King identified the *Eikon Basilike* as the king's own work. 

Restored to his benefice during the Restoration, Henry King died on the 30th of September 1669. His literary contributions include *The Psalmes of David from the New Translation of the Bible, turned into Meter* (1651) and *Poems, Elegies, Paradoxes and Sonets* (1657). He also served as one of the executors for John Donne.
