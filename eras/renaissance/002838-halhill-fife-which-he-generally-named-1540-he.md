# Henry Balnaves

## Early Life and Education
Henry Balnaves was a Scottish reformer and politician born around 1512 in Kirkcaldy. He received his education at St Andrews and also studied on the continent. During his time abroad, he adopted Protestant views. Upon his return to Scotland, Balnaves continued his studies in law.

## Political and Legal Career
Balnaves entered the legal profession and was appointed a lord of session in 1538. Around the same time, he married Christian Scheves. In 1539, he was granted the estate of Halhill in Fife, a location after which he is generally named. 

By the year 1540, Balnaves had been sworn into the privy council of James V. He became known as a member of the political party that favored an ecclesiastical reformation and an alliance with England. Records from 1543 describe him as the treasurer to James. Following the death of James V in 1542, the regent Arran appointed Balnaves as secretary in the new government established for the infant Queen Mary in January 1543.

## Reform and Diplomacy
As a reformer, Balnaves promoted an act that permitted the reading of the Scriptures in the vulgar tongue. His diplomatic efforts included serving as one of the commissioners tasked with arranging a marriage treaty between the future Edward VI and the young queen. During his time in London, Balnaves was not viewed as being as complaisant as some of his fellow commissioners; consequently, he was not informed of all the engagements made by his colleagues.

## Downfall and Imprisonment
Balnaves' political standing declined when the regent Arran shifted his allegiance to the priestly party. He was particularly disliked by Beton, who "loved him worst of all." As a result of these political shifts, Balnaves was deprived of his offices in November 1543 and imprisoned in Blackness Castle. He died in 1579.
