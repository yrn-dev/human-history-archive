# Elias Ashmole

## Early Life and Professional Beginnings
Elias Ashmole was an English antiquarian born on May 23, 1617, in Lichfield. He was the son of a saddler. In 1638, Ashmole became a solicitor. By 1644, his career progressed when he was appointed as a commissioner of excise. This appointment brought him to Oxford during the period when the Royalist Parliament was sitting there.

## Military and Social Advancement
While at Oxford, Ashmole established a friendship with Captain George Wharton (who later became Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole received a commission from the king to serve as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse. In 1646, Ashmole became the first amateur, or gentleman, to be "accepted" and initiated as a Freemason.

## Personal Life and Financial Status
Ashmole's domestic life was marked by two marriages. His first wife died in 1641. In 1649, he married Lady Mainwaring, a relative of his first wife who was approximately twenty years his senior. This marriage provided Ashmole with a level of affluence that allowed him to dedicate his full time to his preferred studies. Although his wife petitioned for a separation in 1657, she failed to win her case and returned to live with him until her death in 1668.

## Intellectual Interests and Public Office
Ashmole's early intellectual interests included astrology, a passion sparked by William Lilly and George Wharton; he met Lilly and other astrologers in London in 1646. Over the following years, however, his interest in astrology subsided, and he shifted his focus toward antiquarian research and heraldry.

During the period between his marital crisis in 1657 and his wife's death in 1668, Ashmole enjoyed significant favor at court. He held a variety of official positions, serving as:
* Windsor herald
* Commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

Ashmole eventually declined the office of Garter king-at-arms, allowing the position to go to Sir William Dugdale. In 1668, Ashmole married Sir William Dugdale's daughter.

## Antiquarian Contributions and the Ashmolean Museum
Ashmole was noted for his diligence and patient research in the field of antiquities. In 1672, he published a practically exhaustive work titled *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*.

In 1677, Ashmole presented a collection of curiosities to the university of Oxford, establishing the Ashmolean Museum. This was the first public museum of curiosities in the kingdom. A large portion of the collection had been inherited by Ashmole from a friend, John Tradescant. Ashmole stipulated that a suitable building must be constructed to house the collection, and the museum was not finally installed until 1683. Following this, he provided a further gift of his library to the university.
