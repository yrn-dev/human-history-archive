# Historical Records of Installations and Proposals

The archive contains various accounts of individuals and bodies being installed into specific roles or positions, as well as various proposals ranging from matrimonial and political to technical and strategic.

## Political and Administrative Installations

In ancient Greece, the Four Hundred were formally installed in the senate-house. Upon their installation, they divided themselves by lot into separate prytanies, likely ten in number with forty members each, mirroring the structure of the former senate of Five Hundred to avoid disturbing the people's accustomed distribution of the year. This installation was solemnized through sacrifice and prayer. Once established, the Four Hundred implemented large changes to the administration of affairs with a rigor and strictness that had been unknown under the previous constitution. They also imprisoned, banished, and executed several political enemies. While it was proposed among the Four Hundred to pass a vote of restoration for those under sentences of exile, the majority rejected this to ensure that Alkibiadês would not be included, though they did not pass a general law, instead reserving him as a special exception.

In a different historical context, the Jacobin Commune of Paris was installed at the Hôtel de Ville. This body existed alongside the Legislative Assembly, which had replaced the National Assembly under a new constitution. The royalists had supported a Jacobin proposal to make members of the National Assembly ineligible for the Legislative Assembly, believing that a body clipped of experience would be politically incompetent and would eventually allow France to fall back into the hands of its legitimate masters.

## Royal Companions and Marital Proposals

The archive records several instances of individuals serving as companions to royalty or receiving marriage proposals. Gerald Fitzgerald, the 15th Earl of Desmond, was the son of James, the 14th earl, and More O'Carroll. In January 1541, as part of a submission to Henry VIII, James agreed to send Gerald to be educated in England. Later, at the accession of Edward VI, proposals were renewed for Gerald to serve as the companion to the young king, though these projects were not carried out.

In the case of Anne Boleyn, daughter of Sir Thomas Boleyn and Elizabeth (daughter of Thomas Howard), her early life included serving as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, wife of Francis I, after accompanying Mary Tudor to France in 1514. Upon her return to England in 1521 or 1522, she attracted many suitors, including the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt and Henry Percy, heir of the earl of Northumberland. However, the king stopped a marriage with Percy and provided Sir James Butler as an alternative match. Anne remained unmarried.

Anne Boleyn's relationship with Henry VIII is a subject of historical surmise, with some suggesting it began as early as 1523. However, there is no proof that the king's passion existed prior to the divorce proceedings in May 1527. By August 16, 1527, a letter from Mendoza, the imperial ambassador to Charles V, openly connected Anne's name with the king's as a possible wife should Catherine's divorce be granted. While Wolsey opposed this alliance and negotiated for a marriage with Renee, daughter of Louis XII, Henry sent his secretary, William Knight, to Rome to seek facilities for a marriage with Anne. Unlike her sister Mary, Anne did not intend to be the king's mistress but sought to be his queen, driven by motives of ambition.

## Other Proposals and Riddles

The archive records a narrative involving Samson, who proposed a riddle to thirty youth of Timhath during a wedding feast. He offered a linen shirt and a garment as a reward for anyone who could expound the riddle within seven days. The riddle stated: "That a devourer produced sweet food out of itself, though itself were very disagreeable." After three days, the youth, unable to solve it, threatened a damsel to force her to discover the answer from her husband. Samson eventually revealed the answer—referring to his slaughter of a lion and the honey-combs he found in its breast—which the damsel then revealed to the youth.

In diplomatic history, Russia and China dealt with "pacific programmes" regarding Manchuria. Russia sought a conventional title for its tenure of Manchuria, while the United States, Great Britain, and Japan worked to strengthen China's resolve. This led to a treaty where Russia pledged to withdraw troops in three installments. However, Russia later proposed new conditions that would have strengthened its grasp on Manchuria rather than loosening it.
