# Rerum Scoticarum Historia

## Overview and Publication
The *Rerum Scoticarum Historia* is a history of Scotland authored by Buchanan. The work was completed shortly before the author's death in 1579 and was subsequently published in 1582.

## Content and Methodology
The *Rerum Scoticarum Historia* is divided into different chronological focuses. The greater portion of the book covers the period personally known to Buchanan, and for this reason, it is considered to be of great value. In contrast, the earlier sections of the work rely to a considerable extent on the legendary history provided by Boece.

Buchanan's stated objective in writing the history was to "purge" the national history of "sum Inglis lyis and Scottis vanite," as noted in a letter to Randolph. Despite this goal, the author's claim of freedom from partisanship was exaggerated; he unconsciously criticized his own work by stating that it would "displease many" and "content few."

## Context of the Author's Final Works
The *Rerum Scoticarum Historia* was one of two major works that occupied Buchanan's final years. The other was a treatise published in 1579 titled *De Jure Regni apud Scotos*. Written as a dialogue intended to teach sound political principles to his pupil, *De Jure Regni apud Scotos* argued that the people are the source of all political power. It asserted that a king is bound by the conditions under which supreme power was granted and that it is lawful to punish or resist tyrants. 

Due to these doctrines, the legislature made persistent efforts to suppress *De Jure Regni apud Scotos* for a century. It was condemned by an act of parliament in 1584 and again in 1664, and the university of Oxford burned the work in 1683.
