# William Caxton

## Role as Publisher and Translator
William Caxton was a 15th-century publisher and translator. While his activities did not mark a new era in literary composition, the introduction of the printing press through his work fundamentally changed the preservation and dissemination of culture, which ultimately had a profound effect on literary production. Caxton's publications spanned the entire field of 15th-century literature. Through the quaint prefaces of his works, it is evident that he attempted to guide public taste toward the best works of the past, a goal exemplified by his printing and reprinting of the *Canterbury Tales*.

## Literary Contributions
Despite his numerous publications, none of Caxton's works served as the herald of a new literary era, as neither he nor the writers he printed produced anything new in spirit or form. The only book he introduced to the world that maintains permanent interest as literature is Sir Thomas Malory's *Morte Darthur*. 

The *Morte Darthur* is a compilation of older romances and is regarded as the only book of permanent literary significance produced in England during the 15th century. Caxton described "thystorye" (the history) of Arthur as being "so gloryous and shynyng, that he is stalled in the first place of the moost noble, beste and worthyest of the Crysten men." The work is noted for its vivid diction and a syntax that possesses a naive charm, despite being occasionally faulty. It reflects the beauty and warmth of the old knight's conception of chivalry and a love for the great men and deeds of a visionary past. Rather than being the start of a new species of literature, the book is viewed as the final flower of an old one.

## The Caxton Press and Legacy
The printing press established by Caxton in Westminster was later succeeded by Wynkyn de Worde. De Worde maintained a shop in Fleet Street. Additionally, the Caxton Society has been responsible for printing a French poem, *Le Chastel d'amour*, which is sometimes attributed to Robert Grosseteste.
