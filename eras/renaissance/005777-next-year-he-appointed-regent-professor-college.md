# George Buchanan

## Early Life and Education
George Buchanan (1506–1582) was a Scottish humanist born in February 1506. He was the son of Agnes Heriot, who belonged to the family of the Heriots of Trabroun in Haddingtonshire; this family included George Heriot, the founder of Heriot's hospital. Buchanan's father, a younger son of an old family, possessed the farm of Moss in the parish of Killearn, Stirlingshire, but died at an early age, which left his children and widow in poverty.

While it is said that Buchanan attended Killearn school, the details of his early education are not well known. In 1520, his uncle, James Heriot, sent him to the university of Paris. According to an autobiographical sketch, Buchanan spent his time there writing verses, a task he describes as being driven "partly by liking, partly by compulsion," as it was the prescribed task for youth at the time.

## Academic Career in Paris and Scotland
The death of his uncle in 1522 forced Buchanan to return to Scotland, as he could no longer afford to stay in Paris. Following a period of recovery from a severe illness, he joined the French auxiliaries brought over by John Stewart, duke of Albany, and participated in an inroad into England that proved unsuccessful.

Buchanan entered the university of St Andrews in the following year, graduating with a B.A. in 1525. His primary motivation for attending St Andrews was to hear the lectures on logic given by the celebrated John Major. When Major moved to Paris, Buchanan followed him in 1526. He continued his studies in Paris, graduating with a B.A. in 1527 and an M.A. in 1528.

In 1529, Buchanan was elected Procurator of the "German Nation" in the university of Paris, a position to which he was re-elected four times in four successive months. The year following his M.A. graduation, he was appointed as a professor, or regent, in the college of Sainte-Barbe. He taught at this college for more than three years before resigning his regentship in 1531.

## Later Appointments
In 1532, Buchanan became the tutor to Gilbert Kennedy, the 3rd earl of Cassilis. He remained in this role and returned to Scotland with the earl around the beginning of 1537.
