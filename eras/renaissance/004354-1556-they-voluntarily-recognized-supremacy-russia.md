# The Bashkirs

## Early History and Independence
The Bashkirs were first mentioned by European writers Joannes de Plano Carpini (c. 1200-1260) and William of Rubruquis (1220-1293). These travelers encountered the people in the upper regions of the river Ural, where they referred to them as "Pascatir" and noted that they spoke the same language as the Hungarians. Until the arrival of the Mongolians around the middle of the 13th century, the Bashkirs existed as a strong, independent people who were troublesome to their neighbors, specifically the Petchenegs and the Bulgarians.

## Russian Supremacy and Administration
By the time the Kazan kingdom fell, the Bashkirs were in a weak state. In 1556, they voluntarily recognized the supremacy of Russia. Following this recognition, the city of Ufa was founded to provide the Bashkirs with defense against the Kirghiz. Under Russian rule, the Bashkirs were subjected to a fur-tax.

The relationship between the Bashkirs and the Russian government was marked by several periods of unrest:
* **1676:** A rebellion occurred under a leader named Seit, though the people were eventually reduced with difficulty.
* **1707:** A second rebellion took place under the leadership of Kusyom and Aldar, prompted by the ill-treatment they received from Russian officials.
* **1735:** A third and final insurrection began during the foundation of Orenburg, lasting for six years.

In 1786, the Bashkirs were freed from their taxes, and an irregular army was formed from among them in 1798. In later years, they were divided into cantons. While they generally gave little trouble, some disputes arose with the government regarding questions of land.

## Mode of Life and Social Structure
The Bashkirs are divided into two primary lifestyles: settled and nomadic.

**Settled Bashkirs**
Those who are settled engage in bee-keeping, cattle-rearing, and agriculture. This group is described as living without want.

**Nomadic Bashkirs**
The nomadic population is further subdivided based on the districts where they wander, categorized as those of the steppes and those of the mountains. Their primary occupation is the rearing of cattle. This activity is performed in a negligent manner; they fail to collect sufficient winter fodder for their herds, which results in the death of part of their livestock.

## Physical and Cultural Characteristics
The Bashkirs are described as strong and muscular individuals with swarthy skin. Their physical features include large heads, small foreheads, black hair, narrow and flat eyes, and ears that always stick out.

Culturally, they are characterized as being hospitable but suspicious. They are described as being apt to plunder and "to the last degree lazy." Due to their circumstances, they are usually very poor. During the winter months, their diet consists partly of a badly prepared cheese known as *skurt* and a type of gruel called *yuryu*.
