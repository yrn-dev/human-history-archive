# Horizons of the Renaissance: The Age of Discovery

## Overview
The Age of Discovery, coinciding with the height of the Renaissance, marked a pivotal shift in global geopolitics, economics, and science. Driven by the desire to bypass Ottoman-controlled land routes to the spice markets of Asia, European powers—primarily Portugal and Spain—funded daring maritime expeditions. This era was characterized by the synthesis of Renaissance humanism, which encouraged empirical observation, and advancements in cartography and navigation (such as the use of the astrolabe and the development of the caravel). While these voyages expanded the known world for Europeans, they simultaneously initiated centuries of colonization, exploitation, and the devastating "Columbian Exchange."

## Key Facts

### Christopher Columbus
Sailing under the Spanish Crown, Columbus sought a westward route to the East Indies. While he is often credited with "discovering" America, historians emphasize that Indigenous populations had thrived there for millennia, and Norse explorers had reached Newfoundland centuries prior. A point of historical debate remains the exact nature of his motivations—whether they were purely commercial or driven by a messianic desire to fund a new crusade.

### Vasco da Gama
Unlike Columbus, da Gama successfully reached the intended destination: Calicut, India. By sailing around the Cape of Good Hope at the southern tip of Africa, he established a Portuguese maritime empire. This shifted the economic center of gravity from the Mediterranean to the Atlantic, severely undermining the monopoly of Venetian and Ottoman traders.

### Ferdinand Magellan
Magellan’s expedition was the most grueling of the three. Although Magellan himself was killed in the Philippines in 1521, his crew completed the first circumnavigation of the Earth. This voyage provided empirical proof of the Earth's sphericity and revealed the vastness of the Pacific Ocean, which was far larger than Renaissance scholars had estimated.

## Did You Know?
* **The Tordesillas Line:** In 1494, the Treaty of Tordesillas effectively divided the "New World" between Spain and Portugal along a meridian 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands.
* **The Spice Value:** During this era, peppercorns and cloves were so valuable they were sometimes used as currency or collateral for loans.
* **The Magellan Paradox:** While Magellan is credited with the circumnavigation, the first person to actually circle the globe may have been his enslaved interpreter, Enrique of Malacca, who returned to his native region before the ship returned to Spain.
