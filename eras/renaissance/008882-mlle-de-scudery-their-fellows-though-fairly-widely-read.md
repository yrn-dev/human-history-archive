# Mlle de Scudery and Their Fellows

Mlle de Scudery and her associates were prominent figures in the literary landscape of 17th-century France, particularly known for the production of elaborate romances. Their work and the critical reactions to it provide insight into the evolving standards of French literature and the tensions between traditional and emerging styles of the period.

## The Elaborate Romances
Mlle de Scudery and La Calprenede were central figures in the creation of fashionable, elaborate romances. These works were widely read in manuscript form during their time. However, they eventually became targets of satire as literary tastes shifted toward more concise and vigorous forms of expression.

Antoine Furetière, a French scholar and member of the French Academy, addressed these fashionable romances in his 1666 work, *Le Roman bourgeois*. Furetière used this text to cast ridicule upon the romances of Mlle de Scudery and La Calprenede. Unlike the burlesque style found in Scarron's *Roman comique*, Furetière’s approach involved stringing together various portraits and episodes drawn from everyday life, providing a descriptive account of the times without a strict attempt at sequence.

## Critical Opposition and Boileau
The "lucubrations" of Mlle de Scudery, La Calprenede, and their fellows were further challenged by the poet Boileau. In 1664, Boileau composed a prose work titled *Dialogue des heros de roman*. This piece served as a satire on the elaborate romances of the era and is credited with effectively abolishing the influence of these works. 

Despite the impact of the *Dialogue des heros de roman*, the book was not published until 1713. It is suggested that this delay in publication was out of regard for Mlle de Scudery. Boileau's broader literary project involved a revolt against older poets and a desire to demonstrate the poetical capabilities of the French language through terseness and regularity of structure, contrasting with the style of the romance writers.

## Related Literary Figures and Circles
The intellectual environment surrounding these writers included various literary circles and figures. Boileau, along with Moliere, Racine, Chapelle, and Antoine Furetiere, met to discuss literary questions at the *Pomme du Pin* and the *Moulon Blanc*.

Additionally, the Scudery name was associated with other critical literary disputes of the time. Georges de Scudery was an active critic who launched open attacks on the play *Le Cid*. In his 1637 work, *Observations sur le Cid*, Georges de Scudery maintained that the play's subject was "utterly bad," claiming that the action was poorly conducted, the versification was frequently faulty, the beauties were generally stolen, and that all rules of dramatic composition had been violated. Georges de Scudery was among the writers, alongside J. de Mairet and others, who engaged in a pamphlet-war following the success of the play.
