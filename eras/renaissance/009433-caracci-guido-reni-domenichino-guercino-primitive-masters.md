# Caracci, Guido Reni, Domenichino, Guercino, &c. The primitive masters

The artistic tradition of Bologna is characterized by a progression from primitive masters to the eclectics of the 16th and 17th centuries. The Museo Civico in Bologna serves as a primary repository for this history, housing a picture gallery that surveys both earlier Bolognese paintings and the works of the Bolognese eclectics, including the Caracci, Guido Reni, Domenichino, and Guercino.

## The Primitive Masters and Early Influence
The primitive masters of Bologna are described as not being of great excellence. However, the works of 15th-century masters are considered to be of considerable merit, specifically those of Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara (1460–1535) and Francesco Francia (1450–1517). 

Another significant figure in the development of the Bolognese school was Bartolommeo Bagnacavallo (1484–1542), born Ramenghi. Bagnacavallo studied under Francia and later in Rome as a pupil of Raphael, where he contributed to the decoration of the Vatican gallery. Upon returning to Bologna, he became a leading artist and is credited with great improvements in the general style of the Bolognese school. While some considered his designs inferior to other productions of Raphael's school, his work was distinguished by graceful delineation and rich colouring. His works were carefully studied and imitated in some respects by the Caracci and Guido Reni.

## Domenichino
Domenico Zampieri, known as Domenichino (1581–1641), was the son of a shoemaker born in Bologna. His nickname, "Domenichino," refers to his short stature, while his companions once called him "the Ox" (Bue) due to his reserved and studious manners, which were mistaken for dullness. He initially studied under Denis Calvart but later joined the academy of the Caracci under Agostino. He eventually moved to Rome to study under Annibale Caracci.

Domenichino was known as an accurate and expressive designer who shunned society to observe the "play of the passions" in public theatres and walks, aiming to delineate the soul and call forth heartfelt emotions. His work is noted for colors that are true to nature and for the majesty of his mantles and draperies. Among his leading works are the "Martyrdom of St Agnes" and "Adam and Eve" in the Gallery of Bologna, as well as the "Communion of St Jerome" (painted circa 1614 for the church of La Carita).

His career was marked by professional rivalry. In Rome, Lanfranco accused him of plagiarizing Agostino Caracci's "Communion of St Jerome." Later, in Naples, where he was engaged to paint frescoes of the life of St Januarius in the Cappella del Tesoro, he was targeted by the "Cabal of Naples"—a group consisting of the painters Caracciolo, Ribera, and Corenzio—who attempted to exclude him through harassment, including rubbing out his fresco work.

## Guercino
Giovanni Francesco Barbieri (1591–1666), known as Guercino (meaning "the squinting"), was a historical painter born in Cento. He began his development at age seventeen under Benedetto Gennari. After moving to Bologna in 1615, his style was initially formed by the Caracci, though he was deeply influenced for a time by the shadows and strong colouring of Caravaggio. His later works shifted toward the lightness and clearness associated with Guido Reni.

Guercino was highly esteemed by princes, nobles, and fellow artists. He was noted for the extreme rapidity of his execution, completing 144 paintings and 106 large altar-pieces. His most famous work is the *St Petronilla*, painted in Rome for Gregory XV. He continued to teach and paint until his death in 1666, having amassed a handsome fortune.

## Guido Reni and Other Interactions
Guido Reni is cited as one of the most distinguished masters of the period, often compared to Domenichino. In one instance, Annibale Caracci preferred a work by Domenichino (the "Scourging of St Andrew") over one by Reni. 

Reni's career also intersected with the painter Belisario Corenzio (c. 1558–1643). When Reni arrived in Naples in 1621 to paint in the chapel of St Januarius, Corenzio suborned an assassin to kill him. While Reni survived and withdrew to Rome, the assassin killed Reni's assistant.

## Legacy and Collections
The works of these masters are distributed across several significant sites:
* **Museo Civico (Bologna):** Contains works by the Caracci, Reni, Domenichino, and Guercino, as well as the 15th-century masters and Raphael's *S. Cecilia* (c. 1515).
* **Loreto:** The cathedral church of the Holy House contains mosaics by Guido Reni and Domenichino.
* **Palazzo Apostolico (Loreto):** The picture gallery contains works by the Caracci.
