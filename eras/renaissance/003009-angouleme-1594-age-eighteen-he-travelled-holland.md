# Jean Louis Guez de Balzac

## Early Life and Education
Jean Louis Guez de Balzac was a French author born in Angouleme in 1594. During his youth, specifically at the age of eighteen, he traveled in Holland accompanied by Theophile de Viaud. The relationship between the two men later deteriorated, resulting in the exchange of bitter recriminations.

## Rise to Prominence
Balzac early in his career established connections with influential figures, including the duc d'Epernon and his son, Louis, the Cardinal de la Valette. It was the Cardinal who took Balzac to Rome. He developed a significant reputation through letters written to his acquaintances and to various individuals holding high positions within the French court. 

His success led to him becoming an habitue of the Hotel de Rambouillet, where he received numerous compliments. While Richelieu offered him lavish promises and praise, Balzac never received the preferment he had expected from him.

## Literary Career and Contributions
Balzac is primarily remembered for his *Lettres*. A first collection of these letters was published in 1624 and was received with great favor; a second collection followed in 1636, and the *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* was printed the following year. Although the subject matter of these letters is described as affected and empty, they are credited with a real mastery of style. Balzac introduced precision and clearness into French prose and encouraged the development of the language on national lines by emphasizing idiomatic elements. This work is considered a reform of French prose parallel to the reform executed by Malherbe in verse.

In addition to his correspondence, Balzac authored several longer works and dissertations on style, including:
* *Le Prince* (1631), an eulogy of Louis XIII.
* *Socrate chretien* (1652), which is regarded as the best of his longer works.
* *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).

## Later Years and Death
Balzac eventually retired to the chateau of Balzac. From this residence, he maintained correspondence with figures such as Valentin Conrart and Jean Chapelain. In 1634, he was elected to the Academy. He died in Angouleme on February 18, 1654.
