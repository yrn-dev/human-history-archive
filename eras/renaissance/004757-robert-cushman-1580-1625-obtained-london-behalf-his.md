# Robert Cushman

## Overview
Robert Cushman (c. 1580–1625) was an associate of the Separatist movement and a key figure in the early efforts to establish English settlements in North America. He is primarily noted for his role in securing the legal authority necessary for the migration of his associates to the New World.

## Legal Contributions and the Virginia Company
In 1619, Robert Cushman acted on behalf of his associates in London. During this time, he worked alongside William Brewster to obtain a land patent from the Virginia Company. This legal instrument was essential for the group's plans to settle in America.

## Association with William Brewster
Cushman's activities were closely linked with those of William Brewster, a leader of the "Pilgrims." Brewster had previously been involved in the Separatist church of Scrooby and had moved to Holland in 1608 to seek freedom of worship. While in Leiden, Brewster had been the ruling elder of the Congregation and had engaged in the secret printing of books proscribed by the English government between 1616 and 1619. Following the seizure of these printing types and the arrest of Brewster's partner, Thomas Brewer, Brewster escaped and collaborated with Cushman in London to secure the aforementioned land patent in 1619.

## Legacy and Timeline
Robert Cushman's life spanned from approximately 1580 until his death in 1625. His efforts in London provided the foundational legal framework that preceded the 1620 emigration of the "Mayflower" passengers and the subsequent founding of the Plymouth Colony.
