# Henry Stafford and the Barony of Stafford

The history of the Stafford family's titles and estates during the 16th century is characterized by a cycle of forfeiture and restoration, primarily linked to the dukedom of Buckingham.

## The Fall of the 3rd Duke of Buckingham
The ducal title of Buckingham was held by the Stafford family until the early 16th century. Edward Stafford (1478–1521), the 3rd duke, served as lord high constable at the beginning of the reign of Henry VIII. However, Edward fell from favor due to his opposition to Wolsey. He was condemned for treason and executed on May 17, 1521. Following his execution, the title of duke was forfeited under an attainder.

## Restoration of Henry Stafford
Henry Stafford (1501–1563) was the only son of the 3rd duke. During his father's lifetime, Henry had been styled as the earl of Stafford. Following the attainder of his father in 1521, Henry's status was gradually restored. In 1522, he was given back his estates. Later, in 1547, he was restored in blood by parliament and granted the title of Baron Stafford.

## Succession and Later History of the Barony
The title of Baron Stafford continued in this line until 1640, when it became extinct upon the death of Roger, the 5th Baron. In that same year, the barony of Stafford was granted to William Howard (1614–1680). Two months after receiving the barony, Howard was created Viscount Stafford; he was subsequently beheaded in 1680. 

The family honors continued as Howard's son was created earl of Stafford in 1688, though this specific title became extinct in 1762. The descent to the barony of 1640 was eventually established in 1825 by the House of Lords in the person of Sir G.W. Jerningham, and the title remained within the Jerningham family.

## Origins of the Stafford Connection to Buckingham
The Stafford family's association with the Buckingham title began through marriage. Anne, the sister of Humphrey (who was the son of Thomas of "Woodstock," the earl of Buckingham created in 1377), became countess of Buckingham in her own right. Anne married Edmund Stafford, earl of Stafford. Upon Anne's death in 1438, the title passed to her son, Humphrey Stafford, earl of Stafford. Humphrey was created duke of Buckingham in 1444, establishing the ducal line that lasted until the execution of the 3rd duke in 1521.
