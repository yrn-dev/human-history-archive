# Michel Baron

Michel Baron (1653–1729) was a prominent French actor and playwright who became a dominant figure on the French stage during the late 17th century. Born in Paris, Baron was the son of a talented actress (who died in 1662) and a leading actor (who died in 1655). His family name was originally Boyron.

## Early Career and Association with Moliere
Baron began his theatrical career at the age of twelve when he joined the *Petits Comediens Dauphins*, a company of children in which he was considered the brightest star. His talent attracted the attention of Moliere, who, with the permission of the king, secured Baron for his own company. Although Baron eventually withdrew from Moliere's company due to a misunderstanding with the dramatist's wife, he rejoined in 1670. During this second tenure, he appeared in Corneille's *Tite et Berenice* in the role of Domitien, as well as in *Psyche*. Baron remained a member of Moliere's company until the playwright's death.

## Mastery of the French Stage
Following his time with Moliere, Baron joined the company at the Hotel de Bourgogne. From this period until his retirement in 1691, he was regarded as the undisputed master of the French stage. In this capacity, he created many of the leading roles in the tragedies written by Racine. 

In addition to his acting, Baron was a writer and translator. He authored two comedies, *L'Homme a bonnes fortunes* (1686) and *La Coquette* (1687), and wrote the works *Les Enlevements* (1685) and *Le Debauche* (1689). He also translated and acted in two plays written by Terence.

## Later Life and Family
After his retirement in 1691, Baron returned to the stage in 1720, reappearing at the Palais Royal where he performed a multitude of parts. He died on December 22, 1729.

The Baron family continued to have a presence in the theater through his descendants. His eldest son, Etienne Michel Baron (1676–1711), was also a fine actor. Etienne Michel Baron left behind a son and two daughters, all of whom performed at the Comedie Francaise.
