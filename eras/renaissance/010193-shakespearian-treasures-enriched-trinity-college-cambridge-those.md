# Shakespearian Treasures and Trinity College, Cambridge

## The Capell Collection
During the reign of George III, a tradition of public-spiritedness among book collectors continued, leading to the establishment of specialized collections rather than general libraries containing only a few rarities. As part of this trend, the Shakespearian treasures collected by Capell enriched Trinity College, Cambridge. This occurred during a period when other notable collections were also being distributed to public institutions; for example, Malone's collections were sent to the Bodleian library at Oxford, and Garrick bequeathed a fine collection of English plays to the British Museum.

## Academic and Literary Connections to Trinity College
Trinity College, Cambridge, has maintained various connections to Shakespearian scholarship and the arts. Alexandre Beljame (1842–1906), a French writer and Shakespearian scholar, served as the Clark lecturer on English literature at Trinity College, Cambridge, during 1905–1906. Beljame was recognized for his academic work on Shakespeare, receiving an academic prize in 1902 for his editions of *Julius Caesar*, *Othello*, and *Macbeth*.

The college also has historical ties to the theatrical world. Barton Booth (1681–1733), an English actor known for his roles as Brutus and Hotspur, as well as his portrayal of the ghost in *Hamlet*, was once a student at Trinity College, Cambridge, from which he ran away in 1698 to pursue a theatrical career in Dublin.

## Broader Institutional Context
Trinity College, Cambridge, appears frequently in the records of academic and professional achievement across various disciplines. Its fellows and alumni have included:
*   **Science and Mathematics:** Sir Joseph John Thomson, the Cavendish Professor of Experimental Physics; Augustus De Morgan, who gained a Trinity scholarship in 1825; and a former Fellow and Assistant Tutor who served as a Professor of Mathematics at the University of Manchester.
*   **Law and Humanities:** John Percival Postgate, a Professor of Latin and editor of the *Classical Quarterly*; and Sir Herbert Stephen, a Barrister-at-Law.
*   **Theology and Classics:** Alfred Barry, who gained a first-class in the classical tripos in 1848; and the Rev. Frederick Field. Additionally, W. Aldis Wright served as a fellow of Trinity College and acted as secretary for a company appointed to work on the Old Testament.

## History of Public Book Collections
The acquisition of the Capell treasures at Trinity College is part of a wider historical movement toward public ownership of libraries. In the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries, large-scale book buyers often sought public resting-places for their collections. Other examples of such bequests to Cambridge colleges include the books of Sir Thomas Smith to Queens' College, the library of Archbishop Williams to St John's College, and the library of Pepys to Magdalene College. Archbishop Parker's books were left to Corpus Christi College under severe restrictions.

In contrast to these college bequests, other massive collections contributed to national institutions. The library of George III, containing over 80,000 books and 440 manuscripts, passed to the British Museum in 1823. The British Museum was further enriched in 1846 by the library of Thomas Grenville and the collection of the Rev. C.M. Cracherode. The foundation of the British Museum in 1753 was prompted by the need to care for the Cotton manuscripts and the offer of Sir Hans Sloane's collection of approximately 50,000 printed books.
