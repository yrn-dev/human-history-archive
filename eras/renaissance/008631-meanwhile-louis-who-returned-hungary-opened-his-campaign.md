# King Louis of Bohemia and Hungary

The history of the region involving Bohemia and Hungary in the early 16th century is marked by the reign of King Louis, the son of King Vladislav and Anna de Candale. Louis's path to power began early, as he was crowned king of Bohemia at the age of three. Following the death of his father, Vladislav, in 1516, Louis succeeded him.

## Early Reign and Guardianship
Upon his succession, the guardianship of the young king was entrusted to Emperor Maximilian I and Sigismund, the king of Poland, according to the instructions left by Vladislav. While the Bohemian estates accepted this arrangement, they strictly forbade the guardians from interfering in the internal affairs of Bohemia. During this period, the country was ruled almost without control by the great Bohemian nobles, specifically the supreme burgrave, Zdenek Leo, lord of Rozmital.

## Constitutional Developments in Bohemia
The nominal reign of King Louis began with a significant event for the constitutional development of Bohemia: the diet of St Wenceslas in 1517. The members of the estates assembled on September 28, the anniversary of the saint. During this meeting, the estates settled long-standing causes of discord. The citizens renounced certain previously claimed privileges, and in exchange, the other two estates recognized municipal autonomy and tacitly sanctioned the presence of citizens at diet meetings, a practice that had been occurring informally since 1508. On October 24, the three estates declared their agreement to live together in friendly intercourse as members of the same race and country.

## Arrival in Prague and Administrative Changes
In 1522, Louis arrived in Bohemia from Hungary, where he had also been elected king. Upon reaching Prague, he took decisive action by dismissing all Bohemian state officials, including the powerful Leo of Rozmital. To manage the country during his absences, Louis appointed Charles of Munsterberg—a grandson of King George and cousin of Prince Bartholomew—as the regent of Bohemia. He also appointed John of Wartenberg as the burgrave.

## The Turkish Conquest and the Battle of Mohacs
The later years of Louis's reign were overshadowed by the advances of the Turks, who were making significant headway in Hungary. This culminated in the battle of Mohacs on August 28, 1526. In this conflict, Suleiman the Magnificent defeated and killed King Louis. 

Following this victory, the Turks conquered and held the greater part of Hungary, including Buda-Pesth, until the peace of Karlowitz in 1699. The Turkish threat continued to escalate, with Suleiman the Magnificent nearly taking Vienna in 1529.

## Aftermath and the Habsburg Struggle
The death of Louis left a power vacuum in Hungary and Bohemia. Louis's sister, Anne, had married Ferdinand, the brother of Emperor Charles V. Ferdinand spent much of his life attempting to secure Hungary and combating the Turks. He faced an active rival in John Zapolya, who received support from Suleiman. 

The Emperor Charles V was greatly concerned by the Turkish advances and attempted to drive them back. However, he struggled to unite the German princes against the enemy. While Charles eventually formed an alliance against the Turks with Francis I of France in 1538, internal German struggles involving the Protestant Schmalkaldic League forced the Emperor to divert his attention away from a great campaign to recover Hungary for Christendom. Eventually, Ferdinand agreed to pay Suleiman an annual tribute for the small portion of Hungary—approximately 12,228 square miles—that he managed to hold.
