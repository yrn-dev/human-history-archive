# BOIARDO, MATTEO MARIA, COUNT

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1434–1494) was an Italian poet and nobleman. Born at Scandiano, one of his family's seignorial estates near Reggio di Modena, he came from an illustrious and noble house that was established at Ferrara, though the family originally hailed from Reggio. There is some discrepancy regarding his birth date; Tiraboschi places it around 1434, while Mazzuchelli suggests 1420.

## Education and Public Career
Boiardo entered the university of Ferrara at an early age. During his studies, he acquired a strong proficiency in Latin and Greek, as well as knowledge of Oriental languages. He eventually earned doctorates in both law and philosophy.

Following his education, Boiardo served at the court of Ferrara, where he was favored by Duke Borso d'Este and the subsequent duke, Hercules. He held several honorable positions during his career. In 1478, he was appointed governor of Reggio. Three years later, he was elected captain of Modena and was simultaneously reappointed as the governor of the citadel and town of Reggio. He remained in this position until his death in 1494, although the specific month of his passing is not recorded.

## Literary Works and Style
Boiardo is primarily recognized for his contribution to the romantic epic. His most significant work, *Orlando Innamorato*, was finished in 1486. This poem is regarded as one of the most important pieces of Italian literature, serving as the first romantic epic of sufficient quality to act as a model for others; it is noted as having undoubtedly influenced Ariosto's *Orlando Furioso*.

The *Orlando Innamorato* was composed for the amusement of the court of Duke Hercules, though Boiardo did not write it within the court's precincts. Instead, he typically retired to his estates, such as Scandiano, to compose. According to Tiraboschi, Mazzuchelli, Vallisnieri, and Castelvetro, Boiardo incorporated descriptions of the pleasant surroundings of his chateau into the poem. Furthermore, he drew the names of several of his heroes—including Agramant, Sacripant, Gradasse, and Mandricardo—from the names of his own peasants, as he felt their "uncouthness" was appropriate for Saracen warriors.

## Influences and Subject Matter
Regarding the inspiration for *Orlando Innamorato*, Gravina and Mazzuchelli asserted that Boiardo used Homer's *Iliad* as his model. They suggested the poem was a "reflex image" of the *Iliad*, noting that Angelica occupies the role of Helen and Paris is besieged in a manner similar to the city of Troy. However, the actual subject matter of the poem is derived from the *Fabulous Chronicle* of the pseudo-Turpin. Despite this source, the detailed plot bears little resemblance to the *Fabulous Chronicle*, with the exception of the presence of principal warriors such as Oliver, Roland, and Charlemagne.

## Publication History
Boiardo did not live to see the completion or publication of his poem. *Orlando Innamorato* was printed in Scandiano in 1495, the year following his death, under the supervision of his son, Count Camillo. A Latin letter from Antonia Caraffa di Reggio, dated the kalends of June 1495, is prefixed to the poem. While the first edition is without a date, a second edition appeared in Venice before the year 1500, and the work was reprinted there twice more during the following twenty years.

Boiardo's work influenced later poets; for instance, Berni remodelled Boiardo's epic in 1541, while Teofilo Folengo ridiculed the school of poetry to which Boiardo belonged in the 1526 work *Orlandino*.
