# BORDONE, PARIS

Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. He began his artistic training in 1509 when he entered the *bottega* of Titian.

## Artistic Development and Style
Much of the biographical information regarding Bordone is attributed to Vasari. According to Vasari, Bordone did not spend many years under Titian's tutelage and instead attempted to imitate the manner of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, evidence suggests that the Giorgionesque elements found in Bordone's early works were actually derived from Titian. Bordone imitated Titian so closely that some of his paintings continue to be attributed to Titian to this day. For example, the "Baptism of Christ" in the Capitoline gallery is ascribed to Bordone by Dr. Bode and Crowe and Cavalcaselle, while Morelli identifies it as an early work by Titian.

Bordone's work is characterized by a nobility of style and a golden harmony of colour inherited from Titian, as well as a dignified approach to portraiture and a realistic conception of the human figure. Conversely, his work is noted for some inconsistencies; his nudes are described as slightly coarse in form, and the action of his figures is frequently affected and unnatural.

## Career and Commissions
Bordone was a child of the Renaissance who produced a wide variety of subjects, including religious pictures, allegories, nymphs, cupids, mythological scenes, and subjects based on the fables of Ovid. While he worked across many genres, he excelled specifically as a portraitist.

In his early career, Bordone executed numerous important mural paintings in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, though all of these have since perished. In 1538, he was invited to the court of Francis I in France. While there, he painted many portraits; however, no trace of these remains in French collections, except for two portraits at the Louvre which were acquired later. During his return journey from France, Bordone undertook significant works for the Fugger palace at Augsburg, but these have also been lost.

## Surviving Works
Bordone's principal surviving work is the "Fisherman and Doge," located at the Venice Academy. Other notable surviving works include:
* **National Gallery, London:** A portrait of a lady and "Daphnis and Chloe."
* **Bridgwater House:** A "Holy Family."
* **Tadini collection at Lovere:** A "Madonna."
* **Other locations:** Paintings in the Duomo of Treviso and two mythological pictures located in the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese.

## Historiography
Since the time of Vasari, very little has been written about Paris Bordone, aside from references in general works on Italian painting. In 1900, the committee of the fourth centenary of Paris Bordone in Treviso published *Della Vita e delle Opere di Paris Bordone*, authored by G. Biscaro and L. Barlo. Additionally, P.G. Molmenti published a sixteen-page paper on the artist in the *Nuova Antologia* on November 16, 1900.
