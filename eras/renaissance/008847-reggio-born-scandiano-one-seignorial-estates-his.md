# Matteo Maria Boiardo

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1434–1494) was an Italian poet born into a noble and illustrious house. Although his family was established at Ferrara, they were originally from Reggio. Boiardo was born at Scandiano, which was one of the seignorial estates belonging to his family and located near Reggio di Modena. There is some discrepancy regarding his birth date; Tiraboschi places it around the year 1434, while Mazzuchelli suggests 1420.

## Education and Public Career
At an early age, Boiardo entered the university of Ferrara. During his studies, he acquired a strong knowledge of Latin and Greek, as well as Oriental languages. He eventually earned doctorates in both law and philosophy.

Boiardo spent time at the court of Ferrara, where he was favored by Duke Borso d'Este and later by his successor, Hercules. This relationship led to several honorable employments. In 1478, he was named governor of Reggio. Three years later, he was elected captain of Modena and was subsequently reappointed as the governor of the town and citadel of Reggio. He remained in this position until his death in 1494, though the specific month of his passing is not recorded.

## Literary Works and the Orlando Inamorato
Boiardo composed almost all of his works for the amusement of the court of Duke Hercules, though he typically did not write them within the court's precincts. Instead, he would retire to his estates, such as Scandiano, to focus on his compositions.

His most significant contribution to Italian literature is the poem *Orlando Inamorato*. This work is regarded as one of the most important poems in the Italian language and served as the first example of a romantic epic that could act as a model; it is noted for undoubtedly influencing Ariosto's *Orlando Furioso*. While some writers, including Mazzuchell and Gravina, claimed that Boiardo used Homer's *Iliad* as a model—suggesting that Angelica represents Helen and the siege of Paris mirrors that of Troy—the actual subject matter is derived from the *Fabulous Chronicle* of the pseudo-Turpin. Aside from principal characters such as Roland, Charlemagne, and Oliver, there is little resemblance between the detailed plots of the two.

Boiardo utilized his own surroundings for inspiration, inserting descriptions of the agreeable environs of his chateau into the poem. He also drew the names of his heroes from his own peasants; names such as Agramant, Sacripant, Gradasse, and Mandricardo were chosen because their uncouthness seemed appropriate for Saracen warriors.

Boiardo did not live to finish *Orlando Inamorato*. It was printed in Scandiano the year after his death under the supervision of his son, Count Camillo. A Latin letter from Antonia Caraffa di Reggio prefixed to the poem is dated June 1495. A second edition was printed in Venice before the year 1500.

## Other Writings
In addition to his epic poetry, Boiardo authored several other works:
* *Il Timone* (a comedy, printed in Scandiano, 1500)
* *Sonnetti e Canzoni* (Reggio, 1499)
* *Carmen Bucolicon* (Reggio, 1500)
* *Cinque Capitoli in terza rima* (Venice, 1523 or 1533)
* *Apulejo dell' Asino d'Oro* (Venice, 1516, 1518)
* *Asino d'Oro de Luciano tradolto in volgare* (Venice, 1523)
* *Erodoto Alicarnasseo istorico, tradotto di Greco in Lingua Italiana* (Venice, 1533 and 1538)
* *Rerum Italicarum Scriptores*
