# Five New Playes (1652)

*Five New Playes* is a collection of dramatic works by the English dramatist Richard Brome, edited by Alexander Brome. Published around 1652, the volume includes the play *Madd Couple Well Matcht*, which is believed to have been acted in 1639. Other works contained within this specific edition are *Novella* (acted 1632), *Court Begger* (acted 1632), *City Witt*, and *The Damoiselle or the New Ordinary*.

## Richard Brome
Richard Brome (d. 1652) was an English dramatist whose genius was centered entirely in comedy. He left behind fifteen pieces. Brome's early career was marked by his role as a servant to Ben Jonson, a relationship that eventually evolved into a friendship. The influence of Jonson is evident in Brome's work through the development of his plots, the amount of curious information included, and his strongly marked characters. 

Brome's reputation was established with the play *The Northern Lasse* (1632). Knowledge of his personal character is derived primarily from lines written to him by Ben Jonson, which were prefixed to that play. In addition to the works in *Five New Playes* (1652?), Brome authored several other pieces:
* **Published separately:** *The Antipodes* (acted 1638, printed 1640), *The Sparagus Garden* (acted 1635, printed 1640), *A Joviall Crew, or the Merry Beggars* (acted 1641, printed 1652, and later revised as an "opera" in 1731), and *The Queenes Exchange* (printed 1657).
* **Included in Five New Playes (1659):** *The English Moor, or The Mock Marriage*; *The Love-Sick Court, or The Ambitious Politique*; *Covent Garden Weeded*; *The New Academy, or The New Exchange*; and *The Queen and Concubine*.
* **Collaborations:** Brome collaborated with Thomas Heywood on *The late Lancashire Witches* (printed 1634).

The complete *Dramatic Works of Richard Brome* were published in 1873.

## Alexander Brome
The editor of the 1652 collection, Alexander Brome (1620-1666), was an English poet and an attorney by profession. He edited two volumes of Richard Brome's plays. Alexander Brome was known for his satirical verses—specifically those written against the Rump and in favor of the Royalists—as well as many drinking songs. 

In 1661, he published *Songs and other Poems*, a volume featuring epigrams, translations, epistles, elegies, epitaphs, ballads, and songs on various subjects, including political songs. This volume featured an introductory eclogue by Izaak Walton. Due to his wit and gaiety, he was referred to as the "English Anacreon" in Edward Phillips's *Theatrum Poetarum*. His other works include a 1666 translation of Horace (produced by himself and others) and a comedy titled *The Cunning Lovers* (1654).
