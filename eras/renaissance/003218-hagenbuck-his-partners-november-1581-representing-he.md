# Christopher Hagenbuck and the 1581 Banking Proposal

## The Petition to Queen Elizabeth
In November 1581, Christopher Hagenbuck and his partners submitted a petition to Queen Elizabeth. The document, which was originally printed in Italian, proposed the establishment of an "office" designed to manage a very large sum of money annually. Hagenbuck represented that this method could be instituted without any expense to the Queen.

The primary objectives of the proposed office were to ensure that the Queen would always be provided with any "notable sum of money" she might wish and to provide similar benefits to the State and the people. Furthermore, Hagenbuck asserted that this system would keep the country in a state of abundance and eliminate the "extreme usuries" that were devouring the people and the Queen.

## Terms and Agreement
As a condition for explaining the details of his plan, Hagenbuck requested a payment of 6% every year of the total mass of money received by the office for a period of twenty years. Queen Elizabeth responded by agreeing to grant Hagenbuck and his partners 4% for a term of twenty years. This grant was to be confirmed under the great seal. Although the document was signed by Francis Walsingham, the records indicate that nothing further appears to have come of the proposal.

## Historical Context and Similar Proposals
The timing of Hagenbuck's petition suggests a connection to contemporary developments in Europe. Specifically, the date of the document aligns with the establishment of the Banco della Piazza di Rialto in Venice. It is considered likely that the concept of establishing a bank had become familiar to Hagenbuck through his commerce with Venice and was a concept circulating among those connected with business at the time.

Hagenbuck's proposal was not an isolated event in English state papers. Similar proposals were recorded in 1621 and 1622, and again in 1662 and 1666. However, like the 1581 petition, these subsequent proposals were never carried into practice.
