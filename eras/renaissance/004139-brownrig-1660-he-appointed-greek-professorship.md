# Isaac Barrow

## Academic Appointments and Greek Professorship
Upon arriving in England, Isaac Barrow received ordination from Bishop Brownrig. In 1660, he was appointed to the Greek professorship at Cambridge. At the start of his tenure in this office, Barrow intended to lecture on the tragedies of Sophocles; however, he changed his plans and instead chose to lecture on the rhetoric of Aristotle. These lectures were subsequently lost after they were lent to a friend who failed to return them.

## Mathematical Career and the Royal Society
In July 1662, following a recommendation from Dr. John Wilkins—who served as the master of Trinity College and later became the bishop of Chester—Barrow was elected as the professor of geometry at Gresham College. Shortly thereafter, in May 1663, he was chosen as a fellow of the Royal Society during the first election conducted by the council after they had obtained their charter.

During the same year, the executors of Henry Lucas appointed Barrow as the first professor of a mathematical chair at Cambridge, which had been founded according to the terms of Lucas's will. Although holding two professorships was not considered inconsistent, Barrow chose to resign his position at Gresham College on May 20, 1664.

## Transition to Divinity and Later Career
Barrow continued in his mathematical chair until 1669, at which point he resigned the position to his pupil, Isaac Newton. This resignation marked Barrow's decision to abandon the study of mathematics in favor of the study of divinity. At the time he left his professorship, he held the status of a fellow of Trinity College. Following this transition, he received a small sinecure in Wales from his uncle, and Dr. Seth Ward, the bishop of Salisbury, granted him a prebend in that church.

In 1670, Barrow was created a doctor in divinity by mandate. On February 13, 1672, by the king's patent, he was appointed to succeed Dr. Pearson as the master of Trinity College following Pearson's promotion to the see of Chester. Barrow's academic leadership continued into 1675, when he was chosen as the vice-chancellor of the university.

## Death and Legacy
Isaac Barrow died on May 4, 1677. He was interred in Westminster Abbey. Following his death, his friends contributed to the erection of a monument in the abbey, which was surmounted by his bust.
