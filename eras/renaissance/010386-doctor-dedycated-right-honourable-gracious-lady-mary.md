# Doctor Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde was a learned physician of the 16th century who authored several works focusing on medicine, domestic hygiene, and general knowledge. He is identified in historical records as a "Physycke Doctor."

## The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge
Boorde's most entertaining work is titled *The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*. Written around 1547, this text was dedicated to Lady Mary, the daughter of King Henry VIII, who is described as "the right honourable, and gracious lady Mary." 

The book serves as a comprehensive guide intended to teach a man how to speak parts of various languages and understand the customs, fashions, and usages of different countries. Additionally, the work provides information on the majority of coins and currency currently in use across various regions. Within this text, Boorde uses lively verse to describe the Englishman, noting specific foibles such as obstinacy, fickleness, and a fondness for new fashions. The book also includes geographical descriptions of countries and a model dialogue written in the Cornish language.

## Other Medical and Scientific Works
Beyond his work on general knowledge, Boorde wrote several texts regarding health and science. These include:

*   ***Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor***: Published by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though undated earlier editions of this work exist.
*   ***The Brevyary of Health***: Dated approximately 1547.
*   ***The Princyples of Astronamy***: Dated approximately 1547.
*   ***A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV***: An almanac created by Boorde for the year 1545.

Additionally, a work titled "The Peregrination of Doctor Board" was printed in 1735 within *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, volume ii, by Thomas Hearne.

## Lost Works and Attributions
The archive notes that two of Boorde's works, the *Treatyse upon Berdes* and the *Itinerary of Europe*, have been lost. 

There are also several "jest-books" that have been attributed to Boorde, although these attributions lack authority. These include *Scogin's Jests* (1626), *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam* (with the earliest extant edition from 1630), a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*, and a piece printed by Wynkyn de Worde titled *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton, with his wyfe, and his daughter, and of two poore scholers of Cambridge*.
