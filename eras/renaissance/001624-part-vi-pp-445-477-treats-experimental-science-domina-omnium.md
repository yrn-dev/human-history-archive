# Experimental Science: Domina Omnium Scientiarum

Experimental science, referred to as *domina omnium scientiarum*, is a field of knowledge characterized by its reliance on experience rather than mere argument. While argument may decide a question, it is insufficient to provide the mind with certainty or satisfaction; such conviction is only achieved through intuition or immediate inspection, which is the primary contribution of experience.

## Methods of Knowledge and Experience
Knowledge is divided into two primary methods: argument and experience. Experience itself is further categorized into two distinct types: external and internal. External experience, commonly known as experiment, is limited in its scope; it cannot provide complete knowledge of spiritual matters, nor can it provide complete knowledge even of corporeal things. Internal experience, by contrast, involves the illumination of the mind by divine truth, a process of supernatural enlightenment that consists of seven grades.

## Prerogatives of Experimental Science
Experimental science is distinguished from operative arts and speculative sciences. It possesses three significant prerogatives over all other sciences:
1. It has the ability to verify the conclusions of other sciences through direct experiment.
2. It can discover truths that other sciences are unable to reach.
3. It opens a knowledge of the past and future by investigating the secrets of nature.

As a practical application of this inductive research method, the investigation into the cause and nature of the rainbow serves as a primary example.

## Historical Development and Alchemy
The roots of modern experimental science are linked to the work of Arab experimental chemists, known as alchemists. These early practitioners often kept their results and methods secret due to the potential far-reaching consequences and advantages of their discoveries. Through their work, they developed various technical and metallurgical devices, including essences, tinctures, distilling, dyes, alloys, and optical glass.

The primary goals of the alchemists were the discovery of the *elixir vitœ*—a stimulant intended to prolong life indefinitely and revivify age—and the "philosopher’s stone," which was believed to allow the transmutation of base metals into gold. While these two goals were not achieved, the patient experimenting of the Arab alchemists spread into the Christian world. Over time, these activities became more cooperative and social, and the alchemists eventually evolved into the first experimental philosophers. This transition shifted the focus from the search for immortality and gold toward the methods of modern experimental science, which offer the promise of illimitable power over human destiny and the world.

## Integration in Broader Scientific Works
Experimental science was envisioned as a component of a more comprehensive system of knowledge. In the proposed *Liber Sex Scientiarum*, a work intended to embrace the principles of all sciences, experimental science was positioned as a final stage of study. This outline suggested a progression starting with the causes of error and grammar, followed by logic, physics, mathematics, and speculative alchemy, concluding with experimental science.

## Application and Limitations in Other Fields
The application of experimental methods varies across different scientific disciplines:

*   **Physiology and Anatomy:** In the work of Kölliker, direct experimental research was used to enrich physiology, specifically through researches on poisons such as curare. Kölliker advocated for the joining of anatomy and physiology, protesting the specialized differentiation of modern development.
*   **Psychology:** Experimental methods have shifted the study of the association of ideas away from the introspection used by older writers. In the field of experimental psychology, association is now treated as a division of the study of mental reactions.
*   **Astronomy:** Unlike other fields, astronomy is primarily a science of observation. Because the matter of the heavens cannot be subjected to the same experimental scrutiny as matter that can be handled at will, direct experiment cannot play a part in the pursuit of astronomical knowledge.
