# BOISSARD, JEAN JACQUES

## Life and Education
Jean Jacques Boissard (1528–1602) was a French Latin poet and antiquary born in Besancon. He pursued his studies at Louvain; however, he secretly departed from that seminary due to his disgust with the severity of his master. Following his departure, he traveled through a significant portion of Germany before arriving in Italy. Boissard remained in Italy for several years, during which time he frequently experienced great financial straits.

## Antiquarian Pursuits and Travels
Boissard's time in Italy fostered a deep interest in antiquities. He established a collection consisting of various curious monuments sourced from Rome and the surrounding areas. With the goal of traveling through Greece, he visited the islands of the Archipelago, but a severe illness forced him to return to Rome. He resumed his antiquarian activities with great ardour and, upon completing his collection, returned to his native country.

## Religious Affiliation and Death
Boissard embraced the Protestant religion. Because he was not permitted to profess this faith publicly, he withdrew to Metz. He died in Metz on October 30, 1602.

## Literary Works and Influence
Boissard authored several important works, including:
*   *Poemata* (1574)
*   *Emblemata* (1584)
*   *Habitus Variarum Orbis Gentium* (1581), which featured seventy illuminated figures.
*   *Theatrum Vitae Humanae* (1596)
*   *Icones Virorum Illustrium* (1597)
*   *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum* (1597)
*   *Romanae Urbis Topographia* (1597–1602), a work noted for being very rare.
*   *De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis* (1605)

His work *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum* (published in Frankfort, 1596) served as a significant source for the English historian Richard Knolles. Knolles availed himself largely of Boissard's text for his own 1603 publication, *Generall Historie of the Turkes*.
