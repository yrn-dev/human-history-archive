# BAIUS, MICHAEL (1513-1589)

## Early Life and Academic Career
Michael Baius, also known as De Bay, was a Belgian theologian born in 1513 at Melun in Hainault. He received his education at Louvain University, where he studied theology and philosophy with distinguished success. This academic achievement led to a series of appointments within the university. In 1552, Charles V appointed him to the position of professor of scriptural interpretation.

## Theological Influence and Conflict
At Louvain, Baius established a significant reputation as a leader in the 16th-century anti-scholastic reaction. This movement operated under the banner of St Augustine. Baius' own Augustinian predilections eventually brought him into conflict with Rome regarding matters of free-will and grace. 

His writings are characterized by Harnack as containing unconscious tendencies toward Protestantism mixed with Catholic orthodoxy, with a particularly strong emphasis on the reality of actual and original sin. Due to these views, Pope Pius V issued the Bull *Ex omnibus afflictionibus* in 1567, which condemned seventy-nine propositions from the writings of Baius. Although Baius submitted to this condemnation, a renewal of the condemnation occurred in 1579 under Gregory XIII, triggered by indiscreet utterances from Baius and his supporters.

## Public Appointments and Later Life
Despite the theological conflicts with Rome, Baius maintained his professional standing. He was not removed from his professorship and further ascended in university administration, becoming the chancellor of Louvain in 1575. In 1563, he had been nominated as one of the Belgian representatives for the council of Trent, though he arrived too late to participate significantly in the deliberations. Baius died in 1589 while still holding the dignities of chancellor and professor.

## Legacy and Published Works
Baius is regarded primarily as a forerunner to Cornelius Jansen. His principal works were published in two parts in a single collected volume at Cologne in 1696, though some of his larger treatises remained unpublished. A detailed study of the author and his books was published in 1867 by Linsenmann, titled *Michael Baius, und die Grundlegung des Jansenismus*, in Tuebingen.
