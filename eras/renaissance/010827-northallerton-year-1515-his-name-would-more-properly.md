# Northallerton

Northallerton is a location in the county of York. Historically, it has served as a center for education and ecclesiastical administration.

## Notable Residents and Associations

The area surrounding Northallerton is associated with the birth of the English writer and scholar Roger Ascham. Born about the year 1515, Ascham hailed from Kirby Wiske, a village located in the North Riding of Yorkshire near Northallerton. Ascham was the third son of John Ascham, who served as the steward to Lord Scrope of Bolton. His mother, Margaret, is noted to have been well connected, although her family name is unknown. Records regarding Ascham's early life were primarily collected and edited by Edward Grant, the headmaster of Westminster, who delivered a panegyrical oration on Ascham's life in 1576. It is noted that Ascham's name would be more properly spelt Askham, likely deriving from Askham in the West Riding.

In later centuries, the town continued to be a site of education and religious leadership. Thomas Burnet, an English divine born about the year 1635 at Croft in Yorkshire, received his education at Northallerton before attending Clare Hall, Cambridge. Later, in 1729, John Balguy, an English philosopher and divine born in Sheffield, became the vicar of Northallerton. Balguy was known for his authorship under the pseudonym Silvius and his works concerning moral virtue and divine rectitude.
