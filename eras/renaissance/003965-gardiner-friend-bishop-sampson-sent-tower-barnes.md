# Robert Barnes and the Conflict of 1540

The career of Robert Barnes (1495–1540) was marked by a transition from established ecclesiastical roles to becoming an English reformer and martyr. Educated at Cambridge, Barnes graduated as a Doctor of Divinity in 1523 and served as the prior of the convent of Austin Friars. During the early third decade of the 16th century, he was among the Cambridge men who gathered at the White Horse Tavern for theological discussion and Bible-reading.

## Early Persecution and Exile
Barnes's challenges with religious authorities began in 1526 when he was brought before the vice-chancellor for preaching a sermon deemed heterodox. Following an examination by Wolsey and four other bishops, Barnes was given the choice to abjure his views or be burnt. He chose to abjure and was subsequently imprisoned in the Fleet prison and later at the Austin Friars in London.

In 1528, Barnes escaped to Antwerp and later visited Wittenberg, where he met Martin Luther. During this period, he encountered Stephen Vaughan, an advanced reformer and agent for Thomas Cromwell. Vaughan highly praised Barnes's writing in a letter to Cromwell, though he predicted that Barnes would "seal it with his blood."

## Diplomatic Role and Return to England
Barnes returned to England in 1531, serving as a primary intermediary between the English government and Lutheran Germany. His diplomatic utility was utilized by the crown in two major instances:
* In 1535, he was dispatched to Germany to attempt to persuade Lutheran divines to approve of King Henry VIII's divorce from Catherine of Aragon.
* Four years later, he was involved in negotiations regarding the marriage of Anne of Cleves.

While these missions were part of Cromwell's policy, the political climate shifted. By 1538, Henry VIII had refused to adopt Lutheran theology. This shift was codified in the statute of Six Articles in 1539. The subsequent disgust the king felt toward Anne of Cleves in 1540 led to the ruin of the agents who had pursued the Lutheran policy.

## The Struggle with Bishop Gardiner
The tension between the Protestant and reactionary parties within Henry VIII's council reached a peak in the spring of 1540. The conflict was signaled by a sermon delivered by Barnes at St Paul's Cross, in which he attacked Bishop Gardiner. This sparked a bitter struggle, resulting in Barnes being forced to recant and apologize. In response to Barnes's invective, Gardiner delivered a series of his own sermons at St Paul's Cross to counteract the reformer's claims.

The political tide shifted briefly a month or so later when Cromwell was made earl of Essex. During this period, Bishop Sampson, a friend of Gardiner, was sent to the Tower.
