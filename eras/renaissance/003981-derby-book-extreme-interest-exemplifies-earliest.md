# Derby

Derby is a town and county center in England with a history spanning from early Anglo-Saxon settlements to its development as a significant industrial hub. It is also associated with the titles of the Earls of Derby and the establishment of world-renowned equestrian races.

## History and Early Settlements
The district of Derbyshire was first settled by the West Angles during their northern conquests in the 6th century. These settlers, who moved up the valleys of the Dove and the Derwent, became known as the Pecsaetan. The region later served as the northern division of Mercia; notably, the Mercian witenagemot assembled at Repton in 848.

During the 9th century, the area faced frequent raids by the Danes. In 874, the Danes wintered at Repton and destroyed its monastery, which served as the burial site for the kings of Mercia. Under Guthrum, Derby was established as one of five Danish burghs. The town was recovered by Æthelflæd in 917, though it later fell under Danish rule again until it was regained by Edmund in 942.

## Antiquities and Architecture
Derbyshire possesses a wealth of ecclesiastical architecture. While monastic remains are limited, notable sites include:
* **Repton:** Features portions of a priory incorporated into school buildings and a church chancel assigned to the second half of the 10th century, with an even earlier crypt supported by four round pillars.
* **Dale Abbey:** Founded in the early 13th century for the Premonstratensian order, it retains an east window and a rest-house for pilgrims.
* **Beauchief Abbey:** Founded around 1175 by Robert Fitzranulf, the remaining village church features three Norman arches and a stately transitional Norman tower.

Other architectural highlights include the late Norman building of Melbourne church, which contains discovered mural paintings, and the Norman nave pillars and font at Youlgrave church. Pre-Conquest remains are also found in the chancel arches of Sawley and Marston Montgomery, as well as a carved font in Wilne church.

## Industry and Economy
Derby is a center for numerous important manufactures. The town is particularly celebrated for its china, and silk-throwing serves as its principal industry. Silk production is also carried out in Duffield and Belper. Other significant local industries include:
* **Textiles:** The manufacture of cotton hosiery, woollen goods, lace, net curtains, braids, cords, gimp trimmings, and elastic web weaving via power looms.
* **Heavy Industry and Mining:** The principal mineral of the region is coal, which supports smelting furnaces in Derby, Ilkeston, Chesterfield, and Alfreton. While ironstone is not extensively wrought locally, large quantities are imported for smelting due to the coal supply. Other minerals raised in small amounts include zinc, gypsum, and lead. Derbyshire is also a primary English county for limestone quarrying.
* **Transport and Chemicals:** The Midland Railway Company maintains its principal works at Derby. The town and its surrounding neighborhood also host several important colour and chemical works.

## The Epsom Derby and Oaks
The twelfth earl of Derby originated two famous sweepstakes at Epsom. On May 14, 1779, he established the Oaks (named after his hunting-box or seat "The Oaks" at Woodmansterne) for three-year-old fillies. The first Oaks was won by Lord Derby's own bay filly, Bridget.

In 1780, the earl established the Derby, a sweepstake for three-year-old colts with a stake of 50 sovereigns each (half forfeit). The first Derby was won by Sir C. Bunbury's chestnut colt, Diomed, who defeated eight other opponents. Both races are run annually at the end of May over a distance of 1(1/2) miles. Until 1839, the Derby was held on a Thursday, but it is now held on a Wednesday, with the Oaks following on Friday. In 1901, Volodyovski won the Derby.

## The Earls of Derby
The Earls of Derby have held significant land interests in England. In 1485, Henry VII granted the manor of Bolton (historically known by names such as Bodleton, Botheltun, and Bolton-le-Moors) to the first earl of Derby. While the manor is currently held by various lords, the earls of Derby continue to hold a fourth part of it.

Additionally, the earls of Derby were patrons of the arts. In January 1595, the poet Barnfield published *Cynthia, with certain Sonnets*, dedicating the preface to William Stanley, the new earl of Derby. This work is noted for being one of the earliest studies of both Shakespeare and Spenser.
