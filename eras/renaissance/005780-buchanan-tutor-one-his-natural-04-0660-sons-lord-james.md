# George Buchanan as Tutor to Lord James Stewart

## Appointment and Context
George Buchanan (1506–1582), a Scottish humanist and poet, served as a tutor to Lord James Stewart, one of the natural sons of King James V. This appointment occurred during a period when Buchanan was residing in the west country within the household of Lord Cassilis. 

At the time of his engagement by James V, Buchanan's religious stance was similar to that of Erasmus; while he did not repudiate the doctrines of the Church of Rome, he felt free to criticize its practices. Although he was interested in the arguments of the Reformers, he did not formally join their ranks until 1553.

## Literary Works and Influence
The appointment as tutor to Lord James Stewart was influenced by Buchanan's early literary output. His first production in Scotland was a poem titled *Somnium*, which served as a satirical attack on monastic life and the Franciscan friars. This assault on the monks was viewed favorably by James V, who not only engaged Buchanan as a tutor but also encouraged him to pursue more daring literary efforts.

Under these circumstances, Buchanan wrote the poems *Franciscanus & Fratres* and *Palinodia*. Although these works remained unpublished for many years, they resulted in Buchanan becoming an object of the bitterest hatred from the Franciscan order and their associates.

## Subsequent Career and Tutelage
Following his time with Lord James Stewart, Buchanan's life was marked by periods of exile and further academic appointments. In 1539, during a bitter persecution of Lutherans, Buchanan was arrested, though he managed to escape to London and then Paris. He later served as a professor of Latin at the college of Guienne in Bordeaux, where he taught Montaigne.

Buchanan returned to Scotland in 1560 or 1561 and continued his career as an educator for the Scottish royalty. In April 1562, he was installed as the tutor to the young Queen Mary, with whom he read Livy daily. Later, in 1570, following the assassination of the regent Murray, Buchanan was appointed as one of the preceptors to the young King James VI. It was through Buchanan's tuition that James VI acquired his scholarship. During this period, Buchanan also educated John Erskine, the 2nd or 7th Earl of Mar, alongside the king.

## Later Life and Offices
In addition to his roles as a royal tutor, Buchanan held several high-ranking positions. He served as the principal of St Leonard's College, St Andrews, starting in 1566, and was made moderator of the general assembly in 1567, despite being a layman. His political career included serving as director of chancery and lord privy seal, a position that granted him a seat in parliament, which he held at least until 1579. George Buchanan died on September 28, 1582.
