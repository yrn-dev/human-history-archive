# Buckingham

The title of Buckingham has been held by several notable figures in English history, including members of the Villiers and Grenville families, whose lives and works are documented across various biographical and literary records.

## George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham
The 2nd Duke of Buckingham was a man of letters and a patron of other writers. He is noted for having provided an equivalent pension from his own funds to Dryden after Dryden was dismissed from the laureateship. According to Matthew Prior, the Duke was responsible for the first recognition of both Wycherley and Samuel Butler. 

In his own literary pursuits, Buckingham wrote satires and occasional verses that displayed undeveloped poetical gifts. A collection of these was published by Tom Brown in 1704, though it included pieces not written by the Duke. He is most recognized as the author of *The Rehearsal*, a clever and amusing satire targeting the heroic drama and specifically Dryden. First performed at the Theatre Royal on December 7, 1671, and published in 1672, the play became popular and was later imitated by Sheridan in *The Critic* and by Fielding in *Tom Thumb the Great*. Prior notes that Buckingham delayed the publication of *The Rehearsal* until he was certain that Dorset would not "rehearse upon him again."

Buckingham's other published works include *The Battle of Sedgmoor* and *The Militant Couple* (both 1704), as well as two adapted plays: *The Chances* (1682), altered from a play by Fletcher, and *The Restoration or Right will take place* (1714), adapted from Beaumont and Fletcher's *Philaster*. Wood mentions a work titled *A Demonstration of the Deity*, though no trace of it currently exists.

Regarding his character, Horace Walpole noted that while Buckingham possessed wit comparable to Rochester or his first master, he suffered from a "want of principles." Congreve reported that even while dying, the Duke "slabbered" more wit than most people possessed in their best health. He was married three times; his first wife was Mary, the widow of Charles Berkeley, Earl of Falmouth. He died at Bath on January 29, 1706.

## Richard Grenville, 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos
Richard Grenville (1823–1889) was the only son of the 2nd Duke. Educated at Christ Church, Oxford and Eton, he served as the representative for the borough of Buckingham in parliament from 1846 to 1857 while he was still the Marquess of Chandos. Between 1853 and 1861, he served as chairman of the London & North-Western railway.

After succeeding to the dukedom, Grenville held several high-ranking government positions in the Conservative government of 1866–1868, serving as secretary for the colonies and lord president of the council. He later served as the governor of Madras from 1875 to 1880 and was chosen as chairman of committees in the House of Lords in 1886.

He married twice and had three daughters. Because he left no son, the dukedom became extinct upon his death. However, other titles were passed to his heirs: the earldom of Temple went to his nephew William Stephen Gore-Langton, the viscounty of Cobham went to his kinsman Charles George, 5th Baron Lyttelton, and the Scottish barony of Kinloss—a title he established in 1868—passed to his eldest daughter, Mary. His widow later married the 1st Earl Egerton of Tatton in 1894.

## Biographical and Literary Sources
The lives of the Dukes of Buckingham have been traced through numerous authorities. For the 2nd Duke, key sources include C.H. Firth's article in the *Dict. of Nat. Biography* (1899), Lady Burghclere's *George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham* (1903), and biographies by Brian Fairfax (1758) and the author of *Hudibras* in *The Genuine Remains of Mr Samuel Butler* (1759). Other relevant documents include *A Conference on the Doctrine of Transubstantiation* (1714) and *The Declaration of the ... Duke of Buckingham and the Earls of Holland and Peterborough* (1648).

For other figures associated with the name or era, sources include the *Fairfax Correspondence* (1848-1849) and various calendars of state papers and manuscripts. The works of the 2nd Duke were most recently edited in a two-volume 8vo edition by T. Evans in 1775.
