## Royal Attendance and Political Conflict
During the period of the English Civil War, York served as a location for royal activity. John Ashburnham, an English Royalist and son of Sir John Ashburnham of Sussex, entered the king's service early in his career. Ashburnham, who eventually became one of the king's chief advisers and held his full confidence, attended King Charles at York during the outbreak of the war with Scotland. Ashburnham's career was marked by significant royal service, including being made groom of the bedchamber and serving as treasurer of the royal army.

## Horse Racing in York
York was a center for horse racing, with records indicating that races commenced there in 1711. By 1727, there was a royal plate run for in York specifically for six-year-old horses weighing 12 stone each, consisting of 4-mile heats. The nature of royal prizes evolved during this era; in 1721, George I provided 100 guineas in specie instead of the gold cup at York that had been presented by Anne. Following this change, the plates for the king or queen were provided in cash.
