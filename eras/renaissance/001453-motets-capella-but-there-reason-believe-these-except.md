# Johann Sebastian Bach and His Vocal Works

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685-1750) was a German musical composer born into a family of significant importance in music history for nearly two hundred years. The family's genealogy, which Bach himself drew up and his son Philipp Emanuel completed, identifies Veit Bach—a miller and baker—as the family founder. Veit's son, Hans Bach, known as "der Spielmann," was the first professional musician in the family. Sebastian's grandfather was Christoph, the second son of Hans. Other notable ancestors include Heinrich of Arnstadt, whose sons Johann Michael and Johann Christoph were among the greatest forerunners of J. S. Bach. Sebastian particularly admired Johann Ludwig, a descendant of Veit Bach, and copied twelve of his church cantatas, occasionally adding his own work to them.

## Ecclesiastical Vocal Music

Bach's body of ecclesiastical work includes several major compositions and smaller forms. His larger works include the Mass in B minor, the Magnificat in D, and the Passions according to St Matthew and St John. He also composed a funeral ode for the Duchess Eberhardine, which is now known to have been arranged from portions of a lost Passion according to St Mark. Additionally, he wrote four short masses consisting only of the Kyrie and Gloria, which were mainly compiled from church cantatas.

In the category of works without orchestra, Bach composed five motets *a capella*. However, there is reason to believe that these were intended to be partly supported by the organ, with the exception of *Komm Jesu komm*. A sixth motet features an obligato figured-bass accompaniment. Other early choruses exist, though most were utilized in later works, alongside a large collection of plain chorales that include several original melodies.

Regarding attribution, the motet *Ich lasse dich nicht* ("I wrestle and pray") was formerly ascribed to Sebastian Bach, but it has long been known to be the work of one of his uncles, Johann Christoph.

## Secular Vocal Music

Bach's secular vocal output includes two works entitled *Dramma per Musica*: *Der Streit zwischen Phoebus und Pan* and *Der zufrieden gestellte Aeolus*. These works show no more essential connection to the stage than Handel's *Acis and Galatea*. 

His cantatas in this genre consist of seven solo and seven choral pieces. The latter three choral cantatas were almost entirely absorbed into the B minor mass and the Christmas oratorio. Among the solo cantatas, two are burlesque and two are Italian; one of the Italian works is Bach's only developed work for voice and clavier. He also composed several tunes with clavier bass that almost foreshadow the modern song.

## Publications and Instrumental Works

During his lifetime, almost the only works of Bach that were published were instrumental collections, most of which he engraved himself. In the realm of church cantatas, only one work—*Gott ist mein Koenig*—was published while he was alive. This piece was written when he was nineteen years old and is described as a very great work.

His instrumental output includes various clavier concertos. Seven of these were arranged from other sources and violin concertos. He also composed three concertos for two claviers (two of which were arranged from concertos for two violins) and two concertos for three claviers.
