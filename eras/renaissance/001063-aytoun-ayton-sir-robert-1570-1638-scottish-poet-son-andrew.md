# AYTOUN, SIR ROBERT (1570-1638)

Sir Robert Aytoun (also spelled Ayton), a Scottish poet, was born in 1570. He was the son of Andrew Aytoun of Kinaldie, located in Fifeshire.

## Education and Early Career
Aytoun received his education at the university of St Andrews. He was incorporated as a student of St Leonard's College in 1584 and subsequently graduated with an M.A. in 1588. Following his studies, he spent several years living in France. 

His rise to prominence occurred upon the accession of James VI to the English throne. While in Paris, Aytoun wrote a Latin panegyric that brought him into immediate favor at court. This professional ascent continued through the early 17th century; he was knighted in 1612 and held various lucrative offices. He served as the private secretary to the queens of both James I and Charles I.

## Literary Work and Style
Although Sir Robert Aytoun held a high reputation among his contemporaries as a writer, he possessed no ambition to be known as such. His literary output is divided between Latin and English works. In his English writing, he is noted as one of the earliest Scots to utilize the southern standard as a literary medium.

### Latin Works
Aytoun's Latin poetry includes the panegyric written for James VI, as well as the following pieces:
* _Epicedium in obitum Thoma Rhodi_
* _Basia, sive Strena ad Jacobum Hayum_
* _Lessus in funere Raphaelis Thorei_
* _Carina Caro_
* Various minor occasional and epitaphic pieces.

### English Works
His first English poem was titled _Diophantus and Charidora_, a work he mentions within his Latin panegyric to James. Aytoun also produced a number of sonnets, songs, and other pieces focused on amatory subjects.

## Preservation and Publication
The preservation of Aytoun's work has occurred through several different sources. His Latin poems were printed in _Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum_ (Amsterdam, 1637) on pages 40-75. His English poems were preserved in a manuscript in the British Museum (_Add. MSS._ 10,308), which had been prepared by his nephew, Sir John Aytoun. Additionally, some of his English poetry appears in the _Bannatyne Miscellany_ (p. 299, 1827) and in Watson's _Collection_ (1706-1711).

In 1871, Charles Rogers published _The Poems of Sir Robert Aytoun_ in London as a private printing, collecting both the Latin and English works. However, this edition—and a previous 1844 issue by the same editor—is considered unsatisfactory. The collection includes poems that cannot be ascribed to Aytoun, including "Sweet Empress" and the old version of "Auld Lang Syne," which are certainly not his work. The poem "I do confess thou'rt smooth and fair" is also suspected of not being by Aytoun.

## Death and Legacy
Sir Robert Aytoun died in London. He was buried in Westminster Abbey on February 28, 1638. Further details regarding his public career are available in the _Register of the Privy Council_ and the printed _Calendars of State Papers_ from that era. Biographies of Aytoun can be found in the _Dict. of Nat. Biog._ by Grosart and in the memoir included in the Rogers edition of his poems.
