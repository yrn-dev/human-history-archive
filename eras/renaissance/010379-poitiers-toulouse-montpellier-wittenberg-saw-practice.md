# Poitiers, Toulouse, Montpellier and Wittenberg

The cities of Poitiers, Toulouse, Montpellier, and Wittenberg appear in historical records as centers of academic study, medical practice, and religious development. While they served different roles across various eras, they were linked by the movements of scholars, physicians, and theologians.

## Academic and Medical Study
During the 16th century, these cities were destinations for those pursuing advanced education in medicine. Andrew Boorde, an English physician and author born in Sussex, visited the universities of Poitiers, Toulouse, Montpellier, and Wittenberg as part of his studies abroad. Boorde, who had previously been a member of the Carthusian order and was later freed from his monastic vows, traveled to these institutions after being dispensed from religion in 1521.

Montpellier, in particular, emerged as a significant center for medical training. Paul Joseph Barthez, a French physician born in Montpellier in 1734, began his medical studies there in 1750 and earned his doctor's degree in 1753. He later returned to the city to hold a medical professorship in 1759 and was created joint chancellor of the university in 1774. Similarly, Jean Astruc, a French physician and Biblical critic, graduated in medicine at Montpellier in 1703 and later served as a professor of medicine there after a period as the chair of anatomy at Toulouse between 1710 and 1717.

## Religious and Philosophical Developments
Toulouse and Wittenberg were pivotal sites for the establishment of religious orders and the emergence of theological reform.

In Toulouse, the Dominican order, also known as the Friars Preachers, established their first house in 1215 at the church of St Romain. St Dominic returned to Toulouse to resolve that the order would adopt the Rule of St Augustine. By 1216, the community at Toulouse was confirmed as a congregation of Canons Regular of St Augustine with a specific mission to preach.

Wittenberg became the center of a major religious shift in the early 16th century through the work of Martin Luther. Luther was sent to Wittenberg in 1508 to assist the university founded by Frederick the Wise. After graduating as Doctor of the Holy Scripture and taking the Wittenberg doctor's oath to vigorously defend evangelical truth, he succeeded Staupitz as professor of theology. In 1517, Luther appeared in Wittenberg to offer disputations against various orthodox practices and doctrines. His subsequent publication of the *Resolutions* discarded medieval ideas and practices regarding Indulgences, leading to a decrease in their sale in Germany and a conflict with the Papal Curia.

## Intellectual Influence and Civic Life
Beyond medicine and theology, these cities were sites of philosophical dissemination. In the 17th century, Pierre-Sylvain Regis worked to make Cartesianism popular by adjusting its metaphysics for general apprehension. In 1665, Regis visited Toulouse on a mission from Cartesian chiefs, where his lectures drew significant interest from the civic corporation and local women. He brought a similar enthusiasm to Montpellier in 1671.

## Regional and Military Context
The cities are also noted for their administrative and military history. Poitiers is recorded as the site of a decisive defeat in 732, where Charles Martel, the Mayor of the Palace, defeated the Moslim forces who had crossed the Pyrenees in 720. 

In later administrative structures, Toulouse served as the center of an *academie* (educational circumscription) for the department of Aveyron, while Montpellier served as the location for its court of appeal.
