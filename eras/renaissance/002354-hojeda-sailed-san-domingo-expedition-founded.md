# San Sebastian and the Expedition of Ojeda

## Foundation of San Sebastian
In 1509, the explorer Ojeda (also referred to as Hojeda) departed from San Domingo leading an expedition. The primary objective of this voyage was the establishment of a new settlement, which he founded and named San Sebastian. 

## The Role of Enciso and Balboa
Following the foundation of the settlement, Ojeda left specific instructions with Enciso, who was a lawyer from the town. Enciso was tasked with fitting out two ships to transport necessary provisions to the new colony. Enciso set sail for this purpose in 1510.

Among those who accompanied Enciso was Vasco Nunez de Balboa. Balboa, a gentleman by birth (*hidalgo*) born in Jerez de los Caballeros around 1475, had previously settled in Hispaniola to cultivate land near Salvatierra. However, his farming efforts were unsuccessful, and he became burdened by oppressive debts. To escape the unpleasantness of the town caused by these debts, Balboa managed to join Enciso's expedition by concealing himself in a cask of victuals intended for the voyage.

## The Ruins of San Sebastian and the Move to Darien
Upon the arrival of Enciso's expedition at San Sebastian, the party discovered that Ojeda had already departed and that the settlement had fallen into ruins. While Enciso remained undecided on how to proceed, Balboa proposed that the group sail for Darien, located on the Gulf of Uraba. Balboa had previously visited this area while on a voyage of discovery to the western seas with Rodrigo de Bastidas in 1501.

The proposal was accepted, and the adventurers founded a new town called Sta Maria de la Antigua del Darien.

## Conflict and the Rise of Balboa
The new settlement at Darien was soon marred by quarrels among the adventurers. These conflicts led to the deposition of Enciso, who was imprisoned and eventually sent back to Spain. He was accompanied by the alcalde Zamudio, an ally of Balboa.

With Enciso removed, Balboa assumed authority. He began a campaign to conquer the surrounding territory, utilizing bravery, courtesy, and just dealing to secure the friendship of various native chiefs. During these excursions, Balboa learned of the existence of gold in Peru and an ocean on the other side of the mountains, information provided to him by the cacique Comogre.

## Legal Challenges
Balboa's leadership was later challenged by legal actions initiated from Spain. He received letters from Zamudio informing him that Enciso had filed a complaint with the king. This resulted in a sentence that condemned Balboa and summoned him to return to Spain. Faced with this situation, Balboa resolved to attempt a great enterprise in hopes of achieving success.
