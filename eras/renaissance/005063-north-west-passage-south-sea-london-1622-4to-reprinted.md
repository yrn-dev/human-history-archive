# A Treatise on the North-West Passage to the South Sea

## Overview
*A Treatise on the North-West Passage to the South Sea* is a work published in London in 1622 in quarto (4to) format. The text focuses on the possibility of a North-West Passage to the South Seas, specifically proposing a route that passes through Hudson's Bay and the continent of Virginia. The work was later reprinted in volume iii, page 852, of Purchas's *Pilgrims*.

## Authorship and Context
The treatise was written by Henry Briggs (1556–1630), an English mathematician. Briggs was born at Warley Wood near Halifax, Yorkshire, and was a graduate of St John's College, Cambridge (1581), where he later obtained a fellowship in 1588. His academic career included serving as the reader of the physical lecture founded by Dr Thomas Linacre in 1592 and becoming the first professor of geometry at Gresham House (later Gresham College) in London in 1596.

In 1619, Briggs was appointed the Savilian professor of geometry at Oxford, a position he took after resigning from Gresham College on July 25, 1620. It was during his time at Oxford that he published the small tract regarding the North-West Passage in 1622.

## Related Mathematical Works
The publication of the treatise on the North-West Passage occurred alongside Briggs's extensive contributions to mathematics, particularly in the field of logarithms. Briggs is noted for proposing a change to the scale of logarithms from the hyperbolic form used by John Napier to a system where unity is the logarithm of the ratio of ten to one. Following conferences with Napier in Edinburgh in 1616 and 1617, Briggs published the first chiliad of his logarithms in 1617.

Other works by Briggs include:
* *Arithmetica Logarithmica* (London, 1624, folio), which provided logarithms for thirty thousand natural numbers to fourteen places.
* *Trigonometria Britannica* (printed in Gouda in 1631 and published in 1633), which contained tables of logarithmic sines and tangents, natural sines, and secants.
* *Logarithmorum Chilias prima* (London, 1617, 8vo).
* *Euclidis Elementorum VI. libri priores* (London, 1620, folio).

## Historical Context of North-West Passage Exploration
The pursuit of a North-West Passage was a broader objective for various navigators during the 16th and 17th centuries. For example, in 1534, the French navigator Jacques Cartier led an expedition of two ships and sixty-one men from St Malo to search for a north-west passage to the East. Cartier explored the strait of Belle Isle, the coast of Newfoundland, and the river St Lawrence.

In the early 17th century, English navigator William Baffin (1584–1622) also sought the passage. Baffin served as chief pilot under Captain James Hall in 1612 and later worked for the Muscovy Company. In 1615, as pilot of the ship *Discovery* under Captain Robert Bylot, Baffin examined Hudson Strait. In 1616, he sailed further north than his predecessor Davis, reaching approximately latitude 77° 45', and discovered Baffin Bay and the sounds named Lancaster, Smith, and Jones. Despite these discoveries, hopes of finding a passage to India via this route eventually diminished.
