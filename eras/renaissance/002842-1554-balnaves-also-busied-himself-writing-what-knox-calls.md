# Balnaves

Balnaves was a Scottish figure active during the 16th century who served as a paid agent for the English cause in Scotland and later held high judicial office. He is noted for his involvement in the defense of St Andrews, his diplomatic activities between Scotland and England, and his contributions to religious writing.

## Military and Political Activity in St Andrews
Balnaves was an active defender of the castle of St Andrews. While he did not participate in the murder of Beton, he became a paid agent for England. In December 1546, he received £100 from Henry VIII, and in 1547, Protector Somerset granted him an annuity of £125. During this period, he served as the English paymaster for the forces stationed at St Andrews.

In July 1547, the castle surrendered to the French fleet, and Balnaves was taken prisoner and transported to Rouen. Despite his captivity, Protector Somerset continued his pension and attempted to secure his release. Balnaves remained useful to the English government by providing information; in June 1554, Mary Tudor sent him £50 as a reward for these services.

## Return to Scotland and Public Office
Balnaves was permitted to return to Scotland in 1557 to regain his property. This permission was likely granted under the assumption that the burnings carried out by Mary Tudor would diminish his English sympathies and that he would serve Scotland during the threatening war between the two Catholic nations.

However, the accession of Queen Elizabeth altered his position. Mary of Guise accused Balnaves of engaging in "practices out of England." He took an active role in the rising of 1559 and was commissioned by the Congregation to seek assistance from the English government via Sir Ralph Sadleir at Berwick. In February 1560, he was selected as one of the Scottish representatives to negotiate with the duke of Norfolk.

In 1563, Balnaves was restored to his position as lord of session. He was also appointed by the General Assembly to be one of the individuals responsible for revising the *Book of Discipline*. His judicial career continued through the late 1560s; in 1567, he served as one of the judges for Bothwell regarding the murder of Darnley, and in 1568, he accompanied Moray to the York inquiry concerning the guilt of Queen Mary. Balnaves resigned his judicial office in 1574 and died in Edinburgh in 1579.

## Literary and Religious Works
Balnaves contributed to the religious literature of the Reformation. He wrote a work that John Knox described as "a comfortable treatise of justification." This text was found in manuscript form among the papers of the reformer Knox, featuring a preface written by Knox. The work was eventually published in Edinburgh in 1584 under the title *The Confession of Faith*.

Additionally, Balnaves has been identified as a Scots bard based on a single ballad titled "O gallandis all, I cry and call," which appeared in Allan Ramsay's *Evergreen* (published in two volumes between 1724 and 1727).
