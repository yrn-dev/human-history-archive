# Jean Jacques Boissard and His Works

Jean Jacques Boissard (1528–1602) was a French Latin poet and antiquary born in Besancon. His intellectual development was shaped by extensive travel and a deep interest in the monuments of antiquity.

## Life and Education
Boissard began his studies at Louvain, but he secretly departed from the seminary due to the severity of his master. Following his departure, he traveled through a significant portion of Germany before arriving in Italy. During his several years of residence in Italy, Boissard frequently faced great financial straits. However, this period was pivotal in developing his passion for antiquities. He established a collection consisting of various curious monuments sourced from Rome and its surrounding areas.

Later, Boissard visited the islands of the Archipelago with the goal of traveling through Greece, but a severe illness forced him to return to Rome. He resumed his antiquarian pursuits with great ardour and, upon completing his collection, returned to France. Because he was not permitted to publicly profess the Protestant religion, which he had embraced, he withdrew to Metz. He died there on October 30, 1602.

## Major Literary Works
Boissard produced a wide range of works, often focusing on history, iconography, and the study of different peoples. His bibliography includes:

*   ***Poemata*** (1574)
*   ***Habitus Variarum Orbis Gentium*** (1581), which featured seventy illuminated figures.
*   ***Emblemata*** (1584)
*   ***Theatrum Vitae Humanae*** (1596)
*   ***Icones Virorum Illustrium*** (1597)
*   ***Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum*** (1597)
*   ***Romanae Urbis Topographia*** (1597–1602), a work that is now considered very rare.
*   ***De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis*** (1605)

## Influence on Later Historians
Boissard's research on the Ottoman Empire served as a resource for other historians. Richard Knolles, an English historian educated at Lincoln College, Oxford, published his *Generall Historie of the Turkes* in 1603. This work, which was dedicated to King James I, availed itself largely of Boissard's *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum* (Frankfort, 1596).
