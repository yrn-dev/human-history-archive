# Cesare Borgia's Campaign in the Romagna

## Military Expedition of 1500
In October 1500, Cesare Borgia launched a military campaign into the Romagna. This expedition was conducted on the strength of Venetian friendship and was supported by an army consisting of 10,000 men. 

## Conquests and Surrenders
The campaign resulted in the rapid acquisition of several cities. Pandolfo Malatesta of Rimini and Giovanni Sforza of Pesaro fled their positions, leading their respective cities to open their gates to Cesare. The city of Faenza offered resistance, as its population remained devoted to their leader, Astorre Manfredi. Manfredi was described as a virtuous and handsome youth of eighteen years. 

In April 1501, Manfredi surrendered under the promise that his life would be spared. However, Cesare broke this word and sent Manfredi to Rome as a prisoner. In Rome, Manfredi was put to death after being foully outraged. Following the capture of Castel Bolognese, Cesare returned to Rome in June to engage in Franco-Spanish intrigues regarding the partition of Naples.

## Governance and Reputation
As a result of these conquests, Cesare became the lord of an extensive territory, and the pope appointed him duke of Romagna. Cesare was regarded as a terror to all of Italy due to his good fortune, his utter lack of scruple, and his cruelty. He was characterized as morose, unsympathetic, and possessing an insatiable avidity that would not brook any opposition.

## The Conspiracy of La Magione
Cesare's power was later shaken by a conspiracy centered at La Magione, a castle located near Perugia. The plotters included several princes whom Cesare had deposed, members of the Orsini family, and some of his own captains. Among these captains were G.P. Baglioni, Oliverotto da Fermo, and Vitellozzo Vitelli; these men had been granted estates by Cesare but conspired against the Borgias for fear of losing them.

This conspiracy led to risings in the Romagna and at Urbino, resulting in the defeat of papal troops. Although Cesare initially found himself without allies and faced a general Italian turn against the Borgia family, the situation changed when the French king promised assistance. This promise frightened the confederates into agreeing to terms.

## The Betrayal at Senigallia
Many of the conspirators lacked military or political skill and were prone to betraying one another. To demonstrate their repentance, Vitelli, Oliverotto, Paolo Orsini, and the duke of Gravina seized Senigallia in Cesare's name, as the town was still held for the duke of Urbino. Upon arriving at the town, Cesare decoyed the unsuspecting *condottieri* into his house, where he had them all arrested.
