# Paris Bordone

Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. He began his artistic training in 1509 when he entered the *bottega* of Titian. Much of the factual record regarding Bordone's life is attributed to the writings of Vasari, and subsequent research has provided little additional information.

## Artistic Style and Influence
According to Vasari, Bordone spent a limited number of years with Titian and attempted to imitate the style of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, evidence suggests that the Giorgionesque elements found in Bordone's early works were actually derived from Titian. Bordone's imitation of Titian was so precise that some of his paintings continue to be attributed to Titian to this day.

Bordone's work is characterized by a nobility of style and a "golden harmony of colour" inherited from Titian, as well as a dignified approach to portraiture and a realistic conception of the human figure. Conversely, critics note that his nudes are somewhat coarse in form and that the action of his figures often appears affected and unnatural.

## Career and Commissions
Bordone was a prolific artist of the Renaissance, producing religious pictures, allegories, nymphs, cupids, mythological scenes, and subjects based on the fables of Ovid. While he worked across various genres, he was considered to excel specifically as a portraitist.

His career included several major commissions, many of which are now lost:
* **Mural Paintings:** He executed numerous important murals in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, all of which have perished.
* **French Court:** In 1538, Francis I invited Bordone to France. He painted many portraits at the court, though no trace of these remains in French collections, with the exception of two portraits at the Louvre which were acquired later.
* **Augsburg:** During his return journey from France, he undertook significant works for the Fugger palace in Augsburg, but these have also been lost.

## Surviving Works
Despite the loss of many of his murals and portraits, several of Bordone's works survive in various collections:
* **Venice Academy:** His principal surviving work, the "Fisherman and Doge."
* **National Gallery, London:** A portrait of a lady and "Daphnis and Chloe."
* **Bridgwater House:** A "Holy Family."
* **Other Locations:** The Tadini collection at Lovere holds a "Madonna," and the Duomo of Treviso contains his paintings. Additionally, the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese each hold two mythological pictures.

The attribution of certain works remains a point of scholarly debate. For example, while Dr. Bode and Crowe and Cavalcaselle attribute the "Baptism of Christ" in the Capitoline gallery to Bordone, Morelli identifies it as an early work by Titian.

## Historiography and Literature
For a long period following the time of Vasari, very little was written about Paris Bordone outside of general works on Italian painting. This changed in 1900 when the committee of the fourth centenary of Paris Bordone in Treviso published *Della Vita e delle Opere di Paris Bordone*, authored by G. Biscaro and L. Barlo. Additionally, the *Nuova Antologia* published a sixteen-page paper on the artist by P.G. Molmenti on November 16, 1900.
