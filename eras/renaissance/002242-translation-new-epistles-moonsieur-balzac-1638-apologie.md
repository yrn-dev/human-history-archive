# Translation of New Epistles by Moonsieur D'Balzac (1638) and Apologie for Laymen's Writing in Divinity

## Overview of the Works
The *Translation of New Epistles by Moonsieur D'Balzac*, published in 1638, and the *Apologie for Laymen's Writing in Divinity, with a Short Meditation upon the Fall of Lucifer*, published in 1641, are literary and theological works authored by Sir Richard Baker, Knight. These texts were produced during a period of Baker's life spent primarily in writing while he was imprisoned.

## Authorship and Context
Sir Richard Baker is the author of these works. During his time of imprisonment, Baker focused his efforts on writing a variety of texts. His bibliography from this period is extensive and includes diverse subjects ranging from historical chronicles to religious meditations. 

In addition to the 1638 translation and the 1641 *Apologie*, Baker's other writings include:
* *Cato Variegatus* (or *Catoes Morall Distichs, Translated and Paraphrased*), published in 1636.
* *Meditations on the Lord's Prayer*, published in 1637.
* *Motives for Prayer upon the seaven dayes of ye weeke*, published in 1642.
* A translation of Malvezzi's *Discourses upon Cornelius Tacitus*, published in 1642.
* *Theatrum Redivivum, or The Theatre Vindicated*, which served as a response to William Prynne's *Histrio-Mastix* (1642).
* Various *Meditations* upon the psalms of David, which were later edited and collected by A. B. Grosart in 1882.

Baker's most significant work is the *Chronicle of the Kings of England from the Time of the Romans' Government unto the Death of King James*, published in 1643. While this *Chronicle* enjoyed extreme popularity for many years and was translated into Dutch in 1649, it is noted for having slight historical value due to numerous inaccuracies. The *Chronicle* was further continued by Edward Phillips, a nephew of John Milton, up until the year 1658.

## Subject of the Translation
The 1638 work by Baker is a translation of epistles by "Moonsieur D'Balzac." This refers to Jean Louis Guez de Balzac (1594-1654), a French author born in Angouleme. Guez de Balzac was known for his mastery of style and is credited with reforming French prose by introducing precision and clearness, emphasizing idiomatic elements in a manner parallel to Malherbe's reforms in verse. 

Guez de Balzac's reputation was largely built upon his letters, which were written to acquaintances and high-ranking members of the French court. A first collection of these *Lettres* was published in 1624, followed by a second collection in 1636 and a *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* the following year. Though described as affected and empty in matter, these letters were highly regarded for their style. Guez de Balzac was elected to the Academy in 1634 and authored other works such as *Le Prince* (1631), *Socrate chretien* (1652), and *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).
