# Jean Antoine de Baif

Jean Antoine de Baif (1532–1589) was a French poet and a member of the Pleiade. Born in Venice in 1532, he was the natural son of the scholar Lazare de Baif, who served as the French ambassador at Venice during that time.

## Education and Early Influences
Baif grew up as an enthusiast for the fine arts, a passion likely fostered by his childhood surroundings. He is noted for surpassing all leaders of the Renaissance in France in his zeal. His father provided him with a comprehensive education, which included the study of Latin under Charles Estienne and Greek under Ange Vergece, a calligraphist and Cretan scholar who designed Greek types for Francis I.

At the age of eleven, Baif was placed under the tutelage of the famous Jean Daurat. During this period, he shared his studies with Ronsard, who was eight years his senior. Because Baif was well-versed in Greek and Latin, he assisted Ronsard in overcoming the difficult initial stages of learning the Greek language; in exchange, Ronsard introduced Baif to the complexities of French versification.

## Literary Work and Contributions
Baif possessed an extraordinary facility for writing, though it is noted that the sheer mass of his work has negatively impacted his reputation. His literary output includes:
*   Numerous volumes of short poems, which were generally congratulatory or amorous in nature.
*   Paraphrases and translations of works by various authors, including Catullus, Martial, Anacreon, Theocritus, Moschus, and Bion.

## Musical and Poetic Innovations
Baif resided in Paris and maintained the favor of the court. In 1567, he founded an *academie de musique et de poesie* with the goal of creating a closer union between poetry and music. His home became renowned for hosting charming concerts, which were frequently attended by Henry III and Charles IX.

In his efforts to regulate French versification, Baif developed a system based on quantity. While he was not the pioneer of this approach—Jacques de la Taille had written *Maniere de faire des vers en francais comme en grec et en latin* in 1562 (printed in 1573), and other poets had conducted similar experiments—Baif continued these efforts. These 16th-century poets did not recognize that the system of quantity was incompatible with French rhythm. Among Baif's specific innovations was the *vers baifin*, a line consisting of 15 syllables. Additionally, he considered reforms for French spelling, and his theories are exemplified in *Etrenes*.

## Collected Works and Bibliography
The *Oeuvres en rime* of J. A. de Baif, published in five volumes between 1881 and 1890, form part of the *Pleiade francaise* of M. Ch. Marty-Laveaux. Other relevant scholarly works include:
*   *Poesies choisies de J. A. de Baif* (1874) by Becq de Fouquieres, which contains a valuable introduction.
*   *Hist. de la litt. francaise classique* (1904, bk. iii. pp. 398-422) by F. Brunetiere.
*   *Lazare de Baif*, 1496?-1547 (1900) by L. Pinvert.
*   *Les Origines de l'Academie Francaise* (1887) by Edouard Fremy, which provides an account of the academy.
