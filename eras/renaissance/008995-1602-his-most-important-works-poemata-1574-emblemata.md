# Jean Jacques Boissard

Jean Jacques Boissard (1528–1602) was a French Latin poet and antiquary born in Besancon. His career was marked by extensive travel across Europe and a dedicated pursuit of ancient monuments and historical records.

## Early Life and Education
Boissard pursued his studies at Louvain. However, he became disgusted by the severity of his master and secretly departed from the seminary. Following his departure, he traveled through a significant portion of Germany before arriving in Italy.

## Italian Residence and Antiquarianism
Boissard remained in Italy for several years, during which time he frequently faced great financial straits. Despite these difficulties, his time in Italy fostered a deep interest in antiquities. He established a collection consisting of the most curious monuments sourced from Rome and the surrounding areas. 

While in the region, Boissard attempted to travel through Greece, visiting the islands of the Archipelago. This journey was cut short by a severe illness, which forced him to return to Rome. Upon his return, he resumed his antiquarian pursuits with great ardour until his collection was complete.

## Later Life and Religious Beliefs
After completing his collection, Boissard returned to his native country. During his travels or residence abroad, he had embraced the Protestant religion. Because he was not permitted to profess this faith publicly in his home region, he withdrew to Metz. He remained there until his death on October 30, 1602.

## Literary and Scholarly Works
Boissard was a prolific author whose works spanned poetry, topography, and the study of diverse cultures and occult subjects. His most important works include:

*   ***Poemata*** (1574)
*   ***Emblemata*** (1584)
*   ***Habitus Variarum Orbis Gentium*** (1581), which featured seventy illuminated figures.
*   ***Theatrum Vitae Humanae*** (1596)
*   ***Icones Virorum Illustrium*** (1597)
*   ***Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum*** (1597)
*   ***Romanae Urbis Topographia*** (1597–1602), a work noted for being very rare.
*   ***De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis*** (1605)
