# George Villiers, 1st Duke of Buckingham

## Early Life and Education
George Villiers, the 1st Duke of Buckingham, was born in August 1592. He was a younger son of Sir George Villiers of Brooksby and Mary, the daughter of Anthony Beaumont of Glenfield, Leicestershire. After being left a widow early, Mary educated George for the life of a courtier. This training included being sent to France with Sir John Eliot. Described as being "by nature contemplative," Villiers responded well to this education, becoming proficient in fencing and dancing, and acquiring a limited ability to speak French.

## Rise to Royal Favor
In August 1614, Villiers was brought to the attention of King James in the hope that the monarch would take a fancy to him. The timing of this introduction was favorable due to the political climate following the death of Salisbury. Since Salisbury's passing, James had assumed the business of government himself, but he sought a companion who could amuse him, chat with him, and serve as a private secretary. The king specifically required someone to handle the burden of refusing importunate suitors. Furthermore, James viewed the selection of a youth as an opportunity to provide training in the arts of statesmanship, a field in which the king believed himself to be a perfect master.

James's previous attempt to find such a favorite had been unsuccessful. Robert Carr, who had been elevated to the earl of Somerset, had become peevish toward the king following his rise in status. Carr had eventually positioned himself as the leader of a party advocating for a close alliance with Spain.

## Political Influence and the Spanish Conflict
Villiers eventually rose to the rank of duke, a title granted by James during the favorite's absence. However, the significant splendor that surrounded Buckingham later stemmed from sources beyond royal favor. Buckingham placed himself at the head of a popular movement opposed to Spain. 

By February 1624, James summoned parliament, acknowledging—though reluctantly—that the Palatinate could only be recovered through the use of force. During this period, Buckingham took an independent political position with the assistance of the heir apparent. This pressure, combined with persuasion, half-drove James to declare that all negotiations with Spain were at an end. At this time, Buckingham was the most popular man in England.
