# Vitellozzo Vitelli, Oliverotto da Fermo, and G.P. Baglioni

The figures of Vitellozzo Vitelli, Oliverotto da Fermo, and G.P. Baglioni were prominent captains and landowners involved in the political upheavals of Italy during the early 16th century, specifically in their opposition to the expansion of Cesare Borgia.

## Conflict with Cesare Borgia
In October 1500, Cesare Borgia entered the Romagna with an army of 10,000 men, supported by the friendship of Venice. During this campaign, Cesare secured the cities of Rimini and Pesaro after the flight of Giovanni Sforza and Pandolfo Malatesta. He also captured Faenza in April 1501, despite the devotion of the people to their eighteen-year-old lord, Astorre Manfredi. Although Cesare promised to spare Manfredi's life, he broke this word, sending the youth to Rome as a prisoner, where he was put to death after being foully outraged.

Following the acquisition of Castel Bolognese and the conquest of Urbino and Camerino, Cesare was created duke of Romagna by the pope. He became a terror throughout Italy due to his insatiable avidity, his lack of scruples, and his cruelty.

## The Conspiracy of La Magione
Cesare's power was eventually shaken by a plot known as the conspiracy of La Magione, named for the castle near Perugia where the conspirators met. This alliance consisted of several princes whom Cesare had deposed, the Orsinis, and some of his own captains. Among these captains were Vitellozzo Vitelli, Oliverotto da Fermo, and G.P. Baglioni. These men had been granted estates by Cesare but joined the conspiracy because they feared they would lose those holdings.

The resulting risings in Romagna and Urbino led to the defeat of papal troops. For a time, it appeared that all of Italy might turn against the Borgia family. However, the confederates were frightened into seeking terms when the French king promised assistance to Cesare. The archive notes that many of these conspirators lacked military or political skill and were prone to betraying one another.

## The Betrayal at Senigallia
To demonstrate their repentance and return to Cesare's favor, Vitelli, Oliverotto, and Paolo Orsini, along with the duke of Gravina, seized the town of Senigallia in Cesare's name, as the town was still held for the duke of Urbino. 

Upon arriving at Senigallia, Cesare decoyed the unsuspecting *condottieri* into his house. Once there, he had them all arrested.

## Related Historical Context
The Baglioni family also appears in other contexts within the archive. Baccio d'Agnolo (c. 1460-1543), a Florentine architect and sculptor, bore the family name Baglioni, though he was known as Baccio. Additionally, the political instability following the collapse of the Borgias led Niccolò Machiavelli to visit the Baglioni at Perugia in 1505.
