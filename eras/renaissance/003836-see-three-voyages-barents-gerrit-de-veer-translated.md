# Willem Barents

Willem Barents was a Dutch navigator born around the middle of the 16th century. He is primarily known for his expeditions to find a north-east passage to eastern Asia.

## Expeditions and Discoveries

Barents led three distinct journeys in pursuit of a northern route to Asia. In 1594, he departed from Amsterdam commanding two ships. During this voyage, he reached the west coast of Novaya Zemlya and followed the coastline northward, though he was eventually forced to turn back as he approached the northern extremity of the land.

The following year, Barents commanded a second expedition consisting of seven ships. This fleet headed for the strait located between Vaygach Island and the Asiatic coast; however, the expedition arrived too late to find open water.

His third journey also failed to achieve its objective and resulted in his death. During the outward leg of this voyage, Barents utilized two ships and sighted Spitsbergen and Bear Island. At some point, the two ships became separated.

## Wintering and Death

After rounding the north of Novaya Zemlya, Barents' vessel became beset by ice, compelling him and his crew to winter in the north. When the ship was not released by early 1597, the party abandoned the vessel on June 13, departing in two open boats. While most members of the party escaped, Willem Barents died on June 30, 1597.

In 1871, the house where Barents and his men had wintered was discovered. This site yielded many relics, which are currently preserved at the Hague. Additionally, a portion of Barents' journal was found in 1875.

## The Barents Sea

The Barents Sea is the part of the Arctic Ocean named after the navigator. It is demarcated by the islands of Spitsbergen, Franz Josef Land, and Novaya Zemlya, as well as the north coast of Europe and various smaller intervening islands. Excluding the great inlet of the White Sea to the south, the sea extends from approximately 20deg to 60deg E. and from 67deg to 80deg N. The southern region, located off the Kola peninsula's Murman coast, is sometimes referred to as the Murman Sea.

## Historical Records

Details of Barents' voyages are documented in *The Three Voyages of Barents* by Gerrit de Veer. The original text was published in Amsterdam in 1598 and was later translated by the Hakluyt Society in 1876.
