# Henry VIII

## Overview of Reign and Administration
Henry VIII was the monarch of England during a period of significant religious and political transition. During his reign, the city of Kilkenny served as the occasional residence of the lord-lieutenant. The administrative and personal history of his tenure is documented in extensive records, including the *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* (volumes iv through xv) and the *Chronicle of King Henry VIII*.

## Religious Transitions and the Monasteries
The reign of Henry VIII was marked by the Dissolution of the monasteries. Prior to this period, the Benedictines had flourished in England, with nearly 300 Black Benedictine houses—comprising both men's and women's institutions—existing at the time of the Dissolution. These included most of the chief religious houses in the country. 

While grave charges were historically brought against the monks of these institutions, serious historians, including J. Gairdner and F. A. Gasquet, no longer credit these accusations. Following the Dissolution, a momentary revival occurred during the reign of Mary, during which Westminster Abbey was restored and some surviving monks were brought together.

## Marriage and Personal Life
The personal life of Henry VIII, particularly his marriages, is a subject of significant historical record. He married Anne Boleyn, a union for which he was granted a dispensation. This dispensation was notable for including the suspension of all canons regarding impediments created by "affinity rising *ex illicito coitu*" in any degree, even the first. 

Historical accounts regarding the nature of his relationship with Anne Boleyn vary; the *Chronicle of King Henry VIII* identifies her as his mistress. While some contemporary beliefs suggested specific circumstances surrounding their union, other historians, such as Froude, have rejected these stories. The marriage is commonly recorded as having been antedated to November 14, 1532.

## Legal and Political Actions
The legal mechanisms of Henry VIII's reign included the use of Acts of Attainder. A detailed account of such legal proceedings is contained in the published Act of Attainder 25 Henry VIII. c. 12. His interactions with the church and state are further detailed in the works of historians such as Pollard, who wrote on Henry VIII and Cranmer, and James Gairdner, who analyzed the English church in the 16th century.
