# Recent Developments in Specialized Fields

Across various disciplines of human history and professional practice, recent years have seen a shift toward the refinement of methods, the rediscovery of obscure figures, and the application of new technologies to increase efficiency and knowledge.

## Accounting and Mathematics
In the field of book-keeping, considerable attention has been devoted to modifying methods to increase the speed of results, reduce clerical work, and ensure greater certainty in handling data. One such device is tabular book-keeping, which replaces usual forms with books ruled with numerous columns. This system can be applied to ledgers—allowing a single opening to record many personal accounts if entries per individual are few—or to books of first entry. In the latter case, it allows a single book to handle multiple classes of transactions; for example, a business with several departments can use separate columns for each to simplify the preparation of departmental trading accounts.

Parallel interests in precision and calculation were evident in the work of Charles Babbage (1792-1871). An English mathematician and mechanician, Babbage was drawn early to the errors found in astronomical and other calculation tables due to computational inaccuracies. He spent many years developing a calculating engine, receiving government aid and spending his own fortune. While he published *Economy of Machines and Manufactures* (1834) after studying European machinery, his great calculating engine was never completed because he sought to change it from a difference engine to an analytical engine, a risk the government would not accept.

## Literature and Theology
Recent scholarly attention has been directed toward Richard Barnfield (1574-1627), an English poet whose existence was once doubted and whose career was largely unknown. Discoveries by Dr. A. B. Grosart revealed that Barnfield was the son of Richard Barnfield and Maria Skrymsher. He matriculated at Brasenose College, Oxford, in November 1589 and graduated in February 1592. In November 1594, he published *The Affectionate Shepherd*, a florid romance in two books of six-line stanza that served as a paraphrase of Virgil's second eclogue.

In theology, the study of homiletics—the science of the analysis, composition, and delivery of sermons—has seen increased attention due to the formation of lectureships such as the "Lyman Beecher" course at Yale University. Additionally, the study of Greek monachism has been highlighted by modern Western scholars visiting monasteries such as Mount Athos and St Catherine's on Mount Sinai in search of manuscripts. These expeditions, including accounts by Robert Curzon (1834, 1837) and Athelstan Riley (1887), describe a life focused on long church services and devotional contemplative exercises, where manual labor has almost disappeared and intellectual study is rarely cultivated.

## Natural Sciences and Medicine
The practice of keeping birds in large aviaries has received considerable attention in recent years. While the custom began in the 15th century following geographical research and was common among the wealthy in the 16th century—as noted by Bacon in 1597—modern practitioners recognize that these aviaries provide pleasure and significantly increase knowledge of avian modes of living and habits.

In dental medicine, the difficulties associated with gold—including its color, high thermal conductivity, and the annoyance of its insertion—have led to the use of cements of zinc oxide, guttapercha, and amalgam. Recently, much attention has been devoted to porcelain restorations. This process involves pressing a .001 inch thick piece of platinum foil into a cavity to create a matrix, which is then filled with a mixture of powdered porcelain and water or alcohol. The mass is fused and dried, with additional porcelain powder used to counteract shrinkage, resulting in fillings that are scarcely noticeable.

## Agriculture and Regional Development
In certain settled regions, such as those settled by the Spaniards, agricultural focus has shifted over time. While cattle-raising was the primary industry, sheep-raising became profitable by the mid-19th century, leading to enormous exports of dressed meats, live-stock, and wool. Since 1890, butter and cheese-making have gained prominence. Though cereals received little attention until 1875, subsequent efforts increased the production of alfalfa, barley, oats, Indian corn, linseed, and wheat. By 1900, the area devoted to wheat alone reached 1,960,000 acres. Additionally, fruit-growing has progressed on the islands of the Paraná delta, particularly with figs, grapes, strawberries, pears, and peaches.
