# Toledo

Toledo is a city in Spain that has served as a site of significant political, religious, and personal events involving various historical figures.

## Francis Borgia and the Spanish Court
Francis Borgia (1510–1572), a Roman Catholic saint and general of the order of Jesuits, had a complex relationship with the honors bestowed upon him by the state. Born in Gandia and educated in Saragossa, Borgia initially showed a strong inclination toward monastic life. However, in 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. During his time there, he was created marquis of Lombay and appointed master of the horse to the empress after marrying Eleanor de Castro.

Borgia's commitment to a religious life was reinforced in 1539 when he was tasked with convoying the body of the empress to her burial-place in Granada. Upon his return to Toledo, Borgia found that new honors were thrust upon him against his will; he was appointed commander of the order of St James and viceroy of Catalonia. He governed from Barcelona with energy and efficiency until 1543. After succeeding his father as duke of Gandia, he resigned his viceroyalty to pursue a more congenial life. Following the death of Eleanor in 1546, he became a member of the Society of Jesus, though a papal dispensation allowed him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years for the sake of his young children.

## Baldassare Castiglione
The Italian diplomat and man of letters Baldassare Castiglione (1478–1529) also had ties to the region. Castiglione, who had served the duke of Milan and the duke of Urbino, was sent to Spain in 1524 to help arrange a dispute between Charles V and Clement VII. During this period, he was received with the highest honors, naturalized, and appointed bishop of Avila. 

Castiglione's later years were marked by tragedy and accusation. After the Imperialists sacked Rome in 1527, Castiglione was accused of treachery, despite having placed fidelity as the highest virtue of his ideal "courtier." He died at Toledo in 1529; it was said that he died of grief and shame due to these imputations.

## Diego Duque de Estrada
Toledo was the birthplace and a site of legal conflict for Diego Duque de Estrada (1589–?), a Spanish soldier, adventurer, and memoir writer. Born in Toledo on August 15, 1589, Estrada was later forced to flee after killing his betrothed and another gentleman in a fit of jealousy. While the code of honor of the time was seen by some as justification, the law viewed the act as assassination. 

Estrada was eventually arrested in Ecija and brought back to Toledo. There, he was subjected to extreme ferocity during torture in an attempt to extort a general confession regarding his life. He managed to escape from prison with the help of friends and a nun from a facing religious house, eventually making his way to Naples to enter the service of the duke of Osuna.

## Religious Administration
The city has also been the seat of high ecclesiastical authority. In the political dealings of Castelar, who renewed relations with the Vatican and ended persecutions of religious orders, a prelate was nominated for the archbishopric of Valencia. This individual subsequently became the archbishop of Toledo and remained a close friend of Castelar.
