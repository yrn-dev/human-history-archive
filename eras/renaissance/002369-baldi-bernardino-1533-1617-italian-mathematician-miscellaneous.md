# BALDI, BERNARDINO (1533-1617)

Bernardino Baldi was an Italian mathematician and miscellaneous writer born on June 6, 1533, in the city of Urbino, where he was descended from a noble family. He is described as perhaps the most universal genius of his age.

## Education and Early Career
Baldi pursued his studies at Padua with success and extraordinary zeal. During his life, he is said to have acquired sixteen languages, although Tiraboschi notes that the inscription on his tomb limits this number to twelve. His time at Padua was interrupted by the appearance of the plague, which forced him to return to his native city.

Shortly after his return to Urbino, Baldi was appointed as the tutor to Ferrante (Ferdinand) Gonzaga. Through this connection, he received the rich abbey of Guastalla. Baldi served as the abbot of Guastalla for twenty-five years before retiring to his native town.

## Diplomatic Service and Later Life
In 1612, the duke employed Baldi as his envoy to Venice. While there, he distinguished himself by delivering a congratulatory oration before the Venetian senate on the occasion of the election of Andrea Memmo as the new doge. Baldi remained in Urbino until his death on October 12, 1617.

## Literary and Scientific Contributions
Baldi was a prolific author, said to have written more than a hundred different works, though the majority of these remained unpublished. His writings demonstrate his capabilities across a wide array of disciplines, including:
* Mathematics
* Theology
* Geography
* History
* Poetry
* Antiquarianism

One of his notable contributions is the *Cronica dei Matematici*, published in Urbino in 1707. This work is an abridgment of a larger project to which Baldi devoted twelve years of labor; the original larger work was intended to contain the lives of more than two hundred mathematicians.
