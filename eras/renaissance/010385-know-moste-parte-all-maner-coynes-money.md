# The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge

## Overview and Purpose
*The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge* is a work authored by Andrew Boorde, a learned physician and Doctor of Physycke. Written around 1547, the text was dedicated to Lady Mary, the daughter of King Henry VIII. The primary objective of the book is to instruct a reader in several diverse areas of practical and cultural knowledge: specifically, how to speak a portion of all various languages and how to understand the fashion and usage of different countries. Additionally, the work is designed to teach the reader about the majority of all types of money coins that are current in every region.

## Content and Structure
The book employs a descriptive and often lively style. Boorde begins by describing the Englishman, utilizing verse to detail various foibles, including obstinacy, a fondness for new fashions, and fickleness. This is followed by a geographical description of the country.

The author organizes the rest of the work by dealing with various countries in a consistent manner. For example, the section on Cornwall includes a model dialogue in the Cornish language.

## The Author's Other Works
Andrew Boorde is credited with several other authentic publications focusing on medicine, health, and astronomy. These include:
* *The Brevyary of Health* (dated approximately 1547).
* *The Princyples of Astronamy* (dated approximately 1547).
* *Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor*, which saw a publication by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though earlier undated editions exist.
* "The Peregrination of Doctor Board," which was printed in 1735 within *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, vol. ii.
* *A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV*.

The archive notes that Boorde's *Treatyse upon Berdes* and his *Itinerary of Europe* have been lost. Furthermore, several jest-books are attributed to him without authoritative confirmation, including *Scogin's Jests* (1626), *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam* (earliest extant edition 1630), a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*, and *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton, with his wyfe, and his daughter, and of two poore scholers of Cambridge*.
