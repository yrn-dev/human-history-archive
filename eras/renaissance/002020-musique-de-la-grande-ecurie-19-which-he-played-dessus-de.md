# Musique de la Grande Ecurie

The *Musique de la Grande Ecurie* (also referred to as the *Grande Ecurie du Roi*) was a musical ensemble associated with the French royal court. The group employed various wind instrumentalists, including players of the hautbois and the *haute-contre de hautbois*.

## Personnel and Instrumentation

The ensemble featured musicians specializing in the hautbois family of instruments. By the end of the 17th century, the *Grande Ecurie du Roi* included two players of the *haute-contre de hautbois* (the alto Pommer or cor anglais). 

Notable members of the ensemble included Jean Hotteterre, a court musician who died in 1678. Hotteterre played the *dessus de hautbois* within the band. He is credited with introducing specific improvements to the drones of the musette.

## Related Instruments and Development

The musicians of the *Musique de la Grande Ecurie* operated during a period of significant evolution for wind instruments. The hautbois played a prominent role in French court music, and its relative, the *haute-contre de hautbois*, was likely provided with keys in France.

The musette, an instrument inflated by a small arm-worked bellows, was also prevalent at the French court from the end of the 15th century. The instrument reached a fully developed state by the beginning of the 17th century. Following the tenure of Jean Hotteterre, his son Martin Hotteterre (d. 1712) further developed the instrument by adding a second, shorter chaunter known as the *petit chalumeau*. This new chaunter featured six keys, while the *grand chalumeau* possessed seven keys, eight finger-holes, and a vent-hole in the bell.
