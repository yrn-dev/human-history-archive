# 1574-1800

The period between 1574 and 1800 encompasses significant developments in European history, including the literary activities of scholars, the evolution of state and church governance, and the production of specialized artistic and historical records.

## Literary and Scholarly Figures
During the late 16th and early 17th centuries, notable intellectuals contributed to the theological and historical landscape. Juan de Mariana (1536-1624), a Spanish historian and member of the Society of Jesus, taught theology in Rome and later provided expositions on the writings of Thomas Aquinas in Paris. In 1574, Mariana returned to Spain due to ill health, spending the remainder of his life in Toledo in literary activity until his death in Madrid in 1624.

In the 18th century, German literature saw contributions from figures such as Ewaldi Kleist. Kleist is recognized for his poetical genius and fine taste, particularly in his 1749 poem in hexameters, *Der Frühling*, which drew ideas from Thomson's *Seasons*. His other works include odes, idylls, elegies, and the 1759 epic poem *Cissides und Paches*, which depicts two Thessalian friends who die heroically in a battle against the Athenians. Kleist published collections of his *Gedichte* in 1756 and 1758.

## Book-plates and Possession Marks
The study of book-plates (ex-libris) and marks of possession became a subject of detailed bibliographic record. Various authorities have documented the composition and history of these marks. Notable works include Sir A.W. Franks' *Notes on Book-plates*, which covers the period from 1574 to 1800 (published privately in 1887), and Friedrich Warnecke's *Die deutschen Bucherzeichen* (1890). Other significant studies include Henri Bouchot's *Les Ex-libris et les marques de possession du livre* (1891), Egerton Castle's *English Book-plates* (1892), and Walter Hamilton's works on French book-plates (1892) and dated book-plates (1895).

## Church and State Governance
The 16th through 18th centuries were marked by the development of church constitutions and the administration of free cities. In Germany, the evolution of the Protestant church is documented in works such as Richter's *Die evangelischen Kirchenordnungen des sechzehnten Jahrhunderts* (1846) and Sehling's *Geschichte der protestantischen Kirchenverfassung* (1907). These studies examine concepts such as the *Kirchenordnung* (church order), *Konsistorien* (consistories), and the *Territorialsystem*.

Simultaneously, the history of the imperial free cities (*Reichsstädte*) was recorded by authors such as J. J. Moser in his *Reichsstädtisches Handbuch* (1732) and D. Hänlein in *Anmerkungen über die Geschichte der Reichsstädte* (1775). Later analyses, such as those by A. Wendt (1804) and G. W. Hugo (1838), explored the description and mediatization of these cities.

## Military and Political History
The 18th century was characterized by significant military conflicts, most notably the Silesian Wars and the War of the Austrian Succession. Historical records of these events include the Prussian official *Die I. und II. schlesischen Kriege* (1890-1895) and the Austrian official *Kriege der Kaiserin Maria Theresia*. These conflicts involved key figures such as Frederick the Great and Empress Maria Theresa.
