# St Francis Borgia

St Francis Borgia (1510–1572) was a Roman Catholic saint, the duke of Gandia, and the general of the order of Jesuits. Born in Gandia, Valencia, on October 10, 1510, he was noted for his piety from a young age.

## Early Life and Court Career
Borgia received his education starting at the age of twelve in Saragossa, where he was under the charge of his uncle, the archbishop. During this time, he developed a strong inclination toward monastic life. However, in 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. Borgia distinguished himself in this environment and married Eleanor de Castro, a high-ranking Portuguese lady. Following his marriage, he was appointed master of the horse to the empress and was created marquis of Lombay.

His service to Charles V included accompanying him on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and into Provence in 1536. In 1539, following the death of the empress, Borgia was tasked with convoying her body to its burial place in Granada. This experience reinforced his desire to leave the court and his resolve to embrace a monastic life should he survive his wife.

## Political Service and Transition
Despite his personal inclinations, Borgia was granted further honors in Toledo against his will, being named the commander of the order of St James and the viceroy of Catalonia. While serving as governor in Barcelona, he maintained a life of great austerity while performing his official duties with efficiency and energy.

In 1543, after succeeding his father as the duke of Gandia, Borgia obtained permission to resign his viceroyalty and retire to Gandia. During this period, he maintained correspondence with Ignatius Loyola and provided powerful encouragement to the recently founded order of Jesus. One of his primary actions upon returning to Gandia was the construction of a Jesuit college.

## Religious Life and Later Years
Following the death of Eleanor in 1546, Borgia resolved to join the society of Jesuits. Due to family and political circumstances, a papal dispensation was granted, allowing him to keep his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children. 

In 1550, Borgia visited Rome, where he was received with distinction and provided the funds necessary for the construction of the Collegium Romanum. He returned to Spain the following year to formally resign his estate and rank. He eventually rose to become the general of the order of Jesuits.

## Published Works and Biography
Several works written by St Francis Borgia have been published. The principal among these are a treatise titled *Rhetorica Concionandi* and a series of *Exercises*, which are similar to the *Exercitia Spiritualia* of Loyola. His collected works, the *Opera Omnia*, were published in Brussels in 1675.

The life of St Francis Borgia was recorded by his confessor, Pedro de Ribadeneira. Other references to his life can be found in the *Breviarium Romanum* (specifically the second nocturn for October 10) and A. Butler's *Lives of the Saints*.
