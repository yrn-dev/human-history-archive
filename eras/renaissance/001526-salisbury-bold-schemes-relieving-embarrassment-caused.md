# Salisbury's Bold Schemes

## The Fourth Parliament of James
In 1610, the fourth parliament of James convened. During this session, conflicts repeatedly arose between the concept of liberty and the royal prerogative. Despite the efforts and advice provided by Bacon, these clashes persisted.

## Financial Relief Efforts
During this period, Salisbury developed bold schemes intended to relieve the embarrassment caused by the king's reckless extravagance. However, these schemes proved to be abortive. Following these failed efforts, the House was dissolved in February 1611.

## Aftermath and Political Transition
Shortly after the dissolution of the House, Salisbury died. His death created a political vacancy that Bacon believed he could fill using his sagacity and political skill to serve the king. While it is uncertain if Bacon directly applied for the post of secretary, his hopes were disappointed as the king chose to undertake the duties of that office himself. 

Bacon also made two unsuccessful attempts to secure the mastership of the wards; the first occurred upon Salisbury's death (the position was given to Sir George Carey) and the second occurred upon Carey's subsequent death.

## Later Developments
By 1613, a new opportunity for promotion emerged when the death of Sir Thomas Fleming created a vacancy in the chief justiceship of the king's bench. Bacon proposed to the king that Coke be transferred from the court of common pleas to the king's bench. Bacon's primary motive for this suggestion was likely to reduce the risk of Coke colliding with the king on matters of prerogative, as Bacon was more circumspect and tender in handling such issues. Following Coke's promotion, Hobart filled the resulting vacancy, and Bacon eventually became the attorney-general.
