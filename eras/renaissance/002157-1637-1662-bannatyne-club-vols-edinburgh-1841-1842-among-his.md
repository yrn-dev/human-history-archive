# Bannatyne Club

The Bannatyne Club is an organization founded in Edinburgh in 1823. The club was established to honor the name of George Bannatyne and is dedicated to the publication of literary and historical materials derived from Scottish sources.

## George Bannatyne and the Bannatyne Manuscript
The club's namesake, George Bannatyne (1545–?1608), was a native of Forfarshire, specifically Newtyle. He served as an Edinburgh merchant and was admitted as a burgess in 1587. In 1568, during a period when the "pest" was prevalent in the capital, Bannatyne retired to his home county. During this time, he compiled copies of poems written by Scots poets from the 15th and early 16th centuries. 

This effort resulted in a work spanning eight hundred folio pages, organized into five parts. Known as the "Bannatyne Manuscript," the document passed from his only daughter, Janet, to her husband's family—the Foulises of Woodhall and Ravelston—before eventually entering the Advocates' library. Along with the "Maitland Folio" and "Asloan" manuscripts, the Bannatyne Manuscript serves as a primary repository for Middle Scots poetry, particularly for the works of Alexander Scott, Lyndsay, Dunbar, and Henryson. 

The manuscript's contents were partially reprinted by Allan Ramsay in *Ever Green* (1724) and later by Lord Hailes in *Ancient Scottish Poems* (1770). The Hunterian Club issued the full text between 1873 and 1902. In 1829, the Bannatyne Club published *Memorials of George Bannatyne* as its thirty-third issue, which included an account of the manuscript by David Laing and a memoir by Sir Walter Scott.

## Publications of the Club
The Bannatyne Club has published a wide array of historical, literary, and ecclesiastical documents. Notable publications include:

*   **Ecclesiastical and Legal Texts:** The club published *Statuta Ecclesiae Scoticanae* in 1866. In 1854, through the munificence of the duke of Buccleuch, the club issued a facsimile reprint of the Aberdeen Breviary (originally printed in 1509-1510), a Scottish form of the Sarum Office revised by William Elphinstone.
*   **Personal Papers and Memoirs:** The club published the *Letters and Journals of Robert Baillie* (1637-1662) in three volumes between 1841 and 1842, edited by D. Laing. Additionally, Lord Minto edited the *Correspondence* (1702-1708) of George Baillie in 1842 for the club.
*   **Military and Political Records:** In 1849, the club published *The Darien Papers* by J. H. Burton. In 1883, the club published various papers and despatches of Mackay, the inventor of the ring bayonet.
