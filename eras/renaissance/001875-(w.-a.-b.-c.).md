### Greatest Shear
To determine the greatest shear at a specific section (C) when concentrated loads travel from a left abutment, the position of the loads is analyzed. If $W_1$ is the first load, the greatest shear at C may occur when $W_1$ is at C. If $W_1$ passes beyond C, the shear may be greatest when $W_2$ is at C. 

If $R$ represents the resultant of the loads on the bridge when $W_1$ is at C, the reaction at B and the shear at C is $Rn/l$. When the loads advance a distance $a$ so that $W_2$ reaches C, the shear at C becomes $R(n+a)/l - W_1$, plus any reaction $d$ at B caused by additional loads entering the girder. The shear increases by bringing $W_2$ to C if $Ra/l + d > W_1$.

If the load is distributed to bracing intersections via cross girders and rail, the calculation is modified. For example, if the action of $W$ is distributed to A and B by flooring, the loads at A and B are $Wx/p$ and $W(p-x)/p$. In this scenario, if $W_2$ is at C, the shear becomes $R(n+a)/l + d - W_1$ if $a > p$, or $R(n+a)/l + d - W_1a/p$ if $a < p$.

### Greatest Bending Moment
For a travelling live load of $w$ per foot advancing from the left abutment with its centre at $x$, the reaction at B is $2wx^2/l$. The bending moment at section C (at distance $m$ from the left abutment) is $2wx^2/(l-m)/l$. For uniform travelling loads, the bending moments reach their maximum when the loading of the span is complete.

In cases involving a series of travelling loads at fixed distances, let $W_1$ and $W_2$ be resultants on either side of C at distances $x$ and $x+a$ from the left abutment. The reaction at B is $W_1x/l + W_2(x+a)/l$, and the bending moment at C is expressed as $M = W_1x(l-m)/l + W_2m\{1-(x+a)/l\}$.
