# Margaret

The name Margaret is associated with various historical and legendary figures across European history, including royalty, saints, and the families of scholars.

## Roger Ascham's Mother
Margaret was the mother of the English writer and scholar Roger Ascham (c. 1515–1568), who was born in Kirby Wiske, Yorkshire. While her family name is unknown, she is said to have been well connected. This information, along with other details of Ascham's early life, was provided by Edward Grant, the headmaster of Westminster, who edited Ascham's letters and delivered a panegyrical oration on his life in 1576.

## Royal Figures in Scotland and Norway
Several women named Margaret held significant roles in the history of Scotland and Norway:

*   **Margaret (1283–1290):** Known as the "maid of Norway," she was the daughter of King Eric II of Norway and Margaret, daughter of King Alexander III of Scotland. Following the death of Alexander in 1886, she became the queen of Scotland. A marriage was arranged between her and the son of King Edward I of England (the future Edward II), but she died in the Orkneys around the end of September 1290. Following her death, a woman from Leipzig claimed to be the queen around 1300, but she was burned as a witch at Bergen.
*   **Margaret of Scotland (Queen Mother):** Crowned in Edinburgh in March 1504, she was the sister of Henry VIII. She bore several children, including James V (born 1512) and a posthumous son, Alexander, duke of Ross (born 1514). After the death of James IV at Flodden in 1513, she was appointed sole guardian of James V. Her tenure was marked by political conflict between English and French factions in Scotland. She privately married Archibald Douglas, earl of Angus, in 1514, which led to her removal from the regency in 1515 by John Stewart, duke of Albany. After a divorce from Angus in 1527, she married Henry Stewart, Lord Methven.
*   **Margaret of Norway:** The daughter of King Inge, she married King Magnus of Norway, who was known by several names, including Magnus Barefoot, Magnus the Tall, and Magnus the Strife-lover.

## Continental European Nobility
The name is also prominent among the nobility of France, Austria, and Italy:

*   **Margaret of Provence (1221–1295):** Daughter of Raymond Berenger V, count of Provence, she married Saint Louis of France on May 27, 1234. She accompanied the king on his crusade and was noted for her heroism at Damietta. She maintained a strong devotion to her sister Eleanor's husband, Henry III of England, and had a hostile relationship with her brother-in-law, Charles of Anjou. She spent years claiming rights to the county of Provence before accepting an income from the county of Anjou.
*   **Margaret of Austria (1522–1586):** The natural daughter of Charles V and Margaret van Ghent, she served as the regent of the Netherlands from 1559 to 1567. She was married first to Alexander de' Medici, duke of Florence, and later to Ottavio Farnese, duke of Parma. As regent, she faced discontent regarding Spanish despotism and the Inquisition. She resigned her post in 1567 and retired to Italy, where she died in 1886.
*   **Margaret Maultasch (1318–1369):** The countess of Tirol and daughter of Henry, duke of Carinthia. She was married to John Henry of Bohemia in 1330, but the marriage was later declared dissolved by Emperor Louis IV on the grounds that it had not been consummated. Louis then married her to his son, Louis, margrave of Brandenburg, in 1342. This action led Pope Clement VI to place both Louis and his son under a ban until 1559. After the deaths of her husband and her son Meinhard, she handed Tirol to Rudolph IV, duke of Austria.

## Religious and Other Figures
*   **St. Margaret (Sancta Margarita):** A virgin and martyr celebrated by the Church of Rome on July 20. Legend describes her as a native of Antioch and daughter of a pagan priest, Aedesius. Because of her Christian faith, she lived with a foster mother keeping sheep. She was tortured and killed after refusing a marriage proposal from Olybrius, the "praeses orientis," which had been offered in exchange for her renouncing Christianity. In Greek tradition, she is known as Marina (celebrated July 17) and has been identified with St. Pelagia. Over 250 churches in England are dedicated to her.
*   **Margaret of Flanders:** Mentioned as the countess and wife of William of Dampierre.
