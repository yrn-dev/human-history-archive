# Louis XIII

Louis XIII (1601–1643) was the King of France and the son of Henry IV and Marie de' Medici. He ascended to the throne in 1610 following the assassination of his father.

## Early Reign and Regency
Upon Louis XIII's accession, his mother, Marie de' Medici, immediately assumed full powers as regent. She sought to reverse the policies established by her husband, aiming instead to align France with the Austrian house and Spain. To solidify this alliance, two marriages were arranged in 1615: Louis XIII married Anne of Austria, the daughter of King Philip III of Spain, while the king's sister, Princess Elizabeth, married the Spanish prince who later became Philip IV. These arrangements were carried out despite opposition from the French nobles and Protestants.

During the subsequent years, France experienced political intrigue and civil war. Throughout this period, the queen regent relied on the Marshal d'Ancre. Louis XIII is described as having been a backward boy whose education was largely neglected. In his youth, he appeared to acquiesce to the rule of his mother's favorites and her hold on power.

## Rise to Power and Personal Character
In 1617, Louis XIII developed a strong attachment to Charles d'Albert, sieur de Luynes. With Luynes' assistance, the king arrested Marshal d'Ancre, who was assassinated after resisting. This event marked the beginning of a relationship between Louis XIII and his mother characterized by either open or concealed hostility, which lasted until her death.

The personal nature of Louis XIII is described as melancholy, lethargic, and timid. He was fond of field sports and was known for concealing his purposes from even his closest friends, to the extent that he sometimes seemed unconscious of them himself. While his court lacked the scandals associated with the reign of Henry IV, it is noted that Madame d'Hautefort and Mademoiselle de la Fayette are said to have been his mistresses.

## Legacy and Historical Records
Louis XIII was survived by his brother, Gaston, who caused unexpectedly little trouble during the wars of the Fronde that followed the king's death. 

Information regarding the life of Louis XIII is preserved in several contemporary memoirs, most notably those of Montrésor, Omer Talon, Fontenay-Mareuil, Bassompierre, and Gaston d'Orléans. While the Memoirs of Richelieu exist, they focus primarily on diplomacy and politics. Modern scholarly works on the king's personal life include those by M. Topin, Rossignol, and R. de Beauchamp.

The king's influence extended into the arts and literature of his era. In 1631, the author Jean Louis Guez de Balzac published an eulogy for the king titled *Le Prince*. Additionally, the dramatist Corneille was commissioned in 1646 to write verses for the *Triomphes poetiques de Louis XIII*.
