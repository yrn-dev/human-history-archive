# December 1595 and the Return of Thomas Arundell

## Military Service and Honors
Thomas Arundell, 1st Baron Arundell of Wardour (c. 1562–1639), was the son of Margaret, daughter of Sir Henry Willoughby, and Sir Mathew Arundell of Wardour Castle in Wiltshire. His father belonged to the ancient Arundells of Lanherne family in Cornwall. In 1579, Queen Elizabeth personally recommended Arundell to Emperor Rudolph II. While serving with imperial troops in Hungary against the Turks, Arundell distinguished himself. During the siege of Esztergom (also known as Gran) on August 13, 1595, he personally captured the banner of the enemy.

## Title and Return to England
As a result of his service, Emperor Rudolph II created Arundell a count of the Holy Roman Empire in December 1595. Following this honor, Arundell attempted to return to England, but he suffered a shipwreck in January 1596, barely preserving his life.

## Conflict with the Crown and Peers
Arundell's assumption of a foreign title caused significant resentment and jealousy among English peers, who typically granted precedence to foreign nobles only as a matter of courtesy. His father also objected to Arundell's superior rank and promptly disinherited him.

Queen Elizabeth was seriously displeased by the acquisition of the title. She remarked that faithful subjects should keep their eyes at home and not gaze upon foreign crowns, comparing such behavior to chaste wives who should have no glances but for their own spouses. Upon his arrival in England, the Queen committed Arundell to the Fleet prison and sent a long letter of remonstrance to the emperor.

## Legal Troubles and Later Life
Arundell remained under arrest until April, when he was liberated following an examination. He was confined again in April 1597, though he was declared innocent of all charges except for "practising to contrive the justification of his vain title with Ministers beyond the seas." He was liberated again in December of that year and placed under his father's care.

The following year, Arundell was arrested once more on accusations of conspiracy against the government. He petitioned for a license to lead a sea expedition, stating that his goal was "honour which some base minds call ambition," but these requests were refused. By 1599, he appeared to be restored to favor. On May 4, 1605, James I created him Baron Arundell of Wardour. However, he later fell under temporary suspicion during the time of the Gunpowder Plot and encountered further trouble in 1623.
