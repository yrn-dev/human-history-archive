# Florence

Florence is an inland Italian city that served as a trading and financial center. During the fifteenth century, it experienced a second "Periclean age" under the almost monarchical rule of the Medici family. Towards the end of the fourteenth century, the city became a hub for the imitation, restoration, and rediscovery of antique art, a movement known in its narrower sense as the "Renaissance."

## Architecture and Early Art
Prior to the Medici era, Florence had already produced significant works of art. These include the Duomo, created by Brunelleschi (born 1377, died 1446), and the tower of Giotto (born 1266, died 1337). Brunelleschi's construction of the cupola of S. Maria del Fiore was made possible by his studies and measurements of other Roman buildings, including the Pantheon dome, during a period in Rome where he was accompanied by the sculptor Donatello.

Donatello (c. 1386–1466), the son of Niccolo di Betto Bardi of the Florentine Woolcombers' Gild, was a leading spirit of the early Renaissance. After initial training in a goldsmith's workshop and a brief period in Ghiberti's studio, Donatello traveled to Rome with Brunelleschi. There, they were regarded as "treasure seekers" while pursuing excavations and studies of classic art. This period allowed Donatello to acquire knowledge of ornamentation and classic forms.

## The Life and Work of Fra Bartolommeo
Fra Bartolommeo (also known as Baccio Bella Porta), a portrait and historical painter, was born near Florence at Soffignano in 1475 and died in Florence in 1517. He began his artistic education with Cosimo Roselli before studying the works of Leonardo da Vinci. One of his most significant early works was a fresco of the Last Judgment, created with the assistance of Mariotto Albertinelli.

While working on pieces for a Dominican friars' convent, Bartolommeo became influenced by Savonarola. Following Savonarola's cruel death, Bartolommeo entered the convent and abandoned his art for several years. He eventually resumed painting under the obedience of his superior. During this time, he formed a close friendship with Raphael, who had come to Florence. The two artists exchanged technical knowledge: Bartolommeo learned the rules of perspective from Raphael, while Raphael improved his handling of drapery and colouring through the *frate*.

Bartolommeo later visited Rome, where he felt a sense of inferiority when viewing the masterpieces of Michelangelo and Raphael. Despite this, he remained friendly with Raphael, leaving two unfinished pictures for the younger artist to complete upon his departure from Rome. To counter criticisms that his figures were too small and draped, Bartolommeo painted the undraped figure of St Sebastian and the figure of St Mark (located at Florence), the latter being considered his masterpiece. The painting of St Sebastian was so expressive of agony and suffering that it had to be removed from the convent chapel where it was exhibited.

## Other Notable Florentines
Florence was the birthplace or residence of various influential figures across different centuries:

*   **Niccolò Machiavelli:** In the spring of 1526, Machiavelli was employed by Clement VII to inspect the fortifications of Florence. After a period of diplomatic and administrative work in Lombardy, Bologna, and Civita Vecchia, he returned to Florence. He fell ill and died on June 22, following the administration of medicine on June 20.
*   **Carlo Dolci (1616–1686):** A painter born in Florence who focused primarily on sacred subjects. A disciple of Jacopo Vignali, Dolci was known for a delicate style and a remarkably slow pace of work. He died in Florence in January 1686.
*   **Lorenzo Bellini (1643–1704):** An anatomist and physician born in Florence. After thirty years as a professor at Pisa, he returned to Florence to serve as the physician to grand duke Cosimo III. He died in Florence on January 8, 1704.
*   **Angelo Maria Bandini (1726–1800):** Born in Florence, Bandini was a scholar of antiquities who served as the principal librarian to the Laurentian library for forty-four years. He died in 1800, leaving his remaining fortune to charitable purposes and founding a public school.

## Printing and Scholarship
Florence was a site of significant publishing activity. In 1478, Francesco Berlinghieri published a version of Ptolemy's work in Florence, which included four or five modern maps. In later centuries, the city remained a center for the publication of scholarly works, including various editions of Machiavelli's writings published by A. Parenti (1843), A. Usigli (1857), and others between 1873 and 1877. Additionally, the biography of Bartolommei was published in Florence in 1905 by his daughter, Matilde Gioli.
