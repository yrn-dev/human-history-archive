# Athalie

*Athalie* is a dramatic work by the French playwright Racine. It is characterized as one of Racine's few attempts at the tragic style of dramatic writing.

## Performance and Musical Adaptation
The play has been featured in the repertoire of noted French performers. Marie Francoise Dumesnil (1713–1803), a prominent actress of the Comedie Francaise, performed the role of Athalie with great effect. 

In addition to its theatrical presence, the work has been adapted musically. During a prolonged stay in St Petersburg, the French composer of comic opera Francois Adrien Boieldieu (1775–1834) set the choruses of *Athalie* to music.

## Stylistic Context
The dramatic style of Racine is often compared to that of other masters. In a comparison of poets, Racine is described as imitating the swan when copying the ancients in his own unique manner, characterized by a grace peculiar to the bird and an elegance of motion that involves floating upon the air, rising, and falling again.

Racine's standing in the tragic style was viewed differently across different eras. During his own time, he was a "rising star" whose reputation grew even as he contended with the towering reputation of the older poet Corneille. Boileau, a contemporary and friend of Racine, described him as an "extremely clever fellow" and claimed to have taught him the art of elaborate rhyming (*rimer difficilement*). However, it was the 18th century that eventually exalted Racine above Corneille.
