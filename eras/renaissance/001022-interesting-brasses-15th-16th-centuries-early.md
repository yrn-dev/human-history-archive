# Aylesford

Aylesford is a town located in the Medway parliamentary division of Kent, England. It is situated on the right bank of the Medway at the base of a hill, approximately 3-1/2 miles northwest of Maidstone. As of 1901, the town had a population of 2,678. It is served by the South-Eastern & Chatham railway.

## St. Peter's Church
The town is home to the ancient church of St. Peter, which underwent restoration in 1878. The architecture of the building is principally Perpendicular, though it also contains portions that are Decorated and Norman. The church features an early embattled tower and contains interesting brasses dating from the 15th and 16th centuries.

## Religious Foundations and Antiquities
To the west of the town center are the remains of a Carmelite friary, which was founded in 1240; part of the site is currently occupied by a residence. It is claimed, though not with certainty, that this foundation was the first house of Carmelites established in England. Furthermore, the first general chapter of the order was held at this location in 1245.

The surrounding neighborhood contains several remains of antiquity. One such site is a cromlech known as Kit's Coty House, located about a mile northeast of the village. According to tradition, this site marks the burial-place of Catigern, who was killed in A.D. 455 during a battle between the Saxons and the Britons. The name of the site has also been attributed to the Celtic term *Ked-coit*, meaning "the tomb in the wood." 

Near Kit's Coty House is a larger group of monuments called the Countless Stones, a name derived from a popular belief that they cannot be counted. Additionally, large numbers of British coins have been discovered in the area. Approximately 2 miles to the north, at Horsted, is the supposed tomb of Horsa, who also fell in the battle of A.D. 455.
