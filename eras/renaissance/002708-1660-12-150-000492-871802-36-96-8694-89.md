# 1660

The year 1660 was marked by significant political transitions in Europe, particularly in England and Scandinavia, as well as contributions to science and the arts.

## The English Restoration
In 1660, Charles II, the son of Charles the "Martyr," returned to England and was welcomed back with manifestations of personal loyalty. This event, known as the Restoration, signaled the end of the Puritan era and the return of "Merrie England." Upon his return, Charles II took control of the state's foreign affairs.

During this period, Downing, a diplomat who had remained in his post during the interregnum following the fall of Richard Cromwell, made his peace with Charles II in April 1660. Downing communicated Thurloe's despatches to the King and renounced principles he had acquired in New England, which he then viewed as errors. Consequently, Downing was knighted in May 1660 and continued his embassy in Holland. He was also confirmed as a teller of the exchequer and granted land near St James's Park for building, which became known as Downing Street. Additionally, Downing was returned for Morpeth in the convention parliament of April 1660. He strongly supported the Navigation Act of 1660.

## Scandinavian Political Changes
In Denmark and Norway, 1660 saw the Treaty of Copenhagen. The revolution of 1660 had beneficial effects for Norway. With the disappearance of the *Rigsraad*—which had represented the Danish crown and exercised sovereignty over both kingdoms—Norway ceased to be a subject principality. Constitutionally, Norway was placed on an equality with Denmark, united with it but no longer subordinate. While the Norwegian people had hoped for an administration independent of the Danish government, this was not realized; Copenhagen remained the headquarters of the Norwegian administration until 1814.

## Science, Literature, and the Arts
The year 1660 saw the publication of two works in dialogue form by the French physicist Jean Baptiste Duhamel: *Astronomia physica* and *De meteoris et fossilibus*. Duhamel, born in 1624, had served as an Oratorian priest and was appointed almoner to the king in 1656.

In Sweden, Erik Dahlberg began the fine collection of drawings titled *Suecia antiqua et hodierna*, a project that continued until 1716. In the realm of biography and history, White Kennett, an English bishop and antiquary, was born in Dover in August 1660.
