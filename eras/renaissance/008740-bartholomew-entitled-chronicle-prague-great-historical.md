# Bartos (Bartholomew)

Bartos, also referred to as Bartholomew, was a historian and official in 16th-century Bohemia. He is primarily recognized for his contributions to the native literature of the period, a time when Bohemia experienced a state of comparative tranquillity.

## Professional Life and Works
Bartos was known by the surname "pisar," which translates to "the writer." This designation reflects his professional career, as he served for many years as a secretary for the city of Prague. 

His primary contribution to historical literature is a work entitled the *Chronicle of Prague*. This text is regarded as having great historical value. In this chronicle, Bartos provides a description of the various troubles that affected the city of Prague and Bohemia in general. These events occurred during the reign of King Louis, who is characterized as an absentee and weak sovereign.

## Historical Context
Bartos is categorized as one of three historians of merit from the 16th century in Bohemia. He is mentioned alongside Wenceslas Hajek of Libocan and Sixt of Ottersdorf. While the work of Wenceslas Hajek—the *Kronika ceska*—was widely circulated and translated into several languages, it is noted for being grossly partial and inaccurate. In contrast, the works of Bartos and Sixt of Ottersdorf are described as being very little known.

The historical landscape of the era also included the writings of the community of the Bohemian Brethren. Later historical accounts of the region were produced by figures such as Sixt of Ottersdorf, who wrote the *Memorials of the Troubled Years 1546 and 1547* regarding a failed Bohemian rising against Ferdinand I of Austria.

## Biography
The exact year of Bartos's birth is uncertain. However, historical records confirm that he died in 1539.
