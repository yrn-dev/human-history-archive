# Events and Figures of 1546

The year 1546 is associated with several significant figures across religious, legal, and ecclesiastical history, including the death of Martin Luther and the transition of Francis Borgia into the Jesuit order.

## Martin Luther
In early 1546, Martin Luther experienced declining health, characterized by more continuous bodily pains and frequent attacks of illness. Despite these conditions and the entreaties of his elector and wife, Luther left Wittenberg on January 23, 1546, during bitterly cold weather. He traveled to resolve a trifling dispute regarding inheritance that threatened the peace of the House of Mansfeld. 

Luther served as an arbiter and successfully brought an end to the strife. On February 14, he preached in Eisleben with his usual fervour, though he concluded his final pulpit appearance by stating that while more was to be said about the Gospel, he was too weak to continue. Following the signing of deeds of reconciliation on February 16 and 17, Luther became very ill. He died on the early morning of February 18, 1546, at the age of sixty-three.

## Francis Borgia and the Society of Jesus
Francis Borgia, the duke of Gandia and a Roman Catholic saint, had a long history of public service before his religious transition. Born in 1510, he was educated at Saragossa and showed an early inclination toward monastic life. However, he spent many years at the court of Charles V, serving as master of the horse to the empress and accompanying Charles on expeditions to Provence in 1536 and Africa in 1535. He also served as the commander of the order of St James and the viceroy of Catalonia, a position he held until 1543.

Borgia's determination to embrace a monastic life was confirmed in 1539 while convoying the body of the empress to Granada. After retiring to Gandia, he encouraged the recently founded order of Jesus and established a Jesuit college. Following the death of his consort, Eleanor, in 1546, Borgia resolved to become a member of the society himself. Due to family and political circumstances, a papal dispensation allowed him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children. He eventually resigned his estate and rank upon returning to Spain in 1551, having previously visited Rome in 1550 to provide funds for the building of the Collegium Romanum. He later served as the general of the order of Jesuits.

## William Barclay
The year 1546 also marks the birth of William Barclay in Aberdeenshire. Barclay became a Scottish jurist who pursued his education at Aberdeen University before moving to France in 1573. In Bourges, he studied law under Cujas and earned a doctor's degree. His career in France included appointments by Charles III, duke of Lorraine, as a master of requests, a counsellor of state, and a professor of civil law at the newly-founded university of Pont-a-Mousson.

Barclay's career was later impacted by his opposition to the Jesuits, specifically regarding their proposal to admit his son, John, as a member of their society. This conflict led to his departure from France in 1603. Although King James offered him preferment in England on the condition that he join the Church of England, Barclay refused and returned to France in 1604 to serve as a professor of civil law at the university of Angers, where he died in 1608. His written works include a defense of the rights of kings titled *De Regno et Regali Potestate, &c.* (1600) and *De Potestate Papae, &c.* (1609), the latter of which opposed the pope's usurpation of temporal powers.
