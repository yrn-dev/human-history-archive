# Illustrium majoris Britanniae

*Illustrium majoris Britanniae scriptorum, hoc est, Angliae, Cambriae, ac Scotiae Summarium* is regarded as the most important work of the author Bale. Published in 1548 and 1549 in Ipswich and Wesel for John Overton, the initial version of this chronological catalogue of British authors and their works covered five centuries. A subsequent edition, printed at Basel between 1557 and 1559 under the title *Scriptorum illustrium majoris Britanniae ... Catalogus*, was almost entirely rewritten and expanded to contain fourteen centuries.

## Research and Methodology
Bale's work was partly based on the *Commentarii* and *Collectanea* of John Leland. However, Bale was an indefatigable worker and collector who personally examined many valuable libraries belonging to the Carmelite and Augustinian houses prior to their dissolution. 

The necessity of this work is highlighted by the wholesale destruction of library monuments during this period. In his notebook, Bale records visiting Norwich—which he describes as the second city by name—where he observed that library monuments had been turned over to the use of "worldly occupiers," including sopesellers, candelmakers, and grossers. Bale noted that he saved as much information regarding the titles of works and names of authors as he could in Norfolk, Southfolke, and Norwich, stating that he would have extended this effort throughout the entire realm had he been able to bear the charges.

## Value and Content
Despite the presence of inaccuracies and the "abuse lavished on Catholic writers," the work is considered invaluable because it preserves a significant amount of information that would otherwise have been hopelessly lost. 

The materials used for the two published catalogues are preserved in Bale's autograph notebook, which is held in the Selden Collection of the Bodleian Library, Oxford. Unlike the completed published works, which are defaced by "personalities," the notebook is arranged alphabetically without ornament and generally provides the sources from which the information was derived. This notebook was later prepared for publication as *Index Britanniae Scriptorum quos ... collegit Ioannes Baleus* (Clarendon Press, 1902) by Dr. R. Lane Poole and Miss Mary Bateson, as part ix. of *Anecdota Oxoniensia*.

## Influence and Later Works
Bale's research served as the foundation for the work of John Pits (also known as Pitseus, 1560-1616), an English Catholic exile. Pits produced *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus* (Paris, 1619), which is more commonly known by its running title *De illustribus Angliae scriptoribus*. This work was actually the fourth book of a more extensive project.

In his version, Pits omitted the Protestant divines and Wycliffite authors mentioned by Bale. The most valuable portion of Pits' work consists of the lives of Catholic exiles residing in other French towns and Douai. In a note regarding John Bale (*Nota de Joanne Bale*), Pits asserted that Bale's *Catalogus* misrepresented the material provided by Leland. However, evidence suggests that Pits was only acquainted with Leland's work second-hand through the writings of Bale.
