# Barberini Palace and Library

The Barberini palace and library in Rome serve as enduring evidence of the wealth and magnificence of the Barberini family. Originally of Tuscan extraction, the family settled in Florence during the early 11th century and eventually acquired significant influence and riches.

## Family Ascent and Influence
The family's power reached a peak in 1623 when Maffeo Barberini was raised to the papal throne as Urban VIII. During his papacy, he extended the family's status by appointing his brother, Antonio, as a distinguished soldier, and granting two of his nephews the rank of cardinal. A third nephew, Taddeo, was given the principality of Palestrina.

This rapid increase in power caused jealousy among neighboring princes. Odoardo Farnese, the duke of Parma, waged war against Taddeo and defeated the papal troops. Following the death of Urban VIII in 1644, the family faced hostility from his successor, Innocent X. Taddeo fled to Paris, where he died in 1647, marking the extinction of the family in the male line.

The Barberini name was later preserved through female lines. Cornelia, daughter of Taddeo, married Prince Giulio Cesare Colonna di Sciarra in 1728, who incorporated her name into his own. Subsequently, the name passed to Donna Maria, daughter and heiress of Prince Enrico Barberini-Colonna, and her husband, Marquis Luigi Sacchetti. Sacchetti received the title of prince of Palestrina and permission to use the Barberini name.

## The Palace and Library
The Barberini palace is noted for its magnificence, though the methods used to adorn it were viewed as ruthless. The family plundered ancient buildings to decorate their palaces, leading to the vulgar saying, "Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini" (What the barbarians did not do, the Barberini did).

Regarding the palace's art collection, the "sleeping Faun" statue was discovered by workmen in the Barberini palace after the ditch of St. Angelo was cleansed under the papacy of Urban VIII. The statue was found with its right arm, a thigh, and a leg broken.

The Barberini library in Rome is a repository of significant manuscripts. Its holdings include:
*   **Venetian Manuscripts:** A fragment of a Venetian version of the travels of Marco Polo (listed as No. 56 in certain records) is housed there. This fragment contains a passage regarding the marriage of Nicolo, noting that he took a wife and left her pregnant while waiting in Venice for the election of a new Pope.
*   **Scholarly Works:** The library was associated with S. Pieralisi, who served as its librarian and edited the work *De vita et lipsanis S. Marci Evangelistae* by Canon Molini of Venice, published in 1864.
