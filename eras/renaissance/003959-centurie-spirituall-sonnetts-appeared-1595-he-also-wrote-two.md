# Barnabe Barnes

Barnabe Barnes (1569?–1609) was an English poet and dramatist. He was the fourth son of Dr. Richard Barnes, the bishop of Durham, and was born in Yorkshire, possibly at Stonegrave, which was a living held by his father. His birth is recorded as occurring in either 1568 or 1569.

## Education and Early Career
In 1586, Barnes entered Brasenose College, Oxford. During his time there, Giovanni Florio served as his servitor. In 1591, Barnes traveled to France in the company of the earl of Essex, who was serving against the prince of Parma at the time.

## Poetic Works
Upon returning from France, Barnes published *Parthenophil and Parthenophe, Sonnettes, Madrigals, Elegies and Odes*. This work was entered on the Stationers' Register in 1593 and was dedicated to William Percy, whom Barnes called his "dearest friend." Percy contributed a sonnet to the eulogies that were prefixed to a later work by Barnes titled *Offices*. 

*Parthenophil* is characterized by passages of beauty and freshness, though some of its elaborate conceits are considered strained or over-ingenious. The work is a series of love lyrics from which a story can be outlined, although the incidents are slight. As with other Elizabethan sonnet-cycles, it is difficult to determine which parts of the work represent real personal experience and which are intellectual exercises imitating Petrarch. Barnes appears to have borrowed the title from Hieronymus Angerianus, a Neapolitan writer of Latin verse, and he was well acquainted with the work of contemporary French sonneteers. It is believed that *Parthenophil* may have been printed for private circulation; a copy in the library of the duke of Devonshire is thought to be unique.

In 1595, Barnes published his second work, *A Divine Centurie of Spirituall Sonnetts*. Both *Parthenophil* and *Spirituall Sonnetts* were later edited by Dr. A. B. Grosart in a limited issue in 1875. Additionally, *Parthenophil* was included in volume v. of *An English Garner* by Professor E. Arber, and later in the 1904 new edition of *An English Garner* (*Elizabethan Sonnets*), edited by S. Lee.

## Dramatic Works and Prose
Barnes authored two plays. The first, *The Divil's Charter* (1607), is a tragedy concerning the life of Pope Alexander VI and was performed before the king. The second, *The Battle of Evesham* (also known as *Hexham*), exists only as a manuscript traced to the beginning of the 18th century, but that manuscript is now lost.

In 1606, Barnes wrote a prose treatise titled *Offices enabling privat Persons for the speciall service of all good Princes and Policies*, which he dedicated to King James.

## Literary Affiliations and Controversies
Barnes was a supporter of Gabriel Harvey and experimented with classical metres. This partisanship led to conflict with Thomas Nashe, who accused Barnes of stealing a nobleman's chain at Windsor and made other accusations, though these were apparently made without any proof.
