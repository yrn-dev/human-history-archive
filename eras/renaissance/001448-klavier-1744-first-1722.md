# Johann Sebastian Bach: Works and Influence

Johann Sebastian Bach was a composer whose output spanned several distinct periods, including his time in Arnstadt-Muehlhausen, Weimar, and Coethen, concluding with a final period from 1733 to 1750. While his style underwent clear and important changes, his final period is characterized by choral works that became more terse and numerous, and instrumental music that showed an increasing preference for large-scale designs.

## Church and Orchestral Music
Bach composed 190 church cantatas, some of which are known only from fragmentary sets of parts. Within this group, 40 were written for solo voices and approximately 60 were founded more or less on chorales. The remaining cantatas, which almost always included a chorale for congregational singing, were practically short oratorios and were frequently titled as such by Bach. His other church works include three wedding cantatas, the Easter oratorio, and the Christmas oratorio, the latter consisting of six similar cantatas forming a connected design for performance over six separate days.

His orchestral output includes six Brandenburg concertos for various combinations, two violin concertos, one concerto for two violins, and four orchestral suites. He also produced a colossal "torso" of a concerted violin-movement that served as a prelude to a church cantata that is now lost.

## Clavier and Chamber Music
Bach's instrumental music reached a peak during his appointment as *Kapellmeister* to the duke of Coethen from 1717 to 1723. Central works from this period include the Brandenburg concertos, the French and English suites, the solo violin and violoncello sonatas, and the first book of *Das wohltemperirte Klavier*.

*Das wohltemperirte Klavier*, written for clavichord, consists of two books (the first from 1722 and the second from 1744). Each book contains 24 preludes and fugues, with one in every major and minor key. The objective of this work was to stimulate tuning by "equal temperament" to avoid sacrificing the euphony of remoter keys.

Other significant clavier and organ collections include:
*   ***Klavier-Uebung***: Primarily for harpsichord, this four-book collection includes 15 two-part inventions, 15 three-part symphonies, six partitas, the "Goldberg" variations, four duets, the Italian concerto, the French overture, and a collection of organ choral-preludes featuring the "St Anne" prelude and fugue in E flat.
*   **Organ Works**: Bach wrote six sonatas, four groups of six organ preludes and fugues, and *Das Orgelbuechlein*, a planned collection of short choral-preludes that remained less than half executed. He also composed 18 larger chorale-preludes (including his final composition), six "Schuebler" chorales arranged from cantata movements, three independent toccatas, and the Passacaglia.
*   **Chamber Music**: His works in this category include three sonatas for clavier and flute; a suite and six sonatas for clavier and violin; three for clavier and viola da gamba; two trios with figured bass; two flute-sonatas; a violin suite with figured bass; six sonatas (three sonatas and three partitas) for solo violin; and six suites for solo violoncello.

## Unclassified Works
Two major instrumental works remain unclassified. *Das musikalische Opfer* contains two great fugues, various puzzle-canons, and a trio for flute, violin, and figured bass, all based on a theme provided by Frederick the Great. *Die Kunst der Fuge* is a progressive series of fugues on a single subject, written in open score as abstract studies, though most are written in an admirable clavier style.

## Legacy and Influence
Bach's influence was a subtle and universal force in the development of music, though it was often only recognized by other great composers. Mozart developed a new depth of harmony and polyphonic richness after reading manuscript copies of Bach's motets and *Das wohltemperirte Klavier*. Beethoven studied Bach's accessible works profoundly, frequently quoting them in his sketch-books. A comparison between Beethoven's 31st variation of his "Thirty-three Variations on a Waltz by Diabelli" and Bach's 25th "Goldberg Variation" reveals a kinship in their most intimate expressions of feeling.

Later composers such as Chopin showed a love for *Das wohltemperirte Klavier* in his Preludes and Etudes. Schumann was also influenced by Bach's texture and rhythm, and he assiduously cultivated fugues late in his career.

## Editions and Scholarship
The *Bach-Gesellschaft* provides the only complete modern edition, though its merit is unequal. Early editors often ignored details of ornamentation and figured bass. Scholarly methods were later introduced by Wilhelm Rust in the 9th *Jahrgang*. The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* was edited by Kroll, whose text was later supplemented by an autograph from the British Museum. The Steingraeber edition, edited by Dr. Hans Bischoff, is regarded as the best edition of the clavier works, as it clearly distinguishes the editor's nuances from Bach's own rare indications and provides comprehensive explanations of ornaments.
