# Companions of Columbus

The companions of Christopher Columbus were the crew and associates who accompanied the Genoese mariner on his voyages to the new world. Their experiences were characterized by significant psychological strain, unexpected geographical discoveries, and contributions to early cartography.

## Experiences and Fears of the Crew
During the momentous first voyage, which lasted two months and nine days, the crew was plagued by doubts and fears. A primary concern among the companions was the possibility that they might sail on forever. To comfort the crew, they looked for signs of land, such as the sighting of birds, a branch containing strange berries, and a pole that had been worked with tools.

The crew also experienced considerable alarm when encountering the sea of Sargasso in the Atlantic ocean south of the Azores. This area is covered by an immense mass of sea-weed, which can be so thickly accumulated that a considerable wind is required to propel a vessel through it. A. von Humboldt observed that the vivid accounts provided by Columbus's first companions regarding these difficulties bear a striking resemblance to the exaggerated tales attributed to the Phoenicians concerning navigation beyond the Columns of Hercules, the Isle Sacrée (Ierné), and Cerné.

## The First Landing and Subsequent Expeditions
On the night of October 11, 1492, at ten o'clock, Columbus sighted a light ahead. Land was sighted the following morning, and Columbus landed on the shores of the new world while the day was still young, bearing the royal banner of Spain and richly apparelled. 

By early 1493, Columbus returned to Europe bringing cotton, gold, strange birds and beasts, and two "wild-eyed painted Indians" for the purpose of baptism. Because it was believed he had found India rather than Japan, the discovered islands were named the West Indies. In the same year, a much larger expedition was launched consisting of fifteen thousand men and seventeen ships, authorized by the Pope to take possession of the lands for the Spanish crown. Following this, a "swarm of Spanish adventurers" began exploring the new territories.

## Notable Companions and Cartography
Among the companions of Columbus was Juan de la Cosa. He is credited with producing the earliest extant chart of the world that depicts the discoveries in the new world, dated 1500.

## Literary and Historical References
The history of Columbus and his companions has been documented in various works. Washington Irving authored *Voyages and Discoveries of the Companions of Columbus* in 1831. This work is further supplemented by notes written by Varela, which are found in the *Biblioteca del Comercio del Plata* in Monte Video.

Other historical sources cited in the archive regarding these discoveries include G. F. de Oviedo's *Historia general ... de las Indias* (1526), M. F. de Navarrete's *Coleccion de los Viajes y Descubrimientos* (1825-1837), and D. M. T. Quintana's *Vidas de Espanoles celebres*.
