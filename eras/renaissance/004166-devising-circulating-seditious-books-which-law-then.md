# Devising and Circulating Seditious Books

In historical legal and political contexts, the acts of devising and circulating materials deemed seditious have led to severe judicial penalties, including imprisonment and execution. These actions often intersect with religious dissent, political instability, and the control of legal or sacred texts.

## The Case of John Greenwood and Henry Barrowe
In late 16th-century England, the charge of "devising and circulating seditious books" was used against proponents of rigid Separatism. John Greenwood, an English Puritan and Separatist who had been a chaplain to Lord Rich and a "teacher" of the Separatist church, was arrested in October 1586. He was later committed to the Fleet prison in May 1587 due to a default of bail for conformity. While imprisoned, Greenwood wrote several controversial tracts in collaboration with a fellow prisoner, Henry Barrowe.

Barrowe, also a Puritan and Separatist, had been detained in November 1586 after visiting Greenwood in the Clink. Barrowe was committed to the Gatehouse and later to the Fleet Prison, where he remained for nearly six years. During his detention, Barrowe maintained the principles of separatism and denounced the ritual of the Church.

On December 5, 1592, Greenwood was arrested again. In March 1593, both Greenwood and Barrowe were tried and condemned to death on the specific charge of "devising and circulating seditious books." Following two respites, including one at the foot of the gallows, John Greenwood was hanged on April 6, 1593.

## Conflict Over the Circulation of Sacred Texts
The circulation of religious texts has frequently been a source of conflict, both between different religious factions and between religious groups and governing authorities.

### The Bible Societies
The advance of certain Bible societies was hindered by controversies regarding the nature of the texts they circulated. One fundamental law of a society required the circulation of the Bible alone, without comment; this was attacked by divines of the Church of England, including Bishop Marsh, who argued that the Prayer Book should accompany the Bible. 

Further disputes arose regarding the circulation of Bibles containing Deutero-canonical books. In 1826, the society resolved to exclude the circulation of books or parts of books termed "Apocryphal." This decision led supporters in Glasgow and Edinburgh to form independent organizations, which eventually merged in 1861 as the National Bible Society of Scotland. Additionally, disputes over the rendering of the term "baptize" in the "Serampore Versions" led to the 1839 founding of the Bible Translation Society to issue versions with distinctively Baptist renderings.

### The Books of the Law in Cesarea
In Cesarea, sedition manifested through the provocation of religious laws. A man of a seditious temper sacrificed birds at the entrance of a synagogue using an earthen vessel, an act that polluted the site and affronted the laws of the Israelites. While the moderate Israelites sought help from governors, the seditious elements among both the Israelites and the Gentiles engaged in fighting.

During the conflict, the Israelites took their books of the law and retired to Narbata. When John and twelve principal men sought help from Florus in Sebaste, Florus had the men imprisoned. They were specifically accused of carrying the books of the law out of Cesarea.

## Legal and Administrative Contexts of "Devising"
The term "devise" appears in various historical contexts beyond sedition, often referring to the creation of laws, financial schemes, or the transfer of property.

### Legal and Financial Devising
In the context of English land law, the power to devise lands by will was not available until 1540 (32 Henry VIII. c. 1), and corporations were initially excluded from this benefit. The Mortmain Acts and subsequent legislation, such as the Wills Act 1837 and the Mortmain and Charitable Uses Act 1888, regulated how land could be devised, generally requiring a licence for corporations to take land.

In the United States, the national banking system was devised by Mr. Chase, the secretary of the treasury, to meet the financial needs of the Federal government during the Civil War. This scheme created a compulsory market for government bonds by allowing banks with Federal charters to issue circulating notes secured by those bonds.

### Historical and Textual Devising
In historical narratives, the term is used to describe the invention of ancestry or laws. For example, accounts of Brutus and the progeny of kings were viewed by some as having been devised to provide the Britons with a noble ancestor, eventually linking them to Trojan tales to align British origins with those of the Romans.
