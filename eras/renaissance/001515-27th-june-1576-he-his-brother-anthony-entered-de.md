# Francis Bacon

## Early Life and Education
Francis Bacon (1561–1626) was an English essayist, statesman, and philosopher born on January 22, 1560/1, at York House in the Strand, London. He was the youngest son of Sir Nicholas Bacon. His mother, the second wife of Sir Nicholas, was a daughter of Sir Anthony Cooke, who had served as tutor to Edward VI. Bacon's mother was a woman of considerable culture and skill in classical studies, and she was a warm adherent to the Puritan or Reformed Church.

Due to his extremely delicate health, Bacon likely received much of his early instruction at home. In April 1573, he entered Trinity College, Cambridge, where he resided for three years with his brother, Anthony. During his time at the university, Bacon studied the sciences as they were taught at the time. He concluded that the results and methods of these sciences were erroneous. While he maintained a reverence for Aristotle, Bacon grew to despise the prevailing Aristotelian philosophy, viewing it as fruitless and useful only for disputation. He believed philosophy needed a new method and a true purpose, a conception that remained with him as he left the university.

## Legal Training and Early Career
On June 27, 1576, Francis Bacon and his brother Anthony were entered *de societate magistrorum* at Gray's Inn. A few months after this entry, Bacon was sent abroad to Paris with Sir Amyas Paulet, the English ambassador. The disturbed state of society and government in France provided him with valuable political instruction. While it was once believed that his *Notes on the State of Christendom* resulted from these observations, Spedding has shown there is no reason to ascribe these notes to Bacon; they are more probably attributed to one of Anthony's correspondents.

## Financial Hardships and Professional Shift
The sudden death of Sir Nicholas Bacon in February 1578/9 had a serious impact on Francis's fortunes. Sir Nicholas had set aside a considerable sum of money to purchase an estate for his youngest son, as he was the only one otherwise unprovided for. Because of the sudden death, this plan was not executed, and Francis received only a fifth of that money. This event was one of the gravest misfortunes of his life, leaving him with insufficient means. Consequently, Bacon acquired a habit of borrowing and remained in debt for the rest of his life. Because it became necessary for him to adopt a profession, he chose law and returned to reside at Gray's Inn in 1579.

## Political Activity
Bacon appears to have become acquainted with the earl of Essex, a favorite of Queen Elizabeth, perhaps as early as 1588. By the end of 1591, Bacon was serving as the earl's confidential adviser. In this capacity, he and his brother Anthony worked diligently in the service of the earl. In February 1593, Bacon took a seat in parliament for Middlesex. This particular session of the House had been summoned following the discovery of one of the many popish plots that occurred during the reign of Elizabeth.
