# Book Collecting and the Value of Editions

## Valuation of Editions
In the field of book collecting, there is generally a marked decline in the interest directed toward early editions other than the first. This lack of interest consequently leads to a decrease in the prices paid for these subsequent early editions. However, an exception occurs when an edition contains important changes in the text; in such instances, the edition in which these changes first appear gains some of the prestige typically associated with an original issue.

## Collection Strategies and Utility
One recognized approach to book collecting involves the acquisition of as many editions of a single work as possible. If this practice results in the collection of numerous late editions that offer no value regarding the text, the only usefulness of such a collection is that it may serve as an index to the popularity of the author.

Conversely, a long sequence of intermediate editions can be of great aid in studying the development of a final text. This is particularly true for the following types of works:
* Translations of the Bible
* Liturgical works
* Editions published during the lifetime of the author

## Notable Collectors and Bindings
Historical records highlight several prominent collectors of fine books and the specific characteristics of their bindings:

**Jean Grolier**
The viscount d'Aguisy and treasurer of France in 1545 was a significant collector. Most of his books were bound for his own use and feature his name, *Io Grolierii et Amicorum*, as well as the legend *Portio mea domine sit in terra viventium*. Some of his bindings may have been designed by Geoffrey Tory, who was an engraver and printer to Francis I of France.

**Tommaso Maioli**
An Italian collector active around the same time as Grolier, Maioli utilized the same form of legend. His books are distinguished by the presence of atoms of gold remaining within the irregularities of the leather.

**Demetrio Canevari**
A physician to Pope Urban VIII, Canevari had his books bound in deep red or dark green morocco. These bindings feature a colored cameo stamp—though the color has worn off in most cases—depicting Apollo driving a chariot with one black horse and one white horse toward a mountain featuring a silver Pegasus. This stamp is surrounded by the Greek legend *ORTHOS KAI MAE LOXIOS*.

## Regional Binding Trends
Bindings created for cardinals and popes in Italy are regarded as being of high merit and significant interest. However, as a general rule, later Italian bindings are considered disappointing.
