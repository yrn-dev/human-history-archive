# Cesare Borgia

Cesare Borgia (1476–1507), the son of Pope Alexander VI and Vanozza dei Cattanei, was a prominent figure of the Italian Renaissance who served as the duke of Valentinois and Romagna. Born in Rome while his father was a cardinal, Cesare became the favorite son of the Pope, who utilized extensive nepotism to advance his career.

## Ecclesiastical Career and Transition
Following his father's elevation to the papacy in 1492, Cesare was created archbishop of Valencia and was made a cardinal a year later. During the early years of the pontificate, he was known for leading a profligate life at the Vatican. In January 1495, he accompanied Charles VIII as a hostage to ensure the Pope's good behavior during the conquest of Naples, though Cesare eventually escaped at Velletri to return to Rome.

By 1497, Cesare began contemplating a transition from ecclesiastical dignities to a secular career. In August 1498, during the consistory, Cesare requested and was granted permission by the Pope and the cardinals to renounce the priesthood "for the good of his soul."

## Political Ambitions and Alliances
Cesare's shift to secular power was influenced by the death of his brother Giovanni, the duke of Gandia, in 1497. While some attributed the murder of Giovanni to rivalry over Donna Sancha or incestuous relations with their sister Lucrezia, it is considered more probable that Cesare envied Giovanni's position and sought to secure his father's full favor.

In October 1498, Cesare traveled to France as a papal legate to Louis XII. He delivered a papal bull annulling Louis's marriage to Jeanne of France, facilitating the king's marriage to Anne of Brittany. In return, Cesare received military assistance for his own enterprises and the duchy of Valentinois. During this time, he sought to marry Carlotta, the daughter of the king of Naples, but was resolutely refused by both the princess and her father, who viewed him as "a priest, the bastard of a priest." Consequently, in May 1499, he married Charlotte d'Albret, the sister of the king of Navarre.

## Conquest of Romagna
Pope Alexander VI sought to carve a principality for Cesare out of territories owing nominal allegiance to the papacy by subduing local despots in Romagna. Establishing headquarters at Cesena, Cesare led an army comprising 300 French lances, 4000 Gascons and Swiss, and Italian troops. He successfully attacked Imola and besieged Forli. The city of Forli was held by Caterina Sforza, the widow of Girolamo Riario; she surrendered on January 22, 1500, and was later placed in a convent.

Cesare's methods during this period were observed by Machiavelli, who was sent as an envoy to Cesare's camp in October 1502. Machiavelli admired Cesare's combination of diplomatic prudence and audacity, as well as his use of fraud, cruelty, and native troops to cement a new state. This period included the murder of Cesare's disaffected captains.

## Decline and Fall
Cesare's power was inextricably linked to his father. In the summer of 1503, both Cesare and Pope Alexander VI were seized with fever after attending a banquet hosted by Cardinal Adriano Castellesi. The Pope died on August 18, 1503, while Cesare was still incapacitated. This coincidence led to Cesare's ruin, as his enemies rose against him. The Orsinis rebelled in Rome, and deposed princes in Romagna sought to regain their lands.

After attempting to intimidate the cardinals with his bravos and Don Michelotto, Cesare was forced to leave Rome in September. Although he hoped for a friendly successor, the election of Pius III led to the rapid disintegration of his dominion. The lords of Pesaro, Rimini, and Piombino regained their territories, and Guidobaldo returned to the duchy of Urbino.

Following the death of Pius III on October 18, 1503, Giuliano della Rovere was elected as Pope Julius II on November 1. While Julius II initially showed no ill-will, he insisted that Cesare's territories be restored to the church. After attempting to use Cesare to block Venetian intervention in Romagna, Julius II had him arrested, effectively ending Borgia's power.
