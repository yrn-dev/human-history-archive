# Michael Baius

## Early Life and Education
Michael Baius, also known as De Bay, was a Belgian theologian born in 1513 in Melun, located in Hainault. He pursued his higher education at Louvain University, where he studied theology and philosophy. His academic performance was characterized by distinguished success, which subsequently led to a series of academic appointments.

## Academic Career at Louvain
Baius spent much of his professional life at Louvain University, an institution that had been founded in 1423 by Duke John IV of Brabant and was noted for its theological teaching. In 1552, Charles V appointed Baius as the professor of scriptural interpretation at the university. He eventually rose to the highest administrative level of the institution, becoming the chancellor of Louvain in 1575. He held both the chancellorship and his professorship until his death in 1589.

## Theological Positions and Conflicts
At Louvain, Baius established himself as a prominent leader in the 16th-century anti-scholastic reaction. This intellectual movement operated under the banner of St Augustine. Baius' own Augustinian predilections led to significant conflicts with Rome, specifically regarding the nature of free-will and grace. 

His writings are noted for placing great importance on the fact of actual and original sin. The historian Harnack described these works as a curious mixture of unconscious tendencies toward Protestantism and Catholic orthodoxy.

## Papal Condemnations
The theological disputes between Baius and the Roman authorities resulted in formal condemnations. In 1567, Pope Pius V issued the Bull *Ex omnibus afflictionibus*, which condemned seventy-nine propositions found in the writings of Baius. Although Baius submitted to this decree, a renewal of the condemnation occurred in 1579 under Pope Gregory XIII, triggered by indiscreet utterances made by Baius and his supporters.

## Other Activities and Legacy
In 1563, Baius was nominated as one of the Belgian representatives to the council of Trent. However, he arrived too late to play an important role in the council's deliberations. 

Historically, Baius is regarded as a forerunner to the more celebrated Cornelius Jansen.
