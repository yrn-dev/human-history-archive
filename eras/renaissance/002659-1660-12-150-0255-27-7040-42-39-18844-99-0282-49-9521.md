# 1660

The year 1660 was marked by significant political transitions, diplomatic treaties, and intellectual contributions across Europe. It is most notably associated with the Restoration in England and a constitutional revolution affecting the relationship between Denmark and Norway.

## Political Events and the English Restoration
In England, 1660 saw the Restoration of the monarchy under Charles II. During this period, Downing, an able diplomatist who had been maintained in his post following the fall of Richard Cromwell, made his peace with Charles II in April 1660. Downing communicated Thurloe's despatches to the king and renounced principles he had acquired in New England, which he then viewed as errors. As a result of the Restoration, Downing was knighted in May 1660 and retained his embassy in Holland and his tellership of the exchequer. He was also granted a piece of land for building purposes adjoining St James's Park, now known as Downing Street. 

Additionally, Downing was returned for Morpeth in the convention parliament of April 1660, a constituency he represented in every subsequent parliament until his death. In this political climate, Downing strongly supported the Navigation Act of 1660.

## Scandinavian Affairs and the Revolution of 1660
In Scandinavia, 1660 was the year of the Treaty of Copenhagen. This period also involved the reign of Frederick III (1648–1670). 

The revolution of 1660 had a beneficial impact on Norway. With the disappearance of the *Rigsraad*—which had represented the Danish crown and exercised sovereignty over both kingdoms—Norway ceased to be a subject principality. Constitutionally, Norway was placed on an equality with Denmark, united with it but no longer subordinate. While the Norwegian people had hoped for an administration independent of the Danish government, this was not realized; Copenhagen remained the headquarters of the Norwegian administration until the cessation of the Union in 1814. Despite this, Norway's condition improved. Following the revolution, a land commission was appointed in January 1661 to investigate economical and financial conditions, leading to the transformation of fiefs into counties and the removal of tax immunity for nobles.

## Intellectual and Cultural Contributions
The year 1660 saw the publication of several scientific and artistic works. Jean Baptiste Duhamel, a French physicist and Oratorian priest, published two works in dialogue form in 1660: *Astronomia physica* and *De meteoris et fossilibus*. 

In Sweden, Erik Dahlberg began the fine collection of drawings titled *Suecia antiqua et hodierna*, which was produced between 1660 and 1716. In the realm of fashion, historical records include illustrations of French noblemen from approximately 1660.

## Notable Births
White Kennett, an English antiquary and bishop, was born in Dover in August 1660. He later became the bishop of Peterborough and published *Parochial Antiquities* in 1695.
