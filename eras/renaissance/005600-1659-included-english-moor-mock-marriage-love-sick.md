# 1659

The year 1659 is associated with various literary publications, political shifts in England and North Africa, and the birth of notable figures.

## Literary Works and Publications
In the field of English drama, the year 1659 saw the publication of *Five New Playes* by Richard Brome. Brome, an English dramatist who had been a servant and friend to Ben Jonson, was noted for his genius in comedy. This specific collection included several works: *The English Moor, or The Mock Marriage*; *The Love-Sick Court, or The Ambitious Politique*; *Covent Garden Weeded*; *The New Academy, or The New Exchange*; and *The Queen and Concubine*.

In the realm of religious scholarship and biblical study, R. Gell published *Essay toward the Amendment of the Authorized Version*, and W. Kilburne published *Dangerous Errors in ... Bibles*. Additionally, the year 1659 marked the publication of *The Appeal of Injured Innocence* by Fuller, a reply to Heylyn's *Examen Historicum* inscribed to George Berkeley, 1st Earl Berkeley.

## Political and Regional Developments
In North Africa, 1659 marked a significant change in the governance of cities such as Algiers, Tunis, and Tripoli. From 1587 until 1659, these regions were ruled by Turkish pashas sent from Constantinople for three-year terms. However, a military revolt in Algiers in 1659 reduced the pashas to nonentities. Following this event, these cities became anarchical military republics that chose their own rulers and lived by plunder, although they remained nominally part of the Turkish empire.

In England, the year was characterized by ongoing political activity. Sir George Downing, an English soldier and diplomatist, represented Carlisle in the parliament of 1659. Downing, who had previously served as scout-master-general of Cromwell's forces in Scotland and as a teller of the exchequer, was among the first to urge Cromwell to restore the old constitution and take the royal title.

## Notable Births
John Dunton, an English author and bookseller, was born on May 4, 1659, in Graffham, Huntingdonshire. Born into a family of clergymen, Dunton later became a bookseller in London and served as the treasurer of the Whig apprentices during the struggle leading to the Revolution. He is remembered primarily for his work *Life and Errors of John Dunton* (1705).
