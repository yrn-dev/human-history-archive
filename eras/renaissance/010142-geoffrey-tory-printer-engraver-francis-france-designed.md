# Geoffrey Tory

Geoffrey Tory was a professional printer and engraver who served Francis I. of France. In addition to his roles in printing and engraving, Tory was involved in the design of book bindings.

## Contributions to Bookbinding
Tory is credited with the design of several fine bindings. The archive's records indicate that he created some of these bindings for his own personal use. Furthermore, it is considered quite possible that he designed some bindings for Jean Grolier, the viscount d'Aguisy.

## Historical Context of French Book Production
Tory's work occurred during a period of significant transition in French book production. In the second quarter of the 16th century, printers in Lyons and Paris produced small sextodecimos, which were half the size of Aldine octavos. While these smaller formats found a ready market, they were eventually ousted by printers from Leiden and Antwerp who produced even smaller books in small twelves or 24mo.

During this era, the physical construction of books evolved to reduce both weight and size, specifically through the use of pasteboards as a substitute for wooden sides. This period also saw the introduction of gold tooling on bindings. In the second half of the 16th century, France experienced a trend of very elaborate decoration in bookbinding, a practice that continued until it was eventually checked by a sumptuary law.

## Contemporary Collectors
Tory's potential association with Jean Grolier places him among the circle of great book collectors of the 16th century. Grolier, who served as the treasurer of France in 1545, was a prominent collector of fine books. Many of the volumes bound for Grolier featured his name, *Io Grolierii et Amicorum*, and the legend *Portio mea domine sit in terra viventium*. Similar forms of legends were utilized by other collectors of the time, such as the Italian collector Tommaso Maioli.
