# Henry Briggs

Henry Briggs (1556–1630) was an English mathematician born at Warley Wood, near Halifax, in Yorkshire. He is primarily recognized for his contributions to the development of logarithms and geometry.

## Academic Career and Professorships
Briggs graduated from St John's College, Cambridge, in 1581 and was granted a fellowship there in 1588. In 1592, he served as the reader of the physical lecture established by Dr Thomas Linacre. By 1596, he became the first professor of geometry at Gresham House (later Gresham College) in London. 

In 1619, Briggs was appointed the Savilian professor of geometry at Oxford. Consequently, he resigned his position at Gresham College on July 25, 1620. Following his move to Oxford, he was incorporated as a master of arts.

## Contributions to Logarithms
During his tenure at Gresham House, Briggs proposed a modification to the scale of logarithms. He sought to change the hyperbolic form originally provided by John Napier to a system where unity is assumed as the logarithm of the ratio of ten to one. 

Briggs communicated this suggestion to Napier and later visited him in Edinburgh in 1616 and again the following year to discuss the change. These conferences resulted in an agreement on the proposed alteration. Upon returning from his second visit to Edinburgh in 1617, Briggs published the first chiliad of his logarithms.

## Published Works
Briggs authored a wide array of mathematical and navigational texts:

*   **Navigation and Astronomy:** He published *A Table to find the Height of the Pole, the Magnetical Declination being given* (1602) and "Tables for the Improvement of Navigation," which appeared in the second edition of Edward Wright's *Certain Errors in Navigation detected and corrected* (1610). He also wrote *A Description of an Instrumental Table to find the part proportional, devised by Mr Edward Wright* (1616 and 1618).
*   **Logarithms and Geometry:** His major works include *Logarithmorum Chilias prima* (1617), *Lucubrationes et Annotationes in opera posthuma J. Neperi* (1619), *Euclidis Elementorum VI. libri priores* (1620), and *Arithmetica Logarithmica* (1624). The latter, published in folio, contains the logarithms of thirty thousand natural numbers calculated to fourteen places of figures beyond the index.
*   **Trigonometry:** Briggs completed a table of natural sines to fifteen places, as well as tangents and secants to ten places. He also produced a table of logarithmic sines and tangents for the hundredth part of every degree to fourteen places. These were printed at Gouda in 1631 and published in 1633 as *Trigonometria Britannica*.
*   **Other Writings:** In 1622, he published a small tract titled *A Treatise on the North-West Passage to the South Sea* (also referred to as *North-West Passage to the South Seas, through the Continent of Virginia and Hudson's Bay*), which was later reprinted in Purchas's *Pilgrims*. He also wrote two letters to Archbishop Usher and a work titled *Mathematica ab Antiquis minus cognita*.

Some of his works remained unpublished, including *Remarks on the Treatise of Longomontanus respecting the Quadrature of the Circle* and *Commentaries on the Geometry of Peter Ramus*.

## Death and Character
Henry Briggs died on January 26, 1630, and was buried in the chapel of Merton College, Oxford. In the *Lives of the Gresham Professors*, Dr Smith describes Briggs as a man of great probity who was contented with his own station, preferred a studious retirement, and was a contemner of riches.
