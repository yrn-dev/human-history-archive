# Henry Barrowe

## Early Life and Education
Henry Barrowe was born around 1550 in Shipdam, Norfolk. He belonged to a family that was related by marriage to the lord keeper Bacon and was likely related to Aylmer, the bishop of London. In November 1566, Barrowe matriculated at Clare Hall, Cambridge, where he later graduated with a B.A. in 1569-1570.

## Early Adulthood and Religious Conversion
Following his graduation, Barrowe spent a period of time "following the court," during which he led a life described as licentious and frivolous. Between 1576 and the following few years, he was a member of Gray's Inn, although he was never called to the bar.

Around 1580 or 1581, Barrowe's life changed after he was deeply impressed by a sermon. This event led him to retire to the country, where he engaged in meditation and study, eventually adopting the strictest form of Puritanism.

## Separatism and Association with John Greenwood
Barrowe later entered into an intimate relationship with the Separatist leader John Greenwood. He adopted Greenwood's views without reserve, and it is noted that Barrowe's own influence may have partially shaped those views. Although he did not reside strictly in London, Barrowe was associated with a group known as "the brethren of the Separation." He became conspicuous within their secret meetings due to his eloquence and natural earnestness.

## Arrest and Imprisonment
On November 19, 1586, Barrowe traveled from the country to visit John Greenwood, who had been imprisoned in the Clink. Upon this visit, Barrowe was detained by the gaoler and brought before Archbishop Whitgift. Barrowe contested the legality of his arrest and refused to provide bail for future appearances or take the *ex officio* oath. Consequently, he was committed to the Gatehouse.

After nearly six months of detention and several irregular examinations conducted by high commissioners, Barrowe and Greenwood were formally indicted in May 1587. They were charged with recusancy under an act that had originally been directed against Papists. The court ordered them to remain in the Fleet Prison until they could provide heavy bail for conformity.

## Final Years and Death
Barrowe remained a prisoner for the rest of his life, a period spanning nearly six years. His confinement varied between close confinement and periods where he was granted "the liberty of the prison." 

He underwent further examinations, including one on March 18, 1588, before the privy council at Whitehall, which occurred following a petition to the queen. During these proceedings, Barrowe vigorously defended the principle of separatism and denounced the prescribed ritual of the Church. He died in 1593.
