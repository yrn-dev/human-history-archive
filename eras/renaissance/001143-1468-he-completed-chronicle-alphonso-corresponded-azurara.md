# Gomes Eannes de Azurara

Gomes Eannes de Azurara (died 1474) was a prominent Portuguese chronicler who entered the field of letters in his middle life. His professional trajectory began in the royal library, where he likely served as an assistant to Fernao Lopes during the reign of King Duarte (1433–1438). By 1452, Azurara had assumed sole charge of the royal library, and on June 6, 1454, he succeeded Fernao Lopes as the royal chronicler and chief keeper of the archives.

## Literary Works and Historical Contributions

Azurara produced several significant historical accounts, often serving as supplements to earlier works or fulfilling royal commissions. His *Chronicle of the Siege and Capture of Ceuta*, written in 1450, served as a supplement to the *Chronicle of King John I* by Fernao Lopes. In 1453, he completed the first draft of the *Chronicle of the Discovery and Conquest of Guinea*. This work is a primary authority for early Portuguese voyages of discovery in the ocean and down the African coast, particularly those conducted under the auspices of Prince Henry the Navigator; it provides both geographical information and an account of the prince's life work.

Under the commission of King Alphonso V in 1456, Azurara wrote a history of Ceuta—described as "the land-gate of the East"—covering the period from its capture in 1415 until 1437 under the governorship of D. Pedro de Menezes. This work was completed in 1463. Subsequently, the king charged him with recording the deeds of D. Duarte de Menezes, the captain of Alcacer. To gather materials for this chronicle, Azurara traveled to Africa and spent a year studying the scenes of the events he described, completing the chronicle in 1468.

His bibliography includes the following titles:
* *Chronica del Rei D. Joam I. Terceira parte em que se contem a tomada de Ceuta* (published 1644)
* *Chronica do Descobrimento e Conquista de Guine* (published 1841; English version issued by the Hakluyt Society 1896–1899)
* *Chronica do Conde D. Pedro (de Menezes)* (printed in *Ineditos de Historia Portugueza*, vol. ii, 1792)
* *Chronica do Conde D. Duarte de Menezes* (printed in *Ineditos*, vol. iii, 1793)

## Style and Intellectual Influence

Azurara's writing style differed from that of his predecessor, Fernao Lopes, as he lacked Lopes' "picturesque ingenuousness." Instead, Azurara frequently displayed his erudition through philosophical reflections and quotations, reflecting the influence of the early Renaissance. His pages feature a wide array of medieval, early Christian, and leading classical writers. He was well-versed in European romances and chronicles, and he studied the best authors from Spain and Italy. Additionally, Azurara had mastered astrology and the geographical systems of the ancients. As a historian, he is characterized as accurate, laborious, and conscientious.

## Relationship with the Crown

Azurara maintained a relationship of "affectionate intimacy" with King Alphonso V through their correspondence. In recognition of his literary services, the king rewarded him with no fewer than three *commendas* of the Order of Christ.
