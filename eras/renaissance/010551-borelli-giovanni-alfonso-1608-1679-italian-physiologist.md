# BORELLI, GIOVANNI ALFONSO (1608-1679)

Giovanni Alfonso Borelli was an Italian physicist and physiologist who is recognized as the founder of the iatrophysical school. Born in Naples on January 28, 1608, he spent his career contributing to the fields of mathematics, astronomy, and anatomy.

## Academic Career and Life
Borelli held several prestigious academic appointments during his life. In 1649, he was appointed as a professor of mathematics at Messina, and later, in 1656, he took a similar position at Pisa. He returned to Messina in 1667. By 1674, he was obliged to retire to Rome. During his final years in Rome, he lived under the protection of Christina, the queen of Sweden, until his death on December 31, 1679.

## Contributions to Physiology and Anatomy
Borelli is most noted for his work in the iatro-mechanical school of medicine. This school of thought entered into the mechanical study of anatomy, a direction suggested by Descartes, though Borelli's work was likely more dependent upon the positive researches conducted by Galileo.

His best-known work, *De motu animalium* (published in Rome, 1680-1681), sought to explain the movements of the animal body based on mechanical principles. Within this work, Borelli specifically explained the function of the air-bladder and the mechanism of swimming.

## Astronomical and Mathematical Works
Borelli made significant contributions to astronomy and mathematics. In 1665, he published a letter under the pseudonym Pier Maria Mutoli titled *Del movimento della cometa apparsa il mese di decembre 1664*, in which he became the first person to suggest the idea of a parabolic path. In 1666, he published *Theorica mediceorum planetarum ex causis physicis deducta* in Florence, a work in which he examined the influence of attraction on the satellites of Jupiter.

His other published works span various scientific and mathematical topics:
*   *Euclides Restitutus* (Pisa, 1658)
*   *Della Causa delle Febbri maligni* (Pisa, 1658)
*   *Apollonii Pergaei Conicorum libri v., vi. et vii.* (Florence, 1661)
*   *De Renum usu Judicium* (Strassburg, 1664)
*   *De vi percussionis* (Bologna, 1667)
*   *Meteorologia Aetnea* (Reggio, 1669)
*   *De motionibus naturalibus a gravitate pendentibus* (Bologna, 1670)
