# Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde was a learned physician and author known for several works concerning medicine, domestic hygiene, and geography. He is most noted for his entertaining book, *The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*, written around 1547. This work was dedicated to Lady Mary, the daughter of King Henry VIII.

## The Introduction of Knowledge
In *The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*, Boorde provides instruction on speaking various languages and describes the customs and fashions of different countries. The text also includes information regarding the various coins of money currently in use across different regions. 

Within this work, Boorde offers a lively verse description of the Englishman, detailing various foibles such as obstinacy, fickleness, and a fondness for new fashions. The book further contains a geographical description of England and a model dialogue written in the Cornish language. Boorde applied a similar structural approach to the descriptions of other countries featured in the text.

## Other Authentic Works
Beyond his *Introduction of Knowledge*, Boorde authored several other authentic texts:
*   ***Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor***: Published by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though undated and earlier editions of this work undoubtedly exist.
*   ***The Brevyary of Health***: Dated approximately 1547.
*   ***The Princyples of Astronamy***: Dated approximately 1547.
*   ***A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV***.
*   **"The Peregrination of Doctor Board"**: This piece was printed by Thomas Hearne in 1735 within *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, vol. ii.

The archive notes that two of his works, the *Treatyse upon Berdes* and the *Itinerary of Europe*, have been lost.

## Attributed Works
There are several jest-books attributed to Andrew Boorde, although these attributions lack authority. These include *Scogin's Jests* (1626) and *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam*, the earliest extant edition of which dates to 1630. Additionally, a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli* and a work printed by Wynkyn de Worde titled *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton, with his wyfe, and his daughter, and of two poore scholers of Cambridge* are attributed to him.
