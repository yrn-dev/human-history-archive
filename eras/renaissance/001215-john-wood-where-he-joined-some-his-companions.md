## The Mission of John (Gaddis)
In the account provided by Josephus, Jonathan and his brother Simon, having become aware of a plot by Bacchides to slay Jonathan through treachery, fled with all their companions into the wilderness nearest to the city, eventually abiding at a lake called Asphar. 

When Jonathan learned that Bacchides was advancing upon them, he dispatched his brother John, who was also known as Gaddis, to the Nabatean Arabs. The purpose of this mission was to lodge his baggage with the Nabateans, who were friends of the Israelites, until the battle with Bacchides had concluded. However, the sons of Ambri set an ambush for John from the city of Medaba. They seized John and those who were with him, plundered their possessions, and slew John and all his companions.

## Ignatius and His Companions
In the early 16th century, Ignatius and his companions traveled through Spain and France. While in Salamanca, the Dominicans imprisoned the group due to doubts regarding their orthodoxy. The companions were chained foot to foot and fastened to a stake in the middle of their cell. After twenty-two days, they were sentenced; while no fault was found in their teaching or life, they were forbidden from defining sins as venial or mortal without four years of further study.

Ignatius subsequently traveled to Paris in February 1528 to continue his studies, agreeing with his companions that they should remain in Salamanca until he could determine if there were means for them all to pursue study. Later, in the autumn of 1535, Ignatius returned to Spain, leaving Faber in charge of the companions to complete their studies. During this period, Faber gained three additional adherents. After visiting Spain and the public hospice at Azpeitia, Ignatius traveled to Venice, where he waited for a year until his companions could join him.

## The Jacobite Rising of 1715
During the Jacobite rising of 1715, William Gordon, the 6th Viscount Kenmure, raised the royal standard of Scotland at Lochmaben on October 12. He was joined by approximately two hundred gentlemen, including George Seton (5th earl of Wintoun), William Maxwell (5th earl of Nithsdale), and Carnwath. 

This force later effected a junction at Rothbury with James Radclyffe (3rd earl of Derwentwater) and Thomas Forster. The earl of Derwentwater had joined Forster at Green-rig in October with about seventy followers. The combined forces, totaling some fourteen hundred men, were later reinforced at Kelso by a brigade under William Mackintosh. After crossing the English border to join the Lancashire Jacobites, the group was eventually defeated; Kenmure was taken prisoner at Preston on November 13.

## The Babington Conspiracy
In 1586, Anthony Babington, an English conspirator and former page to Mary, queen of Scots, became a leader of a plot alongside the priest John Ballard. Their objective was to murder Elizabeth and her ministers, liberate Mary, and organize a general Roman Catholic rising in England. The conspiracy was supported by Philip II of Spain, who promised an expedition once the queen was assassinated. The plot was uncovered after Babington's correspondence with Mary was intercepted by the spies of Walsingham, and Ballard was seized on August 4, subsequently betraying his comrades.
