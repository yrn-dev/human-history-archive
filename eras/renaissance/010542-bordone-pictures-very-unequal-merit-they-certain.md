# Paris Bordone

Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. He began his professional training in 1509 when he entered the *bottega* of Titian. According to Vasari, who provides nearly all the known facts regarding Bordone's life, the artist did not spend many years under Titian's tutelage and instead sought to imitate the manner of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, evidence suggests that the Giorgionesque elements found in Bordone's early works were actually derived from Titian. Bordone imitated Titian so closely that some of his paintings are still attributed to Titian today. An example of this attribution dispute is the "Baptism of Christ" in the Capitoline gallery; while Dr. Bode, Crowe, and Cavalcaselle attribute the work to Bordone, Morelli identifies it as an early work by Titian.

## Career and Commissions

Bordone's career included several significant commissions across Italy and Europe. He executed numerous important mural paintings in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, though all of these works have since perished. In 1538, he was invited to the court of Francis I in France. While there, he painted many portraits, but no trace of these remains in French collections, with the exception of two portraits at the Louvre which were acquired later.

During his return journey from France, Bordone undertook works of great importance for the Fugger palace at Augsburg, but these have also been lost.

## Artistic Style and Merit

The merit of Bordone's paintings is described as very unequal. His work is characterized by a certain nobility of style and a golden harmony of colour, both of which he derived from Titian. He is noted for his dignified character in portraiture and a realistic conception of the human figure. Conversely, his nudes are considered a little coarse in form, and the action of his figures is frequently affected and unnatural.

As a child of the Renaissance, Bordone's body of work spanned several genres, including:
* Religious pictures
* Mythological scenes
* Allegories
* Nymphs and cupids
* Subjects based on the fables of Ovid

Despite his versatility, he is considered to have excelled most as a portraitist.

## Surviving Works

While many of his murals and court portraits are lost, several of Bordone's works survive in various collections:
* **Venice Academy:** The "Fisherman and Doge," which is his principal surviving work.
* **National Gallery, London:** A portrait of a lady and "Daphnis and Chloe."
* **Bridgwater House:** A "Holy Family."
* **Tadini Collection, Lovere:** A "Madonna."
* **Other locations:** Paintings in the Duomo of Treviso, as well as two mythological pictures located in the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese.

## Historical Scholarship

Since the time of Vasari, very little has been written regarding Paris Bordone, aside from references in general works on Italian painting. In 1900, the committee of the fourth centenary of Paris Bordone in Treviso published *Della Vita e delle Opere di Paris Bordone* by G. Biscaro and L. Barlo. Additionally, P.G. Molmenti published a sixteen-page paper on the artist in the *Nuova Antologia* on November 16, 1900.
