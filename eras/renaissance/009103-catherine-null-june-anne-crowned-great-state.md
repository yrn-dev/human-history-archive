# Anne Boleyn

## Early Life and Education
Anne Boleyn (c. 1507–1536) was the daughter of Elizabeth and Sir Thomas Boleyn, who later became the earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde. Her mother was the daughter of Thomas Howard, the earl of Surrey and subsequent duke of Norfolk. While some later writers have ascribed her birth to 1501 or 1502, Camden records her birth year as 1507.

In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, she remained in France and served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I. She returned to England in either 1521 or 1522.

## Relations with Henry VIII
Upon her return to England, Anne attracted many suitors and admirers, including the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt and Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland. The king stopped a potential marriage between Anne and Percy, providing Sir James Butler as an alternative match; however, Anne remained unmarried. Between 1522 and 1525, Henry bestowed various favors and grants upon Anne's father, which some have interpreted as a sign of the king's affection.

Unlike her sister Mary, who had fallen victim to Henry's solicitations, Anne did not intend to be the king's mistress, but rather his queen, with her conduct appearing to be governed by ambition. While some surmise their relationship began as early as 1523, there is no proof that Henry's passion existed prior to the divorce proceedings initiated in May 1527. Anne was first openly linked to the king as a possible wife in a letter dated August 16, 1527, from the imperial ambassador Mendoza to Charles V. During this time, Wolsey opposed the alliance and attempted to negotiate a marriage for Henry with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII. Simultaneously, Henry sent his secretary, William Knight, to Rome to seek facilities for a marriage with Anne.

## Reign and Fall
Anne Boleyn became the queen of Henry VIII, though the archive's sources do not provide the specific date of her coronation beyond a reference to a 1533 publication regarding her "Noble Tryumphaunt Coronacyon." During her marriage, she gave birth to a child every year. However, Henry eventually became disappointed by her failure to provide a male heir.

Anne's downfall was marked by charges of incest and other misdeeds. A principal witness for the incest charge was the wife of Rochford, a woman later executed for her role in the intrigues of Catherine Howard. Anne was condemned by two grand juries, a petty jury, and a tribunal of nearly all the lay peers of England. This sentence of guilt was supported by her own uncle and father.

## Trial and Execution
Anne's guilt remains a subject of historical debate. While she protested her entire innocence to Sir William Kingston and made no confession on the scaffold, some records suggest her guilt was probable. Among her supposed lovers, only Smeaton made a full confession, though it is possible this was obtained through hopes of pardon or threats of torture. Norris also confessed but later claimed he had been betrayed into doing so. Other accused parties made general confessions on the scaffold, which were typical declarations of repentance and submission to the king's will rather than admissions of specific crimes.

On the scaffold, Anne is reported to have prayed for God to save the king and grant him a long reign, describing Henry as a "gentle and sovereign lord" and a merciful prince.
