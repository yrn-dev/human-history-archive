# Edmund Bolton

Edmund Bolton (born c. 1575; died after 1633) was an English poet and historian. According to his own accounts, he was born in 1575 and was raised as a Roman Catholic. His education took place at Trinity Hall, Cambridge, and he later resided at the Inner Temple in London.

## Court Life and Influence
Bolton served as a retainer to the duke of Buckingham. Through the influence of the duke, Bolton was able to secure a small position at the court of King James I. During his time, Bolton developed a scheme for the establishment of an English academy. While King James I viewed the project favorably, the plan ultimately fell through following the king's death.

## Literary and Historical Works
Bolton was a prolific writer whose works spanned poetry, criticism, and history. In 1600, he contributed to the publication *England's Helicon*. Among his most significant works is *Hypercritica* (published c. 1618), a short critical treatise that is considered valuable for its notices of authors contemporary to Bolton. This work was later reprinted in 1815 within Joseph Haslewood's *Ancient Critical Essays* (vol. ii.).

In 1624, he wrote *Nero Caesar, or Monarchic Depraved*, a work that included specific notes regarding British affairs. Bolton also authored a *Life of King Henry II* intended for Speed's *Chronicle*. However, this piece was substituted with a life written by Dr. John Barcham because Bolton's Catholic sympathies were evident in his treatment of Thomas Becket.

## Later Life
Records indicate that Edmund Bolton was still living in 1633, though the exact date of his death remains unknown.
