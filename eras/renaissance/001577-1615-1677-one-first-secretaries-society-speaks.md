# Nehemiah Grew

Nehemiah Grew (1641–1712) was an English physiologist and vegetable anatomist. Born in Warwickshire, he was the only son of Obadiah Grew, a Nonconformist divine and the vicar of St Michael's in Coventry.

## Education and Early Career
Grew graduated from Cambridge in 1661. Ten years later, he earned a medical degree (M.D.) from Leiden, where he produced a thesis titled *Disputatio medico-physica ... de liquore nervoso*. In 1664, Grew began conducting observations on the anatomy of plants. 

## Relationship with the Royal Society
Grew's academic contributions led to his association with the Royal Society. In 1670, Bishop Wilkins communicated Grew's essay, *The Anatomy of Vegetables begun*, to the society. Based on the recommendation of Bishop Wilkins, Grew was elected a fellow of the Royal Society in 1671. 

Following the publication of his essay in 1672, Grew settled in London and established an extensive practice as a physician. In 1673, he published *Idea of a Phytological History*, which was composed of papers he had communicated to the Royal Society during the previous year.

## Secretarial Tenure and Later Works
In 1677, Nehemiah Grew succeeded Henry Oldenburg as the secretary of the Royal Society. During his tenure, he edited the *Philosophical Transactions* between 1678 and 1679. In 1681, he published a descriptive catalogue of the rarities preserved at Gresham College at the request of others; this publication included papers he had read to the Royal Society regarding the *Comparative Anatomy of Stomachs and Guts*.

Grew's primary work, *Anatomy of Plants*, was published in 1682. This work was largely a collection of his previous publications and was organized into four books:
* *Anatomy of Vegetables begun*
* *Anatomy of Roots*
* *Anatomy of Trunks*
* *Anatomy of Leaves, Flowers, Fruits and Seeds*

This major work featured eighty-two plates and included seven appended papers, most of which were chemical in nature.

## Other Publications and Legacy
Beyond his primary work on plant anatomy, Grew authored several other texts, including:
* *Sea-water made Fresh* (1684)
* *Tractatus de salis ... usu* (1695), which he later rendered as *The Nature and Use of the Salt contained in Epsom and such other Waters* (1697)
* *Cosmologia sacra* (1701)

Nehemiah Grew died suddenly on March 25, 1712. In his honor, Linnaeus named a genus of trees *Grewia* (belonging to the natural order Tiliaceae).
