# The Scholemaster

*The Scholemaster* is a work authored by Ascham, which began in 1563. The creation of the text was prompted by events occurring during the "great plague" in London that same year. While the court was located at Windsor, Ascham dined on December 10 with other ministers and Sir William Cecil, the secretary of state. During this gathering, Cecil reported "strange news" regarding several scholars from Eaton who had run away from their school due to a fear of beating. Cecil expressed the opinion that schoolmasters should employ more discretion in correction than was commonly practiced.

This report led to a debate among those present, with the group divided between those who supported flogging and those who opposed it; Ascham acted as the champion for the anti-floggers. Following this, the treasurer, Sir Richard Sackville, informed Ascham that his own hatred of learning—which had resulted in personal loss—was caused by the brutality of a "fond schoolmaster." As Sackville now had a young son, this experience informed the discourse on the right order of teaching.

## Publication and Editorial History

*The Scholemaster* saw several reprints and editions over the following centuries. It was reprinted in 1571 and 1589. In the 18th century, it was edited by the Rev. J. Upton in 1711 and again in 1743. Later scholarly editions were produced by Prof. J.E.B. Mayor in 1863 and Prof. Edward Arber in 1870. Additionally, Dr. Giles published the work in 1864-1865 as part of a four-volume set of select letters that included Ascham's *Toxophilus* and a biography written by Edward Grant.

## About the Author

Ascham's professional life involved various diplomatic and academic roles. He served as Latin secretary to Queen Mary, an appointment made through Bishop Gardiner, which included a pension of L20 per year. During an embassy to Venice, Innsbruck, and Louvain, Ascham spent four or five days a week reading Greek with the ambassador. He noted that the university at Louvain was very inferior to Cambridge. 

Following the accession of Elizabeth, Ascham resumed his studies with Princess Elizabeth in 1555, reading the orations of Aeschines and *De Corona* by Demosthenes in Greek. On October 5, 1559, despite being a layman, he was granted the prebend and canonry of Wetwang in York minster. 

Ascham's other writings include the *Toxophilus*, which was republished in 1571, 1589, 1788, and later by Prof. Edward Arber in 1868 and 1902. His letters were first collected and published in 1576, with subsequent editions in Nuremberg in 1611 and a re-editing by William Elstob in 1703. In 1771, James Bennett edited Ascham's English works, which included a life written by Dr. Johnson; this was later reprinted in 8vo in 1815.
