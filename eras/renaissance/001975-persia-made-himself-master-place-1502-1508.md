# Persia and the Control of Bagdad

## The Safawid Conquest of Bagdad
The city of Bagdad experienced a long period of shifting control between various powers. For a century and a half, the Tatars held possession of the city until approximately A.D. 1400, when it was captured by Timur. Following this, Sultan Ahmed Ben Avis, who had sought refuge with the Greek emperor, managed to repossess the city. However, he was eventually expelled in 1417 by Kara Yusuf of the Kara-Kuyunli ("Black Sheep") Mongols.

By approximately 1468, the descendants of the Kara-Kuyunli were driven out by Cassim (also known as Uzun Hasan) of the Ak-Kuyunli ("White Sheep") Mongols. The Ak-Kuyunli and their descendants maintained their reign in Bagdad until the arrival of Shah Ismail I. As the founder of the Safawid royal house of Persia, Shah Ismail I made himself master of the city around 1502 or 1508.

## Turkish-Persian Contention
Following the Safawid conquest, Bagdad became a long-term object of contention between the Persians and the Turks. The city was captured by Suleiman I the Magnificent and subsequently retaken by Shah Abbas the Great in 1620. 

The conflict continued into the following decades. In 1638, Sultan Murad IV besieged the city with an army consisting of 300,000 men. Despite an obstinate resistance from the defenders, the city was forced to surrender. In defiance of the terms of the capitulation, most of the inhabitants of Bagdad were massacred.

## Early Persian History
In earlier historical records, the relationship between the Medes and Persians is noted through royal lineages. Cambyses, the son of Achemenes, who was the king of Persia, married Mandane. Mandane was the daughter of Astyages (referred to in Scripture as Ahasuerus), who reigned for thirty-five years. From the marriage of Cambyses and Mandane, Cyrus was born. Cyrus was born one year after his uncle, Cyaxares (referred to in Scripture as Darius the Mede), who succeeded Astyages in the kingdom of the Medes.
