# The University of Prague

The universities at Prague serve as the primary educational establishments in Bohemia, a region noted for having the lowest proportion of illiterates among the Austrian provinces. In the modern era, these establishments consist of two separate universities: one German and one Czech. The rectors of these two universities hold *ex officio* positions within the local diet, which is composed of 242 members.

## Foundation and Early Influence
The university of Prague was founded in 1348 by Charles IV. The establishment of this institution served as a stimulus for educational development in neighboring regions; for example, Casimir of Poland was inspired by Charles IV's example to establish and endow the first university of Cracow on May 12, 1364.

## Religious Conflict and the Hussite Era
During the later reign of Wenceslas, Bohemia experienced incipient religious conflict. The influence of the Church of Rome had been weakened by pious priests, including Waldhauser and Milic, who attacked the immorality of the clergy. This alienation was further exacerbated by the church schism, in which rival pontiffs engaged in medieval theological strife.

As a result, much of the Bohemian nation supported Huss. While King Wenceslas was characterized by irresolution during these disputes, Queen Sophia openly favored Huss, serving as his confessor for a period. Huss eventually proceeded to the council of Constance under a letter of safe conduct provided by Sigismund, the king of the Romans and brother of Wenceslas. However, he was declared a heretic and executed by burning on July 6, 1415.

The execution of Huss triggered a civil war in Bohemia. On September 2, 1415, the nobles of Moravia and Bohemia met in Prague and issued the *Protestatio Bohemorum*. In this document, they defended Huss as a "good, just and catholic man" who had been convicted of no offense. They further labeled anyone claiming that heresy existed in Bohemia as "liars, vile traitors and calumniators," and "sons of the devil." The nobles declared their intent to defend the law of Jesus Christ and its preachers regardless of human decrees or the cost of their own blood. This protest acted as a declaration of war against the Roman church and initiated the Hussite wars.

## Later Political and Academic Developments
In the 19th century, the academic landscape of Prague continued to evolve with the foundation of the national university in 1882, which coincided with a significant development in Bohemian literature. 

During the late 19th century, the university was a site of linguistic and nationalist tension. Ernst Mach, a physicist and psychologist who served as a professor of physics at Prague from 1867 to 1895, acted as *Rector Magnificus* in 1789 and 1880. In this capacity, Mach fought against the introduction of the Czech language in place of German at the Prague University.
