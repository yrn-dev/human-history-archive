# Bolton Abbey

## Overview and Location
Bolton Abbey is a village situated in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is located 5-1/2 miles from Ilkley via the Midland railway and 22 miles northwest of Leeds. The village derives its name from the foundation of Bolton Priory, though this naming is considered inaccurate. The ruins of the priory are noted for being among the most exquisitely situated in England, positioned near the right bank of the upper Wharfe in a valley characterized by hills and beautiful woodland.

## Architectural History
The ruins of the priory exhibit various architectural styles. The earliest section of the church dates to the transitional Norman period. The nave is described as perfect and is constructed in the Early English and Decorated styles. Other portions of the site, specifically the choir and transepts, are in ruins, and the remaining domestic buildings are slight.

## Early Ownership and Foundation
The manor of Bolton Abbey, along with the rest of the district of Craven, was granted to Robert de Romili by William the Conqueror. While the manor is not mentioned in the Domesday survey, Robert de Romili evidently held it in 1086. 

In 1120, Cicely de Romili (the heiress of Robert) and her husband, William de Meschines, founded and endowed a priory at Emmesay (or Embsay) near Skipton. This priory was later moved to the current site in 1151 by their daughter, Alice de Romili. Alice, the wife of William FitzDuncan, gave the manor to the monks in exchange for other lands.

## Post-Dissolution Ownership
Following the dissolution of the monasteries, the manor was sold in 1542 to Henry Clifford, the 2nd earl of Cumberland. The manor subsequently passed to his descendants, the dukes of Devonshire, who hold it currently.
