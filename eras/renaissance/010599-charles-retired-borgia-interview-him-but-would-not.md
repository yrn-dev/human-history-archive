# The Borgia Family and Associated Historical Figures

The name Borgia is associated with several prominent figures in European history, most notably during the Italian Renaissance and the subsequent era of the Catholic Reformation. These individuals ranged from powerful political adventurers and popes to recognized saints.

## Cesare Borgia
Cesare Borgia (1476–1507), the Duke of Valentinois and Romagna, was the son of Pope Alexander VI (Rodrigo Borgia) and Vanozza dei Cattanei. Born in Rome, Cesare was his father's favorite son and the primary beneficiary of the Pope's extensive nepotism. After being created archbishop of Valencia and later a cardinal, Cesare eventually exchanged his ecclesiastical dignities for a secular career.

Cesare was characterized as a clever and unscrupulous adventurer. His methods of conquest were described as treacherous and ferocious, though he was noted for governing his subjects with justice and firmness once control was established. His rise to power was heavily dependent on the support of the papacy; following the death of his father, his career ended and his system collapsed.

Cesare's life was marked by violence. He is believed to have murdered his elder brother, Giovanni, the Duke of Gandia, in 1497—possibly due to rivalry over the affection of Donna Sancha or a desire for his father's sole favor. He also murdered the husband of his sister, Lucrezia, and betrayed numerous other people. Despite his lack of military ability, he possessed significant administrative power and dexterity.

## Influence on Machiavelli
Cesare Borgia served as a primary inspiration for Niccolò Machiavelli. In October 1502, Machiavelli was sent as an envoy to Cesare's camp in Romagna. During this time, Machiavelli observed Cesare's intricate intrigues, including the murder of disaffected captains. 

Machiavelli developed a strong admiration for Cesare's combination of diplomatic prudence, audacity, and the adroit use of fraud and cruelty. In his work *The Prince*, Machiavelli idealized Cesare as a "radiant creature" and a model of the perfect prince, praising his self-reliance and his ability to cement a new state from scattered elements.

## Francis Borgia
Francis Borgia (1510–1572) represents a different trajectory for the family name. Born in Gandia, Valencia, he was noted for his piety from a young age. In 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. Francis distinguished himself there, marrying Eleanor de Castro and being appointed master of the horse to the empress. He accompanied Charles V on an African expedition in 1535 and into Provence in 1536.

The death of the empress in 1539 strengthened Francis's resolve to embrace a monastic life. Despite this, he was appointed viceroy of Catalonia and commander of the order of St James. He served as viceroy with energy and efficiency until 1543, when he succeeded his father as the duke of Gandia and resigned his official duties.

Francis became a member of the recently founded Society of Jesus (Jesuits) after the death of his wife in 1546. Due to political and family circumstances, a papal dispensation allowed him to keep his worldly possessions for four years for the sake of his children. He eventually resigned his rank and estate, later serving as the general of the order of Jesuits. He is recognized as a Roman Catholic saint and authored works including a treatise titled *Rhetorica Concionandi* and a series of *Exercises*.

## Related Figures and Context
The Borgia family's prominence was centered around Pope Alexander VI (Rodrigo Borgia), who served as Pope until 1503. His pontificate was characterized by a mood of moral relaxation, during which he lived openly with an unmarried wife and used the resources of Christendom to advance his family.

In the broader historical context of the era, other figures such as Charles V (born 1500) rose to power, becoming Emperor in 1520 and being crowned by the Pope in 1530. Additionally, the archives mention a different Charles, known as "the Bold" (born 1433), who sought to reunite Burgundy with its northern possessions and attempted to obtain the royal title through negotiations with Emperor Frederick III.
