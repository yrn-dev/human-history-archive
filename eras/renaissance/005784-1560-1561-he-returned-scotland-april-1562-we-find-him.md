# George Buchanan

George Buchanan was a scholar and administrator who served in various royal and academic capacities in Scotland during the late 16th century. He is noted for his roles as a tutor to royalty, a college principal, and a high-ranking official in the Scottish government.

## Royal Tutelage and Academic Career
In 1560 or 1561, Buchanan returned to Scotland. By April 1562, he was installed as the tutor to the young Queen Mary, with whom he read Livy on a daily basis. During this period, Buchanan openly joined the Reformed Church, also known as the Protestant Church. 

His academic and financial standing grew steadily; in 1564, he received a valuable gift from the queen consisting of the revenues of Crossraguel Abbey. In 1566, the earl of Murray appointed him as the principal of St Leonard's College, St Andrews.

## Political and Religious Leadership
Buchanan's reputation for administrative capacity and learning led to his involvement in the governance of the Reformed Church. He began attending the general assemblies in 1563, and by 1567, despite being a layman, he was appointed moderator of the general assembly.

He maintained a close association with the regent Murray, accompanying him into England. During this time, his work titled *Detectio*, which was published in 1572, was presented to the commissioners at Westminster.

## Service to James VI and State Offices
Following the assassination of Murray in 1570, Buchanan was appointed as one of the preceptors for the young King James VI. The scholarship acquired by James VI is attributed to Buchanan's tuition.

While serving as the royal tutor, Buchanan held several other significant positions of state:
* **Director of Chancery:** He held this office for a short duration.
* **Lord Privy Seal:** In this role, he was entitled to a seat in the parliament. 

Records indicate that he continued to hold the office of lord privy seal for several years, remaining in the post at least until 1579.

## Death
George Buchanan died on September 28, 1582.
