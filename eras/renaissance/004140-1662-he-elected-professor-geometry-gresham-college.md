# Gresham College

Gresham College is an educational institution in London founded by Sir Thomas Gresham. Sir Thomas Gresham (1519–1579) was a London merchant and the founder of the Royal Exchange. Descended from a Norfolk family, he was the son of Sir Richard Gresham, a former lord mayor and agent for Henry VIII. Sir Thomas served an eight-year apprenticeship and became a member of the Mercers' Company in 1543. He spent significant time in the Low Countries, particularly Antwerp, where he acted as an agent for Henry VIII and provided financial advice to the English government. His methods for raising the value of the pound sterling on the Antwerp bourse were successful enough to discharge nearly all of King Edward's debts.

## Professors of Geometry

The college has hosted several notable professors of geometry over the centuries. Henry Briggs (1556–1630), an English mathematician born in Yorkshire and a graduate of St John's College, Cambridge, became the first professor of geometry in Gresham House (later Gresham College) in 1596. During his tenure, Briggs proposed changing the scale of logarithms from the hyperbolic form used by John Napier to a system where unity is the logarithm of the ratio of ten to one. Following conferences with Napier in Edinburgh in 1616 and 1617, this change was agreed upon, and Briggs published the first chiliad of his logarithms in 1617. He resigned his post at Gresham College on July 25, 1620, after being appointed Savilian professor of geometry at Oxford.

In July 1662, the professorship of geometry was filled by Barrow, who was elected on the recommendation of Dr John Wilkins, the bishop of Chester and master of Trinity College. Barrow held this position until May 20, 1664, when he resigned to become the first professor in a mathematical chair at Cambridge founded by the will of Henry Lucas. Barrow later resigned that mathematical chair in 1669 to his pupil, Isaac Newton, as he chose to pursue the study of divinity.

Robert Hooke (1635–1703), an English experimental philosopher and curator of experiments for the Royal Society, was nominated as professor of geometry in Gresham College in 1665. Hooke subsequently resided at the college.

## Other Professorships and Faculty

Beyond geometry, the college appointed professors in other disciplines. John Bull (c. 1562–1628), an English composer and organist, was appointed music professor at Gresham College in 1596. Because the foundation rules of the college required lectures to be delivered in Latin—a requirement Bull could not meet—the executors of Sir Thomas Gresham granted him a dispensation to lecture in English. Bull delivered his first lecture on October 6, 1597, and remained in the position until 1607, when he resigned.

## Historical Records

Information regarding the history of Gresham College, the Royal Exchange, and Sir Thomas Gresham is preserved in several accounts. These include *Worthies* by Fuller and *Gresham Professors* by Ward. The most comprehensive account is provided by J. M. Burgon in the two-volume work *Life and Times of Sir Thomas Gresham* (1839). Other available records include a *Brief Memoir of Sir Thomas Gresham* (1833) and *The Life of Sir Thomas Gresham, Founder of the Royal Exchange* (1845).
