# Caterina Sforza

Caterina Sforza was an Italian noblewoman and the widow of Girolamo Riario, the lord of Forli. Her history is closely tied to the territories of Forli and Imola and the political ambitions of the Borgia family.

## Territorial Holdings and Imola
In 1480, the town of Imola—an ancient station on the Via Aemilia known as Forum Cornelii—came into the possession of Girolamo Riario. The town was acquired as the dowry of his wife, Caterina Sforza. Imola had a long history, having been mentioned by Prudentius in the 5th century A.D. and later serving as a location where Martial published his third book of epigrams. During the Lombard period, the town was referred to as Imolas.

## Conflict with Cesare Borgia
At the end of the 15th century, Pope Alexander VI sought to carve out a principality for his son, Cesare Borgia, in the Romagna region. This plan involved subduing local despots in territories that owed nominal allegiance to the pope. To achieve this, Cesare established his headquarters at Cesena and commanded an army comprising 4,000 Gascons and Swiss, 300 French lances, and various Italian troops.

After Cesare's forces attacked and immediately secured the surrender of Imola, he laid siege to Forli. The city was held by Caterina Sforza. During the siege, Sforza held out gallantly against the Borgia forces. However, she was eventually forced to surrender on January 22, 1500.

## Later Life and Legacy
Following her surrender, Cesare Borgia treated Caterina Sforza with consideration. She spent the remainder of her days in a convent. In 1500, the territories she had held were incorporated into the States of the Church by Caesar Borgia.
