# George Buchanan

George Buchanan is recognized as one of the most distinguished scholars of Scotland, noted particularly for his exceptional mastery of the Latin language. His command of Latin was so complete that he wrote in the language as if it were his mother tongue, employing a style characterized by elasticity and freshness rather than rigid adherence to any single classical author. Beyond his linguistic skill, Buchanan possessed significant originality of thought and a rich vein of poetical feeling.

## Literary Works and Translations
Buchanan's body of work spans poetry, drama, and prose. His translations of the Greek plays and the Psalms are regarded as more than simple versions. In the realm of drama, his two tragedies, *Jephthes* and *Baptistes*, earned a lasting European reputation for their academic excellence. His smaller satirical poems are noted for their wit and phrasing.

His other poetic contributions include:
* *Fratres Fraterrimi*
* *Elegiae*
* *Silvae*
* Two sets of verses titled *Iambon Liber* and *Hendecasyllabon Liber*
* Three books of *Epigrammata*
* A book of miscellaneous verse
* *De Sphaera* (in five books), which was based on a poem by Joannes de Sacrobosco and written to defend the Ptolemaic theory against the Copernican view.

In prose, Buchanan authored *Chamaeleon*, a vernacular satire directed at Maitland of Lethington, which was first printed in 1711. He also wrote a Latin translation of Linacre's Grammar (1533), *Libettus de Prosodia* (1640), and *Vita ab ipso scripta biennio ante mortem* (1608).

## Editions and Biographies
There are two primary editions of Buchanan's works: a two-volume folio edited by Ruddiman in 1715, and a 1725 quarto edition edited by Burman. His *Vernacular Writings*—which include two letters, a tract on the Reformation of St Andrews University, *Ane Admonitioun to the Trew Lordis*, and the *Chamaeleon*—were edited for the Scottish Text Society by P. Hume Brown.

Several biographies have been produced, including:
* *Memoirs of the Life and Writings of George Buchanan* by David Irving (1807 and 1817).
* *George Buchanan, Humanist and Reformer* (1890) and *George Buchanan and his Times* (1906), both by P. Hume Brown.
* *George Buchanan, a Biography* (1906) by Rev. D. Macmillan.

## Commemorations and Legal Records
In 1906, Buchanan's quatercentenary was celebrated across various centers in Scotland. This occasion led to several studies and encomia, most notably *George Buchanan: Glasgow Quatercentenary Studies* (1906) and *George Buchanan, a Memoir* (1907), edited by D.A. Millar. The *Glasgow Quatercentenary Studies* also contains a discussion regarding the authorship of a 1642 verse translation of *Baptistes* titled *Tyrannicall-Government Anatomized*, which some have attributed to Milton.

Additionally, the Portuguese historian G.J.C. Henriques discovered and published the records of Buchanan's trial. These were released in 1906 under the title *George Buchanan in the Lisbon Inquisition. The Records of his Trial, with a Translation thereof into English, Facsimiles of some of the Papers, and an Introduction*.
