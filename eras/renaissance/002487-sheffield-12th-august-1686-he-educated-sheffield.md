# Sheffield

Sheffield is an English town and manufacturing center characterized by its industrial contributions, particularly in the production of steel and scissors, and its role as a hub for various professional and political figures.

## Industry and Geography
Sheffield is noted for its steel industry, where steam power is utilized to roll out bars of steel and turn grindstones. Unlike other industrial centers such as Leeds, where machinery is described as the "intelligent agent" and men as its slaves, the industrial aspect of Sheffield is characterized by the skill and accuracy of the human hand. The town's show-rooms have historically displayed items such as "Persian" scissors from firms founded by the Rodgers family.

The region surrounding Sheffield is home to ganister, a slightly plastic siliceous sand found in the neighborhood of the town. This material is used for the lining of Bessemer steel converters.

## Notable Residents and Figures
Several prominent individuals were born or active in Sheffield:

*   **John Balguy (1686–1748):** An English philosopher and divine born in Sheffield on August 12, 1686. He was educated at the Sheffield grammar school and St John's College, Cambridge. Balguy began his authorship under the pseudonym "Silvius" during the "Bangorian controversy," supporting Bishop Hoadley. His later works, including *The Foundation of Moral Goodness* and an essay on *Divine Rectitude*, focused on the nature of virtue and the moral perfections of the Deity.
*   **Samuel Bailey (1791–1870):** A British author and philosopher born in Sheffield in 1791. Bailey was a merchant who established trade connections in the United States and served as the chairman of the Sheffield Banking Company. A "philosophic radical" and ardent liberal, he stood for Sheffield in political elections without success. He authored numerous works on political economy and reasoning, including *Essays on the Formation and Publication of Opinions* (1821). Upon his death, he left over £80,000 to the town of Sheffield.
*   **James Silk Buckingham (1786–1855):** An English traveller and author who served as the member of parliament for Sheffield from 1832 to 1837, during which time he strongly advocated for social reform.

## Cultural and Religious History
Sheffield has served as a location for various cultural and religious activities. In 1792, the actor Charles Kemble made his first recorded appearance in the town, performing the role of Orlando in *As You Like It*. In the realm of religion, the English Wesleyan divine Jabez Bunting ministered in Sheffield as part of a career that spanned over fifty-seven years across several English cities.

## Commerce and Transport
Sheffield is a major destination for the distribution of agricultural goods; for example, Kentish fruit-growers send quantities of their produce to the city. In terms of transport, the town is connected via the Midland and Great Central railways, as well as the Midland & Sheffield railway, which also serves nearby areas such as Marple.
