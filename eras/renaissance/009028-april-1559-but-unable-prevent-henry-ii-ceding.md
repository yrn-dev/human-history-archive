# April 1559

The month of April 1559 was marked by significant diplomatic activity in France and territorial expansions in Portuguese India.

## The Treaty of Cateau-Cambresis
In April 1559, negotiations took place that resulted in the treaty of Cateau-Cambresis. Francois de Boivin, the Baron de Villars, was a participant in these negotiations. Boivin had previously served as a secretary to Charles, Marshal Brissac, and had accompanied him to Piedmont in 1550 during the war with Spain. Following the French defeat at St Quentin in 1557, Boivin had been dispatched to ensure that King Henry II of France maintained the support of Brissac. Despite his involvement in the April 1559 negotiations, Boivin was unable to stop Henry II from ceding the conquests that had been made by Brissac.

## Portuguese Conquests in India
During 1559, the Portuguese expanded their holdings in India. The territory of Damaun proper was conquered by the Portuguese in this year. This followed earlier activity in the region; the town of Damaun had been sacked and burnt by the Portuguese in 1531, and later, in 1558, it was taken again by the Portuguese, who established a permanent settlement and converted the local mosque into a Christian church.
