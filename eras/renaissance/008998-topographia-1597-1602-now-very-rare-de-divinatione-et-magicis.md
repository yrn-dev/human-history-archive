# Romanae Urbis Topographia and De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis

The works *Romanae Urbis Topographia* and *De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis* are publications authored by Jean Jacques Boissard, a French Latin poet and antiquary.

## Author Biography
Jean Jacques Boissard (1528–1602) was born in Besancon. His early education took place at Louvain; however, he secretly departed from that seminary due to the severity of his master. Following this, he traveled through a significant portion of Germany before arriving in Italy. Boissard remained in Italy for several years, during which time he frequently experienced great straits.

It was during his residence in Italy that Boissard developed a passion for antiquities. He established a collection consisting of the most curious monuments from the vicinity of Rome and the city itself. He later attempted to travel through Greece, visiting the islands of the Archipelago, but a severe illness forced him to return to Rome. After completing his collection, he returned to his native country. Because he was not permitted to publicly profess the Protestant religion, which he had embraced, he withdrew to Metz. Boissard died in Metz on October 30, 1602.

## Romanae Urbis Topographia
*Romanae Urbis Topographia* was produced between 1597 and 1602. The archive notes that this specific work is now very rare.

## De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis
The work titled *De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis* was published in 1605.
