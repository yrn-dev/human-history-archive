# English Garner (Elizabethan Sonnets)

The *English Garner* is a literary collection that has included works of Elizabethan poetry. A specific edition titled *Elizabethan Sonnets*, edited by S. Lee and published in 1904, serves as a reference for the study of the sonnet form during the Elizabethan era.

## Inclusion of Barnabe Barnes
The *English Garner* has been used to preserve the works of Barnabe Barnes (1569?–1609), the fourth son of Dr. Richard Barnes, the bishop of Durham. Barnes was an English poet who attended Brasenose College, Oxford, in 1586 and later traveled to France in 1591 with the earl of Essex. 

His work *Parthenophil and Parthenophe, Sonnettes, Madrigals, Elegies and Odes* was included in volume v. of *An English Garner* by Professor E. Arber. This specific work, *Parthenophil*, was dedicated to William Percy and is characterized by passages of beauty and freshness, though some of its elaborate conceits are considered strained or over-ingenious. The work is largely indebted to contemporary French sonneteers and borrows its title from the Neapolitan Latin writer Hieronymus Angerianus. While a story can be outlined from these love lyrics, the incidents are slight, reflecting the typical Elizabethan sonnet-cycle where it is difficult to distinguish between intellectual exercises in the imitation of Petrarch and real personal experience.

## Context of Elizabethan Sonnets
The 1904 edition of *Elizabethan Sonnets* edited by S. Lee is part of a broader scholarly interest in the period's poetry. Other significant contributors to the study of Elizabethan sonnets and lyrics include J. Erskine, who wrote *The Elizabethan Lyric* (1903), and M.F. Crow, who reprinted Fulke Greville's *Caelica* (a collection of 110 sonnets) in *Elizabethan Sonnet Cycles* (1898).

The Elizabethan era saw various approaches to the sonnet. For example, Samuel Daniel's sonnets are noted for their grace and tenderness, though they depart from the Italian form by closing with a couplet, a practice also seen in the sonnets of Wyat and Surrey. Additionally, the poet Barnfield published *Cynthia, with certain Sonnets* in January 1595. This volume contains a sequence of twenty sonnets that celebrate the charms of a young man (identified by initials J. U. or J. V.). These sonnets are noted for being closer in manner to those of Shakespeare than any other Elizabethan works, despite preceding Shakespeare's publication by fourteen years. Barnfield's work also shows the influence of Michael Drayton's *Idea*, which also contained amatory sonnets addressed to a young man.
