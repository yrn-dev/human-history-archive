# Schmalkalden and the Schmalkaldic League

The league of Schmalkalden, also referred to as the Schmalkaldic league, was a political and religious alliance involving various German figures and territories during the period of the Reformation.

## Membership and Participation
The league included several German rulers and entities. Barnim XI, the duke of Pomerania (who ruled from 1523 and later shared the duchy with his nephew Philip I), adopted the doctrines of Martin Luther and joined the league of Schmalkalden. However, Barnim XI did not participate in the subsequent war associated with the league. 

Due to this neutral stance, Barnim XI found himself without supporters, which eventually forced him to submit to the emperor Charles V. As a result of this submission, he was required to pay a heavy fine and accept the *Interim* issued from Augsburg in May 1548.

## Impact on Upper Lusatia
The conflict involving the league of Schmalkalden had significant repercussions for the district of Upper Lusatia. During the 14th century, Upper Lusatia had become known as the district of the six towns (*Sechsstädtelandes*), consisting of Bautzen, Görlitz, Zittau, Löbau, Lauban, and Kamenz. These "six towns" were severely punished for their involvement in the war of the league of Schmalkalden.

Around the time of these events, reformed teaching progressed rapidly throughout Lusatia, leading the majority of the inhabitants to become Protestants.

## Related Figures in Saxony
The archives mention several electors of Saxony during the broader era of religious and political upheaval in the region:

*   **John Frederick I**: The elector of Saxony who, in the case of the merchant Hans Kohlhase, set a price upon the merchant's head after Kohlhase launched a feud against the whole of Saxony.
*   **John George I**: The elector of Saxony who, early in the Thirty Years' War in 1620, conquered both Upper and Lower Lusatia. He was permitted to keep these territories as payment for his assistance to the emperor Ferdinand I.
*   **Frederick of Saxony**: An earlier elector who prohibited the Dominican monk John Tetzel from entering his territories to prevent the sale of Indulgences.

## Legal and Territorial Transitions
The territories surrounding these conflicts often shifted between powers. Upper Lusatia, for instance, passed from the margrave of Brandenburg in 1253 to Bohemia, and later to the German king Ferdinand I via the kingdoms of Hungary and Bohemia. By the treaty of Prague in 1635, the two Lusatias were definitely transferred from Bohemia to Saxony, though the emperor (as king of Bohemia) maintained a certain supremacy to protect the rights and privileges of Roman Catholics.
