# Coronacyon of Queen Anne

The *Noble Tryumphaunt Coronacyon of Queen Anne* is a historical document dating from 1533, with a reproduction published in 1884. It serves as a record of the coronation of Anne Boleyn, who became the queen of Henry VIII of England.

## Historical Context of Queen Anne
Anne Boleyn (c. 1507–1536) was the daughter of Elizabeth, daughter of Thomas Howard (earl of Surrey and later duke of Norfolk), and Sir Thomas Boleyn, who later became the earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde. Her early life included time in France, where she accompanied Mary Tudor in 1514 for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Anne remained in France following the king's death, serving as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I.

Upon her return to England in 1521 or 1522, Anne attracted numerous suitors and admirers, including the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt and Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland. Although a match with Sir James Butler was provided for her, Anne remained unmarried.

## Ascent to the Throne
Anne's relationship with King Henry VIII is a subject of historical debate. While some suggest the relationship began as early as 1523, there is no proof that the king's passion preceded the divorce proceedings initiated in May 1527. Anne's name was first openly linked to the king as a potential wife in a letter dated August 16, 1527, sent by Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, to Charles V.

Unlike her sister Mary, Anne did not intend to be the king's mistress; she sought to be his queen, and her actions were seemingly driven by ambition. This alliance was opposed by Wolsey, who instead negotiated a potential marriage between Henry and Renee, the daughter of Louis XII.
