# Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour

## Early Life and Succession
Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour, was born on July 21, 1607. He was the son of Thomas, the 2nd baron, and Blanche, who was the daughter of Edward, earl of Worcester. Upon the death of his father in 1643, Henry succeeded to the family estates and the title.

## Civil War and Royalist Activities
A Roman Catholic and strong royalist, Arundell dedicated himself to supporting the king's cause. He distinguished himself in 1644 by recapturing his castle at Wardour from parliamentarian forces. The parliamentarians had previously seized the castle the year prior, despite a brave defense led by Arundell's mother. 

Following these conflicts, Arundell was listed in 1648 as one of the delinquents who were exempted from pardon in proposals sent to Charles in the Isle of Wight. While his estates were confiscated, he was permitted around 1653 to compound for them for the sum of £35,000.

## Legal Troubles and the Interregnum
In 1652, Arundell was arrested after acting as a second in a duel that resulted in the death of one of the combatants. He was tried in 1653, during which he attempted to plead his peerage. However, this privilege was disallowed because the House of Lords had been abolished.

## Restoration and Diplomatic Service
Following the Restoration, Arundell regained possession of his family estates. In 1663, he was appointed master of the horse to Henrietta Maria. Due to his position, he was one of the few individuals trusted by the king regarding alliances with France and projects to restore the Roman Catholic religion.

Arundell's diplomatic involvement increased in 1669 when he participated in a secret council assembled by Charles II. In October of that year, he was sent to France. While the official reason for the trip was the funeral of Henrietta Maria, his actual purpose was to negotiate with Louis XIV. These negotiations resulted in the treaties of Dover in 1670. Later, in 1676, he was privy to negotiations between James and Rome conducted through Coleman.

## The Popish Plot and Imprisonment
In 1678, Titus Oates accused Arundell of participating in the "popish plot." Consequently, he was one of five Roman Catholic peers arrested and imprisoned in the Tower in October. He was impeached by parliament and found guilty of high treason by the Middlesex grand jury. 

While Lord Stafford was executed in December 1680, the proceedings against the other peers were eventually interrupted. Arundell was one of the three surviving peers released on bail on February 12, 1684. Following the accession of James II on May 22, 1685, the charges against him were annulled.
