# Robert Baillie

## Early Life and Education
Robert Baillie (1602–1662) was a Scottish divine born in Glasgow. He pursued his higher education in his hometown, graduating from the university in 1620. Following his graduation, Baillie dedicated himself to the study of divinity.

## Ecclesiastical and Academic Career
After receiving his ordination, Baillie served for several years as a regent at the university. In 1631, he was appointed to the living of Kilwinning, located in Ayrshire. 

Baillie's involvement in church and state affairs grew during the mid-17th century. In 1638, he served as a member of the famous Glasgow Assembly. Shortly after this period, he acted as a preacher or chaplain, accompanying the Scottish army and Leslie. 

His academic career progressed in 1642 when he was appointed professor of divinity at Glasgow. The following year, he was chosen as one of five Scottish clergymen sent to the Westminster Assembly.

## Political Involvement and Later Years
In 1649, Baillie served as one of the commissioners dispatched to Holland. The purpose of this mission was to settle the terms of admission to the government and invite Charles II to Scotland. Throughout his career, Baillie remained active in the various minor disputes affecting the church.

In 1661, he reached a high administrative position when he was appointed principal of Glasgow University. Robert Baillie died in August of the following year. It is noted that his death was likely hastened by his mortification regarding the apparently firm establishment of episcopacy in Scotland.

## Legacy and Character
Baillie is described as a man of ability and learning whose views were not extreme. While he played a secondary role in the major stirring events of his era, he is chiefly remembered today for his *Letters*.
