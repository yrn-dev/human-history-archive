# Ulrich Boner

## Biography
Ulrich Boner (also referred to as Bonerius) was a German-Swiss writer of fables who lived during the 14th century. Born in Bern, he was a member of an old Bernese family. Records mention him between the years 1324 and 1349, though no records of him exist before or after this period.

Regarding his religious and social status, Boner is believed to have taken clerical orders and become a monk. However, because he subsequently married, it is certain that he only received the "tonsure." This status allowed him the benefit of the *clerici uxoriati*, a designation that permitted those who divested themselves of clerical garb to return to secular life.

## Literary Work
Boner wrote a collection of fables in Middle High German titled *Der Edelstein*, completed around 1349. The collection consists of one hundred fables. In composing this work, Boner based his material principally on the *Anonymus* (edited by I. Nevelet in 1610) and the works of Avianus, who wrote in the 4th century.

Boner is noted for treating his sources with originality and considerable freedom. His writing is characterized by a clear and simple style. While the collection maintains a necessarily didactic tone, Boner relieved this with touches of humour.

## Dedication and Publication
Ulrich Boner dedicated *Der Edelstein* to Johann von Rinkenberg, who was a poet and Bernese patrician, as well as the *advocatus* (*Vogt*) of Brienz. Johann von Rinkenberg died circa 1350.

The work was printed in Bamberg in 1461. It is claimed that this publication was the first book printed in the German language.
