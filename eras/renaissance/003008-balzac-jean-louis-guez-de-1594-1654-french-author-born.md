# BALZAC, JEAN LOUIS GUEZ DE (1594-1654)

## Early Life and Education
Jean Louis Guez de Balzac was a French author born in Angouleme in 1594. At eighteen years of age, he traveled to Holland in the company of Theophile de Viaud, a relationship that later resulted in bitter recriminations between the two men.

## Courtly Influence and Reputation
Balzac was befriended early in his life by the duc d'Epernon and his son, Louis, Cardinal de la Valette. It was the Cardinal who took Balzac to Rome. During this period, Balzac developed a significant reputation through letters written to his acquaintances and to various individuals holding high positions at the French court. This success brought him many compliments and made him an habitue of the Hotel de Rambouillet. While the success appeared to turn his head slightly, he also received lavish promises and praise from Richelieu, although Richelieu never provided the preferment that Balzac expected.

## Literary Career and Works
Balzac's fame is primarily based on his *Lettres*. A first collection of these letters was published in 1624 and was received with great favor; a second collection followed in 1636, and the *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* was printed in 1637. While the matter of these letters is described as affected and empty, they are noted for a real mastery of style. Balzac is credited with introducing precision and clearness into French prose and encouraging the development of the language on national lines by emphasizing its most idiomatic elements. This work in prose is considered a reform parallel to the work of Malherbe in verse.

In addition to his letters, Balzac authored several longer works and dissertations:
* *Le Prince* (1631), an eulogy of Louis XIII.
* *Socrate chretien* (1652), which is regarded as the best of his longer works.
* *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).
* Various dissertations focusing on style.

## Later Years and Death
Balzac eventually retired to the chateau of Balzac. From this residence, he maintained correspondence with figures such as Valentin Conrart and Jean Chapelain. In 1634, he was elected to the Academy. He died in Angouleme on February 18, 1654.
