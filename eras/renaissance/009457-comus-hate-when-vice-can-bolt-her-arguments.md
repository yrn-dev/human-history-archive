# Comus, "I hate when vice can bolt her arguments"

The phrase "I hate when vice can bolt her arguments" appears in the work *Comus* by Milton. In this specific context, the use of the word "bolt" refers to the act of mooting or discussing.

## Etymology and Linguistic Development of "Bolt"
The word "bolt" originates from Old English and is comparable to the German word *Bolz*, meaning an arrow. Historically, the term referred to a cross-bow shaft or a "quarrel," as well as a pin used to fasten a door. The verb "to bolt" was derived from the swift flight of an arrow, which led to its application in describing a horse's movement or the expression "bolt upright," meaning straight upright. In American usage, "bolt" has also been used to describe the act of refusing to support a candidate nominated by one's own party.

The sense of a straight pin for fastening expanded to include various types of appliances. From the concept of "fastening together," the term was applied to a definite length of fabric in a roll, such as 40 feet of canvas.

## Sifting and Discussion
A separate linguistic root for "bolt" (or "boult") comes from the Old French *buleter*, which originates from the Medieval Latin *buretare* or *buletare*, meaning to sift. This meaning is evidenced in Shakespeare's *Winter's Tale*, which mentions "fann'd snow" that is "bolted by the northern blasts twice o'er." Similarly, Burke used the term figuratively, stating that a committee report was "examined and sifted and bolted to the bran." It is from this specific sense of sifting or examining that the meaning "to moot" or "discuss" was derived, as seen in Milton's *Comus*.

### Mechanical and Industrial Uses of Bolts
In engineering and machinery, bolts serve as critical fasteners:
*   **Boilers:** In stationary, marine, and locomotive boilers, bolts are used to secure doors. In cases where doors are fitted outside a boiler, several bolts are used so that the failure of one will not allow the door to be forced off by steam pressure.
*   **Firearms:** In the Nordenfeldt machine gun, bolts are part of a box containing locks, strikers, and spiral springs. These bolts push cartridges into the barrel. In other gun mechanisms, the bolt may push back a hammer or lock the breech.
