# Violent Quarrels and Marital Dissensions in History

The historical record contains numerous instances of violent quarrels and deep dissensions arising from marital arrangements, familial disputes, and political alliances. These conflicts have ranged from private domestic disputes to catalysts for international warfare.

## Domestic and Familial Conflicts

In the English court, a significant dispute arose involving Bacon and the marriage of his daughter. Bacon sought to reconcile himself with Buckingham by proposing a marriage between his daughter, who possessed an ample fortune, and Sir John Villiers, the nearly penniless brother of the marquess. This match was viewed as distasteful by both Lady Hatton and her daughter, leading to a violent quarrel. Bacon initially supported Lady Hatton's position, believing the marriage to be unsuitable. However, after expressing these views to the king and Buckingham, he received bitter letters of reproof. Buckingham compared Bacon's actions to his previous unfaithfulness to Essex. To appease the indignation of the king and Buckingham, Bacon changed his course, apologized, and used every means in his power to advance the match.

In ancient Macedonia, Philip experienced a violent rupture with his son, Alexander, following Philip's decision to repudiate his wife, Olympias, on allegations of infidelity. This decision was reportedly made at the instance of Philip's subsequent wife, Kleopatra, the niece of the Macedonian Attalus. The repudiation caused violent dissensions among the partisans of the two queens and created strong resentment in Alexander. During a marriage banquet, Attalus proposed a toast for a legitimate son to be born to Philip and Kleopatra to succeed to the throne. Alexander responded in wrath, asking if he was being proclaimed a bastard, and hurled a goblet at Attalus. Philip reacted by drawing his sword and charging at his son, but he fell to the ground due to intoxication and passion. Following this violent quarrel, father and son separated; Alexander escorted his mother to Epirus before visiting the Illyrian king.

## Marital Disputes and Political Consequences

Marital instability has frequently led to broader political instability. In the case of Margaret Maultasch, the heiress of Tirol, a quarrel with her husband, John Henry (margrave of Moravia and son of King John of Bohemia), led her to flee to the protection of Louis. Louis seized this opportunity to declare the marriage void and, in 1342, united Margaret with his son Louis. This acquisition of Tirol provided Louis with many enemies, including John of Bohemia and the Luxemburg family, eventually leading to war in Germany and an alliance between John and Pope Clement VI.

Similarly, the marriage of Margaret (1489-1541), daughter of Henry VII of England, to James IV of Scotland in 1503 was marred by financial disputes. Henry VII provided his daughter with a scanty dowry, and the resulting quarrels over this matter embittered relations between England and Scotland, despite a treaty of perpetual peace.

## Deception and Diplomatic Ruptures

In the ancient world, quarrels often sparked military expeditions. Cambyses, son of Cyrus, marched against Egypt after being deceived by Amasis. Cambyses had requested the hand of Amasis's daughter, a request encouraged by an Egyptian who held a grudge against Amasis for delivering a physician of the eyes to Persia. Amasis, fearing the Persians, sent Nitetis—the daughter of the former king Apries—disguised as his own daughter. When Nitetis later revealed her true identity to Cambyses, the king was moved to great anger, which led to his expedition against Egypt.

In another instance, a quarrel erupted between Herod the tetrarch and Aretas, the king of Arabia Petres. Herod had married the daughter of Aretas but later entered into an agreement to marry Herodias, the wife of his brother Herod. The agreement required Herod to divorce the daughter of Aretas. Upon discovering this design, the daughter of Aretas returned to her father and informed him of Herod's intentions. This became the primary cause of enmity between Aretas and Herod, who also disagreed over their limits in the country of Gamalitis. The resulting conflict led to the destruction of Herod's army through the treachery of fugitives from the tetrarchy of Philip.

## Other Notable Dissensions

Beyond marital and familial ties, quarrels have shaped regional geopolitics. A memorable quarrel between Corinth and Korkyra (B.C. 433-432) led to Athenian intervention. This event excited a violent anti-Athenian spirit in Corinth and provided Athens with a maritime footing in Italy and Sicily, which influenced the broader Peloponnesian quarrel.

In Scotland, a bitter quarrel occurred between Graham and Sir John Dalrymple regarding the execution of government orders. The dispute resulted in Graham being acquitted of charges of oppression by the privy council, while Sir John was condemned to imprisonment and a fine.
