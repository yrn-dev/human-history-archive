# Wladislaus II

Wladislaus II was a monarch whose reign extended from 1490 to 1516. He was the son of Casimir, who had utilized the intervention of the Curia and the shifting nature of European politics to procure Wladislaus's election as king of Bohemia. This political maneuver was part of a broader effort by the pope to remove the Hussite king of Bohemia, George Podebrad, as a preliminary step toward establishing a league against the Turk.

## Political Context and Accession
The path to Wladislaus's position was influenced by the rivalry between his father, Casimir, and Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Matthias Corvinus had actively worked against Casimir, even stimulating the Teutonic Order to rise against him. The death of Matthias Corvinus in 1490 provided significant relief to Poland and created a shift in regional power. Following this death, the city of Breslau, which had been under the rule of Matthias Corvinus, returned to the subjecthood of Bohemia.

## Administration and Statecraft
During the reign of Wladislaus II, the Hungarian state was heavily influenced by Tamas Bakocz. Bakocz, a Hungarian ecclesiastic and statesman who had previously served as a secretary to King Matthias I, rose to prominence under Wladislaus II. Bakocz held several high-ranking ecclesiastical positions during this period, becoming successively the bishop of Eger—which was the richest of the Hungarian sees—the archbishop of Esztergom in 1497, a cardinal in 1500, and the titular patriarch of Constantinople in 1510.

From 1490 until his own death in 1521, Bakocz served as the leading statesman of Hungary and held primary responsibility for the nation's foreign policy. Under his guidance, Hungary maintained a consistently friendly relationship with Venice and formed a family compact with the Habsburgs. Bakocz also ensured that Hungary did not join the league of Cambrai.

## Later Reign and Succession
The influence of Bakocz remained significant at court even after the death of Wladislaus II in 1516, although he initially experienced a loss of influence immediately following the king's passing. 

The territories associated with the Bohemian crown eventually transitioned further. In 1526, Ferdinand (who later became emperor) was chosen as the king of Bohemia, at which point Bohemia and the rest of Silesia passed to the Habsburgs.
