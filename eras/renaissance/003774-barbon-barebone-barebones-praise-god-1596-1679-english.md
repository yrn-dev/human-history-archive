# BARBON, PRAISE-GOD

Praise-God Barbon (c. 1596–1679), also referred to as Barebone or Barebones, was an English leather-seller, religious leader, and political figure. He was admitted as a freeman of the Leathersellers Company on January 20, 1623, and later became a liveryman on October 13, 1634.

## Religious Life and Beliefs
Barbon served as a minister to a congregation that met at his own residence, "The Lock and Key," located in Fleet Street, where his preaching drew large audiences. His specific religious convictions are not entirely clear; while his opponents labeled him an Anabaptist (likely meaning Baptist) and a Brownist, Barbon authored two books in support of paedobaptism. His congregation had separated from a larger Baptist group specifically over this controversy. He was also a member of the Jacob church in Southwark, which began as an Independent church before becoming the first Calvinistic or Particular Baptist Church. Later in life, Barbon belonged to the sect known as the Fifth Monarchy men.

Due to his beliefs, Barbon was frequently ridiculed and abused by opposing parties. His meetings were often the site of riots, and on December 20, 1641, a mob stormed his house, an event from which he narrowly escaped with his life.

## Political Activity
A man of substantial property, Barbon was summoned by Oliver Cromwell on June 6, 1653, to serve as a member for London in an assembly of nominees. This body was called "Barebone's Parliament" in derision. Although his name appears occasionally in the records of this assembly, he does not appear to have participated in the debates.

In 1660, Barbon was highly active in attempts to prevent the Restoration. He published a book by Needham titled *News from Brussels in a Letter from a Near Attendant on His Majesty's Person...*, which contained unfavorable anecdotes regarding the morals of Charles. On February 9, he presented a petition to Parliament proposing that all officials abjure the Stuarts and that anyone publicly proposing the Restoration be deemed guilty of high treason.

## Legal Troubles and Death
Barbon's political conduct led to several attacks from royalists. On March 31, he was forced to sign an engagement with the council promising not to disturb the peace. On November 26, 1661, he was arrested alongside James Harrington and John Wildman. He was imprisoned in the Tower until July 27, 1662, when he was released on bail.

Barbon was married and died in 1679; he was buried on January 5, 1680. Among his written works is *A Discourse tending to prove... Baptism*.

## Family and Legacy
Nicholas Barbon (c. 1640–1698), who is probably the son of Praise-God Barbon, became a noted English economist. Born in London, Nicholas studied medicine at Leiden and graduated M.D. at Utrecht in 1661, later becoming an honorary fellow of the College of Physicians in 1664. He was involved in the rebuilding of London following the great fire of 1666 and is credited as the institutor of fire-insurance in England around 1680. 

Nicholas Barbon served as M.P. for Bramber in 1690 and 1695 and founded a land bank that was united with that of John Briscoe in 1696. His writings on foreign trade, rent, and value are considered advanced for his time. His major works include *Apology for the Builder; or a Discourse showing the Cause and Effects of the Increase of Building* (1685), *A Discourse of Trade* (1690), and *A Discourse Concerning Coining the New Money Lighter* (1696).
