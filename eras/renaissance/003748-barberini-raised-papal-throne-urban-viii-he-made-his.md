# Urban VIII and the Barberini Family

Maffeo Barberini was raised to the papal throne as Urban VIII in 1623. He belonged to a powerful Italian family of Tuscan extraction that had settled in Florence during the early part of the 11th century and had acquired significant wealth and influence.

## Family Patronage and Power
During his papacy, Urban VIII extended considerable favor to his relatives. He appointed his brother, Antonio, a distinguished soldier, and two of his nephews as cardinals. A third nephew, Taddeo, was granted the principality of Palestrina. The increasing power of the Barberini family caused great jealousy among neighboring princes. This tension led Odoardo Farnese, the duke of Parma, to wage war against Taddeo, resulting in the defeat of the papal troops.

The wealth and magnificence of the family are still evident in their library and palace in Rome. However, the family gained a reputation for ruthlessly plundering ancient buildings to decorate their own palaces, which gave rise to the saying, "Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini" (what the barbarians did not do, the Barberini did).

## Ecclesiastical and Legal Actions
Urban VIII's papacy involved the continuation of legal proceedings against those accused of heresy. Following the death of Gregory XV in July 1623, the Inquisition revived proceedings against an ex-archbishop named Dominis. These proceedings continued under Urban VIII. Dominis died in prison on September 8, 1624, but his trial proceeded posthumously. On December 20, judgment was pronounced over his corpse in the church of Santa Maria sopra Minerva; his body was subsequently removed from its coffin, dragged through the streets of Rome, and publicly burnt in the Campo di Fiore.

In 1627, Urban VIII gave the final form to the *In Coena Domini*, a papal bull first published in 1363. This bull served as a statement of ecclesiastical censure against various crimes and offenses, including sacrilege, piracy, forgery, attacks on person and property, schisms, heresies, and the infringement of ecclesiastical and papal privileges.

## Art and Antiquities
The papacy of Urban VIII is also associated with the recovery of ancient art. While the ditch of St. Angelo was being cleansed under his direction, workmen discovered the "sleeping Faun," a statue that later became part of the Barberini palace. The statue was found with its right arm, a thigh, and a leg broken.

## Decline of the Barberini Line
Following the death of Urban VIII in 1644, his successor, Innocent X, displayed hostility toward the Barberini family. This prompted Taddeo Barberini to flee to Paris, where he died in 1647. With Taddeo's death, the family became extinct in the male line. 

The family name was later preserved through female descendants. In 1728, Taddeo's daughter, Cornelia, married Prince Giulio Cesare Colonna di Sciarra, who added the Barberini name to his own. Eventually, the name passed to Donna Maria, the daughter and heiress of Prince Enrico Barberini-Colonna, and her husband, Marquis Luigi Sacchetti. Sacchetti was granted permission to bear the name of Barberini and received the title of prince of Palestrina.
