# Poesies choisies de J. A. de Baif (1874)

The *Poesies choisies de J. A. de Baif*, published in 1874, is a work authored by Becq de Fouquieres. The publication is noted for containing a valuable introduction. It serves as a reference for the study of Jean Antoine de Baif, a prominent French poet and member of the Pleiade.

## Subject of the Work: Jean Antoine de Baif
Jean Antoine de Baif (1532–1589) was a French poet born in Venice in 1532. He was the natural son of Lazare de Baif, who served as the French ambassador at Venice during that time. Growing up in an environment that fostered an enthusiasm for the fine arts, Baif became a leading figure of the Renaissance in France.

Baif received an extensive education provided by his father. He studied Latin under Charles Estienne and Greek under Ange Vergece, a calligraphist and scholar from Crete who designed Greek types for Francis I. At the age of eleven, Baif was placed under the tutelage of the renowned scholar and poet Jean Daurat. During this period, Baif shared his studies with Ronsard, who was eight years his senior. While Baif assisted Ronsard in navigating the difficulties of the Greek language, Ronsard introduced Baif to the complexities of French versification.

## Literary Contributions and Innovations
Baif was a prolific writer, though it is noted that the sheer volume of his work has negatively impacted his reputation. His output included numerous volumes of congratulatory or amorous short poems. Additionally, he produced paraphrases and translations of works by various classical authors, including Martial, Catullus, Anacreon, Theocritus, Moschus, and Bion.

Baif is recognized for his attempts to reform French poetry and language. He developed a system to regulate French versification based on quantity, an effort shared by other poets of the era, such as Jacques de la Taille, who wrote *Maniere de faire des vers en francais comme en grec et en latin* in 1562 (printed in 1573). Baif's specific innovations included the *vers baifin*, a line consisting of 15 syllables. He also considered reforms regarding French spelling, theories which are exemplified in *Etrenes*.

## The Academie de Musique et de Poesie
In 1567, Baif founded the *academie de musique et de poesie*. The objective of this institution was to create a closer union between the arts of poetry and music. Baif's residence became well-known for hosting charming concerts, which were frequently attended by Henry III and Charles IX.

## Context within the Pleiade
Baif was a member of the Pleiade, a society dedicated to the reformation of French literature and language. This group was formed under the direction of Jean Daurat, who served as their president. The original group consisted of five poets—Baif, Ronsard, Belleau, Remy, and Pontus de Tyard—who were all pupils of Daurat. They later expanded to seven members with the addition of the dramatist Etienne Jodelle and Joachim du Bellay, naming themselves "La Pleiade" in emulation of the seven Greek poets of Alexandria.
