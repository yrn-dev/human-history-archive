# Protestants

The term Protestant is associated with various historical figures and groups across different regions and eras, ranging from the religious reformers of the 16th century to political populations in Ireland.

## Martin Bucer and the Reformation
Martin Bucer (1491–1551) was a German Protestant reformer born in Alsace at Schlettstadt. Originally a member of the Dominican order, which he entered in 1506, Bucer studied at Heidelberg. During his time there, he became acquainted with the works of Luther and Erasmus and witnessed a disputation between Luther and several Romanist doctors. 

Bucer converted to reformed opinions and, following a papal dispensation in 1521, abandoned his order and married a nun. By 1522, he served as a pastor at Landstuhl in the palatinate, traveling to propagate reformed doctrine. After being excommunicated in 1523, he established his headquarters at Strassburg, where he succeeded Matthew Zell.

Bucer's theological views on the sacrament of the Lord's Supper were decidedly Zwinglian. However, he sought to maintain church unity with the Lutheran party. Especially after the death of Zwingli, Bucer attempted to create a statement of belief that would unite Swiss, south German, and Lutheran reformers, an effort that led to accusations of obscurity and ambiguity.

## Influence and Later Life in England
Bucer's expertise was sought internationally; Henry VIII of England consulted him regarding the divorce from Catherine of Aragon. In 1548, Bucer was sent to Augsburg to sign the *Interim*, an agreement between Protestants and Catholics. Bucer's stout opposition to this project resulted in many difficulties for him.

Consequently, he accepted an invitation from Cranmer to move to England in 1549, where he was appointed regius professor of divinity at Cambridge. He received favor from the protector Somerset and Edward VI, and he was consulted on the revision of the Book of Common Prayer. 

Bucer died on February 27, 1551, and was buried with great state in the university church. In 1557, commissioners under Mary dug up his body and burnt it, and demolished his tomb. The tomb was later reconstructed by order of Elizabeth. Bucer is credited with writing ninety-six treatises, including a work titled *Deregno Christi* and an exposition and translation of the Psalms. His doctrines were later utilized by Milton in treatises concerning divorce.

## Protestantism in Ireland
In the context of British and Irish history, the Protestant population in Ireland, particularly the Ulster Protestants, played a significant role in the political tensions of the late 19th century. During the efforts of liberal England to address Irish complaints, the first Irish Home Rule Bill was introduced by Gladstone in 1886. 

This proposal, intended to give Irish affairs to the Irish people for the first time in history, was viewed as faulty and dangerous in some respects. Specifically, it failed to provide satisfactory assurances to the Protestant Irish, and the Ulster Protestants in particular, regarding protection from possible injuries by the "priest-ridden illiterates" of the south. While these fears may have been fancied, they contributed to the breaking of the Liberal Party and the subsequent replacement of Gladstone's government by a coalition known as the Unionist Government.
