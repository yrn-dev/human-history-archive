# Singspiele and the Works of Jakob Ayrer

## Overview of Jakob Ayrer
Jakob Ayrer (died March 26, 1605) was a German dramatist whose early life is largely undocumented, though it appears he arrived in Nuremberg as a boy. During his time there, he ascended to the professional position of imperial notary. Ayrer's literary contributions are diverse; in addition to his dramatic works, he authored a rhymed *Chronik der Stadt Bamberg* (edited by J. Heller in 1838) and produced a translation of the Psalms that remained unpublished.

## The Opus Theatricum
Ayrer's primary legacy as a playwright is preserved in a collection titled *Opus Theatricum*, which was printed in Nuremberg in 1618. This comprehensive collection consists of sixty-six plays in total, divided into the following categories:
*   Thirty tragedies and comedies.
*   Thirty-six *Singspiele* and *Fastnachtsspiele* (Shrovetide plays).

## Influence and Style
In the context of German drama, Ayrer is regarded as the virtual successor to Hans Sachs. However, Ayrer's style was significantly shaped by the *Englische Komoedianten*—troupes of English actors who visited the European continent during the late 16th and 17th centuries. These troupes introduced the repertory of the Elizabethan theatre to Ayrer.

From these English influences, Ayrer adopted specific theatrical devices to enliven his dramas, including the use of spectacular effects and sensational incidents. He also borrowed the character of the clown from these foreign models. Despite these additions, Ayrer's plays are considered hardly more dramatic in a true sense than those of Hans Sachs, and they are noted as being inferior to Sachs' work in terms of poetic qualities.

## Literary Sources and Parallels
Certain comedies by Ayrer show clear evidence of sharing sources with the works of William Shakespeare. Specifically, the plots of *Von der schoenen Phoenicia* and *Von der schoenen Sidea* were evidently drawn from the same sources used for Shakespeare's *Tempest* and *Much Ado about Nothing*.

## Academic Reception and Publications
Ayrer's dramas were edited by A. von Keller and published by the Stuttgart Lit. Verein between 1864 and 1865. His work has been discussed in several scholarly contexts, including L. Tieck's *Deutsches Theater* (1817) and J. Tittmann's *Schauspiele des sechzehnten Jahrhunderts* (1888). Additionally, A. Cohn's *Shakespeare in Germany* (1885) provides translations of the two comedies mentioned as having parallels to Shakespeare's plays.
