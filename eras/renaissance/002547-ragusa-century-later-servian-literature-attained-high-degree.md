# Servian Literature and the Role of Ragusa

## Development of Servian Literature
Servian literature experienced significant growth and evolution, particularly in its relationship with the spoken language and regional centers of culture. In the city of Ragusa, Servian literature attained a high degree of excellence approximately one century after 1493. The intellectual life of Dalmatia during the 15th, 16th, and 17th centuries reached a level higher than that achieved by any purely Slavonic peoples on the Balkan Peninsula. Within this context, the historians and poets of Ragusa are recognized as key contributors to Servian literature.

## Linguistic Evolution and Reform
The Servian language, like those of the Bulgarians and Rumanians, borrowed many words from Greek and a large number of terms from Turkish, though it did not adopt Turkish structural forms. For a period, the ecclesiastical Russian-Slavonic served as the formal literary language. However, a literary movement later emerged under the influence of Vuk Karajich and Obradovich. Despite opposition from the priesthood, this movement saw the popular idiom supersede the ecclesiastical Russian-Slavonic.

## Cultural Context in the Balkans
The preservation of the Servian language was vital in saving the nationality from extinction during periods of Turkish rule, when Turkish and Greek were the only officially recognized or systematically taught languages. While Turkish was the official speech of the conquering race, Greek became the language of the Christian upper classes due to the privileges of the Greek church and their intellectual and literary superiority. 

Servian dialect enjoyed certain advantages over Bulgarian because it extended into regions that escaped the Turkish yoke. For example, the first Slavonic printing-press was established in free Montenegro in 1493. In contrast, Bulgarian ceased to be a written language for nearly four centuries, except within a few monasteries, until a literary revival began around the middle of the 18th century.

## Ragusa and Dalmatian Intellectual Life
The development of literature in Ragusa was influenced by the unique demographic history of Dalmatia. Following the Slavonic migration in the first half of the 7th century, the region was divided between Slavs in the country districts and a Latin or Italian population that sought safety in large towns such as Zara and Ragusa. These two communities were often hostile, a division intensified by the schism between Western Christianity (the Papacy) and Eastern Christianity (the Orthodox or Bogomil creeds). It was not until the 15th century that these rival races contributed to a common civilization through the literature of Ragusa.

## Epic Poetry and National Figures
Servian poetry frequently features legendary figures who personify the race. A prominent example is Marko Kralyevich, the son of Prince Vukashin (who died in 1371). While historical records note that Marko headed a revolt against the new Servian ruler and later served the sultan of Turkey before being killed in battle around 1394, he is more celebrated in legend. In Servian poetry, he is depicted as a figure of enormous physical strength who fought against oppressors, specifically the Turks. These poems were published in 1878 and later translated into German in 1883.
