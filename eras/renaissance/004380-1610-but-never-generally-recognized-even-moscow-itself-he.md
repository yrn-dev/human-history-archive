# Basil IV, Shuisky

## Rise to Power
Basil IV, Shuisky, was a leading boyar of Muscovy during the reigns of Boris Godunov and Theodore I. During the reign of Tsar Boris, Shuisky acted in obedience to secret orders by traveling to Uglich to investigate the death of Demetrius, the infant son of Ivan the Terrible. Although Demetrius had been murdered by agents of Boris, Shuisky reported that the death was a suicide.

Following the death of Boris and the accession of Theodore II, Shuisky changed his account to gain favor with the first false Demetrius. By recognizing the pretender as the real Demetrius, Shuisky facilitated the assassination of the young Theodore. However, Shuisky later plotted against the false Demetrius. In May 1606, he procured the pretender's death by publicly confessing that the reigning tsar was an impostor and that the real Demetrius had indeed been slain. This act was noted as particularly vile because the pseudo-Demetrius had previously forgiven Shuisky for one conspiracy.

## Reign as Tsar
On May 19, 1606, Shuisky's adherents proclaimed him tsar. His reign lasted until July 19, 1610, though he was never generally recognized. His authority was minimal, even within Moscow; he remained on the throne only because the dominant boyars had no alternative candidate to put in his place.

Shuisky's unstable position was maintained for a time through two primary means: the popularity of his heroic cousin, Prince Michael Skopin-Shuisky, who fought his battles and led his armies, and the assistance of Swedish soldiers. The support of the Swedes was purchased through a disgraceful cession of Russian territory.

## Deposition and Death
In 1610, Basil IV was deposed and made a monk. He was subsequently taken as a trophy by the Polish grand hetman, Stanislaus Zolkiewski. Shuisky died in Warsaw in 1612.
