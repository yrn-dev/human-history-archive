# German Draughtsmen and the History of the Book-Plate

The history of the book-plate, viewed as an accessory to book binding or a minor work of decorative and symbolical art, originated in Germany. These ornamental marks of possession were found in great numbers in Germany long before the fashion spread to other nations. The German style is characterized as being heavy and over-elaborated, a quality that did not affect neighboring countries.

## The Little Masters and Early Practitioners
The development of the book-plate in Germany involved several prominent artists. Albrecht Durer is known to have supplied designs for many plates and engraved at least six himself between 1503 and 1516, some of which were of very important size. Other notable plates are attributed to Hans Holbein and Lucas Cranach.

A group of draughtsmen known as the "Little Masters" also contributed significantly to this art form. This group included:
* Virgil Solis
* Matthias Zundt
* Jost Amman
* Saldorfer
* Georg Hupschmann

The influence of these draughtsmen on German decorative styles persisted through subsequent centuries and continues to the present day, despite the effort for originality among modern designers and the influence of French and Italian fashions during the 18th and 17th centuries.

## The Works of Jost Amman
Jost Amman was a versatile artist whose work extended beyond book-plates into heraldry and the illustration of musical instruments. In 1589, he produced *Wappen und Stammbuch*, a work that includes the arms of the Herzog von Wirtenberg and the Sultzberger aus Tirol (which features "Ein Jagerhornlin"). A facsimile reprint of this work was published in Munich in 1881 by Georg Hirth as volume iii of *Liebhaber Bibliothek*.

Additionally, Amman provided illustrations depicting the use of musical instruments. He produced a picture of an armed knight on horseback playing a pair of kettledrums accompanied by banners.
