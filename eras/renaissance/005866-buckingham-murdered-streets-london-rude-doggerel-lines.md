# The Duke of Buckingham and the Petition of Right

## Parliamentary Conflict and the Petition of Right
The Duke of Buckingham played an active role as a member of the House of Lords during a parliamentary battle that resulted in the concession of the Petition of Right. During these proceedings, Buckingham resisted the demands of the House of Commons for as long as possible, specifically opposing the requirement that the king abandon his claim to imprison individuals without showing cause.

When the king provided the first answer to the petition on June 2, the House of Commons found the response unsatisfactory. The Commons suspected, likely correctly, that this answer had been dictated by Buckingham. In response, they prepared a remonstrance regarding the state of the nation. During this process, Coke identified the Duke of Buckingham as the source of all the misfortunes that had occurred, stating that the duke was "the cause of all our miseries" and describing him as "the grievance of grievances."

## Political Fallout and Public Unrest
Although the king eventually granted a satisfactory answer to the petition on June 7, the House of Commons continued with their remonstrance. On June 11, the Commons demanded that Buckingham be removed from office. King Charles refused to surrender the duke and subsequently prorogued parliament in anger a few days later.

This political deadlock led to highly excited popular feeling. Lampoons were circulated freely among the public. During this period of unrest, Dr. Lambe—a quack doctor and dabbler in astrology who was believed to have influence over Buckingham—was murdered in the streets of London. Following this event, rude doggerel lines circulated, announcing that the duke should share the same fate as the doctor.
