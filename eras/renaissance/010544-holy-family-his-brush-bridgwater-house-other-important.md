# Holy Family

The term "Holy Family" appears in the archive's records in the contexts of fine art and religious prayer.

## In Art
The Venetian painter Paris Bordone (1495–1570), a former student of Titian who entered Titian's *bottega* in 1509, produced a work titled "Holy Family." This specific painting is located at Bridgwater House. 

Bordone's body of work is characterized by a nobility of style and a golden harmony of colour derived from Titian, as well as a dignified approach to portraiture and a realistic conception of the human figure. While he excelled as a portraitist, he also painted a variety of religious pictures, allegories, mythological scenes, and subjects from the fables of Ovid. His artistic output is noted for being of unequal merit; while his style is often noble, his nudes are described as slightly coarse in form, and the action of his figures is frequently affected and unnatural.

## In Religious Context
The concept of the Holy Family is referenced in a prayer addressed to Jesus Christ, identifying him as having lived a "holy family life" with his "holy parents." In this context, the prayer asks for mercy upon families at home and seeks protection for German youth, villages, towns, fields, vineyards, trade, and handicraft. The text further characterizes the divine as the "friend of children" and the "father of the fatherless" and "God of the widows," noting that Jesus himself had a mother whose heart experienced pain and grief.
