# George of Podebrad

George of Podebrad was the only Hussite king of Bohemia. He is remembered as one of the rulers of Bohemia whose memory has most endeared itself to his countrymen, alongside Charles IV.

## Rise to Power and Early Reign
Before ascending to the throne, George of Podebrad served as the leader of the national party of the nobility. This party was one of two rival factions—the other being the Roman or Austrian division led by Ulrich of Rosenberg—that emerged during a period of conflict over whether the Bohemian crown was hereditary or elective. The two parties eventually reached an agreement called the "Letter of Peace" (_list mirny_). Under this agreement, the national party abandoned the candidacy of Prince Casimir of Poland for the throne, which allowed for the eventual succession of the heir of Albert of Austria. In exchange, those who signed the Letter of Peace pledged to support John of Rokycan as archbishop of Prague and to recognize the Compacts.

The early and more prosperous part of George's reign was based on a firm alliance with Matthias Corvinus, the king of Hungary. Through the influence of Matthias, George was crowned by the Romanist bishop of Waitzen. During this initial period, George's primary supporters were the towns and the men of the smaller nobility. It was also during this prosperous phase that George intended to establish a national dynasty.

## Conflict with the Papacy and Internal Strife
Over time, George's relationship with the Romanist nobles deteriorated, leading to a protracted struggle with the papal see. This conflict hindered George's far-reaching ambitions, including an attempt to obtain supremacy over Germany. After negotiations with Rome failed, George assembled the estates at Prague in 1452. There, he declared his readiness to risk his crown and his life to defend his faith, vowing to remain true to the communion in both kinds until his death.

Hostility from the Romanist party increased, culminating in 1465 when many nobles of the Roman religion formed a confederacy against the king at a meeting at Zelena Hora (Grunberg). In 1466, Pope Paul II provided moral support to these confederates by releasing all Bohemians from their oath of allegiance to George and pronouncing a sentence of excommunication against him.

## War with Matthias Corvinus
The papal influence also affected George's former ally, King Matthias of Hungary, who deserted George to support the league of Zelena Hora. This led to desultory warfare between the two parties. While George was initially successful, the tide turned when Matthias invaded Moravia and captured its capital, Brunn. On May 3, 1469, Matthias was proclaimed king of Bohemia at a meeting of Catholic nobles in Olmutz, Moravia. Although George achieved some successes against his rival in the following year, the war was temporarily halted by George's death in 1471.

## Military Achievements and Succession
In addition to his political and religious struggles, George of Podebrad achieved military success against a German army of crusaders at the pass of Neumark.

Following George's death, the question of succession became a point of contention. In his later years, George had offered the Bohemian throne to Vladislav (also known as Wladislaus or Ladislaus), the son of King Casimir of Poland, in hopes of obtaining Polish aid against Matthias of Hungary. While no formal agreement had been made, the national party in Bohemia supported Vladislav's claim as a victory against the anti-clerical influence of Matthias, who was an unconditional adherent of Rome. The Polish envoys promised that Vladislav would maintain the Compacts. Consequently, Vladislav was elected king at Kutna Hora on May 27, 1471.
