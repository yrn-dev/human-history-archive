# Original Letters edited by Sir H. Ellis

## Overview
The *Original Letters*, edited by Sir H. Ellis, is a historical source utilized in the study of English history, specifically regarding the Tudor period. It is cited as an authority in scholarly works concerning the life and times of Henry VIII and the figures surrounding his court.

## Role in Tudor History
The *Original Letters* serves as a primary reference for documenting the events and figures of the 16th century. It is listed among the bibliography of authorities used to examine the history of Henry VIII, the divorce of Catherine of Aragon, and the life of Anne Boleyn. 

The text is categorized into different series and volumes. Specific references to the work include:
*   **First Series:** Volume II, page 37 and pages 53 et seq.
*   **Second Series:** Volume II, page 10.

## Documentation of the Reformation
The *Original Letters* provides evidentiary support for the events of the English Reformation. Specifically, it is used to substantiate the narrative of the torture of Anne (not to be confused with Anne Boleyn), who was arraigned before a commission on June 18, 1546. 

Following her condemnation to be burnt based on her confession, Anne was committed to Newgate and later taken to the Tower. According to the records, she was racked by the solicitor-general, Rich, and the lord chancellor, Wriothesley. While some historians, such as Jardine and Lingard, have disputed these accounts of torture, the *Original Letters* (specifically the 2nd Series, vol. ii, p. 177) is cited as a contemporary letter that substantiates the claim that she was tortured before being burnt at Smithfield on July 16.
