# Henry IV of France

## Accession and Political Conflict
Henry IV of France was the legitimate heir to the French throne, though he was a Protestant. During his struggle for the crown, he was at war with the Catholic League. His position was further complicated by the ambitions of Philip II of Spain, who sought the conquest of the French throne. Between 1587 and 1592, Philip II dedicated the resources of Spain to this goal, providing aid to the Catholic League and transporting troops. However, these efforts failed as the country eventually rallied to support Henry IV.

## Diplomatic Efforts and International Relations
During his rise to power, Henry (then King of Navarre) utilized various representatives to secure international support. One such figure was the French scholar and diplomatist Jacques Bongars. Bongars had entered the service of Segur Pardaillan, a representative of Henry. In 1587, Bongars was dispatched on a mission to several princes in northern Europe and visited England to seek assistance from Queen Elizabeth for Henry of Navarre.

Following his ascension, Henry IV continued to employ Bongars as a diplomatist. In 1593, Bongars became the French king's representative at the courts of the imperial princes. In this capacity, Bongars worked vigorously to support Henry's objective of curtailing the power of the house of Habsburg, a service to which he dedicated his money and health.

## Administration and Favourites
The reign of Henry IV involved the elevation of various nobles and the management of powerful figures. One such individual was Jean Louis de Nogaret, a favourite of Henry III (known as one of *les Mignons*), who continued to hold influence under Henry IV. In the 16th century, King Henry (later Henry IV) sold the independent lordship of Epernon to Nogaret, and in 1581, it was raised to the rank of a duchy. Under the reign of Henry IV, Nogaret exercised a level of government in Provence that was practically independent.

Other notable figures of the era included Roger de Saint-Lary de Termes, who was a favourite of Henry III, Henry IV, and Louis XIII. He served as the governor of Burgundy and royal master of the horse.

## End of Reign
Henry IV's service to the state and his diplomatic efforts, including those led by Bongars, continued until the king's murder in 1610. Following his death, Jean Louis de Nogaret was instrumental in the appointment of Marie de' Medici to the regency, which allowed Nogaret to maintain considerable influence over the government.
