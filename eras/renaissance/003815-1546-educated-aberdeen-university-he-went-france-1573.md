# William Barclay

## Early Life and Education
William Barclay (1546–1608) was a Scottish jurist born in Aberdeenshire in 1546. He received his education at Aberdeen University before traveling to France in 1573. While in France, he studied law at Bourges under Cujas, where he eventually earned his doctor's degree.

## Career in France
Following his studies, Barclay was appointed as a professor of civil law by Charles III, the duke of Lorraine, at the newly-founded university of Pont-a-Mousson. In addition to his academic role, he was created a master of requests and a counsellor of state. 

Barclay's tenure in France was interrupted in 1603 when he was forced to leave the country. This departure was the result of enmity incurred with the Jesuits, stemming from Barclay's opposition to a proposal that would have admitted his son, John, as a member of their society.

## Return to England and Final Years
Upon returning to England, King James offered Barclay considerable preferment. However, this offer was contingent upon Barclay becoming a member of the Church of England. Barclay refused the condition and returned to France in 1604. There, he was appointed as a professor of civil law at the university of Angers, where he remained until his death in 1608.

## Written Works
Barclay authored several significant legal and political texts:

*   ***De Regno et Regali Potestate, &c.*** (Paris, 1600): In this work, Barclay provided a strenuous defence of the rights of kings. Within this text, he refuted the doctrines of Jean Boucher, "Junius Brutus" (Hubert Languet), and George Buchanan.
*   ***De Potestate Papae, &c.*** (London, 1609): This work opposed the usurpation of temporal powers by the pope. This publication prompted a celebrated reply from Cardinal Bellarmine.
*   **Commentaries**: Barclay also wrote commentaries on various titles of the Pandects.
