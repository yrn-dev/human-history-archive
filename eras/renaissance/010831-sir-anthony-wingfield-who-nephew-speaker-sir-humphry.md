# Sir Humphry Wingfield

## Public Life and Legal Career
Sir Humphry Wingfield was a barrister who held a prominent position in the English government during the early 16th century. In 1533, he served as the speaker of the House of Commons.

## Educational Influence and the House of Wingfield
Sir Humphry was known for his fondness for children and frequently had many of them brought up within his own home. These children were placed under the guidance of a tutor named R. Bond. 

One of the notable individuals educated in Sir Humphry's house was Ascham. Ascham's time in the Wingfield household was characterized by a specific focus on physical activity and sport, specifically archery. Sir Humphry personally supported this pursuit; during term times, he would bring bows and shafts from London to the field to observe the children shooting.

This early exposure to archery under Sir Humphry's patronage had a lasting impact on Ascham. It influenced the subject of Ascham's earliest English work, the *Toxophilus*, and shaped his views on the importance of including archery within educational establishments. This influence likely extended to the statutes of other Elizabethan schools, such as Harrow and St Albans.

## Clarification of Kinship
Historical records, specifically those cited by Ascham in the *Toxophilus*, clarify a common biographical error. While some sources—originating with Grant—incorrectly identified Sir Anthony Wingfield as the person responsible for Ascham's early education, the archive confirms that it was Sir Humphry Wingfield who provided this tuition. Sir Anthony Wingfield is identified as the nephew of the speaker, Sir Humphry.
