# Buckingham

Buckingham was a writer and playwright known for his satirical works and occasional poetry. While his poetical gifts were considered undoubted, they were also described as undeveloped. A collection of his occasional verses and satires was first published by Tom Brown in 1704, though this collection included several pieces not written by Buckingham. Additionally, an article in the *Quarterly Review* from January 1898 provided a few extracts of interest from a commonplace book belonging to him.

## Dramatic Works and Satires

Buckingham authored *The Rehearsal*, a clever and amusing satire targeting the heroic drama and specifically Dryden. The play was first performed at the Theatre Royal on December 7, 1671, and published in 1672. Described as a "gracious piece of fooling" produced by Buckingham and his friends, it was a popular work that was later imitated by Sheridan in *The Critic* and by Fielding in *Tom Thumb the Great*.

In addition to his original satires, Buckingham published two adapted plays:
* *The Chances* (1682), which was altered from a play of the same name by Fletcher.
* *The Restoration or Right will take place* (published 1714), adapted from Beaumont and Fletcher's *Philaster*.

Other published works attributed to him include *The Militant Couple* and *The Battle of Sedgmoor*, both published in 1704.

## Other Writings and Legacy

Beyond his plays and poetry, a work titled *A Demonstration of the Deity* is named by Wood, although no trace of this text currently exists. The most recent edition of Buckingham's works was produced by T. Evans in 1775, consisting of two 8vo volumes.
