# VASCO NÚÑEZ DE BALBOA

Vasco Núñez de Balboa (c. 1475–1517) was a prominent Spanish explorer and conqueror in America, recognized as the discoverer of the Pacific Ocean. Born in Estremadura at Jerez de los Caballeros around 1475, Balboa was a gentleman by birth (*hidalgo*), although he was poor.

## Early Life and Arrival in the Americas
The details of Balboa's early life are sparse until 1501, when he joined a voyage of discovery to the western seas led by Rodrigo de Bastidas. Following this, he settled in Hispaniola, where he attempted to cultivate land near Salvatierra. These agricultural efforts were not successful, and Balboa soon found himself burdened by oppressive debts.

In 1510, an adventurous lawyer named Enciso set sail from San Domingo to deliver provisions to San Sebastian, a settlement founded by Ojeda. Seeking to escape his creditors in town, Balboa managed to join the expedition by concealing himself in a cask of victuals transported from his farm to the ship. Upon arrival, the expedition found that Ojeda had departed and the settlement of San Sebastian lay in ruins.

## Establishment of Darien
While Enciso remained undecided on how to proceed, Balboa proposed sailing for Darien on the Gulf of Uraba, a location he had previously visited while with Bastidas. The proposal was accepted, and the adventurers founded a new town called Sta Maria de la Antigua del Darien.

Internal conflict soon arose among the settlers. Balboa, allied with the alcalde Zamudio, deposed Enciso, who was imprisoned and eventually sent back to Spain. Having assumed authority, Balboa began the conquest of the surrounding region. He gained the friendship of several native chiefs through his just dealing, kindness of heart, bravery, and courtesy.

## Discovery of the Pacific
During his excursions into the interior, Balboa learned of the existence of gold in Peru and an ocean on the other side of the mountains from the cacique Comogre. In 1513, Balboa reached a peak in Darien, where he saw the Pacific Ocean stretching inland in the Gulf of San Miguel.

## Conflict with the Spanish Crown
Shortly after his return to Darien, Balboa received letters from Zamudio. These letters informed him that Enciso had filed a complaint with the king, resulting in a sentence that condemned Balboa and summoned him to Spain. In response to this news and the resulting despair, Balboa resolved to undertake a great enterprise in hopes of achieving success.
