# February, his schemes checked for the moment; his father rewarded him

## The Campaigns of Cesare Borgia
In July 1497, Cesare Borgia served as a papal legate in Naples, where he crowned Frederick of Aragon as king. Following the death of the duke of Gandia, the pope sought to utilize Cesare to execute various political schemes and attempted to secure a wealthy marriage for him. Cesare desired to marry Carlotta, the daughter of the king of Naples; however, both Carlotta and her father refused the alliance, citing his status as a priest and the bastard of a priest.

In August 1498, Cesare requested and received permission from the pope and the cardinals to renounce his priesthood "for the good of his soul." On October 1, he traveled to France as a papal legate to Louis XII. His mission was to deliver a papal bull annulling Louis's marriage to Jeanne of France, as Louis intended to marry Anne of Brittany. In return for this service, Cesare received military assistance for his own enterprises and the duchy of Valentinois. While in France, he again unsuccessfully attempted to win over Carlotta of Naples, eventually marrying Charlotte d'Albret, the sister of the king of Navarre, in May 1499.

## Conquests in Romagna
Pope Alexander contemplated using Cesare to subdue turbulent local despots in Romagna to carve out a principality from territories that owed nominal allegiance to the pope. Establishing headquarters at Cesena, Cesare led an army composed of Italian troops, 4,000 Swiss and Gascons, and 300 French lances. He successfully attacked Imola, which surrendered immediately, and subsequently besieged Forli. The city was held by Caterina Sforza, the widow of Girolamo Riario. Despite a gallant defense, Sforza was forced to surrender on January 22, 1500. Cesare treated her with consideration, and she later lived in a convent.

## Return to Rome and Rewards
In February, following the expulsion of the French from Milan by the Sforzas, Cesare returned to Rome. At this time, his schemes were checked for the moment. His father rewarded his successes by conferring many honours upon him and appointing him *gonfaloniere* of the church. While remaining in Rome, Cesare participated in carnival festivities and bull fights. Later, in July, the duke of Bisceglie, who was the third husband of Lucrezia Borgia, was murdered by assassins.
