# AVILES, PEDRO MENENDEZ DE (1519-1574)

Pedro Menendez de Aviles was a Spanish seaman and the founder of St Augustine, Florida. Born on February 15, 1519, in Aviles, Asturias, he was born into a gentry family and was one of nineteen siblings. His career began at the age of fourteen when he ran away to sea, spending the next sixteen years as a corsair in a life of adventure.

## Early Imperial Service
In 1549, during a period of peace between Spain and France, Emperor Charles V commissioned Aviles to clear the Canaries and the north coast of Spain of French pirates. By 1554, he was appointed captain-general of the *flota*, the convoy responsible for trade between America and Spain. This appointment was made by the emperor despite the opposition and will of the Casa de Contratacion, the governing board of American trade. 

Prior to sailing to America in 1554, Aviles accompanied the prince of Spain (the future Philip II) to England for his marriage to Queen Mary. As commander of the *flota*, Aviles earned the hearty approval of the emperor through his diligence and success in returning treasure. However, his refusal to accept bribes to allow breaches of regulations made him unpopular with merchants, and his high-handed manner further offended the Casa de Contratacion.

## Military and Naval Commands
Aviles was reappointed commander in 1557. Due to the ongoing hostility of the Casa de Contratacion, he sought service elsewhere. During the war with France, in which England and Spain were allies, Aviles spent the period until the end of 1559 transporting recruits and money from Spain to Flanders. Following the restoration of peace, he commanded the fleet that returned Philip II to Spain from the Low Countries.

He returned to the command of the *flota* in 1560, completing successful voyages to America and back in 1560 and 1561. Despite these successes, his relationship with the Casa de Contratacion remained strained. In 1563, upon returning from a voyage, he was arrested by order of the Casa and imprisoned for twenty months. While the specific charges against him are unknown, Aviles wrote a letter to the king avowing his innocence.

## Colonization of Florida
Following his release from prison, Aviles intended to sail to the Bermudas to search for his son, Juan, who had been shipwrecked the previous year. During this time, French Huguenots were attempting to establish a colony in Florida. Because the Spaniards had already explored the region, they claimed it as their own; furthermore, the area's location on the trade route from Mexico made foreign possession dangerous.

To address this, Philip II entered into an *asiento* (contract) with Aviles on March 20, 1565. The king advanced 15,000 ducats to Aviles, naming him the proprietor of any colony he could establish in Florida, provided the money was repaid. Aviles departed on July 28, 1565, leading 1,500 men with ten sloops and one 600-ton vessel. 

On August 28, he entered and named the Bay of St Augustine, where he began constructing a fort. On September 20, 1565, he captured the French post of Fort Caroline. In October, he exterminated a group of Frenchmen led by the Huguenot Jean Ribault, who had arrived to relieve the colony. Aviles slew nearly all the prisoners and hung their bodies from trees with the inscription, "Not as Frenchmen but as Lutherans." Aviles viewed the Frenchmen as heretics and pirates, and his letters indicate he believed no honest man could suggest he had gone too far in the massacre.

In 1568, a French sea-captain named Dominique de Gourgues sought revenge for the massacre by capturing Fort San Mateo (the renamed Fort Caroline) and hanging the garrison with the inscription, "Not as Spaniards but as murderers."

## Final Years and Personal Life
Aviles remained in Florida managing his colony until 1567, at which point he returned to Spain. He made one final voyage to Florida before his death on September 17, 1574. He had married Maria de Solis at a young age and is survived by three daughters.
