# A True Description of Unthankfulnesse and The Passionate Shepheard

*A True Description of Unthankfulnesse* (1602) and *The Passionate Shepheard* (1604) are poetical works authored by Breton. These titles are part of a larger body of literary output produced by the author between 1577 and 1614.

## Literary Context and Works of Breton
Breton's poetic career includes a wide variety of titles spanning several decades. His early works include *The Workes of a Young Wit* (1577) and *A Floorish upon Fancie* (1577). Later contributions include *The Pilgrimage to Paradise* (1592), *Pasquil's Mistresse* (1600), *Pasquil's Fooles cappe* (entered at Stationers' Hall in 1600), and *Pasquil's Passe and Passeth Not* (1600). 

His output around the turn of the century was prolific, featuring *Melancholike Humours* (1600) and *Marie Magdalen's Love: a Solemne Passion of the Soules Love* (1595). The latter consists of two parts: a prose treatise, which is likely written by another hand, and a poem composed in six-lined stanzas, which is certainly the work of Breton.

## Works of the Early 17th Century
The period between 1601 and 1605 saw the publication of several of Breton's works, including *A Divine Poem* (1601), which contains "The Blessed Weeper" and "The Ravisht Soul," as well as *The Soules Heavenly Exercise* (1601) and *An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart* (1601). 

Following these, Breton published *The Soules Harmony* (1602), *The Mother's Blessing* (1602), *Olde Madcappe newe Gaily mawfrey* (1602), and *A True Description of Unthankfulnesse* (1602). This was followed by *The Passionate Shepheard* (1604), *The Honour of Valour* (1605), and *The Soules Immortall Crowne* (1605). His later recorded work includes *An Invective against Treason; I would and I would not* (1614).

## Other Attributions and Prose
Breton also contributed to other miscellanies of verse, such as *England's Helicon*. Some works are only partially his, such as *The Arbor of Amorous Devises* (entered at Stationers' Hall, 1594). Additionally, *Bryton's Bowre of Delights* (1591), edited by Dr Grosart in 1893, was an unauthorized publication containing poems that Breton himself disclaimed.

In addition to his poetry, Breton wrote twenty-two prose tracts. Notable examples include *Wit's Trenchmour* (1597), *The Wil of Wit* (1599), and *A Poste with a Packet of Mad Letters* (1603). Certain other works have been erroneously attributed to him, specifically *The Passion of a Discontented Mind* (1601), *Mary Magdalen's Lamentations* (1604), and *Sir Philip Sidney's Ourania by N.B.* (1606).
