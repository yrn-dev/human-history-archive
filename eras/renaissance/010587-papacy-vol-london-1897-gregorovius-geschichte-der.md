# Ferdinand Gregorovius and the History of the Papacy

## Biography of Ferdinand Gregorovius
Ferdinand Gregorovius (1821–1891) was a German historian born on January 19, 1821, in Neidenburg. He pursued his studies at the university of Konigsberg and spent several years in teaching before relocating to Italy in 1852. He remained in Italy for more than twenty years and was granted citizenship of Rome. Gregorovius died in Munich on May 1, 1891.

## Major Works on Rome and the Papacy
Gregorovius is primarily recognized for his erudite work, *Geschichte der Stadt Rom im Mittelalter* (Stuttgart, 1859-1872), which has been translated into Italian (Venice, 1874-1876) and into English by A. Hamilton in 13 volumes (1894-1900). This work covers the history of Rome from approximately A.D. 400 until the death of Pope Clement VII in 1534. In this text, Gregorovius describes the historic system of the papacy as having remained inseparable from that of the Empire from the time of Charles the Great to that of Charles V.

Other notable works by Gregorovius include:
* *Die Grabdenkmaler der Papste* (Leipzig, 1881), translated into English by R. W. Seton-Watson in 1903.
* *Urban VIII. im Widerspruch zu Spanien und dem Kaiser* (Stuttgart, 1879), which the author translated into Italian himself.
* *Lucrezia Borgia* (Stuttgart, 1874).
* *Wanderjahre in Italien* (5 vols., Leipzig, 1888-1892).
* *Geschichte der Stadt Athen im Mittelalter* (1889).
* *Geschichte des Kaisers Hadrian und seiner Zeit* (Konigsberg, 1851).
* *Corsica* (Stuttgart, 1854).
* *Kleine Schriften zur Geschichte der Kultur* (Leipzig, 1887-1892).

Additionally, Gregorovius engaged in poetic and dramatic writing, producing the drama *Der Tod des Tiberius* (1851) and various *Gedichte* (Leipzig, 1891).

## Contribution to Papal History and Scholarship
The writings of Gregorovius serve as key authorities for various historical periods of the papacy. His work *Rome in the Middle Ages* (translated by Mrs. G. W. Hamilton) is cited as a primary source for the study of several popes and eras:
* **Honorius II:** Volume 4 of *Rome in the Middle Ages* (London, 1896) is listed as a source for the life of Honorius II.
* **Gregory VIII:** Volume 4 (London, 1896) is also cited regarding the 57-day pontificate of Gregory VIII (Alberto de Mora), who served from October 21 to December 17, 1187.
* **The Avignon Papacy:** Volume 6 (London, 1900-1902) is utilized for the study of the papacy during the 14th century, specifically concerning the return of Popes Urban V and Gregor XI from Avignon to Rome.
* **The Borgias:** Volume VII of *Geschichte der Stadt Rom* (Stuttgart, 1881) is cited as a chief authority for the life of Cesare Borgia.

## Cesare Borgia and Papal Support
In the context of the Italian Renaissance, Cesare Borgia is described as an unscrupulous and clever adventurer whose methods of conquest were treacherous and ferocious. Despite these methods, he governed his subjects with justice and firmness, leading many to prefer his rule over local despots and the anarchy of factions. 

Historical analysis indicates that Borgia was not a man of genius; rather, his success was primarily due to the support he received from the papacy. Following the death of his father, Borgia's career ended, and he ceased to play a prominent role in Italian affairs, demonstrating the unsound basis upon which his system had been constructed.
