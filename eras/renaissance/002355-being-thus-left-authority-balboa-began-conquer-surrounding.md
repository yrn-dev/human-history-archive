# VASCO NUNEZ DE BALBOA

## Early Life and Arrival in the Americas
Vasco Nunez de Balboa was born around 1475 in Estremadura, specifically at Jerez de los Caballeros. Although he was born a gentleman (*hidalgo*), he was poor. The details of his early life are sparse until 1501, when he joined a voyage of discovery to the western seas led by Rodrigo de Bastidas. Following this, Balboa settled in Hispaniola, where he attempted to cultivate land near Salvatierra. This venture was not successful, and he soon found himself burdened by oppressive debts.

## Expedition to San Sebastian and Darien
In 1509, an expedition led by the famous Ojeda sailed from San Domingo and established the settlement of San Sebastian. Enciso, a lawyer from the town, was tasked with fitting out two ships to provide provisions for this new colony. In 1510, Balboa—seeking to escape the unpleasantness of his debts—managed to join Enciso's voyage. It is said that he achieved this by concealing himself in a cask of "victuals for the voyage" that was transported from his farm to the ship.

Upon arriving at San Sebastian, the expedition discovered that the settlement was in ruins and Ojeda had departed. While Enciso remained undecided on how to proceed, Balboa proposed sailing for Darien on the Gulf of Uraba, a location he had previously visited while with Bastidas. The proposal was accepted, and the adventurers founded a new town called Sta Maria de la Antigua del Darien.

## Rise to Authority and Exploration
Conflict soon erupted among the members of the expedition. Balboa, supported by his ally, the alcalde Zamudio, deposed Enciso. Enciso was imprisoned and eventually sent back to Spain. 

Left in authority, Balboa began to conquer the surrounding country. He gained the friendship of several native chiefs through his just dealing, kindness of heart, courtesy, and bravery. During one of these excursions, Balboa learned of the existence of gold in Peru and an ocean on the other side of the mountains; this information was provided to him by the cacique Comogre.

## Legal Conflict and Final Ambitions
Shortly after returning to Darien, Balboa received letters from Zamudio. These letters informed him that Enciso had filed a complaint with the king, resulting in a sentence that condemned Balboa and summoned him to Spain. Faced with this news and in a state of despair, Vasco Nunez resolved to undertake a great enterprise, trusting that its success would provide a solution to his predicament.
