# The Umbrian School of Painting and the Umbrian Language

## The Umbrian School of Painting
Until near the middle of the 15th century, the Umbrian school of painting remained far behind the schools of the North and Florence. However, the school experienced a sudden advancement into the first rank through the work of Perugino and several of his followers. Among these followers, Benedetto Bonfigli holds a particularly distinguished position. Born in Perugia, Bonfigli is known for several important extant works, most notably a series of frescoes depicting the life of St Louis of Toulouse, located in the communal palace of Perugia.

## The Umbrian Language and Dialect
In the study of ancient linguistics, the term "Umbrian" is used to describe the dialect of an ancient set of liturgies. This serves as the only existing monument of significant length regarding the tongue spoken in the Umbrian district prior to its latinization. 

The application of the name "Umbrian" is a subject of scholarly debate, as the term is often considered too wide. For instance, an inscription from Tuder, likely dating to the 3rd century B.C., displays a medial -d- and a final -s- that were preserved, unlike the changes that occurred in the dialect of Iguvium. Conversely, inscriptions from Assisium and Fulginia align well with the Iguvine dialect.

Furthermore, the language known as Umbrian was restricted to a limited area, which cannot yet be proven to extend far beyond the eastern half of the Tiber valley, specifically from Urvinum Mataurense to Interamna Nahartium. This differs from the usage by some archaeologists, such as Professor Ridgeway in *Early Age of Greece*, who uses "Umbrians" in a broader sense to refer to the race associated with the Early Iron age Villanova culture. A primary problem in Italian history remains the determination of the actual historical relationship between the language of Iguvium and the [Greek: 'Ombroi] mentioned by Herodotus.
