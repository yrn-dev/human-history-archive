# BAINBRIDGE, JOHN (1582-1643)

## Early Life and Education
John Bainbridge was an English astronomer born in Ashby-de-la-Zouch, located in Leicestershire. Before dedicating himself to the study of astronomy, he began his professional life as a physician, a practice he maintained for several years. During this period, he also kept a school.

## Career in London and Oxford
Bainbridge eventually moved to London, where he was admitted as a licentiate of the college of physicians on November 6, 1618. He gained professional attention through the publication of a work concerning the comet of 1618. This publication led Sir Henry Savile (1549-1622) to appoint him in 1619 to the Savilian chair of astronomy at Oxford, a position that had been recently founded by Savile. 

While at Oxford, Bainbridge was incorporated into Merton College. He further advanced his academic standing within the university, serving as the junior reader of Linacre's lectures in 1631 and as the senior reader in 1635.

## Published Works and Contributions
Bainbridge authored several works and translations. His publications include *An Astronomical Description of the late Comet*, published in 1619, and *Canicularia*, published in 1648. He also provided translations of Ptolemy's *De Planetarum Hypothesibus* and Proclus' *De Sphaera*, both of which were published in 1620. Additionally, the library of Trinity College, Dublin, holds several of his manuscript works.

## Death
John Bainbridge died at Oxford on November 3, 1643.
