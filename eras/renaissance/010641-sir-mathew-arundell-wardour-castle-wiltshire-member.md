# Sir Mathew Arundell of Wardour

Sir Mathew Arundell of Wardour was a member of the ancient family of the Arundells of Lanherne in Cornwall. He resided at Wardour Castle in Wiltshire.

## Family and Descendants
Sir Mathew Arundell was married to Margaret, who was the daughter of Sir Henry Willoughby. Together they had a son, Thomas Arundell, 1st Baron Arundell of Wardour, born around 1562. 

The family's connection to Wardour Castle continued through subsequent generations. Thomas's son, the 2nd Baron, fathered Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour (c. 1607–1694). Henry, a Roman Catholic and strong royalist, succeeded to the family estates and title upon his father's death in 1643.

## Conflict with Thomas Arundell
Sir Mathew Arundell experienced significant familial conflict with his son, Thomas. In December 1595, while serving with imperial troops against the Turks in Hungary, Thomas was created a count of the Holy Roman Empire by Emperor Rudolph II. This occurred after Thomas captured an enemy banner with his own hand during the siege of Gran or Esztergom on August 13, 1595.

Upon Thomas's return to England in January 1596, his assumption of this foreign title caused resentment. Sir Mathew objected to his son's superior rank and, as a result, promptly disinherited him.

## Wardour Castle in the English Civil War
The family seat at Wardour Castle became a site of military conflict during the 17th century. In 1643, the parliamentarians took the castle, despite a brave defence of the place led by the mother of Henry Arundell. In the same year, Edmund Ludlow, an English parliamentarian, was appointed governor of the castle. Ludlow maintained a tenacious defence until he was forced to surrender on March 18, 1644. Shortly thereafter, Henry Arundell distinguished himself by recapturing the castle from the parliamentarians.
