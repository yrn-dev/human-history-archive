# Villa Borghese and the Doria Palace

The Villa Borghese and the Doria palace are prominent architectural and cultural landmarks in Rome, associated with two of the city's noble families and housing significant collections of art.

## Villa Borghese
The Villa Borghese, located outside the Porto del Popolo, was planned by the Florentine architect Baccio d'Agnolo. The estate is currently the property of the Italian government and is known as Villa Umberto I. It serves as the repository for a splendid gallery of pictures that were previously housed in the Borghese palace in Rome, a building described as one of the most magnificent in the city.

The villa's collections include a variety of artworks, such as:
*   **Paintings:** The archive notes the presence of a "Madonna" by Lotto and two mythological pictures by the Venetian painter Paris Bordone.
*   **Sculpture:** The villa contains a statue of a figure in a sitting posture with an open hand. While some vulgarly attribute this statue to Belisarius, others suggest it represents Augustus propitiating Nemesis, though some scholars argue the work is irreconcilable with the state of arts from that period.

## The Borghese Family
The Borghese are a noble Italian family of Sienese origin, first mentioned in 1238. The family's prominence in Rome grew with Marcantonio Borghese, the father of Camillo Borghese. Camillo was elected pope in 1605 under the name Paul V. Through his influence, Paul V created his nephew the prince of Vivero in 1609, and in 1610, Philip III of Spain granted him the title of prince of Sulmona.

The family further solidified its status among the higher Roman nobility in 1614 when the prince's son, Paolo, married Olimpia, the heiress of the Aldobrandini family. In the 19th century, Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese (born 1775), married Pauline, the sister of Napoleon and widow of General Leclerc. He was made duke of Guastalla in 1806 and served as governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces before retiring to Florence, where he died in 1832.

## Doria Palace and the Doria-Pamphilii Family
The Doria-Pamphilii-Landi are a princely Roman family of Genoese extraction. The house was founded by Ansaldo d'Oria, a 12th-century consul of Genoa, though the authentic pedigree is traced back to Paolo d'Oria in 1335. The family's most famous member was Andrea Doria, an admiral to Emperor Charles V and the perpetual censor of Genoa in 1528. He was created prince of Melfi in 1531 and marquis of Tursi in 1555.

Over the following centuries, the family acquired numerous titles, including the duchy of Tursi (1594), the principality of Avella (1607), and the duchy of Avigliano (1613). In 1760, they were granted the title of *Reichsfurst* (prince of the Holy Roman Empire) and inherited the titles and fiefs of the house of Pamphilii-Landi of Gubbio.

The Doria-Pamphilii palace in Rome is a splendid edifice constructed in the 17th century that contains a valuable collection of paintings, including two mythological works by Paris Bordone. Additionally, the family owns the Villa Doria-Pamphilii, noted for its gardens; this villa served as the headquarters for Garibaldi during the siege of 1849.
