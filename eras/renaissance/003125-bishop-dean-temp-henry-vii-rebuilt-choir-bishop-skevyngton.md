# Bangor Cathedral

## Overview and Location
Bangor, formerly known as Bangor Fawr to distinguish it from other towns of the same name in Brittany, Ireland, and Wales, is a seaport and market-town located in Carnarvonshire, North Wales. It is situated near the northern entrance of the Menai Straits, approximately 240 miles northwest of London. The city serves as a municipal and contributory parliamentary borough within the Carnarvon district.

## The Cathedral
The city's buildings include a small cathedral, a deanery, and a disused bishop's palace. The cathedral is cruciform in design and features a low pinnacled tower. It was constructed on the site of a church that was destroyed by the English in 1071; this original church was dedicated to and perhaps founded by St Deiniol around the year 525.

Throughout its history, the cathedral has suffered several periods of damage and destruction. It was severely handled in 1102 and 1212, and was destroyed in 1211. Additionally, the building was burned during the time of Owen Glendower. Between 1866 and 1875, the cathedral was restored by Sir G. Scott.

## Ecclesiastical Contributions
The structure of the cathedral was shaped by the contributions of various bishops. During the time of Henry VII, Bishop Dean rebuilt the choir. Later, in 1532, Bishop Skevyngton added the nave and the tower.

## City Infrastructure and Economy
Beyond its ecclesiastical sites, Bangor contains other churches, including a small Roman Catholic church. The city is a center for education, housing the University College of N. Wales (established 1883), which includes a hall for female students. Other educational institutions in the city include the Normal, Baptist, Independent, and N. Wales Training Colleges.

The local economy is supported by the slate-quarries of Lord Penrhyn, located at Bethesda, six miles away. These quarries provide the primary export for the city via Port Penrhyn, which is situated at the mouth of the stream Cegid. As of 1901, the population of Bangor was 11,269. The city is divided into Upper and Lower Bangor, with the Lower section consisting practically of a single street.
