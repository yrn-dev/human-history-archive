# Events and Institutions of 1595

The year 1595 is associated with several distinct historical developments across Europe, ranging from the administration of English boroughs and the movement of religious orders to military conflicts in France and maritime exploration.

## The Bridgittines and the House at Vastein
The Bridgittines were an order of Augustinian canonesses established around 1350 by St Bridget of Sweden and received approval from Urban V. in 1370. The order operated as a "double order," meaning that while each convent was governed by an abbess, it also included a small community of canons who served as chaplains. 

The order had a significant impact on the literature and culture of Scandinavia, spreading extensively throughout Norway and Sweden. The head house of the order, located at Vastein by Lake Vetter, remained active until its suppression in 1595. At its height, the order maintained a total of 80 houses, including those located in other lands.

## Municipal Administration in Macclesfield
In 1595, Queen Elizabeth issued a new charter to the town of Macclesfield. This charter established a formal borough constitution, which included the appointment of a high steward, 24 capital burgesses, 2 aldermen, and a mayor. This administrative structure was later confirmed by Charles II in 1666 and James I in 1605. Additionally, the 1595 charter granted the town an annual fair to be held in June.

## Military Conflict in Doullens
During the late 16th century, the town of Doullens in northern France—the capital of an arrondissement in the department of Somme—experienced military occupation. In 1595, the town was besieged and occupied by the Spaniards. It remained under this occupation until 1598, when it was restored to France via the treaty of Vervins. Doullens is noted for its 15th and 16th-century citadel, which served as a state prison before becoming a reformatory for girls.

## Maritime Exploration of Linschoten
The year 1595 marked a period of Arctic exploration for Linschoten, who was born in Haarlem around 1563. Having previously traveled to Goa with the Portuguese East India fleet between 1583 and 1589, Linschoten participated in Dutch Arctic voyages during 1594 and 1595. Between 1595 and 1596, he produced his work *Navigatio ac itinerarium*, a compilation based on his own travels and the accounts of other travelers. This work was later translated into several languages, including German and English (1598), Latin (1599), and French (1610).
