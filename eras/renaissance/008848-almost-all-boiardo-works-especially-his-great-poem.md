# Matteo Maria Boiardo

Matteo Maria Boiardo (1434–1494) was an Italian poet whose works, most notably his romantic epic, served as a foundational model for subsequent Italian literature. His writing was primarily composed for the amusement of Duke Hercules and his court, although Boiardo did not write within the court's precincts. Instead, he typically retired to his estates, such as Scandiano, to devote himself to his compositions.

## Orlando Innamorato

The *Orlando Innamorato* is considered one of the most important poems in Italian literature, as it provided the first example of a romantic epic worthy of serving as a model. It is noted for having undoubtedly influenced Lodovico Ariosto's *Orlando Furioso*. 

### Composition and Influence
Boiardo's process for the poem involved incorporating descriptions of the agreeable environs of his own chateau. Furthermore, he drew the names of several heroes—including Agramant, Sacripant, Gradasse, and Mandricardo—from the names of his own peasants, as he felt their "uncouthness" made them appropriate for Saracen warriors.

Regarding the poem's literary models, Gravina and Mazzuchelli suggested that Boiardo used Homer's *Iliad* as a model, proposing that the poem is a reflex image of the *Iliad* in which Angelica takes the place of Helen and Paris is besieged similarly to the city of Troy. However, the actual subject matter of the poem is derived from the *Fabulous Chronicle* of the pseudo-Turpin. While the *Orlando Innamorato* features principal warriors such as Oliver, Roland, and Charlemagne, there is little resemblance between its detailed plot and that of the *Fabulous Chronicle*.

### Publication History
Boiardo did not live to finish the poem. It was printed in Scandiano the year after his death, managed by his son, Count Camillo. While the book's title lacks a date, a Latin letter from Antonia Caraffa di Reggio prefixed to the poem is dated the kalends of June 1495. A second edition appeared in Venice before the year 1500, and the work was reprinted twice more in Venice during the following twenty years. Other sources indicate the work was finished in 1486.

## Other Works

Beyond his great epic, Boiardo produced a variety of other literary works, including comedies, poetry, and translations:

*   **Dramatic and Poetic Works:** He wrote the comedy *Il Timone* (published in Scandiano, 1500), *Sonnetti e Canzoni* (Reggio, 1499), and *Carmen Bucolicon* (Reggio, 1500). He also wrote *Cinque Capitoli in terza rima*, published in Venice in either 1523 or 1533.
*   **Translations:** Boiardo translated the *Asino d'Oro* of Apuleius (Venice, 1516, 1518) and the *Asino d'Oro* of Lucian into the vernacular (Venice, 1523). Additionally, he translated the historian Herodotus of Halicarnassus from Greek into the Italian language (*Erodoto Alicarnasseo istorico*), published in Venice in 1533 and 1538.
*   **Other:** He is associated with *Rerum Italicarum Scriptores*.

## Legacy and Literary Context

Boiardo is situated within a broader tradition of "artificial or secondary epics" in modern Europe that were founded on the imitation of the *Aeneid* and the *Iliad*. In Italy, the *Chanson de Roland* inspired several romantic epics, including those by Luigi Pulci (*Morgante Maggiore*), Francesco Bello (*Mambriano*), and Lodovico Ariosto. 

Boiardo's influence extended into the 16th century; in 1541, Berni remodelled Boiardo's epic. Later, in 1526, Teofilo Folengo wrote *Orlandino*, a work that ridiculed the entire school of poetry to which Boiardo belonged.
