# BARTOLOMMEO DI PAGHOLO, FRA (1475-1517)

Fra Bartolommeo di Pagholo (1475–1517) was an Italian portrait and historical painter. He was also known as Baccio—a shortened form of Bartolommeo—and Bella Porta, the latter name derived from his residence near the Porta Romana.

## Early Life and Education
Born in 1475 at Soffignano, near Florence, Bartolommeo received his initial artistic training from Cosimo Roselli. Following his time with Roselli, he dedicated himself to studying the great works produced by Leonardo da Vinci. His early works are noted for their beauty and grace, with his most significant early production being a fresco of the Last Judgment, created with the assistance of his friend Mariotto Albertinelli.

## Religious Influence and Hiatus
While working on several pieces for a convent of Dominican friars, Bartolommeo met Savonarola. Savonarola exerted a significant influence over him, and the painter was so deeply affected by Savonarola's cruel death that he entered the convent shortly thereafter. For several years, he abandoned his art entirely.

## Relationship with Raphael
Bartolommeo eventually resumed his painting in obedience to his superior. Around this time, Raphael arrived in Florence, and the two artists formed a close friendship. This relationship resulted in a mutual exchange of technical skills: Bartolommeo learned the rules of perspective from Raphael, becoming highly skilled in the discipline. Conversely, Raphael's handling of drapery and his colouring improved following his meeting with the *frate*.

## Later Career and Works
Bartolommeo later visited Rome, where he felt a sense of inferiority upon viewing the masterpieces of Raphael and Michelangelo. Despite this, he remained on friendly terms with Raphael; upon leaving Rome, Bartolommeo left two unfinished paintings in Raphael's care, which Raphael then completed.

Throughout much of his career, Bartolommeo's figures were generally draped and small. Because some critics alleged these qualities were defects resulting from a lack of power, he painted two specific works to prove his capability:
* **St Mark**: Located in Florence, this work is considered his masterpiece and features a magnificent figure.
* **St Sebastian**: An undraped figure noted for its natural and beautiful colouring and its strong expression of agony and suffering. This painting was eventually removed from the convent chapel where it had been exhibited.

Bartolommeo died in Florence in 1517.
