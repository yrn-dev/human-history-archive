# Revision of the French Breviaries (17th-18th Centuries)

During the 17th and 18th centuries, a movement of revision emerged in France that resulted in the modification of approximately half of the country's Breviaries. This movement was driven by both historical scholarship and theological shifts.

## Historical and Theological Foundations
The historical impetus for these revisions came from the work of Louis Sébastien le Nain de Tillemont and Jean de Launoy (1603-1678). Jean de Launoy, known as "le dénicheur des saints," along with le Nain de Tillemont, demonstrated that numerous lives of the saints were false. 

Theologically, the movement was produced by the Port Royal school. This school of thought encouraged individuals to focus more on their communion with God rather than the invocation of saints.

## Implementation and Characteristics
The revision was primarily executed through the adoption of a specific rule: all responses and antiphons were required to be in the exact words of Scripture. This requirement effectively removed the entire class of appeals to created beings. 

In addition to the textual changes, the services were shortened and simplified. The reformers sought to make the weekly use of the entire Psalter a reality, a practice that had become mere theory in the Roman Breviary because it was frequently superseded by services for saints' days.

## Notable Reformed Breviaries
These reformed French Breviaries were characterized by a deep knowledge of Holy Scripture and the careful adaptation of various texts. Examples of these works include:
* The Paris Breviary of 1680, produced by Archbishop François de Harlay (1625-1695).
* The Breviary of 1736, produced by Archbishop Charles Gaspard Guillaume de Vintimille (1655-1746).

The influence of Gallican and Jansenist thought extended beyond France, reaching Italy and Germany. In the latter, Breviaries based on these French models were published in towns such as Mainz, Münster, and Cologne.

## Opposition and Suppression
A strong Ultramontane movement arose against these Breviaries during the pontificate of Pius IX. This opposition was inaugurated by Montalembert, though its primary literary advocates were Louis François Veuillot (1813-1883) of the *Univers* and Dom Gueranger, a learned Benedictine monk and abbot of Solesmes. 

This movement succeeded in suppressing the reformed Breviaries throughout France. The final diocese to surrender its version was Orleans in 1875.

## Later Official Efforts
Under the direction of Pope Benedict XIV (who served as pope from 1740 to 1758), a special congregation was established to collect materials for an official revision; however, nothing was published from these efforts. Subsequent changes to the texts have been minute and very few. In 1902, Pope Leo XIII appointed a commission, presided over by Monsignor Louis Duchesne, to consider the Pontifical, the Missal, the Breviary, and the Ritual.
