# Sir Robert Aytoun

Sir Robert Aytoun (1570–1638) was a Scottish poet and public official. The son of Andrew Aytoun of Kinaldie, Fifeshire, he was born in 1570 and received his education at the university of St Andrews. He was incorporated as a student of St Leonard's College in 1584 and graduated with an M.A. in 1588.

## Public Career and Life
Aytoun spent several years living in France. Following the accession of James VI to the English throne, Aytoun wrote a Latin panegyric while in Paris, a work that brought him immediate favor at the royal court. He was knighted in 1612 and subsequently held various lucrative offices, serving as the private secretary to the queens of both Charles I and James I. 

Aytoun died in London and was buried in Westminster Abbey on February 28, 1638. During his lifetime, he maintained a high reputation both personally and as a writer, although he did not possess an ambition to be known as a poet. Details regarding his public career are preserved in the *Register of the Privy Council* and the printed *Calendars of State Papers* of the era.

## Literary Works
Aytoun's literary remains are written in both English and Latin. In his English writing, he is noted as one of the earliest Scots to utilize the southern standard as a literary medium. His first English poem was titled *Diophantus and Charidora*, a work he references within his Latin panegyric to James. His English output includes a number of sonnets, songs, and pieces focusing on amatory subjects.

His Latin works include the aforementioned panegyric to James, as well as:
* *Epicedium in obitum Thoma Rhodi*
* *Basia, sive Strena ad Jacobum Hayum*
* *Lessus in funere Raphaelis Thorei*
* *Carina Caro*
* Various minor epitaphic and occasional pieces.

## Preservation and Collections
Aytoun's Latin poems were printed in *Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum* (Amsterdam, 1637), specifically on pages 40–75. His English poems were preserved in a manuscript in the British Museum (*Add. MSS.* 10,308), which was prepared by his nephew, Sir John Aytoun.

Some of his English poems appear in the *Bannatyne Miscellany* (1827, p. 299) and in Watson's *Collection* (1706–1711). 

In 1871, Charles Rogers published *The Poems of Sir Robert Aytoun* (London, privately printed), which collected both the Latin and English works. This edition is considered unsatisfactory, though it is viewed as an improvement over Rogers's first issue from 1844. The 1871 edition includes additional poems that cannot be ascribed to Aytoun; specifically, the old version of "Sweet Empress" and "Auld Lang Syne" are certainly not his, and the poem "I do confess thou'rt smooth and fair" is suspected of being the work of another.

Biographical information on Aytoun can be found in a memoir within Rogers's edition, as well as a memoir by Grosart in the *Dict. of Nat. Biog.*
