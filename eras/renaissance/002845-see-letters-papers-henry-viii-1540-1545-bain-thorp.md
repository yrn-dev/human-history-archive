# Letters and Papers of Henry VIII

The *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* is a primary historical collection of documents and correspondence from the reign of King Henry VIII. It serves as a foundational source for historians researching the political, religious, and personal affairs of the English monarchy during the 16th century.

## Scope and Content
The collection encompasses a wide range of state activities, including diplomatic negotiations, ecclesiastical disputes, and the private correspondence of the King and his advisors. The archive is utilized to document key events of the Tudor period, such as the King's divorce from Catherine of Aragon and his subsequent marriage to Anne Boleyn.

Specific volumes and entries provide critical evidence regarding the legal and religious transitions of the era. For example, the records include details on the marriage of Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn, which was commonly antedated to November 14, 1532. The *Letters and Papers* also contain accounts from figures such as Cranmer, who identified himself as the only authority on certain matters, though he only became aware of them a fortnight later.

## Religious Reform and Conflict
The *Letters and Papers* are frequently cited in the study of the English Reformation and the figures who were caught in the struggle between Protestant and reactionary parties. 

One such figure was Robert Barnes (1495–1540), an English reformer and former prior of the convent of Austin Friars. The archive preserves correspondence regarding Barnes's intellectual contributions; specifically, Stephen Vaughan, an agent of Thomas Cromwell, wrote to Cromwell recommending Barnes's book, describing it as a unique piece of work and predicting that Barnes would "seal it with his blood." Barnes later served as an intermediary between the English government and Lutheran Germany, attempting to induce Lutheran divines to approve of Henry VIII's divorce from Catherine of Aragon.

The collection also documents the broader theological shifts of the reign, including the King's 1538 refusal to adopt Lutheran theology and the subsequent implementation of the statute of Six Articles in 1539.

## Administrative and Diplomatic Use
Beyond religious matters, the *Letters and Papers* are used in conjunction with other state records to reconstruct the administrative history of the period. They are often cited alongside the *Calendar of State Papers* (including Scottish, Venetian, and Foreign series), the *Acts of the English Privy Council*, and the *Register of the Great Seal of Scotland*.

The archive provides a factual basis for analyzing the movements and communications of courtiers and diplomats. It is used by historians to verify or debunk claims, such as the recognition that a well-known letter attributed to Anne Boleyn was actually an Elizabethan forgery.
