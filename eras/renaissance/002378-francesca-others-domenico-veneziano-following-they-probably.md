# Domenico Veneziano and His Following

Domenico Veneziano was a master painter and a key figure in a group of scientific realists and naturalists in art. This artistic circle included other notable figures such as Paolo Uccello and Andrea del Castagno.

## Influence and Following
The influence of Domenico Veneziano was particularly manifest in the work of Alessio Baldovinetti (1427–1499), a Florentine painter who was a follower of the group of naturalists and scientific realists. The works of Baldovinetti share a quality of "grave majesty" with those of Piero della Francesca and other artists who followed Domenico Veneziano.

## Alessio Baldovinetti
Born on October 14, 1427, and dying on August 29, 1499, Baldovinetti is noted for his determined patience and precision. His work often features beautiful landscape backgrounds, and he is described by Vasari as an inveterate experimentalist in technical matters who delighted in drawing landscapes from nature exactly as they appeared, including elements such as cities, roads, plants, fruits, rocks, bridges, and rivers.

Baldovinetti's technical approach to wall-painting involved laying in compositions in fresco and finishing them *a secco* using a mixture of liquid varnish and yolk of egg. Although this was intended to protect the paintings from damp, the vehicle eventually scaled away, rendering the experiment a failure.

Recorded and datable works associated with Baldovinetti include:
*   A great fresco of the Annunciation in the cloister of the Annunziata (commissioned in 1462), which demonstrates his ability to space figures in a landscape with a sense of distance and air.
*   A cartoon of the Nativity (1463), executed in tarsia by Giuliano de Maiano in the cathedral sacristy.
*   The Portuguese chapel in the church of S. Miniato (dating from 1466), featuring an Annunciation on an oblong panel and frescoes of the four Fathers of the Church and four Evangelists.
*   A small panel in the Florence Academy depicting the Transfiguration, the Marriage of Cana, and the Baptism.
*   An Annunciation in the Uffizi (formerly in the church of S. Giorgio).
*   Several Madonnas of characteristic quality, including one in the Louvre (formerly in the Duchatel collection) and one in the collection of Madame Andre in Paris.

Works from the years 1460–1465 are characterized by a delicate charm. However, in his later preserved works, while the laborious finish remains, there is a growing predominance of the technical and scientific searcher over the artist, resulting in a lack of care for composition and a certain commonness of type and harshness.

## Andrea del Castagno
Andrea del Castagno (1390–1457) was a painter of the Florentine school, born probably at Castagno in the district of Mugello. While he imitated the naturalists of his era and Masaccio in his boldness of attitude, he was considered deficient in colouring and grace. One of his few extant principal works is the equestrian figure of Nicola di Tolentino located in the cathedral of Florence.

Castagno was associated with Domenico Veneziano through their shared artistic movement and a tradition—though not attested by contemporary records—that they worked together with Baldovinetti on the decorations of the chapel of S. Egidio in Santa Maria Nuova between 1441 and 1451.

Historically, Castagno was burdened for four centuries by the false charge that he assassinated Domenico Veneziano to monopolize the secret of oil painting as practiced by the Van Eycks in Flanders. This charge has been proven untrue, as Domenico died four years after Andrea.
