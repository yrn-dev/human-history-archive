# Catherine of Portugal and the Marriage to Charles II

The marriage of the infanta Catherine of Portugal to King Charles II of England was a diplomatic union that resulted in a significant territorial transfer between the Portuguese and British crowns. This alliance was settled through a treaty with Portugal, though it faced internal political opposition within the English court.

## Political Opposition
The marriage was not universally supported in England. The 2nd earl of Bristol, who had returned to England at the restoration, was a vocal opponent of the union. Driven by a desire to maintain a prominent figure in parliament and a strong inclination toward Spain in foreign affairs, Bristol actively opposed the king's marriage to Catherine. In an attempt to divert the king's choice, Bristol persuaded Charles II to send him to Italy to evaluate the Medici princesses. However, these efforts were unsuccessful, as the royal marriage and the treaty with Portugal were finalized during Bristol's absence.

## The Cession of Bombay
A primary outcome of the marriage was the transfer of the island of Bombay. In 1661, Bombay was ceded to the English crown as part of the dowry (or dower) of the infanta Catherine of Portugal upon her marriage to Charles II. 

Prior to this transfer, the island had been under Portuguese rule, where lands were parcelled out into fiefs via a system known as *aforamento*. These fiefs were granted to individuals or religious corporations, such as the Jesuits and Franciscans, in exchange for quit-rents or military service. The rule of these religious orders was characterized by intolerance, which hindered the growth of the settlement; by the time of the 1661 transfer, the population was only 10,000.

## British Occupation and Administration
Although the island became British property in 1661, the British did not actually occupy Bombay until 1665. The transition was marked by significant difficulty, as the British encountered strong opposition to the cession from the Portuguese and specifically from the religious orders.

The acquisition was not highly esteemed in England, and the administration provided by crown officers was unsuccessful. Consequently, in 1668, the crown transferred Bombay to the East India Company for an annual payment of £10. Along with this transfer, the Company was granted powers for the administration of justice and the island's defence. To secure the territory, a European regiment was enrolled and fortifications were constructed, which later served to deter a planned attack by the Dutch in 1673.

## Subsequent Status of Bombay
Following the transfer to the East India Company, Bombay was placed under the factory of Surat. Its importance grew over the following century; by 1687, it was positioned at the head of all Company possessions in India. This status remained until 1753, when the government of Bombay became subordinate to the government of Calcutta.
