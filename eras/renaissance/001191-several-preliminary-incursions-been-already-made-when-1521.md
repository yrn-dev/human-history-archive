# Military Incursions and Territorial Conflicts

The history of human conflict is characterized by various forms of incursions, ranging from small-scale raids and strategic probes to large-scale invasions intended to establish new dynasties or secure strategic strongholds. These actions have occurred across diverse geographies and eras, often serving as precursors to broader political shifts.

## Strategic Incursions in Classical and Ancient History

In the Hellespont region, the Spartan officer Anaxibius was dispatched as harmost to Abydos to counter the influence of Thrasybulus of Athens. Anaxibius utilized a land force to conduct incursions on cities within the territory of the satrap Pharnabazus. Simultaneously, he reinforced his squadron with triremes from the harbor of Abydos, allowing him to seize merchant vessels traveling to Athens or its allies.

In Judea, Alexander, the son of Aristobulus, conducted an incursion that led to the arming of ten thousand footmen and fifteen hundred horsemen. He fortified the locations of Macherus, near the mountains of Arabia, and Alexandrium, a fortress near Corem. This activity prompted a response from Rome; Gabinius, the commander of Roman forces in Syria, dispatched Marcus Antonius and other commanders. These forces, including Romans and Jews led by Malichus and Pitholaus, eventually engaged Alexander's forces in a pitched battle near Jerusalem, resulting in approximately three thousand deaths and three thousand captives.

In ancient Macedonia, the region experienced incursions by the Goths and Avars toward the end of the 4th century. While these specific incursions were not of lasting significance, the region later underwent a major ethnographic change due to Slavonic immigration occurring between the 3rd and 7th centuries.

## Dynastic Conquests in Central and South Asia

The career of Baber (Zahir ud-din-Mahomet), the founder of the Mogul dynasty, exemplifies the use of strategic captures to establish a kingdom. Born in 1483 as a descendant of Timur and the son of King Omar Sheik of Ferghana, Baber ascended the throne at age twelve in 1495. After securing his position against his uncles, he captured Samarkand in 1497.

Baber's path to power was marked by volatility. He lost both Samarkand and his native kingdom due to noble rebellions and troop desertions. In 1501, he was defeated and driven from Samarkand by Shaibani Khan, the ruler of the Uzbegs. After three years of wandering, Baber crossed the snowy Hindu Kush in 1504 to besiege and capture Kabul, which re-established his fortunes. Despite later revolts and a period of flight, his personal courage led his opponents to return to their allegiance. By 1511, with aid from Shah Ismael of Persia, he again attempted to regain his native country.

## European Territorial and Religious Conflicts

In the borderlands between France and Spain, the town of Fuenterrabia served as a strategic point of contention. French troops made unsuccessful attempts to seize the location in 1476 and 1503. However, a campaign in 1521 proved more successful, though the fortress was retaken by Spain in 1524. The town later surrendered to the duke of Berwick and French troops in 1719, and fell again to the French in 1794, at which point it was dismantled.

In Scandinavia, King Olaf of Norway sailed to Denmark and made incursions into Seeland to plunder the land. These ravages occurred while King Onund Olafson of Sweden was ravaging Scania. The two kings eventually formed a union at the Gaut river to oppose King Canute, proclaiming their intent to conquer Denmark. They subjected the surrounding country to their rule, utilizing fire and sword to lay waste to the land.

## Internal Strife and Civil Unrest

Incursions have also occurred within cities during periods of internal collapse. During a period of sedition, the "men of power" and high priests seized the upper city (Mount Sion), while the seditious faction controlled the temple and the lower city. For seven days, the two sides engaged in perpetual slaughter, utilizing stones, slings, and darts, and launching troop-based incursions to gain control of the opposing sectors.
