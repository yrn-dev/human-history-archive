# Ermolao Barbarus

Ermolao Barbarus, also known as Hermolaus Barbarus, was an Italian scholar who lived from 1454 to 1493. Born in Venice on May 21, 1454, he was sent to Rome at an early age to study under Pomponius Laetus. He later completed his educational pursuits at the university of Padua, where he was appointed as a professor of philosophy in 1477.

## Career and Public Life
Barbarus maintained a connection to his native Venice, visiting the city in 1479, though he returned to Padua following an outbreak of plague in Venice. His scholarly reputation led to his appointment on various missions to high-ranking individuals, including Pope Innocent VIII. In 1491, Pope Innocent VIII nominated Barbarus to the significant office of patriarch of Aquileia.

However, this appointment became a source of legal and political conflict. Barbarus had accepted the position without first obtaining the sanction of the Venetian senate, an act that was contrary to the law. Consequently, the senate refused to ratify the appointment. Under the threat of vicarious punishment—specifically the confiscation of his father's property—Barbarus was banished and forced to resign the patriarchate.

## Final Years and Death
Following his resignation and banishment, Barbarus remained in Rome. During this period, he lived in receipt of a small pension provided by the pontifical government. He died on June 14, 1493, though some sources suggest his death occurred two years later. It is probable that he died from the plague.

## Scholarly Contributions
Barbarus was an active editor and translator of classical works. His most notable contributions include:

*   ***Castigationes Plinianae* (1492):** In this work, Barbarus claimed to have made 5,000 corrections to the text of Pliny's *Natural History*.
*   ***Paraphrases* (1480):** His work on certain writings of Aristotle by Themistius.
*   ***Rhetorica*:** A work by Aristotle, which was published in 1544.
*   ***Castigationes in Pomponium Melam* (1493):** Published in the year of his death.

His work on Pliny is noted in historical records for specific substitutions; for instance, where Pliny allowed *quinquaginta millia* for a certain distance, Barbarus substituted *centum*.
