# The Barberini Family

The Barberini were a powerful Italian family of Tuscan extraction who established themselves in Florence during the early part of the 11th century. Over time, the family acquired significant influence and wealth, which is evidenced today by their magnificent library and palace in Rome.

## Rise to Power under Urban VIII
The family's prominence peaked in 1623 when Maffeo Barberini was elevated to the papal throne as Urban VIII. During his papacy, Urban VIII utilized his position to benefit his relatives. He appointed his brother, Antonio, as a distinguished soldier, and elevated two of his nephews to the rank of cardinal. A third nephew, Taddeo, was granted the principality of Palestrina.

## Conflicts and Decline
The rapid increase in the family's power caused great jealousy among neighboring princes. This tension led Odoardo Farnese, the duke of Parma, to wage war against Taddeo Barberini, resulting in the defeat of the papal troops.

Following the death of Urban VIII in 1644, the family's fortunes shifted. His successor, Innocent X., displayed hostility toward the Barberini family. As a result of this hostility, Taddeo fled to Paris. He died there in 1647, marking the extinction of the family in the male line.

## Succession of the Name and Title
Although the male line ended with Taddeo, the Barberini name and titles continued through female descendants:
* **Cornelia Barberini**: The daughter of Taddeo, she married Prince Giulio Cesare Colonna di Sciarra in 1728, who incorporated her name into his own.
* **Donna Maria**: The daughter and heiress of Prince Enrico Barberini-Colonna, she passed the name to her husband, Marquis Luigi Sacchetti. Marquis Sacchetti was granted permission to bear the name of Barberini and received the title of prince of Palestrina.

## Legacy and Reputation
The Barberini are remembered for their magnificence and wealth, but also for their ruthless methods of acquisition. The family plundered ancient buildings to decorate their own palaces, a practice that gave rise to the saying, "Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini."
