# The 15th Century

The 15th century was a period characterized by significant developments in the arts, architecture, literature, and religious thought across Europe, as well as shifts in global exploration and communication.

## Arts and Architecture
In Italy, the 14th and 15th centuries saw a revival and development of decorative brick-work, also known as terra-cotta. During this time, Italians discovered the method of applying colored enamels to these materials. The Della Robbias, working in the 15th century, executed some of the finest work of this class.

Architectural developments of the 15th century are also evident in France. In Dordogne, the chateau of Jumilhac-le-Grand dates to this period, and St Jean-de-Cole possesses a chateau with elements from the 15th, 16th, and 18th centuries. In Brittany, the town of Montfort-sur-Meu features a cylindrical keep from the 15th century, which survives as a remnant of its old ramparts. Additionally, the picturesque château at Combourg dates from the 14th and 15th centuries.

## Literature and Drama
The 15th century contributed to the ancient cycles of Scripture plays, known as Mysteries. While the original comic intentions in some of these works—such as the pageants of the Shepherds and Cain—originated with 14th-century writers, and the comic nature of the Noah pageant is evidenced by the testimony of Chaucer's Miller, the 15th-century writer demonstrated original power and unexampled skill in England regarding the development of situation and character. Notable examples include the comic scenes in the *Wakefield* (or *Towneley*) *Plays*, specifically the episode of Mak and the fictitious infant. In contrast, the *York Plays* produced the final pageant of the *Last Judgment*, which is characterized by noble elocution, a fine conception of the situation, and a majestic, slow movement. This skill in comic development is contrasted with the work of Lydgate, as seen in his *Pageant for Presentation before the King at Hereford*.

In the realm of printing, the 15th century is noted for the production of finely printed books.

## Religious and Intellectual Thought
In the period of the Hussite wars, Peter Chelcicky (born in the late 14th century and died in 1460) emerged as an independent religious writer. Described as the Tolstoy of the 15th century, Chelcicky was a strenuous enemy of the Church of Rome but did not join any Hussite parties. His primary beliefs included a horror of bloodshed and a determination to accept all decrees of worldly authorities unresistingly, even those that were unjust. His major work is the *Sit viry* (the net of faith), and he also produced polemical letters to Bishop Nicolas of Pelhrimov and Archbishop John of Rokycan, as well as a *Postilla*.

Regarding ancient studies, some have assigned the authorship of two books on *Hieroglyphics*—which claim to be Greek translations of an Egyptian original by a man named Philippus—to the 15th century. While many statements in these books appear absurd, they provide evidence that the tradition of the values of hieroglyphic signs had not yet become extinct by the time of the author.

## Exploration and Geography
The beginning of the 15th century marked a turning point in the Norse settlements of Greenland. Communication between these settlements and the motherland of Norway was broken off at the end of the 14th and the beginning of the 15th century. Consequently, the knowledge the Norsemen possessed regarding their distant colony was gradually forgotten.
