# Historical Events and Developments Around 1587

The year 1587 serves as a significant chronological marker across various global regions and disciplines, marking transitions in North African governance, Japanese political and religious policy, European fortifications, and the evolution of English drama.

## North African Governance and Piracy
In North Africa, the period ending in 1587 represented a distinct era of maritime activity. From 1518 until the death of Uluch Ali in 1587, Algiers served as the primary seat of government for the beylerbeys of northern Africa, who exercised rule over Algeria, Tunisia, and Tripoli. During this first period, the beylerbeys acted as admirals of the sultan, commanding large fleets to conduct serious military operations for political objectives. While they were ferocious slave-hunters, the archive notes that their Christian enemies were neither more chivalrous nor more humane.

Following 1587, the nature of these operations shifted; plunder became the sole objective of the beylerbeys' successors. This brigandage was carried out by a corporation or class of captains known as *reises*, who commanded cruisers funded by capitalists. These *reises* paid ten per cent of the value of their prizes to the treasury of the pasha (or successors titled Bey, Dey, or Agha). 

The maritime capabilities of these pirates evolved during the 17th century. While they previously relied on galleys—which were confined to the coast and the Mediterranean—a Flemish renegade named Simon Danser introduced sailing ships. This innovation allowed pirates to range into the Atlantic as far as Iceland or the Canaries. Algiers became the most formidable port of this trade, making the term "Algerine" synonymous with Barbary pirate, though similar activities occurred in Tunis, Tripoli, and Moroccan towns such as Salli.

## Japanese Political and Religious Affairs
In Japan, 1587 was a year of significant activity for Hideyoshi. He took up residence in a specific location that year and, during the following New Year's festival, requested a visit from the Emperor. The Emperor complied in May 1587. The resulting five-day celebration followed precedents set by the Ashikaga shoguns Yoshimasa and Yoshimitsu, though Hideyoshi used his superior resources to enhance the event. The festivities included dancing, singing, couplet composing, and extensive gift-giving; presents to Court nobles ranged from 300 koku of rice to 5,530 ryo of silver.

Simultaneously, 1587 marked a turning point in the treatment of Christians in Japan. An edict was issued in 1587 against Christians, leading to the persecution of Jesuits.

## European Fortifications and Literature
In Tuscany, the town of Grosseto underwent significant structural changes during the late 16th century. Between 1574 and 1587, Francis I constructed fortifications that, along with later work by Ferdinand I (1587–1609), created a hexagonal enceinte with projecting bastions and two gates.

In the realm of English literature, 1587 marks the beginning of Christopher Marlowe's career as a dramatist (which lasted until 1593). In this year, Marlowe produced *Tamburlaine the Great*, an heroic epic in dramatic form consisting of two parts of five acts each. The play was printed in 1590 and was noted for its "meteoric blank lines" and its departure from the "riming mother wits" of older drama. Marlowe's work in this period established tragedy and created a specific diction and versification that influenced later writers, including Milton and Shakespeare.
