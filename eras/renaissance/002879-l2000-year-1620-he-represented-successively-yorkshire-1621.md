# 1620

The year 1620 was marked by significant developments in colonial expansion, political shifts in European courts, and advancements in mathematical and astronomical science.

## Colonial Enterprises and Migration
In 1620, John Carver, one of the "Pilgrim Fathers," emigrated to America aboard the *Mayflower*. Carver, who had previously taken refuge in Holland around 1607 due to religious persecution and served as a deacon in the church at Leiden, founded the Plymouth colony. Following the signing of the "Compact," his election as governor was confirmed. He was subsequently re-elected for the following year on March 23, 1620 (1621 N.S.), though he died in early April from the effects of sunstroke.

Simultaneously, George Calvert, the 1st Baron Baltimore, began his involvement in colonial ventures. In 1621, he established a small settlement in Newfoundland, though his efforts toward colonial enterprise became a primary focus of his later life.

## European Political Affairs
In England, George Calvert continued his ascent in public service. Having served as secretary to Robert Cecil and held various clerkships, Calvert was granted a pension of L2000 a year in 1620. He would later represent Yorkshire in the House of Commons in 1621.

In France, the political landscape was influenced by the rise of Charles, the first duke of Luynes. A favorite of Louis XIII, Luynes had previously arranged the assassination of Concini in 1617. In 1620, Luynes suppressed an uprising of nobles. Additionally, the family of Luynes expanded its titles during this period; Léon, seigneur de Brantes, became the duke of Luxemburg-Piney through his marriage to Margaret Charlotte of Luxemburg in 1620.

In the imperial service, the military struggle between the house of Austria and Protestant princes continued. René Descartes, who had volunteered for the Bavarian service, was present at the battle of Weisser Berg near Prague on November 8, 1620, an event that blasted the hopes of the elector palatine.

## Science and Mathematics
The year 1620 saw a milestone in the application of mathematics to astronomy. Johannes Kepler published an ephemeris for 1620, dedicated to Baron Napier. In this work, Kepler employed logarithms for the first time. This invention was later detailed in his treatise *Chilias Logarithmorum*, which circulated in manuscript form three years before its 1624 printing and helped popularize the method in Germany.

## Notable Births
The year 1620 marked the birth of Jacques de Castelnau, the grandson of the diplomat Castelnau. Jacques de Castelnau would later distinguish himself as a marshal of France during the ministries of Mazarin and Richelieu in the wars against Spain and Austria.
