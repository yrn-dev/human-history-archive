# BANCROFT, RICHARD (1544-1610)

Richard Bancroft was a high-ranking English cleric who served as the archbishop of Canterbury. Born in 1544 at Farnworth in Lancashire, he pursued his higher education at Cambridge, where he attended both Jesus College and Christ's College. He earned his B.A. degree in 1567 and his M.A. in 1570.

## Early Ecclesiastical Career
Following his ordination around 1570, Bancroft served as chaplain to Richard Cox, the bishop of Ely. In 1575, he was presented to the rectory of Teversham in Cambridgeshire, and by the following year, he had become one of the university preachers. His academic advancement continued alongside his clerical duties; he graduated as a B.D. in 1580 and a D.D. in 1585. During this period, he was presented to the rectory of St Andrew's, Holborn, in 1584.

## Rise to Prominence
Bancroft's rapid promotion within the church was attributed to his zeal as a champion of the church and his perceived abilities. In 1585, he was appointed as the treasurer of St Paul's cathedral in London, and a year later, in 1586, he became a member of the ecclesiastical commission. He also served as a canon of Westminster starting in 1587.

On February 9, 1589, Bancroft delivered a sermon at Paul's Cross based on 1 John iv. 1. This sermon was a passionate attack on the Puritans, in which he denounced the right of private judgment, caricatured Puritan motives, and described their proceedings. He spoke of the divine right of bishops in language so strong that a councillor to the queen suggested it constituted a threat against the supremacy of the crown. Following this, in 1590, he was made a prebendary of St Paul's. He also served as chaplain to Archbishop Whitgift and Lord Chancellor Hatton.

## Leadership and Episcopacy
In June 1597, Bancroft was consecrated as the bishop of London. Due to the incapacity for business and the age of Archbishop Whitgift, Bancroft was virtually invested with the power of primate and assumed sole management of ecclesiastical affairs. In this capacity, he directed proceedings against several individuals, including Thomas Cartwright and his associates, "Martin Mar-Prelate," and John Penry. In the case of Penry, Bancroft caused "seditious writings" to be intercepted and delivered to the lord keeper.

Beyond his ecclesiastical duties, Bancroft was involved in diplomacy. In 1600, he was part of an embassy sent to Embden to attempt to settle disputes between the Danes and the English, though this mission failed.

## Archbishopric and Later Years
Bancroft was present at the death of Queen Elizabeth and eventually ascended to the position of archbishop of Canterbury. In October 1610, he sent an official invitation to the French scholar Casaubon to come to England. Casaubon arrived in the suite of the ambassador, Lord Wotton of Marley, and was received with flattery by James I. The English bishops viewed Casaubon as an "Anglican ready made" because his independent study of the Fathers had led him to a *via media* between Romanism and Puritanism.
