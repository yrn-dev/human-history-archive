# Arctic Ocean and the Barents Sea

The Arctic Ocean forms the northern boundary of Asia, with a coastline that generally falls between 70 and 75 degrees North, placing it within the Arctic circle. The extreme northern point of this region is Cape Chelyuskin (also known as Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi), located at 78 degrees North. The boundary of Asia meets the Arctic Ocean at a point nearly opposite the island of Novaya Zemlya, following a line from the Caspian Sea that runs north along the Ural Mountains near the 60th meridian.

## The Barents Sea
The Barents Sea is a specific portion of the Arctic Ocean. It is demarcated by the islands of Spitsbergen, Franz Josef Land, and Novaya Zemlya, as well as the north coast of Europe and various smaller intervening islands. Named after a Dutch navigator, the sea extends from approximately 67 to 80 degrees North and from 20 to 60 degrees East. The area off the Kola peninsula's Murman coast, located in the southern part of the Barents Sea, is occasionally referred to as the Murman Sea.

## Exploration of the Region
Willem Barents, a Dutch navigator born around the middle of the 16th century, conducted three expeditions to search for a north-east passage to eastern Asia. 

In 1594, Barents sailed from Amsterdam with two ships, reaching the west coast of Novaya Zemlya. He followed the coast northward but was forced to turn back near its northern extremity. In 1595, he led a second expedition of seven ships toward the strait between Vaygach Island and the Asiatic coast, though he arrived too late to find open water. 

During his third journey, Barents sighted Spitsbergen and Bear Island. After rounding the north of Novaya Zemlya, his vessel became beset by ice, forcing his party to winter in the north. On June 13, 1597, the party left the ship in two open boats; while most members escaped, Barents died on June 30, 1597. In 1871, the house where Barents spent the winter was discovered, and part of his journal was recovered in 1875.

## Scientific Observations
The region has served as a site for astronomical study. On August 9, 1896, William Shackleton photographed the "flash spectrum" of the reversing layer—a phenomenon representing direct emissions from a low-lying solar envelope—during a total solar eclipse observed in Novaya Zemlya.

## Asia's Eastern Extremity
While the Arctic Ocean defines the north, the most easterly point of Asia is East Cape (also known as Dezhnev or Vostochnyi), located at 190 degrees East at the entrance of the Bering Strait. From this point, the boundary of the continent follows the China Sea and the Northern Pacific coast down to the extremity of the Malay Peninsula. This coastline is characterized by the recession of the Yellow Sea and the Sea of Okhotsk, as well as the projections of the Korea and Kamchatka peninsulas.
