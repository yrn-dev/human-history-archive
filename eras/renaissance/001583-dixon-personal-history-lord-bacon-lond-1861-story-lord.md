# Dixon, Personal History of Lord Bacon and Story of Lord Bacon's Life

## Overview of Works by W. Hepworth Dixon
W. Hepworth Dixon is the author of two biographical works concerning the life of Lord Bacon. The first, titled *Personal History of Lord Bacon*, was published in London in 1861. The second, titled *Story of Lord Bacon's Life*, was published in the same city in 1862. These texts are listed among the biographical sources available for the study of Lord Bacon's life.

## Context of Lord Bacon
The subject of Dixon's biographies, Sir Francis Bacon (1561–1626), was later known as Lord Verulam and served as the Lord Chancellor of England. He is recognized as a leader in the development of systematic scientific publication during the seventeenth century. Bacon advocated for the use of experiment and observation, principles he shared with his pupil and the experimental philosopher of Colchester, Dr. Gilbert (1540–1603). 

To articulate his vision of a great service dedicated to scientific research, Bacon utilized the form of a Utopian story in his work, *The New Atlantis*. His influence contributed to the eventual rise of national bodies for the exchange of knowledge and the encouragement of research, such as the Florentine Society and the Royal Society of London. These organizations provided a platform for inventions and a critical perspective on the theological history of the world.

## Related Biographical and Philosophical Literature
Dixon's works exist alongside a wide array of other biographical and critical studies of Lord Bacon. Other notable biographical sources include:
* J. Spedding's *The Life and Letters of Lord Bacon* (1861) and *Life and Times of Francis Bacon* (1878).
* Dr. Rawley's *Life*, found in the Ellis-Spedding editions.
* P. Woodward's *Early Life of Lord Bacon* (1902).
* T. Fowler's *Francis Bacon* (1881), part of the "English Philos." series.
* R. W. Church's *Bacon*, part of the "Men of Letters" series (1884).
* John Campbell's *Lives of the Chancellors* (1845).

In addition to biographical accounts, Bacon's philosophy has been analyzed by numerous scholars. These include Kuno Fischer, who published *Fr. Bacon* in 1856 (with a second edition in 1875 and an English translation in 1857), and G. L. Craik, who wrote the three-volume *Lord Bacon, his Writings and his Philosophy* (1846–1847, edited 1860). Other philosophical examinations were conducted by A. Dorner, J. v. Liebig, Ad. Lasson, E. H. Boehmer, Ch. Adam, Barthelemy St Hilaire, and H. Heussler.

## Major Works of Lord Bacon
The archival records highlight several of Bacon's most significant publications, which have been the subject of various editions and introductions:
* ***Novum Organum***: Notably edited by T. Fowler (1878, 1889), which included a bibliography and an introduction to Bacon's philosophy, superseding an earlier 1855 edition by G. W. Kitchin.
* ***Essays***: This work has seen more than twenty editions since 1870, including versions edited by Archbishop Whately, W. Aldis Wright, F. Storr and Gibson, E. A. Abbott, John Buchan, A. S. West, and W. Evans.
* ***Advancement of Learning***: Edited by W. Aldis Wright (1866, 1900), F. G. Selby (1892–1895), and H. Morley (1905).
