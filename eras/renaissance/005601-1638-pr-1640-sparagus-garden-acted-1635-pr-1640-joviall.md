# Richard Brome

Richard Brome (d. 1652) was an English dramatist whose creative output consisted of fifteen pieces. His work was characterized by strongly marked characters, the development of his plots, and the inclusion of curious information. These elements reflect the significant influence of Ben Jonson, who was Brome's master. Over time, the relationship between the two men evolved from that of master and servant into a friendship. Much of the available information regarding Brome's personal character is derived from lines written to him by Jonson, which served as a prefix to the 1632 play *The Northern Lasse*, the work that established Brome's reputation.

## Dramatic Works and Style
Brome's genius was situated entirely within the realm of comedy. His plays were published both in collections and as separate volumes. 

The collection *Five New Playes* (published around 1652 and edited by Alex. Brome) included the following works:
* *Madd Couple Well Matcht* (acted circa 1639)
* *Novella* (acted 1632)
* *Court Begger* (acted 1632)
* *City Witt*
* *The Damoiselle or the New Ordinary*

A subsequent volume titled *Five New Playes* (1659) contained:
* *The English Moor, or The Mock Marriage*
* *The Love-Sick Court, or The Ambitious Politique*
* *Covent Garden Weeded*
* *The New Academy, or The New Exchange*
* *The Queen and Concubine*

Additionally, Brome published several plays separately, including *The Antipodes* (acted 1638, printed 1640), *The Sparagus Garden* (acted 1635, printed 1640), and *The Queenes Exchange* (printed 1657). He also wrote *A Joviall Crew, or the Merry Beggars*, which was acted in 1641 and printed in 1652; this work was later revised as an "opera" in 1731.

## Collaborations and Legacy
Beyond his solo works, Brome collaborated with Thomas Heywood on *The late Lancashire Witches*, which was printed in 1634. His collected works, *The Dramatic Works of Richard Brome*, were published in 1873.
