# Academic and Artistic Institutions

The history of academic and artistic instruction across Europe is characterized by a diverse array of state and free universities, specialized professional schools, and prestigious academies of fine arts. These institutions have served as centers for higher learning, the preservation of history, and the development of the visual arts.

## Higher Education in Belgium
Higher instruction in Belgium is divided between universities and their attached schools. The universities at Ghent and Liege operate as state institutions, while those at Brussels and Louvain are free. Louvain is the only university among them that maintains a faculty of theology. As of the 1904-1905 academical year, the student populations were as follows:
*   Louvain: 2,134 students
*   Liege: 1,983 students
*   Brussels: 1,082 students
*   Ghent: 899 students

This resulted in a total of 6,098 inscribed students. Liege is particularly noted for its attached technical schools.

## Specialized and Professional Schools
Beyond general universities, there are numerous state-aided schools for specific purposes:

**Military Instruction:** Brussels hosts both the *Ecole Militaire* and the *Ecole de Guerre* (staff college), the latter of which has an average attendance of twenty. Other military education includes the school of cadets at Namur, army schools at various stations such as Bouillon, and regimental schools and evening classes for illiterate soldiers. Additionally, a riding school at Ypres provides obligatory courses for the horse artillery and cavalry.

**Arts and Music:** The Royal Academy of Fine Arts at Antwerp is a famous school of painting; it is supported by eighty-four other academies for teaching drawing throughout the kingdom. Musical education is provided by royal conservatoires in Liege, Ghent, Antwerp, and Brussels, supplemented by sixty-nine minor conservatoires.

**Commercial and Navigation Schools:** There are 181 schools for professional and commercial education, with the Commercial Institute of Antwerp noted as an excellent school for clerks. Navigation is taught at schools in Nieuport, Ostend, and Antwerp. Following the 1906 wreck of the training-ship "Comte de Smet de Naeyer," it was decided to establish a stationary training-ship in the Scheldt, similar to the "Worcester" on the Thames.

## Learned Societies and National Academies
The Belgian Royal Academy was founded in 1769 and later revived in 1818. To encourage literary style and research, the government provides periodical prizes. Other academic bodies include the French Academy, which crowned a book on Froissart written by the Belgian historian Baron Constantine Bruno Kervyn de Lettenhove.

## The Royal Academy and Artistic Career Paths
The Royal Academy has played a significant role in the careers of various painters and architects. John Downman, an English portrait painter known for his exquisite pencil drawings, was educated at the Royal Academy schools and later became an associate of the institution. 

In the realm of architecture, the English architect Giuseppi Bonomi was the subject of a dispute within the Royal Academy. While Sir Joshua Reynolds, the president, wished for Bonomi to be a full Academician to fill the vacant chair of perspective, the majority of Academicians opposed this. Bonomi was elected as an associate only, a decision reached by the president's casting vote.

## Influence of Academies on Individual Artists
Many artists utilized academies as stepping stones for their professional development:
*   **Pieter Breughel:** A Flemish painter and son of a peasant from the village of Breughel, he received instruction from Koek. After spending time in Italy and France, he went to Antwerp, where he was elected into the Academy in 1551. He eventually settled and died in Brussels.
*   **Jacob Maris:** A Dutch landscape painter famous for his depictions of misty skies and water, Maris began his studies at the Antwerp Academy before studying in Paris at Hébert's studio.
*   **Gabriel Francois Doyen:** A French painter who obtained the Grand Prix at age twenty, Doyen gained admission to the Academy following the success of his work "Death of Virginia" in 1758. He was later appointed a professor at the Academy of Painting in 1776.
*   **Heinrich Julius Klaproth:** A German Orientalist and traveler, Klaproth was appointed to the academy in St Petersburg, which later despatched him to the Caucasus for linguistic and ethnographical exploration between 1807 and 1808.
