# Elzevirs and French First Editions

In the field of book collecting, the value of a volume is often determined by its appeal to the mind, the imagination, or the eye. A book's aesthetic beauty may be derived from its illustrations, the harmony of its paper and print, or its binding. In France, there has historically been a strong attraction to fine bindings. Consequently, the high prices frequently quoted for French first editions and Elzevirs are often due more to their 17th and 18th century jackets than to the actual content of the books themselves.

## The Elzevir Publishers
The Elzevirs were publishers based in Leiden. They are noted for producing miniature editions, particularly those published around 1635, which have attracted collectors. However, the archive notes that printing in the 17th century was generally at its worst, reaching its lowest depths in England during the second quarter of that century.

The Elzevir firm was involved in the publication of various significant works:
* **Greek Texts:** Bonaventura and Abraham Elzevir published two editions in 1624 and 1633. These were based primarily on the text of Beza. The preface to the 1633 edition referred to the first as "textum... nunc ab omnibus receptum," which served as the origin for the term "Textus Receptus" (or T.R.), a name often applied to the ordinary Greek Text.
* **Philosophical Works:** Elzevir published the collected works of Descartes in nine volumes in 1713.
* **Other Publications:** The Elzevirs published the letters of Grotius, specifically the *Epistolae ad Gallos* (12mo, Leiden, 1648), though this specific work is noted for containing press errors. Additionally, an edition of Adam of Bremen's work is attributed to Elzevir.

## Influence on Biblical Scholarship
The Elzevir text became the basis for all non-critical editions on the European continent. While England more generally followed the 1550 edition of Stephanus, both the Stephanus and Elzevir editions were important because they established a definite text for comparison. This allowed scholars to move toward the investigation and collation of other manuscripts.

Subsequent scholars utilized the Elzevir text for further study:
* **John Fell:** In 1675, the dean of Christ Church published the Elzevir text accompanied by an enlarged apparatus.
* **Griesbach:** Griesbach used the Elzevir edition as the basis for constructing his critical text. In instances where he differed from the Elzevir reading, he placed the Elzevir reading in the inner margin alongside other readings he deemed worthy of consideration.

## 17th Century Bookbinding and Printing
The appreciation for old bindings has seen a significant increase in England since 1891, following an exhibition at the Burlington Fine Arts Club. While French bindings were highly prized, England produced its own sought-after styles, including embroidered bindings, English blind stamped bindings, and those attributable to Samuel Mearne. Mearne's leather bindings, produced during the time of Charles II, were the daintiest England had produced to that point and were imitated to some extent from the great French binder Le Gascon.

During the first half of the 17th century, many English books featured attractive copper-engraved portraits or frontispieces despite poor printing quality. Small New Testaments, Bibles, and prayer-books were often covered in gold or silver thread and gay embroideries of coloured silks. For trade purposes, sheepskin and rough calf were the most common materials. Over time, the practice of lettering books on the back became more favorable than lettering on the fore-edges or sides.
