# Extra Services in the Roman Breviary

In the context of the Roman Breviary, "Extra Services" refers to a specific section of the liturgical book that contains offices outside the primary seasonal and saint-specific cycles.

## Placement and Structure
The Roman Breviary is organized into four seasonal parts: winter, spring, summer, and autumn. Within each of these seasonal divisions, the content is further categorized into five distinct parts:
1. The Psalter
2. *Proprium de Tempore* (the special office of the season)
3. *Proprium Sanctorum* (special offices of saints)
4. *Commune Sanctorum* (general offices for saints)
5. Extra Services

These five components are occasionally published as separate volumes.

## Contents of Extra Services
The Extra Services section includes specific liturgical offices that are not tied to the general seasonal or saint-specific calendars. These include:
* **The Little Office of the Blessed Virgin Mary**: A dedicated office for the Blessed Virgin Mary.
* **The Office of the Dead**: This service is obligatory on All Souls' Day.
* **Diocesan Offices**: Offices that are peculiar to each individual diocese.

## Related Liturgical Context
The Extra Services exist alongside other complex structures within the Breviary. The *Proprium Sanctorum* provides liturgical formularies, psalms, and lessons for saints' festivals based on the secular month, consisting largely of hagiological biography. The *Commune Sanctorum* contains ancient offices for twelve different groups or classes, such as confessors, virgins, martyrs, and apostles. These general offices include patristic homilies on the Gospels read at the third nocturn, which serve as a summary of theological instruction.

Because approximately 90% of the days in the year have been allotted to saints over the centuries, the *Proprium Sanctorum* has encroached upon the *Proprium de Tempore*, which remains a primary concern for those seeking to revise the Breviary.

## Comparison with Other Breviaries
While the Roman Breviary follows the four-season structure described above, other versions have differed in format. For example, the Sarum or Salisbury Breviary, which saw wide use with editions printed in Venice (1483) and Paris (1556, 1557), never exceeded two parts, whereas modern Breviaries are nearly always printed in four volumes.
