# "The Blessed Weeper" and "An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart"

"The Blessed Weeper" and "An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart" are religious poetical works written by the English poet Nicholas Breton. Both pieces were published in 1601.

## Authorship and Context
Nicholas Breton (1545?–1626) was an English poet from an old family based at Layer-Breton, Essex. The son of William Breton and Elizabeth Bacon, he was likely born in the parish of St Giles without Cripplegate at a "capitall mansion house" in Red Cross Street. While there is no official record of his university attendance, the diary of the Rev. Richard Madox indicates that Breton was "once of Oriel College" and was present in Antwerp in 1583.

Breton was a popular writer among his contemporaries, though he was forgotten by the subsequent generation. His body of work is diverse, consisting of satires, pastoral poems, religious poems, and various prose tracts. Although his references to Mary Magdalene and his praise of the Virgin led some to suggest he was a Catholic, his prose writings provide abundant proof that he was an ardent Protestant.

## Religious Poetry of Nicholas Breton
The works "The Blessed Weeper" and "An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart" belong to Breton's collection of religious poetry. "The Blessed Weeper" is included within a larger work titled *A Divine Poem*, which also contains "The Ravisht Soul." 

Breton's religious poems are characterized by a certain "sweetness" and "excess of fluency," which some find wearisome; however, these works are viewed as the expression of an earnest and devout mind.

## Other Works by Nicholas Breton
Breton produced a wide array of poetry and prose throughout his career. His poetical output includes:
*   **Early Works:** *The Workes of a Young Wit* (1577) and *A Floorish upon Fancie* (1577).
*   **Later Poetry:** *The Pilgrimage to Paradise* (1592), *Marie Magdalen's Love: a Solemne Passion of the Soules Love* (1595), *The Soules Heavenly Exercise* (1601), *The Soules Harmony* (1602), *The Mother's Blessing* (1602), and *The Soules Immortall Crowne* (1605).
*   **Pasquil Series:** *Pasquil's Fooles cappe* (1600), *Pasquil's Mistresse* (1600), and *Pasquil's Passe and Passeth Not* (1600).
*   **Other Titles:** *Melancholike Humours* (1600), *Olde Madcappe newe Gaily mawfrey* (1602), *A True Description of Unthankfulnesse* (1602), *The Passionate Shepheard* (1604), and *The Honour of Valour* (1605).

His prose works include *Wit's Trenchmour* (1597), *The Wil of Wit* (1599), and *A Poste with a Packet of Mad Letters* (1603). The latter contains letters signed "N.B." that are considered autobiographical, detailing personal griefs such as imprisonment, sickness, and experiences in war.

## Patronage and Later Life
Breton enjoyed the patronage of Mary, countess of Pembroke, writing many works in her honor. This relationship continued until 1601, the same year "The Blessed Weeper" and "An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart" were published, at which point the countess appears to have withdrawn her favor. 

Breton married Ann Sutton in 1593 and had a family. He is believed to have died shortly after the publication of his final work, *Fantastickes*, in 1626.
