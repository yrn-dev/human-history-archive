# Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum

*Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum* is a collection of poetry published in Amsterdam in 1637. The work serves as a repository for the Latin verse of several Scottish writers, including Sir Robert Aytoun, Dempster, and Barclay.

## Contributions of Sir Robert Aytoun
Sir Robert Aytoun (1570–1638) was a Scottish poet and the son of Andrew Aytoun of Kinaldie, Fifeshire. Educated at the university of St Andrews, he was incorporated as a student of St Leonard's College in 1584 and graduated with an M.A. in 1588. Aytoun spent several years living in France, where he wrote a Latin panegyric upon the accession of James VI to the English throne. This work brought him immediate favour at court, and he was subsequently knighted in 1612. He served as private secretary to the queens of Charles I and James I and held various other lucrative offices.

Aytoun's Latin poems are printed in the first volume of *Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum* on pages 40 through 75. These works include the aforementioned panegyric, as well as *Basia, sive Strena ad Jacobum Hayum*, *Lessus in funere Raphaelis Thorei*, *Carina Caro*, and an *Epicedium in obitum Thoma Rhodi*. The collection also contains various epitaphic and occasional minor pieces.

While Aytoun's Latin works were published in *Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum*, his English poems—which established him as one of the earliest Scots to utilize the southern standard as a literary medium—were preserved in a manuscript in the British Museum (*Add. MSS.* 10,308) prepared by his nephew, Sir John Aytoun. His first English poem was *Diophantus and Charidora*.

## Other Contributors
The volume also features the work of other scholars and poets:

*   **Dempster**: A portion of Dempster's Latin verse is printed in the first volume of the collection, spanning pages 306 to 354. Dempster was known for his versatility in history, biography, law, criticism, and philology, though he was criticized for a style that was often barbarous and a tendency toward romancing and absurd exaggerations in Scottish questions.
*   **Barclay**: Barclay's shorter poems, organized into two books, are printed in *Delitiae Poetarum Scotorum* on pages 76 through 136 of the first volume. In the dedication of these poems to Prince Charles of England, Barclay makes reference to an earlier publication of his titled *Sylvae*.

## Subsequent Collections and Editions
The works of Sir Robert Aytoun were later collected by Charles Rogers in *The Poems of Sir Robert Aytoun*, privately printed in London in 1871. This followed an earlier, less satisfactory issue by Rogers in 1844. The 1871 edition is noted for including additional poems that cannot be ascribed to Aytoun; specifically, "Sweet Empress" and the old version of "Auld Lang Syne" are certainly not his, and the poem "I do confess thou'rt smooth and fair" is suspected of being the work of another. 

Some of Aytoun's English poetry also appeared in the *Bannatyne Miscellany* (1827) and Watson's *Collection* (1706-1711).
