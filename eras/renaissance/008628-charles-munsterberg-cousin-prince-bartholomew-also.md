# Charles of Munsterberg

## Appointment and Role
Charles of Munsterberg was a nobleman appointed as the regent of Bohemia by King Louis. His appointment occurred in 1522 upon the arrival of King Louis in Bohemia from Hungary, where Louis had also been elected king. In the process of restructuring the Bohemian administration, King Louis dismissed all existing state officials, including the powerful supreme burgrave Zdenek Leo, lord of Rozmital, and installed Charles of Munsterberg to serve as regent during the king's absences.

## Lineage and Relations
Charles of Munsterberg was a grandson of King George. He was also a cousin of Prince Bartholomew of Munsterberg. Prince Bartholomew, another grandson of King George, had previously served as the leader of a confederacy formed by the towns in 1513 to defend their rights against the curtailment of privileges during the reign of King Vladislav.

## Political Context in Bohemia
The appointment of Charles of Munsterberg took place following a period of significant constitutional instability and civil discord in Bohemia. Under the reign of King Vladislav, the royal prerogative had decreased while the power of the nobility reached an unprecedented height. This shift occurred at the expense of peasants and townsmen. A decree in 1487 established serfdom in Bohemia, and the "regulations of King Vladislav" codified the rights of knights and nobles while ignoring those of the towns, assuming townsmen possessed no inherent rights beyond privileges granted by the sovereign with noble consent.

By 1517, during the nominal reign of King Louis (who had been crowned at age three), the estates met at the diet of St Wenceslas on September 28. At this meeting, the three estates agreed to live together in friendly intercourse. The citizens renounced certain claimed privileges, while the other estates recognized municipal autonomy and sanctioned the presence of towns at the meetings of the diet.
