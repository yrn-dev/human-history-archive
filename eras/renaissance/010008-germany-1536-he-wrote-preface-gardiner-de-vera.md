# Germany

## Diplomatic and Religious Relations with England
During the 16th century, Germany served as a focal point for English diplomatic efforts and religious reform. Robert Barnes, an English reformer and former prior of the convent of Austin Friars, became a primary intermediary between the English government and Lutheran Germany after returning to England in 1531. Barnes had previously visited Wittenberg, where he became acquainted with Luther.

In 1535, Barnes was dispatched to Germany with the objective of persuading Lutheran divines to approve of the divorce of King Henry VIII from Catherine of Aragon. Later, four years following this mission, he was involved in negotiations regarding the marriage of Anne of Cleves. These efforts were part of a policy directed by Thomas Cromwell. However, by 1538, Henry VIII had refused to adopt Lutheran theology, a position further solidified by the statute of Six Articles in 1539.

## Ecclesiastical and Political Missions
Other English figures engaged with Germany and its surrounding regions for political and academic purposes. In 1536, an unnamed individual was sent to North Germany to advance "the cause of the Gospel." This person also wrote a preface to Gardiner's *De vera Obedientia* in 1536; this text denied papal supremacy and asserted royal supremacy, a position that was received with delight by the Lutherans.

Academic pursuits also led English citizens to German institutions. Andrew Boorde, an English physician and author, visited the university of Wittenberg as part of his broader studies in medicine and surgery across Europe. Similarly, Roger Ascham traveled to Innsbruck as part of an embassy. Ascham's letters from this period were later published in English in 1553 as a "Report" on Germany.

## Modern History and Peace Treaties
In the early 20th century, Germany was a central party to the conclusion of the Great War. Following the conflict, the international community established the Covenant of the League of Nations. The formal end of hostilities was marked by the signing of the armistice terms and the subsequent Treaty of Peace with Germany in 1919.
