# Archbishop Parker

Archbishop Parker (d. 1575) was a prominent ecclesiastical figure in England who contributed significantly to the academic, literary, and religious landscape of his time. His legacy is marked by his patronage of education, his efforts in biblical scholarship, and his leadership within the Church of England.

## Academic Patronage and Bequests
Archbishop Parker provided support for students from the King's School in Canterbury, establishing a system of scholars. One such recipient was the English dramatist Christopher Marlowe, who entered Cambridge as one of Parker's scholars from the King's School. Marlowe matriculated at Benet (Corpus Christi) College on March 17, 1571.

Following his death, Parker's personal library was left to Corpus Christi College at the same university, although the bequest was made under severe restrictions. This act was part of a broader historical trend during the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries where large book collections were often bequeathed to public resting-places or educational institutions.

## Biblical Scholarship and the Bishops' Bible
Between 1563 and 1565, Archbishop Parker took the initiative to improve the authorized *Great Bible*. This effort was intended to challenge the increasing popularity of the Calvinistic *Genevan Bible*. Parker undertook the labor of organizing the project by dividing the Bible into parcels and distributing them among learned men and bishops. These individuals were tasked with perusing and collating their assigned sections and adding short marginal notes to correct or illustrate the text. This project resulted in the creation of the Bishops' Bible.

## Ecclesiastical Leadership and Influence
As a leader in the church, Parker's tenure was characterized by a certain level of severity, particularly in his treatment of nonconformists. This approach was noted by Burghley, who later advised Parker's successor, Archbishop Grindal, to mitigate such severity. 

Parker's influence extended to the preservation of English history. Under his direction, an edition of Asser's *Annales rerum gestarum Alfredi magni* (a chronicle of English history from 849 to 887 and an account of the life of Alfred) was printed in 1574. This edition was based on a manuscript that dated from approximately 1000, though the printed version contained various alterations and interpolations.
