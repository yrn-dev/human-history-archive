# 1667: A Year of Scientific, Political, and Personal Transitions

The year 1667 serves as a point of intersection for various figures across Europe, marking significant shifts in scientific inquiry, national governance, and the granting of aristocratic titles.

## Scientific Developments and Publications
The mid-to-late 17th century saw a proliferation of works in physics, astronomy, and mathematics. Giovanni Alfonso Borelli, an Italian physicist and physiologist, published *De vi percussionis* in Bologna in 1667. Borelli, who had served as a professor of mathematics at Pisa starting in 1656, would later return to Messina in 1667. His broader body of work sought to apply mechanical principles to the movements of the animal body, establishing him as the founder of the iatrophysical school.

In the field of astronomy, Giovanni Domenico Cassini was enhancing his reputation through his determinations of the rotation periods of Venus, Mars, and Jupiter between 1665 and 1667. Cassini, who had been a professor of astronomy at the university of Bologna since 1650 and served as a hydraulic engineer for the Bologna senate, would eventually be recruited by Louis XIV in 1669 to work at the observatory in Paris.

## Political and Diplomatic Affairs
In Sweden, the year 1667 marked the end of the life of Count Gustaf Bonde. A statesman and lord high treasurer, Bonde was a persistent advocate for a pacific policy and strict economy. He had opposed the Polish adventure of Charles X and, in 1661, had proposed a plan to make Sweden independent of foreign subsidies through trade development and the recovery of alienated estates. Despite his tenacity, his program was undermined by the ambition of oligarchs and the financial ruin caused by a costly expedition against Bremen in 1665. Bonde retired from government shortly before his death in 1667.

In England, the political landscape saw shifts in administrative roles. Sir George Carteret, who had served as the treasurer of the navy from 1661 to 1667, transitioned to the role of deputy treasurer of Ireland in 1667. While Carteret had provided valuable service during the Dutch War, he had faced censure from parliament due to his lax accounting methods.

## Aristocracy and Personal Milestones
The year 1667 saw the first bestowal of the English title of duke of Kendal. In May of that year, the title was granted to Charles, the infant son of the duke of York (the future James II), who died in the same year.

Additionally, 1667 marked the birth of Ehrengarde Melusina, born on December 25 in Emden. The daughter of Gustavus Adolphus, count of Schulenburg, Melusina would later become the mistress of King George I and be created duchess of Kendal in 1719.

In the realm of English craft and industry, the year 1667 falls within a period of family growth for John Dwight, the distinguished potter. Between 1667 and 1671, three of Dwight's children were baptized in Wigan, Lancashire, where he resided while serving as secretary to Bishop Hall. Dwight would later gain fame for his stoneware and his patents for transparent earthenware.
