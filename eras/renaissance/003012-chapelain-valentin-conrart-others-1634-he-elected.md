# Jean Louis Guez de Balzac

## Life and Career
Jean Louis Guez de Balzac (1594–1654) was a French author born in Angouleme. During his youth, at the age of eighteen, he traveled in Holland accompanied by Theophile de Viaud, a relationship that later resulted in bitter recriminations between the two men. Balzac early in his career found patronage from the duc d'Epernon and his son, Cardinal de la Valette, the latter of whom took him to Rome.

Balzac developed a significant reputation through letters written to his acquaintances and to various high-ranking figures at the French court. This success made him an habitue of the Hotel de Rambouillet. While Richelieu offered him lavish promises and praise, he never provided Balzac with the preferment he desired. Balzac eventually retired to the chateau of Balzac. He died in Angouleme on February 18, 1654.

## Literary Contributions and Style
Balzac is primarily remembered for his *Lettres*. A first collection of these letters was published in 1624 and received with great favor; a second collection followed in 1636, and the *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* was printed the following year. Although the matter of these letters is described as affected and empty, they demonstrate a mastery of style. Balzac is credited with introducing precision and clearness into French prose, emphasizing idiomatic elements to develop the language on national lines. This work is considered a reform of French prose parallel to the reform executed by Malherbe in verse.

In addition to his letters, Balzac authored several longer works and dissertations on style, including:
* *Le Prince* (1631), an eulogy of Louis XIII.
* *Socrate chretien* (1652), noted as the best of his longer works.
* *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).

## Professional Associations and the Academy
While retired at his chateau, Balzac maintained correspondence with others, including Valentin Conrart and Jean Chapelain. In 1634, Balzac was elected to the Academy. 

The influence of Valentin Conrart extended beyond his correspondence with Balzac; Conrart hosted a circle at his house that later developed into the Académie Française. This circle included writers such as Jean Desmarets (Sieur de Saint-Sorlin), who was admitted to the group following the 1631 success of his romance *Ariane*. Furthermore, Valentine Conrart was responsible for collecting Balzac's *Oeuvres* into two volumes in 1665. Balzac's work also reached an international audience, with numerous English translations appearing from the 17th century.
