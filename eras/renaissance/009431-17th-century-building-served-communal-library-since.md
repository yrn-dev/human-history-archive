# 17th Century Architecture and Institutions

The 17th century left a significant mark on the urban landscapes of Europe and North America, manifesting in the construction of civic buildings, religious structures, and the development of academic and maritime infrastructure.

## Civic and Administrative Buildings
In France, several towns retain prominent 17th-century administrative structures. In Dole, both the hotel de ville and the Hotel-Dieu date from this period. Similarly, in Castres, the hotel de ville—which houses the municipal library and a museum—occupies the former bishop's palace, a structure designed by Jules Mansart in the 17th century. 

In the United States, the township of Marblehead in Massachusetts contains a variety of houses dating back to the 17th and 18th centuries.

## Religious Structures and Art
The 17th century saw the construction and ornamentation of various ecclesiastical buildings. In Castres, the church of St Benoit, formerly a cathedral, dates from the 17th and 18th centuries. In Honfleur, the church of St Leonard's dates from the 17th century, though it retains a rose-window and an ogival portal from the 16th century. 

Interior architectural and artistic additions from this era are also noted in several locations. The abbey-church in Luxeuil-les-Bains features a curious 17th-century organ loft designed as an immense bracket supported by a colossal figure of Hercules. In Calais, the church of Notre-Dame possesses a fine high altar from the 17th century. Additionally, the church of St Andre in Grenoble houses a 17th-century monument dedicated to Bayard (1476-1524).

## Academic and Cultural Institutions
The University of Bologna, while founded much earlier, was associated with Marcello Malpighi, a renowned anatomist of the 17th century. The university's physical history is marked by various transitions; while it lacked a fixed residence for centuries, a building was constructed for it in 1562 by Carlo Borromeo. This specific building has served as the communal library since 1838.

In other regions, the turn of the century saw the development of monastic libraries. The abbey of Tepl, founded in 1193, has an actual building dating from the end of the 17th and the beginning of the 18th century, which contains a library of incunabula and rare manuscripts.

## Other 17th Century Developments
Beyond civic and religious architecture, the 17th century influenced urban infrastructure and residential building. In Honfleur, the West basin of the port dates from this period. In Luxeuil-les-Bains, several buildings from the 17th century remain alongside a 15th-century cloister. In the town of Douai, houses from the 17th century are described as numerous. In Calais, the belfry is attributed to the 16th and early 17th century.
