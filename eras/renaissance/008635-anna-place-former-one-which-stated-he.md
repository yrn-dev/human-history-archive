# Anna

## Anna of Bohemia
Anna, the daughter of King Vladislav, played a central role in the succession of the Bohemian throne following the death of Louis. During a period of great disorder and near anarchy in Bohemia, Archduke Ferdinand of Austria (later Emperor Ferdinand I) laid claim to the throne as Anna's husband. Ferdinand was one of several candidates, including King Sigismund of Poland, the dukes Louis and William of Bavaria, various German princes, and Bohemian noblemen such as Leo of Rozmital.

On October 23, 1526, a group of twenty-four electors—chosen equally from the three estates—elected Ferdinand of Habsburg as king. This event marked the permanent accession of the Habsburg dynasty to the Bohemian throne. Although Ferdinand was devoted to Rome, he initially showed moderation in religious matters to pursue his goal of establishing a hereditary right to the throne. In 1541, after a great fire in Prague destroyed all state documents, Ferdinand obtained the consent of the estates to replace the original charter. While the former document stated that he had become king by election, the new charter stated that he had been recognized as king due to the hereditary rights of his wife, Anna. This substitution caused significant dissatisfaction and contributed to subsequent troubles.

## Anna Comnena
Anna Comnena was a Byzantine princess and author of the *Alexiad*. In her writings, she records the conquests of her father in Asia Minor, the war of Epirus, and his Cilician war against Bohemond and Tancred. Her work also details the transactions of her apostolic father regarding the Manichaeans, a heresy she sought to refute, and provides information on the sect of Gnostics known as the Bogomiles, founded by a monk named Basil.

Anna Comnena's accounts include the transplantation of Jacobites and Paulicians from Armenia to Thrace by John Zimisces in A.D. 970. She also records a story—supported by Greek writers Glycas and Zonaras—regarding a "Barbarian" who was shut up with a dead cock, though this specific detail is unknown to Latin sources. Additionally, Anna wrote about her own betrothal, praising and bewailing a "handsome boy" with hawk's eyes and red and white skin who was betrothed to her after the rupture of his "barbaric nuptials."

## Anna Visscher
Anna (1584–1651) was one of the daughters of Roemer Visscher in Holland. Described as a woman of singular beauty, graceful manners, and universal accomplishment, she was a successful author of miscellaneous writings and a descriptive and didactic poem titled *De Roemster van den Aemstel* (The Glory of the Aemstel). Along with her sister Tesselschade, Anna's company attracted many gifted youths of the era, several of whom were unsuccessful suitors.

## Anna, Countess of Macclesfield
Anna, the daughter of Sir Richard Mason of Sutton, was the wife of Macclesfield. In March 1698, she was divorced by an act of parliament, marking the first time such a divorce was granted without a prior decree from an ecclesiastical court. She had two children known as Savage, whose reputed father was Richard Savage, 4th Earl Rivers; the poet Richard Savage claimed to be the younger of these children. Around the year 1700, Anna married Colonel Henry Brett. She died in 1753 at the age of eighty-five. Her daughter, Anna Margaretta Brett, served as a mistress to George I.
