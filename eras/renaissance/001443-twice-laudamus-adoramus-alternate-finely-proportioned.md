# Musical and Structural Proportions in the Works of Bach

The compositions of Bach exhibit a meticulous approach to structural design and the adaptation of existing musical material. This is particularly evident in his masses and passions, where the balance of movements and the proportion of musical elements are carefully managed to create cohesive works of art.

## The A Major Mass and the Gloria
The A major mass is regarded as a work that appears original, featuring a Kyrie described as one of Bach's most individual utterances. The Gloria of this mass begins with a spirited symphony for strings accompanying a triumphant polyphonic chorus. The structure of the Gloria is characterized by specific shifts in mood and tempo:

*   **Et in terra pax**: At these words, the time changes, and a single solemn melody in the altos is softly accompanied by two flutes.
*   **Laudamus te**: The material from the beginning of the Gloria returns.
*   **Adoramus te**: The triumphant material is interrupted by a calm slow movement in a different key and for a different voice.

In a finely proportioned design, the "laudamus" and "adoramus" sections alternate twice. The sequence concludes with the words "gratias agimus tibi propter magnam gloriam tuam," which are set for the full chorus using the music of the slow movement, accompanied by strings and flutes.

## Adaptation and Originality
Bach frequently utilized adaptations from earlier works, such as church cantatas, to compile his masses. For example, the Gloria of the A major mass shares the same framework—with no difference in harmony or bar—as the last chorus of the cantata *Halt im Gedaechtniss*. Despite this shared framework, the two are considered independent works of art. In the cantata version, the beginning is for instruments only, and the slow movement is scored for two oboe d'amore and a flute, with the basses singing "Peace be unto you" while separated from the rest of the chorus.

Other adaptations vary in their effectiveness. In the G minor mass, a tremendous denunciatory chorus titled *Herr, deine Augen sehen nach dem Glauben* is used for the *Kyrie eleison*, though it is noted as being unfit for that specific text. The four short masses were evidently compiled for use in a church with a small orchestra, and with the exception of four movements, all are traceable to church cantatas.

## Self-Criticism and the Passions
Bach's commitment to perfect proportions is also seen in his process of self-criticism. The *Passion according to St John* underwent two revisions, during which it was reduced in scale through the omission of some of its finest numbers to achieve a result of perfect proportions. 

The material removed during these revisions was repurposed in various ways:
*   One magnificent aria with chorale remained unused.
*   Two numbers were replaced by substitutes that were considered finer.
*   Other rejected sections were transformed into one of the most remarkable church cantatas, *Du wahrer Gott*.
*   The greatest of the figured chorales was transferred to the *Passion according to St Matthew*, where it serves as the crown of the first part.
