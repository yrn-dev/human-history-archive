# RICHARD BROME

## Biography and Influence
Richard Brome (d. 1652) was an English dramatist whose professional origins were as a servant to Ben Jonson. Brome owed a significant debt to Jonson, whose influence is evident in the development of Brome's plots, the amount of curious information included in his works, and his strongly marked characters. Over time, the initial relationship of master and servant evolved into a friendship. Much of the available knowledge regarding Brome's personal character is derived from lines written to him by Ben Jonson, which served as a prefix to the 1632 play *The Northern Lasse*, the work that established Brome's reputation.

## Dramatic Works and Style
Brome's genius was situated entirely within the realm of comedy. He is credited with leaving fifteen pieces. His works were published through various collections and separate editions:

**Five New Playes (ed. by Alex. Brome, 1652?)**
This collection contained the following works:
* *Madd Couple Well Matcht* (acted 1639?)
* *Novella* (acted 1632)
* *Court Begger* (acted 1632)
* *City Witt*
* *The Damoiselle or the New Ordinary*

**Five New Playes (1659)**
This later collection included:
* *The English Moor, or The Mock Marriage*
* *The Love-Sick Court, or The Ambitious Politique*
* *Covent Garden Weeded*
* *The New Academy, or The New Exchange*
* *The Queen and Concubine*

**Separately Published Works**
Several of Brome's plays were published independently:
* *The Antipodes* (acted 1638, published 1640)
* *The Sparagus Garden* (acted 1635, published 1640)
* *A Joviall Crew, or the Merry Beggars* (acted 1641, published 1652; this work was later revised in 1731 as an "opera")
* *The Queenes Exchange* (published 1657)

Additionally, Brome collaborated with Thomas Heywood on the work *The late Lancashire Witches*, which was published in 1634.

## Legacy and Publications
The *Dramatic Works of Richard Brome* were published in 1873. Further analysis of his contributions to English dramatic literature can be found in A.W. Ward's *History of English Dramatic Literature* (1899), specifically volume iii, pages 125-131.
