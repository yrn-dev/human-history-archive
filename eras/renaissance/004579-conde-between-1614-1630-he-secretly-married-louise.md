# Francois de Bassompierre

## Early Life and Court Career
Francois de Bassompierre (1579–1646) was a French courtier born at the castle of Harrouel in Lorraine. He was the son of Christophe de Bassompierre (1547–1596) and belonged to an old family that had served the dukes of Lorraine and Burgundy for generations. After receiving his education in Italy and Bavaria, Bassompierre was introduced to the court of Henry IV in 1598. He became a great favorite of the king and fully participated in the dissipations associated with court life.

## Military Service
Bassompierre's military career spanned several decades and various theaters of war. In 1600, he participated in a brief campaign in Savoy, and in 1603, he fought in Hungary on behalf of the emperor against the Turks. In 1621 and 1622, he served during the Huguenot rising, services which earned him the dignity of marshal of France. He was present with the royal army during the siege of La Rochelle in 1628 and distinguished himself in 1629 during a campaign against rebels in Languedoc.

In 1615, Bassompierre purchased the position of colonel-general of the Swiss and Grisons from Henri, duc de Rohan. Due to this role, he was sent to raise troops in Switzerland when Louis XIII marched against Savoy in 1629. His military career concluded following a short campaign in Italy.

## Diplomatic Career
While successful in the military, Bassompierre's career as a diplomatist is characterized as a failure. In 1621, he served as envoy extraordinary to Madrid to resolve a dispute regarding Spain's seizure of the Valteline forts, resulting in a fruitless treaty. In 1625, he was sent on a mission to Switzerland that was equally futile. Finally, in 1626, he was sent to London to secure the retention of the attendants and Catholic ecclesiastics of Henrietta Maria, the wife of Charles I.

## Political Involvement and Personal Life
Bassompierre was involved in the internal power struggles of the French court. In 1614, he assisted Marie de' Medici in her struggle against the nobles. However, following her failure in 1617, he remained loyal to King Louis XIII and assisted the royalists in routing the supporters of Marie de' Medici at Ponts-de-Ce in 1620.

Regarding his personal life, the personal influence of Henry IV had previously deterred Bassompierre from marrying Charlotte de Montmorency, the daughter of the constable Montmorency and later the princesse de Conde. Instead, between 1614 and 1630, Bassompierre was secretly married to Louise Marguerite, who was the widow of Francois, prince de Conti. Through this marriage, Bassompierre became implicated in the plot to overthrow Richelieu on the "Day of Dupes" in 1630.
