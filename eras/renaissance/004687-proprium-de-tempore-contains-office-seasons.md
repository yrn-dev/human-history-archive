# Proprium de Tempore

The *Proprium de Tempore* is a primary division of the Roman Breviary, serving as the special office of the seasons of the Christian year. This liturgical conception, which spans from Advent to Trinity, developed gradually over time.

## Structure and Contents
The *Proprium de Tempore* provides the complete service for every week-day and Sunday. Its contents include:
*   Proper hymns, responsories, and antiphons.
*   A structured course of daily Scripture-reading.

The daily Scripture readings average approximately twenty verses per day. These readings are arranged according to the liturgical season as follows:
*   **Advent:** Isaiah.
*   **Epiphany to Septuagesima:** Pauline Epistles.
*   **Lent:** Patristic homilies, with Genesis read on Sundays.
*   **Passion-tide:** Jeremiah.
*   **Easter to Whitsun:** The Apocalypse, Catholic epistles, and Acts.
*   **Whitsun to August:** Kings and Samuel.
*   **August to Advent:** Prophets, Maccabees, and Wisdom books.

## Relation to the Roman Breviary
The Roman Breviary is organized into four seasonal parts: winter, spring, summer, and autumn. Within each of these seasonal parts, the *Proprium de Tempore* is one of five components. The other four are the Psalter, the *Proprium Sanctorum* (special offices of saints), the *Commune Sanctorum* (general offices for saints), and Extra Services. These five parts are frequently published as separate volumes.

## Conflict with the Proprium Sanctorum
A significant problem for those revising the Breviary is the encroachment of the *Proprium Sanctorum* upon the *Proprium de Tempore*. Because approximately 90% of the days in the year have been allotted to a saint over the centuries, the offices of saints often displace the seasonal office.

This conflict also affects the use of the Psalter. If the *Proprium de Tempore* were strictly adhered to, it would provide equal opportunities for the recital of every psalm. However, the multiplication of saints' festivals—which often utilize the same special psalms—results in the constant repetition of about one-third of the Psalter, while the remaining two-thirds are rarely recited.

## Harmonization and Rubrics
The difficulty of harmonizing the *Proprium de Tempore* with the *Proprium Sanctorum* is only partially addressed by thirty-seven chapters of general rubrics. To provide further assistance, a "Catholic Churchman's Almanack" known as the *Ordo Recitandi Divini Officii* is published in various dioceses and countries. This document provides minute directions for proper reading for every day of the year.
