# Ermolao Barbaro

## Life and Education
Ermolao Barbaro (also known as Hermolaus Barbarus) was an Italian scholar born in Venice on May 21, 1454. During his youth, he was sent to Rome to study under Pomponius Laetus. He later completed his academic education at the university of Padua, where he was appointed as a professor of philosophy in 1477. 

Barbaro returned to Venice two years after his appointment at Padua, but he was forced to return to Padua following an outbreak of the plague in his home city. Throughout his career, he was sent on various missions to high-ranking individuals, including Pope Innocent VIII.

## Political Conflict and Banishment
In 1491, Pope Innocent VIII nominated Barbaro to the significant office of patriarch of Aquileia. However, this appointment was not ratified by the Venetian senate. The senate's refusal was based on the fact that Barbaro had accepted the position without first obtaining the senate's sanction, which was contrary to the law. 

As a result of this action, Barbaro was banished and compelled to resign from the patriarchate. This resignation was enforced under the threat that his father's property would be confiscated as a vicarious punishment. Following these events, Barbarus resided in Rome, where he received a small pension from the pontifical government.

## Death
Ermolao Barbaro died in Rome on June 14, 1493, although some sources suggest his death occurred two years later. It is probable that he died from the plague.

## Scholarly Works
Barbaro was an editor and translator of several classical works. His most notable contributions include:

*   ***Castigationes Plinianae* (1492):** In this work, Barbaro claimed to have made 5,000 corrections to the text of Pliny's *Natural History*.
*   ***Themistius' Paraphrases* (1480):** Paraphrases of certain works written by Aristotle.
*   ***Aristotle's Rhetorica*:** This work was published in 1544.
*   ***Castigationes in Pomponium Melam* (1493):** A scholarly correction of Pomponius Mela.
