# History of Bretagne

The study of Bretagne's history is documented through a wide array of antiquarian, administrative, and genealogical sources, ranging from early modern chronicles to 19th-century sociological and archaeological studies.

## Antiquities and Early Settlement
The antiquities of Bretagne have been a subject of political controversy. Early illustrations of the region's history are found in the works of Hadrian Valesius (*Notitia Galliarum*), M. D’Anville (*Notice de l’Ancienne Gaule* and *Etats de l’Europe*), Longuerue (*Description de la France*), and the Abbe de Vertot, who published a two-volume *Hist. Critique de l’Etablissement des Bretons dans les Gaules* in 1720.

There are differing historical interpretations regarding the arrival of the insular Britons. Authors such as Gallet (*Mémoires sur la Bretagne*) and Daru (*Histoire de Bretagne*) suggest that Bretagne was independent at the time the insular Britons sought refuge there, and that most arrived as fugitives rather than conquerors. Conversely, M. Lappenberg (*Geschichte von England*) proposes that the region was settled by a military colony of British soldiers (*laeti* or *Milites limitanei*) during the usurpation of Maximus (381, 388), who provided the colony with its civilization and name.

## Administrative and Social History
The administrative evolution of Bretagne is detailed in several specialized works. A. Dupuy authored *La Réunion de la Bretagne à la France* (1880) and *Études sur l'administration municipale en Bretagne au XVIII^e siècle* (1891). The region's rural social structures are analyzed by Henri Sée in *Étude sur les classes rurales en Bretagne au moyen âge* (1896) and *Les Classes rurales en Bretagne du XVI^e siècle à la Revolution* (1906), while A. du Chatellier examined the agricultural classes in *L'Agriculture et les classes agricoles de la Bretagne* (1862).

Specific historical events and figures have also been the focus of study. J. Lemoine wrote on the "bonnets rouges" or the revolt of the stamped paper in 1675 (*La Révolte du papier timbré ou des bonnets rouges en Bretagne*), and the influence of the duc d'Aiguillon is explored in works by M. Marion and B. Pocquet.

## Archaeological and Ecclesiastical Records
Anatole Jean-Baptiste Antoine de Barthelemy (1821–1904), a French numismatist and archaeologist, contributed significantly to the record of Bretagne. In collaboration with J. Geslin de Bourgogne, he published *Etudes sur la revolution en Bretagne* (1858). Between 1855 and 1879, Barthelemy produced an exhaustive six-volume work titled *Anciens eveches de Bretagne; histoire et monuments*. He also published *Choix de documents inedits sur l'histoire de la ligue en Bretagne* in 1880.

## Cultural and Literary Perspectives
The region's folklore and artistic nature are captured in various 19th-century texts. F. M. Luzel documented *Légendes chrétiennes de la Basse-Bretagne* (1881) and *Veillées bretonnes* (1879), while Anatole le Braz wrote *Vieilles Histoires du pays breton* (1897) and *La Légende de la mort* (1902). H. du Cleuziou provided a visual and descriptive account in *Bretagne artistique et pittoresque* (1886), and T. A. Trollope recorded impressions in *A Summer in Brittany* (1840).

## General Histories
Comprehensive histories of the region have been produced by several authors over the centuries, including:
* Bertrand d'Argentré (*Histoire de Bretagne*, 1586)
* Dom Lobineau (*Histoire de Bretagne*, 1702)
* Dom Morice (*Histoire de Bretagne*, 1742-1756)

* Arthur de la Borderie (*Histoire de Bretagne*, 1896 seq.)

Additionally, J. Loth examined the migration of Bretons to Armorique between the 5th and 7th centuries in *L'Émigration bretonne en Armorique du V^e au VII^e siècle* (1883).
