# Walsingham and the Babington Plot

## The Babington Conspiracy
In April 1586, a plot was formed to murder Queen Elizabeth and her ministers. The conspiracy aimed to organize a general Roman Catholic rising within England to liberate Mary, queen of Scots. The leadership of this plot was held by the priest John Ballard and Anthony Babington. Babington, the son of Henry Babington of Dethick in Derbyshire and Mary, daughter of George, Lord Darcy, had been raised secretly as a Roman Catholic. As a youth, he served as a page to Mary, queen of Scots, at Sheffield, developing an ardent devotion to her.

The conspiracy was viewed as the most dangerous of recent years by Walsingham and Mendoza, the Spanish ambassador, the latter of whom was one of the plot's chief instigators. The movement involved a large number of Roman Catholics and had ramifications throughout the country. Philip II of Spain supported the enterprise, describing it as "just and advantageous to the holy Catholic faith" and "so Christian." He promised to provide an expedition once the assassination of the queen had been carried out.

## Role of Walsingham and the Betrayal
Anthony Babington's involvement was characterized by vanity and open folly. Seeking a token of appreciation from Mary for his services, he engaged in a long correspondence with her. These communications were intercepted by the spies of Walsingham. 

The plot collapsed on August 4, when John Ballard was seized. Following his capture, Ballard betrayed his comrades, an act that likely occurred under torture. In the aftermath, Babington attempted to secure his own safety and organize the foreign expedition by applying for a passport to go abroad, claiming he intended to spy on refugees. While his passport was delayed, Babington offered to reveal a dangerous conspiracy to Walsingham, but Walsingham did not reply. Subsequently, the ports were closed.
