# The Sackpfeife and Dudelsack

The *Sackpfeife* or *Dudelsack* of Germany is a family of bag-pipe instruments of some importance. As described and illustrated by Michael Praetorius, these instruments were produced in no fewer than five distinct sizes.

## General Construction
The bag-pipe is a complex reed instrument of great antiquity that serves as a link between the primitive organ and the syrinx by utilizing a reservoir for wind supply. This allows the performer to regulate sound-producing pressure using their arm. The instrument consists of an air-tight leather bag featuring three to five apertures, each containing a short tube or fixed stock. These stocks serve as air-chambers to protect the reeds and act as sockets for the pipes.

There are three types of pipes used in the instrument:
1. A blow-pipe, which is a simple valved insufflation tube used to fill the bag.
2. The chaunter (or chanter), which is the melody-pipe. It is always made to speak via a double-reed and may feature a bell, keys, lateral holes, and either a cylindrical or conical bore.
3. The drones, which are jointed pipes with a cylindrical bore. These produce a single fixed note and generally terminate in a bell, lacking lateral holes.

## Varieties of the German Sackpfeife
The family of German bag-pipes includes several specific models with varying pitches and configurations:

### The Grosser Bock and Bock
The *Grosser Bock*, or double-bass bag-pipe, is a formidable instrument characterized by a single, very long cylindrical drone. Both the drone and the chaunter terminate in a curved ram's horn, which gives the instrument its name. The chaunter of the *Grosser Bock* features a thumb-hole at the back, a vent-hole in front, and seven finger-holes. Its drone is tuned to G, one octave below the chaunter. The *Bock* is constructed similarly but is pitched a fourth higher, in C.

### The Schaeferpfeife and Huemmelchen
The *Schaeferpfeife* is equipped with two drones tuned to F and B flat. According to Praetorius, the upper notes of its chaunter suffer from faulty intonation; this cannot be corrected because the instrument lacks the thumb-hole found in all other varieties of the family. The *Huemmelchen* also possesses two drones, which are tuned to C and F.

### The Dudey
The *Dudey*, also known as the treble sackpfeife, is the smallest member of the family. It features three drones tuned to E flat, B flat, and E flat. The compass of its chaunter ranges from F or E flat to C or D.

## Other Bag-pipe Variations
Beyond the standard family, Praetorius described a specific *sackpfeife* from Magdeburg. This version was pitched a third lower than the *Schaeferpfeife* and was somewhat larger. It featured two chaunters mounted in a single stock, each with a thumb-hole at the back and three holes in front. The left-hand chaunter sounded G, A, B, C, D, while the right-hand chaunter sounded D, E, F, G, A, allowing the performer to play simple two-part melodies. This Magdeburg instrument featured a conical bore and lacked keys.

Other regional variations include the French *musette*, which is similar in pitch to the *Huemmelchen* but is inflated using bellows rather than a blow-pipe. The *Calabrian bag-pipe* uses a blow-pipe for inflation and a bag made of goatskin with the hair remaining on the exterior. It contains two chaunters and two drones fixed in one stock; the drones and chaunters all utilize double reeds. In the Calabrian variety, each chaunter has three or four finger-holes, and the right-hand pipe includes a key enclosed in a perforated box to cover the fourth hole.
