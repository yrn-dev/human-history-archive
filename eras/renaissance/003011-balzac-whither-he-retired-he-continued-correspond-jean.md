# Jean Louis Guez de Balzac

## Life and Early Career
Jean Louis Guez de Balzac (1594–1654) was a French author born in Angouleme. At eighteen years of age, he traveled to Holland in the company of Theophile de Viaud, a relationship that later resulted in bitter recriminations between the two men. Early in his career, Balzac was befriended by the duc d'Epernon and his son, Louis, Cardinal de la Valette, who brought him to Rome.

Balzac developed a significant reputation through letters written to his acquaintances and various high-ranking figures at the French court. This success made him an habitue of the Hotel de Rambouillet, though it appears his success turned his head to some extent. While Richelieu offered him lavish promises and praise, he never provided the preferment that Balzac expected.

## Literary Contributions and Style
Balzac is primarily remembered for his *Lettres*. A first collection was published in 1624 and was received with great favor; a second collection followed in 1636, and the *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* was printed the following year. Although the subject matter of these letters is described as affected and empty, they demonstrate a mastery of style. Balzac is credited with introducing precision and clearness into French prose, emphasizing idiomatic elements and encouraging the development of the language on national lines. This work is viewed as a reform of French prose parallel to the reform Malherbe executed in verse.

In addition to his correspondence, Balzac authored several longer works and dissertations on style. These include:
* *Le Prince* (1631), an eulogy of Louis XIII.
* *Socrate chretien* (1652), considered the best of his longer works.
* *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).

## Later Years and Recognition
Balzac eventually retired to the chateau of Balzac. During this period of retirement, he maintained correspondence with others, including Valentin Conrart and Jean Chapelain. In 1634, he was elected to the Academy. He died on February 18, 1654, in Angouleme.
