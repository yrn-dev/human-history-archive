# Bibliography of Illustrated Books and Art (1469–1525)

The study of illustrated books from the late 15th and early 16th centuries involves a diverse array of regional bibliographies and specialized art histories. During this period, primitive woodcuts in incunabula became highly appreciated, leading to a significant increase in the price of rare volumes.

## Regional Bibliographies and Woodcuts

The archive identifies several key scholarly works focusing on the production of illustrated books across Europe between 1469 and 1525. 

In Italy, the primary references include Lippmann's *Wood Engraving in Italy in the 15th Century* (1888) and Kristeller's *Early Florentine Woodcuts* (1897). Additionally, the duc de Rivoli (Prince d'Essling') authored the *Bibliographie des livres a figures venitiens 1469-1525*, which was published in 1892 with a new edition released in 1906.

For the German region, Muther's *Die deutsche Bucherillustration der Gothik und Fruhrenaissance* (1884) is cited as a leading text. Material concerning France is found in Claudin's *Histoire de l'imprimerie en France* (1900), while Sir W.M. Conway's *The Woodcutters of the Netherlands in the 15th Century* (1884) covers Holland and Belgium.

## Market Value and Notable Works

The market for illustrated books of this era varies based on the rarity and celebrity of the volume. Some well-known works have remained more accessible; for instance, the *Nuremburg Chronicle* (1493) seldom fetches more than L30, and the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* (Venice, 1499) seldom exceeds L120. However, rarer books from this period are often priced in the hundreds.

## Related Artistic Studies

Beyond general bibliographies, specific artists and ornamental styles of the early 16th century have been documented. Butsch's *Die Bucherornamentik der Renaissance* (1878) provides information on book ornamentation. 

Extensive literature also exists regarding the works of Albrecht Durer. His life and art are detailed in various texts, including M. Thausing's *Durer, Geschichte seines Lebens und seiner Kunst* (1876, 2nd ed. 1884) and A. Springer's *Albrecht Durer* (1892). Specific periods of Durer's travels are also analyzed, such as his stay in Basel from 1492 to 1494 (D. Burckhardt, 1892) and his stay in Venice from 1494 to 1495 (G. von Terey, 1892). Other contributions to Durer studies include the Durer Society's Publications (10 vols., 1898-1907), edited by S.M. Peartree and C. Dodgson.
