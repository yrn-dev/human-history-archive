# The Fall of Cesare Borgia and the Papacy of 1503

The year 1503 marked a period of significant political instability in Rome and the surrounding Italian territories, characterized by the sudden decline of Cesare Borgia's power and a rapid succession of popes.

## The Decline of Cesare Borgia
Early in 1503, Cesare Borgia returned to Rome, where he participated in the reduction of the last rebel Orsinis. By the summer, he was in the process of gathering troops for a new expedition in central Italy. However, his ambitions were halted when both he and his father, Pope Alexander VI, were simultaneously struck by fever. 

The illness was linked to a banquet held in the summer of 1503 at the sumptuous palace of Cardinal Adriano Castellesi (also known as Corneto). The banquet continued until nightfall despite an unhealthy season in which a malignant form of ague was rife. Cardinal Adrian was the first of the party to fall ill, followed by the pope a week later. Pope Alexander VI died on August 18, 1503.

The timing of the pope's death proved ruinous for Cesare, as he remained incapacitated at the time. This coincidence allowed his enemies to rise against him; the Orsinis again asserted themselves in Rome, and deposed princes in Romagna began preparing to reclaim their territories. Although Cesare attempted to intimidate the cardinals using his bravos and Don Michelotto, the effort failed, and he was forced to leave Rome in September.

## Papal Successions and the Loss of Territory
Cesare hoped the Spanish cardinals would elect a candidate favorable to his house. The resulting conclave elected Francesco Todeschini-Piccolomini as Pius III. While Pius III was peaceful and respectable, he was in poor health and old age. During his brief tenure, Cesare's dominion began to collapse. With Venetian assistance, Guidobaldo, the duke of Urbino, returned to his duchy, and the lords of Pesaro, Rimini, and Piombino regained their lands. Only Cesena, held by a governor loyal to Cesare, remained under his control.

Pius III died on October 18, 1503, leading to another conclave. Cesare attempted to prevent the election of Giuliano della Rovere, an enemy of his house, but della Rovere's position had improved to the point that Cesare was forced to come to terms with him. On November 1, Giuliano della Rovere was elected as Pope Julius II.

## The End of Borgia's Power
Pope Julius II expressed no personal ill-will toward Cesare but insisted that the territories Cesare held must be restored to the church, stating a desire to recover what predecessors had wrongfully alienated. Julius II initially attempted to use Cesare as a tool to prevent Venice from establishing a protectorate over the principalities in Romagna. When this proved unsuccessful, the pope had Cesare arrested. This event ended Borgia's power, and he was compelled to surrender all his castles in Romagna, with the exception of one.
