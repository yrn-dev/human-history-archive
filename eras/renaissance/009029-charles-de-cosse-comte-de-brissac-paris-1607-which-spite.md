# Charles de Cosse, comte de Brissac

Charles de Cosse, the comte de Brissac, was a French military leader and nobleman who served as the grandmaster of artillery and held the governorships of both Picardy and Piedmont. He was the eldest son of René de Cossé, who had served as the *premier panelier* (chief pantler) to Louis XII.

## Military Career and Command
In 1550, Charles de Cosse took command of French troops during the war with Spain, traveling to Piedmont to lead these forces. His military activities in the regions of Piedmont, Montferrat, and the duchy of Milan were later documented by his secretary, François de Boivin, Baron de Villars. Boivin, who entered Brissac's service as a secretary, accompanied the marshal to Piedmont and remained in his employ during subsequent conflicts.

Following the French defeat at St Quentin in 1557, Boivin was dispatched to assure King Henry II of the continued support of Brissac. Although Boivin participated in the negotiations that resulted in the treaty of Cateau-Cambresis in April 1559, he was unable to prevent King Henry II from ceding the conquests that had been made by Brissac.

## Titles and Family
The fief of Brissac, located in Anjou, had been acquired at the end of the 15th century by the Cossé family, a noble family from the same province. In 1560, Brissac was established as a countship for Charles. 

Charles was one of two sons of René de Cossé to become marshals of France. His brother, Artus, served as the superintendent of finance and the *grand panetier* of France, distinguishing himself during the religious wars. The family's status continued to rise in subsequent generations; Charles II de Cossé, who served as governor of Paris and fought for the League, was created a marshal of France in 1594 by Henry IV after opening the gates of Paris to the king. In 1611, Brissac was elevated to a duchy within the peerage of France.

## Historical Documentation
The life and military campaigns of Charles de Cosse are detailed in the work *Memoires sur les guerres demelees tant dans le Piemont qu'au Montferrat et duche de Milan par Charles de Cosse, comte de Brissac*, published in Paris in 1607. Written by François de Boivin, the text is considered a valuable eye-witness testimony of the war, despite some drawbacks. A revised edition of these memoirs was later included in the *Memoires relatifs a l'histoire de France* (tome x.), edited by J.J.F. Poujoulat and J.F. Michaud in 1850.
