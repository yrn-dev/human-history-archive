# Guillaume Gouffier, Seigneur de Bonnivet

Guillaume Gouffier, Seigneur de Bonnivet (c. 1488–1525), was a French soldier and one of the most powerful royal favourites during the reign of Francis I of France. He was the younger brother of Artus Gouffier, seigneur de Boisy, who served as the tutor to Francis I. Having been brought up alongside the future king, Bonnivet maintained the intimacy and confidence of the monarch throughout his life, aided by his brilliant wit and handsome face.

## Military and Political Career
Bonnivet's rise in the royal court led to his appointment as the admiral of France in 1515. His influence extended into diplomacy and high-stakes politics; in 1519, he superintended the candidature of Francis I during the imperial election. To secure the votes of the electors, Bonnivet spent vast sums of money, though these efforts were ultimately unsuccessful.

Within the royal circle, Bonnivet was the implacable enemy of the constable de Bourbon and played a role in the constable's downfall. His military career was marked by both aggression and failure. In 1521, while in command of the army of Navarre, he occupied Fuenterrabia. He was likely responsible for the decision not to restore the location, an action that resulted in the renewal of hostilities.

## Italian Campaigns and the Battle of Pavia
In 1523, Bonnivet succeeded Marshal Lautrec in the command of the army of Italy. While he succeeded in entering the Milanese, he suffered a defeat that forced a disastrous retreat, during which the chevalier Bayard perished. Despite this, Bonnivet remained a principal commander of the army that Francis I led back into Italy at the end of 1524.

The culmination of his career occurred in February 1525. According to the historian Brantome, it was at Bonnivet's suggestion that the battle of Pavia was fought. Following the engagement, Brantome records that Bonnivet, recognizing the disaster he had caused, courted death and died heroically in the fight on February 24, 1525.

## Personal Life and Character
Despite his shortcomings as a diplomatist and general, Bonnivet's personal charms ensured his standing with the king. However, he was known for leading a licentious life. Brantome identifies him as the successful rival of the king in seeking the favours of Madame de Chateaubriand. Additionally, it is considered very probable that Bonnivet was the hero of the fourth story of the *Heptameron*, in which Marguerite d'Angouleme resisted his importunities.
