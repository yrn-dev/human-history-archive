# Toxophilus, Scholemaster, and the Life by Edward Grant

The *Toxophilus* and *Scholemaster* are significant English works authored by the scholar and writer Roger Ascham (c. 1515–1568). These texts, along with a biographical account of Ascham's life written by Edward Grant, have been subject to various editions and scholarly revisions over several centuries.

## Authorship and Early Life of Roger Ascham
Roger Ascham was born around 1515 in Kirby Wiske, a village located near Northallerton in the North Riding of Yorkshire. He was the third son of Margaret and John Ascham; the latter served as the steward to Lord Scrope of Bolton. Much of the available information regarding Ascham's early life is derived from Edward Grant, the headmaster of Westminster, who edited Ascham's letters and delivered a panegyrical oration on his life in 1576.

Ascham did not receive a traditional school education. Instead, he was educated in the home of Sir Humphry Wingfield, a barrister who served as speaker of the House of Commons in 1533. Under the guidance of a tutor named R. Bond, Ascham and other children in the household engaged in archery as their primary sport. Sir Humphry Wingfield personally supported this activity, bringing bows and shafts from London during term times to oversee the children's shooting in the field.

## Toxophilus and the Influence of Archery
The *Toxophilus* is Ascham's earliest English work. The text reflects the influence of his upbringing with Sir Humphry Wingfield, as Ascham attributes significant educational importance to the practice of archery. This perspective likely influenced the inclusion of archery in the statutes of various Elizabethan schools, including Harrow and St Albans. 

In the *Toxophilus* (specifically on page 120), Ascham identifies his educator as Sir Humphry Wingfield. This detail corrects a recurring mistake originated by Edward Grant, who had previously identified the educator as Sir Anthony Wingfield, the speaker's nephew.

## Scholemaster and Academic Development
The *Scholemaster* is another of Ascham's primary English works. His academic trajectory leading toward such scholarly writing began around 1530 when, at approximately fifteen years of age, he entered St John's College, Cambridge. At the time, this was considered the most learned and largest college in either university.

While at Cambridge, Ascham was influenced by Sir Thomas Smith and John Cheke, the latter of whom became a fellow during Ascham's first year. He was further guided by Robert Pember, noted for his facility with the Greek tongue. Following Pember's advice, Ascham adopted a method of learning Greek by teaching it to boys. In his Latin studies, he focused specifically on the works of Caesar and Cicero.

Ascham earned his B.A. on February 18, 1534/5. During his time at the college, the master, Dr. Nicholas Metcalfe, praised students who pursued "the new learning." Despite this, Ascham faced a period of difficulty when he spoke against the Pope, resulting in a "grievous rebuke" and punishment. Consequently, other fellows were warned not to vote for him during an election, though Ascham later regarded the day of his election as his "birthday."

## Publication History and Editions
The works of Roger Ascham and the biographical accounts provided by Edward Grant have a extensive publication history:

*   **Letters:** Ascham's letters were first published in 1576 and saw several editions, including one in Nuremberg in 1611. They were later re-edited by William Elstob in 1703.
*   **The Scholemaster:** This work was reprinted in 1571 and 1589. It underwent further editing by the Rev. J. Upton in 1711 and 1743, Prof. J.E.B. Mayor in 1863, and Prof. Edward Arber in 1870.
*   **The Toxophilus:** This text was republished in 1571, 1589, and 1788, with later editions produced by Prof. Edward Arber in 1868 and 1902.
*   **Collected Works:** In 1771, James Bennett edited Ascham's English works, which included a life written by Dr. Johnson; this was reprinted in 1815. Later, in 1864-1865, Dr. Giles published a four-volume set containing select letters, the *Toxophilus*, the *Scholemaster*, and the life written by Edward Grant.
