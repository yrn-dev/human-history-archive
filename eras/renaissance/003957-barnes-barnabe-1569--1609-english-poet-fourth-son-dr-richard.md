# BARNES, BARNABE (1569?-1609)

Barnabe Barnes was an English poet and playwright born in Yorkshire, possibly at Stonegrave, in either 1568 or 1569. He was the fourth son of Dr. Richard Barnes, who served as the bishop of Durham.

## Education and Early Life
In 1586, Barnes entered Brasenose College, Oxford. During his time there, Giovanni Florio served as his servitor. In 1591, Barnes traveled to France in the company of the earl of Essex, who was serving against the prince of Parma at the time.

## Poetic Works and Style
Upon returning from France, Barnes published *Parthenophil and Parthenophe, Sonnettes, Madrigals, Elegies and Odes*, which was entered on the Stationers' Register in 1593. This work was dedicated to William Percy, described as his "dearest friend," and Percy contributed a sonnet to the eulogies found in a later work titled *Offices*. The *Parthenophil* is characterized by passages of beauty and freshness, though some of its elaborate conceits are considered strained or over-ingenious. It is believed that the copy of this work held in the duke of Devonshire's library is unique, as it may have been printed for private circulation.

Barnes's poetry was heavily influenced by contemporary French sonneteers. He appears to have borrowed the title of *Parthenophil* from Hieronymus Angerianus, a Neapolitan writer of Latin verse. While a story can be outlined from the series of love lyrics in *Parthenophil*, the incidents are slight. Like other Elizabethan sonnet-cycles, the work exists as a blend of intellectual exercise in the imitation of Petrarch and the potential expression of real personal experience.

In 1595, Barnes published his second major work, *A Divine Centurie of Spirituall Sonnetts*.

## Dramatic and Prose Works
Barnes authored two plays. The first, *The Divil's Charter* (1607), was a tragedy focusing on the life of Pope Alexander VI and was performed before the king. The second, *The Battle of Evesham* (also referred to as *Hexham*), exists only as a manuscript traced to the beginning of the 18th century, though that manuscript is now lost.

In 1606, Barnes wrote a prose treatise titled *Offices enabling privat Persons for the speciall service of all good Princes and Policies*, which he dedicated to King James.

## Literary Affiliations and Controversies
Barnes was a supporter of Gabriel Harvey and experimented with classical metres. This alignment led to conflict with Thomas Nashe, who accused Barnes of various things, including the theft of a nobleman's chain at Windsor, though these accusations were apparently made without any proof.
