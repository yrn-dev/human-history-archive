# The Decameron and the Heptameron

The *Decameron* by Boccaccio and the *Heptameron* by the queen of Navarre are recognized as perhaps the best known examples of a specific species of literature. This literary tradition finds its common origin in the old French *fabliaux*, although some of the tales within these works are classical in nature or evidently Eastern.

## The Decameron
The *Decameron* is a foundational work of this genre. One of its most well-known features is its opening, which describes the plague that struck Florence in 1348.

## The Heptameron
The *Heptameron* was written by Marguerite d'Angoulême, the queen of Navarre. The work is constructed on the lines of Boccaccio's *Decameron* and consists of seventy-two short stories. These tales are told to one another by a company of gentlemen and ladies whose journey homewards from Cauterets is interrupted by the swelling of a river.

The book is considered strongly characteristic of the French Renaissance, reflecting the sensuality of the period, though in a form less coarse than the works of Rabelais. It also possesses a poetical spirit. Internal evidence suggests the *Heptameron* may have been a joint work involving several men of letters from Marguerite's household. Bonaventure Des Périers, who served as the queen's secretary and *valet-de-chambre*, transcribed the *Heptameron* for her; some writers have suggested that the work was actually his.

The *Heptameron* was not printed until 1558, ten years after the author's death, appearing under the title *Les Amants fortunés*. English translations of the work appeared later, including one in 1887 by A. Machen and an anonymous translation in 1894.

## Matteo Bandello and the Novelle
Matteo Bandello (1480–1562) was an Italian novelist born near Tortona. After a careful education and a period in the church, he resided in Mantua, where he educated Lucrezia Gonzaga. Following the battle of Pavia, which resulted in the confiscation of his property and the burning of his house in Milan, Bandello fled to France with the Italian general Cesare Fregoso. He eventually became the bishop of Agen in 1550.

Bandello is primarily famous for his extensive collection of *Novelle*, or tales, published in 1554 and 1573. These novels are considered the best written in imitation of the *Decameron*. While Italian critics have criticized Bandello's style as inelegant and negligent, and some of the tales are noted for gross obscenity, the works hold significant historical value. They provide insight into the social life of the period and exerted a major influence on Elizabethan drama. Specifically, Bandello supplied the stories that served as the basis for several of William Shakespeare's plays, likely via Paynter or Belleforest.

## Related Literary Contexts
The *Heptameron* exists alongside other works by the queen of Navarre, including *Letters* and a miscellaneous collection of poems, farces, and mysteries titled *Les Marguerites de la marguerite des princesses*. Other works attributed to her include the religious poem *Le Miroir de l'âme pécheresse*, which was translated by Queen Elizabeth, and *Le Navire*, a piece expressing despair over the death of her brother.

The influence of these narrative styles extended into various forms of Renaissance literature. For instance, Bonaventure Des Périers wrote the *Cymbalum Mundi* (1537), a work of skepticism consisting of four dialogues in imitation of Lucian. Additionally, the court of Marguerite d'Angoulême was known for permitting free discussions and inquiry, though this license of thought was often displeasing to both Catholics and Calvinists.
