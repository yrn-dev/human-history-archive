# Paris Bordone

Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. He began his professional training in 1509 when he entered the *bottega* of Titian. According to Vasari, who provides nearly all the known facts regarding Bordone's life, the artist did not spend many years under Titian's tutelage and instead sought to imitate the style of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, evidence suggests that the Giorgionesque elements in Bordone's early works were actually derived from Titian. Bordone's imitation of Titian was so precise that some of his paintings continue to be attributed to Titian to this day.

## Attribution of the "Baptism of Christ"
A point of scholarly contention exists regarding the "Baptism of Christ" located in the Capitoline gallery. Dr Bode, as well as Crowe and Cavalcaselle, ascribe this work to Paris Bordone. Conversely, Morelli identifies the painting as an early work by Titian.

## Career and Commissions
Bordone executed numerous important mural paintings in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, though all of these works have since perished. In 1538, he was invited to the court of Francis I in France. While he painted many portraits during his time there, no trace of them remains in French collections, with the exception of two portraits at the Louvre which were acquired later. On his return journey from France, Bordone undertook significant works for the Fugger palace at Augsburg, but these have also been lost.

## Artistic Style and Subjects
Bordone's body of work is characterized by unequal merit. His strengths include a dignified approach to portraiture, a realistic conception of the human figure, a certain nobility of style, and a golden harmony of color derived from Titian. In contrast, his figures are frequently unnatural or affected in their action, and his nudes are described as being somewhat coarse in form.

As a painter of the Renaissance, Bordone worked across several genres:
* **Portraiture:** This was the area in which he excelled.
* **Religious Works:** He painted a number of religious pictures.
* **Mythology and Allegory:** His output included mythological scenes, nymphs, cupids, and subjects based on the fables of Ovid.

## Surviving Works
The principal surviving work by Bordone is the "Fisherman and Doge," which is held at the Venice Academy. Other notable surviving pieces include:
* **The National Gallery, London:** A portrait of a lady and "Daphnis and Chloe."
* **Bridgwater House:** A "Holy Family."
* **Tadini collection at Lovere:** A "Madonna."
* **Other locations:** Two mythological pictures located in the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese, as well as paintings in the Duomo of Treviso.

## Historical Documentation
Very little has been written about Paris Bordone since the records of Vasari, aside from general references in works on Italian painting. In 1900, two specific contributions were made to his scholarship: a paper by P.G. Molmenti published in the *Nuova Antologia* on November 16, and a publication titled *Della Vita e delle Opere di Paris Bordone* by G. Biscaro and L. Barlo, released by the committee of the fourth centenary of Paris Bordone in Treviso.
