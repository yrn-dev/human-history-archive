# Ducal Authority and Francis II of Brittany

## Development of Ducal Power
Following a period where ducal power in Brittany strengthened and successfully curbed the influence of feudal nobles, the region experienced more than a century of undisturbed peace. However, this stability was interrupted in 1341 by the death of John III, who left no direct heir. This event triggered a war of succession between the houses of Montfort and Blois that lasted until 1364. This conflict functioned as an incident of the Hundred Years' War, with the houses of Montfort and Blois receiving support from the kings of England and France, respectively.

In 1364, John of Montfort was recognized as the duke under the title of John IV. His reign was marked by constant trouble, specifically his struggle with Olivier de Clisson. In contrast, John V, who died in 1442, was noted for an able and pacific policy.

## Administrative and Military Growth
During the reigns of John V and his successors—Francis I, Peter II, and Arthur III—ducal authority expanded remarkably. The dukes established a standing army and successfully implemented the levying of hearth taxes, known as *fouages*, throughout Brittany.

## Francis II and Conflicts with France
Francis II (1435–1488) faced significant conflict with the French crown. He fought against Louis XI, most notably during the War of the Public Weal. This friction arose as Louis XI attempted to assert royal control over the duchy of Brittany, which was practically independent at the time. During the "league of public welfare" in 1465, the rebellious duke of Brittany was joined with the territories of Charles the Bold.

Following his struggle with Louis XI, Francis II engaged in a conflict against Charles VIII known as "The Mad War" (*La Guerre Folle*).

## Annexation of the Duchy
The death of Francis II led to the invasion of Brittany by the king of France. In 1491, the king forced Francis's daughter, Anne of Brittany, to marry him. This event prepared the way for the reunion of France and Brittany. After the death of Charles VIII, Anne married Louis XII.

The definitive annexation of the duchy was settled by Francis I, who married Claude, the daughter of Anne of Brittany and Louis XII. Through a contract in 1532, the annexation was finalized, though the contract guaranteed the maintenance of the liberties and privileges of Brittany.

## Later Provincial Status
Until the time of the Revolution, Brittany maintained its own estates. However, the royal power consistently attempted to reduce the province's privileges. This led to vigorous resistance, particularly between 1760 and 1769, when the duc d'Aiguillon (E. A. de V. du Plessis Richelieu) fought against both the parliament and the estates.
