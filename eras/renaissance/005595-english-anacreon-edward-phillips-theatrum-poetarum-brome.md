# English Anacreon in Edward Phillips's Theatrum Poetarum

The designation "English Anacreon" is a title attributed to the poet Alexander Brome within the work *Theatrum Poetarum*, authored by Edward Phillips. This title was awarded to Brome as a result of his wit and gaiety.

## Alexander Brome
Alexander Brome (1620–1666) was an English poet who worked professionally as an attorney. His literary output was diverse, encompassing satirical verses and drinking songs. His satirical work was characterized by its support of the Royalists and its opposition to the Rump.

In 1661, Brome published a collection titled *Songs and other Poems*. This volume featured a variety of content, including:
* Songs on various subjects.
* A series of political songs.
* Epigrams and translations.
* Ballads, elegies, epitaphs, and epistles.

This specific volume included an introductory eclogue written by Izaak Walton, who wrote the piece in praise of Brome. Beyond his poetry, Brome was the author of a comedy from 1654 titled *The Cunning Lovers*. In 1666, he published a translation of Horace, which included work by himself and other contributors.

## Relationship with Richard Brome
Alexander Brome also served as an editor for the plays of Richard Brome (d. 1652). Richard Brome was an English dramatist and a former servant of Ben Jonson, a relationship that evolved into a friendship. Richard Brome's work was heavily influenced by Jonson, particularly in the development of plots and the creation of strongly marked characters.

Richard Brome's reputation was established by the play *The Northern Lasse* (1632), which featured lines prefixed to it by Ben Jonson. His genius was centered entirely on comedy, and he left behind fifteen pieces. His works include:
* *Five New Playes* (edited by Alexander Brome, 1652?), which contained *Madd Couple Well Matcht* (acted 1639?), *Novella* (acted 1632), *Court Begger* (acted 1632), *City Witt*, and *The Damoiselle or the New Ordinary*.
* A second collection of *Five New Playes* (1659), which included *The Queen and Concubine*, *Covent Garden Weeded*, *The New Academy, or The New Exchange*, *The Love-Sick Court, or The Ambitious Politique*, and *The English Moor, or The Mock Marriage*.
* Separately published works such as *The Antipodes* (acted 1638, printed 1640), *The Sparagus Garden* (acted 1635, printed 1640), *The Queenes Exchange* (printed 1657), and *A Joviall Crew, or the Merry Beggars* (acted 1641, printed 1652).
* A collaboration with Thomas Heywood titled *The late Lancashire Witches* (printed 1634).

## Edward Phillips
Edward Phillips, the author of *Theatrum Poetarum* and the individual who bestowed the title of "English Anacreon" upon Alexander Brome, is identified as a nephew of John Milton. In addition to his work on poets, Phillips contributed to historical documentation; he continued the *Chronicle of the Kings of England from the Time of the Romans' Government unto the Death of King James* (originally published in 1643 by Baker) down to the year 1658.
