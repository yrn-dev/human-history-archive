# Francis Borgia

Francis Borgia (1510–1572) was a Roman Catholic saint, the duke of Gandia, and the general of the order of Jesuits. Born in Gandia, Valencia, on October 10, 1510, he was noted for his piety from his boyhood.

## Early Education and Court Life
From the age of twelve, Borgia was educated at Saragossa, where he was under the charge of his uncle, the archbishop. During this period, he began to exhibit a strong inclination toward the monastic life. However, in 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. Borgia distinguished himself during his time at court and married Eleanor de Castro, a Portuguese lady of high rank. Following this marriage, he was appointed master of the horse to the empress and was created marquis of Lombay.

Borgia accompanied Charles V on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and traveled into Provence in 1536. In 1539, following the death of the empress, Borgia was tasked with convoying her body to its burial-place in Granada. This experience solidified his determination to leave the court and his resolve to embrace a monastic life should he survive his wife.

## Political Career and Governance
Despite his desire to withdraw from public life, Borgia was given new honors upon his return to Toledo, though these were thrust upon him against his will. He was appointed commander of the order of St James and served as the viceroy of Catalonia. While governing from the seat of his government in Barcelona, Borgia maintained a life of great austerity while discharging his official duties with efficiency and energy.

He continued in this role until 1543, when he succeeded his father in the dukedom. At this time, he obtained permission to resign his viceroyalty and retired to Gandia to pursue a more congenial mode of life.

## Transition to the Jesuit Order
Borgia had previously maintained correspondence with Ignatius Loyola and became a powerful encourager of the recently founded order of Jesus. Upon his return to Gandia, one of his primary concerns was the construction of a Jesuit college. Following the death of Eleanor in 1546, Borgia resolved to become a member of the society himself.

Due to family and political circumstances, he obtained a papal dispensation. This allowed him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children. In 1550, Borgia visited Rome, where he was received with every mark of distinction and provided the funds necessary for the building of the Collegium Romanum. He returned to Spain the following year and formally resigned his estate and rank.
