# BASIN, THOMAS (1412-1491)

Thomas Basin was a historian and the bishop of Lisieux. He was likely born in Caudebec, Normandy, though the devastation of the Hundred Years' War meant that he spent much of his childhood moving between different locations.

## Education and Early Career
Basin entered the university of Paris in 1424, achieving the status of master of arts in 1429. He continued his education by studying law at Pavia and Louvain. His early professional career included attending the council of Ferrara and serving as a professor of canon law at the new university of Caen. Additionally, he served as a canon of the church at Rouen and as the vicar-general for the bishop of Bayeux.

## Ecclesiastical and Political Life
In 1447, Basin became the bishop of Lisieux. During his tenure, he was heavily involved in the conflicts between the French and the English. He served under King Charles VII of France and subsequently under Louis XI. At the request of Louis XI, Basin authored a memorandum that detailed the misery experienced by the people and proposed various measures to alleviate their condition.

Basin's relationship with the crown soured in 1464 when he joined the league of the Public Weal. This led to him falling into disfavour with the king, resulting in the seizure of the temporalities of his see. Following a period of exile in several different locations, Basin traveled to Rome and renounced his bishopric. In 1474, Pope Sixtus IV granted him the title of archbishop of Caesarea.

## Later Years and Death
Basin spent several years in Trier focusing on his writings before moving his residence to Utrecht. He died there on December 3, 1491, and was buried in the church of St John, Utrecht.

## Literary Works
Basin produced a variety of writings, including a defense of Joan of Arc titled *Opinio et consilium super processu et condemnatione Johanne, dicte Puelle*. He wrote a work in French titled *Advis de Monseigneur de Lisieux au roi* (published in Paris, 1677), as well as a *Peregrinatio* and a *Breviloquium*, which served as an allegorical account of his own misfortunes. He also authored an *Apologia* to respond to charges brought against him by Louis XI, and a suggestion for the reform of the administration of justice called *Libellus de optimo ordine forenses lites audiendi et deferendi*.

His principal work is the *Historiae de rebus a Carolo VII. et Ludovico XI. Francorum regibus eorumque in tempore in Gallia gestis*. While this work is considered to be of considerable historical value, it is marred to some extent by Basin's dislike for Louis XI. Although the work was once attributed to a priest of Liege named Amelgard, it is now practically certain that Basin was the author.
