# De Interpretation Naturae Prooemium

The *De Interpretation Naturae Prooemium* is a fragment written by Francis Bacon, likely around the year 1603. In this work, Bacon provides an analysis of his own mental character and outlines the specific objectives he intended to pursue upon entering public life.

## Personal Philosophy and Objectives
In the opening sentence of the fragment, Bacon writes, "Ego cum me ad utilitates humanas natum existimarem" ("since I thought myself born to be of advantage to mankind"). While this statement may appear arrogant, it is characterized as the arrogance of Aristotle's *megalopsuchos*—one who considers himself worthy of great things and is, in fact, worthy.

Bacon's life was driven by a constant striving toward three primary ends:
1. The discovery of truth to produce good for the human race.
2. A practical desire to be of service to his own country.
3. A desire to do "somewhat for the church."

To realize these projects, Bacon aimed to secure an honorable post within the state that would provide him with the necessary means to achieve these goals.

## Mental Character
The fragment and associated records describe Bacon's intellect as acute, far-seeing, and quick, yet balanced by a cautious, meditative, and methodical approach. He is noted for being free from prejudice and possessing an unusually amiable disposition.

## Related Works and Context
While *De Interpretation Naturae Prooemium* focuses on his personal character and ambitions, Bacon authored several other treatises and works. These include the *New Atlantis*, a philosophic romance describing an ideal state where political machinery and state guidance implement the principles of a new philosophy. Other notable tracts include:
* *De Principiis atque Originibus secundum Fabulas Cupidinis et Caeli*: A work using mythological stories to discuss the nature of primitive matter, the origin of things, the limits of science, and the philosophies of Telesio and Democritus.
* *Cogitationes de Natura Rerum*: Likely written early, possibly in 1605.
* *De Fluxu et Refluxu Maris*: A treatise on the theory of tides, written probably around 1616.
