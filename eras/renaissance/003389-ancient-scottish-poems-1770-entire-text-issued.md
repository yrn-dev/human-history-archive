# Ancient Scottish Poems (1770)

*Ancient Scottish Poems*, published in 1770 by Lord Hailes, is a significant collection of Middle Scots poetry. It is noted for providing a more correct printing of the material than previous efforts, such as Allan Ramsay's *Ever Green* (1724), which had reprinted portions of the same source material with modifications.

## The Bannatyne Manuscript
The primary source for Lord Hailes's 1770 work was the "Bannatyne Manuscript." This collection was compiled by George Bannatyne (1545–?1608), a native of Newtyle, Forfarshire, and an Edinburgh merchant who became a burgess in 1587. In 1568, while the "pest" was raging in the capital, Bannatyne retired to his native county. During this time, he occupied himself by writing out copies of poems authored by Scots poets of the 15th and early 16th centuries.

The resulting manuscript consists of eight hundred folio pages divided into five parts. The document descended from Bannatyne to his only daughter, Janet, and subsequently to her husband's family, the Foulises of Woodhall and Ravelston near Edinburgh. It eventually passed into the Advocates' library, where it remains preserved.

## Significance in Middle Scots Poetry
The Bannatyne Manuscript is considered one of the chief repositories of Middle Scots poetry, alongside the "Asloan" and "Maitland Folio" manuscripts. It is particularly essential for preserving the texts of the era's greater poets, including:
*   Henryson
*   Dunbar
*   Lyndsay
*   Alexander Scott

Regarding the works of William Dunbar, the Bannatyne MS. (1568) is listed as one of the chief authorities for his texts, alongside the Asloan MS. (c. 1515), the Chepman and Myllar Prints (1508), and the Maitland Folio MS. (c. 1570-1590).

## Later Editions and Legacy
While Lord Hailes's 1770 edition was a correction of earlier versions, the entire text of the Bannatyne Manuscript was later issued in a generally accurate and handsome form by the Hunterian Club between 1873 and 1902.

The legacy of George Bannatyne's work was further honored in 1823 with the establishment of the Bannatyne Club in Edinburgh. This club was dedicated to publishing literary and historical material derived from Scottish sources. In 1829, the club released its thirty-third issue, *Memorials of George Bannatyne* (1545-1608), which featured an account of the manuscript by David Laing and a memoir written by Sir Walter Scott.
