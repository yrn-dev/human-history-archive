# Wohltemperirtes Klavier

The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* (Well-Tempered Clavier) is a collection of compositions for clavichord by Johann Sebastian Bach. The work consists of two books, each containing 24 preludes and fugues. These are written in every major and minor key with the specific objective of encouraging the use of "equal temperament" tuning, which prevents the sacrifice of euphony in remoter keys to the benefit of more common ones.

## Composition and Development
The first book of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* was composed during Bach's tenure as *Kapellmeister* to the duke of Coethen, a position he held between 1717 and 1723. This period is noted as the era of Bach's central instrumental works. The second book was completed later, in 1744. A comparison between the two books reveals a shift in Bach's style, with the second book showing an increasing preference for designs on a large scale.

## Editorial History
The *Bach-Gesellschaft* (B. G.) produced the only complete modern edition of Bach's works, though the quality of this edition is described as unequal. Early editors are noted for their ignorance of Bach's language, often dismissing details of figured bass, ornamentation, and variants between scores and parts.

Significant improvements were made in the 9th *Jahrgang* when Wilhelm Rust introduced scholarly methods. The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* appeared in *Jahrgang* xiv., edited by Kroll. Kroll also made this text available in the *Edition Peters*, replacing a previous version by Czerny that is characterized as a result of corrupt tradition. The volumes produced by Rust and Kroll are considered the best within the *Bach-Gesellschaft*.

Later updates to the *Bach-Gesellschaft* occurred in *Jahrgang* xliv., where Kroll's text of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* was supplemented using evidence from the British Museum autograph. Additionally, the Steingraeber edition of the clavier works, edited by Dr. Hans Bischoff, is regarded as incomparably the best. This edition provides a straightforward single text while utilizing footnotes for all variants and clearly distinguishing Bach's own rare indications from the editor's phrasing signs and nuances.

## Influence on Later Composers
The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* exerted a subtle and universal force on the development of music, even during periods when Bach's musical language seemed forgotten. 

*   **Mozart:** While advancing toward mastery, Mozart studied manuscript copies of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* provided by Baron von Swieten. This study, alongside his study of Handel and motets, helped Mozart develop a depth of harmony and polyphonic richness.
*   **Beethoven:** Beethoven profoundly studied all accessible works by Bach. His specific rendering of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* is said to be preserved in the *tempo* and expression marks of Czerny's edition, though it is suggested that Beethoven may have been unaware of Bach's actual meaning in several important respects.
*   **Chopin:** Chopin's love for the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* is evident in his Preludes and Etudes. The influence of the polyphonic style is seen even in composers like Chopin who rarely used it directly.
