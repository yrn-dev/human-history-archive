# Montenegro and the Italian Peninsula

## Geography of the Balkan Peninsula
The Balkan Peninsula is the most easterly of the three large peninsulas forming the southern extremities of the European continent. Its area is 184,779 square miles, which is more than twice the size of the Italian Peninsula. The peninsula is bounded by the Black Sea, the Sea of Marmora, and the Aegean to the east; the Mediterranean to the south; and the Ionian Sea and the Adriatic to the west. Its northern boundary is generally marked by the courses of the Kulpa, Save, and Danube rivers, stretching from the Adriatic Sea near Fiume to the Kilia mouth of the Danube.

The surface of the peninsula is almost entirely mountainous. The northern region is enclosed by the Balkans and the Carpathians, which together form the anti-Dacian system. Other complex mountain systems include the Dinaric Alps and parallel ranges in Bosnia. In the west, the landscape is characterized by the mountains of Albania and Montenegro, as well as the lofty Shar Dagh.

## History and Ethnography of the Region
The ethnographic composition of the peninsula was significantly altered by a great Slavonic immigration that began in the 3rd century A.D. and continued for four centuries. At the start of this movement, the Byzantine empire held nominal or actual possession of the regions south of the Danube, and much of the native Thraco-Illyrian population spoke Latin. 

In the 7th century, Serbo-Croats invaded north-western regions, including Montenegro, Bosnia, Herzegovina, Croatia, Servia, and northern Albania. These invaders appropriated old Roman colonies on the Adriatic coast and either assimilated or expelled the Illyrian population. The Illyrians in Dalmatia are now represented by the Mavro-Vlachs or slavonized Morlachs. Simultaneously, the aboriginal Illyrian element in Albania maintained itself in the mountains, eventually forcing back the immigrant race.

## Montenegro: Towns and Commerce
Dulcigno is a seaport of Montenegro located on the Adriatic Sea, approximately 8 miles west of the Albanian frontier. The town is built partly along the shore and partly on a promontory overlooking its bay, surrounded by forests and hills. It features a walled old quarter with a medieval castle that was once of great strength. The town's appearance is described as having a Turkish air, characterized by minarets, mosques, and bazaars. While Turks form the bulk of the inhabitants, Italians and Albanians are also fairly numerous. Dulcigno contains an ancient Latin church and a Roman Catholic cathedral.

Montenegrin commerce utilizes several outlets, with Cattaro serving as one of the chief ports. The region engages in a lucrative transit trade with Turkey, Montenegro, Bosnia, Herzegovina, and various Mediterranean and Adriatic ports.

## Italian Figures and Events of the 15th Century
Historical records from the late 15th century highlight various Italian figures and political events. Matteo Maria Boiardo, an Italian poet from a noble house in Ferrara, served as the governor of Reggio in the year 1478. He was later elected captain of Modena three years after this appointment.

In Florence, the painter Andrea del Castagno (1390–1457) was known for his boldness of attitude, imitating naturalists and Masaccio. Although some historical accounts previously claimed he assassinated Domenico Veneziano to steal the secret of oil painting, this was proven false as Veneziano died four years after Castagno. Castagno was commissioned in 1435 to paint fallen leaders of the Albizzi and Peruzzi in the Palazzo del Podesta; these were not the men of the Pazzi conspiracy, as that event did not occur until 1478, long after the painter's death.

## Honors and Orders of Montenegro
Montenegro established specific orders of merit in 1852. The Order of Danilo, also known as the Order of the Independence of Montenegro, is a general order of merit consisting of four classes with subdivisions. Additionally, the Order of St Peter was founded in 1852 as a family order in a single class, reserved exclusively for members of the princely family.
