# AYSCUE, SIR GEORGE (d. 1671)

Sir George Ayscue (sometimes erroneously recorded as Ayscough or Askew) was a British admiral who originated from an old family in Lincolnshire. While he was knighted by Charles I, the specific details of his early career remain unknown until 1646, when he was granted a naval command.

## Civil War and Early Commands
During the final years of the first civil war, Ayscue served as one of the senior officers within the fleet. He played a critical role in 1648 when Sir William Batten departed for Holland with a portion of his squadron; Ayscue's influence was instrumental in ensuring that a large part of the fleet remained loyal to the Parliament. In recognition of this service, he was appointed admiral of the Irish Seas in 1649. Following his conduct during the relief of Dublin, he received the thanks of Parliament. In 1651, Ayscue was employed under Blake to assist in operations for the reduction of Scilly.

## West Indies and the First Dutch War
Ayscue was subsequently dispatched to the West Indies to lead a squadron tasked with the conquest of other islands under royalist control and the conquest of Barbadoes. After successfully completing this mission, he returned to England to participate in the first Dutch War. 

During this conflict, Ayscue had a sharp encounter with a Dutch convoy while traveling up the Channel to the Downs, during which he captured several prizes. While Robert Blake was sent north with 60 vessels to scatter the Dutch herring fleet, Ayscue remained in the Downs with 16 ships. Despite playing a prominent role in the war, Ayscue lost his command following an indecisive battle off Plymouth on August 16, 1652, although he was assigned an annuity.

## Later Career and the Restoration
Following his loss of command, Sir George lived in retirement for several years. During the later years of the Commonwealth, he was despatched by Cromwell to Sweden to serve as a naval adviser. 

Upon the Restoration, Ayscue returned to England and served as one of the commissioners of the navy. He returned to active sea service during the outbreak of the second Dutch War in 1664, hoisting his flag as rear-admiral of the Blue. In this capacity, he took part in the battle of Lowestoft on June 3, 1665. 

In June 1666, during the Four Days' Battle (June 11th–14th), Ayscue served as admiral of the White under Monck. On the third day of this engagement, his flagship, the "Prince Royal," was captured. Ayscue remained a prisoner in Holland until the peace was established. It is considered doubtful whether he ever flew his flag at sea again after this event.

## Death and Legacy
Sir George Ayscue is supposed to have died in 1671. A portrait of him, painted by Lely, is located in the Painted Hall at Greenwich.
