# Jean Louis Guez de Balzac

Jean Louis Guez de Balzac (1594–1654) was a French author born in Angouleme. He is recognized for his contributions to French prose, specifically for introducing a level of precision and clearness that served as a reform parallel to the work of Malherbe in verse.

## Early Life and Career
At eighteen years of age, Balzac traveled to Holland in the company of Theophile de Viaud, a relationship that later resulted in bitter recriminations between the two men. During his early career, he was befriended by the duc d'Epernon and his son, Cardinal de la Valette, the latter of whom took Balzac to Rome. 

Balzac developed a significant reputation through letters written to his acquaintances and to various individuals holding high positions at the French court. This success led to him becoming an habitue of the Hotel de Rambouillet and receiving numerous compliments. While Richelieu offered him lavish promises and praise, Richelieu never provided Balzac with the preferment he had expected.

## Literary Works and Style
Balzac's fame is primarily based on his *Lettres*. A collection of these letters was published in 1624 and was received with great favor; a second collection followed in 1636, and the *Recueil de nouvelles lettres* was printed the following year. Although the matter of these letters is described as affected and empty, they demonstrate a real mastery of style. By emphasizing the most idiomatic elements of the language, Balzac encouraged the development of French prose on national lines.

In addition to his letters, Balzac authored several other works:
* *Le Prince* (1631): An eulogy of Louis XIII.
* *Socrate chretien* (1652): Considered the best of his longer works.
* *Aristippe ou de la Cour* (1658).
* Various dissertations regarding style.

## Later Life and Death
Balzac eventually retired to the chateau of Balzac. From this residence, he maintained correspondence with figures such as Valentin Conrart and Jean Chapelain. In 1634, he was elected to the Academy. He died on February 18, 1654, in Angouleme.
