# Johann Joseph Fux

Johann Joseph Fux (1660–1741) was an Austrian musician and musical scholar who served as a prominent court composer in Vienna. He is remembered as an unsurpassed contrapuntist among his contemporaries and the author of the influential theoretical work *Gradus ad Parnassum*.

## Early Life and Court Appointments
Fux was born in 1660 at Hirtenfeld, Styria. While the details of his youth and early musical training are unknown, he established himself in Vienna by 1696, where he served as an organist at one of the city's principal churches. In 1698, Emperor Leopold I appointed Fux as his "imperial court-composer," providing him with a monthly salary of approximately L6. 

Fux remained in the service of the imperial court for the remainder of his life, serving Leopold I and his successors, Joseph I and Charles VI. In 1704, he expanded his court dignities by becoming an organist at St Stephen's cathedral. He was married to the daughter of Schnitzbaum, a government secretary.

## Musical Works and Style
Fux composed numerous operas, though these did not differ essentially from the Italian *opera seria* style prevalent during his era. One notable example was *La Constanza e la Fortezza*, which Fux composed specifically for the 1723 coronation of Emperor Charles VI as king of Bohemia. The opera was performed in an open-air theatre in Prague. The high regard in which Charles VI held Fux is illustrated by the fact that, despite Fux suffering from gout at the time, the emperor had him transported from Vienna in a litter and provided him a seat in the imperial box.

Fux's sacred compositions are considered of greater importance than his operas. These include masses, oratorios, motets, and psalms. Among these, the *Missa Canonica* is highlighted as a celebrated *tour de force* of learned musicianship, as it was written entirely using the canon, a highly difficult contrapuntal device.

## Theoretical Contributions and Influence
Fux is most distinguished as a musical scholar and contrapuntist. His major theoretical work, *Gradus ad Parnassum* (originally titled *Gradus ad Parnassum sive manuductio ad compositionem musicae regularem, methoda nova ac certa nondum ante tam exacta ordine in lucem edita, elaborata a Joanne Josepho Fux*), was published in Vienna in 1715. The work provided a thorough treatment of counterpoint and its developments and was translated into most European languages.

The influence of *Gradus ad Parnassum* was extensive; it is noted as the text on which all later classical composers were trained. For example, Johann Sebastian Bach is believed to have owed part of his musical development to the study of Fux and this specific work.

## Later Life and Legacy
Fux's life in the "great world" of the imperial court was largely eventless, with his primary difficulties stemming from the intrigues of Italian rivals at court. He died in Vienna on February 13, 1741.

A biography of Fux was published in 1871 by Ludwig von Kochel in Vienna. This biography was based on minute original research and includes a complete catalogue of the composer's numerous works.
