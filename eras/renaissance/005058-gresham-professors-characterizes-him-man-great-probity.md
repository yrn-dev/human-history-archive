# Gresham College and its Professors

Gresham College, located in London, was founded by Sir Thomas Gresham. The institution is associated with several notable professors across various disciplines, including geometry and music.

## Sir Thomas Gresham
Sir Thomas Gresham (1519–1579) was a London merchant and the founder of both Gresham College and the Royal Exchange. Descended from an old Norfolk family, he was the only son of Sir Richard Gresham, a leading merchant and former lord mayor who had been knighted for his services as an agent to Henry VIII in negotiating loans with foreign merchants.

Although his father intended for him to enter the same profession, Sir Thomas attended Caius College, Cambridge, for an unspecified duration. He served an eight-year apprenticeship under his uncle, Sir John Gresham, who was also a merchant. In 1543, at age twenty-four, he became a member of the Mercers' Company and traveled to the Low Countries. There, he operated as a merchant and acted as an agent for Henry VIII. In 1544, he married the widow of William Read, a London merchant, though he continued to reside primarily in Antwerp.

Gresham became a key advisor to the English government during financial crises. In 1551, he was called to address financial embarrassment caused by the mismanagement of Sir William Dansell, the "king's merchant" in the Low Countries. Gresham proposed and implemented ingenious, though arbitrary and unfair, methods to raise the value of the pound sterling on the Antwerp bourse, which successfully discharged nearly all of King Edward's debts. He frequently served on diplomatic missions and received grants of lands from Edward as reward for his services, which eventually reached an annual value of approximately £400.

## Henry Briggs
Henry Briggs (1556–1630) was an English mathematician born at Warley Wood, near Halifax, in Yorkshire. He graduated from St John's College, Cambridge, in 1581 and became a fellow in 1588. After serving as reader of the physical lecture founded by Dr Thomas Linacre in 1592, he became the first professor of geometry at Gresham House (later Gresham College) in 1596.

While lecturing at Gresham House, Briggs proposed changing the scale of logarithms from the hyperbolic form established by John Napier to one where unity is the logarithm of the ratio of ten to one. He visited Napier in Edinburgh in 1616 and 1617 to discuss this change. Following an agreement between the two, Briggs published the first chiliad of his logarithms in 1617.

In 1619, Briggs was appointed Savilian professor of geometry at Oxford, and he resigned his position at Gresham College on July 25, 1620. His later works include a 1622 tract on the North-West Passage to the South Seas and the 1624 *Arithmetica Logarithmica*, which listed logarithms of thirty thousand natural numbers to fourteen places. He also produced a table of logarithmic sines and tangents, as well as natural sines, tangents, and secants, published in 1633 as *Trigonometria Britannica*. Briggs died on January 26, 1630, and was buried in Merton College chapel, Oxford. In *Lives of the Gresham Professors*, Dr Smith describes Briggs as a man of great probity who was contented with his station, a contemner of riches, and preferred a studious retirement.

## John Bull
John Bull (c. 1562–1628) was an English organist and composer from Somersetshire. After serving as organist in Hereford cathedral and joining the Chapel Royal in 1585, he earned a Mus. Bac. from Oxford. He became the organist in Queen Elizabeth's chapel in 1591 and received a doctor of music degree from Cambridge University in 1592.

In 1596, Bull was appointed music professor at Gresham College. Because the foundation rules required lectures to be delivered in Latin—which Bull was unable to do—the executors of Sir Thomas Gresham granted him a dispensation to lecture in English. His first lecture took place on October 6, 1597.

Bull traveled to France and Germany in 1601, where he was highly respected. Despite offers of employment from various continental princes, he returned to England and was granted freedom of the Merchant Taylors' Company in 1606. He performed for King James I on July 16, 1607, and was appointed one of the king's organists that same year. Bull resigned his Gresham professorship in 1607 and married Elizabeth Walter. He later returned to the continent for health reasons, serving as an organist in the arch-duke's chapel at Brussels in 1613 and at the cathedral of Notre Dame in Antwerp in 1617, where he died in March 1628.
