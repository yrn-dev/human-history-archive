# John Amos Komensky (Comenius)

## Overview
John Amos Komensky, also known as Comenius (1592-1670), is recognized as the greatest writer associated with the community of the Bohemian Brethren. A prolific author, his body of work consists of 142 writings, many of which were composed in his native language.

## Literary Contributions and the Bohemian Language
Comenius wrote extensively in his native tongue, producing numerous educational and devotional works. Among his writings in the national language, the most significant is the *Labyrinth of the World*. This work is an allegorical tale and is considered perhaps the most famous piece of literature written in Bohemian.

## The Bohemian Brethren and Literary Context
Comenius's work emerged from the influence of the Bohemian Brethren, a community whose indirect founder was Chelcicky. The brotherhood had a profound impact on Bohemian literature, as nearly all of their theological and historical works were written in the national language. This practice served to make the language more polished and refined.

Key milestones of the brotherhood's literary activity included:
* **The Bible of Kralice:** A famed translation of the Scriptures produced by several divines of the brotherhood and first printed in 1593 at Kralice in Moravia.
* **Theological Works:** Brother Gregory, known as the patriarch of the brotherhood, authored the *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan* and the book *On good and evil priests*.
* **Controversial and Historical Works:** Following Brother Gregory's death in 1480, literary activity increased. Notable authors included Brothers Lucas, Blahoslav, and Jaffet, as well as Bishop Augusta. Jan Bilek wrote an account of Bishop Augusta's captivity.

## Preservation of Bohemian Works
While the brotherhood produced numerous historical works, the majority perished during the 17th century. This loss occurred when nearly all anti-Roman books written in Bohemia were destroyed. Consequently, only fragments of certain works survived, such as Blahoslav's *History of the Unity* and the biographies of William and Peter of Rosenberg from Wenceslas Brezan's *History of the House of Rosenberg*.
