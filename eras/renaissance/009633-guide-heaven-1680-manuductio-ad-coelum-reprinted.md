# Manuductio ad Coelum and Related Works of John Bona

## Overview of John Bona
John Bona (1609–1674) was an Italian author and cardinal born on October 10, 1609, in Mondovi, Piedmont. His ecclesiastical career began in 1624 when he joined the Congregation of Feuillants. Within this order, he held several leadership positions, serving as the abbot of Mondovi, the prior of Asti, and eventually as the general of the order. In 1669, he was created cardinal by Clement IX. Following the death of Clement IX, Bona was considered a potential candidate for the papacy during the subsequent conclave. He died on October 27, 1674.

## Literary Contributions and Devotional Works
The writings of John Bona primarily focus on devotional and liturgical subjects. His work *De Sacrificio Missae* is regarded as the classical treatise in its field, with a new edition published by Ildephonsus Cummins in 1903. Other significant works include:

*   ***De Libris Liturgicis* and *Divina Psalmodia***: These texts contain prayers, verses, and rubrical information.
*   ***De Discretione Spirituum***: A work addressing higher phases of mysticism.
*   ***Via Compendii ad Deum***: This text was translated into English in 1876 by Henry Collins, O. Cist., under the title *An Easy Way to God*.
*   ***Principia Vitae Christianae***: A new edition of this work, edited by D. O'Connor, was released in 1906.

The most noted editions of Bona's collected works were published in Paris (1677), Turin (1747), and Antwerp (1777).

## The Guide to Heaven
One of Bona's works, the *Manuductio ad Coelum*, was translated by Sir Roger L'Estrange. This translation, titled *The Guide to Heaven*, was first published in 1680 and was later reprinted in 1898.
