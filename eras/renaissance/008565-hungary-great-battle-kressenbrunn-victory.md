# The Battle of Kressenbrunn and Its Aftermath

## The Conflict and the Battle of Kressenbrunn
The expansion of the Bohemian dominions under Premysl Ottakar II led to repeated wars with Hungary. Premysl Ottakar II, who had ruled Moravia during his father Wenceslas I's reign and later became one of Bohemia's greatest kings, had acquired the archduchies of Austria and was recognized as the ruler of Styria by its nobility. These territorial extensions brought him into direct conflict with the Hungarian crown. In 1260, this struggle culminated in the great battle of Kressenbrunn, where Premysl Ottakar II decisively defeated Bela, the king of Hungary.

## Territorial Expansion and the Power of Ottakar II
Following the victory at Kressenbrunn, the power of Premysl Ottakar II rose to its greatest height. He expanded his possessions to include Istria, Carinthia, and portions of northern Italy. His dominions stretched from the Adriatic to the Giant Mountains in Bohemia, encompassing nearly all parts of the territory west of the Leitha that would later comprise the Habsburg empire.

Due to his immense military power and great wealth, his contemporaries referred to him by two distinct titles: "the man of iron" and "the man of gold."

## Internal Policy and German Immigration
Despite his Slavic nationality, Premysl Ottakar II encouraged the immigration of Germans into his territories. This decision was driven by political rather than racial motivations; he sought to utilize German townsmen as a counterpoise to the overwhelming power held by the Bohemian nobility.

## Later Transitions of Power
By 1273, Rudolph, count of Habsburg, was elected king of the Romans. While it is considered very probable that the German crown had been offered to Premysl Ottakar II previously, he had refused it.
