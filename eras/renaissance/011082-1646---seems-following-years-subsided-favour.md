# Events and Figures of 1646

The year 1646 is associated with various developments across military, intellectual, and personal histories, ranging from the English Civil War and naval commands to the evolution of scientific interests and the deaths of prominent European figures.

## Military and Political Developments
In England, the year 1646 was marked by conflict and shifting dynamics during the Civil War. In Cornwall, which had previously been secured for the king following victories at Bradoc Down, Launceston, and Stratton, the situation changed in 1646. Dissensions arose among the king's officers, specifically involving the refusal of Sir Richard Grenville to serve.

In the naval sphere, 1646 marked the beginning of a significant command for Sir George Ayscue. A British admiral from a Lincolnshire family who had been knighted by Charles I, Ayscue received a naval command in 1646. He subsequently served as one of the senior officers of the fleet during the latter years of the first civil war.

In Poland, the year 1646 marked the death of Stanislaus Koniecpolski. An illustrious Polish soldier and member of an ancient family, Koniecpolski had served as the *hetman wielki koronny* (commander in chief of Poland). His career was defined by his strategic successes against the Tatars—including victories at Martynow, Sasowy Rogi, and Paniawce—and his efforts to neutralize the advance of Gustavus Adolphus in Prussia.

## Intellectual and Scientific Shifts
The year 1646 saw a transition in the personal interests of Elias Ashmole, an English antiquarian and founder of the Ashmolean Museum. Ashmole, who had become a solicitor in 1638 and a commissioner of excise in 1644, met William Lilly and other astrologers in London in 1646. While his interest in astrology was aroused by Lilly and George Wharton, this interest seems to have subsided in the following years, giving way to his dedication to antiquarian research and heraldry. Additionally, 1646 was the year Ashmole was initiated as a Freemason, becoming the first "amateur" or gentleman to be accepted.

In the realm of philosophy, 1646 was the year Heinrich Regius published his work on physical philosophy, titled *Fundamenta physices*. This publication suggested to the world that Regius had ceased to be a thorough adherent of the philosophy he had previously adopted with enthusiasm.

## Other Notable Occurrences
The year 1646 also marks the birth of James Douglas, the 2nd Marquess of Douglas. He would later succeed his grandfather in 1660 and leave a lineage that included the 3rd Marquess, Archibald, and Lady Jane Douglas.
