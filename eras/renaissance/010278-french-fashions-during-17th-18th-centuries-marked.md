# French Fashions of the 17th and 18th Centuries

During the 17th and 18th centuries, French fashions exerted a significant influence on the decorative arts, clothing, and cultural habits of other European nations. This influence extended from the specific design of headwear and textiles to the broader aesthetic styles of the era.

## Headwear and the Cocked Hat
A distinguishing feature of 18th-century fashion was the three-cornered cocked hat. While the practice of cocking up one side of a high-crowned felt or beaver hat existed as early as the Elizabethan age, the specific fashion of cocking up three sides originated from the French court. At least one of these sides was typically fastened with a loop of ribbon, a detail from which the cockade developed. 

By the mid-18th century, the black cockade had become a sign of military men in England. The cocked hat evolved into various forms, ranging from those with three sides slightly curled upward to the "great Khevenhueller cock," a martial style where a wide-brimmed hat was flapped up at both the front and rear. Depending on the fashion of the time, these hats were sometimes edged with feathers or laced with silver and gold.

## Textiles and Materials
French textile production in the middle of the 18th century included the creation of luxury fabrics, such as pink silk brocaded with silver, white, and coloured silks. These materials contributed to the overall aesthetic of the period's dress and ornamentation.

## Influence on Decorative Arts and Language
The reach of French fashion extended beyond clothing into the realm of decorative arts and intellectual life. In Germany, French fashions invaded during the 17th and 18th centuries, though they did not displace the influence of earlier German draughtsmen on decorative styles. In the Netherlands, French influence was felt through the poetic clubs of the 17th and 18th centuries, which led to the borrowing of French words into the Dutch language. This linguistic trend faced opposition from figures such as Vondel, Hooft, and others who resisted these "bastard words."

In the field of book production, the 18th century saw a revival of book illustration through the French *livres-a-vignettes*. French artists, including Gravelot, were responsible for illustrating English books during this time.

## Court and Musical Culture
The French court also influenced the development of musical instruments. The *hautbois* (oboe) played a vital role in court music. During the 17th century, the *haute-contre de hautbois* (cor anglais) was developed; by the end of that century, the musicians of the Grande Ecurie du Roi included two players of this instrument. It is probable that the instrument was provided with keys in France due to its importance in the court.

## Furniture Styles
French influence is also evident in the furniture of the period. The Louis XV style is characterized by specific designs, such as the arm-chair made of carved and gilt wood featuring a stuffed back, seat, and arms.
