# C. Clavier and Organ Music

The clavier and organ music of Johann Sebastian Bach represents a vast body of work characterized by architectural complexity and technical innovation. His compositions for these instruments range from pedagogical exercises and systematic studies of harmony to large-scale independent works and arrangements of other composers' pieces.

## Clavier Collections and Works
Bach's primary collections for the clavier include *Das wohltemperirte Klavier*, written for the clavichord. This work consists of two books, each containing 24 preludes and fugues—one in every major and minor key. The purpose of this collection was to encourage the use of "equal temperament" tuning, which preserves the euphony of remote keys rather than sacrificing them for the sake of more common ones.

Another major collection is the *Klavier-Uebung*, primarily intended for the harpsichord. This work is divided into four books:
1. 15 three-part symphonies and 15 two-part inventions.
2. 6 partitas.
3. The "Goldberg" variations, 4 duets, and a collection of organ choral-preludes, including the "St Anne" prelude and fugue in E flat.
4. The French overture and the Italian concerto.

Additionally, Bach composed 6 "English" and 6 "French" suites. His other clavier works are distributed across two *Jahrgaenge* of the *B.-G.*. In the realm of concerted music, Bach wrote 7 clavier concertos arranged from other sources and violin concertos, as well as 2 concertos for three claviers and 3 concertos for two claviers (two of which were arranged from concertos for two violins).

## Organ Music
Bach's organ output is extensive, including collections and independent pieces. Beyond the material found in the *Klavier-Uebung*, his organ collections comprise:
* 6 sonatas.
* 4 groups of 6 organ preludes and fugues.
* *Das Orgelbuechlein*, a planned collection of short choral-preludes. Although the autograph pages were headed with titles for the intended chorales, the work was not half completed.
* 18 larger chorale-preludes, which include his final composition.
* 6 "Schuebler" chorales, all of which were arranged from cantata movements.

Other significant organ works include the Passacaglia and three great independent toccatas. The remaining organ works and choral-preludes are found across three *Jahrgaenge* of the *B.-G.*.

## The Toccata Form
In the hands of Bach, the toccata evolved from a form of quasi-extemporization into a noble and plastic architectural structure. While earlier composers like Buxtehude used the form to "try" the touch of the keyboard before moving into a fugue that often collapsed quickly, Bach developed the fugue into a convincing climax. 

Examples of this development include the organ toccata in C, where disjointed introductory runs build into "grand piles of musical architecture," and the toccata in F for organ, which features long, smooth canonic passages and a quiet fugue. In the E minor clavier-partita, the toccata utilizes a highly organized group of contrasted themes that are later recapitulated after a leisurely fugue.

## Unclassified and Arranged Works
Two major instrumental works defy simple classification. *Das musikalische Opfer* was composed based on a theme given to Bach by Frederick the Great and includes two great fugues, a trio for violin, flute, and figured bass, and various puzzle-canons. *Die Kunst der Fuge* is a progressive series of fugues on a single subject, written in open score as abstract studies, though most are written in an admirable clavier style.

Bach also frequently arranged the works of others. He produced 16 harpsichord arrangements of concertos generally attributed to Vivaldi, as well as 4 organ arrangements of Vivaldi concertos. One notable arrangement is the Concerto in A minor for orchestra and 4 claviers, based on a Vivaldi concerto for 4 violins. He also arranged two sonatas for clavier from Reinken's *Hortus Musicus*.

## Related Clavier Figures
The tradition of clavier music continued through Bach's son, Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714–1788). A premier European clavier player, he composed nearly two hundred sonatas and solos, including the set *mit veraenderten Reprisen* (1760-1768). He is also noted for his masterly treatise, *Versuch ueber die wahre Art das Clavier zu spielen*, which first appeared in 1753. Other notable clavier players of the era included Georg Benda, a famous player and oboist known for his melodramas.
