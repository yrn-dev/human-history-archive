# Spanish Viceroys of Naples

The office of the Spanish viceroy of Naples served as a key administrative and military position in Italy, representing the Spanish crown. Throughout various periods, these officials were tasked with maintaining order against internal threats, managing diplomatic relations with neighboring powers, and overseeing the cultural and political interests of Spain.

## Governance and Internal Security
During the period just before and after 1600, the Spanish viceroy of Naples was engaged in efforts to suppress brigandage. Italy's division into small states provided favorable conditions for brigands, as outlaws could flee across borders to escape pursuit. A notable example was Marco Sciarra of the Abruzzi, who led 600 men and terrorized the region. When hard pressed by the Spanish viceroy, Sciarra was able to cross the border into the papal states to evade capture. He only faced significant pressure when the viceroy and the pope combined their efforts against him, eventually leading Sciarra to seek service with Venice.

## Military and Early Administration
Earlier in the Spanish administration of Naples, the role of viceroy was combined with military command. In 1501, Gonzalo, known as the Great Captain, was chosen to command the Spanish part of a coalition with Louis XII of France for the division and spoliation of the kingdom of Naples. Serving as both general and viceroy, Gonzalo remained in Italy until 1507. His tenure was characterized by mountain warfare in Calabria, where he utilized veterans of the Granadine war—including light cavalry and foot soldiers armed with crossbows, arquebuses, and sword and buckler—to conduct guerrillero warfare against French forces and Swiss pikemen.

## Diplomatic Intrigue and the Venetian Plot
In the early 17th century, the viceroy of Naples was involved in high-level Spanish diplomatic schemes. The duke of Ossuna, serving as viceroy, collaborated with the Spanish ambassador to Venice, Alphonso Bella Cueva (the Marquis of Bedmar), to bring Venice under Spanish power. This plot was scheduled for execution on Ascension Day 1618. 

The viceroy employed various agents for this conspiracy, including Diego Duque de Estrada. A soldier and adventurer who had fled to Naples after committing a double murder in Toledo, Duque de Estrada entered the service of the duke of Ossuna. He was among the disguised Spanish soldiers sent into Venice to destroy the arsenal, though the conspiracy was betrayed, allowing him to escape.

## Patronage and Court Life
The viceroys of Naples also acted as patrons to artists and men of letters. Benavente, a viceroy of Naples, served as a protege to the Spanish dramatist Guillen de Castro y Bellvis, appointing him as the governor of Scigliano. Later, Don Pedro di Toledo, another viceroy of Naples, appointed the Italian painter Belisario Corenzio as his court painter. Corenzio, a Greek-born artist known for his rapid execution and unscrupulous conduct, successfully supplanted the artist Ribera in the viceroy's favor.
