# Anne Boleyn

Anne Boleyn (c. 1507–1536) served as the queen of Henry VIII of England. She was the daughter of Elizabeth, daughter of Thomas Howard (later duke of Norfolk and earl of Surrey), and Sir Thomas Boleyn, who later became the earl of Ormonde and Wiltshire. While some later writers have ascribed her birth to 1501 or 1502, Camden records her birth year as 1507.

## Early Life and French Influence
In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France, where she served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I. She returned to England in either 1521 or 1522.

Upon her return, she attracted numerous suitors and admirers. Among these were Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland, and the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt; the *Chronicle of King Henry VIII* asserts that she was Wyatt's mistress. Although the king stopped a marriage between Anne and Henry Percy and instead provided Sir James Butler as a match, Anne remained unmarried.

## Relationship with Henry VIII
Between 1522 and 1525, Henry bestowed various favors and grants upon Anne's father, which some have interpreted as a sign of the king's affection for Anne. Unlike her sister Mary, who had fallen victim to Henry's solicitations, Anne did not intend to be the king's mistress, but rather his queen, with her conduct appearing to be driven by ambition.

The exact start of the relationship between Anne and Henry is unknown, though some surmise it began as early as 1523. However, there is no proof that Henry's passion existed prior to May 1527, when proceedings for divorce were initiated. On August 16, 1527, Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, wrote a letter to Charles V that first openly connected Anne's name to the king as a possible wife should Catherine be divorced. While Henry sent his secretary, William Knight, to Rome to facilitate the marriage, Cardinal Wolsey opposed the alliance and instead negotiated a marriage for the king with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII.

## Marriage and Reign
The marriage of Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn is commonly antedated to November 14, 1532, though according to Cranmer, the only authority on the matter, he only learned of it a fortnight later. 

During her marriage, Anne gave birth to a child every year. However, the king eventually became disappointed that she did not provide him with a male heir.

## Trial and Execution
Anne Boleyn was eventually accused of misdeeds, including charges of incest. A principal witness for the incest charge was the wife of Rochford, a woman described as having an infamous character who was later executed for her role in the intrigues of Catherine Howard. 

The guilt of Anne Boleyn remains unproved due to the absence of the records upon which her sentence was based. To Sir William Kingston, she protested her total innocence, and she made no confession on the scaffold, though she expressed her submission. While Smeaton made a full confession, it is possible this was the result of hopes for pardon or threats of torture. Norris also confessed, though he later claimed he had been betrayed into doing so. Other accused parties "confessed in a manner" on the scaffold, though such statements were often general declarations of repentance and submission to the king's will rather than admissions of specific crimes.

Despite the lack of records, some argue her guilt is probable, noting that it is unlikely that a tribunal of nearly all lay peers of England, along with two grand juries and a petty jury, would unanimously pass a sentence of guilt contrary to the facts. This sentence was further supported by Anne's own uncle and father.

On the scaffold, Anne is reported to have said, "I pray God save the king, and send him long to reign over you, for a gentler nor a more merciful prince was there never; and to me he was ever a good, a gentle and sovereign lord."
