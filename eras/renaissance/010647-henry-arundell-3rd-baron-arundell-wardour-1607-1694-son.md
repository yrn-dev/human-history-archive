# HENRY ARUNDELL, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour

Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour, was a Roman Catholic and royalist born on July 21, 1607. He was the son of Blanche, the daughter of Edward, earl of Worcester, and Thomas, the 2nd baron. Upon the death of his father in 1643, Henry succeeded to the family estates and the title.

## Civil War and Interregnum
During the English Civil War, Arundell supported the cause of the king. In 1644, he distinguished himself by recapturing his castle at Wardour from parliamentarians. The castle had been seized by the parliamentarians the previous year, despite a brave defense led by Arundell's mother. 

Following these events, Arundell was listed in 1648 as one of the delinquents who were exempted from pardon in the proposals sent to Charles in the Isle of Wight. His estates were confiscated, though by approximately 1653 he was permitted to compound for them for the sum of £35,000.

In 1652, Arundell was involved in a duel that resulted in the death of one of the combatants; because he was the second at the duel, he was arrested. During his trial in 1653, he attempted to plead his peerage, but this privilege was disallowed because the House of Lords had been abolished.

## Service under the Restoration
Following the Restoration, Arundell regained his family estates. In 1663, he was appointed master of the horse to Henrietta Maria. He became one of the few individuals trusted by the king regarding alliances with France and projects to restore the Roman Catholic religion.

In 1669, Arundell participated in a secret council assembled by Charles II. In October of that year, he was sent to France. While the official reason for the trip was the funeral of Henrietta Maria, his actual purpose was to negotiate with Louis XIV. These negotiations led to the treaties of Dover in 1670. Later, in 1676, Arundell was privy to negotiations between James and Rome conducted through Coleman.

## The Popish Plot and Imprisonment
In 1678, Titus Oates accused Arundell of participating in the popish plot. Consequently, in October of that year, he was one of five Roman Catholic peers arrested and imprisoned in the Tower. He was impeached by parliament and found guilty of high treason by the Middlesex grand jury.

While Lord Stafford was found guilty and executed in December 1680, the proceedings against the other peers were eventually interrupted. On February 12, 1684, Arundell was one of three surviving peers released on bail. Following the accession of James II on May 22, 1685, the charges against him were annulled.
