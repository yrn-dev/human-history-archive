# Paris Bordone and the Fugger Palace Works

## Early Life and Training
Paris Bordone (1495–1570) was a Venetian painter born in Treviso. In 1509, he entered the *bottega* of Titian. According to Vasari, who provides nearly all the known facts regarding Bordone's life, the artist did not spend many years under Titian's tutelage and instead sought to imitate the style of Giorgione to the best of his ability. However, evidence suggests that the Giorgionesque elements in Bordone's early output were actually derived from Titian. Bordone imitated Titian so closely that some of his paintings continue to be attributed to Titian to this day.

## Artistic Style and Subject Matter
Bordone's work is characterized by an unequal merit. His paintings possess a dignified character in portraiture, a realistic conception of the human figure, a certain nobility of style, and a golden harmony of colour inherited from Titian. Conversely, his figures are frequently affected and unnatural in their action, and his nudes are described as being somewhat coarse in form.

As a figure of the Renaissance, Bordone produced a wide variety of subjects, including:
* Religious pictures
* Mythological scenes
* Allegories
* Nymphs and cupids
* Subjects based on the fables of Ovid

While he worked across these genres, he was most successful as a portraitist.

## Career and Commissions
Bordone executed many important mural paintings in Vicenza, Treviso, and Venice, though all of these works have since perished. In 1538, he was invited to the court of Francis I in France. While there, he painted numerous portraits; however, no trace of these remains in French collections, with the exception of two portraits at the Louvre which were acquired later.

On his return journey from France, Bordone undertook works of great importance for the Fugger palace at Augsburg. These works have also been lost sight of.

## Surviving Works
The principal surviving work by Bordone is the "Fisherman and Doge," located at the Venice Academy. Other notable surviving pieces include:
* "Daphnis and Chloe" and a portrait of a lady (National Gallery, London)
* "Holy Family" (Bridgwater House)
* "Madonna" (Tadini collection at Lovere)
* Paintings located in the Duomo of Treviso
* Two mythological pictures located in the Doria palace and the Villa Borghese

Regarding the "Baptism of Christ" in the Capitoline gallery, scholars are divided: Crowe, Cavalcaselle, and Dr. Bode attribute the work to Bordone, while Morelli identifies it as an early work by Titian.
