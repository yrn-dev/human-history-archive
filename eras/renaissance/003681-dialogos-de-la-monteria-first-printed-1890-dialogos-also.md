# Dialogos de la monteria

The *Dialogos de la monteria* is a literary work first printed in 1890. It is of particular significance to scholars of Spanish poetry due to its role in preserving fragments of works by the poet Luis Barahona de Soto.

## Connection to Luis Barahona de Soto

Luis Barahona de Soto (born approximately 1535 in Lucena, Cordova; died 1595) was a Spanish poet and physician who was educated at Granada and practiced medicine in Cordova. His primary known work is the *Primera parte de la Angelica*, published in 1586, which served as a continuation of the *Orlando furioso*. 

For a long period, the second part of the *Angelica* was believed to have been lost. However, fragments of this second part have since been identified within the anonymous *Dialogos de la monteria*. In addition to the fragments of the *Angelica*, the *Dialogos* also contain fragments of another poem by Barahona titled *Los Principios del mundo*.

## Literary Context and Merit

Barahona de Soto is described by Cervantes as "one of the best poets not only in Spain, but in the whole world," though this is noted as friendly hyperbole. Despite this, Barahona is recognized for possessing high merits, including an exceptional mastery of Italian methods transplanted to Spain, an ingenious fancy, and poetic imagination.

## Publication and Editing

The *Dialogos de la monteria* was edited by F. R. de Uhagon and published in Madrid in 1890. Other scholarly works related to the author of the fragments found within the *Dialogos* include the biographical, bibliographic, and critical study *Luis Barahona de Solo, estudio biografico, bibliografico, y critico*, published in Madrid in 1903 by F. Rodriguez Marin.
