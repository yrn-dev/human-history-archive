# The Election of Julius II and the Influence of Spanish Cardinals

The election of Pope Julius II in 1503 occurred during a period of significant political instability in Italy, characterized by the collapse of the power of Cesare Borgia and the competing interests of various European powers and ecclesiastical factions.

## The Fall of Cesare Borgia
In early 1503, Cesare Borgia returned to Rome and participated in the reduction of the last rebel Orsinis. While preparing for a new expedition in central Italy during the summer, both Cesare and his father, the pope, were seized by fever. The pope died on August 18, 1503. This coincidence proved ruinous for Cesare, as he was incapacitated at the time of his father's death.

Following the pope's passing, Cesare's enemies rose against him. In Rome, the Orsinis resurfaced, and in Romagna, deposed princes sought to reclaim their territories. Cesare attempted to intimidate the cardinals using his bravos and Don Michelotto, but the cardinals refused to be intimidated. Consequently, Cesare was forced to leave Rome in September, placing his hopes on the Spanish cardinals to elect a candidate who would be friendly to his house.

## The Papacy of Pius III
The resulting conclave led to the election of Francesco Todeschini-Piccolomini, who took the name Pius III. Although Pius III showed a disposition to be respectable and peaceful, he was in poor health and of advanced age. During his brief tenure, Cesare Borgia's dominion rapidly disintegrated. With the assistance of Venice, Guidobaldo, the duke of Urbino, returned to his duchy. Similarly, the lords of Pesaro, Rimini, and Piombino regained their lands. Only Cesena, which was defended by a governor loyal to Cesare, continued to hold out.

## The Election of Julius II
Pius III died on October 18, 1503, necessitating a new conclave. At this time, Cesare Borgia still maintained the support of the Spanish cardinals. He specifically wished to prevent the election of Giuliano della Rovere, who was an enemy of the Borgia house. However, della Rovere's chances for the papacy had improved to the extent that it became necessary to come to terms with him.

On November 1, Giuliano della Rovere was elected and assumed the name Julius II. While he did not display ill-will toward Cesare, he declared that the territories held by Cesare must be restored to the church, stating, "we desire the honour of recovering what our predecessors have wrongfully alienated."

## The End of Borgia Power
Pope Julius II was determined to prevent Venice from establishing a protectorate over the principalities in Romagna. After an unsuccessful attempt to use Cesare as a tool to keep the Venetians out, Julius II had Cesare arrested. This marked the end of Borgia's power, and he was forced to surrender all his castles in Romagna, with the exception of one.

## Giuliano della Rovere's Earlier Activities
Prior to his election as pope, Giuliano della Rovere served as a cardinal and the commendatory of the abbey at Grottaferrata, succeeding Cardinal Bessarion in that role. During the end of the 15th century, he fortified the village of Grottaferrata, adding massive towers that gave the site a picturesque appearance; his arms are still visible there.
