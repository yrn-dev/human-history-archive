# Winchester College

Winchester College is an English educational institution that has served as a foundation for numerous scholars, divines, and public figures over several centuries.

## Historical Scholars and Alumni
The archive records several notable individuals who received their education at Winchester College across different eras. In the 15th century, William Grocyn (born approximately 1446) was sent to the college before being elected to a scholarship at New College, Oxford, in 1465. Grocyn later became a fellow at Oxford and a lecturer at Exeter College, where he helped introduce the "new Greek learning" to his countrymen following his studies in Italy.

In later centuries, the college continued to produce prominent figures. Robert Lowth (1710–1787), an Orientalist and divine who eventually became the Bishop of London and Dean of the Chapel Royal, was educated on the foundation of Winchester College. Lowth is noted for his work on Hebrew poetry and his biography, *Life of William of Wykeham*.

In the 17th century, Thomas Ken (1637–1711), a non-juring bishop and hymnologist, entered Winchester College in 1652. Ken later returned to the college as a fellow and served as a curate in one of its lowest districts. During this period, he composed hymns and authored the *Manual of Prayers for the use of the Scholars of Winchester College*, which was first published in 1674.

## Associated Figures and Clergy
The college has maintained strong ties with ecclesiastical figures and other academic institutions. George Butler (1819–1890), the son of the former head master of Harrow, served as a canon of Winchester. Additionally, the archive mentions that Thomas Ken served as a prebendary of the cathedral and chaplain to the bishop while maintaining his fellowship at the college.
