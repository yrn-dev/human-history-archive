# Edmund Bolton

## Life and Education
Edmund Bolton (also spelled Boulton) was an English poet and historian who, by his own account, was born in 1575. He was raised as a Roman Catholic and received his education at Trinity Hall, Cambridge. Following his studies, he resided at the Inner Temple in London.

## Courtly Career and Projects
Bolton served as a retainer to the duke of Buckingham. Utilizing the duke's influence, Bolton was able to secure a small position at the court of James I. During this period, Bolton formulated a project to establish an English academy. While King James I viewed the scheme favorably, the project ultimately fell through following the king's death.

## Literary and Historical Works
Bolton was a prolific writer whose works spanned poetry, criticism, and history. In 1600, he contributed to *England's Helicon*. Among his most significant works are:

*   ***Hypercritica*** (1618?): A short critical treatise that is considered valuable for the notices it contains regarding contemporary authors. This work was later reprinted in 1815 within Joseph Haslewood's *Ancient Critical Essays* (vol. ii).
*   ***Nero Caesar, or Monarchic Depraved*** (1624): A work that includes special notes concerning British affairs.

Bolton also wrote a *Life of King Henry II* intended for Speed's *Chronicle*. However, this piece was substituted with a life written by Dr. John Barcham because Bolton's Catholic sympathies were evident in his treatment of Thomas Becket.

## Death
Records indicate that Bolton was still living in 1633, though the exact date of his death remains unknown.
