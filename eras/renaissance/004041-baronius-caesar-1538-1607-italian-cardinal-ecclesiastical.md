# BARONIUS, CAESAR (1538-1607)

Caesar Baronius was an Italian ecclesiastical historian and cardinal born in Sora. He received his education in Naples and Veroli before moving to Rome, where he joined the Oratory in 1557 under the guidance of St Philip Neri. Baronius eventually succeeded St Philip as the superior of the order in 1593.

## Ecclesiastical Career and Papal Relations
Baronius served as the confessor to Pope Clement VIII, who appointed him as the librarian of the Vatican and elevated him to the rank of cardinal in 1596. Despite his standing, Baronius was twice nearly elected pope during subsequent conclaves. On both occasions, his election was blocked by Spain. This opposition stemmed from his work *On the Monarchy of Sicily*, in which Baronius supported the claims of the papacy against the claims of the Spanish government.

## The Annales Ecclesiastici
Baronius is most recognized for his work *Annales Ecclesiastici*, which was undertaken by the order of St Philip as a response to the *Magdeburg Centuries*. The production of this work followed nearly thirty years of Baronius lecturing on Church history at the Vallicella, during which time he was trained by St Philip for this specific task. Between 1588 and 1607, Baronius produced twelve folios for the *Annales*.

In this work, Baronius treats history in a strict chronological order and keeps theology in the background. While the work contains many errors—particularly regarding Greek history, where Baronius relied on secondhand information—it is regarded as an honest attempt to write history driven by a sincere love of truth. This reputation for integrity was noted by Sarpi, who warned Casaubon against suspecting Baronius of bad faith or disloyalty to the truth. Baronius himself expressed a desire for his errors to be corrected rigidly and severely, quoting St Augustine in this regard.

## Historical Perspectives and Controversies
The *Annales Ecclesiastici* contains various positions on early Church history and papal tradition:

*   **Constantine and the Papacy:** Baronius acted as an "unblushing advocate" for the legend that Constantine was baptized in Rome thirteen years before his death. Regarding the act of donation, Baronius suggested that Rome was offered by Constantine but refused by Silvester, and he viewed the act of donation as a forgery created by the Greeks.
*   **Early Church Figures:** In his writings, Baronius employed the works of the "infidel" Zosimus for a specific service against the Arian Eusebius. He also displayed extreme "good humor" toward the emperor Justinian during the early years of the emperor's reign, when Justinian courted the popes.
*   **Later Church History:** Baronius expressed fierce rejection of a decree by the clergy and people of Rome from A.D. 964. He also wrote invectives against certain popes, though these were later tempered by the historian Muratori. Additionally, Baronius used passionate execrations to describe acts of sacrilege occurring around A.D. 536 and 538.

## Other Works and Contributions
Beyond the *Annales*, Baronius undertook a new edition of the Roman martyrology in 1586, which he used to purify the text of numerous inaccuracies. His research also utilized other sources, such as a Latin translation of a Greek Life of St. Nicon the Armenian provided by the Jesuit Sirmond, which provided information on the Peloponnesus and Crete in the 10th century.
