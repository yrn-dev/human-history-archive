# AYLMER, JOHN (1521-1594)

## Early Life and Education
John Aylmer was an English divine born in 1521 at Aylmer Hall, located in Tivetshall St Mary, Norfolk. During his childhood, his precocity was noted by Henry Grey, the marquis of Dorset (who later became the duke of Suffolk). Grey provided the means for Aylmer to attend Cambridge, where he is believed to have served as a fellow of Queens' College.

## Early Career and Exile
Around 1541, Aylmer was appointed as a tutor to Lady Jane Grey and served as chaplain to the duke. His initial ecclesiastical preferment was the archdeaconry of Stow within the diocese of Lincoln. However, Aylmer's tenure there was interrupted when he opposed the doctrine of transubstantiation during convocation. This opposition resulted in his deprivation of office and forced him to flee into Switzerland.

While in exile in Switzerland, Aylmer engaged in scholarly and polemical work. He assisted John Foxe in the Latin translation of the *Acts of the Martyrs* and authored a response to John Knox's work, *Blast against the Monstrous Regiment of Women*, titled *An Harborowe for Faithfull and Trewe Subjects, &c.*

## Return to England and Ecclesiastical Rise
Following the accession of Elizabeth, Aylmer returned to England. He resumed his position in the Stow archdeaconry in 1559 and subsequently obtained the archdeaconry of Lincoln in 1562. During that same year, he participated in the famous convocation of 1562, which was responsible for settling the discipline and doctrine of the Church of England and reforming it.

## Episcopacy and Reputation
In 1576, Aylmer was consecrated as the bishop of London. His tenure in this role was marked by notoriety due to his harsh treatment of individuals who held differing views on ecclesiastical matters, regardless of whether they were Papists or Puritans. Because of this, various attempts were made to transfer him to a different see.

Aylmer's conduct and character were targeted in the *Marprelate Tracts*. Additionally, in the July section of Spenser's *Shepheard's Calendar*, he is characterized as "Morrell," described as a bad shepherd. It has been noted that his reputation as a scholar hardly balanced his inadequacy as a bishop during the transition period in which he lived.

## Death and Legacy
John Aylmer died in June 1594. A biography of his life was written by John Strype in 1701.
