# The Orsini Family and Related Historical Events

The Orsini family appears across several centuries of Italian and ecclesiastical history, characterized by their involvement in high-level political conflicts, the papacy, and territorial disputes.

## Conflicts with the Papacy and the Borgias
In the early 14th century, the Orsini family was involved in the downfall of Pope Boniface VIII. After the Pope had been arrested at Anagni in 103 by Guillaume de Nogaret, an agent of the French king, he was eventually liberated by townspeople and returned to Rome. However, upon his return, he was seized and imprisoned by the Orsini family. Boniface VIII died as their prisoner a few weeks later.

Centuries later, the Orsini were again embroiled in conflict, this time with Cesare Borgia. During Cesare's expansion in the Romagna, several deposed princes, including the Orsinis, joined a conspiracy at La Magione. This plot included Cesare's own captains—such as G.P. Baglioni, Oliverotto da Fermo, and Vitellozzo Vitelli—who feared the loss of their estates. This alliance led to risings in Romagna and Urbino, resulting in the defeat of papal troops.

## The Seizure of Senigallia
Following the conspiracy, some of the plotters sought to regain Cesare Borgia's favor. To demonstrate their repentance, Paolo Orsini, Vitellozzo Vitelli, Oliverotto, and the duke of Gravina seized the town of Senigallia in Cesare's name, as the town was still held for the duke of Urbino. 

Upon arriving at Senigallia, Cesare Borgia decoyed these unsuspecting *condottieri* into his house. Once there, he had them all arrested.

## Later Influence and the Papacy
The Orsini family continued to be a factor in Roman politics into the early 16th century. In early 1503, Cesare Borgia participated in the reduction of the last rebel Orsinis. However, following the death of the pope on August 18, 1503, and the subsequent incapacitation of Cesare due to fever, the Orsinis "raised their heads once more" in Rome, contributing to the shaking of Cesare's position.

In the 18th century, the family produced a pope. Piero Francesco Orsini, born on February 2, 1649, to the ducal family of Orsini-Gravina, was elected pope on May 29, 1724, taking the name Benedict XIII. Before his papacy, he had been a Dominican (as Vincentius Maria), a cardinal in 1672, and the archbishop of Benevento in 1686. As pope, he attempted to reform clerical morals and confirmed the bull *Unigenitus*. He allowed Dominicans to preach the Augustinian doctrine of grace, despite the Jesuits. He left the management of state affairs to Cardinal Nicolo Coscia. Benedict XIII died on February 21, 1730.
