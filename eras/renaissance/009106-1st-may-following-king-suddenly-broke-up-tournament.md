# The Arrest and Execution of Anne Boleyn

## The Tournament at Greenwich
On the 1st of May, the king abruptly ended a tournament taking place at Greenwich. This sudden action left those in attendance in a state of consternation and bewilderment. The reason for the king's behavior became clear shortly thereafter: inquiries had been conducted following reports regarding the ill-conduct of the queen. As a result of these inquiries, several individuals reputed to be her lovers were arrested.

## Charges and Legal Proceedings
On the 2nd of May, Anne was committed to the Tower. She faced charges of adultery with various people, which included her own brother, Lord Rochford. On the 12th of May, four men—Mark Smeaton, William Brereton, Henry Norris, and Sir Francis Weston—were declared guilty of high treason.

On the 15th of May, an assembly of twenty-six peers unanimously condemned both Lord Rochford and Anne. The proceedings were presided over by the duke of Norfolk, Anne's uncle, acting as lord steward. While delivering the sentence, the duke wept, stating that his niece was to be beheaded or burned, according to the king's pleasure.

The trials were marked by the reactions of those close to Anne. Her former lover, the earl of Northumberland, was seized by a sudden illness and left the court. Anne's father was excused from attending the proceedings against her, though he had been present at the trials of the other offenders, where he declared that he was convinced of his daughter's guilt.

## The Invalidity of the Marriage
On the 16th of May, Anne informed Cranmer of a supposed impediment to her marriage with the king, likely in an attempt to save herself. Some accounts suggest this impediment was a previous marriage to Northumberland, although Northumberland solemnly and positively denied this. While this information was never disclosed publicly, it was reviewed by a committee of ecclesiastical lawyers and the archbishop. On the 17th of May, they pronounced the impediment sufficient to invalidate the marriage.

## Execution and Aftermath
The reputed lovers of the queen were all executed on the 17th of May. Anne herself suffered death on the 19th of May on Tower Green. The execution was carried out by the executioner of Calais, who had been brought to England specifically for this purpose, and her head was struck off with a sword.

According to Sir William Kingston, the governor of the Tower, Anne faced her death with courage and a level of levity. Kingston noted that while other men and women he had seen executed were in great sorrow, Anne seemed to find "much joy and pleasure in death," even laughing heartily as she placed her hands around her "little neck" and commented on the skill of the executioner.

On the day following Anne's execution, Henry was betrothed to Jane Seymour.
