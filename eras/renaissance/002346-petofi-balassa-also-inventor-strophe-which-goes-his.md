# Balint Balassa

## Early Life and Education
Baron Balint Balassa of Kekko and Gyarmat (1551–1594) was a Magyar lyric poet born at Kekko. His early education was provided by his mother, Anna Sulyok, who is described as a highly gifted Protestant zealot, and by the reformer Peter Bornemissza. 

Balassa's initial literary contribution was a translation of Michael Bock's *Wuertzgertlein fuer die krancken Seelen*. This work was produced between 1570 and 1572 for the purpose of comforting his father, who had been imprisoned for a political offence. Following his father's release, Balint accompanied him to court and attended the coronation diet of Pressburg in 1572.

## Military Career and Personal Life
Balassa joined the army and spent time at the fortress of Eger, where he fell in love with Anna Losonczi, the daughter of the hero of Temesvar. Although his verses indicate that this love was not unrequited, Balassa was eventually drawn away by another mistress. Consequently, Anna Losonczi married Krisztof Ungnad. Balassa later expressed his love for Anna through gifts and verses, though she remained faithful to her marriage vows; he subsequently immortalized her memory in his poetry.

In 1574, Balassa was sent to assist Gaspar Bekesy against Stephen Bathory. During this mission, his troops were scattered, and he was severely wounded and taken prisoner. After a captivity of two years, he disappeared from the historical record until 1584.

## Later Years and Misfortunes
In 1584, Balassa married Christina Dobo, the daughter of the commandant of Eger. This marriage led to significant hardships, as the greedy relatives of his wife pursued him through legal processes that nearly ruined him. In 1586, Balassa converted to Catholicism to escape these persecutions, prompting his wife's relatives to declare that he and his son had become Turks.

Following his desertion of his wife, Balassa was expelled from Hungary. From 1589 to 1594, he lived as a vagabond in Poland, where he engaged in numerous amours with women ranging from princesses to cithara players. He returned to Hungary in 1594 due to the Turkish war and died of wounds sustained during the siege of Esztergom that same year.

The poems of Balint Balassa are categorized into four divisions, one of which is religious hymns.
