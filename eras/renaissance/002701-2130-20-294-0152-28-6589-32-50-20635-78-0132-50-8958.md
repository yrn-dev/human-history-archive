# Lower Austria and Related Archive Records

## Geography of Lower Austria
Lower Austria, known in German as *Niederoesterreich* or *Oesterreich unter der Enns* ("Austria below the river Enns"), is an archduchy and crownland of Austria. The region is bounded to the north by Moravia and Bohemia, to the west by Upper Austria and Bohemia, to the south by Styria, and to the east by Hungary. It encompasses an area of 7,654 square miles.

The Danube river divides the province into two parts, entering at the most westerly point and exiting at the eastern extremity near Pressburg. The area north of the Danube consists of low hilly country called the *Waldviertel*, which continues the Moravian and Bohemian plateau. Notable altitudes in this region include the Peilstein in the Weinsberger Wald (3,478 ft.), the Gfoehler Wald (2,368 ft.), and the Manhartsgebirge (1,758 ft.). Near Vienna, the Bisamberg reaches 1,180 ft.

The southern division is primarily mountainous and hilly, characterized by the Lower Austrian Alps. The highest summit in the province is the Schneeberg at 6,806 ft. Other principal groups include the Raxalpe (6,589 ft.), the Oetscher (6,205 ft.), the Duerrenstein (6,156 ft.), and the Voralpe (5,802 ft.). Additional groups east of the Semmering ridge include the Wechsel (5,700 ft.) and the Leithagebirge (1,674 ft.). The Wiener Wald, an offshoot of the Alpine group, reaches 2,929 ft. in the Schoepfl and ends northwest of Vienna at the Leopoldsberg (1,380 ft.) and Kahlenberg (1,404 ft.).

## Alexander Dallas Bache
Alexander Dallas Bache (1806–1867) was an American physicist and the great-grandson of Benjamin Franklin. Born in Philadelphia on July 19, 1806, Bache graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1825. He served as a lieutenant in the corps of engineers, spending one or two years erecting coast fortifications, and also worked as an assistant professor at West Point.

Between 1828 and 1841, and again from 1842 to 1843, he served as the professor of chemistry and natural philosophy at the University of Pennsylvania. From 1836 to 1838, he traveled to Europe to examine educational systems on behalf of the trustees of what would become Girard College in 1848. Following the death of Professor F. R. Hassler in 1843, Bache was appointed superintendent of the United States coast survey. He implemented a comprehensive plan involving numerous observing stations and a division of labour to map the entire coast, while simultaneously collecting meteorological and magnetic observations. He died on February 17, 1867, in Newport, Rhode Island.

## Editions of Francis Bacon's Works
Several significant editions of the works of Bacon exist across various languages. T. Fowler published an edition of *Novum Organum* in 1878 (with an 1889 edition) that included a bibliography and an introduction to Bacon's philosophy, superseding G. W. Kitchin's 1855 edition. The *Essays* have seen over twenty editions since 1870, including those by W. Aldis Wright (1862), Archbishop Whately (6th ed., 1864), E. A. Abbott (1879), John Buchan (1879), F. Storr and Gibson (1886), and W. Evans (1897). A facsimile of the first edition was released in New York in 1904.

Other notable publications include:
*   *Advancement of Learning*: Edited by W. Aldis Wright (1866; 5th ed., 1900), F. G. Selby (1892–1895), and H. Morley (1905). It was also included in the "World's Classics" series in 1906 with an introduction by Prof. T. Case.
*   *Wisdom of the Ancients and New Atlantis*: Published in "Cassell's National Library" in 1886 and 1903.
*   *New Atlantis*: Edited by G. C. M. Smith in 1900.
*   *Kleinere Schriften*: Published by J. Fuerstenhagen in Leipzig in 1884.
