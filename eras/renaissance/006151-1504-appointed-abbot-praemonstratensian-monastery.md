# Johann Bugenhagen

Johann Bugenhagen (1485–1558), also known by the surname Pomeranus, was a German Protestant reformer. He was born on June 24, 1485, at Wollin, located near Stettin.

## Early Career and Academic Appointments
Bugenhagen attended the university of Greifswald, where he achieved significant distinction as a humanist. In 1504, he was appointed as the rector of the town school at Treptow by the abbot of the Praemonstratensian monastery at Belbuck. He continued his ecclesiastical progression in 1509, when he was ordained as a priest and served as a vicar in the collegiate *Marienkirche* at Treptow. Later, in 1517, he returned to the abbey school at Belbuck, where he was appointed as a lecturer on the Church Fathers and the Bible.

## Conversion and the Reformation
Bugenhagen's religious views shifted in 1520 after reading Luther's *De Captivitate Babylonica*. This work converted him into a zealous supporter of the Reformer's views, and he subsequently won over the abbot of Belbuck to these beliefs. 

In 1521, Bugenhagen traveled to Wittenberg. There, he developed a close friendship with Melanchthon and Luther. He began lecturing and preaching at the university, and he married in 1522. His organizing skills and zeal allowed his reforming influence to extend beyond the university. Between 1528 and 1530, he arranged church affairs in several regions, including Hamburg, Brunswick, Pomerania, and Lübeck.

## Work in Denmark and Later Life
In 1537, Christian III invited Bugenhagen to Denmark. He remained in the country for five years, during which time he organized schools and the church. Although he held the rank of only a presbyter, he performed the consecration of the new Danish bishops.

Bugenhagen spent the remaining years of his life at Wittenberg. Despite the perils of persecution and war, he refused to leave the city, as he considered it the home of the Reformation. He died on April 20, 1558.

## Literary Contributions
Bugenhagen authored numerous works, including a history of Pomerania, though this specific text remained unpublished until 1728. His *Interpretatio in Librum Psalmorum*, published in 1523, is regarded as perhaps his best book. Additionally, he is remembered for providing assistance to Luther in the translation of the Bible.
