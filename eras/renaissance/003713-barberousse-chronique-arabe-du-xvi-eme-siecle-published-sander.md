# La Fondation de la regence d'Alger, histoire des Barberousse, chronique arabe du XVI^{eme} siecle

## Overview
*La Fondation de la regence d'Alger, histoire des Barberousse, chronique arabe du XVI^{eme} siecle* is a historical text providing a Moslem version of the story of the Barbarossas. The work was published in Paris in 1837 by Ferdinand Denis and Sander Rang.

## Historical Context and Utility
The chronicle serves as a source for the history of the Barbarossas and the establishment of the regency of Algiers. Within the broader study of the Ottoman Empire, it is noted as a "curious" version of events. It is cited as a necessary authority to correct other accounts of the Barbarossas, such as those found in Joseph von Hammer-Purgstall's *The History of the Ottoman Empire* (French translation by J. J. Hellert, 1835-1843).

## Related Authorities on the Barbary States
The *chronique arabe* exists alongside several other primary and secondary authorities used to reconstruct the early history of the Barbary states and the campaigns of the Barbarossas:

*   **Primary Authorities:** Other essential early sources include Luis del Marmol Carvajal's *Descripcion de Africa* (1573), Diego de Haedo's *Topographia e Historia General de Argel* (1612), and Pere Pierre Dan's *Histoire de Barbarie et de ses corsaires* (1637).
*   **Ottoman Records:** *The History of the Maritime Wars of the Turks* by Hajji Khalifa (translated by J. Mitchell, 1831) is reported to be based on evidence collected by order of Sultan Suleiman.
*   **Later Research:** H. D. de Grammont's *Histoire d'Alger* (1887) utilizes original authorities and discusses the origin of the name "Barberousse" in the *Revue Africaine* (No. 171). Additionally, Ad. Jurien de la Graviere provided technical knowledge of their campaigns in *Doria et Barberousse* (1886) and *Les Corsaires barbaresques et la marine de Soliman le Grand* (1887).
