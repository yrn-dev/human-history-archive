# Nota de Joanne Bale and the Catalogus of John Bale

The *Nota de Joanne Bale* is a specific assertion made by John Pits (also known as Pitseus), an English Catholic exile who lived from 1560 to 1616. In this note, Pits claims that the *Catalogus* written by John Bale was a misrepresentation of the materials provided by John Leland.

## The Work of John Pits
John Pits founded his work, *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus* (published in Paris, 1619), upon the earlier work of John Bale. This publication is more commonly recognized by its running title, *De illustribus Angliae scriptoribus*, and serves as the fourth book of a more extensive project. 

In composing this work, Pits omitted the Protestant divines and Wycliffites that Bale had included. The most significant portion of Pits' work consists of the lives of Catholic exiles who resided in French towns, including Douai. It is within this context that Pits included the *Nota de Joanne Bale*, asserting that Bale had misrepresented Leland's matter. However, archive records suggest that Pits likely had no direct acquaintance with Leland's original work, having accessed it only second-hand through Bale.

## John Bale's Catalogues
John Bale (1495–1563), the Bishop of Ossory, was an English author and an ardent partisan of the Reformers. He is noted as an indefatigable worker and collector who examined many valuable libraries of the Carmelite and Augustinian houses prior to their dissolution. 

Bale produced two significant catalogues of British authors and their works:
*   **Illustrium majoris Britanniae scriptorum...**: Published in 1548 and 1549 (Ipswich and Wesel), this work originally contained five centuries.
*   **Scriptorum illustrium majoris Britanniae... Catalogus**: Printed at Basel between 1557 and 1559, this edition was almost entirely rewritten and expanded to cover fourteen centuries.

Bale's *Catalogus* was partly based on the *Commentarii* and *Collectanea* of John Leland. While the work is considered invaluable because it preserved information that would otherwise have been lost, it is also characterized by inaccuracies and the use of abuse directed toward Catholic writers.

## Evidence from Bale's Notebook
The archive contains information regarding Bale's autograph notebook, which is preserved in the Bodleian Library's Selden Collection at Oxford. This notebook contains the materials Bale collected for his two published catalogues. 

Unlike the completed *Catalogus*, the notebook is arranged alphabetically and lacks the "personalities" that deface the final published work. Furthermore, the notebook generally provides the sources from which Bale derived his information. This notebook was later prepared for publication as *Index Britanniae Scriptorum quos ... collegit Ioannes Baleus* (Clarendon Press, 1902) by Dr. R. Lane Poole and Miss Mary Bateson.

In this notebook, Bale recorded the wholesale destruction of libraries. He specifically noted his time in Norwich, the "second citye of name," where he observed library monuments being used by worldly occupiers such as sopesellers, candelmakers, and grossers. Bale recorded as many authors' names and titles of works as he could from Norwich and other places in Southfolke and Northfolke, though he noted he could not have done so throughout the entire realm due to the charges involved.
