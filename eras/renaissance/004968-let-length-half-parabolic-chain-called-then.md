# Parabolic Chains and Related Curves

In the study of structural engineering and mathematics, a chain under specific loading conditions forms a mathematical curve. While a chain of uniform weight per unit of length assumes the form of a true catenary, a chain under a weight that is uniformly distributed relative to a horizontal line assumes the form of a parabola. This parabolic form is generally adopted for the construction of suspension bridges.

## Mathematical Properties of the Parabolic Chain

For a suspension bridge with a span $L$ and supports at equal heights, the horizontal tension $H$ at the vertex is expressed as $H = wL^2/8y$. If $x$ represents the distance from the vertex to the point of support, this is also expressed as $H = wx^2/2y$. The value of $H$ is equal to the compression on the top flange or the maximum tension on the bottom flange of a girder with a similar load, equal span, and a depth equal to the dip of the suspension bridge.

At any point $F$ on the curve at a distance $x$ from the vertex, the vertical component $V$ is the sum of the loads between the vertex $O$ and point $F$, or $wx$. The resultant tension $R$ at any point at a distance $x$ from the vertex is determined by the equation $R^2 = H^2 + V^2 = w^2x^4/4y^2 + w^2x^2$, which simplifies to $R = wx\sqrt{1+x^2/4y^2}$. The angle $i$ between the tangent at any point (with coordinates $x$ and $y$ measured from the vertex) is defined by $\tan i = 2y/x$.

## Chain Length and Deflection

The length of half of a parabolic chain, denoted as $s$, is calculated using the formula $s = x + 2y^2/3x$. 

To determine the relationship between a change in the dip ($\Delta y$) and a corresponding change in the length of the half chain ($\Delta s$), the following approximate expressions are used:
*   $\Delta s = 4y\Delta y/3x$
*   $\Delta y = 3x\Delta s/4y$

These equations allow for the calculation of deflection resulting from a change in temperature or any given stress applied to the chains.

## Engineering Applications

### Suspension Bridges and Viaducts
Parabolic principles are applied in various large-scale structures. The Tower Bridge in London utilizes suspension chains constructed as braced girders to maintain stiffness against unsymmetrical loading. These chains are carried across the center span as horizontal ties resting on high-level footway girders.

In France, the Garabit viaduct features a crescent-shaped arch with a span of 541 ft. and a rise of 213 ft. The center line of this arch follows nearly the parabolic line of pressures.

### Other Parabolic Forms
Beyond heavy engineering, parabolic shapes appear in other contexts. In 15th-century Italy, hunting horns measuring 3 or 4 ft. in length were used; these instruments featured a bell that was already slightly parabolic.
