# John Hervey, 1st Earl of Bristol

John Hervey, 1st Earl of Bristol, was a British politician and peer who lived from 1665 to 1751. Born on August 27, 1665, he received his education at Clare Hall, Cambridge.

## Political Career and Peerages
Hervey entered parliament in March 1694, serving as the member for Bury St Edmunds. His political ascent was marked by his support for the Hanoverian succession and his zeal in promoting the principles of the revolution. As a result of these efforts, he was created Baron Hervey of Ickworth in March 1703. In October 1714, he was further elevated to the rank of earl of Bristol.

## Family and Descendants
The 1st Earl was married twice. His first wife was Isabella, the daughter of Sir Robert Carr, Bart., of Sleaford, who died in 1693. Together they had one son, Carr, Lord Hervey (1691–1723). Carr followed in his father's footsteps by being educated at Clare Hall, Cambridge, and served as the member for Bury St Edmunds from 1713 until 1722. Carr died unmarried on November 14, 1723; it has been suggested that he was the father of Horace Walpole.

The 1st Earl's second marriage was to Elizabeth, the daughter and co-heiress of Sir Thomas Felton, Bart., of Playford, Suffolk. Elizabeth died in 1741, and the couple had sixteen children: ten sons and six daughters.

Among their sons were:
* **John (1696–1743):** He assumed the courtesy title of Lord Hervey following the death of his half-brother Carr in 1723. He was known as a politician and a writer.
* **Thomas (1699–1775):** He served as a member for Bury from 1733 to 1747 and held various court offices. Thomas was known for a reckless lifestyle that led to pecuniary and other difficulties, and he suffered from very poor health. He wrote numerous pamphlets. Upon his death, Dr Johnson described him as one of the genteelest men who ever lived, despite being a vicious man. Thomas also eloped with Elizabeth, the wife of Sir Thomas Hanmer.
* **Felton (1712–1773):** He also served as a member for the family borough of Bury St Edmunds.

The lineage continued through Felton's grandson, Felton Elwell Hervey-Bathurst (1782–1819), who adopted the additional name of Bathurst and was created a baronet in 1818. Upon his death in 1819, the title passed to his brother, Frederick Anne (1783–1824).

## Death and Succession
John Hervey, 1st Earl of Bristol, died on January 20, 1751. Following his death, his estates and title descended to his grandson.
