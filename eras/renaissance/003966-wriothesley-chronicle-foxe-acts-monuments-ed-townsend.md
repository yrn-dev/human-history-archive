# Wriothesley's Chronicle and Foxe's Acts and Monuments

Wriothesley's *Chronicle* and Foxe's *Acts and Monuments* (edited by G. Townsend) are historical sources frequently cited in the study of the English Reformation and the reign of Henry VIII. Both works provide accounts of the religious and political upheavals of the 16th century, including the trials of reformers and the actions of the English government.

## Wriothesley's Chronicle
Wriothesley's *Chronicle* serves as a contemporary record of the Tudor period. One specific account detailed in the *Chronicle* concerns the trial and execution of Anne, who was arraigned before a commission on June 18, 1546. This commission included the duke of Norfolk, the lord mayor, Heath, Bonner, and St John. 

According to the *Chronicle*, Anne was condemned to be burnt based solely on her confession, without the empaneling of a jury or the calling of witnesses. Following her condemnation, she was committed to Newgate and subsequently taken to the Tower. Wriothesley's *Chronicle* records that the solicitor-general, Rich, and the lord chancellor, Wriothesley, personally operated the rack during her torture. Despite this, the text notes that she "would not convert for all the pain." Anne was eventually burnt at Smithfield on July 16, 1546.

## Foxe's Acts and Monuments
Foxe's *Acts and Monuments*, in the edition edited by G. Townsend, is a primary source for the history of the reforming party in England. The work draws upon various authorities, including two tracts by Bale printed in November 1546 and January 1547.

The text is utilized as a reference for several key figures and events of the Reformation:
* **Religious Figures:** The work is cited in relation to the life and career of Hooper, a representative of the extreme reforming party in England. Hooper held Zwinglian views and sought to spread a form of religion based on the *Consensus Tigurinus* (1549), which was negotiated between Calvinists and Zwinglians in Switzerland.
* **Ecclesiastical Conflict:** The *Acts and Monuments* is cited regarding the history of Bonner, who refused the oath of supremacy in May 1559 during the accession of Elizabeth. This led to his imprisonment in the Marshalsea and a later indictment on charges of praemunire.
* **Biblical History:** The work is referenced in discussions regarding Wycliffite versions of the Bible.

## Historical Context and Related Sources
Both Wriothesley's and Foxe's works are often used in conjunction with other archival materials to reconstruct the events of the 16th century. These include the *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII*, the *Acts of the Privy Council* (specifically those from 1542-1547 and 1542-1569), and the *Narratives of the Reformation*.

In the case of the trial of Anne, while Wriothesley's account of her torture is substantiated by two other contemporary chronicles and a contemporary letter, it has been disputed by other historians such as Lingard and Jardine.
