# Letters and Papers of Henry VIII

The *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* is a comprehensive collection of historical documents and official records pertaining to the reign of King Henry VIII. It serves as a primary source for historians researching the political, religious, and personal affairs of the English monarchy during the 16th century.

## Scope and Content
The collection is organized into numerous volumes, with specific references spanning from volume iv through volume xx. The documents within these volumes provide detailed evidence on a wide array of historical events and figures. For example, volumes xi and xii are cited in relation to the English monasteries and accounts of rebellion, including examinations and answers to interrogations. Other portions of the collection, specifically those covering the years 1540 to 1545, are utilized alongside Scottish state papers and the acts of the English Privy Council to document domestic and foreign affairs.

## Application in Religious and Ecclesiastical History
The *Letters and Papers* are frequently cited in the study of the English Reformation and the restructuring of the church. The records are used to support histories of the church written by authors such as Gairdner and Dixon, as well as the works of Foxe and Burnet. Specifically, volumes vi and vii are referenced in studies concerning the life of Blessed John Fisher.

The collection also provides critical data regarding the clergy and the state's relationship with religious authorities. It is cited in the records of individuals such as Bonner, who faced charges of praemunire after refusing the oath of supremacy in May 1559.

## Documentation of the Monarchy and Court
The archive is a central source for reconstructing the personal life and legal maneuvers of Henry VIII and his consorts. 

### Anne Boleyn
The *Letters and Papers* provide specific details regarding Anne Boleyn. Volume vi, page 300, contains information from Cranmer regarding the authority of certain matters, noting that Cranmer himself only became aware of them a fortnight later. Volume x (pages 374, 381, 385) is cited regarding the events surrounding her life. The archive also helps clarify historical misconceptions; for instance, a well-known letter attributed to Anne Boleyn is recognized through these records as an Elizabethan forgery.

### Other Figures
The documents are used to trace the early life of figures such as Lord Burghley. Additionally, the collection contains records from 1537 and 1542 that are referenced in studies of continental figures and academic records, such as those from the *Album academiae vitebergensis*.

## Relation to Other Historical Sources
The *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* are often used in conjunction with other primary and secondary sources to provide a complete historical picture. These include:
* **Chronicles:** The *Chronicle of Henry VIII* (translated by M.A.S. Hume) and Wriothesley's *Chronicle*.
* **State Papers:** The *Calendar of State Papers* for England and Spain, as well as Venetian and Foreign papers.
* **Legal and Parliamentary Records:** The *Acts of the Privy Council*, the *Lords' Journals*, and the *State Trials*.
* **Biographical Works:** The *Dictionary of National Biography* and various histories by A.F. Pollard, J.A. Froude, and P. Friedman.

The collection was edited by Brewer and Gardiner, whose prefaces are noted as significant resources for researchers.
