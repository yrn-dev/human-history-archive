# The Winter's Tale

*The Winter's Tale* is a play written by William Shakespeare during his final creative period, which began around 1608. This phase of his work is characterized by the composition of tragi-comedies, including *Pericles, Prince of Tyre*, *Cymbeline*, and *The Tempest*.

## Literary Context and Structure
As a tragi-comedy, *The Winter's Tale* follows a specific narrative trajectory. The play begins with a series of estrangements, calumnies, or crimes, but eventually moves toward a final scene of "enthralling beauty." In this resolution, love is recovered and those who were lost reappear. This structure reflects a shift in Shakespeare's later work toward recording the "happy turns" by which tragic disasters are averted, providing a sense of retrieval and hope through the depiction of young and happy creatures.

To achieve this effect, Shakespeare utilized the "free atmosphere and loose action" of the *roman d'aventure*. This style had been adapted by Beaumont and Fletcher, who may have provided Shakespeare with successful theatrical effects and who subsequently studied Shakespeare's own work.

## Linguistic and Botanical References
The text of *The Winter's Tale* contains specific imagery and vocabulary that illustrate the language and natural observations of the period.

### The Use of "Bolted"
In the play, Shakespeare uses the phrase, "The fann'd snow, That's bolted by the northern blasts twice o'er." In this context, the word "bolted" is derived from "bolt" or "boult," meaning to sift. This usage is related to the Old French *buleter* (from the Medieval Latin *buretare* or *buletare*). This sense of sifting is also seen in other literary works, such as Burke's description of a committee report being "sifted and bolted to the bran," or Milton's *Comus*, where the term is used to mean discussing or mooting arguments.

### Natural Imagery
The play includes references to the natural world, specifically in Act IV, Scene 3:
*   **The Marigold:** Shakespeare refers to "The marigold, that goes to bed wi' the sun." The marigold (*Calendula officinalis*) is a garden plant known for orange-coloured blossoms.
*   **The Kite:** The character Autolycus provides the advice, "When the kite builds, look to lesser linen." This is a practical observation regarding the habits of the kite, a bird that commonly frequented the drying-grounds of laundresses in olden times.

## Scholarly Reception
*The Winter's Tale* has been the subject of extensive academic study. American Shakespearian scholar Horace Howard Furness (1833–), a graduate of Harvard and a member of the bar, included the play in his Variorum edition. Furness aimed to summarize the conclusions of the best textual, critical, and annotative authorities across all languages. His volume on *The Winter's Tale* was published in 1898. In this specific volume, as with his works on *Othello* and others published from 1858 onward, Furness used the First Folio text as his foundation.
