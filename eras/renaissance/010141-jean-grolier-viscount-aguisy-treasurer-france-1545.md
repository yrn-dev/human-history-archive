# Jean Grolier

## Biography and Role
Jean Grolier, who held the title of viscount d'Aguisy, served as the treasurer of France in 1545. He is primarily recognized in historical records as a great collector of fine books.

## Book Collecting and Bindings
Grolier commissioned bindings for many of the books in his collection. These bindings are characterized by the inclusion of his name, *Io Grolierii et Amicorum*, and a specific legend: *Portio mea domine sit in terra viventium*. 

Archive illustrations identify a binding made for Grolier as being crafted from pale brown morocco and featuring gold tooling. It is noted that Geoffrey Tory, who served as an engraver and printer to Francis I of France, designed several fine bindings; some of these were for Tory himself, while others were quite possibly designed for Jean Grolier.

## Comparative Binding Styles
The practices of Grolier are sometimes compared to other collectors of the same era. For instance, Tommaso Maioli, an Italian collector, utilized the same form of legend as Grolier. Other notable binding styles of the period include those of Demetrio Canevari, the physician to Pope Urban VIII, whose books were bound in deep red or dark green morocco and featured a colored cameo stamp depicting Apollo driving a chariot with one black and one white horse toward a mountain with a silver Pegasus. 

While bindings created for cardinals and popes in Italy are often regarded as having high merit and interest, later Italian bindings are generally described as disappointing.
