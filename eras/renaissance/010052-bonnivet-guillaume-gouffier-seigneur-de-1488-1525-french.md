# BONNIVET, GUILLAUME GOUFFIER, SEIGNEUR DE

Guillaume Gouffier, Seigneur de Bonnivet (c. 1488–1525), was a French soldier and royal favorite who rose to significant power during the reign of Francis I. He was the younger brother of Artus Gouffier, the seigneur de Boisy, who served as the tutor to Francis I. Having been brought up alongside the future king, Bonnivet became one of the most influential figures in the royal circle following the accession of Francis.

## Political and Diplomatic Career
Bonnivet's ascent within the French administration was marked by high-ranking appointments and diplomatic efforts. In 1515, he was appointed admiral of France. During the imperial election of 1519, he was tasked with superintending the candidature of Francis I. In an attempt to secure the votes of the electors, Bonnivet spent vast sums of money, though these efforts ultimately proved unsuccessful.

Within the royal court, Bonnivet was known for his brilliant wit and handsome appearance, qualities that allowed him to maintain the confidence and intimacy of the king despite his failures as a diplomat and general. He was also characterized as a man of licentious life. According to the writer Brantome, Bonnivet was the successful rival of the king in seeking the favors of Madame de Chateaubriand. Additionally, it is considered very probable that he was the hero of the fourth story of the *Heptameron*, where Marguerite d'Angouleme resisted his importunities.

## Military Commands and Conflicts
Bonnivet's military career was marked by both strategic aggression and significant defeats. He was an implacable enemy of the constable de Bourbon and played a role in the constable's downfall. In 1521, while in command of the army of Navarre, Bonnivet occupied Fuenterrabia. He is believed to have been responsible for the failure to restore the town, an action that led to the renewal of hostilities.

In 1523, Bonnivet succeeded Marshal Lautrec in the command of the army of Italy. Although he succeeded in entering the Milanese, he suffered a defeat that necessitated a disastrous retreat. It was during this retreat that the chevalier Bayard perished.

## Death at the Battle of Pavia
At the end of 1524, Bonnivet served as one of the principal commanders of the army led by Francis I into Italy. This campaign culminated in the battle of Pavia. According to Brantome, the battle was fought at Bonnivet's suggestion. Upon realizing the disaster he had caused, Bonnivet is said to have courted death heroically during the fighting. He died at the battle of Pavia on February 24, 1525.
