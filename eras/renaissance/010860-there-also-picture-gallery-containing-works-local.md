# Picture Galleries and Art Collections in European Towns

The archive contains records of various picture galleries and art collections situated within European towns, ranging from ducal palaces and civic museums to private estates and specialized institutes. These collections house works from diverse periods, including the early Renaissance, the Dutch school, and the works of specific masters such as Van Dyck and Correggio.

## Galleries in Germany and Austria

In the German town of Dessau, the capital of the duchy of Anhalt, the ducal palace contains a gallery of pictures featuring works by Titian, Rubens, Lippi, Van Dyck, and Cimabue. Additionally, the town's Amalienstift possesses its own picture gallery. The Schlosskirche in Dessau is noted for paintings by Lucas Cranach, specifically "The Last Supper," which includes portraits of several reformers.

In Bohemia, Austria, the town of Dux (Czech *Duchcov*) contains a castle belonging to Count Waldstein. This castle houses a picture gallery featuring two portraits of Wallenstein painted by Van Dyck, as well as a museum containing relics of the general and a collection of arms and armour.

## Italian Galleries and Museums

Italy hosts several significant collections across various provinces:

*   **Bologna:** The Museo Civico is described as one of Italy's most important museums. Its picture gallery provides a survey of 16th and 17th-century Bolognese eclectics, including Guercino, Domenichino, Guido Reni, and the Caracci. The collection includes works of merit from 15th-century masters Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara (1460-1535) and Francesco Francia (1450-1517). A primary treasure of this collection is Raphael's *S. Cecilia*, painted around 1515 for the church of S. Giovanni in Monte.
*   **Lovere:** Located in the province of Bergamo, the Palazzo Tadini in Lovere contains a gallery of old pictures, alongside a zoological collection and sculptures by Canova and Benzoni.
*   **Other Regional Collections:** In another Italian location, the public picture gallery is housed in the old ducal palace, which was begun in 1578 by Ammanati. Additionally, the Palazzo Mansi in the same area contains a collection of Dutch pictures.

## English Institutions and Collections

In Staffordshire, England, the market town of Burslem is home to the Wedgwood Institute, founded in 1863 to commemorate Josiah Wedgwood. This institute comprises a picture gallery, a museum, a free library, and a school of art.

## Notable Works and Artists in Galleries

The archive details the placement of specific works by renowned artists within various galleries:

**Correggio**
Several works by Correggio are distributed across European galleries. The Dresden gallery holds the oil painting of the Nativity known as "Night" (*La Notte*), undertaken in 1522 for Alberto Pratoneris, as well as "St George" (painted towards 1532) and "St Sebastian." The "Magdalene lying at the entrance of her Cavern," purchased by Augustus III of Saxony for 6000 louis d'or, is also in Dresden. The gallery at Parma contains the oil painting of St Jerome, known as "Day" (*Il Giorno*), painted from 1527 onwards for Briseida Bergonzi. The Berlin gallery houses the "Leda," which features figures of bathing girls and was begun in 1532.

**Carlo Dolci**
The works of the Florentine artist Carlo Dolci are found in several locations. His most important composition, "St Andrew praying before his Crucifixion" (1646), is in the Pitti gallery. Other notable works include "St Cecilia" in Dresden and the "Four Evangelists" at Florence.

**Philip de Koninck**
The Dutch landscape painter Philip de Koninck, a pupil of Rembrandt, has portraits in the galleries of Christiania and Copenhagen. His landscapes are located in Cologne, Berlin, Florence (Uffizi), Brussels, and Amsterdam. The National Gallery in London holds a replica of his "Vue de l'embouchure d'une rivière," while the original is at the Hague.
