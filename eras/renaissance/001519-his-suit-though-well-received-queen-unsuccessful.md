# Early Career of Francis Bacon

## Initial Court Applications
In 1580, Francis Bacon took the first step in his professional career by seeking a post at court. He made this application through his uncle, the lord treasurer Burghley. Although the queen received his suit well, the effort was unsuccessful. The specific particulars regarding this application are totally unknown.

## Legal Training and Parliament
Following the disappointment of his court application, Bacon spent two years working quietly at Gray's Inn. He was admitted as an outer barrister in 1582. By 1584, he had taken a seat in parliament representing Melcombe in Dorsetshire, though the notes from that session provide no information regarding the reputation he earned during his time there.

## Relationship with Burghley
Around 1584, Bacon made another application to his uncle, Burghley, apparently seeking to expedite his progress at the bar. This request led to a severe letter from Burghley, who took Bacon to task for pride and arrogance, noting that he had "taken his zeal for ambition." Bacon vehemently disclaimed these qualities. Despite this friction, Bacon's advancement at the bar was unusually rapid, suggesting that his uncle's influence may have been used on his behalf.

## Patronage and Professional Struggles
In 1589, Bacon received a substantial piece of patronage from Burghley: the reversion of the clerkship of the Star Chamber. This office was valued at approximately L1600 per year; however, the position did not actually become vacant for nearly twenty years. 

During this period, Bacon's affairs did not prosper, and a significant portion of his life passed by. During this time, he wrote on the condition of parties within the church and authored a now-lost tract titled *Temporis Partus Maximus*, in which he recorded his thoughts on philosophical reform. Despite these intellectual pursuits, he failed to obtain the position he considered an indispensable condition for success. A long and eloquent letter written to Burghley provides further insight into Bacon's character and hints at the reasons why his uncle was slack in promoting him.
