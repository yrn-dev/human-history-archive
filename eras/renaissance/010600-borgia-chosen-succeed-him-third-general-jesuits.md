# Francis Borgia

## Early Life and Court Career
Francis Borgia (1510–1572) was a Roman Catholic saint and the Duke of Gandia. Born in Gandia, Valencia, on October 10, 1510, he was noted for his piety from a young age. Starting at age twelve, he was educated in Saragossa under the guardianship of his uncle, an archbishop. During this period, Borgia developed a strong inclination toward monastic life.

However, in 1528, his father sent him to the court of Charles V. Borgia distinguished himself in this environment and married Eleanor de Castro, a high-ranking Portuguese lady. Following this marriage, he was appointed master of the horse to the empress and created the marquis of Lombay. His service to Charles V included accompanying him on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and a trip to Provence in 1536.

## Transition to Religious Life
A pivotal moment in Borgia's life occurred in 1539 upon the death of the empress. He was tasked with convoying her body to its burial place in Granada. This experience solidified his resolve to embrace a monastic life, provided he survived his wife. Despite this determination, he was granted further honors against his will upon returning to Toledo, being appointed as the commander of the order of St James and the viceroy of Catalonia.

While serving as viceroy at Barcelona, Borgia maintained a life of great austerity while efficiently managing his official duties. In 1543, after succeeding his father as the duke of Gandia, he obtained permission to resign his viceroyalty and retire to Gandia.

## Association with the Jesuits
During his retirement, Borgia maintained correspondence with Ignatius Loyola and became a powerful encourager of the recently founded order of Jesus. His commitment to the order was evidenced by his decision to build a Jesuit college in Gandia. Following the death of his wife, Eleanor, in 1546, Borgia resolved to join the society himself.

Due to family and political circumstances, a papal dispensation was granted, allowing him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years after taking his vows for the benefit of his young children. In 1550, he visited Rome, where he provided the funds necessary for the construction of the Collegium Romanum. He formally resigned his estate and rank upon returning to Spain the following year.

## Contributions and Influence
Beyond his membership in the society, Francis Borgia played a role in the official recognition of Jesuit texts. While serving as the duke of Gandia, he petitioned Pope Paul III to examine and approve *The Book of the Spiritual Exercises*, written by Ignatius. The pope appointed censors who described the work as wholesome, highly useful, and replete with holiness. This led to the pope confirming the book on July 31, 1548, via the breve *Pastoralis officii cura*.
