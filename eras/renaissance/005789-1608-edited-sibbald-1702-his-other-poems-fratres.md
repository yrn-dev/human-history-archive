# George Buchanan

George Buchanan is recognized as one of the most significant scholars of Scotland. He is noted for a mastery of the Latin language that has seldom been surpassed by any modern writer, possessing a command of the language so complete that he wrote in Latin as if it were his mother tongue. His writing style is characterized by a certain elasticity and freshness, rather than being rigidly modeled after any specific classical author.

## Literary Works and Poetry

Buchanan possessed a rich vein of poetical feeling and originality of thought. His body of work includes translations of the Greek plays and the Psalms, which are described as more than mere versions. He also wrote smaller satirical poems noted for their wit and happy phrasing. 

His academic reputation in Europe was established early through two tragedies, *Baptistes* and *Jephthes*, which enjoyed undiminished renown for their academic excellence. His other poetic contributions include:
* *Fratres Fraterrimi*
* *Elegiae*
* *Silvae*
* Two sets of verses titled *Iambon Liber* and *Hendecasyllabon Liber*
* Three books of *Epigrammata*
* A book of miscellaneous verse

## Prose and Academic Treatises

In addition to his poetry, Buchanan produced several prose works. He wrote *Chamaeleon*, a satire written in the vernacular against Maitland of Lethington, which was first printed in 1711. His academic and linguistic contributions include a Latin translation of Linacre's Grammar, published in Paris in 1533, and *Libettus de Prosodia*, published in Edinburgh in 1640.

He also authored *Vita ab ipso scripta biennio ante mortem* (1608), which was later edited by R. Sibbald in 1702. Furthermore, he wrote *De Sphaera* in five books. This work was suggested by the poem of Joannes de Sacrobosco and served as a defense of the Ptolemaic theory in opposition to the new Copernican view.

## Legacy and Editions

The works of Buchanan have been preserved and organized by later scholars. Burmann published an edition of Buchanan's works as part of his contributions to classical scholarship.
