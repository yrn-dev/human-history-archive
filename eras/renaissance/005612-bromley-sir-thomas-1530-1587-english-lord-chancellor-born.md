# BROMLEY, SIR THOMAS (1530-1587)

## Early Life and Education
Sir Thomas Bromley was born in Staffordshire in 1530. He received his higher education at Oxford University and subsequently entered the legal profession, where he was called to the bar at the Middle Temple.

## Professional Advancement
Bromley's rise in his profession was accelerated by the influence of his family and the patronage of the lord keeper, Sir Nicholas Bacon. His early career was marked by several significant appointments; he was named the recorder of London in 1566 and attained the position of solicitor-general in 1569. 

## Parliamentary and Judicial Career
Throughout his career, Bromley served in parliament, representing the constituencies of Guildford, Wigan, and Bridgnorth in succession. Following the death of Sir Nicholas Bacon in 1579, Bromley was appointed to the office of lord chancellor. 

In his capacity as an equity judge, Bromley was noted for possessing profound and great knowledge. He is specifically recognized for his judgment in Shelley's case, which is considered a landmark in the history of English real property law.

## Final Years and Death
In 1586, Bromley presided over the commission responsible for the trial of Mary, queen of Scots. The responsibilities associated with her execution and the strain of the trial proved to be too much for his strength. Sir Thomas Bromley died on April 12, 1587, and was buried in Westminster Abbey.
