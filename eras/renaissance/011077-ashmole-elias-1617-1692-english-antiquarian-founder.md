# ASHMOLE, ELIAS (1617-1692)

Elias Ashmole was an English antiquarian and the founder of the Ashmolean Museum at Oxford. Born on May 23, 1617, in Lichfield, he was the son of a saddler. His early professional career began in 1638 when he became a solicitor, and by 1644, he had been appointed as a commissioner of excise.

## Military and Early Career
Ashmole's appointment as commissioner of excise brought him to Oxford during the period when the Royalist Parliament was sitting there. While at Oxford, he established a friendship with Captain George Wharton (later Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole obtained a commission from the king to serve as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse. In 1646, he became the first amateur, or "gentleman," to be accepted and initiated as a Freemason.

## Personal Life and Affluence
Ashmole's domestic life was marked by two marriages. His first wife died in 1639. In 1649, he married Lady Mainwaring, a relative of his first wife who was approximately twenty years his senior. This marriage provided Ashmole with a level of affluence that allowed him to dedicate his full time to his preferred studies. Although his wife petitioned for a separation in 1657, she failed to win her case and returned to live with him until her death in 1668.

## Intellectual Interests and Research
Ashmole's early intellectual interests included astrology, a passion sparked by William Lilly and George Wharton. He met Lilly and other astrologers in London in 1646, though this interest eventually subsided. In later years, he shifted his focus toward antiquarian research and heraldry.

His diligence in these fields is evidenced by his work, *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*, published in 1672. This exhaustive study was the result of years of patient research. In this work, Ashmole addressed the origins of the Order of the Garter, noting the "vulgar and more general story" regarding the garter of the countess of Salisbury. He also commented on the Knights of the Bath, arguing that the ceremonies of their creation suggest that King Henry IV did not institute a new order but rather restored the ancient manner of making knights bachelors.

## Public Offices and Later Life
Between 1657 and 1668, Ashmole enjoyed significant favor at court and held a succession of official positions. These included serving as:
* Windsor herald
* Commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

Despite his standing, he refused the office of Garter king-at-arms, allowing it to go to Sir William Dugdale. Ashmole married Dugdale's daughter in 1668.

## The Ashmolean Museum
In 1677, Ashmole presented the university of Oxford with the Ashmolean Museum, which served as the first public museum of curiosities in the kingdom. A large portion of the museum's collection had been inherited by Ashmole from a friend, John Tradescant. Ashmole stipulated that the gift was conditional upon the erection of a suitable building to house the collection; consequently, the items were not finally installed until 1683. Following this contribution, he also made a further gift of his library to the university.
