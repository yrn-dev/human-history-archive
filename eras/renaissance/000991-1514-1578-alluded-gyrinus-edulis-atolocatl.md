# Axolotl

The axolotl is a species of salamander larvae belonging to the genus *Amblystoma*. Known to the people of Mexico from the remotest times, the animal has been regularly brought from neighboring lakes to the Mexico market as a wholesome and agreeable article of food.

## Physical Characteristics
The axolotl can grow to a length of eleven inches. In appearance, it is a nearly exact image of the larvae of the British newt, possessing a plump, moderately long body and a strongly compressed tail with a low dorsal crest. Its head is large and thick, featuring a large pendent upper lip and small eyes that lack lids. The animal possesses two pairs of well-developed limbs with free digits.

The most characteristic physical feature of the axolotl is the presence of three large appendages on each side of the back of the head. These external gills are fringed with filaments that, when fully developed, resemble black ostrich feathers; these organs allow the animal to breathe oxygen dissolved in the water. The mouth contains small teeth arranged in several rows, as well as an elongate patch of additional teeth on each side of the front of the palate, inserted on the palatine and vomerine bones. The coloration of the axolotl is typically blackish, brownish grey, or dark olive-grey, often marked with round dots or black spots.

## Nomenclature and Classification
The animal has been referred to by various names. Francisco Hernandez (1514-1578) alluded to it as *atolocatl*, *Gyrinus edulis*, *piscis ludicrus*, and *lusus aquarum*. The name *axolotl* has remained in use in Mexico and other regions to the present day.

In the beginning of the 19th century, Humboldt brought the first specimens of the *Axolotl* (or *Gyrinus edulis*) to the naturalist Cuvier. Cuvier recognized that these Batrachians were not related to Perennibranchiates such as *Proteus* and *Siren*, but instead represented the larval form of an air-breathing salamander. This opinion was initially ignored by most systematists. More than half a century later, it was discovered that the axolotl breeds in its branchiferous condition. Consequently, the genus *Siredon* (a name given by J. Wagler) was unanimously maintained and classified among the permanent gill-breathers.
