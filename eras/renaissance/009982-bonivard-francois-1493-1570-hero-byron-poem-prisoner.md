# BONIVARD, FRANCOIS (1493-1570)

Francois Bonivard was a Savoyard figure of the Renaissance and Reformation, best known to later audiences as the subject of the poem *The Prisoner of Chillon* by Lord Byron. Born at Seyssel to an old Savoyard family, Bonivard's life was characterized by political conflict with the dukes of Savoy and a later transition to Protestantism.

## Ecclesiastical Career and Political Conflict
Bonivard entered the clergy and was educated by his uncle. In 1510, he succeeded his uncle as the prior of the Cluniac priory of St Victor, located near Geneva. In this capacity, Bonivard opposed the efforts of the duke of Savoy to maintain his rights as the lord of Geneva; these efforts were supported by the bishop of the city, who was a relative of the duke.

This opposition led to Bonivard's first period of incarceration. From 1519 to 1521, he was imprisoned by the duke at Gex. Following this imprisonment, Bonivard lost his priory and developed an increasingly anti-Savoyard stance.

## Imprisonment at Chillon
In 1530, Bonivard was seized once again by the duke of Savoy. He was imprisoned for four years in the castle of Chillon, where he was held underground. His captivity ended in 1536 when he was released by the Bernese, who had wrested Vaud from the duke.

While the legendary accounts popularized by Lord Byron may suggest otherwise, the archive indicates that Bonivard's imprisonments were for political reasons. He did not become a Protestant until after his release in 1536. Upon his release, he discovered that his priory had been destroyed in 1534.

## Later Life and Literary Work
Following his release, Bonivard obtained a pension from Geneva. His personal life was marked by four marriages and persistent debt resulting from his extravagances.

In 1542, he was officially tasked with compiling a history of Geneva from the earliest times. He produced a manuscript titled *Chroniques de Geneve*, which concluded with events up to the year 1530. In 1551, this manuscript was submitted to Calvin for correction. Although written in the mid-16th century, the work was not published until 1831, with the best edition appearing in 1867. While the archive describes the work as partial and uncritical, it is considered his best title to fame.

## Literary Legacy
Bonivard's life served as the inspiration for the poem *The Prisoner of Chillon*. Lord Byron began writing this poem between June 27 and June 29, 1816, after visiting the castle of Chillon on June 26. The poem was published on December 5, 1816. However, the archive notes that Bonivard's real history and character differ widely from the legendary account presented in Byron's work.
