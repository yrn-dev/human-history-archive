## Sir Thomas Wyatt and the Poetic Revival
Sir Thomas Wyatt (d. 1542) was a figure at the court of Henry VIII who played a central role in the revival of English lyric poetry. His work marked a return to a sense of metre and a style of love-poetry more plangent than any seen in England since the time of Chaucer. 

Wyatt is credited with the gradual rediscovery of the iambic decasyllabic line, which had been previously discovered for England by Chaucer. He utilized this line to construct sonnets, terzines, and octaves, incorporating Italian measures. Through these forms, Wyatt sought to move English verse out of the "slough of formlessness" that had persisted since Skelton. His work eventually achieved perfect formal accuracy. 

In addition to his formal contributions, Wyatt was the first and last poet of his era to successfully employ a genial and natural Horatian style of satire. Although his original poems remained unpublished until the eve of Elizabeth's reign, they appeared in 1557 in Tottel's *Miscellany*, alongside the works of Henry Howard, earl of Surrey (d. 1547), and other followers such as Grimoald.
