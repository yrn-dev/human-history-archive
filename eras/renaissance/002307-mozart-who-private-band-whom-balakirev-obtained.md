# Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart was a composer whose work exerted a profound influence on subsequent musicians and the development of various musical forms. His compositions were enthusiastically adopted by the aristocracy of his time, and his technical approach to music served as a point of study and comparison for later masters, most notably Ludwig van Beethoven.

## Influence on Other Composers
Mozart's work provided a significant model for Ludwig van Beethoven. In his early style, Beethoven occasionally imitated Mozart, though some critics suggest this was sometimes to the detriment of Beethoven's own richness of thought. For instance, the slow movement of Beethoven's first three pianoforte sonatas, *op.* 2, illustrates such imitation. Furthermore, Beethoven utilized the "luxurious Mozartesque rondo form" in the finale of his second sonata, though he did so based on the suitability of the form rather than mere imitation.

Beyond Beethoven, Mozart's imagination was fired by the melodramas of Georg Benda. In 1778, Mozart wrote to his father expressing great enthusiasm for Benda's *Medea* and *Ariadne auf Naxos*, which he considered excellent and kept with him. Mozart viewed Benda's approach as a potential solution to the problems of operatic recitative. The only specimen of this influence produced by Mozart is the melodrama found in his unfinished 1780 operetta, *Zaide*.

## Notable Works and Instrumentation
Mozart is recognized for his "incomparable setting" of Da Ponte's libretto for *Don Giovanni*. This work was so influential that it annihilated earlier operas on the same subject by composers such as Righini, Tritto, Gazzaniga, Gardi, and Le Tellier. The popularity of the Don Juan legend is attributed in great measure to Mozart's setting, which also introduced the French romantic school to the character.

Mozart was also a foremost appreciator of the basset horn. He utilized this instrument with great effect in several compositions:
* **Operas:** He wrote a fine obbligato for the basset horn in the aria "Non piu di Flori" from *La Clemenza di Tito*, and also used it in *Zauberfloete*.
* **Chamber Music:** He composed a short adagio for bassoon and two basset horns, as well as another piece for three basset horns and two clarinets.
* **Sacred Music:** In his *Requiem*, the reed family is represented by two bassoons and two basset horns with independent parts.

## Interactions with Beethoven
In the spring of 1787, a young Ludwig van Beethoven visited Vienna. During this visit, he took a few lessons from Mozart and astonished him with his extemporizations. Later in life, Beethoven's personal orthodoxy led him to hold objections toward Mozart's *Don Giovanni*.

## Connection to Balakirev
While Mozart himself is not directly linked to the 19th-century Russian composer Mili Alexevich Balakirev, a connection exists through Balakirev's early education. As a boy, Balakirev lived with Oulibichev, the author of a *Life of Mozart*. Oulibichev maintained a private band, and it was from him that Balakirev obtained a valuable education in music.
