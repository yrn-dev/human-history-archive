# Alexander Balloch Grosart

Alexander Balloch Grosart (1827–1899) was a Scottish literary editor and divine who dedicated much of his career to the reproduction of rare Elizabethan literature. Born on June 18, 1827, in Stirling to a building contractor, he was educated at Edinburgh University.

## Clerical Career
Grosart entered the ministry as a Presbyterian minister in Kinross in 1856. He later served in Liverpool starting in 1865, and moved to Blackburn three years later. He remained in the ministry until his resignation in 1892. He died in Dublin on March 16, 1899.

## Editorial Work and Libraries
Dr. Grosart is primarily remembered for his extensive efforts to reprint rare texts, a pursuit driven by his strong interest in Puritan theology. His early editorial work focused on Puritan divines, including Herbert Palmer, Thomas Brooks, and Richard Sibbes. He also produced editions of Richard Gilpin's *Demonologia sacra* (1867) and the *Poems* of Michael Bruce (1865), and published a bibliography of the writings of Richard Baxter in 1868.

Between 1868 and 1876, Grosart produced the "Fuller Worthies Library" for private subscribers. This series comprised thirty-nine volumes and included works by authors such as George Herbert, John Donne, Andrew Marvell, Sir Philip Sidney, Henry Vaughan, Fulke Greville, Sir John Davies, and Thomas Fuller. The final four volumes of this series were dedicated to authors who were otherwise inaccessible or little known.

Following this, Grosart began the "Chertsey Worthies Library" in 1876, which featured editions of works by Abraham Cowley, Francis Quarles, Nicholas Breton, Henry More, John Davies of Hereford, and Dr. Joseph Beaumont. These two libraries were produced simultaneously until 1881.

Subsequently, Grosart began the "Huth Library," named after the bibliophile Henry Huth, who owned many of the original texts. This library included the prose tracts of Thomas Dekker, as well as the works of Gabriel Harvey, Thomas Nash, and Robert Greene. Grosart edited the complete works of Robert Greene for the Huth Library between 1881 and 1886.

## Other Publications and Editions
Grosart's editorial range extended to various other rare works and authors:
* **Rare Books:** Between 1875 and 1881, he published *Occasional Issues of Unique and Very Rare Books*, which included Robert Dover's *Annalia Dubrensia*.
* **Poetry:** In 1875, he edited a limited issue of the *Spirituall Sonnetts* and *Parthenophil*.
* **Drama:** He edited *The Temptacyon of our Lorde* in volume i of the *Miscellanies of the Fuller Worthies Library* (1870). This play was written in 1538 by the author of *Kynge Johan*.
* **Correspondence:** He edited the *Lismore Papers* (1886–1887) in ten volumes, which contained autobiographical notes, a diary from 1611 until the author's death, and correspondence.
