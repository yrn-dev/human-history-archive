# Maitland's Essays on the Ref.; Froude's and R.W. Dixon's

The works of S.R. Maitland, James Anthony Froude, and R.W. Dixon constitute significant historical references regarding the English Reformation and the ecclesiastical history of the period. These authors are frequently cited alongside other primary and secondary sources, such as the *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII*, Foxe's *Acts and Monuments*, and the works of Pollard and Gairdner, to provide accounts of the religious and political upheavals of the 16th century.

## James Anthony Froude
James Anthony Froude (1818–1894) was an English historian born in Dartington, Devon. Educated at Westminster and Oriel College, Oxford, Froude was initially associated with the High Church movement and the ecclesiastical revival. He assisted J.H. Newman with the *Lives of the English Saints* and was ordained as a deacon in 1845. However, his religious views shifted under the influence of Carlyle's teaching, leading to a public break with the High Church party. This transition was first signaled in his 1847 volume *Shadows of the Clouds* (published under the pseudonym "Zeta") and later declared in his autobiographical and heretical work, *Nemesis of Faith*.

In the field of history, Froude is recognized as a champion of Protestantism. His *History* is described as a powerful and magnificently conceived account of the Reformation in England. The work is noted for its remarkable literary merit, characterized by a well-balanced, orderly, and symmetrical narrative. Froude's prose is distinguished by a graceful simplicity and lucidity, avoiding affected or labored language. 

His historical writing is praised for its picturesque power and ability to sustain stirring narration. Notable examples include his description of the coronation procession of Anne Boleyn, the account of lightning striking the spire of St Paul's, and his comparison of the monks of the London Charterhouse to the Spartans at Thermopylae. Beyond his narrative histories, Froude authored a series of essays titled *Short Studies on Great Subjects* (1867–1882), which further showcase his mastery of English prose.

## R.W. Dixon and S.R. Maitland
R.W. Dixon and S.R. Maitland are cited as authorities on the Reformation and church history. Dixon's *Histories* and *Hist. of the Church* are utilized as references for the study of figures such as Bishop Bonner—who died in the Marshalsea in 1569 after refusing the oath of supremacy—and the extreme reforming party represented by figures like Hooper.

S.R. Maitland's *Essays on the Ref.* similarly serves as a source for the historical record of the Reformation period. Maitland is also identified as one of the historians who, in the generation following Macaulay and Carlyle, helped test the new doctrine that history should be treated as a science. This school of historical writing, which emerged more dominantly after 1881, moved away from the traditional pathway of Belles Lettres to rely more heavily on statistics, documents, and impersonal material.

## Historical Context and Usage
The works of Froude, Dixon, and Maitland are often employed to document the lives and conflicts of the English clergy and the state. For instance, they are cited in accounts of the legal challenges regarding the consecration of Bishop Horne of Winchester and the Zwinglian views of Hooper, who sought to spread a form of religion in England based on the *Consensus Tigurinus* of 1549.

While Froude represented the "applied history" of his era as a champion of Protestantism, the broader transition in historical research led by figures like Maitland and Gardiner shifted the focus toward the process of change as the primary subject matter.
