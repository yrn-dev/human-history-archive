# Anabaptism

## Origins and Development
The Anabaptist movement began in Zuerich, where a group including Simon Strumpf, Felix Manz (d. 1527), Konrad Grebel (d. 1526), and Wilheld Reubli (1480-1554) separated from Zwingli to propose the creation of a separate church. The movement gained rapid momentum, attracting adherents such as Hans Broedli, the pastor of Zollikon, as well as scholarly saints including Hans Denck (ca. 1495-1527) and Balthasar Hubmaier (ca. 1480-1528).

The definitive institution of "Anabaptism" occurred when Joerg Blaurock, Konrad Grebel, and others took the step of baptizing one another and subsequently partaking together in the Lord's Supper. This action moved the group beyond the mere repudiation of infant baptism and established a new religious community.

## Core Doctrines and Beliefs
The Anabaptists sought to model their community after primitive Christianity, rejecting all traditions and accretions that appeared later than the records of the New Testament. They aimed to return to a simple church founded on brotherly love.

Central to their faith were two primary articles:
1. **Inner Light:** A belief in religious feeling, conscience, or "inner light" as the sole true ground or beginning of religion. This belief linked them to the Zwickau prophets.
2. **Non-Resistance:** The conviction that true Christians are like sheep among wolves and must not defend themselves.

Additionally, the movement repudiated the use of force and advocated for a scriptural communism of goods. They asserted that Christians should exercise patience and love toward one another, maintaining independence from worldly tribunals.

## The Rejection of Infant Baptism
The most radical doctrine of the movement was the assertion that infant baptism (paedobaptism) was unscriptural. This refusal to baptize infants, and the resulting formation of a separate church, led to the condemnation of the group by Zwingli and the banishment of several members.

The mode of baptism practiced by the Anabaptists was sprinkling or pouring rather than immersion. For example, Blaurock and Grebel baptized each other and other adherents while kneeling together in an ordinary room. Balthasar Hubmaier, who baptized 300 converts in 1525, performed these baptisms using a single bucket.
