# Brother Hildebrand Brandenburg of Biberach

## Historical Significance
Brother Hildebrand Brandenburg of Biberach is noted in historical records primarily for his association with the early development of the book-plate, or *ex-libris*. A book-plate is a printed label used to indicate the ownership of individual volumes. This practice is nearly as old as the printed book itself and serves a similar purpose to the symbolical personal devices or hand-painted armorials found in medieval manuscripts.

## The Buxheim Book-Plates
The earliest known examples of movable book-plates are German. According to Friedrich Warnecke of Berlin, a leading authority on the subject, the oldest of these are specific woodcuts that depict a shield of arms supported by an angel. These plates were pasted into books that Brother Hildebrand Brandenburg of Biberach presented as gifts to the Carthusian monastery of Buxheim.

The date of these book-plates is fixed to approximately the year 1480, based on the recorded date of the gift. In an imitation of devices used in old manuscripts, these woodcut plates were hand-painted.

## Comparative Chronology of Book-Plates
The examples provided by Brother Hildebrand Brandenburg of Biberach predate the earliest known book-plates discovered in other regions. Following the German examples from circa 1480, the chronological sequence of early book-plates includes:
* **France:** The oldest discovered is that of Jean Bertaud de la Tour-Blanche, dated 1529.
* **England:** A gift-plate used by Sir Nicholas Bacon for books presented to the university of Cambridge.
* **Holland:** A plate belonging to Anna van der Aa, dated 1597.
* **Italy:** An example attributed to the year 1622.
* **America:** The earliest known example is a plain printed label belonging to John Williams, dated 1679.
