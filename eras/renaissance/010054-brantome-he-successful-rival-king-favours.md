# Guillaume Gouffier, Seigneur de Bonnivet

Guillaume Gouffier, Seigneur de Bonnivet (c. 1488–1525), was a French soldier and one of the most influential royal favourites during the reign of Francis I of France. He was the younger brother of Artus Gouffier, the seigneur de Boisy, who served as the tutor to Francis I. Having been brought up alongside the future king, Bonnivet maintained the intimacy and confidence of the monarch throughout his life, aided by his brilliant wit and handsome appearance.

## Military and Diplomatic Career

Bonnivet rose to significant power following the accession of Francis I, being appointed admiral of France in 1515. His role extended into diplomacy; during the imperial election of 1519, he managed the candidature of Francis I. In an attempt to secure the votes of the electors, Bonnivet spent vast sums of money, though these efforts ultimately proved unsuccessful.

His military career was marked by both command and controversy. In 1521, while leading the army of Navarre, he occupied Fuenterrabia. It is believed that he was responsible for the failure to restore the location, which led to a renewal of hostilities. In 1523, Bonnivet took over the command of the army of Italy from Marshal Lautrec. Although he entered the Milanese, he suffered a defeat that necessitated a disastrous retreat, during which the chevalier Bayard died.

## The Battle of Pavia and Death

At the end of 1524, Bonnivet served as one of the principal commanders of the army led by Francis I into Italy. According to the accounts of Brantome, the battle of Pavia was fought based on a suggestion made by Bonnivet. Brantome further notes that upon realizing the disaster he had caused, Bonnivet sought and found a heroic death during the fighting. He died at the battle of Pavia on February 24, 1525.

## Personal Life and Rivalries

Bonnivet was known for leading a licentious life. He was an implacable enemy of the constable de Bourbon and played a role in the constable's downfall. 

Regarding his personal relationships, Brantome records that Bonnivet was the successful rival of the king in competing for the favours of Madame de Chateaubriand. Additionally, it is considered very probable that Bonnivet was the hero of the fourth story of the *Heptameron*, in which Marguerite d'Angouleme had to resist his importunities.
