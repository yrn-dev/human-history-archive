# Redargutio Philosophiarum

The *Redargutio Philosophiarum*, also referred to as the treatise on the "idols of the theatre," is a work completed by Francis Bacon. It was written during a period of Bacon's life following his appointment as solicitor in June 1607, a time when he was pursuing a "cherished project of reorganizing natural science." This project had previously been surveyed in his work *Advancement*.

## Context and Purpose
The *Redargutio Philosophiarum* is part of a broader effort by Bacon to qualify the human intellect for the attainment of truth through a process of expurgation. According to Bacon, the expurgation of the intellect is achieved through three specific types of *redargutiones* (refutations or corrections):
1. *Redargutio philosophiarum* (the refutation of philosophies).
2. *Redargutio demonstrationum* (the refutation of demonstrations).
3. *Redargutio rationis humanæ nativæ* (the cross-examination of human reason in its native or spontaneous process).

This approach to the "cross-examination of human reason" is noted for its correspondence to the Socratic elenchus described by Plato in the *Menon* and *Sophist*, which Plato characterized as a "sovereign purification" necessary for happiness.

## The Idols of the Mind
The concepts explored in Bacon's work relate to the *Idola*—false notions of things or erroneous ways of looking at nature. These are classified into four general classes of errors or fallacies into which the human mind is prone to fall. While the *Redargutio Philosophiarum* specifically addresses the "idols of the theatre," the broader classification of *Idola* includes:

*   **Idola Tribus (Idols of the Tribe):** Fallacies inherent to the human race. These include the tendency to believe nature is more regular than it is, the habit of supporting preconceived opinions by ignoring negative cases, and the tendency to generalize from too few observations. These errors also stem from the weakness of the senses, the influence of passions on understanding, and the erroneous belief that "man is the measure of the universe."
*   **Idola Specus (Idols of the Cave):** Errors resulting from the individual's specific mental or bodily constitution, leading to a view of things colored by favorite subjects or personal dispositions.
*   **The Third Kind:** Errors that creep insensibly into the mind and are considered inseparable.
*   **The Fourth Kind:** Errors that are imposed from without.

The classification of these idols, as presented in the *Novum Organum*, has received historical acceptation, though the treatment of these fallacies varied in works written prior to that text.
