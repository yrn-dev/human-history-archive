# Thomas Fuller (1608-1661)

Thomas Fuller was an English historian and divine who lived from 1608 to 1661. He was the eldest son of Thomas Fuller, who served as the rector of Aldwincle St Peter's in Northamptonshire. Born at his father's rectory, he was baptized on June 19, 1608. His godfather and uncle was Dr. John Davenant, the bishop of Salisbury.

## Education and Early Career
Fuller was described by Aubrey as having a "pregnant wit" as a boy. At the age of thirteen, he was admitted to Queens' College, Cambridge, where Dr. John Davenant presided and his cousin, Edward Davenant, served as a tutor. Fuller was noted for being quick and apt in his studies, earning his B.A. in Lent 1624-1625 and his M.A. in July 1628.

Following an election of fellows at his college in which he was overlooked, Bishop Davenant moved him to Sidney Sussex College in November 1628. In 1630, Fuller received the curacy of St Benet's, Cambridge, from Corpus Christi College.

## Historical Writings and Observations
Fuller is noted for his work *The Worthies of England*, which was later reprinted by P.A. Nuttall in 1840 and John Nichols in 1811. In this work, he mentions a proverbial saying regarding the legal system in London, stating that London juries "hang half and save half."

Fuller also utilized Stow's *Chronicles* to record events regarding the Anabaptists in England. He noted that in 1538, four Dutch Anabaptists—three men and one woman—bore faggots at Paul's Cross. He further recorded that three days after this event, a man and a woman of the same sect were burnt in Smithfield.

## Legacy and Bibliography
Fuller's works are characterized by a "quaint wit" that has led to the publication of several modern volumes of extracts. His *Collected Sermons* were edited in 1891 by W.E.A. Axon and J.E. Bailey.

Detailed accounts of his life and writings have been produced by other authors, including:
* William Oldys in *Biographia Britannica*, vol. iii (1750), which drew from Fuller's own works and an anonymous *Life of ... Dr Thomas Fuller* (1661).
* J.E. Bailey in *The Life of Thomas Fuller, with Notices of his Books, his Kinsmen and his Friends* (1874), which provides a detailed bibliography of his works.

## Distinctions
Thomas Fuller must be distinguished from other individuals of the same or similar names, specifically William Fuller (1608-1675), who was the bishop of Lincoln, Limerick, and dean of St Patrick's; and William Fuller (c. 1580-1659), who served as dean of Durham and Ely.
