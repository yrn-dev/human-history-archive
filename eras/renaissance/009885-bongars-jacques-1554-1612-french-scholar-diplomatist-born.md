# BONGARS, JACQUES (1554-1612)

Jacques Bongars was a French diplomatist and scholar who served the French crown during the late 16th and early 17th centuries. Born in Orleans, he was raised in the reformed faith.

## Education and Early Travels
Bongars began his early education at Jena and Marburg. Upon returning to France, he pursued further studies at Bourges and Orleans. His early intellectual and professional development included a period of time spent in Rome and travels throughout eastern Europe.

## Diplomatic Career
Bongars entered the service of Segur Pardaillan, who was a representative of Henry, king of Navarre (later Henry IV of France). In 1587, Bongars was dispatched on a mission to various princes in northern Europe. This assignment included a visit to England, where he sought assistance from Queen Elizabeth for Henry of Navarre.

He continued his career as a diplomatist for King Henry. In 1593, he was appointed as the French king's representative at the courts of the imperial princes. In this capacity, Bongars worked vigorously to support Henry's efforts to reduce the power of the house of Habsburg, a service to which he dedicated significant personal funds and health. He remained active in these labors until the murder of the king in 1610.

## Scholarly Works and Publications
In addition to his diplomatic duties, Bongars was a prolific scholar and editor. His published works include:

*   **Justinus**: In 1581, he published an abridgment of Justin's abridgment of the history of Trogus Pompeius, titled *Justinus, Trogi Pompeii Historiarum Philippicarum epitoma de manuscriptis codicibus emendatior et prologis auctior* in Paris.
*   **Gesta Dei per Francos**: Published in Hanover in 1611, this work was a collection of writings by several contemporary French authors who described the crusades.
*   **Rerum Hungaricarum scriptores varii**: A collection published in Frankfort in 1600.
*   **Epistolae**: His letters were published in Leiden in 1647, with a French translation appearing in Paris between 1668 and 1670.

## Death and Legacy
Following the death of King Henry IV, Bongars returned to France. He died in Paris on July 29, 1612. 

His professional and personal papers have been preserved in several locations. In 1632, many of his papers were presented to the library at Bern, where a list of them was compiled in 1634. Other copies of instructions and papers are held in the British Museum and various libraries in Paris.
