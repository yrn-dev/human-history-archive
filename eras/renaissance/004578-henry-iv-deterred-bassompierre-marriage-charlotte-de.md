## Henry IV of France
Henry IV was a monarch whose court was characterized by scandals. During his reign, he maintained a close relationship with Francois de Bassompierre (1579-1646), a French courtier and son of Christophe de Bassompierre. Bassompierre, who had been educated in Italy and Bavaria, was introduced to the court of Henry IV in 1598 and became a great favorite of the king, participating fully in the dissipations of court life.

The personal influence of Henry IV was significant enough to affect the private lives of his courtiers; specifically, the king deterred Bassompierre from marrying Charlotte de Montmorency, the daughter of the constable Montmorency and the future princesse de Conde.

Additionally, Honoré d'Albert (d. 1592), the seigneur de Luynes, served Henry IV as well as the three preceding Valois kings. During his service, Honoré d'Albert held the positions of governor of Beaucaire, commissary of artillery in Languedoc, and colonel of the French bands.

## Henry IV of the House of Luxemburg
In the history of the House of Luxemburg, Count Henry IV played a pivotal role in the advancement of the family's fortunes. He was elected as the German king in 1308 and was subsequently crowned emperor under the name Henry VII. 

Henry IV's lineage continued through his son, John, the king of Bohemia, who died at the field of Crécy. The imperial legacy further extended to John's eldest son, Emperor Charles IV, and other family members such as Baldwin, the archbishop of Treves (1285-1354), who was active in imperial affairs.
