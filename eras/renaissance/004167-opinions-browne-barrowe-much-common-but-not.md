# The Religious Opinions of Browne and Barrowe

The theological perspectives of Robert Browne and Henry Barrowe shared significant commonalities, though they were not identical in their ultimate goals and views on the nature of the established church.

## Shared Beliefs and Congregationalism
Both Browne and Barrowe advocated for congregational independency. A central tenet of their shared belief system was the conviction that the Church possessed both the right and the duty to implement necessary reforms. They maintained that such reforms should be carried out independently, without the need to await permission from the civil power.

## Divergent Ideals and Separation
Despite their shared support for independency, the two men differed on the purpose and necessity of separation from the established order:

*   **Robert Browne:** Browne viewed separation primarily as a means to an end. His ultimate ideal was the achievement of a spiritual democracy.
*   **Henry Barrowe:** Barrowe held a more rigid view, asserting that the entire established church order was polluted by relics of Roman Catholicism. Consequently, he insisted that separation was essential to maintain pure discipline and worship.

## The Life and Trial of Henry Barrowe
Henry Barrowe's convictions led to significant legal conflict. During a period of imprisonment in the Fleet prison, he collaborated with a fellow prisoner, John Greenwood, to write several controversial tracts. Greenwood was a Puritan and Separatist who had previously served as chaplain to Lord Rich and had renounced his ordinations by the bishops of London and Lincoln as "wholly unlawful."

Barrowe and Greenwood were eventually tried together on charges of "devising and circulating seditious books." Both were condemned to death. Following two respites—one of which occurred at the foot of the gallows—Barrowe and Greenwood were hanged on April 6, 1593.

## Historical Attributions
Some historians, including H. M. Dexter, have credited Henry Barrowe as the author of the "Marprelate Tracts." However, this attribution is considered improbable.
