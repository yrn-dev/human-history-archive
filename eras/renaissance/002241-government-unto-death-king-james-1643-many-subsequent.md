# Government unto the Death of King James

The phrase "Government unto the Death of King James" refers to the scope of a historical work titled *Chronicle of the Kings of England from the Time of the Romans' Government unto the Death of King James*, published in 1643. This work was written by Sir Richard Baker, a knight who composed the text primarily during his time in imprisonment.

## The Chronicle and its Authorship
Sir Richard Baker's *Chronicle* was published in 1643 and saw many subsequent editions. The work gained significant popularity for many years, though its historical value is considered very slight due to numerous inaccuracies. In 1649, the text was translated into Dutch. Later, Edward Phillips—the nephew of John Milton—continued the history of the kings down to the year 1658.

Beyond this historical chronicle, Baker was a prolific writer. His other works include:

## English Monarchy and Governance
The period surrounding the death of King James I and the subsequent reign of Charles I was marked by significant political instability. James I, who was King of England and Scotland starting in 1603, along with Charles I, are noted as having wrecked the foundations of absolutism that had been prepared by earlier monarchs, such as Henry VIII (and his minister Wolsey) and Queen Elizabeth (and her minister Burleigh).

Charles I ascended to the throne in 1625. His reign was characterized by conflict, leading to the English Civil War. Robert Greville, the 2nd Lord Brooke, became an active member of the parliamentary party after being imprisoned by Charles I at York in 1639 for refusing to take an oath to fight for the king. Lord Brooke defeated the Royalists in August 1642 at a skirmish in Kineton and later seized Lichfield, where he was killed on March 2, 1643.

The political crisis culminated in 1649 with the execution of Charles I for treason to his people, an event described as a new turn in European political thought. Following this, Britain existed as a republic for twelve years until 1660.
