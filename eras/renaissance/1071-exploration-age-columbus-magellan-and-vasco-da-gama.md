# Horizons of the Renaissance: The Age of Discovery

## Overview
The Age of Discovery, coinciding with the height of the Renaissance, marked a pivotal shift in global geopolitics and human understanding. Driven by a desire to bypass Ottoman-controlled land routes to the spice markets of Asia, European powers—primarily Portugal and Spain—invested in maritime exploration. This era was characterized by the synthesis of Renaissance humanism, which encouraged empirical observation, and advancements in cartography and navigation (such as the use of the astrolabe and the development of the caravel). While these voyages expanded the known world, they also initiated centuries of colonization and the devastating impact of European diseases on indigenous populations.

## Key Facts

### Christopher Columbus
Sailing under the Spanish crown, Columbus sought a westward route to Asia. While he believed he had reached the "Indies," he had actually encountered the Caribbean. Historians continue to debate the extent of Columbus's administrative failures as Governor of Hispaniola and the precise nature of his motivations, though his voyages undeniably linked the Afro-Eurasian and American hemispheres.

### Vasco da Gama
Unlike Columbus, da Gama successfully achieved the primary goal of the era: a direct sea route to the East. By rounding the southern tip of Africa, he established a Portuguese maritime empire in the Indian Ocean. This shifted the economic center of gravity from the Mediterranean to the Atlantic, significantly weakening the monopoly of Venice and the Ottoman Empire.

### Ferdinand Magellan
Magellan’s expedition was the ultimate proof of the Earth's sphericity. Although Magellan himself was killed in the Philippines in 1521, his crew completed the first circumnavigation. This voyage revealed the vastness of the Pacific Ocean, proving it was far larger than any contemporary map had suggested.

## Did You Know?
* **The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494):** To prevent conflict, the Pope helped Spain and Portugal divide the "New World" along a meridian line; Spain got the west, and Portugal got the east (which is why Brazil speaks Portuguese today).
* **The "Spice" Motivation:** Black pepper and cloves were so valuable they were often used as currency or collateral for loans because they were essential for preserving food and masking tastes.
* **The Victoria:** Of the five ships that left Spain with Magellan in 1519, only one—the *Victoria*—returned in 1522, carrying only 18 of the original 270 crew members.
