# Vladislav, King Sigismund of Poland, and the Dukes Louis and William

## Vladislav of Poland and the Bohemian Throne
Vladislav, the son of King Casimir of Poland, was elected as the king of Bohemia at Kutna Hora on May 27, 1471. This election followed a period of instability after the reign of George of Podebrad, who had offered the succession to Vladislav in hopes of obtaining Polish aid against King Matthias of Hungary. The election of Vladislav was viewed as a victory for the national party and reflected the strong anti-clerical sentiment in Bohemia, as Polish envoys promised that Vladislav would maintain the Compacts, whereas Matthias was an unconditional adherent of Rome.

The early years of Vladislav's reign were defined by a struggle with Matthias of Hungary, who continued to claim the Bohemian throne. This conflict involved prolonged and desultory warfare until 1478, when the treaty of Olmutz secured Bohemia for Vladislav. Under the terms of this treaty, Matthias retained the "lands of the Bohemian crown"—specifically Lusatia, Silesia, and Moravia—for the remainder of his life, after which they were to be restored to Bohemia.

While Vladislav remained faithful to his promise regarding the Compacts and did not prevent the use of communion in both kinds, his general policy was reactionary. He appointed government officials in Prague from the section of the Utraquist party closest to Rome and oversaw the severe persecution of the Bohemian Brethren, the extreme Hussites. This led to riots in Prague, where advanced Hussites stormed three town halls, and the nobility of the same faith formed a league to protect themselves. Peace was temporarily restored following a meeting of all estates at Kutna Hora in 1485.

## Later Reign and Succession
In 1490, Vladislav was elected king of Hungary. In 1502, he married Anna de Candale, who had connections to the French royal family. They had two children: a daughter, Anna, and a son, Louis. Vladislav died in Hungary in 1516.

His son Louis succeeded him and had been crowned king of Bohemia at the age of three. According to Vladislav's instructions, the emperor Maximilian I and Sigismund, king of Poland, were appointed as guardians for the young king. While the Bohemian estates recognized this decision, they refused to grant the guardians any right to interfere in Bohemian affairs. Consequently, the country was ruled almost without control by great Bohemian nobles, most notably the supreme burgrave, Zdenek Leo, lord of Rozmital.

## The Reign of King Louis
The nominal reign of King Louis began with a significant constitutional event in 1517 known as the diet of St Wenceslas. Assembling on September 28, the estates settled long-standing discords. The citizens renounced certain claimed privileges, while the other two estates recognized municipal autonomy and sanctioned the citizens' presence at diet meetings, a practice that had been occurring informally since 1508. On October 24, the three estates agreed to live together in friendly intercourse as members of the same race and country.

Louis arrived in Bohemia from Hungary in 1522, having also been elected king of that country. Upon arriving in Prague, he dismissed all state officials, including Leo of Rozmital. He appointed John of Wartenberg as burgrave and Charles of Munsterberg—a grandson of King George and cousin of Prince Bartholomew—as regent during his absences.

## Sigismund of Poland and the Succession Crisis
Following the death of Louis, Bohemia entered a state of anarchy and disorder. Because the last two kings had resided primarily in Hungary, the Bohemians had failed to establish a strong indigenous government despite the agreements of the diet of St Wenceslas. 

Several candidates laid claim to the Bohemian throne, including:
* Archduke Ferdinand of Austria (the future emperor Ferdinand I), as the husband of Anna, the daughter of King Vladislav.
* King Sigismund of Poland.
* The dukes Louis and William of Bavaria.
* Various German princes and Bohemian noblemen, including Leo of Rozmital.

The diet entrusted the election to twenty-four members chosen equally from the three estates. On October 23, 1526, these electors chose Ferdinand of Habsburg. This event marked the permanent accession of the Habsburg dynasty to the Bohemian throne.

## Sigismund of Poland and External Conflicts
During his reign, Sigismund of Poland faced significant external pressures. In 1512, Basil III, the tsar of Muscovy, captured the great eastern Polish fortress of Smolensk. This was achieved with the help of Prince Michael Glinsky, a rebel Lithuanian who provided the tsar with engineers and artillery from western Europe. The loss of Smolensk was the first serious injury Muscovy inflicted on Poland. Due to the exigencies of his position, Sigismund was compelled to acquiesce in the surrender of the fortress in 1522.
