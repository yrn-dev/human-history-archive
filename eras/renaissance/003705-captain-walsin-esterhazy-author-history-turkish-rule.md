# Captain Walsin Esterhazy

## Historical Contributions
Captain Walsin Esterhazy was an author who wrote a history regarding the Turkish rule in Africa. In 1840, Esterhazy proposed a theory concerning the origins of the name "Barbarossa," which was the designation given by Christians to a family of 16th-century Turkish sea rovers and admirals, including Arouj, Khizr (also known as Khair-ed-Din), and Hassan. 

Esterhazy ventured the guess that the term "Barbarossa" was simply a mispronunciation of "Baba Arouj." This supposition gained wide acceptance. However, other records indicate that contemporaries did not apply the prefix "Baba" to Arouj, whose name appeared in Italian or Spanish forms as "Ordiche," "Orux," or "Harrach." Additionally, an Arab chronicle published in 1837 by F. Denis and S. Rang explicitly stated that "Barbarossa" was the name Christians applied to Khair-ed-Din. It is believed the nickname was actually derived from the Latin word for beard (*barba*) due to the family's tawny or red beards.
