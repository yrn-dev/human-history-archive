# Navarre

The term Navarre is associated with various historical figures, royal titles, and cultural works, primarily centered around the kingdom and its titular rulers.

## Marguerite de Navarre
Marguerite d'Angoulême (1492–1549), also known as Marguerite de Navarre, was the daughter of Charles d'Orléans, count d'Angoulême, and Louise of Savoy. Born on April 11, 1492, she was the sister of King Francis I. Throughout her life, she bore several names: Marguerite de Valois by family, Marguerite d'Alençon from her first husband (whom she married in 1509), and Marguerite de Navarre following her 1527 marriage to Henri d'Albret, the titular king of Navarre.

Marguerite was a highly influential figure in France, becoming one of the most powerful women in the kingdom after her brother's accession to the throne. Although Navarre was not reconquered for her and Henri d'Albret as Francis I had promised, Marguerite was assigned ample apanages. She maintained miniature courts at Pau and Nérac, which were noted for their intellectual brilliancy.

## Patronage and Intellectual Influence
Marguerite de Navarre was a primary patroness of letters and a defender of advocates of the Reformed doctrines. Her court attracted numerous men of letters, including N. Denisot, V. Brodeau, J. Peletier, C. Marot, and Bonaventure Des Périers. She also provided protection to E. Dolet and Rabelais. 

Bonaventure Des Périers served as the queen's secretary and *valet-de-chambre*, and he transcribed the *Heptaméron* for her. While the free discussions at her court encouraged a license of thought, the queen eventually found it prudent to disavow Des Périers after the publication of his skeptical work, *Cymbalum Mundi* (1537), though she continued to assist him privately until 1541.

## Literary Works of Marguerite de Navarre
The literary output of Marguerite de Navarre is diverse, spanning prose, poetry, and drama:

*   ***Heptameron***: A work consisting of seventy-two short stories told by a company of ladies and gentlemen delayed by a swelling river. Not printed until 1558 under the title *Les Amants fortunés*, it is considered characteristic of the French Renaissance. Internal evidence suggests it may have been a joint work involving the men of letters in her household.
*   ***Les Marguerites de la marguerite des princesses***: A miscellaneous collection including farces, mysteries, spiritual songs, and devotional poems. One mystery play regarding the Nativity provides evidence of the link between carol-singing and dancing, featuring a chorus of shepherds and shepherdesses.
*   ***Letters***: A collection of interesting and high-quality correspondence.
*   ***Dernières poésies***: Not printed until 1896, this collection includes *Les Prisons* (an allegorical poem), *Le Navire* (expressing despair over the death of her brother), and various *comédies* regarding the death of Francis I.

Additionally, her religious poem *Le Miroir de l'âme pécheresse* was translated by Queen Elizabeth.

## Other Figures Associated with Navarre
Several other historical figures are linked to the title or region of Navarre:

*   **Marguerite de Valois (1553–1615)**: The daughter of Henry II and Catherine de' Medici, she was the niece of the second Marguerite and great-niece of the first. She married Henry of Navarre (later Henry IV) on the eve of St Bartholomew's Day. Though their marriage was later dissolved by the pope, they remained friends. She is known in romance and anecdotic history as "Reine Margot" and left behind admirably written memoirs.
*   **Charles the Bad**: The king of Navarre who was supported by Étienne Marcel, the provost of the merchants of Paris, during a conflict with the dauphin Charles in the 14th century.
*   **Émilie Madeleine Brohan (1833–1900)**: An actress of the Comédie Française who made her debut on September 1, 1850. Her debut performance was in a new comedy by Scribe and Legouvé titled *Les Contes de la reine de Navarre*, in which she created the part of Marguerite.
