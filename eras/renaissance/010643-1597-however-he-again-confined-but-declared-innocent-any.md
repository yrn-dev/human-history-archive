# Events and Figures of 1597

The year 1597 was marked by significant military mobilizations in East Asia, maritime exploration in the Arctic, and various political and literary developments in Europe.

## Military Campaigns in Korea
In East Asia, the Japanese military redirected its focus toward Korea after being induced not to pursue a quarrel with the Middle Kingdom. On March 19, 1597, nine new corps were mobilized for oversea service. The addition of these troops brought the total Japanese force in Korea to 141,000 men. 

Despite the size of the army, activity did not immediately increase as Japanese commanders sought practical assurances regarding their command of the sea. This caution followed five years of war, during which Korea was no longer capable of contributing to the invaders' commissariat. However, the Japanese naval architects successfully adapted to the situation. While the Korean navy utilized the same vessels that had been considered formidable five years prior, the Japanese achieved signal victories at sea, resulting in the near annihilation of the Korean fleet.

## Arctic Exploration and the Death of Willem Barents
The Dutch navigator Willem Barents, born around the middle of the 16th century, conducted three journeys in search of a north-east passage to eastern Asia. His first expedition in 1594 reached the west coast of Novaya Zemlya, and a second expedition of seven ships in 1595 attempted to find open water at the strait between Vaygach Island and the Asiatic coast.

During his third journey, Barents sighted Spitsbergen and Bear Island. After rounding the north of Novaya Zemlya, his vessel became beset by ice, forcing the crew to winter in the north. When the ship remained trapped in early 1597, the party departed in two open boats on June 13. While most members of the party escaped, Barents died on June 30, 1597. In 1871, the house where he had wintered was discovered, and part of his journal was recovered in 1875.

## Political Affairs in England
In England, Thomas Arundell, 1st Baron Arundell of Wardour, faced a period of instability with the crown. Having been created a count of the Holy Roman Empire by Rudolph II in 1595, Arundell had previously been imprisoned by Queen Elizabeth for his assumption of the foreign title. After being liberated in April 1596, he was confined again in April 1597. During this period, he was declared innocent of all charges except for "practising to contrive the justification of his vain title with Ministers beyond the seas." He was liberated in December 1597 and placed under his father's care, though he would be arrested again the following year on accusations of conspiracy against the government.

Additionally, Sir Richard Baker, the author of the *Chronicle of the Kings of England*, was elected to parliament in 1597 as the representative for East Grinstead. Baker had previously served as a member of parliament for Arundel in 1593.

## European Literary and Administrative Developments
The year 1597 saw the publication of several works by the French antiquary and Latin poet Jean Jacques Boissard. These included *Icones Virorum Illustrium* and *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum*. Boissard also began the work *Romanae Urbis Topographia*, which spanned from 1597 to 1602. In the realm of scholarship, Casaubon produced work between 1597 and 1600.

In the Netherlands, the city of Groningen remained in a state of conflict with the "Ommelanden," despite having been captured by Maurice of Nassau in 1594. In 1597, a decree was issued by the States intended to settle the feud between the city and the Ommelanden, though the conflict had persisted since 1581.
