# BONONCINI, GIOVANNI BATTISTA

Giovanni Battista Bononcini (also spelled Buononcini) was an Italian musical composer. He was the son of Giovanni Maria Bononcini, a composer recognized as the author of the 1673 treatise *Il Musico Prattico* published in Bologna. Giovanni Battista was also the brother of the composer Marc' Antonio Bononcini, a relative with whom he has frequently been confused.

## Early Life and Education
While it has been stated that Giovanni Battista was born in Modena in 1672, evidence suggests his birth date should likely be placed approximately ten years earlier. He received his musical education as a pupil of his father and of Colonna.

## Career in Italy and Europe
Bononcini began producing operas in Rome in 1694, where he composed *Serse* and *Tullo Ostilio*. His career subsequently took him to various European courts. In 1696, he was present at the court of Berlin. Between the years 1700 and 1720, he divided his professional time between Italy and Vienna.

## Period in London
In 1720, the Royal Academy of Music summoned Bononcini to London. During his time there, he produced several operas and enjoyed the protection of the Marlborough family. However, his tenure in England ended in controversy. Around 1731, it was discovered that he had previously passed off a madrigal written by Lotti as his own original work. Following a long period of correspondence regarding this matter, he was obliged to leave the country.

## Later Years
After leaving London, Bononcini remained in France for several years. In 1748, he was summoned back to Vienna to compose music in honor of the peace of Aix-la-Chapelle. Following this commission, he moved to Venice to work as a composer of operas. No further details regarding his life are known beyond this period.
