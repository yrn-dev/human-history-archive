# He shall be parson of Honington or Cist

The phrase "He shall be parson of Honington or Cist" is a line of verse appearing in the translation of Sebastian Brant's *Ship of Fools*. This translation was produced by Alexander Barclay, a British poet who lived from approximately 1476 to 1552.

## Alexander Barclay
Alexander Barclay was born around 1476, though his exact nationality has been a subject of dispute. William Bulleyn, a native of Ely who likely knew Barclay during his time at a monastery there, claimed that Barclay was born "beyonde the cold river of Twede." This northern origin is further suggested by the occasional use of Scottish words in his vocabulary and the spelling of his name. 

Barclay spent his early life at Croydon. While it is not certain if he was educated at Cambridge or Oxford, it is presumed he earned a degree, as he is referred to as a doctor of divinity in his will and uses the title "Syr" in his translation of Sallust. Evidence from his works and his familiarity with European literature indicates that he spent time abroad.

## Chaplaincy and the Ship of Fools
Barclay was appointed as the chaplain of the college of St Mary Ottery in Devonshire by Thomas Cornish. Cornish served as the provost of Oriel College, Oxford, from 1493 to 1507, and was also a suffragan bishop in the diocese of Bath and Wells. 

While serving at St Mary Ottery, Barclay translated the *Ship of Fools* by Sebastian Brant. In this work, Barclay introduced his own neighbors into the satire, writing: "For if one can flatter, and beare a Hauke on his fist, He shall be parson of Honington or Cist."
