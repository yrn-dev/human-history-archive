# Honduras

Honduras is a republic located in Central America. It is bounded to the north by the Caribbean Sea, to the east by Nicaragua, to the south by the Pacific Ocean, Nicaragua, and Salvador, and to the west by Guatemala. As of 1905, the republic had an area of approximately 46,500 square miles and a population of 500,136.

## Geography and Etymology
The name Honduras is Spanish for "depths," a term attributed to the difficulties original Spanish explorers faced when attempting to find anchorage off the coast. Similarly, the easternmost headland was named Cape Gracias a Dios (Cape "Thanks to God") for analogous reasons. 

The northern coast is largely unbroken, with the exception of the Carataska Lagoon, a land-locked lake on the east with a narrow sea entrance. However, several small estuaries and bays serve as harbors, including Trujillo, La Ceiba, Ulua, Omoa, and Puerto Cortes. The Caribbean basin bounded by Honduras, Guatemala, and British Honduras is known as the gulf or bay of Honduras. The republic also possesses several islets and the Bay Islands group. On the Pacific coast, Honduras has a short but commercially valuable littoral of about 60 miles on the Bay of Fonseca, one of the world's finest natural harbors; the islands of Gueguensi, Sacate Grande, and Tigre in this bay belong to Honduras.

## History
Christopher Columbus first landed on the American continent at Cape Honduras in 1502, claiming the territory for Spain. In 1524, Hernando Cortes ordered the establishment of the first settlement, sending his lieutenant Christobal de Olid to found a Spanish colony. When Olid attempted to create an independent principality, Cortes marched through the mountains of Guatemala and southern Mexico to regain control. In the spring of 1525, Cortes reached the colony and founded the city of Puerto Cortes. After appointing a new governor, Cortes returned to Mexico in 1526.

By 1539, Honduras was incorporated into the captaincy-general of Guatemala. At this time, the province's mines were considered the richest discovered in the New World, leading to the growth of several large cities. Honduras remained under the authority of the Spanish crown until 1821. Later history includes a struggle for Central American unity and an invasion by William Walker and fellow adventurers between 1856 and 1860.

## Economy and Industry
The economic development of Honduras has been a slow process, hindered by national bankruptcy, political disturbances, heavy import and export duties, and a scarcity of capital and labour. Despite this, the country possesses great natural wealth.

**Agriculture and Forestry:** The country is known for unsurpassed mahogany and cedar, though the supply of these and other dye-woods and cabinet-woods has been reduced by reckless destruction. Rubber-planting is a successful modern industry that supplements wild rubber. Banana plantations are of great importance, particularly in the northern maritime province of Atlantida, where coco-nuts are also grown. Other produced goods include tobacco, coffee, sugar, maize, beans, oranges, and lemons, with rice, cocoa, indigo, and wheat grown in more limited areas. Dairy farming is successful, and cattle are bred extensively and exported to Cuba; however, sheep-farming is almost unknown.

**Mining and Minerals:** Honduras ranks first among Central American states in mineral resources. Gold is worked by an American company and silver by a British company. In the 18th century, Spaniards extracted immense quantities of silver and gold from mines near Tegucigalpa. While deposits of platinum, opals, copper, iron, nickel, lead, zinc, antimony, lignite, and coal have been found, they have not been exploited on a large scale. In 1904, the total value of exported ores was £174,800, rising to £239,426 in 1905.

**Trade:** In a normal year, total exports range from £500,000 to £600,000, and imports range from £450,000 to £550,000. Bananas are the most valuable export commodity aside from minerals.

## British Honduras
British Honduras (formerly called Belize or Balize) is a British crown colony in Central America. It is bounded by the Mexican province of Yucatan to the north and northwest, the Bay of Honduras to the northeast and east, and Guatemala to the south and west. As of 1905, it had a population of 40,372 and an area of 7,562 square miles. Its frontier is defined by conventions from 1859 and 1893, running from the mouth of the river Sarstoon, through the rapids of Gracias à Dios, to Garbutt's Rapids on the Belize river, and then north to the Mexican frontier along the river Hondo.

**Belize:** The capital and principal seaport, Belize, is located on the Caribbean Sea. Its population in 1904 was 9,969. The town is characterized by wooden houses with wide verandahs and high roofs. Key buildings include the Wesleyan church, the Roman Catholic convent, the hospital, the British bank of Honduras, government house, the court house, and Fort George. The town is surrounded by mangrove swamps, making it prone to yellow fever, cholera, and other tropical diseases, though sea breezes and high tides mitigate the climate. Exports from Belize include mahogany, cedar, rosewood, logwood, sarsaparilla, cocoanuts, sugar, fruit (especially bananas), turtles, deerskins, and tortoiseshell.

**Administration:** From 1638 to 1786, colonists were independent and elected their own magistrates. These precedents were codified as "Burnaby's Laws" following a 1756 visit by Admiral Sir W. Burnaby. The home government appointed a superintendent in 1786. An executive council was created in 1839, followed by a legislative assembly in 1853. In 1862, British Honduras became a colony with a lieutenant governor subject to the governor of Jamaica. In 1870, the assembly was replaced by a legislative council. By 1884, the lieutenant governor became governor and commander-in-chief, independent of Jamaica. The colony is divided into six districts: Belize, Corosal, Orange Walk, the Cayo, Stann Creek, and Toledo (with Punta Gorda as its capital). English common law is valid, modified by local enactments and the *Consolidated Laws of British Honduras* (revised 1884–1888).
