# Sir Thomas Bodley and University Libraries

## The Refoundation of the Oxford University Library
Sir Thomas Bodley refounded the university library at Oxford during his own lifetime. This institution became a recipient of significant contributions from other figures; for instance, Laud provided liberal donations to the library, and Selden bequeathed his books to the collection.

## Book Collecting and Public Ownership
During the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries, the practice of book-collecting often served as an added grace in the creation of fine libraries rather than existing as a separate pursuit. Most large-scale book buyers of this era operated with a public objective, or their efforts were recognized by having their treasures placed in public resting-places.

This trend toward public ownership is evidenced by several significant bequests to university colleges:
* **Cambridge University:** Sir Thomas Smith (who died in 1577) bequeathed his books to Queens' College. Archbishop Parker left his books to Corpus Christi College, though these were left under severe restrictions. The library of Archbishop Williams was given to St John's College, and the library of Pepys was left to Magdalene College. Additionally, George I bought the books of Bishop Moore—which included almost unique Caxton quartos—and presented them to the university library at Cambridge.
* **Other Institutions:** Archbishop Usher's library was purchased for Trinity College, Dublin, and Archbishop Marsh founded a library in Dublin after purchasing the printed books of Stillingfleet.

## Other Notable Collections and Public Institutions
Beyond university libraries, various specialized collections were transitioned into public or institutional hands. The heraldic collections of Ralph Sheldon (d. 1684) were given to Heralds' College, and the mathematical and scientific books of Thomas Howard, earl of Norfolk (d. 1646), were provided to the Royal Society by his grandson.

In the 18th century, the movement toward public access continued. While the accumulations of Thomas Rawlinson (d. 1725) were sold via auctions and Harley's printed books were sold to a bookseller named Osbourne, Richard Rawlinson (d. 1755) bequeathed the best of his own books to Oxford. The Harleian MSS. were offered to the nation at a price significantly below their value.

The foundation of the British Museum in 1753 was driven by the need to better care for the remaining Cotton manuscripts and the offer of the great collections of Sir Hans Sloane, which included approximately 50,000 printed books. These were vested in trustees for public use in 1702, though they were partially destroyed by fire in 1731.

## Corpus Christi College and Related Figures
Corpus Christi College appears in the archive in multiple contexts. In Cambridge, the college received the restricted bequest of Archbishop Parker and provided the curacy of St Benet's, Cambridge, to the historian and divine Thomas Fuller in 1630. In Oxford, the college has been associated with various scholars, including Hugh Chisholm, a former scholar of the college and editor of the 11th edition of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, and Rev. Charles Plummer, who served as a Fellow and Chaplain of the college.
