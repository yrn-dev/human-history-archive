# Cesare Borgia

Cesare Borgia (1476–1507), the duke of Valentinois and Romagna, was the son of Pope Alexander VI and Vanozza dei Cattanei. Born in Rome during his father's tenure as a cardinal, Cesare was the favorite son of the Pope, who practiced extensive nepotism on his behalf. Following his father's elevation to the papacy in 1492, Cesare was created archbishop of Valencia and became a cardinal a year later. During the early years of the pontificate, he was known for leading a profligate life at the Vatican.

## Early Career and the Murder of Giovanni Borgia
In January 1495, Cesare accompanied Charles VIII as a hostage to ensure the Pope's good behavior during the conquest of Naples, though he eventually escaped at Velletri to return to Rome. By 1497, Cesare began to exhibit the violence for which he would later become notorious. In that year, his brother Giovanni, the duke of Gandia, was murdered. The deed was attributed to Cesare, likely with reason. 

Several motives for the murder were suggested. Some indicated a rivalry between the brothers for the affection of Donna Sancha, the wife of the Pope's youngest son, Giuffre. Other hints suggested incestuous relations between Cesare, the duke, and their sister, Lucrezia. However, it is considered more probable that Cesare envied his brother's splendid position and desired to enjoy the full extent of his father's favors, as he contemplated moving from ecclesiastical dignities toward a secular career.

## Transition to Secular Power
Following the death of the duke of Gandia, Pope Alexander VI required Cesare to execute his political schemes. In July 1497, Cesare served as papal legate in Naples, where he crowned Frederick of Aragon king. The Pope attempted to arrange a wealthy marriage for him, and Cesare sought to marry Carlotta, the daughter of the king of Naples. However, both Carlotta and her father refused the alliance, characterizing Cesare as "the bastard of a priest" and a priest himself.

In August 1498, Cesare requested and received permission from the Pope and the cardinals to renounce the priesthood "for the good of his soul." On October 1, he traveled to France as a papal legate to Louis XII. His mission was to deliver a papal bull annulling Louis's marriage to Jeanne of France so that Louis could marry Anne of Brittany. In exchange for this service, Cesare received military assistance for his own enterprises and the duchy of Valentinois. While in France, he again unsuccessfully attempted to win over Carlotta of Naples, eventually marrying Charlotte d'Albret, the sister of the king of Navarre, in May 1499.

## Campaigns in Romagna
Pope Alexander VI planned for Cesare to carve out a principality for himself from territories owing nominal allegiance to the papacy by subduing turbulent local despots in Romagna. With the support of the French king, Cesare established his headquarters at Cesena. He commanded an army composed of Italian troops, 4,000 Swiss and Gascons, and 300 French lances.

Cesare first attacked Imola, which surrendered immediately. He then besieged Forli, which was held by Caterina Sforza, the widow of Girolamo Riario. Despite a gallant defense, Sforza was forced to surrender on January 22, 1500. Cesare treated her with consideration, and she spent her remaining days in a convent.

## Return to Rome
Cesare's schemes were temporarily checked when the Sforzas expelled the French from Milan, leading to his return to Rome in February. Pope Alexander VI rewarded his successes by conferring many honors upon him and naming him *gonfaloniere* of the church. During this period in Rome, Cesare participated in carnival festivities and bull fights.
