# Francois de Bassompierre

## Early Life and Court Introduction
Francois de Bassompierre (1579–1646) was a French courtier born at the castle of Harrouel in Lorraine. He was the son of Christophe de Bassompierre (1547–1596) and belonged to an old family that had served the dukes of Lorraine and Burgundy for several generations. After receiving an education in Italy and Bavaria alongside his brothers, Bassompierre was introduced to the court of Henry IV in 1598. He became a great favorite of the king and participated fully in the dissipations associated with court life.

## Military Career
Bassompierre's military service spanned several decades and various regions. In 1600, he took part in a brief campaign in Savoy, and in 1603, he fought in Hungary on behalf of the emperor against the Turks. During the struggle of Marie de' Medici against the nobles in 1614, he assisted her; however, following her failure in 1617, he remained loyal to King Louis XIII. He supported the royalists in 1620 when they routed the supporters of Marie de' Medici at Ponts-de-Ce.

His services during the Huguenot rising of 1621–22 earned him the rank of marshal of France. He was present with the king's army during the 1628 siege of La Rochelle and distinguished himself in 1629 during the campaign against rebels in Languedoc. In 1615, Bassompierre had purchased the position of colonel-general of the Swiss and Grisons from Henri, duc de Rohan. Consequently, when Louis XIII marched against Savoy in 1629, Bassompierre was sent to Switzerland to raise troops. His military career concluded following a short campaign in Italy.

## Diplomatic Missions
Bassompierre's career as a diplomat is characterized as a failure. In 1621, he served as envoy extraordinary to Madrid to address a dispute regarding Spain's seizure of the Valteline forts, resulting in the signing of a fruitless treaty. In 1625, he was sent on a mission to Switzerland that was equally futile. Finally, in 1626, he was dispatched to London to ensure the retention of the attendants and Catholic ecclesiastics of Henrietta Maria, the wife of Charles I.

## Personal Life and Political Intrigues
Bassompierre's personal life was marked by royal influence and secret alliances. The personal influence of Henry IV prevented him from marrying Charlotte de Montmorency, the daughter of the constable Montmorency and later the princesse de Conde. Between 1630 and 1614, he was secretly married to Louise Marguerite, the widow of Francois, prince de Conti. Through this marriage, he became involved in a plot to overthrow Richelieu during the "Day of Dupes" in 1630.

## Memoirs and Historical Significance
Bassompierre authored memoirs that serve as a chief source of information regarding the life of King Louis XIII. The best edition of these *Memoires* was issued by the Societe de l'Histoire de France in 1877. Additional accounts regarding the marshal can be found in the *Historiettes de la princesse de Conti, et du marechal de Bassompierre* by G. Tallemant des Reaux, published between 1854 and 1860.
