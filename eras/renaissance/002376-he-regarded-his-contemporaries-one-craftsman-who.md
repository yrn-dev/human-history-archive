# Sigmund Schnitzer

## Professional Reputation
Sigmund Schnitzer was a craftsman based in Nuremberg who gained significant renown for his skill in instrument making. According to an 18th-century historical work, he was recognized "almost everywhere" as a maker of *fagotte*. These instruments were noted for several specific qualities: they were of extraordinary size, possessed pure intonation, spoke easily, and were characterized by skilful workmanship.

Because his instruments were so highly appreciated, Schnitzer was kept in a state of continual work to produce *fagotte* for music lovers. This appreciation extended beyond his home region, reaching as far as Italy and France, in addition to his reputation throughout Germany.

## Contemporary Records and Classifications
Despite his later renown as a maker of *fagotte*, contemporary records from the 16th century provide a more limited description of his role. Johann Neudorfer, a chronicler of Nuremberg's craftsmen and artistic celebrities writing in 1549, identified Sigmund Schnitzer simply as a *Pfeifenmacher und Stadtpfeifer*.

## Historical Analysis of Invention
There has been retrospective speculation regarding whether Schnitzer was the first person to invent the *fagotto* by doubling the tube of the bass pommer back upon itself to reduce its great length. However, historical evidence suggests this is unlikely for two reasons:

First, Neudorfer, a contemporary and fellow-citizen, did not note Schnitzer as an inventor of a new form of instrument; it is argued that such a fact would not have been omitted by a chronicler of the city's celebrities. Second, the eulogistic descriptions of Schnitzer as a clever craftsman who produced *fagotte* of extraordinary size were written by Doppelmaier nearly two centuries after the supposed invention of the instrument. This suggests that the value of the invention was realized through later retrospection rather than contemporary recognition of a new discovery.
