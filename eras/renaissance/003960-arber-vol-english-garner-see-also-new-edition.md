# Edward Arber

Professor Edward Arber was a scholar and editor associated with the publication and preservation of various English literary, historical, and religious texts during the 19th and early 20th centuries. His work spanned a wide range of materials, including Elizabethan poetry, early English prose, and controversial religious tracts.

## Literary Editions and Elizabethan Works
Professor Arber contributed significantly to the availability of Elizabethan literature. He included the work *Parthenophil* in volume v. of *An English Garner*. This work was also featured in a 1904 new edition of *An English Garner* titled *Elizabethan Sonnets*, edited by S. Lee.

Arber also focused on the works of John Lyly. In 1868, he produced an edition of *Euphues* based on early editions of the text.

## Educational and Classical Texts
Arber's editorial work extended to educational texts from the 16th century. He edited *The Scholemaster* in 1870, a work that had previously been reprinted in 1571 and 1589 and edited by the Rev. J. Upton and Prof. J.E.B. Mayor. Additionally, Arber republished *Toxophilus* in both 1868 and 1902.

## Religious and Controversial Publications
In the field of religious history and controversy, Arber authored the *Introductory Sketch to the Martin Marprelate Controversy* in 1880. While this work provides a list and full titles of related documents and tracts, as well as a discussion regarding authorship, it does not provide a connected account of the matter. 

Furthermore, Arber was involved in the reproduction of important tracts through his *English Scholar's Library*, published between 1879 and 1880. This library reprinted several important tracts, similar to the *Puritan Discipline Tracts* series produced by Petheram.

## Other Editorial Contributions
Arber's work included the reproduction of early English religious documents. In 1871, he produced a photo lithograph of the *Pref. to Genesis* (Parker Soc.). He also provided an edition of the *Rehearsal* in 1868, which included a biography of the 2nd Duke of Buckingham.
