# Bohemia to Borgia, Francis

## Bohemia: Conflict and Administration
Bohemia served as a primary theater of hostilities during much of the Thirty Years' War, a conflict that began in its capital. In 1631, Saxons occupied large portions of the region and attempted to re-establish Protestantism. Later in the war, Swedish troops frequently pillaged the area, culminating in the 1648 capture of part of Prague by General Konigsmark. Despite efforts by Swedish envoys, imperial representatives remained inflexible regarding the toleration of Bohemian Protestants.

In the early 18th century, the possibility of the male line of the house of Habsburg becoming extinct led the estates of Bohemia to meet in Prague on October 16, 1720. There, they recognized the Pragmatic Sanction, which proclaimed the indivisibility of the Habsburg realm and sanctioned female succession to the throne. However, upon the death of Emperor Charles VI, Archduchess Maria Theresa faced significant opposition. Charles, the elector of Bavaria, claimed the Bohemian throne and invaded with French, Saxon, and Bavarian troops. He occupied Prague, and on December 19, 1741, many Bohemian knights and nobles swore allegiance to him.

The military situation shifted as Maria Theresa recovered her lands. During her reign and that of her son, Joseph II, internal administration was altered to limit Bohemian autonomy. This included a 1749 decree abolishing separate law-courts in Bohemia and the appointment of an Austro-Bohemian chancellor to oversee the administration of Bohemia and the German domains of the house of Habsburg.

## Regional Geography and Industry
Within Bohemia, Kladno is noted as a mining town located 18 miles west-north-west of Prague by rail. As of 1900, its population was 18,600, consisting mostly of Czechs. The region is rich in coal-fields and iron-mines, hosting some of the largest steel and iron works in Bohemia. Nearby is the mining town of Buschtehrad, which grew around a castle that was owned by the lords of Kolovrat from the 15th century until 1630, eventually passing through the grand-dukes of Tuscany to Emperor Francis Joseph. Kladno itself was sold in 1705 by Grand-duchess Anna Maria of Tuscany to the cloister in Brewnow, with its mining industry beginning in 1842.

## Cesare Borgia
Cesare Borgia (1476–1507), the son of Pope Alexander VI (Rodrigo Borgia), was a figure of the Italian Renaissance characterized by ambition, audacity, and unscrupulousness. He is described as having used ferocious and treacherous methods of conquest, though he governed his subjects with justice and firmness once control was established. His rise to power as the duke of a wide area of Central Italy was heavily dependent on the support of the papacy.

Borgia's personal history included the murder of his sister Lucrezia's husband and his own elder brother. While he possessed administrative power and dexterity, he showed little military ability. His influence collapsed rapidly following the death of his father. Cesare Borgia served as an inspiration for Machiavelli, who idealized him in *The Prince* as a superb and successful prince.

## Francis Borgia
Francis Borgia (1510–1572) was a Roman Catholic saint, the duke of Gandia, and the general of the order of Jesuits. Born in Gandia, Valencia, on October 10, 1510, he was educated at Saragossa under his uncle, the archbishop. Though inclined toward monastic life from a young age, he was sent to the court of Charles V in 1528. He married Eleanor de Castro, a high-ranking Portuguese lady, and was appointed master of the horse to the empress and created marquis of Lombay.

Borgia accompanied Charles V on an expedition to Africa in 1535 and to Provence in 1536. In 1539, he convoyed the body of the empress to her burial place in Granada, an event that strengthened his resolve to enter monastic life. Despite this, he was appointed commander of the order of St James and viceroy of Catalonia. He governed from Barcelona with energy and efficiency and austerity until 1543, when he succeeded his father as duke and retired to Gandia.

In Gandia, he built a Jesuit college and encouraged the order of Jesus. Following the death of his wife in 1546, he sought to join the society. A papal dispensation allowed him to retain his worldly possessions and dignities for four years for the sake of his children. In 1550, he visited Rome and provided the funds for the construction of the Collegium Romanum. He formally resigned his estate and rank in 1551.

St. Francis Borgia authored a treatise titled *Rhetorica Concionandi* and a series of *Exercises* similar to those of Loyola. His *Opera Omnia* were published in Brussels in 1675, and his life was documented by his confessor, Pedro de Ribadeneira.
