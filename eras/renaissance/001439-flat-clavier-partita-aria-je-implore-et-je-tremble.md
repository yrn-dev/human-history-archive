# B flat Clavier-Partita and "Je t'implore et je tremble"

The B flat Clavier-Partita is a work composed by Sebastian Bach. Within the broader context of Bach's output, partitas are categorized as chamber music; specifically, the archive notes that Bach composed six sonatas for violin alone, which consisted of three sonatas and three partitas. Additionally, the *Klavier-Uebung*—a collection chiefly for harpsichord comprising four books—includes six partitas.

## Connection to Gluck's Iphigenie en Tauride

The B flat Clavier-Partita is noted for its influence on the composer Gluck, specifically in the work *Iphigenie en Tauride*. In the aria "Je t'implore et je tremble," Gluck reproduced the first twelve bars of the *Gigue* from Bach's B flat Clavier-Partita in every detail of figure and harmony. 

This instance is characterized as plagiarism, though it is described as being unconscious. The archive distinguishes such occurrences from a "profound indebtedness" that leads to original achievement. In Gluck's case, his specific training practically deprived him of the direct influence of Bach, which might have otherwise been useful for his choral and harmonic expression. Despite this, the archive suggests that the indirect influence of Bach was inescapable for all modern music.

## Context of Bach's Clavier Works

The B flat Clavier-Partita exists within a vast body of clavier and organ music produced by Bach. His primary collections include:

*   ***Das wohltemperirte Klavier***: Written for clavichord, consisting of two books with 24 preludes and fugues each, designed to stimulate tuning by "equal temperament."
*   ***Klavier-Uebung***: A collection for harpsichord containing 15 two-part inventions, 15 three-part symphonies, six partitas, the "Goldberg" variations, four duets, and a collection of organ choral-preludes.
*   **Suites**: Six "French" and six "English" suites.

Other clavier works are documented across two *Jahrgaenge* of the *B.-G.* (Bach-Gesellschaft). Additionally, Bach produced seven clavier concertos arranged from other sources, including violin concertos, and several concertos for multiple claviers.

## Scholarly Editions

The study of Bach's clavier works, including the partitas, has been facilitated by various editions. The *Bach-Gesellschaft* is the only complete edition, though its merit is described as unequal. Early editors are noted for their ignorance of Bach's language, often dismissing details of figured bass and ornamentation. Later, Wilhelm Rust introduced scholarly methods in the 9th *Jahrgang*.

For clavier works specifically, the edition by Dr. Hans Bischoff (the Steingraeber edition) is cited as incomparably the best. This edition provides a straightforward text while using footnotes to list all variants and distinguishing the editor's phrasing signs and nuances from the rare indications provided by Bach himself.
