# The Schaeferpfeife and Related German Sackpfeifen

The *Schaeferpfeife* is one of five sizes of the German *Sackpfeife* (or *Dudelsack*), all of which were described and illustrated by Michael Praetorius. These instruments are characterized by the use of a drone ground bass that sounds without intermission.

## The Schaeferpfeife
The *Schaeferpfeife* is equipped with two drones tuned to B flat and F. The compass of its chaunter ranges from F4 to F5. According to the explanations provided by Praetorius, the upper notes of the *Schaeferpfeife's* chaunter suffer from faulty intonation. This issue cannot be corrected because the instrument lacks the thumb-hole that is typically found in all other varieties of the *Sackpfeife*.

## Other Varieties of German Sackpfeifen
Praetorius detailed several other sizes and types of the instrument:

*   **Grosser Bock**: A double-bass bag-pipe described as "formidable-looking." It features a single cylindrical drone of great length and a chaunter; both terminate in a curved ram's horn. The chaunter possesses a thumb-hole at the back, a vent-hole in front, and seven finger-holes. Its drone is tuned to G, one octave below the chaunter.
*   **Bock**: Similar in construction to the *Grosser Bock*, but pitched a fourth higher in C.
*   **Huemmelchen**: This variety features two drones tuned to F and C, with a chaunter compass ranging from C5 to C6.
*   **Dudey**: The smallest of the family, the treble sackpfeife. It has three drones tuned to E flat, B flat, and E flat. Its chaunter compass ranges from F or E flat to C or D.
*   **Magdeburg Sackpfeife**: A different kind of instrument seen by Praetorius in Magdeburg. It is pitched a third lower and is somewhat larger than the *Schaeferpfeife*. It is distinguished by having two chaunters mounted in one stock, each with one thumb-hole at the back and three holes in front. The left-hand chaunter sounds the notes G, A, B, C, D, while the right-hand chaunter sounds D, E, F, G, A. This configuration allows the performer to play simple two-part melodies. Its drones are tuned to G3 and D4, and its chaunter compass is D4 to D5. Praetorius described this instrument in 1618 as having a conical bore and no keys.

## General Construction and Mechanics
The main characteristic of the bag-pipe is the drone, which utilizes a beating-reed. This reed is created by making a cut partly across a piece of cane or reed near the open end and splitting back toward a joint to create a tongue or flap. This is fixed in a socket within the drone, which in turn fits into the stock. Sound is produced when the performer's arm forces air from the bag into the drone-pipe, causing the reed's tongue to vibrate. Because the drone-pipe is a cylindrical tube with a reed mouthpiece, it possesses the acoustic properties of a closed pipe, producing a note of a pipe twice its length. Drones are tuned using sliding-joints.

The instrument's layout places the blow-pipe and the chaunter at opposite ends of the bag. The bag rests under the performer's arm while the drones point over the shoulder. There are two primary methods of inflating the bag: using a blow-pipe or using a small bellows connected by a valved feed-pipe.
