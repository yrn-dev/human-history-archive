# Unclassified Instrumental Works of J.S. Bach

Within the catalog of Sebastian Bach's compositions, there are two significant instrumental works that cannot be classified. These works are *Das musikalische Opfer* and *Die Kunst der Fuge*.

## Das musikalische Opfer
*Das musikalische Opfer* is a volume of compositions based on a theme provided to Bach by Frederick the Great. The contents of this volume include:
* Two great fugues.
* Various puzzle-canons.
* A trio for figured bass, violin, and flute, described as splendid.

## Die Kunst der Fuge
*Die Kunst der Fuge* consists of a progressive series of fugues. All of these fugues are based on one and the same subject. The work was written in open score, giving it the appearance of entirely abstract studies. With the exception of the extreme contrapuntal *tours de force*, the pieces are written in an admirable clavier style and are considered to be of great musical value.

## Context of Bach's Musical Art
Bach is characterized not as a reformer or an inventor of new forms, but as a spectator of all musical existence who prioritized truth over whether a composition was new or old. While it is doubtful if forms such as the arpeggio-prelude were his invention, he left no form as he found it. 

A notable example of this is the Da Capo Aria. Although Bach did not outwardly alter this conventional form, he developed it in a great variety of ways. He did not abolish the form—as Gluck did because it was unfit for dramatic music—because Bach did not intend to write dramatic music in the strict sense. Instead, he avoided abusing the Da Capo Aria as the staple form for opera, which was the custom of the time.

Bach's approach to art was to be complete and consistent in all aspects. This spirit of truthfulness distinguished him from contemporaries like Beethoven, who invented new means of expression and forms with every work. Bach's influence became all-pervading because he acted as an unbiased seeker of truth rather than a prophet of a new art.

## Other Organ Works
Beyond the unclassified works, Bach's organ music includes several distinct collections:
* Six sonatas.
* Four groups consisting of six organ preludes and fugues each.
* *Das Orgelbuechlein*, a collection of short choral-preludes. This work was carefully planned, with titles of intended chorales written on blank autograph pages, though it was not half executed. Had it been completed, it would have been a larger volume than the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier*.
* Eighteen larger chorale-preludes, which include the final composition of Bach's life.
* The six "Schuebler" chorales, which were arranged from movements of cantatas.
* Three great independent toccatas and the Passacaglia.

The remaining choral-preludes occupy one *Jahrgang*, while the other organ works occupy two more.
