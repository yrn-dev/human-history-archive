# St Paul's Cathedral, London

St Paul's Cathedral is a prominent landmark in London. Its architectural design and the various figures associated with its administration and artistic adornment are documented across several historical records.

## Architecture and Construction
The cathedral's dome was designed by Sir Christopher Wren, who addressed structural challenges by utilizing a system of three shells. The innermost shell features a central eye and serves as the dome visible from the interior. The middle shell is conical in shape and supports the lantern, with the weight transferred directly to the base, which is reinforced with iron ties and the surrounding portico. The outermost shell is constructed from timber and covered in lead. These domes are situated on lofty drums, distinguishing their external appearance from other structures such as the Pantheon in Rome or Sta Sophia in Constantinople.

## Ecclesiastical Administration
The cathedral has been served by various high-ranking church officials. Richard Bancroft (1544–1610), who later became the archbishop of Canterbury, was appointed treasurer of St Paul's cathedral in 1585. In 1586, Bancroft was made a member of the ecclesiastical commission. He later became a prebendary of the cathedral in 1590. On February 9, 1589, Bancroft delivered a sermon at Paul's Cross based on 1 John iv. 1, in which he strongly attacked the Puritans, denounced private judgment, and asserted the divine right of bishops.

Other notable figures associated with the cathedral include John Dolben (1625–1686), an English divine who served as a prebendary of St Paul's in 1661 before eventually becoming the archbishop of York.

## Artistic Works and Monuments
The cathedral houses several significant sculptures and monuments created by English artists. Thomas Banks (1735–1805), an English sculptor born in London, contributed monuments to Captain Burges and Captain Westcott. 

Thomas Brock, an English sculptor and pupil of Foley, created a sepulchral monument to Lord Leighton within the cathedral, a work described as possessing beauty, refinement, and singular significance.
