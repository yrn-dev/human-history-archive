# Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon and Early Spanish Exploration

## Early Career and Ambitions
Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon was a Spanish colonizer and adventurer born around 1475, likely in Toledo, Spain. His career in the Americas began in 1502 when he accompanied Nicolas Ovando to Hispaniola (Santo Domingo). While stationed there, Ayllon held several positions of authority, serving as a member of the superior court of Hispaniola and as a magistrate for various towns, including La Concepcion. During this period, he engaged in various commercial enterprises that yielded great profit.

Ayllon eventually became interested in expanding Spanish settlements onto the North American mainland. To further this goal, he organized exploring expeditions to scout the region for potential colonization.

## The Gordillo Expedition
In 1521, Ayllon dispatched Francisco Gordillo on an exploring expedition. During this voyage, Gordillo touched the coast of the Florida peninsula and continued to coast northward for some distance. The report Gordillo provided regarding the region was favorable enough that, by 1523, Ayllon was able to obtain a charter from Charles V. This charter, though described as rather indefinite, granted Ayllon the right to plant colonies in the area.

## The Colonization Attempt of 1526
Following another reconnoitring expedition in 1525, Ayllon personally led a colonization effort in early 1526. He set out with a group consisting of approximately 500 colonists and about 100 African slaves. 

As the expedition moved along the coast, they stopped at several locations. At one point, the group remained long enough to build a new ship to replace one that had been wrecked; this event is considered the first instance of shipbuilding on the North American continent.

## The Settlement of San Miguel
Sailing northward to approximately latitude 33deg 40', Ayllon began the construction of a town named San Miguel. The precise location of San Miguel is a subject of dispute among writers. Some suggest it was located on the exact site where Jamestown, Virginia, was later established, while others, such as Lowery, contend it was situated near the mouth of the Pedee river.

The settlement is notable for the employment of negro slaves, which was undoubtedly the first instance of such labor in the region that later became the United States.

## Collapse of the Colony
The site chosen for San Miguel proved to be unhealthy. Many of the colonists succumbed to fever, including Ayllon himself, who died on October 18, 1526. 

The death of their leader triggered dissensions among the remaining colonists. During this instability, some of the slaves rebelled and escaped into the forest. By December, the town was abandoned. The remaining colonists embarked for Hispaniola, though fewer than 150 of them arrived safely.
