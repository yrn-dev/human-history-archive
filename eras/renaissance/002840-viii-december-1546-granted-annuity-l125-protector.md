# Balnaves and the English Cause in Scotland

## Role as an English Agent
Balnaves served as a paid agent for the English cause in Scotland. He became involved in the defense of the castle of St Andrews, where he was noted as one of the most active defenders. Despite his activities during this period, he did not participate in the murder of Beton.

## Financial Support and Appointments
Balnaves received financial backing from the English government through several different channels:
* **Henry VIII:** In December 1546, he received L100 from King Henry VIII.
* **Protector Somerset:** In 1547, Protector Somerset granted him an annuity of L125. 

In addition to these funds, Balnaves was appointed as the English paymaster of the forces stationed in St Andrews.

## Captivity and Continued Support
In July, the castle of St Andrews surrendered to the French, resulting in Balnaves being taken prisoner to Rouen. Although Protector Somerset made efforts to secure his release, these attempts were unsuccessful. Despite his captivity, Somerset continued to pay his pension. 

Balnaves remained useful to the English government by providing information. His services were further recognized in June 1554, when Mary Tudor sent him L50 as a reward.

## Literary Contributions
Balnaves authored a work described by Knox as "a comfortable treatise of justification." This text was discovered in manuscript form among the papers of the reformer Knox, featuring a preface written by Knox. The work was eventually published in Edinburgh in 1584 under the title *The Confession of Faith*.
