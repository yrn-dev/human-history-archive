# Jean Jacques Boissard and His Works

Jean Jacques Boissard (1528–1602) was a French Latin poet and antiquary born in Besancon. His academic pursuits began at Louvain, though he secretly departed from that seminary due to the severity of his master. Following his departure, Boissard traveled through a significant portion of Germany before arriving in Italy. During his several years of residence in Italy, he frequently faced great financial straits, but the experience fostered a deep interest in antiquities.

## Collections and Travels
While in Italy, Boissard developed a passion for collecting curious monuments from Rome and the surrounding areas. His desire for exploration led him to visit the islands of the Archipelago with the intent of traveling through Greece; however, a severe illness forced him to return to Rome. He resumed his antiquarian pursuits with great ardour and, upon completing his collection, returned to France. Because he was not permitted to publicly profess the Protestant religion he had embraced, he withdrew to Metz, where he remained until his death on October 30, 1602.

## Literary and Antiquarian Works
Boissard authored several significant works across various genres, including poetry, emblems, and historical records. His bibliography includes:
*   *Poemata* (1574)
*   *Habitus Variarum Orbis Gentium* (1581), which featured seventy illuminated figures.
*   *Emblemata* (1584)
*   *Theatrum Vitae Humanae* (1596)
*   *Icones Virorum Illustrium* (1597)
*   *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum* (1597)
*   *Romanae Urbis Topographia* (1597–1602), a work noted for being very rare.
*   *De Divinatione et Magicis Praestigiis* (1605)

## Influence on Historical Writing
Boissard's work served as a resource for other historians. Richard Knolles (c. 1545–1610), an English historian and former master of a school at Sandwich, Kent, published his *Generall Historie of the Turkes* in 1603. This work, which was dedicated to King James I, drew largely upon Boissard's *Vitae et Icones Sultanorum Turcicorum*, published in Frankfort in 1596. Although the *Generall Historie of the Turkes* has since been superseded, it is recognized for its arrangement and style.
