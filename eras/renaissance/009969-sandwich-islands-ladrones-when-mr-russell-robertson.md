# Sandwich Islands, the Ladrones, and the Bonin Islands

The Pacific Ocean contains several distinct archipelagos and island groups, including the Sandwich Islands, the Ladrones (Marianas), and the Bonin Islands, each with unique geological, biological, and colonial histories.

## The Ladrones (Marianas)
The archipelago known as the Ladrones, or the Marianas, is located in the north-western Pacific Ocean between 12° and 21° N and 145° E. The islands were discovered by Magellan on March 6, 1521, when he observed and sailed between the two southernmost islands. Magellan named them *Islas de las Velas Latinas* ("Islands of the Lateen Sails"), while his crew called them *Islas de los Ladrones* ("Islands of the Thieves") due to the thieving tendencies of the inhabitants. In 1668, the islands were renamed *Las Marianas* in honor of Maria Anna of Austria.

The archipelago consists of two groups: a northern group of ten volcanic main islands (with Agrigan, Anatahan, Alamagan, and Pagan being inhabited) and a southern group of five coralline limestone islands (Rota, Guam, Aguijan, Tinian, and Saypan), all of which are inhabited except Aguijan. The volcanic islands reach elevations of approximately 2,700 feet and experience earthquakes and volcanic activity. The southern islands are of slight elevation and fringed by coral reefs.

The native population, known to early Spanish colonists as Chamorros, were typical Micronesians with a considerable civilization. At the time of Spanish occupation in 1668, they were estimated at 40,000 to 60,000, but this number fell to 1,800 in less than a century. They have since intermarried with natives of the Carolines and Tagal immigrants from the Philippines. On Tinian, remains of the Chamorro civilization exist in the form of two rows of massive square stone columns, 14 feet high and 5 feet 4 inches broad.

Economically, the islands produce coffee, cocoa, sugar, cotton, tobacco, yams, sweet potatoes, manioc, areca palms, and coco-nuts, with copra serving as the principal export. In 1899, Spain sold the entire archipelago, excluding Guam, to Germany for £837,500. During the early 20th century, the German Admiral von Spee used the Ladrone Islands as a rendezvous point, meeting the *Emden* there on August 12 before proceeding toward the Marshall Islands.

## The Bonin Islands
The Bonin Islands (originally *Bunin-jima*, meaning "without inhabitants") were discovered toward the end of the 16th century and added to the fief of the Daimyo Ogasawa Sadayori, who named them Ogasawara-jima. Communication with the islands ceased in 1635 due to a Japanese government veto on sea-going vessels. After fitful communication was restored in 1728, it was interrupted again until 1861.

In 1830, a small party comprising two Americans, two British subjects, and a Dane sailed from the Sandwich Islands to Port Lloyd. They brought Hawaiian natives with them and established a settlement on Peel Island (Chichi-jima), where they hoisted the British flag. By 1853, Commodore Perry found thirty-one inhabitants on Peel Island, including four English, four American, one Portuguese, and several natives from the Ladrones, the Sandwich Islands, and elsewhere.

In 1875, Mr. Russell Robertson, formerly the H.B.M. consul in Yokohama, visited the colony. He reported that the population had grown to sixty-nine, only five of whom were "pure whites." Robertson observed that the settlers lived comfortably on cultivated land but lacked laws, religion, education, and a system of government. The settlers spoke English and viewed themselves as a British colony. However, in 1861, the British government renounced its claim to the islands, recognizing Japan's right of possession. By the time of the archive's records, the population had grown to approximately 4,500 with regular steam communication.

## The Sandwich Islands
The Sandwich Islands are characterized by their volcanic geology, where basalts are the prevalent lavas. The islands possess a unique avifauna, entirely lacking Coraciiformes and Psittaci. They exhibit an extraordinary development of the *Drepanidae* family, likely descended from Central or South America. Other notable bird species include the Sandwich island goose (*Bernicla sandvicensis*), which has become domesticated in Europe, as well as a raven (*Corvus*), a coot (*Fulica*), a peculiar genus of Meliphagine (*Acrulocercus*), and various thrushlike birds and flycatchers.
