# Barbary Corsairs and Related Historical Records

## Definition and Nature of Corsairs
The term "corsair" is derived from the Mediterranean Latin *cursarius* (a pirate) and *cursus* or *cursa* (from *currere*, meaning to run), which in Late Latin referred to a plundering foray. In the Mediterranean context, the term was applied to privateers from the Barbary coast who plundered the shipping of Christian nations. While the word became synonymous with "pirate" in the English language, corsairs were not strictly pirates because they operated under commissions from their respective governments. In French, the word *corsaire* continues to be used for "privateer," and the term *guerre de course* describes the use of "commerce-destroyers" in naval warfare.

## Authorities on Barbary History
The early history of the Barbary states is documented by several primary authorities. These include Luis del Marmol Carvajal, who wrote *Descripcion de Africa* (Granada, 1573); Diego de Haedo, author of *Topographia e Historia General de Argel* (Valladolid, 1612); and Pere Pierre Dan, who wrote *Histoire de Barbarie et de ses corsaires* (Paris, 1637).

Further historical accounts include the *Histoire d'Alger* by H. D. de Grammont (Paris, 1887), which utilizes original authorities, and Sir R. L. Playfair's *Scourge of Christendom* (London, 1884), which details the history of the British consulate in Algiers. Additionally, Ad. Jurien de la Graviere published several treatises in Paris regarding the regular wars and the epoch of the beylerbeys, titled *Doria et Barberousse* (1886), *Les Corsaires barbaresques* (1887), *Les Chevaliers de Malte* (1887), and *La Guerre de Chypre* (1888). For perspectives on American involvement, Gardner Weld Allen wrote *Our Navy and the Barbary Corsairs* (New York, 1905).

## Related Maritime and Colonial Narratives
Beyond the Barbary coast, other maritime histories involve the buccaneers of the West Indies. A work by a buccaneer named Exquemelin, *De Americaensche Zee Roovers* (Amsterdam, 1678), was translated into multiple languages, including an English edition titled *The Bucaniers of America* and a French translation by Frontignières titled *Histoire des avanturiers qui se sont signalez dans les Indes*. Other sources on this subject include:
* Raynal's *History of the Settlements and Trade of the Europeans in the East and West Indies* (English translation 1782).
* Dampier's *Voyages*.
* Geo. W. Thornbury's *Monarchs of the Main, &c.* (1855).
* Lionel Wafer's *Voyage and Description of the Isthmus of America* (1699).
* The works of Père Charlevoix, specifically *Histoire de l'isle Espagnole, &c.* and *Histoire et description générale de la Nouvelle France*.

While some of these narratives are to be received with caution, Captain James Burney's *History of the Buccaneers of America* (London, 1816) is considered a really authentic narrative. Further evidence is found in the *Calendar of State Papers*, Colonial Series (London, 1860 et seq.).
