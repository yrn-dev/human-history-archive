# Kynge Johan

*Kynge Johan* is a dramatic work written circa 1548 that serves as a pivotal point in English theatrical history, marking the transition between the old morality play and the English historical drama. The play was authored by Bale, a writer known for his vehemence and scurrility in attacking his enemies, as well as a strong bias against Roman Catholic writers.

## Content and Themes
The play functions as a polemic against the Roman Catholic Church. In the narrative, King John is portrayed as a champion of English rites standing against the Roman see. He is described as a "noble Kynge" who, similar to a "faythfull Moses," withstood a "proude Pharao" for the sake of the "poore Israel." 

Within the work, King John is characterized as a Lollard and is accused of using "heretycall langage." The plot concludes with the King being poisoned by a monk of Swinestead. The play further suggests that while John fought for the people, the English remained in the bondage of Rome until "duke Josue," identified as the late King Henry, led them into "the lande of mylke and honye."

## Dramatic Structure and Style
Bale's writing in *Kynge Johan* is described as doggerel. The play blends real historical figures with allegorical characters. For example, the nation is represented by the character Ynglonde *vidua*. The role of the "Vice," a staple of pure morality plays, is filled by the jocular character Sedwyson, who represents sedition.

The production of the play was designed for a limited cast, as stage directions indicate that a single actor was intended to perform multiple roles; one such direction instructs the actor to "Go out Ynglond, and dress for Clargy."

## Manuscript and Discovery
The manuscript of *Kynge Johan* was discovered between 1831 and 1838 among corporation papers in Ipswich. It is believed the play was likely performed in Ipswich, as the text contains references to charitable foundations established by King John in that town and its surrounding neighborhood. 

The manuscript is described at its conclusion as consisting of two plays, although there is no obvious division between them, save for the notation of the end of the first act. While Bale's name does not appear within the manuscript itself, the first part was corrected by him, and the latter half is written in his handwriting. Additionally, Bale lists a play titled *De Joanne Anglorum Rege*, written *in idiomate materno*, in the catalog of his works.

## Historical Significance
*Kynge Johan* represents an early attempt at historical drama, produced fourteen years before the production of *Gorboduc*. Despite its importance as a transitional piece, it does not appear to have directly influenced the creators of the chronicle histories; for instance, it was apparently unknown to the authors of *The Troublesome Raigne of King John* (1591).
