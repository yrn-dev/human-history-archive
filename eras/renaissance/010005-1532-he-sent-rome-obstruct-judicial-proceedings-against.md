# Edmund Bonner

Edmund Bonner (1500?–1569) was the bishop of London. His parentage is a subject of historical dispute; one account, printed with circumstantial details by Strype, suggests he was the natural son of Elizabeth Frodsham and George Savage, the rector of Davenham, Cheshire. Elizabeth Frodsham later married a sawyer of Hanley in Worcestershire named Edmund Bonner. However, Sir Edmund Lechmere, a contemporary of Strype, asserted that Bonner was born legitimately, though the evidence for this claim is described as not very satisfactory.

## Education and Early Career
Bonner was educated at Broadgates Hall, which is now known as Pembroke College, Oxford. He graduated as a bachelor of canon and civil law in June 1519. He was ordained around the same time and later admitted as a Doctor of Civil Law (D.C.L.) in 1525.

By 1529, Bonner served as the chaplain to Cardinal Wolsey. He was present with the cardinal at Cawood during the time of Wolsey's arrest. Following this, Bonner was transferred to the service of the king, a move that may have occurred through the influence of Cromwell.

## Diplomatic Missions to Rome and France
In January 1532, Bonner was sent to Rome for the purpose of obstructing the judicial proceedings against Henry within the papal curia. 

Later, in October 1533, Bonner was assigned the "unmannerly task" of informing Clement VII of Henry's appeal from the pope to a general council. At the time of this communication, Clement VII was a guest of Francis I in Marseilles. While Burnet tells a story that Clement threatened to burn Bonner alive during this encounter, there appears to be no good authority to support this account.

## Later Appointments
As a reward for these and other services, Bonner was granted several livings. In 1535, he was appointed as the archdeacon of Leicester.
