# Portuguese Colonial and Diplomatic History (16th–17th Centuries)

## Administration and Rule in Bombay
Under Portuguese rule, the island of Bombay did not prosper. The administration utilized a system known as *aforamento*, where lands were gradually divided into various fiefs. These were granted under the Portuguese crown to religious corporations or individuals in exchange for equivalent quit-rents or military service. The Jesuits and Franciscans were granted the northern districts, where they constructed several churches.

The growth of the settlement was hindered by the intolerance of this rule. By 1661, the population of the settlement stood at only 10,000. During this period, the English recognized the location's safety from land attacks and the excellence of its harbour, viewing it as a valuable naval base. This led the English to fight the battle of Swally (1614-1615) and attempt to capture the site in 1626. In 1654, the directors of the Company brought this suggestion to the attention of Cromwell.

Bombay eventually became British property in 1661 as part of the dowry of the infanta Catherine of Portugal upon her marriage to Charles II. However, the British did not actually occupy the area until 1665, facing significant opposition to the cession from the Portuguese and specifically from the religious orders. In 1668, the crown transferred the territory to the East India Company, which placed it under the factory of Surat.

## Expansion and Loss in the East Indies
In 1512, Antonio D'Abreu annexed and discovered the Banda Islands for the Portuguese. However, by the beginning of the 17th century, the Portuguese were expelled from these islands by the Dutch.

## Conflict with Dutch Commercial Companies
The Portuguese faced sustained opposition from Dutch commercial enterprises in both the East and West. The Dutch East India Company was formed by the states-general to regulate trade, prosecute war with Portugal and Spain, and function as a government in remote seas. This was a response to the competition and fighting between Dutch traders, the natives, and the Portuguese.

Similarly, the Dutch West India Company was founded on June 3, 1621, to protect and regulate contraband trade in the African and American possessions of Portugal and Spain. In its early years, the company offset heavy armament expenses by plundering Portuguese and Spanish carracks and galleons.

## Portuguese Possessions in South America
The Dutch West India Company targeted Portuguese possessions in South America, guided in part by the advice of refugees in Amsterdam and their fellows in religion who were numerous in Brazil. After a failed attempt to occupy Bahia in 1623—which was immediately retaken by a combined Portuguese and Spanish armament—the Dutch established a firm footing in Pernambuco.

The Portuguese eventually regained their position in Brazil. The Dutch position became untenable due to the religious hostility of the Roman Catholic Portuguese, the monopolist tendencies of Dutch colonization, and the support Portugal received from England and France following her revolt from Spain. By the treaty of 1661, the Dutch resigned all claims to the country.

## Diplomatic Relations
In 1657, Sir George Downing was appointed as the resident at The Hague. Among his duties was to mediate between Holland and Portugal, as well as to defend English traders' interests against the Dutch.
