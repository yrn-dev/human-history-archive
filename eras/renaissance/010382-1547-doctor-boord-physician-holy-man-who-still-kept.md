# Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde (also spelled Borde), an English author and physician, was born at Boord's Hill, Holms Dale, Sussex. His early life included education at Oxford and admission into the Carthusian order while he was still under age. In 1521, he was dispensed from religion to serve as the suffragan bishop of Chichester, although he never actually filled this office. By 1529, he was freed from his monastic vows, citing an inability to endure the "rugorosite off your relygyon."

## Medical Education and Travels
Following his release from monastic vows, Boorde traveled abroad to study medicine. Upon returning to England, he was summoned to attend the duke of Norfolk. His academic pursuits continued as he visited the universities of Montpellier, Toulouse, Poitiers, and Orleans, as well as Wittenberg. Additionally, he observed the practice of surgery in Rome and joined a pilgrimage to Compostella in Navarre.

In 1536, Boorde studied medicine at Glasgow, where he recorded observations regarding the "devellyshe dysposicion of a Scottysh man" and their perceived lack of love or favor for Englishmen. Around 1538, he embarked on his most extensive journey, visiting Jerusalem and nearly every European country except for Turkey and Russia. He wrote a full itinerary of these travels, which was sent to Thomas Cromwell but subsequently lost.

## Relationship with Thomas Cromwell
Boorde's history with the Charterhouse was marked by conflict. In 1534, he was in London at the Charterhouse, and by 1536, he wrote to Thomas Cromwell complaining that he was in "thraldom" there. During this period, he also wrote two letters to the prior of the Charterhouse in 1535 and 1536, arguing for his complete release from monastic vows. 

Cromwell set him at liberty and entertained him at his house in Bishops Waltham, Hampshire. It appears Cromwell entrusted Boorde with a mission to investigate public feeling abroad regarding the English king. While in Catalonia, Boorde sent Cromwell seeds of rhubarb, a plant that would not be generally cultivated in England for another two hundred years.

## Literary Works
Boorde eventually settled at Montpellier, where he completed several works before 1542, including his *Brevyary*, his *Dietary*, and the *Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*. The latter is recognized as the earliest continental guide book. Dedicated to Lady Mary, daughter of King Henry VIII, this work teaches the reader about the usage and fashion of various countries, parts of different languages, and the various coins of money current in every region. In this book, Boorde uses lively verse to describe the foibles of the Englishman, including his obstinacy, fickleness, and fondness for new fashions. The text also includes geographical descriptions and a model dialogue in the Cornish language.

His other authentic works include:
* *Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor* (published by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though earlier undated editions exist).
* *The Brevyary of Health* (c. 1547).
* *The Princyples of Astronamy* (c. 1547).
* *A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV*.
* "The Peregrination of Doctor Board," printed in 1735.

Boorde's *Treatyse upon Berdes* and his *Itinerary of Europe* are lost. While several jest-books—such as *Scogin's Jests*, *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam*, and *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton*—as well as a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*, are attributed to him, these lack authority.

## Later Life
Boorde likely returned to England in 1542, residing in Pevensey and Winchester. He is described as a learned physician who produced sensible and amusing works on medicine and domestic hygiene.
