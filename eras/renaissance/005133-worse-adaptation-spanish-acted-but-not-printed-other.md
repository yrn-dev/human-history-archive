# Worse and Worse

*Worse and Worse* is a dramatic work identified as an adaptation from the Spanish. While the play was acted, it was not printed.

## Authorship and Context
The play is ascribed to the man known as the 3rd earl of Bristol (who succeeded his father, the 2nd earl). He is described as a figure of brilliant abilities and a great orator, though his natural gifts were marred by instability of character and a restless ambition. 

Clarendon characterized him as a man of incomparable parts who did not gain wisdom from experience or misfortune, noting his extraordinary facility for making both enemies and friends. Horace Walpole further described his life as "one contradiction," noting that he embraced popery after writing against it, and served as a persecutor of Lord Clarendon after being converted during his prosecution of Lord Strafford. Despite his romantic bravery, he was an unsuccessful commander.

## Literary and Other Works
In addition to *Worse and Worse*, the earl of Bristol authored several other works:
* **Dramatic Works:** He wrote the comedy *Elvira* (1667), which was printed in R. Dodsley's *Select Collect. of Old English Plays*.
* **Political and Religious Writings:** He wrote an *Apologie* in 1643, found in the Thomason Tracts, which served to justify his support of the king's cause.
* **Collaborations:** He is credited, along with Sir Samuel Tuke, as the author of *The Adventures of Five Hours* (1663).
* **Speeches:** He delivered eloquent and pointed speeches, many of which were printed and are preserved in the *Biog. Brit.* and the Thomason Tracts.

The catalogue of his library was published in 1680.

## Personal Life
The earl of Bristol married Lady Anne Russell, the daughter of Francis, the 4th earl of Bedford. The couple had two daughters and two sons. One son, Francis, died unmarried before his father; the other son, John, succeeded him as the 3rd earl of Bristol.
