# Pasquil's Mistresse, Pasquil's Passe and Passeth Not, and Melancholike Humours

The works *Pasquil's Mistresse* (1600), *Pasquil's Passe and Passeth Not* (1600), and *Melancholike Humours* (1600) are poetical works authored by Breton. These three titles belong to a broader collection of his literary output produced during the late 16th and early 17th centuries.

## Literary Context and Other Works
Breton's bibliography includes a variety of poetical and prose works. His earlier poetical efforts include *The Workes of a Young Wit* (1577), *A Floorish upon Fancie* (1577), and *Bryton's Bowre of Delights* (1591), though the latter was an unauthorized publication containing some poems that Breton disclaimed. Other works from the 1590s include *The Pilgrimage to Paradise* (1592) and *Marie Magdalen's Love: a Solemne Passion of the Soules Love* (1595). The latter is divided into two parts: a prose treatise likely written by another hand, and a poem in six-lined stanza certainly written by Breton.

In the years immediately following the publication of the *Pasquil* series and *Melancholike Humours*, Breton produced several other titles in 1601, including *A Divine Poem* (which contains "The Blessed Weeper" and "The Ravisht Soul"), *The Soules Heavenly Exercise*, and *An Excellent Poem, upon the Longing of a Blessed Heart*. His output continued into 1602 with *The Soules Harmony*, *The Mother's Blessing*, *A True Description of Unthankfulnesse*, and *Olde Madcappe newe Gaily mawfrey*. Later works include *The Passionate Shepheard* (1604), *The Honour of Valour* (1605), and *The Soules Immortall Crowne* (1605), as well as *An Invective against Treason; I would and I would not* (1614).

Additionally, Breton authored twenty-two prose tracts, such as *Wit's Trenchmour* (1597), *The Wil of Wit* (1599), and *A Poste with a Packet of Mad Letters* (1603). He also contributed to other miscellanies of verse and *England's Helicon*. Certain works, such as *The Passion of a Discontented Mind* (1601), *Mary Magdalen's Lamentations* (1604), and *Sir Philip Sidney's Ourania by N.B.* (1606), have been erroneously attributed to him.
