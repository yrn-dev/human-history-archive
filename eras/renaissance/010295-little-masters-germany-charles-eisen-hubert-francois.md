# Little Masters of Germany

The Little Masters of Germany are recognized within the history of art and book-plate collecting as significant contributors to decorative design. Their work is cited as being of high interest, often possessing a value that exceeds the value of the volumes in which their designs are found affixed.

## Role in Book-Plate Collecting
In the field of "ex-librisme," or book-plate collecting, the designs of the Little Masters of Germany are regarded as worth collecting. They serve as personal relics of well-known personages or as specimens of decorative fashions from the past. Their work is categorized alongside that of other notable artists and engravers, including Dürer and Holbein.

## Comparison with Other Artists
The archive lists the Little Masters of Germany among a broad spectrum of artists whose book-plates are considered valuable. This group includes:
*   **Early and Classical Artists:** Dürer, Holbein, and D.N. Chodowiecki.
*   **French and European Designers:** Charles Eisen, Hubert Francois Bourguignon (known as Gravelot), and Simon Gribelin.
*   **British Engravers and Artists:** W. Marshall, W. Faithorne, David Loggan, Sir Robert Strange, Hogarth, Cipriani, Bartolozzi, John Keyse Sherwin, William Henshaw, Hewitt, and Bewick.
*   **Modern Contributors:** Thomas Stothard, Thackeray, Millais, Maclise, Bell Scott, T.G. Jackson, Walter Crane, Caldecott, Stacy Marks, Edwin Abbey, Kate Greenaway, Gordon Browne, Herbert Railton, Aubrey Beardsley, Alfred Parsons, D.Y. Cameron, and Paul Avril.

## Context of Collection
The practice of collecting these plates is a subject of debate among bibliophiles. Some enthusiastic collectors have attempted to claim a scientific basis for "ex-librisme." Conversely, some intolerant bibliophiles have criticized the removal of book-plates from old books, characterizing the act as vandalism. Despite these disagreements, the artistic merit of the Little Masters of Germany remains a point of consensus for those valuing decorative history.
