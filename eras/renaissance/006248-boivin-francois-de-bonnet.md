# BOIVIN, FRANCOIS DE

Francois de Boivin, the Baron de Villars, was a French chronicler who died in 1618. He is primarily recognized for his service to Charles, Marshal Brissac, for whom he served as a secretary.

## Military and Diplomatic Career
Boivin's professional activities were closely tied to the military campaigns of Marshal Brissac. In 1550, Boivin accompanied the marshal to Piedmont, where Brissac had gone to take command of French troops during the war with Spain. Boivin remained in this service for several years. Following the defeat of French forces at St Quentin in 1557, he was dispatched to provide assurance to King Henry II regarding the continued support of Brissac.

In April 1559, Boivin participated in the negotiations that resulted in the treaty of Cateau-Cambresis. Despite his involvement in these diplomatic efforts, he was unable to prevent King Henry II from ceding the conquests that had been achieved by Brissac.

## Written Works
Boivin authored two notable works. The first, *Memoires sur les guerres demelees tant dans le Piemont qu'au Montferrat et duche de Milan par Charles de Cosse, comte de Brissac*, was published in Paris in 1607. This text is considered a valuable eye-witness account of the war, despite containing some drawbacks. A carefully revised edition of these memoirs was later included in the *Memoires relatifs a l'histoire de France* (tome x.), edited by J.J.F. Poujoulat and J.F. Michaud in 1850.

His second work, *Instruction sur les affaires d'etat*, was published in Lyons in 1610.
