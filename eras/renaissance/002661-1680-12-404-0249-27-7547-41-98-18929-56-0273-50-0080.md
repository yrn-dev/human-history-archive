## Political Transitions in Japan
In 1680, the Tokugawa Bakufu entered a new period following the dangerous illness of the fourth shogun, Ietsuna. A council of chief Bakufu officials was convened to determine a successor. During this council, the prime minister, Sakai Tadakiyo, suggested that an Imperial prince be invited to assume the office of shogun, following the example of Kamakura. 

This proposal was vehemently opposed by Hotta Masatoshi, a junior minister, who asserted that the dignity of the shogunate belonged to Tsunayoshi alone. Masatoshi noted that Tsunayoshi was the only brother of Ietsuna and the legitimate son of the late shogun, Iemitsu. Following this intervention, Sakai Tadakiyo retired from the council chamber, and Tsunayoshi ascended as shogun. This marked the beginning of the middle period of the Tokugawa Bakufu, which lasted from Tsunayoshi until the tenth shogun, Ieharu, in 1786.

## Notable Deaths and Biographies

*   **William Bedloe (1650–1680):** An English informer born in Chepstow. After moving to London in 1670 and associating with Jesuits, he traveled Europe under various aliases, including Lord Newport, Lord Cornwallis, and Captain Williams. In 1678, he provided the English government with an account of a supposed popish plot and received £500 for his details regarding the murder of Sir E.B. Godfrey.
