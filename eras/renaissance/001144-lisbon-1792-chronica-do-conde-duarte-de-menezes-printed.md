# Chronica do Conde D. Duarte de Menezes

The *Chronica do Conde D. Duarte de Menezes* is a historical work authored by the Portuguese chronicler Gomes Eannes de Azurara. The text was printed in Lisbon in 1793 as part of the third volume of the *Ineditos*.

## Authorship and Composition
The chronicle was commissioned by King Alphonso V, who charged Gomes Eannes de Azurara with recording the deeds of D. Duarte de Menezes, the captain of Alcacer. To ensure the accuracy of the account, Azurara traveled to Africa, where he spent one year in the town collecting materials and studying the specific scenes of the events he was tasked to describe. He completed the chronicle in 1468.

Gomes Eannes de Azurara (who died in 1474) is recognized as the second notable Portuguese chronicler by date. He entered the career of letters in middle life, likely serving as an assistant to Fernao Lopes in the royal library during the reign of King Duarte (1433-1438), before taking sole charge of the library in 1452. By June 6, 1454, Azurara had succeeded Fernao Lopes as the royal chronicler and chief keeper of the archives.

## Literary Style and Influence
Writing under the influence of the early Renaissance, Azurara’s work is characterized by a desire to display erudition. Unlike the "picturesque ingenuousness" found in the works of Fernao Lopes, Azurara frequently employed philosophical reflections and quotations. His writing reflects a broad intellectual range, incorporating the works of medieval, early Christian, and leading classical writers, as well as the romances and chronicles of Europe. He was well-versed in the works of Spanish and Italian authors, and he had mastered astrology and the geographical systems of the ancients. As a historian, he is described as accurate, laborious, and conscientious.

## Related Works by Azurara
The *Chronica do Conde D. Duarte de Menezes* is one of several significant historical projects undertaken by Azurara:

*   **Chronicle of the Siege and Capture of Ceuta (1450):** A supplement to Fernao Lopes' *Chronicle of King John I*.
*   **Chronicle of the Discovery and Conquest of Guinea (completed 1453):** A primary authority on early Portuguese ocean voyages and discoveries down the African coast, particularly those under Prince Henry the Navigator. This work includes biographical and geographical information regarding the prince.
*   **History of Ceuta (1463):** Commissioned by King Alphonso V in 1456, this work covers Ceuta—referred to as "the land-gate of the East"—from its capture in 1415 until 1437, specifically under the governorship of D. Pedro de Menezes.

## Historical Context and Recognition
Azurara maintained a relationship of "affectionate intimacy" with King Alphonso V. In recognition of his literary services, the king rewarded him with no fewer than three *commendas* of the order of Christ.

The subjects of Azurara's chronicles, such as the Menezes family, appear in other historical records of the period. For instance, D. Duarte de Menezes served as a governor in Tangier, where he dubbed Joao de Castro a knight when Castro was eighteen years old.
