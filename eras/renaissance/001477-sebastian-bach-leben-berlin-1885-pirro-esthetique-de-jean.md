# Johann Sebastian Bach

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750) was a German musical composer and one of the most significant figures in the history of polyphonic music. He was born into a musical dynasty that maintained its position in Thuringia for nearly two hundred years, producing musicians of European importance. The family's influence was so pervasive that in Erfurt, musicians were referred to as "Bachs" even after the family had left the town.

## Family Origins and Lineage
The Bach family genealogy, compiled by J. S. Bach and completed by his son Philipp Emanuel, identifies Veit Bach—a miller and baker—as the family founder. Veit's son, Hans Bach ("der Spielmann"), was the family's first professional musician. Sebastian's grandfather was Christoph, a son of Hans. Other notable ancestors included Johann Michael and Johann Christoph (sons of Heinrich of Arnstadt), the latter of whom is believed by some to have written the motet *Ich lasse dich nicht*. Sebastian Bach held particular admiration for another descendant of Veit, Johann Ludwig, whose church cantatas Sebastian copied and occasionally expanded with his own work.

## Early Life and Education
Baptized in Eisenach on March 23, 1685, Bach lost both parents by the age of ten. He was subsequently raised and taught music by his elder brother, Johann Christoph, an organist at Ohrdruf. Due to his brother's jealousy, Bach was denied access to a manuscript volume containing works by Buxtehude, Froberger, and other organists. For six months, Bach secretly copied the volume by moonlight, an effort that caused permanent damage to his eyesight.

Following his brother's death in 1700, the fifteen-year-old Bach moved to Lueneburg. His soprano voice earned him a position as a chorister at the school of St Michael. While he received a general education there, his musical growth was primarily the result of self-study. He studied the works of contemporary and classical composers, including Pachelbel, Muffat the elder, Froberger, Buxtehude, Caspar Kerl, Frescobaldi, and likely Johann Joseph Fux, author of the *Gradus ad Parnassum*. During his youth, Bach also made foot journeys to Hamburg to hear the Dutch organist Reinken and was exposed to the foundations of German opera being established by Keiser.

## Career Highlights and Later Years
A significant moment in Bach's career occurred in 1747 when he was summoned to Potsdam to visit Frederick the Great. This visit was prompted by the success of Bach's son, Philipp Emanuel, who served as the king's court-composer. During this visit, Bach played on Silbermann's newly invented pianofortes and tested the organs of Potsdam's churches. Frederick provided a theme for Bach to extemporize upon, which later became the basis for the composition *Das musikalische Opfer*.

In the two years following this visit, Bach's vision began to fail, eventually leading to total blindness. He died of apoplexy on July 28, 1750. At the time of his death, he was recognized as one of the premier clavier players and organists of his era, though much of his compositional output remained unknown.

## Legacy and Influence
Upon his death, Bach's manuscripts were divided among his sons, and many were lost. It took nearly a century for modern proponents of polyphonic art to recover a fraction of his greater works. 

Bach's influence on subsequent composers was profound:
* **Mendelssohn:** Discovered the *Passion according to St Matthew* at age twelve, which influenced his design of oratorios.
* **Wagner:** The close polyphonic texture and harmonization of Wagner's *Leitmotif* schemes were foreshadowed by Bach.
* **Brahms:** Developed Bach's artistic devices alongside those of Beethoven to unify polyphony and form.

While Gluck and Berlioz are noted as the only great composers who escaped his direct influence, Gluck nonetheless reproduced a portion of Bach's B flat Clavier-Partita *Gigue* in the aria "Je t'implore et je tremble" from *Iphigenie en Tauride*. Additionally, Bach's sons, whom he encouraged to cultivate new art-forms, exerted a significant influence on modern music.

## Family Descendants
Four of Bach's sons became musicians. The eldest, Wilhelm Friedermann Bach (1710–1784), was regarded as the most gifted, mastering counterpoint and improvisation. Despite appointments as organist of the Sophienkirche in Dresden (1733) and musical director of the Liebfrauenkirche in Halle (1747), his career was marred by dissolute habits. He resigned from his post in 1764 and died in poverty in Berlin on July 1, 1784. His works, including fugues, polonaises, and a sestet for strings, clarinet, and horns, are partially preserved in the Royal library at Berlin.
