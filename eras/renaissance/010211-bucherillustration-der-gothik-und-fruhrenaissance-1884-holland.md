# Bucherillustration der Gothik und Fruhrenaissance (1884)

## Overview
*Die deutsche Bucherillustration der Gothik und Fruhrenaissance*, published in 1884, is a scholarly work authored by Muther. The text serves as a primary resource for the study of German book illustration during the Gothic and early Renaissance periods.

## Context of Illustrated Books
The study of illustrated books from these periods is characterized by a high level of demand. While a small number of volumes have avoided becoming rare due to their early celebrity, the price of most illustrated books from these eras has increased inordinately. 

Within the field of incunabula, primitive woodcuts are currently viewed with very high appreciation. Market values for specific notable works vary; for example, the *Nuremburg Chronicle* (1493) seldom fetches more than L30, whereas the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* (Venice, 1499) may reach prices of L120. Rarer illustrated books are often priced in the hundreds.

## Regional Bibliographic Resources
The study of early book illustration is divided by regional focus. While Muther's 1884 work focuses on Germany, other essential texts cover different European regions:

*   **Holland and Belgium:** Sir W.M. Conway authored *The Woodcutters of the Netherlands in the 15th Century* (1884), which is the designated resource for these regions.
*   **Italy:** Key texts include Lippmann's *Wood Engraving in Italy in the 15th Century* (1888), Kristeller's *Early Florentine Woodcuts* (1897), and the duc de Rivoli's (Prince d'Essling's) *Bibliographie des livres a figures venitiens 1469-1525* (1892, with a new edition in 1906).
*   **France:** Material regarding French illustration is found in Claudin's *Histoire de l'imprimerie en France* (1900).

## Related Studies in Ornamentation and Engraving
Beyond the Gothic and early Renaissance periods, other works provide insight into the evolution of book art. Butsch's *Die Bucherornamentik der Renaissance* (1878) offers information on illustrated books from the early 16th century. 

However, certain areas of the medium have been imperfectly appreciated, specifically the "pretty French books" from the middle of the 16th century, as well as later English and Dutch copper-engraved book illustrations. For the English copper-engraved works, the archive points to Colvin's *Early Engraving and Engravers in England* (1905).
