# Anne Boleyn

## Early Life and Education
Anne Boleyn (c. 1507–1536) was the daughter of Elizabeth and Sir Thomas Boleyn. Her father later held the titles of earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde, while her mother was the daughter of Thomas Howard, the earl of Surrey and subsequent duke of Norfolk. There is some disagreement regarding her birth date; while Camden records it as 1507, other later writers have ascribed her birth to 1501 or 1502, though these dates are not conclusively proven.

In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France, where she served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I.

## Return to England and Suitors
Anne returned to England in either 1521 or 1522. During this period, she attracted many suitors and admirers. Among her admirers was the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt. One of her suitors was Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland; however, the king intervened to stop a marriage between Anne and Percy, providing Sir James Butler as an alternative match. Despite this, Anne remained unmarried.

Between 1522 and 1525, King Henry bestowed a series of favours and grants upon Anne's father. Some have interpreted these actions as a symptom of the king's affections for Anne, though this is viewed doubtfully.

## Relationship with Henry VIII
Unlike her sister Mary, who had been a victim of Henry's solicitations, Anne had no intention of becoming the king's mistress. Her conduct appears to have been governed by ambition, as she intended to be his queen. 

The exact start date of the relationship between Anne and Henry is unknown. While some surmise it began as early as 1523, there is no proof that Henry's passion existed prior to the divorce proceedings initiated in May 1527, as the famous love letters associated with the pair are undated.

## Political Opposition and Marriage Efforts
Anne's name first became openly connected to the king as a potential wife—contingent upon the divorce of Catherine—in a letter dated August 16, 1527, written by Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, to Charles V.

He was instead negotiating a marriage for the king with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII. Despite Wolsey's opposition, Henry sent his secretary, William Knight, on a separate mission to Rome to secure the facilities necessary for his marriage to Anne.
