# Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara

## Biography and Career
Lorenzo Costa (1460–1535) was an Italian painter born in Ferrara. Although his origins were in Ferrara, he moved to Bologna early in his life and is consequently ranked as a member of the Bolognese school of painting.

During his career, Costa produced a variety of works, including frescoes. In 1438, he painted several frescoes on the walls of the Bentivoglio chapel in San Giacomo Maggiore, including his famous work, "Madonna and Child with the Bentivoglio family." Following this project, he completed many other works.

In 1509, Costa relocated to Mantua. While there, he was under the patronage of the Marquis Francesco Gonzaga. He eventually died in Mantua.

## Artistic Influence and Legacy
Costa maintained a close friendship with the artist Francia, who was significantly influenced by Costa's work. The merit of Costa's paintings is recognized in the collections of the Museo Civico in Italy, where his 15th-century works are noted for their considerable merit.

While his chief works are located in Bologna, some of his art is held in other international collections; for example, his "Madonna and Child enthroned" is housed in the National Gallery in London.

## Family of Painters
The artistic tradition continued through Costa's descendants. He had two sons, Ippolito (1506–1561) and Girolamo, both of whom were painters. The lineage of painters extended further to Girolamo's son, Lorenzo the younger, who lived from 1537 to 1583.
