# Padua

Padua is an Italian city that has served as a significant center for law, medicine, art, and political power throughout the late medieval and early modern periods.

## Political History and the House of Carrara
During the 14th century, Padua was ruled by the Carraresi (or Carrara), a powerful family of Longobard origin. The first recorded member of this house was Gamberto, who died before 970. The family's history was marked by shifts in political allegiance between the Ghibellines and the Guelphs. In 1312, Jacopo da Carrara led the Paduans against Cangrande della Scala of Verona; following a peace negotiation in 1318, the Paduans elected Jacopo as their lord. Upon his death in 1324, he was succeeded by his nephew Marsiglio.

Marsiglio initially surrendered the city to Cangrande but regained the lordship in 1337 by introducing Venetian troops and arresting Alberto della Scala. He was succeeded in 1338 by Ubertino, a ruler known for both his treachery and his patronage of the arts, including the construction of the castle of Este, the Palazzo dei Principi, and various roads and canals. Following Ubertino's death in 1345, the lordship passed through several members of the family, including Jacopo da Carrara, a friend of Petrarch.

In the late 14th and early 15th centuries, the city faced repeated conflicts with the Visconti. Francesco Novello seized Padua in 1390 and reigned in peace for several years. However, by November 1405, internal discontent, plague, hunger, and the treachery of his captains forced Novello to surrender to Venice. Novello and his sons, Francesco III and Jacopo, were later strangled in a Venetian prison in 1406. The house of Carrara ceased to trouble the region after Marsiglio, another son of Novello, was killed in 1435 during an attempt to recover the city.

## Academic and Medical Contributions
Padua established itself as a prominent site for higher education. In the 14th century, the Italian jurist Paulus Castrensis served as a professor at Padua in 1429, having also taught at Vienna, Avignon, Florence, Bologna, and Perugia.

The city was also a center for medical study. Leopoldo Marco Antonio Caldani, an Italian physician and anatomist born in Bologna, studied under G.B. Morgagni at Padua. After a period of teaching in Bologna, Caldani returned to Padua in 1771 to succeed Morgagni in the chair of anatomy, a position he held until 1805.

## Art and Architecture
In 1443, the artist Donatello was invited to Padua to decorate the high altar of S. Antonio. His first project there was a bronze crucifix for the high altar, noted for its nobility of expression. Donatello remained in Padua for ten years, during which time he established a workshop comprising painters, gilders, bronze-casters, metal-workers, and stone-carvers. 

Between 1443 and 1453, Donatello worked on the high altar—completing it in 1450 with seven statues, twenty-two reliefs, and the crucifix—and the equestrian statue of the Condottiere Erasmo de' Narni (known as Gattamelata). The Gattamelata statue, finished in 1453, is characterized by its majestic repose and searching realism.
