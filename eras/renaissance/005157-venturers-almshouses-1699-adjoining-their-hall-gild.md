# Merchant Venturers' Almshouses

## Overview and History
The Merchant Venturers' almshouses are buildings of historic interest dating to 1699. They are located adjoining the hall of the Merchant Venturers. The gild associated with these buildings was established during the 16th century.

## Context of Local Architecture
The almshouses are situated among various other examples of ancient domestic and conventual architecture. Notable nearby domestic structures include Canyng's house, which features a high-quality Perpendicular oak roof, and Colston's house on Small Street, which contains Tudor windows and arches from a grand Norman hall.

The area also contains several monastic relics, including:
*   **The Dominican priory:** This site preserves an Early English dormitory—featuring an oak roof and a row of fifteen original windows—and an Early English refectory.
*   **St Bartholomew's hospital:** This site contains Early English arcades and a double arch.
*   **Other relics:** The monastic remains are completed by the Holy Trinity Hospital and the small chapel of the Three Kings of Cologne, both of which are Perpendicular in style, as well as the remains of the house of the Augustinian canons attached to the cathedral.

## Nearby Historic Sites
In the vicinity of these historic buildings, other sites of note include a small house near St Mary Redcliffe. This house served as the school for the poet Chatterton. Records that Chatterton claimed contained manuscripts—which were actually his own—are kept in a chest within the church of St Mary, and his memorial is located in the churchyard. Additionally, a house located in Wine Street is recorded as the birthplace of Robert Southey, the poet-laureate, in 1744.
