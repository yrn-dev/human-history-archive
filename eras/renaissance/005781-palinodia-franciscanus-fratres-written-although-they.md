# Palinodia and Franciscanus & Fratres

The poems *Palinodia* and *Franciscanus & Fratres* were written by the scholar and poet Buchanan during a period of his life when he maintained a critical stance toward the practice of the Church of Rome, though he did not at that time repudiate its doctrines.

## Composition and Context
Buchanan's early literary efforts in Scotland began while he was in the household of Lord Cassilis in the west country. His first production was a poem titled *Somnium*, which served as a satirical attack on monastic life in general and the Franciscan friars specifically. This critique of the monks was viewed favorably by James V.

As a result of this reception, James V. engaged Buchanan as a tutor for one of his natural sons, Lord James Stewart (distinct from the son who later became the regent Murray). Encouraged by the King to pursue more daring literary efforts, Buchanan wrote *Palinodia* and *Franciscanus & Fratres*.

## Impact and Consequences
Although *Palinodia* and *Franciscanus & Fratres* remained unpublished for many years, the content of the works led the Franciscan order and their associates to view the author with the "bitterest hatred." 

During this era, attacking the church was a dangerous endeavor. In 1539, a bitter persecution of Lutherans occurred, during which Buchanan was arrested. He successfully escaped and traveled with difficulty to London and then to Paris.

## Later Career and Recognition
While in Paris, Buchanan encountered Cardinal David Beaton, who was serving as an ambassador and was an enemy of his. Following an invitation from André de Gouvéa, the principal of the newly founded college of Guienne in Bordeaux, Buchanan moved to Bordeaux and was appointed professor of Latin.

It was during his residence in Bordeaux that Buchanan completed several of his most significant works, including:
*   The dramas *Baptistes (sive Calumnia)* and *Jephthes (sive Votum)*.
*   Translations of *Alcestis* and *Medea*.

During this time, Buchanan formed a lasting friendship with Julius Caesar Scaliger and later earned the admiration of Joseph Scaliger, who wrote an epigram about him. Buchanan's former pupil, Montaigne, acted in his tragedies and, in the essay *Of Presumption*, categorized Buchanan as one of the foremost Latin poets of his era, alongside Turnebus, Montdore, de L'Hopital, Béza, and Aurat.
