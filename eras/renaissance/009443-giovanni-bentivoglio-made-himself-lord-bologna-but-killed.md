# Giovanni Bentivoglio and the Governance of Bologna

The political history of Bologna is characterized by frequent shifts in sovereignty, alternating between independence, the rule of local lords, and the authority of the papacy. Among the figures who sought to control the city was Giovanni Bentivoglio, whose tenure as lord was marked by both ambition and instability.

## Rise and Fall of Giovanni Bentivoglio
In 1401, Giovanni Bentivoglio established himself as the lord of Bologna. His period of rule was brief, however, as he was killed during a rebellion in 1402. Following his death, the city returned to the control of the Visconti.

The Bentivoglio family eventually regained control of the city. After a series of struggles involving the papacy, the Bentivoglio secured Bologna again in 1438. They maintained their hold over the city until 1506, at which point Pope Julius II drove them out and returned Bologna to the sway of the papacy.

## Historical Context of Bologna's Governance
The instability of the Bentivoglio era was part of a broader pattern of shifting power in the city. Bologna was subject to the Lombards for a short time and remained generally under the rule of the exarchate of Ravenna until Pippin gave it to the papacy in 756. The city was sacked by Hungarians in 902, and while the exact date of its acquisition of freedom and its motto *Libertas* is uncertain, its first commune "constitution" dates to approximately 1123.

Between the 12th and 14th centuries, Bologna was frequently engaged in war, supporting the Guelph cause against the neighboring cities of Emilia and Romagna, as well as against Frederick II. In 1249, the Bolognese captured Enzio, the son of the emperor, and kept him imprisoned for the remainder of his life. Internal conflicts between Ghibellines and Guelphs eventually weakened the commune. This instability allowed Taddeo de' Pepoli to make himself master of the town in 1337. In 1350, Pepoli's son sold the city to Giovanni Visconti of Milan. Ten years later, the city was given to the papacy, though it soon revolted to recover its liberty before the rise of Giovanni Bentivoglio in 1401.

## Cultural and Artistic Influence
The Bentivoglio family's presence in Bologna coincided with significant artistic activity. Lorenzo Costa, a painter born in Ferrara who joined the Bolognese school, painted frescoes on the walls of the Bentivoglio chapel in San Giacomo Maggiore. His works there include the "Madonna and Child with the Bentivoglio family," completed in 1438.

## Later Papal and National Integration
Following the expulsion of the Bentivoglio in 1506, Bologna remained under the papacy, with only a few exceptions. These included the Napoleonic period from 1796 to 1815 and the revolutions of 1831 and 1821. This period of papal influence lasted until 1860, when Bologna became part of the kingdom of Italy.
