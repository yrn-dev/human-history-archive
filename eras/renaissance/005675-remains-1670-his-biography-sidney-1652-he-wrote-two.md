# Fulke Greville and the Works of Lord Brooke

## Literary Contributions and Publications
Fulke Greville is most recognized for his biography of Sidney. The work, titled *The Life of the Renowned Sr. Philip Sidney*, encompasses a broad scope, detailing the "true Interest of England" regarding foreign princes and the suppression of Spanish power as stated by Sidney. The text covers Sidney's death, his designs, counsels, and principal actions, while also providing a brief account of the policies and maxims utilized by Queen Elizabeth in her government.

In addition to this biography, Greville included autobiographical material within a treatise on government. He had expressed an intention to write a history of England during the Tudor period; however, this was not realized because Robert Cecil denied him access to the required state papers.

## The Works of Lord Brooke
Lord Brooke was a writer who addressed current political topics as well as theological and philosophical subjects. He is described by Milton as a friend of toleration. During his lifetime, Brooke published only four poems—including an elegy on Sidney that appeared in *The Phoenix Nest* in 1593—and the *Tragedy of Mustapha*. Posthumous publications of his work include a volume released in 1633 and a volume titled *Remains* published in 1670.

## Dramatic Works and Style
Brooke authored two tragedies based on the Senecan model: *Alaham* and *Mustapha*. He stated that these tragedies were not intended for the stage, and the critic Charles Lamb suggested they are more akin to political treatises.

*   ***Alaham***: Set in Ormuz, the play's prologue features the ghost of a former king of Ormuz who reveals a curse destined for a doomed family, establishing a gloomy tone that persists throughout the piece.
*   ***Mustapha***: The theme of this play is derived from Madeleine de Scudéry's *Ibrahim ou l'illustre Bassa* and focuses on the ambition of the sultana Rossa.

The plays utilize choruses that function as philosophical dissertations, though they often have a slight connection to the rest of the drama. In *Mustapha*, the third chorus is a dialogue between Eternity and Time, while the fifth chorus presents an invective against the evils of superstition, followed by a chorus of priests that fails to remove the impression of scepticism.

Charles Lamb characterized Brooke's writing as being "nine parts Machiavel and Tacitus, for one of Sophocles and Seneca," noting that both his love-poems and plays are "frozen and made rigid with intellect." Lamb further observed an obscurity of expression in Brooke's poetry, attributing this not to a lack of verbal lucidity, but to the subtlety and intensity of his thought.

## Family and Succession
Lord Brooke left no sons. Consequently, his barony was inherited by his cousin, Robert Greville (c. 1608-1643), who became the 2nd Lord Brooke. Robert Greville was an active member of the parliamentary party and participated in the Civil War, defeating Royalists at a skirmish in Kineton in August 1642. After seizing Lichfield, he was killed there on March 2, 1643. He had previously been imprisoned at York in 1639 by Charles I for refusing to take the oath to fight for the king.

The family lineage continued through descendants; in 1746, Francis Greville, the 8th baron (1710-1773), was created earl of Warwick, a title that remains within the family.
