# Richard Bancroft's Sermon at Paul's Cross

## The Sermon of February 1589
On February 9, 1589, Richard Bancroft delivered a sermon at Paul's Cross. The discourse was based on the biblical text of 1 John iv. 1. The substance of this sermon was a passionate attack directed at the Puritans. During the address, Bancroft caricatured the motives of the Puritans and described their proceedings and speeches. 

## Theological and Political Arguments
In the sermon, Bancroft denounced the exercise of the right of private judgment. He used the occasion to set forth the divine right of bishops. The language employed was so strong that one of the councillors to the queen considered the sermon to be a threat against the supremacy of the crown.

## Career of Richard Bancroft
Richard Bancroft (1544–1610) was the archbishop of Canterbury, born in Farnworth, Lancashire. He received his education at Cambridge, attending both Jesus College and Christ's College. He earned his B.A. in 1667 and his M.A. in 1670. Following his ordination, he served as chaplain to Richard Cox, the bishop of Ely, and was presented to the rectory of Teversham in Cambridgeshire in 1755. 

Bancroft's rise within the church was rapid due to his zeal as a champion of the church and his abilities. He became a preacher to the university in 1576 and was presented to the rectory of St Andrew's, Holborn, in 1584. He attained a B.D. in 1580 and a D.D. five years later. In 1585, he was appointed as the treasurer of St Paul's cathedral in London, and he became a member of the ecclesiastical commission in 1586. He also served as a canon of Westminster starting in 1587.

## Later Ecclesiastical Influence
Following his sermon at Paul's Cross, Bancroft was made a prebendary of St Paul's in 1590. He served as chaplain to Archbishop Whitgift and Lord Chancellor Hatton. In June 1597, he was consecrated as the bishop of London. Because Archbishop Whitgift suffered from incapacity for business and old age, Bancroft was virtually invested with the power of primate and took sole management of ecclesiastical affairs.

In this capacity, Bancroft directed proceedings against several individuals, including John Penry, Thomas Cartwright and his friends, and "Martin Mar-Prelate." In the case of John Penry, Bancroft caused "seditious writings" to be intercepted and delivered to the lord keeper. 

Beyond his ecclesiastical duties, Bancroft engaged in diplomacy. In 1600, he was part of an embassy to Embden intended to settle disputes between the Danes and the English, though this mission failed. Bancroft was present at the death of Queen Elizabeth.
