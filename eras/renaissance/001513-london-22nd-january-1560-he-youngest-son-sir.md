# Francis Bacon

## Early Life and Family
Francis Bacon, who held the titles of Viscount St Albans and Baron Verulam, was born on January 22, 1560/1, at York House in the Strand, London. He was the youngest son of Sir Nicholas Bacon. His mother, the second wife of Sir Nicholas, was the daughter of Sir Anthony Cooke, who had previously served as the tutor to Edward VI. Bacon's mother was a woman of significant culture, well-versed in the classical studies of her time, and a devoted adherent of the Puritan or Reformed Church.

Due to a health condition that remained extremely delicate throughout his life, Bacon likely received a large portion of his early education and instruction at home.

## Education and Academic Views
In April 1573, Bacon entered Trinity College, Cambridge. He resided there for three years alongside his brother, Anthony. During his time at the university, Bacon studied the various sciences as they were taught at the period. Through these studies, he reached the conclusion that both the results attained and the methods employed by the academic establishment were erroneous.

While Bacon maintained a reverence for Aristotle—despite appearing to have limited knowledge of him—he developed a disdain for the prevailing Aristotelian philosophy. He viewed this philosophy as fruitless and useful only for the purpose of disputation, believing that the goals it set for itself were mistaken. Bacon concluded that philosophy required a new method and a redefined true purpose. He left the university with the initial concepts of this new approach in his mind.

## Legacy and Career
Francis Bacon is remembered as an English essayist, statesman, and philosopher. His life spanned from 1561 to 1626.
