# George Buchanan

## Early Career and Academic Appointments
George Buchanan returned to Scotland in 1560 or 1561. By April 1562, he was installed as the tutor to the young Queen Mary, with whom he read Livy on a daily basis. During this period, Buchanan openly joined the Reformed Church, also known as the Protestant Church. In 1566, he was appointed as the principal of St Leonard's College in St Andrews by the earl of Murray. 

Buchanan's financial situation was bolstered two years prior to this appointment when the queen granted him the revenues of Crossraguel Abbey. His reputation for administrative capacity and learning grew steadily, leading to his appointment as moderator of the general assembly in 1567, despite the fact that he was a layman. He had been a participant in the assemblies since 1563.

## Political and Royal Service
Buchanan accompanied the regent Murray on a trip to England. During this time, his work titled *Detectio*, which was published in 1572, was presented to the commissioners at Westminster. 

Following the assassination of Murray in 1570, Buchanan was appointed as one of the preceptors for the young king, James VI. It was through Buchanan's tuition that James VI acquired his scholarship. While serving as the royal tutor, Buchanan held several other significant offices. He served briefly as the director of chancery and subsequently became the lord privy seal. This latter position granted him a seat in the parliament, an office he held for several years, continuing at least until 1579.

## Death
George Buchanan died on September 28, 1582.
