# Doubling by the Third Hand

In the context of card games, specifically regarding no-trump declarations, doubling by the third hand is universally understood to mean that the player who performs the action possesses a very strong suit which they are capable of establishing.

## Strategic Requirements for Doubling
The strength required to double varies depending on the conventions being employed by the players. Under the short suit convention, a player should not double unless they possess significant strength; for example, holding six cards of a suit headed by the ace, king, and queen provides about an even chance that the suit will be selected. 

Conversely, under the heart convention, such extreme strength is not necessary. A player may double with a strong suit of six hearts and good cards of re-entry, as enough tricks can be saved to compensate for the doubled value of the hand. Because these conventions differ, it is recommended that a player ascertain which convention is being followed before the game begins.

## Partner Responses and Conventions
When a player doubles, their partner responds based on the established conventions to identify the doubler's strongest suit:
* **Short Suit Convention:** The partner leads either a heart or their own shortest suit, as this is considered the most likely candidate for the third player's strongest suit.
* **Heart Convention:** The partner's response is guided by the specific requirements of the heart-based strategy.

## General Doubling Principles for Leaders
While doubling by the third hand has a specific universal meaning, the leader's approach to doubling a no-trump declaration is different. A leader typically doubles only when they hold a suit of seven cards that is probably established, or a suit that can be established with the loss of one trick, provided they also have good cards of re-entry. 

Specific hand compositions that constitute a sound double for the leader include:
* Seven cards of a suit that includes the ace, king, and queen, even if no other cards of value are held.
* Six cards of a suit including the king, queen, and knave, provided the player also holds two aces in other suits.
