## Attribution and Authorship
*Mary Magdalen's Lamentations* (1604) and *The Passion of a Discontented Mind* (1601) are literary works that have been associated with the English poet Nicholas Breton. However, archive records indicate that these attributions are erroneous.

## Nicholas Breton
Nicholas Breton (1545?–1626) was an English poet from an old family based at Layer-Breton, Essex. The son of William Breton and Elizabeth Bacon, he was likely born in the parish of St Giles without Cripplegate. While there is no official university record, accounts from the Rev. Richard Madox suggest he was once a member of Oriel College and was present in Antwerp in 1583. 

Breton was a popular writer among his contemporaries, though he was forgotten by the subsequent generation. His body of work consists of satires, prose tracts, and pastoral and religious poems. His religious poetry is characterized by a certain sweetness and fluency, reflecting a devout and earnest mind. Although his references to Mary Magdalene and his praise of the Virgin led some to suggest he was a Catholic, his prose writings provide abundant proof that he was an ardent Protestant.
