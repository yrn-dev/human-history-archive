# Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde was a learned physician and author who produced several works concerning medicine, domestic hygiene, and geography during the 16th century.

## Literary Works and Contributions
Boorde is the author of *The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge* (c. 1547), which is described as his most entertaining work. This text serves as a guide to teach a man how to speak parts of various languages and understand the usage and fashions of different countries. Additionally, it provides information on the majority of coins of money current in every region. In this work, Boorde uses lively verse to describe the Englishman, noting specific foibles such as obstinacy, fickleness, and a fondness for new fashions. The book further includes geographical descriptions of countries and a model dialogue written in the Cornish language, following a similar pattern for each country discussed.

Boorde also wrote on health and medicine. His authentic works in this field include *The Brevyary of Health* (1547?) and *Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor* (Thomas Colwell, 1562), though the latter is known to have earlier undated editions.

## Astronomy and Other Writings
In the field of astronomy, Boorde authored *The Princyples of Astronamy* (1547?). He also produced *A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV*. 

Other writings attributed to Boorde include "The Peregrination of Doctor Board," which was printed by Thomas Hearne in *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, vol. ii. (1735). Two of his works, the *Treatyse upon Berdes* and the *Itinerary of Europe*, have been lost.

## Attributed Works
Several jest-books are attributed to Boorde, although these attributions lack authority. These include:
* *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam* (the earliest extant edition of which dates to 1630).
* *Scogin's Jests* (1626).
* *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton, with his wyfe, and his daughter, and of two poore scholers of Cambridge* (printed by Wynkyn de Worde).
* A Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*.
