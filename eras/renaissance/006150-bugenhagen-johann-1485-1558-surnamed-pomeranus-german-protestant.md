# BUGENHAGEN, JOHANN (1485-1558)

Johann Bugenhagen, also known by the surname Pomeranus, was a German Protestant reformer who played a significant role in the organization of the Reformation across several regions of Northern Europe.

## Early Life and Academic Career
Born on June 24, 1485, at Wollin near Stettin, Bugenhagen began his intellectual development at the university of Greifswald, where he achieved distinction as a humanist. His early professional career was marked by appointments in education and the church. In 1504, the abbot of the Praemonstratensian monastery at Belbuck appointed him as the rector of the town school at Treptow. By 1509, he had been ordained as a priest and served as a vicar in the collegiate *Marienkirche* at Treptow. Later, in 1517, he was appointed to the abbey school at Belbuck as a lecturer on the Church Fathers and the Bible.

## Conversion and Association with Luther
Bugenhagen's transition to the Reformation occurred in 1520 after reading Martin Luther's *De Captivitate Babylonica*. This work converted him into a zealous supporter of Luther's views, and Bugenhagen subsequently won over the abbot and others to these perspectives. In 1521, he traveled to Wittenberg, where he developed a close friendship with both Melanchthon and Luther. 

While at the university in Wittenberg, Bugenhagen served as a lecturer and preacher. He married in 1522. His influence extended beyond the university due to his organizing skills and zeal for reform. He is also remembered for providing assistance to Luther in the translation of the Bible.

## Ecclesiastical Organization
Bugenhagen was instrumental in arranging church affairs in various cities and regions. In 1528, he organized the church affairs of Hamburg and Brunswick. This work continued in 1530, when he arranged the affairs of Pomerania and Lübeck.

In 1537, Bugenhagen was invited to Denmark by Christian III. He spent five years in Denmark, where he organized the schools and the church. Despite holding the rank of only a presbyter, he performed the consecration of the new Danish bishops.

## Final Years and Literary Works
Bugenhagen spent the remainder of his life in Wittenberg. He remained in the city despite the perils of persecution and war, as he viewed Wittenberg as the home of the Reformation. He died on April 20, 1558.

Among his numerous written works, Bugenhagen authored a history of Pomerania, though this remained unpublished until 1728. His *Interpretatio in Librum Psalmorum*, published in 1523, is considered perhaps his best book.
