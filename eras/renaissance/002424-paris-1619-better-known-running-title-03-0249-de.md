# Paris, 1619

The year 1619 in Paris was marked by significant activity in the fields of historical scholarship, literature, and the publication of academic works.

## Historical and Biographical Publications
Several notable texts were published in Paris during 1619. Among these was the *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus*, written by John Pits (also known as Pitseus), an English Catholic exile who lived from 1560 to 1616. This work, which is the fourth book of a more extensive project, is more commonly recognized by its running title, *De illustribus Angliae scriptoribus*. Pits based this work on the writings of Bale, though he omitted the Protestant and Wycliffite divines that Bale had included. The most valuable portion of Pits' work focuses on the lives of Catholic exiles residing in French towns, including Douai. In his *Nota de Joanne Bale*, Pits claimed that Bale's *Catalogus* misrepresented the material of Leland, although evidence suggests Pits may have only known Leland's work second-hand via Bale.

Additionally, the French geographer and historian Andre du Chesne (1584–1640) published two significant works in Paris in 1619. The first was *Histoire des Papes jusqu'a Paul V*, and the second was *Historiae Normanorum scriptores antiqui*, a folio volume that currently serves as the only source for certain texts. Du Chesne, who is generally regarded as the father of French history, was the royal geographer and historiographer under the patronage of Cardinal Richelieu.

Other publications from Paris in 1619 include *Histoire de la maison de Luxembourg*, authored by N. Vigner.

## Notable Births and Figures
Paris was the birthplace of Antoine Furetiere on December 28, 1619. Furetiere became a scholar and miscellaneous writer who initially practiced law as an advocate before taking orders and becoming the abbe of Chalivoy in the diocese of Bourges in 1662. He was admitted to the French Academy in 1662 following the success of his satires, including *Voyage de Mercure* (1653) and *Nouvelle Allegorique, ou histoire des derniers troubles arrives au royaume d'eloquence* (1658). 

Furetiere is well known for *Le Roman bourgeois* (1666), a work that ridiculed the fashionable romances of La Calprenede and Mlle de Scudery by presenting portraits and episodes drawn from life. He also authored the *Dictionnaire universel*, published posthumously in 1690. This dictionary led to a conflict with the French Academy, which claimed exclusive privilege over such a publication and accused Furetiere of purloining their stores. This resulted in his expulsion from the Academy in 1685, an event he later satirized in *Couches de l'academie* (1687).

## Social and Cultural Context
During the early 17th century, the social landscape of France included specific leisure activities. Billiards, for instance, was played exclusively by the richer middle class and the aristocracy until the first part of the century. Subsequently, the privilege of maintaining billiard-rooms was granted to the *billardiers paulmiers*, and the game became the primary betting game in France until the era of Louis Philippe.
