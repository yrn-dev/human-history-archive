# Westminster Assembly

The Westminster Assembly was a gathering of clergy and officials during the mid-17th century. In 1643, the assembly included a delegation of five Scottish clergymen who were selected to attend. Among these five was Robert Baillie, a Scottish divine and professor of divinity at Glasgow.

## Robert Baillie
Robert Baillie (1602–1662) was born in Glasgow and graduated from the university there in 1620. Following his graduation, he dedicated himself to the study of divinity. He was ordained and served as a regent at the university for several years before being appointed to the living of Kilwinning in Ayrshire. 

Baillie's involvement in ecclesiastical and political affairs began significantly in 1638, when he served as a member of the Glasgow Assembly. Shortly thereafter, he acted as a preacher or chaplain, accompanying the Scottish army and Leslie. In 1642, he was appointed professor of divinity at Glasgow, and the following year he was chosen as one of the five Scottish clergymen sent to the Westminster Assembly.

Beyond his role in the assembly, Baillie was involved in diplomatic efforts. In 1649, he served as one of the commissioners sent to Holland to settle the terms of admission for Charles II to the government and to invite him to Scotland. Throughout his career, he remained active in the minor disputes of the church. In 1661, he became the principal of Glasgow University. He died in August 1662, a death likely accelerated by his mortification regarding the apparent firm establishment of episcopacy in Scotland. Baillie is remembered as a man of ability and learning whose views were not extreme; he is chiefly known today for his *Letters*, which provide a lively and historically important account of the period.

## Related Political Context
The period surrounding the Westminster Assembly was marked by significant political instability and opposition to the monarchy. For example, John Balmerino (d. 1649), a chief among the Covenanters and a trusted counsellor to the marquess of Argyll, was a sturdy opponent of Charles I in Scotland. Balmerino presided over the Edinburgh parliament in August 1641 and was among the Scottish commissioners who visited England in 1644.

Other figures of the era also navigated the tensions between the Commonwealth and royalist interests. Isaac Dorislaus (1595–1649), an Anglo-Dutch lawyer and diplomat, assisted in preparing the charge of high treason against Charles I. While negotiating an alliance between the Dutch Republic and the Commonwealth, Dorislaus was murdered by royalist refugees at the Hague on May 10, 1649.
