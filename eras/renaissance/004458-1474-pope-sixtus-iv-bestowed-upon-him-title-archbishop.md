# Pope Sixtus IV

Pope Sixtus IV was a pontiff of the Catholic Church whose tenure in the late 15th century was marked by ecclesiastical appointments, efforts toward scientific reform, and political conflicts with European powers.

## Ecclesiastical Appointments and Canonizations
During his papacy, Sixtus IV exercised his authority through the granting of titles and the formal recognition of church figures. In 1474, he bestowed the title of archbishop of Caesarea upon Thomas Basin. Basin was a historian and former bishop of Lisieux who had renounced his bishopric after falling into disfavour with the king of France and spending time in exile.

Additionally, Sixtus IV was responsible for the formal canonization of Bonaventura in 1482. Bonaventura was a theologian regarded by his contemporaries as the "Doctor Seraphicus" and was later ranked as one of the great doctors of the church.

## Calendar Reform
In 1474, Pope Sixtus IV initiated a project to reconstruct the church calendar. This effort was prompted by the realization that the ancient calendar was based on two erroneous suppositions: that a year contains 365¼ days and that nineteen solar years are exactly equal to 235 lunations. These errors caused the calendar to lose its correspondence with the seasons and the accuracy of the new moons.

To address this, Sixtus IV invited Regiomontanus, who was the most celebrated astronomer of that era, to Rome to oversee the reconstruction. However, the project was suspended following the premature death of Regiomontanus. The reformation of the calendar was not completed until the following century under Pope Gregory XIII, who issued a brief in March 1582 establishing the Gregorian Calendar (or "New Style").

## Political Relations and Conflicts
The papacy of Sixtus IV was characterized by significant tension with the French monarchy and the Medici family. Sixtus IV was an enemy of both the Medici and the king of France. 

These tensions escalated in 1478 following the Pazzi conspiracy, during which Sixtus IV threatened Florence. In response, King Louis of France aided Lorenzo dei Medici in forming an alliance with Naples. This alliance exerted enough pressure on the papacy to force Sixtus IV to come to terms.
