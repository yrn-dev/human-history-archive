# Leo of Rozmital

## Political Influence in Bohemia
Zdenek Leo, lord of Rozmital, was a prominent member of the great Bohemian nobility and served as the supreme burgrave. During the early part of the nominal reign of King Louis, Leo of Rozmital ruled the country almost without control. This period of significant influence occurred while King Louis was young; although Louis had been crowned king of Bohemia at the age of three, he did not arrive in Bohemia from Hungary until 1522.

## Dismissal from Office
The period of Leo of Rozmital's dominance ended upon the arrival of King Louis in Prague in 1522. Upon his arrival, the King dismissed all Bohemian state officials, which included the powerful Leo of Rozmital. Following this dismissal, the King appointed John of Wartenberg as burgrave and named Charles of Munsterberg—a grandson of King George and cousin of Prince Bartholomew—as the regent of Bohemia to serve during the King's absences.

## Candidacy for the Bohemian Throne
Following the death of King Louis, Bohemia entered a state of great disorder and near anarchy. This instability was attributed to the fact that the two most recent kings had primarily resided in Hungary, and the Bohemians had failed to establish a strong indigenous government to replace the absentee monarchs, despite the temporary agreement reached at the diet of St Wenceslas in 1517.

In the ensuing struggle for succession, several candidates laid claim to the Bohemian throne. These included King Sigismund of Poland, the dukes William and Louis of Bavaria, various German princes, and several Bohemian noblemen. Among the Bohemian candidates, Leo of Rozmital was the most important.

## The Election of 1526
To resolve the succession, the diet decided to entrust the election of the new king to twenty-four members. These electors were chosen in equal numbers from the three estates. On October 23, 1526, the electors chose Ferdinand of Habsburg as their king. Ferdinand laid claim to the throne as the husband of Anna, the daughter of King Vladislav. This election marked the permanent accession of the Habsburg dynasty to the Bohemian throne.
