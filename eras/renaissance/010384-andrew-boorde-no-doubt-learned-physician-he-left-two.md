# Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde (also spelled Borde), an English author and physician, lived from approximately 1490 to 1549. He was born in Holms Dale, Sussex, at Boord's Hill.

## Education and Early Life
Boorde was educated at Oxford. While still under age, he was admitted as a member of the Carthusian order. In 1521, he received a dispensation from religion to serve as the suffragan bishop of Chichester, although he never actually filled this office. By 1529, he was freed from his monastic vows, stating that he was unable to endure the "rugorosite off your relygyon."

## Medical Studies and Travels
Following his release from monastic vows, Boorde traveled abroad to study medicine. Upon his return to England, he was summoned to attend the duke of Norfolk. His academic pursuits led him to visit several universities, including Wittenberg, Toulouse, Poitiers, Orleans, and Montpellier. Additionally, he observed surgical practices in Rome and joined a pilgrimage to Compostella in Navarre.

In 1534, Boorde was at the Charterhouse in London. By 1536, he wrote to Thomas Cromwell to complain that he was in "thraldom" there. After Cromwell set him at liberty and entertained him at his home in Bishops Waltham, Hampshire, Boorde appears to have been entrusted with a mission to investigate public sentiment abroad regarding the English king. During this time, he wrote to Cromwell from various locations; while in Catalonia, he sent Cromwell rhubarb seeds, two hundred years before the plant became generally cultivated in England.

In 1536, Boorde studied medicine at Glasgow, where he recorded observations on the Scots, noting the "devellyshe dysposicion of a Scottysh man, not to love nor favour an Englishe man." Around 1538, he embarked on his most extensive journey, traveling to Jerusalem and visiting nearly every European country except Turkey and Russia. He wrote a full itinerary of these travels, which was sent to Cromwell but subsequently lost. Boorde eventually settled in Montpellier. He likely returned to England in 1542, residing in Pevensey and Winchester.

## Literary Works
Boorde is described as a learned physician who produced several works. Before 1542, while in Montpellier, he completed his *Dietary*, his *Brevyary*, and *The Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*. The latter is considered the earliest continental guide book and was dedicated to Lady Mary, the daughter of King Henry VIII (c. 1547). This work teaches the reader about the usage and fashion of various countries, parts of different languages, and the various coins of money current in every region. In this book, Boorde uses lively verse to describe the foibles of the Englishman, including his obstinacy, fondness for new fashions, and fickleness. The text includes geographical descriptions of countries and a model dialogue in the Cornish language.

His other authentic works include:
*   *Here foloweth a Compendyous Regimente or Dyetary of health, made in Mountpyllor* (published by Thomas Colwell in 1562, though earlier undated editions exist).
*   *The Brevyary of Health* (c. 1547).
*   *The Princyples of Astronamy* (c. 1547).
*   *A Pronostycacyon or an Almanacke for the yere of our lorde MCCCCCXLV*.
*   "The Peregrination of Doctor Board," printed in 1735 in *Benedictus Abbas Petroburgensis*, vol. ii.

Two of his works, the *Treatyse upon Berdes* and his *Itinerary of Europe*, are lost. Several jest-books are attributed to him without authority, including *Scogin's Jests* (1626), *The Merie Tales of the Mad Men of Gotam* (earliest extant edition 1630), a Latin poem titled *Nos Vagabunduli*, and *A mery jest of the Mylner of Abyngton, with his wyfe, and his daughter, and of two poore scholers of Cambridge* (printed by Wynkyn de Worde).
