# Richard Barnfield and the Publication of Cynthia

## Biography of Richard Barnfield
Richard Barnfield (1574–1627) was an English poet born in Norbury, Staffordshire. He was the son of Maria Skrymsher and Richard Barnfield (or Barnefield), who married in April 1572. Barnfield was baptized on June 13, 1574. Following the death of his mother in early 1581 during the birth of a daughter, his care and that of his siblings were seemingly managed by his mother's unmarried sister, Elizabeth Skrymsher.

Barnfield's academic career began in November 1589 when he matriculated at Brasenose College, Oxford. He received his degree in February 1592 and completed the exercise required for his master's gown, though he left the university abruptly without obtaining his M.A. It is conjectured that he arrived in London in 1593, where he likely became acquainted with Spenser, Drayton, and Watson. Additionally, the death of Sir Philip Sidney, which occurred while Barnfield was a school-boy, is believed to have inspired some of his earliest poetic works and strongly affected his imagination.

## The Affectionate Shepherd
In November 1594, at the age of twenty-one, Barnfield published his first work, *The Affectionate Shepherd*. This work was published anonymously and dedicated with familiar devotion to Penelope, Lady Rich. Described as a florid romance written in two books of six-line stanza, the poem follows the manner of Shakespeare and Lodge and focuses on the complaint of Daphnis for the love of Ganymede. Barnfield later admitted that the work was a paraphrase or expansion of Virgil's second eclogue.

While the poem was successful, it faced censure from a moral perspective. It is noted as an extraordinary example of the licence introduced to England from Italy during the Renaissance. The work is characterized by its "coloured touches of nature," fancy, and epithets. Barnfield is credited with a level of picturesqueness that surpassed immediate imitators of *Venus and Adonis*, specifically in his descriptions of "speckled flower call'd sops-in-wine," "fine ruff-footed doves," and his desire to catch the "long-bill'd woodcock" by the "bright glimmering of the starry light."

## Cynthia, with certain Sonnets
Two months after his first publication, in January 1595, Barnfield published his second volume, *Cynthia, with certain Sonnets*. Unlike his previous work, Barnfield signed the preface of this volume, dedicating it to William Stanley, the new earl of Derby, in terms that suggest a close personal relationship.

The volume contains two primary components:
*   **Cynthia**: A panegyric dedicated to Queen Elizabeth. It is written in the Spenserian stanza and is likely the earliest extant example of this form outside of *The Faerie Queene*.
*   **Sonnets**: A sequence of twenty sonnets. These poems are of unusual poetic merit, though their subject matter is described as preposterous. They celebrate the charms of a young man with the initials J. V. or J. U., about whom nothing else is known.

These sonnets are of significant historical interest because they preceded the publication of Shakespeare's sonnets by fourteen years, yet they are closer to Shakespeare's manner than any other Elizabethan sonnets. They also preceded Spenser's *Amoretti*. The work shows the influence of Drayton's *Idea*, which had been published a few months prior and also contained amatory sonnets addressed to a young man.
