# Charles V and the Colonization Efforts of Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon

## The Charter of Charles V
In 1523, the Spanish adventurer and colonizer Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon obtained a charter from Charles V. This document was described as rather indefinite in nature, but it provided Ayllon with the legal right to plant colonies. This grant followed a favorable report regarding the North American mainland provided by Francisco Gordillo, whom Ayllon had sent on an exploring expedition in 1521. Gordillo's mission had touched the coast of the Florida peninsula and continued northward for some distance.

## The Expedition and Settlement of San Miguel
Following the acquisition of the charter, Ayllon conducted another reconnoitring expedition in 1525. In early 1526, he launched a colonization effort, departing with approximately 100 African slaves and 500 colonists. During the voyage, the expedition stopped at several coastal locations. At one of these stops, the group remained long enough to build a new ship to replace one that had been wrecked; this event is considered the first instance of shipbuilding on the North American continent.

Sailing north to approximately latitude 33deg 40', Ayllon began constructing a town named San Miguel. The precise location of San Miguel remains a subject of dispute among writers. Some suggest it was built on the site where Jamestown, Virginia, was later established, while others, such as Lowery, contend it was located near the mouth of the Pedee river.

## Labor and Demise of the Colony
The settlement of San Miguel marked the first instance of the employment of negro slaves in the region that would later become the United States. However, the colony faced severe hardships due to the unhealthy nature of the site. Fever killed many of the colonists, including Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon, who died on October 18, 1526.

The death of Ayllon led to the collapse of the settlement. Dissensions broke out among the remaining colonists, and some of the slaves rebelled and escaped into the forest. By December, the town of San Miguel was abandoned. The remaining colonists embarked for Hispaniola, though fewer than 150 of them arrived safely.
