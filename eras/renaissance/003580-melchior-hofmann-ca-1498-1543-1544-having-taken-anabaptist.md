# Melchior Hofmann

Melchior Hofmann (ca. 1498–1543 or 1544) was a figure associated with the Anabaptist movement during the 16th century. His activities contributed to the spread of Anabaptist teachings in Northern Europe and preceded a period of significant volatility within the movement.

## Early Activities and Travels
In 1524, Hofmann participated in a business journey to Stockholm. He was accompanied by Melchior Rinck and Bernt Knipperdolling (also referred to as Knipperdollinck), a prosperous cloth-merchant from Münster. This expedition, while originating as a business venture, developed into a religious errand that ultimately proved abortive.

## Influence on the Anabaptist Movement
Hofmann is credited with taking Anabaptist teaching to Holland. This expansion of the movement's reach led to the emergence of Jan Matthisson (also known as Matthys or Matthyszoon) in Haarlem. Matthisson became a preacher of vengeance who claimed to prophesy the establishment of the kingdom of heaven and a speedy end to the world. 

Matthisson's influence grew as he obtained many adherents, eventually dispatching de Kniper and Boekebinder to the city of Münster to realize his ideals. This sequence of events linked Hofmann's initial dissemination of teachings in Holland to the subsequent radicalization and violence in Münster.

## The Anabaptist Context and the Münster Crisis
The broader Anabaptist movement of this era was characterized by a lack of a common confession or a homogeneous community, largely due to persecution. Despite this fragmentation, certain doctrines were generally held, including:
* The practice of the baptism of believers only.
* The rejection of the Lutheran doctrine of justification by faith, which Anabaptists viewed as onesided.
* The simple practice of the breaking of bread, intended as a remembrance of the death of Christ and a means for brothers and sisters in Christ to renew the bond of brotherly love as the foundation of neighbourly life.

While some leaders preached non-resistance, the persecution of the movement deprived Anabaptists of such noble leaders and provoked others toward vengeance. This culminated in the events at Münster, where Anabaptists seized the city in 1532. By 1533, all residents who did not embrace Anabaptism were driven from the town. Bernt Knipperdolling became burgomaster in February 1534. 

The city subsequently became a scene of cruelty and "grossest licence" under the leadership of John of Leiden (Johann Bockelson), who took the place of Jan Matthisson after the latter was killed in early 1534. The Anabaptist rule in Münster finally fell in 1535 when the city was taken by the besieging bishop.
