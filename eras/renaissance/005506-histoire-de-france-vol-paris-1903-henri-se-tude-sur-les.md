# History of France and Regional Studies

The study of French history is documented through a vast array of specialized literature, ranging from comprehensive national histories to detailed regional analyses focusing on provinces such as Brittany, Burgundy, and the Dauphiné.

## National and General Histories
Broad accounts of the nation's development are represented by the works of Ernest Lavisse, specifically his *Histoire de France*, with volume i published in Paris in 1903 and subsequent volumes appearing as late as 1905. Other significant contributions to the understanding of French history include the *Memoires relatifs a l'histoire de France* (tome x), edited by J.F. Michaud and J.J.F. Poujoulat in 1850. For researchers of the 16th century, Henri Hauser provided a bibliography of printed sources in *Les Sources de l'histoire de France, XVI^e siècle*, vol. 1 (1906).

## Regional Histories and Administration

### Brittany
The history of Brittany is extensively documented across several centuries. Early works include Bertrand d'Argentré's *Histoire de Bretagne* (1586), followed by Dom Lobineau (1702) and Dom Morice (1742-1756). More recent scholarly efforts include Arthur de la Borderie's *Histoire de Bretagne* (1896 seq.) and A. Dupuy's research on the union of Brittany with France (1880), as well as Dupuy's studies on 18th-century municipal administration in the region (1891).

Social and cultural studies of Brittany include Henri Sée's examinations of rural classes, covering the Middle Ages (1896) and the period from the 16th century to the Revolution (1906). Other works focus on specific events, such as J. Lemoine's account of the 1675 "bonnets rouges" or "papier timbré" revolt, and studies on the Duc d'Aiguillon by M. Marion (1898) and B. Pocquet (1900-1902). Cultural and folkloric aspects are captured in the works of F. M. Luzel and Anatole le Braz.

### Burgundy and the Dauphiné
The Duchy of Burgundy is detailed in the *Histoire générale et particulière de Bourgogne* by U. Plancher (1739-1781) and the *Description générale et particulière du duché de Bourgogne* by Courtépée (1774-1785). The Capetian and Valois dynasties of Burgundy are analyzed in works by E. Petit de Vausse (1885-1905) and B. de Barante (1833-1836).

The Dauphiné region is documented through J. J. Guiffrey's *Histoire de la reunion du Dauphine a la France* (1868) and J. M. De Valbonnais' *Histoire de Dauphine* (1722). Local histories include A. Prudhomme's *Histoire de Grenoble* (1888) and J. Roman's *Histoire de la ville de Gap* (1892). The revolutionary period in the region is covered by J. A. Felix Faure regarding the assemblies of Vizille and Romans (1887) and O. Chenavas regarding the Revolution of 1788 (1888).

## Political and Biographical Studies
French history is further detailed through the study of key political figures and conflicts. The 14th-century bourgeoisie and the government of Étienne Marcel are analyzed by F. T. Perrens (1860) and Hon. R. D. Denman (1898). The reign of Louis XIII is explored through the works of Le Vassor (1757), Griffet (1758), and B. Zeller's critical studies on the connétable de Luynes (1879).

Military history is preserved in the eye-witness accounts of François de Boivin, Baron de Villars, whose *Memoires sur les guerres demelees* (1607) details conflicts in Piedmont, Montferrat, and the Duchy of Milan, including the treaty of Cateau-Cambresis in 1559. Additionally, H. Lemonnier's *Les Guerres d'Italie* (1903) provides insight into France's Italian campaigns.

## Religious and Social History
The intersection of religion and state is a recurring theme in French historical literature. The works of Gregoire include studies on religious sects (1810), the liberties of the Gallican church (1818), the marriage of priests in France (1826), and the influence of Christianity on the condition of women (1821). The religious history of the French Revolution is further examined by A. Gazier (1883, 1887).

Social history also encompasses the study of marginalized groups, such as the "races maudites" of France and Spain discussed by Francisque Michel (1846) and the study of "Parias" (Cagots and Bohémiens) by V. de Rochas (1877).
