# Anne Boleyn

## Early Life and Education
Anne Boleyn (born approximately 1507) was the daughter of Elizabeth, daughter of Thomas Howard (later duke of Norfolk), and Sir Thomas Boleyn, who later became the earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde. While Camden records her birth year as 1507, some later writers have ascribed her birth to 1501 or 1502, though this has not been conclusively proven.

In 1514, Anne traveled to France to accompany Mary Tudor for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France and served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I.

## Return to England and Suitors
Anne returned to England in 1521 or 1522. During this period, she attracted numerous suitors and admirers. Among her admirers was the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt. Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland, was among her suitors; however, the king intervened to stop a marriage between Anne and Percy, providing Sir James Butler as an alternative match. Despite this, Anne remained unmarried.

Between 1522 and 1525, Henry VIII bestowed various favors and grants upon Anne's father. Some have interpreted these actions as a symptom of the king's affections for Anne, though this is considered very doubtful.

## Relationship with Henry VIII
Anne's conduct was governed by motives of ambition; unlike her sister Mary, who became the king's mistress, Anne had no intention of filling that role and instead sought to become the queen. 

The precise start date of the relationship between Anne and Henry VIII is unknown. While some surmise it began as early as 1523, there is no evidence to prove that the passion of Henry existed prior to the proceedings for divorce initiated in May 1527. This uncertainty is due to the fact that the celebrated love letters associated with the relationship are undated.

## Rise to Prominence
Anne's name first became openly connected to the king as a potential wife—contingent upon the divorce of Catherine—in a letter dated August 16, 1527. This letter was written by Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, to Charles V. 

At the time of this correspondence, Wolsey was absent from England. Unlike Henry, Wolsey was not blinded by passion and opposed the alliance with Anne, instead negotiating a marriage with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII. Despite this opposition, Henry sent his secretary, William Knight, on a separate mission to Rome to secure the facilities necessary for his marriage to Anne.
