# Andrew Boorde

Andrew Boorde (also spelled Borde) was an English author and physician who lived from approximately 1490 to 1549. Born at Boord's Hill in Holms Dale, Sussex, he received his education at Oxford.

## Ecclesiastical Career and Dispensations
While still under age, Boorde was admitted as a member of the Carthusian order. In 1521, he was "dispensed from religion" for the purpose of acting as the suffragan bishop of Chichester, although he never actually filled that office. By 1529, he was freed from his monastic vows; Boorde attributed this decision to his inability to endure what he described as the "rugorosite off your relygyon."

Despite this release, Boorde's relationship with the monastic life remained complex. In 1534, he was again in London at the Charterhouse. By 1536, he wrote to Thomas Cromwell to complain that he was in "thraldom" there. Following this communication, Cromwell set him at liberty and entertained him at his home in Bishops Waltham, Hampshire. During this period, Boorde wrote two letters to the prior of the Charterhouse in 1535 and 1536, in which he argued anxiously for a complete release from his monastic vows.

## Medical Studies and European Travels
After leaving his monastic vows in 1529, Boorde traveled abroad to study medicine. Upon his return to England, he was summoned to attend the duke of Norfolk. His academic and professional pursuits led him to visit several universities, including those at Montpellier, Toulouse, Poitiers, Orleans, and Wittenberg. Additionally, he observed the practice of surgery in Rome and joined other English nationals on a pilgrimage to Compostella in Navarre.

In 1536, Boorde studied medicine at Glasgow. During his time there, he recorded observations regarding the Scots, noting the "devellyshe dysposicion of a Scottysh man, not to love nor favour an Englishe man."

Around 1538, Boorde embarked on his most extensive period of travel. He visited nearly every European country with the exception of Turkey and Russia, and he traveled as far as Jerusalem. He documented these journeys in a full itinerary, which he sent to Thomas Cromwell, though the document was unfortunately lost.

## Literary Works and Later Life
Boorde eventually settled in Montpellier. Before 1542, he completed three notable works: his *Dietary*, his *Brevyary*, and the *Fyrst Boke of the Introduction of Knowledge*. The latter is recognized as the earliest continental guide book.

Boorde likely returned to England in 1542, spending his final years living in Pevensey and Winchester.
