# The Bohemian Brethren and Their Literary Contributions

The Bohemian Brethren were a community whose indirect founder was Chelcicky. This group exerted a significant influence on Bohemian literature, particularly through their commitment to writing in the national language.

## Language and Theological Works
The community's historical and theological works were almost entirely written in the national language. This practice served to refine and polish the language significantly. Among the most notable achievements of the brotherhood was the *Bible of Kralice*, a famed translation of the Scriptures. This project was the joint effort of several divines within the brotherhood and was first printed in 1593 at Kralice in Moravia.

Brother Gregory, who was known as the patriarch of the brotherhood, produced a large volume of writings focused primarily on theological subjects. His most important works include the book *On good and evil priests* and the *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan*.

## Literary Activity and Controversy
Following the death of Brother Gregory in 1480, the community experienced a period of discord. This internal conflict resulted in a surge of literary activity, specifically the production of numerous controversial works. These were authored by members of the community including Augusta (a bishop), as well as Brothers Jaffet, Blahoslav, and Lucas. Additionally, the young priest Jan Bilek wrote an account regarding the captivity of Bishop Augusta.

## Historical Writings and Loss of Records
Members of the brotherhood produced numerous historical works, though the majority of these were destroyed in the 17th century during a period when nearly all anti-Roman books in Bohemia were perished. Only fragments of certain works survived this destruction, such as Blahoslav's *History of the Unity* (a history of the brotherhood). Another historian of the community, Wenceslas Brezan, wrote a *History of the House of Rosenberg*, but only the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg from this work have been preserved.

## John Amos Komensky (Comenius)
The most prominent writer associated with the brotherhood was John Amos Komensky, also known as Comenius (1592-1670). He authored 142 works, many of which—including numerous educational and devotional writings—were written in his native tongue. His most important work in the native language is the *Labyrinth of the World*, an allegorical tale that is perhaps the most famous work written in Bohemian.
