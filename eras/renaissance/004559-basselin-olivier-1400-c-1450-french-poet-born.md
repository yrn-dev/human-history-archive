# OLIVIER BASSELIN

## Life and Occupation
Olivier Basselin (c. 1400–c. 1450) was a French poet born in Normandy, specifically in the Val-de-Vire, around the end of the 14th century. By occupation, Basselin was a fuller; tradition continues to identify the location of the mill he used for this work.

## Literary Work and the Vaux-de-Vire
Basselin is associated with drinking songs that achieved fame under the name of Vaux-de-Vire. In modern times, this term has been corrupted into the word "vaudeville." 

The authenticity of the works attributed to Basselin has been a subject of scholarly debate. At the start of the 17th century, a Norman lawyer named Jean Le Houx published a collection of songs claiming they were the work of Olivier Basselin. However, there is very little doubt that Le Houx was actually the author of these songs, as well as the ones he credited to himself. 

Conversely, it has been suggested that Basselin's name can be safely linked to certain songs published at Caen in 1866 by M. Armand Gaste, which are preserved in the *Bibliotheque Nationale* at Paris. This dispute regarding the authorship of the songs and the relationship between Basselin and Le Houx is discussed in the 1897 work by M. V. Patard titled *La Verite dans la question Olivier Basselin et Jean le Houx a propos du Vau-de-Vire*. Additionally, the *Vaux-de-Vire* was edited by A. Gaste in 1875 and subsequently translated by J. P. Muirhead in 1885.

## Death
According to various traditions, Basselin was killed during the English wars around the middle of the 15th century. It is possible that he died at the battle of Formigny in 1450.
