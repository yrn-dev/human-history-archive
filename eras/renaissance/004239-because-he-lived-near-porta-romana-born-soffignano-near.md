# Porta Romana and Related Sites

The term "Porta Romana" appears in the archive's records in several distinct contexts, referring to a specific gate in the town of Ascoli Piceno, a location in Rome associated with the Lupercalia, and a geographical marker for the residence of the painter Fra Bartolommeo.

## Ascoli Piceno
In the town of Ascoli Piceno, an episcopal see and the capital of the province of Ascoli Piceno in the Marches of Italy, the Porta Romana is a Roman gate featuring a double-arched design. This structure is adjacent to the remains of ancient city walls constructed from rectangular stone blocks measuring 2 feet in height. Evidence of fortifications that predate these walls has also been discovered at this location.

## Roman Traditions and the Lupercalia
In the city of Rome, the Porta Romana served as a landmark on the western side of the Palatine hill. Located in front of this gate, near the Casa Romuli and the Ficus Ruminalis, was the cave of Lupercus. According to legend, this cave was the site where the she-wolf suckled the twins Romulus and Remus. A bronze wolf was placed within the cave in 296 B.C.

The cave was the center of the Lupercalia, an ancient pastoral festival possibly predating Rome, held in honor of Lupercus. The festival took place on February 15th and was managed by a corporation of priests known as the Luperci. The rites involved the sacrifice of a dog and goats, followed by a ritual where two young men had their foreheads touched with a bloody knife and the blood wiped away with wool dipped in milk. After a sacrificial feast, the Luperci ran in two bands around the walls of the old Palatine city, striking people with thongs cut from the skins of the sacrificed animals. These thongs, called *februa*, were believed to prevent sterility in women. The festival's purpose was to ensure the prosperity of the people, the increase of flocks, and the fruitfulness of the land. The cave of Lupercus was later rebuilt by Augustus, and the festival continued until A.D. 494, when Gelasius transformed it into the feast of the Purification.

## Fra Bartolommeo
The name "Porta Romana" is also associated with the Italian portrait and historical painter Bartolommeo di Pagholo (1475–1517), who was also known as Baccio Bella Porta. He acquired the name Bella Porta because he lived near the Porta Romana. 

Born in Soffignano, near Florence, Fra Bartolommeo began his artistic education under Cosimo Roselli before studying the works of Leonardo da Vinci. His early work is noted for its beauty and grace, specifically his fresco of the Last Judgment, created with the assistance of Mariotto Albertinelli. His life was significantly impacted by Savonarola; following Savonarola's cruel death, Bartolommeo entered a convent and abandoned his art for several years. 

Upon resuming his work, he formed a close friendship with Raphael. Through this relationship, Bartolommeo mastered the rules of perspective, while Raphael improved his handling of drapery and coloring. Later in life, Bartolommeo visited Rome, where he felt a sense of inferiority when viewing the works of Raphael and Michelangelo. He remained friends with Raphael, leaving two unfinished paintings for the younger artist to complete upon his departure from Rome. To counter criticisms that his figures were too small and draped, Bartolommeo painted the undraped figure of St Sebastian and the figure of St Mark, the latter of which is considered his masterpiece.
