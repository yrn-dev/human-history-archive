# The Liberation of the Earl of Bristol (March 1628)

The events of March 1628 regarding the liberation of the Earl of Bristol represent a significant confrontation between the English peerage and the authority of King Charles I. This resolution followed years of political conflict, accusations of treason, and the imprisonment of the Earl.

## Background and Conflict with Buckingham
The Earl of Bristol's difficulties began during his diplomatic efforts in Spain. While he urged a marriage treaty that he believed would provide favorable conditions for Frederick, the negotiations were eventually wrecked in March 1623 by the arrival of Charles and Buckingham. Bristol subsequently incurred the resentment of these men—driven largely by the "implacable jealousy" of Buckingham—after he wrote a letter to King James informing him that Spanish ministers found Buckingham unpopular. Bristol also attempted to maintain peace with Spain after the departure of Charles and Buckingham.

By March 1624, upon his return to Dover, Bristol found that Charles and Buckingham held complete ascendancy over the king. He was forbidden from appearing at court and ordered to confine himself at Sherborne. Buckingham required him to answer a series of interrogatories, but Bristol refused to inculpate himself and instead demanded a trial by parliament.

## Legal Battles and Imprisonment
Following the death of James, Charles I removed Bristol from the privy council and ordered him to absent himself from the king's first parliament. In January 1626, Charles angrily refused Bristol's request to be present at the coronation, accusing him of attempting to pervert his religion while in Spain.

In March 1626, after the second parliament assembled, Bristol applied to the Lords, who supported his rights. Although the Lord Keeper Coventry sent a letter requesting that he not use the writ of summons sent by the king, Bristol took his seat and demanded justice against Buckingham. The king attempted to obstruct this by having the attorney-general bring Bristol to the bar on May 1st on an accusation of high treason. The Lords ordered that the charges against Buckingham and the charges against Bristol be investigated simultaneously.

These proceedings were halted on June 15th due to the dissolution of parliament. Subsequently, Charles ordered a prosecution in the Star Chamber, and Bristol was sent to the Tower.

## Liberation in March 1628
The Earl of Bristol remained imprisoned in the Tower until March 17, 1628. His release coincided with the assembling of the third parliament of Charles I. During this assembly, the peers insisted on Bristol's liberation and the restoration of his seat in the House of Lords.

## Context of the Period
The year 1628 was marked by other significant political developments in England, including the English Petition of Right. This period of tension between the monarchy and the legislative bodies continued into the following years; by 1629, Charles I began a period of eleven years of rule without a parliament.
