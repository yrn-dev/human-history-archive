# Annotationes in XXIV. libros Pandectarum

The *Annotationes in XXIV. libros Pandectarum* is a scholarly work authored by Budé and published in 1508. The text is noted for its significant influence on the study of Roman law, achieving this impact through the application of history and philology.

## Author and Literary Contributions
Budé was a scholar who wrote with facility in both Latin and Greek, though his Latin was characterized as being somewhat harsh and full of Greek constructions, making it inferior to his Greek. In addition to his work on the *Pandectarum*, he authored the *Commentarii linguae Graecae* in 1529. This latter work consisted of an extensive collection of lexicographical notes and contributed greatly to the study of Greek literature within France.

Budé maintained correspondence with the most learned men of his era, including Thomas More and Erasmus; the latter described Budé as the "marvel of France."

## Personal Life and Legacy
Budé's personal beliefs were a subject of suspicion. He requested to be buried at night, and following his death, his widow retired to Geneva, where she openly professed Protestantism. These factors led to suspicions that Budé held leanings toward Calvinism, although the author E. de Budé, in the work *Vie de G. Budé* (1884), refutes the notion that his ancestor held Protestant views.

During the massacre of St Bartholomew, members of Budé's family were forced to flee France. Some family members sought refuge in Switzerland, while others settled in Pomerania, where they adopted the name Buddeus or Budde.
