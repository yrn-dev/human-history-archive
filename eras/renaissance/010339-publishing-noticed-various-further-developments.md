# Publishing and the Book Trade

## The Low Countries as a Center of Bookselling
During the 16th and 17th centuries, the Low Countries emerged as the primary center of the global bookselling world. This era saw the production of high-quality folios and quartos associated with names such as Plantin, Blauw, and Jansen. These works frequently bore imprints from cities including Antwerp, Leiden, Utrecht, and Amsterdam. Additionally, the Elzevirs were known for producing "charming little pocket classics." During this same period, the southern towns of St Omer and Douai served as sources for polemical works written in English.

## Bibliographical Literature and Collection Trends
The literature surrounding book-collecting has evolved to reflect the changing ideals of collectors. Historically, general manuals provided the necessary information for "old-fashioned" or "omnivorous" collectors. Key works in this category include:
*   **Manuel du libraire**: First published in 1810 by Brunet, it reached a fifth edition between 1860 and 1864, with a supplement released from 1878 to 1880.
*   **Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature**: First published in 1834 by W.T. Lowndes and later revised by H.G. Bohn between 1857 and 1864.
*   **Tresor de livres rares et precieux**: Published in Dresden (1859-1867) with a supplementary volume in 1869 by J.G.T. Graesse, though it added little to the information already provided by Brunet.

In more recent times, these general manuals have been superseded by handbooks on special books and more detailed bibliographies. Despite the potential utility of new editions of Lowndes and Brunet for booksellers and librarians, no publisher has produced them.

## History of Engraving and Iconography
The history of engraving is categorized as a part of iconography. In England, W.Y. Ottley published an *Early History of Engraving* in two 4to volumes in 1816, intending to start a series of notices on engravers and their works. In France, Georges Duplessis, the keeper of prints in the national library, authored a *History of Engraving in France* (1888) and a manual titled *Les Merveilles de la gravure* (1871). Duplessis also published notices of engravers to accompany reproductions by M. Amand Durand.

Other significant works in the field include:
*   **Jansen**: Published a two-volume 8vo work in Paris in 1808 regarding the knowledge of prints of the 15th and 16th centuries and the origin of plate and wood engraving.
*   **General Works**: These include *Le Peintre-graveur* by Adam Bartsch (1803-1843) and J.D. Passavant (1860-1864), *Graphic Arts* by P.G. Hamerton (1882), *Essay on Prints* by William Gilpin (1781), *The Print Collector* by J. Maberly (1844), *Introduction to the Study and Collection of Ancient Prints* by W.H. Wiltshire (1874), and *Fine Prints* by F. Wedmore (1897).

The field of iconography has recently received an impetus from photographic processes, which have improved the facilities for reproducing engravings.

## Notable Authors and Publications
Various authors across different disciplines have contributed to the archive of published works:
*   **Francis Douce (1757-1834)**: An English antiquary who published *Illustrations of Shakespeare and Ancient Manners* (1807) and a *Dissertation on the various Designs of the Dance of Death* (1833). He also contributed papers to *The Gentleman's Magazine* and *Archaeologia*.
*   **Louvet**: Published a portion of his Memoirs in 1795 titled *Quelques notices pour l'histoire et le récit de mes périls depuis le 31 mai 1793*, written while in hiding. A complete edition of the *Mémoires de Louvet de Couvrai* was published in Paris in 1889, edited by F. A. Aulard.
*   **Bonnet**: His published works include *Traite d'insectologie* (1745), *Recherches sur l'usage des feuilles dans les plantes* (1754), and the anonymously published *Essai de psychologie* (1754). He also published *Essai analytique sur les facultes de l'ame* (Copenhagen, 1760), *Considerations sur les corps organises* (Amsterdam, 1762), *Contemplation de la nature* (Amsterdam, 1764-1765), and *Palingenesie philosophique* (Geneva, 1769-1770).
*   **Danish Poets**: Hans Christian Andersen published his first collection of *Fairy Tales* in 1835, followed by annual Christmas publications. Carl Christian Bagger published volumes in 1834 and 1836. C. L. Emil Aarestrup published erotic poetry in 1838. Frederik Paludan-Müller produced the satiric epos *Adam Homo* (1841-1848) and the tragedy *Kalanus*.
