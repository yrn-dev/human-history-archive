# Elias Ashmole

## Early Life and Career
Elias Ashmole was an English antiquarian born in Lichfield on May 23, 1617, to a father who was a saddler. He entered the legal profession in 1638, becoming a solicitor, and later served as a commissioner of excise in 1644. His professional duties brought him to Oxford during the period when the Royalist Parliament was sitting there. While at Oxford, Ashmole developed a friendship with Captain George Wharton (who later became Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole received a commission from the king to serve as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse.

In 1646, Ashmole was accepted as a Freemason, marking the first time a gentleman or amateur was initiated into the order. During this same year, while in London, he met William Lilly and other astrologers. Although Wharton and Lilly initially aroused his interest in astrology, this passion eventually subsided in favor of antiquarian research and heraldry.

## Domestic Life and Affluence
Ashmole's personal life was marked by two marriages. His first wife died in 1641. Eight years later, in 1649, he married Lady Mainwaring, a relative of his first wife who was approximately twenty years his senior. This marriage provided Ashmole with a level of affluence that allowed him to dedicate his full time to his preferred studies.

The marriage later faced instability; in 1657, Lady Mainwaring petitioned for a separation. However, she failed to win her case and subsequently returned to live with Ashmole. Following this domestic crisis and leading up to her death in 1668, Ashmole experienced a period of significant favor at court.

## Court Appointments and Later Life
During his time in favor at court, Ashmole held several successive positions, including:
* Windsor herald
* Comptroller, commissioner, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

In 1668, the year of his wife's death, Ashmole married the daughter of Sir William Dugdale. In doing so, he refused the office of Garter king-at-arms, allowing Sir William Dugdale to take the position instead.

## Contributions to Antiquarianism and Oxford
Ashmole is noted for his diligence and patient research in the field of antiquities. In 1672, he published *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*, a work described as practically exhaustive.

His most enduring legacy is the Ashmolean Museum, the first public museum of curiosities in the kingdom. The majority of the museum's collection was inherited by Ashmole from a friend named John Tradescant. Five years after the publication of his work on the Order of the Garter, Ashmole presented the collection to the university of Oxford. He stipulated that a suitable building must be constructed to house the collection, a condition that resulted in the collection not being finally installed until 1683. Following this, he made further gifts of his library to the university.
