# Experientia Literata

## Overview of Baconian Methodology
In the development of his inductive process, Francis Bacon utilized several specific tools and concepts to refine the acquisition of knowledge. Among these is *Experientia Literata*, which serves as evidence that Bacon was not ignorant of a deductive method or the use of hypotheses. While Bacon preferred his own method with deliberate consciousness, he did not entirely reject the syllogism; rather, he viewed it as incompetent for "the principal things" and more suited for use in mathematics.

## The Role of Induction and Axioms
Bacon maintained that induction was necessary in physics due to the "inconstancy of the physical body" and the "flux of matter," which allows for the formation of well-defined notions. Once the axioms concerning these notions have been established through induction, the syllogism may then be employed.

In the process of establishing axioms, Bacon emphasized the need to test whether an axiom is framed precisely to the measure of the particulars from which it was derived. If an axiom is "larger and wider," it must be observed whether it confirms this wideness by indicating new particulars. This serves as a collateral security to ensure the thinker does not "loosely grasp at shadows and abstract forms" or remain stuck in previously known information, but instead focuses on things that are "solid and realized in matter."

## Related Conceptual Tools
Alongside *Experientia Literata*, Bacon employed the *Vindemiatio* and specific hints regarding the testing of axioms to support his reasoning. His broader conception of *Prima Philosophia* serves as a framework for the generalizations of various sciences, though the content of this philosophy varies as those sciences progress.

## Final Causes and Physics
Bacon's approach to physics required the rigid exclusion of the consideration of final causes, as the knowledge of these causes does not lead to practical works. However, he noted that there is no inherent opposition between physical and final causes. In the ultimate resort, the mind is led to refer facts to Providence and God, viewing the universe as the work of reason. Furthermore, the idea of the final cause remains fruitful in sciences that deal with human action.
