# Frankfort-on-Main

Frankfort-on-Main is a German city located on the Main river. It has served as a center for intellectual work, technical innovation, and political administration throughout its history.

## Political Status and Governance
The political status of Frankfort-on-Main shifted significantly during the Napoleonic era and the subsequent reorganization of Germany. Following the imperial deputation of 1803, Frankfort was one of only six cities—alongside Hamburg, Lübeck, Bremen, Augsburg, and Nuremberg—permitted to maintain their *Reichsfreiheit*, allowing them to hold directly of the Empire.

This arrangement changed with the dissolution of the Empire in 1806, at which time Frankfort became the seat of a duchy for Karl Theodor von Dalberg. Dalberg, the elector and archbishop of Mainz, was appointed prince primate of the Confederation of the Rhine. By 1815, with the establishment of the German Confederation, Frankfort was recognized as a free city. However, it lost this independence and was annexed by Prussia following its role in the war of 1866.

## Intellectual and Literary Contributions
The city has been a residence for various scholars and historians. Hiob Ludolf (1624–1704), a German orientalist and expert in the Ethiopian language, retired to Frankfort-on-Main in 1678. After returning from a trip to England in 1684, he dedicated himself to literary work in the city until his death. In 1690, he was appointed president of the *collegium imperiale historicum*.

Johann Friedrich Bohmer (1795–1863), a German historian born in Frankfort on April 22, 1795, also spent much of his professional life there. Bohmer served as the librarian and archivist for the city and was the secretary of the *Gesellschaft fur altere deutsche Geschichtskunde*. His work focused on the collection and tabulation of imperial documents from the middle ages. Many of his major publications were produced in Frankfort, including the *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313* (1831), the *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Karolorum* (1833), and the *Codex diplomaticus Moeno-Francofurtanus. Urkundenbuch der Reichsstadt Frankfurt* (1836).

## Technical and Musical Innovation
Frankfort-on-Main appears in records as a site of technical publication and invention. In 1615, Solomon de Caus published *Les Raisons des forces mouvantes* in Frankfort. This work includes detailed technical diagrams of a large stationary barrel-organ operated by hydraulic power. The text provides specific problems (25, 28, 29, and 30) regarding the machine, including the arrangement of the bellows for wind supply and a section of the barrel showing pin tablature representing six bars of music.

Later, in the 19th century, the city was the site of musical instrument innovation. In 1836, a system for mechanical kettledrums, designed to allow a performer to tune the drum to a fixed note with a single movement, was patented in Frankfort-on-Main by Einbigler.

Frankfort-on-Main serves as a regional hub for transportation and trade. It is connected by a main railway line to the town of Biekkich, located in the Prussian province of Hesse-Nassau.
