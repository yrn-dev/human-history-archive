# Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus

The *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus* is a historical work published in Paris in 1619. It was authored by John Pits (also known as Pitseus), an English Catholic exile who lived from 1560 to 1616. The work is more commonly recognized by its running title, *De illustribus Angliae scriptoribus*.

## Relationship to the Work of Bale
The *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus* was founded upon the work of Bale. Specifically, the text serves as the fourth book of a more extensive project. 

While Pits utilized Bale's research, he made significant editorial changes to the content. Pits omitted the Protestant divines and the Wycliffite divines that had been mentioned in Bale's original writings. In a section titled *Nota de Joanne Bale*, Pits asserts that Bale's *Catalogus* was a misrepresentation of the material provided by Leland. However, evidence suggests that Pits may have only been acquainted with the work of Leland second-hand, having accessed it through Bale.

## Content and Value
The most valuable portion of the *Relationum historicarum de rebus anglicis tomus primus* consists of the lives of Catholic exiles. These individuals were resident in various French towns, including Douai.

## Background on Bale's Contributions
The foundations of Pits' work rely on the extensive collecting efforts of Bale. Bale's most significant contribution was the *Illustrium majoris Britanniae scriptorum, hoc est, Angliae, Cambriae, ac Scotiae Summarium...*, first published in 1548 and 1549 in Ipswich and Wesel. This was followed by a rewritten edition printed at Basel between 1557 and 1559 under the title *Scriptorum illustrium majoris Britanniae... Catalogus*.

Bale's chronological catalogue of British authors and their works was based partly on the *Commentarii* and *Collectanea* of John Leland. Bale was an indefatigable worker who personally examined many valuable libraries belonging to Carmelite and Augustinian houses before they were dissolved. 

Bale's own autograph notebook, preserved in the Bodleian Library's Selden Collection, reveals the raw materials he collected for his catalogues. This notebook is arranged alphabetically and lacks the personalities and ornaments found in his completed works, often citing the sources of his information. This notebook was later published as *Index Britanniae Scriptorum quos ... collegit Ioannes Baleus* in 1902.

Bale recorded the tragedy of the wholesale destruction of library monuments during his time. In Norwich, he noted that such monuments had been turned over to the use of worldly occupiers, including sopesellers, candelmakers, and grossers. Bale sought to save as many names of authors and titles of works as possible from Norfolk and Southfolke, though he noted he could not have covered the entire realm due to the charges involved. Despite inaccuracies and the abuse Bale directed toward Catholic writers, his work is considered invaluable because it preserved information that would otherwise have been lost.
