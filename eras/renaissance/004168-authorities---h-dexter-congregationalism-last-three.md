# H. M. Dexter and Congregationalism

## Overview of H. M. Dexter's Works
H. M. Dexter is an author cited as an authority on the history of Congregationalism. His notable works include *The Congregationalism of the Last Three Hundred Years* (also referred to as *Congregationalism*, published in New York in 1880) and *The England and Holland of the Pilgrims*. In his 1880 publication, Dexter provides a detailed summary of the Martin Marprelate controversy, including quotations from the pamphlets, spanning pages 129 to 202.

## Perspectives on Church Reform and Separation
In the study of early church reformers, Dexter's work is used to analyze the differing views of figures such as Browne and Barrowe. While both men advocated for congregational independency and believed the Church had the right and duty to implement necessary reforms without waiting for permission from civil powers, their ultimate goals differed. 

Browne viewed separation as a means to achieve a "spiritual democracy." In contrast, Barrowe viewed the established church order as being polluted by relics of Roman Catholicism, making separation essential for maintaining pure discipline and worship. Dexter, among others, has credited Barrowe as the author of the "Marprelate Tracts," although this attribution is considered improbable.

## Congregationalism as a System
Within the broader context of ecclesiastical organization, Congregationalism is distinguished from other systems such as Episcopacy and Presbyterianism. While Episcopacy vests chief ecclesiastical authority in a bishop within a defined diocese, and Presbyterianism is characterized by government by elders, Congregationalism is defined by the autonomy of the individual church or the community of worshippers.

*   **Robert William Dale (1829-1895):** An English Nonconformist divine educated for the Congregational ministry at Spring Hill College, Birmingham. He served as the sole pastor of Carr's Lane Chapel in Birmingham from 1859 until his death. Dale was a strong advocate for disestablishment and secular education, which he argued was the only legitimate outcome of Nonconformist principles.
*   **Gregory Dexter:** A leading member of the Providence church alongside William Wickenden, Chad Brown, and Thomas Olney. Following a schism in 1652, Gregory Dexter and a majority of members contended for the laying on of hands as indispensable to fellowship and for general redemption. He later became a leading statesman in Providence Plantations and Rhode Island, alongside Clarke and Williams.
