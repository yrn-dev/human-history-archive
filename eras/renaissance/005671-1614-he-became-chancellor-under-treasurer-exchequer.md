# Fulke Greville, 1st Baron Brooke

Fulke Greville, 1st Baron Brooke (1554–1628), was an English poet and statesman. The only son of Sir Fulke Greville, he was born at Beauchamp Court, Warwickshire. His early education began in 1564 at Shrewsbury school, where he entered on the same day as his lifelong friend, Philip Sidney. Greville later matriculated at Jesus College, Cambridge, in 1668.

## Early Career and Literary Circles
In 1576, Sir Henry Sidney, the president of Wales, provided Greville with a post associated with the court of the Marches. He resigned this position in 1577 to accompany Philip Sidney to court. During this period, Greville became a great favorite of Queen Elizabeth, though he faced disgrace on more than one occasion for leaving the country against her wishes.

Greville was a member of a literary clique known as the "Areopagus," led by Gabriel Harvey. Along with Philip Sidney and Sir Edward Dyer, Greville supported the introduction of classical metres into English verse. In 1585, Greville and Sidney planned to join Sir Francis Drake on an expedition against the Spanish West Indies, but Queen Elizabeth forbade Drake from taking them. Similarly, the Queen refused Greville's request to join the army of Leicester in the Netherlands. Following the death of Philip Sidney on October 17, 1586, Greville shared Sidney's legacy of books with Dyer and wrote the *Life of the Renowned Sir Philip Sidney* as a monument to his friend.

## Military and Parliamentary Service
Greville's experience with warfare concluded around 1591, when he served briefly in Normandy under Henry of Navarre. His political career included serving as the secretary to the principality of Wales starting in 1583. He represented Warwickshire in parliament during several periods: 1592–1593, 1597, 1601, and 1620.

## Financial and State Offices
Greville held several high-ranking financial positions throughout his career. In 1598, he was appointed treasurer of the navy, a role he maintained into the early years of the reign of James I. In 1614, he became the under-treasurer and chancellor of the exchequer. During the reign of James I, Greville was a valued supporter of the king's party, although he advocated for the summoning of a parliament in 1615. 

His ascent in the treasury continued in 1618 when he became a commissioner of the treasury. In 1621, he was raised to the peerage as Baron Brooke. This title had previously belonged to the family of his paternal grandmother, Elizabeth Willoughby.
