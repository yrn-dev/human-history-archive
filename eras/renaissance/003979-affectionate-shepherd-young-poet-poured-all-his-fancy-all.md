# The Affectionate Shepherd

*The Affectionate Shepherd* is a work of English poetry written by Richard Barnfield (1574–1627). Published anonymously in November 1594 when the author was twenty-one years old, the work is described as a sort of florid romance. It was dedicated with familiar devotion to Penelope, Lady Rich.

## Composition and Style
The poem is structured in two books using a six-line stanza, a style similar to the works of Shakespeare and Lodge. Barnfield later expressly admitted that the poem served as a paraphrase or expansion of Virgil's second eclogue. The narrative of the work deals extensively with "the complaint of Daphnis for the love of Ganymede."

Critics have noted that the poem is an extraordinary example of the licence introduced to England from Italy during the Renaissance. While the work achieved success, it was not without censure from a moral perspective. The poet utilized the conventional outlines of the shepherd story to incorporate his "coloured touches of nature," his epithets, and all his fancy.

The work is recognized for its picturesqueness, specifically in its descriptions of nature. Examples of these vivid details include "speckled flower call'd sops-in-wine," "fine ruff-footed doves," and the poet's expressed desire to catch the "long-bill'd woodcock" by the "bright glimmering of the starry light." Despite the "absurd subject," the poem is cited as having equalled the immediate imitators of *Venus and Adonis* in its descriptive quality.

## About the Author
Richard Barnfield was born in Norbury, Staffordshire, and baptized on June 13, 1574. He was the son of Maria Skrymsher and Richard Barnfield (or Barnefield), who married in April 1572. Following the death of his mother in early 1581, Barnfield was cared for by his unmarried aunt, Elizabeth Skrymsher.

Barnfield's education included matriculation at Brasenose College, Oxford, in November 1589, where he received his degree in February 1592. Although he performed the exercise for his master's gown, he left the university abruptly without obtaining an M.A. It is conjectured that he arrived in London in 1593, where he may have become acquainted with Spenser, Drayton, and Watson. His early verses were strongly inspired by the imagination of Sir Philip Sidney, whose death occurred while Barnfield was still a school-boy.

Following *The Affectionate Shepherd*, Barnfield published *Cynthia, with certain Sonnets* in January 1595. This volume included a panegyric on Queen Elizabeth written in the Spenserian stanza and a sequence of twenty sonnets addressed to a young man with the initials J. U. or J. V.

## Editorial History
*The Affectionate Shepherd* has been preserved through several editions. It was edited by J. P. Collier in *Illustrations of Old English Literature* (vol. i., 1866), by J. Boswell for the Roxburghe Club in 1816, and by Mr. J. O. Halliwell-Phillipps for the Percy Society's *Early English Poetry* (vol. xx.).
