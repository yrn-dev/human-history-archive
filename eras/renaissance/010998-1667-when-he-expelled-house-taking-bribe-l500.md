## Political Events in England
In 1667, the political landscape of England saw the return of the 2nd Earl of Bristol to a position of legislative power. Having previously been excluded from office due to his religion and having experienced a period of exile from court—during which he avoided apprehension for two years—Bristol was welcomed back following the desired fall of Clarendon. On July 29, 1667, he officially took his seat in the Lords.

## Birth of Abraham de Moivre
The year 1667 also marks the birth of Abraham de Moivre, an English mathematician of French extraction. Born on May 26, 1667, in Vitry, Champagne, de Moivre was born into a French Protestant family. He would later seek refuge in England in 1685 following the revocation of the edict of Nantes. De Moivre became a distinguished mathematician and a personal friend of Sir Isaac Newton, eventually being admitted to the Royal Society of London in 1697, as well as the Academies of Paris and Berlin. He was notably selected by the Royal Society to adjudicate the intellectual contest between G. W. Leibnitz and Newton.
