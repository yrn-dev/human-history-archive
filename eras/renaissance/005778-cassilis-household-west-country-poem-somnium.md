# Somnium and the Household of Lord Cassilis

## The Poem Somnium
The poem *Somnium* was the first production written in Scotland by Buchanan. It is characterized as a satirical attack directed at monastic life in general and specifically upon the Franciscan friars. This work was produced during the period when Buchanan resided in the household of Lord Cassilis in the west country.

## Impact and Subsequent Works
The assault on the monks contained within *Somnium* was not viewed unfavorably by James V. Consequently, Buchanan was engaged by the king to serve as a tutor to Lord James Stewart, one of his natural sons (distinct from the son who later became the regent Murray). Encouraged by James V. to pursue more daring efforts, Buchanan authored the poems *Franciscanus & Fratres* and *Palinodia*. Although these works remained unpublished for many years, they resulted in Buchanan becoming an object of the bitterest hatred from the Franciscan order and their associates.

## Other Literary Works of Buchanan
Beyond his satirical poetry, Buchanan was recognized as one of the foremost Latin poets of his time, a classification shared with Turnebus, de L'Hopital, Béza, Aurat, and Montdore according to the essay *Of Presumption*. While residing in Bordeaux, where he was appointed professor of Latin by André de Gouvéa at the newly founded college of Guienne, Buchanan completed several significant works:
*   The dramas *Baptistes (sive Calumnia)* and *Jephthes (sive Votum)*.
*   Translations of *Alcestis* and *Medea*.

During this period, Buchanan's pupil, Montaigne, acted in his tragedies. Buchanan also established a lasting friendship with Julius Caesar Scaliger and later earned the admiration of Joseph Scaliger.
