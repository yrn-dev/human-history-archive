# Affinity Rising Ex Illicito Coitu

The term "affinity rising *ex illicito coitu*" refers to a specific legal and canonical impediment to marriage arising from illicit sexual intercourse. In historical ecclesiastical contexts, such impediments could prevent a marriage from being recognized as valid unless a formal dispensation was granted to suspend the relevant canons.

## Application in the Marriage of Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn
The concept of affinity rising *ex illicito coitu* appears in the historical records regarding the marriage of King Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn. While some sources, such as the *Chronicle of King Henry VIII*, state that Anne was the King's mistress, other accounts suggest a more complex legal obstacle. 

There is no direct proof of certain claims regarding their relationship, but contemporary belief was reflected in the extraordinary terms of the dispensation granted to Henry VIII to marry Anne. This dispensation included the suspension of all canons relating to impediments created by "affinity rising *ex illicito coitu* in any degree even in the first." However, this interpretation of the events is rejected by Froude in *Divorce of Catherine of Aragon*.

## General Principles of Affinity and Consanguinity
In broader legal terms, prohibitions on marriage are categorized by consanguinity (blood relation) and affinity (relation by marriage). Historically, these prohibitions were designed to prevent marriages between persons in the ascending and descending lines *in infinitum*, as well as between collaterals to the third degree inclusive, based on civil law computation.

The legal status of these prohibitions evolved over time:
* **Statutory Changes:** The statute 32 Hen. VIII. c. 38 established that no prohibition, except for God's law, should impeach any marriage without the "Levitical degrees." This statute underwent various changes, being repealed in part by 2 & 3 Edw. VI. c. 23 and in whole by 1 & 2 P. and M. c. 8, before being revived by 1 Eliz. c. 1.
* **The Act of 1835:** This act decreed that all marriages celebrated between persons within the prohibited degrees of affinity or consanguinity would be absolutely null and void. Prior to this, such marriages were considered voidable. The act did, however, legalize marriages within prohibited degrees of affinity (excluding consanguinity) that had been celebrated before August 31, 1835.

## Evolution of Canonical Rules
The rules governing relationship impediments were modified by various ecclesiastical authorities to reduce confusion and uncertainty:
* **Pope Gregory I:** Limited the impediment to the 7th degree of relationship inclusive using civil computation, a rule later adopted as the law of the empire by Charlemagne.
* **Innocent III:** Through the 4th Lateran Council, a decree was issued permitting marriages between a husband and the relations of his wife (and vice versa) beyond the 4th degree inclusive, according to canonical computation. This remains the rule of the Roman Catholic Church.
* **Council of Trent:** Limited impediments due to spiritual affinity to specific relationships, such as those between a baptizer and the baptized, the baptizer and the parents of the baptized, the baptizer and the godparents, and the godparents and the baptized or their parents. For example, a godmother is prohibited from marrying the father of the child she held at the font.
