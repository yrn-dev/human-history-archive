# George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham

The life and career of George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham, have been documented across various historical and biographical sources. He was a member of the Villiers line and held the dukedom and marquessate that had been created for his predecessor.

## Titles and Succession
The titles of marquess and duke of Buckingham were originally created by King James I for George Villiers, the 1st Duke of Buckingham, in 1618 and 1623, respectively. These titles became extinct upon the death of the 2nd Duke in 1687. While the dukedom and marquessate ended, the earldom—created in 1617—was claimed by a collateral line asserting descent from John Villiers, 1st Viscount Purbeck. This claim was eventually extinguished in 1774 following the death of George Villiers, a clergyman.

## Professional Associations
George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham, served as the chancellor of the university of Cambridge. During his tenure in this role, he is said to have employed the poet Samuel Butler, the author of *Hudibras*, as his secretary. While some biographers of Butler express doubt regarding this appointment, the satire written by Butler on Buckingham in his *Characters* (published in *The Genuine Remains of Mr Samuel Butler* in 1759) suggests an intimate knowledge of the Duke, lending probability to the claim that Butler served as his secretary.
