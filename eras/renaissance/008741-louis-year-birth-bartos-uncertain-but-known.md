# Louis

The name Louis is associated with several historical figures of prominence, including kings of France and the Holy Roman Empire, as well as a sovereign of Bohemia.

## Louis the Bavarian
Louis the Bavarian served as emperor during the early 14th century. He was served for many years by Castruccio Castracani degli Antelminelli, who had previously been a supporter of Frederick of Austria. In 1325, after Castruccio defeated the Florentines at Altopascio, the emperor appointed him duke of Luni, Volterra, Pistoja, and Lucca. Two years later, Castruccio captured Pisa and was made imperial vicar. However, the relationship between Louis and Castruccio eventually became less friendly.

## Louis XI of France
Louis XI (1423–1483) was the son of Marie of Anjou and Charles VII. Born on July 3, 1423, at Bourges, his early childhood coincided with the appearance of Joan of Arc and the coronation of his father at Reims. He spent much of his youth at the castle of Loches, where he was educated by tutors including Jean Majoris, for whom the theologian John Gerson wrote a manual of instructions, and the pious Bernard of Armagnac. It has been suggested that these tutors influenced Louis's preference for the company of the bourgeoisie and the poor over the nobility.

In June 1436, at nearly thirteen years of age, Louis married Margaret, the daughter of James I of Scotland. His political career began in 1439 when he was sent to Poitou to suppress brigandage and to Languedoc to direct defenses against the English. During this time, he was induced by rebellious nobles to lead the Praguerie. Although Charles VII pardoned him, Louis continued his military career, fighting the English in 1440 and assisting his father in suppressing the revolt of the count of Armagnac in 1443. In 1444, he led an army of 15,000 to 20,000 mercenaries and brigands against the Swiss of the canton of Basel. Following an ineffective siege, he made peace with the Swiss confederation. Later, in 1469, Louis XI founded the Order of St Michael for a limited number of knights of noble birth.

## Louis XIV of France
Louis XIV (1638–1715) was born on September 5, 1638, at Saint-Germain-en-Laye to Louis XIII and Anne of Austria. His birth was viewed as a political triumph because the king's brother, Gaston of Orleans, had been a determined opponent of the policies of the minister Richelieu. Louis XIV became king on May 14, 1643, upon his father's death, though he did not begin to rule for sixteen years. During his minority, power was held by Cardinal Mazarin and the queen-mother. 

The royal authority was weakened by the childhood of the king and domestic troubles known as the Fronde, which forced the court to flee Paris twice. These humiliations contributed to the development of Louis XIV's later autocratic ideas. By 1653, the Fronde had ended, and France saw diplomatic and military success through the peace of Westphalia (1648) and the peace of the Pyrenees (1659). The latter treaty stipulated that Louis marry his cousin, the Infanta Maria Theresa, to cement the friendship between France and Spain. In 1693, Louis XIV founded the Order of St Louis to recognize military merit.

## Other Figures Named Louis
*   **Louis XV:** This king founded the Order of Military Merit in 1759, which was originally intended for Protestant officers. Secret correspondence between Louis XV and his diplomatic agents from 1752 to 1774 was later published in *Le Secret du roi*.
*   **Louis XVI:** A history of his reign was written by F. X. J. Droz. He had an elder son who died in 1789, and a daughter, Marie Thérèse Charlotte (1778–1851). Marie Thérèse, known as the "orphan of the Temple," spent three years in prison and later married her cousin, Louis, duke of Angoulême.
*   **King Louis of Bohemia:** Described as a weak and absentee sovereign, his reign was marked by troubles that befell Bohemia and the city of Prague. These events were recorded in the *Chronicle of Prague* by the writer Bartos (also known as Bartholomew), who served as the secretary for the city of Prague and died in 1539.
