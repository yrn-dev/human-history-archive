# Roger Ascham

## Life and Background
Roger Ascham (c. 1515–1568) was an English writer and scholar. He was born around the year 1515 in Kirby Wiske, a village located in the North Riding of Yorkshire near Northallerton. He was the third son of Margaret and John Ascham; the latter served as steward to Lord Scrope of Bolton. While the family name of his mother is unknown, it is said that she was well connected. Much of the available information regarding Ascham's early life is derived from the work of Edward Grant, the headmaster of Westminster.

## Collected Letters and Publications
Ascham's letters were collected and edited by Edward Grant, who also delivered a panegyrical oration on Ascham's life in 1576. These letters were first published in 1576 and subsequently went through several editions, with the most recent of these appearing at Nuremberg in 1611. In 1703, the letters were re-edited by William Elstob.

Later editorial efforts included a publication by Dr. Giles in 1864–1865, which consisted of four volumes of select letters. This collection also included the life written by Edward Grant and two of Ascham's other works, the *Toxophilus* and the *Scholemaster*.

## Major Works
Ascham authored several notable English works, which were edited by James Bennett in 1771 (including a life written by Dr. Johnson) and reprinted in 8vo format in 1815.

### The Scholemaster
*The Scholemaster* was reprinted in 1571 and 1589. It underwent several later editions edited by various scholars:
* The Rev. J. Upton edited the work in 1711 and 1743.
* Prof. J.E.B. Mayor edited it in 1863.
* Prof. Edward Arber edited it in 1870.

### Toxophilus
The *Toxophilus* was republished in 1571, 1589, and 1788. Prof. Edward Arber also published editions of this work in 1868 and 1902.
