# Frederick of the Palatinate

Frederick, the elector palatine of the Rhine and son-in-law of James I, is known in history as the "winter king." His brief tenure as the king of Bohemia occurred during a period of intense conflict involving the Bohemian estates and the house of Habsburg.

## Election and Coronation
The Bohemian crown became vacant in March 1619 following the death of Matthias. On July 31, the Bohemian estates formally deposed Ferdinand. Subsequently, on August 26, the estates elected Frederick, the elector palatine, as their king. Frederick and his queen, Elizabeth of England, arrived in Bohemia in October. Their coronation took place shortly thereafter at St Vitus's cathedral in Prague.

## Military Conflict and the Battle of the White Hill
Warfare with Austria continued throughout 1619. During this time, Thurn occupied Moravia—which aligned itself with Bohemia—and advanced toward Vienna, though he was eventually forced to retreat.

The situation for Bohemia turned fatal in 1620. Ferdinand, who had succeeded Matthias as emperor, sought to reconquer Bohemia. He was supported by several allies, including Spain, Poland, various Italian states, and the Lutheran elector of Saxony. Additionally, the powerful duke Maximilian of Bavaria joined forces with Ferdinand.

In September 1620, a large imperialist army entered Bohemia under the command of Bouquoi, Tilly, and the duke of Bavaria. Following a series of skirmishes in which the Bohemians were defeated, the imperial forces reached the outskirts of Prague on the evening of November 7. 

On the morning of November 8, the imperialists attacked the Bohemian army, which was positioned on a slightly fortified plateau known as "Bila Hora" (White Hill). The struggle lasted only a few hours, resulting in a Bohemian defeat. By the evening of the battle, the imperialists had occupied the port of Prague on the left bank of the Vltava (Moldau).

## Downfall and Exile
Following the defeat at the White Hill, King Frederick lost all courage and hurriedly departed Prague the next morning. This event marked the downfall of Bohemian independence and the beginning of a decline for the national tongue.

Frederick's sovereign status was supported by some English interests; for example, Edward Sackville, the 4th Earl of Dorset, was one of the leaders of an English contingent that fought for Frederick at the battle of the White Hill in 1620.

Following his departure from Prague, Frederick went into exile. He was followed for a time by some of his officials, including the historian Paul Skala ze Zhore, who served as an official for the "winter king" and later spent the final years of his life in Freiberg, Saxony.
