# George Calvert, 1st Baron Baltimore

## Early Life and Education
George Calvert, the 1st Baron Baltimore (c. 1580–1632), was an English statesman. He was the son of Leonard Calvert and Alice, who was the daughter of John Crosland of Crosland. Calvert was born at Kipling in Yorkshire and received his education at Trinity College, Oxford.

## Public Service and Political Career
Following a period of travel on the continent, Calvert entered public service as the secretary to Robert Cecil, who later became the earl of Salisbury. His career in administration included several appointments:
* In 1606, he was appointed clerk of the crown in Clare and Connaught.
* In 1608, he became a clerk of the council.
* In 1609, he was returned to parliament for Bossiney.

Calvert provided assistance to James I in his discourse against the Arminian theological professor of Leiden, Vorstius. By 1613, he was tasked with managing Italian and Spanish correspondence and was dispatched on a mission to Ireland to investigate grievances. As a result of these services, he was granted a knighthood in 1617. This was followed by a secretaryship of state in 1619 and a pension of L2000 per year in 1620.

## Parliamentary Representation and Religious Transition
Calvert represented Oxford University (1624) and Yorkshire (1621) in the House of Commons. In his official role, he was responsible for obtaining supplies and communicating the policy of the king. However, he was distrusted by parliament due to his support for the Spanish marriage and the unpopular alliance with Spain.

Shortly after the failure of the Spanish marriage scheme, Calvert declared himself a Roman Catholic. On February 12, 1625, he resigned his office. Upon his resignation, he was created Baron Baltimore of Baltimore and was granted large estates in Ireland.

## Colonial Enterprises
After leaving public life, Calvert focused his attention on colonial ventures. In 1621, he established a small settlement in Newfoundland. In 1623, he procured a charter for this settlement under the name of Avalon, and he personally visited the site in 1627.

Due to the unsuitable climate and various disputes, Calvert sailed from Newfoundland to Virginia. However, he was forbidden from settling in Virginia unless he took the oaths of supremacy and allegiance.

## Death and Legacy
George Calvert returned home and died on April 15, 1632, before he could secure a new concession. On June 20, 1632, the charter of Maryland passed the great seal in favor of his son, Cecilius, the second Lord Baltimore, who founded the colony.
