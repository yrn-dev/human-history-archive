# Letters and Papers of Henry VIII

The *Letters and Papers of the Reign of King Henry VIII* is a comprehensive collection of state papers and historical records from the reign of Henry VIII. The work was prepared by the English historian John Sherren Brewer.

## Compilation and Authorship
John Sherren Brewer (1810–1879) was a historian born in Norwich and educated at Queen's College, Oxford. After a career that included roles as a chaplain, a lecturer in classical literature at King's College, London, and a professor of English language and literature and modern history, Brewer was commissioned in 1856 by the master of the rolls to prepare a calendar of the state papers of Henry VIII. 

Brewer served as a reader and preacher at the Rolls while conducting the vast amount of research required for the project. In 1877, he was granted the crown living of Toppesfield, Essex, by Disraila, where he continued his work. Brewer's *Introductions* to the *Letters and Papers*, published separately in 1884 under the title *The Reign of Henry VIII*, are regarded as a scholarly and authoritative history of the king's reign.

## Content and Historical Utility
The *Letters and Papers* serves as a primary source for various historical inquiries regarding the Tudor period. The archive is cited in relation to numerous events and figures, including:

*   **Anne Boleyn:** The records are used to examine the timeline of her marriage, which was commonly antedated to November 14, 1532, and the dispensations granted to Henry VIII to marry her. These dispensations included the suspension of canons regarding impediments created by "affinity rising *ex illicito coitu* in any degree even in the first."
*   **Ecclesiastical and Political Matters:** The volumes are referenced in studies of the English monasteries, the English Church in the 16th century, and the roles of figures such as Thomas Cranmer.
*   **International Relations:** The collection is used alongside other state papers, such as the *Calendar of Scottish State-Papers* and the *Calendar of State Papers England and Spain*, to document diplomatic and foreign affairs.

## The Attributed Letter to Henry VIII
