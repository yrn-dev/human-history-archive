# John Ball (1585-1640)

## Early Life and Education
John Ball was an English puritan divine born in October 1585 at Cassington, Oxfordshire. He pursued his higher education at Oxford, where he attended St Mary's Hall. In 1608, he completed his studies and took his B.A. degree.

## Tutorship and Early Ministry
Following the completion of his degree in 1608, Ball traveled to Cheshire. There, he served as a tutor to the children of Lady Cholmondeley. During this period of his life, he adopted Puritan views. After receiving ordination without subscription, he was appointed to a small curacy located in Whitmore, Staffordshire.

## Ecclesiastical Conflict and Later Career
Ball's tenure at Whitmore was cut short when he was deprived of his position by John Bridgeman, the high church bishop of Chester. This action by Bishop Bridgeman resulted in much suffering for Ball. Following his removal from the curacy, he transitioned into a role as a schoolmaster. In this capacity, he developed a high and wide reputation based on his piety and his scholarship.

## Literary Works
John Ball was a prolific author of religious texts. His most popular work was *A Short Catechisme, containing all the Principal Grounds of Religion*, which saw 14 editions published before 1632. Other valuable contributions to his body of work include his *Treatise of Faith*, published in 1632, and *Friendly Trial of the Grounds tending to Separation*, published in 1640. The latter work is noted for defining his specific position regarding the church.

## Death
John Ball died on the 20th of October 1640.
