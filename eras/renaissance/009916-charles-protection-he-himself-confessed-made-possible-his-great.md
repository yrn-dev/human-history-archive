# The Career of Boniface under Charles's Protection

## Missionary Work in Germany
The career of Boniface was made possible by the protection of Charles, a fact that Boniface himself confessed. Utilizing this protection, Boniface entered heathen Germany to conduct a systematic crusade. His efforts included the overturning of idols, the baptism of converts, and the establishment of monasteries and churches. 

To support these endeavors, Boniface called for a band of missionary helpers from England, consisting of monks and nuns. Among these assistants were several individuals who achieved fame, including St. Lull—who later succeeded Boniface in the see at Mainz—as well as St. Burchard (bishop of Wurzburg), St. Gregory (abbot at Utrecht), St. Lioba, St. Walburge, and St. Thecla. Willibald served as Boniface's biographer.

## Ecclesiastical Organization and Reform
Boniface's ascent within the church hierarchy continued with his creation as archbishop in 732. Following a third visit to Rome in 738, he organized the church in Bavaria by establishing four bishoprics: Passau, Salzburg, Freising, and Regensburg.

His influence expanded further in 741 when Pope Zacharias appointed him as a legate and tasked him with the reformation of the entire Frankish church. This work was carried out with the support of Pippin and Carloman, the mayors of the palace who had succeeded Charles Martel. Similar to his work in Bavaria, Boniface organized the east Frankish church into four bishoprics—Eichstadt, Buraburg, Wurzburg, and Erfurt—and placed his own monks in charge of them. In 742, Boniface presided over the first German council.

## Monastic Culture and Theological Conflict
Boniface founded the abbey of Fulda to serve as a center for German monastic culture. He placed the abbey under the leadership of the Bavarian Sturm, whose biography provides picturesque glimpses of the era. The rule established at Fulda was stricter than the Benedictine rule.

During this period, Boniface engaged in a disciplinary and theological controversy with Virgil, the Irish bishop of Salzburg. Virgil held the heresy that worlds other than our own existed. Despite his scientific leanings, Virgil held his own against Boniface and was canonized after his death.

## Activities in France
Boniface found greater success in France, where he dealt with a Frankish bishop of Neustria named Adalbert (or Aldebert). Adalbert had caused significant disturbance by claiming to receive relics directly from angels rather than from Rome. By planting crosses in open fields, Adalbert encouraged people to desert their churches and gained a substantial following throughout Neustria.
