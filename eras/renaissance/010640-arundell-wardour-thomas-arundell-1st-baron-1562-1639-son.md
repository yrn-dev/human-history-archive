# ARUNDELL OF WARDOUR, THOMAS ARUNDELL, 1ST BARON

Thomas Arundell, 1st Baron Arundell of Wardour (c. 1562–1639), was an English nobleman and soldier. He was born around 1562 to Margaret, the daughter of Sir Henry Willoughby, and Sir Mathew Arundell of Wardour Castle in Wiltshire. His father belonged to the ancient Arundells of Lanherne family based in Cornwall.

## Military Service and Foreign Title
In 1579, Queen Elizabeth personally recommended Arundell to Emperor Rudolph II. Serving with imperial troops in Hungary against the Turks, Arundell distinguished himself in combat. During the siege of Esztergom (also known as Gran) on August 13, 1595, he captured the enemy's banner with his own hand. In recognition of his service, Emperor Rudolph II created him a count of the Holy Roman Empire in December 1595.

Arundell returned to England in January 1596, though his return was marked by a shipwreck in which he barely survived.

## Conflict with the Crown and Family
The adoption of a foreign title caused significant friction both within Arundell's family and at the royal court. His father objected to Thomas holding a superior rank and promptly disinherited him. Among the English peers, his title sparked jealousy, despite the common courtesy of giving precedence to foreign nobles.

Queen Elizabeth expressed serious displeasure, stating that "faithful subjects keep their eyes at home and not gaze upon foreign crowns," comparing such behavior to chaste wives who should have no glances but for their own spouses. Upon his arrival in England, the Queen committed him to the Fleet and sent a long letter of remonstrance to the emperor.

Arundell remained under arrest until April, when he was liberated following an examination. He was confined again in April 1597, though he was declared innocent of all charges except for "practising to contrive the justification of his vain title with Ministers beyond the seas." He was liberated again in December and placed under his father's care. However, the following year, he was arrested once more on accusations of conspiracy against the government.

## Later Life and Peerage
Arundell petitioned for a license to undertake a sea expedition, asserting that his goal was "honour which some base minds call ambition," but these requests were refused. He appeared to be restored to favor in 1599.

On May 4, 1605, King James I created him Baron Arundell of Wardour. Despite this elevation, he fell under temporary suspicion during the period of the Gunpowder Plot. He encountered further trouble again in 1623.

## Legacy and Family
The barony established by Thomas Arundell remained within the Arundell family, which continued to be Roman Catholic. He was succeeded by his son, Thomas, the 2nd baron. His grandson, Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour (c. 1607–1694), continued the family's Roman Catholic faith and royalist leanings, eventually regaining family estates after they had been confiscated during the mid-17th century.
