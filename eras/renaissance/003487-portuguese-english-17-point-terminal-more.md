# Portuguese and English Global Interactions

## Maritime Trade and Competition in India
Beginning in the 17th century, the rise of English factories and trade in the Indian seas led to constant broils and jealousies with the Portuguese. The Portuguese had previously won the Indian sea trade from Red Sea Arabs using larger ships with heavier armament, allowing Lisbon to surpass Venice as a mart for oriental spices. However, by the 17th century, the Dutch began challenging this monopoly.

Tensions between the English and Portuguese were particularly embittered by English efforts to open traffic with Persia, a trade the Portuguese had long monopolized through their possession of Hormuz. The officers of Shah Abbas sought English naval assistance to attack the Portuguese occupation of Hormuz. Following maritime conflicts in 1620 and 1621, the council at Surat resolved on a state of maritime war against the Portuguese flag in November 1621.

In December 1621, the English joined "the duke of Shiraz" (Imam Kuli Khan, governor of Fars) in an expedition. Although English crews initially resisted, stating they were not engaged for non-merchandizing business, five English vessels eventually aided in attacks on a Portuguese fort at the east end of Kishm and the city of Hormuz. The siege of Hormuz began on February 18, 1622, and ended on May 1, 1622, when the Portuguese surrendered after a ten-week defense. Despite this, England remained at peace with Spain, the crown to which Portugal was then subject.

## Relations in Japan
The English maintained a factory at Hirado, which was eventually closed voluntarily without serious friction between the English and the Japanese. The English left their stores and houses in the care of a local feudatory. When the English company attempted to re-open business in 1673 by sending a merchantman called the *Return*, the Japanese authorities denied permission. The stated reason was that the king of England had married a Portuguese princess, and consequently, British subjects could not visit Japan. This response likely reflected the Japanese Government's feelings toward Roman Catholics.

## Colonial Territories and Administration
In 1494, the Pope divided the new world between Spain and Portugal, granting Portugal Brazil and all lands east of a line 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands. Portuguese overseas enterprise also expanded southward and eastward; Vasco da Gama sailed from Lisbon round the Cape to Zanzibar and Calicut in 1497. Portugal established and fortified trading stations across the Indian Ocean, maintaining possessions in Mozambique, Goa, Macao in China, and part of Timor.

In Portuguese East Africa (Mozambique), the capital is Lourenço Marques. The city is located on the north bank of the English river (also known as the Espirito Santo) in Delagoa Bay. As of 1904, the population was 9,849, including 4,691 Europeans and 1,690 Asiatics. Lourenço Marques serves as the nearest seaport to the Rand gold mines, with over 50% of Johannesburg's import trade passing through it. While Great Britain and its possessions account for some 40% of this import trade, Portugal, Germany, Norway, Sweden, and America also contribute.

## Linguistic and Cultural Intersections
The English language has been influenced by various sources, and in the context of Bantu languages, departures from the standard terminal vowel *a* in verbal roots are often attributed to borrowed foreign words from English, Portuguese, or Arabic (as seen in Swahili).

In terms of vocabulary, the English word "duenna" is derived from the Spanish *duena* (Latin *domina*). In both Spanish and Portuguese households, a duenna is an elderly lady appointed to charge the young girls of the family, occupying a position between a companion and a governess.

## Demographic Presence
In certain island regions, populations are primarily of Portuguese origin with Flemish and Moorish strains. These areas also host considerable numbers of English, Irish, and Scottish immigrants, particularly in St Michael's and Fayal. In 1900, the total number of resident foreigners in such a region was 1,490.
