# Most of the First Movement of the A Major Sonata for Clavier and Flute

The A major sonata for clavier and flute is an instrumental work attributed to Johann Sebastian Bach. Within the archive's records of Bach's instrumental output, the piece is categorized among his chamber music, which includes three sonatas for clavier and flute.

## Physical State and Composition
A significant portion of the first movement of the A major sonata for clavier and flute is preserved in a specific manner: it was written in the spare staves located at the bottom of a larger score. Due to the nature of its placement on the manuscript, some portions of these staves have been cut off.

## Context within Bach's Instrumental Works
The sonata exists alongside a broad body of Bach's chamber and instrumental music. His documented works for similar combinations include:

*   Six sonatas and a suite for clavier and violin.
*   Three sonatas for clavier and viola da gamba.
*   Two flute-sonatas and a violin suite with figured bass.
*   Two trios with figured bass.
*   Six suites for violoncello alone and six sonatas (comprising three sonatas and three partitas) for violin alone.

Other instrumental works by Bach include the six Brandenburg concertos, various violin concertos, and four orchestral suites.

## Attribution and Authenticity
The archive notes that identifying the authorship of Bach's works can be complex because his autographs typically only list the composer's name on the outside sheet. When these sheets are lost, editors must rely on the music itself to determine authenticity. 

This issue of attribution extends to the sonatas for clavier and flute. While the A major sonata is listed among his works, the second of the three sonatas for clavier and flute is considered "extremely suggestive" of Bach's sons. Although Philipp Emanuel Bach ascribed this second sonata to his father, the archive notes that he may have docketed it wrongly while arranging copies of his father's works. Furthermore, a "twin brother" of that second sonata exists in the *B.-G.* ix. Anhang ii. for which Philipp Emanuel did not vouch.
