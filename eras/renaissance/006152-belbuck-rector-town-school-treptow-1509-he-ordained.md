# JOHANN BUGENHAGEN

## Early Life and Academic Career
Johann Bugenhagen (1485–1558), who was also known by the surname Pomeranus, was a German Protestant reformer. He was born on June 24, 1485, at Wollin, located near Stettin. Bugenhagen attended the university of Greifswald, where he achieved significant distinction as a humanist.

## Appointments at Treptow and Belbuck
In 1504, the abbot of the Praemonstratensian monastery at Belbuck appointed Bugenhagen as the rector of the town school at Treptow. He continued his ecclesiastical advancement in 1509, when he was ordained as a priest and subsequently became a vicar in the collegiate *Marienkirche* at Treptow. By 1517, he returned to the abbey school at Belbuck, where he was appointed as a lecturer on the Church Fathers and the Bible.

## Conversion and Association with Luther
Bugenhagen's theological views shifted in 1520 after reading Luther's *De Captivitate Babylonica*, which converted him into a zealous supporter of the Reformer. In this capacity, he succeeded in winning over the abbot, among others, to these views. In 1521, Bugenhagen traveled to Wittenberg, where he developed a close friendship with Melanchthon and Luther. He married in 1522 and spent time lecturing and preaching at the university.

## Reforming Influence and International Work
Bugenhagen utilized his organizing skills and zeal to extend the influence of the Reformation beyond the university. His administrative efforts included arranging church affairs in Hamburg and Brunswick in 1528, followed by Pomerania and Lübeck in 1530. 

In 1537, Christian III invited Bugenhagen to Denmark. He remained in Denmark for five years, during which time he organized schools and the church. Although he held only the rank of presbyter, he performed the consecration of the new Danish bishops.

## Final Years and Literary Contributions
Bugenhagen spent the remainder of his life in Wittenberg, choosing to stay in the city—which he regarded as the home of the Reformation—despite the perils of persecution and war. He died on April 20, 1558.

His written works are numerous. He authored a history of Pomerania, though this remained unpublished until 1728. His *Interpretatio in Librum Psalmorum*, published in 1523, is considered perhaps his best book. Additionally, he is remembered for providing assistance to Luther in the translation of the Bible.
