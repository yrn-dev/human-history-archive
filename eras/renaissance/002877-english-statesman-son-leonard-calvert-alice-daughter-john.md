# George Calvert, 1st Baron Baltimore

George Calvert, 1st Baron Baltimore (c. 1580–1632), was an English statesman born at Kipling in Yorkshire. He was the son of Leonard Calvert and Alice, who was the daughter of John Crosland of Crosland. Calvert received his education at Trinity College, Oxford, and traveled on the continent before beginning his career in public service.

## Early Public Service and Political Career
Calvert first entered public service as the secretary to Robert Cecil, who later became the earl of Salisbury. His ascent through the administrative ranks included an appointment in 1606 as the clerk of the crown in Clare and Connaught. By 1608, he had become a clerk of the council, and in 1609 he was returned to parliament representing Bossiney.

During his tenure, Calvert provided assistance to James I in his discourse against the Arminian theological professor of Leiden, Vorstius. In 1613, he was tasked with managing Italian and Spanish correspondence and was dispatched on a mission to Ireland to investigate grievances. These services resulted in his knighthood in 1617. He was subsequently appointed secretary of state in 1619 and granted a yearly pension of L2000 in 1620.

## Parliamentary Activity and Religious Transition
Calvert served in the House of Commons as the representative for Yorkshire in 1621 and for Oxford University in 1624. In his official capacity, he was responsible for obtaining supplies and communicating the policies of the king. However, he was distrusted by parliament due to his support for the Spanish marriage and the unpopular alliance with Spain.

Following the failure of the Spanish marriage scheme, Calvert declared himself a Roman Catholic. On February 12, 1625, he resigned his office. Upon his resignation, he was created Baron Baltimore of Baltimore and was granted large estates in Ireland.

## Colonial Enterprises
After leaving public life, Calvert focused his attention on colonial ventures. In 1621, he established a small settlement in Newfoundland. In 1623, he procured a charter for this settlement under the name of Avalon, a location he visited personally in 1627.

Due to the unsuitable nature of the climate and various disputes, Calvert sailed from Newfoundland to Virginia. However, he was forbidden from settling in Virginia because he would not take the oaths of supremacy and allegiance.

## Death and Legacy
George Calvert returned to England and died on April 15, 1632. He passed away before a new concession could be secured for his colonial ambitions. On June 20, 1632, the charter for Maryland passed the great seal in favor of his son, Cecilius, the second Lord Baltimore, who went on to found the colony.
