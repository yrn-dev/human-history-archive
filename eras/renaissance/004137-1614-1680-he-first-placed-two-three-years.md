# Notable Figures and Events of 1614–1680

The period between 1614 and 1680 saw significant developments across Europe and Asia, characterized by the careers of influential artists, mathematicians, and political figures, as well as shifts in governance and parliamentary representation.

## Political and Legal Developments

In England, the year 1614 marked the introduction of a bill in parliament intended to secure representation for the borough of Barnard Castle and the city and county of Durham. This movement faced strong opposition from the bishop, who viewed it as an infringement of his palatinate rights. Consequently, the county was not summoned to return members to parliament until 1654.

In the realm of nobility, William Howard (1614–1680) was granted the barony of Stafford in 1640. Two months later, he was created Viscount Stafford. His life ended in 1680 when he was beheaded.

In Japan, the Keicho era ended in 1614. Later, the ex-Emperor Go-Mizu-no-o continued to administer public affairs from his place of retirement until his death in 1680 at the age of eighty-five.

## Arts and Architecture

The mid-to-late 17th century produced several distinguished artists in Italy and the Netherlands. Giovanni Francesco Grimaldi (1606–1680), an Italian painter and architect known as Il Bolognese, was a relative of the Caracci family and a pupil of Albani. Grimaldi served as the architect to Pope Paul V and was patronized by subsequent popes. In approximately 1648, Cardinal Mazarin invited him to France, where he spent about two years working on fresco-painting in the Louvre and on buildings for Louis XIV and the Cardinal. Grimaldi, who was known for a light touch and a strong use of green, specialized in portraits, history, and landscapes. He eventually returned to Rome, where he served as president of the Academy of St Luke before dying on November 28, 1680.

In the Netherlands, Gerhard Dou (1613–1680) became a prominent painter born in Leiden. After initial training under Bartholomew Dolendo and Peter Kouwhoorn, Dou spent three years as a pupil of Rembrandt. While he initially adopted Rembrandt's skill in coloring and chiaroscuro, Dou eventually developed a distinct, minute, and elaborate style. He was known for his extreme precision, sometimes spending five days to paint a single hand, and he manufactured his own brushes to achieve this detail. Dou frequently used a concave mirror and a frame crossed with silk thread to ensure exactness. He specialized in small still-life paintings and subjects depicted in candle or lantern light.

## Science and Scholarship

Isaac Barrow (1630–1677) was an English divine and mathematician and the son of Thomas Barrow, a London linen-draper. Barrow's early education began with two or three years at the Charterhouse school, though his conduct there suggested little hope for scholarly success. His prospects improved after moving to Felsted school in Essex under master Martin Holbeach. In 1643, he entered St Peter's College and later Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied science, literature, and natural philosophy.

Barrow's academic interests were broad; he initially pursued the medical profession through the study of chemistry, botany, and anatomy before turning to astronomy, geometry, and chronology. His travels took him to France and Italy. During a voyage from Leghorn to Smyrna, he demonstrated personal bravery during an attack by an Algerine pirate. In Smyrna, he was received by the English consul, Mr. Bretton, and in Constantinople by Sir Jonathan Dawes and the English ambassador, Sir Thomas Bendish. While in Constantinople, Barrow studied the works of St Chrysostom, whom he preferred over all other Fathers. He returned home through Holland and Germany in 1659.

## Military Careers

Percy Kirke (c. 1646–1691) was an English soldier and the son of George Kirke. He received his first commission in 1666 in the Lord Admiral's regiment and later served in the Blues. By 1680, Kirke had attained the rank of lieutenant-colonel and shortly thereafter became colonel of one of the Tangier regiments. He later served as the governor of Tangier, where he was noted for both his distinctions in office and the roughness of his manners.

## Literature and Humanism

In the early 17th century, Dutch literature was influenced by figures such as Hendrick Laurenssen Spieghel (1549–1612) and Roemer Pieterssen Visscher (1547–1620). Spieghel wrote the didactic epic *Hertspieghel* in 1614 and the philological work *Tweespraeck van de nederduytsche letterkunst*. In 1614, Roemer Visscher first collected and published Spieghel's writings alongside his own verses. Visscher was regarded by his disciples as the "Dutch Martial" and is seen as a figure at the threshold of new Renaissance literature in Holland.
