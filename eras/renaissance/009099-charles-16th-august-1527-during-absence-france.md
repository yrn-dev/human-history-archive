# Charles V

## Early Life and Accession
Charles V was born in 1500. By 1520, he had become Emperor. His reign was characterized by extensive conflicts and alliances across Europe and beyond, notably involving his efforts against the Turk and Francis I, the King of France.

## Military Campaigns and Italian Conflicts
The primary battlefield for the conflicts between Charles V, Francis I, and the alliance involving Henry VIII was North Italy. These campaigns were marked by dull generalship on both sides, with military progress largely dependent on the arrival of reinforcements. 

During these struggles, a German army invaded France but failed to capture Marseilles. This force subsequently retreated into Italy, where they lost Milan and were besieged in Pavia. Francis I attempted a long siege of Pavia but was unsuccessful; he was eventually defeated, wounded, and taken prisoner by fresh German forces.

Despite this victory, Charles V faced shifting political loyalties. Both the Pope and Henry VIII, fearing that Charles was attaining excessive power, turned against him. This instability contributed to a period of confused fighting that lasted ten years and impoverished all of Europe.

## The Sack of Rome
In 1527, unpaid German troops stationed in Milan, under the command of the Constable of Bourbon, conducted a raid upon Rome. The troops stormed and pillaged the city. During the ensuing slaughter and looting, the Pope was forced to take refuge in the Castle of St. Angelo. The crisis eventually ended when the Pope paid the German troops four hundred thousand ducats to secure their departure.

## Diplomatic Relations and the English Court
Charles V maintained diplomatic channels with the English court through imperial ambassadors. On August 16, 1527, the imperial ambassador Mendoza wrote a letter to Charles V. In this correspondence, Anne Boleyn was first openly connected to King Henry VIII as a potential wife should the King obtain a divorce from Catherine. At the time of this letter, Wolsey was absent in France; Wolsey had opposed the alliance with Anne Boleyn and had instead been negotiating a marriage between Henry VIII and Renee, the daughter of Louis XII.

## Later Reign and Abdication
Charles V eventually achieved triumph in Italy. In 1530, he was crowned by the Pope at Bologna, making him the last German Emperor to receive such a coronation. 

Following decades of rule, Charles V abdicated in 1556. He died in 1558.
