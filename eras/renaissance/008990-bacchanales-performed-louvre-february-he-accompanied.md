# Bacchanales at the Louvre

The *Bacchanales* was a ballet performed at the Louvre in February. The production involved the participation of Francois le Metel de Boisrobert, a French poet who had a share in the ballet.

## Francois le Metel de Boisrobert
Francois le Metel de Boisrobert (1592–1662) was born in Caen. He received training in law and practiced at the bar in Rouen for a period of time. Around 1622, he moved to Paris, and by the following year, he had established a position at court through his involvement in the *Bacchanales* ballet.

Boisrobert was a man of diverse talents and interests. He was a poet and a raconteur whose humor and skill made him indispensable to Cardinal Richelieu, whom he had been introduced to in 1623. He became one of five poets tasked with implementing the dramatic ideas of Richelieu. Additionally, Boisrobert suggested the plan for the Academy to Richelieu and served as one of its most active and earliest members.

His personal life was marked by a passion for play and a friendship with Ninon de l'Enclos. Due to the undisguised looseness of his life, his enemies frequently used these facts against him, leading to several periods of disgrace. Despite these lapses, he was never disgraced for long. In his later years, he was required to dedicate more attention to his duties as a priest, having previously taken orders and been appointed a canon of Rouen.

Boisrobert was financially wealthy due to the benefices granted by Richelieu, and he used this wealth to be liberal toward other men of letters. Following the death of Richelieu, he served Mazarin faithfully during the Fronde. He died on March 30, 1662. His literary contributions include volumes of verse and several comedies; it is noted that Moliere's *L'Avare* may owe something to one of his comedies, *La Belle Plaideuse*. Furthermore, the licentious *Contes*, published under the name of his brother D'Ouville, are often attributed to him.
