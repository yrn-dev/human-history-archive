# Compostella in Navarre

Compostella in Navarre is associated with religious pilgrimage and the history of military religious orders in Spain.

## The Order of St James of Compostella
The Order of Knights of St James of Compostella, also known as the Order of St James of the Sword, is one of the three most ancient orders of Spain. Originally founded as a military religious order, similar to the Hospitallers and the crusading Templars, its purpose was to fight for the true faith against the Moors in Spain. 

Tradition attributes the foundation of the order to Ramiro II, King of Leon, during the 10th century to commemorate a victory over the Moors. However, historical records date the order from a confirmation by Pope Alexander III in 1175. The order acquired great wealth and a significant reputation through its involvement in the wars against the Moors. In 1493, the grand-mastership was annexed by Ferdinand the Catholic, and Pope Adrian VI permanently vested the order in the crown of Spain in 1522.

In the modern era, the order continues to exist as an order of merit and is organized into three classes. The badge of the order consists of a red ribbon and a red lily-hilted sword representing St James.

## Pilgrimage and Visitors
Compostella has served as a destination for pilgrims. Andrew Boorde, an English physician and author born in Sussex, visited Compostella in Navarre on a pilgrimage with others of his nation. Boorde was a well-traveled figure who also visited the universities of Montpellier, Toulouse, Poitiers, and Orleans, and observed surgical practices in Rome.

## Linguistic Records
The location is also referenced in linguistic studies. The *Le Codex de Saint-Jacques de Compostella*, lib. iv., published in Paris in 1882 by J. Vinson and R. P. F. Fita, provides the first Basque vocabulary.
