# Printing and Publications at Bamberg in 1461

## The Printing of Der Edelstein
In 1461, the city of Bamberg saw the printing of a collection of fables titled *Der Edelstein*. The work was written in Middle High German by Ulrich Boner (also known as Bonerius), a 14th-century German-Swiss writer born in Bern. Boner was descended from an old Bernese family and is recorded between the years 1324 and 1349. While he took clerical orders and became a monk, it is certain he received only the "tonsure," as he subsequently married. This status as *clerici uxoriati* allowed him to return to secular life after divesting himself of clerical garb.

*Der Edelstein*, completed around 1349, consists of one hundred fables based primarily on the *Anonymus* and the works of Avianus from the 4th century. Boner dedicated this collection to Johann von Rinkenberg, a poet and Bernese patrician who served as the *Vogt* (advocatus) of Brienz and died circa 1350. The text is characterized by a clear and simple style, utilizing touches of humor to relieve the didactic tone of the collection, and demonstrates originality and freedom in the treatment of its sources. Due to its production in 1461 at Bamberg, it is claimed that *Der Edelstein* was the first book printed in the German language.

## Early Printed Illustrations
The year 1461 also marks a significant development in the visual elements of printed books. Albrecht Pfister produced several small books at Bamberg around 1461 that featured printed illustrations. These early illustrations took the form of rude woodcuts. 

This period of early printing was characterized by a slow development of the title-page, although other aspects of book completion independent of handwork progressed more rapidly. Following the early efforts at Bamberg, illustrations became abundant in books printed at Augsburg in the early 1470s and were common across France, Germany, and the Low Countries by the 1480s. In Italy, the full development of such illustrations occurred later, around 1490. While experiments with copper engraving were attempted in France and Italy during the 15th century, they did not achieve success.

## Context of the City of Bamberg
Bamberg was first mentioned in 902 and developed near the castle of Babenberch, which provided the name for the Babenberg family. Following the extinction of that family, the area passed to the Saxon house. In 1007, Emperor Henry II founded the see. By the middle of the 13th century, the bishops of Bamberg served as princes of the Empire. The see remained so until it was secularized in 1802 and subsequently assigned to Bavaria in 1803.

Additionally, the number 1461 is significant in ancient Egyptian chronology. Because the ancient Egyptian calendar consisted of thirty-day months with five supplementary days added at the end of the year—and lacked intercalation—it lost a fourth of a day annually. This caused the commencement of the year to revolve through the seasons every 1461 years. Consequently, 1461 Egyptian years are equal to 1460 Julian years. This is referred to as a "vague" year. The historian Manetho utilized this Sothiac period of 1461 Egyptian years to construct his system of Egyptian history, including a long series of years governing gods and demigods comprising seventeen such periods.
