# Leopold and the Roman Empire

The historical records of the archive contain references to several figures named Leopold associated with imperial and royal titles across different eras, including the Babenberg dynasty, the Habsburg monarchy, and the Belgian throne.

## Leopold and the Revolutionary Crisis
On October 6, 1700, Leopold was crowned Roman emperor at Frankfort. It was in his capacity as emperor, rather than as a Habsburg, that he first encountered direct antagonism with the France of the Revolution. While his sister, Marie Antoinette, was the wife of Louis XVI, this familial connection did little to strengthen the Franco-Austrian alliance, which had been practically non-existent since 1763.

The conflict was precipitated by the Constituent Assembly's decree of August 4, which abolished feudal rights and affected the possessions of various German princes in Lorraine and Alsace. Leopold protested this move as the sovereign of Germany, a protest that eventually expanded to be made on behalf of Europe. On July 6, 1791, Count Kaunitz issued a circular letter calling for sovereigns to unite against the Revolution; this act marked the beginning of the Concert of Europe and served as a final manifesto for the Holy Roman Empire as a center of political unity.

Although a common policy was proclaimed in the declaration of Pillnitz on August 27, it was undermined by the specific interests of the powers. Both Prussia and Austria were preoccupied with the Polish question, fearing that a crusade against France would leave Poland—which had proclaimed a new constitution on May 3—vulnerable to Russia. Consequently, Leopold initially maintained a moderate stance. However, the crisis escalated when Leopold refused to disperse the corps of *emigres* protected by German princes on the French frontier and continued to insist on the rights of dispossessed princes in Alsace and Lorraine. This led the French Assembly to adopt a decree on January 25, 1792, stating that war would be declared if no satisfactory reply was received from the emperor by March 1.

## Leopold I and the Habsburg Monarchy
Earlier in the 17th century, Leopold I succeeded his father, Ferdinand, in 1657, having already ruled the Austrian dominions for two years. He was crowned emperor the following year and reigned for 48 years. His tenure was characterized by significant internal and external policy shifts for Austria.

His external conflicts included a long struggle with France driven by the ambitions of Louis XIV, which eventually culminated in the War of Spanish Succession. He also engaged in a war with Sweden from 1657 to 1660, ending in the peace of Oliva, which safeguarded the frontier of Hungary and secured Polish independence.

Leopold I also led campaigns against the Turks. The first conflict, caused by Ottoman aggression in Transylvania, ended on August 1, 1664, with Montecuculi's victory at St Gothard on the Raab. However, the peace of Vasvar (August 10) left the Turks in possession of the fortress of Ersekujvar and Nagyvarad, while recognizing Transylvania as an independent principality. A subsequent Turkish war resulted from Leopold's internal policies in Hungary, specifically the suppression of the constitution in 1658 and the persecution of Protestants. In 1683, spurred by Louis XIV and invited by Hungarian malcontents, the Turks overran Hungary and reached the walls of Vienna. The capital was saved on September 12 by a victory gained over the Turks by John Sobieski.

## Other Historical Leopolds
The archive notes other figures of the name Leopold in different contexts:

*   **Leopold of the Babenbergs:** In 976, Leopold, a count in the Donnegau and member of the Franconian Babenberg family, was described as the margrave of the East Mark. He likely received this position as a reward for his fidelity to emperor Otto II during the Bavarian rising of 976. He expanded the area of the mark at the expense of the Hungarians before being succeeded by his son, Henry I, in 994.
*   **Leopold III:** In 1379, Leopold III and his brother Albert III divided their lands; Leopold received Tirol, Carinthia, and Styria. He was killed in 1386 at the battle of Sempach.
*   **Leopold of Saxe-Cobourg-Gotha:** Elected by the Belgian congress on June 4, 1831, he was inaugurated as King of the Belgians on June 21. He had previously fought in the army of the Allies against Napoleon in 1813 and 1814.
*   **Leopold II of Belgium:** The son of Leopold I, he was a pioneer of commercial and colonial expansion for Belgium. Between 1854 and 1865, he undertook extensive tours of Egypt, Palestine, Greece, Italy, Switzerland, Turkey, Asia Minor, Spain, Algeria, Tunisia, Malta, and India.

## Commemorations and Orders
The Order of Leopold was founded in 1808 by emperor Francis I to honor the memory of his father, Leopold II. This order, intended for military and civil service, consists of three classes. Its badge features a red enamelled cross with the imperial crown and the inscription *Integritati et Merito*.
