# Ferdinand Gregorovius and the History of Rome

## Biography of Ferdinand Gregorovius
Ferdinand Gregorovius was a German historian born on January 19, 1821, in Neidenburg. He pursued his studies at the university of Konigsberg and spent several years engaged in teaching before relocating to Italy in 1852. Gregorovius remained in Italy for more than twenty years, during which time he was granted citizenship of Rome. He died in Munich on May 1, 1891.

Beyond his historical research, Gregorovius was a poet. His literary contributions include a drama titled *Der Tod des Tiberius* (1851) and a collection of *Gedichte* published in Leipzig in 1891.

## Geschichte der Stadt Rom im Mittelalter
Gregorovius's extensive acquaintance with Italy and its history led to the creation of his major work, *Geschichte der Stadt Rom im Mittelalter* (published in Stuttgart between 1859 and 1872, with subsequent editions). This erudite work examines the history of Rome from approximately A.D. 400 until the death of Pope Clement VII in 1534. 

According to the author, the text describes the manner in which the historic system of the papacy remained inseparable from that of the Empire from the era of Charles the Great to that of Charles V. The work has been translated into Italian, funded by the Romans (Venice, 1874-1876), and into English by A. Hamilton in a 13-volume set published between 1894 and 1900.

## Other Historical and Literary Works
Gregorovius authored several other significant historical texts:
* *Geschichte des Kaisers Hadrian und seiner Zeit* (Konigsberg, 1851), translated into English by M. E. Robinson in 1898.
* *Corsica* (Stuttgart, 1854), translated into English by R. Martineau in 1855.
* *Lucrezia Borgia* (Stuttgart, 1874), translated into English by J. L. Garner in 1904.
* *Die Grabdenkmaler der Papste* (Leipzig, 1881), translated into English by R. W. Seton-Watson in 1903.
* *Urban VIII. im Widerspruch zu Spanien und dem Kaiser* (Stuttgart, 1879), which the author translated into Italian himself in Rome in 1879.
* *Geschichte der Stadt Athen im Mittelalter* (1889).
* *Kleine Schriften zur Geschichte der Kultur* (Leipzig, 1887-1892).
* *Wanderjahre in Italien*, a five-volume work published in Leipzig between 1888 and 1892.

## Influence and Use in Scholarship
The works of Gregorovius serve as primary authorities for historians researching the Italian Renaissance and the papacy. Specifically, volume vii of *Geschichte der Stadt Rom* (Stuttgart, 1881) is cited as a chief authority for the life of Cesare Borgia. 

Cesare Borgia is described as a clever and unscrupulous adventurer of the Renaissance whose methods of conquest were treacherous and ferocious. Despite these methods, he governed his subjects with justice and firmness, leading his subjects to prefer his rule over local despots and the anarchy of factions. His success was largely attributed to the support of the papacy; following the death of his father, his career ended, demonstrating the unsound basis of his system.
