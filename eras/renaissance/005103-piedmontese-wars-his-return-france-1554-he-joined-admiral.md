# Piedmontese Wars

The Piedmontese wars encompass a series of military conflicts involving the region of Piedmont and the state of Sardinia, spanning from the 16th century through the mid-19th century. These conflicts involved various European powers, including France, Spain, and Austria, and were often intertwined with the broader struggle for Italian independence and unity.

## Early Modern Conflicts and French Involvement
During the 16th century, Piedmont was a site of military campaigns involving French forces. François de Beauvais, Seigneur de Briquemault (c. 1502–1572), conducted his first military campaign in the Piedmontese wars while serving under the count of Brissac. Briquemault later returned to France in 1554, where he joined Admiral Coligny.

In the 17th century, the region continued to be a point of transition for noble families. The de Broglie family, originally Piedmontese, emigrated to France in 1643. François Marie, the head of the family and Comte de Broglie, served as a lieutenant-general and died during the siege of Valenza on July 2, 1656.

## The Spanish-Piedmontese War of the 18th Century
In the mid-18th century, conflict erupted between Spain and Piedmont in the Alps. This campaign was characterized by limited results, with the most notable incident being a combat at Casteldelfino, which was won in person by the king of Sardinia. 

During this period, broader Italian policy was influenced by Spanish interests. A Spanish squadron led by Admiral Don Jose Navarro, supported by a French squadron under M. de Court, moved toward Toulon in 1741. This geopolitical tension coincided with the struggle in Germany between Great Britain (allied with Maria Theresa, queen of Hungary) and France (supporting the Bavarian claimant of the empire). British involvement in the region included the appointment of Sir Thomas Mathews as minister to the court of Turin and commander-in-chief of the fleet.

## 19th Century Wars of Independence
The 19th century saw Piedmont at the center of the Italian revolutionary movement and wars against Austrian domination. 

### The Campaigns of 1848 and 1849
Following the outbreak of war with Austria in 1848, several military figures rose to prominence within the Piedmontese forces. Giacomo Durando, born in Mondovi, Piedmont, took command of the Lombard volunteers as a major-general. His brother, Giovanni Durando, who had previously served in the armies of Spain, Portugal, and Belgium, fought against the Austrians in Venetia until the fall of Vicenza, after which he returned to Piedmont as a major-general. In the 1849 campaign, Giacomo Durando served as aide-de-camp to the king, while Giovanni Durando commanded the first Piedmontese division.

### Political Mobilization and the Role of Volunteers
The wars of this era were supported by Liberal and democratic movements. Marquis Ferdinando Bartolommei, an Italian revolutionist and statesman, established relations with Cavour and Piedmontese liberals while residing in Turin. As war between Piedmont and Austria became imminent, Bartolommei organized and personally funded an expedition of Tuscan volunteers to join the Piedmontese army. He also served as the president of the Tuscan branch of the Societa Nazionale.

## Later Military and Political Developments
The Piedmontese military leadership continued to play a role in international conflicts and domestic governance. Giovanni Durando served in the war of 1859 and the war of 1866, where he commanded the I. Army Corps. Giacomo Durando's career transitioned into statesmanship; he served as the war minister during the Crimean campaign, replacing General La Marmora, and later became a senator and the minister for foreign affairs in the Rattazzi cabinet.
