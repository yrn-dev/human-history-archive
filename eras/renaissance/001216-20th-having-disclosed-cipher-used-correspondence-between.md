# Ciphers and Cryptography

The term "cipher" refers to various systems of secret writing, numerical notation, and symbolic representation used throughout human history for communication, calculation, and identification.

## Historical Development of Numerical Ciphers
The development of numerical ciphers is closely linked to the progress of mathematics. The Babylonians are credited with inventing an extremely simple method of ciphering, or potentially discovering the convenience of the duodecimal system. Their mathematical system utilized units such as the *soss* (60), the *ner* (600), and the *sar* (3600), with the *soss* corresponding to a degree of the equator.

Regarding the origin of modern ciphers (numerals), one theory maintained by M de Villoison suggests they were not inventions of the Arabs or Indians. Instead, these ciphers were used by Latin and Greek arithmeticians before the age of Boethius. Following a period where science was extinct in the West, these systems were adopted by Arabic versions from original manuscripts and later restored to the Latins around the 11th century.

## Secret Correspondence and Decryption
Ciphers have been used extensively to protect sensitive correspondence. In the 16th century, Anthony Babington, an English conspirator and devotee of Mary, queen of Scots, entered into a long correspondence with the queen. Babington was part of a plot involving the priest John Ballard and the Spanish ambassador Mendoza to murder Queen Elizabeth and her ministers to liberate Mary and organize a Roman Catholic rising in England. This correspondence was intercepted by the spies of Walsingham.

The process of making clear that which is hidden in such communications is referred to as a "key." In the context of a cipher, a key is an interpretation regarding the arrangement of words or letters.

## Symbolic and Decorative Ciphers
Beyond secret communication and mathematics, ciphers are used as decorative or identifying marks. This is evident in the design of ceremonial keys used by court officials in several European courts during the 18th and 19th centuries. The terminals of these keys were often decorated with royal monograms, crowns, and ciphers.

## Linguistic Prefixes
In the study of Bantu tongues, specifically in the northern half of the Congo basin and Cameroon, various prefixes are utilized. Within this linguistic family, there is a 20th prefix, *Mu-*. This prefix functions as a preposition meaning "into" or "in" and is often combined with the suffix *-ni*. Unlike other regular prefixes, the 20th prefix does not appear to have a complete concord, as it is used only as a preposition or adjectivally and lacks a pronominal accusative.
