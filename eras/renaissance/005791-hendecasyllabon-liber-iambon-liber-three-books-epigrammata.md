# Hendecasyllabon Liber, Iambon Liber, and Epigrammata

The *Hendecasyllabon Liber*, *Iambon Liber*, and the three books of *Epigrammata* are poetic works authored by Buchanan, who is recognized as one of the greatest scholars of Scotland.

## Author and Linguistic Style
Buchanan was noted for a mastery of the Latin language that was seldom surpassed by any modern writer, writing in Latin as though it were his mother tongue. His literary style was characterized by a certain elasticity and freshness, rather than being rigidly modeled after any specific classical author. In addition to his linguistic command, Buchanan possessed originality of thought and a rich vein of poetical feeling.

## Poetic Works
Buchanan's poetic output was diverse, ranging from academic tragedies to satirical and miscellaneous verse. His contributions include:

*   **Verse Collections:** He produced two sets of verses titled *Hendecasyllabon Liber* and *Iambon Liber*, as well as three books of *Epigrammata*.
*   **Other Poetry:** His other poetic works include *Fratres Fraterrimi*, *Elegiae*, *Silvae*, and a book of miscellaneous verse.
*   **Tragedies and Translations:** He wrote two tragedies, *Baptistes* and *Jephthes*, which earned a European reputation for academic excellence. He also produced translations of the Greek plays and the Psalms, which are described as more than mere versions.
*   **Satirical Works:** His smaller satirical poems are noted for their wit and happy phrasing.

## Other Writings
Beyond his poetry, Buchanan authored several prose works and technical texts:
*   **Satire:** He wrote *Chamaeleon*, a satire written in the vernacular against Maitland of Lethington, which was first printed in 1711.
*   **Educational and Biographical Texts:** He produced a Latin translation of Linacre's Grammar (published in Paris, 1533), *Libettus de Prosodia* (Edinburgh, 1640), and *Vita ab ipso scripta biennio ante mortem* (1608), the latter of which was edited by R. Sibbald in 1702.
*   **Scientific Defense:** He wrote *De Sphaera* in five books. This work was suggested by the poem of Joannes de Sacrobosco and served as a defense of the Ptolemaic theory against the new Copernican view.
