# Bassano

## Geography and History
Bassano is a city located in the province of Vicenza in Venetia, Italy. It is situated at the foot of the Venetian Alps, 30 miles north of Padua and 24 miles northeast of Vicenza by rail. The city is well positioned on the Brenta river, which is spanned at this location by a covered wooden bridge.

The city apparently came into existence around A.D. 1000. Its possession was a matter of dispute between Vicenza and Padua, and it was briefly under the power of Gian Galeazzo Visconti of Milan, who provided fortifications. By the beginning of the 15th century, Bassano passed to Venice. Under Venetian government, the city's industries flourished, particularly the manufacture of majolica—which continues to this day—and its printing-press.

In the 11th century, a German named Eccelin founded the Ezzelini family after obtaining fiefs in the district from Conrad II. The Ezzelini were prominent figures in North Italian history during the 14th and 13th centuries. In the 13th century, the Ezzelini erected a castle in the upper portion of the town above the river, as well as a tower that remains a conspicuous feature. More recently, on September 8, 1796, the city was the site of a battle between the Austrians and the French, resulting in a French victory.

## Demographics and Architecture
As of 1901, the population of the town was 7,553, while the commune had a population of 15,097. The city's architecture includes the church of S. Francesco, which began in the 12th century in the Lombard Romanesque style and was continued in the 13th century in the Gothic style. Some houses in the city still feature traces of paintings on their facades.

## Arts and Culture
The cathedral, the museum, and several other churches in Bassano house paintings by the da Ponte family, who were active in the 16th and early 17th centuries and were surnamed Bassano after their place of birth. The museum also holds letters and drawings by the sculptor Antonio Canova.

The most eminent member of the da Ponte family was Jacopo da Ponte (1510–1592), a Venetian painter born in Bassano. Educated by his father, who was also an artist, Jacopo completed his studies in Venice before returning to settle in Bassano upon his father's death. 

Jacopo's artistic style evolved over his lifetime; he began as a copier of his father before being strongly influenced by the beautiful coloring and lightness of Titian, eventually adopting a style recognized as his own. His work is characterized by:
* **Subjects:** He preferred humble subjects, generally painting landscapes, cattle, villagers, and peasants, along with some historical designs and portraits.
* **Technical Skill:** His work is noted for unexceptionable perspective and chiaroscuro, fresh and brilliant flesh-tints, and well-observed local colors. While his compositions lack grandeur or eloquence, they possess truth and force.
* **Notable Works:** Although he painted few great pictures, his altar-piece of the Nativity at Bassano is highly regarded by judges; Lanzi considers it the finest work of its class.

While Jacopo is said to have finished many pictures, genuine works are rare and valuable, as many purported originals are actually copies by others or by the sons of Bassano.

## Other References to Bassano
The title "duc de Bassano" was held by Maret, who served as Napoleon's minister of Foreign Affairs in the spring of 1811, replacing the duc de Cadore. In this role, Maret concluded treaties between France and Prussia and France and Austria prior to the 1812 invasion of Russia. He remained as Napoleon's private secretary through the campaigns of 1814 and 1815. Following the second restoration of the Bourbons, he was exiled to Grätz before returning to France in 1820. He was made a peer of France by King Louis Philippe after the Revolution of 1830 and died in Paris in 1839.
