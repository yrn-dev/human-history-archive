# BOLEYN, ANNE (c. 1507-1536)

Anne Boleyn was a Queen of England and the second wife of King Henry VIII. She was the daughter of Sir Thomas Boleyn—who was later created earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde in 1529—and Elizabeth, the daughter of Thomas Howard, earl of Surrey and later duke of Norfolk. According to Camden, Anne was born in 1507, although other writers have suggested earlier dates, specifically 1501 or 1502.

## Early Life and Time in France
In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France, where she served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I. She returned to England in either 1521 or 1522.

Upon her return, Anne attracted many suitors and admirers. Among these was the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt, who some sources describe as her mistress. Another suitor was Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland; however, King Henry VIII stopped a marriage between Anne and Percy, providing Sir James Butler as an alternative match. Despite this, Anne remained unmarried.

## Relationship with Henry VIII
The exact start of Anne's relationship with Henry VIII is unknown, with some surmising it began as early as 1523. However, there is no proof that the king's passion existed prior to May 1527, when proceedings for his divorce from Catherine of Aragon began. Between 1522 and 1525, Henry bestowed various favors and grants upon Anne's father, which some have interpreted as a symptom of the king's affection.

Unlike her sister Mary, who is believed by contemporary accounts to have fallen victim to the king's solicitations, Anne had no intention of becoming Henry's mistress. Her conduct was governed by ambition, as she intended to be his queen. Hever Castle, the seat of the Boleyns, served as the scene of Henry VIII's courtship of Anne.

## Ascent to the Throne
Anne's name was first openly linked to the king as a potential wife in a letter dated August 16, 1527, written by Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, to Charles V. During this time, Cardinal Wolsey opposed the alliance and instead negotiated a marriage for Henry with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII. Henry, however, acted independently, sending his secretary William Knight to Rome to seek facilities for a marriage with Anne.

Henry VIII's desire to divorce his first wife in favor of Anne Boleyn coincided with a broader political and religious shift. In 1530, Henry joined the company of Protestant princes, a move driven by his desire for the divorce and his wish to loot the vast wealth of the English church.

## Family and Legacy
Anne Boleyn was the aunt of Catherine, the daughter of William Carey. Catherine later married Francis Knollys, a member of parliament and privy councillor under Queen Elizabeth.
