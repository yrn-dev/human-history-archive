# Ossory

The name Ossory appears in the historical archives as a geographical region, a religious jurisdiction, and as a title and name associated with the Butler family and the dukedom of Ormonde.

## Geography and Religious Jurisdictions
Ossory was formerly a kingdom, a portion of which later became the county of Kilkenny. Within this region, the Cistercian monastery at Jerpoint is said to have been founded by Dunnough, who was a King of Ossory. In modern administrative and religious terms, the area is divided into the Protestant diocese of Ossory and the Roman Catholic dioceses of Ossory and Kildare and Leighlin.

## The Earldom of Ossory and the Butler Family
The title of Earl of Ossory is linked to the Butler family in Ireland. In 1522, Sir Piers Butler—known as "red Piers"—served as the lord deputy and chief governor of Ireland. Following an agreement with the co-heirs of the 7th earl of Ormonde, the earldom of Ormonde was placed at the king's disposal, and on February 23, 1527/8, Sir Piers Butler was created the earl of Ossory. 

By 1536, the Irish estates were declared forfeit to the crown under the Act of Absentees, but they were granted to him as "earl of Ossory and Ormonde." He later received a patent for the Ormonde earldom around February 22, 1537/8. His son and heir, James the Lame, who had been created Viscount Thurles in 1535/6, obtained an act of parliament in 1543/4 that confirmed the earldom grant and restored the "pre-eminence" of the ancient earldom of 1328.

## Lord Ossory and the Duke of Ormonde
In the 17th century, the line of Ormondes included James Butler, a marquess (created in 1632) and later a duke of Ormonde (created in 1661 in Ireland and 1682 in England). He served as lord-lieutenant and was a loyal soldier and statesman who proclaimed Charles II in 1649.

The duke's son, Ossory, is described as "the eldest hope with every grace adorned." He was known for his bravery and his protective relationship with his father. In 1670, a company of ruffians attempted to assassinate the duke of Ormonde by dragging him from his coach in St James's Street to take him to the gallows at Tyburn. During this period, Ossory issued a threat that if any harm befell his father, he would pistol Buckingham, even if Buckingham were positioned behind the king's chair. This threat is credited as a possible reason the duke was saved from assassination. Ossory died eight years before his father.

## Political Conflicts with Buckingham
Lord Ossory was also involved in the political intrigues of the 17th century involving Buckingham and the chancellor Clarendon. During debates regarding a 1666 bill to prohibit the import of Irish cattle—a bill Buckingham supported to oppose Clarendon and the duke of Ormonde—Buckingham claimed that anyone opposing the bill possessed either an "Irish understanding" or an "Irish interest." Lord Ossory challenged Buckingham on this assertion. While Buckingham avoided the encounter, Ossory was sent to the Tower.
