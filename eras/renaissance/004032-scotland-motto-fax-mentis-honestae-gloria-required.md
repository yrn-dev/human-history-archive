# Scotland and the Motto "Fax mentis honestae gloria"

## The Baronetage of Nova Scotia
The baronetage of Nova Scotia was established in 1624. This system was designed as a method to promote the "plantation" of the province of Nova Scotia. King James announced an intention to create one hundred baronets to facilitate this goal. Under the terms of the plan, each baronet was required to support six colonists for a period of two years. Alternatively, they could pay 2,000 marks in place of this support. Additionally, they were required to pay 1,000 marks to Sir William Alexander, who had been granted the province by charter in 1621 and later became the earl of Stirling.

In exchange for these contributions, Sir William Alexander was to receive a "free barony" consisting of 16,000 acres in Nova Scotia and was to be designated a baronet of "his Hienes Kingdom of Scotland."

## Implementation under Charles I
Following the death of King James, Charles I carried out the scheme. The first Scottish baronet was created on May 28, 1625. The creation charter for these baronets, whether "of Scotland or of Nova Scotia," included several specific covenants:
* The total number of baronets was never to exceed one hundred and fifty.
* The heirs apparent of the baronets were to be knighted upon reaching the age of adulthood.
* The honor was reserved for those who fulfilled the financial conditions, specifically the payment of 3,000 marks (equivalent to £166, 13s. 4d.) toward the plantation of the colony.

## The Badge and Motto
On November 17, 1629, King Charles I wrote to the "contractors for baronets." He acknowledged that these contractors had advanced significant sums to Sir William Alexander for the plantation, relying on the security of payments from future baronets. To provide further inducement for applicants, the king empowered the contractors to offer additional incentives.

On that same date, the king granted all Nova Scotia baronets the right to wear a specific badge. This badge was suspended around the neck by an orange tawny ribbon and featured an azure saltire with a crowned inescutcheon of the arms of Scotland. The badge bore the motto "Fax mentis honestae gloria."

## Expansion and Cessation
Because the required number of baronets could not be completed through the initial criteria, Charles I announced in 1633 that the honor could be extended to gentlemen from Ireland and England. These individuals began receiving the honor in 1634. Despite this expansion, the king was only able to create a total of slightly more than one hundred and twenty baronets.

The link between the honor and the grant of lands in Nova Scotia ended in 1638. Following the union with England in 1707, the creation of Scottish baronetcies ceased. From that point forward, both Scotsmen and Englishmen received baronetcies of Great Britain.
