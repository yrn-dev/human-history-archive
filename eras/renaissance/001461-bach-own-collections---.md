# Bach's Collections

Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750) was a German musical composer whose work spanned various forms, including organ music, instrumental collections, and church cantatas. During his lifetime, the majority of the works published were instrumental collections, most of which Bach engraved himself. Among his church cantatas, only one—*Gott ist mein Koenig*, written when he was nineteen—was published while he was alive.

## Organ Music Collections
Bach's organ music is categorized into several distinct collections and groups, in addition to the material found in the third part of the *Klavier-Uebung*:

*   **Sonatas and Preludes:** His collections include six sonatas and four groups consisting of six organ preludes and fugues each.
*   **Das Orgelbuechlein:** This is a collection of short choral-preludes. While Bach carefully planned the volume—heading all blank autograph pages with the titles of intended chorales—the work was not half executed. Had it been completed, it would have been a larger volume than the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier*.
*   **Chorale-Preludes:** Bach produced 18 larger chorale-preludes, a group that includes his final composition. Additionally, he wrote the six "Schuebler" chorales, which were all arranged from movements of cantatas.
*   **Other Works:** Beyond these collections, Bach composed three great independent toccatas and the Passacaglia. His remaining choral-preludes fill one *Jahrgang*, while his other organ works fill two more.

## Unclassified Instrumental Works
Two significant instrumental volumes are considered unclassifiable:

*   **Das musikalische Opfer:** A volume of compositions based on a theme given to Bach by Frederick the Great. It contains various puzzle-canons, two great fugues, and a trio for figured bass, violin, and flute.
*   **Die Kunst der Fuge:** This is a progressive series of fugues based on a single subject. Written in open score as abstract studies, the work is noted for its great musical value and admirable clavier style, with the exception of the extreme contrapuntal *tours de force*.

## Other Notable Compositions and Arrangements
Bach's body of work includes the *Magnificat*, described as one of his most profound and terse compositions, and the *Passion according to St Matthew*. He also wrote *Der Streit zwischen Phoebus und Pan*, in which he utilized the grand Italian operatic style for Pan's prize-aria to answer critics who accused him of provincial ignorance and pedantry.

Bach was known for constantly rearranging his own compositions, viewing the ability to adapt a work to a fresh environment as a test of its quality. He demonstrated a unique power to reproduce perfect works in different media, adding parts to complete contrapuntal and harmonic schemes or adapting the same music to different words.

## Legacy and Publication
Following Bach's death, his work was rediscovered through the efforts of figures such as Mendelssohn, Schumann, Wesley, and Crotch. In 1850, a society was established to ensure the correct publication of his remaining works. Wilhelm Rust, during his fourteen-year editorship of the *Bach-Gesellschaft*, provided critical insight into Bach's style and restored valuable harmonies from incomplete texts.

Bach's influence extended to later composers; for example, the *Passion according to St Matthew* influenced Mendelssohn's design of oratorios, and Wagner's *Leitmotif* schemes were foreshadowed by Bach's polyphonic textures and harmonization. Additionally, Bach's *Goldberg* variations are noted as one of the longest pianoforte works extant.
