## The Bannatyne Club
The publication of Sir James Melville's *Memoirs* was made possible by the Bannatyne Club, an organization founded in Edinburgh in 1823. The club was established to honor George Bannatyne (1545–?1608), a native of Newtyle, Forfarshire, and an Edinburgh merchant who became a burgess in 1587. Bannatyne is noted for his role as a collector of Scottish poems; during a period when the "pest" raged in the capital in 1568, he retired to his native county and transcribed poems from 15th and early 16th century Scots poets. 

His work, which spanned eight hundred folio pages across five parts, became known as the "Bannatyne Manuscript." This manuscript is one of the chief repositories of Middle Scots poetry, preserving the texts of poets such as Alexander Scott, Lyndsay, Dunbar, and Henryson. The manuscript passed from Bannatyne to his daughter Janet, then to the Foulises of Woodhall and Ravelston, and finally to the Advocates' library.

The Bannatyne Club devoted itself to publishing historical and literary material derived from Scottish sources. In addition to Sir James Melville's *Memoirs*, the club's publications include:
* *Memorials of George Bannatyne* (1829), featuring an account of the manuscript by David Laing and a memoir by Sir Walter Scott.
* *Letters and Journals of Robert Baillie* (1637–1662), published in three volumes between 1841 and 1842, edited by D. Laing.
* George Baillie's *Correspondence* (1702–1708), edited by Lord Minto in 1842.
* *Statuta Ecclesiae Scoticanae* (1866).
* Despatches and papers of Mackay, published in 1883.
* The ballad "Auld Robin Gray," written by Lady Anne Barnard in 1772, which was edited for the club by Sir Walter Scott.

## Calendar of State Papers
The *Calendar of State Papers* (Cal. of State) and related registers provide essential documentary evidence for the public career of various historical figures. For instance, the public career of Robert Aytoun is documented within the printed *Calendars of State Papers* and the *Register of the Privy Council* of his period.

The archive's sources identify several specific series of these papers:
* **Scottish Series:** This includes the *Register of Privy Council of Scotland* (volumes i–iii) and the *Cal. of State Papers (Scottish)*, specifically cited in volume ii (pages 218 and 250). Other volumes of the *Scottish Series* were handled by Thorpe (volumes i and ii) and Bain (volumes ii–iv).
* **Foreign Series:** The *Cal. of State Papers (Foreign)* includes records from 1579–1580.
* **Domestic Series:** The *Cal. of State Papers, Dom. Series* contains numerous references to figures such as Bristol.

These calendars are often used in conjunction with other archival materials, such as *The Border Papers* (vol. i) and the *Hamilton Papers* (vol. ii, Foreign), to provide a comprehensive bibliography for Scottish historical research.
