# Conquest of Guinea

The early Portuguese voyages of discovery along the African coast and within the ocean are documented primarily through the works of the Portuguese chronicler Gomes Eannes de Azurara. His writing serves as the central authority for the historical record of these expeditions, particularly those conducted under the auspices of Prince Henry the Navigator.

## The Chronicle of Azurara
Gomes Eannes de Azurara was a notable Portuguese chronicler who entered the royal library during the reign of King Duarte (1433-1438), serving as an assistant to Fernao Lopes. By 1452, Azurara held sole charge of the library, and on June 6, 1454, he succeeded Lopes as the royal chronicler and chief keeper of the archives.

In 1453, Azurara completed the first draft of the *Chronicle of the Discovery and Conquest of Guinea*. This work provides a geographical and biographical account of the early Portuguese voyages down the African coast, with a specific focus on the life work of Prince Henry the Navigator. Azurara's approach to history was influenced by the first Renaissance; he frequently utilized philosophical reflections and quotations from medieval, early Christian, and classical writers. His scholarship extended to the astrology and geographical systems of the ancients, as well as the romances and chronicles of Europe and the works of Spanish and Italian authors.

## Portuguese Maritime Expansion
The Portuguese maritime adventures along the west African coast and in the eastern Atlantic did not occur in isolation. They were preceded in the early fifteenth, fourteenth, and thirteenth centuries by the Genoese, Catalonians, and Normans. 

Beyond the African coast, Portuguese exploration extended further into the East. Voyages toward the North-East, moving beyond the Golden Chersonese, brought knowledge of various islands and provinces of India. According to the geography of the Portuguese gentleman John de Barros, these voyages reached the Land of China. Portuguese explorers ascertained that the coast of China runs South-West and North-East, with the city of Canton located at 30⅔° of latitude. Further exploration identified three sea-board provinces: Zanton, Mangi, and Quinzai, the latter being the King's Residence and the principal city, situated at 46° of latitude.

## Other African and Global Engagements
In addition to the conquest of Guinea, Portuguese military and colonial efforts extended to other regions. In North Africa, Azurara documented the history of Ceuta, described as "the land-gate of the East," from its capture in 1415 until 1437. He also wrote a chronicle regarding the deeds of D. Duarte de Menezes, the captain of Alcacer, for which Azurara spent a year in Africa collecting materials and studying the locations of the events.

In India, the Portuguese established an episcopal church named St. Thome in the city of Malabar (or Meliapour), located a league from Madras. In the region of the Gulf of Cambay, the Portuguese established a settlement at Damaun. The town of Damaun was first sacked and burnt by the Portuguese in 1531, then taken again in 1558, leading to a permanent settlement where a mosque was converted into a Christian church. The territory of Damaun proper was conquered by the Portuguese in 1559, and the area of Nagar Havili was later ceded to them by the Mahrattas in 1780 as indemnification for piracy.
