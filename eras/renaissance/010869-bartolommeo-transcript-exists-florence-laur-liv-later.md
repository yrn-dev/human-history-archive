# Asconius Pedianus and the Preservation of His Works

## Life and Career of Asconius Pedianus
Quintus Asconius Pedianus was a Roman historian and grammarian who lived from approximately 9 B.C. to A.D. 76 (or alternatively A.D. 3 to 88). He was likely a native of Patavium, known today as Padua. In his later years, Asconius resided in Rome, where he eventually died at the age of eighty-five after having spent twelve years in blindness.

During the reigns of the emperors Nero and Claudius, Asconius compiled historical commentaries on the speeches of Cicero. To produce these works, he drew from a wide array of sources, including the *Acta Publica* (the Gazette), the *commentarii* (shorthand reports or "skeletons") of Cicero's unpublished speeches, and the life of Cicero written by Tiro. He also utilized letters and speeches from contemporaries of Cicero, as well as various historical writers such as Antias, Tuditanus, Atticus, Varro, and Fenestella; the latter was a contemporary of Livy whom Asconius frequently criticized.

## The Historical Commentaries
Only five of Asconius's commentaries are preserved, though they exist in a very mutilated condition. These include the commentaries *in Pisonem*, *pro Scauro*, *pro Milone*, *pro Cornelio*, and *in toga Candida*. These notes are written in good Latin and focus primarily on antiquarian, historical, and legal matters. 

The dating of these works can be narrowed through internal evidence. In a note regarding the speech *pro Scauro*, Asconius refers to Longus Caecina (who died in A.D. 57) as still living, while his phrasing implies that Claudius (who died in A.D. 54) was no longer alive. Consequently, this specific statement must have been written between A.D. 54 and A.D. 57.

It should be noted that another commentary on Cicero's Verrine orations exists; however, this work is universally regarded as spurious due to its grammatical character and inferior Latinity.

## Transmission and Manuscripts
The works of Asconius were rediscovered in 1416 by Poggio, who found them in a manuscript at St Gallen. Although the original St Gallen manuscript has since been lost, three transcripts were created by Poggio, Bartolommeo da Montpulciano, and Zomini (also known as Sozomenus) of Pistoia.

The current locations of these transcripts are as follows:
*   The transcript by Poggio is located in Madrid (Matritensis x. 81).
*   The transcript by Zomini is held in the Forteguerri library at Pistoia (No. 37).
*   A copy of the transcript made by Bartolommeo exists in Florence (Laur. liv. 5).

Manuscripts produced later than these three are derived from the copy made by Poggio.

## Other Attributed Works
Beyond the historical commentaries on Cicero, several other works have been attributed to Asconius Pedianus. These include a treatise on long life and health—which may have been a symposium written in imitation of Plato—a defense of Virgil against those who detracted from him, and a biography of Sallust.
