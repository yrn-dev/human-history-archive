# John Stewart, Duke of Albany

## Political Role and Regency
John Stewart, Duke of Albany, was a central figure in the political instability of early 16th-century Scotland. As the next heir to the Scottish crown after the sons of Margaret, he led the faction that favored an alliance with France. This put him in direct opposition to the English faction, which was largely supported by Margaret.

Following the death of James IV on September 9, 1513, at the war that ended at Flodden, Margaret was appointed sole guardian of the infant King James V. However, the conflict between the French and English factions grew nearly to the level of civil war when Albany returned to Scotland from France in the spring of 1514 at the invitation of the Scottish parliament.

The political situation shifted further when Margaret privately married Archibald Douglas, Earl of Angus, on August 6, 1514. This marriage alienated powerful nobility, including the earls of Home and Arran, and provided the council with a pretext to remove Margaret from the guardianship of the king and the regency in July 1515. To secure the surrender of the king, Albany was required to blockade Margaret in Stirling Castle.

## Conflicts with the House of Douglas
Albany's regency was marked by friction with Gavin Douglas. After Albany assumed the regency, he used intercepted correspondence and the "purchase" of the bishopric of Dunkeld from Rome—which was contrary to statute—as a basis to attack Douglas. Consequently, Douglas was imprisoned in several locations, including the castles of St Andrews, Dunbar, and Edinburgh, before the pope intervened to secure his release.

By July 1516, a diplomatic peace was established between Albany and Douglas. In May 1517, the bishop of Dunkeld accompanied Albany to France to conduct negotiations that resulted in the treaty of Rouen.

## Later Influence and Relations with Margaret
Albany's frequent absences in France, where he was detained by Francis I at the request of Henry VIII, allowed internal noble feuds to escalate. This included a plot by the earl of Arran to seize the earl of Angus. During this period, Margaret began to agitate for a divorce from Angus, a move in which Albany likely aided her.

When Albany returned to Scotland in November 1521 with a large body of French men-at-arms, his association with Margaret led to accusations that he intended to marry her and suggestions that she was his mistress. His arrival forced Angus, Gavin Douglas, and others to flee to the Borders.

Supported strongly by the Scottish parliament, Albany's presence compelled Angus to withdraw to France until 1524. However, Albany was himself obliged to retire to France in May 1524.

## Military Activities
Beyond his political administration, Albany was involved in military operations. He brought French auxiliaries to Scotland, and among those who joined these forces was the humanist George Buchanan. Buchanan took part in an unsuccessful inroad into England under the leadership of the duke of Albany.
