# Art and Figures of London

The city of London has served as a significant center for the exhibition of international art, the residence of diverse painters, and a gathering place for literary and social celebrities.

## Paintings in London Galleries
The National Gallery in London houses works by the Venetian painter Paris Bordone (1495–1570), including a portrait of a lady and a piece titled "Daphnis and Chloe." Bordone, who entered Titian's *bottega* in 1509, was known for a nobility of style and a golden harmony of colour derived from Titian. While his nudes are described as slightly coarse and his figures occasionally unnatural, he was considered an excellent portraitist. Additionally, a "Holy Family" by Bordone is located at Bridgwater House.

## Portraiture and Artists in London
Various international and local artists have left their mark on the city's portraiture:

*   **Michael Dahl (1656–1743):** A Swedish portrait painter born in Stockholm. After studying under Ernst Klocke and spending time in Paris and Rome—where he painted Queen Christina—Dahl returned to England in 1688. He became a rival to Kneller and eventually died in London. His portraits are found in many houses, including those at Petworth and Hampton Court.
*   **Samuel Dirksz van Hoogstraten (1627–1678):** A Dutch painter and pupil of Rembrandt. As part of a round of travels in 1651, Hoogstraten visited London, among other cities such as Rome and Vienna.
*   **Samuel Lover (1797–1868):** An Irish artist, novelist, and musician who moved to London around 1835. A member of the Royal Hibernian Academy, Lover painted Lord Brougham in his robes as lord chancellor.
*   **Count Alfred Guillaume Gabriel D'Orsay (1801–1852):** A French dandy and artist who resided in London with the widowed Countess of Blessington. Their home, located first at Seamore Place and later at Gore House, became a hub for London's fashionable artistic and literary society. D'Orsay produced numerous portraits and statuettes of his friends, noted for their truthfulness and vigour.
*   **Sir Joshua Reynolds:** A prominent painter who produced a fine portrait of Mary, Countess of Cork and Orrery.

## Literary and Social Circles
London's social landscape was often intertwined with its artistic community. Mary Monckton, the Countess of Cork and Orrery (1746–1840), maintained a home in London that served as a favorite meeting place for literary celebrities. Her frequent guests included Dr. Johnson, Reynolds, Burke, Horace Walpole, and Sheridan. In her later years as Lady Cork, her circle expanded to include figures such as Byron, Sir Walter Scott, Lord John Russell, Sir Robert Peel, Canning, Castlereagh, Theodore Hook, and Sydney Smith.

## The Legend of Daphnis
The subject of "Daphnis and Chloe" refers to the legendary hero of the shepherds of Sicily. According to authorities such as Aelian and Diodorus Siculus, Daphnis was the son of a Sicilian nymph and Hermes. Born or exposed in a grove of laurels, he was raised by shepherds or nymphs and became a master of the syrinx. 

The legend tells of his love for a nymph who warned him that infidelity would lead to blindness; when he broke this promise, he was smitten with blindness. He died shortly thereafter, with accounts varying on whether he fell from a cliff, was transformed into a rock, or was taken to heaven by Hermes. Theocritus provides a different account, focusing on Daphnis's victory in a poetical competition, his amour with Nais, and his love for Xenea. In later traditions, he is identified as a Phrygian hero and the teacher of Marsyas.
