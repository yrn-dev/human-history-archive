# Protector Somerset and the Events of 1547

## Military Campaigns in Scotland
In 1547, Protector Somerset led a military campaign known as the Pinkie campaign. This operation included the battle of Pinkie, where the English army was victorious. Among the leaders of the army during this battle was William, 13th Lord Grey de Wilton, who gained significant fame as a soldier. Following the battle of Pinkie, Protector Somerset conferred the dignity of banneret upon Sir Ralph Sadler, Sir Francis Bryan, and Sir Ralph Fane. This is considered by the better opinion to be the final occasion on which the dignity of banneret was granted.

Other figures associated with the campaign included Cecil, who accompanied Protector Somerset and served as one of two "judges of the Marshalsea" in the courts-martial; the other judge was William Patten. Both Cecil and Patten began writing independent accounts of the campaign, with Cecil sharing his notes for Patten's narrative.

## Administration of Forces in St Andrews
During this period, an individual named Balnaves became a paid agent for the English cause in Scotland. Although he did not participate in the murder of Beton, he was a highly active defender of the castle of St Andrews. In 1547, Protector Somerset granted Balnaves an annuity of L125 and appointed him as the English paymaster of the forces in St Andrews. 

Balnaves remained under English support even after the castle of St Andrews surrendered to the French in July, resulting in his capture and transport as a prisoner to Rouen. Protector Somerset continued Balnaves' pension and made unsuccessful attempts to secure his release.

## Legal and Political Context
The year 1547 also saw legislative changes in English law regarding the "benefit of clergy." An act passed in 1547 stipulated that any person entitled to the benefit of clergy would be allowed it regardless of whether they had been married multiple times to a single woman, several women, or to widows.

Politically, the Protector's influence extended to various military and administrative figures. William, 13th Lord Grey de Wilton, for example, was later imprisoned in 1551 due to his association as a friend of the fallen protector, the duke of Somerset. The duke of Somerset himself was eventually executed by beheading in 1552.
