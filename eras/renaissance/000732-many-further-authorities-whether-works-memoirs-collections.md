# Historical Works, Memoirs, and Collections

The preservation of history and scientific discovery relies upon a diverse array of authorities, ranging from formal academic works and official collections of documents to personal memoirs and correspondence. These sources provide the primary evidence for the study of statesmen, scientific progress, and the personal lives of historical figures.

## Historical and Political Authorities

In the study of the Austrian Empire, a vast literature exists regarding race and language. Legal questions on these topics are detailed in Josef Ulbrith and Ernst Mischler's *Oesterr. Staatswoerterbuch* (published in three volumes between 1894 and 1897, with a second edition in 1904). Other notable works include Dummreicher's *Suedostdeutsche Betrachtungen* (1893), the works of Hainisch and Herkner titled *Die Zukunft der Deutsch-Oesterreicher* (1892 and 1893 respectively), L. Leger's *La Save, le Danube et le Balkan* (1884), Bressnitz von Sydacoff's *Die panslavistische Agitation* (1899), and Bertrand Auerbach's *Les Races et les nationalites en Autriche-Hongrie* (1898). For biographical data on the empire, C. von Wurzbach's *Biographisches Lexikon des Kaisertums Oesterreich* (60 volumes, 1856-1891) and the *Allgemeine deutsche Biographie* serve as primary resources.

Other significant historical authorities include the *Memoires* of Castelnau, which cover the eleven years from 1559 to 1570. Written for his son during his final embassy to England, these memoirs are noted for their impartiality, veracity, and clearness. They were first printed in 1621 and later expanded in 1659 by Le Laboureur and in 1731 by Jean Godefroy.

## Memoirs and Personal Records

Memoirs often serve as essential tools for reconstructing the lives of political figures and military campaigns. Baron Pierre Emmanuel Albert Ducasse, a French historian, utilized papers from the imperial family to compile the *Memoires du prince Eugene* (1858-1860) and the *Refutation des memoires du duc de Raguse* (1857). Ducasse also wrote *Memoires pour servir a l'histoire de la campagne de 1812 en Russie* (1852) and *Memoires du roi Joseph* (1853-1855). While some of his later works, such as *Les Dessous du coup d'etat* (1891) and *Souvenirs d'un officier du 2^e Zouaves*, contain piquant anecdotes, they are sometimes characterized as tittle-tattle.

Personal memoirs also provide insight into the relationship between individuals and monarchs. Friedrich Melchior Grimm wrote a *Memoire historique* regarding his attachment to Empress Catherine II, which was published in 1880 in the Collection of the Russian Imperial Historical Society. This was accompanied by Catherine's correspondence with Grimm (1774-1796), in which she addressed him familiarly as "Georges Dandin" or "Heraclite."

## Scientific Works and Collections

Scientific authorities often combine published treatises with physical collections. René Louiche Desfontaines, a French botanist, published the two-volume *Flora Atlantica sive historia plantarum quae in Atlante, agro Tunetano el Algeriensi crescunt* in 1798, following a scientific expedition to North Africa. He also produced the *Tableau de l'école botanique du muséum d'histoire naturelle de Paris* (1804), later titled *Catalogus plantarum horti regii Parisiensis* (1831). His Barbary collection was left to the Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle.

In the field of geology, Dr. Buckland's reputation is based on his scientific works, most notably *Reliquiae Diluvianae* (1823), which examined organic remains in caves and fissures to discuss the Biblical account of the Deluge. He also authored a volume on "Geology and Mineralogy" as part of the *Bridgewater Treatises* (1836), designed to show the "goodness, wisdom, and power of God, as manifested in the Creation."

## Collected Works and Correspondence

The publication of collected works often includes supplementary memoirs to provide biographical context. For example, the collected works of William King were published with *Historical Notes and Memoirs of the Author* by John Nichols in 1776. Similarly, the collected works of a Baptist mission figure were accompanied by a *Memoir* compiled by Dr. Ryland, and later a memoir prefixed by his son in 1852. Another set of collected works (published 1811-1812 in New York) was republished in 1850 with a *Memoir* by Rev. Tryon Edwards.

Correspondence serves as another critical authority. The *Correspondance litteraire, philosophique et critique* of Grimm (1753-1769) underwent several editions, including a 15-volume "Nouvelle Edition" by M. Jules Taschereau in 1829 and a standard 16-volume edition by M. Tourneux (1877-1882). Bolingbroke's political and philosophical writings, including essays on the "Rise and Progress of Monotheism" and "Authority in Matters of Religion," were published in various editions between 1754 and 1841. His letters are preserved across multiple sources, including the *Marchmont Papers*, the *Hardwick State Papers*, and collections in the British Museum.
