# Anne Boleyn

Anne Boleyn (c. 1507–1536) was the Queen of England and the second wife of King Henry VIII. She was the daughter of Elizabeth, daughter of Thomas Howard (the earl of Surrey and later duke of Norfolk), and Sir Thomas Boleyn, who was later created earl of Wiltshire and Ormonde in 1529.

## Early Life and Education
While Camden records her birth year as 1507, some later writers have ascribed her birth to an earlier date, specifically 1501 or 1502, though this has not been conclusively proven. In 1514, Anne accompanied Mary Tudor to France for the princess's marriage to Louis XII. Following the death of the king, Anne remained in France and served as one of the women in waiting to Queen Claude, the wife of Francis I.

## Return to England and Courtship
Anne returned to England in 1521 or 1522. During this period, she attracted many suitors and admirers, including the poet Sir Thomas Wyatt. Henry Percy, the heir of the earl of Northumberland, was among her suitors, but the king stopped a marriage between them and instead provided a match for her in Sir James Butler. Despite this, Anne remained unmarried.

Between 1522 and 1525, King Henry VIII bestowed a series of favors and grants upon Anne's father; some have interpreted these as symptoms of the king's affection for Anne, though this is viewed doubtfully. Unlike her sister Mary, who had fallen victim to the king's solicitations, Anne had no intention of becoming the king's mistress. Her conduct was seemingly governed by ambition, as she intended to be the king's queen.

## Relationship with Henry VIII
The exact beginning of the relationship between Anne and Henry VIII is unknown. Some have surmised it began as early as 1523, but there is no proof that the king's passion existed prior to the divorce proceedings initiated in May 1527. The love letters associated with the pair are undated, and a well-known letter attributed to Anne is now recognized as an Elizabethan forgery.

Anne's name first appeared openly as a possible wife for the king—contingent upon the divorce of Catherine of Aragon—in a letter dated August 16, 1527, sent by Mendoza, the imperial ambassador, to Charles V. During this time, Cardinal Wolsey opposed the alliance and attempted to negotiate a marriage for Henry with Renee, the daughter of Louis XII.

## Marriage and Titles
In 1532, Anne Boleyn was created marchioness of Pembroke. The marriage between Anne and Henry VIII was commonly antedated to November 14, 1532, although according to Cranmer, the only authority on the matter, he only became aware of it a fortnight later.

The marriage was preceded by a dispensation granted to Henry, which included the suspension of all canons regarding impediments created by "affinity rising *ex illicito coitu* in any degree even in the first." While some sources, such as the *Chronicle of King Henry VIII*, claim she had been the king's mistress, there is no direct proof of this; the claim rests primarily on contemporary belief and the specific terms of the aforementioned dispensation. Froude rejects this narrative entirely.

## Political and Religious Context
Henry VIII's desire to divorce his first wife in favor of Anne Boleyn, combined with a wish to loot the wealth of the English church and turn against the Emperor in favor of Francis I, led him to join the company of Protestant princes in 1530. This occurred despite Henry having previously written a book against heresy and receiving the title "Defender of the Faith" from the Pope.
