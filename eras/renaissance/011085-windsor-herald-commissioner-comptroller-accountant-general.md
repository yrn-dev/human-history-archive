# Windsor Herald, Commissioner, Comptroller and Accountant-General of Excise

The titles of Windsor herald, commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise were held by Elias Ashmole, an English antiquarian and the founder of the Ashmolean Museum at Oxford.

## Elias Ashmole
Born in Lichfield on May 23, 1617, to a saddler, Elias Ashmole began his professional life as a solicitor in 1638. By 1644, he had been appointed as a commissioner of excise. During the period when the Royalist Parliament sat at Oxford, Ashmole formed a friendship with Captain George Wharton (later Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole received the king's commission as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse.

Ashmole's personal life included a marriage in 1649 to Lady Mainwaring, a relative of his first wife who had died eight years prior. Lady Mainwaring was approximately twenty years his senior. This union provided Ashmole with the affluence necessary to dedicate his time to his studies. While he initially developed an interest in astrology—prompted by William Lilly and George Wharton—his focus eventually shifted toward antiquarian research and heraldry. Although his wife petitioned for a separation in 1657, she later returned to live with him and remained so until her death in 1668.

## Court Appointments and the Order of the Garter
Between the domestic crisis of 1657 and 1668, Ashmole enjoyed significant favor at court. During this time, he was successively appointed to several roles, including:
* Windsor herald
* Commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

Ashmole's involvement with heraldry extended to the Order of the Garter. He published a comprehensive work in 1772 titled *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*, which was the result of years of patient research. Despite his expertise, he refused the office of Garter king-at-arms, allowing the position to go to Sir William Dugdale, whose daughter Ashmole married in 1668.

The Order of the Garter is a "most noble" order with five officers: the prelate, chancellor, registrar, king of arms, and usher. The role of the king of arms, known as the Garter Principal King of Arms, originally had its duties discharged by the Windsor herald. The order's chapel is located at St George's Chapel, Windsor.

## Later Contributions
In 1677, Ashmole presented the university of Oxford with the Ashmolean Museum, the first public museum of curiosities in the kingdom. Much of the collection had been inherited from his friend, John Tradescant. Ashmole stipulated that a suitable building must be constructed to house the collection, a process that was not completed until 1683. He also made a further gift of his library to the university.
