# William Count Slavata

William Count Slavata (1572–1652) was a government official and historian who served the house of Habsburg. He is noted for his involvement in the political upheavals of early 17th-century Bohemia and his subsequent historical writings.

## Political Career and the Bohemian Uprising
Slavata was a faithful servant of the Habsburgs. His political career was marked by the Bohemian uprising, which began in 1618. During the onset of this conflict, Slavata was among the government officials who were thrown from the windows of the Hradcany palace.

## Literary Works and Memoirs
In 1637, Slavata published a work titled *Pamety* (memoirs). These memoirs deal exclusively with the events of 1618 and 1619, a period in which Slavata played a significant role. 

During the final years of his life, Slavata utilized his leisure to compose a more extensive work titled *Historicke Spisovani* (historical works). This vast collection consists of fourteen large manuscript volumes. The previously written *Pamety* are included within this larger work, occupying two of the fourteen volumes. These two volumes were later edited and published by Dr. Jos. Jirecek.

## Historical Context and Legacy
Slavata was a contemporary and political opponent of Paul Skala ze Zhore (1582–c. 1640), another historian of the period. While Slavata was aligned with the imperialist party, Skala was an adherent of the nationalist party and an official for Frederick of the Palatinate, the "winter king."

The works of Slavata, along with those of Skala and Comenius, represent some of the last important writings in the Bohemian language before a prolonged period of decline. Following the downfall of Bohemian independence in 1620, the national tongue entered a period of decay, becoming almost extinct as a written language by the 18th century. This decline was furthered by the new Austrian government, which sought to discourage the use of the Bohemian language. It was not until the romantic movement of the early 19th century that a revival of the language began.
