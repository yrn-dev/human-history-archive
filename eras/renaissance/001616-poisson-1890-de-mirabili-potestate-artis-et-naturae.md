# De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae

*De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae* is a work attributed to Roger Bacon, first printed in 1542. It is one of several printed works associated with Bacon, which include *Speculum Alchimiae* (1541), *Libellus de Retardandis Senectutis Accidentibus* (1590), and various collections of tracts and grammars.

## Translations and Publications
The text of *De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae* was translated into English in 1659. It exists alongside other significant works by Bacon, such as the *Opus Majus* (of which *Perspectiva* and *Specula Mathematica* are the fifth and fourth parts, respectively) and the *Opera hactenus Inedita* edited by J. S. Brewer in 1859.

## Content and Attributions
The work is noted for a specific passage that has led some to claim the invention of gunpowder for Roger Bacon. However, the archive indicates that careful research has shown very little can be accurately ascribed to Bacon regarding his mechanical discoveries. 

While Bacon is known to describe a method for constructing a telescope, there is no evidence to conclude he actually possessed the instrument. Similarly, while he was likely acquainted with the principles behind the construction of spectacles, it does not appear that he made them. Burning-glasses, by contrast, were in common use during his time.

## Philosophical and Scientific Beliefs
The author of *De Mirabili Potestate Artis et Naturae* and related texts, such as *De Secretis*, held several beliefs that are viewed with caution by modern researchers. These include:
* A belief in astrology.
* A belief in the doctrine of signatures.
* A belief in the philosopher's stone.
* The conviction that the circle had been squared.

Due to these factors, the "wonderful predictions" found in his *De Secretis* are suggested to be taken *cum grano salis*.
