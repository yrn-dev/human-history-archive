# Corneille's Tite et Berenice and Psyche

The plays *Tite et Berenice* and *Psyche* are works by the French dramatist Pierre Corneille. These productions are associated with the career of the actor Michel Baron and the later creative period of Corneille's life.

## Production and Performance
Michel Baron (1653–1729), a prominent French actor born in Paris, performed in both *Tite et Berenice* and *Psyche*. Baron had previously been a star of the *Petits Comediens Dauphins* and had joined Moliere's company with the king's permission. After a period of withdrawal due to a misunderstanding with Moliere's wife, Baron rejoined Moliere's company in 1670. It was upon his return to the company that he reappeared in the roles of Domitien in *Tite et Berenice* and in the production of *Psyche*. Baron remained with Moliere's company until the dramatist's death, later becoming the undisputed master of the French stage at the Hotel de Bourgogne until his retirement in 1691.

## Composition and Reception of Psyche
*Psyche* was produced in 1671. Corneille did not work alone on this piece; P. Quinault and Moliere served as his coadjutors. The work is noted for showing signs of renewed vigour in Corneille's writing. This period of productivity followed a series of other works, including *Othon* (1664), *Agesilas* (1666), and *Attila* (1667). However, the success of *Psyche* was followed by plays such as *Pulcherie* (1672) and *Surena* (1674), which were considered failures even by those who faithfully followed Corneille.

## Critical Context
The reception of Corneille's works often involved comparisons with other dramatists. In the case of *Berenice*, an unlucky comparison with Racine weighed heavily against the play. Despite such comparisons and the manifest inferiority of his final five or six plays, Corneille maintained a towering reputation. He was recognized as the foremost of dramatists and as a master by Moliere. Even his rival, Racine, is said to have assured his son that Corneille's verses were "cent fois plus beaux" than his own.

## The Life of Pierre Corneille
Pierre Corneille's career was marked by periods of great success and temporary retirement. In 1652, following the disfavour received by *Pertharite*, he resolved to quit the stage for six years, during which he focused on the *Examens* of his plays, three *Discourses on Dramatic Poetry*, and a verse translation of the *Imitation of Christ*. He returned to playwriting in 1659 after being persuaded by Fouquet, resulting in the play *OEdipe*.

Corneille moved his residence from Rouen to Paris in 1662 and was granted a pension of 2000 livres at the proposal of Colbert. In his later years, his pension was suspended for unknown reasons between 1674 and 1681, and again in 1683. He died at his house in the rue d'Argenteuil on September 30, 1684.
