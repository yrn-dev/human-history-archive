# 1554

The year 1554 is associated with various legal, religious, and administrative developments across different regions and figures, ranging from the granting of municipal charters and trade rights in English towns to papal dispensations and religious persecution.

## Municipal Charters and Trade
In 1554, several English towns saw the confirmation or creation of their corporate status and trade privileges:

*   **Bridgwater:** The incorporation of Bridgwater, which had originally been established by a charter of Edward IV in 1468, was confirmed in 1554. Additionally, the town's trade capabilities were expanded during this year with the addition of a second market on Thursday, as well as fairs held at Midsummer and on the 21st of September.
*   **Buckingham:** Queen Mary created Buckingham a free borough corporate in 1554. This charter established a government consisting of a steward, twelve principal burgesses, and a bailiff. The charter also defined the town's boundaries, extending in length from Padbury Mill bridge to Chackmore bridge and in width from Thornborowe bridge to Dudley bridge.

## Religious Events and Persecutions
The year 1554 was a period of significant religious transition and conflict in England:

*   **The Case of Bishop Hooper:** In March 1554, Hooper was deprived of his bishopric because he was a married man. Although there was no statute at that time to condemn him to the stake, he remained imprisoned. The situation escalated in December 1554 with the revival of the heresy acts. This legal shift led to his condemnation and subsequent execution by burning on February 9, 1555.
*   **Papal Dispensations:** During 1554, Charles obtained a bull from Pope Julius III. This document granted him a dispensation from fasting and permitted him to break his fast early in the morning, even on occasions when he was to take the sacrament.
