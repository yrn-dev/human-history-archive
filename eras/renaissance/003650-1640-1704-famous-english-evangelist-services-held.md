# Benjamin Keach

## Overview
Benjamin Keach (1640–1704) was a prominent English evangelist. He is noted in historical records primarily for his influence on the development of Baptist congregations in the American colonies through the work of his son.

## Influence in the American Colonies
The labors of Elias Keach, the son of Benjamin Keach, were instrumental in the formation of the Pennepek church in 1688. This occurred during a period when Quaker colonies, which offered a significant degree of religious liberty, began attracting many Baptists from Wales, England, and New England.

While the Pennepek church was formally established in 1688, services had been conducted in Philadelphia under the auspices of the church starting in 1687. However, the organization did not become independent until 1698. The church eventually came to possess the Keithian meeting-house after several Keithian Quakers joined the congregation.

## Baptist Cooperation and Organization
The Pennepek church was part of a broader trend of Baptist growth in the colonies, which included the founding of a Baptist church at Cold Spring in Bucks county, Pennsylvania, around 1684 by Thomas Dungan, an Irish Baptist minister. 

By 1707, the Philadelphia Association was established as a delegated body. This association was created to set churches in order and consult on matters that were wanting within them. From its beginning, the body was highly influential in several areas:
* Promoting educational and missionary cooperation among Baptists.
* Maintaining sound doctrine.
* Supplying churches with suitable ministers and silencing those deemed unworthy.

Additionally, Seventh-day Baptist churches began to form within the bounds of the association from 1705 onward following the appearance of Sabbatarianism.
