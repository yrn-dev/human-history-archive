# Influences on the Early Musical Development of Johann Sebastian Bach

## Early Life and Education
Johann Sebastian Bach was born in Eisenach and baptized on March 23, 1685. Following the death of his parents when he was ten years old, he was placed under the care of his elder brother, Johann Christoph, who served as an organist at Ohrdruf. Johann Christoph provided Sebastian's initial musical instruction.

During this period, a conflict arose regarding Sebastian's access to musical literature. His elder brother, reportedly jealous of Sebastian's talent, forbade him from accessing a manuscript volume containing the works of great organists, specifically including Buxtehude and Froberger. In response, Sebastian spent six months waking up every night to copy the volume by moonlight, reaching through the lattice of the bookcase to do so. This effort resulted in the permanent ruin of his eyesight, a condition evident in his later portraits and the blindness he experienced in his final years. Once the copying was complete, his brother discovered the duplicate and confiscated it.

## Studies in Lueneburg
In 1700, at the age of fifteen, Sebastian moved to Lueneburg after the death of his brother left him to rely on his own resources. Due to his beautiful soprano voice, he was appointed as a chorister at the school of St Michael. While this position provided for his general education and required a routine of choral work, Bach focused more heavily on instrumental music than on vocal music. 

The archive contains little information regarding his specific musical instructors during his time in Lueneburg. Instead, his musical growth is attributed primarily to his own relentless study of both contemporary and classical composers.

## Key Musical Influences
Bach's development was shaped by the study of several prominent composers. Among the classical and contemporary figures he studied were:
*   **Frescobaldi** (c. 1587)
*   **Caspar Kerl** (1628-1693)
*   **Buxtehude**
*   **Froberger**
*   **Muffat the elder**
*   **Pachelbel**
*   **Johann Joseph Fux** (1660-1741), the author of *Gradus ad Parnassum*, a work used for the training of all later classical composers.

Additionally, Bach took several holiday expeditions on foot to Hamburg to hear the Dutch organist Reinken. While in Hamburg, he was also exposed to Keiser, who was establishing German opera on a grand scale. Although this work fired Bach's imagination, it did not directly influence his musical style.

## Family Legacy
Johann Sebastian Bach belonged to a family that remained important in music history for nearly two hundred years. The family genealogy, which Sebastian helped draw up and his son Philipp Emanuel completed, identifies Veit Bach—a miller and baker—as the founder. The first professional musician in the family was Veit's son, Hans Bach, known as "der Spielmann."

Among his ancestors, Sebastian particularly admired Johann Ludwig, whose church cantatas Sebastian copied (twelve in total), occasionally adding his own work to them. Other significant forerunners included Heinrich of Arnstadt's sons, Johann Michael and Johann Christoph. It is currently supposed, though disputed, that Johann Christoph authored the motet *Ich lasse dich nicht* ("I wrestle and pray"), which was previously attributed to Sebastian Bach.
