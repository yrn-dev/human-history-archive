# Discours au roi

The *Discours au roi* is a literary work published in 1666 by the author known as the Sieur D.... It was released as part of a larger publication titled *Satires du Sieur D....*, which also contained seven satires. The publication of this volume was prompted by the appearance of two unauthorized editions of the author's work.

## Literary Development and the Epistles
Following the publication of the *Satires du Sieur D....* and the *Discours au roi*, the author began publishing epistles from 1669 onwards. These *Epitres* were characterized by a style that was more polished and exquisite, thoughts that were more mature, and a tone that was graver than that of the previously published satires.

## Relationship with Louis XIV
The *Epitres* brought the author into the favor of King Louis XIV, who requested his presence at court. During this encounter, the king inquired as to which of his verses the author considered to be his best. The author responded diplomatically, identifying some as yet unprinted lines written in honor of the grand monarch as his "least bad" verses. After reciting these lines, he was granted a pension of 2000 livres.

## Later Masterpieces and Influence
In 1674, the author published *Oeuvres diverses du sieur D....*, which included two of his masterpieces: *Le Lutrin* and *L'Art poetique*. 

*   ***L'Art poetique***: Written in imitation of Horace's *Ars Poetica*, this work established a code for future French verse. It is divided into four books:
    *   The first and last books focus on general precepts, primarily emphasizing the rule of *bon sens*.
    *   The second book addresses the ode, the epigram, the elegy, the satire, and the pastoral.
    *   The third book focuses on epic and tragic poetry.
    While the work analyzes various kinds of verse composition and their specific principles, some views suggest its rules tend to make poetic efforts too mechanical and hamper them. The maxims within *L'Art poetique* also influenced English literature via a revised translation by Dryden and an imitation in Pope's *Essay on Criticism*.
*   ***Le Lutrin***: This is a mock heroic poem. Four cantos were published in 1674, and the work served as a model for Alexander Pope's *The Rape of the Lock*, although the latter is considered superior in its richness of imagination.

## Critical Standing
Despite his contributions, the author is not considered a great poet, although he was a great critic in verse, even if he was not infallible. He is credited with providing the utmost service in destroying the exaggerated reputations of mediocrities during his time, though his own judgment was occasionally at fault.
