# BARBIERI, GIOVANNI FRANCESCO

Giovanni Francesco Barbieri (1591–1666), also known as Guercino—a name derived from his squinting—was a prominent Italian historical painter. He was born in Cento, a village located near Bologna.

## Artistic Development and Style
Barbieri's artistic abilities developed with great rapidity. At seventeen years of age, he became associated with Benedetto Gennari (1550–1610), a well-known painter belonging to the Bolognese school. As his reputation grew beyond his home village, he moved to Bologna in 1615, where his work received significant admiration.

His early style was modeled after that of the Caracci. However, he was deeply influenced by the shadows and strong colouring utilized by Caravaggio, and for a considerable time, his works showed evident traces of this influence. In his later years, some of his pieces shifted toward the manner of his contemporary, Guido, characterized by increased clearness and lightness.

## Career and Works
Guercino was highly esteemed during his lifetime by his fellow artists, who ranked him among the first of painters, as well as by the princes and nobles of Italy. He was noted for the extreme rapidity with which he executed his work. His output was extensive, consisting of approximately 144 paintings, including no fewer than 106 large altar-pieces created for churches.

His most famous work is considered to be the *St Petronilla*, which he painted in Rome for Gregory XV; this piece is currently located in the Capitol. In 1626, he began working on frescoes in the Duomo at Piacenza. Barbieri continued his work as a painter and teacher until his death in 1666, having amassed a handsome fortune through his professional labours.

## Legacy and Recognition
Guercino is recognized as one of the Bolognese eclectics of the 17th century. His works are represented in the picture gallery of the Museo Civico, one of Italy's most important museums, which provides a survey of the masters of the Bolognese school. A biography of his life was written by J. A. Calvi and published in Bologna in 1808.

## Family
Giovanni Francesco had a brother, Paolo Antonio Barbieri (1603–1649), who was also a celebrated painter. Unlike Giovanni Francesco's focus on historical painting, Paolo Antonio specialized in animals and still life, painting insects, flowers, and fruits after nature with a strong character of life and a lively tint of colour.
