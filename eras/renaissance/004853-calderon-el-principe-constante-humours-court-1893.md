# Calderón's El Príncipe Constante and The Humours of the Court

## Pedro Calderón de la Barca
Pedro Calderón de la Barca (1600–1681) was a prominent Spanish poet and dramatist born in Madrid on January 17, 1600. The son of a treasury secretary and a mother of Flemish descent, Calderón was initially educated at the Jesuit College in Madrid to pursue a religious life. He later abandoned this path to study law at Salamanca and participated in literary fêtes in Madrid between 1620 and 1632.

Calderón rose to prominence as a playwright and, following the death of Lope de Vega in 1635, was regarded as the leading Spanish dramatist of his era. He enjoyed significant royal patronage from Philip IV, who appointed him a knight of the order of Santiago in 1636–1637 and commissioned spectacular plays for the royal theatre in the Buen Retiro.

## Dramatic Style and Methods
Calderón is characterized by a lofty philosophic imagination and a wealth of poetic diction, though his intellectual interests often leaned toward theological mysteries rather than human passions. While not considered an expert in invention or observation, he possessed an unrivalled dexterity in manipulating stage resources and a skill for creating ingenious variants of existing themes.

Critics note that Calderón frequently recast the works of his predecessors, a common practice among Spanish dramatists of his time. For example, he adapted several plays by Lope de Vega, including *Saber del mal y del bien* and *Selva confusa*. In the case of *El Alcalde de Zalamea*, he transformed one of Lope de Vega's rapid improvisations into a finished masterpiece.

## El Príncipe Constante
*El Príncipe constante* is one of Calderón's productions. The play is associated with a period of personal conflict for the author; after Calderón and his friends broke into a cloister to seize an actor who had stabbed his brother Diego, the preacher Hortensio Félix Paravicino denounced the act in a sermon before Philip IV. Calderón responded by including a mocking reference to Paravicino's "gongoristic verbiage" within *El Príncipe constante*. Although this reference was later cancelled, the incident resulted in Calderón being committed to prison, though he was released shortly thereafter.

The subject matter of *El Príncipe constante* later served as the basis for the tragedy *The Christian Captives* (1890), written by the English poet Robert Bridges.

## The Humours of the Court
*The Humours of the Court* (1893) is a comedy written by the English poet Robert Bridges. The play was founded on the works of two Spanish dramatists: it drew from Calderón's *El secreto á voces* and Lope de Vega's *El Perro del hortelano*.
