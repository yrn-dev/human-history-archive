# Krvavy'sneem

The "Krvavy'sneem," translated as the "bloody diet," refers to a specific assembly of the estates in Bohemia during the reign of Ferdinand. The event is characterized by the execution of the primary leaders of a revolt.

## Historical Context and the Reign of Ferdinand
Ferdinand's governance of Bohemia was marked by a strategy to render the nobility and knights amenable to the royal will. He sought to achieve this through the influence of his court and the establishment of the Jesuits in Bohemia. By this period, the power of the cities had been thoroughly undermined, and the peasantry was no longer considered dangerous due to the establishment of serfdom.

## The Bloody Diet
The "Krvavy'sneem" occurred on August 22, following the success of Ferdinand's policies. During this diet, four principal leaders of the revolt were sentenced to death: two citizens of Prague and two knights. These individuals were decapitated in the square located outside the Hradcany palace, the site where the estates had met that day.

## Later Developments under Ferdinand and Maximilian
In the final years of his life, specifically in 1562, Ferdinand secured the coronation of his eldest son, Maximilian, as the king of Bohemia to ensure the succession of the throne. Ferdinand's roles extended beyond Bohemia, as he also acceded to the Hungarian throne, became king of the Romans, and eventually became emperor following the death of Charles V. Ferdinand died in 1564. Following this period, the "Compacts" were abolished.

## The Role of the Diet in Bohemian History
The "Krvavy'sneem" is one instance of the significant role the diet played in the constitutional and religious development of Bohemia. Other notable assemblies include:

*   **The Diet of St Wenceslas (1517):** A meeting where the three estates agreed to live together in friendly intercourse. The citizens renounced certain privileges, while the other estates recognized municipal autonomy.
*   **The Diet of 1575:** A prolonged session where the Bohemian estates demanded the recognition of the "Confession of Augsburg." When King Maximilian refused, the majority of the diet—comprised of advanced Utraquists and members of the Bohemian brotherhood—created the *Confessio Bohemica*.
*   **The Diet of January 20, 1609:** An important meeting where the estates formulated twenty-five "Articles" demanding complete religious liberty for all classes and the recognition of the *Confessio Bohemica* as a fundamental law of the kingdom.
*   **The General Diet of 1615:** Convoked by Matthias, this assembly included representatives from the "lands of the Bohemian crown." It reaffirmed enactments regarding the national language, decreeing that Bohemian be the official ("authorized") language of the country.
