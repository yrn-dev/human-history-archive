# Anti-Skeltonum

*Anti-Skeltonum* is a lost literary work attributed to Alexander Barclay. Its existence is noted by Bale.

## Context and Authorship
The work was written by Alexander Barclay, a priest who produced several writings in his youth. Barclay's literary perspective was characterized by a lack of sympathy for the anti-clerical diatribes of John Skelton; consequently, Barclay attacked Skelton on more than one occasion. *Anti-Skeltonum* served as one of these attacks.

## Other Works by Alexander Barclay
In addition to the lost *Anti-Skeltonum*, Barclay is known for the following works:

*   ***Certayne Ecloges of Alexander Barclay, Priest***: These are among the earliest examples of the English pastoral. The first three eclogues are dialogues between Cornix and Coridon, borrowed from Aeneas Sylvius Piccolomini (Pope Pius II) and featuring an eulogy for John Alcock, the founder of Jesus College, Cambridge, and bishop of Ely. The fourth eclogue is based on Mantuan's *De consuetudine divitum erga poetas* and includes an elegy for Sir Edward Howard, the lord high admiral of England, who died in 1513 during an attack on the French fleet in the harbour of Brest. The fifth eclogue, titled "Fyfte Eglog of Alexandre Barclay of the Cytezen and the uplondyshman," is also based on Mantuan and features a discussion between two shepherds, Faustus and Amintas, regarding the merits of country and town life. These pastorals function as satires on social evils and corruptions, featuring rustic characters of the Colin Clout type and descriptions of village life, such as football and Sunday games.
*   ***The Castell of Laboure***: Published in 1506 by Wynkyn de Worde, this work was translated from the French of Pierre Gringoire.
*   ***Introductory to write and to pronounce Frenche***: Published by Robert Copland in 1521.
*   ***The Myrrour of Good Maners***: A translation of Dominicus Mancinus's *De quatuor virtutibus*, published by Richard Pynson.
*   ***Cronycle compyled in Latyn***: A work by the renowned Sallust, published by Richard Pynson.
