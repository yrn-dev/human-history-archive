# Baber

Baber, also known as Babar or Zahir ud-din-Mahomet, was a prominent conqueror of India and the founder of the Mogul (Mongol) dynasty. Born on February 14, 1483, he was a descendant of Timur and Jengis. His father, Omar Sheik, served as the king of Ferghana in a district of what is now Russian Turkestan.

## Early Life and Struggles for Power
Baber succeeded to the throne of Ferghana in 1495 following the death of his father, at the age of twelve. Despite attempts by his uncles to remove him from power, he remained settled as sovereign and sought to expand his territories. In 1497, he captured Samarkand, a city to which he believed he had a natural and hereditary right.

His hold on power was frequently unstable. A rebellion of his nobles caused him to lose his native kingdom, and while attempting to recover it, his troops deserted him, leading to the loss of Samarkand as well. Although he eventually regained both locations, he was defeated in a major engagement in 1501 by Shaibani (Sheibani) Khan, the ruler of the Uzbegs, who drove him from Samarkand.

## Establishment in Kabul
After spending three years attempting to recover his possessions, Baber gathered troops in 1504, crossed the snowy Hindu Kush, and captured the city of Kabul. This victory provided him with a wealthy new kingdom and restored his fortunes. 

His time in Kabul was marked by further volatility. In 1505, he was a small Turkoman chieftain who had been forced into Afghanistan after years of warfare. While he briefly united with Hussain Mirza of Herat against Shaibani in 1505, the expedition ended with Hussain's death. Later, a revolt among leading Moguls drove Baber from Kabul. However, his personal daring and courage intimidated his opponents, leading them to return to their allegiance and allowing Baber to regain his kingdom. In 1510, following the death of Shaibani, Baber again attempted to take his native country, receiving aid from Shah Ismael of Persia and achieving a triumphal result in 1511.

## Conquest of India
Baber laid claim to the Punjab on the basis that Timur had conquered the region 107 years prior. He assembled an army and accumulated guns to facilitate this expansion. At the time, India was divided and open to capable invaders who could provide order and peace.

In 1525, Baber encountered the Sultan of Delhi at Panipat, ten miles north of the town. According to Baber's own estimates, he commanded 25,000 men equipped with guns, while his opponent possessed four times as many men and a thousand elephants. Baber achieved a complete victory. Following this success, he abandoned his title as King of Kabul and assumed the title of Emperor of Hindustan. Baber described this new land as being richer, finer, and more fertile than his own countries. He extended his conquests as far as Bengal.

## The Mogul Dynasty
Baber's reign in India lasted from 1526 until his untimely death in 1530. He is characterized as an exceptionally capable monarch, contemporary with other brilliant rulers such as Suleiman the Magnificent in Turkey, Francis I of France, and Henry VIII of England.

His death temporarily halted the tide of Mongol conquest for twenty-five years. The dynasty he founded continued through six generations of father-to-son succession:
* Baber (1526–1530)
* Humayun (1530–1556)
* Akbar (1556–1605)
* Jehangir (1605–1628)
* Shah Jehan (1628–1658)
* Aurungzeb (1658–1707)

Among these, Akbar is noted as one of the greatest Indian monarchs, having subjugated India as far as Berar. The dynasty concluded with Aurungzeb, who was practically the master of the entire peninsula. This era is regarded as the most splendid age to have dawned upon India up to that time.
