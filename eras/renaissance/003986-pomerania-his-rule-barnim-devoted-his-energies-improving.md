# The Dukes Barnim of Pomerania

The name Barnim was borne by thirteen dukes who ruled over various divisions of the duchy of Pomerania. Among these rulers, Barnim I, Barnim III, and Barnim XI are noted for their significant impacts on the governance, territory, and religious alignment of the region.

## Barnim I (c. 1209-1278)
Known as "the Good," Barnim I was the son of Bogislaus II, the duke of Pomerania-Stettin. He succeeded to the duchy upon his father's death in 1220. After reaching adulthood, Barnim I spent a considerable period engaged in struggles against external enemies. These conflicts led to a situation in 1250 where he was forced to recognize the supremacy of the margrave of Brandenburg.

By 1264, Barnim I had succeeded in uniting the entirety of Pomerania under his rule. Following this unification, he shifted his focus toward improving the internal condition of the duchy. His efforts to modernize and develop the region included the introduction of German customs and settlers, as well as the founding of numerous towns. Additionally, he demonstrated extreme generosity toward ecclesiastical foundations. Barnim I died on either the 13th or 14th of November 1278.

## Barnim III (c. 1303-1368)
Barnim III, referred to as "the Great," was the son of Otto I, duke of Pomerania-Stettin. Even before his father's death in 1344, Barnim III played a prominent role in the government and defense of the duchy. He gained military distinction and experience through a long, intermittent struggle against the representatives of Emperor Louis IV, who had invested his own son, Louis, with the mark of Brandenburg.

A military victory achieved by Barnim III in August 1332 was a key factor in temporarily freeing Pomerania from the "vexatious claim" of Brandenburg regarding supremacy over the duchy. During his rule, he also extended the territory of the duchy through conquest and provided assistance to Emperor Charles IV in his conflict with the family of Wittelsbach. Barnim III died on August 24, 1368.

## Barnim XI (1501-1573)
The son of Bogislaus X, Barnim XI became duke in 1523 following the death of his father. His early reign involved shared rule; he first ruled in common with his elder brother, George, and after George's death in 1531, he shared the duchy with his nephew, Philip I. Barnim XI retained the duchy of Pomerania-Stettin for himself.

His early years as ruler were marked by a quarrel with the margrave of Brandenburg, who sought to annex Pomerania. This was resolved in 1529 via a treaty that ended Brandenburg's supremacy over Pomerania, though it included a condition that the duchy would revert to Brandenburg should the ducal family become extinct.

Regarding religion and politics, Barnim XI adopted the doctrines of Martin Luther and joined the league of Schmalkalden, although he did not participate in the subsequent war. This political position left him without supporters, eventually forcing him to submit to Emperor Charles V. As a result, he was required to pay a heavy fine and accept the *Interim* issued from Augsburg in May 1548. In 1569, Barnim XI handed his duchy over to his grand-nephew, John Frederick. He died at Stettin on June 2, 1573.
