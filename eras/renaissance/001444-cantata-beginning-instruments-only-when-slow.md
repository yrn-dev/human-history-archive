# The Church Cantatas of Johann Sebastian Bach

Johann Sebastian Bach composed a vast body of church music, most notably a collection of 190 church cantatas. While some of these are known only from fragmentary sets of parts, the collection is diverse in form. Approximately 40 of these cantatas are written for solo voices, and about 60 are more or less based on chorales. The remaining works are practically short oratorios, a designation Bach himself frequently applied to them. Almost all of these works contain a chorale intended for congregational singing.

## Composition and Adaptation
Bach's approach to his works often involved the logical and varied adaptation of earlier material. This process of "musical morphology" is evident in how he transplanted works into new environments. For example, movements from the birthday cantata *Was mir behagt ist nur die muntre Jagd* were distributed among the church cantatas *Also hat Gott die Welt geliebt* and *Man singet mit Freuden vom Sieg*. In these instances, Bach sometimes used adaptation to remedy defects in the original version—such as the ill-proportioned bass aria "Ein Fuerst ist seines Landes Pan," which became "Du bist geboren mir zu Gute" in *Also hat Gott*—or to expand a work's proportions, as seen when the finale of *Was mir behagt* was transformed into the triumphant opening chorus of *Man singet mit Freuden*.

Other adaptations involved changing the medium entirely. Two movements of a lost violin-concerto in D minor (preserved in a clavier arrangement) were transformed into parts of the church cantata *Wir muessen durch viel Truebsal in das Reich Gottes eingehen*. In this adaptation, the violin was replaced by an organ played an octave lower, and the addition of new wind instrument parts turned the first movement into an idealization of "much tribulation." The slow movement of this cantata features a four-part chorus singing the words of the title, utilizing a grandeur of conception comparable to the *Passion according to St Matthew*.

## Relationship to the Masses
The relationship between Bach's church cantatas and his masses is significant. The four short masses (consisting only of the Kyrie and Gloria) were mainly compiled from church cantatas and were evidently designed for use in a church with a small orchestra. In the entire collection of these four masses, only four movements cannot be traced back to other extant works.

A primary example of this adaptation is found in the A major mass. The Gloria of this mass is derived from the church cantata *Halt im Gedaechtniss*. While the two versions are considered independent works of art, they share an identical framework of bars and harmonies. However, they differ in their opening structures: in the cantata, the beginning is for instruments only. When the slow movement begins—scored for a flute and two *oboe d'amore*—the basses sing "Peace be unto you," while the other voices sing of the triumph of the faithful. In the mass version, the Gloria begins with a triumphant polyphonic chorus and a spirited symphony for strings.

## Publication and Legacy
During his lifetime, Bach's church cantatas were rarely published; the only exception was *Gott ist mein Koenig*, written when he was nineteen. Most of the works published during his life were instrumental collections that Bach engraved himself. 

Bach's music was largely neglected by his younger contemporaries, who viewed it as crabbed or old-fashioned, until it was rediscovered by Mendelssohn. The *Bach-Gesellschaft* spent over fifty years publishing approximately four-fifths of his choral works for the first time. Despite this, the archive notes that the full influence of his work has hardly yet begun to show itself, and the bulk of the church cantatas remains difficult to present in anthology form due to the scale and excellence of the complete works.

## Catalog of Related Works
Beyond the 190 church cantatas, Bach's ecclesiastical output included:
*   Three wedding cantatas.
*   The Easter oratorio and the Christmas oratorio (the latter consisting of six connected cantatas).
*   The Passions according to St Matthew and St John.
*   The Mass in B minor and the Magnificat in D.
*   Five *a capella* motets (some likely supported by organ) and a sixth with obligato figured-bass.
*   A large collection of plain chorales.

While J. N. Forkel suggested Bach wrote five *Jahrgange* of church cantatas (enough for five years of Sundays and holy days), which would imply 80 lost works, evidence from text-books and fragments suggests that no more than six or seven cantatas are actually lost.
