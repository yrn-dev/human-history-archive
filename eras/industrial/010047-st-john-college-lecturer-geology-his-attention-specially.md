# Thomas George Bonney

Thomas George Bonney (1833– ) was an English geologist. He was born on July 27, 1833, in Rugeley, the eldest son of the Rev. Thomas Bonney, who served as the master of the grammar school in that town.

## Education and Early Career
Bonney received his education at Uppingham and St John's College, Cambridge. In 1856, he graduated as 12th wrangler and was ordained the following year. Between 1856 and 1861, he served as the mathematical master at Westminster school. During this period, he pursued geology primarily as a recreation, focusing his efforts mainly on Alpine regions.

## Academic Appointments and Geological Research
In 1868, Bonney was appointed as a lecturer in geology and a tutor at St John's College. His scientific attention was specifically directed toward the study of metamorphic and igneous rocks. This research took place in Alpine regions as well as various locations across England, including Salcombe, the Lizard, Charnwood Forest, the Scottish Highlands, and Wales.

His academic career progressed in 1877 when he was chosen as the professor of geology at University College, London.

## Professional Leadership and Honors
Bonney held several prominent leadership roles within scientific and professional organizations:
* **Geological Society:** Served as secretary and later as president from 1884 to 1886.
* **British Association:** Served as secretary from 1881 to 1885.
* **Other Presidencies:** He served as president of both the Alpine Club and the Mineralogical Society.

Additionally, in 1887, he was appointed as an honorary canon of Manchester.

## Published Works
Bonney authored several purely scientific texts, including:
* *Cambridgeshire Geology* (1875)
* *The Story of our Planet* (1893)
* *Charles Lyell and Modern Geology* (1895)
* *Ice Work, Past and Present* (1896)
* *Volcanoes* (1899)

Beyond these volumes, he contributed numerous papers to the *Geological Magazine* and the *Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society*. He also wrote popular works concerning English and Welsh scenery, Alpine Regions, and various theological subjects.
