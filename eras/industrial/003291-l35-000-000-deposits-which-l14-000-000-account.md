# Banking and Financial Institutions (Early 20th Century)

## The Bank of France
The Bank of France operates primarily through a substantial note circulation, which averaged L186,300,000 in 1906. This circulation serves as the primary means for most business transactions within France. The limits of this circulation have been extended over several decades: starting at 350 million francs (L14,000,000) on March 15, 1848, it grew to 4,000 million francs (L160,000,000) by January 25, 1893, and reached 5,800 million francs (L232,000,000) in 1906.

The bank has navigated various financial and political crises, including the overthrow of Louis Philippe's government in 1848, the 1870 war with Germany, the 1871 reign of the Commune in Paris, and the payment of a war indemnity that was not completed until 1873. To support agricultural industry and businesses of all sizes, the bank expanded its reach; while it had 78 offices in 1877, this number grew to 447 by 1906. This total includes towns "connected with the branches," a system that provides the advantages of a branch without the expense of opening one.

In 1906, the Bank of France discounted more than 20,000,000 bills totaling L559,234,996, and provided advances on securities amounting to L106,280,124. The bank accepts bills as low as 4s. 2d. for discount, and since March 27, 1890, has granted loans as small as L10. The discount rate in Paris is generally lower and more stable than in London; from May 1900 to January 1906, the rate remained uniform at 3%.

While the Bank of France has not attracted deposits on the scale of the Bank of England, its balance sheet for December 23, 1906, showed approximately L35,000,000 in deposits. Of this amount, L14,000,000 was on account of the treasury and L21,000,000 was for individuals.
