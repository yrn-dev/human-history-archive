## Francis Bacon
Francis Bacon (1561-1626), also known as Baron Verulam and Viscount St Albans, was an English essayist, statesman, and philosopher. Born on January 22, 1560/1, at York House in the Strand, London, he was the youngest son of Sir Nicholas Bacon. His mother, a daughter of Sir Anthony Cooke and a former tutor to Edward VI, was well-versed in classical studies and a warm adherent of the Puritan or Reformed Church.

Due to delicate health, Bacon likely received much of his early education at home before entering Trinity College, Cambridge, in April 1573. During his three years there, he studied the sciences as they were taught at the time but concluded that the methods and results were erroneous. While he maintained a reverence for Aristotle, he grew to despise the prevailing Aristotelian philosophy, viewing it as fruitless and useful only for disputation. This led him to believe that a new method must be devised to teach philosophy its true purpose.

## Roger Bacon
The 13th century produced Roger Bacon, a figure often compared to Thomas Aquinas, Bonaventura, and Albertus Magnus. While these contemporaries enjoyed wider renown during their own time, modern criticism has shifted the balance in favor of Roger Bacon. Some modern views suggest he was a modern thinker who was not appreciated by his age because he was ahead of it, possessing conceptions of science that were clearer and more just than those of his namesake, Francis Bacon. However, this view is considered by some to be exaggerated, as Bacon remained influenced by his national surroundings and worked within established tracks, though he was a systematic thinker.

## Geography and Urban History: Ashland
Ashland is a city located in Boyd County, Kentucky, U.S.A., situated on the Ohio River approximately 130 miles east by north of Frankfort. Settled in 1854 and chartered as a city in 1870, Ashland serves as a distributing center for resources found in the surrounding country, including timber, stone, clay, oil, iron ore, and coal.

The city's population grew from 4,195 in 1890 to 6,800 in 1900 (which included 489 negroes) and reached 8,688 by 1910. Ashland is served by the Norfolk & Western and Chesapeake & Ohio railways. It is connected to Huntington, West Virginia, via an electric line. Local landmarks include Central Park, a natural park of about 30 acres, and Clyffeside Park, a 75-acre pleasure resort maintained by a private corporation. Clyffeside Park features a casino with a seating capacity of 3,000 and serves as a meeting-ground for the Tri-State "Chautauqua." The city's manufacturing sector includes leather, fire brick, furniture, dressed lumber (particularly poplar), sheet steel, steel billets, wire rods, nails, and pig iron.
