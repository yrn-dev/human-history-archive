# Water-Tube Boilers: The Babcock & Wilcox Boiler

The Babcock & Wilcox boiler is a type of water-tube boiler produced in both stationary versions for land purposes and specialized versions for marine use. These boilers are designed to handle various working pressures; while a usual pressure is 180 lb per sq. in., special cases can reach as high as 500 lb per sq. in.

## Land-Purpose Boiler Design
The land-purpose Babcock & Wilcox boiler consists of a horizontal cylinder that serves as a steam chest. This cylinder features dished ends and two specially constructed cross-boxes riveted to the bottom. Positioned beneath this cylinder is a sloping nest of tubes, with the fire located under the upper end of the nest.

The nest of tubes is composed of several separate elements. Each element consists of a front and back header made of sinuous wrought steel, connected by a number of tubes. The connection system is organized as follows:
* **Front Headers:** The upper ends are connected to the front cross-box of the horizontal cylinder via short tubes, while the lower ends remain closed.
* **Back Headers:** The upper ends connect to the back cross-box through longer pipes. The lower ends are connected by short pipes to a horizontal mud drum, which is equipped with a pipe and a blow-off cock.

To ensure the proper circulation of combustion products, brickwork baffles are placed between the tubes and between the nests of tubes and the cylinder. This arrangement forces the products of combustion to pass between the tubes three times. The boiler is enclosed in brickwork up to the height of the horizontal cylinder's center, and the front is fitted with an iron casing lined with brick at the lower section.

## Marine Boiler Design
The marine version of the Babcock & Wilcox boiler is utilized in merchant steamers, yachts, and the British and American navies. It features a horizontal cylindrical steam-chest placed transversely over a group of elements, with the fire located beneath. The entire assembly is enclosed in an iron casing lined with firebrick.

Similar to the land version, each element consists of a front and back header connected by water-tubes, which are given a considerable inclination to facilitate circulation. The headers are made of wrought steel and are sinuous on both sides (except for the outer pairs, which are flat on the outer portions) so that the sinuosities fit into one another.

The marine boiler utilizes two different tube sizes:
* **Large Tubes:** Measuring 3-15/16 in. outside diameter, these are used for the return tubes between the back headers and the steam-chest, as well as the two lower rows.
* **Small Tubes:** Measuring 1-13/16 in. outside diameter, these are arranged in groups of two or four at nearly all the sinuosities of the headers. This grouping ensures that furnace gases are well mixed and maintain contact with the heating surfaces.

The connection system for the marine boiler includes short nipples connecting the upper ends of the front headers to the steam-chest. At the bottom, these headers connect to a square-section pipe extending across the boiler's width. Nearly vertical tubes provide additional connections between this cross-pipe and the bottom of the steam-chest. The back headers connect to the steam-chest at their upper ends via two long horizontal tubes, while their lower ends are closed.

## Maintenance and Construction
Both land and marine models incorporate specific features for cleaning and tube replacement. The headers contain holes on opposite sides; one side connects the headers to the tubes, and the other provides access for cleaning and fixing the tubes in position.

In the land boiler, these outer holes are oval and closed by special fittings where watertightness is secured by outer cover plates. These oval fittings are designed to be placed from the outside and are constructed to prevent a great outrush of water or steam if a bolt breaks.

The marine boiler uses two types of fittings for header access:
* **Oval Fittings:** Used for the larger holes opposite groups of two tubes, similar to those in the land boiler.
* **Conical Fittings:** Used for smaller holes opposite groups of four tubes. These conical holes have a larger diameter on the inside and are closed by a forging consisting of a conical portion and a bolt, with a close-ended nut.

Because they are water-tube boilers, they require frequent cleaning if the feed-water used is impure. However, the straightness of the tubes allows their condition to be ascertained easily.
