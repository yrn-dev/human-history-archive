# Kwaka Dua III

Kwaka Dua III, also known as Prince Prempeh, was a monarch of Ashanti who was chosen as king on March 26, 1888. His ascension occurred following a period of significant political instability and a desolating civil war within Ashanti.

## Accession and Early Reign
The path to the reign of Kwaka Dua III was marked by a series of leadership crises. Following the war of 1873-74, the Ashanti deposed Kofi Karikari and placed his brother, Mensa, on the golden stool, which served as the symbol of sovereignty. Mensa's rule was characterized as tyrannous, involving repeated human sacrifices and the violation of nearly every article of the Fomana treaty; of the 50,000 oz. of gold stipulated as an indemnity, only 4,000 oz. were paid. 

In 1883, a revolution displaced Mensa, and he was succeeded by Kwaka Dua II. However, Kwaka Dua II died in June 1884, only a few months after his election. The former king, Kofi Karikari, also died in June 1884, leading to a period of disruption and civil war.

The colonial government was forced to intervene in the resulting dispute due to the troubles it caused in the Gold Coast. Consequently, Prince Prempeh was chosen as king on March 26, 1888, taking the name Kwaka Dua III. This election was conducted with the sanction and in the presence of an officer from the Gold Coast government. Following his victory over his enemies, a period of prosperity and peace returned to Ashanti for a time.

## Conflict with the British Government
By 1893, fresh tensions emerged between Ashanti and the tribes of the protectorate. In response, Kwaka Dua III ordered the closure of roads to traders. This prompted interference from the British government, particularly because international agreements had placed the country within the British sphere of influence.

The British dispatched a mission to the king with several demands:
* That he fulfill the terms of the 1874 treaty.
* That he accept a British protectorate.
* That he receive a resident at Kumasi.

Kwaka Dua III declined to treat with the governor of the Gold Coast. Instead, he sent informal agents to England, but the secretary of state refused to receive them. The king did not reply to the demands regarding the protectorate and other matters within the three-week grace period provided by the British mission, which expired on October 31, 1895.

## Historical Context of Ashanti Leadership
The reign of Kwaka Dua III followed a volatile sequence of Ashanti monarchs. Prior to the unrest of the late 19th century, Kwaka Dua I had succeeded Osai Okoto in 1838. Kwaka Dua I was a peace-loving monarch who encouraged trade, though he clashed with the British in 1863 over the refusal to surrender a runaway slave-boy and a fugitive chief. During this era, Kwaka Dua I famously remarked that "the bush is stronger than the cannon" regarding the British military presence. After his death in April 1867 and a subsequent interval of civil war, Kofi Karikari took the throne, swearing that his business would be war, which eventually led to the conflict of 1873-74.
