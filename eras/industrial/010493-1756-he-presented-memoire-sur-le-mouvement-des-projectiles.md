# Jean Charles Borda

Jean Charles Borda (1733–1799) was a French nautical astronomer and mathematician. Born on May 4, 1733, in Dax, he received his education at La Fleche. In his early life, Borda obtained a commission in the cavalry.

## Scientific Contributions and the Academy of Sciences
In 1756, Borda presented a work titled *Memoire sur le mouvement des projectiles* to the Academy of Sciences. Following the presentation of this memoir, the Academy elected him as a member. Throughout his career, Borda provided the Academy of Sciences with a long series of valuable memoirs.

His scientific research had significant practical applications. His work in hydrodynamics proved highly useful for the field of marine engineering. Additionally, Borda improved the repeating and reflecting circles, which became of great service to nautical astronomy.

## Military and Naval Career
Borda's professional life included significant military service. He was present at the battle of Hastembeck before transitioning to the naval service. During his time in the navy, he visited the Canary Islands and the Azores, and he constructed an admirable map of these locations.

In 1782, Borda's frigate was captured by a British squadron. He was taken to England but was released on parole almost immediately, allowing him to return to France.

## Geodetic Research
Borda was involved in the effort to determine an arc of the meridian. In this endeavor, he was associated with P.F.A. Mechain and J.B.J. Delambre. Borda was responsible for inventing the greater number of the instruments employed for this specific task.

Jean Charles Borda died in Paris on February 20, 1799.
