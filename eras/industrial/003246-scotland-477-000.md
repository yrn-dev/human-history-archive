# Scotland

## Banking and Finance
The banking system in Scotland has historically placed significant importance on note circulation. An example of this is the Bank of Dundee, which operated from 1764 but did not begin receiving money from the public until 1792. For the first twenty-eight years of its existence, the bank's entire business consisted of issuing notes, with values ranging between £23,000 and £56,000. By 1864, when the Bank of Dundee was amalgamated with the Royal Bank of Scotland, its note circulation stood at £41,000 and its deposits totaled approximately £700,000.

Following 1792, there was a rapid increase in money deposited with Scottish banks, although the practice of hoarding savings in chests—often in amounts of £10 or £20—persisted for a considerable time. Private banking did not maintain a strong presence in Scotland; while eight private banks existed in 1819, all had disappeared by 1844. By 1906, the bulk of the country's banking business was conducted by ten banks of issue, which operated a total of 1,180 branches. Two other small banks had been established more recently.

In terms of broader financial statistics, the average circulation in Scotland in 1906 was £7,477,000. Additionally, bank deposits in Scotland reached 135,042,000.

## Agriculture and Fruit Culture
In the field of fruit culture, Scotland's production varies by crop when compared to other regions. Scotland grows significantly fewer apples than Ireland, with Ireland maintaining a lead of nearly 5,000 acres. Similarly, Ireland produces more pears than Scotland. However, the production of plums in Scotland is approximately the same as that in Ireland. In the category of small fruits, Scotland is far ahead of Ireland in the cultivation of raspberries and strawberries.

## Cartography
The mapping of Scotland is handled through specific technical projections. The Ordnance maps of Scotland, available in scales of 1 inch, ½ inch, ¼ inch, and 1/10 inch, utilize a Modified Conical, Equal-area projection, also known as Bonne's.
