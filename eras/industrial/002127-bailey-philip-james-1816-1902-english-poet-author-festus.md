# BAILEY, PHILIP JAMES (1816-1902)

Philip James Bailey was an English poet, best known as the author of the voluminous poem *Festus*. Born in Nottingham on April 22, 1816, he was the son of a man who published both verse and prose and served as the editor and owner of the *Nottingham Mercury*, a primary journal in the town, from 1845 to 1852.

## Education and Early Career
Bailey received his education locally until the age of sixteen, at which point he matriculated at Glasgow University. Although he did not complete his degree, he moved to London in 1835 and entered Lincoln's Inn. He did not engage in the serious practice of law, eventually settling at Basford.

## Festus
While residing at Basford, Bailey spent three years composing *Festus*, which was published anonymously in 1839. The work achieved immediate success in both the United States and England. In its country of origin, the poem passed through twelve editions, while in the United States, it saw nearly three times as many editions. By 1889, Bailey published a "Jubilee Edition" of the work, reflecting its enduring popularity across both younger and older generations.

*Festus* is described as a vast pageant of philosophy and theology. Organized into approximately twelve divisions, the poem attempts to represent the relationship between man and God and God to man. Its primary objectives are to preach the immortality of the soul, emphasize the benignity of Providence, and postulate a "gospel of faith and reason combined." 

While the work contains dignified thought and fine lines, it is noted for a certain incoherency in its execution and an ambitious theme, factors that make it difficult to read for anyone other than a most sympathetic student. The poem underwent various incorporations and changes over time, yet it remains a singular example of a work virtually completed in the author's youth that was never reinforced or supplanted by his later achievements.

## Later Life and Legacy
Although Bailey published other verses, he is regarded essentially as a man of one book, known almost exclusively for *Festus*. He died on September 6, 1902.
