# John Sherren Brewer

## Early Life and Education
John Sherren Brewer (1810–1879) was an English historian born in Norwich in 1810. He was the son of a Baptist schoolmaster and received his education at Queen's College, Oxford.

## Academic and Ecclesiastical Career
Brewer was ordained in the Church of England in 1837, subsequently serving as a chaplain to a workhouse located in central London. His academic career began in 1839 when he was appointed as a lecturer in classical literature at King's College, London. In 1855, he succeeded F.D. Maurice to become a lecturer in modern history and a professor of English language and literature.

## Journalistic and Official Work
From 1854 onwards, Brewer was involved in journalistic endeavors, contributing to the *Morning Post*, the *Morning Herald*, and the *Standard*. 

In 1856, Brewer was commissioned by the master of the rolls to prepare a calendar of the state papers of Henry VIII, a project that required a vast amount of research. In connection with this work, he was appointed as a preacher and a reader at the Rolls.

## Later Years and Historical Contributions
In 1877, Disraeli secured the crown living of Toppesfield, Essex, for Brewer. While residing there, he continued his work on the *Letters and Papers of the Reign of King Henry VIII*. The introductions to this work were published separately in 1884 under the title *The Reign of Henry VIII*, and are considered an authoritative and scholarly history of the reign of Henry VIII. Additionally, Brewer directed the production of new editions of several standard historical works.

John Sherren Brewer died in February 1879 at Toppesfield.
