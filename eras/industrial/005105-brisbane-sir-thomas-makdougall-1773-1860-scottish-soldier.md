# BRISBANE, SIR THOMAS MAKDOUGALL (1773-1860)

Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane was a Scottish astronomer and soldier who served as the governor of New South Wales and held several prominent scientific leadership roles.

## Early Life and Military Career
Born on July 23, 1773, at Brisbane House near Largs in Ayrshire, Brisbane entered the army in 1789. His military service included deployments to the Peninsula, the West Indies, and Flanders. In 1814, he was sent to North America. Although he was recalled following Napoleon's return from Elba, he did not arrive in time to participate in the battle of Waterloo. He eventually attained the rank of general in 1841.

## Governorship of New South Wales
In 1821, Brisbane was appointed governor of New South Wales, an office he held for four years. During his tenure, he attempted to improve the colony's condition by encouraging the reclamation of land and the breeding of horses. He also introduced tobacco plants, sugar-cane, and the vine. While the finances of the colony fell into confusion during his administration, his instigation of exploring parties led to the discovery of the Brisbane river, which was named in his honor.

## Astronomical and Scientific Contributions
Brisbane's scientific interests were a central part of his life and career. In 1822, he established an astronomical observatory at Paramatta. Observations conducted there between 1822 and 1826 resulted in the *Brisbane Catalogue*, which was printed in 1835 and contained 7,385 stars. The Paramatta observatory was discontinued in 1855.

Upon returning to Scotland, Brisbane resided primarily at Makerstoun in Roxburghshire. There, he maintained a large, admirably equipped observatory similar to the one at Brisbane House. Beginning in 1841, he conducted important magnetic observations at Makerstoun. These results were published in the *Transactions* of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and earned him the Keith prize of the Royal Society of Edinburgh in 1848.

## Public Honors and Leadership
Brisbane held several high-ranking titles and positions:
* **Titles:** He was made a baronet in 1836 and a G.C.B. in 1837.
* **Scientific Societies:** Following the death of Sir Walter Scott in 1833, he was elected president of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. In 1834, he acted as president of the British Association.
* **Philanthropy:** He founded two gold medals to encourage scientific research, one awarded by the Scottish Society of Arts and the other by the Royal Society of Edinburgh.

Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane died on January 27, 1860, at Brisbane House.

## The City of Brisbane
The city of Brisbane was founded in 1825 as a penal settlement and was named after Sir Thomas Brisbane during his time as governor of Australia. Originally belonging to New South Wales, the town became a free settlement in 1842 and was incorporated in 1859, the same year it became the capital of Queensland. 

By 1903, South Brisbane had become a separate city. The municipal governments of both the city and South Brisbane were managed by a mayor and ten aldermen, while divisional boards and shire councils controlled the suburbs. Chief suburbs include Indooroopilly, Highgate, Woolongabba, Bulimba, Breakfast Creek, Toowong, Milon, Paddington, Red Hill, New Farm, Fortitude Valley, and Kangaroo Point. As of 1901, the population of the city proper was 28,953, South Brisbane had 25,481, and the total metropolitan area population was 119,907.
