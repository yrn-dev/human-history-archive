# Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences

The *Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences* is a mathematical work authored by Boole. Published in 1860, it was designed to serve as a sequel to his previous work, the *Treatise on Differential Equations*, which had appeared in 1859.

## Author and Mathematical Approach
Boole is described as one of the most eminent mathematicians to recognize that symbols of operation could be treated as distinct objects of calculation, separate from symbols of quantity. His work is characterized by an unrivalled power and skill in tracing results, as well as a perfect confidence in any result derived from treating symbols according to their primary conditions and laws.

In composing his systematic treatises, Boole combined profound investigation into the philosophy of the subject with elementary exposition. These works are considered valuable contributions to their respective branches of mathematics and embody many of the author's more important discoveries.

## Relationship to Other Works
The *Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences* follows the *Treatise on Differential Equations*. In the latter work, specifically within the 16th and 17th chapters, Boole provides a lucid account of a general method in analysis—originally described in his famous memoir printed in the *Philosophical Transactions* for 1844—and the general symbolic method. The employment of this symbolic method was instrumental in Boole's chief discoveries.
