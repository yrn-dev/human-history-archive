# Naval and Political Figures of the Late 18th and 19th Centuries

## Samuel Barrington
Samuel Barrington (1720–1800) was a British admiral and the fourth son of the 1st Viscount Barrington. Entering the navy at a young age, he achieved the rank of post-captain by 1747. During the Seven Years' War, he commanded the "Achilles" (60) under Hawke in the Basque roads. In 1759, the "Achilles" engaged in two hours of fighting to capture a powerful French privateer. That same year, during the Havre-de-Grace expedition, his ship carried the flag of Rear-Admiral Rodney. In 1760, he sailed with John Byron to destroy the fortifications at Louisburg. By the peace of 1763, Barrington had spent twenty-two years almost continuously afloat.

In 1768, he was appointed to the frigate "Venus" as governor to the duke of Cumberland, who remained with him through the ranks from midshipman to rear-admiral. In 1778, Barrington became a rear-admiral and traveled to the West Indies. In conjunction with the army, he captured the island of Santa Lucia from the French and repulsed an attempt by the Comte d'Estaing to retake the island. Although later superseded by Byron, Barrington served as Byron's second-in-command and was present at St. Kitts and Grenada on July 22nd and 6th, 1779, respectively. Upon returning home, he was offered the command of the Channel fleet but refused it. His final active service occurred in October 1782 during the relief of Gibraltar. He flew his flag as admiral for a short period in 1790 but did not serve in the French revolutionary wars. He died in 1800.

## Sir William Cornwallis
Sir William Cornwallis (1744–1819) was a British admiral and the brother of the 1st Marquess Cornwallis. Born on February 20, 1744, he entered the navy in 1755 and reached post-rank in 1766. Until 1779, his duties primarily involved regular navy work in convoy. In 1779, he commanded the "Lion" (64) within Admiral Byron's fleet. During the battle off Grenada on July 6, 1779, the "Lion" was very roughly handled and was forced to sail to Jamaica alone.

In March 1780, Cornwallis and two other vessels fought a superior French force off Monti Cristi, followed by another encounter near Bermuda in June. The French force they engaged was transporting the troops of Rochambeau to North America and was too strong for Cornwallis's squadron, which consisted of a frigate, two fifty-gun ships, and two small liners. Cornwallis later participated in the second relief of Gibraltar before returning to North America. He served with Rodney in the battle of Dominica on April 12, 1782, and with Hood in actions at the Basse Terre of St Kitts. Cornwallis wrote verses regarding the Dominica action, which indicated he held a low opinion of Rodney's conduct during the battle. From 1788 to 1794, he served as commodore in the East Indies, where he helped reduce Pondicherry and participated in the war with Tippoo Sahib. He was promoted to rear-admiral on February 1, 1793, and became a vice-admiral on July 4, 1794.

## Sir Robert Calder
Sir Robert Calder (1745–1818) was a British admiral born on July 2, 1745 (o.s.), in Elgin, Scotland. The second son of Sir Thomas Calder of Muirton, he entered the navy as a midshipman at age fourteen. In 1766, he served as lieutenant of the "Essex" in the West Indies under Captain the Hon. George Faulkner. He attained the rank of post-captain in 1782. In 1796, Sir John Jervis named him captain of the fleet, and he participated in the battle off Cape St Vincent on February 14, 1797. For his role as the bearer of the victory despatches, he was knighted by George III, received the thanks of parliament, and was created a baronet in 1798.

Promoted to rear-admiral in 1799, Calder was sent in 1801 with a small squadron to pursue a French force under Admiral Gantheaume that was conveying supplies to Egypt. After an unsuccessful pursuit, he struck his flag at the peace. He was recalled to service when war broke out again and was promoted to vice-admiral in 1804. In 1805, he was employed in the blockade of the ports of Corunna and Ferrol to prevent Napoleon I's invasion of England. Despite having a force inferior to the enemy, he refused to be enticed to sea. When Napoleon moved to raise the blockade of Ferrol, Rear-Admiral Stirling joined Calder to intercept the Spanish and French fleets. On July 22, 1805, the enemy fleet came into sight through a fog; despite being outnumbered, Sir Robert entered into action, engaging in a combat that lasted four hours.

## Other Notable Figures
The archive contains records of various other figures from this era:
* **Thomas Denman, 1st Baron (1779–1854):** An English judge born in London on July 23, 1779. He was a prominent lawyer who defended the Luddites and served as counsel for Queen Caroline. He was an M.P. for Wareham (1818) and Nottingham, and served as attorney-general under Lord Grey in 1830. He became lord chief justice of the King's Bench in 1832 and was raised to the peerage in 1834. He is noted for his decision in *Stockdale v. Hansard*.
* **Joseph Grimaldi (1779–1837):** A celebrated English clown born in London on December 18, 1779. He began appearing on stage at Drury Lane before age two and at Sadler's Wells at age three. He was a peerless clown of pantomime, most successful in *Mother Goose* at Covent Garden.
* **Fox Maule Ramsay, 11th Earl of Dalhousie (1801–1874):** A politician who served as under-secretary for home affairs and secretary-at-war (1846–1852). He was secretary of state for war from 1855 to 1858 during the end of the Crimean War. He was a supporter of the Free Church of Scotland.
