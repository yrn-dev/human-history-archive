# Nicolai Prjevalsky's Fourth Journey and Explorations

## Overview of Nicolai Prjevalsky
Nicolai Prjevalsky was a great Russian explorer who conducted extensive expeditions across Asia. His work focused on defining the geography and hydrography of remote regions, particularly in western China and the heart of the Asiatic continent.

## Early Expeditions and Discoveries
Between 1871 and 1873, Prjevalsky crossed the Gobi desert from the north to reach Kansu in western China. During this period, he mapped the hydrography of the region and defined the geography of Tsaidam, an area from which the great rivers of Burma, Siam, and China emanate. His southward penetration brought him to within a month's march of Lhasa.

In 1876, Prjevalsky visited Lop Nor and discovered the Altyn Tagh range. By 1879, he followed the Urangi river to the Altai Mountains. Through these travels, he demonstrated the extraordinary physical changes that had occurred in the heart of the Asiatic continent since the time Jenghiz Khan massed his vast armies in those provinces. During this time, he also traversed the Gobi desert from Hami to Sachu—a location that served as a junction between his journeys and those of Krishna—and crossed and named the Dzungarian extension of the Gobi desert. Before returning to Russia, he visited the sources of the Salween and the Hwang-ho (Yellow river).

## The Fourth Journey (1883-1885)
Prjevalsky's fourth journey took place between 1883 and 1885. This expedition began in Sining, which is described as the great trade centre of the Chinese borderland. From Sining, he traveled through northern Tibet, which involved crossing the Altyn Tagh to reach Lop Nor. 

Continuing his journey, he utilized the Cherchen-Keriya trade route to reach Khotan. From Khotan, he followed the Tarim river to Aksu.

## Regional Context of Kansu and Sining
The regions explored by Prjevalsky, such as Kansu, are noted for specific valuable products. Musk and rhubarb are the most renowned and valuable products of the province of Kansu, though the province produces relatively little of them. The industry for these articles is held by the Tanguts of the province. 

Regarding the musk trade in the vicinity of Sining, reports from 1892 indicate that the trade was increasing, with traders from Ssŭ-ch’uan and Canton purchasing musk for four times its weight in silver. The purity of the musk is tested by its colour, as Tibetans sometimes adulterate it by mixing it with blood and tsamba. The optimal time for purchasing musk is from the seventh to the ninth moon, spanning from the latter part of August to the middle of November. In the local languages, Mongols refer to musk as *owo* (with "white musk" being *tsahan owo*), while Tibetans call it *latsé* (with "white musk" being *latsé karpo*).
