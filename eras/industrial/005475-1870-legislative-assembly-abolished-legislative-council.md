# Legislative Transition in British Honduras (1870)

The administrative and constitutional history of British Honduras is characterized by a transition from colonial independence to a structured system of British crown governance. A significant shift in this progression occurred in 1870, when the colony's legislative assembly was abolished and replaced by a legislative council.

## Early Governance and Codification
From 1638 until 1786, the colonists maintained complete independence, electing their own magistrates who were responsible for all executive and judicial functions. The precedents and customs established during this era were eventually codified and published as "Burnaby's Laws" following a visit by Admiral Sir W. Burnaby in 1756; these laws were recognized as valid by the crown.

The move toward centralized British control began in 1786 with the appointment of a superintendent by the home government. Although the office of superintendent remained vacant between 1790 and 1797, it was revived and continued until 1862. Further administrative structures were added in the mid-19th century, including the establishment of an executive council in 1839 and a legislative assembly in 1853. This assembly consisted of eighteen elected members and three nominated members.

## The 1870 Transition and Subsequent Reforms
In 1862, British Honduras was officially declared a colony. At that time, its chief magistrate was a lieutenant governor who was subject to the governor of Jamaica. 

A major constitutional change took place in 1870 when the legislative assembly was abolished and a legislative council was substituted in its place. The composition of this new body was later fixed in 1892, consisting of five unofficial members and three official members.

Further changes to the executive leadership occurred in 1884, when the lieutenant governor was elevated to the position of governor and commander-in-chief. This change rendered the office independent of Jamaica. The governor is supported by an executive council composed of three unofficial and three official members.

## Administrative and Legal Framework
For the purposes of administration, the colony is organized into six districts:
* Belize
* Corosal
* Orange Walk
* The Cayo
* Stann Creek
* Toledo

Punta Gorda serves as the capital of the Toledo district, while the other districts are named after their primary towns.

The legal system of British Honduras is based on English common law, which remains valid throughout the colony. This law is subject to the operation of the *Consolidated Laws of British Honduras*—a collection of customs and ordinances officially published and revised between 1884 and 1888—as well as modifications made by local enactments. Legal appeals may be directed to the supreme court of Jamaica or the privy council.
