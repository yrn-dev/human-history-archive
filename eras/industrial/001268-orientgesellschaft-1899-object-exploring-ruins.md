# Orientgesellschaft (1899 Expedition)

The *Orientgesellschaft* (also referred to as the *Deutsche Orientgesellschaft*) is a German organization involved in the archaeological exploration of ancient sites. In 1899, the society despatched a large-scale expedition with the specific object of exploring the ruins of Babylon.

## Excavations at Babylon
The work conducted by the *Deutsche Orientgesellschaft* beginning in 1899 has been a primary source for the modern understanding of Babylon's topography. These excavations focused on the city as it existed during the reign of Nebuchadrezzar, as the older version of Babylon—which was destroyed by Sennacherib—left few or no traces behind.

During the course of these explorations, the expedition successfully laid bare the great processional road and the palace of Nebuchadrezzar. The resulting data, combined with the inscriptions of Nebuchadrezzar and accounts from classical writers, has allowed for a detailed mapping of the site.

## Topography of the Site
The remains of the city are primarily located on the eastern bank of the Euphrates. The topography is characterized by three vast mounds:
*   **Babil**: Located to the north.
*   **Qasr** (also known as the **Mujelliba** or "Palace"): Located in the center.
*   **Ish[=a]n 'Amran ibn 'Ali**: Located to the south, featuring an outlying spur known as the Jumjuma.

Further to the east are three lines of rampart and the Ish[=a]n el-Aswad, or "Black Mound." One of the rampart lines encloses the Babil mound on its eastern and northern sides, while a third rampart forms a triangle with the southeast angle of the other two. Additionally, the remains of the ancient Borsippa and further ramparts are situated to the west of the Euphrates.

## Related German Archaeological Work
The *Orientgesellschaft* expedition was part of a broader pattern of German archaeological interest in the region. Prior to the 1899 Babylon expedition, a German expedition led by Dr. Koldewey explored the cemetery of El Hibba (located immediately south of Tello) between 1886 and 1887, providing the first insights into the burial customs of ancient Babylonia. Following the work at Babylon, Dr. W. Andrae conducted excavations at Qal`at Sherqat, which is the site of Assur.

## Documentation and Records
The findings and communications of the society were documented in the *Mittheilungen der deutschen Orientgesellschaft*, with records spanning from 1899 to 1906.
