# James Brindley

James Brindley (1716–1772) was a celebrated English engineer known for his mechanical ingenuity and his pivotal role in the development of the British canal system. Born in Thornsett, Derbyshire, Brindley came from humble beginnings and received little to no formal education.

## Early Career and Mechanical Work
At seventeen years of age, Brindley began an apprenticeship as a millwright near Macclesfield. Following the completion of this apprenticeship, he established his own business as a wheelwright in Leek. He quickly gained a reputation for his skill and ingenuity in the repair of various types of machinery. His early professional successes included the design and installation of an engine in 1752 used for draining coal-pits at Clifton in Lancashire, and the completion of machinery for a silk-mill at Congleton three years later.

## Canal Engineering and the Duke of Bridgewater
Brindley's career reached a turning point in 1759 when he was consulted by the Duke of Bridgewater, who sought to improve the outlets for coal on his estates. Brindley advised the construction of a canal from Worsley to Manchester. Despite significant difficulties, Brindley's genius allowed him to surmount all obstacles, achieving a crowning triumph with the construction of an aqueduct at Barton, which carried the canal 39 feet above the river Irwell.

The success of the Worsley canal led to further ambitious projects. Brindley served as the engineer for the Duke's subsequent effort to create an improved waterway between Liverpool and Manchester. This project presented even more formidable challenges, specifically the requirement to carry the canal over Sale Moor Moss. Brindley's engineering capabilities proved superior to these obstacles, and the work was brought to a triumphant conclusion.

## Major Works and Legacy
Brindley was an enthusiastic believer in the utility of canals, once reportedly stating that the purpose of navigable rivers was "to feed canals." Throughout his career, he laid out over 360 miles of canals. His extensive portfolio of work included:
* The extension of his first project to the Mersey at Runcorn.
* The design and near-completion of the "Grand Trunk Canal," which connected the Mersey with the Humber and Trent.
* The planning of the Chesterfield, Oxford, and Staffordshire and Worcestershire Canals.

## Personal Character and Methodology
Brindley was noted for retaining a peculiar roughness of demeanour and character throughout his life. However, this lack of formal training was more than compensated for by his innate power of thought and remarkable fertility of resource. He possessed a high degree of engineering faculty and a specific method for solving complex problems: when faced with a difficulty, he would retire to bed and remain there thinking until the solution became clear to him.

James Brindley died on September 30, 1772, at Turnhurst, Staffordshire.
