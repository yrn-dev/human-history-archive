# The Life of Charlotte Brontë

The *Life of Charlotte Brontë*, written by Mrs Gaskell, is recognized as one of the chief literary biographies of its subject. First published in 1857, the work presents the story of the Brontë family with a level of interest comparable to a novel.

## Publication History and Revisions
Following its initial release in 1857, the *Life* sparked various controversial questions. These disputes concerned the identity of the school "Lowood" in the novel *Jane Eyre* (specifically its connection to Cowan Bridge school), the nature of the relationship between Branwell Brontë and the wife of his employer, and the perceived peculiarities of Mr Brontë. Due to these issues and other minor points, the third edition of the biography was considerably changed.

While the work has been reprinted many times, the Haworth edition of 1902, issued by the original publishers Smith, Elder & Co., is considered its most satisfactory form. This specific edition includes a large number of letters written by Charlotte Brontë to her publisher, George Smith.

## Subsequent Biographical Works
Following Mrs Gaskell's biography, several other works provided supplemental material or new perspectives on the Brontë sisters:
* **T. Wemyss Reid:** Published *Charlotte Brontë: a Monograph* in 1877, which was the first new material supplied to supplement Gaskell's work.
* **A.C. Swinburne:** Inspired by Reid's monograph, Swinburne issued a separate essay titled *A Note on Charlotte Brontë* in 1877.
* **Augustine Birrell:** Published *Life of Charlotte Brontë* in 1887.
* **Clement Shorter:** Produced several works, including *Charlotte Brontë and her Circle* (1896), *Charlotte Brontë and her Sisters* (1906), and *The Brontës: Life and Letters* (1907), the latter of which contains all of Charlotte Brontë's letters in chronological order.
* **William Wright, D.D.:** Wrote *The Brontës in Ireland* in 1893.
* **Butler Wood:** Edited the Brontë Society publications from 1895 to 1907.
* **Madame Duclaux (Miss A. Mary F. Robinson):** Wrote a separate biography of Emily Brontë in 1883 and an essay in *Grands Écrivains d'outre-Manche*.

## Context of Charlotte Brontë's Life
Charlotte Brontë (1816–1855) was one of the six children of Patrick Brontë, a clergyman of the Church of England and perpetual incumbent of the parish of Haworth. Along with her sisters Emily and Anne, Charlotte developed precocious literary aspirations during a prolonged home life at Haworth under the guidance of her aunt, Elizabeth Branwell.

Charlotte's early education included a period at the Clergy Daughters' school at Cowan Bridge in 1824. The drastic discipline and bad food of this institution were later reflected in her novel *Jane Eyre* through the fictional school "Lowood" and the character Mr Naomi Brocklehurst, who is widely accepted as a counterpart to the school's principal, the Rev. William Carus Wilson.

## Literary Career and Personal Life
Charlotte Brontë's professional literary journey began with a failed volume of poems published in 1846 under the pseudonym Currer Bell (alongside Ellis and Acton Bell). She later submitted a novel titled *The Professor* (originally *The Master*) to Smith, Elder & Co. While the firm found it too short for the required three-volume format, they encouraged a longer work, leading to the publication of *Jane Eyre* in October 1847.

Other major novels by Charlotte include *Shirley* (published October 1849), and *Villette* (published January 1853). While she is considered undistinguished as a poet, her novels—particularly *Jane Eyre* and *Villette*—are noted for their independence, intensity, and rough individuality.

In her personal life, Charlotte faced significant tragedy, losing her siblings Branwell, Emily, and Anne within a span of eight months between September 1848 and May 1849. In 1854, she married Arthur Bell Nicholls, her father's curate, in Haworth church on June 29. The couple spent their honeymoon in Ireland before returning to Haworth to live with Mr Brontë.
