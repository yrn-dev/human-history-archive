# Athanasia, oder Grunde fur die Unsterblichkeit der Seele

*Athanasia, oder Grunde fur die Unsterblichkeit der Seele* (2nd ed., Mainz, 1838) is a work authored by the Austrian philosopher and priest Bernhard Bolzano.

## Author Biography
Bernhard Bolzano (1781–1848) was born in Prague on October 5, 1781. He showed early distinction in his studies and was ordained as a priest in 1805. Following his ordination, he was appointed as a professor of the philosophy of religion at Prague University. In his lectures, Bolzano sought to demonstrate that reason was in complete harmony with Catholic theology, an approach that garnered significant interest from younger thinkers of the era.

Despite this interest, Bolzano's views faced substantial opposition. He was only able to maintain his professorial chair through the protection of the archbishop, Prince Salm-Salm. In 1820, Bolzano was forced to resign after being accused of connections to revolutionary societies among students. Furthermore, Rome condemned several doctrines extracted from his writings, and he was suspended from his priestly functions. Bolzano spent the remainder of his life dedicated to literary work until his death in Prague on December 18, 1848.

## Philosophical Influences and Methodology
In the field of philosophy, Bolzano was influenced by Kant. He followed the monadology of Leibnitz and the ethics of Reinhard. 

Bolzano's broader intellectual contributions include the advocacy of a scientific method for the study of logic, as detailed in his four-volume work *Wissenschaftslehre, oder Versuch einer neuen Darstellung der Logik* (Sulzbach, 1837). He also produced the *Lehrbuch der Religionswissenschaft* (Sulzbach, 1834), which consists of four volumes providing a philosophic representation of the dogmas of Roman Catholic theology.
