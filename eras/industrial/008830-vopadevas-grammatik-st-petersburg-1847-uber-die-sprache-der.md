# Vopadevas Grammatik

## Overview
*Vopadevas Grammatik* is a philological work published in St Petersburg in 1847. It was authored by Otto von Bohtlingk, a distinguished 19th-century German scholar specializing in Sanskrit and comparative philology.

## Authorship and Context
The author, Otto von Bohtlingk (1815–1904), was born in St Petersburg on May 30 (June 11 O.S.), 1815. His academic foundation in Oriental languages—specifically Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic—was established between 1833 and 1835 at the university of St Petersburg. He furthered his studies in Germany at the university of Berlin and subsequently in Bonn from 1839 to 1842.

Upon returning to St Petersburg in 1842, Bohtlingk became attached to the Royal Academy of Sciences, eventually being elected as an ordinary member of the society in 1855. His professional career also included appointments as a "Russian state councillor" in 1860 and later as a "privy councillor," which carried a title of nobility. In his later years, he resided in Jena (from 1868) and Leipzig (from 1885), where he lived until his death on April 1, 1904.

## Relation to Other Works
*Vopadevas Grammatik* was published following Bohtlingk's first major scholarly contribution, an edition of Panini's *Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln* (Bonn, 1839-1840). That earlier work served as a criticism of the philological methods employed by Franz Bopp; Bohtlingk later republished it in 1887 in Leipzig under the title *Paninis Grammatik mit Ubersetzung*, which included a complete translation.

Following the publication of *Vopadevas Grammatik* in 1847, Bohtlingk continued to produce significant works in the field of linguistics and Indian studies, including:
* *Uber die Sprache der Jakuten* (St Petersburg, 1851)
* *Uber den Accent im Sanskrit* (1843)
* *Indische Spruche* (St Petersburg, 1870-1873)
* Critical examinations and translations of the *Chhandogya-upanishad* and *Brihadaranyaka-upanishad* (both St Petersburg, 1889)

Bohtlingk's most significant achievement, described as his *magnum opus*, was the *Sanskrit-Worterbuch*. This seven-volume dictionary was published in St Petersburg between 1853 and 1875 (with a new edition from 1879 to 1889). The project took twenty-three years to complete and was produced with the assistance of Albrecht Weber and Rudolf Roth.
