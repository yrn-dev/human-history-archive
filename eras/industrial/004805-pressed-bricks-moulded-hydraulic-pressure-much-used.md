# Pressed Bricks

Pressed bricks are a category of building materials characterized by being moulded under hydraulic pressure. They are primarily utilized for facing work. A distinguishing physical feature of these bricks is the presence of a deep frog, or hollow, on one or both of their horizontal faces. This design serves two purposes: it reduces the overall weight of the brick and provides an excellent key for the mortar.

## The Brick Press and Manufacturing
Brick presses are generally constructed entirely of iron, though other metals are occasionally employed for the dies and moulds. The essential components of a brick press include a frame or box for moulding the clay, a ram that carries a die or plunger to provide the necessary pressure, and a mechanism to push the finished brick out of the moulding box.

While some presses are designed for hand power, most are adapted for belt-driving. In recent years, hydraulic presses have seen increased use, particularly in America and Germany. The primary variations in these machines are found in the methods used to actuate the ram. In many modern presses, especially those utilizing semi-plastic clay, pressure is applied simultaneously from the top and bottom via a second ram working upwards from beneath.

## Materials and Processes
The production of pressed bricks varies based on the type of clay used:

*   **Plastic and Tempered Clay:** The highest quality bricks are still pressed from plastic or tempered clay.
*   **Semi-Plastic Clay:** There has been significant development in the manufacture of semi-plastic or "dust-made" bricks, particularly in districts using shales. Semi-plastic clay consists of ground shale or clay that is damp enough to adhere under pressure. To produce this, hard clay or shale is crushed using heavy rollers in an iron grinding-pan with a perforated bottom. The resulting powder is passed through a sieve or screen to remove coarse particles before being fed into the press. Because the material is a coarse, damp powder, the presses used for semi-plastic bricks are generally more heavily built and capable of exerting greater pressure than those used for plastic clay.

## Related Hydraulic Pressing Applications
Hydraulic pressure is also employed in the creation of other brick-like blocks and materials:

*   **Mortar Bricks:** These are unburnt blocks of artificial stone made from a mixture of sand and slaked lime. Some versions are moulded under hydraulic presses and subsequently treated with carbon dioxide under pressure, sometimes with the application of mild heat.
*   **Sand-Lime Bricks:** These blocks are made by mixing dry sand with approximately one-tenth of its weight in powdered slaked lime. The mixture is moistened and moulded under powerful presses capable of exerting pressure of about 60 tons per square inch. They are then hardened in steel cylinders using high-pressure steam.
*   **Briquettes:** Known as "patent fuel," these consist of small coal and 8 to 10% gas pitch. The mass is moulded into blocks and compacted by a pressure of ½ to 2 tons per square inch using a machine with a rotating die-plate similar to those used for semi-plastic clay bricks.

## Comparison with Other Brick Types
In Great Britain, pressed bricks are distinguished from various other brick qualities:
*   **Stocks:** Hard, sound, well-burnt bricks for ordinary purposes.
*   **Hard Stocks:** Over-burnt bricks used for wall footings.
*   **Shippers:** Hard-burnt bricks of imperfect shape used as ship ballast.
*   **Rubbers or Cutters:** Sandy bricks suitable for rubbing to shape.
*   **Grizzles:** Under-burnt bricks used for inferior work.
*   **Place-bricks:** Defective, under-burnt bricks for temporary work.
*   **Chuffs:** Badly burnt bricks that are cracked and defective in shape.
*   **Burrs:** Vitrified lumps used for garden work or rough walling.
