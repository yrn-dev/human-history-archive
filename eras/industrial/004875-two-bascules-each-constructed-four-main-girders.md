# Tower Bridge and Bascule Bridge Systems

## Overview of Bascule Bridges
A bascule bridge is a type of movable bridge designed to be closed for the transport of roads, railways, or aqueducts, while opening to allow for the passage of navigation. The original form of this system is the fortress draw-bridge, which utilizes a single leaf (bascule) that rotates around a horizontal hinge at one abutment, raised by counterweights and chains. 

A more common variation consists of two leaves or bascules, with one hinged at each abutment. In these designs, each bascule is prolonged backwards beyond the hinge to create a balance; this prolongation sinks into the piers during the opening process. When the bridge is closed, the two bascules are locked at the center.

## The Tower Bridge, London
The Tower Bridge in London is a suspension bridge that incorporates a secondary bascule bridge in its center span to facilitate the passage of ships. The structure features a total length of 940 ft., with approaches extending 1260 ft. to the north and 780 ft. to the south. The width between parapets is 60 ft., narrowing to 49 ft. across the center span.

The bridge's architecture includes two main towers in the river and two towers on the shore abutments, which support the suspension chains. These towers are constructed from a steel skeleton enclosed in brickwork and a facing of Portland stone and granite. High-level footways are provided for use when the bascules are raised; these footways are 141 ft. above Trinity H.W. and utilize main girders of the suspended and cantilever types. The middle girders of these footways are 120 ft. long and are attached to cantilevers via links.

The suspension chains are designed as braced girders to resist unsymmetrical loading. They are carried across the center span as horizontal ties resting on the high-level footway girders. These ties are connected to hanging chains by 20 in. diameter pins with 5 in. thick half rings. Rocker bearings under these pins transmit the load to the towers' steel columns.

## Bascule Mechanism of Tower Bridge
The opening bridge between the river towers consists of two bascules that rotate in a vertical plane, pivoted near the faces of the piers. When these are raised, they leave an unobstructed width of 200 ft. between the main river piers.

### Construction and Dimensions
Each bascule is constructed with four main girders. These girders project 100 ft. beyond the face of the piers and 62 ft. 6 in. within the face of the piers. Over the river, these are lattice girders. The structural layout includes:
*   **Transverse Girders:** Inserted between main girders at 12 ft. intervals.
*   **Floor:** Composed of buckled plates divided into rectangles of 3 ft. by 3½ ft. by longitudinal and subsidiary transverse girders, paved with wood blocks (pine blocks dowelled).
*   **Rotation:** The bascules rotate through an angle of 82°. The axis of rotation is located 5 ft. 7 in. below the roadway and 13 ft. 3 in. inside the face of the piers.

### Weight and Operation
Each leaf, including its ballast, weighs approximately 1070 tons. The rear ends of the bascules, located in the pier chambers, carry 365 tons of counterweight (ballast). 

The rotation occurs on forged steel shafts that are 48 ft. long and 21 in. in diameter. This axis is supported by eight bearings, which consist of rings of live rollers 22 in. long and 4-7/16 in. in diameter. The bascules are operated by hydraulic machinery, specifically pinions driven by hydraulic engines working in steel sectors with a 42 ft. radius. While the bascules can be lifted or lowered in one minute, the process usually takes one and a half minutes.

## Other Bascule Examples
Other examples of bascule systems include a road bridge at Konigsberg. This bridge features two fixed spans of 39 ft. and a central span of 60 ft. between bearings (41 ft. clear) with balanced bascules. Each bascule at Konigsberg uses two main girders—6 ft. deep at the hinge and diminishing to 1 ft. 7 in. at the center of the span—hung on a horizontal shaft 8-5/8 in. in diameter. 

The Konigsberg bridge utilizes a 12 ft. long depressed cantilever arm as a counterweight. It is operated by hydraulic power via an accumulator with a 34-ton load, providing pressure water at 630 lb per sq. in. This system allows the bridge to open in 15 seconds and close in 25 seconds.
