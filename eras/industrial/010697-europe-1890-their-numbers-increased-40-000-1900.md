## Migration and Foreign Residency in Belgium
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the emigration of Belgians from their own country was characterized as small with little variation. In 1900, 13,492 Belgians emigrated, a figure that rose to 14,752 by 1904. It is estimated that a significant number of Belgians resided abroad, with 400,000 living in France, 15,000 in Holland, 12,000 in Germany, and 4,600 in Great Britain.

Conversely, Belgium hosted a variety of foreign residents. In 1900, the nationalities of these residents included:
*   French: 85,735
*   Germans: 42,079
*   Dutch: 54,491
*   Luxemburgers: 9,762
*   English: 5,096
*   Other nationalities: 14,411

## European Influence and Immigration to the United States
European populations contributed significantly to the demographic makeup of the United States, specifically in the state of Illinois. By 1900, 51.2% of the population of Illinois was of foreign parentage, meaning either one or both parents were foreign-born. 

The principal foreign element in Illinois was German, with Teutonic immigration being especially large during the decade ending in 1860. Immigrants from the United Kingdom were the second most important group, followed by those from Scandinavian countries in third, and immigrants from southern Europe in fourth.

Beyond demographics, European civilization influenced religious movements in other parts of the world. The Arya Samaj, a movement in India, was supported by educated natives who sought a faith that harmonized with the ethics of European civilization and the discoveries of modern science. These supporters utilized the movement to engage in polemics with the rationalist and agnostic literature of Europe.
