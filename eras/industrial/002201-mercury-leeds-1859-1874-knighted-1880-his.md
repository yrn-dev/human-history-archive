# Sir Edward Baines

## Biography and Career
Sir Edward Baines (1800–1890) was a British politician, editor, and author. He served as the Member of Parliament (M.P.) for Leeds from 1859 to 1874. In recognition of his services, he was knighted in 1880. In addition to his political career, Baines was the editor and later the proprietor of the *Leeds Mercury*.

## Family and Relations
Sir Edward was the son of Edward Baines (1774–1848), an English politician and newspaper proprietor who was born in Walton-le-Dale, near Preston, Lancashire. The elder Baines had been apprenticed to a printer in Preston before moving to Leeds to start his own business. In 1801, with the help of party friends, Edward Baines purchased the *Leeds Mercury*, transforming it into a powerful organ of Liberal opinion in Leeds through his political articles. 

The elder Baines was an advocate for parliamentary reform and a supporter of the Liberal party, though he maintained independent views. He strongly opposed government interference in national education and advocated for the separation of church and state—a position shared by his son, Edward. Edward Baines (the father) served as a member of parliament for Leeds from 1834 to 1841, having succeeded Macaulay.

Sir Edward also had a brother, Matthew Talbot Baines (1790–1860). Matthew Talbot Baines entered the bar and served as the recorder of Hull in 1837. His political career included serving as M.P. for Hull in 1847 and as president of the Poor Law Board in 1849. After being returned for Leeds in 1852, he again served as president of the Poor Law Board until 1855, and later entered the cabinet in 1856 as the chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster.

## Published Works
Sir Edward Baines authored a work titled *History of the Cotton Manufacture*, published in 1835, which remained a standard authority for a long period. Additionally, a biography titled *Life* (1861), detailing the life of his father, was written by Sir Edward.
