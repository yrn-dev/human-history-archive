# The 18th Century

The 18th century was a period marked by significant developments in the arts, book production, urban planning, and regional political struggles across Europe and the Near East.

## Book Production and Literature
During this century, France and England saw notable improvements in presswork, type, and paper. Toward the end of the century, these improvements extended to Italy and Germany. Books generally became more elegant and neat. Book illustration experienced a revival through the French *livres-a-vignettes*, and French artists, including Gravelot, illustrated English books. In the final quarter of the century, woodcut illustrations—which became known as wood engravings due to the use of the graver—were greatly revived through the work of Bewick.

Binding techniques for the average work improved, although the best binders of the 18th century remained inferior to those of the 17th century until the arrival of Roger Payne. Trade bindings shifted away from the common use of calf and sheepskin, moving instead toward paper boards.

In terms of publishing and pricing:
*   **Poetry:** Prior and Pope followed the lucrative practice of publishing poetry by subscription at high prices, though Pope sold single poems for 1s. and 1s. 6d.
*   **Novels:** These were typically published in several volumes. Prices remained steady for many years, starting at 1s. 6d. per volume at the beginning of the century before rising. For example, Miss Burney's *Evelina* (3 vols., 1778) cost 7s. 5d., *Cecilia* (5 vols., 1782) cost 12s. 6d., and *Camilla* (5 vols., 1796) cost L1:1s.
*   **Reference Works:** Johnson's *Dictionary* (2 vols. folio, 1755) was priced at L4:4s. in sheets and L4:15s. in boards.

## Urban Development and Regional Changes
In France, the city of Bordeaux experienced a period of architectural and commercial activity in the middle of the 18th century. The marquis de Tourny, the *intendant* of Guienne, improved the city by laying out public squares and widening its streets. 

In the district of Dombes in eastern France, the Legislative Assembly decided at the end of the 18th century to reduce the area of rain-water pools. These pools, some dating back to the 15th century, had been created by landed proprietors for fish-breeding; however, this policy had resulted in depopulation and disease. At the end of the century, the pools covered twice their present extent.

In Brittany, the royal power attempted to reduce provincial privileges, leading to vigorous resistance. This struggle was particularly intense between 1760 and 1769, during which the duc d'Aiguillon fought against the parliament and the estates.

## Political and Social Conditions in the East
In the Balkans and the Near East, the 18th century was characterized by varying degrees of instability and local autonomy:
*   **Banjaluka:** This city saw encounters between Turks and Austrians in 1737. During the last half of the 18th century, no Bosnian city possessed greater importance or prosperity.
*   **Macedonia and Thrace:** The condition of the subject population worsened during a period of anarchy that coincided with the decadence of central power in the 17th and 18th centuries. Toward the end of the 18th century, local governors became practically independent. Ismail Bey maintained an army of 10,000 men at Serres, while western Macedonia fell under the control of Ali Pasha of Iannina.

## Music and Art
The 18th century saw the continued development of the cor anglais (the *haute-contre de hautbois*), an alto Pommer that was improved with key-work. While its development began earlier, 18th-century examples of the instrument are documented.

In the realm of decorative arts, the 18th century saw the invasion of French and Italian fashions in Germany. This occurred despite the long-standing influence of German draughtsmen—such as Durer and the "Little Masters"—on the decorative styles and the tradition of ornamental book-plates.

## Architecture
The century also left marks on religious architecture. Certain church structures from this period include the addition of belfries and aisles, as well as the installation of marble altars.
