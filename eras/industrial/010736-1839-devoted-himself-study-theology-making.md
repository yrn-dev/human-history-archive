# Theological Studies and Academic Appointments in the 19th Century

The 19th century saw various scholars and divines engaging in the study of theology, often intersecting with philosophy, history, and other academic disciplines. This period was marked by the development of specific doctrinal histories and the reconciliation of faith with speculative thought.

## Isaac August Dorner
Born on June 20, 1809, in Neuhausen-ob-Eck, Wurttemberg, Isaac August Dorner was a German Lutheran divine. He received his education at Maulbronn and the university of Tubingen. Following two years of service as an assistant to his father, a pastor at Neuhausen, Dorner traveled to Holland and England to complete his studies and familiarize himself with different types of Protestantism.

Upon returning to Tubingen in 1834, Dorner eventually became a professor extraordinarius of theology in 1837. During his time as a student, he was taught by Christian Friedrich Schmid, a vigorous opponent of F. C. Baur. Encouraged by Schmid, Dorner authored a history regarding the development of the doctrine of the person of Christ, titled *Entwicklungsgeschichte der Lehre von der Person Christi*. The first part of this work was published in 1835, and it was completed in 1839. The work served as an indirect reply to the *Life of Jesus* published by his colleague, Strauss. 

In 1839, Dorner was invited to Kiel to serve as professor ordinarius. He later held professorships in theology at Konigsberg (1843), Bonn (1847), and Gottingen (1853), before settling at Berlin in 1862, where he served as a member of the supreme consistorial council. In 1867, he published *Geschichte der protestantischen Theologie* (translated as *History of Protestant Theology*).

## Karl Daub
Karl Daub (1765–1836) was a German Protestant theologian born in Cassel. He studied theology, philology, and philosophy at Marburg in 1786 and became a professor ordinarius of theology at Heidelberg in 1795. Daub led a school that attempted to bring about a speculative reconstruction of orthodox dogma by reconciling philosophy and theology.

His intellectual development was influenced by Hegel, Schelling, and Kant. Because of these shifting phases, he was referred to as the "Talleyrand of German thought." Daub's speculative theology was noted for ignoring historical criticism; he used philosophical dialectic to connect metaphysical ideas directly to Gospel narratives, viewing them as supernatural. This approach led him to deduce that Jesus embodied the philosophical idea of the union of man and God, while Judas Iscariot embodied the idea of Satan or a rival god.

His writings reflect these stages: *Lehrbuch der Katechetik* (1801) was influenced by Kant; *Theologumena* (1806), *Einleitung in das Studium der christl. Dogmatik* (1810), and *Judas Ischarioth* (1816, 2nd ed. 1818) were written in the spirit of Schelling; and *Die dogmatische Theologie jetziger Zeit oder die Selbstsucht in der Wissenschaft des Glaubens* (1833) and *Vorlesungen uber die Prolegomena zur Dogmatik* (1839) were Hegelian in principle.

## Johann Joseph Ignaz von Dollinger
Born in Bamberg, Bavaria, on February 28, 1799, Johann Joseph Ignaz von Dollinger was a church historian and theologian. After studying natural philosophy at the university in Wurzburg, he turned to the study of philology and mental philosophy in 1817. In 1818, he began studying theology, which he considered to be the foundation of every other science.

Dollinger focused heavily on the independent study of ecclesiastical history, a subject that was taught with indifference in Roman Catholic Germany at the time. He was ordained as a priest on April 5, 1822, after studying at Bamberg. In 1823, he served as a professor of canon law and ecclesiastical history at the lyceum in Aschaffenburg. He eventually became a professor of theology at Munich in 1826, a position he held for the remainder of his life.

## Other Scholars and Theological Pursuits
Various other figures integrated theology into their broader academic or literary careers:

*   **Nikolaas Beets (1814–1903):** A Dutch poet who studied theology in Leiden. While he early on focused on poetry and produced the successful imaginative work *Camera Obscura* (1839), he later became a pastor and a professor at the university of Leiden.
*   **Johannes Dumichen (1833–1894):** A German Egyptologist who studied theology and philology in Breslau and Berlin before devoting himself to Egyptian inscriptions and becoming a professor of Egyptology at Strassburg in 1872.
*   **Georg Bernhard Bilfinger (1693–1750):** A German mathematician and philosopher who initially devoted himself to theology as a boy. He later attempted to connect theology and philosophy in his treatise *Dilucidationes philosophicae, de deo, anima humana, mundo*.
*   **Johann Albrecht Bengel (1687–1752):** A Lutheran scholar who studied the works of August Franke, Johann Arndt, and Philipp Spener in theology at the university of Tubingen. He later served as a theological tutor (*Repetent*) and the master of the *Klosterschule* at Denkendorf.
