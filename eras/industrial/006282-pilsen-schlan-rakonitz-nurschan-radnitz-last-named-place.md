# Pilsen, Schlan, Rakonitz, Nurschan and Radnitz

The locations of Pilsen, Schlan, Rakonitz, Nurschan, and Radnitz are situated within Bohemia, a region noted for its industrial significance and mineral wealth. These areas are specifically linked by the extraction of coal, which is mined in each of these locations. Among these, Radnitz is distinguished for containing the oldest coal mines in Bohemia, dating back to the 17th century.

## Pilsen
Pilsen is identified as one of the important towns of Bohemia, with a population of 68,292. In the historical context of the region's administration, the town held a high rank; in 1547, the emperor Ferdinand granted the city of Budweis the privilege of ranking at the diet next to Prague and Pilsen.

Industrially, Pilsen is a center for the manufacture of agricultural and industrial machinery. The town is also associated with the production of beer, specifically "Pilsener," which is available in both bottle and draught varieties. According to chemical analyses by A. Doemens, Pilsener Bottle beer has an original gravity of 1047.7, an alcohol content of 3.47%, and extractives (solids) at 4.90%, while the draught version has an original gravity of 1044.3, 3.25% alcohol, and 4.58% extractives.

Demographically, Pilsen experienced a significant shift in its population composition during the 19th century. In 1861, Germans held a majority in the town. However, by 1880, the German population had declined to less than a quarter of the total. This shift was part of a broader trend in Bohemia where the Slav races grew in number due to economic causes, such as migration from the country to the towns, as well as the influence of nationalist movements and the establishment of parliamentary government and popular education.

## Schlan and Surrounding Areas
Schlan is one of the Bohemian locations where coal is mined. The area near Schlan is also associated with the early life of the Bohemian musical composer Anton Dvorak (1841–1904). At the age of twelve, Dvorak was sent by his father to Zlonic, a location near Schlan, to live with an uncle while attending higher-grade school classes. 

During his time in Zlonic, Dvorak studied elementary theory and the playing of the pianoforte and organ under the instruction of A. Liehmann, who served as the town's chief musician and organist. Dvorak eventually returned to Zlonic for a period to assist his father, who had established a business there, before eventually settling in Prague to pursue his musical career.

## Rakonitz and Nurschan
Rakonitz and Nurschan are listed as sites of coal mining within Bohemia. Along with Pilsen, Schlan, and Radnitz, they contribute to the region's status as a major manufacturing center in Europe, as the abundance of coal and iron has favored the development of metallurgic industries concentrated around the mines.
