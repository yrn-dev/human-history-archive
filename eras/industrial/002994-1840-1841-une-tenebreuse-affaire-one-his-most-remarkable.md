# Events and Publications of 1840 and 1841

The years 1840 and 1841 were marked by significant literary publications, diplomatic missions, scientific achievements, and the expansion of medical practices across Europe and the United States.

## Literary and Academic Works
During this period, several scholars and writers published substantial works. Jean Alexandre Buchon, a French scholar and Liberal, published a collection of *Chroniques étrangères relatives aux expéditions françaises pendant le XIII^e siècle* in 1840. He also concluded his *Choix de chroniques et mémoires sur l'histoire de France*, which spanned from 1836 to 1841. In the field of drama, the works of Kotzebue were published under the title *Theater* in 40 volumes between 1840 and 1841.

In Ireland and England, the poet George Darley published an introduction to the works of Beaumont and Fletcher in 1840, followed by the play *Ethelstan* in 1841. He also wrote the play *Thomas a Becket* in 1840. Additionally, Sir Thomas Phillipps edited correspondence from the Hague dating from 1627 and published it in 1841.

## Diplomacy and State Affairs
The year 1840 saw a major shift in the career of Bunsen following the accession of Frederick William IV to the Prussian throne on June 7. Bunsen, who had served as the Prussian ambassador to Switzerland from 1830 to 1841, was tasked with establishing a Prusso-Anglican bishopric in Jerusalem to demonstrate the unity of Protestantism. Bunsen's special mission to England took place from June to November 1841. With the support of the British government, the bishop of London, and the archbishop of Canterbury, the bishopric was established.

In the United States, Stephen Arnold Douglas was active in Illinois politics. In 1840, he worked extensively to carry the state for Van Buren and served as the secretary of state of Illinois for a few months. By 1841, Douglas had become a judge of the supreme court of Illinois, a position he held until 1843.

## Science and Medicine
In 1841, the French mineralogist and geologist Ours Pierre Armand Petit Dufrenoy, in collaboration with Elie de Beaumont, published a comprehensive geological map of France. This publication was the culmination of thirteen years of investigation (1823–1836) and five years of writing the accompanying text (1836–1841). The final work was published with two quarto volumes of text between 1841 and 1848.

Simultaneously, homoeopathy continued to spread throughout the United States. The system became known in New Jersey, Maine, and New Hampshire in 1840, and reached Michigan in 1841.

## Professional Transitions
The early 1840s also marked transitions for various men of letters. Robert Bell, an Irish man of letters who had been engaged in journalism since settling in London in 1828, concluded his journalistic career in 1841, subsequently moving into miscellaneous literary work.
