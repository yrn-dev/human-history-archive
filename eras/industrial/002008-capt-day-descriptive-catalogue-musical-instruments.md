# Descriptive Catalogue of Musical Instruments

The *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments exhibited at the Royal Military Exhibition, London, 1890*, compiled by Captain C. R. Day and published in 1891, serves as a significant reference for the documentation and illustration of various historical and global musical instruments. The catalogue provides detailed descriptions, plates, and technical specifications for a wide array of instruments, ranging from early wind instruments to traditional folk pipes.

## Wind Instruments and Early Bassoons
The archive's sources reference Captain Day's catalogue in the study of early bassoon-type instruments and related woodwinds. Specifically, the catalogue includes a dolcian in C (pl. vi. H), which is used for comparison with other early forms such as the "double curtail" described by Randle Holme or the single curtail by J. C. Denner. Additionally, the catalogue provides heliogravures of early basset horns (pl. v.), contributing to the historical record of the instrument's evolution.

## Bag-pipes and Folk Instruments
Captain Day's work extensively documents various types of bag-pipes and regional folk instruments. The catalogue includes illustrations of the following:
*   **The Cornemuse**
*   **The Irish bag-pipe**
*   **The Musette**
*   **The Border bag-pipe**
*   **The Highland bag-pipe**

Of particular note is a 15th-century Highland bag-pipe, dated MCCCCIX (1409), which was owned by Messrs J. & R. Glen of Edinburgh. This instrument is illustrated in the catalogue (pl. ix. A) and described on page 57. The instrument features two drones inserted into a single stock shaped like a wide-spread fork; these drones are tuned to A, which is in unison with the lowest note of the chaunter. The chaunter itself possesses a thumb-hole at the back and seven finger-holes in front.

Furthermore, the catalogue is cited in relation to the biniou, a traditional instrument of Breton peasants in Morbihan and Cornouailles. The biniou is often played alongside the bombard, a survival of the medieval chalemie, hautbois, or musette.

## Specialized and Rare Instruments
The *Descriptive Catalogue* also documents rare and curious instruments. This includes a small instrument in F, which is one of only three known existing examples of its type (the others being located in Brussels and the museum of the Conservatoire in Paris). This specific instrument is reproduced in the catalogue as pl. iv. F.

Additionally, the catalogue provides illustrations of the Cornetto Curvo and the Cornetto Muto. These instruments utilize pipes of short length that produce the fundamental note as well as the second (octave) and sometimes the third (twelfth) note of the harmonic series. To connect these harmonics diatonically, the pipes are bored with lateral holes for the fingers; six holes are sufficient to provide the different intervals of the scale.
