# The Forth Bridge

The Forth Bridge is a cantilever bridge constructed across the Forth, located at the only narrowing of the waterway within a distance of 50 miles. At this specific location, the channel is approximately one mile in width and is divided by the island of Inchgarvie.

## Design and Construction
The original design for the structure was a stiffened suspension bridge; however, this plan was abandoned following the fall of the Tay bridge in 1879. Construction of the current bridge began in 1882 and was completed in 1889. 

The bridge has a total length of 5,330 feet. Its structural composition includes:
*   A central tower located on Inchgarvie, measuring 260 feet.
*   Piers at Queensferry and Fife, each measuring 145 feet.
*   Two central girders situated between cantilevers, each 350 feet long.
*   Six cantilevers, each measuring 680 feet.

The bridge features two main spans, each 1710 feet in length. The extreme height of the towers above high water is 361 feet, and the clear headway is 157 feet.

## Engineering and Materials
The bridge was constructed using 38,000 tons of steel, a figure that does not include the approach viaducts. A double line of rails is carried by an internal viaduct consisting of lattice girders.

To ensure stability and balance, the outer ends of the shore cantilevers are loaded to balance the rolling load, 200 tons of additional weight, and half the weight of the central girder. The engineering of the bridge includes specific provisions to account for:
*   Longitudinal expansion resulting from changes in temperature.
*   Distortion caused by the sun acting on one side of the structure.
*   The effects of wind acting on one side of the bridge.
