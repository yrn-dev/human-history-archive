# Voyage of H.M.S. "Beagle"

The voyage of H.M.S. "Beagle" was a surveying expedition that took place between December 27, 1831, and October 2, 1836. The expedition is noted for its scientific contributions, particularly in the fields of geology and zoology.

## Appointment of the Naturalist
The naturalist for the expedition was Charles Darwin. Prior to the voyage, Darwin had studied at Shrewsbury and Shrewsbury School under Dr. Samuel Butler. He later attended Edinburgh to prepare for a medical career, though he was found unfitted for the profession. In 1828, he moved to Cambridge to study for the clergy, matriculating at Christ's College and receiving his degree in 1831. 

During his time at Cambridge, Darwin studied geology with Adam Sedgwick and developed a friendship with John Stevens Henslow. Following a geological excursion in North Wales in August 1831, Darwin received a letter from Henslow urging him to apply for the position of naturalist on the "Beagle." Although Darwin's father initially disliked the idea, the support of Darwin's uncle, the second Josiah Wedgwood, led to his appointment.

## Itinerary and Survey Areas
The "Beagle" departed on December 27, 1831. The expedition's route included the following locations and regions:
* **The Atlantic:** The ship visited the Cape de Verde and other Atlantic islands.
* **The Americas:** The expedition conducted surveys along the coasts of South America and its adjacent islands, which included the Galapagos.
* **Oceania and Asia:** The voyage included visits to Australia, New Zealand, Tasmania, Tahiti, Keeling Island, the Maldives, and Mauritius.
* **Other Stops:** The ship visited St Helena and Ascension.
* **Return Journey:** On the way home, the expedition visited the Azores, de Verdes, and Brazil.

The voyage concluded on October 2, 1836. It is considered practically certain that Darwin did not leave Great Britain again after this date.

## Scientific Results and Publications
The scientific findings of the voyage were disseminated through several publications. Charles Darwin produced work on coral islands and the geology of the countries visited, as well as his *Journal of a Naturalist* and various contributions to the official narrative of the expedition. Specifically, in 1844, Darwin published *Geological Observations on the Volcanic Islands visited during the Voyage of H.M.S. "Beagle"* in London.

In addition to geological studies, the voyage contributed to the study of ichthyology. L. Jenyns published a work titled "Fishes" as part of the *Voyage of H.M.S. Beagle* in London in 1842.
