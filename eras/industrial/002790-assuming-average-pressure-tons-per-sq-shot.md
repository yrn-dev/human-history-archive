# Ballistics of the 6-Inch Gun

The study of interior ballistics for a 6-inch gun involves the calculation of pressure, energy, and velocity as a shot advances through the bore during the combustion of a propellant, such as cordite. A primary objective in this field is the adjustment of the charge's gravimetric density (G.D.) relative to the weight of the shot to ensure that the maximum pressure does not exceed safe limits.

## Charge and Chamber Specifications
In a 6-inch range table, a specific charge is described as having a weight of 13 lb 4 oz (P = 13.25) and a nature of cordite, size 30. The gravimetric density (G.D.) is 0.504. The specific volume of the charge, measured in cubic inches per lb when filling the chamber in a state of gas, is 55.01. The product of these two values is 27.73.

The chamber capacity (C) for this configuration is 730 cubic inches. This volume is equivalent to a length of bore of 2.15 ft (or 25.8 in.), a measurement referred to as the equivalent length of the chamber (E.L.C.). If the shot were unable to move, the closed chamber pressure resulting from the explosion of a charge at this G.D. would reach nearly 49 tons per sq. in., a level considered too great to be safe.

## Pressure and Energy Calculations
The movement of the shot during the combustion of the cordite prevents the maximum pressure from reaching unsafe levels. For a scenario where the pressure limit is fixed at 16 tons per sq. in. (corresponding to a G.D. of 0.2), the powder-gas occupies a volume (b) of 1825 cubic inches. This volume corresponds to the shot advancing 3.225 ft.

Under the assumption of an average pressure of 8 tons per sq. in., the shot acquires an energy of 730 foot-tons, calculated as $8 \times 1/4[\pi]d^2 \times 3.225$. At this average pressure, the shot reaches a velocity of approximately 1020 f/s. The time required to travel the 3.225 ft at an average velocity of 510 f/s is approximately 0.0063 sec.

## Mean Effective Pressure and Work
The mean effective pressure (M.E.P.), measured in tons per sq. in., can be represented graphically as the height of a rectangle equal to a specific area. The M.E.P. multiplied by the cross-section of the bore ($1/4[\pi]d^2$) provides the mean effective thrust of the powder on the base of the shot. When this value is further multiplied by the length of the shot's travel up the bore (l), the resulting work is realized in inch-tons. This work is equal to the M.E.P. multiplied by the volume of the rifled part of the bore (the difference between the total bore volume B and the powder-chamber volume C).

For a 6-inch gun with a length (L) of 216 in., the volume B - C is 6100 cubic inches, resulting in an M.E.P. of approximately 6.4 tons per sq. in. It is noted that maximum pressure may exceed the mean pressure by a ratio of 2 or 3 to 1.

## Gas Behavior and Expansion
Once the combustion of the cordite is considered complete—which occurs at the stage where p = 16 tons per sq. in. and b = 1825 cubic inches—the subsequent expansion of the gas is assumed to follow an adiabatic law. In this law, pressure varies inversely as the $m^{th}$ power of the volume, with $m = 1.2$ serving as a good average value for cordite.

The work done in expanding the gas from a volume b to any volume B is expressed as $pb/\{m - 1\}[1 - (b/B)^{m-1}]$. For the 6-inch gun, the expansion up to the muzzle results in a ratio of $B/b = 3.75$.
