## Bridge Engineering and Load Calculations

In the study of bridge design, specific variables and formulas are used to determine the weight of main girders and the effects of traveling loads.

### Girder Weight and Stress
The weight of main girders ($W_g$) is calculated to carry the total live load ($W_l$) and the total flooring load ($W_f$), in addition to their own weight. This is expressed by the formula $W_g = (W_l+W_f)k/(1-k)$. In provisional designs where $W_g$ is neglected, the actual bridge members must be larger than the provisional design by a ratio of $k/(1-k)$.

Empirical relations provided by Waddell allow for the comparison of girder weights per foot run ($w_1, w_2$) for different spans ($l_1, l_2$) and live loads ($p, p'$). For example, the ratio $w_2/w_1 = ½ [l_2/l_1+(l_2/l_1)^2]$. Additionally, a partially rational approximate formula for the weight of main girders ($w_3$) is given as $w_3 = (w_1+w_2)l^2/(Cds-l_2)$ or $w_3 = (w_1+w_2)lr/(Cs-lr)$, where $l$ is the span, $s$ is the average stress on the gross section of metal, $d$ is the depth of the girder at the center, and $r$ is the ratio of span to depth ($l/d$). The constant $C$ varies based on the material and girder type:
*   **Iron braced parallel or bowstring girders:** $C$ varies from 1500 to 1800.
*   **Steel girders:** $C$ varies from 1200 to 1500.

A related constant $K$ is deduced from previously designed bridges with the same working stresses, ranging from 6000 to 7200 for iron and 7200 to 9000 for steel.

### Traveling Loads and Bending Moments
For a traveling live load of $w$ per foot run advancing from a left abutment, the bending moment at a section $C$ (at distance $m$ from the left) increases as the load advances until the span is completely covered. In the case of a series of traveling loads at fixed distances, the bending moment $M$ at section $C$ is determined by the resultants $W_1$ and $W_2$ on either side of the section.

The greatest bending moment occurs when one of the greatest loads is at the section and a specific condition regarding the sum of the loads ($W_1$ through $W_n$) relative to the span $l$ and position $x$ is satisfied.

### Shear and Impact Stresses
The greatest shear at a section $C$ occurs as concentrated loads advance. If $R$ is the resultant of the loads on the girder when $W_1$ is at $C$, the shear is $Rn/l$. This may increase if $W_2$ moves to $C$, depending on whether $Ra/l+d > W_1$ (where $d$ is the reaction at $B$ due to additional loads).

Impact stresses are handled via empirical allowances added to live load stresses:
*   **Floor stringers:** 20%
*   **Floor cross girders:** 15%
*   **Main girders (40-ft. spans):** 10%
*   **Main girders (100-ft. spans):** 5%
