## London Clearing House and Banking
In the early 19th century, the turnover of cheques in the United Kingdom was substantial. While the London Clearing House provided official figures, the total yearly turnover of cheques is estimated to be at least twice as great as those registered by the London office alone. This discrepancy is due to the inclusion of clearing houses in large towns across Scotland, Ireland, and England, as well as numerous daily exchanges, settlements between bank offices with multiple branches, and transactions where one bank customer pays another.

According to the *Appendix to the Second Report, Committee of House of Commons, Banks of Issue* (1841), the London clearings in 1839 involved 29 banks with figures of £954,401,600. In 1840, the number of banks remained at 29, and the figures were £978,496,800.
