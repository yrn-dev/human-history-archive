# Keilberg

## Geography and Topography
The Keilberg is the highest point of the Erzgebirge (known in Czech as *Rudo Hori*), reaching an altitude of 4,080 ft. The Erzgebirge mountains form the north-west frontier of Bohemia and maintain an average altitude of 2,600 ft. Due to the presence of easy passes traversed by good roads, as well as a great number of cultivated areas and numerous mining villages, these mountains often possess the appearance of a hilly undulating plain.

## Regional Settlements and Landmarks
Several villages in the region are constructed very close to the mountain summits. One such settlement is Gottesgab, which sits at an altitude of 3,345 ft.; this location is the highest place in central Germany and Bohemia.

The Erzgebirge connects to other mountain ranges and landmarks:
* **To the West:** The range combines with the Fichtelgebirge through the Elstergebirge. These are further united with the Bohmerwald via the plateau of Waldsassen.
* **To the East:** The Erzgebirge is separated from the Elbsandsteingebirge by the Nollendorf pass. This pass serves as an ancient military route to Saxony and was used by Napoleon I following the battle of Dresden in 1813.
* **To the South:** The region extends to the "Thermopylae of Bohemia," which was the site of the battles of Arbesau and Kulm.

Further east, the Elbe reaches its lowest point in Bohemia at an altitude of 367 ft. before escaping into Saxony.

## Surrounding Frontiers and Plateaus
The north-east frontier is defined by the Sudetes, which include several ranges:
* **Lausitzergebirge:** 2,500 ft.
* **Isergebirge:** Featuring the Tafelfichte, the highest peak at 3,683 ft.
* **Jeschkengebirge:** 3,322 ft.
* **Riesengebirge (Czech *Kroknose*):** These are among the highest mountains in central Europe after the Alps, peaking at the Schneekoppe at 5,264 ft.
* **Heuscheuergebirge:** 2,532 ft.
* **Adlergebirge:** 3,664 ft.

The fourth side of the rhomb is formed by the Bohemian-Moravian Hills, described as a broad series of low hills or a plateau composed of primitive rocks, reaching altitudes of 2,500 ft. in some areas.
