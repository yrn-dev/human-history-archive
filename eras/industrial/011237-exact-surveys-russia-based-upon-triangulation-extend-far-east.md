# Exact Surveys in Russia

## Scope and Extent of Triangulation
Exact surveys in Russia, which are based upon triangulation, extend eastward as far as Chinese Turkestan. In terms of longitude, this reach extends to approximately 75 degrees East of Greenwich. 

The progress of these geodetic surveys has long since covered the European portion of the empire. A direct system of triangulation, executed with high scientific precision, connects St Petersburg with Tiflis, located on the southern slopes of the Caucasus. Furthermore, St Petersburg is connected to Greenwich through European systems of triangulation. Consequently, Russia has adopted the Greenwich meridian as the zero point for all of its longitude values.

## Challenges in Asiatic Russia
While the European surveys are extensive, the surveys of Asiatic Russia are separated from those in Europe by the Caspian Sea. Beyond the eastern shores of this inland sea, it has not been possible to implement a system of direct geodetic measurements using first-class triangulation. This is primarily due to the arid nature of the trans-Caspian deserts, which have acted as an insuperable obstacle to the rigorous geodetic methods employed in Europe.

Because of these obstacles, Russian geography in central Asia relies on means other than direct measurement to determine the coordinate values of latitude and longitude for any specific point.

## The Trans-Caspian Triangulation
For the triangulation of the trans-Caspian region, the astronomical observatory at Tashkent is used as the initial starting point. This specific triangulation is classified as second-class and currently extends to the Pamir frontier beyond Osh. To determine the longitude of the Tashkent observatory, the telegraphic method was used differentially with Pulkova.

## General Mapping and Regional Surveys
Within European Russia, surveys conducted in the manner of other European countries since 1816 have been limited to the more densely peopled governments. Most other regions are covered only by "military surveys." 

Several maps serve the empire:
* **General J. A. Strelbitzki's Map:** Published between 1865 and 1880, this 10-verst map (1:420,000) is the most readily available map of the entire country.
* **Topographic Map (1:126,000):** This map covers all of western Russia, including Poland and the country of the Don Cossacks, and is intended to eventually extend over the whole empire.
* **Regional Scales:** Certain governments, including Kief, Moscow, the Crimea, Bessarabia, and Volhynia, have been published on a scale of 1:24,000.
* **Finland:** This region was re-surveyed between 1870 and 1895, with a map on a scale of 1:42,000 nearing completion for areas as far as 61° N.
