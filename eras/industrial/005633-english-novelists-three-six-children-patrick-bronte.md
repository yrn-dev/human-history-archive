# The Brontë Sisters

Charlotte (1816–1855), Emily (1818–1848), and Anne (1820–1849) Brontë were English novelists and three of the six children of Patrick Brontë. Their lives and literary achievements are characterized by a sense of pathos and intensity, often documented in biographies such as the *Life of Charlotte Brontë* by Mrs. Gaskell.

## Family Background and Early Life
Patrick Brontë was born on March 17, 1777, in Emsdale, Co. Down, Ireland, to parents of the peasant class. Originally named Brunty, he changed his name upon entering St John's College, Cambridge, in 1802. Before his academic pursuits, he worked as a schoolmaster and a weaver in Ireland. Following his time at Cambridge, he served as a curate in Wethersfield, Essex (1806), and briefly in Wellington, Salop (1809). By the end of 1809, he moved to Yorkshire, holding curacies at Dewsbury and Hartshead-cum-Clifton.

In 1812, at Hartshead, Patrick married Maria Branwell, a Cornishwoman. They had two daughters, Maria (1813–1825) and Elizabeth (1814–1825). The family later moved to Thornton, near Bradford, where four more children were born: Charlotte, Patrick Branwell (1817–1848), Emily Jane, and Anne. In April 1820, three months after Anne's birth, Patrick accepted the living of Haworth, a village near Keighley in Yorkshire. Maria Brontë died in September 1821; she is remembered through letters to Patrick and a manuscript essay titled *The Advantages of Poverty in Religious Concerns*.

## Literary Careers and Works
The sisters initially attempted to publish poetry, but the failure of these efforts led them to pursue novel writing. 

### Charlotte Brontë
Charlotte's first novel, originally titled *The Master*, was retitled *The Professor*. When submitted to the firm Smith, Elder & Co., the reader R. Smith Williams and the employers found it too short for the required three-volume format. In response, Charlotte wrote a longer novel, *Jane Eyre*, which was published in October 1847 and received wide acclaim, though it later faced a critical review in the *Quarterly* by Miss Rigby (Lady Eastlake) in December 1848. Her other notable novel, *Villette*, is cited for passages of magnificent poetry.

### Emily Brontë
Emily is recognized as both a poet and a novelist. Her novel *Wuthering Heights* is described as a monument of intensity, characterized by grey melancholy and grimness. As a poet, her works "Old Stoic" and "Last Lines" are considered among the finest achievements of poetry by any woman in English literature.

### Anne Brontë
Anne wrote two novels, including *Agnes Gray* and *The Tenant of Wildfell Hall* (published in June 1848). While her novels survive largely due to the vitality of the Brontë tradition, she also maintains a place in religious communities as a hymn writer.

## Publication and Identity
The novels of Emily and Anne were accepted by T. C. Newby and published together in three volumes in December 1847 under the pseudonyms Ellis and Acton Bell. These were released two months after *Jane Eyre*, which was published by Currer Bell. Because Newby attempted to convince the public that these works were written by the author of *Jane Eyre*, Charlotte and Anne visited London in the summer of 1848 to interview Charlotte's publishers in Cornhill to establish their separate identities.

## Final Years and Legacy
The family suffered a series of rapid losses in 1848 and 1849. Patrick Branwell died on September 24, 1848, followed by Emily on December 19, 1848, at Haworth. Anne fell ill and died in Scarborough on May 28, 1849, where she was buried. Within eight months, Charlotte lost all three of her youth companions and returned to Haworth to care for her father, who was becoming blind.

The sisters' lives have been the subject of extensive biographical study. Mrs. Gaskell's *Life of Charlotte Brontë* (1857) is a primary source, though its third edition was revised due to controversies regarding the identity of the school "Lowood" in *Jane Eyre*, the relationship between Branwell Brontë and his employer's wife, and the peculiarities of Mr. Brontë. Other contributing scholars and biographers include T. Wemyss Reid, A.C. Swinburne, Clement Shorter, Augustine Birrell, William Wright, and Madame Duclaux (A. Mary F. Robinson), who wrote a separate biography of Emily Brontë in 1883.
