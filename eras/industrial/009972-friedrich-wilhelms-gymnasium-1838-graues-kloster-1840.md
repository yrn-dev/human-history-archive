# Friedrich-Wilhelms Gymnasium and Graues Kloster

The Friedrich-Wilhelms gymnasium and the Graues Kloster (also known as the *Gymnasium zum Grauen Kloster*) were educational institutions located in Berlin. Both schools were associated with prominent German scholars and the development of the Prussian educational system.

## The Graues Kloster
The *Gymnasium zum Grauen Kloster* (Greyfriars Gymnasium) was established in Berlin by Frederick the Great. In the late 18th century, the institution was superintended by Anton Friedrich Büsching, a German geographer and theologian. Büsching, who had previously organized a flourishing school in St Petersburg, was called to lead the Graues Kloster in 1766. Through his example and writings, Büsching provided a new impulse to education throughout Prussia.

In the mid-to-late 19th century, the institution continued to be a site of significant academic leadership. Hermann Bonitz, a German scholar born in 1814, served as an *Oberlehrer* at the Graues Kloster starting in 1840. Later, in 1867, Bonitz was appointed as the director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium.

## Friedrich-Wilhelms Gymnasium
The Friedrich-Wilhelms gymnasium was another Berlin-based educational institution. It is noted in the archive for the tenure of Hermann Bonitz, who served as an *Oberlehrer* at the school beginning in 1838.

## Associated Academic Influence
The staff at these institutions often contributed to broader scholarly and systemic educational changes. Hermann Bonitz, who taught at both the Friedrich-Wilhelms gymnasium and the Graues Kloster, was a noted commentator on Aristotle and Plato. Beyond his teaching roles, Bonitz held positions as a professor at the gymnasium at Stettin (1842) and the university of Vienna (1849), and served as a member of the council of education in 1864. 

Bonitz's influence extended to the structural organization of Prussian schooling; he was chiefly responsible for the system of examination and teaching utilized in Prussian high schools after 1882.
