# January 1777

The month of January 1777 is associated with the birth of Marianne Elisa and the execution of the French poisoner Antoine François Desrues.

## Marianne Elisa
Marianne Elisa was born on January 3, 1777, in Ajaccio. Through the efforts of her brothers, she was admitted to the establishment of St Cyr near Paris, where she studied as a "king's scholar." This period of her education ended in 1792 when revolutionists disrupted the establishment, leading Napoleon to take charge of her and return her to Ajaccio.

Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted her the principality of Piombino and later Lucca. In 1808, she successfully sought the grand duchy of Tuscany. Marianne Elisa married a well-connected Corsican named Felix Bacciochi on May 5, 1797. Because Bacciochi was almost a nullity, her own ability and pride allowed her to exert significant influence over Italian affairs and administration.

Her relationship with Napoleon was often strained, and between 1813 and 1814, she provided assistance to Murat in his enterprises. After the fall of her brother, she retired to Bologna and later to Santo Andrea near Trieste, holding the title of countess of Compignano. She died on August 6, 1820.

## Antoine François Desrues
Antoine François Desrues (1744–1777) was a French poisoner born in Chartres to humble parents. After moving to Paris, he established himself as a grocer. Although his business appeared to flourish and he was regarded as a man of great devotion and piety, his personal extravagance left his finances in a deplorable state by the time he gave up his shop in 1773.

Desrues entered negotiations with a Madame de la Mothe to purchase a country estate. He invited her and her sixteen-year-old son to stay with him in Paris while the transfer was pending. During their visit, Desrues poisoned both the mother and the son. He then attempted to take possession of the property by forging a receipt for the purchase money.

The disappearance of the mother and son led to suspicions, resulting in the arrest of Desrues and the discovery of the bodies. He was found guilty and condemned to be burned and torn asunder alive. The sentence was carried out in 1777. Throughout the process and until the end, Desrues maintained hypocritical protestations of his innocence. The case caused a great sensation, and a dramatic version of the events was performed in Paris as late as 1828.
