# Panini's Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln

## Overview and Publication
*Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln* is a scholarly work published in Bonn between 1839 and 1840. It was the first great work produced by the German Sanskrit scholar Otto von Bohtlingk. The text served as an edition of the grammatical rules of Panini.

## Purpose and Critical Context
While presented as an edition of Panini's rules, the work was in reality a criticism of the philological methods employed by Franz Bopp. 

## Later Revisions and Translation
Forty-seven years after the initial publication, Bohtlingk revisited the material. In 1887, he republished the work in Leipzig under a new title, *Paninis Grammatik mit Ubersetzung*, which included a complete translation of the text.

## Author Biography
The author, Otto von Bohtlingk (1815–1904), was born in St Petersburg on May 30 (June 11 O.S.). He began his academic training in Oriental languages at the university of St Petersburg from 1833 to 1835, focusing specifically on Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic. He later pursued further studies in Germany, spending time in Berlin and subsequently in Bonn from 1839 to 1842.

Following his return to St Petersburg in 1842, Bohtlingk became attached to the Royal Academy of Sciences, where he was elected an ordinary member in 1855. His career included appointments as a "Russian state councillor" in 1860 and later as a "privy councillor," a role that carried a title of nobility. In his later years, he resided in Jena (starting in 1868) and Leipzig (starting in 1885), where he remained until his death on April 1, 1904.

## Contribution to Philology
Bohtlingk is regarded as one of the most distinguished scholars of the 19th century, with contributions of pre-eminent value to comparative philology and Indian studies. Beyond his work on Panini, his bibliography includes:
* *Vopadevas Grammatik* (St Petersburg, 1847)
* *Uber die Sprache der Jakuten* (St Petersburg, 1851)
* *Indische Spruche* (St Petersburg, 1870-1873), which received an index published by Blau in Leipzig in 1893.
* A translation and critical examination of the *Chhandogya-upanishad* (St Petersburg, 1889)
* A translation of the *Brihadaranyaka-upanishad* (St Petersburg, 1889)
* A treatise on Sanskrit accents titled *Uber den Accent im Sanskrit* (1843)

His *magnum opus* is the *Sanskrit-Worterbuch*, a seven-volume dictionary published in St Petersburg between 1853 and 1875 (with a new edition from 1879 to 1889). This massive undertaking took twenty-three years to complete and was achieved with the assistance of Albrecht Weber (born 1825) and Rudolf Roth (1821-1895).
