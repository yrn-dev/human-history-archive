# Boundary Commissions of 1884-1886 and 1895

The Boundary Commissions of 1884-1886 and 1895 were instrumental in the demarcation of the northern political boundaries of Afghanistan. These commissions were necessitated by the advance of Russia toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts, which created a requirement for a definite boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and Russia's trans-Caspian conquests.

## The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886

The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886 determined the north-western limits of Afghanistan. This commission fixed a boundary between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). The resulting line is described as being almost entirely artificial in its construction. 

Key geographical markers of this boundary include:
* **Zulfikar**: The point where the boundary leaves the Hari Rud, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs.
* **The Kushk**: The point where the boundary crosses the Kushk represents the most southerly point of the line, situated about 60 miles north of Herat.
* **Khamiab**: The point where the boundary joins the Oxus. This location is roughly 150 miles above the crossing-point of the Russian Trans-Caspian railway at Charjui.

From Khamiab, the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia, and continues to the river's source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## The Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895

Following the limits established by the previous commission, the Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 demarcated the boundary beyond Victoria Lake. This commission extended the line to the glaciers and snowfields that overlook the Chinese border. 

While this commission defined the limits, the actual dividing line between the Russian Pamirs and Chinese Turkestan—where the rugged Sarikol range intervenes—remained indefinite.

## Geographic and Cartographic Contributions

The work of these commissions provided a significant increase in the knowledge of Central Asiatic geography. During the 1884-1886 commission, the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan were mapped by Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich. 

Concurrent with these efforts, other explorers contributed to the mapping of the region:
* **Ney Elias**: Crossed from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs to reach the commission's camp, identifying the "Dragon Lake" (Rangkul) during his journey.
* **Captain (Sir William) Lockhart**: Led a mission that crossed the Hindu Kush into Wakhan and returned to India via the Bashgol valley of Kafiristan.
* **Colonel Woodthorpe**: Used the opportunity provided by Lockhart's mission to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush and verify previous explorations by W.W. M'Nair.

## Other Afghan Boundary Demarcations

In addition to the northern limits, Afghanistan's southern and eastern boundaries were defined by a series of minor commissions. These commissions operated based on the Kabul agreement of 1893. This process lasted nearly four years and concluded with the Mohmand settlement following an expedition in 1897.
