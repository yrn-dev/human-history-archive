# 1774–1789: Scientific, Political, and Literary Developments

The period between 1774 and 1789 was marked by significant contributions to natural history, the emergence of new political structures in America, and various advancements in cartography and literature.

## Natural History and the Work of Buffon
A central achievement of this era was the continued publication of the *Histoire naturelle, générale et particulière* by Buffon. This work is recognized as the first to present previously disconnected facts of natural history in a popular and intelligible form, thereby diffusing a general taste for the study of nature. While the work aimed to be a general encyclopedia of the sciences, some sections, particularly those concerning mineralogy, are noted for their weakness.

The first edition of the *Histoire naturelle* was published in Paris between 1749 and 1804 across forty-four quarto volumes. Between 1774 and 1789, seven volumes were published as a supplement to the first fifteen volumes (which had been produced between 1749 and 1767). The fifth volume of this supplement, published in 1779, is the famous *Époques de la nature*. Other related volumes during this broader period included nine volumes on birds (1770–1783) and five volumes on minerals (1783–1788). Additionally, a second edition of the work began in 1774 and was completed in 1804 in thirty-six quarto volumes; this version recast the supplement and suppressed anatomical descriptions.

## American Political Leadership and Governance
In the American colonies, the years 1774–1789 saw the rise of patriot leaders during the War of Independence and the subsequent formation of the United States government.

Charles Carroll of Carrollton, an American political leader of Irish ancestry, was active in various extra-legal bodies from 1774 to 1776, including the provincial convention, committees of observation, committees of correspondence, and the council of safety. He served in the continental congress from 1776 to 1779, where he was a member of the board of war and signed the Declaration of Independence on August 2, 1776. Later, he served as a member of the United States Senate from 1789 to 1792.

Francis Hopkinson, an author and statesman, became a member of the council of New Jersey in 1774. He served as a delegate to the Continental Congress in 1776 and 1777 and was part of the committee that framed the Articles of Confederation. From 1779 to 1789, Hopkinson served as the judge of the court of admiralty in Pennsylvania.

## Intellectual and Literary Works
The late 18th century saw various scholarly and literary publications across Europe. In 1779, the Bipont Editions—a famous 50-volume series of Greek and Latin classical authors—were first issued in Bipontium (the modern Latin name for Zweibruecken or Deux-Ponts in Bavaria).

In the realm of music, Burney wrote *A Plan for a Music School* in 1774 and, in 1779, provided the Royal Society with an account of the infant Crotch, who was noted for remarkable musical talent.

In France, the *Memoires secrets pour servir a l'histoire de la Republique des Lettres*, a register of news and scandals from the salon of Madame Doublet de Persan, was continued by Pidansat de Mairobert and others, reaching 36 volumes between 1774 and 1779.

In England, Horsley published *Remarks on the Observation ... for determining the acceleration of the Pendulum in Lat. 70 deg. 51'* in 1774. Between 1779 and 1785, he produced a five-volume quarto commentary on the *Isaaci Newtoni Opera quae extant Omnia*.

## Cartography and Exploration
England's naval conquest and scientific exploration were reflected in the cartographic works of the period. J. F. W. Desbarres published *Atlantic Neptune* in 1774 and a *North-American Pilot* in 1779, the latter of which first made known the naval surveys of J. Cook and others. Thomas Jefferys published *American Atlases* in 1778 and *West Indian Atlases* in 1775. Additionally, James Rennell, the surveyor-general of India, published the *Bengal Atlas* in 1781.

## Other Notable Events and Figures
The period included various social and political transitions. Louis Charles Machault d'Arnouville served as the bishop of Amiens from 1774 until the Revolution. At the estates general of 1789, he was an uncompromising Conservative who voted against every reform.

In English social history, the year 1779 saw a duel between Charles James Fox and Mr. Adams, while 1789 saw a duel between the duke of York and Colonel Lennox.
