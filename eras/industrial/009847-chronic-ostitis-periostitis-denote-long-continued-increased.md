# Chronic Ostitis and Periostitis

Chronic ostitis and periostitis are medical conditions characterized by an increased and long-continued vascular supply. These conditions primarily affect the shafts of the bones.

## Symptoms and Diagnosis
The primary symptom of these conditions is a dull pain within the bone, which tends to worsen at night. The affected area of the bone becomes tender and thickened, forming a lump known as a "hard node." This node is clearly visible through the use of X-rays. While septic ostitis may be confused with rheumatism or erysipelas, it can be distinguished by the presence of central tenderness and thickening.

## Causes and Pathology
The increased vascular supply associated with chronic ostitis and periostitis may be caused by rheumatism, syphilis, or injury. In more serious cases, a sub-periosteal abscess may strip a considerable area of the bone bare, which can lead to necrosis. 

In severe instances, inflammatory engorgement and devastating abscesses may result in "multiple necrosis," where several bones are affected simultaneously and large pieces of bone are killed outright. In some cases, the diaphysis (the piece of bone between the joint-ends) may be found loose within a large abscess cavity.

## Treatment and Surgical Intervention
Initial treatment for the affected limb involves keeping it elevated and ensuring it is rested. Pain may be eased through the use of fomentations and leeches, while iodide of potassium is cited as the most useful medicinal treatment.

Once the disease is recognized, surgical intervention is required. This involves making an incision down to the bone to scrape out the affected area, followed by disinfection using a solution of corrosive sublimate. To clear the bone of pus and germs, the surgeon may need to open the shaft of the bone for much of its length. Because these cases are serious, surgeons are cautioned that they are more likely to err by doing too little than by doing too much.

In certain scenarios, immediate amputation of the limb may be necessary to save the patient's life. Amputation may also be required at a later date if there is grave constitutional disturbance or long-continued suppuration.

## Related Bone Inflammations
Ostitis, meaning inflammation of the bone, can be either chronic or acute. Acute ostitis, also known as septic osteomyelitis, is a serious disease often found in young people. It is caused by the cultivation of virulent germs—specifically *Staphylococcus pyogenes aureus*—in the marrow and delicate growing tissue of the bone. These germs, which have a yellow tinge and collect in grape-like clusters, typically enter the bone via the blood-stream from the gullet, the membrane lining the mouth, or other parts of the alimentary canal. While these staphylococci may circulate harmlessly in a healthy individual, an injured bone provides an attractive focus for the germs.

Additionally, chronic inflammation of tuberculous origin affects the spongy ends of long bones, the vertebrae, and the bones of the feet and hands. In this form of ostitis, bacilli in the spongy tissue cause colorless corpuscles from the blood to collect around them, forming greyish-white heaps called tubercles. When these tubercles exist in large numbers at the expense of living tissue, "rarefying ostitis" is produced. If these tubercles break down, they form tuberculous abscesses. These abscesses should be surgically opened and the foci scraped out and disinfected; if allowed to open spontaneously, they are likely to become infected with pus-producing germs, accelerating destruction. Treatment for tuberculous foci includes the use of a splint for absolute rest, plenty of fresh air, iron, cod-liver oil, and potentially the injection of tuberculin. When tubercles run together and break down cancellous tissue, the condition is called caries (meaning decay or rottenness), though this term is also sometimes applied to syphilitic ulceration of the bone.

## Systemic Complications
Chronic bone disease, along with syphilis or long-continued suppuration in other parts of the body, can lead to albuminoid disease. This is a degeneration of the kidney caused by the irritation of the organs by toxic products.
