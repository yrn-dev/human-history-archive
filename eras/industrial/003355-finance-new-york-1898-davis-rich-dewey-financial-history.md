# Finance (New York, 1898)

The financial landscape of New York at the end of the 19th century was characterized by a complex infrastructure of banking houses, specialized financial figures, and a vast body of academic and professional literature documenting the evolution of American currency and credit.

## Banking and Financial Leadership
In New York, banking houses played a central role in both national and urban development. August Belmont, who succeeded his father as the head of his family's banking house, was a prominent figure in this sector. His work was particularly focused on railway finance and the financing and construction of the New York subway. Beyond his private banking interests, Belmont held leadership roles in political finance, serving as the chairman of the finance committee of the Democratic National Committee in 1904.

## Professional and Academic Literature
By 1898, a significant volume of literature existed to analyze the history and theory of finance in the United States and New York. Key authorities and texts utilized for the study of this period include:

*   **Historical Overviews:** Alexander D. Noyes published *Thirty Years of American Finance* in 1898. Other comprehensive works include Albert S. Bolles' *Financial History of the United States* (1884-1886) and Davis Rich Dewey's *Financial History of the United States* (1903).
*   **Banking and Currency:** The evolution of the sector was documented in William G. Sumner's *A History of American Currency* (1884) and *A History of Banking in the United States* (1896). Other specialized texts include Charles A. Conant's *A History of Modern Banks of Issue* (1896) and *The Principles of Money and Banking* (1905), as well as John Jay Knox's *History of Banking in the United States* (1900).
*   **Specific Financial Events:** J. S. Gibbons provided a detailed account of the New York financial environment in *The Banks of New York, their Dealers, the Clearing-House and the Panic of 1857* (1858).
