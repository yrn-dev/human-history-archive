# HISTORIOGRAPHY OF AUSTRIA AND RELATED GERMAN WORKS

The study of Austrian history and the broader German political landscape has been documented through a wide array of scholarly works, many of which were published in Leipzig during the 19th and early 20th centuries. These texts range from general histories of the House of Austria to specialized diplomatic analyses and studies of specific revolutionary periods.

## General Histories of Austria
Early English-language scholarship on the subject was led by Archdeacon William Coxe, whose *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792* was published in three volumes in 1817. This work was later continued by W. Kelly in 1853, with a new edition appearing in 1873. However, subsequent research eventually superseded Coxe's work.

Other significant general histories include J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates*, published in five volumes between 1834 and 1850. In the mid-19th century, A. Springer contributed to the field with *Geschichte Oesterreichs seit dem Wiener Frieden*, 1809, published in two volumes between 1863 and 1865, covering events up to 1849. Later, Franz X. Krones (Ritter von Marchland) produced a five-volume *Handbuch der Gesch. Oesterreichs* (1876-1879) and a work on modern Austrian history from the 18th century to the present (1879), the latter written from a German-liberal perspective.

## Studies of the 1848 Revolutions
The revolutions of 1848 and 1849 are a recurring theme in the archive's sources. Count F. von Hartig's *Genesis der Revolution in Oesterreich im Jahre 1848* (Leipzig, 1851) was noted for creating a great sensation at the time of its publication. W. H. Stiles provided an eyewitness account in the two-volume *Austria in 1848-1849* (New York, 1852).

Further analysis of this era is found in the works of J. A. Freiherr von Helfert, specifically *Gesch. Oesterreichs vom Ausgange des Oktoberaufstandes*, 1848 (Leipzig and Prague, 1869-1889). Helfert's work is cited as representing the Austrian view of the revolution. Other perspectives on the period include W. Rogge's *Oesterreich von Vilagos bis zur Gegenwart* (1872, 1873) and *Oesterreich seit der Katastrophe Hohenwart-Beust* (1879), both of which were written from a "somewhat violent German standpoint."

## Diplomatic and Specialized Histories
Diplomatic history has been explored through various focused studies. A. Beer published *Zehn Jahre oesterreichischer Politik, 1801-1810* (Leipzig, 1877) and *Die orientalische Politik Oesterreichs seit 1774* (Prague and Leipzig, 1883). Other diplomatic works include A. Fournier's study of Gentz and Cobenzl (1880) and F. von Demelitsch's volume on Metternich's foreign policy from 1809-1812 (1898).

The relationship between Austria and Russia was examined by H. Uebersberger in *Oesterreich und Russland seit dem Ende des 15ten Jahrhunderts*, with the first volume covering 1488 to 1605 (Vienna, 1905). Additionally, H. Bidermann wrote on the general state idea of Austria (*Gesch. der oesterreichischen Gesamtstaatsidee*) between 1526 and 1804.

## Other German and Regional Historical Works
Beyond the specific history of the Austrian state, the archive contains references to various other German historical studies published in Leipzig:
* **Early Medieval and Carolingian History:** B. Simson's *Jahrbücher des fränkischen Reichs* (or *deutschen Reiches*) under Ludwig the Pious (1874-1876) and E. Dümmler's *Geschichte des ostfränkischen Reiches* (1887-1888).
* **Imperial and Regional Studies:** S. Riezler's work on the literary opponents of the Popes during the time of Ludwig the Bavarian (1874).
* **Military Science:** Ed. von Bülow and von Rüstow published *Militärische und vermischte Schriften von Heinrich Dietrich v. Bülow* in Leipzig in 1853.
* **Biographical Works:** A. Springer also authored a two-volume biography of Friedrich Christoph Dahlmann (1870-1872).
