# Boiler Accessories

Boilers are vessels designed to heat water or other liquids to the boiling point, serving as the apparatus for producing steam. This process converts the potential energy of fuel, such as coal, into mechanical work via a steam-engine. To function safely and efficiently, all boilers must be equipped with specific mountings and accessories.

## Water Level Management and Regulation
A critical requirement for all boilers is that the water level be maintained above the highest part of the heating surfaces. The method of regulating this level varies by boiler type:
*   **Automatic Regulation:** In some water-tube boilers used on shipboard and certain land boilers, a mechanism actuated by a float automatically regulates the feeding.
*   **Hand Regulation:** Most boilers rely solely on hand regulation, though those with automatic systems still provide means for manual control.

To ascertain the actual water level, a glass water-gauge is used. This device consists of a glass tube and three cocks: two that communicate directly with the boiler (one positioned above and one below the desired water level) and a third that serves as a blow-out for testing the gauge's operation and cleaning it. Additionally, three small try-cocks are fitted at the proper water level—one exactly at the level, one above, and one below.

## Feeding Systems and Valves
The feeding of the boiler is achieved through one of three primary methods: an injector, an independent steam-pump, or a pump driven by the main engine. Feed-water enters the boiler through a "check-valve." The lift of this valve is managed by a hand-wheel and screw; when the feed-pump is inactive, boiler pressure keeps the valve on its seating.

In marine environments, "evaporators" are used to replace water lost through engine use, condenser condensation, or leakages in the evaporation cycle. These devices distill fresh water from sea-water using heat from live steam admitted into copper pipe coils. The resulting brine is removed via blow-out means, while the condensed water is returned to the feed-water.

## Corrosion Prevention and Maintenance
Corrosion can occur when chemicals used to purify or soften feed-water have a corrosive effect on boiler-plates. A significant cause of corrosion is oxygen from the air entering the boiler in solution with the feed-water. To mitigate this, best practices dictate delivering feed-water at positions where evolved air passes directly to the steam space rather than disengaging on under-water surfaces.

In cases where corrosion is a concern, zinc slabs are fitted in the water spaces. Rolled zinc is preferred over cast zinc, and the slabs are secured on studs kept bright to ensure direct metallic contact with the boiler-plate. This creates galvanic action where the zinc acts as the negative metal and dissolves, keeping the shell metal electro-positive. It is necessary to ensure that fragments of wasting zinc do not fall onto the heating surfaces.

## Construction and Material Requirements
Boilers are primarily constructed from steel and iron. Because they are subject to high pressures and temperatures, they must be made of materials that offer strength and high ductility when heated to furnace temperatures. To prevent grooving or fractures, designs must allow for elasticity and freedom of movement. This is achieved through:
*   The selection of iron or steel with a high percentage of elongation.
*   The use of bending and temper tests (both cold and hot).
*   Judicious staying, the limitation of tube lengths, and the flanging of certain parts.

In boilermaking, flanging is extensively used for tube-plates, end-plates, and furnace flues. Welding is common for stand pipes and furnace flues, and plates are frequently bent into cylindrical shapes for furnaces and shells. Holes in boiler work are generally drilled through the seams of adjacent plates.

## Essential Boiler Features
Every boiler must possess several essential features to operate safely:
1.  **Receptacle:** A container strong enough to withstand the highest intended steam pressure while holding water and steam.
2.  **Furnace:** A dedicated area for burning fuel.
3.  **Heating Surface:** A sufficient area to transmit heat from combustion to the water.

Desirable qualities for a boiler include a design that allows for reasonably perfect combustion, high evaporative efficiency, and a compact, cost-effective build. Furthermore, the design should allow for easy cleaning to maintain efficiency and require only an average amount of skill and attention for upkeep.
