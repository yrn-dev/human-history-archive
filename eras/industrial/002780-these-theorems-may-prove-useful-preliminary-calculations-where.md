# Theorems in Mathematics, Physics, and Textual Criticism

The term "theorem" is applied across various disciplines to describe proven principles, mathematical laws, or established theoretical frameworks used to analyze data and functions.

## Mathematical Theorems of Functions and Calculus

In the general theory of functions, G.F.B. Riemann established that a function with a finite differential coefficient at all points of an interval is continuous throughout that interval. However, a function may be continuous without being differentiable. This finding followed an attempt by Ampere to prove the opposite—that every continuous function must be differentiable. Further examples of continuous functions that are not differentiable were provided by Dini, Darboux, Weierstrass, and Riemann.

Within the study of differentiable functions, the "theorem of intermediate value" is considered the most important. Another significant result is Taylor's theorem, which states that if a function $[f](x)$ and its first $n$ differential coefficients are continuous in the interval between $a$ and $a + h$, then $h^2 [f](a + h) = [f](a) + h[f]'(a) + \dots \frac{f''(a)}{2!} h^{n-1} + \dots \frac{[f]^{(n-1)}(a)}{(n-1)!} + R_n$.

In the realm of integration, the "first and second theorems of the mean" concern the integral of the product of two integrable functions, $[f](x)$ and $[\phi](x)$. The first theorem of the mean posits that if $[\phi](x)$ is one-signed throughout the interval between $a$ and $b$, there exists a number $M$ between the greatest and least values of $[f](x)$ in that interval such that $\int_a^b M [\phi](x)dx = \int_a^b [f](x)[\phi](x)dx$.

## Complex Variables and Monogenic Functions

The study of complex or imaginary quantities involves several key theorems. Cauchy's theorem relates to the existence of a complex integral. Laurent's theorem is proved during the consideration of the differentiation and integration of series of functions of a complex variable. Other significant results include Mittag-Leffler's theorem and Weierstrass's theorem regarding the primary factors of an integral function.

Additionally, a theorem exists stating that the absolute value of a sum is less than the sum of the absolute values of the terms. This is used to prove that the integral $\int [f](z)dz$ round the perimeter of a region is zero, provided the closed path of integration belongs to a region where specific conditions are satisfied.

## Elliptic and Algebraic Functions

Abel's Theorem appears in multiple contexts. In one generalization, it is noted that for a doubly periodic function, the sum of the values of the argument in one parallelogram of periods for which the function takes a given value is independent of that value. In the context of integrals of algebraic functions, Abel's Theorem is enunciated regarding the sum of $m$ integrals $\int_{(b_i) \text{ to } (a_i)} R(s, z)dz$, which is equal to the sum of the coefficients of $t^{-1}$ in specific expansions of the function.

Further theorems relate to plane curves. For a curve $[f]$ of order $n$ with $1/2(n-3)n$ double points, coordinates are expressible as elliptic functions. A theorem states that if a variable curve $[\phi]$ of order $m$ passes through the double points of curve $[f]$, the sum of the values of the argument $u$ at the remaining intersections is unaffected by the variation of the coefficients of $[\phi]$, except for additive aggregates of the periods.

## Application in Ballistics

In the study of pneumatic dynamite guns, such as the Zalinski gun, theorems are used to calculate pressure, energy, velocity, and time curves based on the behavior of pressure up the bore:

*   **Uniform Pressure:** If pressure is uniform (a straight line parallel to AM), the energy-curve is a straight line through A, the velocity-curve is a parabola, and the time-curve is also a parabola.
*   **Uniformly Falling Pressure:** If the pressure-curve is a straight line sloping downwards, the energy-curve is a downward-curving parabola, the velocity-curve is an ellipse or circle, and the time-curve is a sinusoid.
*   **Uniformly Rising Pressure:** If the pressure-curve is a straight line sloping upwards, the energy-curve is an upward-curving parabola and the velocity-curve is a hyperbola.

These theorems are useful for preliminary calculations when the pressure-curve is nearly straight. When no observable law exists, Simpson's rule or a planimeter must be used to read the area of the pressure-curve.

## Theorems in Textual Criticism

In the study of ancient manuscripts, the work of Westcott and Hort (WH) is summarized into two theorems:
1. The text preserved in later manuscripts is not primitive but is constructed from earlier texts.
2. These earlier texts are classified as Neutral, Alexandrian, and Western, with the Neutral form being the primitive one.

While the first theorem is generally accepted as proved, the second has faced criticism and likely requires modification.
