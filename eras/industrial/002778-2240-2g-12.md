# Mean Effective Pressure (M.E.P.)

Mean Effective Pressure, denoted as M.E.P., is a measurement used in the analysis of gun ballistics to represent the average pressure exerted by powder-gas as a shot advances through the bore. It is expressed in tons per square inch.

## Graphical Representation and Calculation
The M.E.P. can be represented graphically using an indicator diagram, similar to those used for steam engines. This diagram uses a curve to show the relationship between the pressure of the powder-gas and its volume. In such a representation, the M.E.P. is illustrated by the height AH, where the rectangle AHKB is equal to the area APDB.

To determine the mean effective thrust of the powder on the base of the shot in tons, the M.E.P. is multiplied by the cross-section of the bore (1/4$\pi$d², where d is the diameter). To find the work realized in inch-tons, this value is further multiplied by $l$, the length of the travel of the shot up the bore (AB). Consequently, the work is equal to the M.E.P. multiplied by the volume of the rifled part of the bore (B - C), where B represents the total volume of the bore and C represents the volume of the powder-chamber.

## Mathematical Formulas and Energy
The relationship between muzzle-energy (E) and work in foot-tons is expressed by the equation:
$E = w/2240 V^2/2g = \{B - C\} / 12 \times \text{M.E.P.}$

From this, the formula for Mean Effective Pressure is derived as:
$\text{M.E.P.} = w/2240 V^2/2g \times 12/\{B - C\}$

In these calculations, $V$ denotes the velocity at the muzzle. While the net work realized by the powder-gas as the shot advances is equated to the kinetic energy ($e$) of the shot, certain factors such as friction, the recoil of the gun, and the rotation of the shot (determined by the axial radius of gyration $k$ and the angle of the rifling $\delta$) are considered. The fraction due to the rotation of the shot is typically less than 1% and may be ignored in subsequent calculations.

## Practical Application and Examples
In a practical application involving a 6-inch gun with a length (L) of 216 inches, the volume $\{B - C\}$ is 6100 cubic inches. In this instance, the M.E.P. is approximately 6.4 tons per square inch. It is noted that the maximum pressure can exceed the mean pressure in a ratio of 2 or 3 to 1, a result demonstrated in experiments conducted by Sir Andrew Noble using a 6-inch gun capable of being lengthened to 50 feet or 100 calibres.

## Related Pressure Measurements
The study of powder-gas involves various pressure and volume measurements. For example, cordite-gas at standard atmospheric pressure (14.7 lb per sq. in.) and 0°C occupies 700 ccs per gramme. When this same gas is compressed into 5 ccs at the temperature of explosion, it reaches a pressure of 16 tons per square inch. This is equivalent to 2440 atmospheres, based on the calculation $16 \times 2240 / 14.7$. In general terms, one ton per square inch is approximately 150 atmospheres.
