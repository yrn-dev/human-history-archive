# The Bombay Presidency (1853-1860)

The Bombay presidency during the mid-19th century was characterized by territorial expansion and the management of internal stability during a period of widespread unrest in India. Between 1853 and 1860, the presidency was under the leadership of Lord Elphinstone.

## Territorial Expansion and Administration
Prior to this period, the presidency had undergone significant growth. By 1803, it consisted of Surat, Bankot, Salsette, and the islands of the harbour. Throughout the early 19th century, the framework expanded to include the Gujarat districts (taken over in 1805 and enlarged in 1818) and the settlement of Mahi Kantha and Kathiawar between 1807 and 1820. Following the defeat and capture of Baji Rao, the last of the peshwas, in 1817-1818, the presidency incorporated large portions of his dominions, including Nasik, Poona, Sholapur, Ahmednagar, Belgaum, Dharwar, and Kaladgi.

By the time Lord Elphinstone took office in 1853, the presidency had further expanded through the addition of Sind (1843) and Aden (1839), as well as the lapse of certain native states. In 1853, the presidency also acquired the lease of the Panch Mahals from Sindhia.

The administration of the region had been influenced by the earlier policies of Mountstuart Elphinstone (governor from 1819 to 1827), who sought to rule on native lines and avoid changes for which the population was not yet ready, while simultaneously codifying laws, establishing schools and courts, and stopping the grosser abuses of the previous regime.

## The Crisis of the Mutiny
The period between 1853 and 1860 was defined by the crisis of the Mutiny of 1857. While the Indian Mutiny was a significant event in the broader empire, the Bombay presidency passed through this crisis without experiencing a general rising. 

Despite the lack of a general uprising, there were several localized disturbances:
* **Military Outbreaks:** Troops at Kolhapur, Ahmedabad, and Karachi engaged in outbreaks that were quickly suppressed. As a result of these actions, two regiments were disbanded.
* **Regional Rebellions:** Isolated and local rebellions occurred in the southern Mahratta country, among the Bhils, and within Gujarat.

The general stability of the presidency during this crisis is attributed to the leadership of Lord Elphinstone. However, the archive notes that the establishment of an orderly administration had created some underlying tension. Specifically, the regularity of tax collection became "doubly unwelcome" because it coincided with a general fall in prices, leading to feelings of resentment and misgiving among the population, even though they were generally prosperous and contented.

## Broader Imperial Context
The stability of the Bombay presidency occurred against a backdrop of imperial consolidation and controversy. James Andrew Broun Ramsay, the 1st Marquess and 10th Earl of Dalhousie, served as a governor-general and is described as one of the "master-builders of the Indian empire." While some critics on the eve of his death claimed his reckless annexations and centralizing activity aggravated the crisis of the 1857 mutiny, others view him as a far-sighted administrator whose policies consolidated British rule and provided the foundations that allowed his successors to stem the tide of rebellion.

Following the tenure of Lord Elphinstone, the presidency continued to evolve. Under Sir Bartle Frere (1862-1867), the region saw its highest point of agricultural prosperity due to the American Civil War, which created an enormous demand for Indian cotton in Europe.
