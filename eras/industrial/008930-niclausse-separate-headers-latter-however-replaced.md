# Niclausse

The Niclausse is a type of large-tube boiler utilized in various navies, including the British navy, as well as in several land-based electric-light installations.

## Design and Structure
The boiler is constructed around a horizontal steam-chest. Positioned beneath this chest is a series of elements arranged side by side over the fire. To protect the structure from the direct action of the fire, the entire assembly is enclosed in an iron casing lined with firebrick.

Each individual element consists of a header with a rectangular cross-section. These headers are typically made of malleable cast iron with cast-in diaphragms, although steel is sometimes used; in steel versions, the diaphragms are made of the same material and the bottoms are closed using a riveted steel plate. To allow for the easy removal of any single element, the headers are bolted to socket-pieces that are riveted to the bottom of the steam-chest.

## Tube Construction and Arrangement
The Niclausse boiler features two rows of inclined, close-ended tubes per element. These tubes are made of seamless steel and slope downwards toward the back of the boiler at an inclination of 6 degrees to the horizontal. 

The tubes are designed with a specific, peculiar construction:
* **Back Ends:** The lower or back ends are reduced in diameter and fitted with screwed cap-nuts that provide a complete closure.
* **Front Ends:** The front ends are thickened via upsetting. The sections that fit into the diaphragm and header walls are turned to gauge. 
* **Lanternes:** The upper and lower portions of the tubes between the fitting sections are cut away, leaving only the side portions; this specific end configuration is known as a "lanterne."

The tube-holes in the headers and diaphragms are accurately bored at an angle to match the inclination of the tubes, with the holes in the header walls being slightly conical.

## Water Circulation
The boiler employs a specialized system for water circulation. Inside each generating tube is a small circulating tube made of thin sheet steel. This inner tube is open at its lower end. At the other end, it is secured to a smaller "lanterne" that extends from the diaphragm to the front of the header. This smaller lanterne serves to close the front end of the generating tube. Consequently, when the tubes are installed, only the small inner circulating tubes communicate with the space between the front of the header and the rest of the system.

## Comparison with the Durr Boiler
The Niclausse boiler shares some similarities with the "Durr" boiler, which is used in the British and German navies. Both utilize inner tubes and a diaphragm in the water-chamber to provide circulation. However, they differ in several key technical aspects:
* **Headers:** While the Niclausse uses separate headers, the Durr replaces these with a single large water-chamber constructed from steel plates with welded joints.
* **Tube Security:** In the Niclausse, tubes are secured by "lanternes" to two plates. In the Durr, tubes are secured to the inner plate only via conical joints, and the outer plate holes are closed with small round doors fitted from the inside.
* **Installation:** Tubes in the Durr boiler are forced into position individually using a small portable hydraulic jack.
* **Materials:** The Durr boiler uses cap-nuts made of a special manganese and copper heat-resisting alloy to close the lower ends of the caps.
