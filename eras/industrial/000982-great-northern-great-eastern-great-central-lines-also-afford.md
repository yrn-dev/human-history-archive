# Communications of the Isle of Axholme

## Geography of Axholme
Axholme is an island located in the north-west region of Lincolnshire, England. It is situated between the rivers Don, Idle, and Trent, and is further isolated by drainage channels that connect to these rivers. The island's topography consists primarily of a plateau with a slight elevation that rarely exceeds 100 feet. The total area of the island is approximately 47,000 acres and encompasses the parishes of Crowle, Epworth, Haxey, Althorpe, Belton, Luddington, and Owston.

The name Axholme is a hybrid term derived from three different linguistic sources: the Celtic word *uisg* (meaning water), the Anglo-Saxon *ey* (meaning island), and the Norse *holm*, which carries the same meaning as the Anglo-Saxon term.

## Land Reclamation and Settlement
In its earliest periods, the area appears to have been covered by forest. Following the destruction of much of this forest, the region became largely a swamp. In 1627, King Charles I, who served as the lord of the island, contracted a Dutchman named Cornelius Vermuyden to reclaim the marshes and meres to make the land suitable for tillage.

This reclamation project resulted in the arrival of a significant number of Flemish workmen who settled in the district. Despite violent attempts by the English peasantry to expel them, the Flemish settlers remained in sufficient numbers to influence the accent and physical appearance of the local inhabitants into the present day. As a result of these efforts, the land is now extremely fertile. The principal towns on the isle are Epworth and Crowle, the latter of which has a population of 2,769.

## Railway Communications
The Isle of Axholme is served by several railway systems that provide essential communications for the region. The Axholme joint light railway runs through the isle in a north-south direction, providing a connection between Haxey junction and Goole. Additionally, communications are afforded by the Great Central, Great Eastern, and Great Northern lines.
