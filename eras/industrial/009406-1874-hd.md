## Legislation and Finance
In the field of finance, the act of 1874 established new categories for building societies. Under this legislation, societies are divided into three groups: those established before 1856 that remain under the act of 1836 without being incorporated under the 1874 act; those established before 1874 under the act of 1836 that have since been incorporated under the act of 1874; and those established after the act of 1874 was passed. While the first category continues to act via trustees—with only 62 such societies remaining—the second and third categories combined exceed 2,000 in number.

## Geography and Exploration
Significant geographical research and exploration occurred in 1874, particularly regarding Central Asia and the Southern Ocean.

### Central Asian Studies
Various reports and letters were published in the *Geographical Magazine* and *Ocean Highways* during 1874. These included Mr. Ashton Dilke's work on the Valley of the Ili (May 16) and letters regarding Belasaghun (June 17 and August 8). Eugene Schuyler published a work on Karakorum and Bala Sagun in December 1874, which included a note by Col. Yule. Other contributions included geographical notes on the basins of the Zarafshán and the Oxus by Alexis Fedchenko, and remarks on "Notices of Southern Mangi" by Mr. Phillips. Additionally, Sir Frederic Goldsmid's "Telegraph and Travel" appeared in the *Geographical Magazine* in April and October of that year.

### The Kerguelen Islands
In January 1874, the staff of the "Challenger" expedition visited and surveyed various parts of the Kerguelen Islands. Later that year, from October 1874 to February 1875, the islands were occupied by expeditions from the United States, Germany, and England for the purpose of observing the transit of Venus.

## Professional Transitions
The year 1874 marked a significant transition in the career of John Bascom. After serving as the professor of rhetoric at Williams College from 1855 to 1874, he moved to the University of Wisconsin, where he served as president and professor of mental and moral philosophy from 1874 until 1887.
