# Asheville

Asheville is a city located in the United States within the mountainous Blue Ridge region of western North Carolina. It serves as the county-seat of Buncombe county and is situated approximately 210 miles west of Raleigh. The city is positioned on a high terrace on the east bank of the French Broad river, at the mouth of the Swannanoa, at an elevation of about 2,300 feet above the sea.

## Geography and Infrastructure
Asheville is located at the junction of three branches of the Southern railway. The city is characterized by beautiful scenery and a dry, equable climate, which has established it as a popular health and pleasure resort in the south. It serves as a winter resort for those from the north and a summer resort for southerners. The area is surrounded by great forests and features an extensive system of suburban and city parks connected by a series of beautiful drives.

## Population and Public Facilities
The population of Asheville has shown steady growth over the early 20th century. In 1890, the population was 10,235; by 1900, it had increased to 14,692 (of whom 4,724 were negroes); and by the 1910 census, the population reached 18,762.

The city's public infrastructure includes several key buildings:
* City hall
* The court house
* The Federal building
* An auditorium
* The public library

Social life in the city is supported by various social clubs, including the Tahkeeostee, the Albemarle, the Asheville, the Elks, and the Swannanoa Country clubs.

## Education
Asheville and its immediate vicinity host several educational institutions:
* **Presbyterian Institutions:** Under the control of the Woman's Board of Home Missions of the Presbyterian Church, the city has a collegiate and normal institute for young women (established 1892) and a home industrial school for girls (established 1887), both of which occupy the same campus.
* **Industrial Schools:** The Asheville farm school for boys serves as an industrial school for negroes.
* **Other Schools:** The Asheville school for boys is located 5 miles west of the city. Additionally, the Bingham school, which was founded in 1793 by William Bingham in Pittsboro, N.C., was moved to a site 3 miles north-west of Asheville in 1891.

## Biltmore Estate
Located approximately 2 miles south-east of Asheville is Biltmore, the estate of George W. Vanderbilt. Spanning 125,000 acres, it is considered likely the finest country place in the United States. The estate's central feature is a chateau of French Renaissance design, modeled after the famous chateau at Blois in France, measuring 375 by 150 feet. 

The estate also includes a model village maintained by Mr. Vanderbilt, which provides a church, a hospital, an industrial school for whites, and an elementary school. Both the village and the chateau were designed by Richard M. Hunt.
