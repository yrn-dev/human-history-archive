# Barytocalcite

Barytocalcite is a rare mineral species characterized chemically as a double salt of barium and calcium carbonates, with the chemical formula $\text{BaCa}(\text{CO}_3)_2$. In this composition, the two carbonates are present in equal molecular proportions.

## Physical Properties and Crystallography
Barytocalcite crystals belong to the monoclinic system and typically exhibit a blade-shaped or prismatic habit. These crystals are white and transparent. The mineral possesses a specific gravity of 3.65 and a hardness of 4.

The mineral is characterized by specific cleavages. There are perfect cleavages parallel to the prism faces, which are inclined at an angle of 73° 6'. Additionally, there is a less perfect cleavage parallel to the basal plane; the angle between this basal plane and the prism faces is 77° 6'. These three cleavages approximate the angles (74° 55') found between the three cleavages of calcite. Due to these factors, there are several points of superficial resemblance between barytocalcite and calcite.

## Occurrence and Distribution
Barytocalcite is found only at Alston Moor in Cumberland. In this locality, it occurs as diverging groups of crystals that line cavities within the Mountain Limestone.

## Chemical Distinctions
Barytocalcite is chemically distinct from bromlite, another mineral found in the north of England. While barytocalcite is a double salt of barium and calcium carbonates in equal molecular proportions, bromlite consists of an isomorphous mixture of these same carbonates in various proportions. Furthermore, bromlite crystallizes in the orthorhombic system, whereas barytocalcite is monoclinic.
