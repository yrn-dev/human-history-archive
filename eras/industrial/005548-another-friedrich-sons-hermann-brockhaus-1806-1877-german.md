# HERMANN BROCKHAUS (1806-1877)

Hermann Brockhaus was a German Orientalist born in Amsterdam on January 28, 1806. He was a son of Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus, a German publisher who had established a large printing-house in Leipzig and published the celebrated *Konversations-Lexikon*. While his brothers, Friedrich and Heinrich, continued the family business, Hermann pursued an academic career.

## Academic Career
Brockhaus began his professional academic ascent in 1838 when he was appointed as an extraordinary professor in Jena. In 1841, he received a call to serve in a similar capacity at Leipzig. He remained at Leipzig, where he was eventually appointed as an ordinary professor of ancient Semitic in 1848. He continued his work in Leipzig until his death on January 5, 1877.

## Scholarship and Linguistic Expertise
Brockhaus operated as an Oriental scholar in the traditional sense, focusing his efforts on gaining familiarity with the literature and principal languages of the East rather than specializing in a single tongue. His linguistic studies included:
*   Hebrew
*   Arabic
*   Persian
*   Pali
*   Zend
*   Chinese

He was also capable of lecturing on Sanskrit, which eventually became his specialty.

## Major Works and Contributions
The most significant contribution of Brockhaus's career was the *editio princeps* of the *Katha-sarit-sagara* ("The Ocean of the Streams of Story"). This work is a large collection of Sanskrit stories compiled by Soma Deva during the 12th century. This publication provided the first impetus for a scientific study regarding the spreading and origin of popular tales. Through this work, other scholars, including Prof. Benfey, were able to trace a great bulk of Western and Eastern stories back to a Buddhistic and Indian source.

Other notable publications by Professor Brockhaus include:
*   A critical edition of the "Songs of Hafiz."
*   An edition of the philosophical play *Prabodhachandrodaya* ("The Rise of the Moon of Intelligence").
*   A publication of the text of the "Zend-Avesta" using Latin letters.
