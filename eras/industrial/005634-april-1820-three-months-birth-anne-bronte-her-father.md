# The Brontë Family

The Brontës were an English family of literary distinction, centered around the clergyman Patrick Brontë and his children. The family is most noted for the novelists Charlotte, Emily, and Anne, whose works and lives are closely associated with the village of Haworth in the West Riding of Yorkshire.

## Patrick Brontë and Early Family Life
Patrick Brontë was born on March 17, 1777, in Emsdale, Co. Down, Ireland. Born to parents of the peasant class with the original surname Brunty, he changed his name upon entering St John's College, Cambridge, in 1802. Before his clerical career, he worked as a schoolmaster and a weaver in Ireland. Following his time at Cambridge, he served as a curate in several locations: Wethersfield in Essex (1806), Wellington, Salop (1809), Dewsbury, Yorkshire (late 1809), and Hartshead-cum-Clifton.

In 1812, while at Hartshead, Patrick married Maria Branwell, a Cornishwoman. They had two daughters, Maria (1813–1825) and Elizabeth (1814–1825). The family later moved to Thornton, near Bradford, where four more children were born: Charlotte, Patrick Branwell (1817–1848), Emily Jane, and Anne. In April 1820, three months after Anne's birth, Patrick accepted the living of Haworth, where he served as the perpetual incumbent of the parish for the final forty-one years of his life.

Maria Brontë died in September 1821. She is remembered through letters to her lover, whom she called "dear saucy Pat," and a manuscript essay titled *The Advantages of Poverty in Religious Concerns*.

## The Literary Careers of the Sisters
Charlotte, Emily, and Anne Brontë began their literary efforts with a volume of verse published in 1846 by Aylott & Jones. To disguise their identities, they used the pseudonyms Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell. The volume contained nineteen poems by Charlotte, twenty-one by Emily, and twenty-one by Anne. While the venture was a commercial failure—selling only two copies at a cost of approximately £50—it marked the start of their public writing.

The sisters then turned to novels. Charlotte's first attempt, *The Master* (later retitled *The Professor*), was deemed too short for the required three-volume format by the firm Smith, Elder & Co. In response, she wrote and published *Jane Eyre* in October 1847, which received wide acclaim. Emily wrote *Wuthering Heights* and Anne wrote *Agnes Gray*. These two novels were published together in three volumes in December 1847 by T. C. Newby. Anne later published *The Tenant of Wildfell Hall* through Newby in June 1848.

Because Newby attempted to persuade the public that all these works were written by the author of *Jane Eyre*, Charlotte and Anne visited London in the summer of 1848 to interview publishers and establish their separate identities.

## Patrick Branwell Brontë
The only son, Patrick Branwell, was initially the idol of his sisters and aunt. He entered the Royal Academy as a pupil in 1835, though he returned home after a month of extravagance in London. His subsequent career was fragmented; he studied art in Leeds, worked as a tutor to the son of a Mr. Postlethwaite in Barrow-in-Furness, and served as a booking-clerk at Luddenden Foot and Sowerby Bridge station. He also worked as a tutor in the family of a clergyman named Robinson, where Anne served as a governess.

Branwell eventually became a "confirmed drunkard" and returned to Haworth, where he spent his final years in "painful sottishness." He died on September 24, 1848. Despite later rumors, Charlotte stated that Branwell never knew his sisters had published their work and had no part in their novels.

## Later Years and Deaths
The family suffered a series of rapid losses in 1848 and 1849. Following Branwell's death in September 1848, Emily died at Haworth on December 19, 1848. Anne fell ill and was taken by Charlotte to Scarborough for the sea air, but she died there on May 28, 1849. In the span of eight months, Charlotte lost all three of her youth companions.

During this period, Charlotte had been writing a novel titled *Shirley*, which she completed after Anne's death and published in October 1849. She later published *Villette* in January 1853. In June 1854, Charlotte married Arthur Bell Nicholls, her father's curate, in Haworth church. The couple made their home in Haworth with Patrick Brontë, who was becoming blind.
