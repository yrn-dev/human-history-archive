# A Letter to the Proprietors of East India Stock

## Overview
*A Letter to the Proprietors of East India Stock, on the subject of Lord Clive's Jaghire, occasioned by his Lordship's Letter on that Subject* is a publication from 1764, printed in octavo (8vo) format. The work is attributed to Lord Ashburton, who is supposed to have authored the piece.

## Context and Subject Matter
The pamphlet addresses the subject of Lord Clive's Jaghire. This specific topic was prompted by a letter written by Lord Clive himself on the same matter. Lord Ashburton is also associated with other writings from this period, including a pamphlet regarding the law of libel and a response to a Dutch memorial.

## Related Historical Figures
Lord Clive is a central figure in the context of this publication. His activities in India included sending a force from Calcutta to Colonel Forde, who captured Masulipatam from the French in 1759, thereby establishing complete English power in much of that district. Additionally, in 1765, Lord Clive granted a tract of land to the titular Mogul emperor, Shah Alam, which included the area of Allahabad and part of the tract known as Kora (an ancient town in Northern India's Fatehpur district).

Other figures associated with the administration of British interests in India include Sir George Hilaro Barlow, an Anglo-Indian statesman who served in the Bengal Civil Service starting in 1778 and later as governor of Madras. During his tenure in Madras, Barlow experienced a mutiny of officers in 1809, an event noted as being similar to a mutiny that had previously occurred under Clive.
