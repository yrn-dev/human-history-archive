# The Baring Family

The Barings are a family of English bankers and financiers. The family's presence in England began with John Baring, the son of a Lutheran minister from Bremen, Germany. After arriving in England, John Baring established a cloth manufactory at Larkbear, located near Exeter.

## Foundation of Baring Brothers
The banking firm known as Baring Brothers was founded in 1770 by Francis Baring (1740-1810) in conjunction with his brother, John. Francis Baring was born at Larkbear and had previously worked for a commercial firm in London. By the time of his death in 1810, Francis had developed the business to such an extent that he was considered the first merchant in Europe. 

In addition to his banking interests, Francis Baring served as a director of the East India Company, holding the position of chairman from 1792 to 1793. For these services, he received a baronetcy. He also had a political career, sitting almost continuously in parliament as a Whig from 1784 to 1806.

## The Second Generation
Francis Baring had five sons. The eldest, Sir Thomas Baring (1772-1848), became a collector and patron of art. The management of the family business passed to the second son, Alexander Baring (1774-1848), who is better known as Lord Ashburton.

Alexander Baring was highly successful in expanding the firm's operations within America. His influence among the American commercial community was strengthened by his marriage to the daughter of William Bingham, a United States senator and wealthy resident of Philadelphia.

## Political and Diplomatic Career of Lord Ashburton
Lord Ashburton was active in British politics, representing various constituencies in parliament from 1806 to 1835, during which time he was a strong opponent of reform. In 1834, during the first administration of Sir Robert Peel, he served as the master of the mint and president of the Board of Trade. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Ashburton in 1835.

In 1842, the British government appointed him as commissioner to the United States. This appointment was based on his intimate knowledge of American institutions and customs. His mission was to negotiate other disputes and the settlement of the north-eastern boundary question. This resulted in the conclusion of the Ashburton treaty in Washington in 1842, which fixed the frontier between Canada and Maine.

## Later Management
Following the death of Lord Ashburton in 1848, the affairs of the banking house were managed by Thomas Baring (1799-1873). Thomas Baring was the son of Sir Thomas Baring and served as the parliamentary representative for Huntingdon starting in 1844.
