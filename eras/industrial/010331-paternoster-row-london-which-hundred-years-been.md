# Paternoster Row, London

Paternoster Row is a location in London that served as the center of the book distribution industry for over a hundred years. It functioned as a critical hub where the various roles of the book trade—specifically the distinction between the publisher (the literary promoter) and the bookseller (the shopkeeper)—intersected.

## Role in the Book Trade
Within the structure of the 19th-century book industry, Paternoster Row was the primary site for wholesale bookselling. The wholesale bookseller acted as an essential distributing agency positioned between the publisher and the retail bookseller. Because it was impossible for retail booksellers to stock half of all published works, they relied on the wholesale agencies of Paternoster Row for miscellaneous supplies. Collectors from retail shops were frequently seen in the Row, busily obtaining books ordered by the reading public. Similarly, booksellers located in the country utilized these agencies to secure their supplies.

The scale of operations in Paternoster Row was significant; at the leading houses in this department, almost any book could be found or information regarding a specific title could be obtained. One such establishment maintained a constant stock of over 1,000,000 books.

## The Subscription Process
Paternoster Row served as the first point of contact for publishers wishing to "show" or "subscribe" a new book. This subscription process was considered critical, as the fate of a book was sometimes determined by the number of copies subscribed to through these agencies.

## Notable Publications and Printers
The Row was home to various printing and bookselling establishments that handled a wide array of literary works:

*   **Aylott & Jones:** In 1846, the shop of Aylott & Jones published a volume of verse titled *Poems*, by Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell. These names were pseudonyms used to disguise the identities of Anne, Emily, and Charlotte Brontë. The volume contained twenty-one poems by Emily, twenty-one by Anne, and nineteen by Charlotte. The venture cost the sisters approximately £50, though only two copies were sold.
*   **A. Bettesworth and C. Hitch:** The English translation of *The Method of Teaching and Studying the Belles Lettres*, a piece of criticism published in four volumes, was first printed for this firm in Paternoster Row.
