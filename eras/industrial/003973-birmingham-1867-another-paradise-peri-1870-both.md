# Birmingham and Musical Compositions of John Francis Barnett

## Political and Administrative Developments in 1867
In 1867, the city of Birmingham experienced a change in its parliamentary representation. Having been enfranchised in 1832 with two representatives, the city was assigned three members in 1867. This preceded a further increase to seven members in 1885, which coincided with the creation of seven parliamentary divisions: Central, North, South, East, West, Edgbaston, and Bordesley.

## Notable Figures and Events in Birmingham (1867)
The year 1867 marked the passing of Charles Rann Kennedy, who died in Birmingham on December 17. Kennedy had been a barrister and served as a professor of law at Queen's College, Birmingham, from 1849 to 1856. He was recognized for his scholarship, specifically his blank verse translation of the works of Virgil (1861) and his translation of the orations of Demosthenes (1852-1863).

In the realm of music, 1867 saw the successful introduction of the cantata *The Ancient Mariner* in Birmingham. The work was composed by John Francis Barnett, a pianist and composer who had previously studied in Germany in 1857 and performed at a Gewandhaus concert in Leipzig in 1860.

## Musical Works of John Francis Barnett
John Francis Barnett, the son of Joseph Alfred and nephew of John Barnett, established himself as a composer and teacher. Following his early success with a symphony in A minor in 1864, he produced several compositions for strings, pianoforte, and orchestra. 

In addition to *The Ancient Mariner* (1867), Barnett composed another cantata titled *Paradise and the Peri*, which was brought out in 1870. Both works were met with great success. Barnett continued his compositional output with the oratorio *The Raising of Lazarus*, written in 1873 and produced at Hereford in 1876. Throughout his career, he also composed various pianoforte pieces and other cantatas, while serving as a professor at the Royal College of Music and the Guildhall School of Music.

## City Infrastructure and Charities
Birmingham maintained a variety of medical and social charities. The general hospital, founded by Dr. Ash, opened in 1779, while the Queen's hospital was founded in 1840 by W. Sands Cox. Other facilities included a free children's hospital established in 1864 by Dr. Heslop, a free women's hospital, and a dispensary for out-patients located in Paradise Street, maintained by the parish of Birmingham. 

To support these institutions, the city established public fundraising organizations, including "Hospital Sunday" in 1859 and the Saturday Hospital collection in 1873. Additionally, a musical festival was held every three years to benefit the general hospital. Non-medical charities in the area included the deaf and dumb asylum and the blind institution at Edgbaston, as well as Sir Josiah Mason's orphanage at Erdington.
