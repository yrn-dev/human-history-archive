# Bordentown

Bordentown is a city located in Burlington county, New Jersey, U.S.A. It is situated on the east bank of the Delaware river, 6 miles south of Trenton and 28 miles northeast of Philadelphia. The city is positioned on a broad, level plain 65 feet above the river and is characterized by wide, beautifully shaded streets.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
Bordentown is served by several modes of transport. Its rail connections include the Pennsylvania railway and the Camden & Trenton railway; the latter is an electric line that forms part of the route between New York and Philadelphia. Additionally, the city is served by passenger and freight steamboat lines operating on the Delaware. Historically, the Camden & Amboy railway was begun in 1831 and completed from Bordentown to South Amboy (a distance of 34 miles) in 1832, marking it as one of the first railways in the United States.

## Population and Governance
The population of Bordentown has fluctuated slightly over the turn of the century:
* 1890: 4,232
* 1900: 4,110
* 1905: 4,073
* 1910: 4,250

The city was laid out by Joseph Borden, for whom it was named. It was first incorporated as a borough in 1825, re-incorporated in 1849, and eventually chartered as a city in 1867.

## Education and Institutions
The city serves as the seat for several notable institutions:
* The Bordentown Military Institute, which includes the Woodward memorial library.
* The state manual training and industrial school for coloured youth.
* St Joseph's academy for girls.
* St Joseph's convent and the mother-house of the Sisters of Mercy.

## Economy and Industry
Bordentown possesses a diverse industrial base. Its manufacturing and production facilities include:
* Iron foundries, forges, and machine shops.
* Ship-yards.
* Shirt factories.
* A woollen mill.
* Canning factories.
* A pottery dedicated to the manufacture of sanitary earthenware.

## History and Notable Residents
The site was first settled in the 18th century by several Quaker families. The city was home for some years to Francis Hopkinson and his son Joseph Hopkinson, whose residences remain standing. 

Between 1817 and 1832, and again from 1837 to 1839, Bordentown was the residence of Joseph Bonaparte, the ex-king of Spain. He lived on a magnificent estate known as "Bonaparte's Park," where he hosted distinguished guests, including Lafayette. Because Bonaparte was an alien, the legislature of New Jersey passed a special law to enable him to own real property; it is said that the state's nickname "Spain" was received in reference to this law. Other notable residents include Prince Napoleon Lucien Charles Murat, the second son of Joachim Murat, who lived there for many years, and Rear-Admiral Charles Stewart, whose home was the estate known as "Ironsides."
