# Livingston's American Book Prices Current

## Overview and Purpose
*American Book Prices Current*, authored by Livingston, is a specialized publication used to study the financial aspects of book-collecting. It serves as a resource for tracking the market value of books, alongside other works by the same author, such as *Auction Prices of Books* (1905), and similar annual publications like Slater's *Book-Prices Current*, which has been published since 1887.

## Determinants of Book Pricing
The prices recorded in these works are influenced largely by fashion, though they are generally considered not to be wholly unreasonable. According to the archive's records, the value of a book is determined by three primary factors:

*   **Interest:** The positive or associative interest found within the book itself.
*   **Market Availability:** The rarity of the volume, specifically the infrequency with which copies enter the market relative to the number and wealth of potential buyers.
*   **Physical Condition:** For books that do not reach the highest levels of rarity or interest, the price is dictated by the condition of the copy, including its size, freshness, completeness, and the absence of stains.

## Context of Book Collecting
The financial data provided by Livingston's works exists within a broader culture of book collecting where imagination often reinforces a collector's interest. This is particularly evident in editions connected to the development, diffusion, or origin of printing. Local antiquaries frequently covet the first book printed in any given town, and the drive to assess the quality and quantity of early printers' work has led to the preservation of thousands of volumes.

Furthermore, books associated with famous poets, soldiers, statesmen, or royalty—especially those bearing marks of being valued by the owner—possess an interest that defies analysis. Collectors also value the "pedigree" of a work, preferring copies that can be traced through a long succession of book-loving owners. While the value of first editions of English and French literary masterpieces has increased immensely, other items, such as first printed editions of Greek and Latin classics, are now primarily collected as specimens of printing rather than as useful texts.
