# Arboreous Forms Resistant to Tropical Heat and Drought

In the study of botanical distributions, certain arboreous forms exhibit a specialized ability to survive in environments that lack the humid and equable heat characteristic of equatorial and truly tropical climates. These specific plants are distinguished by their capacity to resist excessive drought and high temperatures, particularly those encountered during the northern Indian hot months, which occur from June to April.

## Drought-Resistant Flora of the Indian Plain
While much of the Malayan peninsula and the western archipelago are covered in dense forests—characterized by the presence of the thorny cane (a palm of the genus *Calamus*) and trees from orders such as Dipterocarpaceae, Meliaceae, Sapindaceae, and Terebinthaceae—other regions require flora capable of enduring harsher conditions. 

The arboreous forms that least require humid, equable heat and are best equipped to withstand the intense heat and drought of northern India include:
*   **Leguminosae:** Specifically *Acacia*, *Bauhinia*, *Butea*, and *Dalbergia*.
*   **Other Genera:** *Bombax*, *Skorea*, *Nauclea*, *Lagerstroemia*, and *Bignonia*.
*   **Other Forms:** A few varieties of palms and bamboos.

These species extend far beyond the tropic, providing a tropical appearance to the forests located at the extreme northern border of the Indian plain.

## Regional Comparisons of Forest Flora
The flora of the humid regions differs significantly from those adapted to drought. In the perennially humid areas of the Malayan peninsula, the forest is composed of a wide array of species, including figs, oaks, laurels, nutmegs, Sapotaceae, and Dilleniaceae, as well as *Dracaena* and *Pandanus*. 

A similar flora of humid forests is found along the mountains of eastern India extending to the Himalaya. In these regions, the forest ascends to elevations of 3,000 to 4,000 feet in the west and 6,000 to 7,000 feet in the east. This contrasts with the drought-resistant forms mentioned above, which are specifically adapted to the volatile temperatures and lack of humidity found in the northern plains.

## Environmental Influences on Stability
The survival and stability of organic and inorganic materials are heavily influenced by heat and humidity. In construction, these two factors acting together contribute to the decay or disintegration of materials. Similarly, in the natural world, the ability of a species to resist variable temperatures determines its range. For example, the flora and fauna of the Mesozoic age were largely adapted to shallow, swampy regions and equable conditions. In contrast, the development of specialized coverings, such as hair in ancestral mammals, provided a power of resistance to variable temperatures that allowed for a greater range of existence than that possessed by reptiles.
