# Journals and Periodicals in the Archive

The Human History Archive contains records of various journals, magazines, and newspapers across different centuries and disciplines, ranging from scientific and mathematical publications to political organs and literary papers.

## Mathematical and Scientific Journals
Several figures in the sciences utilized specialized journals to disseminate their research. Boole, known for his work on mathematical topics, published his earliest paper, "Theory of Analytical Transformations," in the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* in 1839. This publication established a friendship with the journal's editor, D.F. Gregory, which continued until Gregory's death in 1844. Boole contributed a total of twenty-two articles to the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* and its successor, the *Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal*. His other contributions include sixteen papers in the third and fourth series of the *Philosophical Magazine*, six important memoirs in the *Philosophical Transactions* of the Royal Society, and papers in *Crelle's Journal* and the *Transactions* of both the Royal Irish Academy and the Royal Society of Edinburgh. In 1862, a piece was published in the *Bulletin de l'Academie de St-Petersbourg* under the name G. Boldt. Additionally, Boole published a paper on the mathematical basis of logic in the *Mechanic's Magazine* in 1848.

In the field of geology, John Jeremiah Bigsby contributed papers to the *American Journal of Science* and other scientific journals during his time as a medical officer and secretary to the Boundary Commission. In the realm of ichthyology, F. Steindachner described discoveries regarding the fresh-water fish fauna of Portugal and Spain in several papers in the *Sitzungsberichte der Akademie zu Wien*, while B. du Bocage and F. de B. Capello contributed knowledge on marine fishes of the Portuguese coast to the *Jorn. Scienc. Acad. Lisb.*

## Philosophical and Literary Publications
The philosophical journal *Mind* was instituted by Bain during his twenty-year professoriate at Aberdeen, with the first issue appearing in January 1876. Edited initially by G. Croom Robertson, the journal received many important articles and discussions from Bain, who personally bore all its expenses until Robertson resigned in 1891. Bain's final contribution to *Mind* appeared in 1894.

In the literary sphere, Joseph Bajza contributed to the literary paper *Aurora*, serving as its editor from 1830 to 1837. He also wrote for the *Athenaeum*, the *Figyelmezo* (or *Observer*), and the *Kritische Blaetter*. De Quincey's career involved various periodicals; he edited the *Westmoreland Gazette* for approximately a year and a half and contributed to the *London Magazine*, where his *Confessions* appeared in 1821. He also wrote for *Tait's Magazine*, *Knight's Quarterly Magazine*, and *Blackwood*. While living in Edinburgh for twelve years starting in 1828, he contributed periodically to the *Edinburgh Literary Gazette*.

## Political and Abolitionist Papers
Journals have frequently served as vehicles for political activism and opposition. In the United States, Gamaliel Bailey edited a religious journal called the *Methodist Protestant* in Baltimore before moving to Cincinnati. In 1836, he joined James G. Birney in the editorial control of the *Philanthropist*, succeeding him as editor in 1837. Bailey managed the paper until 1847 despite violence from mobs that wrecked the printing office three times. From 1843, he also edited a daily paper titled the *Herald*. In 1847, Bailey took control of the *National Era* in Washington, D.C., an abolitional organ that first published Mrs. H. B. Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* between 1851 and 1852.

In Hungary, Joseph Bajza edited the opposition journal *Ellenoer* at Leipzig in 1847, and in March 1848, he was appointed by Kossuth as the editor of *Kossuth Hirlapja*.

In France, Babeuf published the first number of his *Journal de la liberte de la presse* on September 3, 1794, later changing the title to *Le Tribun du peuple* on October 5. Through this publication, Babeuf attacked the economic results of the Revolution and the fallen terrorists. He was also influenced by the *Journal de l'egalite* and the *Ami du peuple*, papers edited by Lebois that maintained the traditions of Marat. Following Babeuf's arrest in February 1795, the *Tribun du peuple* was publicly burnt in the Theatre des Bergeres by the *jeunesse doree*.
