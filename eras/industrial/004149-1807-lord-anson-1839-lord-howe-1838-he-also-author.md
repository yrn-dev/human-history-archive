# George Anson and Related Historical Figures

## Commodore George Anson
George Anson, later known as Lord Anson, was a British naval commander involved in operations against Spain following the declaration of war on October 23, 1739. While other forces under Admiral Edward Vernon targeted the West Indies, Anson was commanded to round Cape Horn to attack the Pacific coast. 

Anson's expedition departed England on September 18, 1740, with six ships. The voyage was characterized by poor preparations, insufficient provisions, and a force smaller than originally intended. By the time Anson returned to England on June 15, 1744, he was alone with his flagship, the "Centurion"; the other vessels had either been lost or failed to round the Horn. Despite these losses, Anson's cruise is regarded as a great feat of endurance and resolution. During his voyage, he harried the coasts of Peru and Chile and captured a Spanish galleon of immense value near the Philippines.

## Exploration of the Marianas
Commodore Anson contributed to the research of the archipelago known as the Ladrones (or "Islands of the Thieves"), which had been discovered by Magellan in 1521. In August 1742, Anson landed on the island of Tinian. These islands, which Magellan called the "Islands of the Lateen Sails," were later renamed Las Marianas in 1668 in honor of Maria Anna of Austria. The archipelago, excluding Guam, was eventually sold by Spain to Germany in 1899 for £837,500.

## The Hon. John Byron
The Hon. John Byron (1723–1786) was a British vice-admiral and the second son of the 4th Lord Byron. As a young man, Byron accompanied Anson on his voyage of discovery around the world. Due to the frequent adverse gales and dangerous storms he encountered during his many years of service, sailors gave him the nickname "Foul-weather Jack."

Byron's naval career included a 1760 expedition to Louisburg, where he commanded a squadron tasked with destroying fortifications, and a prolonged cruise in the South Seas in 1764 aboard the "Dolphin." In 1768, he published a narrative detailing some of his early adventures with Anson. Byron was appointed governor of Newfoundland in 1769, attained flag rank in 1775, and became a vice-admiral in 1778. In July 1779, he fought an indecisive engagement off Grenada against the Count d'Estaing before retiring to private life. He died on April 10, 1786.
