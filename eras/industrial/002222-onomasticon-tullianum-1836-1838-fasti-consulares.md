# Onomasticon Tullianum (1836-1838)

The *Onomasticon Tullianum* is a scholarly work published between 1836 and 1838. It was produced through the collaboration of the scholars Orelli and Johann Georg Baiter.

## Authorship and Production
The work was a joint effort between Orelli and Johann Georg Baiter, a Swiss philologist and textual critic born in Zurich in 1801. Baiter, who served as an *Oberlehrer* at the gymnasium in Zurich from 1833 to 1876, was highly regarded for his accuracy in collations and his ability to locate the best manuscript authorities. He frequently collaborated with other scholars, and Orelli considered him to be his "right-hand man." 

While the *Onomasticon Tullianum* was a collaborative project, Baiter held sole responsibility for the *Fasti Consulares* and *Fasti Triumphales* within the scope of their work. The *Onomasticon Tullianum* was included in the final volume of their edition of Cicero.

## Contents and Contributions
The *Onomasticon Tullianum* serves as a reference for various historical and legal figures and documents. It includes a dissertation by Wunder, located on pages 200-210 of the final volume under the section *ad Ind. Legum*, which discusses the *Lex Licinia de Sodalitiis*.

## Use as a Historical Source

*   **Lucius Lucceius**: The work is referenced in relation to this Roman historian and orator, who was a friend and correspondent of Cicero. Lucceius was a man of literary tastes and wealth who retired from public life after failing to become consul in 60.
*   **Marcus Licinius Lucullus**: The text is cited as a source for the life of Lucullus, who served as consul in 73 with Gaius Cassius Varus and was a representative of the optimates. Lucullus is noted for his military campaigns in Macedonia against the Bessi and Dardani, as well as his support for Cicero during the Catilinarian conspiracy.
