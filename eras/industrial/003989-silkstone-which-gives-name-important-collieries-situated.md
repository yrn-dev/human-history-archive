# Silkstone

## Overview and Location
Silkstone is a parish located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is the parish in which the market town and municipal borough of Barnsley is situated. Barnsley is positioned 15 miles north of Sheffield on rising ground to the west of the river Dearne.

## Industrial Significance
The parish of Silkstone provides the name for important collieries. The surrounding neighborhood of Barnsley is characterized by large coalfields that extend directly under the town. The coal and coke produced in these fields are exported in large quantities to Hull and London.

## Barnsley
As a municipal borough within the parish of Silkstone, Barnsley serves as a center for various industries and institutions.

### Demographics and Governance
The population of Barnsley was 35,427 in 1891 and grew to 41,086 by 1901. The borough covers an area of 2,385 acres and is governed by a mayor, eighteen councillors, and six aldermen.

### Economy and Industry
While the area is known for its coalfields, Barnsley is also a center for the weaving of other cloth and linen, as well as the manufacture of steel and iron. Other industrial activities in the area include:
* The manufacture of wire, needles, and glass.
* The operation of dyeworks, printfields, bleachfields, malt-houses, cornmills, and sawmills.

### Infrastructure and Transport
The town is served by several railway lines, including the Great Northern, Great Central, Hull & Barnsley, Lancashire & Yorkshire, and Midland railways. Additionally, Barnsley is connected to the Aire and Calder Navigation system of canals.

### Institutions and Landmarks
Barnsley contains several notable buildings and educational facilities:
* **Religious Sites:** The town has several churches, the oldest being the parish church of St Mary, which was constructed in 1821 on an early site.
* **Education and Culture:** A free grammar school dating from 1665 is located here, and a philosophical society was established in 1828. The town also features a free library, an institute, a public hall, and a court house.
* **Public Spaces:** A park was presented in 1862 by the widow of Joseph Locke, M.P. In 1905, a monument was erected to honor prominent members of the Yorkshire Miners' Association.

## Regional Landmarks
In the vicinity of the town and parish are two significant historical sites:
* **Monk Bretton Priory:** A Cluniac foundation established in 1157. It retains fragments of its church, Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse.
* **Wentworth Castle:** Built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford, this site is set within a beautiful park and houses a fine collection of portraits of historical interest.
