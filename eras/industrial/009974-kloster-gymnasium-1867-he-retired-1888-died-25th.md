# Graues Kloster Gymnasium

The Graues Kloster (also referred to as the "Greyfriars Gymnasium" or *Gymnasium zum Grauen Kloster*) is an educational institution located in Berlin. It was established by Frederick the Great.

## Administration and Leadership
The gymnasium has been led by several notable figures over its history. In the 18th century, the institution was superintended by Anton Friedrich Büsching, a German geographer and theologian. Büsching, who had previously organized a flourishing school in St Petersburg, was called to lead the Greyfriars Gymnasium after returning to central Europe in 1766. Through his example and writing, Büsching is credited with providing a new impulse to education throughout Prussia.

In the 19th century, Hermann Bonitz served the institution in multiple capacities. Bonitz, a German scholar born in 1814, first became an Oberlehrer at the Graues Kloster in 1840. Later, in 1867, he was appointed as the director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium. Bonitz remained in his professional life until his retirement in 1888.

## Academic Influence and Educational Reform
The leadership at the Graues Kloster often intersected with broader educational reforms in Prussia. Director Hermann Bonitz, for instance, took a significant interest in higher education. He was the individual chiefly responsible for the system of examination and teaching utilized in Prussian high schools following 1882. 

Beyond his administrative duties, Bonitz was a recognized scholar, particularly noted outside of Germany as a commentator on Aristotle and Plato. His scholarly contributions included works such as *Platonische Studien* and *Aristotelische Studien* (1862-1867).
