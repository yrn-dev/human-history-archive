# Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine

The *Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine* is a treatise concerning the gospel of St John, published in 1820. The work attracted significant attention upon its release due to its comprehensive collection and moderated discussion of arguments against the authorship of the gospel by John.

## Authorship and Controversy
The publication of the *Probabilia* sparked numerous replies from other scholars. However, in 1822, the author, Bretschneider, surprised observers by announcing in the preface to the second edition of his *Dogmatik* that he had never actually doubted the authenticity of the gospel. He claimed that the *Probabilia* had been published specifically to draw attention to the subject and to encourage a more thorough defense of the gospel's genuineness.

Despite this later clarification, the work had professional repercussions. In his autobiography, Bretschneider noted that the publication prevented him from being appointed as the successor to Karl C. Tittmann in Dresden. This was due to the actions of the minister Detlev von Einsiedel (1773-1861), who denounced Bretschneider as the "slanderer of John" (*Johannisschänder*).

## Related Works and Academic Contributions
Bretschneider's contributions to the science of exegesis extended beyond the *Probabilia*. His *Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum in libros Novi Testamenti* (published in 1824, with a third edition in 1840) is considered his greatest contribution to the field. This lexicon was noted for its use of the apostolic fathers, Josephus, the Old and New Testament Apocrypha, and the Greek of the Septuagint to illustrate New Testament language.

In 1826, he published *Apologie der neuern Theologie des evangelischen Deutschlands*. This work served as a response to Hugh James Rose, who had published a volume of sermons in England in 1825 titled *The State of the Protestant Religion in Germany*. Rose had categorized Bretschneider as a rationalist; in response, Bretschneider argued that he was not a rationalist in the common sense, but rather a "rational supernaturalist."

## Theological Position
Bretschneider's dogmatic views were characterized as intermediate. He stood between the extreme school of naturalists—which included Julius Wegscheider, J.F. Röhr, and Heinrich Paulus—and the positions held by F.C. Baur and D.F. Strauss. While he recognized a supernatural element within the Bible, he also allowed for other considerations. His *Manual of the Religion and History of the Christian Church* was translated into English in 1857.
