# Bank Holidays in the United Kingdom

## Definition and Legal Framework
Bank holidays in the United Kingdom are specific days designated as close holidays for all banks in Scotland, Ireland, and England. These holidays were formally established by the Bank Holidays Act 1871. Under this act, the sovereign is granted the authority to appoint additional bank holidays by proclamation, similar to the process used for public fasts or days of public thanksgiving. The sovereign may also substitute a different day for a usual bank holiday if it is deemed inexpedient to keep the original date.

## Historical Evolution
Prior to 1834, the Bank of England observed approximately thirty-three days of closure annually, which included various anniversaries and saints' days. In 1834, these closures were significantly reduced to only four days: Christmas Day, the 1st of November, the 1st of May, and Good Friday.

The Bank Holidays Act 1871, which was brought through the House of Commons by Sir J. Lubbock (later known as Lord Avebury), expanded the list of constituted bank holidays for Ireland and England. These included:
* Easter Monday
* The Monday in Whitsun week
* The first Monday of August
* The 26th of December (provided it falls on a week-day)

Additionally, the Bank Holiday (Ireland) Act 1903 designated March 17th as a special bank holiday specifically for Ireland.

## Regional Variations and Regulations
The schedule of bank holidays differs in Scotland, where the observed days are:
* New Year's Day
* Christmas Day
* Good Friday
* The first Monday of May
* The first Monday of August

In instances where New Year's Day or Christmas Day falls on a Sunday, the following Monday is observed as the bank holiday.

Regarding legal and financial obligations, the law stipulates that no individual is required to perform any act or make any payment on a bank holiday that they would not otherwise be compelled to do on Good Friday or Christmas Day. Consequently, performing such an act or making a payment on the day following a bank holiday is considered equivalent to having done so on the holiday itself.
