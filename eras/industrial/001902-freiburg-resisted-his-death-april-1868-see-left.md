# The Archbishopric of Freiburg and Religious Conflict in Baden

The history of the archbishopric of Freiburg is closely linked to the internal political and religious struggles of Baden during the 19th century. The region experienced a prolonged conflict between Liberalism and Clericalism, which manifested in legal disputes over education, the role of the clergy, and the relationship between the state and the Holy See.

## The Concordat and Constitutional Struggle
On June 28, 1859, a concordat was signed with the Holy See. This agreement facilitated the establishment of religious orders and placed education under the oversight of the clergy. However, this arrangement led to a constitutional struggle that concluded in 1863 with a victory for Liberal principles. Following this shift, the communes were made responsible for education, although priests were still permitted a share in its management.

## Liberal Reforms and the Priesthood
The tension between Liberal and Clerical forces persisted beyond 1863. In 1867, Julius von Jolly ascended to the premiership (1823–1891) and implemented several constitutional changes in a Liberal direction. These reforms included the introduction of compulsory education, freedom of the press, and the establishment of the responsibility of ministers.

On September 6, 1867, the government passed a law requiring all candidates for the priesthood to pass government examinations. This mandate was resisted by the archbishop of Freiburg. Following the archbishop's death in April 1868, the see was left vacant.

## The Kulturkampf and Reconciliation
The strife in Baden continued to escalate with the introduction of civil marriage in 1869. The conflict reached its climax in 1870 following the proclamation of the dogma of papal infallibility. This period, known as the "Kulturkampf," raged in Baden as it did throughout the rest of Germany. During this time, the government encouraged the formation of Old Catholic communities.

Reconciliation with Rome was not achieved until 1880, following the fall of the ministry of Julius von Jolly. The archbishopric of Freiburg was eventually filled again in 1882.

## Political Shifts in the Baden Diet
The political landscape of Baden mirrored broader trends across Germany. The National Liberals held a majority in the diet in 1891, though it was by only one seat. By 1893, they were only able to maintain their position with the assistance of the Conservatives. In 1897, the opposition won a majority in the chamber through a coalition consisting of Socialists, Social-democrats, Radicals (_Freisinnige_), and Ultramontanes.
