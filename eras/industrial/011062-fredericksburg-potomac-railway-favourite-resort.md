# Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac Railway

## Overview and Service
The Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac railway is a rail line that serves the village of Ashland in Hanover county, Virginia, U.S.A. Ashland is located 17 miles north-west of Richmond.

## Ashland as a Resort
Due to its connection via the Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac railway, the village of Ashland is considered a favourite resort for those traveling from Richmond.

## Local Geography and History
Ashland was settled in 1845 and received its incorporation in 1856. The village is situated near several historical sites; the birthplace of Patrick Henry is approximately 15 miles distant, and the birthplace of Henry Clay is located about 7 miles outside of the village. According to population records, Ashland had 1,147 residents in 1900 and 1,324 residents by 1910.

## Educational Institutions
Ashland is the site of Randolph-Macon College, a Methodist Episcopal, South institution and one of the oldest Methodist Episcopal colleges in the United States. The college was chartered in 1830 and originally opened in 1832 near Boydton in Mecklenburg county, Virginia, before being moved to Ashland in 1868. 

During the 1907-1908 period, the college had a faculty of 16 and a student body of 150. It publishes an endowed historical series known as *The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College*. The college is part of the "Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies," which is governed by a single board of trustees to correlate the work of preparatory schools with the colleges. Other institutions in this system include:
* **Randolph-Macon Academy (Bedford City, Virginia):** Established in 1890 for boys.
* **Randolph-Macon Academy (Front Royal, Virginia):** Established in 1892 for boys.
* **Randolph-Macon Woman's College (Lynchburg, Virginia):** Established in 1893, with an enrolment of 390 in 1907-1908.
* **Randolph-Macon Institute (Danville, Virginia):** A school for girls admitted into the system in 1897.
