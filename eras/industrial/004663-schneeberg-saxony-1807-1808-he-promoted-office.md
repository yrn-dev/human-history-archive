# Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider

## Early Life and Education
Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider (1776–1848) was a German theologian and scholar born in Gersdorf, Saxony. His academic pursuits began in 1794 when he entered the university of Leipzig, where he spent four years studying theology. Following a period of hesitation regarding his professional path, he decided to seek ordination. In 1802, he completed the examination for *candidatus theologiae* with great distinction. This achievement brought him to the attention of F.V. Reinhard, the court-preacher at Dresden and author of the *System der christlichen Moral* (1788–1815), who became a patron and close friend to Bretschneider for the rest of his life.

## Academic Career and Early Works
Between 1804 and 1806, Bretschneider served as a *Privat-docent* at the university of Wittenberg, delivering lectures on theology and philosophy. During this tenure, he produced a work concerning the development of dogma titled *Systematische Entwickelung aller in der Dogmatik vorkommenden Begriffe nach den symbolischen Schriften der evangelisch-lutherischen und reformirten Kirche*, first published in 1805 (with a 4th edition appearing in 1841). His early literary contributions also included an edition of Ecclesiasticus featuring a commentary in Latin.

## Ecclesiastical Appointments in Saxony
The course of Bretschneider's career shifted due to the advance of Napoleon's French army into Prussia. This geopolitical instability led him to abandon his university career and leave Wittenberg. With the assistance of Reinhard, Bretschneider was appointed as the pastor of Schneeberg in Saxony in 1807. 

His advancement within the church continued in 1808, when he was promoted to the office of superintendent of the church of Annaberg. In this role, Bretschneider was tasked with resolving various matters of ecclesiastical law, acting in accordance with the canon law of Saxony. However, his time in Annaberg was marked by difficulty; the local climate did not agree with him, and the demands of his official duties interfered with his ability to conduct theological studies.

## Later Career and Literary Activity
Seeking a change in circumstances, Bretschneider returned to Wittenberg in August 1812 to earn a degree of doctor of theology. In 1816, he received an appointment as general superintendent at Gotha. He remained in this position until his death in 1848. This final period of his life served as the primary era of his literary activity.
