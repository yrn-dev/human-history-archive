# Lady Anne Barnard

Lady Anne Barnard (1750–1825) was an author and the eldest daughter of James Lindsay, the 5th earl of Balcarres. She was born on December 12, 1750, at Balcarres House in Fife.

## Marriage and Colonial Service
In 1793, Lady Anne married Andrew Barnard, who was the son of the bishop of Limerick. Following their marriage, she secured an appointment for her husband as the colonial secretary at the Cape of Good Hope through Henry Dundas, the 1st Viscount Melville. 

The Barnards traveled to the Cape in March 1797. Lady Anne remained there until January 1802. In 1806, after the British reconquered the Cape, Andrew Barnard was reappointed as colonial secretary. Lady Anne did not accompany him for this second term. Andrew Barnard died in 1807.

## Literary Works and Correspondence
Lady Anne is known as the author of the ballad "Auld Robin Gray," which she wrote in 1772. The music for the ballad was composed by the Rev. William Leeves (1748–1828), a fact he admitted in 1812. Although the work was published anonymously in 1783, Lady Anne did not acknowledge her authorship of the lyrics until two years before her death, when she wrote a letter to Sir Walter Scott. Scott subsequently edited the piece for the Bannatyne Club, adding two continuations.

Additionally, Lady Anne wrote a remarkable series of letters during her time at the Cape of Good Hope to Henry Dundas, who served as the secretary for war and the colonies. These letters were published in 1901 under the title *South Africa a Century Ago*.

## Later Life
Lady Anne spent the remainder of her life residing in London. She died on May 6, 1825.
