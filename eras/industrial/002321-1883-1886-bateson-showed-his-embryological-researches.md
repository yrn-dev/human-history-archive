# Bateson's Embryological Researches on Enteropneusta

## Overview of Enteropneusta
The Enteropneusta is a special class of Vermes. One member of this group, *Balanoglossus*, was first discovered in 1825 by J. F. Eschscholtz at the Marshall Islands. Upon its discovery, *Balanoglossus* was described as a worm-like animal and categorized as belonging to the Echinoderm order of Holothurians, also known as sea-cucumbers. 

In 1865, Kowalevsky discovered that the animal's respiratory organs consist of numerous pairs of gill-slits. These slits lead from the digestive canal through the thickness of the body-wall to the exterior. Following this discovery, the animal was placed by Gegenbaur into the class Enteropneusta.

## Research by W. Bateson (1883-1886)
Between 1883 and 1886, W. Bateson conducted embryological researches that redefined the scientific understanding of the Enteropneusta. Bateson demonstrated that the Enteropneusta exhibit chordate (vertebrate) affinities. These affinities are evident in several key areas:
* The skeletal system
* The nervous system
* The coelomic system
* The respiratory system

Specifically, Bateson found that the gill-slits of the Enteropneusta are formed according to a plan similar to that of the gill-slits of *Amphioxus*. He noted that these slits are subdivided by tongue-bars which depend from the dorsal borders of the slits. 

Bateson's findings were detailed in his "Memoirs on the Direct Development of Balanoglossus," published in the *Quart. Journ. Micr. Sci.* across volumes xxiv. through xxvi. between 1884 and 1886.

## Related Biological Studies
The study of the Enteropneusta and related organisms continued after Bateson's initial research. T. H. Morgan published "Memoirs on the Indirect Development of Balanoglossus" in the *Journ. Morph.* in 1891 and 1894. Other contributors to the study of this group include W. B. Benham, who described *Balanoglossus otagoensis* in 1899, and W. E. Ritter, who identified *Harrimania maculosa* as a new genus and species of Enteropneusta from Alaska in 1900. Additional research was conducted by J. W. Spengel (1893), A. Willey (1899), J. P. Hill (1897-1898), and M. Caullery and F. Mesnil (1900).

Beyond the Enteropneusta, W. Bateson also contributed to the study of variation within the field of entomology.
