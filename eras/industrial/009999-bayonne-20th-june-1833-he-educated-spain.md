# Bayonne

Bayonne is a principal port and one of the principal towns located in the Basses-Pyrenees department of south-western France. Situated at the angle of the Bay of Biscay, the town is positioned near the point where the Adour river enters the sea over a shifting bar.

## Geography and Administration
Bayonne serves as the center of one of the five arrondissements of the Basses-Pyrenees department, alongside Pau, Oloron, Orthez, and Mauleon. The town is also the seat of the diocese of Bayonne. Geographically, the French Pays Basque is partially contained within the arrondissements of Bayonne and Mauleon. Historically, the limits of the diocese of Bayonne may have corresponded more closely to the boundaries of ancient tribes—such as the Vascones, Varduli, Caristi, and Autrigones—than current civil provincial divisions.

## Economy and Infrastructure
As a major port, Bayonne's exports consist primarily of resinous products, salt, wine, minerals, mine-props, and timber. Its leading imports include wool, grain, phosphates, minerals, and coal. The surrounding region supports a considerable fishing population, shared with St Jean-de-Luz. 

Industrial activity in the vicinity includes the *Forges de l'Adour* (large metallurgical works) and chemical works located at Le Boucau, three miles from the town. The broader department produces a variety of manufactures, such as sashes and woollen caps, paper, chocolate, and cord slippers, and maintains flour-mills, saw-mills, and tanneries.

Transportation is facilitated by the Southern Company's railway lines, most notably the line running from Bayonne to Toulouse via Pau and Orthez. Additionally, the Nive, Bidouze, and Adour rivers are navigable on their lower courses.

## Notable Figures and Events
Bayonne is the birthplace of Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat, a French painter born there on June 20, 1833. Bonnat was educated in Madrid under Madrazo; his work, particularly his vivid portrait-painting, reflects the influence of Spanish realists and Velasquez. He later became a leading artist in Paris, winning a medal of honour in 1869, becoming a professor of painting at the Ecole des Beaux Arts in 1888, and succeeding Paul Dubois as director in May 1905. His famous subject pictures include the "Martyrdom of St Denis" located in the Pantheon.

The town also appears in the political history of the Napoleonic era. On June 23, 1808, Napoleon issued a proclamation from Bayonne regarding the crown of Spain. This proclamation announced benevolent intentions to the Spaniards, though reconciliation remained impossible following Napoleon's treatment of Ferdinand VII, the *de facto* king.
