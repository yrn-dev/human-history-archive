# The Edinburgh Magazine and Associated Literary Contributions

The *Edinburgh Magazine* served as a significant venue for literary and scientific contributions during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. It is noted for publishing the works of various figures, ranging from scientific editors to renowned poets.

## Brewster and the Scientific Press
In 1799, Brewster began his literary career as a regular contributor to the *Edinburgh Magazine*. His involvement with the publication grew rapidly, and he acted as its editor at the age of twenty. Brewster's editorial and literary efforts extended beyond this magazine; in 1807, he undertook the editorship of the *Edinburgh Encyclopaedia*, which began publication in 1808 and concluded in 1830. This encyclopedia was particularly strong in its scientific department, featuring many valuable articles written by Brewster himself.

Brewster continued his editorial trajectory by establishing the *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* in 1819 in conjunction with Robert Jameson. This new journal took the place of the *Edinburgh Magazine*. Brewster and Jameson jointly edited the first ten volumes between 1819 and 1824, after which Jameson edited the remaining four volumes (1825–1826) alone. Following his partnership with Jameson, Brewster started the *Edinburgh Journal of Science* in 1824, editing sixteen volumes from 1824 to 1832.

Beyond these journals, Brewster was a prolific writer for various reviews and learned societies. He contributed between three and four hundred papers to the transactions of learned societies and wrote seventy-five articles for the *North British Review* alone. He also contributed to the seventh and eighth editions of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, writing articles on topics including Magnetism, Optics, Hydrodynamics, Stereoscope, Microscope, Electricity, and Voltaic Electricity.

## Robert Burns and Periodical Publications
The *Edinburgh Magazine* was also one of the several publications that featured the poetry of Robert Burns. Burns's work appeared in a variety of newspapers and magazines, including *The Scots Magazine*, *The Edinburgh Herald*, *The Edinburgh Advertiser*, *The Edinburgh Evening Courant*, and *The Caledonian Mercury*. His work was also published in London papers such as *The Morning Chronicle* and *Stuart's Star and Evening Advertiser* (which later became *The Morning Star*).

In addition to these periodicals, Burns's poems appeared in broadsides and a series of penny chap-books titled *Poetry Original and Select*, published by Brash and Reid in Glasgow. Between 1796 and 1799, Stewart and Meikle issued a series of tracts that included poems such as *Holy Willie's Prayer* and *The Jolly Beggars*. These seven numbers were reissued in January 1800 as *The Poetical Miscellany*.

## Other Edinburgh Literary Journals
The literary landscape of Edinburgh included several other journals that attracted contributors and editors. The *Edinburgh Literary Gazette* received contributions from De Quincey, who lived in Edinburgh for twelve years starting in 1828. Similarly, Henry Glassford Bell, a Scottish lawyer and man of letters, became the editor of the *Edinburgh Literary Journal* in 1828, a publication that was eventually incorporated into the *Edinburgh Weekly Chronicle*.

Additionally, the *Edinburgh Review* published an article by Ugo Foscolo regarding Henry Francis Cary's translation of Dante; this article, supplemented by additions from Samuel Rogers, helped bring general acknowledgment to the merit of Cary's work.
