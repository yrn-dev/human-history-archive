# Petroleum and Naphtha

Petroleum and naphtha are mineral substances found in various regions of the world, utilized for illumination, fuel, and industrial applications. These substances occur naturally in springs and wells, and their utility is derived primarily through the process of distillation.

## Natural Occurrences and Geography
Petroleum and naphtha are found in several distinct geographical areas. In the government of Russian Transcaucasia, the Apsheron peninsula contains famous petroleum wells. These wells reach depths ranging from 700 to 1,700 ft. and yield crude naphtha. The oil fields are situated around the town of Baku, including the Bibi-Eybat field to the south, the "black town" (Nobel's) to the south-east, the "white town" (Rothschild's), and the largest field, Balakhany-Sabunchi-Romany, located 8-1/2 m. north of the town.

In Asiatic Turkey, the town of Kerkuk, located in the Mosul vilayet, derives principal importance from its naphtha and petroleum springs. Similarly, in the province of the Euphrates and Tigris, bitumen, naphtha, and white petroleum springs occur at various points, specifically at Hit and southward along the edge of the Arabian plateau, though these remain undeveloped.

Ancient accounts also record the presence of naphtha in Babylonia. Historical records describe a lake of naphtha near Ekbatana that was highly flammable, capable of catching fire from the rays of a distant flame.

## Distillation and Refining
Crude petroleum must undergo distillation to be converted into usable products. From crude naphtha, petroleum or kerosene (also known as lamp oil) is distilled. This process is necessary for several reasons:

*   **Safety and Stability:** Lighter, volatile constituents such as benzene and naphtha must be removed to leave a residue of hydrocarbons. This prevents premature ignition and the danger of explosion. A more complete distillation process results in a higher flash-point and a more viscous fuel.
*   **Impurity Removal:** Refining reduces impurities that hinder combustion and storage, specifically water—which is present in all crude petroleum as it leaves the well—and sulphur, which is found in significant quantities in Texas oil.

The residue remaining after the production of kerosene is known in Russia as *astatki* or *mazut*. While *mazut* is used as fuel and lubricating oil (such as for Transcaspian railway locomotives), *astatki* was once considered a useless incumbrance until it was discovered that it could be used as fuel by spraying or atomizing it with high-pressure steam into a furnace.

## Commercial Production and Transport
The scale of petroleum production has grown significantly over time. In Baku, the output of crude naphtha rose from 5,500 tons in 1863 to 9,833,600 tons by 1904, although a serious fire in 1905 injured business. Lighter oils from Baku are transported via pipes to Batum on the Black Sea for export, while heavier oils are moved by tank railway-cars to Batum, Poti, and Novorossiysk.

On a global scale, petroleum was applied commercially around 1870. By 1898, discoveries of oil in Borneo and Texas provided supplies specifically adapted for fuel purposes. While Texas oil was suitable for kerosene and fuel oil, its market position was eventually weakened by water influx into the wells. Later discoveries in Mexico have helped maintain the global supply.

## Industrial Applications
Beyond illumination and heating, petroleum derivatives are used in gas production. "Carburetted" water gas is produced by using vapours from superheated petroleum naphtha to increase the volume of the gas by 50% or more. This process adds "illuminants" (heavy hydrocarbons) to the gas, which greatly enhances its fuel-value. This method is widely used in the United States due to the abundance of petroleum naphtha and anthracite.

The chemical composition of efficiently working fuel oil is recorded as 88.00% Carbon, 10.75% Hydrogen, and 1.25% Oxygen.
