# Slater's Book-Prices Current

## Overview of the Work
*Book-Prices Current* is a publication authored by Slater that focuses on the financial aspects of book-collecting. The work has been published annually since 1887. It serves as a resource for studying the monetary value of books, alongside similar works such as Livingston's *American Book Prices Current* and Livingston's *Auction Prices of Books* (1905).

## Determinants of Book Value
The pricing of books, as reflected in works like Slater's, is influenced by fashion, though the prices are generally not considered wholly unreasonable. The value of a book is determined by three primary factors:

1.  **Interest:** The positive or associative interest found within the book itself.
2.  **Market Availability:** The rarity of the volume, specifically the infrequency with which copies enter the market relative to the number and wealth of potential buyers.
3.  **Physical Condition:** For books that are not of the greatest rarity or interest, value is dictated by the condition of the copy, including its size, freshness, completeness, and the absence of stains.

## Context of Book Collecting
The financial data provided by Slater exists within a broader culture of collecting where certain books appeal to the imagination more than others. Collectors often prize works associated with famous events or persons, such as poets, soldiers, statesmen, or royalty (for example, Mary queen of Scots). A book's "pedigree"—the series of book-loving owners who previously possessed it—can increase its regard.

Furthermore, first editions are highly valued. This desire is driven partly by the associations created by the imagination and partly by the fact that first editions may be positively superior to reprints for the purposes of study. While the high prices paid during the era of George III for first printed editions of Latin and Greek classics are difficult to justify—as they are often of no value as texts and lack associations with their authors—the prestige of first editions of French and English literary masterpieces has grown immensely. An example of this is the sale of a first folio Shakespeare (1623) in 1906.

## Bibliographical Literature and Evolution
The study of book prices and collecting is supported by a wider body of bibliographical literature. Early general manuals, such as W.T. Lowndes' *Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature* (first published 1834) and Brunet's *Manuel du libraire* (first published 1810), once provided the primary information required by "old-fashioned" collectors. J.G.T. Graesse's *Tresor de livres rares et precieux* (1859-1867) also contributed to this field, though it added little to the information already present in Brunet's work.

In recent history, the "omnivorous collector" has been replaced by those seeking more detailed handbooks and bibliographies on special books. This shift has led to a decrease in the production of new editions of general manuals like those of Lowndes and Brunet.
