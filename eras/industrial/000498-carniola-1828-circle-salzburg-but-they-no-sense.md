# Carniola

Carniola, known in German as *Krain*, is a duchy and crown-land of Austria. Its modern name is derived from the Slavonic word *Krajina*, meaning "frontier." The province covers an area of 3,856 square miles.

## Geography and Topography
Carniola is primarily a mountainous region. The northern portion is occupied by the Alps, while the southern portion consists of the Karst (or Carso) Mountains. The territory is traversed by the Steiner Alps, the Karawankas, and the Julian Alps, all of which are part of the southern zone of the Eastern Alps.

The highest point in the Julian Alps is the Triglav (or Terglou), reaching 9,394 feet. This mass serves as a barrier between the Italian, German, and Slavonic races and contains the only glacier in the province on its northern declivity. Other significant peaks include the Jaluz (8,708 ft.) and the Mangart (8,784 ft.). The Karawankas, which mark the boundary between Carniola and Carinthia, feature the Stou (or Stuhlberg) at 7,344 feet and are crossed by the Loibl Pass (4,492 ft.). The Steiner (or Santhaler) Alps reach their peak at the Grintovc (or Grintouz) at 8,393 feet, located at the threefold boundary of Styria, Carniola, and Carinthia.

The southern Karst region is characterized by subterranean streams, funnels, and caves, most notably the Kreuzberghohle near Laas, the grottos of Adelsberg, and the larger caves of Planina. Other southern divisions include the Hornwald (highest peak Hornbuchl at 3,608 ft.), the Uskokengebirge (3,874 ft.), and the Birnbaumer Wald, which features the Nanos peak (4,275 ft.) and the Krainer Schneeberg (5,890 ft.).

## History
During the Roman Empire, Carniola was part of Pannonia and Noricum. Slavonic populations settled in the region between the end of the 6th century and the beginning of the 7th century. After being conquered by Charlemagne, most of the district was given to the duke of Friuli. By the 10th century, a family based in the castle of Kieselberg near Krainburg began using the title of margrave of Carniola, though other lords, including the bishop of Freising and the duke of Carinthia, held various parts of the territory.

By the end of the 14th century, Rudolph IV of Austria acquired all separate portions of the territory through bequest or inheritance and took the title of duke of Carniola. The duchy remained an Austrian possession, with the exception of the period from 1809 to 1813. During this time, it was ceded to Italy at the peace of Schönbrunn and incorporated into the French Illyrian Provinces, governed in the interest of Napoleon. Following the end of the French occupation in 1813, the province reverted to Austria. In 1816, it became part of the kingdom of Illyria, a kingdom of the Austrian crown that lasted until 1849, the year Carniola became a separate crown-land.

## Administration and Politics
Carniola is bounded by Styria to the northeast, Carinthia to the north, Croatia to the south and southeast, and Istria, Trieste, and Gorz and Gradisca to the west. 

The province is divided into eleven districts and one autonomous municipality, the capital city of Laibach. Other important locations include Idria, Oberlaibach, Gurkfeld, Zirknitz, Adelsberg, Neumarktl, Krainburg, and Gottschee.

Historically, the province utilized a system of provincial diets and estates. The diet of Carniola was revived in 1818, though it was not representative; eligibility was limited to nobles and clergy, with a few town delegates. These bodies primarily dealt with local police matters and registered imperial decrees regarding taxation or recruiting.

In 1900, the population of Carniola was 508,348, with a density of 132 inhabitants per square mile. The population was 95% Slovene and 5% German, with 99% belonging to the Roman Catholic Church. The local diet consists of thirty-seven members, including the bishop of Laibach as an *ex officio* member. Carniola sends eleven deputies to the Reichsrat in Vienna.

## Language and National Identity
Until 1879, the Slovenes lacked government support. However, by 1882, they won a majority in the diet of Carniola, making the province the chief support for Slovene agitation (contrasting with the diet of Styria, which served as the center for German interests). In the same year, Slovenes won the majority in the Laibach town council, which had previously been German. This allowed for the introduction of Illyrian as the official language and the renaming of streets in Illyrian.

Following a resolution by the diet of Carniola and a motion by Slovene members of the Reichsrath, the government recognized Slovenian as a recognized language for the whole of Carniola, as well as for mixed districts in south Carinthia and the district of Cilli in Styria. Additionally, it was determined that a Slovene gymnasium would be maintained in Laibach alongside the German one.
