## Administrative Context in Bagdad
In Bagdad, the British presence is represented by a consul-general who simultaneously serves as the political agent to the Indian government. The status of this official is ranked second only to that of the British ambassador located at Constantinople. To support his operations, the consul-general maintains a guard of sepoys and a gunboat in the river. Within the residency, there is an Indian post-office.

## Postal and Communication Services
Bagdad utilizes two postal services and two lines of telegraph—one Turkish and one British—to maintain communication with Europe. Historically, the British government operated a camel-post that crossed the desert to Damascus. This specific service was abandoned around 1880 following the establishment of a similar service by the Turks. The Turkish camel-post enabled letters to reach Damascus within a period of nine days.
