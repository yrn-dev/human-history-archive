# George Henry Boker's Francesca da Rimini and James Harebell in The Man o' Airlie

## Dramatic Works and Performances
The plays *Francesca da Rimini*, written by George Henry Boker (1823–1890), and *The Man o' Airlie* are associated with the repertoire of the American actor Lawrence Barrett. Barrett, who was born in Paterson, New Jersey, on April 4, 1838, to Irish parents, performed a wide variety of roles throughout his career. Among these parts were the character of Lanciotto in Boker's *Francesca da Rimini* and the role of James Harebell in *The Man o' Airlie*.

## Lawrence Barrett's Career
Lawrence Barrett, whose family name was Brannigan, began his stage career in 1853 in Detroit, appearing as Murad in *The French Spy*. He expanded his professional presence in New York in December 1856, performing as Sir Thomas Clifford in *The Hunchback* at the Chambers Street theatre. By 1858, he had joined the stock company at the Boston Museum.

Beyond the stage, Barrett served as a captain in the 28th Massachusetts infantry regiment during the Civil War. Between 1867 and 1870, he co-managed the California theatre in San Francisco alongside John McCullough. His versatility as an actor is evidenced by his performances in roles such as Hamlet, Macbeth, Lear, Richard III, Shylock, Richelieu, Wolsey, Benedick, David Garrick, Hernani, and Alfred Evelyn. He also performed as Cassius opposite Booth's Brutus and as Othello opposite Booth's Iago.

Barrett toured London in 1867, 1881, 1883, and 1884. His portrayal of Richelieu in the drama by Bulwer Lytton was regarded as his finest part. In addition to acting, Barrett authored a life of Edwin Forrest for the *American Actors Series* (1881) and a sketch of Edwin Booth in *Edwin Booth and his Contemporaries* (1886). He died on March 20, 1891.

## Other Works Titled Francesca da Rimini
The title *Francesca da Rimini* is also associated with other artistic and literary contexts. The Italian actress Eleanora Duse, born in Vigevano, was closely associated with the romanticist Gabriele d'Annunzio. Duse performed in several of d'Annunzio's plays, including *Francesca da Rimini* (1901) and *La Citta morta* (1898). Duse was recognized as Italy's greatest actress by 1885, a reputation that extended to the leading cities of America and Europe. Her acting style was characterized by intense naturalness, poignant intellectuality, and a lack of "make-up," contrasting with the theatrical emotionalism of the French tradition.
