# Charles Badham

## Early Life and Education
Charles Badham was an English scholar born on July 18, 1813, in Ludlow, Shropshire. He was the son of Charles Badham, an excellent classical scholar and translator of Juvenal who served as the regius professor of physic at Glasgow. His mother was a cousin of the poet Thomas Campbell. 

Badham's education began abroad; at approximately seven years of age, he was sent to Switzerland to study as a pupil of Pestalozzi. He later moved to England to attend Eton. In 1830, he was elected to a scholarship at Wadham College, Oxford.

## Academic Performance at Oxford
While at Oxford, Badham obtained a third class in classics in 1836. This result was considered a failure, which may have been attributed to his dislike of the study methods that were fashionable at the university during that period. Furthermore, the archive notes that classical scholarship was in a very unsatisfactory condition at the time. Badham eventually took his degree in 1837.

## European Studies and Further Degrees
Shortly after graduating from Oxford, Badham traveled to Italy. During his time there, he focused on the study of ancient manuscripts, specifically those held in the Vatican library. It was during this period that he established a lifelong friendship with G. C. Cobet. Following his time in Italy, he spent a period of time in Germany.

Upon returning to England, Badham continued his academic pursuits. In 1847, he was incorporated as an M.A. at Peterhouse, Cambridge. He later earned a degree of D.D. from Cambridge in 1852. In 1860, the university of Leiden awarded him an honorary degree of doctor of letters.

## Professional Career
Badham entered holy orders and transitioned into educational leadership. He served as the headmaster of Louth grammar school in Lincolnshire from 1851 to 1854. He subsequently became the headmaster of the Edgbaston proprietary school near Birmingham.

In 1866, Badham left England to move to Australia, where he accepted the professorship of classics and logic at Sydney University. He held this academic position until his death on February 26, 1884.

## Reputation and Scholarship
Despite his early academic struggles at Oxford, Badham's classical attainments were recognized by prominent European critics, including Tischendorf, A. Ritschl, J. A. F. Meineke, F. W. Schneidewin, W. Dindorf, Ludwig Preller, and G. C. Cobet. He was regarded as a textual critic and scholar of eminence and European reputation, though it is noted that he made a comparatively small mark in his native country.

His professional advancement may have been hindered by his hasty temper and a dislike for anything that approached disingenuousness. The archive characterizes him as a good scholar and teacher who, like many in his position, was not a professional success.

## Personal Life
Charles Badham was twice married.
