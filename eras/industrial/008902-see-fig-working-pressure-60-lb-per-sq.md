# Pressure in Industrial and Ballistic Systems

The measurement and management of pressure are critical components across various engineering disciplines, including steam generation, pneumatic blowing engines, and interior ballistics. Depending on the application, working pressures are measured in pounds per square inch (lb. per sq. in.) or tons per square inch.

## Steam Boiler Pressures
Working pressures in stationary and marine boilers vary significantly based on the type of boiler and the engine it serves.

### Tank Boilers
Large stationary boilers, such as the "Lancashire" boiler and its "Galloway" modification, typically range from 26 to 30 feet in length with diameters between 6-1/2 and 8 feet. In older models of these boilers, the working pressure is approximately 60 lb. per sq. in. However, pressures increase for more advanced engines:
* **Compound engines:** 100 lb. to 120 lb. per sq. in.
* **Triple expansion engines:** 150 lb. to 170 lb. per sq. in.
* **Maximum constructions:** Some have been built to withstand 200 lb. per sq. in.

### Marine Boilers
Cylindrical multitubular return tube boilers are widely used in merchant steamers. These boilers, which can reach 17 feet in diameter, generally operate at a working pressure between 160 and 200 lb. per sq. in., though some specific cases utilize pressures as high as 265 lb. per sq. in.

## Pneumatic Blowing Engines
Blowing engines utilize steam cylinders to actuate air cylinders for delivering blasts of air.

### Lackenby Blowing Engines
A design built in 1871 for the Lackenby iron-works in Middlesbrough features high-pressure and low-pressure steam cylinders placed in tandem with air cylinders. This system operates at a speed of 24 revolutions per minute, delivering 15,072 cubic feet of air per minute with 258 horse-power in the air cylinders.

### Davy Bros. Blowing Engines
A larger engine built in 1905 by Messrs Davy Bros. for the North-Eastern Steel Company also in Middlesbrough utilizes two air cylinders, each 90 inches in diameter with a 72-inch stroke. This engine delivers 52,000 cubic feet of air per minute at a speed of 33 revolutions per minute. The working pressure for this specific engine ranges from 12-1/2 to 15 lb. per sq. in. The delivery valves are spring-adjusted to release air only once the desired working pressure is reached.

## Interior Ballistics and Powder Pressure
In the study of firearms and artillery, pressure is often measured in tons per square inch.

### Mean Effective Pressure (M.E.P.)
The mean effective pressure is used to calculate the work realized by the thrust of powder on a shot. For a 6-inch gun with a length (L) of 216 inches and a rifled bore volume (B - C) of 6,100 cubic inches, the M.E.P. is approximately 6.4 tons per sq. in. It is noted that maximum pressure can exceed the mean pressure by a ratio of 2 or 3 to 1.

### Gravimetric Density and Closed Chamber Pressure
The pressure of an explosive is related to its gravimetric density (G.D.), which is the specific gravity of a weight of powder (P) filling a known volume (C). For example, if a charge of cordite with a G.D. of 0.5 were placed in a closed chamber where the shot was not free to move, the resulting closed chamber pressure would be nearly 49 tons per sq. in. To ensure safety, the G.D. is adjusted so that the advance of the shot during combustion prevents the maximum pressure from exceeding safe limits.

### Gas Compression and Temperature
Experiments with cordite-gas show that at 0°C and standard atmospheric pressure (14.7 lb. per sq. in.), one gramme of gas occupies 700 ccs. When this same gas is compressed into 5 ccs at the temperature of explosion, it reaches a pressure of 16 tons per sq. in., which is equivalent to 2,440 atmospheres. This state corresponds to an absolute centigrade temperature of 4,758 (4,485°C or 8,105°F).

## Other Pressure Applications
* **Bridge Engineering:** In the roller bed of a girder for the Kuilenburg bridge (490 ft. span), the pressure on the rollers is limited to approximately $p = 600d$ in lb per inch length of roller, where $d$ represents the roller diameter in inches.
* **Industrial Destructors:** Meldrum "Simplex" destructors utilize steam-jet blowers to provide blast pressures up to 6 inches of water column, although the pressure usually employed is no more than 1½ inches.
