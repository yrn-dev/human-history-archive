# Normand Boiler

The Normand boiler is a type of water-tube boiler. In the general classification of boilers, water-tube boilers are those in which the water is primarily contained within numerous comparatively small tubes, rather than in a large tank. In these designs, the fire is typically positioned beneath a collection of water-containing tubes that constitute the majority of the heating surface, all of which are enclosed within an outer casing made of fire-resisting material or brickwork.

## Design and Construction
The Normand boiler features three chambers, a configuration similar to that of the Yarrow boiler. These chambers are linked by a large number of bent tubes that serve as the heating surface. Additionally, the chambers are connected at each end by large outside circulating tubes.

A defining characteristic of the Normand boiler is its use of "water-walls" to protect the outer casing and manage the flow of heat. The heating tubes are arranged in rows to achieve this:
* **Outer Rows:** On each side, the two outer rows of heating tubes are arranged so that they touch one another for nearly their entire length. This creates a water-wall that protects the outer casing. These tubes enter the steam-chest at approximately the water-level.
* **Inner Rows:** The two inner rows of tubes are bent in a specific form to create a water-wall for a larger portion of the boiler's length.

## Thermal Operation
The arrangement of the inner water-walls serves a functional purpose regarding the products of combustion. By forming these barriers, the boiler compels the products of combustion to follow a definite course as they pass among all the tubes, ensuring efficient heat transfer.

## Comparison with Other Boilers
The Normand boiler differs significantly from other water-tube designs such as the Reed boiler. While the Normand boiler utilizes water-walls to direct combustion products, the Reed boiler has no water-walls; in the Reed design, the products of combustion pass directly from the fire-grate, among all the tubes, to the chimney. Furthermore, the tubes in a Reed boiler are more curved and longer than those found in the Normand boiler.

The Normand boiler also shares the three-chamber and bent-tube configuration with the Blechynden and White-Foster boilers. However, those models differ in how their tubes are removed for maintenance: Blechynden tubes are removed through small doors in the upper part of the steam-chest, while White-Foster tubes are removed via a manhole at the end of the steam-chest.
