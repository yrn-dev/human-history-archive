# Alexander Hamilton (1762-1824)

Alexander Hamilton (1762-1824) was a scholar noted for his expertise in the Sanskrit language and his contributions to the cataloging of Oriental manuscripts.

## Academic Work and Sanskrit Studies
During a period spent in India, Hamilton acquired an acquaintance with Sanskrit. This linguistic proficiency later informed his scholarly activities in France. While in Paris, he became a significant figure in the intellectual circle of eminent men, including L.M. Langles, S. de Sacy, A.L. Chezy, and others. 

Hamilton's primary contribution to the field of Sanskrit studies was the production of a descriptive catalogue of the Sanskrit manuscripts held within the Imperial library. This work was completed conjointly with L.M. Langles.

## Influence on Later Philology
Hamilton's presence in Paris provided a vital resource for subsequent scholars of Eastern languages. In 1812, the German philologist Franz Bopp arrived in Paris under the sponsorship of the Bavarian government to study Sanskrit. Bopp sought out the society of several eminent figures, among whom Alexander Hamilton was regarded as being of particular importance. 

Bopp utilized the resources of the Imperial library, which contained a rich collection of Sanskrit manuscripts—many brought from India by Father Pons in the early 18th century—as well as books issued from the Serampore and Calcutta presses. The environment fostered by scholars like Hamilton contributed to the period of study that led to Bopp's 1816 publication, *Uber das Conjugationssystem der Sanskritsprache in Vergleichung mit jenem der griechischen, lateinischen, persischen und germanischen Sprache*.
