# Cincinnati, Ohio

Cincinnati, Ohio, is a city that served as a center for medicine, theology, journalism, and the arts during the 19th century. It is a key destination for various rail and steamboat lines, including the Pittsburg, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis; the Cleveland, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis; and the Chicago, Indiana & Louisville.

## Education and Theology
The city is home to the Lane Theological Seminary at Walnut Hills. Lyman Beecher, an American clergyman and descendant of a New Haven colony founder, served as the president of the seminary and as a professor of polemic and didactic theology from 1832 to 1850. Beecher, who was known as an eloquent pulpit orator, also preached at the Second Presbyterian church in Cincinnati from 1833 to 1843.

The seminary was the site of significant ideological conflict in February 1834, when debates broke out between students who were pro-slavery and those who were anti-slavery. These debates eventually led to the withdrawal of the anti-slavery students.

## Medicine and Journalism
Gamaliel Bailey, born in New Jersey and a graduate of the Jefferson Medical College in Philadelphia, moved to Cincinnati in 1831. Upon his arrival, he devoted himself almost exclusively to the practice of medicine and served as a lecturer on physiology at the Lane Theological Seminary. 

Following the 1834 Lane Seminary debates, Bailey became an ardent abolitionist. He entered the field of journalism, joining James G. Birney in the editorial control of the *Philanthropist* in 1836. Bailey succeeded Birney as editor the following year and continued to lead the paper until 1847, despite facing violence and having his printing office wrecked by a mob on three separate occasions. Additionally, Bailey edited a daily newspaper called the *Herald* starting in 1843.

## Arts and Literature
Cincinnati and its surrounding areas have produced notable figures in American literature and art. Alice Cary (born 1820) and Phoebe Cary (born 1824) were poets born at Mount Healthy, near Cincinnati. The sisters worked in unbroken companionship, publishing *Poems of Alice and Phoebe Carey* in 1850. Alice Cary later wrote novels and prose sketches, while Phoebe Cary is recognized for writing the 1852 hymn "Nearer Home."

In the field of visual arts, William Jacob Baer was born in Cincinnati on January 29, 1860. After studying in Munich between 1880 and 1884, Baer became a leader in the American revival of miniature-painting. He served as the first president of the Society of Painters in Miniature in New York and created works such as "Madonna with the Auburn Hair," "Daphne," "In Arcadia," and "The Golden Hour."
