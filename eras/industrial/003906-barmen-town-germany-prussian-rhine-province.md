# BARMEN

Barmen is a town located in Germany within the governmental district of Duesseldorf in the Prussian Rhine province. The town is situated in a narrow valley of the river Wupper, stretching for approximately four miles. Within its municipal boundaries, the river is crossed by twenty bridges, and the town is surrounded by high wooded hills. Barmen lies immediately to the east of Elberfeld, and the two locations virtually form a single town.

## Population and Administration
The town experienced significant population growth over the 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1816, the population was 19,030; by 1890, it had increased to 116,144, and by 1905, it reached 156,148. Although Barmen was mentioned in chronicles as early as the 11th century, it did not receive civic rights until 1808, at which time it was formed into a municipality.

## Geography and Infrastructure
Barmen is divided into three primary districts: Lower, Middle, and Upper Barmen. It is served by the main railway line connecting Berlin to Aix-la-Chapelle. Throughout its length, the town is connected to Elberfeld via a tramway, a railway, and a suspended trolley line that hangs over the bed of the Wupper.

## Industry and Commerce
Barmen is recognized as one of Germany's most important manufacturing centers, though its rapid commercial development only began at the start of the 19th century. The town is the chief seat of ribbon weaving in Germany. Its diverse manufacturing output includes:
*   **Textiles:** Lace, thread, braids, cloth goods, cotton, and silks.
*   **Hardware and Goods:** Steel wares, plated goods, machinery, and carpets.
*   **Buttons:** This specific industry employs approximately 15,000 workers.

Additionally, the town hosts extensive breweries, chemical works, soap works, and potteries. It is also known for its print-fields, bleaching-fields, and dyeworks, the latter of which are famous for their Turkey-red. Barmen maintains a very considerable export trade, particularly with the United States.

## Culture and Education
The town possesses a wide array of civic and cultural institutions, including a stately modern town hall, an exchange, a theatre, a picture-gallery, and an ethnographical museum. It also features a Hall of Fame (_Ruhmeshalle_) containing statues of Frederick III and William I, both emperors. Public monuments in the town commemorate the Franco-German War of 1870-71, Bismarck, and the native poet Emil Rittershaus (1834-1897).

Religious life is served by two Roman Catholic churches and nine Evangelical churches. Educational facilities include a missionary theological seminary, several high-grade public schools, and academies specializing in engineering, textile industry, and technical science.

## Recreation
The hills located to the south of the town are developed as public grounds. This area contains numerous villas, a tower that provides an extensive view, and a health resort.
