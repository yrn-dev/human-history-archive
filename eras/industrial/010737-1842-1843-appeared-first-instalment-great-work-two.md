# Norwegian Popular Stories

The publication of *Norwegian Popular Stories* (*Norske Folkeeventyr*) began in 1842-1843. This work was the result of a lifelong collaboration between two collectors of Norwegian folklore, Peter Christen Asbjornsen and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe. Upon its release, the work was recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology.

## The Collaborators
Peter Christen Asbjornsen was born on January 15, 1812, in Christiania. He came from an ancient family of the Gudbrandsdal. Asbjornsen began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories in 1832 at the age of twenty. He later expanded his collection by traveling on foot throughout Norway. Professionally, Asbjornsen was a zoologist and conducted several investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord, supported by the university.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Rinkereke. Moe met Asbjornsen when he was fourteen years old, initiating a close friendship that lasted until their deaths. Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore by 1834. After leaving Christiania University in 1839, Moe studied theology and worked as a tutor in Christiania, using his holidays to collect stories while wandering through remote mountain districts.

## Development of the Work
The partnership between Asbjornsen and Moe began in earnest in 1834 when they discovered their mutual interest in folklore and decided to work in concert, comparing their results. Their joint efforts culminated in the first instalment of *Norwegian Popular Stories*, published in 1842-1843. 

Following the initial release, a second volume was published in 1844. The two friends continued their work, eventually publishing a new collection in 1871. The reach of their work extended internationally, with Sir George Dasent providing English translations of many of the *Folkeeventyr* in 1859.

## Other Contributions
While the *Folkeeventyr* was their primary joint achievement, Asbjornsen pursued other interests. In 1845, he published a collection of Norwegian fairy tales (*huldreeventyr og folkesagn*) independently of Moe. Later, in 1856, Asbjornsen turned his attention to the issue of deforestation in Norway and successfully induced the government to address the matter.
