# Portgordon

## Overview
Portgordon is a thriving fishing village located in Banffshire, Scotland. It is situated approximately 1½ miles west of the town of Buckie.

## Economy and Trade
The village is identified as a site where fishing and miscellaneous trade are conducted. Within the broader context of Banffshire, Portgordon is one of several harbours—including Buckie, Banff, Macduff, Gardenstown, Portsoy, and Cullen—that support these activities. 

The fishing industry in the region is characterized by two primary types of activity: white fishing, which is carried out throughout the entire year, and the herring season, which takes place from June to September. The fishery districts of the county are centered in Buckie and Banff.

## Regional Context
Portgordon is located near Buckie, a police burgh and fishing town on the Moray Firth at the mouth of Buckie burn. Buckie serves as a major hub for the region, acting as the chief seat of line fishing in Scotland and attracting one of the largest Scottish fleets during the herring season. 

To the east of Buckie, approximately 2 miles away, lies Rathven, a fertile district known for containing other relics of the remote past, specifically several interesting Danish cairns.

## Geography and Infrastructure
Portgordon is part of Banffshire, a county whose chief towns include Banff, Buckie, and Keith. The region is served by the Highland and Great North of Scotland railway systems, which provide communication to Elgin, Nairn, Inverness, and Aberdeen.

The geology of the surrounding area is linked to that of Elgin and Aberdeen. The region is largely composed of crystalline schists of sedimentary origin from the Eastern Highland sequence, including quartzite, main limestone, and various slates and schists. Quartzite specifically appears on the coast between Buckie and Cullen.
