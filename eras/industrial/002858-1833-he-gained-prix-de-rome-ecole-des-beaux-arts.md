# Victor Baltard

Victor Baltard (1805–1874) was a French architect known for his contributions to the urban landscape of Paris and his work in ecclesiastical architecture. Born in Paris on June 19, 1805, he was one of two children of an architect who also pursued the profession.

## Education and Early Recognition
Baltard received his education at the Ecole des beaux-arts. In 1833, he was awarded the *prix de Rome* for his design of a military school. While some original records listed the date of this achievement as 1803, this is noted as an error as it predates his birth; contemporary sources confirm 1833 as the correct date.

## Architectural Works and Urban Contributions
Baltard is most widely recognized for his design of the central market in Paris, which remains his most popular achievement. His contributions to the city's infrastructure also included the construction of the cattle market and the slaughter houses of La Villette.

In the realm of religious architecture, Baltard was responsible for the church of St Augustin, a project in which he combined the structural values of steel and stone. He also designed numerous additions to existing churches.

## Influence on Ecclesiastical Decoration
Beyond structural architecture, Baltard played a significant role in the aesthetic transformation of Parisian churches. He was largely instrumental in introducing a systematic scheme of fresco decoration created by modern artists. This initiative was intended to replace the previous "promiscuously decorated" walls, which had been covered in heterogeneous collections of various types of pictures.

## Death
Victor Baltard died in Paris on January 13, 1874, following a professional life characterized by great activity.
