# The Work Done in Expanding to Infinity from p Tons per Sq. In.

In the study of ballistics and the behavior of powder-gases, specifically regarding the combustion of cordite, the calculation of work done during the expansion of gas is a critical component of estimating shot velocity and energy. When the combustion of a charge is considered complete at a specific stage, the subsequent expansion of the gas is analyzed using adiabatic laws.

## Adiabatic Expansion and Work Calculations
When gas expands adiabatically, it is assumed that the pressure varies inversely as some $m^{th}$ power of the volume. For cordite, experimental data indicates that a value of $m = 1.2$ serves as a good average. 

The work performed by the gas during expansion is calculated based on the pressure ($p$) in tons per square inch and the volume ($b$) in cubic inches. The work done in expanding to infinity from $p$ tons per sq. in. at volume $b$ cub. in. is expressed as:
$$\frac{pb}{m - 1} \text{ inch-tons}$$

If the expansion is limited to a specific volume $B$ cub. in., the work is calculated using the formula:
$$\frac{pb}{m - 1} \left[1 - \left(\frac{b}{B}\right)^{m-1}\right]$$

## Practical Application in Artillery
In a practical example involving a 6-inch gun, a fixed limit of 16 tons per sq. in. is assumed, which corresponds to a G.D. (gravimetric density) of 0.2. In this scenario, the powder-gas occupies a volume $b = 1825$ cubic inches, which corresponds to the shot advancing 3.225 feet. 

With an average pressure of 8 tons per sq. in., the shot acquires an energy of 730 foot-tons and reaches a velocity of approximately 1020 feet per second. The time taken to travel the 3.225 feet at an average velocity of 510 feet per second is approximately 0.0063 seconds. If the combustion of the cordite is considered complete at this stage (where $p = 16$ tons per sq. in. and $b = 1825$ cub. in.), the gas then expands adiabatically up to the muzzle. For this specific 6-inch gun, the ratio of the muzzle volume to the initial volume ($B/b$) is 3.75.

## General Principles of Powder-Gas Work
The realization of work $E$ in foot-tons from the expansion of one pound of powder between two gravimetric volumes is inferred through the average pressure $p$. If the gravimetric volume changes from $v - 1/2[\Delta]v$ to $v + 1/2[\Delta]v$ (a volume change of $27.73[\Delta]v$ cubic inches), the work done is $27.73p[\Delta]v$ inch-tons, or $2.31 p[\Delta]v$ foot-tons.

Furthermore, the Mean Effective Pressure (M.E.P.) in tons per sq. in. can be used to determine the work realized in inch-tons by multiplying the M.E.P. by the volume of the rifled part of the bore (the difference between the total bore volume $B$ and the powder-chamber volume $C$). For a 6-inch gun with a length $L = 216$ inches, the volume $B - C$ is 6100 cubic inches, resulting in an M.E.P. of approximately 6.4 tons per sq. in.

## Gas Properties and Pressure
Experimental observations of cordite-gas show that at standard atmospheric pressure and 0°C, one gramme of the gas occupies 700 ccs. When this same gas is compressed into 5 ccs at the temperature of explosion, it reaches a pressure of 16 tons per sq. in. This pressure is equivalent to 2440 atmospheres (based on standard atmospheric pressure being 14.7 lb per sq. in.), with one ton per sq. in. roughly equaling 150 atmospheres.
