# Hudson's Bay Company

The Hudson's Bay Company was a prominent commercial and administrative entity operating in North America, primarily focused on the fur trade and the governance of vast territories. By 1815, the company's fur-trading stations represented the only settlements within the great hinterland of wilderness in Canada, beyond the thinly populated coastal lake and river regions.

## Territorial Governance and Expansion
The company's influence expanded significantly in the 19th century. In 1821, the Hudson's Bay Company amalgamated with the N.W. Company. Following this merger, the company ruled an immense territory for twenty-eight years, a period characterized as one of "beneficent despotism."

The company also established specific administrative districts. In 1835, the Hudson's Bay Company formed a district known as Assiniboia in Rupert's Land, North America. This district was centered around Fort Garry, located at the junction of the Assiniboine and Red rivers, and extended in a circular area with a radius of 50 miles from the fort. Assiniboia was governed by a local council nominated by the Hudson's Bay Company until 1870, when Rupert's Land was transferred to Canada.

## The Fur Trade and Commercial Operations
The Hudson's Bay Company is identified as one of ten large American and Canadian companies that maintained extensive systems for gathering annual hauls of skins from far-scattered trappers. The company's operations included the procurement of high-quality furs; for instance, the finest American and Canadian sable skins are found in the Hudson's Bay Company's districts in the Esquimaux Bay and the East Main.

The company's commercial cycle involved forwarding raw skins to brokers in London. These brokers sorted the skins into lots and issued catalogues to global traders for sale via public auction. The Hudson's Bay Company's sales were held before those of other companies. Because no reserves were placed on any of its lots, the results of these sales were regarded as exact indicators of current market values.

## Personnel and Literary Records
The company employed individuals who later documented their experiences in the wilds of North America. Robert Michael Ballantyne, a Scottish writer of fiction, served in the Hudson's Bay Company for six years after traveling to Canada at the age of sixteen. Upon returning to Scotland in 1847, he published his first book, *Hudson's Bay: or, Life in the Wilds of North America*, in 1848. Ballantyne later wrote numerous adventure stories for youth, including *The Young Fur-Traders* (1856), adhering to a rule of writing from personal knowledge of the scenes he described.
