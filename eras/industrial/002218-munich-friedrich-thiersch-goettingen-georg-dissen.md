# Johann Georg Baiter

Johann Georg Baiter (1801–1877) was a Swiss textual critic and philologist. Born in Zurich on May 31, 1801, he received his early education in his hometown before attending the university of Tuebingen in 1818. Due to a lack of funds, he was forced to return to Zurich, where he spent several years working as a private tutor.

## Academic Training
Between 1824 and 1829, Baiter pursued advanced studies under several prominent scholars across different cities. He studied in Munich under Friedrich Thiersch, in Goettingen under Georg Dissen, and in Koenigsberg under Christian Lobeck.

## Professional Career
From 1833 until 1876, Baiter served as an *Oberlehrer* at the gymnasium in Zurich. He remained in Zurich until his death on October 10, 1877.

## Contributions to Philology
Baiter specialized in textual criticism, focusing primarily on the Attic orators and Cicero. He was noted for his accuracy in collations and his success in locating the best manuscript authorities. Much of his scholarly output was the result of collaborations with other academics. He was particularly close to Orelli, who considered Baiter to be his "right-hand man."

His editorial work includes:
* **Isocrates**: He edited the *Panegyricus* (1831) and later produced a version for the Didot collection of classics (1846).
* **Plato**: Between 1839 and 1842, he worked with Orelli and Winckelmann on a critical edition of Plato that advanced the text through the contribution of two new manuscripts.
* **Attic Orators**: In collaboration with Sauppe, he edited Lycurgus' *Leocratea* (1834) and the *Oratores Attici* (1838–1850).
* **Babrius**: He collaborated with Orelli on *Fabellae Iambicae nuper repertae* (1845).
* **Cicero**: Baiter was associated with Orelli's extensive work on Cicero, assisting with the *Onomasticon Tullianum* (1836–1838) and *Ciceronis Scholiastae* (1833). He was solely responsible for the *Triumphales* and *Fasti Consulares*. Later, he assisted in a second edition of Cicero with Orelli and Halm, and collaborated with Kayser to edit Cicero for the Tauchnitz series from 1860 to 1869.

Additionally, Baiter produced new editions of Horace and Tacitus (originally by Orelli) and translated Leake's *Topography of Athens* alongside Sauppe.
