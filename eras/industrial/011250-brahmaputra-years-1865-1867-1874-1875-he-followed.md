# Geographical Exploration of the Upper Brahmaputra

## Overview of Asian Exploration
The modern period of scientific geographical exploration in Asia has been highly prolific, particularly regarding regions beyond the limits of actual surveys. Much of this activity has focused on the central depression located to the north of the great elevated table-land of Tibet, or on the table-land itself.

## The Expeditions of Nain Singh
Knowledge of the great plateau was significantly expanded through the work of native surveyors attached to the Indian survey, most notably Pundits Krishna and Nain Singh. 

Between the years 1865 and 1867, Nain Singh conducted explorations into the sources of the Upper Brahmaputra as well as the sources of the Indus. Following these efforts, in 1874-1875, Nain Singh followed a specific line of travel that began at the eastern frontiers of Kashmir and led to the Tengri Nor lake. From there, he proceeded to the city of Lhasa, where he remained for several months.

## Related Explorations in Tibet
Other significant journeys contributed to the understanding of the region. Between 1879 and 1882, Pundit Krishna undertook a remarkable journey that extended northwards from Lhasa through Tsaidam to Sachu (also known as Saitu) in Mongolia. 

Krishna subsequently traveled through eastern Tibet to the town of Darchendo (or Tachienlu), which is situated on the borders of China on the high road between Peking and Lhasa. Although he was unable to reach India via Upper Assam, he returned to the vicinity of Lhasa and eventually crossed the Himalayas using a more westerly route. Both Nain Singh and Krishna visited the city of Lhasa during their respective explorations.
