# Gibraltar in 1782

The year 1782 was marked by significant military activity surrounding Gibraltar, involving both naval relief efforts by the British and a siege conducted by Spanish forces.

## The Siege of Gibraltar
During the military operations at Gibraltar in 1782, Spanish forces were engaged in a siege of the territory. Among the Spanish participants was José Cadalso Vazquez, a colonel in the army and a noted author. Cadalso Vazquez, who had previously traveled extensively through Europe and studied various literatures, was killed during the siege on February 27, 1782.

## British Naval Relief Efforts
The British navy conducted operations to provide relief to Gibraltar in 1782. Several high-ranking officers and vessels were involved in these missions:

*   **Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport:** Serving as a rear-admiral of the white and one of Lord Howe's flag-officers, Hood was present at the relief of Gibraltar in 1782 aboard the ship "Queen" (90).
*   **Sir William Cornwallis:** A British admiral who took part in the second relief of Gibraltar before returning to North America to serve in other actions.
*   **Samuel Barrington:** An admiral whose final active service was the relief of Gibraltar in October 1782.

## Related Naval Operations and Losses
The efforts to relieve Gibraltar were linked to broader fleet movements under Lord Howe. In August 1782, the fleet was ordered to refit at Portsmouth with speed before proceeding to Gibraltar. 

This period was marked by the disaster of the "Royal George," the ship on which Rear-Admiral Richard Kempenfelt had hoisted his flag in 1782. While the vessel was being careened to repair a leak below the waterline in preparation for the relief of Gibraltar, the ship sank. While the Admiralty suggested a breeze overturned the vessel, the general opinion within the navy was that the shifting of weights caused the old and rotten timbers to fail. It is estimated that at least 800 people perished in the sinking, including Admiral Kempenfelt.

## Political Context in England
The military conflicts of 1782 occurred during a period of political transition in England. The government led by Lord North, which had lasted twelve years from 1770 to 1782, came to an end in 1782. This administration had been criticized by figures such as Burke, who had spent years guiding the patrician chiefs of his party and drawing protests for the Journals of the Lords. During this era, the House of Commons was a site of frequent political skirmishes and industry, particularly for Burke, who was often accused of speaking too frequently and was the subject of rumors that he was "Junius."
