# SIR JOHN WOLFE WOLFE-BARRY

## Early Life and Education
Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry (1836– ) was an eminent engineer. He received his education at Glenalmond. Following his studies, he was articled as an engineering pupil to Sir John Hawkshaw. During his association with Hawkshaw, Wolfe-Barry was involved in the construction of railway bridges across the Thames at Cannon Street and Charing Cross.

## Professional Career and Engineering Works
In 1867, Wolfe-Barry began practicing as an engineer on his own account. He quickly established an extensive professional connection with railway companies located both within Great Britain and in other countries. 

His portfolio of work included several significant infrastructure projects:
* **Railway Extensions:** He was engaged in extensions of the Metropolitan District railway.
* **Bridges:** He worked on the new Kew bridges and the Tower Bridge over the Thames.
* **Stations and Docks:** His projects included the bridge and St Paul's station of the London, Chatham & Dover railway, as well as the Barry Docks for the Barry railway company near Cardiff.

## Honors and Professional Leadership
Following the completion of the Tower Bridge in 1894, Wolfe-Barry was appointed a C.B. Three years later, he was elevated to K.C.B. In 1896, he was elected as the president of the Institution of Civil Engineers. In 1898, he assumed the additional name of Wolfe.

## Public Service and Publications
Wolfe-Barry contributed his expertise to several royal commissions between 1886 and 1905, including:
* Irish Public Works (1886-1890)
* Highlands and Islands of Scotland (1889-1890)
* Accidents to Railway Servants (1899-1900)
* Port of London (1900-1902)
* London Traffic (1903-1905)

As an author, he published a book titled *Railway Appliances* in 1874. In 1882, he co-authored a work titled *Railways and Locomotives* with Sir F. J. Bramwell.
