# Debenture Bonds

## Definition and Nature
In English law, debenture bonds are a specific type of bond characterized by the fact that they are secured only by the covenant of the company. Unlike other forms of security, they do not carry any fixed or floating charge on the assets of the company.

## Legal Status and Exemptions
Under the legal framework established by the acts of 1878 and 1882 regarding bills of sale, certain instruments are expressly exempt from the statutory definition of a bill of sale. According to the act of 1882, debentures and debenture stock of a company are among these exempt instruments.

## General Context of Bonds in English Law
To understand debenture bonds, it is necessary to place them within the broader context of bonds in English law, which are defined as obligations by deed. The primary design of a bond is to ensure that the obligor (the person giving the bond) either performs a specific act, refrains from doing an act, or pays a sum of money.

To achieve this, the obligor binds himself to a penalty to the obligee. A condition, known as the "defeasance," is typically added; if the obligor fulfills the requirement—such as paying the secured sum, which is often half of the penalty—the bond becomes void. If the condition is not met, the bond remains in full force and becomes absolute at law, charging the obligor and his estate.

## Comparison with Other Bond Types
Debenture bonds differ significantly from other specialized bonds mentioned in legal and financial records:

*   **Lloyd's Bonds:** These are instruments under a railway company's seal that admit indebtedness for goods supplied or work done, including a covenant to pay the amount with interest. They were used by railway companies to increase indebtedness without technically violating their charters.
*   **Bottomry Bonds:** These are contracts of hypothecation where a ship's owner or master borrows money for emergencies (such as necessary repairs) by pledging the ship, or its keel/bottom, as security.
*   **Heritable Bonds:** A term used in Scots law for a bond for money that is joined with a conveyance of land, held by a creditor as security.
*   **Recognizance:** Unlike a standard bond, a recognizance is entered into before a court of record, making it an obligation of record.
*   **Post Obit Bonds:** Bonds given by a legatee or expectant heir, payable on or after the death of the person from whom they expect an inheritance.

## Other Applications of Bonds
Beyond financial instruments like debenture bonds, the term "bond" is applied in various other contexts:

*   **Performance and Fidelity:** Bonds are used to guarantee the fidelity of rent collectors, clerks, or public trust officers, to ensure building contracts are completed, or to prevent a rival business from being operated within certain limits of space and time.
*   **Customs and Warehousing:** "Bonded warehouses" are facilities where goods liable to duty are stored. Historically, importers gave bonds for the payment of duties when goods were removed from these warehouses.
*   **Construction:** In masonry, "bond" refers to the arrangement of bricks, such as English bond, Flemish bond, stretching bond, heading bond, or diagonal (herring bone) bond.
