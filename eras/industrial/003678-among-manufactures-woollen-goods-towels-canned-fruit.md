# Manufactures of Woollen Goods, Towels, and Canned Fruits

The production of woollen goods, towels, and canned fruits is distributed across various global industrial centers, ranging from specialized city manufactures in the United States to long-standing textile traditions in Europe and Asia.

## Woollen Goods and Textiles
Woollen goods are manufactured in a wide variety of geographic locations, often appearing alongside cotton production.

### North American and European Production
In the United States, the city of Baraboo, Wisconsin, lists woollen goods as one of its manufactures. Similarly, Carthage, Missouri, produces woollen goods alongside other industrial items such as flour and mining machinery. In Spain, the town of Badalona, an industrial suburb of Barcelona, manufactures both woollen and cotton goods.

In England, Bolton has been famous for its woollen manufactures since early days. During the reign of Richard I, an aulneger was appointed to stamp and measure bundles of woollen goods, indicating the area was already a center for the woollen cloth trade. The industry grew further in 1337 following the settlement of Flemish clothiers. By 1566, the industry was extensive enough that parliament appointed deputies to assist the aulnegers. Notably, Leland recorded in 1558 that Bolton produced "cottons," which were actually woollen goods.

In France, the port city of Bordeaux exports woollen and cotton goods as part of its international trade.

### Asian Production
In Asia, woollen production is found in several regions. In the capital city of Ilchi, the chief articles produced include woollen and silk carpets, as well as felts. In other regions, woollen cloth and felt are manufactured alongside boots, cutlery, and weapons. Additionally, there are considerable manufactures of woollen and cotton goods in districts served by the Madras and Southern Mahratta railways.

## Towels and Specialized Weaves
The manufacture of towels is specifically linked to certain weaving techniques. Cotton and linen towels are largely produced using a reversible cloth weave. One common design for this is the "perfect honeycomb," characterized by a pattern where the highest number of successive white squares is seven and the highest number of successive black squares is five. These maximum floats form the highest edges of the cell, with the number of like squares decreasing toward the bottom of the cell. Another weave, designated as design B, is also very largely used for the manufacture of towels.

## Canned Fruits and Vegetables
The canning of produce is a notable industry in specific American regional centers. In Baraboo, Wisconsin, the city manufactures canned fruit and vegetables. This industry is supported by the surrounding region, where the growing of small fruits is an important industry and a large nursery is located. Similarly, Carthage, Missouri, produces canned fruits and serves as a jobbing center for a region that produces both fruit and grain.
