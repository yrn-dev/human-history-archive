# Gujarat

Gujarat is a region located in western India, forming the northern division of the Bombay Presidency. This province is a diverse territory consisting of both British districts and native states.

## Geography and Land
Except for its northern parts, Gujarat consists of rich alluvial plains that are highly cultivated. These plains are watered by the Nerbudda and Tapti rivers and are not frequently subject to inundation. Within the broader Bombay Presidency, Gujarat is one of five well-demarcated tracts, alongside Sind, the Konkan, the Deccan, and the Carnatic.

## Administration and Political Divisions
As part of the Bombay Presidency, Gujarat is categorized as the northern division. The presidency itself is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Baluchistan, the Punjab, and Rajputana to the north, Indore, the Central Provinces, and Hyderabad to the east, and Madras and Mysore to the south.

The British districts within this region include Ahmedabad, Broach, Kaira, Panch Mahals, and Surat. Additionally, the area contains numerous native states. These are divided into two primary groups:
*   **North Gujarat:** This group comprises the Palanpur agency, Mahi Kantha agency, Rewa Kantha agency, Cambay, Cutch, and the Kathiawar agency.
*   **South Gujarat:** This group comprises Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.

The native state of Baroda is also located within the Gujarat province. Baroda maintains direct relations with the governor-general and consists of four isolated divisions: Nausari, Baroda, and Kadi (located in Gujarat proper), and Amreli with Okhamandal (located in the Kathiawar peninsula). As of 1901, the population of Baroda was 1,952,692, though this figure reflected a 19% decrease over the previous decade due to plague, epidemics of fever and cholera, and the famines of 1896-1897 and 1900-1901.

## Agriculture and Economy
Wheat is grown specifically in Gujarat and Sind, as well as other northern parts of the presidency; it is exported in large quantities from Karachi and on a smaller scale from Bombay. Barley is also principally grown in the northern parts of the presidency. The Kolis, a caste or tribe of Western India, form the main part of the inferior agricultural population in Gujarat.

## History
Gujarat has been the site of numerous invasions and shifts in power. In 1023, Mahmud of Ghazni invaded the region with a large army, carrying away immense booty and destroying the national Hindu idol of Somnath. Mahommed Ghori also invaded Gujarat and established a garrison in its capital. In 1297, the emperor Ala-ud-din conquered the region.

By the middle of the 14th century, as the power of the Delhi sovereigns weakened, Gujarat became the seat of a new independent kingdom. This lasted until 1573, when Akbar conquered Gujarat and reannexed it to the empire. Later, during the 17th century, the Mahrattas rose to power, and nearly every part of the region fell under their sway.

The Portuguese also established a presence on the coast. Taking advantage of the decay of the kingdom of Gujarat, they occupied Bassein and its dependencies (including Bombay) in 1534, Diu in 1535, and Daman in 1559. Previously, the islands of Bombay had been part of the province or kingdom of Gujarat until they were ceded to the Portuguese by Sultan Bahadur in 1534.

## Language
The language of Gujarat, Gujarati, is described as being mixed in the region of Gujarat and Rajputana. It contains several important survivals of the old language spoken by earlier Aryan inhabitants. Linguistically, Gujarati agrees closely with the languages of the countries to its north, and people from places such as Jaipur, Marwar, Mewar, Ajmere, and Delhi find no difficulty in being understood by the people of Gujarat. However, there is a distinct linguistic frontier between Gujarati and Marathi; people from the south, such as those from Maharashtra or the Konkan, are often not comprehended by the people of Gujarat.
