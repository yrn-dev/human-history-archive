# 1,013,600 Dynes

## Definition and Context
In the field of gasometry, it is necessary to establish a standard pressure to which all measurements can be reduced. A common standard is a mercury column of pure mercury at 0°C with a height of 76 cms. (29.922 in.), which is close to the average height of the barometer. Because the actual force exerted by the atmosphere changes based on the position on the earth's surface and the intensity of gravity, defining standard pressure requires the specification of a particular place.

To avoid this, force can be expressed as the pressure in dynes resulting from a column of mercury with a section of one square centimetre that is supported by the atmosphere. The pressure is calculated as $13.596 H_g$ dynes, where $H$ represents the height at 0°C, $g$ represents the value of gravity, and 13.596 is the density of mercury.

## Regional Variations in Standard Pressure
The value of standard pressure varies by location due to the differing values of gravity. At Greenwich, where $g$ equals 981.17, the standard pressure at 0°C is 1,013,800 dynes. In Paris, the standard pressure is 1,013,600 dynes.

## The Mega-dyne Proposal
The value of 1,013,600 dynes is very close to a mega-dyne, which is defined as one million dynes. Due to this proximity, it has been suggested that a mega-dyne per square centimetre be adopted as the standard pressure. Some modern writers have adopted this unit because it is independent of locality and offers convenience for calculations.
