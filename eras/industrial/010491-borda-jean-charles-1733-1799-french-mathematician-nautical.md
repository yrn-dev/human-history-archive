# BORDA, JEAN CHARLES (1733-1799)

Jean Charles Borda was a French nautical astronomer and mathematician who made significant contributions to marine engineering and the field of nautical astronomy.

## Early Life and Military Career
Born on May 4, 1733, at Dax, Borda pursued his studies at La Fleche. Early in his life, he obtained a commission in the cavalry. His academic and scientific interests were evident by 1756, when he presented a *Memoire sur le mouvement des projectiles* to the Academy of Sciences, resulting in his election as a member. During his military service, Borda was present at the battle of Hastembeck.

## Naval Service and Exploration
Following his time in the cavalry, Borda joined the naval service. During his tenure, he visited the Canary Islands and the Azores, where he produced an admirable map of these regions. In 1782, Borda was captured by a British squadron when his frigate was taken. He was transported to England but was released on parole almost immediately, allowing him to return to France.

## Scientific Contributions
Borda was a prolific contributor to the Academy of Sciences, providing a long series of valuable memoirs. His professional work focused on several key areas:

*   **Hydrodynamics:** His research in this field proved highly useful for the development of marine engineering.
*   **Nautical Astronomy:** Borda improved the repeating and reflecting circles, instruments that were of great service to the field of nautical astronomy.
*   **Geodesy:** Borda was associated with P.F.A. Mechain and J.B.J. Delambre in an effort to determine an arc of the meridian. Borda was responsible for inventing the majority of the instruments used for this specific task.

## Death
Jean Charles Borda died in Paris on February 20, 1799.
