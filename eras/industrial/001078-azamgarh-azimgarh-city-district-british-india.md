# AZAMGARH

Azamgarh, also referred to as Azimgarh, is a city and a district located in British India. It is situated within the Gorakhpur division of the United Provinces.

## Geography and Topography
The district of Azamgarh covers an area of 2,207 square miles. Its boundaries are defined by the river Gogra to the north, which separates it from the Gorakhpur district, and the river Ganges and Ghazipur district to the east. To the south, it is bordered by the districts of Ghazipur and Jaunpur, and to the west by Fyzabad and Jaunpur.

The topography of the region varies. The area along the banks of the Gogra is a low-lying tract of varying width, while the ground rises slightly to the south of this area. The land generally slopes from the north-west toward the south-east, though the general drainage is described as very inadequate. The district is characterized by a series of parallel ridges with summits that are depressed into hollows or beds where rivers flow. Between these ridges are numerous natural reservoirs and low-lying rice lands.

## Economy and Agriculture
The soil in Azamgarh is fertile and highly cultivated. The region produces magnificent crops of indigo, sugar-cane, and rice. Due to the production of indigo, there are several indigo factories located within the district.

## The City of Azamgarh
The town of Azamgarh is situated on the river Tons and is equipped with a railway station. It is said to have been founded around 1665 by Azim Khan, a powerful landholder who possessed large estates in the region. As of 1901, the population of the town was 18,835. In 1898, a branch of the Bengal & North-Western railway was opened to the town.

## History and Administration
The district was ceded to the Company in 1801 by the wazirs of Lucknow. In 1901, the total population of the district was 1,529,785, a figure that represented an 11% decrease over the preceding decade.

In 1857, Azamgarh became a center of mutiny. On June 3, 1857, the 17th Regiment of Native Infantry mutinied in the town, murdering some of their officers and transporting the government treasure to Fyzabad. The district subsequently became a site of conflict between rebels and the Gurkhas. The area was not finally cleared until October 1858 by Colonel Kelly.

## Language
The district is associated with the Western Bhojpuri dialect of Hindi. Documentation regarding this dialect includes J.R. Reid's "Report on the Settlement Operations in the District of Azamgarh" (1881), which contains a full vocabulary and grammar of Western Bhojpuri in its appendices.
