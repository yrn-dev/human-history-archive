# Annual Reports of the British Columbia Board of Trade

## Overview
The *Annual Reports of the British Columbia Board of Trade*, published in Victoria, serve as a documented authority for the study of the region. These reports are categorized among the primary sources used to compile historical and geographical data regarding British Columbia.

## Context within Regional Authorities
The reports of the Board of Trade are part of a broader collection of official and historical records used to document the province. They are cited alongside other significant departmental and governmental publications, including:
*   The *Annual Reports of the Minister of Mines* and various other departmental reports from both the Dominion and Provincial governments.
*   The *Catalogue of Provincial Museum* based in Victoria.
*   The *Reports of the Geological Survey of Canada*, which date from 1871 onward.
*   The *Reports of Canadian Pacific (Government) Surveys* covering the years 1872 to 1880.

These documents complement the work of historians and explorers, such as H.H. Bancroft, whose *History of British Columbia* was published in 1887, and Begg, who published a *History of British Columbia* in 1894. They also align with the data found in Gosnell's *Year Book*, published in Victoria in 1897 and 1903.

## Related Geographic and Scientific Data
While the Board of Trade reports provide administrative and commercial insights, they are utilized in conjunction with scientific records to provide a complete picture of the region. For example, meteorological data for the southern coast of British Columbia is derived from tables prepared by R.F. Stupart, the director of the meteorological service. This data identifies Victoria as a representative station for the southern coast, noting that while Victoria's annual mean temperature is 48.8°F, rainfall is significantly higher on the exposed parts of the outer coast.

Other regional data points often cross-referenced with provincial authorities include the Fraser delta (represented by Agassiz) and the southern interior district (represented by Kamloops).
