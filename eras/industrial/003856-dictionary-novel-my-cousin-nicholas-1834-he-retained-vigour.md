# Lexicographical and Literary Works

## Lexicographical Efforts
In the transition from the old century to the new, the development of the memoir and essay was facilitated by significant co-operative lexicographical efforts. Notable examples of such works include the *New English Dictionary*, the *Dictionary of National Biography*, and the *Victoria County History*. These efforts smoothed the way for various writers, including Mackail, Lang, Gosse, H.D. Traill, Lowes Dickinson, A.C. Benson, E.V. Lucas, Richard le Gallienne, and Hilaire Belloc.

## 19th Century Novelists and Authors
The 19th century saw a diversification of the novel, which expanded into new fields and directions.

### English Novelists
Henry Kingsley (1830-1876), born in Barnack, Northamptonshire, wrote novels characterized by healthy sentiment, abundance of incident, and much vigour. His work *Recollections of Geoffrey Hamlyn* (1859), which focused on Australian life, achieved considerable popularity. Other well-known works by Kingsley include *The Hillyars and The Burtons* (1865) and *Ravenshoe* (1861).

Captain Marryat brought unimpaired vivacity and ripe experience to his novels, which were noted for their direct, lucid narrative and humor that occasionally bordered on farce. His early works include *Frank Mildmay, or the Naval Officer* (1829) and *The King's Own* (1830). He produced a quick succession of tales, including *Newton Forster* (1832), *Jacob Faithful* (1834), and *Peter Simple* (1834). He reached a peak of constructive skill with *Snarley-yow, or the Dog Fiend* (1837). Marryat also wrote for boys, with popular titles such as *The Children of the New Forest* (1847), *The Settlers in Canada* (1844), and *Masterman Ready* (1841).

### Dutch Literature
Dutch literature during this period was heavily influenced by the novels of Sir Walter Scott and was rich in historical novels. J. van der Hage (1806-1854), writing as Jan Frederick Oltmans, authored *The Shepherd* (1838) and *Castle Loevenstein in 1570* (1834). Other contributors to the field included W.P. Walters, who wrote *Lucretia d'Este*, and Lodewijk Mulder, author of *Jan Faessen* (1856). Nicolaes Beets produced the masterpiece of character and humour, *Camera Obscura* (1836). Additionally, Johannes Kneppelhout wrote two amusing volumes on university life at Leiden: *Studentenleven* (1844) and *Studententypen* (1841).

### Danish Literature
In Denmark, the romantic movement eventually gave way to "Naturalists," a school advocated by the critic Dr. Georg Brandes. This new school was led by Schandorph, Drachmann, and J. P. Jacobsen. Jacobsen's romance *Marie Grubbe* is noted for its unexampled beauty. Holger Drachmann (1846-1908), who began as a marine painter, became the leading imaginative writer in Denmark. His work was characterized by freshness, vigour, and a particular focus on life at sea.

## Other Literary Figures
Richard Harris Barham (1788-1845), an English humourist who wrote under the pseudonym Thomas Ingoldsby, is best known for *The Ingoldsby Legends*. These grotesque metrical tales, first appearing in *Bentley's Miscellany* in 1837, are noted for their whimsicality of rhymes and an underlying store of solid antiquarian learning. Barham, who was ordained in 1813 and served as a minor canon of St Paul's cathedral, also contributed to the *Literary Gazette* and the *Edinburgh Review*.
