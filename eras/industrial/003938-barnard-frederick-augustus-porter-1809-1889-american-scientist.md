# BARNARD, FREDERICK AUGUSTUS PORTER (1809-1889)

Frederick Augustus Porter Barnard was a versatile American scientist and educationalist. Born on May 5, 1809, in Sheffield, Massachusetts, he pursued his early higher education at Yale, where he graduated second on the honour list in 1828.

## Early Career and Teaching
Following his graduation, Barnard served as a tutor at Yale. He then transitioned into specialized education, working as a teacher from 1831 to 1832 at the American Asylum for the Deaf and Dumb in Hartford, Connecticut. He continued this work in New York City, teaching at the New York Institute for the Instruction of the Deaf and Dumb between 1832 and 1838.

## Academic Appointments in the South
From 1838 to 1848, Barnard served as a professor of natural philosophy and mathematics at the University of Alabama. During his tenure there, he also filled the chair of English literature for two years and served as a professor of natural history and chemistry from 1848 to 1854.

In 1854, Barnard was ordained as a deacon in the Protestant Episcopal Church. That same year, he moved to the University of Mississippi to serve as a professor of natural philosophy and mathematics. He rose to the position of chancellor of the institution in 1856, a role he held until the start of the Civil War. Because his sympathies lay with the North, he resigned his post and relocated to Washington.

## Government Service and Presidency of Columbia College
While in Washington, Barnard spent some time managing the chart and map department of the United States Coast Survey. In 1864, he was appointed the tenth president of Columbia College in New York City (now known as Columbia University). He remained in this position until the year before his death, marking a tenure longer than that of any of his predecessors.

Under Barnard's leadership, Columbia College experienced rapid growth. He oversaw the establishment of new departments and the great extension of the elective system. He also implemented more adequate provisions for original research and graduate study. During his presidency, student enrolment grew from approximately 150 to more than 1,000.

## Educational Advocacy and Legacy
Barnard was a proponent of expanding educational privileges to women as well as men. In recognition of these efforts, Barnard College for women was established in his honour immediately following his death.

Described as a man of catholic training, Barnard was a physicist, chemist, mathematician, and a scholar of both English and classical studies. He was known as a vigorous, though sometimes prolix, writer on a variety of subjects; this was particularly evident in the annual reports he submitted to the Columbia Board of Trustees. He was also regarded as a skilled public speaker.

Frederick Augustus Porter Barnard died in New York City on April 27, 1889.
