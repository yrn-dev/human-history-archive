# Automatic Appliances connected with Music

The study of automatic appliances connected with music includes the development of mechanical instruments designed to produce music without the direct manual manipulation of notes by a performer. These devices, ranging from small portable organs to more complex mechanical systems, represent a intersection of musical composition and mechanical engineering.

## The Barrel-Organ and Bird-Organ
During the 18th and 19th centuries, a specific type of small barrel-organ known as the bird-organ was in use. This instrument is referred to by several French terms: *serinette*, *turlutaine*, and *merline*. 

An example of such an instrument, currently held in the collection of the Brussels Conservatoire, was described by V. C. Mahillon. This particular bird-organ is constructed in the form of a book, featuring the title "*Le chant des oiseaux, Tome vi.*" on its back. The device measures approximately 8 x 5-1/2 x 2-3/4 inches and is capable of playing eight different tunes. Its musical mechanism consists of ten pewter stopped pipes that provide the scale of G, with the addition of Fb and A two octaves higher (notation: G4 A5).

## Historical Documentation and Composition
The construction of barrel-organs in the 18th century is documented in the work of P. M. D. J. Engramelle titled *La Tonotechnie ou l'art de noter les cylindres et tout ce qui est susceptible de notage dans les instruments de concerts mechaniques*, published in Paris in 1775. This text includes engravings illustrating the art of noting cylinders and other elements of mechanical concert instruments.

The use of these automatic appliances also extended to formal composition. Mozart, for instance, wrote an *Andante* specifically for a small barrel-organ.
