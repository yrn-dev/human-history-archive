# Royalist Movements and Conflicts (1809–1916)

The term "royalist" appears across various historical contexts in the archive, primarily referring to supporters of established monarchies during periods of revolution, independence wars, and international conflict. These movements were prominent in France, South America, and Greece.

## Royalists in France and the Bourbon Restoration
During the Napoleonic era, royalist efforts to restore the monarchy were often conducted from exile. Louis, later Louis XVIII, spent years attempting to convert France to the royalist cause. He maintained a *conseil royal* in Paris founded in 1799, though its activities were hindered by a rival committee led by the comte d'Artois. Following the execution of the duc d'Enghien in March 1804 and Napoleon's assumption of the imperial title in May 1804, the royalist cause appeared hopeless. Louis later took refuge in England, residing at Gosfield in Essex and later at Hartwell in Buckinghamshire.

Following Napoleon's defeats in 1813, royalist hopes revived. Louis issued a manifesto promising to recognize the results of the Revolution. After Paris capitulated in March 1814 and Napoleon abdicated at Fontainebleau—during which time a royalist mob endangered the emperor's life in Provence—the restoration of the Bourbons was effected. Louis XVIII entered Paris on May 2, 1814.

The restoration was marked by internal tension between constitutionalists and the reactionary clerical party of the *émigrés*, led by the duchesse d'Angoulême and the comte d'Artois. This latter group's influence, along with the presence of Blacas, created political instability. After the Hundred Days, during which Louis XVIII fled to Ghent, he re-entered Paris on July 8, 1815. While he initially relied on Talleyrand and Fouché, the elections of 1815 provided a strong royalist majority in the chamber, known as the *chambre introuvable*. By September 1816, the king dissolved this chamber due to its violence. In later years, members of the Chamber of Peers, such as Daru, defended popular liberty against attacks from "ultra-royalists."

## Royalist Conflicts in Venezuela
In the struggle for independence in Venezuela, royalist forces fought against patriot forces led by Bolivar. Bolivar had entered Caracas in triumph on August 4, 1813, and by January 1814, the patriots held entire possession of the country. However, this success was brief. The royalists concentrated their means and engaged in a series of sanguinary encounters.

Bolivar was eventually defeated by Boves in the plains of La Puerta near Cura, forcing him to retreat to Cumana. In July 1814, the Spaniards retook Caracas. By the end of 1814, the royalists had become the undisputed masters of Venezuela.

## Royalist and Patriot Warfare in Upper Peru
In Upper Peru (the region of later-day Bolivia), an almost uninterrupted conflict occurred from July 1809 until August 1825. This war featured alternate successes between the South American patriot forces and the Spanish or royalist forces. The fighting took place primarily between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta.

By 1815, the patriots suffered a total rout in a battle between Oruro and Potosi. This was followed by an insurrection of Creoles and Indians of the southern provinces of Peru seeking independence, but the royalists speedily put down the revolt. In 1816, the Spanish general Laserna, acting as commander-in-chief of Upper Peru, attempted to invade the Argentine provinces to march on Buenos Aires, but he was foiled by irregular *gaucho* troops and forced to retire. For the following six years, patriots maintained guerrilla warfare from the mountains, particularly in the province of Yungas, to harass royalist troops.

## Royalism in Greece
In 1916, royalist sentiment manifested in Greece during the First World War. At the end of the year, a royalist crowd in the port of Athens fired volleys at a landing party of British and French marines. This occurred under the reign of King Constantine of Greece. To prevent the king from aligning with Central European friends, Greece was blockaded, and Italian and French forces coordinated to cut him off.
