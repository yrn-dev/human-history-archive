# Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton

Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton (1827– ) was a French painter born on May 1, 1827, in Courrières, Pas de Calais, France. He demonstrated artistic talent at a young age and began his formal education in 1843 in Ghent, where he studied under the historical painter de Vigne. In 1846, he continued his studies in Antwerp under Baron Wappers, and later worked in Paris under Drolling.

## Artistic Evolution and Subject Matter
Breton's early artistic efforts focused on historical subjects, including "Saint Piat preaching in Gaul." Following the revolution of 1848, he produced a work titled "Misery and Despair." However, Breton eventually determined that he was not suited for historical painting. He shifted his focus toward the memories of nature and the countryside from his youth, becoming essentially a painter of rustic life.

His work primarily centered on the province of Artois, though he made three brief excursions to other regions: Provence in 1864, and Brittany in 1865 and 1873. His visits to Brittany were particularly influential, providing him with some of his most successful studies of religious scenes. Breton's diverse subjects are generally categorized into four classes: labour, rural festivals, religious festivals, and rest.

## Notable Works and Recognition
Breton began exhibiting at the Paris Salon in 1853 with "Return of the Harvesters," and also showed "The Little Gleaner" in Brussels. Over the following decades, he produced a wide array of significant works:

*   **1850s:** "Women Gleaning"; "The Day after St Sebastian's Day" (1855), which earned a third-class medal; "Blessing the Fields" (1857), which earned a second-class medal; "Erecting a Calvary" (1859), currently held in the Lille gallery; and "The Return of the Gleaners" (1859), located in the Luxembourg.
*   **1860s:** "Evening" and "Women Weeding" (1861), for which he received a first-class medal; "Grandfather's Birthday" (1862); "The Close of Day" (1865); "Harvest" (1867); "Potato Gatherers" (1868); and "A Pardon, Brittany" (1869).
*   **1870s:** "The Fountain" (1872), for which he was awarded a medal of honour; "The Bonfires of St John" (1875); "Women mending Nets" (1876), located in the Douai museum; and "A Gleaner" (1877), located in the Luxembourg.
*   **Later Works:** "Evening, Finistère" (1881); "The Song of the Lark" (1884); "The Last Sunbeam" (1885); "The Shepherd's Star" (1888); "The Call Home" (1889); "The Last Gleanings" (1895); "Gathering Poppies" (1897); "The Alarm Cry" (1899); and "Twilight Glory" (1900).

## Professional Honors
In 1886, following the death of Baudry, Breton was elected to the Institut. In 1889, he was made a commander.
