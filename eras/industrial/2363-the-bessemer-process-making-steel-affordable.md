# The Bessemer Process: Making Steel Affordable

## Overview
Before the mid-19th century, steel was a luxury material. While stronger and more flexible than cast iron, the process of creating steel—essentially removing excess carbon from iron—was slow, labor-intensive, and prohibitively expensive. Steel was reserved for specialty items like swords, cutlery, and high-end tools.

This changed in 1856 when Sir Henry Bessemer, an English inventor, patented a method for the mass production of steel. The Bessemer process utilized a "converter"—a large, pear-shaped vessel—to blast air through molten pig iron. This oxidation process burned off impurities (such as carbon, silicon, and manganese) through a chemical reaction that generated its own heat, eliminating the need for additional fuel. By slashing the time required to produce steel from several days to roughly 20 minutes, Bessemer fundamentally altered the global economy, enabling the construction of skyscrapers, massive bridges, and thousands of miles of durable railway tracks.

## Key Facts
*   **The Chemical Catalyst:** The process relied on the principle that oxygen reacts with carbon in molten iron to form carbon monoxide, which escapes as gas. This "boiling" effect purified the metal rapidly.
*   **The Phosphorus Problem:** Early Bessemer converters could not remove phosphorus, meaning only low-phosphorus ores (found primarily in Spain and Sweden) were usable. This led to a historical debate regarding Bessemer's initial "failure" in the UK until Sidney Gilchrist Thomas developed a basic lining for the converter in 1878 to neutralize phosphorus.
*   **Industrial Synergy:** The affordability of steel catalyzed the Second Industrial Revolution. It allowed for the vertical growth of cities (steel frames) and the expansion of the American West via the Transcontinental Railroad.
*   **Economic Displacement:** The process shifted the center of industrial power toward regions with high-capacity ore deposits and coal, fueling the rise of titans like Andrew Carnegie.

## Did You Know?
*   **Self-Heating:** The Bessemer process is exothermic; the reaction between oxygen and impurities creates so much heat that the iron remains molten without any external fuel source.
*   **The "Steel" Illusion:** Many early "Bessemer" steels were actually inconsistent in quality due to the difficulty of timing the carbon removal, leading some engineers to prefer the slower Open-Hearth process for critical structures.
*   **Global Impact:** Before Bessemer, steel cost roughly £40 per ton; after the process was perfected, the price plummeted to around £6–£7 per ton.
