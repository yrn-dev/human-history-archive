# Fenchurch Street Station and Liverpool Street Station

Fenchurch Street station and Liverpool Street station are railway terminals located in London. They serve as key transit points for travel into the county of Essex.

## Regional Connectivity and Railway Lines
The stations provide access to various districts in Essex. Fenchurch Street station and Liverpool Street station are located 8 miles west of Barking, a market town in the Romford parliamentary division of Essex. Barking is served by the London, Tilbury & Southend railway as well as the Great Eastern railway.

Additionally, Liverpool Street station is the point of origin for travel to Loughton, an urban district in the Epping parliamentary division of Essex. Loughton is situated 11½ miles north-north-east of the station via the Great Eastern railway.

## Associated Destinations
The railway connections from these stations facilitate travel to several notable locations:

*   **Barking:** A town on the river Roding near its junction with the Thames. It is known for its ancient market-house, Victoria Gardens, and the church of St Margaret. Historically, it was celebrated for a nunery founded around 670 by Erkenwald, the bishop of London, and later restored in 970 by King Edgar. The town is also a site of industrial activity, including rubber works, chemical works, and the Gaslight and Coke Company's works at Beckton, as well as sewage works at the mouth of the Roding.
*   **Loughton:** A residential district frequented by holiday-makers from London due to its proximity to the woodland scenery of Epping Forest. It is located near the river Roding and contains several modern churches and large earthworks of probable British origin known as Loughton Camp.
