# Communications

The infrastructure of transportation and communications across various regions is characterized by an extensive network of railways, steamer services, canals, and supplementary road transport.

## Railway Systems in Scotland

The Glasgow & South-Western railway is a dominant operator in several areas. In one shire, it owns most of the lines, serving all ports, seaside resorts, and industrial towns. Its main line via Kilmarnock connects to Carlisle and Dumfries, providing a link to England, while its trunk line via Girvan to Stranraer offers the shortest sea passage to the north of Ireland and Belfast. This railway also serves specific locations such as Kilmalcolm, Bridge of Weir, and Kilbirnie. In the coastal town of West Kilbride, the Glasgow & South-Western railway provides a connection to Glasgow, located 35¾ miles away.

Other Scottish railway companies operate in these regions:
* **Caledonian Railway:** This system serves a part of the county via the Lanarkshire & Ayrshire branch and also provides stations in Kilbirnie. In other areas, it runs through Annandale, with a cross-country line from Lockerbie to Dumfries, a small branch from Beattock to Moffat, and a line from Kirtlebridge that crosses the Solway to Bowness. It also provides access from Glasgow to Balloch.
* **North British Railway:** This company serves populous districts and operates a short line from Riddings Junction in Cumberland to Langholm, providing access to Edinburgh via the Waverley route and to Carlisle.
* **Highland Railway:** This line runs through diversified scenery from Inverarnan to Helensburgh.
* **Portpatrick and Wigtownshire Railway:** This line begins at Castle Douglas and leaves the county at Newton Stewart.

In the region around Dumfries, communication extends westwards to Portpatrick, Stranraer, Newton Stewart, Kirkcudbright, and Castle Douglas.

## Maritime and Water Transport

Steamer services are vital for coastal and island connectivity. Ardrossan serves as the principal port for passenger steamer traffic, with services to Belfast, Arran, and, during the season, Douglas in the Isle of Man. Millport on Great Cumbrae is accessible via steamer from Fairlie. In other districts, regular steamer communication exists between Glasgow and coastal towns and villages, with steamers calling at various points on Loch Lomond between Ardlui and Balloch.

In Ireland, the city of Cork is a major seaport at the head of Cork Harbour on the river Lee. The City of Cork Steam Packet Company primarily controls passenger steamers from Penrose Quay on the North Channel to ports including Southampton, London, Plymouth, Liverpool, Glasgow, and Fishguard.

Canal systems also facilitate transport. The Forth and Clyde Canal, opened in 1775, connects with the Clyde at Bowling. In other regions, the Grand Junction Canal reaches the Ouse valley via the Ouzel valley, with branches extending to Buckingham and Aylesbury.

## English Railway and Road Networks

In the south and midlands of England, several major companies operate:
* **Great Western Railway:** This company's main line terminates at Penzance and passes through the county, with branch lines serving Helston, Penryn, Fowey, Falmouth, Newquay, St Ives, Wadebridge, and Bodmin. In other areas, it jointly owns a line with the Great Central company passing through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield. It also serves Bridport, Weymouth, and Dorchester.
* **London & South-Western Railway:** This system serves the north-eastern parts of certain counties, including Bude, Wadebridge, and Launceston, as well as Gillingham and Sherborne. Its branches serve Portland, Weymouth, Dorchester, Swanage, Poole, and Wimborne.
* **London & North-Western Railway:** The main line crosses the north-east of the county, with Bletchley serving as an important junction for branches to Cambridge, Bedford, Fenny Stratford, Banbury, and Oxford.
* **Great Central Railway:** This line continues north-westward from Quainton Road, with a light railway connecting to the village of Brill.
* **Other Lines:** The Somerset & Dorset line (Midland and South Western joint) follows the Stour valley through Wimborne and Blandford. A light railway also connects Axminster to Lyme Regis.

## Supplementary Transport and Local Access

Where railways do not reach, other forms of transport are employed. In some districts, the Great Western company provides motor-car services, such as between Helston and the Lizard and in the vicinity of Penzance.

Coach services are utilized in various regions:
* Between Dalbeattie and Dumfries, and from Dumfries to New Abbey.
* From Eskdalemuir to Langholm.
* Between Carsphairn and New Galloway, and from Dalbeattie to Auchencairn.
