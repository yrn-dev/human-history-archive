# The Early Literary and Professional Career of Honoré de Balzac

## The Garret Period and Early Rebellion
In 1820, Honoré de Balzac experienced a period of significant familial conflict regarding his professional future. His father had chosen the law as a profession for him, a path Balzac followed by attending the necessary schools, obtaining his *licence*, and gaining three years of practical experience working in the offices of a solicitor (M. Guillonnet-Merville) and a notary. However, following financial losses suffered by his father in 1820, the elder Balzac desired that his son become a practicing lawyer. Honoré revolted against this expectation. In an attempt to force his submission through starvation, his family established him in a garret with a very small allowance after they had left Paris.

During this "garret-period," which lasted from 1820 to 1822, Balzac turned to writing. He composed tragedies and maintained a correspondence with his sister, Laure. He completed a tragedy titled *Cromwell*, which was read to friends; while the manuscript remains in the possession of the bibliographer M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul, it has never been published. He also made his first attempts at prose fiction with the novels *Stella* and *Cocqsigrue*, both of which are described as abortions.

## The Oeuvres de Jeunesse
The initial garret-period was succeeded by another of equal length spent at home. Before this second period concluded in 1821, Balzac began to find his way into print. These early productions, later grouped under the title *Oeuvres de jeunesse*, are considered the *incunabula* of his work. The first of these were *Charles Pointel, ou Mon Cousin de la main gauche* and *Les Deux Hector, ou Les Deux Families bretonnes*.

In the following year, he produced six additional works:
* *Le Vicaire des Ardennes*
* *Le Tartare, ou Le Retour de l'exile*
* *L'Heritiere de Birague*
* *Jean Louis, ou La Fille trouvee*
* *Clotilde de Lusignan, ou Le Beau Juif*
* *Le Centenaire, ou Les Deux Beringheld*

This was followed in 1823 by three more titles: *Michel et Christine et la suite*, *L'Anonyme, ou Ni pere ni mere*, and *La Derniere Fee, ou La Nouvelle Lampe merveilleuse*. In 1824, he wrote *Annette et le criminel* as a continuation of *Le Vicaire*, and in 1825, he produced *Wann-Chlore* (later retitled *Jane la pale*).

These early novels originally filled approximately two score volumes. Balzac published them using various pseudonyms, including "Horace de Saint Aubin" and "Lord R'hoone" (an anagram of "Honore"), and collaborated with two or three other writers. While these works are noted for lacking the quality of his later writing—with very few parts deserving of praise—they are considered essential for those seeking to apprehend the true Balzac.

## Transition to Business and Debt
Balzac eventually ceased the production of these early novels. This hiatus was not caused by a conviction that the works were bad—though he held no serious delusions regarding their quality—nor was it due to a lack of financial success. Instead, he turned his attention to business ventures. He engaged in the roles of printer, type-founder, and, to some extent, a publisher. These activities proved to be the most prolonged and disastrous of his business dabblings, resulting in a general failure that burdened him with a load of debt.
