# The Heavy Brigade at Balaklava

The Heavy Brigade was a cavalry unit involved in the action of Balaklava on October 25, 1854, during the Crimean War. The engagement took place in the Crimea, east of Sevastopol, specifically on the Vorontsov ridge and the surrounding valleys.

## Context of the Engagement
The battle was precipitated by the advance of a Russian field army commanded by General Liprandi. Liprandi's objective was to attack the combined Turkish, French, and English forces that were besieging Sevastopol. The Russian corps formed near Traktir Bridge, and on the morning of October 25, its advanced guard moved south to attack the Vorontsov ridge. At the time, the ridge was weakly occupied by Turkish battalions positioned behind slight entrenchments.

Although two British divisions were put into motion once the firing became serious, their orders prevented them from descending into the plain immediately. Consequently, the Turkish forces were forced to resist a Russian assault of greatly superior numbers. While the Turks offered a gallant resistance, the Russians successfully cleared the ridge and captured several guns. Following this, a heavy mass of Russian cavalry crossed the ridge and descended into the Balaklava plain.

## Composition and Position
The British cavalry division, commanded by the Earl of Lucan, was present in the plain during the Russian descent, but Lucan was prevented from engaging the enemy due to the tenor of his orders. The division was split between two brigades:

*   **The Heavy Brigade:** Commanded by Brigadier-General J. Y. Scarlett, this unit was positioned in the Balaklava plain. It consisted of the 1st, 2nd, and 6th Dragoons, as well as the 4th and 5th Dragoon Guards.
*   **The Light Brigade:** Commanded by Lord Cardigan, this unit was located in the valley to the north of the Vorontsov ridge. It comprised the 4th and 13th Light Dragoons (later Hussars), the 8th and 11th Hussars, and the 17th Lancers.

The archive notes that all of these regiments were very weak in terms of their numbers.

## The Charge of the Heavy Brigade
As the Russian cavalry mass moved toward Balaklava, a Turkish battery fired a few shots into the formation. Immediately following this, the Heavy Brigade launched its charge. 

The attack was initially impeded by obstacles of ground. However, as the engagement progressed into a *melee*, the weight of the British troopers gradually broke the enemy's formation. The decisive moment of the action occurred when the 4th Dragoon Guards delivered a charge against the flank of the Russian mass. 

The result was the total collapse of the Russian cavalry, which broke and fled back toward the ridge. The entire famous charge was completed in less than five minutes from its start.
