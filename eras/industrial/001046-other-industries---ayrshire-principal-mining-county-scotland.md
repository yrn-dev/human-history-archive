# Other Industries of Ayrshire

Ayrshire is a south-western county of Scotland, bounded by Renfrewshire to the north, Lanarkshire and Dumfriesshire to the east, Kirkcudbrightshire to the south-east, Wigtownshire to the south, and the Firth of Clyde to the west. The county is a major industrial hub, serving as the principal mining county in Scotland.

## Mining and Extractive Industries
Ayrshire possesses the second largest coalfield in Scotland. The primary coal districts are located in Ayr, Dalmellington, Patna, Maybole, Drongan, Irvine, Coylton, Stevenston, Beith, Kilwinning, Dalry, Kilbirnie, Dreghorn, Kilmarnock, Galston, Hurlford, Muirkirk, Cumnock, and New Cumnock. 

In addition to coal, the county produces a heavy annual output of pig iron, iron ore, and fire-clay. Ironstone is found chiefly in Cumnock, Dreghorn, Kilbirnie, Dalry, Coylton, and Patna, with blast furnaces operating in most of these towns. Other extractive activities include the quarrying of a valuable whetstone, known as the Water-of-Ayr stone, at Bridge of Stair on the Ayr.

## Manufacturing and Engineering
The county hosts a diverse range of leading manufactures. Engineering works are extensive in Kilmarnock and Ayr. The textile industry is widespread, with the following specializations:
* **Cotton and Woollens:** Catrine features bleachfields and cotton factories. Cotton, hosiery, woollens, and other fabrics are produced in Stewarton, Beith, Kilmaurs, Kilbirnie, and Dalry. Kilmarnock and Ayr produce blankets, carpets, and woollens.
* **Specialized Fabrics:** Lace curtain factories are located in Darvel, Newmilns, and Galston. In Kilbirnie, industries include flax-spinning and the manufacture of flannels, wincey, and linen thread.
* **Other Goods:** Boot and shoe factories operate in Ayr and Kilmarnock. Cabinet-making is a considerable industry in Beith, while Dalry produces boxes and cabinets.

Other industrial activities include a trade in chemicals at Irvine and the production of explosives, such as dynamite, in the sandhills near Stevenston. In Dalry, industries include brick-making, coke-making, currying, machine-knitting, and the manufacture of aerated waters. Kilbirnie also maintains rope works and the manufacture of fishing-nets.

## Shipbuilding, Ports, and Fishing
Shipbuilding is conducted at Irvine, Ayr, Troon, and Fairlie, the latter of which is famous for its yachts. The county's leading ports are Troon, Irvine, Girvan, Ayr, and Ardrossan. Ardrossan serves as the principal port for passenger steamer traffic, providing services to Belfast, Arran, and, seasonally, Douglas in the Isle of Man.

Fishing is carried out in various creeks and harbours. These are divided between the fishery districts of Ballantrae and Greenock.

## Agriculture and Dairying
Agriculture in Ayrshire has been significantly improved through the use of guano, other manures, and thorough drainage, the latter of which was extended following Sir Robert Peel's drainage loan. Oats are the primary cereal crop, though barley and wheat are also grown. Potatoes are grown extensively on coast-lands for markets in the north of England and Scotland, while mangolds, carrots, and turnips are widely cultivated.

The county is particularly noted for its dairying. The Ayrshire breed of cows is famous for the excellence and quantity of their milk. While Dunlop cheese was once a well-known product, it was found to be unequal in character and unsaleable in English markets. To remedy this, the Ayrshire Agricultural Association brought a Somerset farmer and his wife in 1855 to teach the Cheddar method. Consequently, first-rate Cheddar cheese is now produced in the county, and the annual cheese show at Kilmarnock is regarded as the most important in Scotland. Additionally, the Ayrshire horse is held in high repute.

## Infrastructure and Education
The Glasgow & South-Western railway owns most of the lines in the shire, serving all seaside resorts, ports, and industrial towns. Its main line via Kilmarnock connects to Carlisle and Dumfries, and thus to England, while its trunk line via Girvan provides the shortest sea passage to the north of Ireland and Belfast. The Caledonian railway company's Lanarkshire & Ayrshire branch also serves part of the county.

Technical and secondary education is supported by county and borough councils, with contributions from the Kilmarnock Dairy School (part of the West of Scotland Agricultural College established in 1899) and the Glasgow and West of Scotland Technical College. Technical classes are available at various local centres, covering subjects such as engineering, mining, plumbing, gardening, agriculture, and science and art.
