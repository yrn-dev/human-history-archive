# RULE V.--VOLUNTARY STRANDING

## Overview and Legal Standing
Rule V addresses the specific conditions under which the voluntary stranding of a ship is classified as a General Average (G.A.) act. This rule is expected to reflect the law in England when the matter is brought before a court for decision.

## Related Acts of Sacrifice
While Rule V focuses on the act of voluntary stranding, Rules VI and VII address the subsequent damage sustained by the ship or its appliances during efforts to force the vessel off the ground after it has stranded. Because these efforts require an abnormal use of the ship's equipment, they are treated as acts of sacrifice. Such operations are likely to cause damage to the boilers, engines, spars, and sails.

The application of these rules is consistent with English law, as demonstrated in the 1895 case of "The Bona" (P. 125). In this instance, the court of appeal determined that both the coal and stores consumed, as well as the damage sustained by the engines while they were worked to get the ship off the ground, were subjects for G.A. contribution under common law.

## Context within General Average Rules
Rule V exists within a broader framework of rules governing maritime sacrifice and compensation:

*   **Jettison of Deck Cargo (Rule I):** No jettison of deck cargo is made good as G.A. Any structure not built into the frame of the vessel is considered part of the deck.
*   **Damage by Jettison and Sacrifice (Rule II):** Damage to cargo or the ship resulting from a sacrifice made for common safety is made good as G.A. This includes damage caused by water entering through openings made for jettison or open hatches.
*   **Extinguishing Fire (Rule III):** Damage caused by water or other means—including scuttling or beaching a burning ship—to extinguish a fire is made good as G.A. However, compensation is not provided for separate packages of cargo, bulk cargo, or portions of the ship that were actually on fire.
*   **Cutting Away Wreck (Rule IV):** Loss or damage resulting from cutting away the remains of spars or other items previously carried away by sea-peril is not made good as G.A.
