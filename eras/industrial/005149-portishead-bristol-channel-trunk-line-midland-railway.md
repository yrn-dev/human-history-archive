# Portishead and the Midland Railway Trunk Line

## Portishead and the Bristol Channel
Portishead is an outport located on the Bristol Channel on the Somerset shore. It features a dock with a total area of 12 acres. The docks at Portishead, along with those at Avonmouth, were constructed between 1877 and 1880. In 1884, these docks at the river entrance were purchased by the city of Bristol.

As part of the broader port system extending from the mouth of the river and the estuary of the Severn to Hanham Mills on the Avon, Portishead contributes to a large trade network with the West Indies, America, and other regions. Principal imports handled by the port include cattle, hides, timber, ore, oils, fruit, grain, and general merchandise. Exports consist of salt, tin, cotton goods, manufactured oils, and machinery. Large steamship companies, including Dominion and Elder Dempster, operate at the port.

## The Midland Railway Trunk Line
A trunk line of the Midland railway serves as a primary connection between Bristol and the north of England. This line runs via Derby, Birmingham, Worcester, and Gloucester. In Bristol, the Midland railway utilizes the central station, Temple Meads, which is also used by the Great Western railway.

The Midland railway's north and west line connects Derby and Bristol. Along this route, the line serves the market town of Bromsgrove in Worcestershire. Bromsgrove's station is located one mile from the town on the Bristol-Birmingham line. Near Bromsgrove, the railway ascends for two miles to surmount the Lickey Hills, a section characterized as one of the steepest gradients in England over such a distance. Additionally, the Midland railway maintains carriage works near Bromsgrove.

## Regional Railway Connectivity
The transport infrastructure of the region is shared between several major companies. While the Midland railway connects Bristol to the north, the Great Western railway connects Bristol to London, the southern and south-western counties of England, and South Wales via the Severn tunnel. The Great Western railway also operates local lines that encircle the city of Bristol to the south and north, providing service to the outports of Portishead and Avonmouth on the Bristol Channel.
