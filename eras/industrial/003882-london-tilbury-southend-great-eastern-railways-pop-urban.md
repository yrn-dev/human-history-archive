# London, Tilbury & Southend and Great Eastern Railways

The London, Tilbury & Southend and Great Eastern railways serve various urban districts and market towns in the region of Essex, England, providing critical transport links to central London stations such as Fenchurch Street and Liverpool Street.

## Barking
Barking is a market town located in the Romford parliamentary division of Essex on the river Roding, near its junction with the Thames. It is situated 8 miles east of Liverpool Street station and Fenchurch Street station in London, and is served by both the Great Eastern and the London, Tilbury & Southend railways. 

The population of the urban district of Barking town was 14,301 in 1891 and grew to 21,547 by 1901. The town is a suffragan bishopric within the diocese of St Albans. Historically, Barking was noted for its nunnery, founded around 670 by Erkenwald, the bishop of London, and later restored in 970 by King Edgar following destruction by the Danes. At the time of the dissolution of the monasteries, the nunnery's revenue was £1084, and the abbess held the rank of baroness *ex officio*. Architectural remnants include a perpendicular turreted gateway and the Norman church of St Margaret, which features perpendicular additions.

The town's economy includes shipping trade, primarily in fish and timber, as well as chemical works. Industrial sites in the parish include extensive rubber works and the Gaslight and Coke Company's works at Beckton. Additionally, great sewage works are located at Barking Creek (the mouth of the Roding), which receive the Northern Outfall sewer from London. Public amenities include recreation grounds and Victoria Gardens, a public pleasure-ground. The town also possesses an ancient market-house that serves as a town-hall.

## Ilford and Loughton
The Great Eastern railway provides access to several other Essex districts:

**Ilford (Great Ilford):** Located 7 miles east-north-east of London on the Roding, this urban district in the Romford parliamentary division saw significant population growth, rising from 10,913 in 1891 to 41,234 in 1901. The area contains paper mills and large photographic material works. Notable landmarks include Claybury Hall, a London County Council lunatic asylum established in 1893, and the 12th-century hospital of St Mary and St Thomas. Originally a leper hospital, the latter now contains a chapel and almshouses; it is owned by the marquess of Salisbury, who serves as "Master" and must maintain six aged inmates and a chaplain. A portion of Hainault Forest is located within the parish. Nearby Little Ilford, a parish on the west side of the Roding, had a population of 17,915 in 1901 and features the church of St Mary, which contains Norman portions and a 1517 monumental brass of a boy in school clothes.

**Loughton:** Situated 11½ miles north-north-east of Liverpool Street station, Loughton is an urban district in the Epping parliamentary division. Its population in 1901 was 4,730. Located near the river Roding on the eastern outskirts of Epping Forest, the village has become a residential center and a destination for holiday-makers from London. The area features several modern churches and large earthworks of likely British origin known as Loughton Camp. Historically, the lordship of the manor was granted to Waltham Abbey.

## Burnham-on-Crouch
Burnham-on-Crouch is an urban district in the southeastern parliamentary division of Essex, located 43 miles east by north from London via a branch of the Great Eastern railway. The population in 1901 was 2,919. The town is a favorite yachting station and is regarded as a watering-place, situated 6 miles from the North Sea. 

The local economy involves boat-building and trade in coal and corn. The Crouch estuary near the town contains extensive oyster beds. Geographically, the Crouch is joined on the south side by the Roch, which creates several low, flat, embanked islands, including Potton, Wallasea, and Foulness. The town's church of St Mary is a notable example of late Perpendicular style, containing a Norman font and Decorated portions.
