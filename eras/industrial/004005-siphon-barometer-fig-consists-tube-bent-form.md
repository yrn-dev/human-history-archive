# Siphon Barometer

A siphon barometer is a specific type of instrument used to measure the weight or pressure of the atmosphere. Unlike the ordinary mercurial or cistern barometer, the siphon barometer dispenses with the use of a separate basin or cistern of mercury. Instead, it utilizes a tube of uniform diameter that is bent round upon itself at its lower end, forming the shape of a siphon.

## Design and Operation
The instrument features a graduated scale that runs along the entire length of the tube. To determine the height of the barometer, the user takes the difference between the readings of the lower and upper limbs. Alternatively, the instrument can be read by using a screw to bring the zero-point of the graduated scale to the level of the surface of the lower limb; this allows the user to read the height directly from the surface of the upper limb.

## Technical Advantages and Limitations
The siphon barometer offers certain technical advantages over the cistern barometer. Specifically, it requires no corrections for errors of capacity or errors of capillarity. In cistern barometers, capillarity causes the mercury level in the tube to sit below the level of the mercury in the vessel, necessitating an addition to the observed height to find the true pressure.

However, the siphon barometer is subject to a different operational challenge. Because the mercury in the lower limb is typically in open contact with the air, it can contract impurities. This accumulation of impurities can seriously interfere with the satisfactory working of the instrument over time.

## Scale Corrections and Materials
The accuracy of the readings may depend on how the graduated scale is attached to the instrument's frame:

*   **Movable Brass Scales:** If a brass scale is attached to a wooden frame but is free to move up and down, corrections for brass scales are used because the zero-point is brought to the level of the lower limb.
*   **Fixed Brass Scales:** If the brass scale is fixed to a wooden frame, brass scale corrections only apply if the zero of the scale is fixed at or near the zero line of the column and is free to expand upwards.
*   **Two-Reading Observations:** In cases where an observation is made using two separate readings on the scale, the scale must be free to expand in one direction.
*   **Partial Fixation:** If only a portion of the scale (for example, the section from 27 to 31 inches) is screwed to a wooden frame, corrections for wooden scales must be used instead of those for brass.

Regardless of the scale material, no correction is required for the expansion of the glass tube containing the mercury.

## Context within Barometric Instruments
The siphon barometer is one of several methods for measuring atmospheric pressure. Other instruments include:
*   **Cistern Barometer:** A tube placed with its open end in a basin of mercury.
*   **Aneroid Barometer:** A liquid-free instrument that relies on volume changes in an exhausted metallic chamber.
*   **Diagonal Barometer:** Suggested by Sir Samuel Morland and Bernardo Ramazzini, this version inclines the upper tube to the lower part to increase readings.
*   **Wheel and Steel-yard Barometers:** Developed by Dr. R. Hooke, these use a float on the mercury surface to magnify oscillations.
*   **Pendant Barometer:** Invented by G. Amontons in 1695, this consists of a vertical funnel-shaped tube closed at the top.
*   **Baroscopes and Barographs:** Baroscopes indicate pressure variations without quantitative data, while barographs automatically record these variations.
