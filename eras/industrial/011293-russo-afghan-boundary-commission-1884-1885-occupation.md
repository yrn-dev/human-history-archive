# Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1885

The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1885 (also cited as 1884-1886) was a diplomatic and scientific endeavor established to determine a definite boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and the trans-Caspian conquests of Russia. This necessity arose following the Russian advance toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts.

## Boundary Demarcation and Geography
The commission fixed a boundary between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). This line is described as being almost entirely artificial in its construction. The boundary leaves the Hari Rud at Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs. The most southerly point of this demarcation occurs where the boundary crosses the Kushk, roughly 60 miles north of Herat.

At Khamiab, the boundary joins the Oxus, which is situated about 150 miles above the crossing-point of the Russian Trans-Caspian railway at Charjui. From this junction, the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia, and continues to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## Scientific Mapping and Exploration
The work of the commission represented a significant extension of scientific geographical mapping on the north-western frontiers of India. The mapping of the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan was carried out by Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich. 

The commission's activities facilitated further geographical discoveries:
* **The Pamirs:** Ney Elias crossed from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs to reach the commission's camp, during which he identified Rangkul, known as the "Dragon Lake."
* **Badghis:** The commission surveyed the district of Badghis, located on the north-west of Afghanistan between the Hari Rud and Murghab rivers. This area includes the Chul formations through which the boundary runs. Following the 1885 survey, the amir largely settled this region with purely Afghan tribes.
* **Bamian:** In November 1885, the Hon. M. G. Talbot and Major Pelham J. Maitland carefully surveyed the surrounding country and fixed the height of the valley of Bamian at approximately 8,500 feet.
* **Geological Research:** L. Griesbach conducted researches in connection with the commission, reporting the formation of a longitudinal ridge (an anticlinal) through the great Balkh plain of Afghan Turkestan. This ridge prevents the northern affluents of the basin from joining the river.

## Broader Strategic Context
The efforts of the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission were part of a wider series of geographical labors that produced maps extending from the Euphrates to the Indus and from the Indus to the Oxus. These efforts were complemented by other regional activities, including the Afghan war of 1878-80, the occupation of Chitral and Gilgit, and the systematic survey of Baluchistan starting in 1880.

While the 1884-1886 commission set the northern limits from the Hari Rud to the Sarikol mountains, subsequent demarcations were required. The Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 later defined the line from the Great Pamir to the glaciers overlooking the Chinese border.
