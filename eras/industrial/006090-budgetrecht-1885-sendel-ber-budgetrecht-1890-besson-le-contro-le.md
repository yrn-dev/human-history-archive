# Budgetrecht and Related Financial Studies

The study of budget law and public finance involves the analysis of national, federal, and local expenditures and taxations. In larger countries, local and municipal taxations and expenditures exist, though they are generally of less account than national finances. In federal systems—specifically the Argentine republic, the German empire, and the United States—a comprehensive knowledge of finances requires the consultation of both the federal budgets and the budgets of the individual states within the federation.

## Literature on Budget Law and Public Finance

Several authorities have produced comparative studies and detailed analyses of budget mechanisms and controls. Stourm's *Le Budget, son histoire et son mécanisme* (1889) is noted as the best book on the subject, providing a comparative study of budgets across different countries. Other significant works in the field include:

*   Siedler, *Budget und Budgetrecht* (1885)
*   Sendel, *Über Budgetrecht* (1890)
*   Besson, *Le Contrôle des budgets en France et à l'étranger* (1899)
*   Bastable, *Public Finance* (3rd ed., 1903)
*   Eugene E. Agger, *The Budget in American Commonwealths* (1907)

## Etymology and Usage of "Budget"

The term "budget" originates from a word applied to a small coffer or a leather-covered case. Cotgrave translates the term *bougette* as a "little coffer or trunk" that was covered with leather. Over time, it became a common term for a despatch box used to store official papers. This etymology is reflected in the parliamentary tradition of the chancellor of the exchequer, who was described as "opening his budget" during the delivery of the annual statement.
