# Henri d'Orleans

Prince Henri d'Orleans was a French explorer who participated in the geographical investigation of Central Asia during the late 19th century. He is noted for his expeditions into the Tibetan region, contributing to the broader body of research concerning the Tibetan plateau.

## Tibetan Expedition
Between 1886 and 1887, Prince Henri d'Orleans undertook a journey across the Tibetan plateau. He was accompanied on this expedition by Pierre G. Bonvalot. The pair successfully crossed the plateau from north to south; however, they failed in their attempt to enter the city of Lhasa.

## Contribution to Asiatic Research
The explorations conducted by Prince Henri d'Orleans and Bonvalot are cited as part of the wider effort to define the character of the Central Asian mountain systems. Their work, alongside that of other French explorers such as J. L. Dutreuil de Rhins and F. Grenard, provided data that necessitated the revision of earlier geographical theories. 

Specifically, their investigations helped refine the conceptions of the Kuen-lun system. Previous geographers, such as K. Ritter and Baron von Richthofen, had attempted to define the Kuen-lun as a border range of the Tibetan plateau or as a complex arrangement of parallel ranges running from the Pamirs eastward. The findings of d'Orleans and his contemporaries, as well as those of Russian, British, American, Hungarian, and Swedish explorers (most notably Sven Hedin), provided the evidence required to recast these earlier descriptions.
