# Events and Figures of 1843–1847

The period between 1843 and 1847 was marked by significant developments in military engineering, naval exploration, academic appointments, and literary production across Europe, the Americas, and Asia.

## Military and Naval Developments
In Belgium, Henri Alexis Brialmont, a military engineer and son of General Laurent Mathieu Brialmont, entered the army as a sub-lieutenant of engineers in 1843. He rose to the rank of lieutenant in 1847, and from that year until 1850, he served as the private secretary to the war minister, General Baron Chazal.

Within the British Royal Navy, several officers saw active service and promotion during this window. Sir Francis Leopold M'Clintock passed his examination for lieutenancy in 1843, subsequently joining the steamship "Gorgon" under Captain Charles Hotham. Until 1847, M'Clintock's service was primarily focused on the American coasts. Similarly, Sir Edward Belcher, who had been knighted in 1843, was engaged in surveying work in the Philippines and the East Indies on board the "Samarang" from 1843 until 1847.

## Academic and Scientific Advancements
The mid-1840s saw notable shifts in university leadership and scientific nomenclature. In Ireland, James MacCullagh, a physicist and mathematician, held the chair of mathematics at Trinity College, Dublin, from 1832 until 1843, at which point he was transferred to the chair of natural philosophy.

In the United States, the university of Louisville established its law department in 1847. In South America, Andres Bello played a prominent role in founding the national university in Chile in 1843, later serving as its rector.

In the field of mineralogy, the rock-forming mineral biotite was named in 1847 by J.F.L. Hausmann. The name was chosen to honor J.B. Biot, a French physicist who had discovered in 1816 that magnesia-micas were nearly or optically uniaxial. In the biological sciences, 1847 saw reported discoveries of another species of the genus *Apteryx*, which later led to the identification of *Apteryx oweni* by John Gould.

## Literature and Scholarship
Various scholars and writers published influential works during this period. In Hungary, Joseph Bajza published the six-volume *Historical Library* (*Tortereti Konyvtar*) between 1843 and 1845, followed by the *Modern Plutarch* (*Uj Plutarch*) from 1845 to 1847, and the *Universal History* (*Vilagtoretet*) in 1847. Bajza also edited the opposition journal *Ellenoer* in Leipzig in 1847.

In South America, Andres Bello produced his *Gramatica castellana* in 1847, which became a leading authority on the subject. In France, Edouard Constant Biot published an essay on the history of public instruction and the corporation of letters in China in 1847.

## Media and Other Records
In the United States, the newspaper *Courier* was established in 1843 by Walter N. Haldeman. In France, bibliographic records and historical notices regarding Daunou were published in 1843 (by Mignet) and 1847 (by Taillandier).
