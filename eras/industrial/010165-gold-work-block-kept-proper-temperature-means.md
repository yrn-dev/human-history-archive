# Gold Blocking in Book-Cover Design

## The Blocking Process
The machines used for blocking designs onto book-covers have remained practically the same for many years, though some small improvements have been made regarding the heating of blocks for gold work and the inking of rollers for colour work. Due to the size of many blocks, the blocking press is described as a large and cumbersome machine. 

The operation involves fixing the block firmly into a strong metal bed. A movable table is positioned in front of the block, equipped with gauges to ensure the book cover is kept exactly in its correct place.

## Gold Work Techniques
For gold work, the block is kept at the proper temperature by means of gas jets. The process for applying gold is as follows:
1. The cover is properly overlaid with gold leaf.
2. The cover is passed on its table directly under the block.
3. The cover is pressed steadily upwards against the block.
4. The cover is lowered and drawn out.
5. Any superfluous gold is rubbed off.

The gold is fastened to the leather or cloth by the solidification of egg albumen, which occurs via heat.

## Temperature and Pressure Requirements
Great care must be exercised regarding the heat of the block during gold blocking. If the block is too hot, the gold will adhere in areas where it is not wanted; conversely, if the block is too cool, the gold will fail to adhere where it is required. 

In addition to temperature control, great nicety is required regarding the exact amount of pressure applied and the precise number of moments the block remains in contact with the gold.

## Comparison with Colour Blocking
The process for colour blocks follows the same general procedure as gold blocking, with two primary differences:
* **Heating:** Unlike gold blocks, colour blocks do not need to be heated.
* **Inking:** Colour blocks are inked by means of a roller for each impression.

Because a separate printing is required for each individual colour, these operations require significant care from the operator. The operator must carefully watch the working of each pull and make immediate readjustments if necessary, making it difficult to estimate the rate at which these printings can be completed.

## Manufacturing Origins
Blocking presses are primarily of German make, although English and Scottish presses are also used extensively.
