# John Bannister

## Early Life and Career
John Bannister was born on May 12, 1760, in Deptford. Although he initially pursued studies to become a painter, he eventually transitioned to the theatrical stage. His first formal appearance as an actor took place in 1778 at the Haymarket, where he performed the role of Dick in the production *The Apprentice*.

## Performances at Drury Lane
In the same year as his debut (1778), Bannister appeared at Drury Lane in a version of Voltaire's *Mahomet* adapted by James Miller. In this production, he played the part of Zaphna, a role he had studied under the guidance of Garrick. The cast of the production also included Mrs. Robinson, who played the part of Palmira ("Perdita").

## Professional Success and Management
Bannister earned a reputation as the best low comedian of his era. His success extended beyond acting into theatrical administration; in 1802, he became the manager of Drury Lane, a position in which he was considered no less successful than he had been as a performer.

## Personal Interests and Later Life
Despite his theatrical career, Bannister maintained a lifelong passion for painting. His circle of friends included artists such as Rowlandson, Morland, and Gainsborough. He retired from his professional activities in 1815 and died on November 7, 1836. Further details of his life are recorded in the two-volume *Memoirs of John Bannister* written by Adolphus in 1838.
