# BROCHANT DE VILLIERS, ANDRÉ JEAN FRANÇOIS MARIE (1772-1840)

André Jean François Marie Brochant de Villiers was a French geologist and mineralogist. Born on August 6, 1772, at Villiers, near Nantes, he pursued his early education at the École Polytechnique. In 1794, he became the first pupil to be admitted to the École des Mines.

## Academic and Professional Career
Brochant de Villiers' professional ascent within the École des Mines began in 1804 when he was appointed as the professor of mineralogy and geology. During this period, the school had been temporarily relocated to Pezay in Savoy; he eventually returned to Paris with the institution in 1815. His career further progressed as he became a member of the Academy of Sciences and served as the inspector general of mines.

## Scientific Contributions and Research
Brochant de Villiers conducted extensive geological and mineralogical investigations. His research included the study of the transition strata of the Tarantaise and the position of granite rocks located at Mont Blanc. Additionally, he wrote on the lead minerals found in Cumberland and Derbyshire.

He also played a significant role in the mapping of France's geology. He was charged with the superintendence of the construction of the geological map of France, a project undertaken by his pupils, Elie de Beaumont and Dufrénoy.

## Publications and Legacy
Brochant de Villiers authored several technical works, including:
*   *Traité élémentaire de minéralogie* (published in two volumes between 1801 and 1802, with a second edition released in 1808).
*   *Traité abrégé de cristallographie* (published in Paris, 1818).

His contributions to the field are further commemorated through the mineral species brochantite. A basic copper sulphate that crystallizes in the orthorhombic system, brochantite was first discovered in the copper mines of the Urals. It was named in 1824 by A. Lévy in honor of A.J.M. Brochant de Villiers.

Brochant de Villiers died in Paris on May 16, 1840.
