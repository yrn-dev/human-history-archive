# Chloroazoimide

## Overview and Chemical Nature
Chloroazoimide, represented by the chemical formula $\text{Cl.N}_3$, is the chloride corresponding to azoimide. It is characterized as a colourless gas that is highly explosive.

## Synthesis
The substance was obtained by F. Raschig in 1908. The preparation process involves acidifying a mixture of hypochlorite and sodium azide. The acidification is achieved using either boric acid or acetic acid.

### Azoimide
Also known as hydrazoic acid ($\text{N}_3\text{H}$), azoimide is a compound of hydrogen and nitrogen first isolated in 1890 by Th. Curtius. It can be prepared through several methods:
* **From dinitraniline**: E. Noelting and E. Grandmougin obtained it by diazotization and conversion into a perbromide, followed by decomposition with ammonia to form dinitrophenylhydrazoate. Hydrolysis with alcoholic potash then produces dinitrophenol and potassium hydrazoate (azide), which is acidified and distilled to yield azoimide.
* **From diazobenzene imide**: It can be prepared from the para-nitro or ortho-nitro compounds. The para-nitro compound is added to a cold solution of caustic potash in absolute alcohol, warmed for two days, acidified with sulphuric acid, and distilled. This method yields approximately 40% of the theoretical amount. The ortho-nitro compound yields approximately 30%.

### Azoimide Salts and Derivatives
* **Ammonium azoimide ($\text{N}_3\text{.NH}_4$)**: This salt is prepared by boiling diazohippuramide with alcoholic ammonia. The resulting solution is allowed to stand for twelve hours, decanted from precipitated hippuramide, and then treated with ether to precipitate the ammonium salt. It is feebly alkaline, readily soluble in water, and extremely explosive.
* **Sodium salt**: W. Wislicenus prepared the sodium salt by passing sodamide over nitrous oxide at high temperatures.
* **Hydrazine azoimide**: This compound is known as $\text{N}_5\text{H}_5$.

## Other Preparations of the Acid
The acid associated with these salts can be produced via the action of nitrous acid on hydrazine sulphate, or through the oxidation of hydrazine using ammonium metavanadate or a combination of sulphuric acid and hydrogen peroxide.
