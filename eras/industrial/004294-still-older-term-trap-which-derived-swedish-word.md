# Trap (Geological Term)

The term "trap" is a geological designation used to describe certain suites of basaltic rocks. It is an older term than several other classifications used by geologists to describe similar rock formations.

## Etymology and Physical Characteristics
The word "trap" is derived from a Swedish word meaning "a stair." This etymology refers to the physical appearance of the rocks in many locations, where superposed sheets of basalt weather into terraced or step-like features.

## Usage in Geology
Many geologists and travellers continue to use "trap" as a general term for entire suites of basaltic rocks. Examples of this usage include the "traps" of the Deccan and the identification of trap-dikes. 

In the early 19th century, the "floetz-traps" (older basalts) were the subject of a significant controversy regarding their origin. The Saxon mineralogist Werner and his school argued that these rocks were of aqueous origin, claiming they were chemical precipitates deposited in primeval seas. Conversely, Hutton and various French geologists maintained that they were volcanic rocks emitted by craters that are now extinct.

## Relationship to Other Basaltic Rocks
The term "trap" is older than other names used by continental geologists to describe pre-Tertiary basalts. For a period, it was widely believed that pre-Tertiary basalts were a distinct class from recent or Tertiary representatives. Consequently, writers used terms such as melaphyre, diabase porphyrite, anamesite, or *diabas-mandel-stein* to indicate states of dolerites and basalts that were more or less altered. However, these are no longer considered to differ in any essential respects from better-preserved basalts.

British petrologists generally use the term "dolerites" to describe basaltic rocks in which the whole mass is thoroughly crystallized and no glassy material is present. In contrast, vitreous basalts may contain a dark brown, almost opaque glassy material containing skeleton crystals of felspar or augite, spherulites, steam vesicles, perlitic cracks, or black grains of magnetite.

## Regional Examples
Specific geological formations identified as traps include:
* **The Rajmahal traps:** Located in Bengal.
* **The Sylhet traps:** Located near the southern margin of the Assam hills; these are correlated with the Rajmahal traps of Bengal.
