# Central, Metropolitan and Great Western Railways

The Great Central, Metropolitan, and Great Western railways are rail systems that operate within England, notably sharing infrastructure and providing critical transport links for market towns and the metropolis of London.

## Operations in Aylesbury and Buckinghamshire
In the market town of Aylesbury, located in the Aylesbury parliamentary division of Buckinghamshire, the Great Central, Metropolitan, and Great Western railways all utilize a common station. Aylesbury is further served by a branch of the London & North-Western railway.

Within the county, the Metropolitan-Great Central joint line provides service to Aylesbury, Amersham, and Chesham (via a branch). This joint line connects with the North-Western Oxford branch at Verney Junction. The Great Central railway utilizes this line, with its main line continuing in a north-westward direction from Quainton Road. Additionally, a light railway connects Quainton Road station to the village of Brill to the south-west.

The Great Central and Great Western companies also jointly own a line that passes through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield. This line connects northward to the Great Central system. Prior to the opening of this joint line in 1906, the only rail service for Prince's Risborough and High Wycombe was the Great Western branch running from Maidenhead to Oxford. From Prince's Risborough, there are further branches extending to Aylesbury and Watlington.

## Great Western Railway and the London Milk Supply
The Great Western railway plays a significant role in the logistics of the London metropolis, particularly regarding the delivery of perishable goods. At the beginning of the 20th century, the facilities and favorable rates provided by railway companies allowed milk produced in favorable conditions at considerable distances to be delivered to London retailers at a lower price than milk produced within the metropolis itself. This contributed to a decline in the number of town "cowkeepers."

The scale of this operation is evidenced by the volume of transport; the annual quantity of milk delivered to London on the Great Western line amounts to approximately 11,000,000 gallons.

## Regional Extensions and Connections
The Great Western system maintains various regional connections across England and Wales:
* **Cheshire and Liverpool:** The Great Western has connections on the Cheshire side with the North-Western, Wirral, and various local lines. This system connects to the low-level at Central Station in Liverpool.
* **Wales:** In the Ceiriog valley, the Great Western and Great Central railways are served via a line from Rhosllanerchrhugog to Glyn Ceiriog.
* **Other Areas:** The main line of the Great Western company crosses the extreme south of Buckinghamshire, passing through Taplow and Slough.

The Great Central company has also expanded its reach through acquisitions, such as the 1905 takeover of the Wrexham, Mold & Connah's Quay railway, which facilitated the transport of mineral wealth from North Wales and Flint into the Birkenhead docks.
