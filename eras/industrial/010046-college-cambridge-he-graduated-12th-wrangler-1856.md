# Thomas George Bonney

## Early Life and Education
Thomas George Bonney was an English geologist born on July 27, 1833, in Rugeley. He was the eldest son of the Reverend Thomas Bonney, who served as the master of the grammar school in that town. Bonney received his education at Uppingham and St John's College, Cambridge. He graduated from Cambridge in 1856, achieving the rank of 12th wrangler.

## Early Career and Ordination
Following his graduation, Bonney was ordained in 1857. Between 1856 and 1861, he served as the mathematical master at Westminster school. During this period, he pursued the study of geology primarily as a recreation, focusing his efforts mainly on Alpine regions.

## Academic and Professional Appointments
Bonney's professional focus shifted toward geology in 1868 when he was appointed as a lecturer in geology and a tutor at St John's College. In 1877, he was chosen to be the professor of geology at University College, London. His ecclesiastical contributions included an appointment as an honorary canon of Manchester in 1887.

## Scientific Research and Specializations
Bonney's scientific attention was specifically directed toward metamorphic and igneous rocks. He conducted studies in various locations, including:
*   Alpine regions
*   The Scottish Highlands
*   Wales
*   Various parts of England, specifically at Salcombe, the Lizard, and Charnwood Forest.

## Leadership and Society Memberships
Throughout his career, Bonney held several prominent leadership positions within scientific and mountaineering organizations. He served as the president of the Alpine Club and the Mineralogical Society. Additionally, he held the roles of secretary and later president of the Geological Society from 1884 to 1886. He also served as the secretary of the British Association between 1881 and 1885.

## Published Works
Bonney authored several purely scientific texts, including:
*   *Cambridgeshire Geology* (1875)
*   *The Story of our Planet* (1893)
*   *Charles Lyell and Modern Geology* (1895)
*   *Ice Work, Past and Present* (1896)
*   *Volcanoes* (1899)

Beyond these volumes, he published numerous papers in the *Geological Magazine* and the *Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society*. He also wrote popular works concerning English and Welsh scenery and Alpine regions, as well as writings on theological subjects.
