# Westminster Review

The *Westminster Review* was a publication that served as a venue for a wide array of intellectual, philosophical, and historical contributions during the 19th century. Its pages featured the work of prominent thinkers, including philosophers, historians, and scientists, often intersecting with the circles of "philosophic radicals" and independent thinkers.

## Philosophical and Scientific Contributions
The review was a significant outlet for the work of Alexander Bain, a Scottish philosopher and educationalist. Bain began contributing to the *Westminster Review* toward the end of his arts course at Marischal College, with his first article, "Electrotype and Daguerreotype," appearing in September 1840. This professional connection marked the beginning of a lifelong friendship between Bain and John Stuart Mill. Bain continued to write for the *Westminster* while serving as a substitute professor of moral philosophy for Dr. Glennie for three successive sessions starting in 1841.

The publication also served as a platform for critical philosophical debate. In 1842, Samuel Bailey published a review of *Berkeley's Theory of Vision*. This work prompted a rejoinder from J. S. Mill, which was published in the *Westminster Review* and subsequently reprinted in *Dissertations*.

## Historical and Academic Articles
The *Westminster Review* published scholarly articles on a variety of historical and classical subjects. George Grote utilized the publication to explore historical positions; specifically, in May 1843, Grote published an article on Niebuhr’s Greek Legends, the contents of which coincided with later chapters in his broader historical work. Additionally, the review contained articles regarding the life and doctrines of Roger Bacon, specifically in volume lxxxi, issues 1 and 512.
