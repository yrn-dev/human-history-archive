## Cultural Debates and Musical Practices
At the beginning of the 19th century, a specific debate arose regarding the use of barrel-organs (*Drehorgeln*) in country churches. This practice was seriously recommended by an anonymous writer in two German publications: *Markische Boten* (specifically Nos. 138 and 139 in 1821) and *Beobachter an der Spree* (in an issue dated 22nd October 1821).

This recommendation was met with opposition from the musical community. Wilke, an organist from Leipzig, published a response in the *Allgem. musik. Zeitung* in 1822, in which he repudiated the suggestion to use barrel-organs in churches, characterizing the recommendation as "laughable."

## Geography of the Spree
The Spree river serves as a central geographic feature for several towns in the Prussian province of Brandenburg. 

*   **Köpenick (Cöpenick):** Located on an island in the Spree, this town is situated 9 miles south-east of Berlin. It is known for its royal residence, built on the site of a palace belonging to the great elector Frederick William, and features a chapel, a banqueting hall, and gardens. Its industries include the production of ink, sealing-wax, linoleum, and glass.
*   **Fürstenwalde:** Located on the right bank of the Spree, this town is 28 miles east of Berlin. One of the oldest towns in Brandenburg, it served as the seat of the bishop of Lebus from 1385 until 1595, when the bishopric was incorporated with the electorate of Brunswick. Its industries include wool-weaving, iron-founding, and the manufacture of gas-fittings, electric lamps, vinegar, and starch.
*   **Lübben:** Situated on the Spree 47 miles south-south-east of Berlin, Lübben is the primary town of the Spreewald. It is recognized for its small pickling cucumbers (*gurken*) and industries such as paper, shoes, hosiery, and saw-mills. The parish church in Lübben is the burial site of the poet Paul Gerhardt (1607-1676), who served as pastor there.
