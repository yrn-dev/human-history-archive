# The Fleshly School of Poetry

"The Fleshly School of Poetry" was an article published in the October 1871 issue of the *Contemporary Review*. It was written by the British poet, novelist, and dramatist Robert Williams Buchanan (1841–1901), who contributed the piece under the pseudonym Thomas Maitland.

## Authorship and Publication
Robert Williams Buchanan was born on August 18, 1841, in Caverswall, Staffordshire. The son of Robert Buchanan, an Owenite journalist and lecturer, he was educated at the university and high school in Glasgow. Before the publication of "The Fleshly School of Poetry," Buchanan had established himself as a poet with works such as *Undertones* (1863), *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). He also wrote a study in mysticism titled *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic* in 1870.

The article in the *Contemporary Review* brought Buchanan notoriety. In 1872, he expanded the contents of the article into a pamphlet.

## Critical Reception and Controversy
The article sparked significant responses from the poets it targeted. D.G. Rossetti replied to the attack in a letter to the *Athenaeum* dated December 16, 1871, which he titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism." Additionally, Mr. Swinburne responded in a 1872 work titled *Under the Microscope*.

In later years, Buchanan expressed regret regarding the violence of his attack. He eventually withdrew from the criticisms contained within the original piece. The tension between the two men persisted, as Buchanan dedicated his work *God and the Man* to Rossetti, whom he referred to as the "old enemy."

## Later Career of Robert Williams Buchanan
Following the controversy of the "Fleshly School" article, Buchanan continued his literary pursuits in other genres. In 1876, he published *The Shadow of the Sword*, which was the first in a long series of novels and is considered one of his best. He also became a successful playwright, authoring several plays including *Lady Clare* (1883), *A Man's Shadow* (1890), *The Charlatan* (1894), and *Sophia* (1886), the latter of which was an adaptation of *Tom Jones*.
