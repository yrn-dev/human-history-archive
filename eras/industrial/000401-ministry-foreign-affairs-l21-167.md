# Ministry of Foreign Affairs

In the context of the Austrian-Hungarian Monarchy, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs is one of three joint ministries established to manage common affairs, alongside the ministries of war and finance. The authority of the joint ministers is strictly limited to these common affairs; they are prohibited from exercising influence or directing government affairs that affect only one half of the monarchy.

## Role and Authority
The minister of foreign affairs is responsible for conducting the international relations of the Dual Monarchy and possesses the authority to conclude international treaties. However, certain types of agreements require the parliamentary assent of both states, specifically commercial treaties and state treaties that involve a change of territory or impose burdens on the state or its parts.

Additionally, the minister of foreign affairs always holds the office of the ministry of the imperial house. In the Dual Monarchy, the diplomatic service was the first branch of public service to adopt the terminology "Imperial and Royal" (*Kaiserlich und Koeniglich*), a designation applied to all branches belonging to the common ministries.

## Nomenclature and Evolution
The title of the office has evolved to reflect the political structure of the monarchy. The minister was originally called the *Reichskanzler*. This title was abandoned in 1871 when Andrassy succeeded Beust, as the term was seen as potentially implying the existence of a single state, which was contrary to Hungarian feeling.

## Budget and Finance
The Ministry of Foreign Affairs is funded through a common budget that covers the expenditure of common affairs. This joint budget is supported by revenues from joint ministries, net proceeds from customs, and proportional contributions (the quota) from Austria and Hungary. 

The quota for these contributions is fixed for several years and typically aligns with the duration of the commercial and customs treaty. For example, until 1897, Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30% of the remaining joint expenditure. Between 1897 and July 1907, the quota was adjusted to 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary; by 1907, Hungary's contribution rose to 36.4%.

## Oversight and Policy
The foreign minister is tasked with explaining and justifying policy during annual meetings of the Delegations. Following English custom, "red books" containing important despatches are sometimes laid before these meetings. The common finances, including those of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, are audited by a joint supreme court of accounts.

## Other International Examples
The archive contains records of foreign ministry officials in other nations:
*   **Germany:** Bernhard von Bülow served as secretary of state for foreign affairs starting in 1897. He was responsible for the policy of colonial expansion, including the acquisition of the Caroline Islands in 1899.
*   **United Kingdom:** Sir Edward Grey served as under-secretary for foreign affairs from 1892 to 1895 and later became foreign minister in December 1905, a post he held through April 1908.
*   **Turkey:** Fuad Pasha entered the foreign ministry as an official around 1836. He served as minister for foreign affairs on five separate occasions starting in 1851 and was noted for his management of Turkish foreign relations and the settlement of affairs in Syria after 1860.
