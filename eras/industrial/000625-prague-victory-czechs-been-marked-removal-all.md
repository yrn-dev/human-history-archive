# Prague and the Czech National Struggle

Prague serves as a central hub of political, religious, and cultural conflict within Bohemia, a region of the Austrian dominions. As the seat of an archbishopric and a major center of government and education, the city has been the focal point for the Czechs' efforts to establish national recognition and political autonomy.

## National and Linguistic Conflict
In Bohemia and Moravia, a vehement contest existed between the Czech and German populations. By 1900, the population of Bohemia was approximately 6,318,280, consisting of roughly 65% Czechs and 35% Germans. While Germans were concentrated in large towns and near the northern and western borders, Czechs occupied the south, south-east, and middle of the country.

The victory of the Czechs over German influence was marked by several symbolic and legal shifts. In Prague, this victory was specifically manifested by the removal of all German street names. The Czech town council further solidified this by passing a by-law that forbade private individuals from putting up street name tablets in German.

## Educational and Legal Advancements
The encroachment of Czech influence on German positions was supported by government ordinances. An 1880 ordinance mandated that business brought before law courts or government offices be handled in the language in which it was introduced, granting the Czech language a position within the government service across Bohemia and Moravia. This was extended in 1886 to the high courts of Prague and Bruenn to avoid frequent translations.

Education also became a primary battleground. Numerous Czech elementary and middle schools were established, and Czech classes were introduced into German schools. A significant turning point occurred in 1882 when Czech classes began at the university of Prague, an event viewed by Germans as a desecration of the oldest German university.

## Political Aspirations and the Bohemian Monarchy
A significant political party, led by Rieger and Palacky, sought the restoration of the Bohemian monarchy in its fullest extent, including Silesia and Moravia. This movement insisted that the emperor be crowned as the king of Bohemia at Prague, following the tradition of his predecessors. They aimed for Bohemia to hold a position within the monarchy similar to that of Hungary.

This cause was supported not only by the Czechs but also by several great nobles of German descent, including Prince Friedrich von Schwarzenberg (the cardinal archbishop of Prague), Count Heinrich Clam-Martinitz, and Count Leo Thun. These nobles hoped a self-governing kingdom would preserve their power against German Liberals. Conversely, Germans in Bohemia advocated for centralization to avoid falling under Czech control.

## Civil Unrest and Political Evolution
The tensions in Prague occasionally escalated into violence. On June 12, a rising of the populace and Czech students broke out, fueled by quarrels between radicals, conservatives, Czechs, and Germans. This uprising was suppressed on June 16 by Prince Windischgraetz. This event is noted as a signal for a universal race war where national rivalry superseded the idea of constitutional liberty.

Later political efforts to reconcile these factions were led by Taaffe. In January, conferences were held in Vienna involving the German party, the Feudal party, and the Czechs. An agreement was reached to revise the electoral law and separate German and Czech districts. However, this was opposed by the "Young Czechs," who were more impatient than the "Old Czechs" and feared the measures would threaten the unity of the Bohemian country. By the elections of 1891, the Young Czechs emerged completely victorious.

## Regional Context and Military History
Prague's influence extended to nearby towns in Bohemia. For example, the town of Kolin, located 40 miles east of Prague, was captured by the men of Prague in 1421, resulting in the expulsion of German inhabitants who refused to accept "the four articles." Later, in 1757, the Battle of Kolin saw the Austrians under Daun defeat the Prussians under Frederick the Great, which led to the evacuation of Bohemia by the Prussians and the raising of the siege of Prague.
