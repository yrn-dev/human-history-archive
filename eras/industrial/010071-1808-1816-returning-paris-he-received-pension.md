# Historical Figures and Works (1808-1816)

The period between 1808 and 1816 saw significant contributions in the fields of botany, linguistics, religious leadership, and the publication of historical and artistic treatises.

## Botanical Explorations of Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland
Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland, born in La Rochelle on August 22, 1773, and known by the real name Goujand, was a French botanist and traveller. Having studied under J.N. Corvisart in Paris and served as a surgeon in the French army, Bonpland spent five years traveling through Colombia, Mexico, and the districts bordering the Amazon and Orinoco alongside A. von Humboldt. During these explorations, he classified and collected approximately 6,000 plants, the majority of which were previously unknown in Europe. 

Bonpland described these findings in *Plantes equinoxiales*, published between 1808 and 1816. Upon his return to Paris, he was granted a pension and the superintendence of the gardens at Malmaison. His other published works include *Description des plantes rares de Navarre* (1813) and *Monographie des Melastomees* (1806). In 1816, Bonpland traveled to Buenos Aires with various European plants, where he was elected professor of natural history. He eventually left this position to explore central South America. In 1821, while traveling to Bolivia, he was arrested by the dictator of Paraguay, Dr. Francia, and remained detained until 1831. He lived in the province of Corrientes at San Borga until 1853, when he moved to Santa Anna, where he died on May 4, 1858.

## Linguistic and Oriental Studies of Heinrich Julius Klaproth
Heinrich Julius Klaproth (1783-1835) was a German traveller and Orientalist born in Berlin on October 11, 1783, to the chemist Martin Heinrich Klaproth. Klaproth focused on Asiatic languages from an early age, publishing his *Asiatisches Magazin* between 1802 and 1803. This work led to an appointment at the academy in St Petersburg. 

Klaproth's career included serving as a member of Count Golovkin's embassy to China in 1805 and conducting a linguistic and ethnographical exploration of the Caucasus from 1807 to 1808. After several years working on Oriental publications for the academy, he moved to Berlin in 1812 and later settled in Paris in 1815. In 1816, through the efforts of Humboldt, the king of Prussia granted Klaproth a salary and the title of professor of Asiatic languages and literature. He was permitted to stay in Paris to publish his works and remained there until his death on August 28, 1835.

## Religious Leadership of Peter Cartwright
Peter Cartwright (1785-1872) was an American preacher for the Methodist Episcopal church. Born in Virginia on September 1, 1785, he moved to Kentucky as a child. After a period of gambling at horse-racing and cards, Cartwright joined the church in June 1801. He was licensed as a regular exhorter in May 1802, earning the nickname "Kentucky Boy," and formed the Livingston circuit in the autumn of 1802. 

Following his ordination as a deacon by Bishop Asbury in 1806, Cartwright was appointed presiding elder in 1808 by Bishop McKendree, under whom he had studied theology. His leadership roles included serving as presiding elder of the Wabash district in 1812 and the Green river district from 1813 to 1816. Later, after serving as presiding elder of the Cumberland district and four years on circuit in Kentucky, he transferred to the Illinois conference in 1823. By 1856, Cartwright had baptized approximately 12,000 persons, received 10,000 into the church, and preached roughly 14,600 times. He died on September 25, 1872, near Pleasant Plains in Sangamon county, Illinois.
