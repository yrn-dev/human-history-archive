# Geological and Scientific Studies of the Late 19th Century

The late 19th century saw significant contributions to the fields of geology, volcanology, and various natural sciences, characterized by a shift from earlier theoretical frameworks toward empirical observation and specialized research.

## Contributions of Thomas George Bonney
Thomas George Bonney (1833–) was an English geologist born in Rugeley on July 27, 1833. Educated at Uppingham and St John's College, Cambridge, he graduated as 12th wrangler in 1856 and was ordained the following year. Between 1856 and 1861, he served as a mathematical master at Westminster school, during which time he pursued geology as a recreation, focusing primarily on Alpine regions.

Bonney's professional academic career advanced in 1868 when he was appointed lecturer in geology and tutor at St John's College. His scientific research focused on metamorphic and igneous rocks across various locations, including the Scottish Highlands, Wales, Charnwood Forest, Salcombe, and the Lizard, as well as Alpine regions. In 1877, he became the professor of geology at University College, London.

Bonney held several prestigious leadership roles, serving as president of the Alpine Club and the Mineralogical Society, as well as secretary and later president (1884–1886) of the Geological Society. He also served as secretary of the British Association from 1881 to 1885 and was appointed honorary canon of Manchester in 1887. His scientific bibliography includes:
* *Cambridgeshire Geology* (1875)
* *The Story of our Planet* (1893)
* *Charles Lyell and Modern Geology* (1895)
* *Ice Work, Past and Present* (1896)
* *Volcanoes* (1899)

Beyond these volumes, he published numerous papers in the *Geological Magazine* and the *Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society*, and wrote popular works on Welsh and English scenery, Alpine Regions, and theological subjects.

## Volcanology and the Work of Christian Leopold von Buch
Earlier developments in the study of volcanoes were heavily influenced by Baron Christian Leopold von Buch (1774–1853), a German geographer and geologist. Born in Pomerania on April 26, 1774, von Buch studied at the mining school of Freiberg under Werner, where he was a contemporary of Alexander von Humboldt.

Initially, von Buch was a zealous supporter of Werner's Neptunian theory, which advocated for the aqueous origin of formations such as basalt. However, his faith in this theory was shaken during excursions to Italy in 1798. His studies of volcanoes provided critical data for refuting existing erroneous ideas about volcanic activity. He visited Vesuvius in 1799 and again in 1805, the latter trip accompanied by Gay Lussac and Humboldt, during which they witnessed a remarkable eruption.

Von Buch also explored the extinct volcanoes of Auvergne in 1802. His observations of the Puy de Dôme—specifically its strata of basaltic lava and its cone of trachyte—led him to conclude that Werner's doctrines on the formation of these rocks were untenable. These findings were detailed in his work *Geognostische Beobachtungen auf Reisen durch Deutschland und Italien* (1802–1809).

## Other Scientific and Academic Publications (1896–1899)
The period between 1896 and 1899 was marked by a proliferation of specialized literature across various disciplines:

**Bacteriology:**
Several influential texts were published during this window, including *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* by Crookshank (1896), *Die Mikroorganismen* (3rd ed.) by Fluegge (1896), and *Micro-organisms and Disease* by Klein (1896). In 1899, Hueppe published *Principles of Bacteriology* (translation) and Duclaux published *Traite de microbiologie* (1899–1900).

**Philology and Literature:**
Sophus Bugge, a Norwegian philologist, published *Helge-digtene i den Aeldre Edda* in Copenhagen in 1896, which was later translated as *The Home of the Eddic Poems* in 1899. In the realm of literary editing, 1896 saw the publication of the best edition of Donne's *Poems* edited by E. K. Chambers, and an edition of the *Life and Works of Robert Burns* revised by William Wallace. In 1899, Edmund Gosse published *The Life and Letters of John Donne*.

**Hagiography and History:**
The Bollandists continued their scientific approach to hagiography, publishing the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* in 1899 and a volume on Greek MSS. in the Vatican in the same year.
