# Brook Farm

Brook Farm was a communistic experiment conducted between 1841 and 1847 on a tract of land located in West Roxbury, Massachusetts, approximately 10 miles from Boston. The project was originated and virtually directed throughout its existence by George Ripley.

## Philosophical Origins and Objectives
The experiment served as a practical manifestation of the spirit of "Transcendentalism" in New England, although many of the most prominent transcendentalists did not take a direct part in the project. The endeavor took place during a period of widespread interest in social reform; Ralph Waldo Emerson noted at the time that "not a reading man but has a draft of a new community in his waistcoat pocket."

The primary aim of Brook Farm was to establish the best conditions for an ideal civilization. George Ripley intended the community to achieve several specific goals:
* To create a more natural union between manual and intellectual labour.
* To combine the roles of the worker and the thinker within the same individual.
* To guarantee the highest level of mental freedom by providing labour that was adapted to the tastes and talents of the individuals.
* To secure for all members the fruits of their industry.
* To eliminate the necessity of menial services by making the profits of labour and the benefits of education available to everyone.

By simplifying social machinery and reducing the amount of labour required for mere existence, the community sought to save the maximum amount of time for spiritual and mental development and education. The ultimate vision was to prepare a society of cultivated, intelligent, and liberal persons whose relationships would allow for a more wholesome and simple life than was possible under the pressure of competitive institutions.

## Organization and Operation
The enterprise began in the summer of 1841 with approximately twenty members. In September of that year, the association formally organized as the "Brook Farm Institute of Agriculture and Education." To establish this unincorporated joint-stock association, the members signed Articles of Association. The project attracted the cooperation of individuals whose later careers indicated they were more than mere visionaries. Despite these efforts, the communistic experiment was ultimately tried unsuccessfully.
