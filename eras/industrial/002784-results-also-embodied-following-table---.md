# Analysis of Results and Data Embodiment

In scientific, mathematical, and statistical documentation, the embodiment of results often takes the form of tables, specialized apparatus, or comprehensive treatises. ## Ballistics and Explosives Research
Experimental results regarding the pressure of powder and bullet velocity have been documented through various methodologies. Chevalier d'Arcy conducted experiments in 1760 by successively shortening the musket barrel to measure velocity using a ballistic pendulum. In contrast, more modern results regarding gunpowder are based on the work of Abel and Noble between 1875 and 1894. Their process involved firing a charge of explosive of varying weight (P lb) within an explosion-chamber of a known volume (C, cub. in.). 

The pressure (p, tons per sq. in.) was recorded using a crusher-gauge. These results are plotted in curves showing the relationship between pressure and either the gravimetric density (D)—the specific gravity of the powder when filling the volume C in a state of gas—or the gravimetric volume (v), which is the ratio of the gas volume to the volume of an equal weight of water. These specific results are further embodied in a table.

Additionally, ballistic results are used to compile range tables. For a 6-in. gun with a muzzle velocity (V) of 2150 f/s and a coefficient of reduction (n) of 0.96, calculations are used to determine the remaining velocity (v), time of flight (t), angle of elevation ([phi]), and descent ([beta]) at ranges of 500, 1000, 1500, and 2000 yards. Discrepancies between calculated and tabulated results can indicate the influence of the muzzle velocity or the coefficient of reduction.

## Demographic and Agricultural Data
Statistical results regarding births in England and Wales are embodied in detailed tables. Data from 1860 to 1907 tracks illegitimate births, their proportion to 1000 of the population, and illegitimate births per 1000 births. For example, in 1860, there were 43,693 illegitimate births (2.2 per 1000 of the population), while by 1907, this number was 36,189 (1.0 per 1000 of the population). Further calculations provide the illegitimate birth-rate based on the unmarried and widowed female population aged 15-45 years; this rate declined from 14.4 per 1000 in 1876-1880 to 7.8 per 1000 in 1907.

## Engineering and Geological Findings
In the field of marine engineering, the British admiralty commissioned trials of boilers to obtain actual results under working conditions. These trials were conducted with great care, utilizing skilled observers to measure feed-water and weigh South Welsh coal of excellent quality. These results are presented in a table to provide a more realistic representation of ordinary working conditions than the particulars published by boiler makers.

In geology, the results of Theodor Kjerulf's research on the structure of the Archaean and Palaeozoic rocks, glacial phenomena, and the geology of southern Norway were embodied in his 1879 work, *Udsigt over det sydlige Norges Geologi*. In physical geography, the term "horst" describes the result of a block of the earth's crust remaining stationary while surrounding land sinks or is crushed; examples of this result include the Black Forest, the Vosges, the Jura, the Table, and the Dole.

## Mathematical and Technological Embodiments
The discoveries of the mathematician Boole were embodied in two systematic treatises completed during his lifetime: the *Treatise on Differential Equations* (1859) and the *Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences* (1860). These works include a lucid account of the general symbolic method and a general method in analysis originally described in a 1844 memoir.

In the realm of physics and invention, Alexander Graham Bell exhibited an apparatus in 1876 that embodied the results of his studies on the transmission of sound by electricity. This apparatus, through subsequent modifications and improvements, became the modern commercial telephone.
