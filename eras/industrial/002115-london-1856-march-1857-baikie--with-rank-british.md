# London and Global Events (1856–1857)

The mid-19th century was a period of significant scientific publication, diplomatic activity, and geopolitical shifts. In London and across Europe, this era was marked by the conclusion of major conflicts and the expansion of colonial and scientific exploration.

## Scientific and Literary Publications in London
London served as a primary hub for the publication of diverse scholarly and narrative works during 1856 and 1857. In 1856, William Balfour Baikie published his *Narrative of an Exploring Voyage up the Rivers Kwora and Binue* in London. This work detailed his experiences as a surgeon and naturalist on a Niger expedition. 

Other notable publications appearing in London during this window included the 1858 edition of *A Narrative of the Mission sent by the Governor-General of India to the Court of Ava in 1855*, compiled by Captain H. Yule. Additionally, 1857 saw the London publication of the English translation of Kuno Fischer's *Fr. Bacon*, translated by John Oxenford.

## The Expeditions of William Balfour Baikie
William Balfour Baikie (1824–1864), a Scottish philologist, naturalist, and explorer, played a prominent role in British exploration of Africa. After studying medicine at Edinburgh and joining the royal navy in 1848, he was appointed as a naturalist and surgeon for a government-supported Niger expedition in 1854. Following the death of Consul Beecroft at Fernando Po, Baikie assumed command. He led the steamer *Pleiad* on a 118-day voyage, ascending the Benue approximately 250 miles beyond previous explorations without losing any men. Although instructed to assist Heinrich Barth, who had crossed the upper Benue in 1851, Baikie could not find trustworthy information regarding him.

In March 1857, holding the rank of British consul, Baikie began another expedition in the *Pleiad*. After two years of exploring the Niger, the vessel was wrecked in the river's rapids. While the rest of his party returned home, Baikie remained. Accompanied by one or two native followers, he landed at the confluence of the Benue and Niger. He established a base of operations at Lokoja, a site that had previously hosted a British government model farm in 1841. After purchasing the site and signing a treaty with the Fula emir of Nupe, Baikie began building houses and clearing the land.

## Professional and Academic Developments
The years 1856 and 1857 saw various professional transitions in London. Charles Lamb Kenney, a journalist and dramatist who had worked as a clerk in the General Post Office and for *The Times*, was called to the bar in 1856. He subsequently became the secretary to Ferdinand de Lesseps and published *The Gates of the East* in 1857 to support the construction of the Suez Canal.

In the field of natural history, Francis Trevelyan Buckland, an English zoologist and former house-surgeon at St George's hospital in London, became a regular writer for the newly established publication *Field* in 1856, focusing specifically on fish.

## Global Political and Historical Context
The broader historical landscape of 1856 was defined by the end of the Crimean War (1854–1856) and the ascension of Alexander II of Russia. In 1857, the Indian Mutiny occurred.

In Canada, 1856 was marked by political tension regarding representation by population. Upper Canada, having increased in size and revenue contribution, demanded proportionate representation. Macdonald became prime minister in 1856, arguing that existing conditions were satisfactory and no constitutional amendment was required, while Brown maintained that such representation was imperative.

In Europe, the mid-1850s saw the rise of national biographical dictionaries. The Austrian dictionary began in 1856, and the Swedish dictionary concluded its 23-volume run in 1857.
