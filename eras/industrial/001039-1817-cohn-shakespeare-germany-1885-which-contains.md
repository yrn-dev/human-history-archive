## Literary Works and Shakespearean Studies
In 1817, Nathan Drake (1766–1836), an English physician and essayist, published his most important production: *Shakespeare and his Times*. This two-volume work provided a comprehensive scope that included a biography of the poet, criticisms of his writings and genius, a new chronology of his plays, a history of the elegant literature, poetry, superstitions, and manners of the age, and a disquisition on the object of Shakespeare's sonnets. G. G. Gervinus noted that the merit of Drake's work lay in its ability to synthesize scattered and tedious material from various other valuable labors, including those of Ritson, Heath, and Tyrwhitt, into a single whole.

Additionally, the year 1817 saw the publication of *Deutsches Theater* by L. Tieck.

## Memoirs and Historical Records
The archive records the publication of the *Memoire sur le comte de Bonneval* in Paris in 1817, written by Prince de Ligne. This work serves as a reference regarding the *Memoirs* published under Bonneval's own name, which are considered spurious.

In the context of the *Greville Memoirs*, the first set of memoirs covers a period extending from 1817 to 1837. These were published in three volumes in 1875, though the release was marked by controversy; some passages caused extreme offence, leading to the suppression of certain text and the recall of issued copies.

## Danish Theatre
The year 1817 marks the death of Peter Thun Feorsom (1777–1817). Feorsom was a Danish dramatist who produced an excellent translation of the works of Shakespeare between 1807 and 1816.
