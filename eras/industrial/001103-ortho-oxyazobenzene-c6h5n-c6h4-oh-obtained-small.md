# Ortho-oxyazobenzene

Ortho-oxyazobenzene, with the chemical formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:N(1)}\text{C}_6\text{H}_4\text{OH(2)}$, is an organic compound that exists as orange-red needles. It is characterized by a melting point between $82.5\text{--}83^\circ\text{C}$.

## Production and Separation
The compound was obtained in small quantities by E. Bamberger (reported in *Ber.*, 1900, 33, p. 3189). It is produced simultaneously with para-oxyazobenzene (benzene-azo-phenol), which is prepared by coupling phenol with diazotized aniline in an alkaline solution. Ortho-oxyazobenzene can be separated from the para compound through distillation in a current of steam, as the ortho compound passes over with the steam.

## Chemical Properties and Reactions
When ortho-oxyazobenzene is reduced using zinc dust in a dilute sal-ammoniac solution, it yields aniline and ortho-aminophenol.

## Theoretical Constitution
The chemical constitution of oxyazo compounds has been a subject of significant debate among chemists. Some argue that these substances are true azophenols of the type $\text{R.N}_2\text{R}_1\text{OH}$, while others propose a quinonoid structure, viewing them as quinone hydrazones of the type $\text{R.NH.N:R}_1\text{:O}$.

Several researchers have investigated this from different perspectives:
* **Physico-chemical analysis:** K. Auwers examined freezing-point depressions and found that while para-oxyazo compounds produce abnormal depressions, ortho-oxyazo compounds produce normal depressions. Based on these results, Auwers concluded that ortho compounds are quinone hydrazones or behave as such, whereas para compounds are phenolic.
* **Pseudo-acid classification:** A. Hantzsch suggested that oxyazo compounds be classified as pseudo-acids. In his view, they possess the configuration of quinone hydrazones in their free condition, but their salts exhibit a normal phenolic type.
* **Chemical reactions:** Th. Zincke and R. Meldola also conducted early studies on the chemical side of these compounds. Meldola attempted to split acetylated azophenols via reduction in acid solution, though he did not achieve satisfactory results.
