# Charles Victor de Bonstetten

Charles Victor de Bonstetten (1745-1832) was a Swiss writer and a member of one of the great patrician families of Bern. He is described as a liberal patrician and a representative of the Gallicized Bern of the 18th century, possessing a character that was more French than Swiss.

## Education and Early Influences
Bonstetten received his education in his native town of Bern, as well as at Yverdon. Between 1763 and 1766, he studied at Geneva, where he imbibed liberal sentiments under the influence of Charles Bonnet and Rousseau. Following this period, his father recalled him to Bern, after which he was sent to Leiden. In 1769, he visited England, where he established a friendship with the poet Gray.

## Political Career and Public Service
After the death of his father in 1770, Bonstetten traveled extensively through Italy. Upon returning to Bern in 1774, he entered political life. However, his liberal ideas rendered him unfitted for this sphere, leading him to encourage and patronize Johannes Muller, who would later become a Swiss historian.

In 1779, Bonstetten was appointed as the Bernese bailiff of Gessenay or Saanen. During his tenure in this position, he wrote his *Lettres pastorales sur une contree de la Suisse*, which was published in German in 1781. In 1787, he was transferred to a similar capacity as bailiff in Nyon. He was forced to retire from this post after participating in a festival in 1791 that celebrated the destruction of the Bastille.

Between 1795 and 1797, Bonstetten served the Swiss Confederation by governing the Italian-speaking districts of Val Maggia, Mendrisio, Locarno, and Lugano. He published a description of these districts in 1797 and is credited with introducing the cultivation of the potato to the region.

## Later Life and Literary Works
The French revolution of 1798 in Switzerland forced Bonstetten back into private life. From 1798 to 1801, he resided in Denmark with his friend Fredirika Brun. In 1803, he settled in Geneva, where he lived for the remainder of his life. While in Geneva, he associated with various distinguished individuals, including Madame de Stael between 1809 and 1817.

During his final years, Bonstetten produced several written works:
*   *Recherches sur la nature et les lois de l'imagination* (1807)
*   *Etudes de l'homme, ou Recherches sur les faculties de penser et de sentir* (1821)
*   *L'Homme du midi et l'homme du nord* (1824): His most celebrated work, which examines the influence of climate on different nations, specifically exalting the north over the south.
