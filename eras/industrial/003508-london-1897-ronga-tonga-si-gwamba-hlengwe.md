# The Ronga and Tonga Languages

The Ronga and Tonga linguistic groups are categorized within the Bantu language families of Africa, appearing in different regional contexts and classifications across South-East and Central Africa.

## The Ronga Languages of South-East Africa
The Ronga (also referred to as Tonga) languages are situated in Portuguese South-East Africa. Their geographic distribution includes Delagoa Bay, the Lower Limpopo valley, and Gazaland, as well as specific patches in the North Transvaal, where the language is identified as Shi-gwamba.

In terms of linguistic relationship, the Ronga languages are described as being almost equally related to the Nyanja group and to Zulu. This relationship likely represents a mingling of the two influences, though the influence of Zulu is noted as predominating.

## The Tonga of Central Zambezia
A separate linguistic group, which some authorities suggest should be ranked independently, is the Subiya-Tonga-Ila group. Within this classification, the Tonga subgroup encompasses the dialects of all Central Zambezia, as well as the Ba-ila (Mashukulumbwe) and the Ba-tetela.

The Tonga language of this region is noted for having a close alliance in structure with the Ila and Subiya languages. While Subiya is considered one of the most archaic of the Bantu languages—more so than Tonga—both languages exhibit only a slight relationship with the Zulu group and possess no strong affinity to Oci-herero.

## Comparative Linguistic Context
The broader Bantu landscape includes various other distinct groups. To the west and south of the Ronga and Tonga regions lies the Bechuana language group, which includes dialects such as Se-suto (the language of Basutoland), Ci-venda, Se-pedi (or Peli), Se-rolon, Se-mangwato, and Se-chilapi. This group extends from the Orange River in the south to the Zambezi in the north.

Further north, the Barotseland languages include Si-luyi, which may be related to the Mabunda of Western Barotseland, as well as the dialects of the A-kwamashi, Ma-bukushu, A-mbwe, and Ambwela.
