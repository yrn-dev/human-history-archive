# Lane Seminary Debates (February 1834)

The Lane Seminary debates were a series of confrontations that took place in February 1834 at the Lane Theological Seminary. These debates occurred between students who held pro-slavery views and those who held anti-slavery views.

## Outcome and Aftermath
The debates resulted in the withdrawal of the anti-slavery students from the seminary. One such participant in the famous debates was Theodore Dwight Weld, a reformer, pamphleteer, and abolition orator, who left the seminary following the events of 1834. Weld later married Angelina Grimké in 1838.

## Influence on Gamaliel Bailey
The events at the seminary had a significant impact on Gamaliel Bailey (1807–1859). Bailey, a graduate of the Jefferson Medical College in Philadelphia and a former editor of the religious journal *Methodist Protestant* in Baltimore, had moved to Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1831. While in Cincinnati, he practiced medicine and served as a lecturer on physiology at the Lane Theological Seminary. 

During the February 1834 debates and the subsequent departure of the anti-slavery students, Bailey became an ardent abolitionist. This shift in conviction led him to enter the field of abolitionist journalism. In 1836, he joined James G. Birney in the editorial control of the *Philanthropist*. Bailey eventually succeeded Birney as the editor of that paper, maintaining his leadership despite acts of violence and the wrecking of his printing office by mobs on three separate occasions. He continued his editorial work with the daily *Herald* starting in 1843, and in 1847, he took control of the *National Era* in Washington, D.C. Under Bailey's leadership, the *National Era* first published Mrs. H. B. Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* between 1851 and 1852.
