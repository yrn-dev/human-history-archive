# Edinburgh and Related Academic Records (1886)

## Academic Publications in Edinburgh
During the late 19th century, Edinburgh served as a primary center for the publication of historical and biographical research. In 1886, J. Anderson published *Scotland in Pagan Times* in Edinburgh. This work is listed among the authorities regarding ancient burial mounds and barrows, alongside other international studies on prehistoric sites in Ireland, Denmark, Sweden, Spain, Portugal, and Japan.

Other regional historical records from the same period include C. Ross's *Antiquities of Kinross-shire*, published in Perth in 1886. This contributed to a broader body of work on the region, which also includes the *History of Fife and Kinross* (1896) and *The Place Names of Fife and Kinross* (1895), both published in Edinburgh.

Other significant essays on Burns were later published in Edinburgh, such as those by J. ## University Appointments and Honors
The academic landscape of Edinburgh and other Scottish universities saw notable leadership changes in 1886. Mr. Balfour, who had previously been made LL.D. of Edinburgh University in 1881, was appointed as the lord rector of St Andrews University in 1886. *   **Asiatic Exploration:** In 1886, Charles Doughty published *Travels in Northern Arabia* in Cambridge. During 1886-1887, Pierre G. Bonvalot, accompanied by Prince Henri d'Orleans, attempted to enter Lhasa while crossing the Tibetan plateau from north to south, though he was unsuccessful. Additionally, Venukoff published "Explorations" in the *Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society* in 1886, and Ney Elias conducted explorations in Central Asia between 1886 and 1887.
*   **Geological Research:** In 1886, the *Zapiski* of the Russian Geographical Society published Chersky's Geological Map of the shores of Lake Baikal. The same year, the *Zapiski* of the East Siberian Branch of the Russian Geographical Society published a "Report of Geological Exploration of Shores of Lake Baikal."
*   **Classical Studies:** The British School in Athens was founded in 1886. Despite facing insufficient endowment compared to French and German institutions, it conducted excavations at sites including Megalopolis, Melos, and Sparta, and participated in the exploration of Cnossus in Crete.

## Notable Deaths
The year 1886 marked the death of John Russell Bartlett on May 28. Bartlett was an American linguistic and historical student born in Rhode Island who is primarily remembered for his 1848 *Dictionary of Americanisms*. His earlier career included serving as a United States commissioner for the boundary survey between the United States and Mexico (1850-1853) and serving as the secretary of state of Rhode Island from 1855 to 1872.
