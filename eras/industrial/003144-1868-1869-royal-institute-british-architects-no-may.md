# Royal Institute of British Architects (1868-1869)

The Royal Institute of British Architects (RIBA) is a professional organization involved in the study of architecture, the promotion of the field, and the scientific testing of building materials.

## Documentation of Masons' Marks
In the *Sessional Papers* of the Royal Institute of British Architects for 1868-1869, specifically in No. 9, the institute published numerous samples of masons' marks collected from various parts of Europe. These samples served as illustrations for a paper written by George Godwin, F.R.S., F.S.A. 

Godwin is credited with first drawing attention to these signs in England, having previously communicated on the subject of "masons' marks in various countries" to the Society of Antiquaries in 1841 and later in *Archaeologia* (vol. xxx. p. 113). The archive notes that such marks are often overlooked by archaeologists until a few are identified, after which they appear in astonishing numbers. These marks are considered a modern method for connecting a stone worker to their specific work. In some instances, a second mark may be present to indicate an overseer, and occasionally three or more marks are observable on some blocks.

## Professional Recognition and Awards
The Royal Institute of British Architects also grants honors for services rendered to the promotion of architecture. An example of this is the King's Medal of the Royal Institute of British Architects, which was awarded in 1903 to the American architect Charles Follen McKim. McKim (1847-1909) was a founder of the American Academy in Rome and is known for his work on the Columbia University buildings, the University Club in New York, the Boston Public Library, and additions to the White House in 1906.

## Technical Research and Material Testing
The institute conducts scientific experiments to determine the structural integrity of building materials. Between 1896 and 1897, the Royal Institute of British Architects carried out experiments using short walls to determine the average loads per square foot at which crushing occurred in various types of brickwork. The results varied based on the quality of workmanship and the type of mortar used:

*   **Staffordshire blue brickwork:** Crushed at 114.34 tons per sq. ft. in lime mortar and 135.43 tons in cement mortar.
*   **Leicester red brickwork:** Crushed at 45.36 tons per sq. ft. in lime mortar and 83.36 tons in cement mortar.
*   **Fletton brickwork:** Crushed at 30.68 tons in lime mortar and 56.25 tons in cement mortar.
*   **Gault brickwork:** Crushed at 31.14 tons in lime mortar and 51.34 tons in cement mortar.
*   **Stock brickwork:** Crushed at 18.63 tons per sq. ft. in lime mortar and 39.29 tons in cement mortar.
