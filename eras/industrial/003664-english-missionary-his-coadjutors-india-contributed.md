# English Missionary Work in India

The history of English missionary activity in India is characterized by diverse approaches to conversion, education, and linguistic translation, involving various denominations and strategies to engage with the native population.

## Baptist Missions and the Influence of William Carey
From the beginning of the 19th century, Baptist communities in the United States—specifically in Philadelphia, Charleston, Boston, and other areas—took a deep interest in the work of William Carey, an English missionary and his coadjutors in India. These communities provided liberal financial contributions to support Carey's efforts.

This interest helped catalyze American Baptist missionary history. In 1812, Adoniram Judson and Luther Rice, who had been sent to India by the American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions, converted to Baptist views. Following this, Rice returned to America to organize missionary societies. In January 1813, "The Baptist Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in India and other Foreign Parts" was formed in Boston, followed by similar societies in the Southern, Middle, and Eastern states. By May 1814, delegates from eleven states met in Philadelphia to organize the "General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions," which became known as the "Triennial Convention" due to its three-year meeting cycle.

## Educational Missions and Alexander Duff
In the early 19th century, Protestant missions in India had primarily seen success among outcaste and low-caste peoples, particularly in south Travancore and Tinevelly, while the Mahommedan and Hindu communities remained largely untouched. Alexander Duff, a Scottish missionary born in 1806, sought to change this by replacing evangelizing methods with educational ones.

Duff arrived in Calcutta in May 1830 after being twice shipwrecked. He established an English school where the Bible served as the center of the curriculum, though secular knowledge was taught from basic levels up to a university standard. Duff utilized the English language because he viewed it as the key to Western knowledge and the primary instrument for higher education in India. 

Duff's educational mission had three primary results:
1. It secured the entrance of Christian ideas into the minds of high-caste Hindus.
2. It led Christian churches at home to recognize education as a missionary agency.
3. It altered the education policy of the Indian government. 

On March 7, 1835, a government minute was adopted stating that the British government's objective in higher education should be the promotion of European literature and science among India's natives, and that funds for education should be used for English education alone.

## Translation and Linguistic Contributions
Missionaries have historically been assiduous in the translation and distribution of the Bible. In south India, Danish Lutheran missionaries, supported by the Society for Promoting Christian Knowledge (founded 1698), produced the first versions of the Bible in Telugu and Tamil.

Other English-speaking contributors focused on local languages. Thomas Stephens, an Englishman who arrived in Goa in 1579, wrote the first Konkani grammar. He also authored a metrical paraphrase of the New Testament in Konkani, which remained a favorite among native Christians.

## Comparative Missionary Approaches
While English missionaries focused on various strategies, other European missionaries in India adopted different methods. Jean Antoine Dubois, a French Catholic missionary who arrived in 1792, believed missionary work required a thorough acquaintance with the native population's character. To achieve this, Dubois abjured European society and adopted Hindu clothing and habits. He is remembered in South India as "the prince's son, the noblest of Europeans." Beyond his missionary work, Dubois founded agricultural colonies and introduced vaccination to prevent smallpox. He also authored *Hindu Manners, Customs and Ceremonies*, a work based on his observations of the caste system, Brahminical life, and religion.
