# 1856

The year 1856 was marked by significant developments in global communications, scientific advancements, political shifts in Canada, and the professional milestones of various figures in law and science.

## Technological and Scientific Advancements

In the field of electrical engineering, 1856 saw the organization of the Atlantic Telegraph Company. This entity was formed by Sir Charles Tilston Bright, Cyrus Field, and J. W. Brett (who controlled the Newfoundland Telegraph Company) for the purpose of carrying out the idea of an electric submarine cable connection between America and Ireland. Sir Charles Bright, an English telegraph engineer who had previously superintended the laying of lines in the British Isles—including the 1853 cable between Donaghadee and Portpatrick—became the engineer-in-chief of the company.

In the realm of optics and measurement, 1856 was the year Sir David Brewster deduced the method for superposing two spaces on one another, a development related to the use of binocular telescopes with parallel axes for measuring purposes.

## Political Developments in Canada

In 1856, the political landscape of Canada was dominated by the question of representation by population. Upper Canada, having increased in size and contributed a larger share of revenue, demanded proportionate representation. This was a point of contention, as La Fontaine had previously argued that such a principle would allow the stronger province to control the weaker one without constitutional restraint. 

During this year, Macdonald became prime minister; he maintained that existing conditions were satisfactory and that no constitutional amendment was necessary. Conversely, Brown asserted that representation by population was imperative. In 1856, Dorion gave notice of a motion suggesting a remedy for the situation.

## Deaths and Publications

The year 1856 marked the end of the life of Étienne Cabet, a French communist and author of *Voyage en Icarie*. Cabet had spent years attempting to realize his ideas of communism in practice, including the establishment of an "Icarian" colony on American soil. After transferring his settlement to Nauvoo, Illinois, he remained as the dictator of the society until he withdrew and died in 1856.
