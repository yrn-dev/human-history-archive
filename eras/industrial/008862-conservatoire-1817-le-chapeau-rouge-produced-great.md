# Conservatoire

The term "Conservatoire" refers to several prominent institutions of musical and dramatic education and preservation, most notably those located in Paris and Brussels. These institutions have served as centers for the training of composers, musicians, and actors, and as repositories for historical instruments and musical manuscripts.

## The Paris Conservatoire

The Paris Conservatoire has been a primary site for the training of French artists across multiple disciplines. In the field of drama, the institution provided rigorous training and competitive prizes. For example, actress Suzanne Desprès trained there and obtained the second prize for tragedy and the first prize for comedy in 1897. Similarly, Augustine Susanne Brohan entered the Conservatoire at age eleven, winning the second prize for comedy in 1820 and the first in 1821. Her daughter, Josephine Félicité Augustine Brohan, was also admitted at a young age and twice received the second prize for comedy.

In musical education, the Conservatoire has been led by distinguished composers. Francois Clement Theodore Dubois studied at the institution under Ambroise Thomas and won the Grand Prix de Rome in 1861 with his cantata *Atala*. Dubois later returned to the Conservatoire as a faculty member, becoming a professor of harmony in 1871 and a professor of composition in 1891, succeeding Leo Delibes. Following the death of Ambroise Thomas in 1896, Dubois was appointed director of the Conservatoire. Other notable students include Herman Bemberg, who studied under Gounod and Massenet.

The institution also maintains a museum of musical instruments. This collection includes rare items such as two flutes measuring 94 cm and 73 cm, as described in Gustave Chouquet's 1884 descriptive catalogue of the *Musee du Conservatoire National de Musique*.

## The Brussels Conservatoire

The Brussels Conservatoire is noted for its academic resources and its museum of musical instruments. The museum's holdings are documented in the descriptive and analytical catalogue produced by Victor Charles Mahillon, published in 1896. This collection includes significant historical instruments, such as a bass clarinet with a serpentine bore created by Nicolas Papalini of Pavia, which is constructed from two slabs of pear-wood and features nineteen finger-holes. The museum also preserves early specimens of the bass clarinet, including one that originally belonged to Adolphe Sax.

Beyond its instrumental collection, the library of the Brussels Conservatoire possesses interesting volumes of chamber-cantatas composed by Marcello for his mistress. Additionally, Victor Mahillon produced a descriptive catalogue between 1893 and 1900 for the museum of musical instruments belonging to the institution.
