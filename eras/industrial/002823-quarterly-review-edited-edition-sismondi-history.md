# Quarterly Review

The *Quarterly Review* is a periodical that has served as a venue for literary criticism, political analysis, and scholarly contributions across various fields of study. Its publications have spanned a wide range of subjects, including linguistics, history, theology, and international affairs.

## Literary and Linguistic Criticism
The *Quarterly Review* has featured significant analyses of English literature and dramatic works. In 1861, H. Morley published a piece on "Euphuism" within its pages. Later, in January 1896, R. W. Bond contributed an article titled "John Lyly, Novelist and Dramatist." 

The publication has also been used to review major literary works, though not always favorably. For example, John Colin Dunlop's *History of Fiction* (1814) received a contemptuous notice in the *Quarterly Review* in July 1815.

## Historical and Geographical Contributions
The journal has published works concerning international relations and historical figures. Sir John Barrow, an English statesman who served as comptroller of the household to Lord Macartney on the first British embassy to China, utilized the *Quarterly Review* to publish interesting articles based on his knowledge of the Chinese language.

Additionally, the *Quarterly Review* has been a source for critiques of historical editions. In July and October 1868 (vol. 125), the publication featured a piece titled "Marco Polo and his Recent Editors." This period of the journal is also referenced in the works of H. Yule.
