# The 1902 Direct Sale of Encyclopaedia Britannica Supplementary Volumes

## Overview of the Publication
In 1902, the London *Times* engaged in the direct sale of supplementary volumes intended for the 9th edition of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*. These specific volumes were also published by *The Times*. This event is noted as a development in the publishing industry that started a reaction toward amalgamating the distinct functions of the publisher and the bookseller.

## Context of the Book Trade
To understand the significance of the 1902 sale, a distinction must be made between the roles of booksellers and publishers. Booksellers are those whose trade consists of selling books through retail or wholesale channels. Publishers, conversely, handle the production of books from the author's manuscripts and serve as intermediaries between the author and the bookseller. While publishers were originally booksellers themselves, they gradually attained a higher rank within the book trade and exerted significant influence on the history of literature.

The 1902 action by the London *Times* represented a shift in these traditional roles. This trend toward combining production and sale was further exemplified in 1905 with the creation of *The Times* Book Club. This scheme combined the business of bookselling with a subscription library, utilizing the organization and publicity means of a newspaper to drive book sales, which introduced a new factor into the bookselling business.

## The Encyclopaedia Britannica Editions
The supplementary volumes sold in 1902 were part of a broader history of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*. The publication history of the work includes several iterations:
* **First Edition:** Three volumes (1768-1771).
* **Second Edition:** Ten volumes (1777-1784).
* **Third Edition:** Eighteen volumes (1788-1797).
* **Fourth Edition:** Twenty volumes (1801-1810).
* **Fifth Edition:** Twenty volumes (1815-1817).
* **Sixth Edition:** Twenty volumes (1823-1824).
* **Seventh Edition:** Twenty-one volumes (1830-1842).
* **Eighth Edition:** Twenty-two volumes (1853-1860).
* **Ninth Edition:** Twenty-five volumes (1875-1889).
* **Tenth Edition:** Consisted of the ninth edition and eleven supplementary volumes (1902-1903).
* **Eleventh Edition:** Twenty-nine volumes (1910-1911).

The copyright for these works in countries subscribing to the Bern Convention is held by the Chancellor, Masters and Scholars of the University of Cambridge.
