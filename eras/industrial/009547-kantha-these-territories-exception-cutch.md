# Kantha

Within the administrative structure of the Bombay Presidency in British India, the term "Kantha" appears in the designation of two specific agencies managing native states in the North Gujarat region: the Mahi Kantha agency and the Rewa Kantha agency.

## Mahi Kantha Agency
The Mahi Kantha agency is one of the chief groups of native states located in North Gujarat. This agency includes the native state of Idar (also referred to as Edar). 

The state of Idar covers an area of 1,669 square miles. As of 1901, its population was 168,557, a figure that represented a 44% decrease over the preceding decade due to famine. The state's estimated gross revenue is £29,000, and it pays a tribute of £2,000 to the Gaekwar of Baroda. In 1901, following the deaths of the raja and his posthumous son, succession passed to Sir Pertab Singh of Jodhpur. Much of the territory within Idar is held by the raja's kinsmen under feudal tenure. The primary products of the region are sugar-cane, oil-seeds, and grain. While the town of Idar—located 64 miles northeast of Ahmedabad with a population of 7,085 in 1901—served as the former capital, the present capital is Ahmednagar, which had a population of 3,200. A railway line from Ahmedabad through Parantij runs primarily through the state of Idar.

## Rewa Kantha Agency
The Rewa Kantha agency is also situated among the chief groups of native states in North Gujarat. The Tapti river, which is considered the most useful of the Gujarat rivers from a commercial perspective, flows through the native state of Rewa Kantha. The Tapti enters the presidency south of the town of Burhanpur, passes through Rewa Kantha and the district of Surat, and eventually empties into the Gulf of Cambay a few miles west of Surat, draining approximately 250 miles of country.

## Regional Geography and Hydrography
The agencies of Mahi Kantha and Rewa Kantha are part of the broader Gujarat division of the Bombay Presidency. The physical landscape of this region is influenced by several river systems. The Mahi river, along with the Sabarmati, rises in the Mahi Kantha hills; these rivers flow southwards to drain the districts of Northern Gujarat before falling into the sea near the head of the Gulf of Cambay.

Geologically, the area of Gujarat and the surrounding peninsula of Kathiawar is largely covered by horizontal lava flows of the Deccan Trap series. In the plains of eastern Gujarat, the land is covered by wind-blown sand and alluvium.

## Administrative Context
The Mahi Kantha and Rewa Kantha agencies operate within the Bombay Presidency, a province consisting of both British districts and native states. The presidency is governed by a governor and divided into four commissionerships: Sind, the southern or Carnatic, the central or Deccan, and the northern or Gujarat. The native states within the presidency, totaling 353 separate units, are administered by collectors of the districts in which smaller states are located or by political agents.
