# Sir Edward Baines (1800-1890)

Sir Edward Baines was a British journalist, author, and politician who served as the editor and later the proprietor of the *Leeds Mercury*. He was the son of Edward Baines (1774-1848), a politician and newspaper proprietor who had purchased the *Leeds Mercury* in 1801.

## Career in Journalism and Authorship
Sir Edward Baines held the positions of editor and proprietor of the *Leeds Mercury*. In 1835, he authored the *History of the Cotton Manufacture*, a work that remained a standard authority for a long period. He also wrote the biography of his father, titled *Life*, published in 1861.

## Political Career and Honors
Baines entered politics and served as the Member of Parliament (M.P.) for Leeds from 1859 to 1874. In 1880, he was knighted. Like his father, Sir Edward was a strong advocate for the separation of state and church and opposed the interference of the government in national education.

## Family Background
Sir Edward was part of a politically active family. His father, Edward Baines, was an ardent supporter of parliamentary reform and a member of parliament from 1834 to 1841, having succeeded Macaulay. The elder Baines had transformed the *Leeds Mercury* into an organ of Liberal opinion in Leeds through his political articles. 

Sir Edward had an elder brother, Matthew Talbot Baines (1790-1860), who pursued a legal career. Matthew Talbot Baines became the recorder of Hull in 1837 and served as M.P. for Hull starting in 1847. He held the position of president of the Poor Law Board in 1849 and again from 1852 to 1855, after being returned for Leeds in 1852. In 1856, he joined the cabinet as the chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster.
