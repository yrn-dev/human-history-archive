# Otto von Bohtlingk

Otto von Bohtlingk (1815–1904) was a distinguished German scholar of Sanskrit and a prominent figure in 19th-century Indian and comparative philology. He was born at St Petersburg on the 30th of May (11th of June O.S.) 1815.

## Education and Early Career
Bohtlingk began his academic pursuits at the university of St Petersburg, where from 1833 to 1835 he studied Oriental languages, with a particular focus on Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic. He later expanded his studies in Germany, spending time first in Berlin and subsequently in Bonn between 1839 and 1842.

In 1842, Bohtlingk returned to St Petersburg and became attached to the Royal Academy of Sciences. His professional ascent within the society continued until 1855, when he was elected as an ordinary member. He later received official titles of nobility, being named "Russian state councillor" in 1860 and subsequently "privy councillor."

## Academic Works and Contributions
Bohtlingk's scholarly output is considered of pre-eminent value to the field of philology. His first major contribution was an edition of Panini's *Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln*, published in Bonn between 1839 and 1840. This work served as a criticism of the philological methods employed by Franz Bopp. Forty-seven years later, in 1887, Bohtlingk revisited this work, republishing it in Leipzig as *Paninis Grammatik mit Ubersetzung*, which included a complete translation.

His subsequent publications included:
* *Vopadevas Grammatik* (St Petersburg, 1847)
* *Uber die Sprache der Jakuten* (St Petersburg, 1851)
* *Indische Spruche* (St Petersburg, 1870-1873), a work published in three parts with an index later provided by Blau in 1893.
* A translation and critical examination of the *Chhandogya-upanishad* (St Petersburg, 1889).
* A translation of the *Brihadaranyaka-upanishad* (St Petersburg, 1889).

Additionally, he authored several smaller treatises, including a notable study on Sanskrit accents titled *Uber den Accent im Sanskrit* (1843).

## The Sanskrit-Worterbuch
Bohtlingk's *magnum opus* was the *Sanskrit-Worterbuch*. This massive undertaking was completed over a period of twenty-three years with the assistance of two friends, Albrecht Weber (born 1825) and Rudolf Roth (1821–1895). The dictionary was first published in seven volumes in St Petersburg between 1853 and 1875, with a new seven-volume edition released between 1879 and 1889.

## Later Life
In 1868, Bohtlingk settled in Jena. He later moved to Leipzig in 1885, where he remained until his death on the 1st of April 1904.
