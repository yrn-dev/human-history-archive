# BOND, SIR EDWARD AUGUSTUS (1815-1898)

## Early Life and Education
Sir Edward Augustus Bond was born on December 31, 1815, at Hanwell. He was the son of a schoolmaster and received his education at Merchant Taylors' school.

## Career at the Public Record Office and British Museum
Bond began his professional career in 1832 when he obtained a post in the public record office. In 1838, he transitioned to the British Museum, where he served as an assistant in the manuscript department. During this period, he came to the attention of his chief, Sir Frederick Madden, who was considered the most eminent palaeographer of the era.

Bond's ascent within the British Museum continued over the following decades. He was appointed Egerton librarian in 1852 and became the assistant keeper of MSS. in 1856. By 1867, he was promoted to the position of keeper. In 1878, he reached the position of principal librarian.

## Contributions to Librarianship and Palaeography
As keeper, Bond performed work of lasting value in the reorganization of the manuscript department. He is credited with the creation of the classified catalogue of MSS. and for improving the punctuality and efficiency of the department's publications. 

During his tenure as principal librarian, Bond supervised the construction of the "White Wing" buildings, which provided necessary accommodation for newspapers, manuscripts, drawings, and prints. Additionally, the purchase of the Stowe MSS. was concluded while he remained in office.

Beyond his administrative duties, Bond contributed to the academic field of palaeography. In conjunction with Sir E. Maunde Thompson, he founded the Palaeographical Society and is credited with first making classical palaeography an exact science.

## Publications and Honors
Bond served as the editor for several significant historical documents, including *The Speeches in the Trial of Warren Hastings* (1859-1861) and four volumes of facsimiles of Anglo-Saxon charters dating from 679 to the Conquest.

His professional achievements were recognized with several honors:
* In 1879, he was made LL.D. of Cambridge.
* In 1885, he was created C.B.
* On January 1, 1898, the day before his death, he was created K.C.B.

Sir Edward Augustus Bond died on January 2, 1898.
