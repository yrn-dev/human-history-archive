# Peter Christen Asbjornsen and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe

Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812-1885) and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813-1882) were collectors of Norwegian folklore. Their professional partnership and life's work were so closely united that it is considered unusual to name them separately.

## Early Lives and Education
Peter Christen Asbjornsen was born on January 15, 1812, in Christiania. He was a member of an ancient family from the Gudbrandsdal, a lineage believed to have ended with him. Asbjornsen began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories at the age of twenty, in 1832, and later became a student at the university in 1833. By profession, he was a zoologist; utilizing the university's assistance, he conducted several investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at the Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Ringerike. Moe first met Asbjornsen when he was fourteen years old, initiating a close friendship that lasted until the end of their lives. Moe attended Christiania University but left in 1839 to study theology. While working as a tutor in Christiania, he spent his holidays wandering through remote districts and mountains to collect stories.

## Collaborative Work in Folklore
In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. The two friends decided to work in concert and eagerly compared their findings. 

Their joint efforts resulted in a major work titled *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*. The first instalment of this work appeared in 1842-1843 and was recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology. A second volume followed in 1844, and they published a new collection in 1871. In 1859, Sir George Dasent translated many of the *Folkeeventyr* into English.

## Individual Contributions and Later Interests
While much of their work was collaborative, Asbjornsen also published a collection of Norwegian fairy tales (*huldreeventyr og folkesagn*) in 1845 without assistance from Moe. Later in his life, Asbjornsen turned his attention to environmental issues; in 1856, he became concerned with the deforestation of Norway and successfully induced the government to address the matter.
