# Events and Figures of the Mid-19th Century (1847-1848)

The years 1847 and 1848 were marked by significant literary publications, diplomatic treaties, and the deaths of various prominent figures across Europe and the Americas.

## Literary Developments and Publications
The late 1840s saw a surge in influential literary works, particularly from the Brontë sisters. In August 1847, Charlotte Brontë submitted a novel to the firm Smith, Elder & Co. after her shorter work, *The Professor*, was deemed too brief for the required three-volume format. This resulted in the October 1847 publication of *Jane Eyre*, which received widespread acclaim. Subsequently, in December 1847, T. C. Newby published the novels of Emily and Anne Brontë together in three volumes. Newby later published Anne Brontë's *The Tenant of Wildfell Hall* in June 1848.

In Hungary, Joseph Bajza, a poet and critic, produced several historical works during this period. These included the six-volume *Historical Library* (1843-1845), the *Modern Plutarch* (1845-1847), and the *Universal History* (1847), some of which were translations from German authors. In 1847, Bajza edited the opposition journal *Ellenoer* in Leipzig, and by March 1848, he became the editor of *Kossuth Hirlapja*.

In the field of genealogy, the *Genealogical and Heraldic Dictionary of the Peerage and Baronetage of the United Kingdom* (known as *Burke's Peerage*), originally issued by John Burke in 1826, began being issued annually in 1847. Sir John Bernard Burke took control of these publications in 1848.

## Diplomatic and Political Activity
In 1842, Lord Ashburton (Alexander Baring) was dispatched to America, leading to the conclusion of the Webster-Ashburton treaty. This agreement settled a compromise regarding the north-east boundary of Maine and arranged for the extradition of certain criminals. Furthermore, the two governments agreed to persuade other powers to close slave markets and committed to maintaining squadrons of at least eighty guns on the coast of Africa to suppress the slave trade.

In Ireland, between 1846 and 1848, Lord Dufferin was active in providing relief for the distress caused by the famine. In Italy, Silvestro Dandolo participated in the revolution of 1848.

## Industrial and Academic Progress
Cyrus Hall McCormick, an American inventor of grain-harvesting machinery, moved to Chicago in 1847. There, he established large manufacturing works for his agricultural machines, specifically the McCormick reaper, which had been patented in 1834.

In the academic sphere, Frederick (Lord Dufferin) attended Christ Church, Oxford, from 1845 to 1847, where he served as President of the Union and took a pass school.

## Notable Deaths (1847-1848)
The period recorded the passing of several individuals across various disciplines:

*   **1847:** James MacCullagh, an Irish mathematician and physicist who had held the chair of mathematics at Trinity College, Dublin (1832-1843) and later the chair of natural philosophy, died by his own hand in October 1847 following mental disease induced by overwork. Girolamo Dandolo, the last admiral of the Venetian republic, also died in 1847 as an Austrian admiral.
*   **1848:** Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton, an English politician and financier who served as master of the mint and president of the board of trade, died on May 13, 1848. John Burke, the genealogist, died in 1848. John, 2nd Marquess of Bute, died in 1848. Within the Brontë family, Branwell died on September 24, 1848, followed by Emily on December 19, 1848.
