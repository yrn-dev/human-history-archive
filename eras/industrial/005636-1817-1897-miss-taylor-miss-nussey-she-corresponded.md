# Ellen Nussey (1817-1897)

Ellen Nussey was a lifelong friend and confidante of the novelist Charlotte Brontë. Her relationship with Brontë began during their shared education and continued through a lifelong correspondence that serves as a significant source for the understanding of Brontë's life story.

## Education and Early Friendship
Ellen Nussey and Mary Taylor were two of ten fellow-pupils at the school of Miss Margaret Wooler, located at Roe Head, Dewsbury. Charlotte Brontë became a pupil at this institution in 1831 at the age of sixteen. During their time at Miss Wooler's school, Nussey and Brontë developed a devoted friendship. When Brontë left the school in 1832 to assist in the education of her younger sisters, she maintained this bond with Nussey and Taylor.

## Relationship with Charlotte Brontë
For the remainder of her life, Nussey corresponded with Charlotte Brontë. This correspondence is noted for revealing a substantial portion of Brontë's life history. During the three years following her departure from Roe Head, Brontë's time at Haworth was occasionally varied by visits to Nussey or Taylor.

The connection between the two families extended beyond friendship. In 1838, after Brontë had returned to Haworth, she received an offer of marriage from Henry Nussey, who was the brother of Ellen. Charlotte refused this proposal, as well as a subsequent offer from a curate named Bryce.

## Later Life and Witness to Marriage
Nussey remained a close associate of the Brontë family into the later years of Charlotte's life. On June 29, 1854, Ellen Nussey acted as a witness at the marriage of Charlotte Brontë to Arthur Bell Nicholls, the curate of Mr. Brontë. The ceremony took place at Haworth church and was performed by the Rev. Sutcliffe Sowden; Miss Wooler also served as a witness.
