# Briton-Ferry

Briton-Ferry is a seaport located in Wales within the mid-parliamentary division of Glamorganshire. It is situated on the eastern bank of the estuary of the Neath river in Swansea Bay.

## Transport and Infrastructure
The town is served by rail stations on the Rhondda & Swansea Bay and the Great Western railways, and is located 174 miles from London by rail. Additional transport links include a tram-line that connects Briton-Ferry with Neath, which is 2 miles distant. The Vale of Neath Canal, constructed in 1797, has its terminus in the town.

## Industrial Development
While the district was once celebrated for its scenery, industrial development has considerably marred this. This growth was primarily driven by the construction of a 13-acre dock in 1861, which is the property of the Great Western Railway Company. Around the same time, the South Wales mineral railway opened up the mining districts of Maesteg and Glyncorrwg. This railway connects these districts to the dock and provides it with coal, which serves as the chief export.

In addition to coal, the town has a large-scale manufacturing industry for tinplates and steel. The area also contains a foundry and iron-works.

## History and Demographics
The population of the urban district was 6,973 in 1901. The town's naming reflects its history; Norman settlers of the 12th century called the ferry across the Neath estuary "La Brittone." This ferry is still in use and was crossed in 1188 by Giraldus and Archbishop Baldwin. However, the Welsh name for the town, Llansawel, has been used since at least the 16th century.
