# Stanley Bridge at Brisbane

The Stanley bridge is a structure located in Brisbane, the capital of Queensland, Australia. Brisbane is situated in Stanley county on the banks of the river Brisbane, approximately 25 meters from its mouth in Moreton Bay.

## Engineering Specifications and Working Stress
In the construction of the Stanley bridge, specific limits for working stress were adopted for various structural components. These limits are measured in tons per square inch. The adopted limits for the bridge are as follows:

*   **Compression boom:** 6.5 tons per sq. in.
*   **Tension boom:** 7.0 tons per sq. in.
*   **Vertical struts:** 5.0 tons per sq. in.
*   **Diagonal ties:** 6.5 tons per sq. in.
*   **Wind bracing:** 8.0 tons per sq. in.
*   **Cross and rail girders:** 6.5 tons per sq. in.

These specifications are noted as examples of the limits adopted by English bridge-builders, who are sometimes hampered in adopting rational limits of working stress by the rules of the Board of Trade and do not all follow the guidance of Wöhler's law.

## Context of Brisbane
The city of Brisbane is built on a series of hills rising from the river-banks. Certain areas, including South Brisbane and Woollongabba, occupy low-lying flats that have experienced disastrous floods. The city's principal buildings and main streets are located on a tongue of land created by a southward bend of the river.

Key landmarks in the vicinity include the Houses of Parliament, the botanical gardens, and the grounds of Government House, which serves as the official residence of the colony's governor. Queen's Street serves as the main thoroughfare, crossing Albert Street and leading via the Victoria Bridge to the separate city of South Brisbane. The Victoria Bridge is a steel structure that replaced a previous bridge destroyed by floods in February 1893.

Other prominent architectural features of the city include:
*   The great domed custom-house on the river-bank.
*   The general post-office and the lands office.
*   The town halls of South Brisbane and Brisbane.
*   The opera house.
*   The Roman Catholic cathedral of St Stephen, which features a detached campanile housing the largest bell in Australia.
*   The Anglican cathedral in Ann Street, whose foundation-stone was laid in 1901 by the prince of Wales (the duke of York).

The city serves as the seat for an Anglican bishop and a Roman Catholic archbishop. Notable commercial and private buildings include the massive treasury buildings and the Queensland National Bank, which is described as a classic Italian structure.
