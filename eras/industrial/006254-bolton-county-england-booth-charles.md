# BOLTON

## Geography and Administration
Bolton (also known as Bolton-le-Moors) is a municipal, parliamentary, and county borough located in Lancashire, England. It is situated 11 miles northwest of Manchester and 196 miles north-by-northwest of London. The borough covers an area of 15,279 acres and is divided into Great Bolton and Little Bolton by the Croal, which is a small tributary of the river Irwell. As its alternative name suggests, the town is surrounded by high moorland.

The parliamentary borough was created in 1832 and returns two members. As of 1901, the population of Bolton was 168,215, an increase from 146,487 in 1891.

## Industry and Infrastructure
The primary source of industry in Bolton is a large number of cotton mills. The town's damp climate specifically assists the specialty of fine spinning. Other significant industrial activities include the bleaching, dyeing, and printing of calico and cotton, as well as spinning and weaving machine making. Additionally, the area is home to iron and steel works and nearby collieries.

The town is served by the Lancashire & Yorkshire and the London & North-Western railways, with the Midland railway also possessing running powers. It is further connected via the Manchester & Bolton canal.

## Urban Development and Landmarks
While Bolton is of early origin, its appearance is described as wholly modern, similar to other great manufacturing towns in the vicinity. The only remaining structures of antiquity are two 16th-century houses in Little Bolton, one of which is a notable example of Tudor work. 

The town's religious center is the church of St Peter; while a parish church existed on the site in the 12th century or earlier, the current building dates from 1870. Other notable municipal and civic structures include:
* A town hall featuring a tower and a fine classical facade.
* A market hall and an exchange.
* Assembly rooms and various benevolent institutions.
* Museums dedicated to art, industry, and natural history.
* Several free libraries.

Education is provided through municipal technical schools and Lever's grammar school, which was founded in 1641. Former masters of the grammar school include John Lempriere, author of the classical dictionary, and the Latin lexicographer Robert Ainsworth.

For recreation, the town has various smaller pleasure grounds and a large public park opened in 1866, which was originally laid out as relief work for unemployed operatives during the cotton famine of the early 1860s. To the northwest on the moors is another public park that includes Rivington Pike, which reaches a height of 1,192 feet.

## Military History
During the English Civil War, Bolton was a site of conflict. In 1643, James Stanley, the 7th Earl of Derby, failed in an attempt to take Bolton Castle. However, in the summer of 1644, Bolton Castle was successfully taken during Prince Rupert's campaign in the north.
