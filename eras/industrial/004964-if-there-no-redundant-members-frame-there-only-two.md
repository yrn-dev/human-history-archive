# Frame Members and Reciprocal Figures

In the study of structural frames, the presence or absence of redundant members determines the number of members abutting at a point of support and the method by which stresses are computed.

## Members at the Point of Support
When a frame contains no redundant members, only two members will abut at the point of support. These two members are sufficient to balance the reaction, regardless of the direction of that reaction. To analyze this, two triangles can be drawn, each utilizing the reaction YX as one side, with the other two sides drawn parallel to the two members. Each of these triangles represents a polygon of forces in equilibrium at the point of support.

By selecting the triangle where the lines XE and YE are parallel to the corresponding members in the frame (naming the apex E), the triangle YXE becomes the reciprocal figure of the three lines YX, XE, and EY. This figure represents the three forces in equilibrium at point YXE of the frame.

## Force Direction and Magnitude
The direction of the reaction YX, which is a thrust upwards, indicates the direction to travel around the triangle YXE to determine the direction of the other two forces. This analysis reveals that the force XE acts down towards point YXE, while the force EY acts away from that point. Consequently, member EY acts as a tie (pulling), and member XE acts as a strut (pushing).

At point XEFA, the forces include the downward load XA and the push from the strut XE. Because the strut is in compression, it pushes at both ends. These forces are represented in a polygon of forces (EXAF), which is the reciprocal figure of the lines meeting at that point in the frame. The polygon is completed by drawing EF parallel to the member EF in the frame, with point F determined by the intersection of lines beginning at A and E.

## Reciprocal Figures
The concept of reciprocal figures was defined by J. Clerk Maxwell in 1864. Two plane figures are considered reciprocal when they consist of an equal number of lines such that:
1. Corresponding lines in the two figures are parallel.
2. Corresponding lines that converge to a point in one figure form a closed polygon in the other.

If a frame without redundant members and its external equilibrium forces are represented by one of these plane figures, the reciprocal figure represents the magnitude and direction of the forces between the joints of the frame, and thus the stress on each member.

## Calculation of Stresses
Reciprocal figures provide a simple method for computing stresses on frame members. According to a proposition in statics, external forces on a bridge or frame in equilibrium can be represented by a closed polygon. Each side of this polygon is parallel to one force and represents that force's magnitude and direction. The sides may be arranged in any order, provided that passing around the polygon in one direction corresponds to the direction of the forces represented.
