# Bombay

## Development and Population Growth
The development of Bombay entered a period of unrestricted growth following the annexation of the Deccan and the British victory over the Mahrattas. During this era, the city's fortunes were vigorously fostered by Mountstuart Elphinstone in 1819. The population of the city experienced significant increases during the 19th century; by 1838, the population had reached 236,000. Over the following fifty years, this number more than doubled, reaching 821,000 by 1891.

This rapid expansion is attributed to several key factors: the foundation of cotton factories, the opening of the Suez Canal, and the influence of railways, with the first railway line being completed in 1853.

## Economic Fluctuations and Challenges
The city's prosperity faced a disruption during 1866-1867 due to a financial crisis. This crisis was caused by a fall in the price of cotton following the termination of the American war. Despite this setback, Bombay recovered and was more prosperous by 1891 than at any previous time.

However, the decade following 1891 was marked by great havoc caused by plague, which negatively impacted both trade and the population. During this period, the population declined by 6% and exports fell by 7%. In contrast, the exports of Calcutta rose by 38% during the same timeframe.

## Notable Figures and Institutions
Sir George Christopher Molesworth Birdwood (1832– ) was a prominent Anglo-Indian official and writer born in Belgaum, within the Bombay presidency. After entering the Bombay Medical Service in 1854 and serving in the Persian War of 1856-57, he held several significant positions in Bombay, including:
* Professor at the Grant Medical College
* Registrar of the university
* Curator of the museum
* Sheriff of Bombay
* Secretary of the Horticultural and Asiatic societies

Birdwood was highly influential and popular in the municipal life of the city. He authored a work titled *Economic Vegetable Products of the Bombay Presidency*, which reached its twelfth edition in 1868. In the field of journalism, he edited the Bombay *Saturday Review* and assisted in the conversion of the *Standard* into the *Times of India*.
