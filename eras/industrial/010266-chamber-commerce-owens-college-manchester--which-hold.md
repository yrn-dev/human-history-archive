# Chamber of Commerce and Owens College, Manchester

## Educational Role in Book-keeping
In England, several organizations have provided formal assessment and certification in the field of book-keeping. Among these are the London Chamber of Commerce and Owens College, Manchester, as well as the Society of Arts. These bodies hold examinations in book-keeping and grant diplomas to candidates who are successful. 

This approach to the subject differs from that of most technical schools and polytechnics, which provide instruction in book-keeping but generally regard the subject merely as a "craft." In contrast, the scientific study of book-keeping has been treated more seriously and upon adequate lines by specific universities, including the Victoria University of Manchester, the university of Birmingham, and the London School of Economics (university of London).

## Owens College, Manchester
Owens College, located in Manchester, serves as an institution for both academic study and the preservation of scientific collections. The college has been used to house significant scientific materials, such as the large collection of fossils provided by the English geologist Edward William Binney (1812-1881). Binney, who settled in Manchester in 1836 and was an authority on Coal Measures, contributed his collection to the college following his geological pursuits in the north of England and Lancashire.

Additionally, the college is associated with historical scholarship. The publication *Owens College Historical Essays*, published in Manchester in 1902, includes works such as those by T. F. Tout regarding the events of the "Mad Parliament" of Oxford and the career of Hugh le Despenser.
