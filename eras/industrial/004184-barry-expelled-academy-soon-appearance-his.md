# Barry and the Royal Academy

## Academic Appointment and Tenure
Following a period of time spent on the continent, Barry was selected as a member of the Royal Academy. In 1782, he was appointed to the position of professor of painting, taking the place of Mr. Penny. For this role, he received an annual salary of £30. During his tenure, Barry advocated for the acquisition of a collection of paintings by the best masters to serve as models for students, specifically proposing several works from the Orleans collection.

## Expulsion and the Letter to the Dilettanti Society
Barry's recommendations regarding the collection of pictures were not well-received. In 1799, he was expelled from the academy. This expulsion occurred shortly after the publication of his *Letter to the Dilettanti Society*. The publication is described as eccentric and amusing, characterized by Barry's enthusiasm for art and his simultaneous contempt for the professors of art who were living at the time.

## Later Life and Death
The expulsion from the academy resulted in the loss of Barry's salary. To assist him with his financial difficulties and to provide him with a larger house to complete his painting of Pandora, the earl of Buchan organized a subscription. This effort raised £1000, which was used to purchase an annuity. 

Barry fell ill on February 6, 1806, and died on February 22 of the same year. He was interred in St Paul's on March 14.
