# Henri Alexis Brialmont

Henri Alexis Brialmont (1821–1903) was a Belgian military engineer and general who developed influential systems of permanent fortification during the late 19th century. Born on May 25, 1821, in Venlo, Limburg, he was the son of General Laurent Mathieu Brialmont.

## Early Career and Education
Brialmont was educated at the Brussels military school and entered the army in 1843 as a sub-lieutenant of engineers. He rose through the ranks to become a lieutenant in 1847, a major in 1861, a lieutenant-colonel in 1864, a colonel in 1868, and a major-general in 1874. Between 1847 and 1850, he served as the private secretary to General Baron Chazal, the war minister.

## Evolution of Fortification Principles
In the initial stage of his engineering career, Brialmont's plans were based on the ideas of Vauban with only slight modifications. His original scheme for the fortification of Antwerp utilized a bastioned trace for both the forts and the enceinte. However, by 1859, during the implementation of the great entrenched camp at Antwerp, Brialmont shifted his approach toward the ideas of Montalembert and the school of polygonal fortification.

Following the events of 1870–71 and the introduction of long-range guns, which created a general confusion of ideas regarding fortification, Brialmont's own specific types and plans became prominent. His final principles were applied to the fortifications on the Meuse at Namur and Liége, as well as the extreme detached forts of the Antwerp region. These principles were characterized by:
* The multiplication of intermediate batteries.
* The suppression of all artillery positions that were open to overhead fire.
* The lavish use of armour to protect artillery located inside the forts.

## Administrative Roles and Publications
Brialmont served as the director of fortifications in the Antwerp district starting in December 1874. Nine months later, he became the inspector-general of the corps of engineers and of fortifications, a role in which he drafted and executed the entire scheme for the defences of Belgium. He attained the rank of lieutenant-general in 1877.

An indefatigable writer, Brialmont originated the *Journal de l'armée Belge* in 1850. Throughout his career, he produced forty-nine pamphlets and twenty-three important works, including essays and reviews. His most significant publications include:
* *La Fortification du temps présent* (1885)
* *Influence du tir plongeant et des obus-torpilles sur la fortification* (1888)
* *Les Régions fortifiées* (1890)
* *La Défense des états et la fortification à la fin du XIX^e siècle* (1895)
* *Progrès de la défense des états et de la fortification permanente depuis Vauban* (1888)

## International Consultancy and Later Years
Brialmont's far-reaching schemes for Belgian fortifications encountered significant opposition, leading to personal disappointment. In 1883, he traveled to Rumania to advise on the country's defence and presided over the plan to transform Bucharest into a first-class fortress. Because he undertook the Bucharest works without the authorization of his sovereign—partly due to suggestions from Austria, which viewed the works as a menace—he was placed *en disponibilité* in his own service.

Despite this, his expertise was considered too valuable to lose, and he resumed command of the Antwerp military district upon his return to Belgium in 1884. During his time in eastern Europe, he also prepared a scheme for the defence of Greece at the request of the Hellenic government. Brialmont retired in 1886, though he continued to supervise the defences in Rumania until his death on September 21, 1903.

## The Bucharest Fortifications
The fortifications of Bucharest, constructed between 1885 and 1896, were based on Brialmont's 1883 project. These defences are of special technical importance as they embody the Brialmont system. The perimeter of the defences is approximately 48 meters, defended by 36 batteries and armoured forts, with the forts situated at a mean distance of 4 meters from the city.
