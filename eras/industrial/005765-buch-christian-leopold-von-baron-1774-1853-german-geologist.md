# BUCH, CHRISTIAN LEOPOLD VON, BARON (1774-1853)

Christian Leopold von Buch, Baron, was a German geographer and geologist born on April 26, 1774, at Stolpe in Pomerania. A member of an ancient and noble Prussian family, he pursued his early scientific education at the mining school of Freiberg between 1790 and 1793. During his time there, he studied under Werner and was a fellow student of Alexander von Humboldt. He later completed his academic training at the universities of Göttingen and Halle.

## Early Works and Neptunian Theory
In his early career, von Buch was a zealous supporter of the Neptunian theory advocated by his master, Werner. During this period, he advocated for the aqueous origin of other formations and basaltic rocks. His early publications include *Versuch einer mineralogischen Beschreibung von Landeck* (Breslau, 1797), which was later translated into English in 1810 as *Attempt at a Mineralogical Description of Landeck* and into French in 1805. In 1802, he published *Entwurf einer geognostischen Beschreibung von Schlesien (Geognostische Beobachtungen auf Reisen durch Deutschland und Italien, Band i.)*.

## Geological Investigations and Shift in Theory
Von Buch's adherence to Neptunianism began to waver following a series of excursions. In 1797, he met Alexander von Humboldt at Salzburg, and together they explored the Alps and the geological formations of Styria. In the spring of 1798, von Buch traveled into Italy, where his faith in the Neptunian theory was shaken.

His observations of extinct volcanoes in Auvergne in 1802 further influenced his scientific views. Specifically, the strata of basaltic lava and the cone of trachyte at the Puy de Dôme led him to conclude that the doctrines of Werner regarding the formation of these rocks were untenable. He documented the results of these investigations in his work *Geognostische Beobachtungen auf Reisen durch Deutschland und Italien*, published in Berlin between 1802 and 1809.

## Volcanology and Northern Studies
Von Buch conducted significant studies on Vesuvius, visiting the volcano first in 1799 and returning in 1805. During the 1805 visit, he was accompanied by Gay Lussac and Humboldt. The group witnessed a remarkable eruption, providing von Buch with the necessary data to refute various erroneous ideas that existed at the time concerning volcanoes.

Following his work in southern Europe, von Buch traveled north and spent two years among the Scandinavian islands. During this period, he conducted important observations regarding geology, climatology, and the geography of plants. His research in the north allowed him to demonstrate that many of the erratic blocks found on the North German plains must have been transported.
