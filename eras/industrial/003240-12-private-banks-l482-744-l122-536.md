# Private Banks in Ireland (1907)

The notes issued by these country banks were almost unknown outside the immediate neighborhood of the locations where they were issued. Although these notes were payable in London, individuals often encountered considerable difficulty when attempting to cash them.

## Evolution of Private Banking Structures
The nature of private banks was shaped by various legal restrictions and acts throughout the 19th century. Until 1862, the number of partners in private banks was restricted to six. An act passed in 1862 increased this limit to ten, although this change did not apply to issuing private banks, which remained restricted to six partners. This increase to ten partners was rarely utilized.

The ability of private firms to conduct business on a large scale was further hindered by legal technicalities, which made large private banks practically impossible under ordinary circumstances. Consequently, banking did not reach its modern form until approximately the middle of the 19th century, following a series of acts passed in 1826-1833, 1844-1845, 1862, and 1879.

## The Impact of the 1844 Act
The act of 1844 had a significant influence on the banking system in England and Wales. While it initially favored small banks, it eventually led to the formation of larger, more powerful banks. Under this act, no new banks of issue were permitted. Private issuing banks with no more than six partners were allowed to continue, amalgamate with other private issuing banks, and retain their joint issues. However, if a private issuing bank joined or was formed into a joint-stock bank, its issue lapsed.

Prior to the act's implementation on October 12, 1844, nine banks (eight private and one joint-stock) ceased issuing notes. Of these, one private bank closed and the Western District Joint-Stock Banking Co. was dissolved; the remaining seven transitioned to issuing Bank of England notes.

## Regional Variations in Private Banking
The prevalence of private banking varied by region. In Scotland, private banking never held a considerable position. While eight private banks existed in 1819, they had all disappeared by 1844. By 1906, the Scottish banking business was practically carried out by ten banks of issue, which operated 1,180 branches.

In England and Wales, the number of private bank offices was 336 in 1844. By 1906, the banking business in England was primarily conducted by approximately sixty joint-stock banks and ten private banks, some of which were private firms organized under a joint-stock form.

## Banking Office Statistics
The number of banking offices in the United Kingdom grew significantly between 1844 and 1906. In Ireland, the number of offices increased from 180 in 1844 to 777 in 1906. This growth affected the ratio of inhabitants to each office; in 1844, there were 45,417 inhabitants per office in Ireland, which decreased to 5,738 by 1906.
