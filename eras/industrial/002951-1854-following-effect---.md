# Events and Legislation of 1854

The year 1854 was marked by significant diplomatic treaties, the enactment of various legal statutes in English and Canadian jurisdictions, and contributions to literature and science.

## Diplomatic Relations in Baluchistan
In 1854, under the governor-generalship of the Marquess of Dalhousie, the British government sought to formalize relations with the Kalat state. General John Jacob, C.B., who served as the commandant and political superintendent on the Sind frontier, was deputed to arrange a treaty with Nasir Khan, the chieftain of Kalat. 

The resulting treaty, executed on May 14, 1854, was an alliance of an offensive and defensive nature. Its primary effects included:
* The annulment of a previous offensive and defensive treaty concluded in 1841 between Major Outram and Nasir Khan II.
* A commitment by Nasir Khan II and his successors to oppose all enemies of the British government and to act in subordinate co-operation with said government.
* A requirement that the Kalat state obtain British consent before entering negotiations with other states.
* Provisions allowing British troops to occupy positions within Kalat territory as deemed advisable by British authorities.
* An obligation for the Baluch chief to prevent his subjects from plundering within or near British territory.

This treaty maintained a policy of non-intervention until it was renewed in 1876 by Lord Lytton.

## English Legal Legislation
Several key pieces of legislation were enacted in England during 1854 concerning financial transactions and electoral integrity.

### Bills of Sale Act 1854
The Bills of Sale Act 1854 represented the first English legislation on its subject. It was introduced to address frauds committed against creditors via secret bills of sale of personal chattels. Such secrets allowed individuals to maintain the appearance of possessing property and being in good circumstances, while granting holders of the bills the power to take possession of property to the exclusion of other creditors. The act mandated that all bills of sale, as defined by the legislation, be registered or they would be void against execution creditors.

### Corrupt Practices Prevention Act 1854
In the realm of election law, the Corrupt Practices Prevention Act 1854 was passed as part of a series of statutes intended to prevent bribery and corruption. This specific act repealed all former legislation on the matter. It was later incorporated into the Corrupt and Illegal Practices Prevention Act 1883, which defined bribery as the offering of money, valuable consideration, or employment to induce a voter to vote or refrain from voting.

## Canadian Political Developments
In Canada, 1854 saw shifts in both the executive and legislative branches. Macdonald served as the attorney-general of Upper Canada from 1854 until 1857, eventually becoming prime minister following the retirement of Colonel Taché. Additionally, Dorion began his tenure as a member of the assembly for the province of Canada, representing the city of Montreal from 1854 to 1861.

## Literature, Science, and Academia
The year 1854 saw the publication of several notable works and academic classifications.

### Literary Works
The author Kingsley published *Alexandria and her Schools* and the second series of *Sermons on National Subjects* in 1854. In the field of linguistics and folklore, the first volume of the *Deutsches Worterbuch* was published in 1854. Additionally, second editions of *Deutsche Rechtsaltertumer* and *Deutsche Mythologie* (in two volumes) were released that year.

### Biological Classification
In the field of zoology, Zenker transferred the family *Argulidae* to the *Branchiopoda* in 1854. This family was later referred to as *Branchiura* by Thorell in 1864.
