# Samuel Bailey

## Life and Career
Samuel Bailey (1791–1870) was a British author and philosopher born in Sheffield. In his early life, he was among the first Sheffield merchants to travel to the United States for the purpose of establishing trade connections. After spending several years working in the business of his father, Bailey retired from all business concerns with an ample fortune, with the sole exception of the Sheffield Banking Company, where he served as chairman for many years.

Politically, Bailey was an ardent liberal, though he did not participate extensively in political affairs. He attempted to stand for Sheffield as a "philosophic radical" on two separate occasions, but these bids were unsuccessful. Bailey died suddenly on January 18, 1870, and left a bequest of over L80,000 to the town of Sheffield.

## Literary and Philosophical Works
The majority of Bailey's life was defined by his varied and numerous publications. His writings are characterized by originality of treatment, elegance of style, and lucidity, though they are not considered to be of first-rate importance.

His first publication, *Essays on the Formation and Publication of Opinions*, was published anonymously in 1821. This work attracted more attention than any of his other writings and saw subsequent editions in 1826 and 1837. In 1829, he published a sequel titled *Essays on the Pursuit of Truth*, which had a second edition in 1844.

Between the publication of these two works, Bailey wrote *Politics, Morals, &c.* and *Questions in Political Economy* (1823), as well as a *Critical Dissertation on the Nature, Measure, and Causes of Value* (1825). The latter work was directed against the school and opinions of Ricardo.

## Economic and Political Writings
Bailey continued to publish on political and economic subjects. In 1835, he released *Rationale of Political Representation*, followed by *Money and its Vicissitudes* in 1837; both of these works are now practically forgotten. During the same period, he authored several pamphlets, including *Defence of Joint-Stock Banks*, *Right of Primogeniture Examined*, and *Discussion of Parliamentary Reform*.

## Later Works and Critiques
In 1842, Bailey published a Review of *Berkeley's Theory of Vision*. This work was considered able and prompted rejoinders from Ferrier in *Blackwood* (reprinted in *Lectures and Remains*, ii) and from J. S. Mill in the *Westminster Review* (reprinted in *Dissertations*). Bailey responded to these critics in 1843 with a publication titled *Letter to a Philosopher*.

His later work includes *Theory of Reasoning*, published in 1851 (with a second edition in 1852). This text provided a discussion on the nature of inference and offered an able criticism regarding the value and functions of the syllogism.
