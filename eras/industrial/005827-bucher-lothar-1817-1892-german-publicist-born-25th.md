# BUCHER, LOTHAR (1817-1892)

## Early Life and Education
Lothar Bucher was a German publicist born on October 25, 1817, in Neu Stettin, Pomerania. His father served as a master at a gymnasium. Bucher pursued his higher education at the university of Berlin, after which he entered the legal profession.

## Political Activism and Exile
In 1848, Bucher was elected as a member of the National Assembly in Berlin, where he emerged as an active leader of the extreme democratic party. His political activities led to legal consequences in 1850 when he and several colleagues were brought to trial for organizing a movement to refuse the payment of taxes. He was sentenced to fifteen months of imprisonment in a fortress; however, he left the country before the sentence could be executed.

Bucher spent the following ten years living in exile, primarily in London. During this period, he served as a special correspondent for the *National Zeitung*, which allowed him to acquire an extensive knowledge of English life. He also authored a work titled *Der Parliamentarismus wie er ist*, a critique of parliamentary government that indicated a significant shift in his political views.

## Career under Bismarck
Bucher returned to Germany in 1860 and developed a close relationship with Lassalle, who eventually appointed him as his literary executor. In 1864, Bucher accepted an offer from Bismarck for a high-ranking position within the Prussian foreign office. The specific reasons for this transition, which marked a complete break from his previous associations and friends, are not clearly known.

From that point until his death, Bucher served as Bismarck's secretary and was likely the individual who held the greatest amount of Bismarck's confidence. His contributions to the Prussian and German state were significant:
* He drafted the text of the constitution for the North German Confederation.
* In 1870, he was dispatched on a highly confidential mission to Spain regarding the Hohenzollern candidature for the Spanish crown.
* He assisted Bismarck during the final negotiations for the treaty of Frankfort.
* He served as one of the secretaries to the congress of Berlin.
* He aided Bismarck in the writing of his memoirs.

## Influence and Economic Views
A man of great ability, Bucher wielded considerable influence, which he primarily directed against the economic doctrines held by the Liberals. In 1881, he published a pamphlet that criticized the principles and influence of the Cobden Club. In his later years, he completely identified himself with the colonial and commercial policies of Bismarck and likely played a significant role in introducing those policies.
