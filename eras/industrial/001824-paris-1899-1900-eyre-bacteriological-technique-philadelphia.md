# Bacteriological Literature and Related Publications (1899-1902)

## Bacteriological Texts and Authorities
During the turn of the 20th century, bacteriological literature expanded to an extent that made comprehensive referencing of original articles difficult. To address this, the New Sydenham Society published *Microparasites in Disease: Selected Essays* in 1886, which provided translations of classical researches from German and French.

Several significant books on bacteriology were published between 1891 and 1908. Notable among these are the works of Duclaux, who published *Traite de microbiologie* in Paris between 1899 and 1900, and Eyre, whose work *Bacteriological Technique* was published in Philadelphia and London in 1902. Other key texts from this era include:
*   *Principles of Bacteriology* by Abbott (7th ed., 1905) and a translation of Hueppe's *Principles of Bacteriology* (1899).
*   *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* by Crookshank (4th ed., 1896).
*   *Die Mikroorganismen* by Fluegge (3rd ed., 1896).
*   *Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien* by Fischer (2nd ed., 1902).
*   *Einfuehrung in das Studium der Bakteriologie* by Guenther (6th ed., 1906).
*   *Manual of Bacteriology* by Hewlett (2nd ed., 1902), and another *Manual of Bacteriology* by Muir and Ritchie (4th ed., 1908).
*   *Micro-organisms and Disease* by Klein (3rd ed., 1896).
*   *Handbuch der pathogenen Mikroorganismen* by Kolle and Wassermann (1904), which is identified as the most important work on the subject and continued to receive supplements.
*   *Vorlesungen ueber die geschichtliche Entwickelung der Lehre von der Bacterien* by Loeffler (1887).
*   *Text-book upon the Pathogenic Bacteria* by McFarland (5th ed., 1906).
*   *Pathogenic Micro-organisms* by Park (1906).
*   *Manual of Bacteriology* by Sternberg (2nd ed., 1896).
*   *Bacteria and their products* by Woodhead (1891).

For detailed information on the bacteriology of infective diseases, the *System of Medicine* (2nd ed., 1907), edited by Clifford Allbutt, is cited. Researchers are directed to the *Index Medicus* and *Centralbl. fuer Bakter. u. Parasitenk.* in Jena for further references.

## Studies in Immunity
The archive identifies two primary authorities regarding the study of immunity: Ehrlich, author of *Studies in Immunity* (English translation, New York, 1906), and Metchnikoff, author of *Immunity in Infective Dis*.

## Related Academic Contributions
The field of pathology and bacteriology also involved contributors such as Robert Muir, M.D., F.R.C.P. (Edin.), a Professor of Pathology at the University of Glasgow and formerly at St Andrews from 1898 to 1899. Muir is the author of the *Manual of Bacteriology* and contributed specifically to the "Pathological Aspects" of bacteriology.
