# Pierre Napoleon

## Early Life and Adventures
Pierre Napoleon was born in Rome on September 12, 1815. He was the son of Lucien Bonaparte. At the age of fifteen, in 1830-1831, he began a life of adventure by joining insurrectionary bands in the Romagna. His travels subsequently took him to the United States to join his uncle Joseph, and in 1832, he traveled to Colombia to be with General Santander. Upon returning to Rome, he was taken prisoner by order of the pope between 1835 and 1836, after which he sought refuge in England.

## Political Career in France
During the revolution of 1848, Pierre Napoleon returned to France. He was elected as the deputy for Corsica to the Constituent Assembly. During this period, he identified as an "out-and-out republican" and aligned his voting record with the socialists. His political positions included support for the national workshops and opposition to the *loi Falloux*.

These political stances were significant in helping his cousin, Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III), gain popular confidence. Although Pierre Napoleon disapproved of the *coup d'etat* carried out by Louis Napoleon on December 2, 1851, he was soon reconciled with the emperor. He accepted the title of prince, an action that led the republicans to abandon him.

## Later Life and Legal Controversy
Following his reconciliation with the emperor, Pierre Napoleon lost his political importance and led a debauched life. He turned his attention to literature, publishing several poems described as mediocre.

In January 1870, he became prominent again due to a violent incident stemming from a controversy with Paschal Grousset. Grousset sent two journalists to provoke Pierre Napoleon into a duel. During a violent discussion with these men, Pierre Napoleon drew his revolver and killed one of them, a man named Victor Noir. This crime caused significant excitement within the republican press, which demanded a trial. Pierre Napoleon was ultimately acquitted by the High Court, which subsequently led to criticism of the government.

## Family and Death
Pierre Napoleon married Justine Eleanore Ruffin, the daughter of a working-man from Paris. Before their marriage, he had two children:
*   **Roland Napoleon**: Born on May 19, 1858. He entered the army but was excluded from it in 1886, subsequently dedicating himself to scientific explorations and geography.
*   **Jeanne**: The wife of the marquis de Vence.

Pierre Napoleon died in obscurity at Versailles on April 7, 1881.
