## Brewing Industry in the United Kingdom
During the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the United Kingdom saw a shift in the materials used for brewing. Prior to the repeal of the Malt Acts, sugar was the only permitted substitute for malt. The use of sugar as a substitute increased significantly over several decades, rising from 295,865 cwt. in 1870 to 1,136,434 cwt. in 1880, and eventually reaching 2,746,615 cwt. by 1905. This indicates that the quantity of sugar used had practically trebled over a twenty-five-year period, while the amount of malt employed did not show a material increase.

By 1905, other substitutes had also gained favor, including preparations of maize and rice, as well as unmalted corn. In that year, 1,348,558 cwt. of rice, maize, and similar substances were used, along with 125,671 bushels of unmalted corn.
