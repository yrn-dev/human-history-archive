# Minerals of Bohemia

Bohemia possesses a diverse array of mineral wealth, with almost every useful metal and mineral present in the region, with the sole exception of salt, which is entirely absent.

## Fuel and Energy Resources
Lignite and coal are the most significant minerals in Bohemia in terms of both value and quantity. Some of the richest lignite fields in Europe are situated in the north-east corner of the region, specifically around Teplitz, Ossegg, Falkenau, Dux, and Brux. Coal mining is conducted around Radnitz, Nurschan, Rakonitz, Schlan, Pilsen, Buschtehrad, and Kladno. The oldest coal mines in Bohemia date back to the 17th century and are located at Radnitz. Additionally, a great quantity of asphalt and a large amount of peat are collected, particularly in the south-west of Bohemia.

## Metals and Industrial Minerals
Bohemia is an important center for the iron industry due to improvements in refining. Iron ores are located at Nucic and Krusnahora, with the primary foundries situated around Konigshof and Kladno. 

The region also extracts several other metals:
*   **Silver:** Extracted at Joachimsthal and Pribram. While the silver mines near Kuttenberg were famous during the middle ages, they have since been abandoned.
*   **Lead:** Extracted at Pribram.
*   **Tin:** Found at Graupen in the Erzgebirge, which is the only location in Austria where this metal is found.
*   **Other Metals:** The region contains copper, cobalt, nickel, and antimony, the latter of which is extracted at Milleschau near Tabor.

Other specialized minerals include uranium and radium near Joachimsthal, graphite near Budweis and Krumau, and porcelain-earth near Carlsbad.

## Miscellaneous Minerals and Gemstones
Bohemia is home to various other minerals, including sulphur, alum, and arsenic. The region is also known for its building stone and various precious stones, specifically the Bohemian garnet, also known as pyrope.
