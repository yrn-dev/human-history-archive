# BONAR, HORATIUS (1808-1889)

## Early Life and Education
Horatius Bonar was a Scottish Presbyterian divine born in Edinburgh on December 19, 1808. He received his early education at the high school and the university of his native city.

## Ministerial Career
Bonar began his professional service with a term of mission work at Leith. In 1837, he was appointed as the parish minister of Kelso. Following the Disruption of 1843, he became a minister of the newly formed Free Church. He served in this capacity until 1866, at which point he moved to the Chalmers memorial church in Edinburgh.

Throughout his career, Bonar achieved several distinguished honors. In 1853, he was awarded a D.D. degree from Aberdeen University. Later, in 1883, he served as the moderator of the general assembly of his church. He died on July 31, 1889.

## Literary and Editorial Work
Bonar was a prolific author of religious literature and held editorial roles for several journals. These included the *Presbyterian Review*, the *Christian Treasury*, and the *Quarterly Journal of Prophecy*. 

He is particularly noted for his contributions to hymnology. Between 1857 and 1866, he published three series titled *Hymns of Faith and Hope*, which saw a new edition in 1886. His influence on modern hymnody is significant, with nearly every modern hymnal containing approximately twenty of his hymns. Notable examples of his work include:
* "I heard the voice of Jesus say"
* "Go, labour on"
* "When the weary, seeking rest"
* "Here, O my Lord, I see Thee face to face"

## Other Contributions
Beyond his own writing and ministry, Bonar contributed to the preservation of the work of other divines. He edited the *Memoirs and Remains* of Robert Murray McCheyne (1813-1843), a Scottish divine who had held pastorates in Dundee and Larbert. The edited volume of McCheyne's works became highly influential, running into more than one hundred English editions.
