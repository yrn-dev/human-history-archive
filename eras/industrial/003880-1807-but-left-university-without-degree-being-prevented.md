# 1807: Notable Figures and Events

The year 1807 marks a point of transition for several figures across the fields of academia, religion, and education, characterized by shifts in professional appointments and the pursuit of scholarly goals.

## Academic Pursuits and Obstacles
In 1807, Edmund Henry Barker, an English classical scholar born in Yorkshire, entered Trinity College, Cambridge, as a scholar. Despite his academic achievements—which included obtaining the Browne medal for Greek and Latin epigrams in 1809—Barker eventually left the university without receiving a degree. This was due to religious scruples that prevented him from taking the oath required at that time. Barker later became a prolific writer and lexicographer, known for editing classical authors and working on a new edition of Stephanus's *Thesaurus Graecae Linguae*.

Similar obstacles regarding institutional requirements were faced by others in the Cambridge system. Augustus De Morgan, who entered Trinity College at age sixteen and a half and placed fourth in the mathematical tripos in 1827, was later prevented from obtaining a fellowship or his M.A. degree. This was caused by his conscientious objection to signing the theological tests required of fellows and masters of arts.

## Religious and Ministerial Transitions
The year 1807 saw significant movements in the ministry of both American and German clergymen. Hosea Ballou, an American Universalist clergyman and the principal American expositor of Universalism, concluded a period of preaching in Barnard, Vermont, and the surrounding towns (1801–1807). In 1807, he began a new tenure preaching at Portsmouth, New Hampshire, a position he held until 1815. Ballou is noted for divesting Universalism of trinitarian views, legalism, and traces of Calvinism.

In Germany, Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider, a scholar and theologian, made a career change in 1807. Having previously served as a *Privat-docent* at the university of Wittenberg from 1804 to 1806, where he lectured on theology and philosophy, he decided to abandon his university career due to the advance of Napoleon's French army into Prussia. Through the assistance of his patron, F.V. Reinhard, Bretschneider became the pastor of Schneeberg in Saxony in 1807.

## Educational Developments
In England, 1807 was a pivotal year for Andrew Bell, a British divine and educationalist. Bell had developed a "monitorial" plan of mutual tuition based on his experiences as superintendent of a male orphan asylum in Madras, India. While his views were initially published in a small pamphlet in 1797, the system gained momentum through the work of Joseph Lancaster. 

Because the success of these schools was largely supported by Nonconformists, members of the Church of England sought to establish similar institutions that would inculcate their own principles. Consequently, in 1807, Bell was called from his rectory of Swanage in Dorset to organize a system of schools aligned with these views. This work eventually led to his appointment in 1811 as the superintendent of the National Society for Promoting the Education of the Poor in the Principles of the Established Church.

## Other Notable Deaths
The year 1807 also marked the passing of John Douglas, a Scottish man of letters and Anglican bishop. Born in 1721, Douglas had served as the bishop of Carlisle (from 1887) and the bishop of Salisbury (from 1791). He was known for his literary controversies, including his defense of Milton and his attacks on the Hutchinsonians and David Hume's rationalism. He died on May 18, 1807.
