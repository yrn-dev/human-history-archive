# Accounting and Book-keeping Bibliography

## Overview of Accounting Authorities
For those researching the bibliography of book-keeping, the catalogue of the library of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales is noted as likely containing the most complete collection of both modern and ancient works on accounting. This collection includes foreign as well as British texts.

## Notable Works on Accounting
Several publications are identified as being of general interest within the field of accounting and book-keeping:

*   **General Book-keeping:** G. van de Linde published *Book-keeping* in 1898. L.R. Dicksee authored *Book-keeping* (the 5th edition of which was released in 1906) and *Advanced Accounting* (2nd edition, 1905).
*   **Reference and Collections:** The *Encyclopaedia of Accounting* was edited by G. Lisle in 1903. Additionally, the editor of *The Accountant* edited the *Accountants' Library* in 1901.
*   **Historical Perspectives:** J.W. Heaps wrote *The Antiquity of Book-keeping* in 1898, and R. Brown edited the *History of Accounting and Accountants* in 1905.

## Related Financial Records
While not part of the accounting bibliography, archival records from the Bank of France provide examples of account types and financial circulation. By the end of 1903, the bank had 59,182 accounts, a number that grew to 66,569. These accounts were categorized as follows:
*   Current accounts with power of discount: 11,178
*   Simple current accounts: 4,576
*   Current accounts with advances: 26,709
*   Deposit accounts: 24,106

The Bank of France primarily operated through note circulation, which averaged £186,300,000 in 1906. The limits of this circulation expanded over several decades, rising from 350 million francs on March 15, 1848, to 5,800 million francs (equivalent to £232,000,000) in 1906.
