## Geographic and Historical Setting
The subject of Feiner's guide, Barcelona, is located in eastern Spain on the Mediterranean Sea. It serves as the capital of the province of Barcelona (since 1833) and was formerly the capital of Catalonia. The city is situated on a small plain between the Llobregat river to the south and the Besos river to the north. Its landscape is characterized by the fortified hills of Montjuich to the south-west, the heights of Tibidabo to the west, and the Montanas Matas to the north-east.

Historically, the city—originally known as Barcino—is traditionally linked to the Carthaginian Hamilcar Barca in the 3rd century B.C. It later became a Roman colony named Julia Faventia under Augustus. By the 2nd century A.D., it had become a leading mart of the western Mediterranean. Following various periods of rule by the Visigoths, Moors (who captured it in 713), and Franks (who took dominion in 801), the counts of Barcelona began ruling as independent monarchs from 874. The city reached a peak of wealth and fame following the union of Catalonia and Aragon in 1149, becoming a major commercial power that traded as far as the Baltic and Alexandria.

## Commercial Significance
The city described in the literature is the principal seaport of Spain. Its port evolved from an open beach to a sophisticated facility; while a mole was licensed in 1438 and the Moll de Santa Creu began in 1474, satisfactory works were not undertaken until the 17th century. Systematic improvements beginning in 1873 added new quays and storage, resulting in an inner and outer harbour that, by 1904, handled 8,075 vessels totaling nearly 5,000,000 tons.

Barcelona's commercial influence was historically codified in the *Consulado del Mar*, a code of maritime law empowered by James I. of Aragon in 1258. The city was also an early adopter of marine insurance. In the modern era, the city is supported by industrial suburbs such as Badalona, which contributes to Barcelona's export trade through the manufacture of glass, sugar, brandy, and various textiles.
