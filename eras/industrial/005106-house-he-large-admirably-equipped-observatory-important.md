# Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane

## Early Life and Military Career
Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane was a Scottish astronomer and soldier born on July 23, 1773, at Brisbane House, located near Largs in Ayrshire. He began his military service in 1789, serving in the Peninsula, the West Indies, and Flanders. In 1814, he was deployed to North America. Although he was recalled following Napoleon's return from Elba, he did not arrive in time to participate in the battle of Waterloo. His military rank eventually reached that of general in 1841.

## Governorship of New South Wales
In 1821, Brisbane was appointed as the governor of New South Wales, a position he held for four years. While the finances of the colony fell into confusion during his tenure, he sought to improve the region's condition through the encouragement of land reclamation and the breeding of horses. He also introduced tobacco plants, sugar-cane, and the vine. Under his instigation, exploring parties were dispatched, one of which discovered the Brisbane river, which was subsequently named in his honor.

## Astronomical Contributions
Brisbane established an astronomical observatory at Paramatta in 1822. Observations conducted at this site between 1822 and 1826 resulted in the *Brisbane Catalogue*, which was printed in 1835 and contained 7,385 stars. The Paramatta observatory was later discontinued in 1855.

Upon returning to Scotland, Brisbane resided primarily at Makerstoun in Roxburghshire. At Makerstoun, as he had at Brisbane House, he maintained a large and admirably equipped observatory. In 1841, he began important magnetic observations at Makerstoun; the results of this work were published in the *Transactions* of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and earned him the Keith prize of the Royal Society of Edinburgh in 1848.

## Public Office and Honors
Brisbane held several prestigious leadership roles within scientific organizations. Following the death of Sir Walter Scott in 1833, he was elected president of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. In the following year, he served as president of the British Association. He was created a baronet in 1836 and received the G.C.B. in 1837.

To further the encouragement of scientific research, he founded two gold medals: one awarded by the Scottish Society of Arts and another by the Royal Society of Edinburgh.

## Death
Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane died on January 27, 1860, at Brisbane House.
