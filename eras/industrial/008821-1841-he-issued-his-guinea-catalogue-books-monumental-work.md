# The "Guinea" Catalogue of Henry George Bohn

The "Guinea" Catalogue is a monumental bibliographical work issued in 1841 by the British publisher Henry George Bohn. The catalogue is noted for its scale, containing a total of 23,208 items.

## Henry George Bohn
Henry George Bohn (1796–1884) was born in London on January 4, 1796. He was the son of a German bookbinder who had settled in England. Bohn entered the book trade in 1831, beginning his career as a dealer in "remainders" and rare books. A man of wide culture and diverse interests, Bohn was a famous rose-grower and a collector of ivories, china, and pictures.

Bohn's professional ambitions included the foundation of a great publishing house. While he achieved significant success, he eventually sold his "Libraries" in 1864 to Messrs. Bell and Daldy (later G. Bell & Sons) after discovering that his sons did not possess a taste for the trade. He died on August 22, 1884, at Twickenham.

## The "Guinea" Catalogue and Auction Sales
Issued in 1841, the "Guinea" Catalogue served as a comprehensive listing of Bohn's offerings. The scope of the work is evidenced by its inclusion of diverse texts; for example, the 1731 work *The Works of the Earls of Rochester, Roscommon and Dorset, the Dukes of Devonshire, Buckinghamshire, &c., with Memoirs of their Lives* was listed as item No. 20841 in the 1841 catalogue.

Beyond his catalogues, Bohn was recognized for his book auction sales. One such sale conducted in 1848 lasted for four days. The catalogue for this event spanned twenty folio pages and included specific scheduling information for the attendees, noting that tea was at 5, dessert at 4, dinner at 2 o'clock, and supper at 10.

## Other Contributions to Publishing
Bohn is principally remembered for the inauguration of his "Libraries" starting in 1846. These consisted of 766 volumes comprising translations and editions of standard works. The subject matter of these libraries was broad, covering archaeology, theology, classics, science, and history. Bohn personally contributed to these collections.

Additionally, Bohn undertook a laborious revision of W.T. Lowndes' 1834 work, *The Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature*. Published in six volumes in 1864, Bohn's revision added considerable material to the original work, including critical and bibliographical notices as well as particulars regarding prices.
