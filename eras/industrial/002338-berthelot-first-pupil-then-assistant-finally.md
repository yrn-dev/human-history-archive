## About Antoine Jerome Balard

Antoine Jerome Balard (1802-1876) was a French chemist born in Montpellier on September 30, 1802. His early career began as an apothecary before he transitioned into teaching. He served as a chemical assistant at the faculty of sciences in his hometown and later held professorships in chemistry at the faculty of sciences and the royal college and school of pharmacy.

Balard gained significant professional reputation in 1826 after discovering bromine, a previously unknown element found in sea-water. This achievement led to his election as the successor to L. J. Thenard in the chair of chemistry at the faculty of sciences in Paris.

In addition to his work with bromine and its compounds, Balard contributed to both applied and pure chemistry. His work included:
* **Chlorine Research:** He was the first to propose that bleaching-powder is a double compound consisting of hypochlorite and calcium chloride.
* **Sea-water Extraction:** He spent considerable time attempting to economically obtain potash and soda from sea-water, though these efforts were eventually nullified by the discovery of Stassfurt deposits, which provided richer sources.
* **Organic Chemistry:** Balard published research on the cyanides, amyl alcohol, the difference in constitution between sulphuric and nitric ether, and the formation of oxamic acid through the decomposition of ammonium oxalate.

Balard died in Paris on April 30, 1876.
