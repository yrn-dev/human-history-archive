# Barnsley

Barnsley, also known as Black or properly Bleak Barnsley, is a municipal borough and market town located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is situated 15 miles north of Sheffield on rising ground to the west of the river Dearne. The town is located within the parish of Silkstone, a name shared by important collieries in the area. As of 1891, the population was 35,427, increasing to 41,086 by 1901.

## Infrastructure and Administration
The borough covers an area of 2,385 acres and is governed by a mayor, eighteen councillors, and six aldermen. Barnsley is well-connected by several railway lines, including the Great Northern, Great Central, Midland, Hull & Barnsley, and Lancashire & Yorkshire railways. Additionally, the town benefits from connections to the Aire and Calder Navigation system of canals.

## Principal Buildings and Institutions
The town features several churches, the oldest of which is the parish church of St Mary; this structure was built in 1821 on an early site. Other significant civic and public buildings include a public hall, a court house, an institute, and a free library.

Educational opportunities in the town include a free grammar school, which dates back to 1665. In 1828, a philosophical society was established. For public recreation, the town has a park that was presented in 1862 by the widow of Joseph Locke, M.P. Additionally, a monument was erected in 1905 to honor prominent members of the Yorkshire Miners' Association.

## Industry and Economy
Barnsley is characterized by numerous factories, though its surrounding neighborhood retains considerable natural beauty. The two primary industries are the weaving of other cloth and linen, and the manufacture of steel and iron. The town's industrial base is further diversified by the presence of:
* Dyeworks, printfields, and bleachfields
* Malt-houses, cornmills, and sawmills
* The manufacture of wire, needles, and glass

The region is supported by large coalfields that extend beneath the town itself. Coal and coke produced in these fields are exported in large quantities to Hull and London.

## Local Landmarks
In the vicinity of Barnsley are two notable historical sites. The first is Monk Bretton Priory, a Cluniac foundation established in 1157. This site retains fragments of the church, some Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse. The second is Wentworth Castle, which was built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford. The castle is situated within a singularly beautiful park and houses a fine collection of portraits of historical interest.
