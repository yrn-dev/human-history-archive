# 1849

The year 1849 was marked by significant literary publications, the deaths of influential figures in education, and pivotal transitions in the personal and professional lives of various individuals across England and America.

## Literary Developments and Charlotte Brontë
In October 1849, Charlotte Brontë published her novel *Shirley*. The writing of this work had been interrupted; while two-thirds of the book were completed, Brontë had laid the story aside to nurse her sister, Anne. She finished the novel following Anne's death. 

Following the publication of *Shirley*, Brontë visited London during the winter as a guest of her publisher, Mr. George Smith. During this visit, she was introduced to Thackeray, the individual to whom she had dedicated her work *Jane Eyre*.

## Deaths and Educational Milestones
The year saw the passing of Mary Mason Lyon, an American educationalist who died at Mount Holyoke on March 5, 1849. Lyon had served as the principal of the Mount Holyoke Female Seminary for nearly twelve years, receiving a salary of $200 per year. Her work is credited as the origin of modern higher education for women in the United States. Lyon's career had been characterized by a commitment to providing educational opportunities for students of moderate means, a plan she had begun developing as early as 1828-1830 through winter "normal" schools at Buckland.

## Personal Transitions and Social Figures
Several notable figures experienced significant life changes in 1849:

*   **Frances Anne Kemble:** The actress and author, known as Fanny Kemble, was divorced in 1849 from Pierce Butler, a Southern planter whom she had married in America in 1834.
*   **William Booth:** The founder of the Salvation Army moved to London in 1849. According to his own account, his passion for open-air preaching led to his severance from the Wesleyan Methodists during this time.
*   **Frances Eliza Hodgson Burnett:** The Anglo-American novelist was born in Manchester, England, on November 24, 1849.

## Other Notable Events and Publications
The year 1849 also saw the beginning of the publication of *Lives of the Princesses of England*, a six-volume work by Mary A. E. Green, which was published between 1849 and 1855.
