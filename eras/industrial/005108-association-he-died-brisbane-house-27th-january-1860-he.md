# Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane

## Early Life and Military Career
Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane was a Scottish astronomer and soldier born on July 23, 1773, at Brisbane House, located near Largs in Ayrshire. He began his military service in 1789, serving in the Peninsula, the West Indies, and Flanders. In 1814, he was dispatched to North America. Although he was recalled following the return of Napoleon from Elba, he did not arrive in time to participate in the battle of Waterloo. His military rank progressed over time, and he became a general in 1841. He was also appointed a baronet in 1836 and G.C.B. in 1837.

## Governance of New South Wales
In 1821, Brisbane was appointed as the governor of New South Wales, a position he held for four years. While the finances of the colony fell into confusion during his tenure, he worked to improve the colony's condition through the encouragement of land reclamation and the breeding of horses. He also introduced tobacco plants, sugar-cane, and the vine. Under his instigation, exploring parties were dispatched, one of which discovered the Brisbane river, which was named in his honor.

## Astronomical and Scientific Contributions
Brisbane established an astronomical observatory at Paramatta in 1822. Observations conducted there between 1822 and 1826 resulted in the *Brisbane Catalogue*, which was printed in 1835 and contained 7,385 stars. The Paramatta observatory was eventually discontinued in 1855.

Upon returning to Scotland, Brisbane resided primarily at Makerstoun in Roxburghshire. He maintained a large, admirably equipped observatory there, similar to the one at Brisbane House. In 1841, he began important magnetic observations at Makerstoun. The results of this work were published in the *Transactions* of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and earned him the Keith prize of that society in 1848.

## Academic Leadership and Legacy
Following the death of Sir Walter Scott in 1833, Brisbane was elected president of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. In the subsequent year, he served as president of the British Association. To encourage scientific research, he founded two gold medals: one awarded by the Scottish Society of Arts and another by the Royal Society of Edinburgh.

Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane died on January 27, 1860, at Brisbane House.
