# The Bessemer Process: Making Steel Affordable

## Overview
Before the mid-19th century, steel was a luxury material. While stronger and more flexible than cast iron and tougher than wrought iron, steel was produced in small batches through the laborious "crucible process," making it prohibitively expensive for large-scale infrastructure. The landscape of the Industrial Revolution shifted dramatically in 1856 when Henry Bessemer, an English inventor, patented a method for the mass production of steel.

The Bessemer process revolutionized metallurgy by introducing the concept of "oxidation." Bessemer discovered that blowing air through molten pig iron removed impurities—specifically carbon, silicon, and manganese—through oxidation. This reaction was exothermic, meaning it generated its own heat, eliminating the need for additional fuel to keep the metal molten. This reduced the time required to turn iron into steel from several days to roughly twenty minutes.

The result was a collapse in the price of steel, which catalyzed the "Age of Steel." This affordability enabled the construction of expansive railway networks, the birth of the skyscraper, and the creation of massive steamships, fundamentally altering global trade and urban geography.

## Key Facts
*   **The Converter:** The heart of the process was the "Bessemer Converter," a large, pear-shaped vessel that could be tilted to pour out the finished steel.
*   **The Phosphorus Problem:** Early Bessemer steel was often brittle because the process could not remove phosphorus. Historians and engineers note that this limited the process to specific, low-phosphorus ores (like those from Cumberland) until Sidney Gilchrist Thomas introduced a basic lining (dolomite) to the converter in 1878.
*   **The Economic Shift:** By the late 19th century, the cost of steel rails dropped by over 80%, allowing railroads to expand across the American West and the European interior.
*   **Historical Debate:** There is a long-standing historical discussion regarding the "invention" of the process. William Kelly in the United States had developed a similar pneumatic process independently around the same time. While Bessemer received the primary fame and patents, Kelly is recognized by many historians as a co-innovator.

## Did You Know?
1. **Self-Heating:** The Bessemer process was so efficient that the chemical reaction itself raised the temperature of the iron, meaning no external fuel was needed during the actual blowing phase.
2. **Rail Longevity:** Steel rails lasted ten times longer than iron rails, which drastically reduced maintenance costs for the expanding railway industry.
3. **Urban Transformation:** Without the affordable steel provided by this process, the structural frames required for the first skyscrapers in Chicago and New York would have been financially impossible.
