# Grinding

Grinding is a process utilized across various human activities, ranging from the prehistoric preparation of food to the specialized mechanical requirements of modern industrial brewing and pharmaceutical preparations.

## Prehistoric Origins
In the Mediterranean region, wild wheat grew, and it is suggested that early humans may have learned to pound and subsequently grind the seeds of this plant for food before they developed the knowledge of how to sow them. This practice of grinding, along with threshing, reaping, sowing, and ploughing, became a hallmark of the Neolithic phase (the New Stone Age). By 10,000 B.C., most of mankind had reached this Neolithic level of culture. These practices were not immediately obvious to primitive humans but were developed through a long process of trials, misconceptions, and unnecessary elaborations.

## Grinding in Brewing
In the context of brewing, grinding is identified as the first of seven distinct main processes, followed by mashing, boiling, cooling, fermenting, cleansing, and racking and storing.

### Mechanical Process
In most modern breweries, malt is moved from bins to the mill. During this transit, it passes through an automatic measuring machine as well as a grading and cleaning apparatus. The mills used are of the smooth roller type. In these systems, the malt is crushed rather than ground. After being hoisted to the top floor, cleaned, and graded, the malt is conveyed to the Malt Mill for crushing. Once crushed, the material is referred to as "grist." This grist then moves to the Grist Hopper before proceeding to the Mashing Machine, where it is mixed with hot water from the Hot Liquor Vessel.

### Technical Requirements
The precision of the crushing process is critical to the quality and efficiency of the beer:
* **Over-grinding:** If the malt is ground too fine, it creates difficulties regarding subsequent clarification and efficient drainage within the mash-tun.
* **Under-grinding:** If the crushing is too coarse, the yield is inadequate because the extraction of soluble matter in the mash-tun is incomplete.

## Grinding of Barley for Other Uses
Beyond brewing, barley is cultivated for the preparation of spirits and domestic cookery. Different levels of grinding produce different products:
* **Pot Barley:** Created by grinding off the outer cuticle of hard, flinty grains.
* **Pearl Barley:** Produced when the attrition is carried further, reducing the grain to small round pellets.
* **Patent Barley:** This is either pearl or pot barley that has been reduced to flour.

Additionally, a preparation of barley known as *decoctum hordei* is included in the British Pharmacopoeia, serving as an emollient and demulcent drink for inflammatory and febrile disorders.
