# The February Revolution and its Impact on Prague

## Background of Bohemian National Aspirations
Following the re-establishment of European peace in 1815, national aspirations in Bohemia, which had been long-suppressed, began to revive. Initially, this movement was expressed through the revival of Bohemian literature. Political action within the lands ruled by the house of Habsburg was rendered impossible due to the absolutist and arbitrary government of Prince Metternich.

Despite this political pressure, the estates of Bohemia began to adopt an oppositional stance toward the government of Vienna in 1845. The estates asserted their right to vote on the country's taxes, a right they claimed was due to them under the constitution of 1627. By 1847, in an effort to gain support from the wider population, the estates determined that their session the following year would propose several reforms: the introduction of the Bohemian language into all higher schools, more stringent control over the country's finances by the estates, and more extensive representation for towns at the diet.

## The Impact of the February Revolution
The planned meeting of the estates was prevented by the revolutionary outbreak of 1848. When news of the February revolution in Paris reached Prague, it triggered very great excitement. This atmosphere of popular agitation led to a vast public meeting on March 11, during which a petition was voted upon and sent to the government of Vienna.

The petition demanded several key reforms:
* The Bohemian language was to be granted equal rights with German in all government offices within the country.
* The convocation of a general diet comprising all Bohemian lands, to be elected via extensive suffrage.
* The introduction of numerous liberal reforms.

## Response from Vienna
The initial deputation that presented these demands in Vienna received an answer that was somewhat equivocal. However, a second deputation received a more definitive response from Emperor Ferdinand on April 8. The emperor declared that equality of rights would be secured for both nationalities in Bohemia. He further stated that a new meeting of the estates of Bohemia, including representatives from the principal towns, would be convoked shortly. Additionally, the question regarding the reunion of Silesia and Moravia to Bohemia was to be left to a general meeting of representatives from all parts of Austria.

## Broader Context of 1848
The events in Prague were part of a wider system of outbreaks around 1848 that overthrew the Orleans monarchy and established a second Republic in France. This period of unrest also saw North Italy and Hungary rise against Austria, and the Poles in Posen rise against the Germans. In Prague, a Pan-Slavic conference was held, which foreshadowed territorial readjustments that would occur in 1919; however, this conference dispersed after Austrian troops suppressed an insurrection in Prague.

Similarly, the news of the February 1848 revolution in Paris brought agitation to a head in other regions. In some German states, popular excitement led to the adoption of the Offenburg programme and forced governments to proclaim amnesties and promise reforms. In these instances, ministries were remodelled in a liberal direction, and delegates were empowered to vote for the establishment of a parliament for a united Germany.
