# Boiler Tube Specifications and Draught Systems

In the construction of boilers, the specifications of the tubes are closely linked to the method used to create the draught necessary for combustion. The dimensions of these tubes vary depending on whether the system relies on natural or forced draught.

## Tube Dimensions by Draught Type
When natural or chimney draught is employed, the tubes are generally manufactured with an outside diameter of 3 or 3-1/4 inches. These tubes are rarely longer than 7 feet. In contrast, boilers that utilize "forced" draught typically employ tubes with a smaller diameter of 2-1/2 inches and a greater length, ranging from 8 to 8-1/2 feet. Regardless of the tube size used, a clear space of 1-1/4 inches is almost always maintained between the tubes.

## Draught Systems in Boilers
Draught is the mechanism required to facilitate combustion. In most boilers, this is "natural," meaning it is produced by a chimney. In marine applications, while natural draught remains more common, many installations use "forced" draught. There are two primary systems for forced draught:

*   **Closed Stokehold:** This system is almost universally used in war vessels and some fast passenger ships. The stokehold compartment is sealed so that air can only exit through the fires. Fans drive air into the compartment to maintain a pressure above that of the outside atmosphere. In large vessels, this pressure typically corresponds to a height of 1 to 1-1/2 inches of water, though torpedo-boats and similar craft may use up to 4 inches. This pressure operates in addition to the chimney-draught provided by the funnel's height.
*   **Closed Ashpit (Howden System):** In this arrangement, the stokehold remains open. Fans drive air through tubes located in the uptake, where the products of combustion pass toward the chimney. This process heats the air, which is then delivered partly into the ashpit below the fire and partly into a casing around the furnace front to enter the furnace above the fire.

In locomotive boilers, the draught is produced by the exhaust steam or the blast.

## Combustion Rates and Efficiency
The type of draught significantly impacts the rate of coal combustion per square foot of grate area per hour:
*   **Natural Draught:** Obtains a combustion rate of approximately 15 to 20 lb.
*   **Forced Draught:** Maintains much higher rates. In larger vessels with moderate air pressure, rates range from 20 lb. to 35 lb. In express types of boilers used in craft such as torpedo boats, rates can reach 70 or even 80 lb.

## Boiler Construction and Strengthening
To withstand operational pressures, various parts of the boiler are stayed and strengthened:
*   **Combustion Chambers:** The backs and flat sides are connected to each other or the boiler shell via numerous screw stays, which usually feature nuts inside the chambers. The tops are typically stayed by strong girders resting on the chamber back plates and tube plates, though some use vertical stays attached to riveted T bars or heavy transverse girder plates.
*   **End Plates:** Plates located below the combustion chambers and in the steam space are secured by longitudinal stays that run the length of the boiler and are fastened with double nuts at both ends.
*   **Tube Plates:** These are strengthened using stay tubes that are screwed into them.

## Specialized Boiler Types
Certain boilers are designed for specific high-power requirements. Express boilers, used in cruisers and torpedo-boat destroyers, are designed for a high rate of fuel combustion achieved through a high degree of forced draught. These boilers use tubes of a smaller diameter than standard boilers; for example, the Yarrow express boiler features tubes that are smaller in diameter and more closely arranged than the large tube Yarrow boiler. 

In the Reed boiler, tubes are more curved and longer than those in the Normand boiler. A distinct feature of the Reed boiler is that tubes are fitted with specially designed nut and screw connections at each end rather than being expanded into the tube plate, allowing for quick replacement. These tubes are reduced in diameter at their lower ends to allow for smaller chambers.
