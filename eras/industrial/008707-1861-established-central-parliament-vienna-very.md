# 1861

The year 1861 was marked by significant political transitions within the Austrian Empire, the unification of Italy, and the onset of the American Civil War. In the Austrian Empire, this period was characterized by a shift away from absolutism toward a constitutional system.

## The Austrian Constitution of 1861
Following the Italian campaign of 1859, the continuation of absolutism in the Austrian Empire became impossible. The government sought to establish a constitutional system that could maintain imperial unity while recognizing the ancient constitutional rights of various countries under the rule of the house of Habsburg. 

An initial decree published on October 20, 1860, had established diets with limited powers, modeled in part on the ancient diets of Bohemia and other imperial regions. While this was received favorably in Bohemia, these hopes were countered by a new imperial decree issued on February 26, 1861. This decree established a central parliament at Vienna endowed with very extensive powers. However, the accompanying electoral system was grossly partial to the Germans.

Although Bohemians initially agreed to send representatives to Vienna, they departed the parliament in 1863, claiming the assembly had encroached upon the constitutional powers of the diet of Prague. The central parliament in Vienna was eventually suspended two years later.

## Political Conflict and the Reichsrath
The constitution of 1861 became a focal point of struggle between two primary factions: the Centralists and the Federalists. The Centralists maintained that the 1861 constitution remained valid and demanded the restoration of a constitutional government through the summoning of the Reichsrath. Conversely, the Federalists argued that any new constitution must be created through agreements with the various territories.

This divide often aligned with racial and religious lines. The "kernel" of the empire—comprising Upper and Lower Austria, Salzburg, Vorarlberg, most of Styria, and parts of Carinthia—was purely German. In these areas, the Liberal party sought to develop central institutions. In contrast, a Conservative and Clerical party supported local institutions to protect against the influence of a centralized bureaucracy and parliament, often aligning with the Federalists.

By 1867, the validity of the February constitution was again debated. Richard Belcredi, who had suspended the 1861 constitution on January 2, 1867, ordered new elections for diets to elect deputies to an extraordinary Reichsrath to consider the *Ausgleich* (compact) with Hungary. This move implied the February constitution no longer existed as law, leading to protests from Liberals and Germans who insisted the 1861 constitution was still in force.

## International Events of 1861
Beyond the internal restructuring of the Austrian Empire, 1861 saw major global political shifts:
* **Italy:** Victor Emmanuel I became the King of Italy.
* **United States:** Abraham Lincoln assumed the presidency of the U.S.A., and the American Civil War began.

## Other Notable Occurrences
In the field of intellectual history, 1861 saw the publication of *Natur und Idee* in Vienna by the German physiologist and psychologist Karl Gustav Carus.
