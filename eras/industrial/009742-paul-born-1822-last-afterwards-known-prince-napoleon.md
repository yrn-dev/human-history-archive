# Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul

Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul (1822–1891), commonly referred to as Prince Napoleon or by the nickname "Plon-Plon," was a French political and military figure and a member of the Bonaparte family. He was born on September 9, 1822, in Trieste. He was the second son of Catherine, princess of Wurttemberg, and Jerome Bonaparte, the king of Westphalia.

## Early Political Career
Prince Napoleon gained popularity early in his life through the expression of advanced democratic ideas. Following the French revolution of 1848, he was elected to the National Assembly as a representative of Corsica. During this period, he assumed the name of Jerome following the death of his elder brother, Jerome Napoleon Charles, in 1847.

Upon the establishment of the Empire, Prince Napoleon was designated as the successor to the throne in the event that Napoleon III died without children. Although he received a liberal dotation, he was not permitted a share in public affairs. He maintained a private profession as the representative of the democratic aspect of the Napoleonic tradition and primarily associated with individuals holding advanced political opinions. Within the court, he acted as a representative of the Liberal party in opposition to the empress Eugenie.

## Military Service and Public Administration
In 1854, Prince Napoleon served as a general of division during the Crimean campaign. While his conduct at the battle of the Alma led to some imputations regarding his personal courage, these claims appear to have been groundless. During this campaign, he was attended by Pierre Emmanuel Albert Ducasse, Baron Ducasse, who served as his attaché.

Following his return to France, he demonstrated significant capacity in the chief direction of the National Exhibition of 1855. In 1858, he was appointed as the minister for Algeria and the Colonies. His administration initially sparked great hopes, but his professional activity was diverted by his marriage in January 1859 to Princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy, the daughter of Victor Emmanuel.

## Role in Italian Liberation and Later Life
During the war for the liberation of Italy, Prince Napoleon commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany. Although it was expected that he would become the ruler of the principality, he refused to exert pressure on the inhabitants, who expressed a preference for union with the Italian kingdom.

In the years that followed, he became known as a great orator, delivering several remarkable speeches, though it is noted that his indiscretion equaled his oratorical skill. He also employed Baron Ducasse to work on the *Correspondance* of Napoleon I.

## Family and Legacy
Prince Napoleon had two sons. His eldest, Prince Napoleon Victor Jerome Frederic (born 1862), became the recognized head of the French Bonapartist party upon his father's death. His second son, Prince Louis Napoleon, served as an officer in the Russian army and was appointed governor of the Caucasus in 1906. He was the brother of Princess Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine.
