# 1815

The year 1815 is associated with significant geopolitical shifts in Europe, the births of notable figures in British professional and medical history, and the activities of the Bonaparte family during a period of political instability.

## Geopolitical Changes and the Congress of Vienna
Following the Revolutionary epoch, the Congress of Vienna in 1815 resulted in territorial redistributions. One such instance was the town of Boppard, located in the Prussian Rhine province on the left bank of the Rhine. Formerly a royal residence under the Merovingian dynasty and later a free imperial city under the Hohenstaufen emperors, Boppard had been possessed by the electors of Trier after being pledged by Emperor Henry VII in 1312. After being absorbed by France, the town was assigned to Prussia in 1815.

## The Bonaparte Family and the Hundred Days
The year 1815 marked the period known as the Hundred Days, during which members of Napoleon's family played varying roles. Joseph Bonaparte's involvement during this time is described as insignificant. Despite this, four days after the Battle of Waterloo, Napoleon urged Joseph to inspirit the Chamber of Deputies to encourage national resistance. Joseph's primary efforts were focused on furthering Napoleon's plans to escape to America. Following Napoleon's surrender to the captain of H.M.S. "Bellerophon" at Rochefort, Joseph relocated to the United States, settling in Bordentown, New Jersey, under the title of comte de Survilliers.

In contrast, Lucien Bonaparte, who had previously resided in Italy and America and had been held under surveillance in England until 1814, returned to Rome and offered his help to Napoleon during the Hundred Days. Lucien stood by Napoleon at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris and was the final person to defend Napoleon's prerogatives at the time of his second abdication.

## Notable Births of 1815
Several individuals of historical note were born in 1815:

### Sir Edward Augustus Bond
Born on December 31, 1815, in Hanwell to a schoolmaster, Bond became a prominent English librarian. After education at Merchant Taylors' school, he entered the public record office in 1832 and joined the British Museum's manuscript department in 1838. He rose through the ranks to become Egerton librarian in 1852, assistant keeper of MSS. in 1856, and keeper in 1867, eventually becoming principal librarian in 1878. Bond is credited with making classical palaeography an exact science and reorganizing the manuscript department, including the creation of a classified catalogue of MSS. He also co-founded the Palaeographical Society with Sir E. Maunde Thompson.

### Grace Horsley Darling
Born on November 24, 1815, in Bamborough, Northumberland, Darling became a British heroine. The daughter of William Darling, a lighthouse keeper at Longstone, she gained fame in September 1838. When the "Forfarshire" struck the Farne Islands, Grace and her father used their coble to rescue survivors. Through their skill and daring, they saved nine people. For this exploit, they received a grant from the treasury and a gold medal from the Humane Society.

### George Napoleon Epps
Born in 1815, George Napoleon Epps was a member of an English family known for their contributions to medicine and commerce. He became a prominent doctor and an ardent convert to homoeopathy.
