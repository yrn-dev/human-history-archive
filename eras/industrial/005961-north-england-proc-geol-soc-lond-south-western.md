# North of England

The North of England has been the subject of various geological and paleontological studies, particularly concerning its glacial history, coal deposits, and fossil records.

## Geological and Glacial Research
Research into the physical landscape of the region includes the study of glacial evidence. Dr. Buckland contributed to this field through his work titled "On the Evidences of Glaciers in Scotland and the North of England," which was published in the *Proceedings of the Geological Society of London*.

## Coal Fields and Regional Surveys
The midland counties and the north have been extensively surveyed by members of the Geological Survey of Great Britain. Alexander Henry Green, an English geologist who joined the Survey in 1861, conducted surveys across Yorkshire and Derbyshire. His contributions to the documentation of the region include a memoir on the Geology of North Derbyshire (published in 1869, with a second edition in 1887) and a memoir on the Yorkshire Coal-field published in 1878.

## Paleontological Discoveries
The region is noted for significant fossil finds, particularly in Yorkshire. In 1821, an assemblage of fossil bones and teeth was discovered in a cave at Kirkdale in Yorkshire. This collection included remains of the rhinoceros, hippopotamus, and elephant. Dr. Buckland documented these findings in an account published in *Philosophical Transactions*.
