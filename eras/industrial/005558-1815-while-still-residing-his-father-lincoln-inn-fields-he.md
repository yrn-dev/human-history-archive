# 1815

The year 1815 is associated with the birth of several notable English figures and significant diplomatic activities in Europe.

## Notable Births of 1815

Two prominent English intellectuals were born in 1815. George Boole, an English mathematician and logician, was born in Lincoln on November 2. The son of a studious tradesman of limited means, Boole received his first lessons in mathematical science from his father. Although his mathematical abilities did not manifest early—his initial favorite subject being classics—he began studying higher mathematics at age seventeen. Boole spent much of his life in the scholastic profession, serving as an assistant-master in a private school at Doncaster at age sixteen, establishing a school in Lincoln, and later moving that school to Waddington. In 1849, he was appointed professor of mathematics at the Queen's College in Cork.

Also born in London in 1815 was Peter Bellinger Brodie, an English geologist. The son of P.B. Brodie, a barrister, and nephew of Sir Benjamin C. Brodie, Brodie developed an early interest in fossils and natural history while residing with his father at Lincoln's Inn Fields. This interest was fostered by visits to the museum of the Royal College of Surgeons during the curatorship of W. Clift. Through Clift's influence, Brodie was elected a fellow of the Geological Society in early 1834. He later attended Emmanuel College, Cambridge, where he studied under Sedgwick. Brodie entered the church in 1838, serving as curate at Steeple Claydon in Buckinghamshire and Wylye in Wiltshire, before becoming rector of Down Hatherley in Gloucestershire and, in 1855, vicar of Rowington in Warwickshire. He published geological observations on these districts and specialized in fossil insects, publishing *History of the Fossil Insects of the Secondary Rocks of England* in 1845. He was a founder of the Warwickshire Naturalists' and Archaeologists' Field Club in 1854 and received the Murchison medal from the Geological Society of London in 1887.

## Diplomatic Events of 1815

In 1815, the crown prince of Bavaria, Louis, attended the Congress of Vienna. During this assembly, Louis was primarily occupied with efforts to secure the restoration of Lorraine and Alsace to Germany. Later that year, he traveled to Paris with the allies, where he used his influence to ensure the return of art treasures that had been carried off by the French. Louis had previously served as governor of the circle of the Salzach and the Inn, residing mainly in Salzburg or Innsbruck. He had established himself as the leader of a small anti-French party in Bavaria, which led to strained relations with Napoleon.

## Historical Context of Lincoln's Inn and Fields

Several entries in the archive reference Lincoln's Inn and Lincoln's Inn Fields across different eras. Lincoln's Inn is one of the four ancient societies known as the Inns of Court in England, which have served as schools for the common law since the 13th century. These societies emerged after the Magna Carta fixed the Common Pleas at Westminster. The Inns of Court are where barristers—the highest class of lawyers with exclusive audience in superior courts—must be members.

Lincoln's Inn has been associated with various figures over the centuries. In 1592, the poet and divine John Donne entered the Inn to study law; he later served as a reader in divinity to the benchers of Lincoln's Inn starting in October 1616. In the legal profession, Hugh McCalmont Cairns (1st Earl Cairns) migrated to the Middle Temple from Lincoln's Inn before being called to the bar in 1844.

Lincoln's Inn Fields also served as a venue for the arts. The actor Christopher Bullock, son of William Bullock, displayed versatility of talent there. Similarly, the actor Edward Kynaston joined Betterton at Lincoln's Inn Fields in 1695, though he took less important roles before retiring in 1699. In more recent history, the archive notes that on May 5, 1900, the first English open epee tournament for amateurs was held at the Inns of Court R. V. School of Arms, Lincoln's Inn.
