# Thomas Wilson Fuel-Saving Society and the Woman's Industrial Exchange

## Charitable Institutions of Baltimore
The city of Baltimore contains a large number of charitable institutions. Some of these are supported entirely or partially by the state of Maryland, while scores of others operate independently. Among these organizations is the Charity Organization Society, which serves as a primary body to organize the work of other institutions. Other notable entities include the Baltimore Association for the Improvement of the Condition of the Poor, which aims to discourage the indiscriminate giving of alms, and the Bay View asylum, which serves as the city poorhouse.

## Specialized Support Societies
Within the network of Baltimore's charitable services, specific organizations address distinct needs of the indigent and vulnerable populations:

*   **Thomas Wilson Fuel-Saving Society:** This institution is dedicated to the purpose of furnishing coal at low rates.
*   **Woman's Industrial Exchange:** This society provides assistance to women in need to help them support themselves.

## Other Baltimore Health and Welfare Institutions
The city's charitable landscape includes various medical and residential facilities. These include the Johns Hopkins hospital, which is recognized for the excellence of its heating and ventilating equipment, as well as Saint Joseph's general hospital and the hospital for the women of Maryland of Baltimore city. Specialized care is provided by the nursery and child's hospital, the Baltimore eye, ear and throat charity hospital, and the Maryland hospital for the insane.

Mental health services are further supported by the Sheppard asylum, intended specifically for the cure of the insane, and the Sheppard and Enoch Pratt hospital. For children, the city provides the Baltimore orphan asylum, Saint Vincent's infant asylum, and the Thomas Wilson sanatorium for children, the latter of which is intended for children under three years of age suffering from disease during the warm summer months.

Additional services include the Free Summer Excursion Society, which provides a change of air for the indigent sick, as well as homes for the aged, homes for friendless children, a home for the incurables, and institutions for the blind, deaf, and dumb.
