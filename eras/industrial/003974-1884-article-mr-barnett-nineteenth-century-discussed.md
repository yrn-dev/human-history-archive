# Samuel Augustus Barnett and the University Settlements Movement

## Biography of Samuel Augustus Barnett
Samuel Augustus Barnett (1844– ) was an English social reformer and clergyman. Born in Bristol on February 8, 1844, he was the son of Francis Augustus Barnett, who worked as an iron manufacturer. Barnett attended Wadham College, Oxford, which he left in 1866 to visit the United States. He entered the ministry shortly thereafter, being ordained to the curacy of St Mary's, Bryanston Square, in 1867 and taking priest's orders in 1868.

In 1872, Barnett became the vicar of St Jude's, Commercial Street, located in Whitechapel. In 1873, he married Henrietta Octavia Rowland. Mrs. Barnett was a philanthropist and had previously been a co-worker with Miss Octavia Hill.

## Social Reform in Whitechapel
Working within their parish, Mr. and Mrs. Barnett dedicated themselves to the poor. Their efforts included the establishment of evening schools for adults and the provision of reasonable entertainment and music for the local population. They also served on managing committees for schools and the board of guardians.

Mr. Barnett focused on the coordination of various charities through cooperation with the parish board of guardians and the Charity Organization Society. He specifically worked to improve the conditions of indoor relief while discouraging outdoor relief, which he believed contributed to the pauperization of the neighborhood. Beyond relief efforts, the Barnetts were involved in the establishment of the children's country holiday fund, the building of model dwellings, and the organization of annual loan exhibitions of fine art at the Whitechapel gallery.

## University Extension and Toynbee Hall
Barnett maintained constant contact with Oxford and sought to bring university-level education to London. In 1877, he presided over a small committee formed to consider the organization of university extension in London. His primary assistants in this endeavor were Frederick Rogers, a member of the vellum binders' trade union, and Leonard Montefiore, a young man from Oxford.

This committee received influential support, leading to the delivery of four courses of lectures in Whitechapel in October. One of these courses, focusing on English history, was delivered by Dr. S. R. Gardiner.

In 1884, Mr. Barnett wrote an article in the *Nineteenth Century* that discussed the question of university settlements. This publication led to the formation of the University Settlements Association in July of that year. Following the construction of Toynbee Hall shortly thereafter, Mr. Barnett served as its warden. Additionally, he served as a select preacher at Oxford.
