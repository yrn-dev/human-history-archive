# Lloyd's Bonds

## Definition and Purpose
Lloyd's bonds are financial instruments issued under the seal of a railway company. These bonds serve as a formal admission of the company's indebtedness to an obligee for a specified amount, typically relating to goods supplied or work performed. The bond includes a covenant stating that the company will pay the specified amount, along with interest, on a future date.

The primary function of Lloyd's bonds was to provide railway companies with a device to increase their indebtedness without technically violating the terms of their charter. The name of these specific instruments is derived from the counsel who settled the form of the bond.

## Context within English Bond Law
In English law, a bond is defined as an obligation by deed. Its general design is to ensure that the obligor (the person giving the bond) either refrains from a specific act, performs a specific act, or pays a sum of money. To secure this, the obligor binds himself to a penalty for the benefit of the obligee. 

A condition, known as the "defeasance," is added to the bond; if the obligor fulfills the requirement—such as paying a sum usually equal to half the penalty—the bond becomes void. If the condition is not met, the bond remains in full force and becomes forfeit or absolute at law, charging the estate and the obligor.

Historically, when a bond was forfeit, the entire penalty was recoverable. However, the court of chancery intervened to prevent oppression, viewing the penalty as a pledge to secure the repayment of the principal, interest, and expenses rather than the substance of the debt. This equitable relief was given statutory recognition in 1705, allowing the payment of the lesser sum, plus costs and interest, to satisfy the bond.

## Comparison to Other Bond Types
Lloyd's bonds differ from other legal and financial instruments in several ways:

*   **Debenture Bonds:** Unlike Lloyd's bonds, debenture bonds are secured only by the company's covenant and do not possess a fixed or floating charge on assets.
*   **Recognizances:** A recognizance is distinguished from a bond by the fact that it is entered into before a court of record, making it an obligation of record.
*   **Heritable Bonds:** In Scots law, a heritable bond is a bond for money that is joined with a conveyance of land, which the creditor holds as security for the debt.
