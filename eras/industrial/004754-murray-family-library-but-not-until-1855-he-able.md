# Murray's Family Library

Murray's Family Library is a publication series that featured popular accounts of significant figures. In 1831, the series published a short popular account of the life of the philosopher Sir Isaac Newton. This work was written by Brewster, who had previously begun his literary career in 1799 as a regular contributor to the *Edinburgh Magazine*, serving as its editor at the age of twenty.

## Relation to the Works of Brewster

The short account published in Murray's Family Library served as a precursor to a more comprehensive study of Sir Isaac Newton. It was not until 1855 that Brewster was able to issue the *Memoirs of the Life, Writings and Discoveries of Sir Isaac Newton*. This later, fuller work was the result of more than twenty years of patient investigation into all available sources and original manuscripts.

Brewster's contribution to the Family Library was part of a broader career of scientific and literary service. His editorial and writing experience included:

*   **Encyclopedias:** He undertook the editorship of the *Edinburgh Encyclopaedia* in 1807 (with the first part appearing in 1808 and the last in 1830), contributing many of its most valuable scientific articles. He was also a leading contributor to the seventh and eighth editions of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, writing articles on subjects such as Magnetism, Optics, Hydrodynamics, Stereoscope, Microscope, and Electricity (including Voltaic Electricity).
*   **Journals:** In 1819, Brewster and Robert Jameson established the *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* to replace the *Edinburgh Magazine*. They jointly edited the first ten volumes from 1819 to 1824. Following this, Brewster started the *Edinburgh Journal of Science* in 1824, editing sixteen volumes between 1824 and 1832.
*   **Other Contributions:** Brewster wrote between three and four hundred papers for the transactions of various learned societies and was a frequent contributor to reviews, including seventy-five articles for the *North British Review*.
