# Long Island and the Bahamas

## Colonial Administration and Settlement
The Bahamas experienced a period of instability that led merchants from Bristol and London to petition the crown to restore order and take possession of the territory. In response, Captain Woodes Rogers was dispatched as the first crown governor, arriving at New Providence in 1718. Following his arrival, families of good character began to settle in the Bahamas. While some progress was made in developing the colony's resources, this growth was periodically interrupted by the tyrannical behavior of several governors who succeeded Captain Woodes Rogers.

## Agricultural Development and Slave Labour
During the American war of independence, the colony saw an influx of colonists who brought wealth and slave labour. This period marked an attempt to implement large-scale cotton cultivation. In 1783, on Long Island, 800 slaves were employed, and nearly 4,000 acres of land were under cultivation. Additionally, the pine-apple was introduced as a crop for cultivation at Eleuthera.

However, agricultural progress was hindered by recurring misfortune. Cotton crops were destroyed by the red bug in 1788 and again in 1794. Consequently, by the year 1800, the cultivation of cotton was almost entirely abandoned.

## Military Conflicts and Territorial Control
The colony's progress was further retarded by various military occupations. In 1776, Commodore Hopkins of the American navy captured the island of New Providence, though he later abandoned it after determining it was untenable. In 1781, the Spanish governor of Cuba retook the island. The Spaniards maintained nominal possession of the Bahamas until 1783. Before peace was officially notified, New Providence was recaptured in June 1783 by Lieutenant-Colonel Deveaux, a loyalist from the South Carolina militia.
