# Midnapur-Jherria Line of the Bengal-Nagpur Railway

The Midnapur-Jherria line is a section of the Bengal-Nagpur railway system in British India. This specific line passes through the district of Bankura.

## Geography of the Bankura District
Bankura is a town and district located within the Burdwan division of Bengal. The district covers an area of 2,621 square miles. Geographically, it serves as a connecting link between the mountainous highlands of Chota Nagpur to the west and the Ganges delta to the east. 

The landscape of the district varies significantly by region:
* **Eastern Boundary:** Adjoining the Burdwan district, the land is flat and alluvial, resembling the typical paddy lands found in Bengal.
* **North and West:** The surface rises into long undulating tracts. In these areas, swamps and rice lands are replaced by forests of low thorny jungle or forest trees. As one moves toward the western boundary, hamlets become smaller and more scattered, eventually disappearing into wild forests.

The district is bounded by the Manbhum district to the west, the Midnapur district to the south, and the Burdwan district to the north and east.

## Infrastructure and Transport
The Bankura district is served by several rail connections. In addition to the Midnapur-Jherria line of the Bengal-Nagpur railway, there is a railway line connecting Bankura to Howrah. Furthermore, the East Indian railway skirts the north-east part of the district beyond the Damodar river.

The broader Bengal-Nagpur railway system originates in the Central Provinces and has its terminus at Howrah. A section of this railway passing through Midnapore is responsible for carrying the East Coast line from Madras. Other significant junctions for the Bengal-Nagpur railway include Bilaspur, where two lines from the west meet on their way to Calcutta.

## Economy and Industry of Bankura
The district of Bankura supports various local industries and natural resource extraction:
* **Forest Products:** Tussur silk and lac are gathered in large quantities from the hilly tracts.
* **Manufacturing:** Silk and cotton are woven within the district, and soap-stone is used for carving plates and other items. Bishnupur, the old capital of the country, remains the primary center for these local industries.
* **Mining and Factories:** The district contains two coal-mines and indigo factories. While minerals and stone quarries exist, they are noted as being little worked.

## Climate and History
The climate in Bankura is generally considered healthy, characterized by wholesome, dry air and a bracing cold season, with fogs occurring rarely. However, the region is susceptible to destructive floods and drought. The district suffered through famines in 1866, 1896-1897, and 1874-1875. During the hot season, the temperature is described as relaxing and very oppressive.

Historically, the Bishnupur raj was among the largest estates in Bengal during the late 18th century. Following the conclusion of the permanent settlement in 1793, the estate was sold due to arrears of revenue.
