# Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky

Prince Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky (1814–1879) was a Russian governor of the Caucasus and a career soldier who spent forty-eight years in active service. He is noted for his systematic and energetic tactics in mountain warfare, which are credited with inaugurating a new era of military operations in the region.

## Early Career and Education
Baryatinsky was privately educated before entering the school of the ensigns of the Guard at the age of seventeen. He received his commission as a cornet in the Life Guards of the cesarevich Alexander on November 8, 1833. His early military service was marked by gallantry in the Caucasus in 1835, an achievement for which he was awarded a gold sword "for valour" upon his return to St Petersburg. On January 1, 1836, he was attached to the suite of Alexander.

## Military Operations in the Caucasus and Europe
Baryatinsky returned to the Caucasus in 1845, where he distinguished himself particularly during an attack on the stronghold of Shamyl, earning the order of St George. In 1846, he provided assistance to Fieldmarshal Paskievich in the suppression of the Cracow rising.

Between 1848 and 1856, Baryatinsky played a leading role in the primary military events of the Caucasus. His most significant exploits during this period included a victory at Mezeninsk in 1850 and various operations against Shamyl at Chechen. His professional ascent continued with his appointment as adjutant-general on January 6, 1853, followed by his appointment as chief of the staff on July 5 of that same year. In 1854, he participated in the Kuruk Dere campaign, which is described as brilliant.

## Command and Administration
On January 1, 1856, Baryatinsky was appointed commander-in-chief of the Caucasian army and subsequently served as the governor of the Caucasus. As an administrator, he maintained a high reputation for competence. Under his leadership, the eastern Caucasus was subdued within three years, resulting in the capture of the long elusive Shamyl. Additionally, Baryatinsky conquered numerous tribes in the western Caucasus located between the rivers Byelaya and Laba.

In recognition of these services, he was created a fieldmarshal. However, the strenuous nature of his labors eventually broke his health. At his own request, he was relieved of his post on December 6, 1862.

## Death
Following his retirement from service, Baryatinsky spent the final days of his life abroad. He died in Geneva.
