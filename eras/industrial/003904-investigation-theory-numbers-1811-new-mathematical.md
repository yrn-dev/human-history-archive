The *Elementary Investigation of the Theory of Numbers*, published in 1811, is one of the principal works of Peter Barlow.

## Author Biography
Peter Barlow (1776–1862) was an English writer specializing in both applied and pure mathematics. Born in Norwich in 1776, Barlow spent forty-one years as the mathematical master in the Woolwich Academy, a position he was appointed to in 1806. His scientific contributions were recognized by various British and foreign scientific societies; he was made a fellow of the Royal Society in 1823 and was awarded the Copley medal in 1825.

Beyond his work in pure mathematics, Barlow focused significantly on steam locomotion, serving on railway commissions in 1836, 1839, 1842, and 1845. He also contributed extensively to the *Encyclopaedia Metropolitana* and compiled numerous useful tables.

## Principal Works and Research
In addition to the *Elementary Investigation of the Theory of Numbers* (1811), Barlow authored the *New Mathematical and Philosophical Dictionary* (1814) and an *Essay on Magnetic Attractions* (1820).
