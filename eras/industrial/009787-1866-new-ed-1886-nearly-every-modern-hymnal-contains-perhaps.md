# 1866

The year 1866 was marked by various developments across religious missions, academic appointments, the publication of scholarly and religious works, and the birth of notable figures.

## Religious Developments and Missions
In Europe, the modern Baptist movement continued its expansion during the 19th century. In 1866, missions were started in Italy, specifically the Spezia Mission. This was part of a broader trend where Baptist churches in Roman Catholic countries were established by missionaries from America or England.

In Scotland, the religious landscape saw a transition for Horatius Bonar (1808–1889), a Scottish Presbyterian divine. After serving as a minister of the newly formed Free Church since the Disruption of 1843, Bonar moved to the Chalmers memorial church in Edinburgh in 1866. Bonar was a prolific writer of religious literature and a noted hymnologist; he published three series of *Hymns of Faith and Hope* between 1857 and 1866, with a new edition released later in 1886.

## Academic and Professional Appointments
Several scholars and educators transitioned into new roles during 1866. Hiram Corson, an American scholar born in Philadelphia, served as a professor of English at Girard College in Philadelphia from 1865 to 1866. In 1866, he moved to St John's College in Annapolis, Maryland, where he remained until 1870.

In the field of education and publishing, Timothy Dwight, a preacher and educationalist and grandson of President Dwight, began serving as an editor of the *New Englander* in 1866. He held this editorial position until 1874, after which the publication became the *Yale Review*.

## Notable Births
Carl Darling Buck was born on October 2, 1866, in Bucksport, Maine. He later became an American philologist and a professor of Sanskrit and Indo-European comparative philology at the University of Chicago. Buck is recognized for his significant work in Italic dialects, including the study of the Oscan-Umbrian verb-system and the Oscan language.
