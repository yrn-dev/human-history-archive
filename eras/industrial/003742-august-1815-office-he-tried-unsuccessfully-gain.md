# August 1815

The month of August 1815 is associated with the births of several notable figures in European politics and arts, as well as significant political transitions in France.

## Political Developments in France
In August 1815, Francois, Marquis de Barbe-Marbois, a French politician born in Metz, was appointed as the minister of justice within the ministry of the duc de Richelieu. Barbe-Marbois had a long and varied public career, beginning as the intendant of San Domingo under the old regime. He later served the revolutionary government, including a mission to Regensburg in 1791 to assist the French ambassador, de Noailles, in negotiations with the diet of the Empire regarding the possessions of German princes in Alsace and Lorraine.

His career was marked by periods of instability; he was arrested on suspicion of treason upon his return from Regensburg and later arrested again on September 4, 1797 (the 18th Fructidor), and transported to French Guiana. He was transferred to Oleron in 1799 and regained his liberty through Napoleon after the 18th Brumaire. Under Napoleon, he held several high-ranking positions, including director of the public treasury and councillor of state in 1801, and senator in 1802. In 1803, he negotiated the treaty for the cession of Louisiana to the United States, for which the First Consul gave him 152,000 francs. He was further elevated to count and grand officer of the legion of honour in 1805, and became president of the *cour des comptes* in 1808.

Despite his servile compliments to Napoleon, Barbe-Marbois helped draft the emperor's act of abdication in 1814. Following this, Louis XVIII named him a peer of France in June 1814 and confirmed his presidency of the *cour des comptes*. However, Napoleon deprived him of these positions during the Hundred Days. Upon his appointment as minister of justice in August 1815, Barbe-Marbois attempted, without success, to gain confidence in that office.

## Notable Births of August 1815
Several influential figures were born during this month:

*   **Bernhard Ernst von Bülow (August 2, 1815):** A Danish and German statesman born at Cismar in Holstein to Adolf von Bülow, a Danish official. He studied law at the universities of Kiel, Göttingen, and Berlin. He began his career in the Danish foreign office and the chancery of Schleswig-Holstein-Lauenburg at Copenhagen. After serving as councillor of legation in 1842 and *chargé d'affaires* in the Hanse towns in 1847, he briefly offered his services to the provisional government of Kiel in 1848. He later represented the duchies of Schleswig and Holstein at the restored federal diet of Frankfort in 1850, where he developed a relationship with Bismarck. He eventually headed the grand-ducal government of Mecklenburg-Strelitz until 1867 and later served as the secretary of state for foreign affairs for Prussia, becoming Bismarck's most faithful henchman.

Born in Benson, Vermont, he became a prominent American editor, journalist, and Baptist clergyman. He worked for various New York City publications, including *The New Yorker* and *The New World*. He is best known for compiling anthologies such as *Prose Writers of America* (1846) and *Poets and Poetry of America* (1842).
*   **Halfdan Kjerulf (September 15, 1815):** Though born in September, Kjerulf is noted in the archive's chronological records for 1815. Born in Christiania to a high government official, the Norwegian composer initially studied law at Christiania University. He did not devote himself entirely to music until the death of his father when he was nearly 26. He is remembered for his solos and national part-songs, as well as his pianoforte music.
