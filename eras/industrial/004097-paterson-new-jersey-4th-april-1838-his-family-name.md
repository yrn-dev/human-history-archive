# Lawrence Barrett

## Early Life and Family
Lawrence Barrett was an American actor born on the 4th of April 1838 in Paterson, New Jersey. He was born to parents of Irish descent, and his family name was Brannigan.

## Acting Career
Barrett began his career on the stage in 1853, making his first appearance in Detroit as Murad in the production *The French Spy*. He expanded his reach to New York in December 1856, where he appeared at the Chambers Street theatre playing the role of Sir Thomas Clifford in *The Hunchback*. By 1858, he had joined the stock company at the Boston Museum.

Throughout his career, Barrett performed a wide variety of parts. His repertoire included:
*   **Shakespearean roles:** Hamlet, Lear, Macbeth, Shylock, Richard III, and Cassius (performed opposite Booth's Brutus).
*   **Other notable roles:** Wolsey, Benedick, Richelieu, David Garnick, Hernani, and Alfred Evelyn.
*   **Specific productions:** He played Lanciotto in George Henry Boker's *Francesca da Rimini* and James Harebell in *The Man o' Airlie*. He also played Othello to Booth's Iago.

Barrett's work took him to London in 1867, 1881, 1883, and 1884. His portrayal of Richelieu in the drama by Bulwer Lytton was regarded as his best part.

## Military Service and Management
During the Civil War, Barrett served as a captain in the 28th Massachusetts infantry regiment, a role in which he served with distinction. Following his military service, from 1867 to 1870, he managed the California theatre in San Francisco in partnership with John McCullough.

## Literary Contributions and Death
In addition to his acting and management, Barrett authored biographical sketches of other actors. In 1881, he wrote a life of Edwin Forrest for the *American Actors Series* published in Boston. He also wrote a sketch of Edwin Booth in the 1886 Boston publication *Edwin Booth and his Contemporaries*.

Lawrence Barrett died on the 20th of March 1891.
