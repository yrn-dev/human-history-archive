# Book-plates

Book-plates, also referred to as personal book-tokens or *ex-libris*, serve as marks of possession for books. Historically, they have been viewed both as a minor work of decorative and symbolical art and as an accessory to book binding.

## History and Origins
The history of the book-plate begins in Germany, where the earliest known examples were produced. The fashion spread from Germany to other countries, and German examples are found in great numbers long before they appeared elsewhere. Between 1503 and 1516, Albrecht Durer engraved at least six plates, some of which were of very important size, and he provided designs for many others. Other notable plates from this era are ascribed to Hans Holbein, Lucas Cranach, and a group known as the Little Masters, which included Jost Amman, Virgil Solis, the Behams, Georg Hupschmann, Matthias Zundt, and Saldorfer.

While Italian and French fashions invaded during the 18th and 17th centuries, the influence of these early German draughtsmen persisted into the present day. The German style is characterized as heavy and over-elaborated, a trait that did not affect neighboring countries.

## Evolution of Design and Production
Historically, the creation of book-plates was almost exclusively the domain of the heraldic stationer, relying on routine skill. In more recent years, however, the composition of these tokens has been recognized as a minor branch of a higher art. This shift has seen the purely heraldic elements become subsidiary, while symbolic or allegorical elements have asserted themselves more strongly.

The popularization of book-plates—which were once largely restricted to important collections or ancestral libraries—has been aided by the development of process work. Process reproduction has made the creation of elaborate and beautiful designs cheaper and more accessible; consequently, the majority of modern plates are reproduced by process. Despite this, some artists continue to use the graver. In England, G.W. Eve and C.W. Sherborn are the best-known artists utilizing this skill, while in America, notable engravers include E.D. French of New York City, K.W.F. Hopson of New Haven, Conn., and J.W. Spenceley of Boston, Mass.

## Collecting and Value
The practice of collecting book-plates has sparked debate. Some enthusiastic collectors, termed "ex-librists," have claimed that "ex-librisme" is a science. Conversely, some bibliophiles have criticized the removal of book-plates from old books as vandalism.

Book-plates are often considered of high interest and may possess a value far exceeding the volume to which they are attached. They serve as personal relics of well-known figures or as specimens of bygone decorative fashions. Notable artists whose designs are worth collecting include:
* **Early and Classical Masters:** Holbein, Durer, the Little Masters of Germany, Charles Eisen, Simon Gribelin, Hubert Francois Bourguignon (known as Gravelot), and D.N. Chodowiecki.
* **British and European Artists:** W. Marshall, David Loggan, W. Faithorne, Francesco Piranesi, Sir Robert Strange, Hogarth, Bartolozzi, Cipriani, William Henshaw, John Keyse Sherwin, and Hewitt.
* **Later and Modern Artists:** Bewick and his imitators, Thomas Stothard, Millais, Thackeray, Maclise, Bell Scott, T.G. Jackson, Walter Crane, Caldecott, Stacy Marks, Edwin Abbey, Kate Greenaway, Gordon Browne, Herbert Railton, Aubrey Beardsley, Alfred Parsons, D.Y. Cameron, and Paul Avril.

## Bibliographic Authorities
The study of book-plates is supported by various publications. The *Ex-libris Series* (London) treats book-plate composition. Other significant guides include:
* *A Guide to the Study of Book-plates* (1880) by the Hon. J. Leicester Warren (Lord de Tabley).
* *Notes on Book-plates, 1574-1800* (1887) by Sir A.W. Franks.
* *Die deutschen Bucherzeichen* (1890) by Friedrich Warnecke.
* *Les Ex-libris et les marques de possession du livre* (1891) by Henri Bouchot.
* *English Book-plates* (1892) by Egerton Castle.
* *French Book-plates* (1892) and *Dated Book-plates* (1895) by Walter Hamilton.
* *Artists and Engravers of British and American Book-plates* (1897) by H.W. Fincham.
* *German Book-plates* (1901) by Count K.E. zu Leiningen-Westerburg, translated by G.R. Denis.
* *Les Ex-libris francais* (1875) by A. Poulet-Malassis.
