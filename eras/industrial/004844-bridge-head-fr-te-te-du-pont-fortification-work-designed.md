# BRIDGE-HEAD

A bridge-head (French: *tête-du-pont*) is a work of fortification designed to cover the passage of a river. This is achieved through the use of fortifications situated on one or both banks of the river.

## Purpose and Function
The primary objective of a bridge-head is to secure the movement of an army over bridges, a process that is characterized as being slow and complicated. Because of these complexities, it is necessary to protect the crossing from hostile interruption. 

To achieve this security, the fortifications that make up the bridge-head must be advanced sufficiently far to ensure that the enemy's artillery is kept out of range of the bridges. Furthermore, the bridge-head provides the necessary room for troops to form up once they have reached the farther bank.

## Evolution of Design
The scale and nature of bridge-head fortifications have changed over time based on the technology of weaponry:

*   **Former Days:** When weapons had a short range, a bridge-head often served as little more than a screen for the bridge itself.
*   **Modern Conditions:** The development of modern weaponry has necessitated a far greater extension of bridge defenses to maintain security.

## Related Fortifications
While the bridge-head is a specific work designed to cover a river passage, other bridges have historically incorporated fortifications for different purposes. For example, the bridge of Narses, which carried the Via Salaria over the Anio in the 6th century, featured a fortification. Such structures became common in later bridges to provide defense or to facilitate the enforcement of tolls.
