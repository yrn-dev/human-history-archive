# Municipal Corporations Act of 1835

The Municipal Corporations Act of 1835 (also referred to in some records as the Municipal Reform Act of 1835) was a piece of legislation that altered the governance and corporate structures of various boroughs. The Act replaced older charters and systems of government, establishing new compositions for local corporations.

## Impact on Barnstaple
In the royal borough of Barnstaple, the Municipal Corporations Act of 1835 replaced a governing structure that had been in force since a charter from James I dated 1610. That previous charter had established a recorder, a clerk of the market, and justices of peace, and had increased the number of chief burgesses to twenty-five. Following the passing of the 1835 Act, the corporation of Barnstaple was reorganized to consist of eighteen councillors and six aldermen.

## Impact on Bristol
The Municipal Reform Act of 1835 served as a check to the diminishing prosperity of Bristol, which had been affected by the rise of Liverpool, the abolition of the slave trade (which ruined the West Indian sugar trade), and high rates levied by the Bristol Dock Company. The Act established a new corporation for the city. This body consisted of 16 aldermen and 48 councillors, who were chosen by the burgesses of each ward. This new corporation was responsible for electing the mayor and later purchased the docks in 1848 to reduce fees.

## Impact on Other Boroughs
The Act replaced various long-standing charters across different municipalities:

*   **Banbury:** The 1835 Act replaced a charter granted by George I in 1718.
*   **Buckingham:** The Act superseded the original grant of 1554, which had created a free borough corporate consisting of a steward, twelve principal burgesses, and a bailiff.
*   **Durham:** The Municipal Reform Act of 1835 replaced a charter granted in 1780 by Bishop Egerton. This 1780 charter had been a confirmation of a 1602 charter that established a government of 24 burgesses, 12 aldermen, and a mayor.
*   **Kendal:** Under the Municipal Reform Act of 1835, the corporation of Kendal was altered. Prior to this, the town had been governed under a 1635 appointment by Charles I, which had established 20 capital burgesses, 12 aldermen, and a mayor.

## Comparison with Later Municipal Legislation
While the 1835 Act reorganized many English boroughs, other regions and later dates saw different corporate structures:

*   **Belfast:** The constitution of the Belfast corporation was modified by the Municipal Corporations Act of 1840, consisting of thirty councillors and ten aldermen. Later, the Belfast Corporation Act of 1896 increased this number to forty-five councillors and fifteen aldermen.
*   **Bideford:** The corporate structure of Bideford was governed by a charter from James I (1610) until the Municipal Corporations Act of 1873, which established twelve common councillors and four aldermen.
*   **Darlington and Darwen:** In Darlington (incorporated in 1867) and Darwen (incorporated in 1788), the corporations consist of eighteen councillors, six aldermen, and a mayor.
