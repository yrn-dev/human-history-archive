# Reisehandbuch fur das Konigreich Bohmen

The *Reisehandbuch fur das Konigreich Bohmen* is a publication authored by F. Rivnac and published in Prague in 1882. It is characterized as being very useful due to the inclusion of numerous and detailed historical notes regarding the Kingdom of Bohemia.

## Context of Bohemian Administration and Society
The work focuses on a region where education is well advanced, noting that Bohemia possesses the lowest proportion of illiterates among the Austrian provinces. The educational landscape is led by two universities located in Prague, one of which is Czech and the other German.

Administratively, Bohemia is organized into ninety-four districts and two autonomous municipalities: the capital, Prague, which has a population of 204,478, and Reichenberg, with a population of 34,204. The region's political representation includes 130 deputies sent to the Reichsrat in Vienna. Additionally, there is a local diet consisting of 242 members, which includes the two university rectors, the archbishop, and three bishops as *ex officio* members.

## Related Historical Literature
The *Reisehandbuch* exists alongside other significant works concerning Bohemian history and antiquities. These include the two-volume *Altertumer und Denkwurdigkeiten Bohmen's* by Mikowec (published in Prague, 1859-1865) and the 15-volume *Die Lander Osterreich-Ungarns in Wort und Bild* by F. Umlauft (Vienna, 1881-1889).

Other historical accounts of the region include:
* **Frantisek Palacky**: Wrote *Geschichte von Bohmen* (Prague, 1844-1867), which covers history up to the year 1526.
* **Adolf Bachmann**: Published a *Geschichte Bohmens* (vol. i. 1899, vol. ii. 1905) also covering up to 1526, noted for having a strongly marked German tendency.
* **Ernest Denis**: Provided a continuous account of Bohemian history from the start of the 15th century through works such as *Jean Hus, et la guerre des Hussites* (1878), *Fin de l'independance boheme* (1890), and *La Boheme depuis la Montagne Blanche* (1903).
* **Count Lutzow**: Authored *Bohemia, a Historical Sketch* (London, 1896).

Furthermore, the *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum*, a collection of historical documents published in Prague in the late 19th century, is noted to have superseded earlier works such as those by Marquard Freher (Freherus), as well as the historical writings of Pelzl, De Florgy, and Pubitschka. For history posterior to 1526, the works of Gindely and Rezek are cited.
