# Henry Baker (1698–1774)

Henry Baker was an English naturalist born in London on May 8, 1698. His early professional life began with an apprenticeship to a bookseller. Following this, he developed a system for instructing the deaf and dumb, a venture that provided him with a considerable fortune.

## Literary and Professional Associations
Baker's success in education brought him to the attention of Daniel Defoe. In 1728, using the name Henry Stonecastle, Baker associated with Defoe to launch the *Universal Spectator and Weekly Journal*. The two families became further linked in 1729 when Baker married Defoe's youngest daughter, Sophia.

In addition to his scientific pursuits, Baker was a writer of poetry. He produced several volumes of verse, which included both original works and translations. One notable example is his 1727 poem, *The Universe, a Poem intended to restrain the Pride of Man*.

## Scientific Contributions and Honors
Baker was recognized by the leading scientific and historical institutions of his time. In 1740, he was elected as a fellow of both the Royal Society and the Society of Antiquaries. He was a frequent contributor of memoirs to the *Transactions* of the Royal Society.

His work in microscopy earned him significant acclaim; in 1744, he was awarded the Copley gold medal for his microscopical observations regarding the crystallization of saline particles. Baker also expanded his influence into the arts, becoming one of the founders of the Society of Arts in 1754, where he served as secretary for a period.

## Publications on Microscopy
Baker authored several works intended to guide others in the use of the microscope. These include:
* *The Microscope made Easy* (1743)
* *Employment for the Microscope* (1753)

## Death and Legacy
Henry Baker died in London on November 25, 1774. He left a legacy to the Royal Society in the form of a sum of L100 via his will, which established the foundation for the Bakerian lecture.
