## English Billiards
The history of modern English billiards is largely defined by the careers of John Roberts, sen., and his son, John Roberts, jun., beginning in 1849. In that year, the elder Roberts assumed the championship title after Kentfield declined to play. Roberts, sen. was noted for his physical power, nerve, and a genius for the game that allowed him to master the spot-stroke through exhaustive practice. He held the title until February 11, 1870, when he was defeated by his pupil, W. Cook, in a match of 1200 points (won by 117).

Following this, the championship transitioned through a series of matches involving Roberts, jun., Cook, and Jos. Bennett. John Roberts, jun. won the title on April 14, 1870, by defeating Cook. Between 1870 and 1885, the championship was contested on "Championship Tables," which featured pockets measuring only 3 inches at the "fall," compared to the 3-5/8-in. or 3-3/4-in. pockets found on ordinary tables. These tables also featured a baulk line only 28 inches from the cushion and a reduced radius for the D (initially 9 1/2 inches, later 10 inches).

Roberts, jun. developed the game by moving away from spot-play in favor of top-of-the-table play to provide the quick scoring and long breaks preferred by the public. He secured several championship victories, including wins over Bowles (May 30, 1870), Bennett (January 30, 1871), and Cook (May 24, 1875; December 20, 1875; May 28, 1877; and March 30–April 1, 1885). His final recorded championship victory in the provided data occurred on June 1–4, 1885, when he defeated Jos. Bennett by 1640 points in a 3000-point match.

Later developments saw the introduction of "Standard Association Tables" by the Billiard Association at the end of the 19th century, which adopted the 3-5/8-in. pocket and a rounder cushion shoulder to require greater accuracy. In 1899, a match for the championship was played between Roberts, jun. and Dawson. While the match used ordinary pockets rather than three-inch pockets, Roberts won by 1814 points. However, Dawson later won the Billiard Association championship twice in 1899. After challenges from Diggle and Stevenson, Stevenson established his superiority and was considered the incontestable English champion by the beginning of 1907.

## Badminton
Badminton, a racket and shuttlecock game named after the duke of Beaufort's seat in Gloucestershire, was played in India before appearing in England around 1873. The Badminton Association in England was established in 1895, utilizing laws based on the 1876 Poona rules and an 1887 code from the Bath Badminton Club. 

The All England championships were instituted in 1899 for mixed, ladies', and gentlemen's doubles, followed by singles championships for ladies and gentlemen in 1900. The first championship match between Ireland and England took place in 1904.

## Political and Familial Championship
The archive also records "championship" in the sense of advocacy or defense. Isaac Barre (1726–1802), a British soldier and politician, used his mastery of invective in his championship of the American cause, opposing the taxation of America. In his speeches, Barre applied the name "Sons of Liberty" to the colonists, a term that subsequently became a common designation for patriotic clubs and organizations opposing the Stamp Act.

In a familial context, George, the 3rd earl of Ashburnham, published a *Narrative* in 1830. This work served as a championship of his ancestor, John Ashburnham, though the archive notes that this defense was entirely unconvincing and uncritical.
