# John Bright

## Personal Life
In June 1847, Bright married Miss Margaret Elizabeth Leatham of Wakefield. The couple had seven children, the eldest of whom was Mr. John Albert Bright.

## Parliamentary Career and Political Positions
Following his marriage, Bright was elected for Manchester in July 1847, alongside Mr. Milner Gibson, in a contest-free election. During the new parliament and the previous session, Bright opposed legislation that sought to restrict hours of labour. As a Nonconformist, he also spoke out against the clerical control of national education.

In 1848, Bright introduced a bill for the repeal of the Game Laws and voted in favor of Hume's household suffrage motion. He was a vocal critic of Lord John Russell's Ecclesiastical Titles Bill, describing the measure as "paltry, miserable," and "a little," while predicting that it would fail.

## Oratory and Influence
Bright spoke extensively on Irish questions. In 1849, while delivering a speech in support of the government bill for a rate in aid, he received loud cheers from both sides of the assembly and was complimented by Disraeli for sustaining the reputation of the House. From that point forward, Bright became an effective participant in debates and gained the ear of the House.

His parliamentary advocacy included speaking against several issues:
* Capital punishment
* Flogging in the army
* Church-rates
* The Irish Established Church

Additionally, Bright supported a motion by Cobden for the reduction of public expenditure and advocated for peace both within and outside of parliament.

## Later Career and the Crimean War
In the election of 1852, Bright was returned for Manchester once again, running on principles of religious freedom, electoral reform, and free trade. However, this period was marked by the onset of war. Bright delivered some of his most impassioned speeches in a fruitless attempt to oppose the Crimean War, though neither the country nor the House would listen to his arguments. In March 1854, Macaulay wrote that upon hearing Bright speak in the House on a Monday, Bright had said "everything I thought."

Bright's most memorable and greatest speech was delivered on February 23, 1855. In this address, he stated, "The angel of death has been abroad throughout the land. You may almost hear the beating of his wings." He concluded the speech with an appeal to the prime minister that moved the House more than it had been moved within living memory. It was noted that a tremor in Bright's voice during the touching portions of his great speeches stirred the feelings of even those who were hostile listeners.
