# Trust Companies and State Banking in the United States

## Trust Companies and the Panic of 1893
Trust companies, particularly those located in New York City, were a disturbing element during the financial panic. At the time of the panic of 1893, these companies represented a relatively small factor within New York's financial landscape. The total capitalization for all trust companies across the United States remained low as late as 1897, totaling $106,968,253, while individual deposits amounted to $566,922,205.

By 1907, these figures had grown significantly. Capitalization for trust companies rose to $276,146,081, and deposits increased to $2,061,623,035.

## Reserve Requirements and Asset Management
Under state law, New York trust companies were required to maintain cash reserves in their vaults equal to only 5% of their demand deposits. While many of these companies held large deposits in national banks, these reserves were inadequate to support the vast amount of credit built upon them.

The instability of these companies was linked not only to the absolute amount of reserves but also to the nature of their business practices. Less conservative companies failed to keep their assets liquid. Rather than utilizing loans on first-class negotiable securities or purchasing commercial paper, some engaged in speculative underwritings and committed their funds to enterprises that required a long period for consummation.

## Evolution of State and National Banking
The resources of banking shifted over time, with the power of note-issue being more significant before the Civil War than in subsequent years, due to the incomplete development of the deposit system. 

In 1835, the combined capital and circulation of state banks was approximately $335,000,000, while deposits were only $83,000,000. By 1907, the national banks saw circulation and capital reach $1,430,000,000, with deposits climbing to $4,322,000,000. This represents a shift where deposits in the earlier period were less than one-third of the combined capital and circulation, whereas in the later period, deposits were three times those items.

## State Bank Circulation Trends
The circulation of state banks experienced wide fluctuations across different eras. The circulation reached a maximum of $149,185,890 in 1837, before declining to $106,968,572 three years later. A minimum was reached in 1843 at $58,563,608.
