# Deductions in General Average and Financial Systems

In the context of maritime law and financial administration, deductions are applied to adjust claims, values, and profits. These deductions vary significantly depending on the nature of the asset, the age of the vessel, or the specific regulatory framework of a banking system.

## Maritime Deductions for "New for Old"

When adjusting claims for General Average (G.A.), repairs are subject to "new for old" deductions. These deductions are generally regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired. However, certain items are exempt from these deductions, including machinery, boilers, and provisions and stores that have not been in use.

### General Provisions and Exemptions
Across various ship types, the expense of straightening bent ironwork (including the labor for its removal and replacement) is allowed in full. Similarly, graving dock dues—including the use of stages, shears, graving dock materials, and expenses related to removals and cartages—are allowed in full. No deductions are made for old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material. In the case of temporary repairs for damage allowable as G.A., no "new for old" deductions are applied.

Regarding painting, no deduction is permitted if the bottom was not painted within the six months prior to the accident.

### Wooden or Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, the following rules apply based on the date of the original register:
*   **Under one year:** No "new for old" deduction is made.
*   **Over one year:** A deduction of one-third is generally applied, with specific exceptions. Anchors are allowed in full, while chain cables are subject to a deduction of only one-sixth. Nails, felt, and labour metalling are subject to a one-third deduction. Metal sheathing is handled by allowing the full cost of a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal, minus the proceeds from the old metal.

### Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel ships, deductions are categorized by the ship's age from the date of original register to the date of the accident:
*   **Up to 1 year (A):** All repairs are allowed in full, except for the coating or painting of the bottom, which incurs a one-third deduction.
*   **Between 1 and 3 years (B):** A one-third deduction is applied to the renewal and repair of sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers (excluding chain and wire), painting, covers, awnings, upholstery, furniture, crockery, glassware, metal, and the woodwork of the hull, masts, and spars. A one-sixth deduction is applied to chain cables, chains, wire ropes, wire rigging, wire hawsers, steam winches, donkey engines, and steam cranes, including their connexions.
*   **Between 3 and 6 years (C):** Deductions remain as in category B, except that a one-sixth deduction is applied to machinery (including mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 6 and 10 years (D):** Deductions remain as in category C, except that a one-third deduction is applied to all hawsers, rigging, ropes, and sheets, as well as the renewal and repair of all machinery (including boilers and mountings) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 10 and 15 years (E):** A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs, except for cementing, the ironwork of the hull, and chain cables, which are subject to a one-sixth deduction. Anchors are allowed in full.
*   **Over 15 years (F):** A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs. Chain cables are subject to a one-sixth deduction, and anchors are allowed in full.

## Contributory Values and G.A. Adjustments

Contributions to General Average are based on the actual values of property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed property. Several deductions are made from these values:
1.  **Shipowner's Freight and Passage-Money:** Deductions are made for crew's wages and port charges that would not have been incurred had the cargo and ship been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice or G.A. act, provided they were not allowed as G.A.
2.  **Property Value:** Deductions are made for all charges incurred regarding the property after the G.A. act, unless those charges are allowed as G.A.

## Financial Deductions in National Banking

In the United States national banking system, banks issued circulating notes based on United States bonds. While banks benefited from "double interest"—earning interest on bonds held by the treasury while lending notes to the public—several deductions reduced these profits:
*   **Par-Value Limits:** Notes could not be issued to the full par-value of the bonds.
*   **Taxation:** A tax of 1% per annum on circulation reduced the potential profit.
*   **Reserves:** Banks were required to set aside lawful money or gold for reserves and redemption purposes.
