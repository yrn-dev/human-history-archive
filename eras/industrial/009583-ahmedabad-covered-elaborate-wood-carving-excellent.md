# Wood-Carving and Manufactures of Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad is a center of significant industrial and artistic activity, particularly noted for its contributions to textile manufacturing and the art of wood-carving.

## Wood-Carving and Architecture
Ahmedabad is famous for its carved wood-work. This artistic tradition is prominently displayed in the city's architecture, where many houses are covered with elaborate wood-carving. While Ahmedabad is a primary center for this craft, excellent examples of such work also exist in other locations, including Surat, Baroda, Nasik, Yeola, and Broach.

## Bombay Blackwood Furniture
The city of Ahmedabad, along with Bombay and Surat, is a site for the manufacture of "Bombay blackwood furniture." This class of articles is crafted from Shisham, also known as blackwood (*Dalbergia*), which is a dark-coloured, hard-grained timber that can achieve a beautiful natural polish with proper treatment.

The quality and style of this furniture vary. Some pieces are considered clumsy or inelegant in form, a defect experts such as Sir George Birdwood suggest is due to the use of original Dutch models. Conversely, smaller items—including ornamental stands, small tables, and flower stands—are highly prized by collectors for their exceedingly graceful contours. At its highest level, the carving is described as lace-like in character. This work is noted for the ingenuity of the craftsmen, who adapt detailed designs to the specific purpose of the article being fashioned.

The production of the most artistic Bombay furniture is handled by a special class of workmen who follow inherited traditions. These designs are often transmitted from father to son, resulting in craftsmen who may know only one specific design. In recent years, there have been efforts under European auspices to modernize the industry by integrating native work into Western furniture designs, though conventional patterns generally remain the standard.

## Textile and Other Industries
Beyond wood-carving, Ahmedabad is a major center for the growth and manufacture of cotton. Large steam mills have been established in the city as part of a broader industrial development in the presidency. By 1905, the presidency contained 432 factories, the vast majority of which were dedicated to the manufacture and preparation of cotton.

Ahmedabad is also involved in the production of silk goods, alongside Bombay, Thana, Nasik, Yeola, and Surat. These silk materials are frequently decorated with woven or printed designs, though this branch of industry has faced decline due to competition from European goods. Additionally, the province maintains a population of hand-loom weavers—numbering 178,000 in 1901—who specialize in the manipulation of designs woven into cloth.

## Salt Production
The city is also a site for government works dedicated to the production of salt. Large quantities of salt are produced at Udu and Kharaghoda in Ahmedabad, from where the product is exported via rail to central India and Gujarat.
