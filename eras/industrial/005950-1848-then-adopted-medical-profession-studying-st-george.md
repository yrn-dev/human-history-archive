# Francis Trevelyan Buckland

## Early Life and Education
Francis Trevelyan Buckland was an English zoologist born on December 17, 1826, in Oxford. He was the son of the geologist Dean William Buckland. His early education took place at Winchester and Christ Church, where he completed his studies and took his degree in 1848.

## Medical Training and Transition to Zoology
Following the completion of his degree in 1848, Buckland entered the medical profession. He pursued his medical studies at St George's hospital in London, and by 1852, he had attained the position of house-surgeon at the institution. During this period, his studies in anatomy served as a gateway to extensive research in the field of zoology, specifically focusing on "out-of-the-way" research.

## Career in Natural History and Fisheries
Buckland's transition from medicine to natural history became formalized in 1856 when he began writing regularly for the newly established publication *Field*, where he focused particularly on fish. He expanded his contributions to the field by starting the publication *Land and Water* in 1866.

In 1867, Buckland was appointed as the government inspector of fisheries. This role required constant travel throughout the country. Through this work, he was largely responsible for increasing the scientific attention given to pisciculture.

## Publications and Death
Throughout his career, Buckland produced various official reports and articles, as well as several books. His published works include:
* *Curiosities of Natural History* (published in four volumes between 1857 and 1872)
* *Fish Hatching* (1863)
* *Logbook of a Fisherman* (1875)
* *Natural History of British Fishes* (1881)

Francis Trevelyan Buckland died on December 19, 1880. A biography titled *Life* was written about him by G.C. Bompas in 1885.
