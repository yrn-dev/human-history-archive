# Tables and Beds in Human History

The terms "table" and "bed" appear across various contexts in the archive, ranging from ancient household furniture and sporting equipment to geological formations and mining terminology.

## Furniture and Ancient Customs
In early civilizations, household appliances were limited; the table, bed, and couch, along with the chair, constituted virtually the entire furniture of early peoples until the end of the European middle ages. Materials used for construction and decoration included wood, ivory, bronze, silver, gold, and precious stones.

Different ancient cultures exhibited distinct styles of furniture:
*   **Egypt:** Furniture was made of wood, gilded, carved, and supported by legs shaped like wild animals.
*   **Nineveh:** Carvings of the bull, ram, and lion were characteristic of their furniture.
*   **Assyria:** Tables and couches were inlaid with precious metals and ivory. Cedar, ebony, and possibly teak, walnut, and rosewood were utilized.
*   **Greece:** Sumptuous furniture was often made of bronze damascened with gold and silver.
*   **Rome:** Tables were constructed from rare woods or splendid marbles.

Customs regarding these items varied. In some ages, it was customary to recline on a bed during meals, though historical figures such as Hannibal are noted for avoiding this practice.

## Bagatelle and Billiard Tables
The archive contains detailed specifications for tables used in games, specifically bagatelle and English billiards.

### Bagatelle
Bagatelle is played on an oblong table or board. The size of this table varies from 6 ft. by 1-1/2 ft. to 10 ft. by 3 ft. The bed of the table is generally made of slate, though wood covered with green cloth is often used for smaller versions. These tables feature:
*   **Cushions:** Sides cushioned with india-rubber.
*   **The Head:** A semicircular head containing nine numbered cups set into the bed to indicate scores.
*   **Layout:** A black ball is placed on a spot approximately 9 inches in front of hole 1. A "baulk" line is drawn across the board about 18 inches from the player's end.

### English Billiards
The English billiard table is a "double-square" or true oblong. Its framework is made of mahogany or other hard wood and supported by six legs. The bed consists of five slabs of slate, each approximately 2 inches thick and 2-2/5 ft. wide by 6 ft. 11/2 in. These slabs are fitted to create a level surface covered by a tightly strained green cloth of fine texture.

The playing area is 6 ft. wide and slightly less than 12 ft. long (short by 1-1/2 in.) because the cushion's inside edge is bevelled. The table stands 2 ft. 8 in. high from the floor to the cloth. Pockets are adjusted into provisions made within the slates, and small bags of netting are attached to these pockets.

## Evolution of Table Cushions
The materials used for billiard cushions have evolved significantly:
*   **Early 19th Century:** Beds were made of wood, stone, or marble. Cushions were made of layers of list and covered in green baize.
*   **Circa 1835:** India-rubber replaced list to provide better resilience, though it hardened in cold weather.
*   **Later Developments:** Vulcanite was used but lacked sufficient "fastness" for ball rebound. Eventually, a substance was created that was elastic and cold-proof.
*   **Late 19th Century:** Pneumatic cushions (air-pumped tubes) were tried but did not gain popularity, whereas "vacuum" cushions yielded good results.

## Geological and Mining "Beds"
The term "bed" is also used in scientific contexts to describe strata or deposits.

### Bone Beds
In geology, a "bone bed" refers to a stratum containing bones of any kind, including brecciated and stalagmitic deposits on cave floors. Specific examples include:
*   **Ludlow Bone Bed:** Found at the base of the Downton Sandstone in the Upper Ludlow series, consisting largely of scales, teeth, and spines of ganoid fish.
*   **Bristol or Lias Bone Bed:** Thin layers of micaceous sandstone containing saurians and fish remains.

### Mining and Sandstone Beds
In South African mining, "banket" refers to beds of auriferous conglomerate found in the Witwatersrand gold-fields. The name derives from a Dutch sweetmeat resembling almond hard-bake, which in turn comes from the word for "bench" or "table-feast."

Additionally, English geological subdivisions include the Bunter Pebble Beds, which consist of brown and red harder sandstones containing quartzose pebbles. These beds are found in various locations, including Sherwood Forest, Cannock Chase, and the cliffs of Budleigh Salterton.
