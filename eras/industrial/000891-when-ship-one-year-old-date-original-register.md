# Ship Registration and General Average Deductions

In the context of maritime claims and the adjustment of General Average (G.A.), the age of a vessel—calculated from the date of its original register—is a primary factor in determining the deductions applied to repair costs. These regulations ensure that claims are adjusted according to the "new for old" principle, where the age of the ship, rather than the age of the specific part being repaired, regulates the deductions.

## Deductions for Iron and Steel Ships
For ships constructed of iron or steel, the period from the date of original register to the date of the accident determines the allowable repairs. When a ship is up to one year old, all repairs are allowed in full. The only exception to this rule is the painting or coating of the bottom, from which one-third of the cost is deducted. For vessels between one and three years of age, different regulations apply.

## Deductions for Wooden and Composite Ships
The rules for wooden or composite ships differ based on the vessel's age at the time of the accident:
*   **Under One Year:** When a ship is under one year old from the date of original register, no "new for old" deduction is made.
*   **Over One Year:** After the first year, a general deduction of one-third is applied.

There are several specific exceptions to the one-third deduction for wooden and composite ships. Anchors are allowed in full, while chain cables are subject to a smaller deduction of one-sixth. No deduction is applied to provisions and stores that have not been in use. Regarding metal sheathing, the cost is allowed in full for a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing, minus the proceeds gained from the old metal. However, labour metalling, felt, and nails remain subject to the one-third deduction.

## General Shipping Provisions and Expenses
Certain expenses are allowed in full for all types of ships regardless of age. These include the labour and cost of taking out and replacing bent ironwork (straightening), as well as graving dock dues. This includes the use of stages, shears, graving dock materials, and expenses related to cartages and removals.

Additionally, the cost of damage to or loss of cargo caused necessarily during reloading, stowing, storing, or discharging is admitted as G.A., provided the costs of those specific measures are also admitted as G.A.

## Wages and Maintenance during Detention
When a ship is detained in a port or enters one for the purposes of repairs, the maintenance costs and wages for the crew, officers, and master during the extra period of detention are admitted as G.A. This applies until the ship is ready to proceed on its voyage. However, if the voyage is abandoned or the ship is condemned, wages and maintenance incurred after the date of abandonment or condemnation are not admitted as G.A.

## Other Registration and Tonnage Contexts
Outside of G.A. repair deductions, registration is used for taxation and international rights:
*   **Taxation:** In certain river systems, ships over 1,500 tons register pay fees upon leaving the river (1s. 5d. per registered ton if loaded at Braila or Galatz, and 11d. per ton if loaded at Sulina). Vessels under 300 tons are exempt.
*   **International Rights:** Under certain allied and associated power agreements, ships of a country without a seacoast may be registered at a single place within its territory.
*   **Trade Trends:** Historical data from the Danube indicates a shift in vessel types, where sailing ships of 200 tons register were replaced by steamers of up to 4,000 tons register.
