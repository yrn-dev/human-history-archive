# Church Missionary Society

The Church Missionary Society (CMS) is an English organization involved in global missionary work, printing, and the establishment of religious missions in various international locations.

## Personnel and Administration
The society has been managed by various secretaries and supported by evangelical divines. Edward Bickersteth (1786–1850), an English evangelical divine born at Kirkby Lonsdale, served as one of the secretaries of the Church Missionary Society starting in 1816. Bickersteth remained in this role until 1830, when he resigned upon receiving the living of Watton, Hertfordshire. Despite his resignation from the secretaryship, he continued to preach and lecture on behalf of the society.

The society also maintained specialized operational facilities. In Islington, the Church Missionary Society operated a printing office, which employed individuals such as the English biblical scholar John Kitto starting in 1825.

## International Missions and Activities
The Church Missionary Society has extended its reach to various parts of the world, including the Middle East. In Bagdad, the society maintains a mission. This presence exists alongside other religious missions in the city, such as those maintained by the Carmelites.

The society's international operations have included the deployment of personnel to strategic locations. For example, John Kitto was transferred from the society's Islington printing office to its establishment in Malta in 1827, where he remained for a period of eighteen months.

## Context of Missionary Work in Bagdad
In Bagdad, where the Church Missionary Society operates, the population is estimated to be between 70,000 and 200,000. The majority of the population (more than two-thirds) are Moslems, primarily Shi'as, though the official classes are an exception. Other significant groups include approximately 6,000 Christians, mainly Nestorians or so-called Chaldaeans, and about 34,000 Jews. The Jewish population resides in their own quarter in the north-western part of the city. Within this urban environment, the Jews are the only segment of the population provided with schools, including an apprentice school for industrial training and schools for boys (established in 1865 by the *Alliance Israelite*) and girls (established in 1899).
