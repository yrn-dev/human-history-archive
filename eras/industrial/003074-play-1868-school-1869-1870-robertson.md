# The Robertson Comedies and the Management of Sir Squire Bancroft

## The Prince of Wales's Theatre
The management of the Prince of Wales's theatre, located off Tottenham Court Road, marked a new era in the development of the English stage. Under the leadership of Sir Squire Bancroft and his wife, Effie Marie Wilton, the playhouse became famous and succeeded in reviving London's interest in modern drama. Effie Marie Wilton, born in 1840, had been the manager of the theatre before her marriage to Bancroft in 1868.

## The Robertson Comedies
Mr. and Mrs. Bancroft were associated in the production of all the comedies written by Robertson. These productions included:
* *Society* (1865)
* *Ours* (1866)
* *Caste* (1867)
* *Play* (1868)
* *School* (1869)
* *M.P.* (1870)

Following the death of Robertson, the Bancrofts continued to produce revivals of these old comedies, utilizing an admirable company of actors to support the works.

## Other Notable Productions and Premieres
Beyond the Robertson comedies, the Bancroft management staged several other premieres that contributed to the fame of the Prince of Wales's theatre. These included *Money* (1872) by Lytton and *London Assurance* (1877) by Boucicault. They also produced *Diplomacy*, which was an adaptation of Sardou's *Dora*. In *Diplomacy* and other plays, the Bancrofts were joined by the actors William Hunter Kendal and his wife, Margaret (Madge) Shafto Robertson.

## Transition to the Haymarket and Retirement
In 1879, the Bancrofts moved their operations to the Haymarket. Their productions at this venue included W. S. Gilbert's *Sweethearts*, Pinero's *Lords and Commons*, and revivals of their previous successes. They also staged Sardou's *Fedora* and *Odette*, the latter of which featured Madame Modjeska.

The Bancrofts retired in 1885 after having made a considerable fortune. Sir Squire Bancroft, who was born on May 14, 1841, and knighted in 1897, returned to the stage in 1889. He joined Sir Henry Irving to perform the role of the abbe Latour in a revival of *Dead Heart* by Watts Phillips.
