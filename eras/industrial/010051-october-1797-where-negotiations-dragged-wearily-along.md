# Congress of Rastadt (October 1797)

The Congress of Rastadt was a diplomatic gathering that took place in October 1797. The proceedings were characterized by negotiations that dragged wearily along before they were eventually broken.

## Diplomatic Personnel and Events
Ange Elisabeth Louis Antoine Bonnier, a French diplomatist, was one of the figures charged with diplomatic missions during the Directory. Bonnier had previously served as a member of the Convention and the Legislative Assembly, where he voted with the majority. He was sent to the congress of Rastadt following a mission to Lille.

The conclusion of the diplomatic efforts at Rastadt ended in violence. On April 28, 1799, as the plenipotentiaries were leaving the town, they were assailed at the gates by Hungarian hussars. It is believed that the hussars were charged with securing the plenipotentiaries' papers. During this attack, Claude Roberjot and Bonnier were killed, while another colleague, Jean Debry, was wounded.

## Related French Diplomatic Activity (1797)
The period surrounding the Congress of Rastadt saw various other French diplomatic and political maneuvers under the Directory:

*   **Rome and Parma:** In March 1797, Joseph (brother of Napoleon and Lucien) was appointed by the Directory as minister to the court of Parma. By early summer, he proceeded to Rome in the same capacity. Joseph was ordered by the French Directory and Napoleon to encourage revolutionary movements in Rome amidst discords between the French Republic and the Vatican. Following a disturbance opposite the French embassy on December 28, 1797, which resulted in the death of General Leonard Duphot, Joseph left Rome. The city subsequently became a republic.
*   **Peace Negotiations with Great Britain:** During the summer of 1797, negotiations for peace with Great Britain took place at Lille. Hugues-Bernard Maret, Duc de Bassano, played a useful part in these negotiations. However, these hopes for peace were frustrated by the victory of the Jacobins in Paris during the *coup d'état* of Fructidor in September 1797.
*   **Internal Political Shifts:** Francois, Marquis de Barthelemy, a French politician and former minister plenipotentiary in Switzerland who had negotiated the treaties of Basel in 1795, was elected to the Directory in May 1797 through royalist influence. He was later arrested during the *coup d'état* of the 18 Fructidor on September 17, 1797, and deported to French Guiana.
