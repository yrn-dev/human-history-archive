# Macmillan & Co., Ltd.

Macmillan & Co., Ltd. is a publishing entity that has provided materials, illustrations, and permissions for a wide range of academic, scientific, and historical works. Its publications span diverse fields including zoology, natural history, and national history.

## Scientific and Natural History Publications

The company is the publisher of the *Cambridge Natural History*, a multi-volume series used as a source for detailed anatomical and biological illustrations. Volume VII of this series, titled "Fishes" (or "Fishes, &c."), provides technical diagrams and figures regarding ichthyology. These include illustrations of the *Cladoselache*, the cranial nerves of a fish, the relations of the pharynx and branchial clefts in Teleosts and Elasmobranchs, and the external features of the teleostean fish *Tilapia dolloi*. Additionally, Volume II of the *Cambridge Natural History*, titled "Worms, &c.," contains illustrations regarding the Kinorhyncha, specifically the genus *Echinoderes*.

Macmillan & Co., Ltd. also granted permission for the use of illustrations from Parker & Haswell's *Text-book of Zoology*, which includes detailed figures of the radialia of the salmon.

## Historical and Biographical Works

The company's publishing reach extends to historical accounts of the English people. Specifically, the archive notes that J. R. Green's *A Short History of the English People* was published by Macmillan & Co., Ltd.

In the realm of biography, the company is associated with the publication of *Studies*, for which a second edition was released in 1901. This edition featured biographical information written by Mr. George A. Macmillan.

## International Branches and Other Publications

The company maintains international operations, including The Macmillan Company of Canada, Ltd., located at Martin’s House in Toronto. This branch was the publisher of the 1923 edition of *The World Crisis, Volume 1*, authored by the Rt. Hon. Winston S. Churchill, who served as First Lord of the Admiralty from 1911 to 1915.
