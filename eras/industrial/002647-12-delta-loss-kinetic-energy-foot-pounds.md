# Loss of Kinetic Energy in Ballistics

In the study of ballistics, the loss of kinetic energy of a projectile is a critical factor in determining its movement and the distance it advances. This loss is mathematically represented and calculated through specific formulas involving the weight of the shot, its velocity, and the resistance it encounters.

## Calculation of Kinetic Energy Loss
When a shot advances a distance denoted as $\Delta s$ (measured in feet) during a specific time interval $\Delta t$, its velocity falls from $v + 1/2\Delta v$ to $v - 1/2\Delta v$. The loss of kinetic energy during this interval, represented as $R\Delta s$, is measured in foot-pounds. 

The formula for this loss is expressed as:
$R\Delta s = w(v + 1/2\Delta v)^2/g - w(v - 1/2\Delta v)^2/g = wv\Delta v/g$

In this equation, $w$ represents the weight of the shot in pounds and $v$ is the mean velocity.

## Relationship to Ballistic Coefficients
The distance $\Delta s$ is further defined as $\Delta s = wv\Delta v/nd^2pg = C\Delta S$. Within this framework, $C$ is known as the ballistic coefficient, also referred to as the driving power of the shot. The ballistic coefficient is defined by the formula $w/nd^2 = C$. 

The term $\Delta S$ is defined as $v\Delta v/gp$, which is also equal to $v\Delta T$. Here, $\Delta T$ represents the time in seconds for the velocity of a standard shot (for which $C=1$) to drop by $\Delta v$.

## Factors Affecting Projectile Motion
The determination of a shot's motion involves several variables:
* **Weight and Diameter:** The shot is characterized by its weight $w$ (in pounds) and its diameter $d$ (in inches).
* **Air Resistance:** The resistance of the air is denoted as $R$ (in pounds).
* **Velocity:** The initial velocity is denoted as $V$ and the final velocity as $v$, both measured in feet per second (f/s).
* **Experimental Constants:** The value $n$ is determined through experiments. For modern rifle bullets, a good average for $n$ is 0.8 or even less.

For the purpose of these calculations, the shot is assumed to move horizontally, and the curving effects caused by gravity are ignored.

## Energy and Work in Gun Systems
The kinetic energy $e$ of a shot, measured in foot-tons, can be equated to the net work realized by powder-gas as the shot advances. This work is represented graphically by the area $ACPM$ on an indicator diagram. The formula for kinetic energy $e$ is:
$e = \{w / 2240\} (1 + \{4k^2 / d^2\} \tan^2 [\delta]) \{v^2 / 2g\}$

In this formula:
* $d$ is the diameter of the shot.
* $k$ is the axial radius of gyration.
* $\delta$ represents the angle of the rifling.

The factor $4(k^2/d^2)\tan^2[\delta]$ accounts for the fraction of energy due to the rotation of the shot. Because this factor is typically small—less than 1%—it may be ignored in subsequent calculations.
