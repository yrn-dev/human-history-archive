# Louis Gabriel Ambroise, Vicomte de Bonald

## Early Life and Political Exile
Louis Gabriel Ambroise, Vicomte de Bonald, was a French politician and philosopher born on October 2, 1754, at Le Monna, located near Millau in Aveyron. Due to his dislike of the principles associated with the French Revolution, Bonald emigrated from France in 1791. During this period of exile, he joined the army of the prince of Conde and eventually settled in Heidelberg.

## Philosophical Works and Return to France
While residing in Heidelberg, Bonald authored his first significant work, a highly conservative three-volume set titled *Theorie du pouvoir politique et religieux*, published in 1796. This work was condemned by the Directory. Upon his return to France, Bonald was viewed as an object of suspicion, which forced him to live in retirement.

His professional activities resumed in 1806 when he became associated with Fievee and Chateaubriand in the management of the *Mercure de France*. Two years later, he was appointed as a councillor of the Imperial University, an institution that Bonald had frequently attacked.

## Political Career and Conservatism
Following the restoration, Bonald served as a member of the council of public instruction. Between 1815 and 1822, he held a seat in the chamber as a deputy. During his tenure, his speeches were characterized by an extreme conservative perspective; he went so far as to advocate for the implementation of literary censorship.

In 1822, Bonald was appointed minister of state, a role in which he presided over the censorship commission. That same year, he was granted the dignity of a peer. However, he lost this status in 1830 after refusing to take the oath.

## Academic Membership and Later Years
From 1816, Bonald was a member of the Academy. After 1830, he ceased taking part in public affairs and retired to his estate at Le Monna. He remained there until his death on November 23, 1840.
