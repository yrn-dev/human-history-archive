# Barley

Barley is a rapidly-growing, shallow-rooted plant used for various agricultural purposes, including livestock feed and the production of malt. Because of its shallow root system, the plant requires the upper layer of soil to be finely pulverized, free from weeds, and supplied with readily available nutriment.

## Cultivation and Soil Requirements
The routine of barley cultivation is heavily influenced by the intended use of the crop. When grown for the maltster, there is a strict demand for uniformity of sample. It is essential that grains germinate simultaneously on the maltster's floor; therefore, the entire crop must reach the same stage of maturity by the time of reaping. To achieve this, the best barley is grown on light, open, and preferably calcareous soils. On rich soils, the crop may grow too rapidly, resulting in a coarse and uneven sample. In cases where the soil condition is too high, farmers often grow wheat before barley to reduce it. Less care is required in cultivation when the barley is intended for feeding livestock.

## Planting and Maintenance
In most crop rotations, barley is grown after a "cleaning" crop, such as turnips, or sometimes after a wheat crop. During autumn and early winter, sheep feed on the roots. Following this, the ground is ploughed to a depth of only 3 or 4 inches to ensure the layer of soil fertilized by the sheep remains within reach of the plant. The ground is left open to the crumbling influence of frost until late winter, at which point it is stirred with harrows and a cultivator, or ploughed with a shallow furrow.

Seeding typically occurs in March and early April, though in early districts it may begin as early as February, provided a fine tilth is obtainable; it rarely extends beyond the end of April. The preferred seed is light in color, plump, and possesses a thin skin covered by fine wrinkles. Sowing is done at a rate of 8 to 12 pecks per acre. The drill is preferred over the broadcast-seeder to ensure uniform depth and even distribution.

Regarding fertilization, a common dressing consists of 2 or 3 cwt. of superphosphate per acre at sowing. If the ground is in poor condition, 1 cwt. of nitrate of soda may be applied once the plant is showing. However, nitrogen must be used with caution, as it increases the albumen content, which causes the barley to keep poorly and lose its germinating capacity. Farm-yard manure is also avoided. After-cultivation may include hoeing, harrowing to maintain tilth, and rolling.

## Harvesting
Barley is cut using a machine or scythe when it is fully ripe and the ears are bending over. It is common practice to allow the crop to lie loose for one or two days, based on the belief that showers, dews, or sunshine improve the color and mellow the crop. The crop may be stacked without being tied into sheaves, although this requires more labor for carrying.

## Regional Production and Economic Value
Barley is grown in various regions with varying levels of importance:

*   **Denmark:** Barley is one of the principal grain crops, grown in quantities roughly equal to rye and significantly more than wheat (which is about one-sixth the amount of barley).
*   **Scotland:** In some areas, such as those where the soil is rich and productive, barley crops are described as excellent. In certain districts, the acreage of barley is maintained specifically to meet the demands of distillers. In other Scottish regions, such as Ayrshire, barley is grown alongside wheat and oats, though oats form the bulk of the cereal crop.
*   **Russia:** In certain provinces, barley is grown alongside wheat (the chief crop), rye, oats, millet, and Indian corn.
*   **Ireland:** In Donegal, barley and wheat are considered inconsiderable crops, and the region lags behind the rest of Ulster in this regard.
*   **United States:** In Illinois, the value of the barley crop showed significant growth at the turn of the century. In 1899, the value of the barley crop was $312,730, an increase of more than 400% over 1889. By 1907, the farm value of the product had risen to $1,265,000.
