# BANKS, SIR JOSEPH, BART. (1743-1820)

Sir Joseph Banks was an English naturalist born on February 13, 1743, in Argyle Street, London. He was the son of William Banks, who had been the son of a successful doctor in Lincolnshire. William Banks served as the sheriff of his county and represented Peterborough in parliament; consequently, Joseph was raised as the son of a wealthy man.

## Education and Early Expeditions
In 1760, Banks attended Oxford, where he developed a strong interest in natural science. During his time at the university, he was responsible for the introduction of botanical lectures. Upon the death of his father in 1764, Banks came into possession of a substantial fortune. 

He undertook his first scientific expedition in 1766, traveling to Labrador and Newfoundland. From this journey, he returned with an extensive collection of insects and plants.

## Voyage with Captain Cook
Shortly after his first expedition, Banks joined Captain Cook's voyage to the Pacific Ocean to observe the transit of Venus. Banks secured his place on the "Endeavour" through the influence of his friend, Lord Sandwich, and he personally funded the fitting out of the vessel. 

To maximize the scientific value of the voyage, Banks made meticulous preparations. He convinced Dr. Daniel Solander, a prominent pupil of Linnaeus, to accompany him. Additionally, he hired painters and draughtsmen to illustrate objects of interest that could not be preserved or transported. Although the three-year voyage involved many hardships, it resulted in a rich harvest of discovery.

Banks intended to join Captain Cook's second expedition and spent significant sums on equipment and assistants. However, he was ultimately forced to relinquish this plan. He later utilized the materials and assistants he had gathered for a 1772 voyage to Iceland, returning via Staffa and the Hebrides.

## Leadership and Public Life
Banks became a fellow of the Royal Society in 1766. In 1778, he succeeded Sir John Pringle as the president of the Royal Society, a position he held until his death. In this capacity, he contributed significantly to the advancement of science within Britain.

Throughout his later life, Banks received several honors:
* In 1781, he was made a baronet.
* In 1795, he was awarded the order of the Bath.
* In 1797, he was admitted to the privy council.

Banks also provided protection to Jose Francisco Correa da Serra, a Portuguese politician and man of science who had fled to England. Under Banks's protection, Correa da Serra became a member of the Royal Society.

## Death and Legacy
Sir Joseph Banks died on June 19, 1820, at Isleworth. His influence extended to other naturalists; for example, he suggested that Sir William Jackson Hooker undertake a botanical expedition to Iceland in the summer of 1809.
