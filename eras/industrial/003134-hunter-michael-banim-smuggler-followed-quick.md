# The Ghost Hunter and The Smuggler

*The Ghost Hunter* and *The Smuggler* are literary works associated with the "O'Hara family" series of tales, authored by the Banim brothers. These works were published in quick succession following other titles such as *The Denounced* and *The Mayor of Windgap*, and were received with considerable favour.

## Authorship and Context
The works were produced by the collaboration of two Irish novelists: John Banim (1798–1842) and his elder brother Michael Banim (1796–1874). In 1822, the brothers planned a series of tales designed to illustrate Irish life, intending for these stories to serve as a counterpart for Ireland to what the Waverley Novels were for Scotland.

While John Banim initiated much of the literary effort, his health suffered severely and permanently following a personal tragedy in his youth. As his health gave way, the production of the "O'Hara family" tales became almost entirely the work of Michael Banim. Specifically, *The Ghost Hunter* is attributed to Michael Banim.

## Literary Style and Reception
The Banim brothers' works, particularly the *O'Hara Tales*, are regarded as masterpieces of faithful delineation. They are noted for their ability to truly and ably depict the lights and shadows of Irish peasant character and strong passions. However, the authors have faced accusations of straining for melodramatic effect, and their incidents are described as striking and sometimes horrible.

Critics have noted that the Banims paid little attention to the more joyous or lighter side of Irish character. Furthermore, while their later works demonstrated considerable ability, they are sometimes characterized as prolix and marked by an evident imitation of the Waverley Novels.

## The O'Hara Family Series
*The Ghost Hunter* and *The Smuggler* belong to a broader collection of narratives that followed the initial success of the *Tales of the O'Hara Family*, the first series of which appeared in April 1825. Other notable contributions to this body of work include:
* *Crohoore of the Bill Hook* (written by Michael Banim)
* *The Nowlans* (published in 1826)
* *The Croppy, a Tale of 1798* (published in 1828), which is considered hardly equal to the earlier tales despite containing some vigorous passages.
