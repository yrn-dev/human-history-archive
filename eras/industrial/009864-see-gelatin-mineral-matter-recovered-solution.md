# Recovery of Mineral Matter in the Gelatin Industry

The industrial utilization of animal bones has been developed to the extent that every constituent of the bone is utilized in commerce. Animal bones are composed of fat, gelatinous substances, and mineral matter, primarily phosphates. While the fat is supplied to the chandler and soap-maker, and the gelatinous matter forms the basis for glue and gelatin, the mineral matter is recovered for use in the manufacture of phosphorus and artificial manures.

## Recovery Process in Gelatin Works
In the gelatin industry, mineral matter must be recovered from its solution in hydrochloric acid. The process begins by running the liquors through filtration to remove any suspended matter. Once filtered, these liquors are transferred into vats where they are mixed with a neutralizer, such as milk of lime.

This neutralization process results in a sequence of precipitations. First, the slightly soluble bicalcium phosphate ($\text{CaHPO}_4$) is precipitated. With the addition of further lime, this is converted into ordinary tricalcium phosphate ($\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$). The resulting contents of the vats are then processed through a filter-press. The remaining cakes are dried on plates supported by racks within heated chambers. The final product is a valuable material used both as a manure and in the production of phosphorus.

## Properties of Calcium Orthophosphates
Tricalcium phosphate ($\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$), also known as the normal salt of the calcium orthophosphates, is the primary inorganic component of bones and the main constituent of commercial "bone-ash." It is found in the mineral apatite (occurring with fluorides) and in concretions known as coprolites. It also forms the basis of the minerals sombrerite, osteolite, and ornithite ($\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\cdot2\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

The normal salt is insoluble in water, though it is readily soluble in concentrated nitric acid and hydrochloric acid. It is also slightly soluble in common salt solutions and solutions of carbonic acid. Artificially, it can be produced as a white gelatinous precipitate that shrinks upon drying by mixing calcium chloride with ammonia and sodium hydrogen phosphate.

## Related Phosphate Salts and Manures
Other acid orthophosphates are derived from these minerals:
*   **Mono-calcium salt ($\text{CaH}_4(\text{PO}_4)_2$):** This can be obtained as crystalline scales containing one molecule of water by evaporating a solution of the normal salt in nitric or hydrochloric acid. It is readily soluble in water, resulting in a solution with an acid reaction.
*   **Di-calcium salt ($\text{Ca}_2\text{H}_2(\text{PO}_4)_2$):** This salt is found in guano and in the cloaca and ureters of the sturgeon. It is produced as rhombic plates by passing carbon dioxide into a liquid mixture of sodium phosphate and calcium chloride dilute solutions.

The artificial manure known as "superphosphate of lime" is composed of calcium sulphate and the mono-calcium salt. This is produced by treating coprolites and ground bones with sulphuric acid.

## Other Industrial Bone Treatments
While the gelatin and glue works consume most of the world's bone supply, specific bones such as "knuckles" and leg bones ("marrows") are treated differently for the manufacture of bone articles (such as knife-handles and buttons). These bones are steeped in a 1% brine solution for three to four days to separate fibrous matter and then simmered in water for approximately six hours. 

This simmering process removes gelatinous matter and fat. The fat is skimmed off for use in soaps or as an oil adulterant, while the stearine is sent to candle-makers. The resulting gelatinous water is filtered and used to create size for cardboard boxes. Additionally, the dry distillation of bones that have only been partially stripped of gelatin produces bone pitch, "bone oil" (a tarry distillate), and animal charcoal (a carbonaceous residue).
