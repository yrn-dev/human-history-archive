# The Leaning Towers of Bologna

The city of Bologna contains two remarkable square brick structures known as the leaning towers: the Torre Asinelli and the Torre Garisenda. These towers date from 1109 and 1110, respectively.

## Structural Dimensions
The two towers differ significantly in their height and the degree of their lean. The Torre Asinelli reaches a height of 320 ft. and is 4 ft. out of the perpendicular. The Torre Garisenda remains unfinished; it stands 163 ft. high and is 10 ft. out of the perpendicular.

## The Urban Context of Bologna
The towers are situated within a town characterized by many fine private palaces dating from the 13th century onwards. A defining architectural feature of Bologna is its arcaded streets, a characteristic that has been maintained in modern additions and the restoration of medieval buildings. For those seeking a panoramic view of the town, the Madonna di S. Luca, located to the south-west, provides a vantage point at an elevation of 938 ft.

## Cultural Institutions
Bologna is also home to the Museo Civico, which is regarded as one of Italy's most important museums. The institution houses significant collections of antiquities from the neighborhood and Bologna. Its picture gallery provides a survey of earlier Bolognese paintings as well as works from the 16th and 17th centuries by the Bolognese eclectics, including Guercino, Domenichino, Guido Reni, and the Caracci. 

While the primitive masters in the collection are not considered to be of great excellence, the works of 15th-century masters—specifically Lorenzo Costa of Ferrara (1460-1535) and Francesco Francia (1450-1517)—are noted for their considerable merit. The most prized treasure of the museum is Raphael's *S. Cecilia*, which was painted around 1515 for the church of S. Giovanni in Monte.
