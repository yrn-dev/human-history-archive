# Richard Grenville, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos

Richard Grenville (1776–1839) was a British politician and peer who served as the 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos. His titles and political career were closely tied to the town of Buckingham and the legacy of the Grenville family.

## Early Political Career and Titles
Between 1797 and 1813, Richard Grenville served as one of the members of parliament for Buckinghamshire. During this period, he was known as Earl Temple, a role in which he took an active part in politics. In February 1813, he succeeded his father to become the marquess of Buckingham.

The marquessate and dukedom associated with the Grenville family were derived from the town of Buckingham, rather than the county. The title of marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham" had originated in 1784 when the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess. This title was later elevated into a dukedom for Richard Grenville in 1822, making him the 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos. This elevation followed his marriage to the only child of the 3rd Duke of Chandos.

## Later Life and Financial Difficulties
The later years of the 1st Duke's life were marked by financial embarrassments. Due to these monetary struggles, he lived outside of England for a period of time. Although he died in 1839, an account of his travels was published posthumously in 1862 under the title *The Private Diary of Richard, Duke of Buckingham and Chandos*.

## Succession and Legacy
Richard Grenville was succeeded by his only child, Richard Grenville (1797–1861), who became the 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos. The 2nd Duke continued the family's political involvement, serving as a member of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1818 to 1839 and holding the office of lord privy seal from September 1841 to January 1842.

The dukedom eventually passed to the 3rd Duke, Richard Grenville (1823–1889), the only son of the 2nd Duke. Upon the death of the 3rd Duke in 1889, the dukedom became extinct as he left no son. His other titles were distributed among his heirs: the Scottish barony of Kinloss passed to his eldest daughter, Mary; the earldom of Temple went to his nephew, William Stephen Gore-Langton; and the viscounty of Cobham passed to his kinsman, Charles George, 5th Baron Lyttelton.
