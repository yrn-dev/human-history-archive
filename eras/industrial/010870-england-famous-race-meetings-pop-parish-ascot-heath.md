# Ascot

## Overview and Geography
Ascot is a village located in England within the Wokingham parliamentary division of Berkshire. It is widely recognized for its race-meetings. The parish of Ascot Heath had a population of 1,927 as of 1901. The village is served by a station on the Southwestern railway, known as Ascot and Sunninghill, situated 29 miles west-south-west of London. The second part of the station's name refers to an adjacent township, which is a civil parish with a population of 4,719.

## The Race-Course and History
The Ascot race-course is situated on Ascot Heath. It was laid out in 1711 by order of Queen Anne, who attended the first meeting held there on August 11 of that year. The course is almost exactly 2 miles in circumference. 

The races were established by the duke of Cumberland, the uncle of George III, and continue to be patronized by royalty in semi-state or state capacities. Early records of the races appear in the first *Racing Calendar* published in 1727, though at that time the events consisted mostly of plates and prizes of small importance. A royal plate for hunters was recorded in 1785.

## Major Races and Events
Meetings at Ascot are held in June and are considered among the most fashionable in England, frequently attended by members of the royal family. The royal procession, a feature for which the meeting is peculiarly famous, was initiated by George IV in 1820.

Several prestigious races are held at the course:
*   **The Ascot Gold Cup:** Instituted in 1807, this is the principal race. Between 1845 and 1853, it was known as the Emperor's Plate because the prize was offered by the emperor of Russia. In 1854, during the Crimean War, it reverted to the name Ascot Gold Cup and was funded by the race fund.
*   **The Queen's Vase:** First given in 1838.
*   **The Royal Hunt Cup:** First given in 1843.
*   **The New Stakes:** Established in 1843 for two-year-olds. It is noted for having a list of winners that includes many of the most famous horses on the turf.
*   **The Ascot Stakes:** Established in 1839.

## Context of English Horse Racing
Ascot exists within a broader tradition of English racing that saw a significant decline during the latter half of the 18th century due to wars. Racing revived rapidly starting in the 19th century, particularly after the conclusion of the French war in 1815. 

In the hierarchy of English racing, Ascot is one of the venues where first-class two-year-old horses typically first appear, usually around the middle of June. This contrasts with other established venues such as Newmarket—often called "the headquarters of the Turf"—and Goodwood, where the best two-year-olds are usually seen.
