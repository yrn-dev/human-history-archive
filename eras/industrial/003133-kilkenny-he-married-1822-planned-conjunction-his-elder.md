# John Banim

John Banim (1798–1842) was an Irish novelist who was sometimes referred to as the "Scott of Ireland." Born in Kilkenny on April 3, 1798, Banim began his education at Kilkenny College in his thirteenth year, where he focused specifically on painting and drawing. He continued his artistic studies for two years at schools associated with the Royal Society in Dublin. Following this period, he returned to Kilkenny to teach drawing.

## Personal Life and Early Struggles
While teaching in Kilkenny, Banim fell in love with one of his pupils. Although the affection was mutual, the young woman's parents intervened and removed her from the city. She died two months later. This event had a profound and permanent impact on Banim's health.

In 1820, Banim moved to Dublin to dedicate himself to literature. His early works included a poem titled *The Celts' Paradise* and a play, *Damon and Pythias*, which was performed at Covent Garden in 1821. During a brief return visit to Kilkenny, he married.

## Literary Career and Collaborations
In 1822, John Banim and his elder brother, Michael Banim (1796–1874), planned a series of tales intended to illustrate Irish life. Their goal was for these stories to serve Ireland in the same way the Waverley Novels served Scotland. 

Following this plan, John moved to London, where he supported himself through writing for the stage and various magazines. In 1824, he published a volume of miscellaneous essays anonymously under the title *Revelations of the Dead Alive*. 

In April 1825, Banim published the first series of *Tales of the O'Hara Family*, which was an immediate and decided success. This collaborative effort included *Crohoore of the Bill Hook*, which was written by Michael Banim. A second series followed in 1826, featuring the Irish novel *The Nowlans*.

## Later Works and Decline
As John Banim's health deteriorated, the subsequent productions of the "O'Hara family" series were primarily the work of Michael Banim. This included *The Croppy, a Tale of 1798* (1828), which, despite containing some vigorously written passages, is considered hardly equal to the earlier tales. Other works released in quick succession and received with favor included *The Denounced*, *The Mayor of Windgap*, *The Smuggler*, and *The Ghost Hunter* (the latter written by Michael Banim).

In his later years, John Banim faced straitened financial circumstances. In 1829, he traveled to France; during his time abroad, a movement was initiated to provide him with relief.
