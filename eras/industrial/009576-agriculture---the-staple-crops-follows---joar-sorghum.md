# Agriculture

Agriculture varies significantly across different global regions, with staple crops and farming methods dictated by soil quality, climate, and local industry. Production ranges from large-scale grain and forage cultivation to specialized commercial crops and livestock rearing.

## Grain and Food Staple Production
In several Asian provinces, rice serves as the primary food grain. In Burma, rice is the staple crop for both Upper and Lower regions, with the agricultural season typically aligned with the south-west monsoon from mid-May to November. In other provinces, rice occupies approximately 66% of the cropped area, featuring three annual harvests: *boro* (spring), *aus* (autumn), and *aman* (winter). The *aman* crop is the most extensive, grown on low land and harvested in December or January, while *aus* is sown on high ground and harvested in August or September. In the plains of certain provinces, rice is almost the only food grain, with 1,194,000 acres under cultivation out of 1,736,000 total cultivated acres in 1901-1902.

In the Deccan and Khandesh, the staple food grains are bajra (*Holcus spicatus*) and joar (*Sorghum vulgare*), while rice is the chief product of the Konkan. In Korea, rice is a primary article of cultivation, with farmers estimating an average yield of thirty-fold for rice and twenty-fold for other grains. Other Korean cereals and root crops include millet, beans, bearded wheat, oats, barley, sorghum, and both Irish and sweet potatoes.

In Bohemia, the potato crop is the staple food of the people. Other principal products include rye, barley, oats, and wheat, though competition from Hungarian wheat has led to the conversion of large tracts of land to beetroot cultivation. In certain British regions, oats are the predominant crop, while barley is grown extensively to meet the demands of distillers.

## Commercial and Industrial Crops
Various regions specialize in fibers, oil-seeds, and other commercial products. Jute is a significant commercial staple in Bengal and is also grown in Sylhet and Goalpara. Cotton is produced in large quantities along the slopes of the Assam range and in the Bombay presidency. In the latter, the superior Hinganghat variety has largely superseded the indigenous plant in Khandesh, and American varieties have been introduced in the southern Mahratta country and the Dharwar collectorate.

Oil-seeds are widely grown across different territories. In Behar, the principal oil-seeds are *lisi* or *masina* (linseed), *til* (sesamum), and *sarisha* (mustard). Other common oil-seeds include castor-oil, safflower, and mustard.

Specialized crops include:
* **Hops:** The Saaz district in Bohemia is celebrated for hops. In British agriculture, hops experience wide yield fluctuations; for example, production dropped from 776,144 cwt. in 1886 to 281,291 cwt. in 1888.
* **Forage:** In the western United States, alfalfa has become the staple leguminous forage crop. In Kansas, its cultivation grew from 34,384 acres in 1891 to 743,050 acres by 1907.
* **Miscellaneous:** Other produced items include sugar-cane, tobacco, turmeric, red pepper, and *ginseng* (specifically at Songdo in Korea).

## Livestock and Forestry
Livestock is a central agricultural industry in several regions. In certain areas, cattle and stock are the staple industry, complemented by dairy farming. In other counties, livestock raising is pursued with great enterprise on hilly land, with a high number of sheep and horses used for breeding and agricultural work. In Korea, magnificent red bulls are bred for transport and ploughing, while pony stallions (10 to 12 hands high) are bred at provincial stations for carrying burdens.

Forestry and land reclamation have also seen significant investment. In Scotland, the earls of Seafield undertook immense plantations between 1811 and 1845, planting 31,686,472 trees—including 18,938,224 Scots firs and 11,904,798 larches—across 8,223 acres. In Bohemia, forests cover 29.01% of the total area.

## Farming Methods and Infrastructure
Agricultural techniques vary by region and crop. In Burma, oxen are used for light soils, while stronger buffaloes are used for marshy lands. Irrigation has been expanded through projects like the Mandalay Canal (40 m), the Shwebo canal (27 m), and the Môn canal (53 m), bringing over 300,000 acres under irrigation. In the United States, the progress of irrigation has been a key factor in the expansion of alfalfa.

Korean agriculture utilizes iron-shod wooden ploughs, bamboo rakes, and large shovels worked by three to five men. Rice is threshed by beating ears on a log, while other grains are processed with flails on mud floors.
