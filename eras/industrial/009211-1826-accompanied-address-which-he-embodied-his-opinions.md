# 1826: Political and Intellectual Developments

The year 1826 was marked by significant administrative proposals in South America, ecclesiastical disputes regarding biblical circulation, and various developments in European literature and politics.

## Bolivar and the Constitution of Bolivia
In 1826, Simon Bolivar focused his efforts on administering the affairs of freed provinces. On May 25 of that year, he presented a project for a constitution for Bolivia to the state's congress. This project was accompanied by an address in which Bolivar embodied his opinions regarding the form of government he believed was most expedient for the newly established republics.

The proposed code was not well-received. Its most extraordinary provision suggested that executive authority be placed in the hands of a president for life. This president would be without responsibility and would possess the power to nominate his own successor. These proposals caused alarm among friends of liberty and created lively apprehensions among republicans in Chile and Buenos Aires. Additionally, in Peru, Bolivar faced accusations that he intended to render himself the perpetual dictator of a confederacy uniting Bolivia, Peru, and Colombia into a single state.

## Religious and Ecclesiastical Events
Several religious organizations and figures reached milestones or faced controversies in 1826:

*   **The Bible Society:** The society faced a controversy regarding the circulation of Bibles containing the Deutero-canonical books of the Old Testament, primarily through affiliated societies on the continent. In 1826, the society resolved that its fundamental law—to circulate the Bible alone without note or comment—distinctly excluded the circulation of books or parts of books usually termed "Apocryphal." This decision failed to satisfy many supporters in Scotland, leading to the formation of independent organizations in Glasgow and Edinburgh, which later amalgamated in 1861 into the National Bible Society of Scotland.

## European Literature and Politics
In Austria, the poet Grillparzer wrote a second historical tragedy in 1826 titled *Ein treuer Diener seines Herrn* (performed in 1828). This work attempted to embody a "more heroic gospel" through the subject of Bankbanus's superhuman self-effacement before Duke Otto of Meran, serving as an illustration of Kant's categorical imperative of duty. However, the subject proved too uncompromising for theatre audiences. In the same year, Grillparzer visited Goethe in Weimar, allowing him to compare the intellectual thraldom of Vienna with the enlightened conditions of the small Saxon duchy.

In British politics, the general election of 1826 saw Stanley renounce his connection with Stockbridge to become the representative of the borough of Preston. This move, influenced by the Derby influence, allowed Stanley to speak with force against the system of rotten boroughs during the Reform Bill debates without being accused of personal inconsistency.
