# Janos Bacsanyi

Janos Bacsanyi (1763–1845) was a Hungarian poet born on May 11, 1763, in Tapolcza. His literary career began in 1785 with the publication of a patriotic poem titled *The Valour of the Magyars*.

## Political Activity and Imprisonment
In 1785, Bacsanyi began working as a clerk in the treasury at Kaschau. During this time, he collaborated with two other Hungarian patriots to edit the *Magyar Museum*, a publication that the government suppressed in 1792. Following this suppression, Bacsanyi was deprived of his clerkship in 1793. In 1794, he was imprisoned in the state prison of the Spielberg, located near Bruenn, for two years due to his involvement in the conspiracy of Bishop Martinovich.

## Literary Career and Exile
After his release from prison, Bacsanyi contributed significantly to the literary review *Magyar Minerva*. He later moved to Vienna, where he married and secured a position in the bank. In 1809, he translated a proclamation from Napoleon to the Magyars. Because this was considered an anti-Austrian act, Bacsanyi was forced to seek refuge in Paris.

Following the fall of Napoleon, Bacsanyi was handed over to the Austrians. He was permitted to live in Linz, provided that he never left the town. While residing there, he continued his literary work, publishing a collection of poems in Pest in 1827, which saw a second edition published in Buda in 1835. Additionally, Bacsanyi edited the poetical works of Faludi and Anyos.

Bacsanyi died in Linz on May 12, 1845.

## Geography of Publication
Bacsanyi's works were published in the sister towns of Buda and Pest, which were later united into the municipality of Budapest in 1872. 

Pest, located on the left bank of the Danube, was originally a German settlement described in the 13th century as "Villa Teutonica ditissima." Though it was destroyed by Tatars in 1241, it was repeopled and created a royal free city by Bela IV in 1244. Pest later became the center of intellectual and nationalistic life.

Buda, located on the right bank, was developed around a fortress and palace built by King Bela IV in 1247 and became the capital of Hungary in 1361. While Buda served as the official center containing government offices and ministries, Pest grew rapidly during the 19th century to become the commercial and industrial hub.
