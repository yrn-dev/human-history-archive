# Historical Events and Developments: 1837–1860

The period between 1837 and 1860 saw significant shifts in political capitals, financial systems, colonial administration, and intellectual production across various regions of the world.

## Political Transitions in Burma
In the Burman empire, the seat of government shifted several times between ancient capitals. Ava, also known by the classical name Yadanapura ("the city of precious gems"), was founded in A.D. 1364 by Thadomin Pay as a successor to Pagan. While it served as the seat of government for approximately four centuries under thirty kings, it was periodically superseded. 

In 1782, Bodaw Pay founded a new capital at Amarapura. However, King Baggidaw deserted Amarapura in 1823 to return the capital to Ava. This arrangement lasted until 1837, when King Tharawaddi deposed Baggidaw, causing the capital to revert to Amarapura. This continued until 1860, when King Mindon occupied Mandalay, which became the last capital. Today, Ava is described as being more like a park than a capital site, though portions of the royal palace and the great council chamber remain visible.

## Financial and Industrial Trends
In the United States, the banking system underwent a transition in the use of resources. Before the Civil War, the power of note-issue was more critical than the deposit system. In 1835, the combined capital and circulation of state banks was approximately $335,000,000, while deposits were only $83,000,000. By 1907, this ratio had inverted, with national bank deposits reaching $4,322,000,000 against a circulation and capital of $1,430,000,000.

State bank circulation experienced significant fluctuations during the mid-19th century. A maximum of $149,185,890 was reached in 1837, followed by a decline to $106,968,572 three years later, and a minimum of $58,563,608 in 1843. Following this low, circulation trended upward, reaching $207,102,477 by 1860 and $238,677,218 by 1863.

Simultaneously, Illinois experienced rapid industrial growth. In 1860, the state ranked eighth in the value of its manufactures, rising from fifteenth in 1850. This growth continued through the end of the century, with the state reaching third place by 1890 and 1900.

## Colonial Administration in Celebes
In the state of Boni on Celebes, Dutch influence was undisputed from 1666 until the beginning of the 19th century. Although the British held Boni for two years starting in 1814, it reverted to the Dutch following the downfall of Napoleon. Native resistance persisted, including an unsuccessful expedition by General van Geen in 1825. Between 1858 and 1859, two expeditions were required to oppose a princess regent's attempt to gain independence. In 1860, a new prince who owed allegiance to the Dutch was established. In Boni, succession to the throne prioritizes the female line over the male line.

## Intellectual and Literary Works
The era was marked by the publication of various scholarly and biographical works:

*   **Literature and Criticism:** Berriet Saint-Prix established a critical text of Boileau's works between 1830 and 1837. This text was later reprinted in 1860 by Garnier freres, featuring notes by Paul Cheron and an essay by Sainte-Beuve.
*   **Memoirs and Diaries:** Henry Greville (1801–1872) kept a diary while serving as an attache to the British embassy in Paris (1834–1844). The *Greville Memoirs* were published in sets, with one covering 1817 to 1837 (published in 1875) and two covering the period from 1837 to 1860 (published in 1885 and 1887).
*   **Theology and History:** Edward Beecher published *History of the Alton Riots* and *Statement of Anti-Slavery Principles* in 1837, and later wrote *The Concord of the Ages* in 1860. Walter Farquhar Hook, who received a D.D. in 1837 and served as an incumbency in Leeds from 1837 to 1859, published *Lives of the Archbishops of Canterbury* between 1860 and 1876.
*   **Natural Science:** E. Rüppell published *Neue Wirbelthiere, "Fische"* in 1837. P. Bleeker, a Dutch East Indian Government surgeon, amassed fish collections from 1840 until his return to Europe in 1860, later publishing the *Atlas ichthyologique des Indes Orientales Néerlandaises* in 1862.

## Notable Appointments
In the United Kingdom, Matthew Talbot Baines (1790–1860) became the recorder of Hull in 1837. He later served as M.P. for Hull in 1847 and as president of the Poor Law Board in 1849 and again from 1852 to 1855, before entering the cabinet as chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster in 1856.
