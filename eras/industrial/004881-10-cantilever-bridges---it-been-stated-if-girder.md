# Cantilever Bridges

Cantilever bridges are a system of bridging that utilizes cantilevers and suspended girders. In this system, the main girder functions virtually as a continuous girder but is hinged at the points of contrary flexure—the points where the bending moment changes sign. This design ensures that these hinges become points where the bending moment is zero, thereby removing ambiguity regarding the distribution and magnitude of stresses.

## Engineering Principles and Advantages
In girder bridges consisting of three or more spans, making the girders continuous can result in an important economy of material. In a three-span bridge, the theoretical advantage of continuity is approximately 49% for dead load and 16% for live load (or 45% and 15% respectively, depending on the allowance for support level uncertainty). However, continuity is often avoided because the settlement of supports can seriously alter the points of contrary flexure and the resulting stresses, potentially rendering the bridge unsafe.

The cantilever system provides the same economy and freedom from stress uncertainty as continuous girders but avoids these risks. This system is capable of spanning distances greater than those practicable with independent girders because it virtually reduces the spans to smaller units regarding stress.

A significant practical advantage of the cantilever system is the method of construction. Cantilevers can be built out from the piers member by member without the need for temporary scaffolding below. This process obviates the difficulty of building in deep water, saves the cost of scaffolding, and ensures that navigation is not interrupted. The center girder may then be lifted from the water level or rolled into place upon the cantilevers.

## Stability and Construction Variations
Cantilever bridges are unstable unless they are rigidly fixed at the piers. Stability can be achieved through the great width of the river piers or by ensuring a great excess of dead load over live load. 

While typical American designs may anchor the shore ends of the cantilevers to abutments, other methods exist. J.A.L. Waddell demonstrated that simple independent spans can be erected by building them out as cantilevers and then converting them into independent girders after erection. In this process, side spans are first erected on staging and anchored to the piers; then, using temporary members, the center span is built out from both sides before the temporary members are removed.

## Notable Examples

### The Forth Bridge
Begun in 1882 and completed in 1889, the Forth bridge was adopted due to exceptional local conditions at the site. It is located at the only narrowing of the Forth in a 50-mile distance, where the channel is divided by the island of Inchgarvie. The bridge has a total length of 5,330 ft, comprising:
* A central tower on Inchgarvie (260 ft).
* Fife and Queensferry piers (145 ft each).
* Six cantilevers (680 ft each).
* Two central girders between cantilevers (350 ft each).
* Two main spans (1,710 ft each).

The towers reach an extreme height of 361 ft above high water, with a clear headway of 157 ft. The structure used 38,000 tons of steel (excluding approach viaducts). To balance the rolling load, the weight of the central girder, and an additional 200 tons, the outer ends of the shore cantilevers are loaded.

### Other International Bridges
* **Lansdowne Bridge (Sukkur, Indus):** Completed in 1889, this bridge features a clear span of 790 ft and a suspended girder 200 ft long. The back guys are the most strained parts, designed for 1,200 tons of stress.
* **Jubilee Bridge (Hugli):** Designed by Sir Bradford Leslie, this bridge uses Whipple Murphy type girders with curved top booms. It features a central double cantilever 360 ft long and two side span girders 420 ft long.
* **Poughkeepsie Bridge (Hudson):** Built between 1886 and 1887, it contains five river and two shore spans. The cantilever spans are 547 ft, the girder spans are 525 ft, and the shore spans are 201 ft.
* **Quebec Bridge (St Lawrence):** This bridge collapsed during construction in 1907. It had a total length between abutments of 3,240 ft, a channel span of 1,800 ft, a suspended span of 675 ft, and shore spans of 562½ ft.
* **Red Rock Bridge (Colorado River):** This bridge features a center span of 660 ft.
* **Tower Bridge (London):** While primarily a suspension bridge, its high-level footways utilize main girders of the cantilever and suspended girder type. The middle girders are 120 ft long and attached to cantilevers via links.
