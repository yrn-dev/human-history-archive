# Cassell's National Library

## Overview
Cassell's National Library is a publication series that featured standard literary works. Notable inclusions in the library include the texts *Wisdom of the Ancients* and *New Atlantis*, which were published in editions dated 1886 and 1903.

## Publishing History and Origins
The series is associated with the publishing legacy of John Cassell (1817–1865), a British publisher born in Manchester. Cassell was a self-educated man who acquired a knowledge of French and a considerable acquaintance with English literature. After working as a joiner and spending time as an active worker for the cause of temperance, he established himself as a tea and coffee merchant in 1847. 

Shortly after, Cassell started a publishing business specifically intended to provide the working classes with good literature. This venture evolved into the firm Messrs. Cassell, Petter, Galpin & Co. by 1859. The company's output was diverse, including the *Popular Educator* (1852–1855), *Cassell's Magazine* (beginning in 1852), the *Technical Educator* (1870–1872), and the *Magazine of Art* (1878–1903). A defining characteristic of Cassell's popular books was their use of illustration; for example, during the Crimean War, Cassell utilized cuts from the Parisian publication *L'Illustration* for his *Family Paper*, which began in 1853.

In 1883, the firm was reorganized as a limited liability company under the name Cassell & Company, Limited.

## Related Works and Authors
While *Wisdom of the Ancients* and *New Atlantis* appeared in Cassell's National Library, other editions of *New Atlantis* were produced by different authors and series. G. C. M. Smith published a version of *New Atlantis* in 1900. Additionally, the *New Atlantis* was included alongside the *Advancement of Learning* in the "World's Classics" series in 1906, featuring an introduction by Prof. T. Case.

The broader publishing house of Cassell also produced other specialized series, such as Cassell's Representative Biographies, which included a biography of John Bright, M.P., written by John M^cGilchrist in 1868.
