# Flemish Bond in Brickwork

## Definition and Mechanism of Bonding
Bonding in brickwork is the specific arrangement used to ensure that the bricks in every course cover the joints of the bricks in the course immediately below. The primary objective of this process is to make the entire combination of bricks act as a single, dependent mass. This arrangement allows weight placed on the topmost brick to be carried down and distributed over an area that increases with every subsequent course.

While a simple arrangement may create a longitudinal bond, such a bond cannot extend its influence beyond the width of a single brick. Consequently, a wall that is two bricks or one and a half bricks thick would effectively function as several independent half-brick thick walls. To resolve this and create a transverse bond while preserving the longitudinal bond, bricks are laid in a definite arrangement of headers (bricks showing their short ends) and stretchers (bricks showing their long sides).

## Characteristics of Flemish Bond
Flemish bond is a method of bonding brickwork that differs from the practice of placing bricks in alternate courses of headers and stretchers. Instead, Flemish bond places bricks alternately as headers and stretchers within the same course. This results in a course that has the same appearance on each face of the wall.

The execution of this variety of bond requires the use of closers. Because of the increased number of joints and the requirement for half bricks, as well as a comparatively weaker transverse tie, Flemish bond is not as strong or as perfect as English bond.

## Applications and Selection
Despite its relative weakness compared to English bond, Flemish bond is frequently selected for the external walls of other buildings and domestic structures. This preference is due to the arrangement of the face joints, which provides a neater appearance and a more desirable aesthetic effect.

In contrast, English bond is preferred for engineering works and buildings erected for manufacturing or similar purposes. In these contexts, the highest importance is placed on compactness and the greatest degree of strength.

## Application in Arches
The concept of bonding also extends to the construction of arches, where the bond is named based on the arrangement of stretchers and headers on the soffit (the underside of the arch). When an arch is built using Flemish bond, the soffit presents the same appearance as the elevation of a wall constructed with that same bond.
