## Sir Charles William Wilson
Sir Charles William Wilson (1836–1907) was a Major-General in the Royal Engineers who served in several high-level administrative and technical capacities for the British government. In addition to his role as the British Commissioner on the Servian Boundary Commission, Wilson served as the Secretary to the North American Boundary Commission from 1858 to 1862.

Wilson's career included significant leadership roles in surveying and military education. He served as the Director-General of the Ordnance Survey between 1886 and 1894, and later held the position of Director-General of Military Education from 1895 to 1898. He was also a published author, writing works such as *Life of Lord Clive* and *From Korti to Khartoum*. He held the honors of K.C.B., K.C.M.G., and was a Fellow of the Royal Society (F.R.S.).

## Context of Servia
The region of Servia has historically been a site of strategic importance and frequent conflict. The city of Belgrade, located at the confluence of the Danube and the Save, was considered the key to Hungary and was believed to secure possession of Servia. Due to its position commanding the traffic between the Upper and Lower Danube, the area saw numerous battles. 

Control of the region shifted frequently between various powers, including the Celts, Romans, Huns, Sarmatians, Goths, Gepids, Franks, and Bulgarians. In the 11th century, the Byzantine emperor Basil II reconquered the area for the Greek empire, before it was taken by the Hungarians under King Stephen in 1124. During the 14th century, the region was held by Servian kings, though the Servian prince George Brankovich ceded Belgrade to the Hungarians in 1427. The area later fell under Turkish possession in 1521 after Sultan Suleiman took the city, remaining so until 1688.
