## Prince Bojidar Karageorgevitch
Prince Bojidar Karageorgevitch (died 1908) was a multi-faceted figure who worked as a goldsmith, designer, art critic, and artist. He contributed writing to the *Magazine of Art* and the Paris publication *Figaro*. His literary work includes the authorship of *Enchanted India*, and he served as a translator for the works of Jokai and Tolstoi.

## Mechanical Integration and Equilibrium
In the study of calculating machines and integrators, there exists a remarkable instrument that operates on the equilibrium of a rigid body under the action of springs, rather than being kinematic. This instrument utilizes a hollow cylinder (designated as H) for rigidity, which is supported on knife-edges (O) and can rotate about its axis.

The mechanism involves springs attached to the prolongation of a horizontal diameter. On the left side, there is a series of $n$ identical small springs ($s$) placed side by side at equal intervals at a distance $a$ from the axis of the knife-edges. On the right side, a single spring ($S$) is positioned at distance $b$. These springs are assumed to follow Hooke's law, where the force asserted ($p$) is equal to $k\lambda$ (the constant multiplied by the elongation beyond natural length).

In a normal position of equilibrium, the relationship is expressed as $nkla = KLb$, where $l$ and $L$ are the elongations of the small and large springs respectively, and $k$ and $K$ are their constants. When the top ends ($C$) of the small springs are raised by distances $y_1, y_2, ... y_n$, the body $H$ turns, causing $B$ to move down by distance $z$ and $A$ to move up by distance $(a/b)z$. The new equilibrium is reached when $ak([\Sigma]y - n (a/b) z) = bKz$, resulting in the formula $z = [\Sigma]y / (n (a/b + l/L))$.

## Engineering and Stress Analysis
In structural engineering, the breaking stress of a bar is not a fixed value but depends on the range of variation of stress when that variation is repeated a very large number of times. This was demonstrated through experiments by Johann Bauschinger, Sir B. Baker, and A. Wöhler.

The statical breaking strength ($K$) is the breaking strength of a bar per unit of section when loaded once gradually to breaking. The breaking strength when subjected to stresses varying alternately and repeatedly an indefinitely great number of times is denoted as $k_{max}$. The range of stress ($\Delta$) is calculated as $k_{max} - k_{min}$ if stresses are of the same kind, or $k_{max} + k_{min}$ if they are of opposite kinds (tension or thrust). Wöhler's results follow the rule: $k_{max} = ½\Delta + \sqrt{K^2 - n\Delta K}$.

The constant $n$ varies between 1.3 and 2 depending on the quality of steel and iron; for mild steel or ductile iron, it is taken as 1.5. Different loading scenarios affect the breaking stress:
* **Statical load:** $\Delta = 0$ and $k_{max} = K$.
* **Alternately loaded and removed:** $\Delta = k_{max}$ and $k_{max} = 0.6 K$.
* **Alternate tension and compression of equal amount:** $\Delta = 2 f_{max}$ and $k_{max} = 0.33 K$.

The safe working stress is determined by dividing $k_{max}$ by the factor of safety. The term "fatigue" is used to describe the diminishing of breaking stress under repeated loading as the range of variation increases. In 1869, Unwin suggested in *Wrought Iron Bridges and Roofs* that working stress should depend on the ratio of live load ($B$) to dead load ($A$).
