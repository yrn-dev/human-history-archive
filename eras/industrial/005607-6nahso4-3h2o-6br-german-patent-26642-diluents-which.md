# 6NaHSO4 + 3H2O + 6Br (German Patent, 26642)

The chemical equation 5NaBr + NaBrO₃ + 6H₂SO₄ = 6NaHSO₄ + 3H₂O + 6Br is associated with German Patent 26642. This specific reaction is utilized in organic chemistry to facilitate reactions that are only possible through the aid of nascent bromine. These reactions are carried out by employing solutions of bromate and sodium bromide, with the amount of sulphuric acid calculated according to the aforementioned equation.

## Properties of Bromine
Bromine possesses chemical properties that are generally intermediate between those of iodine and chlorine. Due to these properties, it requires either a fairly high temperature or the presence of a catalytic agent to achieve union with hydrogen. Bromine does not combine directly with carbon, nitrogen, or oxygen.

When uniting with other elements, bromine forms bromides, a process that often occurs with explosive violence. Specific reactions include:
* **Phosphorus:** Inflames in bromine vapour and detonates in liquid bromine.
* **Potassium:** Reacts energetically.
* **Sodium:** Requires heating to 200° C to react.
* **Iron:** Occasionally used for the purpose of absorbing bromine vapour.

## Applications in Chemistry
In analytical chemistry, the primary use of bromine is based on the oxidizing action of bromine water. Both bromine water and bromine are capable of bleaching organic colouring matters. In the field of organic chemistry, bromine is used extensively as an oxidizing agent, a substituting agent, and for the preparation of addition compounds. 

Researchers have studied bromine reactions in various forms, including:
* Liquid form
* Vapour
* Solution
* In the presence of "bromine carriers"

The action of bromine vapour on organic compounds is affected by sunlight, which can accelerate the reaction, retard it, or result in different products. Additionally, R. Wagner and others proposed using bromine for the extraction of gold, though the general application of this method has been restricted by the cost of bromine.

## Diluents and Solvents
The choice of solvent is important because the nature of the product and the velocity of the reaction may vary depending on the solvent used. Bromine is typically employed in the following diluents:
* Water
* Ether
* Chloroform
* Acetic acid
* Hydrochloric acid
* Carbon bisulphide

Less commonly, hydrobromic acid, potassium bromide, and alcohol are used as diluents. When these processes are complete, any excess bromine is removed by shaking with mercury, using sulphurous acid, or by heating.
