# Alexander Dallas Bache

## Early Life and Education
Alexander Dallas Bache (1806–1867) was an American physicist and the great-grandson of Benjamin Franklin. He was born in Philadelphia on July 19, 1806. Bache pursued his early higher education at the United States Military Academy at West Point, from which he graduated in 1825. Following his graduation, he served for a period as an assistant professor at the academy.

## Military and Academic Career
As a lieutenant in the corps of engineers, Bache spent one or two years working on the erection of coast fortifications. He later transitioned into academia, holding the position of professor of chemistry and natural philosophy at the University of Pennsylvania during the years 1828–1841 and again in 1842–1843.

## Educational Research for Girard College
Between 1836 and 1838, Bache traveled to Europe on behalf of the trustees of an institution that would eventually become Girard College in 1848. Although the college had not yet been opened at the time of his travels, Bache spent those years examining European systems of education. Upon his return to the United States, he published a report on his findings that was considered very valuable.

## Superintendent of the United States Coast Survey
In 1843, following the death of Professor F. R. Hassler, Bache was appointed as the superintendent of the United States coast survey. In this role, he successfully convinced Congress of the significant value of the survey's work, which resulted in the granting of liberal aid. 

Bache utilized this support to implement a comprehensive plan characterized by a skillful division of labour. He oversaw the simultaneous mapping of the entire coast through the establishment of numerous observing stations. Under his direction as general director, the survey also collected a vast amount of meteorological and magnetic observations.

## Death
Alexander Dallas Bache died on February 17, 1867, in Newport, Rhode Island.
