# Lintels

## Definition and Purpose
Lintels are structural elements used over square-headed openings in construction. They serve as an alternative to, or are used in conjunction with, arches. Their primary functions are to preserve the square form of the opening and to provide a surface to receive the fittings installed by joiners.

## Materials and Composition
Lintels may be constructed from several different materials, including:
*   Iron
*   Steel
*   Stone
*   Plain concrete
*   Reinforced concrete (concrete strengthened with steel bars)

## Structural Requirements and Support
The structural requirements for lintels vary based on the materials used. When lintels are made of stone, iron, or plain concrete, they should have relieving arches turned immediately over them. This requirement is waived for lintels constructed from steel or concrete that has been reinforced with steel bars.

## Related Structural Components
In broader construction contexts, other materials are used to support horizontal loads or provide stability:
*   **Wall Plates:** While the building of wood into brickwork is generally avoided, wooden wall plates are necessary when wood joists are utilized. If these plates cannot be supported by iron or corbels of projecting brickwork, they are let flush into the wall, replacing a course of bricks to create a uniform bed for the joists.
*   **Reinforced Concrete Applications:** Beyond lintels, reinforced concrete (also referred to as ferro-concrete or armoured concrete) is used in various structural capacities. This includes the construction of arched bridges, durable fence posts, and piles used as foundations in loose or compressible soil.

## Properties of Reinforced Concrete
In structures where steel and concrete are combined, the two materials share a nearly identical thermal coefficient of expansion, which prevents shearing stress at their junction during temperature changes. 

Concrete provides significant strength to resist compression and serves to protect the internal steel from corrosion. In many designs, a sufficient section of steel is provided to carry all the tension. The coefficient of elasticity for steel (averaging 28,000,000 to 30,000,000 lb per sq. in.) is approximately twelve times that of concrete (which varies between 1,500,000 and 3,000,000 lb per sq. in. depending on the ratio of cement to stone and sand).
