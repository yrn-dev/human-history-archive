# The Dukedom of Bolton and Related Peerages

## The Dukedom of Bolton
The title of duke of Bolton was held by the family of Paulet (or Powlett) from 1689 until December 1794, at which point the dukedom became extinct. The title was first created in April 1689 for Charles Powlett, the 1st duke (c. 1625–1699). Prior to this, Charles had served as a member of parliament for Winchester (1660–1675) and then for Hampshire. He had been the 6th marquess of Winchester following the death of his father in 1675. After supporting the claims of Mary and William to the English throne in 1688, he was restored to the office of lord-lieutenant of Hampshire and the privy council. He was described as an eccentric individual who was hostile to Marlborough and Halifax; it is noted that in 1687 he traveled with 100 horsemen and four coaches, spending his nights giving entertainments and his days sleeping.

The 1st duke was succeeded in February 1699 by his elder son, Charles, the 2nd duke of Bolton (1661–1722). Like his father, the 2nd duke was a supporter of William of Orange and a member of parliament for Hampshire. His career included serving as lord chamberlain of the royal household, governor of the Isle of Wight, and twice as lord justice of the kingdom. He also served as a commissioner for the union of Scotland and England and as lord-lieutenant of Dorset and Hampshire. His third wife was Henrietta, a natural daughter of James, duke of Monmouth. The writer Swift characterized the 2nd duke as "a great booby."

The 3rd duke of Bolton, Charles (1685–1754), was the eldest son of the 2nd duke. He served in parliament from 1705 to 1717 and was created Baron Pawlet of Basing. He attained a high rank in the British army and held many of the public offices previously held by his father. In 1733, he was deprived of several offices after displeasing Sir Robert Walpole, though some were later restored. In 1745, he raised a regiment to fight against the Jacobites. Known as a famous gallant, he married the singer Lavinia Fenton, who had previously been his mistress, as his second wife.

The 4th duke was Harry (c. 1690–1759), the brother of the 3rd duke. Harry had been a member of parliament for forty years and succeeded his brother as lord-lieutenant of Hampshire.

### The Dukedoms of Buckingham
Multiple distinct titles have used the name Buckingham. George Villiers was created earl in 1617, marquess in 1618, and duke of Buckingham in 1623. Both the dukedom and marquessate became extinct in 1687 upon the death of the 2nd duke. While a collateral line claiming descent from John Villiers, 1st Viscount Purbeck, claimed the earldom under a special remainder, the title was not used after the death of John Villiers (who styled himself earl of Buckingham) in 1723. This claim was finally extinguished in 1774 with the death of George Villiers, a clergyman.

Separately, in 1703, John Sheffield, marquess of Normanby, was created duke of Normanby and of the county of Buckingham. These titles became extinct in October 1735 upon the death of his son, Edmund.

A third lineage, the Grenville family, held a title derived from the town of Buckingham rather than the county. In 1784, the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham," which was later elevated to the dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos for his son in 1822. This dukedom became extinct upon the death of the 3rd duke, Richard Grenville (1823–1889), who left no son. Upon his death, his other titles were distributed: the earldom of Temple went to his nephew William Stephen Gore-Langton, the viscounty of Cobham went to his kinsman Charles George (5th Baron Lyttelton), and the Scottish barony of Kinloss passed to his eldest daughter, Mary.

### Other Peerages
*   **Earls of Kent:** The earldom of Kent was held by Joan, "the fair maid of Kent," and later by her husband, Sir Thomas de Holand, who died in 1360. Their son Thomas (1350–1397) succeeded him. The 3rd earl, also named Thomas (1374–1400), was made duke of Surrey in 1397 but was degraded from the dukedom in 1399 and beheaded in 1400. The earldom then passed to his brother Edmund (1384–1408), and became extinct upon Edmund's death in Brittany.
*   **Earls of Derwentwater:** Following the death of John Radclyffe in 1731, his uncle Charles (1693–1746) took the title. Charles had escaped prison after being condemned for high treason in 1715 but was eventually captured in 1745 and beheaded in 1746. His son, James Bartholomew (1725–1786), claimed the title and became 3rd earl of Newburgh. The title became extinct *de jure* and *de facto* in 1814 upon the death of Anthony James, who died without issue.
*   **Barons Lyttelton:** The barony became extinct after the "wicked" Lord Lyttelton, Thomas (1744–1779), died without lawful issue. It was revived in 1794 for his uncle William Henry. The title eventually merged with the viscounty of Cobham in 1889.
*   **Barons Belhaven:** The barony of Hamilton became extinct in December 1868 upon the death of Robert, who died without issue.
