# VARIATIONS IN CLEARINGS AT NEW YORK

The term "clearings" in the context of New York refers to two distinct operational processes: the financial clearing of cheques through banking institutions and the administrative "clearing" of inmate counts within correctional facilities.

## Financial Clearing-House Operations
The system for clearing cheques in the United States is highly developed, second perhaps only to Great Britain. Clearing-houses operate in approximately 112 leading cities. For the year ending September 30, 1907, the aggregate clearings reached $154,662,515,258.

The New York Clearing-House handles a significant portion of this activity; in 1906, its clearings accounted for 67.2% of the total clearings across 55 of the larger cities. The volume of these clearings is subject to great fluctuation, driven by the prosperity of business in the country and the volume of transactions on the stock exchange.

## Influence and Membership of the New York Clearing-House
The Clearing-House Committee of the New York Clearing-House possesses substantial influence over the banking environment. This is primarily exercised through its ability to deny aid during emergencies to banks that are conducted unwisely. This authority was utilized during the panic of 1907 to remove several speculative financial interests from the control of national banks.

During this period, membership in the Clearing-House was limited to the sub-Treasury, state banks, and national banks. While their weekly reports of condition served as an index for the exchanges and the money-market every Saturday, this index was considered incomplete or misleading because trust companies did not provide regular weekly reports. To resolve this, the state superintendent of banking announced in early 1908 that he would use legal powers to require trust companies to submit weekly reports, providing a more complete mirror of New York banking conditions.

## Institutional Count Clearings at MCC New York
In the context of the Metropolitan Correctional Center (MCC) New York, "clearing" refers to the official completion of an institutional inmate count. According to BOP policy and MCC New York SHU Post Orders, the process for clearing a count is as follows:

1. **Conducting the Count**: Two correctional officers (COs) must conduct each count.
2. **Documentation**: The COs must memorialize the number of inmates physically present on an official "Metropolitan Correctional Center; New York, New York; Official Count Slip." This slip requires the date, time, total number of inmates, and the signatures of both officers.
3. **Verification**: Count slips are delivered to the Control Center, where officers compare them against the institution's overall inmate count sheet.
4. **Communication**: COs must relay the count verbally to the Control Center. If a verbal report does not match the count, the Operations Lieutenant must be notified and a recount performed.
5. **Finalization**: An institutional count is only deemed "cleared" or completed after all count slips from every housing unit have been collected and the numbers matched to the overall count sheet.

## Case Study: The 10 p.m. Count of August 9, 2019
An investigation by the OIG revealed a failure in the clearing process at MCC New York on August 9, 2019. At 10 p.m., two officers (Noel and the Material Handler) were responsible for the stand-up count in the SHU. Although security camera video and BOP records determined that the count was not actually performed, the officers completed and signed a count slip listing the inmates as "73 + 1."

By 10:30 p.m., all units had verbally reported their counts to the Control Center. At approximately 10:36 p.m., the Control Center completed the paperwork and officially cleared the 10 p.m. institutional count, despite the fact that staff knew the counts from two housing units were inaccurate.
