# London, 1882

The year 1882 in London saw the publication of several significant works across diverse fields, including linguistics, the study of oriental arts, and the establishment of new legal frameworks for banking and commerce.

## Linguistic Contributions
A notable publication from London in 1882 was the *Swahili-English Dictionary* authored by Dr. L. Krapf. This work is described as containing a mass of invaluable information regarding the Swahili language as it was spoken on the coast of East Africa, although the information within the text is not well-sorted. The dictionary is particularly significant for its preservation of many words that were becoming obsolete at the time.

## Art and Oriental Studies
In the field of textile arts and design, 1882 marked the publication of *Eastern Carpets* by Vincent J. Robinson, which featured water-colour drawings by E. Julia Robinson. Published in a large 4to format, this work preceded the more learned contributions of Bode and Riegl by approximately nine or ten years. The text included examples of carpets ascribed to a 17th-century manufactory in Warsaw and a manufactory at Alcaraz in La Mancha; however, later and more complete investigations have found that these ascriptions are scarcely borne out.

## Legal and Financial Legislation
The year 1882 was pivotal for British banking law with the enactment of the Bills of Exchange Act 1882. This legislation introduced several key protections and definitions for bankers:

*   **Cheques:** The Act defined cheques as "bills drawn on a banker payable on demand." Section 60 of the Act extended protections to bankers regarding cheques, though this definition specifically debars certain types of drafts.
*   **Crossed Cheques:** The Act addressed the payment of crossed cheques. Under Section 79, if a banker pays a crossed cheque otherwise than in accordance with the crossing, the banker is liable to the true owner for any resulting loss and is likely unable to charge the customer for that amount. Conversely, Section 80 and the proviso to Section 79 provide protection to a banker who pays a crossed cheque in accordance with its ostensible tenor.
*   **Liability:** The Bills of Exchange Act 1882 did not repeal Section 19 of the Stamp Act 1853, which relieved bankers from liability if an endorsement on a draft or order payable to order on demand was unauthorized or forged.
