# Paris (1872) and Ramon Sotomayor Valdes

## Publications in Paris, 1872
During the year 1872, Paris served as a site for the publication of various historical and archival works. One such publication was the *Archive Boliviano. Collecion de documentes relativos a la historia de Bolivia*, authored by Vicente de Ballivian y Rizas. Additionally, the city saw the publication of the *Dossier historique de Charlotte Corday* by C. Vatel.

## Ramon Sotomayor Valdes
Ramon Sotomayor Valdes authored a historical study titled *Estudio historico de Bolivia bajo la administracion del General don Jose Maria Acha con una introducion que contiene el compendio de la Guerra de la independencia i de los gobiernos de dicha Republica hasta 1861*. This work was published in Santiago de Chile in 1874.

## Political Context of Bolivia (1870–1879)
The period surrounding the publications of the 1870s was marked by significant political instability and diplomatic tension in Bolivia. In January 1871, a revolution led by Colonel Augustin Morales deposed President Melgarejo. Colonel Morales served as president until he was murdered in November 1872. He was succeeded by Colonel Adolfo Ballivian, who held the presidency until his death in 1874.

During Colonel Ballivian's administration, Bolivia entered into a secret agreement with Peru on February 6, 1873. The stated purpose of this agreement was mutual defense against exterior aggression and the preservation of territorial integrity, specifically regarding potential encroachments by Chile.

## Diplomatic and Territorial Disputes
The tensions between Bolivia and Chile were rooted in a 1866 treaty. Under that instrument, Bolivia recognized the 24th parallel as the boundary of Chilean territory and allowed Chile a half share of customs duties on the coast between the 23rd and 24th parallels. Chile was also permitted to export products from this district without hindrance or tax from Bolivia. In 1870, Bolivia further granted an Anglo-Chilean company the right to work nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel in exchange for $10,000.

Following the death of Adolfo Ballivian, Dr. Tomas Frias became president. He signed a treaty with Chile where Chile agreed to withdraw its claim to half the duties in Bolivian ports, provided that Chilean industries in Bolivian territory remained duty-free for twenty-five years. However, this treaty was not ratified.

## Escalation to War
In 1876, General Hilarion Daza succeeded Dr. Frias as president. General Daza demanded a tax of 10 cents per quintal on all nitrates exported from the country, threatening to seize nitrates held by exporters if the levy was not paid. In response, and to protect Chilean subjects' property, the Chilean fleet blockaded the ports of Tocapilla, Cobija, and Antofagasta. On February 14, 1879, Colonel Sotomayor of Chile occupied Antofagasta. The Bolivian government subsequently declared war on March 1, 1879.
