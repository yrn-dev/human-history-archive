# Footings to Walls

In building construction, footings are the wide courses of brickwork located at the base or foot of a wall. They are primarily utilized for buildings of ordinary weight to ensure the structure is sufficiently provided for in its foundation.

## Purpose and Function
The fundamental reason for a footing is to allow a wall to obtain a bearing upon a breadth of ground that is significantly greater than the wall's own thickness or width above the footing. This expansion compensates for the difference between the ground's power of resisting pressure (the ultimate foundation) and the power of resisting pressure of the wall itself. By increasing the surface area, footings serve to spread the pressure of the structure over a larger area of ground.

## Material and Soil Considerations
The necessity and extent of expanded footings depend on the nature of the ground upon which the building is erected:
*   **Hard Rock:** If a building is erected on rock as hard as the main constituent of the walls, theoretically no expanded footings are necessary.
*   **Variable Soils:** Footings must be expanded based on the resistance power of the structure when building upon clay, sand, strong or weak gravel, or chalk.
*   **Loose Soil:** In cases of made ground or other imperfectly resisting, loose, or badly combined soil, a solid platform is required under the constructed footing. This platform must be wide enough to prevent sinking and bear evenly over the ground. A layer of concrete is considered the most durable, familiar, and effectual material for this purpose.

## Construction and Dimensions
Footings are constructed using hard bricks laid principally as headers; if stretchers are required, they are placed in the middle of the wall. In instances where work is too sharply curved on a plan to allow for stretchers, a "heading bond" is used, where all bricks are laid as headers with their ends to the front and their length across the wall's thickness.

To achieve the necessary width, offsets of 2¼ inches are made on each side of the wall until the width reaches double the thickness of the wall. For example, in a wall that is 13½ inches thick (1½ bricks), the bottom course would be 2 feet 3 inches wide (3 bricks). For increased strength, it is preferable to double the lowest course.

The foundation bed of concrete further extends the bearing surface. While the London Building Act requires a concrete projection of 4 inches on each side of the brickwork, a projection of 6 inches is generally employed to facilitate easier working. In the example of a 2 feet 3 inch wide bottom course, a 6-inch projection on each side results in a total surface bearing on the ground of 3 feet 3 inches.

## Installation and Precision
Strict attention must be paid to the leveling of the lowest course of footings. Any irregularity at this stage may require the use of mortar in subsequent courses to make up the inequality, which creates a liability for the wall to settle unequally and perpetuates the infirmity. It is essential that the brickwork remains perfectly level—both transversely and longitudinally—and perfectly plumb.

To ensure accuracy without the constant use of a level and plumb-rule, a bricklayer may build six or eight courses of bricks at the external angles after clearing the footings. These angles are carefully plumbs and leveled to serve as a gauge. A line is then tightly strained between these angles using steel pins to guide the intervening work. If the distance between the quoins is great, the line must be supported at intervals to prevent sagging. Vertical joints, known as "perpends," must be kept directly over one another.
