# Australasia, South Africa, and Western Canada

## Geographic and Demographic Overview
Australasia and South Africa represent the primary divisions of the British Empire located in the southern hemisphere. Together, these regions cover an area of 5,308,506 square miles. In contrast, the northern hemisphere divisions—consisting of the United Kingdom, Canada, and India (including native states)—cover 5,271,375 square miles. This distribution ensures a complete alternation of seasons across the empire, allowing summer produce from one region to meet the winter requirements of another.

In terms of hemispheric distribution, Canada occupies 3,653,946 square miles in the western hemisphere. Australasia, South Africa, India, and the United Kingdom collectively occupy 6,925,975 square miles in the eastern hemisphere. However, the eastern portions of Australasia border the western hemisphere so closely that the distribution of day and night is nearly complete across the empire's territories.

Demographically, the native populations of these regions vary. Australasia and its islands are recorded as having 824,000 native inhabitants, while South Africa has 5,211,329. Canada's only colored native races are the Red Indians, who are many in tribal variety but few in number, totaling 100,000.

## Expansion and Trade
Trade has served as the most potent bond of union and the most active cause of expansion for the empire. The settlement of Australasia was made possible by British command of the sea, established through commercial wars during much of the 18th century. These same wars resulted in Great Britain acquiring South Africa. In Canada, a trading company was responsible for colonizing the north-western districts.

The bonds of prosperous trade have specifically kept the Australasian colonies within the empire, with the imperial navy providing essential protection for colonial commerce.

## Industrial Production and Mining
The application of industrial science to communication and transport during the 19th century, specifically the development of steam, electricity, and railways, allowed the empire to access the interiors of continents previously considered unapproachable. This shifted economic development away from a strict reliance on river valleys and sea-shores.

The British Empire maintains a significant global position in mining and quarrying, employing more than one-third of the world's workers in these fields. Globally, the empire produces approximately two-thirds of the gold supply, one-third of the coal supply, and one-sixth of the wheat supply.

## Religious Organizations
Baptist churches have established a considerable presence across the British colonies. In Australasia, unions of Baptist churches exist in New Zealand, Tasmania, Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Victoria, and New South Wales. In Canada, there are four such unions, and a union also exists in South Africa. The work in South Africa is supported by the Baptist South African Missionary and Colonial Aid Society based in London.

According to available records, the number of Baptist members and churches varies by region:
*   **Canada:** 985 churches with 103,062 members.
*   **Australasia:** 270 churches with 23,253 members.
*   **South Africa:** 52 churches with 4,865 members.

## Regional Notes
In South Africa, the geography includes the Kalahari Desert and various "trackless parts of the bush." During the Boer War, commanders such as Jan Christiaan Smuts traversed the country from the Atlantic Ocean to the Indian Ocean.
