# Georg Schweinfurth

## Exploration of the Southern Ghazal District
Georg Schweinfurth was an explorer who, between the years 1869 and 1871, traversed the entirety of the southern district of the Ghazal region. During the course of his travels, he crossed the watershed and discovered the Welle, a great river that flows west beyond the Nile watershed.

## Context of the Ghazal River Exploration
The exploration of the Ghazal river followed Egyptian expeditions that ascended the White Nile between 1839 and 1842. Prior to this, Greek geographers had imagined the source of the Nile to be westward toward Lake Chad due to rumors regarding the existence of the Bahr-el-Ghazal. The first map to indicate the course of the Ghazal with relative accuracy was published in 1772 by the French cartographer d'Anville.

Several other explorers contributed to the knowledge of the region around the time of Schweinfurth's travels:
* **John Petherick**: A Welsh mining engineer who explored the Jur and the main stream of the Ghazal for a significant portion of the period between 1833 and 1865.
* **Giovanni Miani**: A Venetian who penetrated the southern regions of the Ghazal basin in 1859 and provided the first reports of the Welle river.
* **Lejean**: A Frenchman who surveyed the main river in 1862 and published a map of it.
* **Alexandrine Tinne**: In 1863, she led a party of scientists and friends, including Germans Hermann Steudner and Theodor von Heuglin, to determine the western extent of the Nile basin. This expedition resulted in the deaths of five of its nine white members due to blackwater fever.

## Subsequent Exploration of the Region
Following Schweinfurth's work, further exploration of the river and its tributaries occurred between 1879 and 1881. These efforts were led by Gessi Pasha, the Italian governor under General C. G. Gordon, as part of attempts to destroy the slave trade in the Ghazal province. During this same period, Wilhelm Junker explored the southern tributaries of the Ghazal.

Later, the Jur (also known as the Sue) was followed throughout its course by Lieutenant A. H. Dye and other members of a French mission led by Colonel (then Captain) J. B. Marchand. This mission crossed from the Congo in October 1897 and reached Fashoda on the White Nile in July 1898.
