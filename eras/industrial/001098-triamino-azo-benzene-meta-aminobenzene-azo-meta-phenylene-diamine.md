# Triamino-azo-benzene

Triamino-azo-benzene, also known by the chemical name meta-aminobenzene-azo-meta-phenylene diamine, is an amino-azo compound. In commercial and industrial contexts, it is referred to by several names, including Bismarck brown, phenylene brown, vesuvine, and Manchester brown.

## Chemical Properties and Appearance
Triamino-azo-benzene has the chemical formula $\text{NH}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_4\text{.N}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NH}_2)_2$. It manifests as brown crystals. These crystals are characterized by their ready solubility in hot water. 

As a member of the amino-azo compound group, it follows general trends of its class; such compounds are typically red or yellowish brown in color. The presence of additional amino groups generally leads to browner shades, whereas the introduction of alkylated amino groups results in redder shades.

## Preparation
The compound is prepared through the action of nitrous acid on meta-phenylene diamine.

## Applications in Dyeing
Triamino-azo-benzene is utilized as a dye. When applied to mordanted cotton, it produces a dark brown color.

## Context within Azo Compounds
Triamino-azo-benzene is part of a broader category of azo compounds, which are characterized by the presence of the chromophorous group $-\text{N:N}-$. According to O.N. Witt, the introduction of such groups into a colorless hydrocarbon like benzene creates a chromogen. These chromogens can be converted into dyestuffs through the addition of auxochromous groups, such as $\text{NH}_2$ or $\text{OH}$.

It is related to other amino-azo-benzenes, such as:

* **Diamino-azo-benzene** (chrysoidine, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{.N}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NH}_2)_2$): Prepared by coupling phenyl diazonium chloride with meta-phenylene diamine, it forms red octahedra and is used to dye wool and silk yellow.
