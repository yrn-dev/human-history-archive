# Captain H. Deasy

## Military and Geographical Contributions
Captain H. Deasy was a British cavalry officer of the 16th Lancers who contributed significantly to the field of geography through his explorations between 1896 and 1898. During this period, he and Captain M.S. Wellby of the 18th Hussars were noted as taking the front position among Tibetan travellers. 

Deasy's primary geographical work involved the continuation of the Pamir triangulation. This specific surveying effort had previously been carried across the Hindu Kush by R.A. Wahab and Sir T.H. Holdich, Colonels of the Pamir Boundary Commission of 1895. Captain Deasy extended this triangulation into the sources of the Zarafshan and the plains of Kashgar.

## Observations in Tibet
In his writings regarding Tibet, Deasy provided detailed accounts of the local wildlife, specifically the Yak. He recorded observing Yaks in herds ranging from ten to thirty animals, and occasionally more, on lofty ground. He noted that while brown specimens were very rare, most of the animals were black. 

Deasy described the Yaks as possessing great agility when moving over stony and steep ground, observing that the animals appeared to enjoy the wind, frost, and snow. He further noted that Yaks are capable of offering formidable resistance to those engaging in sportsmanship.

## Legacy and Subsequent Research
The work conducted by Deasy in western Tibet served as a foundation for further exploration at the beginning of the century. His efforts were extended by Captain C. G. Rawling and Dr. M.A. Stein, who expanded the existing knowledge of Tibetan and Turkestan commerce and ancient fields of industry.
