# Movable Bridges

Movable bridges are specialized structures designed to be closed to carry a railway, a road, or in certain instances, an aqueduct. Their primary function is the ability to be opened to provide free and unobstructed passage for navigation. While most bridges are fixed, movable bridges utilize machinery to facilitate this opening process.

## Types of Movable Bridges

Movable bridges are categorized into several distinct types based on their mechanism of movement:

### Lifting Bridges
Lifting bridges consist of a platform suspended from girders located above. The platform is supported by chains and counterweights positioned at the four corners. To open the bridge, the platform is lifted vertically to the required height. These bridges are noted as being neither very important nor very numerous.

### Rolling Bridges
In rolling bridges, the girders are constructed to be longer than the span. The portion of the bridge that overhangs the abutment is counter-weighted, ensuring that the center of gravity remains over the abutment as the bridge is rolled forward. To address the gap in the approaches created when the bridge is rolled forward, a frame carrying that section of the road is moved into place sideways. An example of this type is found at Sunderland, where a hydraulic press first lifts the bridge to clear the roadway behind it before it is rolled back.

### Draw or Bascule Bridges
The original form of the draw-bridge is the fortress type, which utilizes a single leaf, or bascule, that turns around a horizontal hinge at one abutment. These are raised using counterweights and chains and are supported by abutments at each end when closed. A more common variation features two bascules, one hinged at each abutment, which are locked at the center when closed. In these designs, each bascule is prolonged backwards beyond the hinge to provide balance; this prolongation sinks into the piers during the opening process.

### Swing or Turning Bridges
Swing or turning bridges are the most common type of movable bridge and are used for the largest structures. These bridges revolve around a vertical axis. The bridge is supported by a circular track for conical rollers and a live ring, resting on a circular base plate with a central pivot. Motion is provided by a toothed arc fixed to either the live ring or the revolving platform. The main girders rest on the revolving platform, and the ends of the bridge are shaped as circular arcs to fit the fixed roadway.

There are three primary arrangements for swing bridges:
*   **Center Pier:** The axis of rotation is located on a pier in the center of the river. The bridge is equal-armed, allowing two navigation passages to open simultaneously.
*   **Abutment Axis:** The axis of rotation is on one abutment. These bridges are usually unequal-armed, with the shorter arm positioned over land.
*   **Vertical Crane Post:** In some smaller bridges, the shorter arm is vertical, and the bridge rotates on a vertical crane post at the abutment.

## Engineering Requirements

Movable bridges must be proportioned like fixed bridges when in their closed position, but they must satisfy additional physical conditions. For bridges that revolve around a vertical axis, the center of gravity must always lie within that axis. For rolling bridges, the center of gravity must always remain over the abutment. Additionally, the structure must possess sufficient strength to safely support its own overhanging weight while in motion.

## General Bridge Context

In broader engineering terms, bridges are structures that carry railways, waterways, or roadways across valleys, streams, or other roads and railways. Specific terminology includes "viaducts" for long bridges of several spans, "aqueducts" for bridges carrying canals, and "culverts" for small-span bridges used for drainage. In railway contexts, an "overbridge" is a bridge over the railway, while an "underbridge" carries the railway.
