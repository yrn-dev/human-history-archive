# Louis Lucien

## Early Life and Birth
Louis Lucien was born on the 4th of January 1813 at Thorngrove, located in Worcestershire, England. He was the son of Lucien Bonaparte. Lucien spent the duration of his youth in England and did not travel to France until 1848.

## Political Career in France
Following the revolution of 1848, Louis Lucien entered French politics. On the 28th of November 1848, he was elected as the deputy for Corsica. Although this election was subsequently invalidated, he was returned to office in June 1849 as the deputy for the Seine. During his time in the Legislative Assembly, he sat on the right.

While his cousin carried out a *coup d'etat* on the 2nd of December 1851, Louis Lucien had no direct involvement in the event. He was later named a prince and a senator by Napoleon III, though he remained largely inactive in political affairs throughout the period of the Second Empire.

## Later Life and Philological Studies
After the Third Republic was proclaimed in 1870, Louis Lucien withdrew from France and returned to England. During this final period of his life, he dedicated himself to the study of philology. He focused specifically on the Basque language, producing several notable works, including *Remarques sur plusieurs assertions concernant la langue basque* (1876), *Grammaire basque*, and *Observations sur le basque Fontarabie* (1878).

Louis Lucien died on the 3rd of November 1891. He left no children.
