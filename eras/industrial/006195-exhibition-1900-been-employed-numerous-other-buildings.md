# Paris Exhibition 1900 and Related Construction Methods

## The British Pavilion and Fire-Proof Construction
At the Paris Exhibition of 1900, the British pavilion utilized a specific form of building construction designed to be both fire-proof and burglar-proof. This method involves the use of steel dovetailed sheets that are fixed between small steel stanchions and then plastered in cement on both sides. 

This construction technique allows for the erection of dwellings at a small cost, with walls ranging from 3 to 5 inches in thickness. An example of this application can be found in the Newgate prison cells in London, where the outside walls were only 3 to 4 inches thick. Beyond the Paris Exhibition, this method has been employed in various other buildings located in India (specifically the Delhi durbar), South Africa, Venezuela, and England.

These regulations typically concern the required thickness of a wall or the specific materials used.

In general modern land development, architects must ensure that every portion of a building complies with these building acts. This includes observing regulations regarding frontage lines, open spaces, light and air, distances from surrounding property, and wall thickness. In cities such as New York and London, these Building Acts are extensive and subject to numerous amendments.
