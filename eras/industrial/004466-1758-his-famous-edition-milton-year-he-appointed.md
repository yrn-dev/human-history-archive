# Events and Appointments of 1758

The year 1758 was marked by significant achievements in the fields of printing, science, and diplomacy, as well as various professional appointments and military advancements across Europe.

## Advancements in Printing and Typography
John Baskerville (1706–1775), an English printer born in Worcestershire, achieved a major milestone in 1758 with the publication of his famous edition of Milton. This followed his first work, a royal quarto edition of Virgil, published in 1757. Baskerville had begun experimenting with type-founding around 1750, creating types that were more elegant and distinct than previous versions. 

In addition to his literary publications, 1758 was the year Baskerville was appointed printer to the university of Cambridge. Following this appointment, he undertook editions of the Book of Common Prayer and the Bible. Baskerville is regarded as one of the foremost figures in the advancement of the art of printing, though some contemporaries argued that the success of his work was due more to the quality of his ink and paper than the type itself.

## Scientific Discoveries and Recognition
In the field of optics, 1758 saw the publication of an "Account of some experiments concerning the different refrangibility of light" by the English optician John Dollond (1706–1761). This work described the experiments that led to the discovery of a method for constructing achromatic lenses by combining flint and crown glasses. 

Dollond's work had evolved from a dispute with the suggestions of Leonhard Euler (who proposed a combination of water and glass lenses in 1747) and a reliance on statements by Sir Isaac Newton. After the Swedish physicist Samuel Klingenstjerna noted that Newton's law of dispersion did not align with certain facts, Dollond conducted experiments that successfully produced refraction without colour. For this achievement, the Royal Society awarded Dollond the Copley medal in 1758.
