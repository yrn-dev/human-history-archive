# Buckfast Abbey

## History and Restoration
Buckfast Abbey is located in Devonshire, England, situated between the market towns of Ashburton and Buckfastleigh. The site is said to have been a Benedictine house prior to the Conquest. In 1137, it was refounded for the Cistercians. After a period of disuse, the abbey was restored to use in 1882 by a French Benedictine community. While various parts of the abbey have been rebuilt following the original lines, the fine Perpendicular abbot's tower remains from the earlier structure.

## The Benedictine Order
The Benedictines, also known as Black Monks, follow the Rule of St Benedict of Nursia. The order originated in the Abruzzi at Subiaco, where St Benedict established twelve monasteries, and later at Monte Cassino, which served as the center for the spread of the Benedictine institute. From Monte Cassino, a monastery was also founded at Terracina. 

Benedictine life was firmly planted in England first among countries outside of Italy, following the mission of St Augustine and his companions in 596. The order later spread to Gaul in the 7th century and into Germanic lands from England during the 8th century.

## European Revivals and Congregations
The Benedictine order faced significant disruption in northern Europe due to the French Revolution and the rule of Joseph II of Austria, which led to the suppression of nearly every Benedictine monastery in Europe. By the early 19th century, it is reported that scarcely thirty monasteries survived.

A series of revivals occurred in the latter half of the 19th century, beginning in Bavaria under Louis I. New creations since 1830 include:
* The French congregation, inaugurated by Dom Gueranger in 1833.
* The German congregation of Beuron, established in 1863.
* Two vigorous congregations in the United States.

In England, the congregation consists of a nunnery (Stanbrook Abbey, Worcester), a cathedral priory (Hereford), and three large abbeys: Ampleforth, Downside, and Woolhampton. Additionally, England hosts several nunneries subject to bishops and three or four abbeys belonging to foreign congregations.

## Governance and Polity
The Benedictine polity is characterized by the principle that each monastery is autonomous and self-contained. Each congregation is independent and self-governing, lacking a central authority or superior-general. Instead, each congregation has a president with limited powers.

Pope Leo XIII established an international Benedictine College in Rome for theological studies. The abbot of this college holds the title of "Abbot Primate," granting him precedence among Black Monk abbots. However, the Abbot Primate is considered *primus inter pares* (first among equals) and possesses no superiority over other congregations or abbots.
