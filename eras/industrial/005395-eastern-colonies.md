## Ancient Greek and Chinese Colonies
In antiquity, various Greek city-states established powerful colonies in Italy and Sicily beginning in 735 B.C. These enterprises involved Sparta, Megara, Corinth, Chalkis, the Lokrians, and the Achæans in Peloponnesus. Chalkis served as the metropolis for Cumæ and Naxos; the latter was the most ancient Grecian colony in Sicily, situated on the island's eastern coast between Mount Ætna and the strait of Messina. Additionally, Grecian colonies were established on the eastern side of the Ionian sea, including Epidamnus, Apollonia, Ambrakia, Anaktorium, Leukas, and Korkyra.

In the East, the Han established Chinese colonies at the expense of the Hiongnu. These included T’ung hoang (founded B.C. 111), which is also known as Shachau (or "Sand-district"), as well as Chang-yé (Kanchau, B.C. 111) and Kiu-kaan (Suhchau, B.C. 121). Shachau served as an outpost of China Proper at the eastern verge of the Sandy Desert and was fortified in the 1st century.

## African Colonial Territories
Various colonial administrations and ethnic settlements are noted across Eastern and Central Africa.

### British and Italian Administrations
In Northeast Africa, Asmara serves as the capital of the Italian colony of Eritrea. Located on the eastern edge of the Hamasen plateau at an elevation of 7800 ft., the town is strongly fortified and includes a small Mahommedan colony. Its population in 1904 was approximately 9,000, comprising Abyssinians, over 500 European civilians, 300 Italian soldiers, and 1,000 native troops.

In British Central Africa, the Nyasaland Protectorate—comprising the Shiré province and districts surrounding Lake Nyasa—is administered by a governor under the colonial office, assisted by an executive council and a nominated legislative council of at least three members. To the west, the provinces of North Western and North Eastern Rhodesia are governed by two administrators of the British South Africa Chartered Company.

### South African Eastern Borders
In the Cape Colony, Sir Benjamin D'Urban served as governor starting in January 1834. His tenure included the abolition of slavery and the establishment of municipal and legislative councils. In 1835, D'Urban repelled an unprovoked invasion of the colony's eastern borders by Xosa Kaffirs. To protect the eastern province, he extended the colony's boundary to the Kei river and established military posts. However, Lord Glenelg, the secretary for the Colonies, later instructed D'Urban to relinquish this newly annexed territory, viewing the Kaffirs as victims of systematic injustice. Sir Andries Stockenstrom was subsequently appointed lieutenant-governor for the eastern provinces to implement the home government's policy.

## Linguistic and Ethnic Distributions
The eastern regions of Africa are characterized by specific linguistic groups:
*   **Swahili:** A commercial *lingua franca* and archaic Bantu dialect indigenous to the East African coast south of the Ruvu (Pangani) river. It developed through intermixture with Arabic and spread between the Zambezi and the White Nile. Swahili-speaking colonies exist at Lamu, Malindi, Mombasa, and as far north as the Shebeli river in Somaliland, as well as along the coast of Portuguese and German East Africa south to Angoche.
*   **Zulu-Kaffir Group:** This group occupies the eastern half of Cape Colony, the eastern portion of the Transvaal, Natal, Swaziland, and parts of Rhodesia. Dialects such as Ki-ngoni (or Ci-nongi) and Tebele were carried by Zulu raiders in the early 19th century to Western Nyasaland, South-West Rhodesia, and the plateaus northeast of Lake Nyasa.
