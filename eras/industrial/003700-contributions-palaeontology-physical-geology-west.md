# Contributions to the Palaeontology and Physical Geology of the West Indies

The study of the palaeontology and physical geology of the West Indies has been advanced through a series of specialized research papers and the work of dedicated palaeontologists focusing on fossil records and geological formations.

## Geological Research and Publications
Significant contributions to the understanding of the region's physical geology have been published in the *Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society of London*. Notable works include the research conducted by A. J. Jukes-Browne and J. B. Harrison, who published "The Geology of Barbados" across two volumes in 1891 and 1892. Further specialized study on the basal reef-rocks and Globigerina-marls of Barbados was produced by G. F. Franks and J. B. Harrison in 1898.

In 1895, J. W. Gregory published a specific work titled "Contributions to the Palaeontology and Physical Geology of the West Indies" in the same journal. Additionally, J. W. Spencer contributed to the field in 1902 with a study on the physical and geological development of Barbados, which also included notes regarding Trinidad.

## The Work of Peter Martin Duncan
A primary figure in the palaeontological study of the region was Peter Martin Duncan (1824–1891), an English palaeontologist and former professor of geology at King's College. Duncan became a leading authority on fossil corals. Beginning in 1863, he provided the Geological Society of London with a series of papers focusing on the fossil corals of the West Indian Islands. 

In these papers, Duncan did more than describe the various species; he used his findings to discuss the bearings these fossils had on the physical geography of the Tertiary period. His expertise in corals extended globally and across different geological formations, though his early work on the West Indian Islands established a foundation for his scientific reputation.

## Related Regional Studies
While the geological and palaeontological focus often centered on Barbados and the West Indian Islands, other naturalists contributed to the broader biological and physical understanding of the West Indies and South America. For example, F. Poey, a naturalist from Havana, spent many years studying the fishes of Cuba. His findings were published in two of his own periodicals: *Repertorio fisico-natural de la isla de Cuba* (starting in 1865) and *Memorias sobre la historia natural de la isla de Cuba* (starting in 1851).
