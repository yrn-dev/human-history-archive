# Northern Pacific and the China Sea

The Northern Pacific and the China Sea form a significant portion of the eastern and southern maritime boundaries of the Asian continent. This coastal line is characterized by a deeply broken geography, defined by the projection of several peninsulas and the recession of various seas and gulfs.

## Geography and Boundaries
The boundary of Asia between the extremity of the Malay Peninsula and East Cape (also known as Vostochnyi or Dezhnev), located at the entrance of the Bering Strait at 190 deg. E., follows the coast of the China Sea and the Northern Pacific. This coastline is interrupted by the projections of the Korean and Kamchatka peninsulas. Additionally, the line is shaped by the recession of the Yellow Sea, the Sea of Okhotsk, and the Gulfs of Siam and Tongking.

To the north, the Asian landmass is bounded by the Arctic Ocean, with the coast-line falling between 70 deg. and 75 deg. N. The extreme northern point is Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi (Chelyuskin) at 78 deg. N. In the east, China is situated between the North Pacific and the eastern flank of the Tibetan plateau, with its limits spanning approximately from 40 deg. N. in the north to 20 deg. N. in the south.

## Marine Life
The seas of south Asia, including the Pacific and the China Sea, are home to a diverse array of fish. The *Acanthopterygii* (spiny-rayed fishes) are well-represented, particularly the *Percidae* (perches); the genus *Serranus* is especially numerous in Asia and features very large forms. 

Other notable species include:
*   **Scaly-finned fishes (*Squamipennes*):** Found primarily in southern Asian seas, especially near coral reefs.
*   **Red mullets (*Mullidae*):** Represented by genera that differ from those found in Europe.
*   **Polynemidae:** These range from the Atlantic through the Indian Ocean to the Pacific. This group includes the mango fish, a delicacy inhabiting seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal, and species used to prepare isinglass.
*   **Sciaenidae:** These extend from the Bay of Bengal to China.
*   **Pomfrets (*Stromateidae*):** These extend to the Pacific and China, resembling the Mediterranean dory.
*   **Other species:** The seas of south Asia are also distributed with scabbard fishes (*Trichuridae*), lancet fishes (*Acanthuridae*), sword fishes (*Xiphidae*), as well as gobies, mullets, blennies, and various genera of mackerels.

## Strategic and Political Interests
The region has been a focal point for international communication and territorial ambition, particularly regarding Russia's efforts to secure an ice-free port and an unobstructed avenue to the Pacific. Russia sought to supplement the long ocean route and the Amur waterway by constructing a railway across Asia. While a route along the northern bank of the Amur was possible, it was technically and economically difficult due to a huge semicircular sweep northward. A shorter, straighter line would have required passing through Manchuria in Chinese territory.

Following the war between Japan and China in 1894, Japan took possession of the southern littoral of Manchuria. This development meant Russia could not reach the Pacific more effectively than through Vladivostok without removing Japan from its path. This tension led Russia to seek alliances with France and Germany to oust Japan from Manchuria, arguing that Japanese tenure of the littoral threatened the independence of Korea and the security of the Chinese capital.

## Maritime and Rail Communications
In the Northern Pacific region, military and naval arrangements have shifted based on global conflict. During the war, the British and Japanese Admiralties agreed that Japan would assume responsibility for the whole of the Northern Pacific, with the exception of the Canadian Coast. This allowed the British to redeploy the China squadron to other theaters, including sending the *Triumph* to assist in the Japanese attack on the fortress of Tsing Tau.

In North America, the Northern Pacific is also the name of a railway line that crosses the northern part of a state (alongside the Great Northern and the Oregon Railway & Navigation lines). In British Columbia, the Pacific division of the Canadian Pacific railway runs for approximately 500 m. before reaching its terminus at Vancouver.
