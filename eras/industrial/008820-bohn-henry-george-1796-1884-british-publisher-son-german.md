# BOHN, HENRY GEORGE (1796-1884)

Henry George Bohn was a British publisher and bookseller born in London on January 4, 1796. He was the son of a German bookbinder who had settled in England. A man of wide culture and diverse interests, Bohn was not only a professional in the book trade but also a famous rose-grower and a collector of ivories, china, and pictures.

## Early Career and Book Dealing
Bohn entered the trade as a dealer in "remainders" and rare books in 1831. He became well-known for his book auction sales; notably, an auction held in 1848 lasted for four days. The catalogue for this event spanned twenty folio pages and included a schedule for meals, noting that tea was served at 5, dessert at 4, supper at 10, and dinner at 2 o'clock. In 1841, Bohn issued his "Guinea" Catalogue of books, which was described as a monumental work containing 23,208 items.

## The Libraries and Publishing Ventures
Bohn is principally remembered for the inauguration of the *Libraries* in 1846. This ambitious project consisted of 766 volumes and included translations and editions of standard works covering archaeology, theology, science, history, and classics. These publications included *Bohn's British Classics* (which published an edition of Sir James Prior's life in 1853) and *Bohn's Standard Library* (which featured an edition of the chief works of a Baptist mission figure in 1852, featuring a memoir written by that figure's son). Bohn also published J. R. Planche's *Hist. of British Costume* in 1874.

Although Bohn harbored the ambition of founding a great publishing house, he discovered that his sons lacked a taste for the trade. Consequently, in 1864, he sold the *Libraries* to Messrs. Bell and Daldy, a firm that later became G. Bell & Sons.

## Bibliographical Work and Collaborations
One of Bohn's most laborious and useful undertakings was the revision of *The Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature*. Originally published in 1834 by W.T. Lowndes, the manual was the first systematic work of its kind, though it had brought Lowndes neither money nor fame. Lowndes, who had been reduced to poverty, served as a cataloguer to Bohn before his death in 1843. Bohn's 1864 revision of the manual spanned six volumes and added considerable material to the original work, including critical and bibliographical notices and details regarding prices.

## Death
Henry George Bohn died at Twickenham on August 22, 1884.
