# BARRE, ISAAC (1726-1802)

Isaac Barre was a British politician and soldier who served in the British parliament and the army during the 18th century. He is remembered as a defender of American rights.

## Early Life and Military Service
Born in Dublin in 1726, Isaac Barre was the son of a French refugee. He received his education at Trinity College, Dublin. Following his education, he entered the army. In 1759, Barre participated in the taking of Quebec while serving with Wolfe; during this military engagement, he sustained a wound to his cheek.

## Political Career and the American Cause
Barre entered parliament in 1761. His entry was facilitated by Lord Shelburne, who selected Barre to serve as a "bravo to run down Mr Pitt." Consequently, Barre's early parliamentary presence was marked by a virulent attack on Pitt, although Barre eventually became a devoted adherent of him.

Barre became a vigorous opponent of the taxation of America. He utilized his mastery of invective to champion the American cause. In one of his speeches, he applied the name "Sons of Liberty" to the colonists. This term subsequently became a common designation for patriotic clubs and American organizations directed against the Stamp Act.

## Later Appointments and Retirement
In 1782, Barre was appointed to the treasurership of the navy. This position included a pension of £3,200 per year. Because the government was ostensibly advocating for economy at the time, this appointment caused great discontent. Later, the younger Pitt replaced the pension with the clerkship of the pells, which saved the pension amount for the public.

Barre retired from office in 1790 after becoming blind. He died on July 20, 1802.

## Legacy and Commemoration
The township of Barre in Washington county, Vermont, U.S.A., was organized in 1793. Settled shortly after the close of the War of Independence, the township was named in honor of Isaac Barre due to his role as a defender of American rights in the British parliament. The city of Barre, which was chartered in 1894, was originally a part of this township.
