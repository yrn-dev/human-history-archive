# Sir Alexander John Ball

## Early Life and Naval Career
Sir Alexander John Ball (1759–1809) was a British rear-admiral and governor of Malta, born into a family from Gloucestershire. He began his career in the navy and achieved the rank of lieutenant in 1778. In 1781, he entered into a close association with Rodney. Following Rodney's victory on April 12, 1782, Ball was promoted to commander two days later, and he attained the rank of captain in 1783.

During this period, Ball spent one year in France to live economically and to learn the French language. At the time, Captain Nelson did not hold a favorable opinion of Ball, describing him as a "great coxcomb."

## Command and Relationship with Nelson
Ball did not receive a command until 1790, but from that year forward, he was continuously employed. The animosity previously felt by Nelson was forgotten in 1798, after Ball provided assistance to Nelson's ship during heavy weather; from that point on, the two men became close friends.

Under the command of Nelson, Ball participated in the battle of the Nile. During this engagement, Ball's ship, the *Alexander*, was the specific opponent of *L'Orient*, the flagship of Brueys, which subsequently blew up.

## The Blockade and Governance of Malta
Two months after the battle of the Nile, Ball was ordered to the blockade of Malta, a mission that continued without interruption for two years. Ball managed the blockade by committing it to his first lieutenant while he personally led the local militia and the marines to conduct the siege from the land side. Ball's popularity with the Maltese people was established during this time due to the care he showed for his men.

Following the fall of Malta, Ball practically retired from service, despite urgent requests from Nelson for him to remain afloat. In 1801, Ball was made a baronet and appointed governor of Malta. He served in this capacity until 1809, endearing himself to the local population through his regard for their interests and his opposition to policies that treated the island as a conquered dependency.

## Death and Legacy
The friendship between Ball and Lord Nelson lasted until it was broken by death; Nelson's letters indicate a high regard for Ball. Sir Alexander Ball was also known to be kind to Coleridge, who praised him in "The Third Landing Place" of *The Friend*.

Sir Alexander Ball died on October 20, 1809, and was buried in Malta. His career is further documented in the *Despatches* of Nelson, as edited by Sir H. Nicolas.
