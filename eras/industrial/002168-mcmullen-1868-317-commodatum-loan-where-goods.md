# Bailment and Loans

## Definition and General Principles of Bailment
Bailment, derived from the French word *bailler* (meaning to place in charge of), is a legal arrangement involving the delivery of goods from one party, known as the bailor, to another party, known as the bailee. This delivery occurs for a specific purpose based on an express or implied contract. The terms of the agreement dictate that once the purpose is fulfilled, the goods must be redelivered to the bailor, dealt with according to the bailor's directions, or kept until the bailor reclaims them.

## Classifications of Bailment
Chief Justice Holt, in the 1704 case of *Coggs v. Bernard*, established a classification of bailments that is generally adopted in law. These include:

### Depositum
*Depositum* is a bailment without reward where the bailee keeps goods for the bailor. In this arrangement, the bailee is not permitted to use the entrusted items. The bailee is held liable for gross negligence but is not responsible for ordinary negligence. For example, in *Giblin v. McMullen* (1868), a banker who received no payment for holding a customer's securities was not held liable when a cashier stole them, as there was no proof of gross negligence.

### Commodatum
*Commodatum* refers to a loan where useful chattels or goods are lent *gratis* (free of charge) to the bailee for their use. Because the transaction is a gratuitous loan for the sole advantage of the bailee, the bailee is expected to be a person of competent skill in caring for the item. Consequently, the bailee must exercise great diligence in protecting the goods and is responsible even for slight negligence. In *Bringloe v. Morrice* (1676), it was determined that a person lent a horse to ride was not warranted in allowing their servant to ride it. However, in *Camoys v. Scurr* (1880), a vendor who allowed a defendant to try a horse for sale was permitted to allow a competent person to try the animal.

### Locatio Rei
*Locatio rei* is the lending of goods for hire. Under this classification, the bailee is required to exercise the same level of diligence that a prudent man would use when caring for his own property.

## Distinctions in Loan Types
Historical legal perspectives, specifically those of the civilians, distinguish between different types of loans. The *commodatum* requires the borrower to restore the exact same individual thing they were accommodated with for a temporary need. In contrast, the *mutuum* involves items destined for consumption; the borrower discharges this engagement by substituting the same specific value based on measure, weight, or number.

## Financial Loans and Debt
Loans also manifest as financial instruments for governments and institutions:

*   **Infrastructure and Commissions:** Between 1857 and 1860, Turkey advanced approximately L150,000 as a loan for improvement works. A larger loan of L120,000, guaranteed by the powers and raised in 1868 through the efforts of Sir John Stokes, was paid off by 1882.
*   **National Finance (Bulgaria):** Prior to 1908, Bulgaria contracted various loans, including a 30,000,000 fr. (£1,200,000) loan in 1889 with the Bankverein and Vienna Länderbank, and a 142,780,000 fr. (£5,711,200) loan in 1892. Later loans in 1902 and 1904 were secured by stamp-tax and tobacco dues.
*   **Banking and Money Markets:** In the London money market, "loans at call and short notice" are common. Since 1888 or 1889, "money for the night" has become a usual practice. This "fugitive" money often comes from temporary balances held for foreign governments or subscriptions to loans offered to the public.

## Spiritual Concepts of Loaning
In the sacred texts of the Qur'an, the concept of a loan is presented in a spiritual context. The text asks who will offer a "beautiful loan" or "goodly loan" to Allah. It teaches that for those who do so, Allah will increase it manifold to their credit, double it for them, and provide a liberal or excellent reward.
