# BRISTOL

The name Bristol refers to two distinct urban centers: a major seaport and administrative hub in England, and a city spanning the border of Tennessee and Virginia in the United States.

## Bristol, England
Bristol is an English seaport and city that serves as a municipal, county, and parliamentary borough. It is located 118½ miles west of London, situated primarily within Gloucestershire, though parts of the city extend into Somersetshire. As of 1901, the population of the city was 328,945.

### Geography and Topography
The city is positioned in a basin-like hollow approximately 4 miles in diameter, with extensions reaching the heights to the north. The Avon river forms the boundary between Somerset and Gloucestershire at this location. The river is confined between significant hills, creating a narrow valley floor where the nucleus of the city is located. To the west, between the city and the Bristol Channel (the estuary of the Severn), the valley transforms into a gorge. This gorge is spanned by the Clifton Suspension Bridge. The hills close in again to the east at Keynsham. The Avon enters the Severn estuary only 8 miles below the city.

### Transportation and Infrastructure
Bristol is a significant railway hub served by two primary companies:
*   **The Great Western Railway:** This line enters the Avon valley near Bath to serve Bristol from London. It connects the city to the southern and south-western counties of England and to South Wales via the Severn tunnel. Local lines from this company encircle the city to the north and south, providing service to the Bristol Channel outports of Portishead and Avonmouth.
*   **The Midland Railway:** A trunk line connects Bristol to the north of England, passing through Derby, Birmingham, Worcester, and Gloucester.

Both the Great Western and Midland railways utilize the central station, known as Temple Meads.

## Bristol, United States
In the United States, Bristol is a city located 130 miles northeast of Knoxville, Tennessee, at an altitude of approximately 1700 feet. The city is unique in that it is situated across the boundary of two states, falling within Sullivan County, Tennessee, and Washington County, Virginia.

### Demographics and Administration
The city was settled around 1835, and the Tennessee portion was first incorporated in 1856. The Virginia portion was known as Goodson until it was chartered as a city in 1890; it remains administratively independent of its county. The Tennessee-Virginia boundary line runs through the principal street, dividing the city into two separate corporations.

Population records show steady growth:
*   **1880:** 3,209
*   **1890:** 6,226
*   **1900:** 9,850 (including 1,981 negroes)
*   **1910:** 13,395 (comprising 7,148 residents in Tennessee and 6,247 in Virginia)

### Economy and Education
Bristol serves as an important railway center, served by the Norfolk & Western, the Virginia & South-Western, the Southern, and the Holston Valley railways. Its proximity to great mineral deposits in North Carolina, Kentucky, West Virginia, Tennessee, and Virginia makes it a vital distributing point for coke, coal, and iron. The local economy includes iron furnaces, tobacco factories, furniture factories, packing houses, lumber mills, and tanneries.

The city is home to several educational institutions:
*   **In Virginia:** The Virginia Institute for Women (Baptist, established 1884) and Sullins College for women (Methodist Episcopal, South, established 1870).
*   **In Tennessee:** A normal college for negroes.
