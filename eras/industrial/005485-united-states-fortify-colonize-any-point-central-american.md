# United States and the Central American Mainland

The relationship between the United States and the Central American mainland has been characterized by a combination of diplomatic agreements, territorial arbitration, and a historical divergence from the aggressive imperialism practiced by European Great Powers. A primary legal instrument governing this relationship was the Clayton-Bulwer Treaty of 1850.

## The Clayton-Bulwer Treaty
The Clayton-Bulwer Treaty of 1850 established specific restrictions on the expansion of influence in the region. Under the terms of this treaty, both Great Britain and the United States were forbidden from fortifying or colonizing any point on the Central American mainland. However, an exception to this provision was made for Belize. This exception, along with the settlement of boundary disputes with Guatemala in 1859, helped confirm British legal sovereignty over the colony of Belize, including territories claimed by the British in 1836.

## Comparison with European Imperialism
Historically, the United States followed a different trajectory than the European Great Powers. While the United States possessed worldwide mercantile and financial interests, a growing industrialism requiring overseas markets, and a patriotic population, it did not initially develop an aggressive policy of armaments or subjugation in the same manner as Europe. 

Analysis suggests that had the United States operated as a Europeanized Great Power, the divided states of South and Central America would likely have been subjugated and placed under the disciplinary control of an American "governing class." Such a policy would have also necessitated that Great Britain fortify the Canadian frontier and maintain a large arsenal in the St. Lawrence. Instead, the United States maintained a habit of isolation from old-world entanglements and adhered to the Monroe Doctrine, which served to protect the new world from European interference.

## Diplomatic and Legal Interventions
The United States has acted as an arbitrator in regional disputes within Central America. In 1888, the president of the United States provided an award regarding the boundary question between Nicaragua and Costa Rica, which confirmed a treaty from 1858. Subsequent demarcation difficulties between these two nations were later settled by a treaty in 1896.

## Other Regional Activities
Beyond political and military restrictions, the United States maintained a presence in Central America through religious and social organizations. The American Bible Society, established in 1816, extended its foreign agencies to Central America, as well as to Mexico, the South American republics, Cuba, and the Philippines.
