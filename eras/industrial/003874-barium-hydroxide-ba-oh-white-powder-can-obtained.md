# Barium Hydroxide

Barium hydroxide, represented by the chemical formula $\text{Ba(OH)}_2$, is a chemical compound that appears as a white powder. It is characterized by its solubility properties, being moderately soluble in cold water and readily soluble in hot water. Solutions of the compound exhibit an alkaline reaction and possess the ability to readily absorb carbon dioxide.

## Production Methods
Barium hydroxide can be obtained through several different chemical processes:

*   **Slaking:** The compound can be produced by slaking barium monoxide with the necessary quantity of water. This reaction between the monoxide and water is described as combining very energetically, evolving a significant amount of heat.
*   **Large-Scale Industrial Production:** On a larger scale, the process begins by heating heavy spar with small coal to produce a crude barium sulphide. This sulphide is then heated in a current of moist carbon dioxide to form barium carbonate ($\text{BaS} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 = \text{BaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S}$). Finally, the barium carbonate is decomposed by a current of superheated steam ($\text{BaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} = \text{Ba(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2$), which leaves a residue of barium hydroxide.
*   **Decomposition of Sulphide:** Barium hydroxide can also be formed through the decomposition of barium sulphide by water, which results in the formation of both the hydroxide and hydrosulphide.

## Physical Properties and Forms
In addition to its powder form, barium hydroxide can exist as a hydrate. When a saturated solution of the hydroxide is cooled, it deposits a hydrated form, $\text{Ba(OH)}_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$. This form manifests as colourless quadratic prisms. Upon exposure to air, these crystals lose seven molecules of their water of crystallization.

## Applications in Chemistry
The solution of barium hydroxide is referred to as *baryta-water* and is utilized extensively in practical chemistry for several purposes:

*   **Gas Analysis:** It is employed to determine the amount of carbon dioxide present in the atmosphere.
*   **Organic Chemistry:** It serves as a hydrolysing agent used for the decomposition of substituted aceto-acetic esters and complex ureides.
*   **Condensing Agent:** E. Fischer has utilized barium hydroxide as a condensing agent for the preparation of $\beta$- and $\alpha$-acrose from acrolein dibromide.
*   **Synthesis of Other Compounds:** Baryta-water is used in the preparation of other barium compounds. For example, it is used to prepare barium bromide through saturation, and barium bromate is prepared by the action of excess bromine on baryta-water. Additionally, barium dioxide can be prepared in a hydrated condition ($\text{BaO}_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$) by adding an excess of baryta-water to a hydrogen peroxide solution.
