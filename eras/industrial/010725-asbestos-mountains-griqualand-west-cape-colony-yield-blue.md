# Asbestos in Griqualand West

## Mineral Characteristics and Classification
In the Asbestos Mountains of Griqualand West, located within the Cape Colony, a blue fibrous mineral is extracted and worked under the name of Cape asbestos. This mineral is classified as a variety of amphibole known as crocidolite. 

Geologically, this asbestos occurs in veins within slaty rocks. It is found associated with quartzites and jaspers that are rich in brown iron-ore and magnetite. These deposits are situated within the Griqua Town series, which are categorized in South Africa as Pre-Cape rocks.

## Regional Geography and Geology
Griqualand West is a territorial division of the Cape Province in the Union of South Africa and forms part of the region's great tableland. The territory covers an area of 15,197 square miles, with a general elevation ranging between 3,000 and 4,000 feet above sea level. It is bounded to the north and west by Bechuanaland, to the south by the Orange river, and to the east by the Orange Free State Province and the Transvaal.

The western portion of the region is traversed by several low ranges of rocky hills in a general southwest-northeast direction, including the Asbestos, Langeberg, Vansittart, and Kaap mountains. The landscape is largely barren, transitioning into absolute desert toward the northwest. While the climate is characterized by hot, dry days and frequently cold nights, the soil is extremely fertile in areas where irrigation is possible.

## Economic Activity
Asbestos is worked specifically in the south-western districts of Griqualand West. While the region supports horse-breeding in Kimberley and some crop growing and sheep-farming, its primary wealth is derived from diamonds. These diamonds are found in the district between the Riet and Vaal rivers, as well as along the banks of the Vaal. Between the initial discovery of diamonds in 1867 and the end of 1905, the estimated total yield was 13-1/2 tons, with a value of £95,000,000.

## Comparative Context of Asbestos
Asbestos is widely distributed globally, though it only occurs in sufficient purity and abundance for commercial work in a few locations. Other notable sources include:
* **Italy and Corsica:** Formerly the almost exclusive sources, these workings primarily yield amphibole, sometimes associated with green garnet nodules called "seeds" (*Semenze dell' amianto*).
* **Canada:** Currently the most industrially important source, primarily located in a belt of serpentine in Quebec near Thetford and Black Lake.
* **Other Regions:** Limited deposits are found in Russia, Hungary, Tirol, New Zealand, New South Wales, and Queensland. In the British Isles, it is found in parts of Ireland and among the old rocks of North Wales. In the United States, amphibole-asbestos is obtained from Sall Mountain, Georgia, and it has also been worked in Vermont.

Mineralogically, asbestos is generally divided into amphibole (or hornblende) and serpentine varieties. Amphibole-asbestos is typically grey or white and can be harsh, brittle, and very long. Serpentine-asbestos, such as chrysotile, is usually yellowish or greenish, with shorter fibres that are elastic, flexible, and possess a silky lustre.
