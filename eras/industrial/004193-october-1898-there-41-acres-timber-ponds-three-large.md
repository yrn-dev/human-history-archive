# October 1898 and Related Land and Maritime Records

## Maritime Developments in Barry
In October 1898, specifically on the 10th of the month, Dock No. 2 was opened at Barry. This dock covers 34 acres. The seaport at Barry was established due to the determination of several colliery owners who sought an independent railway from the coalfields and an alternative port to Cardiff. Although they failed in 1883, they secured parliamentary powers in 1884, and construction began in November of that year.

The total extent of the docks at Barry is 114 acres, providing accommodation for the largest vessels afloat. Dock No. 1, which opened on July 18, 1889, occupies the eastern side of the old channel between the mainland and the island; it covers 73 acres, including a 7-acre basin, and features a deep-sea entrance that is well-sheltered. Additionally, there is good anchorage located between Sully and Barry islands. The facilities at Barry include three large graving-docks and 41 acres of timber-ponds. For the purpose of loading coal, the port utilizes seven movable and thirty fixed coal-hoists. By 1906, the total tonnage of exports reached 9,757,380 tons (of which all but 26,491 tons were coal), while imports totaled 506,103 tons.

## Shipping and Trade in Dundee and Grimsby
Other ports maintained significant shipping and docking infrastructure during this era. In Dundee, the docks cover 35-1/2 acres, divided among the Victoria Dock (10-3/4 acres), Camperdown Dock (8-1/2 acres), King William IV. Dock (6-1/4 acres), Earl Grey Dock (5-1/4 acres), and the Tidal Harbour (4-3/4 acres). Dundee's facilities include timber ponds, graving docks, and the Ferry harbour. In 1898, 2,914 vessels totaling 1,390,331 tons entered and cleared the port. Dundee also serves as the seat of the Arctic fishery, though this business declined due to the scarcity of seals and whales.

In Grimsby, the docks cover approximately 104 acres and include the Royal dock, the small Union dock, the extensive Alexandra dock, two large fish-docks, timber yards, and graving docks. By 1906, the Great Central Company began constructing a new dock at Immingham, 5 miles above Grimsby on the Humber, with a depth of 30 to 35 feet and an area of about 40 acres. Grimsby is primarily famous as a fishing port, with a fleet of over 500 vessels, mostly steam trawlers.

## Forestry and Timber Resources
Timber resources varied significantly by region. In the United States, Henry Gannett estimated in 1898 that the timbered area covered 28,300 square miles. These forests are considered the finest and greatest of their kind remaining in the U.S. The region features several pine belts: a south-western belt of about 4,200 square miles containing the world's heaviest growth of long-leaf pine, and an eastern belt of about 3,900 square miles composed of long-leaf pine and some loblolly. Short-leaf pine growth is particularly heavy in the north-western portion of the state. Additionally, the south of the state contains some of the largest and most heavily timbered cypress forests known, located in overflowed and alluvial lands.

In the Bahamas, the Abaco Islands and Andros Island are noted for a profusion of large timber, including bullet, iron, mastic, mahogany, and lignum vitae woods. However, a lack of roads and labour has made it impossible to turn much of this timber to useful account.

## Land Acts and Agricultural Statistics
In the United States, various land acts were passed to benefit settlers. The Timber-Culture Act of 1873 allowed individuals to gain title to 160 acres of public prairie-land if they planted 40 acres of timber and maintained it for ten years. However, this and the Pre-emption Act of 1841 were repealed in 1891 due to fraud.

Agricultural data from 1898 shows specific land use in Great Britain. The total area under orchards in Great Britain in 1898 was 226,059 acres, with 220,220 acres in England, 3,690 acres in Wales, and 2,149 acres in Scotland. During the same year, the area dedicated to small fruit in Great Britain was 69,753 acres, consisting of 63,438 acres in England, 1,044 acres in Wales, and 5,271 acres in Scotland.
