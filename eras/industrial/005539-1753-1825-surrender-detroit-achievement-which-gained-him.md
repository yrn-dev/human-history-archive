# Military and Civil Figures of the War of 1812

The War of 1812 involved significant military engagements between Great Britain and the United States, particularly in the region of Upper Canada and the Great Lakes. These conflicts were characterized by the organization of provincial militias, naval battles on inland lakes, and the strategic capture of key settlements.

## Sir Isaac Brock
Sir Isaac Brock (1769–1812) was a British administrator and soldier born in St Peter Port, Guernsey. He entered the army as an ensign of the 8th regiment at age fifteen and rose to the rank of lieutenant-colonel by 1797. His early career included commanding the 49th regiment during the 1799 expedition to North Holland, where he was wounded at the battle of Egmont-op-Zee, and service with the British fleet during the battle of Copenhagen.

Brock served in Canada from 1802 to 1805, returning in 1806 due to the imminent threat of war with the United States. He managed the garrison at Quebec from September 1806 until August 1810, after which he assumed command of the troops in Upper Canada and became the provisional lieutenant-governor.

During the outbreak of the War of 1812, Brock organized the provincial militia to defend Upper Canada against invasion. On August 16, 1812, Brock led a force of approximately 730 men and 600 Indians, commanded by their chief Tecumseh. This force compelled General William Hull (1753–1825) and an American force of 2,500 men to surrender at Detroit. This victory earned Brock a knighthood of the Bath and the popular designation "the hero of Upper Canada." Brock was later killed on October 13, 1812, at the battle of Queenston Heights. He was honored with a public monument in London's Saint Paul's cathedral. His remains were moved from the bastions of Fort George to a monument on Queenston Heights in 1824; although this monument was destroyed by a fanatic in 1840, a more stately replacement was later erected following a mass-meeting of over 8,000 citizens.

## Thomas Macdonough
Thomas Macdonough (1786–1825) was an American sailor born in Delaware to a father who served as an officer in the Continental Army. He joined the United States navy in 1800 and served as a lieutenant, participating in the bombardment of Tripoli and resisting the seizure of a seaman by the British navy while his ship was under the guns of Gibraltar.

During the War of 1812, Macdonough was ordered to cruise the lakes between the United States and Canada, establishing headquarters on lake Champlain. On September 11, 1814, he achieved a brilliant victory on lake Champlain, using a flotilla of 14 vessels with 86 guns to defeat Captain George Downie's 16 vessels and 92 guns. This action saved Vermont and New York from invasion. In recognition of this achievement, Congress awarded Macdonough a gold medal, and the states of New York and Vermont granted him estates.

## Lewis Cass
Lewis Cass (1782–1866) was an American statesman and general born in Exeter, New Hampshire. After studying law in Marietta, Ohio, and entering the bar at age twenty, he served in the Ohio legislature. During the War of 1812, Cass served under General W.H. Harrison and General William Hull; Cass strongly condemned Hull's surrender at Detroit. He rose through the ranks from colonel of volunteers to major-general of the Ohio militia and eventually brigadier-general in the regular United States army.

In 1813, Cass was appointed governor of the territory of Michigan. In this role, he managed Indian affairs in a territory occupied almost entirely by natives, with only 6,000 white settlers. Over eighteen years, he explored the sources and lakes of the Mississippi river, constructed roads, instituted surveys, and extinguished Indian titles to large tracts of land. 

Cass's later antipathy toward Great Britain was largely informed by his trying relations with British authorities in Canada after the War of 1812, specifically regarding the arousing of Indian hostility against the American government and the persistent searching of American vessels on the Great Lakes. Cass later served as secretary of war from 1831 to 1836 under President Jackson, directing the Seminole and Black Hawk wars.
