# United States Military Academy Graduates and Early Youth Transitions

The United States Military Academy at West Point has served as a foundational institution for various American military engineers, soldiers, and scientists. Several notable figures who graduated from the academy transitioned from diverse early youth experiences—including international emigration and academic study—into distinguished careers in the United States Army and beyond.

## Benjamin L.E. Bonneville
Benjamin L.E. Bonneville (1795–1878) was born in France around 1795. He emigrated to the United States during his early youth and subsequently graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1815. His early military career involved the construction of military roads in the south-west, and he attained the rank of captain of infantry in 1825.

Between 1831 and 1836, Bonneville took a leave of absence from the army to lead an exploring expedition to the Rocky Mountains. This journey, conducted largely on his own responsibility, took him up the Platte river through areas that later became the states of Wyoming and Colorado, continuing into California and the Great Salt Lake basin. After several years of being completely cut off from civilization, his name was struck from the army list. However, he returned with accounts of his adventures, which were edited by Washington Irving and published in two volumes in 1837 as *The Rocky Mountains: or Scenes, Incidents, and Adventures in the Far West*.

Bonneville later returned to military prominence, becoming a major in 1845. During the Mexican War, he was breveted lieutenant-colonel for his gallantry at the battles of Churubusco and Contreras. He reached the rank of colonel in 1855 and commanded the Gila river expedition against the Apaches in 1857. From 1858 to 1861, he was in command of the department of New Mexico. Although retired in 1861, he served as a commandant of barracks and recruiting officer in St Louis, Missouri, during the Civil War, receiving the brevet rank of brigadier-general in 1865. He died on June 12, 1878, at Fort Smith, Arkansas.

## Alexander Dallas Bache
Alexander Dallas Bache (1806–1867), a great-grandson of Benjamin Franklin, was born in Philadelphia on July 19, 1806. He graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1825. Following his graduation, he served as an assistant professor at the academy and spent one or two years as a lieutenant in the corps of engineers working on the erection of coast fortifications.

Bache transitioned into academia and science, serving as a professor of chemistry and natural philosophy at the University of Pennsylvania from 1828 to 1841 and again in 1842–1843. Between 1836 and 1838, he traveled to Europe to examine educational systems on behalf of the trustees of what would become Girard College in 1848. In 1843, he was appointed superintendent of the United States coast survey. In this role, he implemented a comprehensive plan involving numerous observing stations to map the coast and collect meteorological and magnetic observations. Bache died on February 17, 1867, in Newport, Rhode Island.

## George Brinton McClellan
George Brinton McClellan (1826–1885) was born in Philadelphia on December 3, 1826. Before entering military service, he spent two years (1840–1842) at the university of Pennsylvania. He then entered the United States military academy, graduating with high honours in July 1846.

As a lieutenant of engineers during the Mexican War, McClellan participated in battles under General Scott, earning brevets of captain at Chapultepec and first-lieutenant at Contreras-Churubusco. He later served as an assistant-instructor at West Point and conducted explorations in Oregon and the South-West. By 1855, he was promoted to captain of cavalry and was sent to Europe on a military commission to study European armies and the war in the Crimea, later publishing his findings as *Armies of Europe* in 1861. In 1856, he designed the saddle that became known as the McClellan.

After resigning his commission in 1857, he held executive roles in the Mississippi & Ohio railroad and the Illinois Central railroad. With the outbreak of the Civil War in April 1861, he was made major-general of three months' militia by the governor of Ohio, and shortly thereafter, on May 14, was promoted to major-general, U.S.A., commanding the department of the Ohio. His early successes in the region, including the dispersal of Confederates at Philippi, contributed to the creation of the state of West Virginia.

## Alexander McDowell McCook
Alexander McDowell McCook (1831–1903) was born in Columbiana county, Ohio, on April 22, 1831. He graduated from the U.S. military academy in 1852. His early service included operations against the Utes and Apaches in New Mexico from 1853 to 1857. From 1858 to 1861, he served as an assistant instructor of infantry tactics at the military academy.

During the Civil War, McCook became colonel of the 1st Ohio Volunteers in April 1861. He fought in the first battle of Bull Run and held various commands in Tennessee, Mississippi, and Kentucky. He was promoted to brigadier-general of volunteers in September 1861 and major-general of volunteers in July 1862. He earned brevets of colonel at Shiloh, lieutenant-colonel at the capture of Nashville, and brigadier-general at Perryville. In 1865, he was breveted major-general for his wartime services and commanded the district of Eastern Arkansas from February to May of that year.

Following the war, he was commissioned as lieutenant-colonel of the 26th Infantry in 1867 and served in Texas. Between 1886 and 1890, he commanded the infantry and cavalry school and Fort Leavenworth, Kansas. He attained the rank of brigadier-general in 1890 and major-general in 1894. After retiring in 1895, he served on a commission from 1898 to 1899 to investigate the administration of the United States department of war during the war with Spain.
