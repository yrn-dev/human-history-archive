# Political and Legal Events of 1877

The year 1877 was marked by significant political transitions and legislative proposals in both France and the United States, particularly within the state of Louisiana.

## French Political Developments
In France, the political landscape of 1877 was defined by the aftermath of the *coup d'etat* of May 16th. Agenor Bardoux, a French statesman and native of Bourges, emerged as one of the leaders of the "363" following this event. Bardoux, who had been elected as a deputy of the National Assembly in 1871 and re-elected in 1876 and 1877, served as the president of the left centre group in the chamber. In this role, he advocated strongly for the republic while opposing anti-clericalism.

Following the election of the republican chamber after the May 16th *coup d'etat*, Bardoux was appointed minister of public instruction in December 1877. During his tenure, he proposed several republican laws, with a notable focus on compulsory primary education.

Simultaneously, Jules Armand Stanislas Dufaure, another prominent French statesman and leader of the "left-centre" party, returned to power on December 24, 1877. This return followed a period of political instability and a check received by the conservatives on May 16th. Dufaure had previously served as the president of the council and minister of justice, and he remained a central figure in the government until his retirement in February 1879.

## Louisiana State Politics
In the United States, the state of Louisiana experienced a definitive political shift in 1877. Following a period of intense rivalry between two state governments—one supported by Democrats and Liberal Republicans and the other by radical Republicans—the state transitioned to Democratic control. 

While Stephen B. Packard, a radical Republican, was a contestant for the governorship in 1877, Francis T. Nicholls, a Democrat, served as governor from 1877 to 1880. From 1877 onward, Louisiana remained overwhelmingly and uninterruptedly Democratic in both state and national elections. This period also saw the state prosper markedly in material respects, though it was accompanied by the practical disfranchisement of negroes.
