# Tower Bridge, London

The Tower Bridge in London is a suspension bridge that incorporates a secondary bascule bridge within its centre span to allow for the passage of ships. The structure's total length is 940 ft., with additional approaches measuring 1260 ft. on the north side and 780 ft. on the south. The width of the bridge between the parapets is generally 60 ft., though it narrows to 49 ft. across the centre span.

## Structural Composition and Towers
The bridge features four towers: two located in the river and two situated on the shore abutments. These towers support the suspension chains. The main towers are constructed from a steel skeleton that is enclosed in a facing of Portland stone and granite, which is further backed by brickwork. The abutment towers are similar in design to the river towers.

On the abutment towers, the suspension chains are connected to anchor ties via horizontal links that are carried on rockers. The suspension chains themselves are built as braced girders to ensure stability against unsymmetrical loading. Each chain over a shore span is composed of two segments; the shorter segment is attached to the link at the top of the abutment tower, while the longer is attached to the tie at the top of the river tower.

## The Bascule Opening Span
The opening section of the bridge, located between the river towers, consists of two leaves, or bascules. These bascules rotate in a vertical plane and are pivoted near the faces of the piers. When these leaves are raised, they leave an unobstructed width of 200 ft. between the main river piers, extending up to the high-level foot-bridge, which sits 141 ft. above Trinity H.W.

Each bascule contains four main girders. These girders project 62 ft. 6 in. within the face of the piers and 100 ft. beyond them. To provide structural support, bracings and transverse girders are inserted between the main girders at 12 ft. intervals. The floor of the bascule is constructed of buckled plates and paved with wood blocks.

## Mechanical Specifications
The rotation of the bascules is achieved via pinions driven by hydraulic engines that operate in steel sectors with a 42 ft. radius. The axis of rotation is located 5 ft. 7 in. below the roadway and 13 ft. 3 in. inside the face of the piers, with an arc of rotation measuring 82°. This axis is 48 ft. long and 21 in. in diameter, made of forged steel. It utilizes eight bearings consisting of rings of live rollers that are 22 in. long and 4-7/16 in. in diameter.

The weight of each leaf, including its ballast, is approximately 1070 tons, with the ballast in the short arms of the bascules weighing 365 tons.

## High-Level Footways and Suspension System
The bridge includes two high-level footways that remain available for use when the bascules are raised. The main girders for these footways are of the suspended girder and cantilever types, with the cantilevers fixed to the shore side of the towers. The middle girders are 120 ft. in length and are connected to the cantilevers by links.

The main suspension chains are carried across the centre span as horizontal ties that rest on the high-level footway girders. These ties are jointed to the hanging chains using pins 20 in. in diameter. Each pin is surrounded by a ring in halves that is 5 in. thick; one half ring is rigidly attached to the hanging chain and the other to the tie. This design ensures that wear caused by movement is distributed across the length of the pin. The load at this joint is transmitted to the steel columns of the towers via a rocker bearing located under the pins.

The clear width of the two shore spans is 270 ft.
