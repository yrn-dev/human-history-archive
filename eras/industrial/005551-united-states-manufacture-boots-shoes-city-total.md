# Manufacture of Boots and Shoes in the United States

The manufacture of boots and shoes has historically been a significant industrial activity across various regions of the United States, with concentrated production centers in Massachusetts and Virginia, as well as broader industrial presence in states such as Illinois and Kentucky.

## Leading Manufacturing Centers in Massachusetts

Massachusetts has hosted some of the most prominent cities for the boot and shoe industry, most notably Brockton and Lynn.

### Brockton
Located in Plymouth County, Brockton emerged as a premier industrial center. In both 1900 and 1905, it ranked first among all cities in the United States for the manufacture of boots and shoes. The city's total factory product was valued at $24,855,362 in 1900, rising to $37,790,982 by 1905, representing a 52% increase. 

The boot and shoe industry was the dominant economic driver for the city; in 1905, the value of the boot and shoe product alone was $30,073,014, which accounted for 9.4% of the total boot and shoe product of the entire United States. When combined with boot and shoe findings ($2,435,137) and cut stock ($1,344,977), these three categories represented 89.6% of Brockton's total manufactured product. By 1908, the city housed 35 shoe factories, including establishments such as Packard, Keith, Eaton, Walkover, Ralston, and W.L. Douglas. Production volume grew from 14,000,000 pairs of shoes in 1905 to approximately 17,000,000 pairs in 1907. The city also produced related goods, including shoe fittings, tools, blacking, lasts, and wooden and paper packing boxes.

### Lynn
Lynn, a seaport city in Essex County, also maintained a primary role in the industry. In 1900 and 1905, Lynn ranked second only to Brockton in the value of boots and shoes manufactured. However, Lynn outranked Brockton in the three allied industries of findings, cut stock, and the manufacture of boots and shoes.

The industry in Lynn has deep historical roots, having been introduced in 1636 by Philip Kertland. A corporation of shoemakers was present by 1651. The industry grew through the contributions of figures such as John Adam Dagyr, a Welshman who arrived in 1750, and Ebenezer Breed, who introduced the use of morocco around 1783. Historical output records show 80,000 pairs produced in 1767, and by 1795, 600 journeymen and 200 master workmen produced approximately 300,000 pairs of women's shoes.

## Southern and Midwestern Production

Beyond Massachusetts, other states and cities developed significant boot and shoe manufacturing capabilities.

### Virginia
In Lynchburg, the boot and shoe industry was established in 1900. It quickly became the city's most important industry, and by 1905, Lynchburg was the largest manufacturer of these articles in the South and one of the largest distributors in the country. The city's total factory products increased in value by 65.9% between 1900 and 1905, rising from $2,993,551 to $4,905,435.

### Illinois and Kentucky
In Illinois, the manufacture of boots and shoes is listed as one of the state's prosperous industries, alongside clothing and furniture. In Kentucky, boots and shoes are categorized as an important manufacture with a product value exceeding one million dollars in 1905.
