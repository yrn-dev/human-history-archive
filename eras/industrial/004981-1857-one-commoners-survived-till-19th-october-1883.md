# The Bridgewater Trust and Related 19th Century Events

## The Bridgewater Trust and Legal Settlement
Following the death of a duke on March 8, 1803, the ducal title became extinct. While the earldom of Bridgewater was passed to a cousin, John William Egerton (the 7th earl), the duke's estates and canals were placed on trust. The first beneficiary of this trust was the marquess of Stafford, who later became the first duke of Sutherland. He was followed by his son, Francis Leveson Gower (the first earl of Ellesmere), and his issue.

To ensure the trust lasted as long as possible, the legal rule allowing property to be settled for the duration of lives in being plus twenty-one years was utilized. The trust was settled upon a large number of individuals connected to the duke and their living issue, as well as the peers who had taken their seats in the House of Lords on or before the date of the duke's death.

## Expiration of the Trust
The timeline for the expiration of the trust was determined by the longevity of the designated beneficiaries. The last of the peers included in the settlement died in 1857. However, one of the commoners survived until October 19, 1883. Because of this, the trust did not officially expire until October 19, 1903, at which point the entire property came under the undivided control of the earl of Ellesmere.

Regarding the assets within the trust, the canals were transferred in 1872 to the Bridgewater Navigation Company. This company subsequently sold the canals to the Manchester Ship Canal Company in 1887.
