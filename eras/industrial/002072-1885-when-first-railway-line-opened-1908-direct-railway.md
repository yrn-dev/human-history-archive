# Railway Developments in Bahia Blanca and Kimberley

The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw significant expansions in railway infrastructure across various global regions, facilitating commercial growth, the export of raw materials, and improved regional communication.

## Bahia Blanca

Bahia Blanca is a city and port located in Argentina on the Naposta river, situated in the extreme southern part of the province of Buenos Aires. The city is positioned 3 miles from the river's outlet into a deep, well-sheltered bay. While the location dates back to 1828 with the establishment of a trading post and a fort, its emergence as a commercial center began in 1885 with the opening of its first railway line.

The port serves as the natural shipping point for the southern districts of the province of Buenos Aires, as well as the national territories of Neuquen and La Pampa, both of which grew in importance due to the opening of these territories to settlement. Great quantities of wool and wheat are exported from this location. The port's trade has increased rapidly, and it is connected to interior producing districts via five or six railway lines and their associated branches. Infrastructure at the port includes several miles of railway sidings and a shipping pier measuring 1640 feet in length, equipped with spacious warehouses.

Further expanding its connectivity, direct railway communication was opened in 1908 between Bahia Blanca and San Juan and Mendoza.

## Kimberley

In South Africa, the town of Kimberley developed from three distinct mining camps: Du Toits Pan, De Beers (also known as Old De Beers Rush), and Colesberg Kopje (known as New Rush or De Beers New Rush). The town was named after the 1st Earl of Kimberley, the secretary of state for the colonies, who directed that the mines be placed under the protection of Great Britain in 1871.

The region's economy became dominated by diamond mining, particularly after the 1889 amalgamation of interests when the De Beers group purchased the Kimberley mine for £5,338,650. This resulted in the De Beers Consolidated Mines, Ltd. controlling the entire diamond production of the Kimberley fields. Because the town had virtually no industries other than diamond mining and limited horse-breeding, it became largely dependent on the De Beers corporation. Due to the importance of the diamond industry, a railway from Cape Town was constructed and opened in 1885.
