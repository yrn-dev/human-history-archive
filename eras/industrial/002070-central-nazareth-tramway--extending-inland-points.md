# Central and the Nazareth Tramway

The Bahia Central railway and the Nazareth tramway are two of the railway lines that radiate from the city of Bahia (also known as São Salvador) to inland points. These specific lines are distinguished by the fact that they extend inland from points located on the opposite side of the bay from the city.

## The Bahia Central Railway
The Bahia Central railway originates in Cachoeira, which is described as an important inland town of Bahia, Brazil. Cachoeira is situated on the Paraguassu river, approximately 48 miles from São Salvador, and is connected to the city via river-boats. Despite being located on the lower levels of the river at an altitude of 52 feet above sea-level—which leaves the town subject to destructive floods—Cachoeira is a thriving industrial and commercial center in the state, noted for its cotton and cigar factories and the export of tobacco and sugar.

From its starting point in Cachoeira, the Bahia Central railway extends in two directions:
*   **South-west:** The line extends 161 miles to Machado Portella.
*   **North:** The line extends 28 miles to Feira de Santa Anna.

## Regional Context of Bahia
The city of Bahia, the capital of the state of Bahia, is a maritime city located on the western side of the peninsula that separates the Atlantic from the Bay of All Saints (*Bahia de Todos os Santos*). The city is divided into a commercial section and an upper town. 

The commercial section is located on a long, narrow beach between the bluffs and the water-line; it houses the railway station, the custom-house, the post-office, the exchange, the market, the arsenal, and principal business houses. The upper town, situated on the western slope of a low ridge (the peninsula's backbone), rises to altitudes between 200 and 260 feet above sea-level. This area contains the governor's palace, the museum, the public library, schools, hospitals, theatres, churches, and academies of law and medicine.

Communication between the lower commercial section and the upper town is facilitated by steep zigzag roads, a circular tramway, and two elevators. Additionally, four street-car lines connect the suburbs to both the upper and lower towns, with contracts made in 1906 to reconstruct some of these lines for electric traction.

## Other Regional Rail and Transport
Beyond the Bahia Central and the Nazareth tramway, other railways radiate from the city of Bahia to inland points, including a short line to Santo Amaro and the Bahia & Alagoinhas, the latter of which is under construction to Joazeiro on the Sao Francisco river.

The port of Bahia is recognized as one of the most accessible and best harbours on the east coast of South America, serving as a port of call for most steamship lines trading between Europe and the continent, while maintaining a large foreign and coastwise trade.
