# Thomas Bewick and the Development of English Illustration

The history of English illustration and the specialized art of book-plate collecting are significantly marked by the contributions of Thomas Bewick and the school of artists that followed his methods. Bewick is recognized as the point from which the consistent development of English illustration begins to date.

## Technical Innovations and Artistic Style
Thomas Bewick was both an artist and a practical inventor who fundamentally altered the technical history of wood-engraving. He is credited with the invention of the "white line" and "tint" methods of wood-cutting. Artistically, Bewick's approach focused on providing texture and local colour without the use of shadow. This technique ensured a precision of outline that prevented any form from being lost.

While some of his best designs may resemble specimen plates due to this precision, Bewick succeeded in introducing an element of colour into black-and-white illustration. This was a significant departure from the Italian and German schools of the 15th and 16th centuries, which had been entirely colourless in this regard.

## The Bewick School and Influence
Bewick's methods established a new school of illustration. The most "racy" qualities of his woodcuts were the result of the designer also serving as the cutter. This same relationship between designer and cutter provided distinct characteristics to the nearly contemporary works of Calvert and William Blake. While Blake's *Illustrations to the Book of Job* are magnificent in their conventional rendering of shade and light, they still retain a certain colourlessness.

In the broader context of English art, Bewick represents one pole of genius, contrasted with the work of Reynolds. While Reynolds is associated with engravers like Bartolozzi—whose art is described as graceful but not entirely English—Bewick's influence is seen as more foundational to the English tradition.

## Book-Plate Collecting and the Bewick School
In the field of "ex-librisme" (book-plate collecting), Bewick and his imitators are cited as artists whose designs are worth collecting. Book-plates are often valued as personal relics of well-known personages or as specimens of bygone decorative fashions, sometimes possessing a value greater than the volume in which they are found.

Within the variety of book-plate styles, the "landscape-plate" is a specific genre associated with wood engravers of the Bewick school. These plates, along with other pictorial styles such as "book-piles" and "library interiors," often treat armorial elements as secondary to the pictorial composition.

## Other Notable Contributors to Book-Plates
Alongside Bewick and his imitators, several other artists are recognized for their contributions to book-plate design. These include:
*   **Early and Classical Artists:** Holbein, Durer, the Little Masters of Germany, Charles Eisen, Hubert Francois Bourguignon (known as Gravelot), D.N. Chodowiecki, and Simon Gribelin.
*   **English and European Engravers:** W. Marshall, W. Faithorne, David Loggan, Sir Robert Strange, Francesco Piranesi, Hogarth, Cipriani, Bartolozzi, and John Keyse Sherwin.
*   **William Henshaw and Hewitt:** Both are listed among the artists whose book-plate designs are of high interest.
