# William Balfour Baikie

William Balfour Baikie (1824–1864) was a Scottish philologist, naturalist, and explorer. Born on August 21, 1824, in Kirkwall, Orkney, he was the eldest son of Captain John Baikie, R.N. After studying medicine at Edinburgh and earning his M.D. degree, Baikie entered the royal navy in 1848.

## The Niger Expedition of 1854
Through the notice of Sir Roderick Murchison, Baikie was appointed as a naturalist and surgeon for a government-supported Niger expedition sent out in 1854 by Macgregor Laird. During this mission, the senior officer, Consul Beecroft, died at Fernando Po, and Baikie succeeded him in command. 

Commanding the small steamer *Pleiad*, Baikie ascended the Benue river approximately 250 miles beyond the furthest point reached by previous explorers. The voyage lasted 118 days and concluded at the mouth of the Niger without the loss of any crew members. Although the expedition was instructed to attempt to assist Heinrich Barth—who had crossed the upper course of the Benue in 1851—Baikie was unable to find any trustworthy information regarding Barth's location. Upon his return to England, Baikie published the details of this journey in his 1856 work, *Narrative of an Exploring Voyage up the Rivers Kwora and Binue*.

## Later Expeditions and Lokoja
In March 1857, Baikie began a second expedition in the *Pleiad*, this time holding the rank of British consul. After two years of exploring the Niger, the vessel was wrecked while navigating river rapids. This event caused the party to separate; while the other members returned home, Baikie remained determined to fulfill the expedition's goals single-handedly.

Landing at the confluence of the Benue and Niger rivers with one or two native followers, Baikie established a base of operations at Lokoja. This site had previously been used for a model farm established by a British government expedition in 1841, which had been abandoned within a year following the deaths of most white settlers. After purchasing the site and signing a treaty with the Fula emir of Nupe, Baikie began building houses and clearing the land.

## Philological Work and Legacy
Baikie contributed significantly to the study of African languages. In 1861, he privately printed *Observations on the Hausa and Fulfulde (i.e. Fula) Languages*. Later, the Bible Society published his translation of the Psalms into Hausa in 1881. In addition to his African studies, he authored various works concerning Shetland and Orkney. A monument dedicated to his memory is located in the nave of the ancient cathedral of St Magnus in Kirkwall.
