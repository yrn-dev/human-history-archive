# Events and Publications of 1836

The year 1836 was marked by several significant contributions to the fields of geology, literature, and theology, as well as the professional development of scientific researchers.

## Geological and Scientific Works
In 1836, Dr. Buckland published a treatise on "Geology and Mineralogy." This work was produced in accordance with the will of the earl of Bridgewater as part of a series known as the *Bridgewater Treatises*. The primary objective of these treatises was to demonstrate the goodness, wisdom, and power of God as they are manifested in the Creation. While the work on "Geology and Mineralogy" was not intended to be a "manual" of geological science, it served as a valuable storehouse of palaeontological and geological facts to support its specific argument. The work subsequently went through three editions, with the third edition issued in 1858 and edited by the author's son, Francis T. Buckland.

## Academic and Professional Developments
During the period of 1836-1837, James Dwight Dana served as an assistant to Professor Benjamin Silliman in the chemical laboratory at Yale. Dana, an American zoologist, mineralogist, and geologist, had previously graduated from Yale in 1833 and spent two years teaching mathematics to navy midshipmen. Following his time as an assistant to Silliman, Dana spent four years as a geologist and mineralogist for a United States exploring expedition in the Pacific ocean under the command of Captain Charles Wilkes.

## Literary and Bibliographic Publications
The year 1836 saw the publication of Armenian versions of New Testament commentaries, which were issued in Venice across four volumes.

Additionally, 1836 marked the release of the first original First American Edition of Edward Gibbon's *The Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire*. This specific set has served as a reference for later digital renovations and re-proofing of the text.
