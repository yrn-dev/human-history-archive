# BARNETT, JOHN (1802-1890)

John Barnett was an English musical composer born in Bedford. He was the son of Bernhard Beer, a Prussian who settled in England and worked as a jeweller, changing his name upon his arrival.

## Early Life and Education
Barnett's professional exposure to performance began at a young age; at eleven years old, he sang on the stage of the Lyceum in London. Due to the quality of his voice, he was provided with a musical education. Following this training, he began his career by composing lighter pieces for the stage as well as songs.

## Operatic and Literary Works
In 1834, Barnett published a collection titled *Lyrical Illustrations of the Modern Poets*. During the same year, on August 25, he produced *Mountain Sylph* at the Lyceum. This work is the piece with which his name is most closely associated, as it was regarded as the first modern English opera and was received with a warm welcome. He continued his work in opera with the production of *Fair Rosamund* in 1837, followed by *Farinelli* in 1839.

## Teaching and Later Career
Barnett established a significant presence as a singing-master in Cheltenham. His expertise in vocal instruction led to the publication of two works: *Systems and Singing-masters* in 1842 and *School for the Voice* in 1844. He died on April 16, 1890.

## Family Legacy
The musical traditions of the family were continued by Barnett's nephew, John Francis Barnett (born 1837), who was the son of John's brother, Joseph Alfred (also a professor of music). John Francis Barnett was a teacher and composer who studied in Germany in 1857 and performed at a Gewandhaus concert in Leipzig in 1860. He held a queen's scholarship at the Royal Academy of Music and became an accomplished pianist.

John Francis Barnett gained recognition as a composer starting with a symphony in A minor in 1864, later producing various works for strings, pianoforte, and orchestra. His successful cantatas included *The Ancient Mariner* (1867) and *Paradise and the Peri* (1870). His most significant work, the oratorio *The Raising of Lazarus*, was written in 1873 and produced at Hereford in 1876. Additionally, he served as a professor at the Royal College of Music and the Guildhall School of Music.
