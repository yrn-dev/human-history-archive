# ASHBURTON, JOHN DUNNING

John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was an English lawyer and politician. He was born on October 18, 1731, in Ashburton, Devonshire, where he was the second son of an attorney, also named John Dunning. His early education took place at the free grammar school in his native town.

## Legal Career
Dunning began his professional training as an articled clerk to his father. At the age of nineteen, he was admitted to the Middle Temple and was subsequently called to the bar in 1756. His early career was marked by a slow start; for several years, he practiced on the western circuit without receiving a single brief.

His reputation changed in 1762 when he was hired to draft a defense for the British East India Company against grievances memorialized to the crown by the Dutch East India Company. The "masterly style" of this document brought him both emolument and reputation. In 1763, Dunning further distinguished himself as counsel for Wilkes. He established his professional standing through a powerful argument against the validity of general warrants in the case of *Leach v. Money* on June 18, 1763. Following this, his practice grew significantly, and by 1776, it is reported that he earned nearly £10,000 per annum.

## Political Career and Public Office
Dunning entered public service as the recorder of Bristol in 1766. In December 1767, he was appointed solicitor-general, a position he held until May 1770, when he retired alongside his friend Lord Shelburne. In 1771, he was granted the freedom of the city of London.

He was first elected as a member of parliament for Calne in 1768, representing the borough until his promotion to the peerage. During this time, he became a regular member of the opposition and was known for delivering many able speeches in parliament. In 1780, he successfully carried a motion by a majority of eighteen stating that the influence of the crown "had increased, was increasing, and ought to be diminished." 

Dunning was a strong opponent of pensions and the system of sinecure officers. However, he later accepted such a position himself; in 1782, upon the appointment of the marquis of Rockingham as prime minister, Dunning was named chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster, which was a rich sinecure. Around this same time, he was advanced to the peerage.

## Legacy and Connections
Dunning's professional influence extended to other legal figures; for instance, Lloyd Kenyon (later 1st Baron Kenyon) became known to attorneys after answering the cases of Dunning. 

In his hometown of Ashburton, the church of St. Andrew contains a tablet in his memory. The inscription on this tablet was written by Dr. Johnson. The title of Baron Ashburton, which Dunning held, was later taken by Alexander Baring (1st Baron Ashburton), who was the husband of Dunning's aunt.
