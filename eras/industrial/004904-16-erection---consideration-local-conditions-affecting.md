# Erection and Local Conditions

The consideration of local conditions is a critical factor in various fields of construction and planning, often serving as a controlling or governing element in the determination of design and methodology.

## Bridge Erection and Design
In the construction of bridges, local conditions frequently dictate the choice of design and the methods used for erection. The span of a bridge may be fixed by specific local requirements, such as the necessity for navigation, the requirements of the waterway, or the availability of convenient sites for piers.

### Methods of Erection
There are four primary classes of erection methods for bridges:
1. **Erection on staging or falsework**: The character of the staging is determined by available materials, including earth banks, stacks of timber, or built-up staging consisting of piles and trestles. Iron staging is also utilized for its ability to be moved and erected rapidly. A common arrangement involves timber piles placed every 20 to 30 feet to support a timber platform. In instances of deep rivers or great headway, clusters of piles or timber-braced piers may be placed at distances of 50 to 100 feet to carry steel or timber temporary trusses. For example, the Kuilenburg bridge in Holland, with a span of 492 feet, utilized a timber staging containing 5 tons of bolts and 81,000 cubic feet of timber to support a superstructure weighing 2,150 tons.
2. **Floating and raising**: The structure is floated to the site and then raised.
3. **Rolling out**: The structure is rolled out from one abutment.
4. **Member-by-member building**: The bridge is built out member by member, using the completed section as the stage for further additions.

### Economic and Structural Considerations
Engineers prioritize economy while ensuring adequate strength. While the cost of a superstructure increases as the span increases, a more expensive substructure can make a larger span economical. Generally, the most cost-effective arrangement occurs when the cost of a pier and foundation equals the cost of the superstructure of a span. For bridges with large spans, the difficulty and cost of erection become governing considerations.

The cantilever system is often the most economical and simplest method for certain positions, particularly where exceptional local conditions exist, as was the case with the Forth bridge. Cantilevers allow for construction to proceed from the piers member by member without temporary scaffolding below. This avoids the difficulties of building in deep water, saves scaffolding costs, and ensures that navigation is not interrupted. In some instances, independent spans are erected by building them as cantilevers and converting them into independent girders after completion.

## Military and Architectural Erection
In the British military system, the erection of new barrack buildings is guided by the *Barrack Synopsis* and a series of "Standard Plans." The synopsis provides particulars on authorized buildings, fittings, and the cubic contents and areas of rooms. Standard Plans are used to incorporate the best points of previous designs, avoiding the need for a fresh design for every building. These standard types are modified to suit local conditions. In these designs, sound sanitary construction and convenience of plan are prioritized over external effect; architectural impact is achieved through the balance and grouping of parts rather than expensive ornamentation.

## Historical and Cultural Erection
Local conditions and opposition have historically influenced the erection of public structures. James Burbage, an English actor, leased land at Shoreditch where he erected "The Theatre," the first of its kind in London. He was also involved in the erection of the Curtain theatre. Later, despite significant local opposition and various difficulties, he established the Blackfriars theatre near the old Dominican friary in 1596.

## Other Local Considerations
Beyond physical construction, "local conditions" apply to legal and biological contexts:
* **Legal Prosecution**: When Federal law is violated, authorities may consider referring a case to state or local jurisdiction if the local interest is more substantial than the Federal interest. Prosecutors are cautioned to remain alert to local relationships, attitudes, or conditions that might hinder a thorough prosecution by local authorities.
* **Human Differentiation**: Historically, humans subjected to different climates, enemies, and diets in various regions have undergone local modification. Populations isolated by mountains, deserts, or oceans tend to develop special characteristics adapted to their local conditions, though these are often blended back together through migration and interbreeding.
