# Benoit de Boigne

## Early Life and Military Beginnings
Benoit de Boigne, Count, was born on March 8, 1751, in Chambery, Savoy. The son of a fur merchant, Boigne began his military career in 1768 when he joined the Irish Brigade in France. His early service extended to Russia, where he entered the Russian service and was subsequently captured by the Turks.

## Service in India
Motivated by reports of the wealth of India, Boigne traveled to the region. He initially served for a short period with the East India Company before resigning to join Mahadji Sindhia in 1784. His primary objective upon joining Sindhia was to train the troops using European methods of warfare.

Boigne's military value was demonstrated during the battles of Chaksana and Lalsot, where his two battalions held the field despite the defeat of the rest of the Mahratta army by the Rajputs. In 1788, Boigne participated in the battle of Agra, where he restored the fortunes of the Mahratta and established Mahadji Sindhia as the undisputed master of Hindostan.

## Command and Later Career
Following the success at Agra, Boigne was granted command of a brigade consisting of ten infantry battalions. He led this force to victories at Merta and Patan in 1790. These achievements resulted in Boigne being appointed commander-in-chief of Sindhia's army and being permitted to raise two additional brigades of disciplined infantry. In 1793, he defeated the army of Holkar at the battle of Lakhairi.

Upon the death of Mahadji Sindhia in 1794, Boigne possessed the means to make himself the master of Hindostan; however, he chose to remain loyal to Daulat Rao Sindhia.

## Retirement and Death
In 1795, Boigne's health began to fail, leading him to resign his command. He returned to Europe the following year, possessing a fortune of L400,000. He spent the era of Napoleon's lifetime living in retirement. During the subsequent reign of Louis XVIII, he was greatly honoured. Benoit de Boigne died on June 21, 1830.
