# Émilie Madeleine Brohan

## Early Life and Education
Émilie Madeleine Brohan was born in 1833, the second daughter of Susanne Brohan. She pursued formal training in the dramatic arts at the Conservatoire, where she earned the first prize for comedy in 1850.

## Career at the Comédie Française
Following her success at the Conservatoire, Brohan was immediately engaged by the Comédie Française. Rather than debuting in a play from the theatre's existing repertoire, the management staged a new comedy by Legouvé and Scribe titled *Les Contes de la reine de Navarre* specifically for her benefit. She created the role of Marguerite in this production on September 1, 1850.

Brohan achieved immediate success due to her beauty and talent. Her rise within the company was rapid; in less than two years after her debut, she was elected *sociétaire*.

## Personal Life and Return to Stage
In 1853, Brohan married Mario Uchard. The couple was separated shortly thereafter. In 1858, she returned to the Comédie Française, where she performed in leading parts. She continued her professional activity at the theatre until her retirement in 1886.

## Notable Works and Successes
Throughout her career, Brohan was associated with a wide array of plays. While she performed works from the classical repertoire, she is notably remembered for her roles in:
* *Le Monde où l'on s'ennuie*
* *Par droit de conquête*
* *Les Deux Veuves*
* *Le Lion amoureux*

In the latter production, she portrayed the "marquise de Maupas," a role that stood as one of her greatest professional successes. Émilie Madeleine Brohan died in 1900.
