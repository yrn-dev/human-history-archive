# Jens Immanuel Baggesen

Jens Immanuel Baggesen (1764–1826) was a Danish poet known for his versatility and his fluctuating popularity within the literary circles of Denmark and Europe.

## Early Life and Education
Baggesen was born on February 15, 1764, in the seaport of Korsör. His childhood was marked by poverty and emotional distress; he is described as having been a feeble and melancholy child who attempted suicide on more than one occasion. Due to his parents' financial situation, he was sent to the office of the district clerk before the age of twelve to copy documents. Despite these early hardships, Baggesen utilized indomitable perseverance to secure an education, which eventually allowed him to enter the university of Copenhagen in 1782.

## Literary Rise and Decline
Baggesen achieved immediate success upon his first publication. His *Comical Tales* in verse—poems compared to the *Broad Grins* published by Colman the younger a decade later—made him a popular favorite by the age of twenty-one. Following this, he transitioned into serious lyrical writing. His elegance of manner, tact, and versatility enabled him to gain entry into the highest levels of society.

This period of success was interrupted in 1789 by the production of an opera titled *Holge Danske*. The work was poorly received and met with mockery, triggering a reaction against Baggesen. In a rage, he departed Denmark.

## European Travels and Later Works
Baggesen spent several years traveling through Switzerland, France, and Germany. While in Berne in 1790, he married and began writing in the German language, producing the poem *Alpenlied*. He returned to Denmark in the winter of 1790, offering the descriptive Danish poem *Labyrinth* as a peace-offering, which resulted in him being received with unbounded homage.

For the following twenty years, Baggesen lived a life of restless wandering across northern Europe, eventually establishing Paris as his nominal home. During this time, he continued to publish volumes in both German and Danish. One of his most significant German works was *Parthenais* (1803), an idyllic epos written in hexameters.

## Final Years in Copenhagen
Baggesen returned to Copenhagen in 1806. Upon his return, he found that his previous popularity had begun to wane, while a young poet named Oehlenschlager had become the preeminent poet of the era. From 1806 until 1820, Baggesen resided in Copenhagen, where he engaged in nearly constant literary feuds. These conflicts were characterized by mutual abuse, driven largely by Baggesen's determination to ensure that Oehlenschlager was not regarded as a greater poet than himself. He left Denmark for the final time in 1820.

## Geography of Korsör
The birthplace of Baggesen, Korsör, is a seaport located on the east shore of the Great Belt on the island of Zealand. As of 1901, it had a population of 6,054. The town has served as a market town since the 14th century and contains ruins of an old fortified castle from the 14th and 17th centuries. It serves as a critical transportation hub, acting as a point of arrival and departure for steam ferries to Nyborg on Fünen and maintaining regular water communication with Kiel. Its primary imports include coal and petroleum, while its chief exports are cereals, bacon, and fish.
