# Baden-Baden

## Overview and Geography
Baden-Baden (referred to as Baden to distinguish it from other locations of the same name) is a town and fashionable watering-place located in the grand-duchy of Baden, Germany. It is situated on a hill 600 feet high within the valley of the Black Forest, approximately 23 miles south by west of Karlsruhe. The town is connected to Karlsruhe via a branch of the Mannheim and Basel railway. While the town proper is located on the right bank of the Oos, the primary resorts for visitors are found on the left bank.

## Tourism and Population
The town has long been a destination for crowds of visitors from across the globe, attracted by its promenades, gardens, extensive pleasure-grounds, and the brilliance of life during the season. Annually, the number of visitors exceeds 70,000. Historically, French nationals were the prevailing nationality until the war of 1870; however, in more recent years, English, Russian, and American visitors have become more numerous.

The resident population of Baden-Baden has seen steady growth over several decades:
*   1885: 12,779
*   1895: 14,862
*   1905: 16,238

## Thermal Springs and Health
Baden-Baden is noted for its twenty-nine hot springs, which flow from the castle rock at a rate of 90 gallons per minute. The temperature of these waters varies from 37° to 54° R. (115° to 153° Fahr.). The water is distributed through the town via pipes to supply various baths.

The town features two chief bathing-establishments, which are considered the most elegant in Europe. The waters are utilized for the treatment of various internal complaints, such as uric acid and stone, as well as neuralgia, paralysis, skin diseases, and chronic gout and rheumatism.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town contains several significant buildings, including a library, a reading-room, a picture-gallery, a theatre, a *Trinkhalle* (pump-room), and a *Conversationshaus*. While public gaming-tables were once a striking feature of the town, they have since been abolished.

The town's religious and antiquarian sites include:
*   **The Parish Church:** Dating from the 15th century, this is the only building of significant antiquarian interest besides the castles; it houses the tombs of several margraves.
*   **Other Churches:** The town includes a Russian church featuring beautiful frescoes, a Lutheran church, and an English church built in the Norman architectural style.
*   **The Greek Chapel:** Located on the Michaelsberg, this chapel features a gilded dome and was erected over the tomb of a son of the Rumanian prince Michel Stourdza, who died in 1863.
