# The Upper and Lower Parts of the Tubes Between Fitting Portions

In the construction of specific large-tube boilers, particularly the "Niclausse" type used in land-based electric-light installations and various navies, the tubes are engineered with a specialized geometry to facilitate their function within the boiler elements.

## Construction of Niclausse Generating Tubes
The Niclausse boiler consists of a horizontal steam-chest with several elements arranged side by side over a fire. Each of these elements features a header of rectangular cross-section, which is fitted with two rows of seamless steel tubes. These tubes are inclined at 6 degrees to the horizontal, sloping downwards toward the back of the boiler.

The tubes are manufactured with distinct sections to ensure a precise fit within the header walls and the diaphragm. The back or lower ends of the tubes are reduced in diameter and are closed entirely by screwed cap-nuts. Conversely, the front ends are thickened through a process of upsetting, and the specific sections that fit into the diaphragm and header walls are turned to gauge.

## The Lanterne Design
A defining characteristic of these tubes is the treatment of the sections located between the fitting portions. The upper and lower parts of the tubes situated between these fitting portions are cut away, leaving only the side portions. This specific structural modification results in an end termed a "lanterne."

## Internal Circulating Tubes
To manage water circulation, a small tube made of thin sheet steel is fitted inside each generating tube. This inner circulating tube is open at its lower end. At the other end, it is secured to a smaller "lanterne" that extends only from the diaphragm to the front of the header. This smaller lanterne serves to close the front end of the generating tube. 

Under this arrangement, when the tubes are installed, only the small inner circulating tubes maintain communication with the space between the front of the header and the rest of the system.
