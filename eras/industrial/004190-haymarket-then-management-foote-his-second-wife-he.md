# The Haymarket Theatre

The Haymarket is a prominent London theatre that has served as a venue for a wide array of English actors, managers, and dramatists over several centuries. Its history is marked by various management regimes and the performances of some of the era's most noted theatrical figures.

## Early History and Management
In the early 18th century, the Haymarket was a venue where actors such as Christopher Bullock (c. 1690–1724) displayed a considerable versatility of talent. Between 1705 and 1708, the actor Barton Booth performed there alongside Betterton. 

Later in the century, the theatre was under the management of Foote. During this period, Spranger Barry (1719–1777), a British actor born in Dublin, returned to London to play at the Haymarket after an unsuccessful stint as a manager of theatres in Dublin and Cork. Another actor associated with this era was Robert Baddeley (c. 1732–1794), who had previously served as a valet and cook to Samuel Foote, known as "the English Aristophanes"; Baddeley remained at the Haymarket and Drury Lane until his death.

## The 19th Century and John Baldwin Buckstone
In the 19th century, the Haymarket was significantly influenced by John Baldwin Buckstone (1802–1879). Buckstone first joined the theatre for summer seasons in 1833 and later served as its lessee from 1853 to 1878. As manager, Buckstone produced his own plays and farces, including *Uncle Tom* and *Ellen Wareham*, and appeared as Bobby Trot in his work *Luke the Labourer*. 

Under Buckstone's management, the Haymarket hosted an admirable company that included Sothern and the Kendals. He produced works by Robertson, Planché, Tom Taylor, and W. S. Gilbert. Buckstone's daughter, Lucy Isabella Buckstone, also made her first London appearance at the Haymarket in 1875, playing Ada Ingot in *David Garrick*. Additionally, the dramatist James Kenney saw his most popular play, *Sweethearts and Wives*, produced at the Haymarket in 1823.

## The Kendals and the Bancroft Era
William Hunter Kendal (1843– ) joined the Haymarket company in 1866, performing roles ranging from Romeo to burlesque. He was joined by his wife, Margaret (Madge) Shafto Robertson, whose triumphs at the Haymarket included old English comedies and Shakespearian revivals. While Mr. Kendal played roles such as Young Marlowe, Jack Absolute, Charles Surface, and Orlando, Mrs. Kendal performed as Kate Hardcastle, Lydia Languish, Lady Teazle, and Rosalind.

In 1879, the management of the Haymarket passed to Sir Squire Bancroft (1841– ) and his wife, Effie Marie Wilton. The Bancrofts moved to the Haymarket from the Prince of Wales's theatre. Their productions at the Haymarket included Pinero's *Lords and Commons*, W. S. Gilbert's *Sweethearts*, and Sardou's *Fedora* and *Odette*, the latter of which featured Madame Modjeska. The Bancrofts retired from the theatre in 1885 after having made a considerable fortune.

## Notable Visiting Performers
The Haymarket also hosted distinguished international talent. Edwin Booth, a celebrated American actor known for his portrayal of Hamlet, visited London in 1851, 1880, and 1882. During these visits, he performed at the Haymarket theatre with brilliant success.
