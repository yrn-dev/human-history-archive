# Joseph Autran

Joseph Autran (1813–1877) was a French poet and member of the French Academy. Born in Marseilles on June 20, 1813, Autran's literary trajectory was significantly influenced by the poet Lamartine. In 1832, while Lamartine was in Marseilles during a journey to the East, Autran addressed an ode to him. This encounter led Lamartine to persuade Autran's father to support the young man's poetic ambitions, and Autran remained a faithful disciple of Lamartine from that point forward.

## Major Works and Career
Autran is best known for his work *La Mer*, first published in 1835 and later remodelled in 1852 as *Les Poemes de la mer*. This volume, along with his 1838 work *Ludibria ventis*, achieved enough success to secure Autran the position of librarian in his native town of Marseilles.

His literary output extended to various themes and forms:
*   **Rural and Peasant Life:** In 1856, he published *Vie rurale*, a series of pictures depicting peasant life. He also wrote *Epitres rustiques* (1861).
*   **Military Themes:** Inspired by the Algerian campaigns, Autran wrote verses honoring the common soldier. These include *Milianah* (1842), which describes the heroic defense of that town, and *Laboureurs et soldats* (1854).
*   **Other Poetry and Drama:** His other works include *Paroles de Salomon* (1868) and *Sonnets capricieux*. In 1848, he wrote a tragedy titled *La Fille d'Eschyle*, which was performed with great success at the Odeon.

A definitive edition of Autran's collected works was published between 1875 and 1881.

## Later Life and Legacy
Autran's contributions to literature were recognized by his election to the French Academy in 1868. He remained in his hometown of Marseilles, where he died on March 6, 1877.
