# Sir Gilbert Scott

Sir Gilbert Scott was an architect and designer responsible for the construction, restoration, and design of numerous religious and public buildings across England and Scotland. He was a native of the neighborhood of Buckingham.

## Religious Works
Sir Gilbert Scott's architectural contributions to ecclesiastical buildings include both new designs and the restoration of existing structures. In London, he provided the designs for the parish church of St Mary Abbots on High Street, which was built in 1869 in the Decorated style and features one of the loftiest spires in England.

In Scotland, Scott was involved in several projects in Dundee. He designed St Paul's Episcopal church in the Decorated Gothic style, which includes a spire 211 feet high; this church was established through the zeal of Bishop Forbes (1817-1875) and is located on the site of an old castle. Additionally, Scott performed restoration work between 1871 and 1873 on the "Old Steeple," a noble square tower 156 feet high. This tower originally served as the belfry for a church erected by David, earl of Huntingdon, in 1193 as a thank-offering for escaping a shipwreck.

In Buckingham, Scott extensively restored the church of St Peter and St Paul, an 18th-century building situated on the site of the old castle.

## Public and Commemorative Works
Beyond churches, Sir Gilbert Scott designed significant public edifices and monuments. In Dundee, he provided the primary designs for the Albert Institute. This building is considered one of the city's most important edifices, as it houses a museum, a free library, a reference library, an art gallery, and several halls.

Scott also created commemorative monuments. In Ashby-de-la-Zouch, he is credited with the creation of a Queen Eleanor cross that commemorates the countess of Loudoun.
