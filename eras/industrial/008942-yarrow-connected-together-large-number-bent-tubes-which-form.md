# Yarrow Boiler

The Yarrow boiler is a type of water-tube boiler utilized extensively in the British navy as well as several other navies. It is designed in various configurations to meet different maritime needs, ranging from large vessels to express boilers for smaller, high-power craft.

## Design and Construction
The standard Yarrow boiler consists of two lower water-chambers and one large cylindrical steam chest. These three chambers are connected by numerous straight tubes. To facilitate maintenance, most models feature manholes in the ends of the water-chamber and steam-chest to provide internal access. In smaller versions used in torpedo boats, where the water-chambers are too small for manholes, the chambers are constructed in two parts joined by a bolted joint to ensure all tube ends remain accessible.

In some designs, large pipes are installed outside the casings to connect the lower part of the steam-chest with the water-chambers, a feature intended to improve circulation.

## Tube Specifications and Arrangement
The diameter of the tubes varies based on the specific application of the boiler:
* **Large Vessels:** All tubes have an external diameter of 1-3/4 inches.
* **Large Express Boilers:** The tubes nearest to the fire on each side have a diameter of 1-1/4 inches, while the remaining tubes are 1 inch in diameter.

The tubes are arranged so that their centers form equilateral triangles. This specific spacing allows the tubes to be cleaned externally from the front of the boiler and cross-ways in two directions.

## Performance and Scale
The Yarrow boiler is available in both single-ended and double-ended configurations. Double-ended boilers are fired from both ends to achieve larger sizes. For the largest single-ended large tube boiler in use, the specifications include:
* **Steam Drum Diameter:** 4 feet 2 inches.
* **Grate Area:** 73.5 square feet.
* **Heating Surface:** 3,750 square feet.

## Express Boilers
The Yarrow is also categorized as an "express boiler," a class of water-tube boilers frequently used in cruisers and torpedo-boat destroyers. These are employed in scenarios where the maximum power is required relative to the total weight of the installation. 

The express version of the Yarrow boiler follows a construction similar to the large tube Yarrow boiler, with two primary distinctions: the tubes are smaller in diameter and they are arranged much more closely together. These boilers are designed to support a high rate of fuel combustion, which is achieved through a high degree of "forced draught."

## Comparison with Other Three-Chamber Boilers
The Yarrow boiler shares a basic three-chamber layout with other boiler types, though the tube configurations differ:
* **Normand Boiler:** Like the Yarrow, it has three chambers, but they are connected by a large number of bent tubes that form the heating surface. It also utilizes large outside circulating tubes at each end.
* **Blechynden and White-Foster Boilers:** These also utilize three chambers connected by bent tubes. In the Blechynden, tubes can be removed via small doors in the upper part of the steam-chest; in the White-Foster, they are removed through a manhole at the end of the steam-chest.
