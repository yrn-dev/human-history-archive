# Port Improvements of Buenos Aires

The port of Buenos Aires, located in the capital of Argentina, has undergone extensive development to enhance its shipping facilities and accommodate larger vessels. Situated on the west shore of the La Plata estuary, the port faces significant geographical challenges; the estuary is so shallow that vessels can only enter the docks via artificial channels that require constant dredging to remain open.

## Early Improvements at the Boca
Initial efforts to improve the port's facilities began in 1872 at the "Boca," which is the name given to the port located on the Riachuelo (also known as the Matanzas river). These early works focused on the creation of landing facilities and the dredging of a channel 12 meters in length to reach deep water. The total expenditure for these specific improvements was nearly £1,500,000.

Prior to the development of a modern port system, the limitations of the harbor were severe. Ocean-going vessels with a draught exceeding 15 feet were unable to reach the city and were forced to anchor in the outer roads, approximately 12 meters away. During this period, communication between these ships and the shore was maintained through small boats and steam tenders, which connected to carts on the beach or long landing piers.

## The Madero Docks
Because the improvements at the Boca were found to be insufficient, a more ambitious project was initiated in 1887. Based on plans executed by Sir John Hawkshaw, the city constructed the "Madero docks," which are considered the most important feature of the port. This project involved the creation of two basins and four docks situated in front of the city. The construction required the reclamation of 3 meters of shore-line and the dredging of two channels to provide access to deep water.

The infrastructure of the Madero docks includes:
* Two dry docks located in the north basin.
* 24 warehouses.
* Hydraulic cranes.
* 28 meters of railway sidings and connections.

## Costs and Financials
The financial investment in the new port works was substantial. By 1908, the total cost of construction had reached approximately £8,000,000 sterling (equivalent to $40,000,000 gold). Due to continued requirements for extension, the congress decided in September 1908 to borrow an additional £5,000,000.

Maintenance remains a constant expense due to the large amount of silt deposited by the river in the deep-water channels. In 1908, £560,000 was voted for dredging and allied purposes.

Regarding operational performance, the port's revenues in 1907 were $5,452,000 gold, while working expenses totaled $2,213,000 gold. This resulted in a profit of $3,229,000, which represented approximately 8% of the construction cost.

## Shipping and Trade Volume
The port handles a significant volume of maritime traffic. In 1907, there were 29,178 shipping entries, with an aggregate tonnage of 13,335,737 tons. The movement of merchandise during that year consisted of 4,360,000 tons of imports and 2,900,000 tons of produce exports.

## Impact on Urban Development
The construction of the new port and the reclamation of shallow water frontage have significantly altered the geography of Buenos Aires. The city's area has been greatly extended below the original banks of the estuary. While the old city is characterized by narrow streets laid out in uniform rectangular blocks parallel to the cardinal points, the new additions and suburbs do not closely follow this plan.
