# Brickwork Tools and Materials

Brickwork refers to constructions made of bricks. The process of building and shaping these structures requires a variety of specialized tools and materials depending on the hardness of the brick and the desired finish of the work.

## Tools of the Bricklayer
The bricklayer employs several implements to ensure the accuracy and stability of a construction. A trowel is used for spreading mortar, while a plumb-rule is used to maintain perpendicularity or to achieve a regular batter in the case of an inclined wall. To keep work horizontal, a spirit-level is used, often in combination with a straight-edge to test longer lengths. The gauge-rod, which has brick-courses marked upon it, is used to build up the quoins or angles; intermediate work is then kept regular using line pins fixed in the joints and a line.

For the finishing of joints, tools such as the Frenchman, pointing rule, jointer, and raker are used, with the pointing staff being held on a small board known as a hawk.

## Cutting and Shaping Bricks
The method for cutting bricks depends on the material's hardness. All cutting work is first set out using a compass, bevel, and square.

### Hard Bricks
For hard bricks, the brick is placed on a V-shaped cutting block. An incision is made with a tin saw, and the necessary portion is removed using a club-hammer and bolster. Any remaining rough parts are hacked off with a scutch, which is a small axe. For general cutting away, as well as for holes and chases, a cold chisel is used. A large trowel is sufficient for rough cutting.

### Soft Bricks
Soft bricks, including those known as malms and rubbers, are handled differently. To cut these, a frame saw equipped with a blade made of soft iron wire is used. Once cut, the face of the brick is brought to a true surface using a rubbing stone, which is a slab of Yorkshire stone.

## Types of Bricks
Bricks in Great Britain are categorized by various names based on their quality and composition:
*   **Stocks:** Sound, hard, well-burnt bricks used for ordinary purposes.
*   **Hard Stocks:** Over-burnt but sound bricks used where appearance is not a priority, such as in wall footings.
*   **Shippers:** Hard-burnt and sound bricks that have an imperfect shape; these were frequently used as ballast for ships.
*   **Rubbers (or Cutters):** Sandy bricks suitable for rubbing to shape on a stone slab and cutting with a wire saw.
*   **Grizzles:** Under-burnt bricks of fair shape and sound quality, used for inferior work not subject to heavy loads.
*   **Place-bricks:** Defective and under-burnt bricks used for temporary work.
*   **Chuffs:** Badly burnt bricks that are cracked and defective in shape.
*   **Burrs:** Vitrified lumps that ran together during burning, used for garden work and rough walling.
*   **Pressed bricks:** Moulded under hydraulic pressure and often used for facing work. These typically feature a "frog" (a hollow on one or both horizontal faces) to reduce weight and provide a key for mortar.
*   **Blue bricks:** Vitrified, heavy, and impervious bricks made chiefly in North Wales and South Staffordshire. They are used for engineering work requiring great compressional resistance, as well as for coping, channelling, and paving.
*   **Fire-bricks:** Bricks that withstand great heat for use with furnaces; they must be laid with fire-clay rather than cement or lime mortar.
*   **Glazed bricks:** These may be enamelled (dipped in colored slip before burning) for sanitary and decorative purposes, or salt-glazed (brown in color, created by throwing salt on the bricks in the kiln).
*   **Moulded bricks:** Used for plinths, labels, string courses, and cornices. These are often preferred over rubbers because they save labor on cutting and are more durable.

## Specialized Brickwork and Manufacturing
### Gauged and Rough-Cut Arches
Rough-cut arches are created by using an axe to cut bricks into a wedge form for use over windows and doors. Gauged arches, however, use specially made soft bricks that are rubbed and cut to templates to form perfectly fitting voussoirs. Gauged brickwork is set in lime putty with joints no wider than 1/32 in. To ensure stability, the sides of the voussoirs are grouted in Portland cement and gauged out hollow to create a joggle joint.

### Machine Production
Plastic bricks are made from tempered clay or shales using grinding-mills and pug-mills. Grinding-mills use rollers to crush shale or clay (if pebbles larger than a quarter inch are present). The pug-mill uses a central shaft with cutters and knives to knead the clay and force it through a delivery nose. This continuous mass of clay is pushed along an iron plate and cut by a wire cutting frame—either a series of stretched wires or a skeleton cylinder with radial wires or metal disks. Bricks produced this way are called "wire-cuts."
