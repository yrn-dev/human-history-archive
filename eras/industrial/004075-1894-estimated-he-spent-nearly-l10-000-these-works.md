# 1894

The year 1894 is associated with several distinct events and publications across the fields of sports, science, geography, and history as recorded in the archive.

## Sporting Events
In 1894, the Jockey Club oversaw the introduction of new high-stakes races at Newmarket. Specifically, the Princess of Wales's Stakes was run for the first time during the First July Meeting of that year. Additionally, the Jockey Club Stakes was held during the First October meeting. These events were part of a broader trend of "L10,000 races," a scheme originally formulated in 1884 by the managers of Sandown Park for the Eclipse Stakes. Under this system, horses were nominated shortly after birth to encourage large entries, and owners paid increasing forfeits to withdraw. While similar L10,000 race schemes were attempted at Manchester and Leicester, they lapsed; however, the races at Newmarket succeeded.

## Scientific Publications
The year 1894 marked the posthumous release of a volume of work by Joachim Barrande (1799-1883). Barrande was an Austrian geologist and palaeontologist who had spent ten years between 1840 and 1850 conducting systematic researches on the Lower Palaeozoic rocks of Bohemia. His primary work, *Systeme silurien du centre de la Boheme*, began publication in 1852 and consisted of twenty-one quarto volumes of text and plates issued during his lifetime. Following his death, two additional volumes were issued in 1887 and 1894. It is estimated that Barrande spent nearly L10,000 on these scientific works.

Despite the destruction, the city quickly regained its population. Phyöng-yang is situated on rocky heights above a region of stoneless alluvium to the east and the largest and richest plain in Korea to the west. The area is historically and commercially significant, possessing five coal-mines within ten miles and being rich in grain, cotton, silk, and iron.
