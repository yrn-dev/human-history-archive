# Oratores Attici (1838-1850)

The *Oratores Attici* (1838-1850) is a scholarly edition of the Attic orators produced through the collaboration of Johann Georg Baiter and H. Sauppe. This work is part of a broader tradition of textual criticism and philological study focused on the speakers of ancient Athens.

## Authorship and Production
The edition was co-authored by H. Sauppe and Johann Georg Baiter (1801-1877), a Swiss philologist and textual critic. Baiter was born in Zurich and educated at the university of Tuebingen before studying under Friedrich Thiersch in Munich, Georg Dissen in Goettingen, and Christian Lobeck in Koenigsberg. From 1833 to 1876, Baiter served as an *Oberlehrer* at the gymnasium in Zurich.

Baiter was recognized for his accuracy in collations and his ability to locate the best manuscript authorities. His expertise in textual criticism was applied primarily to the Attic orators and Cicero. Much of his scholarly output was collaborative; he was regarded as the "right-hand man" of the scholar Orelli. In addition to the *Oratores Attici*, Baiter and Sauppe collaborated on an edition of Lycurgus' *Leocratea* in 1834.

## Contents and Scope
The *Oratores Attici* includes significant collections of fragmentary works. Specifically, Sauppe collected 355 fragments in volume ii, pages 170-216. Of these fragments, 252 represent 127 speeches that possess a known title. Six of these speeches contain fragments that are considered comparatively large. One such fragmentary speech, *For Pherenicus*, is dated to 381 or 380 B.C., making it the latest known work of the orator Lysias.

## Context within Classical Philology
The 1838-1850 edition by Baiter and Sauppe exists within a lineage of critical editions of the Attic orators. Earlier efforts include the work of August Immanuel Bekker (1785-1871), a German philologist and critic who produced an edition of the *Oratores Attici* between 1823 and 1824. Bekker's edition was the first to be based on codex [Sigma]. Bekker, who was a professor of philosophy at the university of Berlin and a pupil of F.A. Wolf, focused exclusively on textual recension and criticism based on manuscripts.

Other notable contributions to the study of these authors include the *Scholica Hypomnemata* (1837-1862) by Jan Bake, a Dutch philologist and professor at Leiden, which consisted of essays dealing largely with the Attic orators and Cicero.

## Related Collaborative Works
The scholars involved in the *Oratores Attici* were active in other major critical projects of the mid-19th century. Johann Georg Baiter, working with Orelli and Winckelmann, produced a critical edition of Plato between 1839 and 1842, which utilized two new manuscripts to advance the text. Additionally, A. G. Winckelmann published a collection of the *Fragmenta Antisthenis* in Zurich in 1842.

Baiter also maintained a long association with Orelli on works concerning Cicero, assisting with the *Ciceronis Scholiastae* (1833) and the *Onomasticon Tullianum* (1836-1838), and taking sole responsibility for the *Fasti Consulares* and *Triumphales*.
