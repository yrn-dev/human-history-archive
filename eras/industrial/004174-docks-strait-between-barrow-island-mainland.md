# The Docks of Barrow Island

## Location and Development
The docks located in the strait between Barrow Island and the mainland are situated in Barrow-in-Furness, a seaport and borough in Lancashire, England. The town is positioned on the seaward side of a hammer-shaped peninsula in the district of Furness, located between Morecambe Bay and the estuary of the Duddon. 

Barrow Island is one of several islands in the shallow strait between the mainland and Walney Island. The island was connected to the mainland through a process of reclamation that continued until only a narrow channel remained; this remaining channel was subsequently utilized for the construction of the docks.

## Dock Infrastructure and Specifications
The primary docks in the strait were constructed in 1867 and were named the Buccleuch and Devonshire docks. These were later expanded by the addition of the Ramsden docks. These facilities have a depth of 24 feet.

The dock complex includes several specialized facilities:
* A graving dock measuring 500 feet in length.
* A depositing dock designed to accommodate vessels with a draught of 16 feet.
* Two electric cranes, each with a lifting capacity of 150 tons.

The dock authority is the Furness railway company. In addition to industrial use, the docks support passenger steamers that run to Belfast on weekdays.

## Economic Context of Barrow-in-Furness
The growth of the docks and the town of Barrow was driven primarily by the presence of pure haematite iron ore veins in the Furness district. Initially, the area served as a port for exporting this ore to the midlands and South Wales. In 1847, exports reached approximately 50,000 tons, though the population of the town at that time was only 325.

The development of the area accelerated with the opening of the first section of the Furness railway in 1846, which linked Barrow to mines near Dalton. This increase in trade led to the establishment of the iron works of Messrs Schneider & Hannay in 1859, which were later absorbed by the Barrow Haematite Steel Company in 1866. This company utilized the Bessemer process to produce a significant output of steel.

## Related Industries
While steel production was a primary driver, shipbuilding eventually surpassed the steel works in importance. The firm Vickers, Sons & Maxim maintains a yard in the town where they construct various vessels, including numerous vessels of war. Other industrial establishments in the vicinity include:
* A paper-pulp factory.
* Jute works.
* Engineering and wagon works.
* A petroleum storage establishment.
