# William John Broderip

## Early Life and Legal Career
William John Broderip (1789–1859) was an English naturalist born in Bristol on November 21, 1789. After completing his graduation at Oxford, he entered the legal profession and was called to the bar in 1817. For several years following this, he was employed in law-reporting. In 1822, Broderip was appointed as a metropolitan police magistrate, a position he held until 1856. During his tenure in this office, he served first at the Westminster court and then at the Thames police court.

## Contributions to Natural History
Despite his professional obligations in law, Broderip devoted his leisure time to the study of natural history. His efforts and writings contributed significantly to the advancement of zoology within England. He was responsible for writing the zoological articles featured in the *Penny Cyclopaedia*. 

Broderip also contributed a series of articles to *Fraser's Magazine*. These writings were later reprinted in 1848 under the title *Zoological Recreations*. He followed this publication in 1852 with another work titled *Leaves from the Note-book of a Naturalist*.

## Institutional Affiliations and Collections
Broderip was one of the founders of the Zoological Society of London. In addition to his institutional contributions, he assembled a large collection of shells. This collection was eventually acquired by the British Museum.

William John Broderip died in London on February 27, 1859.
