## Mechanical Applications of Cotters
In engineering, specifically regarding crank-axle bearings, "cotters" refer to a specific type of fastening mechanism. The standard method for fastening cranks to an axle involves the use of round cotters. These cotters feature a flat surface set at a slight angle to the axis, which allows them to function as a wedge when driven in tight. To secure the assembly, the small end of the cotter projects through the crank and is held in place by a nut.

Alternative designs exist, such as the Rudge-Whitworth crank-bracket. This specific model utilizes outward cups and is described as "cotterless." In this design, the left crank and axle are forged as a single piece, while the right crank and chain-wheel are fastened using multiple grooves and teeth, a method considered mechanically superior to the cotter type.
