# Dutreuil de Rhins

J. L. Dutreuil de Rhins was a French explorer who conducted extensive investigations in High Asia and Tibet during the late 19th century. His work contributed to the geographical understanding of Central Asia, specifically regarding the Tibetan plateau and the regions of Chinese Turkestan.

## Explorations in Tibet
Between 1891 and 1893, Dutreuil de Rhins was active in the field of Tibet. His efforts in this region were part of a broader period of exploration that included other figures such as the American traveler W.W. Rockhill, the Swede Professor Sven Hedin, and the British explorer Hamilton Bower. While several of his contemporaries attempted to enter the city of Lhasa without success, Dutreuil de Rhins focused his efforts on the broader Tibetan field, where he eventually sacrificed his life to his work.

During this visit, they encountered a site they identified as the most ancient and important city of southern Chinese Turkestan. Local inhabitants identified Yotkàn as the site of the old Capital. This discovery was significant for the study of the region's history, as the site yielded various objects and coins.

## Contributions to Geography and Literature
The explorations of Dutreuil de Rhins provided essential data that helped refine the geographical descriptions of the Kuen-lun mountain system. His work, along with that of other French explorers such as P. G. Bonvalot and F. Grenard, contributed to the revision of earlier orographic theories proposed by geographers like K. Ritter and Baron von Richthofen.

His findings and journeys were documented in several publications. In 1889, he authored *L'Asie Centrale*. His later work was compiled in the *Mission scientifique dans la haute Asie, 1890-1895*, of which the third volume was published in 1897 by F. Grenard. This publication included a map of the region that is described as being very useful for researchers.
