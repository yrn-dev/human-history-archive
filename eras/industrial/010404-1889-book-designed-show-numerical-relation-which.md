# 1889

The year 1889 is associated with various developments in sociology, agricultural standards, academic publishing, and the passing of notable figures in science and exploration.

## Sociological Research and Publications
In 1889, the English sociologist Charles Booth published the earlier portion of his research under the title *Life and Labour*. This work was later embodied in a larger study titled *Life and Labour of the People in London* (1891–1903). The objective of the book was to describe the general conditions of different classes and to demonstrate the numerical relation between comparative comfort and regular earnings, as well as the relation between poverty, depravity, and misery. 

To achieve this, Booth utilized a variety of data sources, including systematic inquiries he directed himself, information from the Charity Organization Society, and records maintained by school-board "visitors," as well as data from relieving officers. A notable feature of the work was the use of maps that employed different shades of colour to represent varying degrees of poverty on a street-by-street basis.

## Agricultural and Livestock Standards
The year 1889 marked a point of official recognition for livestock classification in England. The Hackney Horse Society had previously established a dividing line in 1883 to distinguish ponies from horses, designating any animal measuring 14 hands or under as a "pony." This standard was officially recognized by the Royal Agricultural Society in 1889, as evidenced by the prize-list for the Windsor show, which included pony classes for animals not exceeding 14 hands.

In the realm of crop production, 1889 serves as a baseline for measuring significant growth in agricultural value over the following decade. By 1899, the value of hay and forage crops had increased by more than 200% compared to 1889 levels. Similarly, the value of barley crops in 1899 showed an increase of more than 400% over the 1889 figures.

## Academic and Literary Works
Several important academic editions were published or revised in 1889. T. Fowler released an edition of Francis Bacon's *Novum Organum* (Oxford, 1889), which featured a bibliography and a full introduction regarding Bacon's philosophy in all its relations. This edition superseded a previous 1855 edition by G. W. Kitchin.

## Notable Deaths
The year 1889 saw the passing of several prominent individuals:

*   **Henry William Bristow**: An English geologist and the son of Major-General H. Bristow. He served for forty-six years with the Geological Survey, where he became the director for England and Wales in 1872 before retiring in 1888. He died in London on June 14, 1889.
*   **Sir Henry Yule**: A Colonel (R.E., C.B., K.C.S.I.) who died in 1889. His life and work were later documented in the *Dictionary of National Biography* and through memoirs by Coutts Trotter and his daughter, Amy Frances Yule.
