# Treaties of Peace and Commerce, May 1895

The month of May 1895 was marked by several significant diplomatic agreements and treaties involving Chile, Bolivia, China, and Japan, as well as various territorial adjustments within the British Empire.

## Chile and Bolivia
On May 18, 1895, a treaty was signed at Santiago between Chile and Bolivia. The stated purpose of this agreement was to strengthen the bonds of friendship between the two nations and to address the "higher necessity" of Bolivia's need for free access to the sea to ensure its future commercial prosperity and development.

Under the terms of this treaty, Chile pledged that if it acquired permanent sovereignty and dominion over the territories of Arica and Tacna—either through a direct arrangement or as a result of a plebiscite conducted under the treaty of Ancon with Peru—Chile would transfer those territories to Bolivia. In exchange for this transfer, the republic of Bolivia would pay an indemnity of $5,000,000 silver. If this cession occurred, Chile would move its own frontier from the sea up to the frontier separating that district from Bolivia, advancing north of Camerones to Vitor.

Additionally, Chile committed to using its utmost efforts, either alone or jointly with Bolivia, to obtain possession of Arica and Tacna. Should these efforts fail, Chile bound itself to provide Bolivia with $5,000,000 silver and the roadstead (*caleta*) of Vitor, or another analogous one. Supplementary protocols further specified that any port ceded must fully satisfy Bolivia's present and future commercial requirements.

On May 23, 1895, further treaties of peace and commerce were signed with Chile. Despite these agreements, the provisions regarding the cession of a seaport to Bolivia remained unfulfilled.

## China and Japan
The period of 1894–1895 was characterized by a war between Japan and China. This conflict concluded with a peace conference at Shimonoseki. China was represented by Li Hung-chang, the viceroy of Pehchili, who had been primarily responsible for the diplomacy leading to the war and whose troops had been chiefly engaged in the fighting. Japan was represented by Marquis Ito (who later became a Prince).

The resulting treaty was signed on April 17, 1895. The terms of the treaty included the following:
* **Independence:** The independence of Korea was recognized.
* **Territorial Cessions:** China ceded the islands of the Pescadores and Formosa to Japan, as well as the littoral of Manchuria located south of a line drawn from the estuary of the Liao to the mouth of the river Anping.
* **Indemnities and Occupation:** China was pledged to pay an indemnity of two hundred million taels. Pending the payment of this sum, Japan was permitted to occupy Weihaiwei.
* **Trade and Manufacturing:** The treaty secured the right for foreigners to engage in manufacturing enterprises in China and opened four new places to foreign trade.
* **Commerce and Amity:** A treaty of commerce and amity between the two empires was provided, based on the lines of the treaties China had established with Occidental powers.

## British Imperial Adjustments in 1895
The year 1895 saw several changes to the limits and administration of British territories:
* **Cape Colony:** The southern portion of Bechuanaland was annexed to the Cape Colony.
* **British East Africa:** Territory under royal charter was transferred to the crown.
* **Federated Malay States:** A treaty was established between 1874 and 1895.
* **Cape of Good Hope:** The present limits of this territory were not attained until 1895.

## Bolivian Political Context
Following the war and during the ten-year period of recovery, the presidency of Bolivia was held by three successive leaders: Dr. Pacheco (who succeeded Campero and served a full term), Dr. Aniceto Arce (who served until 1892), and Dr. Mariano Baptista. In 1896, Dr. Severe Alonso assumed the presidency. Under Dr. Alonso's tenure, diplomatic relations with Great Britain were resumed, with Senor Aramayo being sent to London as minister plenipotentiary in July 1897. This mission resulted in an extradition treaty concluded in March 1898.
