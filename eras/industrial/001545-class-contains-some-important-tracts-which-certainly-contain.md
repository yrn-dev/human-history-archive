# Minor Philosophical Tracts of Francis Bacon

The philosophical output of Francis Bacon includes a class of important tracts that, while often containing material later expanded upon in his more elaborate works, remain significant due to their distinct methods of treatment and points of view.

## Key Philosophical Works
Several specific tracts are identified as the most valuable within this class of minor writings:

*   ***The Advancement of Learning***: This work is completely integrated into the *De Augmentis* and serves as the first part of the *Instauratio*.
*   ***Valerius Terminus***: Likely composed around 1603 and possibly retouched later, this piece provides a brief, though somewhat obscure, outline of the first two parts of the *Instauratio*. It is considered important for providing insight into how Bacon's system developed in his own mind.
*   ***Temporis Partus Masculus***: A curious fragment distinguished by an arrogant and offensive style, which is unlike Bacon's other writings. The term *masculus* refers to the power of the new philosophy to bring forth fruit, potentially suggesting that previous "births of time" were imperfect or feminine. This usage aligns with a sentiment in *Letters and Life* (vi. 183), which advocates for words fit to go before deeds rather than "painted" or "flourishing" words.
*   ***Redargutio Philosophiarum***: Composed probably in 1608 or 1609, this highly finished oration contains detailed material that later appears in the *Novum Organum* (book i) regarding the *Idola Theatri*.
*   ***Cogitata et Visa***: Dating from 1607, though possibly altered in its current form, this is perhaps the most important of the minor philosophical writings. Written in sonorous and weighty Latin, it contains the substance of the first book of the *Organum*.
*   ***Descriptio Globi Intellectualis***: Positioned as an intermediate work between the *Advancement* and the *De Augmentis*, this tract details the general classification of the sciences and addresses specific minor points.
