# Tabernacles and Notable Figures of 1799

## The Tabernacle in Ancient Israelite History
According to the accounts of Moses, the tabernacle was a structure built in the wilderness for the honor of God, serving as a temple into which God would descend to be present during prayers. Moses provided specific instructions regarding its measures and construction, noting that it was designed to be portable so that the people could carry it with them when moving.

The purification of the tabernacle and the priests involved a specific apothecary's art. A sweet ointment was prepared by boiling and mixing five hundred shekels of choice myrrh, an equal amount of cassia, and half those weights of cinnamon and calamus, all wet with an hin of olive oil (a measure containing two Athenian choas or congiuses). This ointment was used to anoint the priests, the sacrifices, and the tabernacle itself. 

The tabernacle also housed a golden altar of incense, where incense was offered twice daily, once before sunrise and once at sunset. Additionally, purified oil was maintained for lamps on a sacred candlestick; three lamps provided light throughout the day before God, while the remaining lamps were lit in the evening.

## Artistic Tabernacles of the Nuremberg School
In the later history of sculpture, the German sculptor Adam Krafft (c. 1455–1507) of the Nuremberg school produced significant tabernacle works. His masterpiece is a magnificent tabernacle in the church of St Laurence, which stands 62 feet high and was created between 1493 and 1500. Krafft also constructed a great tabernacle for the Host in Ulm Cathedral, which reached a height of 80 feet and was adorned with statuettes.

## Notable Figures Born in 1799
The year 1799 saw the birth of several influential figures across various disciplines:

*   **Joachim Barrande:** Born on August 11, 1799, in Saugues, Haute Loire, Barrande was an Austrian palaeontologist and geologist. Educated at the Ecole Polytechnique in Paris, he later settled in Prague in 1831. He conducted a detailed ten-year study (1840–1850) of Lower Palaeozoic rocks in Bohemia, collecting over 3,500 species of fishes, crustacea (specifically trilobites), mollusca, brachiopoda, and graptolites. His major work, *Systeme silurien du centre de la Boheme*, consisted of twenty-one quarto volumes published between 1852 and 1881, with two additional volumes appearing posthumously in 1887 and 1894. He was awarded the Wollaston medal by the Geological Society of London in 1855.
*   **Honore de Balzac:** Born on May 20, 1799, in Tours, Balzac became a renowned French novelist. While he assumed the particle "de" after 1830, birth records indicate the family name was originally "Balssa," a name associated with small peasant proprietors or day-labourers in the parish of Canezac.
*   **August Kopisch:** Born on May 26, 1799, in Breslau, Kopisch was a German poet. Originally a student of painting at the Prague academy, a hand injury forced him to turn to literature. He was an expert swimmer who helped discover the blue grotto of Capri. He was granted a pension by Frederick William IV and named a professor in 1838. His works include *Gedichte* (1836), which featured popular pieces such as *Die Historie von Noah*, and a translation of Dante's *Divine Comedy* (1840).

## Figures Who Died in 1799
Several prominent individuals passed away in 1799:

*   **John Bacon:** A British sculptor born in 1740, Bacon died in 1799. He began his career as an apprentice in Mr Crispe's porcelain manufactory at Lambeth. He is noted for inventing a more exact instrument for "getting out the points" when transferring models to marble. His achievements included the first gold medal for sculpture from the Royal Academy in 1769 for a bas-relief of Aeneas escaping Troy, and a bust of George III for Christ Church, Oxford.
*   **Charles Edward Kilmaine:** Born in Dublin in 1751, Kilmaine was a French general. He served in the French army and the Navy, participating in the War of American Independence. He held various ranks, including lieutenant-general, and served as the commandant of Lombardy. He also temporarily served as commander-in-chief of Bonaparte's "army of England" in 1798. He died on December 15, 1799.
