# Alexander Bain

## Academic Career and the Journal Mind
Alexander Bain served as a professor at Aberdeen for twenty years. During this tenure, he authored several works, including the *Higher English Grammar*. A significant achievement of this period was his institution of the philosophical journal *Mind*. The first issue of the journal was published in January 1876, with G. Croom Robertson, a former pupil of Bain from University College, London, serving as the editor. 

Bain was a frequent contributor to *Mind*, providing numerous discussions and important articles. He personally bore all the expenses of the journal until 1891, when Robertson resigned the editorship due to ill-health and the publication passed into other hands.

## Later Works and Publications
Bain resigned his professorship in 1880 and was succeeded by William Minto, who had been one of his most brilliant pupils. Despite his resignation, Bain maintained a keen interest in thought and sought to complete a scheme of work he had mapped out in previous years.

Following his resignation, he published several notable works:
* **1882**: The *Biography of James Mill*, accompanied by *John Stuart Mill: a Criticism, with Personal Recollections*.
* **1884**: *Practical Essays*, a collection of papers and articles that had mostly appeared previously in magazines.
* **1887–1888**: A new edition of the *Rhetoric*, as well as a book titled *On Teaching English*. The latter served as an exhaustive application of rhetorical principles to the criticism of style, intended for use by teachers.
* **1894**: A revised edition of *The Senses and the Intellect*, which represents his final word on the subject of psychology.

## Final Years
The year 1894 marked the end of Bain's contributions to the journal *Mind*. He spent his final years living in privacy at Aberdeen. He married twice during his life but had no children. Alexander Bain died on September 18, 1903.
