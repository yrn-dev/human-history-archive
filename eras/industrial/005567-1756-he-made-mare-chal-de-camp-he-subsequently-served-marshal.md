# Victor François, Duc de Broglie

Victor François, Duc de Broglie (1718–1804), was a prominent French military leader and member of a noble family of Piedmontese origin that had emigrated to France in 1643. He was the son of François Marie, who held the title of comte de Broglie and died as a lieutenant-general in 1656, and the grandson of Victor Maurice, who became a marshal of France in 1724.

## Early Military Career
Victor François began his military service alongside his father at Guastalla and Parma. In 1734, he obtained a colonelcy. During the German War, he participated in the storming of Prague in 1742, an action that led to his appointment as a brigadier. He continued his service on the Rhine throughout 1744 and 1745.

## Rise to Marshal of France
In 1756, Victor François was appointed *maréchal de camp*. Following this promotion, he served in the low countries under Marshal Saxe, where he was present at Maastricht, Val, and Roucoux. By the conclusion of that war, he had attained the rank of lieutenant-general.

During the Seven Years' War, he served under several commanders, including Contades, Soubise, and d'Estrées. He was present at every battle from Hastenbeck onwards. His military career reached a peak in 1759 following his victory over Prince Ferdinand at Bergen. As a result of this success, his own sovereign granted him the rank of marshal of France, and Emperor Francis I named him a prince of the empire.

## Later Campaigns and Political Life
In 1760, Victor François won an action at Corbach, though he suffered a defeat at Vellinghausen in 1761. Following the war, he experienced a period of disgrace that removed him from active employment. He was not recalled to service until 1778, at which time he was given command of the troops intended for operations against England.

During the French Revolution, de Broglie was a determined opponent of the movement. After emigrating, he briefly commanded the "army of the princes" in 1792. He died in Münster in 1804.
