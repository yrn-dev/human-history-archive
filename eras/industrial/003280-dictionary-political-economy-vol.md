# Dictionary of Political Economy

The *Dictionary of Political Economy* is a reference work edited by Sir Robert Harry Inglis Palgrave. Published by Macmillan & Co. between 1894 and 1906, it serves as a general source for articles on banking and related economic subjects.

## Editorial Background
The work was edited by Sir Robert Harry Inglis Palgrave, F.R.S., who possessed a significant background in both banking and economic journalism. Palgrave served as the Director of Barclay & Co., Ltd., Bankers, and held the position of Editor of the *Economist* from 1871 to 1883. In addition to his work on the *Dictionary of Political Economy*, Palgrave authored *Notes on Banking in Great Britain and Ireland, Sweden, Denmark and Hamburg*.

## Content and Application
The *Dictionary of Political Economy* is utilized as a resource for understanding economic crises and banking stability. For instance, the first volume of the dictionary contains articles on "Crises" that provide context for historical financial events. 

These entries are relevant to the analysis of banking reserves and the management of financial pressure. An example of such a crisis occurred in May 1866, when £4,000,000 left a bank in a single day in coin and notes, reducing the bank's reserve to £415,000 by June 1st. During this period, the bank rate was raised to 10%. In contrast, the crisis of October 1890 was less severe due to the actions of the bank's directors and governor, Mr. Lidderdale, who imported £3,000,000 in gold from Paris, ensuring the reserve never fell below £10,000,000.
