# Barmen

Barmen is a town located in Germany within the governmental district of Duesseldorf and the Prussian Rhine province. The town is situated in a narrow valley along the river Wupper, stretching for approximately four miles. Within its municipal boundaries, the river is crossed by twenty bridges, and the town is surrounded by high wooded hills. Barmen is virtually one town with Elberfeld, which lies immediately to its west. The two are connected by a suspended trolley line over the Wupper's bed, as well as by tramway and railway.

## Demographics and Infrastructure
The population of Barmen has seen significant growth over the 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1816, the population was 19,030; by 1890, it had increased to 116,144, and by 1905, it reached 156,148. The town is served by the main railway line connecting Berlin to Aix-la-Chapelle.

The town is divided into three primary districts: Lower, Middle, and Upper Barmen. Its civic infrastructure includes a stately modern town hall and an exchange. Educational and religious facilities are numerous, including two Roman Catholic churches, nine Evangelical churches, a missionary theological seminary, and academies specializing in engineering, textile industry, and technical science, alongside several high-grade public schools.

## Culture and Monuments
Barmen possesses various cultural institutions, including a theatre, a picture-gallery, and an ethnographical museum. The town also features a Hall of Fame (_Ruhmeshalle_) containing statues of Frederick III and William I, both emperors. 

Public monuments are prevalent throughout the town. These include a monument to Bismarck, a commemorative monument for the Franco-German War of 1870-71, and a monument to the poet Emil Rittershaus (1834-1897), who was a native of Barmen. To the south of the town, the hills are utilized as public grounds, featuring numerous villas, a tower providing an extensive view, and a health resort.

## Industry and Commerce
Barmen is recognized as one of Germany's most important manufacturing centres, though its rapid commercial development only began at the start of the 19th century. It serves as the chief seat of ribbon weaving in Germany. The town's diverse manufacturing output includes:
*   **Textiles:** Lace, thread, braids, cloth goods, cotton, and silks.
*   **Hardware and Goods:** Steel wares, plated goods, machinery, and carpets.
*   **Buttons:** An industry employing approximately 15,000 hands.

Additionally, the town is home to extensive breweries, chemical works, soap works, and potteries. It is also known for its print-fields, bleaching-fields, and dyeworks, the latter of which are famous for Turkey-red. Barmen maintains a considerable export trade, particularly with the United States.

## History
While Barmen was mentioned in chronicles as early as the 11th century, it did not receive civic rights until 1808, the year it was formed into a municipality.
