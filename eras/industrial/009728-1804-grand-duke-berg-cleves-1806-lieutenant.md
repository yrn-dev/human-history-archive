# Joachim Murat

## Career and Titles
Joachim Murat was a prominent French military and political figure who rose to several high-ranking positions during the Napoleonic era. In 1804, he achieved the rank of marshal of France. His political ascent continued in 1806, when he was named the grand duke of Berg and of Cleves. 

By 1808, Murat's roles expanded further; he served as the lieutenant of the emperor in Spain. Later that same year, in the early summer, he became the king of Naples. In addition to these titles, he also served as the governor of Paris.

## Marriage and Family
Murat was married to Maria Annunziata Caroline, who was born in Ajaccio on March 25, 1782. Caroline is described as having an ambitious and intriguing nature, which she used to advance her husband's interests. 

The couple's relationship with Napoleon eventually became strained, a situation exacerbated by Caroline's displeasure regarding the distance between Paris and the capital of Naples. This tension led Caroline to associate herself with the equivocal movements of her husband during the period of 1814–1815.

## Later Life and Death
The end of Murat's life was marked by tragedy. He met his end at Pizzo on October 13, 1815. Prior to this event, Caroline had retired to Austrian territory, where she was placed under some measure of restraint. She eventually lived in Trieste alongside her sister, Elisa, until her death on May 18, 1839.

## Regional Impact: The Grand Duchy of Berg
During the period when Murat held the title of grand duke of Berg, the region saw significant administrative changes. For example, the town of Dortmund, which had lost its rights as a free town in 1803 and been annexed to Nassau, was occupied by the French in 1806. In 1808, Napoleon made Dortmund over to the grand duke of Berg. Under this administration, Dortmund became the chief town of the department of Ruhr.
