# Ellesmere

The name Ellesmere is associated with several figures of historical significance in English law and nobility, as well as the management of industrial infrastructure in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Thomas Egerton, Baron Ellesmere
Sir Thomas Egerton (1540–1617) served as Lord Keeper to Queen Elizabeth and later as Lord Chancellor to James I. He was created Baron of Ellesmere in 1603 and was subsequently created Viscount Brackley in 1616. In the early part of 1616, while Baron Ellesmere was dangerously ill, Francis Bacon wrote to the king proposing himself for the office of lord chancellor should it become vacant. Although Bacon received a promise of the reversion of the office, Ellesmere recovered, and the position remained filled. 

Later historical records attribute the work *Observations concerning the Office of Lord Chancellor* (1651) to Lord Chancellor Ellesmere.

## The Earldom of Ellesmere and the Bridgewater Trust
The title of the Earl of Ellesmere is linked to the legacy of Francis Egerton, the 3rd Duke of Bridgewater (1736–1803), who is recognized as the originator of British inland navigation. Upon the Duke's death on March 8, 1803, his ducal title became extinct, while the earldom of Bridgewater passed to his cousin, John William Egerton, the 7th earl.

The 3rd Duke of Bridgewater devised his estates and canals on trust. The first beneficiary of this trust was his nephew, the Marquess of Stafford (who later became the first Duke of Sutherland). The next beneficiary was the Marquess's son, Francis Leveson Gower, who later became the first Earl of Ellesmere, and his issue.

To extend the duration of the trust, a legal rule was employed allowing property to be settled for the duration of lives in being plus twenty-one years. The trust was established by selecting a large number of persons connected to the duke and their living issue, as well as peers who had taken their seats in the House of Lords on or before the date of the duke's death. While the last of these peers died in 1857, one of the commoners included in the trust survived until October 19, 1883. Consequently, the trust did not expire until October 19, 1903, at which point the entire property passed under the undivided control of the Earl of Ellesmere.

## The Bridgewater Canals
The canals associated with the Bridgewater trust underwent several transfers of ownership during the late 19th century. In 1872, the canals were transferred to the Bridgewater Navigation Company. Subsequently, in 1887, the Bridgewater Navigation Company sold the canals to the Manchester Ship Canal Company.
