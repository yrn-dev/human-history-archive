# Dwarf

The term "dwarf" is generally used to describe an individual of a race of normal stature who is extraordinarily under-sized. While dwarfishness is often accompanied by a disproportion of the limbs, many dwarfs throughout history have been singularly graceful and well formed.

## Mythology and Early History
In Scandinavian mythology, the word connoted deformity and smallness, referring to goblins and elfins believed to be kings of mines and metals who lived in the bowels of the earth or on mountains. In the *Heimskringla*, the dwarf Annar is identified as the husband of Night and the father of Earth.

From the earliest historic times, kings and the wealthy competed to obtain dwarfs as attendants. Members of the tiny Akka race from Equatorial Africa were valued at the courts of the early dynasties of the Pharaohs. In ancient Greece, the poet and grammarian Philetas of Cos (circa 330 B.C.), who tutored Ptolemy Philadelphus, was allegedly so tiny that he wore leaden shoes to prevent being blown away. The Romans used the terms *pumilo* or *nanus* to describe both natural and unnatural dwarfs. Some practiced artificial dwarfing, with reported recipes including the anointing of the backbone with the grease of dormice, bats, and moles. Julia, the niece of Augustus, had a freed-maid named Andromeda and a dwarf named Coropas, both of whom measured 2 ft. 4 in. high.

## European Court Dwarfs
Dwarfs were frequently found in European courts. In Spain, the most famous were the hunchbacks of Philip IV, whose features were captured by Velazquez. In Poland, King Stanislas owned Nicholas Ferry (known as Bebe), who measured 2 ft. 9 in. and died in 1764 at age 23.

In England, tradition mentions a Tom Thumb in the court of King Arthur, and some allege a prototype lived at the court of King Edgar. Authenticated English dwarfs include John Jarvis (2 ft. high), a page to Queen Mary I, and Xit, who served Edward VI. Jeffery Hudson (1619-1682), the son of a butcher, was adopted by Henrietta Maria after he stepped out of a pie at a dinner for Charles I and his queen. Hudson, who measured 18 in. at age nine, later served as a captain of horse during the Civil War. After being captured by Turkish pirates, he claimed the suffering caused him to grow; by his thirtieth year, he reached 3 ft. 9 in.

Other dwarfs of Henrietta Maria included Richard Gibson and his wife Anne. The couple together measured just over 7 ft. Gibson served as a drawing-master to the daughters of James II and a miniature painter to Charles I. Later English court dwarfs included Coppernin, who served the princess Augusta of Wales, and a retainer kept by the author Mr. Beckford. In earlier eras, it was custom for noble families to consider a dwarf a "necessity" at dinners, where they were sometimes flung from one guest to another.

## Public Exhibitions and Notable Individuals
In later periods, dwarfs were frequently exhibited for the public. One of the most celebrated in England was the Pole, Borulwaski (1739-1837). A scholar and wit, Borulwaski measured 17 in. at age six and reached 39 in. by age thirty. 

Another notable figure was Richebourg, who died in Paris in 1858 at age 90. Measuring only 23 in. high, he served the Orleans family and was reportedly used during the Revolution to carry dangerous despatches into and out of Paris by being carried in a nurse's arms as an infant.

In the 19th century, Phineas Taylor Barnum, an American showman, achieved a special hit in 1842 by exhibiting Charles Stratton, known as "General Tom Thumb." Barnum toured with Stratton in England in 1844. Stratton, who was 31 in. high at age twenty-five, achieved extraordinary success at the Egyptian Hall in London. This success had a tragic impact on the painter Haydon; while the public rushed to see the dwarf, Haydon's work "The Banishment of Aristides" drew very little interest (L7, 13s. compared to Stratton's L600 in the first week), leading the artist to commit suicide. Stratton later married Lavinia Warren in 1863 and died on July 15, 1883.

Other exhibited dwarfs include:
* **Che-mah**: A Chinese man who appeared in London in 1880, measuring 25 in. high at age 42.
* **Mary Jane Youngman**: An Australian known as the "dwarf-giantess," who at age fifteen was 35 in. high but measured 4 ft. 3 in. round the waist.
* **Aztec dwarfs**: A pair exhibited in London in 1853 who married in 1867 in a ceremony where the bride's robes cost L2000.
* **George Prout (1774-1851)**: A messenger at the Houses of Parliament who was less than 3 ft. high.
* **Other individuals**: Don Francisco Hidalgo (29 in.), Jan Hannema (28 in.), and Signor Hervio Nano, who performed at the Olympic Theatre in 1843.
