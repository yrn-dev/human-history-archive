# Irwell at Barton

The crossing of the river Irwell at Barton is noted in historical engineering for the construction of a famous aqueduct designed to carry a canal at an elevation of 39 feet. This structure was a crowning triumph for the English engineer James Brindley and served as a critical component of the navigable canal connecting Worsley to Manchester.

## The Worsley to Manchester Canal
The canal from Worsley to Manchester was projected by Francis Egerton, the duke of Bridgewater, who sought to improve the outlets for coal obtained from his estates. With the exception of the Sankey canal, this project was the first great undertaking of its kind executed in modern times in Great Britain. 

The construction of the waterway was carried out by James Brindley. Despite the presence of great difficulties, Brindley's genius allowed the project to be completed. The success of this canal was significant enough to encourage similar projects in the future.

## James Brindley
James Brindley (1716–1772) was born in Thornsett, Derbyshire, to parents of humble circumstances and received little or no formal education. He began his professional life as an apprentice to a millwright near Macclesfield at age seventeen, later establishing himself as a wheelwright at Leek. He became recognized for his skill in repairing machinery and his mechanical ingenuity.

Prior to his work for the duke of Bridgewater, Brindley designed an engine in 1752 for draining coal-pits at Clifton in Lancashire and completed machinery for a silk-mill at Congleton in 1755. Brindley was an enthusiastic believer in the utility of canals, once reportedly stating that the use of navigable rivers was "to feed canals."

Throughout his career, Brindley laid out over 360 miles of canals. Following the success of the Worsley to Manchester work, he extended the project to the Mersey at Runcorn. He also planned the Oxford, Chesterfield, and Staffordshire and Worcestershire canals, and nearly completed the "Grand Trunk Canal," which connected the Mersey with the Humber and Trent.

## The Duke of Bridgewater's Contributions
Francis Egerton succeeded to the dukedom at age twelve. After a period of personal instability and the breaking off of an engagement to the duchess of Hamilton, the duke retired to his estate at Worsley and devoted himself to the creation of canals.

Following the completion of the Worsley to Manchester canal, the duke undertook a more ambitious project in 1762, obtaining parliamentary powers to create an improved waterway between Manchester and Liverpool. This project faced formidable obstacles, including the necessity of carrying the canal over Sale Moor Moss. To fund the project, the duke displayed significant self-denial, at one point reducing his personal expenses to £400 a year when his financial resources were nearly exhausted. Both the Worsley and the Liverpool and Manchester canals were completed by the time the duke was thirty-six years old.
