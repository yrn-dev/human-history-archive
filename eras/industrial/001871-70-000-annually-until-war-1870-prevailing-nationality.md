# Baden-Baden

## Overview and Geography
Baden-Baden (referred to as Baden to distinguish it from other locations of the same name) is a town and fashionable watering-place located in the grand-duchy of Baden, Germany. It is situated on a hill 600 feet high within the valley of the Black Forest, approximately 23 miles south by west of Karlsruhe. The town is connected to Karlsruhe via a branch of the Mannheim and Basel railway. The town proper is located on the right bank of the Oos, though the primary resorts for visitors are situated on the left bank.

## Tourism and Demographics
The town's gardens, promenades, extensive pleasure-grounds, and the brilliance of life during the season have long attracted visitors from across the world. The number of annual visitors exceeds 70,000. Prior to the war of 1870, the prevailing nationality of these visitors was French; however, in more recent years, English, Russian, and American visitors have become more numerous.

The resident population of the town has grown over several decades, recorded at 12,779 in 1885, 14,862 in 1895, and 16,238 in 1905.

## Thermal Springs and Health
Baden-Baden is known for its hot springs, which number twenty-nine. These waters flow from the castle rock at a rate of 90 gallons per minute and are distributed through the town via pipes to supply various baths. The temperature of the springs ranges from 37° to 54° R. (115° to 153° Fahr.). 

The town features two chief bathing-establishments, which are considered the most elegant in Europe. The waters are utilized for the treatment of various internal complaints, such as uric acid and stone, as well as neuralgia, paralysis, skin diseases, chronic gout, and chronic rheumatism.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town contains several significant buildings, including a library, a reading-room, a theatre, a picture-gallery, a *Trinkhalle* (pump-room), and a *Conversationshaus*. While public gaming-tables were once a striking feature of the town, they have since been abolished.

The town's religious architecture includes:
* A parish church dating from the 15th century, which houses the tombs of several margraves.
* A Lutheran church.
* An English church built in the Norman style of architecture.
* A Russian church featuring beautiful frescoes.
* A Greek chapel on the Michaelsberg with a gilded dome, erected over the tomb of a son of the Rumanian prince Michel Stourdza, who died in 1863.

Aside from the castles, the parish church is the only building of significant antiquarian interest.
