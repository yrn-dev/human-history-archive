# BONALD, LOUIS GABRIEL AMBROISE, VICOMTE DE (1754-1840)

Louis Gabriel Ambroise, Vicomte de Bonald, was a French politician and philosopher. Born on October 2, 1754, at Le Monna, located near Millau in Aveyron, he became known for his highly conservative views and his contributions to political and social theory.

## Political Career and Public Life
Bonald's public life was heavily influenced by his opposition to the principles of the French Revolution. In 1791, he emigrated from France and joined the army of the prince of Conde. Following this period, he settled in Heidelberg. Upon his eventual return to France, he was viewed as an object of suspicion and was forced to live in retirement.

His return to public prominence began in 1806, when he collaborated with Fievee and Chateaubriand in the management of the *Mercure de France*. In 1808, he was appointed as a councillor of the Imperial University, an institution he had frequently attacked.

Following the restoration, Bonald's political influence grew. He served as a member of the council of public instruction and sat as a deputy in the chamber from 1815 to 1822. During his tenure in the chamber, his speeches were characterized by extreme conservatism, and he went so far as to advocate for literary censorship. In 1822, he was appointed minister of state and presided over the censorship commission. He was made a peer in 1823, though he lost this dignity in 1830 after refusing to take the oath. He had also been a member of the Academy since 1816. After 1830, he withdrew from public affairs and retired to his seat at Le Monna, where he died on November 23, 1840.

## Philosophical Works and Style
Bonald is noted for a writing style that is described as pure, vigorous, and ornate, yet remarkably fine. While he is credited with scattering many fruitful thoughts throughout his writings, critics have suggested that his system does not truly deserve the name of a philosophy, characterizing him as a dilettante in abstract thought. His strength was found in the sincerity and vigour of his statements rather than in the cogency of his reasoning.

His first major work, the highly conservative *Theorie du pouvoir politique et religieux* (published in three volumes in 1796), was condemned by the Directory. Other significant publications include:
* *Essai analytique sur les lois naturelles de l'ordre social* (1800)
* *Du divorce considere au XIXe siecle* (1801)
* *Legislation primitive* (1802)
* *Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales* (two volumes, 1818)
* *Melanges litteraires et politiques, demonstration philosophique du principe constitutif de la societe* (1819, 1852)

His works were first collected in a 12-volume edition between 1817 and 1819, with a later three-volume edition published by the Abbe Migne in 1859.

## Family
The Vicomte de Bonald had four sons:
* **Victor de Bonald (1780-1871):** He followed his father into exile and later served as the rector of the academy of Montpellier after the restoration. He lost this position during the Hundred Days but regained it at the second restoration, eventually resigning in 1830. His writings include *Moise et les geologues modernes* (1835) and *Des vrais principes opposes aux erreurs du XIXe siecle* (1833), as well as a biography of his father.
* **Louis Jacques Maurice (1787-1870):** A cardinal (as of 1841) who was condemned by the council of state for a pastoral letter that attacked the *Manuel de droit ecclesiastique* by Dupin the elder. In 1851, he advocated in the senate for the use of force of arms to maintain the temporal power of Rome. In 1848, he held a memorial service for those who fell in defense of religious and civil liberty.
* **Henri (d. 1846):** A contributor to legitimist journals.
* **Rene:** Served as the interim prefect of Aveyron in 1817.
