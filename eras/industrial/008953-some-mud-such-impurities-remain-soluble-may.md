# Impurities and Solubility

In various scientific and industrial contexts, the presence of impurities affects the physical properties of substances, their chemical behavior, and the methods required for their removal. These impurities may manifest as soluble substances or as precipitates, such as mud and scale.

## Boiler Water and Water-Softening
In boiler systems, impurities found in feed-water do not evaporate with the steam and consequently become concentrated within the boiler water. These impurities typically precipitate in two forms: as scale on heating surfaces or as mud. While scale requires periodical cleaning for removal, some of the mud and soluble impurities can be removed using blow-off cocks.

The accumulation of incrustations on heating surfaces is hazardous; it reduces boiler efficiency by obstructing heat transmission through tubes and plates and can lead to the weakening of plates due to overheating. To mitigate this, boilers such as the Cornish, Galloway, and Lancashire types are used when feed-water is very impure, as they allow for easier cleaning than water-tube or multitubular boilers.

Alternatively, feed-water may be purified via a "softener." Most common impurities in feed-water are magnesia or lime salts, which are soluble in cold water but less so in hot water. In a softener, chemical reagents are mixed with measured quantities of feed-water while the temperature is raised using other means or exhaust steam. This process precipitates most impurities and converts some of the remainder into more soluble salts that stay in solution until they are blown out. The water is filtered before entering the boiler.

## Chemical and Mineral Impurities
Impurities play a significant role in the composition of minerals and chemical compounds:

*   **Marbles:** Crystalline marbles often contain minerals that represent impurities in the original limestone. These include alumina (representing clay admixtures), silicates (derived from infiltrated siliceous deposits or sand), and iron (from hematite or limonite). If the original limestone was highly impure, carbonic acid may be replaced by silica, resulting in a hard, flinty calc-silicate rock.
*   **Brick Clays:** Ordinary brick clays vary in composition and often require the addition of sand or other clays to be suitable for brickmaking. Oxide of iron, present in amounts from 2% to 10%, is a critical impurity that determines the hardness, durability, and color of burnt bricks, ranging from light buff to chocolate.
*   **Chromates:** Alkaline chromates are soluble in water, whereas those of most other metals are insoluble.

## Solubility and Freezing Points
In the study of aqueous solutions and metallic alloys, impurities such as sugar or salt typically lower the freezing point of the solvent. Crystallization does not occur until the temperature falls below the normal freezing point of the pure solvent, with the depression of the freezing point being nearly proportional to the solution's concentration.

As freezing begins, the solvent generally separates in a pure state, increasing the strength of the remaining solution and causing the temperature to continue falling. This relationship is represented by a freezing point curve. If a strong solution is cooled, it may reach a "saturated" point where the solute separates out; this relationship is described by a solubility curve.

Additionally, some soluble salts form hydrates, where the dissolved substance combines with solvent molecules. These hydrates have separate branches of the solubility curve. The most stable hydrate at any given temperature is the one with the least solubility. For example, ZnSO4 typically deposits as a heptahydrate at ordinary temperatures, but converts to a hexahydrate above 39°C and a monohydrate at approximately 80°C.

## Purification of Resins
Dragon's blood is a red-coloured resin obtained from plants such as *Calamus draco* and *Dracaena cinnabari*. The finest variety, known as *jernang* or *djernang*, is produced by gathering the fruits, sifting out impurities, and melting the resin through boiling water or exposure to the sun. This purified resin is then moulded into quills or sticks. An inferior, impurer version is extracted by boiling the fruits.
