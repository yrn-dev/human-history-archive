# The Yarrow Boiler

The Yarrow boiler is a type of water-tube boiler utilized extensively by the British navy as well as several other navies. It is designed to facilitate the generation of steam through a system of chambers and tubes.

## Design and Construction
The basic structure of the Yarrow boiler consists of two lower water-chambers and one large cylindrical steam chest. These three chambers are connected by a series of numerous straight tubes. The arrangement of these tubes is specific; their centers form equilateral triangles. This spacing is intentional, allowing the tubes to be cleaned externally from both the front of the boiler and cross-ways in two directions.

In certain models, the circulation of water is improved by the addition of large pipes located outside the casings, which connect the water-chambers to the lower part of the steam-chest.

## Tube Specifications and Variations
The diameter of the tubes varies depending on the intended use and size of the vessel:
* **Large Vessels:** All tubes used in these boilers have an external diameter of 1-3/4 inches.
* **Large Express Boilers:** These are a specialized class of water-tube boilers used in cruisers and torpedo-boat destroyers where maximum power is required relative to the total weight of the installation. In these versions, the tubes are smaller in diameter and more closely arranged than in standard Yarrow boilers. Specifically, the two rows of tubes nearest to the fire on each side have a diameter of 1-1/4 inches, while the remaining tubes are 1 inch in diameter.

## Dimensions and Access
The largest single-ended large tube boiler in use features a steam drum with a diameter of 4 feet 2 inches, a heating surface of 3,750 square feet, and a grate area of 73.5 square feet. Larger double-ended boilers also exist, which are fired from both ends.

Maintenance access is typically provided via manholes located in the water-chamber ends and the steam-chest. However, in smaller sizes fitted into torpedo boats, the water-chambers are too small for manholes. In these instances, the water-chambers are constructed in two parts joined by a bolted joint, ensuring that all tube ends remain accessible.

## Operational Characteristics
As an express boiler, the Yarrow is designed to support a high rate of fuel combustion. This is achieved through a high degree of "forced draught." 

Like all boilers, the Yarrow requires specific mountings and accessories to function. The water level must be maintained above the highest part of the heating surfaces. This level is monitored using three small try-cocks (positioned above, below, and at the proper water level) and a glass water-gauge consisting of a glass tube and three cocks. The gauge includes two cocks communicating with the boiler and a third blow-out cock used for testing and cleaning.

Feeding the boiler is managed by an injector, an independent steam-pump, or a pump driven by the main engine. The feed-water enters through a check-valve, which is kept on its seating by boiler pressure when the pump is inactive; the lift of this valve is regulated by a hand-wheel and screw. While some shipboard water-tube boilers use a float-actuated mechanism for automatic feeding regulation, most rely on hand regulation.
