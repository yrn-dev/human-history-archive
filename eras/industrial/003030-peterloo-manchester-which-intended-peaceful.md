# Peterloo

## Overview of the Event
The event known as Peterloo took place in 1819 in Manchester. It was organized as a peaceful gathering of people who intended to petition for the repeal of the Corn Law and for Parliamentary reform. Despite the peaceful intentions of the assembly, the event ended in a massacre.

## Samuel Bamford and the Aftermath
Samuel Bamford (1788–1872), an English labour politician and weaver born near Middleton, Lancashire, was a participant in the 1819 meeting. Bamford was a stalwart weaver who generally opposed movements involving physical force and sought to restrain the violent resistance to trade oppression that was common at the time. 

Despite his opposition to violence, Bamford attended the meeting at Peterloo and spoke there. Following the event, he was arrested for a breach of the law, convicted, and sentenced to twelve months' imprisonment. Bamford later documented the conditions of the working classes in the years following the battle of Waterloo in his work, *Passages in the Life of a Radical* (1840–1844).
