# BALAGHAT

Balaghat, a name meaning "above the ghats or passes" (the highlands), is a district of British India located in the Nagpur division of the Central Provinces. The administrative headquarters for the district are situated in the town of Burha. The district encompasses an area of 3,132 sq. m. and forms the eastern portion of the central plateau that divides the province from east to west.

## Geography and Topography
The district is geographically divided into three distinct regions:

1.  **The Southern Lowlands:** This area consists of a slightly undulating plain that is comparatively well drained and cultivated. It is served by the Son, Ghisri, Deo, Bagh, and Wainganga rivers.
2.  **The Mau Taluka:** This is a long, narrow, and irregular-shaped lowland tract lying between the Wainganga river and the hills. It is intersected by peaks and hill ranges covered in dense jungle, generally running from north to south.
3.  **The Lofty Plateau:** This region contains the Raigarh Bichhia tract and consists of irregular ranges of hills running generally from east to west, broken into numerous valleys.

The highest elevations in the district include the Bhainsaghat range at approximately 3,000 feet above the sea, Tepagarh hill at about 2,600 feet, and peaks above Lanji reaching 2,300 or 2,500 feet.

## Hydrography
The primary river system in the district is the Wainganga and its tributaries, which include the Uskal, Nahra, and Bagh. Other smaller streams include the Mahkara and the Masmar. Additionally, the upper plateau is drained by the Jamunia, Halon, and Banjar, which are tributaries of the Nerbudda.

## History and Population
Formerly known as the Raigarh Bichhia tract, the highlands remained neglected and desolate until 1866. At that time, the district of Balaghat was formed, opening the country to enterprising and industrious peasantry from the Wainganga valley. In the middle of the 19th century, the upper part of the district was considered an impenetrable waste; around this period, the first villages on the Paraswara plateau were established by an individual named Lachhman Naik.

Evidence of an earlier, disappeared civilization exists in the form of a handsome Buddhist temple made of cut stone, which dates to a remote period before historic times.

As of 1901, the population of the district was 326,521. This figure represented a 15% decrease over the preceding decade, a decline attributed to the effects of famine. A large portion of the district's area remains covered with vegetation.

## Regional Context
The district of Balaghat serves as the apex of the irregular triangular area—approximately 100,000 sq. m.—where the Indo-Aryan language Marathi is spoken. This linguistic region extends from Balaghat in the Central Provinces to the western coast of the peninsula, stretching from Karwar in the south to Daman on the Gulf of Cambay in the north.
