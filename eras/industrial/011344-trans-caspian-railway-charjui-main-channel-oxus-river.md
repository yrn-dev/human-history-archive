# Trans-Caspian Railway at Charjui and the Oxus River

The Trans-Caspian railway is a significant infrastructure project that bridged the Trans-Caspian deserts. One of its key crossing points is located at Charjui, situated on the Oxus river.

## Construction and Economic Impact
The Trans-Caspian railway was developed to facilitate movement across the region, with a specific section built between 1886 and 1888 extending from Charjui on the Oxus to Kati-kurgan. The construction of this railway greatly promoted trade within the country. 

The region's economy relies on irrigated lowlands where wheat, rice, and other cereals are cultivated for export to the highlands. Cotton is widely grown and exported, and the area also produces silk, flax, hemp, wine, tobacco, and fruits. Manufacturing activities include the production of felt, woollen cloth, silks, and cotton, as well as the creation of pottery, various oils, cutlery, weapons, boots, and saddles. Additionally, gold, iron, salt, and copper are extracted from the earth.

The railway has streamlined the exchange of goods; exports to Russia primarily consist of carpets, fruits, lamb-skins, silk, and raw cotton. In return, manufactured goods and sugar are imported. Imports from India include indigo, tea, shawls, and cottons. In areas where the railway does not reach, particularly in hilly tracts, goods are transported via donkeys, horses, or camels due to a lack of roads.

## The Oxus as a Political Boundary
The main channel of the Oxus river serves as a critical geopolitical marker. Following the work of the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886, a boundary was established between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). 

At Khamiab, the boundary joins the Oxus; from this point, the main channel of the river becomes the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia. This boundary continues from Khamiab—which is located approximately 150 miles above the crossing point of the Russian Trans-Caspian railway at Charjui—to the river's source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## Geographical and Scientific Observations
The Oxus valley has been the subject of various scientific and historical observations. While tigers are found on the southern and western shores of the Caspian, they do not appear to exist in the Oxus valley. However, historical accounts mention the presence of lions in the region, including at Kila’chap on the Oxus and in the plains between the Oxus and Kunduz.

Russian scientists have investigated the depression westward of the Aral and Caspian basins to determine the historical connection between these bodies of water. Earlier theories suggested an ancient connection between the Oxus and the Caspian. However, more recent hypotheses suggest that the Caspian Sea extended eastwards into Trans-Caspian territory during the post-Pleiocene age. This theory is supported by the discovery of shells (currently living in the Caspian) found 100 miles inland at altitudes between 140 and 280 feet above the current Caspian level. Furthermore, the Kara-kum sands appear to be a purely marine formation, showing no fluviatile deposits that would indicate they were delta deposits of the Oxus.

## Regional Context
The Oxus is part of a broader network of waterways and highlands. To the west, the Hari Rud valley provides a narrow passage between the northern and southern sections of the continent that avoids formidable mountain passes, a fact of significance for the development of Asiatic railways. To the east, the Oxus continues toward the Pamirs. In the province of Badakshan, the northern boundary follows the Oxus stream under the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, separating the province from Russian territory.
