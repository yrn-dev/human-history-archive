# Midland Railway and Associated Locations

The Midland railway and its various branches and associated lines serve numerous towns and urban districts across England and Ireland, facilitating trade, industry, and residential growth.

## Locations in Ireland
In County Antrim, Ireland, the Northern Counties (Midland) railway connects several key settlements. Ballymena, located on the Braid (an affluent of the Maine), is situated 33 miles north-north-west of Belfast on this line. The town features branch lines extending to Parkmore on the east coast and to Larne. Ballymoney, a market town 53 miles north-north-west of Belfast via the Northern Counties (Midland) railway, serves as a junction where the Ballycastle railway joins the main line. Ballymoney's urban district had a population of 2,952 in 1901.

Ballycastle, a seaport and watering-place on the north coast of County Antrim, is linked to the Northern Counties (Midland) railway at Ballymoney through the Ballycastle light railway. Additionally, in County Leitrim, the county town of Carrick-on-Shannon is located on the Sligo branch of the Midland Great Western railway, positioned 90 miles west-north-west of Dublin.

## Locations in the English Midlands and North
The Midland railway serves a wide array of districts across England:

*   **Worcestershire:** Bromsgrove is a market town located 12 miles north-north-east of Worcester. Its station is situated one mile from the town on the Bristol-Birmingham line of the Midland railway. The railway here ascends for two miles over one of the steepest gradients in England to surmount the Lickey Hills toward Birmingham. The Midland railway also maintains carriage works near the town.
*   **Leicestershire:** Market Harborough is located 81 miles north-north-west of London via the Midland railway. Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a market town 118 miles north-west by north from London, is situated on the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway.
*   **Cheshire:** Marple, an urban district 12 miles south-east of Manchester, is served by the Midland, the Midland & Sheffield, the Great Central, and the Cheshire lines.
*   **Derbyshire:** Bolsover, an urban district 5-1/2 miles east of Chesterfield, is served by branch lines of both the Great Central and the Midland railways.
*   **Yorkshire:** Ilkley, an urban district in the West Riding of Yorkshire located 16 miles north-west of Leeds, is served by the North-Eastern and the Midland railways.

## Industrial and Economic Impact
The presence of the railway coincides with various local industries. In Ballymena, the linen trade—introduced in 1733—is the primary source of prosperity, with a specialty in brown linen. Ballymoney supports brewing, distilling, tanning, and the manufacture of tobacco, candles, and soap, while also serving as a center for agricultural trade.

In England, the railway serves areas of significant manufacturing and extraction. Bromsgrove is known for the manufacture of buttons and nails. Bolsover and the district surrounding Ashby-de-la-Zouch are characterized by coal-mining; Bolsover also engages in quarrying. Market Harborough hosts factories for stays, shoes, and boots, as well as malt-houses.
