# Broadstairs

Broadstairs is a watering-place located in England within the Isle of Thanet parliamentary division of Kent. It is situated three miles south-east of Margate and is served by the South Eastern & Chatham railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district, which includes both Broadstairs and St Peter's, was 6,466.

## Connection to Charles Dickens
From 1837 to 1851, Broadstairs served as a favourite summer resort for Charles Dickens. In a sketch titled "Our English Watering-Place," Dickens described the location as a place that had been "left high and dry by the tide of years." During his time there, he resided at a house called Fort House. Although Dickens' last visit to Broadstairs occurred in 1851, Fort House later became known as Bleak House due to its association with his novel of the same name, which was written after his visits to the resort had ended. Dickens also mentioned the "Albion" in his writings.

## Development and Amenities
Originally a seaside village characterized by a "semicircular sweep of houses," Broadstairs grew into a considerable town because of the influx of summer visitors. To provide entertainment for these guests, the town developed numerous boarding-houses and hotels, as well as libraries, a fine promenade, and a bathing establishment. 

The town is part of a ring of watering-places around the Isle of Thanet, which also includes Ramsgate, Margate, Westgate, and Birchington. Broadstairs is practically contiguous with Margate to the north-west due to the modern extension of these popular resorts. Additionally, an electric tramway provides a connection between Broadstairs, Margate, and Ramsgate.

## Local Landmarks and History
Broadstairs features a small pier used for fishing-boats, the construction of which first took place during the reign of Henry VIII. An archway that leads down to the shore contains an inscription indicating it was erected in 1540 by George Culmer. Nearby is the site of a chapel of the Virgin; historically, ships passing by were accustomed to lower their top-sails in respect to this chapel.

To the north of Broadstairs on the coast is Kingsgate, located on the North Foreland. This location was originally called St Bartholomew's Gate but was renamed in honour of the landing of Charles II and the duke of York in 1683 during their journey from London to Dover. Nearby is Stonehouse, which served as the residence of Archbishop Tait and where his wife established an orphanage; it currently functions as a preparatory school for boys.

## St Peter's Parish
St Peter's parish is located on the landward side of Broadstairs and is included within the urban district. The parish contains a church with origins dating from the 12th century through to the end of the 16th century.
