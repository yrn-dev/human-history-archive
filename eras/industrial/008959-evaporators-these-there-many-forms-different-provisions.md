# Evaporators

In the context of marine engineering, evaporators are apparatuses used to produce fresh water from sea-water. This process is necessary to replace water lost in marine boilers due to leakages in the evaporation cycle, use within the engine, and condensation in the condenser before the water returns to the boiler as feed-water.

## Principles and Operation
While evaporators exist in many forms with various provisions for cleaning their coils, they all operate on the same fundamental principle. The system is fed with sea-water, and the heat required to trigger evaporation is provided by live steam from the boilers. This steam is admitted into coils made of copper pipe.

The operation results in two primary outputs:
* **Condensed Water:** The water that condenses within the copper coils is returned directly to the feed-water.
* **Evaporated Steam:** The steam produced from the sea-water is directed either into the condenser or into the low-pressure receiver of the steam-engine.

Additionally, evaporators are equipped with means to blow out the brine that is produced as a byproduct of the evaporation process.

## Related Concepts of Evaporation
The process of evaporation occurs across various mechanical, chemical, and biological contexts as documented in the archive.

### Steam Generation and Fuel Efficiency
In boilers—vessels designed to heat liquids to the boiling point to produce steam for mechanical work—evaporative efficiency is a desirable quality. This efficiency depends on the heating surfaces' ability to transmit a large proportion of the heat produced by fuel combustion to the water. 

The "evaporative power" of coal is a measure used to determine its value. While laboratory experiments can calculate this power through chemical analysis or separate calorific factors, these results are typically higher than those obtained during actual combustion in a steam boiler. This discrepancy is due to real-world losses, such as the loss of unburnt coal in cinders and the imperfect combustion of gases. Consequently, the value of coal is often determined by burning a weighed quantity in a boiler furnace and measuring the actual amount of water evaporated by the resulting heat.

### Industrial and Chemical Applications
Evaporation is utilized in the production of chemical compounds and building materials:
* **Borax:** When evaporation occurs at high temperatures in a supersaturated aqueous solution of borax, the pentahydrate ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7\cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$) is deposited.
* **Mortar Bricks:** These unburnt blocks, made from a mixture of sand and slaked lime, harden in the air. This hardening process is brought about partly by the evaporation of water, though it is primarily driven by the conversion of slaked lime into calcium carbonate.

### Biological and Environmental Evaporation
Evaporation also plays a critical role in nature and artificial incubation:
* **Incubation:** In artificial incubators, direct ventilation is required to provide air to eggs, which increases the evaporation of water vapour from the egg's surface. If not checked, eggs may become too dry from the tenth day onwards. Some authorities believe sitting hens secrete an oily substance that retards this evaporation. In the Cornell incubator, the rate of evaporation is monitored by measuring the size of the air-space within the egg-shell on the 1st, 5th, 10th, 14th, and 18th days.
* **Geology:** Certain plains in Greece are described as having level surfaces deposited by water that either retired or evaporated.

## Thermal Management in Weaponry
Evaporation is also a factor in managing the heat of machine gun barrels. In the Maxim gun, the water-jacket contains 7½ pints of water. Rapid fire of 600 rounds (approximately 1½ minutes) raises this water to the boiling point. If firing continues, approximately 1½ pints of water are evaporated for every 1,000 rounds fired.
