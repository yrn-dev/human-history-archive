# Prague

Prague is the capital of Bohemia and has served as a central hub for the region's industrial, political, and cultural development. Throughout its history, the city has been a focal point of military conflict, administrative shifts, and academic growth.

## Industry and Commerce
Prague is a significant center for manufacturing within Bohemia, which is one of Europe's greatest manufacturing hubs. The city is specifically noted for the production of industrial and agricultural machinery, as well as a large establishment dedicated to the production of railway rolling-stock. Additionally, Prague is a center for the manufacture of chemicals. The broader commercial development of the region, including that of Prague, has been stimulated by a network of good roads and an extensive railway system.

## Political and Military History
As the capital of Bohemia, Prague has frequently been the site of major political and military events. The Thirty Years' War began in the city, and the conflict concluded in 1648 when the Swedish general Konigsmark took part of the city. 

In the early 18th century, Prague was the site of a meeting on October 16, 1720, where the estates of Bohemia recognized the Pragmatic Sanction, which proclaimed the indivisibility of the Habsburg realm and sanctioned female succession to the throne. However, the city later became a target of invasion. In December 1741, Charles, the elector of Bavaria, occupied Prague, and many Bohemian knights and nobles pledged allegiance to him.

The city was also a strategic objective during conflicts involving France and Prussia. In 1742, the French army under Broglie was nearly surrounded in Prague by the forces of Prince Charles, though the siege was eventually raised on September 14. Later, during the Seven Years' War, the Prussians reached Prague on September 2, and the Austrian garrison surrendered six days later. The Prussian siege of Prague was eventually raised following the Austrian victory at the Battle of Kolin on June 18, 1757.

## Governance and Social Conflict
Prague has experienced significant shifts in municipal control and ethnic tension. Following a period of instability, Ferdinand arrived in Prague on July 20 with Spanish and Walloon mercenaries. While he treated the nobility with forbearance, he dealt severely with the city of Prague, revising its ancient privileges and confiscating municipal lands. He also imposed royal representatives known as town-captains and town-judges to exercise unlimited control over municipal affairs.

In the late 19th century, Prague became a center for the contest between Czech and German interests. In 1886, an order was issued to avoid frequent translations by allowing business introduced in Czech to be dealt with in that language within the high courts of Prague. Furthermore, in 1882, classes in Czech were introduced at the university of Prague, an event viewed by Germans as a desecration of the oldest German university.

## Culture and Education
Prague has a long history as a center of learning and the arts. The Dominicans established a presence in the city, occupying chairs at the university of Prague as part of a broader policy of founding houses in great university towns.

The city is also linked to the development of national dance. In 1835, the Polka—the chief Bohemian national dance—was adopted by Society in Prague. The dance was documented by Josef Neruda, who had observed a peasant girl singing and dancing the polka. From Prague, the dance spread to Vienna and later to Paris, England, and America.
