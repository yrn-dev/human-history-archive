# Sir David Baird

## Early Life and Entry into Service
Sir David Baird (1757–1829) was a British general born in December 1757 at Newbyth in Aberdeenshire. He began his military career by entering the British army in 1773. By 1779, he was serving as a captain with the 73rd Highlanders (which later became the 71st Highlanders) and was deployed to India.

## Captivity in India
Upon arriving in India, Baird was attached to a force under the command of Sir Hector Munro. This force was dispatched to assist Colonel Baillie's detachment, which was under threat from Hyder Ali. During the ensuing action, the entire force was destroyed. Baird was severely wounded and captured by the Mysore chief. He remained a prisoner for over four years, during which time the captives were treated most barbarously. Baird's wound was so severe that the bullet remained in his body until he was finally released.

## Military Advancement and Return to India
Following his release, Baird continued his ascent through the military ranks, becoming a major in 1787. He visited England in 1789 and purchased a lieutenant-colonelcy in 1790. He returned to India the following year. 

Baird's subsequent service included holding a brigade command during the war against Tippoo. In 1792, he served under Cornwallis during the operations at Seringapatam, and he was promoted to the rank of colonel in 1795. He also served as a brigadier-general at the Cape of Good Hope before returning to India as a major-general in 1798.

## The Assault on Seringapatam
During the final war against Tippoo in 1799, Baird was appointed to the senior brigade command in the army. He led the storming party during the successful assault of Seringapatam, eventually becoming the master of the stronghold where he had previously been held as a prisoner.

Despite his success, Baird felt he had been treated with disrespect and injustice. He was disappointed that the command of the nizam's large contingent had been assigned to Colonel Arthur Wellesley, and he felt further slighted when Wellesley was appointed governor after the fortress was captured.

## Later Career and Recognition
For his gallant conduct during the assault on Seringapatam, Baird received thanks from the East India Company and the parliament. Although the East India Company offered him a pension, he declined it, likely because he hoped to receive the order of the Bath from the government. In 1801, General Baird commanded the Indian army sent to cooperate with Abercromby to expel the French.
