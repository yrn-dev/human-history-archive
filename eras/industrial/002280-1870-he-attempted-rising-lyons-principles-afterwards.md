# Mikhail Bakunin

## Early Life and Political Awakening
Mikhail Bakunin (1814–1876) was a Russian anarchist born into an aristocratic family in Torjok, located in the government of Tver. His early professional career was spent as an officer of the Imperial Guard, serving in Poland. However, Bakunin resigned his commission after developing a disgust for despotism, which was triggered by witnessing the repressive methods used against the Polish people.

## European Travels and Activism
Following his resignation, Bakunin moved to Germany, where he studied Hegel and established connections with leaders of the young German movement in Berlin. He subsequently traveled to Paris, where he met George Sand and Proudhon, and became acquainted with prominent Polish exiles. Bakunin later resided in Switzerland for a period, during which he participated actively in various socialistic movements.

His political activities drew the attention of the Russian government, which ordered him to return to Russia. Upon his refusal to comply, the government confiscated his property. In 1848, Bakunin returned to Paris and published a violent tirade against Russia, an act that led to his expulsion from France.

## Insurrections and Imprisonment
The revolutionary climate of 1848 provided Bakunin with an opportunity to engage in a violent campaign of democratic agitation. In 1849, he participated in the Dresden insurrection, resulting in his arrest and a sentence of death. This sentence was later commuted to life imprisonment. Eventually, he was handed over to Russian authorities, who imprisoned him and finally exiled him to eastern Siberia in 1855.

Bakunin was later permitted to move to the Amur region, from which he successfully escaped. His journey back to Western Europe took him through the United States and Japan, eventually arriving in England in 1861. He spent the remainder of his life in exile in western Europe, primarily residing in Switzerland.

## Later Political Career and the International
In 1869, Bakunin founded the Social Democratic Alliance, though the organization dissolved within the same year. He subsequently joined the International. In 1870, Bakunin attempted a rising at Lyons; this attempt was based on principles that were later exemplified by the Paris Commune.

Bakunin's tenure with the International ended at the Hague congress in 1872, where he was outvoted and expelled by the Marx party. Following this expulsion, he retired to Lugano in 1873. Mikhail Bakunin died in Bern on June 13, 1876.
