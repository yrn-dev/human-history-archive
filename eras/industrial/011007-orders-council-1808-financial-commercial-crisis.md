# Orders in Council (1808) and The Financial and Commercial Crisis

## The Orders in Council and Trade Restrictions
The Orders in Council and the associated restrictions on trade with the United States were subjects of significant political and business opposition in the early 19th century. Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton, an English politician and financier who served as the head of the firm Baring Brothers & Co. starting in 1810, was a notable opponent of these measures. Baring, who viewed politics through the lens of a businessman, opposed the Orders in Council as well as the trade restrictions imposed on the United States in 1812.

### Ancient and Early Modern Crises
In ancient Athens, widespread financial distress threatened the state with ruin and drove much of the agricultural population into slavery. This crisis was further accentuated by a protracted war with the neighboring Megarians. In 594, the Eupatridae granted Solon the power to remodel the state; through economic legislation, Solon restored Athenian agriculture and encouraged commercial enterprise, which established the foundation for the country's material prosperity.

In the late 16th century, the government of the Netherlands faced an "appalling financial crisis" following the sudden death of Governor Don Luis de Requesens in 1575. During this period, the Council of State assumed the regency until the King could appoint a new governor.

### 19th Century Commercial Crises
The 19th century saw several distinct commercial and financial disruptions:
*   **The Crisis of 1873:** A great commercial crisis occurred in 1873, driven by active speculations. This event indirectly affected the influence of the German Liberals in the Reichsrath.
*   **The Italian Bank Crisis of 1893:** In Italy, Luigi Luzzatti served as minister of the treasury in the first Di Rudini cabinet in 1891. During his tenure, he abolished the system of frequent clearings of bank-notes between state banks. This action facilitated the duplication of part of the paper currency and hastened the bank crisis of 1893. Luzzatti later helped save the Bank of Naples from failure during his second term as minister of the treasury in 1896.

## Financial Policy and Trade Disputes
Financial and commercial relations have frequently been used as tools of political and international pressure. Under the covenants of the League, if a high contracting party breaks its agreements, it is deemed to have committed an act of war. In such instances, the League undertakes to immediately subject the offending state to the prohibition of all intercourse between nationals and the severance of all financial or trade relations. This includes preventing any commercial, financial, or personal intercourse between the nationals of the covenant-breaking state and the nationals of any other state, regardless of whether that state is a member of the League.

Within domestic British politics, fiscal policy remained a point of contention. In one instance, a crisis developed among free-trade Unionists when Mr. Balfour, speaking at Sheffield on October 1st, declared his intention to reverse traditional fiscal policy. He proposed doing away with the axiom that import duties should be levied solely for revenue purposes, a stance that led to the resignation of the duke of Devonshire.
