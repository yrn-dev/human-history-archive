# Iron-Gates Rapids

The Iron Gates represent one of the principal obstructions to navigation between Turnu Severin and Old Moldova. Located 34 miles below the Greben section, the river at this point widens after leaving the Kazan defile, where it was nearly blocked by the Prigrada rocky bank.

## Engineering Objectives and Specifications
The general goal of the improvement works in this region was to ensure a navigable depth of water across all seasons. Specifically, the objective was to maintain a depth of 2 metres (6.56 ft.) on the portion of the river located above Orsova, and a depth of 3 metres (9.84 ft.) below the town of Orsova.

To achieve these depths at the Iron Gates, engineers cut a channel through solid rock on the Servian side of the river. This channel measures 80 yards in width and is approximately 2000 yards in length. The depth of the channel was set at 10 ft. Notably, this construction was carried out in the immediate vicinity of traces of an old Roman canal.

## Water Management and Infrastructure
To maintain the necessary water levels, training walls were constructed on both sides of the channel to confine the water. The training wall located on the right bank has a width of 19 ft. 6 in. at the top.

## Related River Obstructions
The Iron Gates are part of a series of obstructions in the region. Other significant obstacles include:

*   **Stenka Rapids:** Featuring a bank of rocks extending almost across the river (985 yards wide) with a rapid length of 1100 yards.
*   **Kozla Dojke Rapids:** Located 9 miles below the Stenka Rapids, these extend for 1100 yards with a fall of 1 in 1000. In this section, the river width varies between 170 and 330 yards, and two banks of rocks cause the current to alternate direction suddenly.
*   **Greben Section:** Considered the most difficult part of the improvement works. Here, a spur of the Greben mountains extends below two shoals, causing the river to narrow to 300 yards at low water before widening to 1.5 miles.
*   **Jucz Rapids:** Situated seven miles lower than the Greben section, where the river-bed has a fall of 1 in 433.

## Other Regional Improvements
Similar to the works at the Iron Gates, other sections required specific engineering interventions to ensure navigability. At Stenka, Kozla Dojke, Islaz, and Tachtalia, channels 66 yards wide were cut into solid rock to a depth of 6 ft. 6 in. below low water. At the Greben spur, the point had to be entirely removed for a distance of 167 yards back from its original face, and a training wall nearly 4 miles in length (7 to 9 ft. high and 10 ft. at the top) was built along the Servian shore. A similar channel and training wall were also constructed at Jucz.
