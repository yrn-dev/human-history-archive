# 1890-1894: Selected Historical Records

The period between 1890 and 1894 is documented across various fields of study in the archive, including educational leadership, agricultural statistics, international exploration, and academic appointments.

## Educational Leadership and Administration
In the United States, Nicholas Murray Butler, an American educator born in New Jersey, held several significant roles during this timeframe. After serving as an adjunct professor of philosophy, ethics, and psychology from 1889 to 1890, Butler became a full professor in 1890. In that same year, he was appointed dean of the faculty of philosophy, a position he held until 1902. 

Butler's administrative influence extended to the New York college for the training of teachers (which later became the Teachers' College of Columbia University), where he served as the first president from 1887 until 1891. Following this tenure, he founded the *Educational Review* in 1891, serving as its editor. By 1894, Butler was elected president of the National Educational Association. Additionally, he served on the New Jersey state board of education from 1887 to 1895 and presided over the Paterson (N.J.) board of education from 1892 to 1893.

## Agricultural and Trade Statistics

Regarding specific imports of milk and cream (excluding condensed), separate classification did not begin until 1894. In that year, 161,633 gallons were imported with a value of £21,371.

## Academic and Professional Appointments
The archive notes several appointments and honors granted to Mr. Balfour during this period. In 1890, he became the lord rector of Glasgow University. In 1891, he received LL.D. degrees from both Glasgow and Dublin Universities and was appointed chancellor of Edinburgh University. He also received a D.C.L. from Oxford University in 1891. Later, in 1894-1895, he served as the captain of the Royal and Antient Golf Club of St Andrews.

## Exploration and Geographic Literature
The early 1890s saw the publication of several works regarding Central Asia, Tibet, and the Balkans. W. W. Rockhill published a *Diary of a Journey through Mongolia and Tibet in 1891 and 1892* in Washington in 1894. Other relevant publications from this era include Wegener's *Versuch einer Orographie des Kuen-lun* (1891) and P. G. Bonvalot's *De Paris au Tonkin à travers le Tibet inconnu* (1892).

In the Balkans, Tomaschek published *Die heutigen Bewohner Macedoniens* in 1891, and Shopoff published *Iz zhivota i polozhenieto na Bulgarite v vilayetite* in 1894. Additionally, Perdrizet began publishing articles on Macedonian epigraphy and archaeology in the *Bulletin de correspondance hellenique* starting in 1894.
