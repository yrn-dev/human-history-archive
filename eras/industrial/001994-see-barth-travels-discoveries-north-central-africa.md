# Travels and Discoveries in North and Central Africa

*Travels and Discoveries in North and Central Africa* is a five-volume work published between 1857 and 1858 in both English and German. Written by the German explorer Heinrich Barth, the text is regarded as a standard authority on the regions it describes, many of which later became part of Nigeria under British rule. The work is noted for its extent of information, variety, interest, and accuracy, having few rivals among similar publications.

## The Expedition
The mission that produced the work was initiated at the request of scientists, including Bunsen. Heinrich Barth and Adolf Overweg, a Prussian astronomer, were appointed as colleagues to James Richardson. Richardson was an explorer of the Sahara who had been selected by the British government for the purpose of opening commercial relations with the states of the western and central Sudan.

The party departed from Tripoli in early 1850. However, the mission suffered losses when Richardson died in March 1851, followed by the death of Overweg in September 1852. These events left Barth to carry out the remainder of the mission alone. He eventually returned to Europe in September 1855.

## Geographic Scope and Research
Barth's expedition is described as one of the most fruitful ever undertaken in inner Africa. His travels included journeys across the Sahara and a traversal of the country from Cameroon in the south and Timbuktu in the west to Bagirmi and Lake Chad in the east. 

During his travels, Barth made prolonged sojourns in Timbuktu and in the ancient emirates or sultanates of Gando, Sokoto, Nupe, Kano, and Bornu. He conducted minute studies of the resources, civilizations, history, and topography of the countries he visited.

## Author Biography
Heinrich Barth was born in Hamburg on February 16, 1821, and graduated from Berlin University in 1844. Before his major African expedition, he traveled through the Mediterranean countries starting in 1845, after studying Arabic in London. His early travels took him from Tangier across North Africa, visiting Cyrenaica and the ancient cities of Barbary. He traveled through Egypt—where he was wounded during an attack by robbers—ascending the Nile to Wadi Halfa and crossing the desert to Berenice. He also traversed Turkey, Asia Minor, Syria, Palestine, and Greece to examine remains of antiquity, returning to Berlin in 1847. He published a narrative of these journeys, *Wanderungen durch die Kuestenlaender des Mittelmeeres*, in 1849.

Following his return from the central African mission in 1855, Barth received no recognition from the British government for his services, except for a C.B. He returned to Germany and produced a collection of Central African vocabularies between 1862 and 1866. His later years included a journey to Asia Minor in 1858 and a visit to Turkey in Europe in 1862. In 1863, he became the president of the Geographical Society and a professor of geography at Berlin University. He died in Berlin on November 25, 1865.

## Legacy and Publications
Beyond the original five-volume set, a two-volume edition of *Travels* was published in London in 1890 as part of the Minerva Library of Famous Books. The impact of his work is further documented in Schubert's 1897 publication, *Heinrich Barth, der Bahnbrecher der deutschen Afrikaforschung*.
