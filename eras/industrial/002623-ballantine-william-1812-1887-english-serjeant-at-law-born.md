# BALLANTINE, WILLIAM (1812-1887)

## Early Life and Education
William Ballantine was born in London on January 3, 1812. He was the son of a police-magistrate based in London. His early education took place at St Paul's school.

## Legal Career
Ballantine was called to the bar in 1834. In the early stages of his life, he developed a varied acquaintance with literary and dramatic society. This experience, combined with an acute intellect and a "pushing character," allowed him to quickly establish a large legal practice. He became particularly noted for his work in criminal cases and gained a reputation as a formidable cross-examiner. During his career, his great rival was Serjeant Parry (1816-1880). In 1856, Ballantine became a serjeant-at-law.

## Notable Cases
The career of Serjeant Ballantine was marked by three significant cases:
*   **The prosecution of Franz Mueller (1864):** Ballantine successfully prosecuted the murderer.
*   **The Tichborne claimant (1871):** Ballantine provided a skilful defence.
*   **The gaekwar of Baroda (1875):** Ballantine defended the gaekwar in a case for which his fee was one of the largest ever known.

## Later Life and Death
In addition to his legal practice, Ballantine published more than one volume of reminiscences. His private life is described as decidedly Bohemian. Despite earning large sums of money throughout his career, he died very poor. He passed away at Margate on January 9, 1887.
