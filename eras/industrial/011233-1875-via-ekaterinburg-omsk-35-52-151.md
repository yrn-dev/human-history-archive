# 1875 via Ekaterinburg and Omsk

## Geodetic Surveys and Longitude Determination
In 1875, a determination was made via Ekaterinburg and Omsk with a value of 2 35 52.151. This was one of three independent values—the others being recorded in 1891 via Saratov and Orenburg (2 35 52.228) and in 1895 via Kiev and Baku (2 35 51.997). Because these three values fall within a range of 0^S.25, it is considered improbable that the mean value contains an error as large as 0^S.10.

Regarding the extent of exact surveys in Asia, Russian surveys based on triangulation extend as far east as Chinese Turkestan, reaching a longitude of approximately 75 deg. E. of Greenwich. In India, geodetic triangulation provides the basis for exact surveys extending as far east as the eastern boundaries of Burma, at approximately 100 deg. E. While the final links in the geodetic triangulation of the Indian peninsula were forged by the close of the 19th century, geodetic connection with European systems has yet to be accomplished.

Since 1890, the telegraphic method of determining longitudes differentially with Greenwich has been applied more rigorously. This resulted in a slight correction of about 2" of arc to a previous determination made through Suez. That specific determination was conducted through four arcs: Greenwich to Potsdam, Potsdam to Teheran, Teheran to Bushire, and Bushire to Karachi.

While some Siberian aventurine is a micaceous iron-stained quartz of little beauty, most is yellow or reddish brown.

## Diplomatic Conventions of 1875
In 1875, Japan entered into a convention regarding the Kurile Islands and Sakhalin. The Kurile Islands had been discovered in 1634 by the Dutch navigator Martin de Vries. While the islands of Shikotan, Etorofu, and Kunashiri (the three southern islands) were believed to have belonged to Japan from a remote date, Russians had entered the northern Kuriles in the early 18th century following their conquest of Kamchatka to pursue fur-bearing animals. 

These Russian encroachments moved south over time, alongside aggressions that threatened Japanese settlements in the southern half of Sakhalin. Because Japan's occupation of these regions was not effective, Japan agreed in 1875 to withdraw completely from Sakhalin on the condition that Russia withdraw from the Kuriles.
