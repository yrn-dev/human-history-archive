# Banyan Days

## Definition and Origin
"Banyan days" is a nautical slang term formerly used within the British navy. The term refers to two specific days in each week during which meat was excluded from the rations provided to the men. This practice was an allusion to the vegetarian diet followed by Hindu merchants.

## Related Terminology
The influence of Hindu customs on naval and Anglo-Indian slang extended to other terms. "Banyan hospital" became a slang expression for a hospital dedicated to animals, referencing the Hindu dislike of taking the life of any animal and their general humanity. 

Additionally, the word "banyan" (or "banian") has several origins and applications. It is an Arab corruption of the Sanskrit word *vanij*, meaning "merchant," which was borrowed by the Portuguese. Europeans originally used the name to describe a specific tree of the fig genus (*Ficus Indica* or *Bengalensis*) found on the Persian Gulf, beneath which Hindu merchants had constructed a pagoda. In Calcutta, the term was once commonly used to describe a head clerk or native broker in a private house or business, though such individuals are now usually known as sircars. In Anglo-Indian usage, "banyan" also referred to an undershirt, referencing the body garments worn by Hindus, particularly the Banyans.
