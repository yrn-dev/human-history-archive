# Bolt

The term "bolt" is an Old English word with diverse meanings and applications, ranging from mechanical fasteners and weaponry to specific linguistic expressions and industrial measurements.

## Etymology and Linguistic Usage
The word "bolt" has several distinct origins. One root is related to the German *Bolz*, meaning an arrow. This association with the swift flight of an arrow led to the verb "to bolt," used when describing a horse or in the phrase "bolt upright," meaning straight upright. In American usage, "bolt" refers to the act of refusing to support a candidate nominated by one's own political party. Historically, the term has also been used to describe a hasty or undignified escape, such as the Kaiser and the Crown Prince bolting into Holland in November 1918, or "welshers bolting from a racecourse."

A separate derivation, "bolt" or "boult," comes from the Old French *buleter* (from the Medieval Latin *buretare* or *buletare*), meaning to sift. This sense appears in the works of Shakespeare, where "bolted" describes snow sifted by northern blasts, and in the writings of Burke, who described a committee report being "sifted and bolted to the bran." From this meaning of sifting comes the sense of mooting or discussing, as seen in Milton's *Comus*.

## Mechanical Fasteners and Construction
In its sense as a straight pin for fastening, the bolt is used across various fields of construction and industry:

*   **Timber and Carpentry:** Bolts are used in "fished joints" to lengthen beams by bolting through timber with iron plates on the top and bottom. They are also used in "scarf joints," where timbers are cut, notched, lapped, and bolted through. In "centering"—temporary framing for concrete floors or brick arches—long bolts are used to suspend a second bearer from a wood bearer resting on steel joists.
*   **Maritime Construction:** Ancient Egyptian *baris* boats were constructed from thorny acacia. Pieces of wood approximately two cubits in length were arranged like bricks and fastened together by running a great number of long bolts through them.
*   **General Utility:** In rope work, "stoppers" for ordinary running ropes are created by splicing a piece of rope to a bolt or a hook and thimble.
*   **Textiles:** Derived from the concept of fastening together, a "bolt" refers to a definite length of fabric in a roll, such as 40 feet of canvas.

## Weaponry and Firearm Mechanisms
The term "bolt" originally referred to a "quarrel" or a cross-bow shaft. In modern firearm mechanisms, the bolt serves as a critical component for loading and firing:

*   **General Function:** In certain guns, the bolt is a cylinder with a guide rib. It is bored through to accommodate a striker or firing pin. When the breech is locked, the bolt is held firm by recoil blocks on the receiver. An extractor with a spring claw is located at the front of the bolt to remove the fired case.
*   **Feeding and Firing:** In some systems, the bolt pulls a cartridge off a carrier and drives it into the breech. Once the bolt is locked, the sear is released. If the trigger were held permanently, the gun would fire immediately upon the bolt locking, creating an automatic action.
*   **Machine Gun Variations:** In the Nordenfeldt gun, a box containing bolts, locks, strikers, and spiral springs moves backwards and forwards. When pushed forward, the bolts drive cartridges into the barrels. In other designs, the bolt pushes back a hammer, which is then held by a sear until the bolt has moved completely forward to lock the breech.
