# Le Boucau

## Industrial Overview
Le Boucau is a location situated 3 miles from Bayonne. The area is characterized by the presence of large metallurgical works, specifically the *Forges de l'Adour*, as well as chemical works.

## Regional Context and Economy
Le Boucau is located within a department where the economy is diversified across various natural resources and industrial activities. The department provides building-stone, salt, and other products from quarries. It is also known for several mineral springs, including those at Salies, St Christau, Eaux-Chaudes, Eaux-Bonnes, and Cambo-les-Bains, the latter of which is visited by the Basques on St John's Eve.

The broader manufacturing sector of the department includes the production of sashes and woollen caps, chocolate, paper, and cord slippers. Additionally, the region maintains saw-mills, flour-mills, and tanneries. Specialized food products, such as "Bayonne hams" and other table delicacies, are prepared at Orthez.

## Transport and Infrastructure
The region's commerce is heavily supported by its geography and infrastructure. Bayonne serves as the principal port for the area, with a significant population engaged in fishing, alongside St Jean-de-Luz. The port's exports primarily consist of minerals, resinous products, salt, wine, mine-props, and timber. Leading imports include wool, grain, phosphates, minerals, and coal.

Water transport is facilitated by the lower courses of the Bidouze, the Nive, and the Adour, all of which are navigable. Land transport is managed by the Southern Company, which operates the railway lines of Basses-Pyrenees; the primary line runs from Bayonne to Toulouse, passing through Pau and Orthez.

## Administrative and Geographic Divisions
The department is organized into five arrondissements: Mauleon, Orthez, Oloron, Bayonne, and Pau. These are further divided into 559 communes and 41 cantons. Administratively, the area belongs to the district of the XVIII. army corps and falls under the educational circumscription (*academie*) of Bordeaux. It also constitutes the diocese of Bayonne.

The principal towns of the region include the capital and seat of a court of appeal, Pau, as well as Bayonne, Orthez, Oloron, St Jean-de-Luz, Eaux-Bonnes, and Biarritz. Other sites of interest include:
* **Lescar**: Home to a church from the 16th and 12th centuries that formerly served as a cathedral.
* **Montaner**: Featuring a stronghold established in 1380 by the viscount of Bearn and count of Foix, Gaston Phoebus.
* **Sauveterre**: A town located on the Gave d'Oloron featuring a church in Gothic and Romanesque styles, an old bridge, and the remains of a feudal castle.
