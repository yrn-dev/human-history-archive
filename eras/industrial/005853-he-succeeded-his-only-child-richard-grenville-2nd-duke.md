# Richard Grenville, 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos

Richard Grenville, 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos (1797–1861), was a British politician, author, and peer. He was the only child of Richard Grenville, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos.

## Early Life and Education
Richard Grenville was educated at Eton and Oriel College, Oxford. Before succeeding to the dukedom, he was known by the titles Earl Temple and later Marquess of Chandos.

## Political Career
Grenville served as a member of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1818 to 1839. During his tenure in parliament, he was responsible for the "Chandos clause" within the Reform Bill of 1832. He later held the office of lord privy seal from September 1841 to January 1842. Due to his opposition to the repeal of the corn laws, he earned the moniker "the Farmers' Friend."

## Financial Decline and Exile
Upon succeeding to the dukedom in 1839, Grenville found the estates to be heavily encumbered. His personal luxurious and generous tastes further exacerbated these financial difficulties, bringing the situation to a climax. In 1847, his creditors seized his residences, forcing the duke to leave England. During this period, many of his landed estates and his personal property were sold.

## Later Life and Literary Works
After returning to England, Grenville devoted himself to literature. He authored several publications focusing on the English court, including:
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of George III.* (published 1853–1855)
* *Memoirs of the Court of England, 1811-1820* (published 1856)
* *Memoirs of the Court of George IV.* (published 1859)
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of William IV. and Victoria* (published 1861)

He died in London on July 29, 1861.

## Personal Life
In 1819, Richard Grenville married Mary, the daughter of John, 1st Marquess of Breadalbane. Mary obtained a divorce from the duke in 1850 and died in 1862. The couple had one son, Richard Grenville, who became the 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos.
