# The Treaty of Prague and the Austro-Prussian War of 1866

The Treaty of Prague, signed on August 23, 1866, served as the concluding agreement of the Austro-Prussian War. This conflict was the culmination of a long-standing rivalry for hegemony in Germany between the house of Habsburg (Austria) and the Hohenzollern monarchy (Prussia). The issue of leadership was definitively decided in favor of Prussia following the victory at Koeniggraetz.

## The Austro-Prussian War of 1866
The war of 1866 saw Prussia and Italy acting as allies against Austria. While Italy suffered defeats at Lissa and Custozza, the Prussian army crushed the Austrian army at the battle of Sadow. This decisive military failure forced Austria into an "abject surrender."

Several other German states were involved in the conflict. Baden, which had been a consistent supporter of Austria between 1850 and 1866, saw its contingents engage in two sharp battles with the Prussian army of the Main under Prince William. Similarly, King Louis of Bavaria and his minister von der Pfordten sided with Austria.

## Terms of the Treaty of Prague
The peace process involved several agreements and treaties. In Bavaria, peace was concluded on August 22, requiring the surrender of small portions of territory and agreement to the foundation of the North German Confederation under Prussian leadership. In Baden, a treaty of peace and alliance was signed with Prussia on August 17, following the resignation of the ministry and Baden's withdrawal from the German confederation.

The primary treaty signed at Prague on August 23, 1866, established that all lands ruled by the house of Habsburg were excluded from Germany. Additionally, Napoleon III procured the insertion of a specific paragraph (paragraph v.) into the treaty regarding northern districts.

## Consequences for the German States
The aftermath of the war led to a significant reorganization of Central European power:

*   **The North German Confederation:** Prussia became the head of this new entity. Austria, Bavaria, Württemberg, Baden, and Hesse were excluded from this confederation. Although Baden sought an offensive and defensive alliance with Prussia, Bismarck prevented its adhesion to the North German Confederation to avoid giving Napoleon III an excuse for intervention.
*   **Austria and Bohemia:** The exclusion of Habsburg lands from Germany led to a decline in German influence within the Austrian empire, particularly in Bohemia. In Bohemia, the war caused a complete change in the constitutional position. While Hungary achieved complete independence, a new constitution in 1867 attempted to maintain waning German influence in the Slavic and German parts of the empire through centralization, a move energetically opposed by the Bohemians.
*   **Other Territories:** The conflict resulted in the annexation of Hanover by Prussia in 1866.

## Path to German Unification
The victory of 1866 set the stage for the eventual unification of the German Empire. The defeat of Bavaria in 1866 highlighted the need for army reform, which was carried out under the Liberal ministry of Hohenlohe and Minister of War Prauckh between December 1866 and February 1870.

When the Franco-Prussian War broke out in 1870, the previously opposing states, including Baden and Bavaria, sided with Prussia. Following the defeat of France—marked by the capitulations at Sedan and Metz and the fall of Paris in January 1871—Germany was unified as an empire, excluding Austria. The King of Prussia was proclaimed German Emperor in a historic assembly at Versailles, where the grand-duke of Baden was the first to hail him as such, and King Louis of Bavaria offered the imperial crown.
