# Dr. Kellner and the Measurement of Gun Bore Pressure

## Overview of Bore Pressure Measurement
In the study of ballistics, measuring the pressure within the bore of a gun is conducted experimentally using a device known as the crusher-gauge. This instrument records the maximum pressure reached by compressing a copper cylinder located in its interior. Depending on the requirements of the experiment, the crusher-gauge may be fastened into the base of the shot or placed directly in the powder-chamber.

Further refinements in these researches were conducted by Sir Andrew Noble. Noble utilized a series of plugs inserted into the side of an experimental gun that reached the bore. These plugs carried both crusher-gauges and chronographic appliances. The chronographic tools registered the passage of the shot, similar to the electric screens used in the experiments of Bashforth, allowing for the inference of the shot's energy and velocity. This data served as an independent control for the records provided by the crusher-gauge.

## The Contributions of Dr. Kellner
As a preliminary step to determining bore pressure, it is necessary to measure the pressure created by exploding a powder charge in a closed vessel. This process involves varying the weight of the charge to alter the density of the powder-gas. While the earliest experiments of this type were performed by Count Rumford in 1792 and Benjamin Robins in 1743, the method was later revived by Dr. Kellner.

Dr. Kellner, a chemist for the War Department, adapted the process by employing steel spheres from bicycle ball-bearings. He used these spheres as safety-valves, loading them to register the specific pressure at which the powder-gas would blow off. This technique allowed him to check and verify the indications provided by the crusher-gauge.

## Material Properties of Bicycle Ball-Bearings
The effectiveness of the steel spheres used by Dr. Kellner is rooted in the high standards of materials required for first-class bicycle manufacturing. In the production of balls and ball-races, homogeneity and hardness without brittleness are of primary importance. To achieve this, a process of case-hardening is applied, which creates a tough interior combined with an extremely hard outer skin. This level of perfection in metallurgy ensures that broken balls or traces of wear in bearings are seldom encountered in high-quality bicycles.
