# 1866

The year 1866 was marked by significant geopolitical shifts in Europe and Asia, the publication of various academic and literary works, and changes in governmental leadership across several nations.

## Geopolitical and Military Events
In Europe, 1866 saw a pivotal military conflict in which Prussia defeated the old Austria at Sadowa. This event, along with the subsequent establishment of the Dual Monarchy in 1867 and the foundation of the new German empire in 1871, served as the starting point for the history of Austria-Hungary.

In Italy, military activity continued as Antonio Giulio Barrili, an Italian novelist and former legal student, served as a volunteer with Garibaldi during 1866 and 1867. Additionally, reports from late 1866 describe an insurrection at Palermo, including a "Week’s Republic" and correspondence dated December 3, 1866.

In Japan, the year marked a transition in leadership. The fourteenth shogun, Iemochi, passed away and was succeeded by Yoshinobu (also known as Keiki). Upon attaining this dignity in 1866, Yoshinobu became an ardent advocate of progress. This era saw the introduction of Occidental fashions at the ceremonies of the Bakufu Court, the engagement of English officers to organize the navy, and the employment of French experts to remodel the army. Simultaneously, the Yedo Government suffered a loss of prestige due to the complete unsuccess of measures taken against Choshu.

## Government and Administration
Administrative changes occurred in various territories and ministries:
*   **Territorial Governance:** In certain territorial administrations, Horace C. Gilson served as Acting Governor until 1866, succeeded by S. R. Howlett in 1866, who was then followed by David W. Ballard, who served from 1866 to 1870.
*   **British Ministries:** Within the ministries of Lord Palmerston and Earl Russell, a transition occurred in the under-secretary positions. One individual served as under-secretary for India from 1864 to 1866, before becoming the under-secretary for war in 1866.

## Literature and Academic Publications
The year 1866 was productive for authors and scholars across multiple disciplines:

**Fiction and Novels**
E. Marlitt established her literary reputation in 1866 with the publication of *Goldelse* (which later reached a 23rd edition by 1890) and *Blaubart*. Her works were generally directed against social prejudices and primarily appealed to juvenile readers. In Italy, Antonio Giulio Barrili published the work *Santa Cecilia* in 1866.

**Scholarship and Reference**
In the field of classical scholarship, Franz Bücheler, a German scholar, held a professorship at Greifswald in 1866 and published *Grundriss der lateinischen Deklination*. In the realm of musicology, A.W. Thayer began the work *Beethovens Leben* (1866–1879), and the *Letters* of Beethoven were published in English translation in 1866.

**Geography and Exploration**
Col. H. Yule was active in publishing geographical notices and collections. In 1866, "Notices of Cathay" appeared in the *Proceedings, R.G.S.* (pp. 270–278). Additionally, Yule translated and edited *Cathay and the Way Thither*, a two-volume collection of medieval notices of China, which was printed for the Hakluyt Society in 1866.

**Other Publications**
The *Statuta Ecclesiae Scoticanae* was published in Edinburgh in 1866 by the Bannatyne Club.
