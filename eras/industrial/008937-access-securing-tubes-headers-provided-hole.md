# Access for Securing Tubes in Boiler Headers

In various designs of water-tube boilers, specifically those developed by Babcock & Wilcox and Durr, the headers are engineered with specific access points to facilitate the fixing, securing, and cleaning of the tubes. These access points typically take the form of holes located on the side of the header opposite the tube connections.

## Babcock & Wilcox Boiler Access
The Babcock & Wilcox boiler utilizes different types of access holes depending on whether the boiler is designed for marine or land use.

### Marine Boilers
In the marine version of the Babcock & Wilcox boiler, tubes are arranged in groups of two or four. Access for securing these tubes is provided by holes positioned on the side of the header opposite each tube. The specific configuration of these holes depends on the tube grouping:
* **Groups of four:** A hole is formed opposite each individual tube. These smaller holes are conical in shape, with the larger diameter located on the inside. They are sealed using special conical fittings where the bolt and the conical portion are created as a single forging, and the nut is close-ended.
* **Groups of two:** A single, larger oval hole is provided opposite each group. These are closed using fittings similar to those found in land boilers.

### Land Boilers
The land-purpose Babcock & Wilcox boiler features headers with holes on two opposite sides. While one side provides the connection between the headers and the tubes, the opposite side provides the necessary access for cleaning and fixing the tubes in position. These outer holes are oval and are secured by special fittings. The watertightness of these joints is maintained by outer cover plates. Because the holes are oval, the inside fitting can be installed from the outside; this fitting is designed to cover the opening to prevent a significant outrush of water or steam if a bolt should break.

## Durr Boiler Access
The Durr boiler, used in the German and British navies, employs a different method for securing tubes. Rather than the separate headers found in other designs, the Durr boiler uses one large water-chamber constructed from steel plates with welded joints. The tubes are secured to the inner plate of this chamber via conical joints. Access is provided through the outer plate, where the holes are closed by small round doors that are fitted from the inside. To fix the tubes in place, a small portable hydraulic jack is used to force each tube separately into position.

## Other Boiler Access Mechanisms
Other boiler types utilize different methods for accessing tube ends or providing maintenance points:
* **Belleville Boilers:** These use junction-boxes made of malleable cast iron. The boxes at the front of the boiler feature small oval holes opposite each tube end. These are closed by an internal door with a cross-bar and bolt, allowing the tubes to be cleaned and examined.
* **Cochran Boilers:** This design features a combustion chamber with a "dry" back. Instead of a water space, it utilizes doors lined with firebrick to provide easy access to the tube ends.
* **Simple Vertical Boilers:** In these installations, a hand-hole is provided in the outer shell opposite each cross-tube. This hole is closed by a suitable door and is used for cleaning purposes.
