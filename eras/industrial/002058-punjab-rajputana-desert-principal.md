# Punjab

## Overview and Geography
Punjab is a region in the north of India. Its geography is characterized by its relationship to major river systems and adjacent arid regions. The province is bordered to the south by the Rajputana desert and to the north and east by the Punjab and Sind. The rainless flora of south-western Asia, which extends from the African desert to the Indus and the southern parts of the Punjab, defines the vegetation of this borderland.

Within the Punjab, the native state of Bahawalpur (also known as Bhawalpur) stretches for more than 300 miles along the left banks of the Indus, the Punjnud, and the Sutlej. Bahawalpur is identified as the principal Mahommedan state in the Punjab, ranking second only to Patiala.

## Political History and Administration
The Punjab was conquered and annexed in 1849 and subsequently established as a distinct province in 1859. Following the end of the war, Dalhousie annexed the region and arranged for the education and custody of the infant maharaja. The province was initially administered by a triumvirate under the personal supervision of the governor-general. Later, John Lawrence served as the sole commissioner after Henry Lawrence was relocated to Rajputana. Dalhousie toured the province twice to settle important matters on-site, leaving a record of progress.

In 1901, the North-West Frontier was created as a subdivision. During this same period, the District of Dera Ismail Khan underwent jurisdictional changes; the trans-Indus tract was allotted to the newly formed North-West Frontier Province, while the cis-Indus tract remained under Punjab jurisdiction. The cis-Indus portions of the Bannu and Dera Ismail Khan districts were combined to form the new Punjab district of Mianiwali.

## Religious and Social History
Historically, the Punjab saw the rise of the Sikhs, a religious body that proclaimed the universal rule of one God. The Sikhs challenged both the Moslem Koran and the Hindu Vedas. While the sect was originally pacific, it eventually sought to establish the kingdom of God by the sword, following the example of Islam.

## Infrastructure and Economy
The Punjab is integrated into the broader Indian railway network. The North-Western line is a significant artery that enters Sind from the Punjab, terminating at Karachi. 

Irrigation is a key feature of the region's agricultural development. On the northern border of the native state of Bikanir, the Ghaggar canal in the Punjab provides irrigation for approximately 5,000 acres. In other areas, such as the district of Mianiwali (formerly part of Dera Ismail Khan), the economy involves the export of wool and wheat.
