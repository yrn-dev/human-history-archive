# LUCIEN BONAPARTE (1775-1840)

Lucien Bonaparte, the prince of Canino, was born on May 21, 1775, at Ajaccio. He received his early education at the schools of Brienne and Autun, following the path of his elder brothers. Although he initially desired to join the French army, defective sight prevented this, leading him instead toward a career in the church. To this end, he entered the seminary at Aix in Provence in 1786. However, his volatile and excitable temperament was poorly suited to the discipline of the seminary.

## Revolutionary Activity and Political Rise
With the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789, Lucien embraced the anti-clerical and democratic movements. Upon returning to Corsica, he became a prominent speaker within the Jacobin club at Ajaccio. He urged his brothers to sever ties with Paoli, the leader of the conservative party that sought an alliance with England against the regicide republic of France. Lucien subsequently led a Corsican deputation to France to solicit aid for the democrats and denounce Paoli. When the Paolists gained dominance, the Bonapartes left the island and joined Lucien at Toulon.

In southern France, Lucien continued his work for the Jacobin cause, adopting the name "Brutus" in the Jacobin club of St Maximin, a town renamed Marathon. On May 4, 1794, while still a minor and without family consent, he married Mlle Catherine Boyer, an act that resulted in a period of penury and near disgrace. Following the *coup d'etat* of Thermidor on July 28, 1794, Lucien—a disciple of Robespierre—fled St Maximin and took a minor position at St Chamans. After a period of arrest and imprisonment, he was released through the influence of Napoleon, who secured him a post as a commissioner in the French army during the campaign in Germany.

## Parliamentary Career and Conflict with Napoleon
Lucien eventually grew to dislike his military duties, as they provided no opportunity for his political intrigue or oratory. He returned to Corsica in hopes of being elected as a deputy, and consequently refused an appointment in the army of Egypt offered by Napoleon in 1798. His political ambitions were realized that same year when he entered the Council of Five Hundred in Paris.

In later years, Lucien resided primarily in Italy, specifically in Rome, where he displayed marked hostility toward the emperor. In December 1807, Napoleon attempted to reach an arrangement where Lucien would be recognized as a French prince on the condition that he annul his marriage. Lucien refused this demand. He resided for a time at his estate of Canino, from which he derived the papal title of prince of Canino.

## Exile and Final Years
Lucien eventually departed for America, but he was captured by a British ship and transported to Malta and then to England. He remained in England under some surveillance until the peace of 1814. During the Hundred Days in 1815, he returned to Rome and offered his assistance to Napoleon. He stood by Napoleon at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris and was the final person to defend the emperor's prerogatives during his second abdication.

Lucien spent the remainder of his life in Italy and died in Rome on June 29, 1840. He is survived by a family consisting of six daughters and four sons. His literary contributions include a two-volume epic titled *Charlemagne, ou l'Eglise delivree* (1814), as well as *La Verite sur les Cent Jours* and a set of *Memoirs*, though the latter two remained unfinished.

## Descendants
Among his children were:
* **Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803-1857):** The prince of Canino and a scientist who married his cousin Zenaide Bonaparte in 1822. He published *American Ornithology* (1825-1833) and various other zoological catalogs on European birds, fish, and mammals. He was an honorary member of the academies of Berlin and Upsala and a correspondent of the Institute of France. Politically, he supported Italian independence and the expulsion of Austrians, serving as a deputy for Viterbo to the national assembly in 1848 before fleeing to Holland and France in 1849. He had eight surviving children, including a son who became a cardinal in 1868.
* **Louis Lucien (1813-1891):** Born at Thorngrove, Worcestershire, England, he lived there until 1848. He served as a deputy for Corsica and later for the Seine. Though named a senator and prince by Napoleon III, he remained largely inactive in politics during the Second Empire. After 1870, he returned to England to study philology, publishing works on the Basque language. He died without children.
