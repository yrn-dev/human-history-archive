# Parliamentary Reform, Right of Primogeniture Examined, and Defence of Joint-Stock Banks

## Parliamentary Reform in Great Britain
Parliamentary reform has been a recurring subject of political agitation and literary discourse in British history. In the early 19th century, figures such as Burdett sought the removal of Roman Catholic disabilities and the reform of parliament. By 1809, Burdett had proposed a scheme for parliamentary reform, and in 1817 and 1818, he suggested measures that anticipated the Chartist movement, including equal electoral districts, annual parliaments, the vote by ballot, and universal male suffrage, though these motions received little support.

Other figures contributed to the discourse through published works and speeches. Samuel Bailey, a Sheffield merchant and "philosophic radical," published a pamphlet titled *Discussion of Parliamentary Reform* and a book titled *Rationale of Political Representation* in 1835. John Bright also produced a significant body of work on the subject, including *Speeches on Parliamentary Reform*, which was revised by the author and published in 1866.

The complexities of reform are further illustrated by the writings of Burke. In his *Letters to Sir Hercules Langrishe*, Burke advocated for the admission of Irish Catholics to the franchise. This position appeared to contradict his previous attitudes regarding parliamentary reform and his opposition to the movement in France, although it was argued that his defense of Catholic relief was based on propositions specific to Ireland.

## The Right of Primogeniture
The right of primogeniture—the right of the firstborn child to inherit—has appeared in both legal discussions and historical conflicts. In the Roman Empire, the fierce Caracalla asserted the right of primogeniture during a period of intense competition with his brother, Geta. This discord between the sons of the emperor Severus threatened the stability of the throne, despite Severus's attempts to maintain an exact balance of favor by conferring the rank of Augustus and the name of Antoninus upon both sons.

In a different context, the subject was examined by the author Samuel Bailey, who published a pamphlet titled *Right of Primogeniture Examined*.

## Defence of Joint-Stock Banks
The financial structures of the 19th century were also subjects of intellectual defense. Samuel Bailey, who served as the chairman of the Sheffield Banking Company for many years, authored a pamphlet titled *Defence of Joint-Stock Banks*. This work was part of a broader series of publications by Bailey on economic and political subjects, which also included *Money and its Vicissitudes* (1837) and a *Critical Dissertation on the Nature, Measure, and Causes of Value* (1825), the latter of which was directed against the school of Ricardo.
