# Jahrbuch

The term *Jahrbuch* (German for "yearbook") appears in the archive's records across various academic, legal, and historical contexts, primarily as a title for periodic publications and scholarly journals used for the documentation of laws, philology, art, and history.

## Legal and Social Documentation
The *Jahrbuch fur Gesetzgebung*, published in Leipzig in 1886, 1888, and 1894, serves as a source for detailed descriptions of social reforms. These legal records are complemented by annual summaries of new laws found in the *Zeitschrift fur Staatswissenschaft* in Stuttgart.

## Philological and Linguistic Research
Several *Jahrbücher* are dedicated to the study of language and classical texts. The *Neue Jahrbücher für Philologie*, based in Leipzig, was edited by Klotz from 1831 to 1855. This publication included a memoir by Richard Klotz in the 1871 edition (pp. 154-163). Additionally, the *Jahrbucher fur Philologie* provided a record of a papyrus fragment related to Epicharmus in its 1889 volume (cxxxix). In the field of antiquity studies, the *Biographisches Jahrbuch fur Altertumskunde* (1890) contains an obituary notice by T. Gompertz that provides a full list of a specific author's writings.

## Art and Imperial History
The archive notes the use of *Jahrbücher* for the preservation of art history and imperial records. The *Jahrbuch der Samml. des Allerhochsten Kaiserhauses* in Vienna published a part i. in 1883, which includes a "Triumphzug des Kaisers Maximilians I." (p. 26 and letterpress, Bd. i. pp. 154-181). Furthermore, the *Jahrbucher* of the museums in Berlin and Vienna are cited as leading German art periodicals containing significant literature on Durer.

## Theology and Other Applications
The *Jahrbucher fur protestantische Theologie* is utilized for theological and geographical research; specifically, volume ix (1883) contains an article by H. Gelzer titled "Kosmas der Indienfahrer."
