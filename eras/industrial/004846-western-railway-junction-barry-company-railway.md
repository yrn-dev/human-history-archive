# Barry Railway and Associated Communications

## The Barry Railway System
The Barry railway serves the urban district and seaport of Barry in Glamorganshire, Wales, located on the Bristol Channel. The railway's terminus is at Barry, which is situated 153 miles by rail from London and 8 miles southwest of Cardiff. The line originates at Hafod in the Rhondda Valley, where it connects with the Taff Vale railway. 

The system includes several key junctions with the Taff Vale railway: at Treforest for Merthyr and Aberdare, and at Cogan for Penarth and Cardiff. Additionally, the Barry railway has junctions with the Great Western main line at St Fagans and Peterstone. A branch extending from the main line at Tyn-y-caeau provides connections to the Brecon & Merthyr railway, the London & North-Western railway, and the Rhymney railway.

## The Vale of Glamorgan Railway
The Vale of Glamorgan railway is worked by the Barry company. This line provides a direct route to Barry from the Garw, Ogmore, and Llynvi coalfields. It features a junction with the Great Western railway at Bridgend.

## Bridgend Junction
Bridgend is a market town in Glamorganshire located on both sides of the river Ogwr. It serves as a significant railway hub with a station situated 165 miles from London on the South Wales trunk line of the Great Western railway. Bridgend acts as the junction for the Barry Company's railway, which travels to Barry via Llantwit Major. Furthermore, the town serves as a natural outlet for the mining valleys of the two Ogwr rivers, the Garw, and the Llynvi, which are connected to Bridgend via Great Western railway branch lines.

## Engineering and Development
The development of the Barry railway company's infrastructure, specifically the Barry Docks near Cardiff, involved the work of the eminent engineer Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry. Sir John, who was articled to Sir John Hawkshaw and later became a K.C.B., was among the engineers engaged in the construction of these docks. His broader professional contributions to the field include the publication of *Railways and Locomotives* (1882) and *Railway Appliances* (1874).

## Geography and Demographics of Barry
The urban district of Barry comprises the ecclesiastical parishes of Cadoxton, Barry, and Merthyr-Dovan, as well as a portion of Sully. This area includes Barry Island, which covers 194 acres and is now joined to the mainland. The district experienced rapid population growth; while the total population of the area was approximately 500 in 1881 (with Barry village alone numbering 85), the population had risen to 27,030 by 1901.

The town is situated where a small brook named Barri enters the sea. Historically known in Welsh as Aber-Barri, the name is believed to be derived from a 7th-century Welsh saint named Baruch, who maintained a cell on the island. During the middle ages, his chapel served as a place of pilgrimage. In the late 11th century, a follower of Fitzhamon settled in Barry and constructed a castle, of which only a gateway remains today.
