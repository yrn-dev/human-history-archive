# British Honduras

British Honduras, formerly known as Belize or Balize, is a British crown colony located in Central America. It is bounded to the north and northwest by the Mexican province of Yucatan, to the northeast and east by the Bay of Honduras (an inlet of the Caribbean Sea), and to the south and west by Guatemala. As of 1905, the colony had an area of 7,562 square miles and a population of 40,372.

## Territorial Boundaries and Sovereignty
The legal sovereignty of Great Britain over the colony, including territories claimed in 1836, was confirmed through a series of treaties and agreements. While some have argued that the defeat of a Spanish attempt in 1798 established British sovereignty as an act of conquest, others note that a 1814 treaty with Spain revived earlier provisions. Furthermore, between 1817 and 1819, acts of parliament described Belize as "a settlement, for certain purposes, under the protection of His Majesty."

Following the independence of Central America between 1819 and 1822, Great Britain secured its position via a 1826 treaty with Mexico that incorporated the provisions of a 1786 treaty. Similar provisions were included in draft treaties with the United States of Central America (1831) and New Granada (1825). In 1836, the British claimed the territories situated between the Sarstoon and Belize rivers. Sovereignty was further solidified by the 1850 Clayton-Bulwer Treaty, which exempted Belize from the prohibition against Great Britain and the United States fortifying or colonizing the Central American mainland. Boundary disputes with Guatemala were settled in 1859.

The current frontier, defined by conventions in 1859 and 1893, begins at the mouth of the river Sarstoon in the Bay of Honduras, follows that river to the rapids of Gracias à Dios, turns right in a straight line to Garbutt's Rapids on the Belize river, and proceeds north to the Mexican frontier, where it follows the river Hondo to Chetumal Bay.

## Commerce and Finance
The colony's economy is characterized by a variety of exports, including timber, fruit, vegetable products, rum, tortoiseshell, turtles, sponges, and deerskins. Principal imports include hardware, cotton goods, spirits, wine, beer, groceries, and specie. This sea-borne trade is primarily shared between the United States and Great Britain.

Financial transitions occurred on October 14, 1894, when the American gold dollar replaced the Guatemalan dollar as the standard coin, ending the legal tender status of silver from Central, North, and South America. The currency system includes government notes in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, 50, and 100 dollars, as well as silver pieces (5, 10, 25, and 50 cents) and bronze one-cent pieces. The half sovereign and British sovereign remain legal tender.

Banking infrastructure includes a government savings bank founded in Belize in 1846, with subsequent branches in principal towns, and the British Bank of Honduras, established in Belize in 1903.

## Government and Public Finance
Revenue is derived chiefly from customs. Between 1901 and 1905, revenue rose from £60,150 to £68,335. During the same period, expenditure—of which education and the cost of police are important items—increased from £51,210 to £61,800. As of 1905, the public debt stood at £34,736, representing the balance of three loans raised in 1881, 1887, and 1891 for public works in Belize; these loans are repayable between 1916 and 1923.

## Security and Regional Relations
The colony faced periodic instability from rebel bands of Indians beyond the Hondo who rose against Mexican rulers between 1849 and 1901. These bands occasionally attacked the north-western and northern marches of the colony, though the last serious raid was foiled in 1872. In a separate diplomatic matter, the Bay Islands were recognized as part of the republic of Honduras in 1859.
