# BONNEVILLE, BENJAMIN L.E. (1795-1878)

Benjamin L.E. Bonneville was an American explorer and military engineer. Born in France around 1795, he emigrated to the United States during his early youth. He pursued a military education at the United States Military Academy at West Point, from which he graduated in 1815.

## Early Military Career and Exploration
Following his graduation, Bonneville was involved in the construction of military roads in the south-west region of the United States. He rose to the rank of captain of infantry in 1825. Between 1831 and 1836, Bonneville obtained a leave of absence from the army to lead an exploring expedition to the Rocky Mountains. This venture was conducted largely on his own responsibility.

During this expedition, he traveled up the Platte river, passing through areas that later became the states of Wyoming and Colorado. His journey took him into California and the Great Salt Lake basin. For several years, Bonneville was absolutely cut off from civilization, a period of absence that resulted in his name being struck from the army list.

## Literary Contributions
Upon his return, Bonneville provided a valuable and interesting account of his adventures. This journal was edited and amplified by Washington Irving and published in 1837 as a two-volume work titled *The Rocky Mountains: or Scenes, Incidents, and Adventures in the Far West; from the Journal of Captain Benjamin L.E. Bonnetville of the Army of the United States*. Later editions of this work were published under the title *The Adventures of Captain Bonneville, U.S.A., in the Rocky Mountains and the Far West*.

## Later Military Service and Rank
Bonneville eventually returned to active military status, achieving the rank of major in 1845. During the Mexican War, he was awarded the brevet rank of lieutenant-colonel for his gallantry displayed in the battles of Churubusco and Contreras. 

He attained the rank of colonel in 1855. In 1857, he commanded the Gila river expedition directed against the Apaches. Subsequently, from 1858 to 1861, he served as the commander of the department of New Mexico.

Although he retired in 1861, Bonneville continued to serve during the Civil War. During this conflict, he acted as a commandant of barracks and a recruiting officer at St Louis, Missouri. In 1865, he received the brevet rank of brigadier-general.

## Death and Legacy
Benjamin L.E. Bonneville died on June 12, 1878, at Fort Smith, Arkansas. In recognition of his contributions, the extinct glacial lake that once covered the region now known as north-western Utah was named in his honour.
