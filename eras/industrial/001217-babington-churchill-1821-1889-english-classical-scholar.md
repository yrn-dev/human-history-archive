# BABINGTON, CHURCHILL (1821-1889)

Churchill Babington was an English archaeologist and classical scholar. Born on March 11, 1821, at Roecliffe in Leicestershire, he received his early education from his father until the age of seventeen. Subsequently, he studied under the tuition of the archaeologist and orientalist Charles Wycliffe Goodwin.

## Academic Career and Clerical Life
Babington entered St John's College, Cambridge, in 1839. He graduated with a B.A. in 1843, achieving the rank of senior optime and placing seventh in the first class of the classical tripos. In 1845, he was awarded the Hulsean Prize for an essay titled *The Influence of Christianity in promoting the Abolition of Slavery in Europe*. 

He was elected to a fellowship in 1846, the same year he took orders and proceeded to the degree of M.A. He later obtained a D.D. in 1879. Babington's clerical career included serving as the vicar of Horningsea, near Cambridge, from 1848 to 1861, and as the vicar of Cockfield in Suffolk from 1866 until his death on January 12, 1889.

## Professorship and Archaeology
From 1865 to 1880, Babington held the Disney professorship of archaeology at Cambridge. His lectures focused primarily on numismatics and Greek and Roman pottery, which he illustrated using his own collections of vases and coins. His archaeological and cataloging work extended to the Fitzwilliam museum, where he catalogued Greek and English coins, and the University Library, where he catalogued classical manuscripts. He also published *Roman Antiquities found at Rougham* in 1872 and produced works (in 1855 and 1865) regarding inscriptions found in Crete by the explorer T. A. B. Spratt.

## Classical Scholarship and Publications
Babington achieved significant fame as a Greek scholar through his editions of the speeches of Hypereides, based on papyri discovered at Thebes, Egypt, in 1847 and 1856. These publications included *Against Demosthenes* (1850), *On Behalf of Lycophron and Euxenippus* (1853), and the *Funeral Oration* (1858).

His scholarly interests were diverse, extending to the Reformation period. In 1855, he published a facsimile edition of the *editio princeps* (published in Venice in 1543) of *Benefizio della Morte di Cristo*, a book attributed to Paleario. This work, which had been nearly destroyed by the Inquisition, was published by Babington with an introduction and versions in English and French.

Other editorial works include:
* The first two volumes of Higden's *Polychronicon* (1858).
* Bishop Pecock's *Repressor of Overmuch Blaming of the Clergy* (1860), which he undertook at the request of the Master of the Rolls.
* An *Introductory Lecture on Archaeology* (1865).

## Natural History and Other Interests
Described as a many-sided man, Babington developed a taste for natural history due to his early familiarity with country life. He became an authority on conchology and held interests in ornithology and botany. His contributions to these fields include:
* Appendices on ornithology and (partially) botany in Potter's *History and Antiquities of Charnwood Forest* (1842).
* *Catalogue of Birds of Suffolk* (1884-1886).
* *Flora of Suffolk* (1889), co-authored with W. M. Hind.

Additionally, in 1849, he wrote *Mr Macaulay's Character of the Clergy ... considered*, a work defending the 17th-century clergy against the strictures of Macaulay; this publication received the approval of Mr Gladstone.
