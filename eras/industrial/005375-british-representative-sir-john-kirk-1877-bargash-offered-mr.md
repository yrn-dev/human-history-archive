# Sir John Kirk

Sir John Kirk (1832–) was a British administrator and naturalist who played a central role in European intervention in East Africa during the late 19th century. Born on December 19, 1832, at Barry, near Arbroath, he was the son of the Rev. John Kirk. Kirk was educated in Edinburgh for the medical profession and served on the civil medical staff during the Crimean War.

## Early Expeditions and Medical Career
In February 1858, Kirk was appointed as the naturalist and physician for David Livingstone's second expedition to Central Africa. For the following five years, he accompanied Livingstone on most of his travels. On September 16, 1859, Kirk became one of the first four white men to see Lake Nyassa. He remained with the expedition until May 9, 1863, when he was invalided home.

## Administration in Zanzibar
The reputation Kirk established during his time in Central Africa led to his appointment in January 1866 as the acting surgeon to the political agency at Zanzibar. His role expanded over the next two decades; he became assistant political agent in 1868, was promoted to consul-general in 1873, and attained the rank of agent in 1880. He eventually retired from this post in 1887.

During his twenty-one years in Zanzibar, Kirk was the virtual ruler of the country for much of that time. He maintained a controlling influence over Seyyid Bargash, who became sultan in 1870. Following the unsuccessful efforts of Sir Bartle Frere, Kirk succeeded in securing the sultan's signature on June 5, 1873, to a treaty that abolished the slave trade within the sultan's dominions.

## Territorial Negotiations and Diplomacy
Kirk was a key figure in the diplomatic struggles over mainland territories. In 1877, Sultan Bargash offered a lease of his mainland territories to Sir William Mackinnon, a British merchant and chairman of the British India Steam Navigation Company. This offer included rights of sovereignty and the administration of customs for 70 years. While the British foreign office declined the offer due to a lack of support, Bargash provided Kirk with a declaration pledging not to cede territory to any power other than Great Britain. This declaration was ignored by the British government.

In 1885, when Germany claimed districts that the sultan considered part of Zanzibar, Kirk intervened. He prevented Bargash from traveling to Berlin in person to protest and instead induced the sultan to submit to the dismemberment of his dominions. Through subsequent delicate negotiations, Kirk worked to prevent German designs from supplanting the British in Zanzibar, achieving this while preserving the Arab form of government.

## Later Influence and the Imperial British East Africa Company
Kirk continued to direct efforts to secure mainland territory for Britain. In May 1887, Bargash granted a concession for the administration of mainland territory—excluding the region recognized as the German sphere of operations—to an association formed by Mackinnon. By international agreement, the sultan's mainland territories were defined as extending 10 miles inland from the coast. 

This association, which aimed to open the hinterland beyond the ten-mile strip, became the Imperial British East Africa Company via a founder's agreement in April 1888 and received a royal charter in September 1888. The sultan provided a further concession to the company in October 1888.
