# Incorporation and Governance in 1838

The year 1838 marked significant administrative changes for several municipalities across different regions, including the granting of charters and the establishment of new governing bodies.

## Birmingham
In the early 19th century, the government of Birmingham was managed by a system of street commissioners. This system consisted of thirteen different bodies, which caused significant difficulty and annoyance. Consequently, there was a demand for the town to be incorporated as a borough. 

In 1838, the crown granted a charter that vested the general government of Birmingham in a mayor, forty-seven councillors, and sixteen aldermen. Despite this incorporation, the powers of this new body were unusually restricted, as other local governing bodies continued to exist. It was not until 1851 that an act of parliament abolished all other governing authorities and transferred all powers to the town council.

## Burlington
In the United States, the settlement of Burlington, Iowa, began in 1833 following the Black Hawk War. By 1838, the population of the area had reached 1,200. During this year, Burlington was chartered as a city by the territory of Wisconsin. This charter was later amended by the territory of Iowa in 1839 and 1841. Additionally, from 1838 to 1840, the territorial legislature of Iowa met in Burlington.
