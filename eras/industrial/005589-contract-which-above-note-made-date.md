# Contract Notes and Dating in Commercial and Legal Agreements

In the context of commercial trade and legal formalities, the contract note serves as a critical memorandum of an agreement. Its function varies depending on the nature of the trade, the rules of the governing exchange, and the legal requirements for enforceability.

## The Liverpool Cotton Association Contract Note
In the American cotton trade, a specific "Delivery Contract Note" is utilized in Liverpool. This document specifies the net weight of American cotton in pounds and the number of American bales to be delivered. The contract is established on a per-pound basis and is governed by the bye-laws, rules, and Clearing House regulations of the Liverpool Cotton Association, Limited.

A key feature of this contract note is the explicit statement that the agreement is made strictly between the parties involved and not with any other person, regardless of whether that person was disclosed or if the contract was entered into for their benefit. The note further specifies that the contract was made on a specified date within the business hours fixed by the Liverpool Cotton Association, Limited.

## The Role of the Broker and the Principal Clause
The use of contract notes often reflects the evolving relationship between a broker and their principal. In many exchanges, rules modify ordinary law to establish that all members are principals; consequently, no agents are recognized on the market. A broker employed by a non-member is treated as buying for themselves and is personally liable for the contract. In futures contracts, the broker must adhere to weekly settlement rules and is responsible for any difference between the contract and market price if the principal fails to take delivery.

To manage these liabilities, exchanges provide printed contract forms for members to use with non-members. These forms often include a "principal clause," which stipulates that the contract is made with the broker as the principal. The purpose of this clause is to allow the broker to buy from or sell to their principal on their own account, rather than acting as an agent to secure a contract with a third party. By employing a broker based on these printed forms, the principal binds themselves to the modifications of the relationship established by the rules of the exchange, such as those of the Liverpool Cotton Association.

## Legal Enforceability and Memoranda
Under English law, specifically section 4 of the Sale of Goods Act 1893 (which re-enacts section 17 of the Statute of Frauds), certain formalities are required to make a contract for the sale of goods legally enforceable. For goods valued at ten pounds or more, the contract is not enforceable unless:
* The buyer accepts and receives part of the goods as sold;
* Something is given in earnest or as part payment to bind the contract;
* A note or memorandum of the contract is made and signed by the party to be charged or their agent.

Modern business typically utilizes the "contract note" as this written memorandum. The sufficiency of a contract note as a legal memorandum depends on several factors, including whether one or two brokers were involved, whether notes were exchanged between brokers, or if they were rendered by each broker to their respective clients.

## Brokerage Procedures and "Bought and Sold" Notes
The method of documenting a sale depends on the number of brokers employed:
* **Single Broker:** If one broker represents both buyer and seller, "bought and sold" notes provide the necessary memorandum.
* **Two Brokers (One dropping out):** If two brokers are employed but one exits after terms are negotiated, the remaining broker signs and sends notes to both parties, acting as the agent for both for the purpose of the statutory memorandum.
* **Two Brokers (Both remaining):** If both brokers remain, each acts only as the agent for their own principal. The buyer's broker sends a note to the seller's broker (e.g., "I, acting on account of A. B... have this day bought from you, acting on account of C. D..."), and a corresponding note is received in return. This ensures both parties have a signed memorandum upon which they can sue.

Historically, there was judicial debate in the early 19th century regarding whether a broker's book entry or "bought and sold" notes constituted a statutory memorandum. However, modern brokers are no longer regulated by statute in London or elsewhere and generally do not keep formal books.

## Historical Dating of Contracts
The practice of dating documents has evolved from primitive states to fixed chronological standards. Early documents were often dated by noticeable events. For example, contract-tablets from the age of Sargon of Agade (dated to either 3800 B.C. or 2800 B.C.) used phrases such as "In the year in which Sargon conquered the land of Amurru." Similarly, tablets from the time of Samsu-ditana (c. 1950 B.C.) referenced the year he made the statue of Marduk.

Over time, the use of regnal years of kings became general. The Babylonians and Assyrians are noted as likely being the first to employ a fixed chronological standard, as evidenced by numerous list of kings, yearly officials, and contract-tablets.
