# Chicago & North-Western Railway, Knitting Works, and Manufactories

## The Chicago & North-Western Railway
The Chicago & North-Western railway is a significant transportation line serving various cities across the Midwestern United States. In Wisconsin, it provides service to Ashland, a port city at the head of Chequamegon Bay, as well as Beloit, located on the Rock river, and De Pere, situated on the Fox river. In the state of Iowa, the railway serves the city of Boone, where it maintains both construction and repair shops. Additionally, the railway extends into Illinois, serving the city of Belvidere in Boone county, and into Michigan, where it serves the city of Marquette on the south shore of Lake Superior. In Ashland, the railway operates specific repair shops.

## Knitting Works
Knitting works are established in several distinct urban centers across the United States. In Wisconsin, these works are found in the city of Ashland and in De Pere. Outside of Wisconsin, knitting mills are also located in Danville, a city in Pittsylvania county, Virginia.

## Manufactories and Industrial Production
Industrial manufacturing varies by region, with specific cities specializing in diverse goods and materials.

### Wisconsin Manufactories
In Ashland, the industrial landscape includes large saw-mills, foundries, machine shops, and iron and steel rolling mills. The city also produces charcoal, wood-alcohol, sulphite fibre, and dynamite. As of 1905, the total value of Ashland's factory product was $4,210,265. 

In De Pere, manufacturing is supported by excellent water-power and includes paper mills, flour mills, machine shops, and brick yards. The city also features dairies, cheese factories, and factories producing sashes, doors, blinds, and pleasure-boats and launches.

Beloit utilizes the water-power of the Rock river to manufacture a wide array of products, including steam pumps, windmills, gas engines, cutlery, flour, and ladies' shoes. The city also produces paper, paper-mill machinery, wood-working machinery, and ploughs, as well as cyclometers. In 1905, the total value of Beloit's factory product was $4,485,224, representing a 60.2% increase over the value in 1900.

### Other Regional Manufactories
In Illinois, the city of Belvidere produces automobiles, bicycles, pianos, boilers, and sewing machines. Its manufacturing output also includes corsets, gloves, mittens, flour, and dairy products, including a Borden's condensed milk factory. In Iowa, the city of Boone serves as an important coal centre and manufactures bricks and tiles from nearby clay, while also operating a packing plant for pork and beef products.

In Michigan, Marquette is known for its charcoal iron blast-furnaces that manufacture pig-iron. These furnaces produce wood alcohol and acetic acid as by-products recovered from charcoal pit smoke. The city's economic importance is further supported by brewery and bottling works, planing-mills and lumber mills, foundries, railway machine-shops, and brownish-red sandstone quarries. The value of Marquette's factory products rose from $1,585,083 in 1900 to $2,364,081 in 1905.

In Virginia, Danville utilizes the water-power of the Dan river for its manufactories. The city is a leading producer of cotton goods in the state and operates several tobacco factories, as well as overall and furniture factories, alongside its flour and knitting mills. In 1905, the "factory" product of Danville was valued at $4,774,818.
