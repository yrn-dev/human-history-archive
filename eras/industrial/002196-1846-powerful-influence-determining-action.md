## The Influence of Edward Baines
In 1846, the actions of the government were powerfully influenced by letters written to Lord John Russell by Edward Baines. Baines (1774–1848) was an English politician and newspaper proprietor who had previously served as a member of parliament for Leeds from 1834 until his resignation in 1841. 

During his time in parliament, Baines supported the Liberal party, though he maintained independent views. He was a strong advocate for the separation of church and state and opposed any government interference in national education. It was on this specific question of national education that he addressed his letters to Lord John Russell, which subsequently played a significant role in determining the government's course of action.

## Career of Edward Baines
Born in Walton-le-Dale, Lancashire, Baines was educated at the grammar schools of Preston and Hawkshead. He began his professional life as an apprentice to a printer in Preston, later moving to Leeds to complete his apprenticeship and establish his own business. Known for his practical ability and shrewdness, Baines took a deep interest in social and political movements, which led him to join the Independents and sympathize with nonconformity.

In 1801, Baines acquired the *Leeds Mercury*. At the time, provincial newspapers lacked significant influence and lacked a system of reporting or "leading articles." Baines transformed the *Mercury* by introducing able political articles, turning the publication into the organ of Liberal opinion in Leeds. This connection provided the Baines family with powerful influence for many years.

Beyond his journalistic work, Baines was an ardent supporter of parliamentary reform. His influence was instrumental in the return of Macaulay for Leeds in 1832. Baines himself succeeded Macaulay in 1834 and was re-elected in 1835 and 1837. In addition to his political career, he authored several historical works, including the *History of the Wars of Napoleon* and directories and gazetteers for the counties of York and Lancaster.
