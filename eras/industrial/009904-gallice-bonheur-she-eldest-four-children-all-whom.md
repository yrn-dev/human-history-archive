# Rosa Bonheur

## Early Life and Family
Marie Rosalie Bonheur, known as Rosa Bonheur, was a French painter born in Bordeaux on March 22, 1822. She was of Jewish origin. According to the Belgian medallist Jacques Wiener, a native of Venloo, he and Rosa's father, Raymond Bonheur, attended synagogue in that town. Another source indicates that Rosa was referred to in common parlance as Rosa Mazeltov; "Mazeltov" is a Hebrew term meaning "good luck," which translates in French (*Gallice*) to "Bonheur."

Rosa was the eldest of four children, all of whom pursued careers as artists. Her siblings included Auguste (1824–1884), who painted landscapes and animals; Isidore (born 1827), a sculptor of animals; and Juliette (1830–1891), who received an "honourable mention" at the exhibition of 1855.

## Artistic Development
Rosa was taught to draw at a young age by her father. Recognizing her remarkable talent, Raymond Bonheur permitted her to abandon the business of dressmaking—a profession she had been placed in against her will—so that she could devote herself entirely to art. Her father died in 1849.

Between 1840 and 1845, Bonheur exhibited her work at the salon, where she received a prize five times. She was further recognized in 1848 when she was awarded a medal.

## Style and Recognition
Rosa Bonheur's fame increased significantly following the exhibition of 1855. From that point forward, her works became highly sought after in England, leading to eager competition for her pieces between public galleries and private collectors.

Her work is characterized by a commitment to representing animals as they truly are, based on her observations of them in the country, a trait she shared with her contemporary Jacques Raymond Brascassat (1804–1867). While her anatomy of animals is described as faultlessly true and her handling as firm and "manly," some critics noted a certain dryness of style and a frequent failure to convey a perfect sense of atmosphere.

## Notable Works and Honors
Among her many works, the archive notes the following:
*   "Hay Harvest in Auvergne" (1835)
*   "Ploughing in the Nivernais" (1848), located in the Luxembourg gallery
*   "The Horse Fair" (1853), for which the original is in the United States and one of two replicas is held in the National Gallery, London.

In recognition of her achievements, the empress Eugenie decorated her with the Legion of Honour. Bonheur was later promoted to the rank of "officer" within the order.
