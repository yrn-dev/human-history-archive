# British Association Committees

The British Association has appointed various committees throughout the 19th century to conduct scientific investigations, establish industrial standards, and evaluate historical technical claims. These committees have operated across diverse fields, including electrical engineering, archaeology, and the history of computing.

## Electrical Standards Committee
In 1861, a committee was established by the British Association for the determination of electrical standards. The formation of this committee was prompted by a paper on electrical standards read before the Association in the same year by Sir Charles Tilston Bright and his partner, Josiah Latimer Clark. Additionally, Thomson induced the British Association to appoint this first famous committee. 

Thomson played a significant role in suggesting much of the work carried out by the committee's members, which included Fleeming Jenkin, Balfour Stewart, and J. Clerk Maxwell. The work produced by this committee formed the foundations of the system of electrical standards that remained in use.

## Archaeological Investigations at Kent's Cavern
The British Association also utilized committees to verify scientific discoveries regarding early human existence. In Devon, England, the Rev. J. McEnery had examined Kent's Cavern in 1825, finding worked flints associated with the bones of extinct mammals. While his contemporaries initially rejected the idea that man existed in Devon contemporaneously with these animals, the discoveries were later verified by Godwin Austen and ultimately by a Committee of the British Association.

From 1865 to 1880, this committee conducted explorations of the cavern under the guidance of Wm. Pengelly. Their work analyzed four distinct strata within the cave:
1. A surface of dark earth containing Roman pottery and remains from the Neolithic, Bronze, and Iron Ages.
2. A stalagmite floor between 1 and 3 feet thick.
3. A layer of red earth containing an engraved antler, flint implements, and bones of animals such as the rhinoceros, mammoth, lion, and hyaena. In some areas, a black band of soil containing charcoal and perforated animal teeth was also found.
4. A bottom layer of hard breccia, up to 12 feet thick, containing flint implements and the remains of bears.

The cavern is particularly noted for remains of the Sabre-toothed tiger (*Machairodus latidens*).

## Evaluation of the Analytical Engine
The British Association appointed a committee to investigate the work of Babbage regarding his "analytical engine," a machine designed to perform any series of possible arithmetical operations using punched cards. While it had been long assumed that Babbage had left complete plans for the machine, the committee reached a different conclusion in its 1878 report.

The committee determined that no detailed working drawings existed. They concluded that the available drawings were merely diagrammatic and were not sufficient for a draughtsman to create working plans. Consequently, the committee stated that the design was "not more than a theoretical possibility" in its current state.
