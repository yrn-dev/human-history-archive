# Events of 1848-1849

The years 1848 and 1849 were marked by significant political upheavals, legislative shifts, and institutional changes across Europe, particularly in France and Austria.

## Political and Parliamentary Activity in Austria
Following a rising in 1848, the Austrian parliament convened in the town of Kremsier, located in Moravia. The parliament met within the palace at Kremsier from November 1848 until March 1849. Kremsier is a town situated on the March in the Hanna region, which serves as the summer residence for the bishop of Olmütz.

## Revolutions and Governance in France
France experienced a revolution in 1848, leading to several shifts in leadership and governance. On February 24, 1848, Lazare Hippolyte Carnot pronounced in favor of the republic. In the resulting provisional government, Lamartine appointed Carnot as the minister of education. During his tenure, Carnot worked to organize primary school systems, proposing laws for free and obligatory primary instruction as well as secondary education for girls. However, Carnot's opposition to purely secular schools—arguing that the schoolmaster and the minister were the two columns supporting the republic—led to his resignation on July 5, 1848.

The revolution also impacted the Bonaparte family. Louis Lucien, the son of Lucien Bonaparte, arrived in France from England in 1848. On November 28, 1848, he was elected as the deputy for Corsica. Although this election was later invalidated, he was returned as a deputy for the Seine in June 1849.

## Financial Measures of the Bank of France
During this period of instability, the Bank of France extended the limits of its note circulation on several occasions. On March 15, 1848, the limit was set at 350 million francs (equivalent to £14,000,000). This was increased to 452 million francs (£18,000,000) on April 27 and May 2, 1848. By December 2, 1849, the circulation limit was further extended to 525 million francs (£21,000,000).

## Academic and Literary Developments
While political unrest occurred, various scholars and authors continued their work. Hermann Bonitz, a German scholar, served as a professor at the university of Vienna in 1849. His academic output during this specific window included the work *Aristotelis Metaphysica*, published between 1848 and 1849.

In the literary world, the Brontë sisters saw the publication of their works. Anne Brontë's *The Tenant of Wildfell Hall* was published in June 1848. This period also marked a time of personal loss for the family; Branwell Brontë died on September 24, 1848, followed by Emily Brontë on December 19, 1848. Anne Brontë died on May 28, 1849, in Scarborough.
