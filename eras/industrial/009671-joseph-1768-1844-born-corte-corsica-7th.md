# I. JOSEPH (1768-1844)

## Early Life and Education
I. Joseph was born on January 7, 1768, in the town of Corte, located in central Corsica. He was the first surviving son of Carlo Buonaparte (1746-1785) and Letizia Ramolino. His father, a law graduate from the university of Pisa, served as an assessor to the royal court of Ajaccio and the neighboring districts following the French conquest of Corsica. Carlo Buonaparte was described as a schemer by nature and a Voltairian by conviction, whose efforts to open careers in France influenced the family's fortunes. In contrast, Joseph's mother, Letizia, was devout, simple, and frugal, helping to maintain her children's connection to Corsica.

Joseph received his early education at a college in Autun, France. In 1784, shortly after his father's death, he returned to Corsica and pursued further studies in law at the university of Pisa.

## Early Career and Political Alignment
By June 1788, Joseph had become a barrister in Bastia. He soon entered local politics and was elected as a councillor of the municipality of Ajaccio. Along with his brothers, Lucien and Napoleon, Joseph aligned himself with the democratic or French side. Following the victory of the Paolist party, Joseph and his family fled Corsica to seek refuge in France.

After a brief period in Paris, where he found himself disgusted by the excesses of the Jacobins, Joseph settled in Marseilles. There, he married Mlle Julie Clary, the daughter of a local merchant. During this time, the Bonaparte family moved frequently to coordinate measures intended to recover Corsica.

## Diplomatic and Military Activity
Joseph participated in the efforts to reclaim Corsica, including a mission to Genoa in 1795. In 1796, he accompanied his brother Napoleon during the early stages of the Italian campaign. He was involved in negotiations with Sardinia that resulted in the armistice of Cherasco on April 28, and he was the one to deliver the news of this armistice to the French government.

Subsequently, Joseph traveled to Leghorn and took part in the French expedition to recover Corsica. Working alongside Miot de Melito, a commissioner of the French Republic, he assisted in the reorganization of the island.

## Diplomatic Missions and Parliamentary Life
In March 1797, the Directory appointed Joseph as the minister to the court of Parma. By the early summer of that year, he proceeded to Rome in the same capacity. During his tenure, tensions rose between the French Republic and the Vatican. It is indicated that the French Directory and Napoleon ordered Joseph to encourage revolutionary movements within Rome.

On December 28, 1797, a disturbance occurred opposite the French embassy, resulting in the death of General Leonard Duphot. Following this event, Joseph left Rome, which shortly thereafter became a republic.

Joseph then returned to Paris to enter parliamentary life. He served as one of the members representing Corsica in the Council of Five Hundred. He did not make a significant mark during his time in the chamber and retired from this position in 1799.
