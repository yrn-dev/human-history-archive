# J. G. Oncken and the Modern Baptist Movement in Europe

## The Establishment of the Hamburg Church
J. G. Oncken (1800-1884) was a central figure in the development of the modern Baptist movement within Europe. In 1834, Oncken formed the first Baptist church in Hamburg. This event served as a catalyst for the expansion of the movement across various European nations.

## Expansion in Protestant Countries and Russia
In Protestant countries and in Russia, the Baptist movement grew without the intervention of missionaries from America or England. Following the establishment of the church in Hamburg, Baptist churches were formed in several other countries during the 19th century. The timeline of this expansion includes:
*   **Denmark:** 1839
*   **Holland and Sweden:** 1848
*   **Switzerland:** 1849
*   **Norway:** 1860
*   **Austria and Rumania:** 1869
*   **Hungary:** 1871
*   **Bulgaria:** 1884

Additionally, Baptist churches began to be established in Finland and Russia during the sixties and fifties.

## Development in Roman Catholic Countries
The emergence of Baptist churches in Roman Catholic nations differed from the pattern seen in Protestant regions, as these were established by missionaries arriving from America or England. The progression of this work occurred as follows:
*   **France:** Work began in 1832.
*   **Italy:** Missions were started in 1866 (the Spezia Mission) and again in 1884 via the Baptist Missionary Society, which also maintained a mission in Brittany.
*   **Spain:** Churches were formed in 1888.

## Baptist Presence in British Colonies
Beyond the European continent, Baptists established a considerable presence in various British colonies. Unions of Baptist churches were formed in New Zealand, Canada (where four Unions exist), and several Australian colonies, including Queensland, Tasmania, Victoria, Western Australia, South Australia, and New South Wales. In South Africa, where a union of Baptist churches was also formed, the work was supported by the Baptist South African Missionary and Colonial Aid Society, which was based in London.
