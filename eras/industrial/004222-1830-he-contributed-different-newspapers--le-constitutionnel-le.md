# Jules Barthelemy Saint-Hilaire

Jules Barthelemy Saint-Hilaire (1805–1895) was a French statesman and philosopher. Born in Paris on August 19, 1805, he spent his early professional years as an active political journalist.

## Journalistic and Early Political Career
Between 1826 and 1830, Saint-Hilaire used the publication *Le Globe* to oppose the reactionary policies of the king. During the revolution of 1830, he participated in the protestation of the journalists, signing the document on July 28, 1830. Following these events, he contributed to several different newspapers, including *Le National*, *Le Constitutionnel*, and the *Courrier francais*. He continued this journalistic work until 1833.

## Academic Pursuits and Philosophy
In 1833, Saint-Hilaire ceased his involvement in politics to focus on the history of ancient philosophy. He undertook a translation of Aristotle, a project that occupied the majority of his life from 1837 to 1892. This scholarly work earned him a significant reputation, leading to his appointment to the chair of ancient philosophy at the College de France in 1838. Additionally, he was granted a seat at the Academy of Moral and Political Science in 1839.

## Statesmanship and Political Life
Saint-Hilaire returned to political life after the revolution of 1848, when he was elected as a republican deputy. However, he was forced to withdraw from this position following the *coup d'etat* of Louis Napoleon. 

In 1855, he served as a member of an international commission sent to Egypt to report on the feasibility of the proposed Suez canal. Through the articles he wrote regarding this mission, he played a large role in making the canal project popular within France.

He was elected as a deputy again in 1869, at which time he joined the opposition to the Empire. In 1871, he dedicated his efforts to the election of Thiers as the president of the republic and subsequently served as Thiers' secretary. In 1875, he was appointed senator for life, aligning himself with the moderate republicans. From September 1880 to November 1881, he served as the minister of foreign affairs in the cabinet of Jules Ferry. The most significant event of his tenure as minister was the annexation of Tunis as a French protectorate, a move he actively promoted.

## Literary Works and Death
Beyond his translation of Aristotle and related studies, Saint-Hilaire authored several principal works, including *Des Vedas* (1854), *Du Bouddhisme* (1856), and *Mahomet et le Coran* (1865). He died on November 24, 1895.
