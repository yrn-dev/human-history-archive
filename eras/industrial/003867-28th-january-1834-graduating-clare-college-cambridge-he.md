# Sabine Baring-Gould

## Early Life and Education
Sabine Baring-Gould was an English novelist born in Exeter on the 28th of January 1834. He pursued his higher education at Clare College, Cambridge, where he graduated. Following his graduation, he spent several years traveling.

## Ecclesiastical Career
Baring-Gould entered the clergy in the 1860s. In 1864, he became the curate of Horbury, located in Yorkshire. He continued his service in the same county, becoming the perpetual curate of Dalton in 1867. By 1871, he had moved to Essex, where he served as the rector of East Mersea.

In 1872, following the death of his father, Baring-Gould inherited the estate of Lew Trenchard in North Devon. His family had been settled at Lew Trenchard for nearly three centuries. Consequently, in 1881, he exchanged his living in Essex for the rectory of Lew Trenchard.

## Literary Works and Research
Baring-Gould was a prolific writer with a "ready pen," publishing works starting in 1854. His writing spanned a wide array of subjects, including religion, mythology, history, travel, folk-lore, and fiction.

### Fiction
He authored several novels, including:

*   *John Herring* (1883), a tale centered on the west country.
*   *Court Royal* (1886).
*   *Red Spider* (1887).
*   *The Pennycomequicks* (1889).
*   *Cheap Jack Zita* (1893).
*   *Broom Squire* (1896), a tale of Sussex.

### Research and Theology
Beyond fiction, Baring-Gould contributed to the study of antiquities, topography, and folk-lore. While these works were written for a popular audience, they were characterized by real learning and serious research. Notable examples include *Book of Were-wolves* (1865), *Curious Myths of the Middle Ages* (1866), and *Curious Survivals* (1892).

Additionally, he wrote numerous volumes of popular theology and sermons. Between 1871 and 1873, he served as the editor of *The Sacristy*, which was a quarterly review focusing on ecclesiastical literature and art.
