# Boydell's Shakespeare Gallery

Boydell's Shakespeare Gallery was a celebrated artistic project organized by Alderman Boydell. The gallery served as a patron for various artists, commissioning works based on the plays and themes of William Shakespeare.

## Contributing Artists
The gallery featured contributions from several prominent artists of the era. Henry Fuseli, who returned to England in early 1779, found a commission awaiting him from Alderman Boydell for the organization of the gallery. Fuseli painted a number of pieces for the patron. 

Additionally, the Italian engraver Francesco Bartolozzi contributed a number of plates to the *Shakespeare Gallery*. Bartolozzi, who resided in London for nearly forty years starting in 1764, was known for his skill in designing and engraving, often working from the pieces of Angelica Kauffmann and Cipriani.

## Influence and Related Projects
The success and concept of Boydell's Shakespeare Gallery inspired other artistic endeavors. Henry Fuseli, for example, sought to create a corresponding "Milton gallery" based on the works of John Milton. Between 1790 and 1799, Fuseli executed forty-seven paintings—many of them very large—for this purpose. He exhibited this series in 1799, though the exhibition proved to be a failure in terms of profit when it closed in 1800.

## Related Shakespearian Works
While not directly part of the gallery's organization, other artists and scholars have produced significant works related to Shakespeare. The English sculptor Thomas Banks created a colossal group titled "Shakespeare attended by Painting and Poetry," which is located in the garden of New Place in Stratford-on-Avon. In the realm of scholarship, Horace Howard Furness, an American scholar born in 1833, produced a comprehensive Variorum edition of Shakespeare's plays between 1871 and 1904, summarizing the conclusions of critical, textual, and annotative authorities.
