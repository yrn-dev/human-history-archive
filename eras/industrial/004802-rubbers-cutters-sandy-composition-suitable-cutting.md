# Rubbers or Cutters

In the context of brickmaking within Great Britain, "Rubbers" or "Cutters" refer to a specific quality of brick characterized by a sandy composition. Because of this composition, these bricks are particularly suitable for being cut with a wire saw and subsequently rubbed to shape on a stone slab.

## Classification Among British Bricks
The terminology used for different qualities of bricks in Great Britain is noted as being most confusing. Rubbers and Cutters exist alongside several other categorized types of bricks, which are distinguished by their burn quality, shape, and intended use:

*   **Stocks:** These are sound, hard, and well-burnt bricks used for ordinary purposes.
*   **Hard Stocks:** While sound, these are over-burnt and are typically utilized in positions where a good appearance is not required, such as the footings to walls.
*   **Shippers:** These are hard-burnt and sound bricks that possess an imperfect shape; they are frequently used as ballast for ships.
*   **Grizzles:** These are sound bricks of fair shape that are under-burnt, making them suitable for inferior work or areas not subject to heavy loads.
*   **Place-bricks:** These are defective and under-burnt, used primarily for temporary work.
*   **Chuffs:** These are badly burnt bricks that are cracked and defective in shape.
*   **Burrs:** These are lumps that have run together or vitrified during the burning process, used for garden work and rough walling.
*   **Pressed bricks:** These are moulded under hydraulic pressure and are commonly used for facing work. They often feature a deep hollow, or frog, on one or both horizontal faces to reduce weight and provide a key for mortar.
