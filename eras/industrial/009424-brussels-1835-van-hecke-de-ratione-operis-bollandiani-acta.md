# The Bollandists and the Acta Sanctorum

The Bollandists are Belgian Jesuits responsible for the publication of the *Acta Sanctorum*, a comprehensive collection of legends and biographies of the saints. The work is organized by the days of the calendar.

## Origins and Early Development
The original concept for the project was conceived by the Jesuit father Heribert Rosweyde. In 1607, Rosweyde issued a prospectus titled *Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis Bibliothecis manuscriptae*, in which he outlined his intention to publish the lives of the saints compiled from manuscripts across eighteen volumes, accompanied by sober notes. Although Rosweyde collected extensive material, he died in 1629 before the work could begin.

The project was then handed to Father John Bolland, who was sent to Antwerp to continue the collection of material and expand the scope of the work. The first two volumes, covering January, were published in 1643. Subsequent volumes were released over the following decades: February in 1658, March in 1668, and April in 1675. During this period, Bolland was joined by collaborators, including Godfried Henschenius (Henschenius), who worked with him from 1635 until 1681, and Daniel van Papenbroeck (Papebroch), who collaborated from 1659 to 1714. The era of Papebroch is regarded as the most brilliant period of the *Acta Sanctorum*, though his critical approach earned him many enemies and required frequent self-defense.

## Interruptions and Relocations
The work continued until the suppression of the Society of Jesus in 1773, with the final volume published at that time being volume iii of October in 1770. Following the dispersion of the Jesuits, the Bollandists received authorization to continue their work. They remained in Antwerp until 1778, when they were transferred to Brussels to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg. While at Coudenberg, they published volume iv of October in 1780 and volume v of October in 1886.

The work ceased in 1788 after the suppression of the monastery of Coudenberg. The remains of the Bollandist library were acquired by the Premonstratensians of Tongerloo. This group attempted to maintain the project, publishing volume vi of October in 1794 at their abbey.

## Re-establishment and Modern Series
The work was resumed in 1837 following the re-establishment of the Society of Jesus in Belgium. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and at the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels.

A new series began in 1845 with the publication of volume vii of October, eventually reaching volume xiii of October in 1883. This series featured the collaboration of several Jesuit fathers: Joseph van Hecke, Joseph van der Moere, Benjamin Bossue, Remi and Victor de Buck, Edu. Carpentier, Ant. Tinnebroeck, and Henr. Matagne. Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan edited the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which appeared in 1864 at the beginning of volume xi of October.

## Scientific Evolution and Analecta Bollandiana
In 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align the work with progress in historical methods. Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt established a quarterly review called *Analecta Bollandiana*. By 1906, this review had reached its 25th volume, edited by P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt. The review serves as a venue for dissertations, inedited texts, and studies intended to remould and continue the *Acta Sanctorum*. Since 1892, it has also included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which provides criticisms of recent hagiographic works.

The Bollandists also expanded their scope to include the analysis of hagiographic manuscripts in major libraries. This resulted in the publication of various library catalogues in the *Analecta* (including those of Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres) and separate volumes dedicated to:
* Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (1886-1889).
* Latin and Greek manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (1889-1896).
* Greek manuscripts in the Vatican (1899).
* Latin manuscripts in the libraries of Rome (beginning in 1905).

Furthermore, they prepared inventories of published hagiographic texts, resulting in the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*. While these scientific endeavors delayed the main collection, they provided a more solid basis for the work. Consequently, volume i for November appeared in 1887, volume ii in 1894 (preceded by the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* by Louis Duchesne and J.B. de Rossi), and the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* (containing the *Synaxarium ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae*) in 1902.

## Editions and References
There are three primary editions of the *Acta Sanctorum*:
1. The original edition (Brussels, Tongerloo, and Antwerp), consisting of 63 volumes published between 1643 and 1902.
2. The Venice edition, which stopped at volume v of September (1734-1770).
3. The Paris edition, which stopped at volume xiii of October (61 volumes, 1863-1883).

Additional related materials include a volume of tables edited by the abbe Rigollot and the work *Acta Sanctorum apologelicis libris ... vindicata* (Antwerp, 1755). Other historical references include L.P. Gachard's *Memoire historique sur les Bollandistes* (Brussels, 1835), Cardinal J.B. Pitra's *Etudes sur la collection des Actes des Saints* (Paris, 1880), and the writing of Joseph van Hecke, "De ratione operis Bollandiani," found in *Acta Sanctorum Octobris*, vii.
