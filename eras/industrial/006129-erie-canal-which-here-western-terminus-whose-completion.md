# Erie Canal

The Erie Canal is a significant artery of trade in the United States that played a critical role in the economic development of various regions, facilitating the movement of people and goods between the eastern states and the western frontier.

## Impact on Buffalo
The Erie Canal has its western terminus at Buffalo. The completion of the canal in 1825 provided the first impetus for the commercial growth of the city. Due to this connection, along with its favorable location for lake transportation and its position on the principal northern trade route between the East and West, Buffalo evolved into one of the Union's most important industrial and commercial centers. 

The city's strategic position allows it to serve as a link between the canal and the Great Lakes. Almost all major steamship transportation lines of the Great Lakes utilize Buffalo as an eastern terminus, providing direct freight and passenger connections to cities such as Detroit, Chicago, Milwaukee, Cleveland, and the "Head of the Lakes" (Duluth-Superior).

## Regional Development and Settlement
The canal's completion was a catalyst for the establishment and growth of various settlements along its route. For example, the village of Ilion in Herkimer County, New York, effectively dates from the completion of the Erie Canal in 1825. On the canal list, the location was referred to as Steele's Creek, though it was also known as Morgan's Landing and, between 1830 and 1843, as Remington's Corners.

Beyond New York, the canal served as a primary route for migration. Immigrants from the Eastern and New England states utilized the Erie Canal and the Great Lakes to find their way to Illinois.

## Relation to Other Infrastructure
The Erie Canal functioned as part of a broader network of transportation. In Illinois, the need to connect the Great Lakes (accessed via the Erie Canal) to the Mississippi River led to the construction of the Illinois & Michigan canal to La Salle, which began in 1836 and was finished in 1848.

In other regions, the Erie name is associated with railway systems. For instance, the city of Hornell in Steuben County, New York, is served by the Erie railway and maintains extensive car shops for the line. Similarly, the city of Kenton, Ohio, is served by the Erie railway.
