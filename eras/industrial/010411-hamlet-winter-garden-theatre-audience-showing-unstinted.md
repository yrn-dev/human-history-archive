# Hamlet at the Winter Garden Theatre

## Edwin Booth and the Winter Garden Theatre
In 1862, the American actor Edwin Booth became the manager of the Winter Garden theatre in New York. During his tenure, specifically between 1864 and 1867, Booth presented a series of Shakespearian productions characterized by a magnificence that was unexampled at the time. Among the plays included in this series were *Othello*, *The Merchant of Venice*, and *Hamlet*.

## The Performance of Hamlet
*Hamlet* was the most famous part played by Edwin Booth. He was considered peculiarly fitted for the role due to his eloquent sensibility, as well as his beauty and extraordinary grace. It is noted that Booth likely performed the part of Hamlet more frequently than any other actor before or since. 

Booth's success in this and other roles, including Richelieu and Richard, was pronounced to be superior to the performances of Edwin Forrest. His career was marked by a commitment to the true text of Shakespeare, a reform he implemented in his productions years before similar changes occurred in England.

## Career Trajectory and Influence
Edwin Booth's path to his triumphs at the Winter Garden theatre began with a challenging start. His first regular appearance was on September 10, 1849, at the Boston Museum, where he played Tressel in Colley Cibber's version of *Richard III* alongside his father, Junius Brutus Booth. While Edwin was noted for his buoyant spirits, lithe figure, and expressive eyes, his father viewed his early work with evident disappointment. Similarly, other members of the theatrical profession believed the genius of the elder Booth had not been passed to the son.

Booth's first New York appearance took place on September 27, 1850, at the National theatre in Chatham Street, where he played Wilford in *The Iron Chest*. He later took over the role of Richard III during his father's illness, but it was only after his father's death that Edwin secured an unassailable position on the stage. Between 1852 and 1856, he performed in the Sandwich Islands, Australia, and California, where he captivated audiences with brilliant acting.

## Later Years and Legacy
Following his time at the Winter Garden, Booth built his own venue, Booth's theatre, at the corner of 6th Avenue and 23rd Street in New York in 1868-1869. There, he organized a stock company that produced various plays, including *Macbeth*, *Julius Caesar*, *The Winter's Tale*, *Romeo and Juliet*, *Much Ado about Nothing*, and *The Merchant of Venice*. Although his ventures were generally successful, his lack of economical management led to the loss of the theatre and his entire fortune in 1874.

Booth eventually regained his wealth and converted his residence in Gramercy Park into The Players' club for members of his profession and others. He continued to perform *Hamlet* throughout his life, including successful engagements at the Haymarket theatre in London during visits in 1851, 1880, and 1882, as well as a visit to Germany where he was received with the highest enthusiasm. His final appearance on stage was as Hamlet in Brooklyn in 1891. He died on June 7, 1893.
