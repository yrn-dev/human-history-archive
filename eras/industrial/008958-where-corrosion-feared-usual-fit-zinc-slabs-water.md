# Zinc Slabs in Boiler Water Spaces

In the maintenance and construction of boilers, the prevention of corrosion is a critical concern. When chemicals are utilized to purify or soften feed-water, it is essential that these substances and their resulting products do not exert a corrosive effect on the boiler-plates. A significant portion of the corrosion that occurs is attributed to oxygen from the air, which enters the boiler in solution within the feed-water. To mitigate this, current best practice involves delivering feed into the boiler at positions that allow evolved air to pass directly into the steam space, preventing it from becoming disengaged on the boiler's under-water surfaces.

## Application and Installation of Zinc Slabs
In instances where corrosion is feared, it is standard practice to fit zinc slabs within the water spaces of the boiler. Experience indicates that rolled zinc is preferable to cast zinc for this purpose. To ensure the effectiveness of these slabs, they are secured on studs that are kept bright; this maintains a direct metallic contact between the boiler-plate and the zinc.

## Galvanic Action and Function
The primary function of the zinc slabs is to initiate galvanic action. In this process, the zinc acts as the negative metal and is gradually dissolved. This action ensures that the metal of the boiler shell remains electro-positive, thereby protecting the shell from corrosion. Because the zinc wastes away over time, care must be taken to ensure that the resulting fragments do not fall upon the heating surfaces of the boiler.

## Related Boiler Materials
The use of zinc for corrosion protection exists alongside various other materials used in boiler construction:
*   **Shells and Stays:** In marine and ordinary land boilers, plates and stays are almost always made of mild steel. Shell plates and long stays typically possess a tensile strength of 28 to 32 tons per sq. in., while flanged plates, furnaces, flues, and short-screwed stays use a softer steel with a strength of 26 to 30 tons per sq. in.
*   **Tubes:** Ordinary land and marine boilers generally use lap-welded wrought iron tubes. Naval water-tube boilers invariably use seamless steel. In locomotive boilers, tubes are frequently made of brass (composed of 70% copper and 30% zinc, or two parts copper to one part zinc by weight), though steel or copper tubes are occasionally employed.
*   **Locomotive Specifics:** Locomotive shells are generally mild steel, while fire-box plates are made of steel in America and copper elsewhere. Fire-box side stays are constructed from special bronze or copper.

## Other Industrial and Medical Uses of Zinc
Beyond boiler protection, zinc and its compounds are utilized in various other capacities:
*   **Medical and Therapeutic:** An oxide of zinc mixed with starch powder is used as a final dusting step in the treatment of bedsores, following a bath in a solution of corrosive sublimate in spirits of wine.
*   **Dental:** Cements consisting of zinc chloride or phosphoric acid mixed with zinc oxide are used for dental restorations.
*   **Incubation:** In certain egg-drawers designed to imitate the conditions of a sitting hen, zinc is used for trays and compartments. These include zinc strips arranged like the limbs of an inverted V to form parallel troughs.
*   **Chemical Extraction:** Metallic zinc is used to precipitate cadmium from a solution of hydrochloric acid during the purification process of the metal.
