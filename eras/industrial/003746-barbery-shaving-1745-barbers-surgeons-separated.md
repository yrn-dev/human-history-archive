# Barbery and Shaving

Barbery is the occupation of shaving or trimming beards and acting as a hairdresser. Historically, the craft was regarded as a profession and was closely linked with the art of surgery.

## Professional Evolution and Regulation
In England, barbers first received incorporation in 1461 under Edward IV. Later, through 32 Henry VIII. c. 42, they were united with the company of surgeons. This union established a strict division of labor: barbers were required to limit their practice to drawing teeth and the minor operations of blood-letting, while surgeons were expressly prohibited from engaging in "barbery or shaving." This combined arrangement lasted until 1745, when 18 George II. c. 15 separated barbers and surgeons into distinct corporations.

In France, a similar separation occurred during the reign of Louis XIV, when barber-surgeons were incorporated as a distinct body, separate from the *perruquiers*. Despite these formal separations, the influence of the barber-surgeon persisted in medical education; for example, the French anatomist Pierre Joseph Desault (1744–1795) began his medical studies by learning from the barber-surgeon in his native village of Magny-Vernois before pursuing further training in military surgery and anatomy.

## The Barber's Shop and Symbolism
The barber's shop historically served as a social hub and a favorite resort for idle persons, acting as a focus for the exchange of news. To entertain customers who were waiting, shops typically kept musical instruments such as a viol or a lute.

The traditional sign of the barber was a striped pole with a suspended basin. These symbols were derived from the craft's surgical history: the basin represented the vessel used to receive blood, and the fillet wrapped around the pole symbolized the ribbon used for bandaging the arm during the process of bleeding.

## Cultural and Religious Practices
Shaving and head-shaving have held various significances across different cultures and religious traditions:

*   **China:** Under the Penal Code, the death of an emperor triggered a period of national tribulation. During the first one hundred days of this period, no man was permitted to have his head shaved. Such restrictions caused significant hardship for barbers, who faced the danger of starvation, though this was often mitigated by the common rice-pot at home and the common-sense of rulers.
*   **Buddhism:** In the accounts of Rubruck, priests of the idolaters were noted to shave their heads and dress in saffron color. The act of shaving the head coincided with the observation of chastity and living in congregations of one to two hundred people.

## Historical Accounts
Barbers appear in various historical narratives as figures of social or political significance. In Athens, a barber was the first to communicate news of a catastrophe to the archons and the public after hearing a stranger discuss the event in his shop. Because he could not provide a satisfactory account of his source, he was initially treated as a fabricator of idle tales and subjected to torture before other witnesses arrived to confirm the news.

In another account, a barber named Trypho claimed that a man named Tero had attempted to persuade him to use his razor to cut the throat of Herod during a trimming session, promising large presents from Alexander in exchange for the act.
