# F. Weiss and P. Schidrowitz

F. Weiss and P. Schidrowitz are researchers whose work contributed to the scientific understanding of the chemical composition of malt. Their findings, alongside the work of W. Windisch and A. Fernbach, established that finished malt contains at least two proteolytic enzymes: a pancreatic enzyme and a peptic enzyme.

## The Role of Nitrogenous Constituents in Brewing
The chemistry of nitrogenous constituents in malt is considered as important as the transformations of starch. Vigorous fermentations cannot occur without nitrogenous compounds of the proper type, as yeast assimilates these compounds in simpler forms, such as amides. 

A primary objective for the maltster is to break down the protein substances in barley to a degree that ensures the wort provides maximum nutritive value for the yeast. Furthermore, the degradation of proteins by yeast is necessary to eliminate a large proportion of nitrogenous matter, which is required for the production of stable beer. There is also evidence suggesting that albumoses assist in the foaming properties of the finished beer.

## Chemical Composition of Beer
Beer generally contains between 3% and 6% alcohol and 4% to 7% solids. The remaining portion consists of water and preservative and flavouring matters derived from materials such as hops and malt. The non-volatile solid matter in beer primarily consists of:
* Maltose (malt sugar)
* Various varieties of dextrin
* Substances positioned between dextrins and sugars
* Nitrogen-containing bodies, including peptones and non-coagulable proteids

Additionally, beer contains mineral matter, specifically potash and phosphates. Due to these components, beer is regarded dietetically as having considerable food value, with its nutritious matter present in a readily assimilable form.

## Analysis of Beer Types
P. Schidrowitz, along with J.L. Baker and Hulton, conducted analyses on various types of beer to determine their original gravity, alcohol percentage, and extractives (solids). Their data includes:

* **Mild Ales:** Examples showed original gravities ranging from 1055.13 to 1071.78, alcohol content from 4.17% to 5.57%, and solids from 5.7% to 7.3%.
* **Light Bitters and Ales:** Examples showed original gravities between 1038.31 and 1050.30, alcohol content between 3.81% and 4.61%, and solids between 3.2% and 4.2%.
* **Pale and Stock Ales:** Examples showed original gravities from 1059.01 to 1076.80, alcohol content from 4.77% to 6.68%, and solids from 5.8% to 7.1%.
