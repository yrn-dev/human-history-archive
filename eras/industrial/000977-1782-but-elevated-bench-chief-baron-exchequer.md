# Chief Baron of the Exchequer

The Chief Baron of the Exchequer was a high-ranking judicial office associated with the court of the exchequer, one of the superior courts of common law. The court of the exchequer was primarily responsible for attending to the business of the revenue. In its historical structure, the administration of justice in causes between the king and his subjects regarding revenue matters was committed to six judges: one chief baron and five puisne barons.

## Origins and Nature of the Office
The title of "baron" in this judicial context is subject to historical conjecture. One theory suggests that these judges were originally selected from the barons of the kingdom. An alternative view posits that they were officers of a branch of the king's *Curia*, which was theoretically composed of his "barons."

The court of the exchequer, along with the common pleas and the king's bench, possessed co-ordinate jurisdiction for many years, although specific cases fell under the exclusive authority of each. While the exchequer handled revenue, the common pleas dealt with private actions between citizens, and the king's bench retained criminal cases and other remaining jurisdictions. By 1830, the court of exchequer chamber was established as a court of appeal for errors in law across all three of these courts.

## Notable Holders of the Office
Various legal figures attained the position of chief baron across different jurisdictions:

*   **Barry Yelverton, 1st Viscount Avonmore (1736–1805):** An Irish judge and member of the Irish parliament for the boroughs of Carrickfergus and Donegal. After serving as attorney-general in 1782, he was elevated to the bench as chief baron of the exchequer in 1783.
*   **Sir John Denham (1559–1639):** He served as the lord chief baron of the exchequer in Ireland before becoming a baron of the exchequer in England in 1617.
*   **Sir Fitzroy Kelly (1796–1880):** An English judge and former attorney-general (1858–1859) in Lord Derby's second administration. He was raised to the bench as chief baron of the exchequer in 1866 and was also appointed a member of the Privy Council.
*   **James Abercromby, 1st Baron Dunfermline (1776–1858):** He served as the chief baron of the exchequer of Scotland starting in 1830, a position he took after resigning his seat in parliament. This specific office was abolished in 1832.

## Evolution and Obsolescence
The judicial landscape of England underwent significant changes in the 19th century. Under the Judicature Act 1873, the court of king's bench became the king's bench division of the High Court of Justice. The title of baron of the exchequer eventually became obsolete in 1875, the year the court of the exchequer was merged into the High Court of Judicature.
