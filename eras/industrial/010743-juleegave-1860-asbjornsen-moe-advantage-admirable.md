# Peter Christen Asbjornsen and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe

Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812-1885) and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813-1882) were collectors of Norwegian folklore whose professional and personal lives were so closely entwined that they are rarely named separately.

## Early Life and Partnership
Peter Christen Asbjornsen was born in Christiania on January 15, 1812. He was a member of an ancient family from the Gudbrandsdal, a lineage believed to have ended with him. Asbjornsen began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories in 1832, at the age of twenty, and later entered the university as a student in 1833. To expand his collection, he traveled on foot throughout Norway.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at the Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Ringerike. He first met Asbjornsen when he was fourteen years old, initiating a close friendship that lasted until their deaths. In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. The two friends decided to work in concert, eagerly comparing their findings.

## Professional Pursuits
While collaborating on folklore, both men pursued other professional paths. Asbjornsen became a zoologist and, with university support, conducted several investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord. 

Moe left Christiania University in 1839 to study theology. While working as a tutor in Christiania, he spent his holidays wandering through remote mountain districts to collect stories.

## Literary Contributions
The partnership between Asbjornsen and Moe resulted in a major work titled *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*. The first installment was published in 1842-1843 and was immediately recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to literature and comparative mythology. A second volume followed in 1844, and a subsequent collection was published in 1871. In 1859, Sir George Dasent translated many of the *Folkeeventyr* into English.

In addition to their joint efforts, Asbjornsen published a separate collection of Norwegian fairy tales (*huldreeventyr og folkesagn*) in 1845 without Moe's assistance.

## Later Activities
In 1856, Asbjornsen turned his attention to the issue of deforestation in Norway, successfully inducing the government to address the matter.
