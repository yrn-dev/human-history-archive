# 1844

The year 1844 is associated with several distinct developments across political, economic, and industrial history, ranging from nationalist movements in the Austrian Empire to monetary reform in Great Britain and industrial milestones in France.

## Political Agitation in the Austrian Empire
During the mid-19th century, the Austrian Empire experienced rising popular discontent and political agitation. This was exacerbated by the death of Francis I in 1835 and the succession of his son, Ferdinand I, who was incapable of ruling due to epilepsy. This necessitated a veiled regency, which led to divided counsels and accentuated administrative vices.

Among the southern Slavs, the "Illyrian" movement had been voiced since 1836 through the *Illyrian National Gazette* of Ljudevit Gaj. Initially, this movement was directed toward a "shadowy Pan-Slav union." However, following interference by the Austrian government in 1844, the movement's focus was exchanged for the more definite objective of reviving "the Triune Kingdom," consisting of Dalmatia, Croatia, and Slavonia, as an entity independent of the Hungarian crown.

## British Monetary Reform and the Bank Act
In Great Britain, 1844 marked a foundational shift in the monetary system with the introduction of the Bank Act of 1844. On May 6, 1844, Sir Robert Peel delivered a speech regarding the currency and the bank charter. Peel expressed hope that Parliament would implement measures to ensure the legitimate profit of commercial enterprise conducted with integrity, put a check on improvident speculations, and inspire confidence in the medium of exchange.

Despite these intentions, the expectations expressed by Peel were not immediately fulfilled. The Bank Act of 1844 served as the foundation of the British monetary system, yet it required suspension on three separate occasions. These suspensions had an injurious effect on domestic prosperity and international credit.

## Industrial and Global Events
In France, the year 1844 marked the death of X. Jouvin (1800-1844), the man credited with reviving the art of manufacturing kid gloves. His influence remained significant in Grenoble, where the "gants Jouvin" were produced. By the time of the archive's recording, Grenoble hosted approximately 80 glove factories employing 18,500 people—15,000 of whom were women—with an annual output of about 800,000 dozen pairs of gloves.

In Japan, the period ending in 1844 is noted for the "Famine in the Tempo Era," which spanned from 1830 to 1844. This era was also characterized by the efforts of statesmen like Mizuno Echizen no Kami, who, starting in 1826, attempted to restore economical habits by issuing decrees against needless expenditures and luxury, though these were largely ignored in practice.
