# Julius Zeyer

## Overview
Julius Zeyer (1841-1901) was a Bohemian writer who contributed extensively to the literature of his region during the latter part of the 19th century. His body of work is characterized by its versatility, as he wrote prolifically in both prose and verse.

## Literary Works
Zeyer is noted for his contributions to both poetry and the novel. Among his poetic achievements, his epic poem entitled *Vysehrad* is regarded as having great value; this work celebrates the ancient glory of the acropolis of Prague. In the realm of prose, Zeyer authored many novels, of which *Jan Maria Plojhar* achieved the greatest success.

## Context of Bohemian Literature
Zeyer's career coincided with a period of significant development in Bohemian literature, particularly following the foundation of the national university in 1882. He is situated among a group of influential writers of the era. Other notable figures in Bohemian literature include:

*   **Poets and Prose Writers:** John Neruda (1834-1891), a talented writer of both poetry and prose known for his philosophical epic *Kosmicke basne* (cosmic poems).
*   **Older Writers:** Bozena Nemceva, author of *Babicka*, and Benes Trebizky, who wrote many historical novels.
*   **Later Poets:** Adolf Heyduk, Jaroslav Vrchlicky (b. 1853), and Svatopluk Cech. Cech is known for works inspired by national enthusiasm, such as *Basne otroka* (the songs of a slave) and *Vaclav z Michalovic, Lesetinsky Kovar* (the smith of Lesetin). Vrchlicky, using the pseudonym Emil Frida, wrote lyric collections including *Sonety Samotare* (sonnets of a recluse), *Poute k Eldoradu* (pilgrimages to Eldorado), and *Rok na jihu* (a year in the south), and was also a brilliant dramatist.
*   **Novelists and Dramatists:** Alois Jirasek, Jacob Arbes (author of *Romanetta*), and Vaclav Hladik, whose novel *Evzen Voldan* represents the life of modern Prague.
