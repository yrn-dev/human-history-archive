# Deductions in General Average Repair Claims

In the adjustment of claims for General Average (G.A.), the York-Antwerp Rules provide a definite scale to regulate deductions from the cost of repairs. These deductions are applied in respect of "new for old," replacing previous customary rules that varied by the place of adjustment and adapting the process to modern shipbuilding conditions.

## General Principles of Repair Deductions
Deductions for repairs are generally regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired. However, there are exceptions to this rule regarding boilers, machinery, and provisions and stores. 

Certain expenses are allowed in full without deduction. These include the cost of straightening bent ironwork (including the labor for its removal and replacement) and graving dock dues. Graving dock dues encompass expenses for removals, cartages, the use of stages, shears, and graving dock materials. Additionally, no deduction is made for provisions and stores that have not been in use, nor for old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material. 

Painting of the bottom is not allowed if the bottom was not painted within the six months preceding the date of the accident. Furthermore, no "new for old" deductions are applied to the cost of temporary repairs for damage allowable as G.A.

## Deductions for Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel ships, deductions are determined by the time elapsed from the date of original register to the date of the accident, categorized by the following age brackets:

*   **Up to 1 year (A):** All repairs are allowed in full, with the exception of the coating or painting of the bottom, from which one-third is deducted.
*   **Between 1 and 3 years (B):** One-third is deducted from repairs to and renewal of furniture, upholstery, crockery, glassware, metal, sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers (excluding chain and wire), painting, covers, awnings, and the woodwork of the hull, masts, and spars. A deduction of one-sixth is applied to chain cables, wire ropes, wire rigging, wire hawsers, steam winches, donkey engines, steam cranes, and their respective connexions. Other repairs are allowed in full.
*   **Between 3 and 6 years (C):** Deductions follow the rules of clause B, with the exception that a deduction of one-sixth (rather than the amounts in B) is applied to machinery (including mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 6 and 10 years (D):** Deductions follow the rules of clause C, except that one-third is deducted from the repairs and renewal of all machinery (including boilers and mountings), all rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers, and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 10 and 15 years (E):** One-third is deducted from all renewals and repairs, except for chain cables and the ironwork of the hull and cementing, from which one-sixth is deducted. Anchors are allowed in full.
*   **Over 15 years (F):** One-third is deducted from all renewals and repairs. Chain cables are subject to a one-sixth deduction, while anchors are allowed in full.

## Deductions for Wooden or Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, no "new for old" deduction is made if the ship is under one year old from the date of original register at the time of the accident. After one year, a general deduction of one-third is applied, subject to the following specific conditions:
*   **Anchors:** Allowed in full.
*   **Chain Cables:** Subject to a deduction of one-sixth only.
*   **Metal Sheathing:** The cost is allowed in full for a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing, minus the proceeds from the old metal.
*   **Metalling:** Nails, felt, and labour metalling are subject to a one-third deduction.

## Related General Average Provisions
Beyond repair deductions, the York-Antwerp Rules address other G.A. costs. If a ship is detained in a port for repairs, the maintenance and wages of the crew, officers, and master are admitted as G.A. until the ship is ready to proceed. However, if the voyage is abandoned or the ship is condemned, these costs are not admitted after the date of condemnation or abandonment.

Damage or loss of cargo caused by discharging, storing, reloading, and stowing is made good as G.A. only if the cost of those specific measures is admitted as G.A. Loss of freight resulting from cargo loss or damage is also made good as G.A. when caused by a G.A. act or when the cargo loss is made good.

Contributory values for G.A. are based on the actual values of property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good for sacrificed property. Deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight at risk for crew wages and port charges that would not have been incurred had the ship and cargo been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice. Personal effects and passengers' luggage not shipped under bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.
