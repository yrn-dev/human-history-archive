# Cigar and Cigarette Manufacturing

The manufacture of cigars and cigarettes is a global industry with significant concentrations in various urban centers and regions, particularly within the United States and Germany. These products are produced both as primary industrial outputs for commerce and as items of personal consumption and charitable gift-giving.

## Manufacturing in the United States

In the United States, several cities and states maintain substantial production capacities for cigars and cigarettes.

### Baltimore, Maryland
Baltimore is a major manufacturing city that experienced rapid growth between 1880 and 1905. By 1905, the city ranked ninth among the manufacturing centers of the United States based on the value of its products. Among its diverse industrial outputs, the manufacture of cigars and cigarettes was notable, with a value of $4,360,366 in 1905.

### Kentucky
Kentucky serves as a primary hub for tobacco products, largely due to the richness of its agricultural produce. Louisville is the state's great manufacturing center and hosts the largest leaf-tobacco market in the world. In 1900, Kentucky produced 34.9% of the entire leaf-tobacco crop of the United States, most of which was handled in Louisville. 

In Louisville, the most valuable manufacture in 1905 was snuff and smoking and chewing tobacco (specifically plug tobacco), valued at $11,635,367. When combined with the production of cigars and cigarettes, which were valued at $1,225,347, these products constituted 15.5% of the city's total factory product value. On a state-wide level, cigars and cigarettes were listed as one of the important manufactures with a product value exceeding one million dollars in 1905.

### Louisiana and New York
In Louisiana, cigars and cigarettes were among the manufactures that produced a value between $1,000,000 and $4,000,000 in 1905. In New York, the city of Kingston—located on the Hudson River—lists cigars and cigarettes, along with tobacco, as its principal manufactures.

## Manufacturing in Germany

Cigar-making is a established industry in several German towns across different regions.

### Saxony and Hanover
In the kingdom of Saxony, the town of Marienberg (with a population of 7,603 in 1905) includes the making of cigars among its industries, alongside silver-mining, toys, lace, flax-dressing, and wool-spinning. In the Prussian province of Hanover, the medieval town of Duderstadt identifies cigar-making as one of its chief industries, alongside sugar-refining and cotton and woollen manufactures.

## Distribution and Consumption in Military Contexts

Cigars and cigarettes also appear as significant items of supply and morale during wartime. In the context of the German Army, these items are frequently sent to the front as *Liebesgaben* (love gifts). 

Such gifts are often organized through public generosity, including spirited appeals from the Crown Prince to support the 5th Army. These supplies are transported in large quantities; for example, Baron Bleichröder of Dresden delivered twenty-nine motor-lorries filled with parcels containing cigarettes, cigars, and pipe-tobacco, among other items like chocolate and underclothing. While these "beloved smokes" are heartily welcome among the soldiers in villages such as Cunel, records indicate that the longing for news from home is often considered a greater priority by the troops.
