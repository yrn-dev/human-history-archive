# Cambridge

Cambridge is referenced in the archive's records primarily in connection with its university, academic achievements, and the influential figures who were educated or conducted research there.

## Academic and Political Figures
Several prominent British statesmen received their education at Trinity College, Cambridge. Arthur James Balfour, born in 1848, attended the college before entering politics. In 1874, Balfour became a Member of Parliament (M.P.) in the Conservative interest for Hertford, a constituency he represented until 1885. Later, in the spring of 1878, he served as the private secretary to his uncle, Lord Salisbury, following Salisbury's appointment as foreign minister.

Similarly, Edward Henry Stanley, the 15th Earl of Derby (1826–1893), was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge. During his time there, he earned a high degree and was a member of a society known as the Apostles. Stanley's subsequent political career included serving as the first secretary of state for India and holding the office of secretary for the colonies.

## Scientific Research and Biology
The university also served as a center for significant scientific advancement, notably through the work of Francis Maitland Balfour (1851–1882). A biologist and the younger brother of Arthur James Balfour, Francis Maitland Balfour entered residence at Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1870. He was elected a natural science scholar in 1871 and achieved second place in the Natural Science Tripos in December 1873.

Following his tripos, Balfour was selected for one of the two seats allocated to the university of Cambridge at the Naples zoological station. His research there led to his election as a fellow of Trinity in 1874. He produced a series of papers, later published as a monograph in 1878, regarding Elasmobranch fishes and the development of nervous and uro-genital systems in Vertebrates. He further contributed to the field with his two-volume treatise, *Comparative Embryology*, published in 1880 (Invertebrates) and 1881 (Vertebrates).

## Religious and Missionary Influence
Cambridge is noted as a location of missionary influence associated with the "Keswick men." The Keswick Convention, an annual summer reunion started in 1874 by Mr. Robert Wilson and Canon Harford-Battersby, focused on "promoting practical holiness" through prayer and discussion. While the convention was held in the town of Keswick, its missionary impact extended to Cambridge and other areas, as documented in the *History of the C.M.S.* (vol. iii) by Eugene Stock.
