# BARRY, SIR REDMOND (1813-1880)

Sir Redmond Barry was a British colonial judge and a prominent figure in the early development of the colony of Victoria. Born in 1813, he was the son of Major-General H. G. Barry of Ballyclough, Co. Cork.

## Education and Early Career
Barry received his education at a military school located in Kent and subsequently attended Trinity College, Dublin. He entered the legal profession and was called to the Irish bar in 1838. Following his initial legal training, he emigrated to Australia. After a brief period residing in Sydney, he moved to Melbourne, a city with which he remained closely identified throughout the rest of his life.

## Judicial and Political Career
After practicing law for several years, Barry served as the commissioner of the court of requests. In 1851, the colony of Victoria was created from the Port Phillip district of New South Wales. Following this transition, Barry became the first solicitor-general of the colony, holding a seat in both the executive and legislative councils.

His legal and administrative career progressed to several high-ranking positions, including:
* Judge of the Supreme Court
* Acting chief-justice
* Administrator of the government

In recognition of his service, he was knighted in 1860 and was later created a K.C.M.G. in 1877.

## Contributions to Education and Culture
Sir Redmond Barry is noted as the first person in Victoria to take an interest in higher education. He successfully induced the local government to allocate large sums of money toward this goal. His efforts led to the founding of the university of Melbourne in 1853, where he served as the first chancellor.

Beyond the university, Barry held leadership roles in several other cultural institutions. In 1854, he became the president of the Melbourne public library. He also served as president of the national gallery and the museum. The archive notes that the prosperity of these two institutions is due to his exertions.

## Other Activities and Later Life
Barry represented Victoria on the international stage, attending the London International Exhibition of 1862 and the Philadelphia Exhibition of 1876. Additionally, he was among the first individuals to foster the volunteer movement within Australia. He died in 1880.
