# The Garabit Viaduct

The Garabit viaduct is a railway bridge located in the Cantal department of France, situated near St Flour. The structure carries the railway at a height of 420 feet above low water.

## Design and Architecture
Designed by Seyrig, the Garabit viaduct shares similarities with the bridge at Oporto. The viaduct has a total length of 1,715 feet. Its primary structural feature is a crescent-shaped arch that crosses the deepest part of the valley; this arch has a span of 541 feet and a rise of 213 feet.

The arch is constructed from two lattice ribs that are hinged at the abutments. These ribs are 65½ feet apart at the springings and narrow to 20½ feet at the crown, where the depth of the arch is 33 feet. The center line of the arch closely follows the parabolic line of pressures. Supporting the railway are lattice roadway girders with a depth of 17 feet, which are supported by the arch ribs at four specific points.

The crescent-shaped arch is complemented by side spans consisting of iron piers. One side of the arch is continued by four lattice girder spans, while the other side is continued by two.

## Construction and Specifications
The construction process involved first rolling the lattice girders of the side spans into place so that they projected beyond the piers. Following this, the arch ribs were built out, utilizing wire-rope cables from the lattice girders above for partial support.

The total weight of the ironwork used in the construction of the viaduct was 3,200 tons. According to the *Annales des travaux publiques* (1884), the cost of the project was £124,000.

## General Context of Viaducts
In the broader context of engineering, bridges are structures designed to carry railways, roadways, or waterways across valleys, streams, or other roads, while maintaining a passage way below. Long bridges consisting of several spans are frequently referred to as "viaducts." In railway terminology, a bridge that carries the railway is specifically termed an "underbridge," whereas a bridge passing over the railway is an "overbridge."
