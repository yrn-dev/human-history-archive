# Asheville Farm School for Boys

The Asheville farm school for boys is an industrial school for negroes located in or near Asheville, North Carolina.

## Geographic Context
Asheville serves as the county-seat of Buncombe county in the United States. It is situated in the mountainous Blue Ridge region of the western part of the state, approximately 210 miles west of Raleigh. The city is positioned at the mouth of the Swannanoa on a high terrace on the east bank of the French Broad river, at an elevation of about 2300 feet above the sea. It is located at the junction of three branches of the Southern railway.

The city is recognized as a popular pleasure and health resort in the south, serving as a winter resort for those from the north and a summer resort for southerners, owing to its beautiful scenery and equable, dry climate.

## Educational Institutions in Asheville
The Asheville farm school for boys is one of several educational establishments in the vicinity. Other institutions include:

*   **The Asheville school for boys**: Located 5 miles west of the city.
*   **The Bingham school**: Founded in 1793 at Pittsboro, N.C., by William Bingham (who died in 1826), and moved to a site 3 miles north-west of Asheville in 1891.
*   **Presbyterian Church Institutions**: Under the control of the Woman's Board of Home Missions of the Presbyterian Church, the area hosts a home industrial school for girls (established 1887) and a collegiate and normal institute for young women (established 1892), both of which occupy the same campus.

## City Infrastructure and Landmarks
Asheville's public buildings include an auditorium, a public library, the Federal building, the court house, and the city hall. The city features an extensive system of suburban and city parks connected by beautiful drives, with great forests located nearby. Social clubs in the city include the Swannanoa Country club, the Tahkeeostee, the Elks, the Asheville, and the Albemarle.

To the south-east of the city, approximately 2 miles away, is Biltmore, the 125,000-acre estate of George W. Vanderbilt. The estate features a chateau of French Renaissance design, modeled after the chateau at Blois in France, and was designed by Richard M. Hunt. Adjacent to the chateau is a model village also designed by Hunt, which includes a church, a hospital, an industrial school for whites, and an elementary school.

## Demographics
According to census data, the population of Asheville grew from 10,235 in 1890 to 14,692 in 1900, and reached 18,762 by 1910. In 1900, 4,724 of the residents were negroes.
