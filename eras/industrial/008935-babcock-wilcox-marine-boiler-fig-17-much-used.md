# Babcock & Wilcox Marine Boiler

The Babcock & Wilcox marine boiler is a water-tube boiler utilized extensively within the British and American navies, as well as in various merchant steamers and yachts. The system is designed to generate steam through a series of water-tubes and headers enclosed within an iron casing lined with firebrick.

## Design and Components
The boiler's structure consists of a horizontal cylindrical steam-chest positioned transversely over a group of elements. The fire is located beneath these elements. Each element is composed of a front header and a back header, which are connected by numerous water-tubes. These tubes are designed with a considerable inclination to facilitate the circulation of water.

The headers are constructed from wrought steel. Except for the outer pairs, which feature flat outer portions, the headers are sinuous on both sides, allowing the sinuosities to fit into one another.

## Tube Configuration and Gas Circulation
The boiler employs tubes of two different sizes to optimize heating and gas flow:
* **Large tubes:** These have an outside diameter of 3-15/16 inches and are used for the two lower rows and the return tubes connecting the back headers to the steam-chest.
* **Small tubes:** These have an outside diameter of 1-13/16 inches.

The smaller tubes are arranged in groups of two or four to nearly all of the header sinuosities. This specific arrangement ensures that furnace gases are well mixed and maintain consistent contact with the heating surfaces.

## Connections and Fittings
The front headers are connected to the steam-chest at their upper ends via short nipples. At the bottom, they are connected to a square-section pipe that extends across the width of the boiler. Nearly vertical tubes provide additional connections between this cross-pipe and the bottom of the steam-chest. The back headers are closed at their bottom ends and connected to the steam-chest at their upper ends by two long horizontal tubes.

To allow for the securing of tubes within the headers, access holes are provided on the side of the header opposite the tubes:
* **Oval holes:** Located opposite groups of two tubes, these are closed by fittings similar to those used in land boilers.
* **Conical holes:** Located opposite groups of four tubes, these are larger on the inside and closed by special conical fittings. In these fittings, the bolt and conical portion are a single forging, and the nut is close-ended.

## Thermal Storage
In applications where the required work is very intermittent, a "thermal storage" system may be employed. This involves placing a large cylindrical storage vessel above the boiler with enough capacity to hold feed-water for periods of maximum output. 

The upper part of this vessel remains in free communication with the boiler's steam space. Feed-water is delivered into the upper part of the vessel and runs over extended surfaces exposed to steam, raising its temperature to that of the steam before it reaches the bottom. During periods of low steam demand, excess feed-water is pumped into the chamber and accumulates at the evaporation point. When extra steam is required, the feed-pump is stopped, and the boiler is fed using the stored hot water. This system also allows many feed impurities to deposit in the chamber, where they are harmless and easily removed.
