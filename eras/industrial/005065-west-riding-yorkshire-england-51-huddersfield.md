# West Riding of Yorkshire

The West Riding of Yorkshire is a region in England characterized by a mix of heavy industrial manufacturing, historic religious foundations, and areas of significant natural beauty. The region's history includes Roman settlements, feudal military arrangements, and the lives of notable literary and religious figures.

## Industrial Centers and Municipal Boroughs

The West Riding contains several prominent municipal boroughs and urban districts defined by their industrial output and transport links.

**Barnsley** (also known as Black or Bleak Barnsley) is a market town and municipal borough located 15 miles north of Sheffield. As of 1901, its population was 41,086, having grown from 35,427 in 1891. The town is situated on rising ground west of the river Dearne in the parish of Silkstone. Its economy is driven by the manufacture of iron and steel, as well as the weaving of linen and other cloth. Additional industries include dyeworks, bleachfields, printfields, sawmills, cornmills, malt-houses, and the production of wire, needles, and glass. The town sits atop large coalfields, with coal and coke being exported to Hull and London. Barnsley is served by the Great Northern, Midland, Great Central, Hull & Barnsley, and Lancashire & Yorkshire railways, and is connected to the Aire and Calder Navigation canal system.

**Brighouse** is a municipal borough located on the river Calder, 5½ miles north of Huddersfield via the Lancashire & Yorkshire railway. Incorporated in 1893, it covers an area of 2,231 acres and had a population of 21,735 in 1901. Situated in the heart of the West Riding's manufacturing district, Brighouse hosts large worsted and woollen factories, and produces soap, machinery, and carpets.

**Castleford** is an urban district located on the river Aire near its junction with the Calder, 9 miles southeast of Leeds. Its 1901 population was 17,386. The local economy is supported by chemical works, earthenware-jar and glass-bottle works, and nearby collieries. It is served by the Lancashire & Yorkshire and North-Eastern railways.

**Bingley** is a market town on the Aire, 5½ miles northwest of Bradford, served by the Midland railway. Its urban district population in 1901 was 18,449. The town's industrial population is primarily employed in the manufacture of cotton and worsted.

## Health Resorts and Natural Landmarks

The region features areas of high natural beauty and specialized health establishments.

**Ilkley** is an urban district located 16 miles northwest of Leeds in the upper valley of the Wharfe. With a 1901 population of 7,455, it is a popular health resort due to its bracing high moorland air and scenery. The town and nearby Ben Rhydding (1 mile east) host several hydropathic establishments. Local institutions include a grammar school, the Ilkley Bath Charitable Institution, and the Siemens Convalescent Home.

**Bolton Abbey** is a village located 22 miles northwest of Leeds and 5½ miles from Ilkley. It is home to the ruins of Bolton Priory, situated on the right bank of the upper Wharfe. The church ruins feature a nave of Early English and Decorated styles, with the earliest sections being of transitional Norman date. The manor was granted by William the Conqueror to Robert de Romili, who held it in 1086. The priory was founded at Embsay in 1120 by William de Meschines and Cicely de Romili, then moved to its current location in 1151 by Alice de Romili. Following the dissolution of the monasteries, the manor was sold in 1542 to Henry Clifford, 2nd earl of Cumberland; it is currently held by the dukes of Devonshire.

## History and Antiquities

The West Riding contains various remnants of Roman and medieval history.

**Roman Presence:**
In Castleford, the Roman fort or village of *Legeolium* (or *Lagecium*) was located; while visible remains are absent, various relics have been found. In Ilkley—identified as *Olicana* of Ptolemy and a town of the Brigantes tribe—a Roman fort existed near the church of All Saints, yielding inscriptions and other small remains.

**Medieval and Feudal History:**
The region's history includes the "castle-guard" arrangement of the feudal system. This required certain baronies to provide knights to guard royal castles, a duty that was eventually commuted into a fixed money payment known as "castle-guard rent." In the vicinity of Barnsley, the Monk Bretton Priory, a Cluniac foundation from 1157, retains fragments of its church, Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse. Additionally, Wentworth Castle was built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford.

**Political History:**
In September 1661, Buckingham was appointed lord-lieutenant of the West Riding of Yorkshire. In 1663, he participated in the suppression of a projected insurrection within the county.

## Notable Figures

The West Riding is associated with several prominent individuals.

**The Brontë Family:**
Patrick Brontë, a clergyman of the Church of England, served as the perpetual incumbent of the parish of Haworth in the West Riding for the final 41 years of his life. Born in Ireland in 1777, he had previously been a schoolmaster and weaver before studying at St John's College, Cambridge. After serving as a curate in Essex and Salop, he held curacies in Hartshead-cum-Clifton and Dewsbury, Yorkshire. In 1812, he married Maria Branwell. While two children were born at Hartshead, four were born at Thornton (3 miles from Bradford): Charlotte, Patrick Branwell, Emily Jane, and Anne. Three of these children attained literary distinction as novelists.

**Other Figures:**
The scholar Roger Ascham (c. 1515-1568) was the son of John Ascham, who served as steward to Lord Scrope of Bolton. While Roger was born in the North Riding, his family name is believed to be derived from Askham in the West Riding.
