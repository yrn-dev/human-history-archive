# 1847

The year 1847 was marked by significant developments in journalism, academic publishing, and the arts across Europe and the United States, as well as the deaths of notable intellectuals.

## Journalism and Political Media
In the United States, 1847 saw a transition in the leadership of abolitionist media. Gamaliel Bailey, an American journalist and former physician who had been editing the *Philanthropist* since 1837—despite having his office wrecked by mobs three times—left that position in 1847. He assumed control of the *National Era*, a new abolitional organ based in Washington, D.C. This publication later became the first to publish Mrs. H. B. Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* during 1851-1852. Bailey had also been editing a daily paper, the *Herald*, since 1843.

In Europe, Hungarian poet and critic Joseph Bajza edited the opposition journal *Ellenoer* at Leipzig in 1847. Bajza, who had previously served as the director of the national theatre at Pest starting in 1837, would later be appointed by Kossuth in March 1848 as the editor of *Kossuth Hirlapja*.

## Academic and Scientific Publications
The year 1847 saw the release of several major scholarly works and editions. In the field of chemistry, the chemist Kopp concluded the publication of his comprehensive four-volume *History of Chemistry*, which had been released between 1843 and 1847. 

In the realm of art history, Jakob Burckhardt, who served as a professor of history at the university of Basel from 1845 to 1847 (and in subsequent terms), published new editions of two major works by Franz Kugler: *Kunstgeschichte* and *Geschichte der Malerei*.

Legal and genealogical records also saw significant updates. The *Genealogical and Heraldic Dictionary of the Peerage and Baronetage of the United Kingdom*, commonly known as *Burke's Peerage*, began being issued annually in 1847. Sir John Bernard Burke began editing this work in 1847, a role he maintained until his death. Additionally, G. Hänel's *Lex Romana Visigothorum* began publication in Berlin in 1847, continuing through 1849.

## Literature and Biography
Several biographical and historical works were published in 1847. Joseph Bajza published his *Universal History* (*Vilagtoretet*) that year, following his earlier historical series *Historical Library* (1843-1845) and *Modern Plutarch* (1845-1847). In the field of biography, two volumes of *Memoirs, with Extracts from her Journals and Letters* regarding Elizabeth Fry were published in 1847, edited by two of her daughters. Furthermore, a memoir of the Scottish chemist and inventor of waterproof fabrics, Charles Macintosh, was published in 1847 by George Macintosh.

In France, Taillandier published *Documents bibliographiques sur Daunou* in 1847, which provided a full list of Daunou's works.

## Notable Deaths
The year 1847 was marked by the death of James MacCullagh, an Irish physicist and mathematician. MacCullagh had been a fellow at Trinity College, Dublin, since 1832, serving as the chair of mathematics until 1843, when he moved to the chair of natural philosophy. He died by his own hand in October 1847, following mental disease induced by overwork on subjects that exceeded the natural range of his powers.
