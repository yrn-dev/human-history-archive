# Risborough, Stony Stratford, Wendover and Winslow

## Geography and Physical Character
The physical landscape surrounding these towns varies based on their location within the county's river basins and hill systems.

### The Chiltern System
Prince's Risborough and Wendover are located in the hilliest part of the county to the south, which is occupied by the Chiltern system. The crest-line of these hills crosses the county at its narrowest point, above the towns of Wendover and Prince's Risborough, in a line not exceeding 11 miles in length. This geographical feature divides the county into two distinct characters; to the south of this line, the land is well-wooded, hilly, and characterized by narrow vales. The Chilterns reach heights of nearly 900 feet within the county.

### The Ouse Basin
Stony Stratford is located in the basin of the river Ouse. The uppermost course of the Ouse passes through several towns, including Stony Stratford, as well as Buckingham, Wolverton, Olney, and Newport Pagnell.

## Communications and Transport
The towns of Prince's Risborough and Stony Stratford are linked to broader transport networks via rail and historical boundaries.

### Railway Services
Prince's Risborough is served by multiple railway companies. The Great Western company operates a branch from Maidenhead to Oxford that serves the town, from which branches extend to Aylesbury and Watlington. Additionally, a line jointly owned by the Great Western and Great Central companies passes through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield. This joint line was connected northward with the Great Central system upon its opening in 1906.

### Historical Boundaries
Stony Stratford is mentioned in the context of the peace between Alfred the Great and Guthrum (ratified approximately in the year 880). The agreement determined the boundary of Guthrum's East Anglian kingdom, specifying that the boundary would run eastwards up the Ouse to Watling Street in the vicinity of Stony Stratford or Fenny Stratford.

## Notable Landmarks
The area between Prince's Risborough and Wendover is home to Chequers Court. Dating from the close of the 16th century, this country house is noted for its beautiful situation high among the Chiltern Hills and its collection of relics belonging to Oliver Cromwell.
