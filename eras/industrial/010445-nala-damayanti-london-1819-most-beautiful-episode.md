# Nala and Damayanti (London, 1819)

## Overview
*Nala and Damayanti* is described as the most beautiful episode of the *Mahabharata*. A critical edition of this story was published in London in 1819. This specific edition included notes and a translation into Latin.

## Authorship and Publication
The 1819 London edition was published by Bopp. Bopp's work on this text was part of a broader engagement with Sanskrit studies. Prior to his work in London, Bopp had spent a brief period of time in Germany. During his stay in London, he developed friendships with H.T. Colebrooke and Sir Charles Wilkins. He also became an acquaintance of Wilhelm von Humboldt, who served as the Prussian ambassador at the court of St James's; Bopp provided instruction in Sanskrit to Humboldt.

## Academic Context and Later Works
The publication of *Nala and Damayanti* was followed by several other editions of episodes from the *Mahabharata*. These included *Indralokagamanam* and three other episodes published in Berlin in 1824, as well as *Diluvium* and three others published in Berlin in 1829. Bopp eventually released a new edition of *Nala* in Berlin in 1832.

Along with A.W. Schlegel's 1823 edition of the *Bhagavadgita*, Bopp's editions of the *Mahabharata* served as excellent aids for early students learning to read Sanskrit texts. In addition to these editions, Bopp contributed to the *Annals of Oriental Literature* (London, 1820) with an essay titled "Analytical Comparison of the Sanskrit, Greek, Latin and Teutonic Languages." In this essay, he expanded his previous grammatical work on the verb to encompass all parts of the grammar.

## Transition in Research
Bopp's activities in editing Sanskrit texts concluded following the publication of the complete *Mahabharata* in Calcutta. After this event, he discontinued his editorial work on such texts and focused his efforts exclusively on grammatical investigations.
