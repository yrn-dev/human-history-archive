# Medicinal Use of Bromine Salts

Bromine is a chemical element of the halogen group with an atomic weight of 79.96. In the field of medicine, it is largely employed in the form of bromides of potassium, sodium, and ammonium, and is sometimes used in combination with other substances and alkaloids.

## Sedative and Hypnotic Applications
Bromide of potassium is described as the most generally applicable and safest sedative for the nervous system. Although its action is very weak, it is perfectly balanced across all nervous tissue. Sir Thomas Lauder Brunton has suggested that this effect occurs because the bromide replaces sodium chloride (common salt) within the fluids of the nervous system.

Because of these properties, bromide of potassium—and bromide of sodium, which may be even safer, though its action is not as certain—is utilized in several capacities:
* As a hypnotic.
* As a standard anaphrodisiac.
* As a sedative for mania and all forms of morbid mental excitement.
* For the treatment of hyperaesthesia of all kinds.

## Specific Medical Conditions
The most striking success of bromine salts is found in the treatment of epilepsy, for which it is considered the specific remedy. In grave cases of *grand mal* epilepsy, doses ranging from ten to fifty grains or more may be administered and continued for long periods without ill effect.

Other conditions in which bromides are frequently used include:
* Insomnia and delirium tremens.
* Asthma and migraine.
* Laryngismus stridulus.
* Hysteria and neuralgia.
* Certain nervous disorders of the heart.
* Nymphomania and spermatorrhoea.
* Symptoms often accompanying the climacteric in women.
* Strychnine poisoning.

## Comparative Use of Bromide Salts
Different salts of bromine are selected based on the patient's condition and the required speed of action:
* **Potassium Bromide:** This is the most certain and rapid of the three common bromides, though it may depress the heart in cases where that organ is in a morbid state.
* **Sodium Bromide:** This salt may be employed when the heart is morbid, as its base is inert.
* **Ammonium Bromide:** This is often invaluable for treating whooping-cough in instances where a sedative is required but a stimulant is also indicated.

Additionally, hydrobromic acid is frequently used to prevent or relieve the singing in the ears and headaches that can follow the administration of salicylates, salicylic acid, or quinine.

## Other Applications of Bromine
Beyond internal medicine, bromine is used for antiseptic purposes in the form of "bromum solidificatum," which consists of a substance such as kieselguhr impregnated with bromine at approximately 75% of its weight. Outside of medicine, the salts of bromine are widely used in photography, with bromide of silver being especially prominent.
