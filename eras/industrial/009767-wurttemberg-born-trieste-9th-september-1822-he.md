# Württemberg

## Political History and the Kingdom of Westphalia
The region of Württemberg has appeared in various historical contexts involving European diplomacy and territorial restructuring. Following the treaty of Tilsit on July 7, 1807, Jerome Bonaparte was raised to the throne of the new kingdom of Westphalia. Jerome had been united to Princess Catherine of Württemberg by an imperial decree from Napoleon, which had simultaneously annulled Jerome's previous marriage to Elizabeth Patterson. 

During Jerome's reign in Westphalia, the kingdom experienced several changes influenced by its connection to France. These included the introduction of the *Code Napoleon*, the abolition of feudalism, and the removal of repressive laws affecting the Jews. Additionally, higher education received some impulse during this period, although Jerome himself was frequently rebuked by the emperor for his debts, fondness for luxury, and numerous *amours*.

## 19th Century Political Shifts
In the mid-19th century, Württemberg was involved in the shifting power dynamics of the German states. Following the battle of Sadow, where the Prussian army crushed the Austrian army, Prussia established a North German Confederation. Württemberg, along with Bavaria, Baden, Hesse, and Austria, was excluded from this confederation.

## Notable Figures and Family Connections
Several individuals of historical note maintained familial or professional ties to Württemberg:

*   **Prince Napoleon (Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul):** Born at Trieste on September 9, 1822, he was the second son of Jerome Bonaparte and Princess Catherine of Württemberg. Known by the sobriquet "Plon-Plon," he was a proponent of advanced democratic ideas. He served as a representative of Corsica in the National Assembly after the revolution of 1848 and later served as the minister for the Colonies and Algeria starting in 1858. He also commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany during the war for the liberation of Italy.
*   **Count Alfred Guillaume Gabriel D'Orsay:** A famous wit and dandy born in Paris in 1801, D'Orsay was the grandson of the king of Württemberg through a morganatic marriage with his mother.
