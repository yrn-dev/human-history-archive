# Claudin's Histoire de l'imprimerie en France

## Overview and Scope
*Histoire de l'imprimerie en France*, published in 1900 and subsequent editions, is a comprehensive work by Claudin. Within the study of historical book production, it is recognized as the primary resource for material regarding the history of printing in France.

## Context in Bibliographic Study
The work is categorized among the essential texts for those researching illustrated books and the evolution of printing. It serves as the French counterpart to other specialized regional studies of the period, such as:
* **Italy:** Works by Lippmann (1888), Kristeller (1897), and the duc de Rivoli (1892, 1906).
* **Germany:** Muther's study on Gothic and Early Renaissance book illustration (1884).
* **Holland and Belgium:** Sir W.M. Conway's research on 15th-century woodcutters (1884).

## Relation to Illustrated Books
Claudin's work provides the necessary material for understanding the development of the printed book in France. This is particularly relevant when contrasted with other eras of illustration. For example, while early 16th-century illustrated books are discussed in Butsch's *Die Bucherornamentik der Renaissance* (1878), the "pretty French books" from the middle of the 16th century are noted as having been imperfectly appreciated. Similarly, the French books of the 18th century were later chronicled by H. Cohen in his *Guide de l'amateur de livre a gravures du XVIII^e siecle* (1886).
