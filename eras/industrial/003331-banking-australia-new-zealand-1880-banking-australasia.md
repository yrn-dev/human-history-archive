# Banking in Australasia

Banking in Australia and New Zealand during the late 19th century was characterized by a significant presence of British interests and a legal framework heavily influenced by English systems.

## Scale and Infrastructure
In Australia and New Zealand, the banking sector comprised 24 banks. These institutions held combined capitals of nearly £18,000,000 and deposits totaling approximately £130,000,000. The infrastructure supporting these operations was extensive, with nearly 1,700 offices serving the region. These banks were part of a larger network of more than 80 joint-stock British banks operating across various colonies.

## Legal Framework and Bankruptcy
The laws governing bankruptcy in Australia and New Zealand were modeled after the English system, though specific applications varied by colony:

*   **English Act Alignment:** New Zealand, Victoria, South Australia, and Western Australia followed the lines of existing English acts.
*   **Creditor-Based Systems:** In New South Wales, Tasmania, and Queensland, the system was based on the English act of 1869, which emphasized creditors' management over officialism.

A notable divergence from the English system occurred regarding the discharge of bankrupts. While the United Kingdom allowed bankrupts to remain undischarged—resulting in a population of 70,000 such individuals—the systems in New Zealand, Victoria, and New South Wales required a bankrupt to apply for discharge within a fixed period. Failure to do so resulted in the individual being found guilty of contempt of court.

## Taxation and Exemptions in New Zealand
In New Zealand, the financial landscape included specific tax regulations where income tax functioned as a complement to a land tax. Trading companies were taxed at a rate of 1s. in the pound, with no exemptions allowed. However, certain financial and community institutions were exempt from income tax, including:
*   Savings banks
*   Friendly societies
*   Co-operative dairy companies
*   Public societies that did not carry on business for profit

## Literature on Banking Practice
The study of banking in the region during this era was documented in specialized texts. Notable works include Edward B. Hamilton's *A Manual of the Law and Practice of Banking in Australia and New Zealand*, published in 1880, and his subsequent work, *Banking in Australasia*, published in 1883.
