# 1828 and the Evolution of American Political Parties

The year 1828 marked a significant turning point in American political history, characterized by a "tremendous victory" for the Jacksonians. This event is described as a great advance in radical democracy, surpassing the victory of 1800. During this era, the political landscape shifted as the Federalist party disappeared and the country largely adopted Jeffersonian democracy through the Republican party.

## The Rise of the Democrats and National Republicans

The Democratic-Republican party, originally associated with Jefferson, underwent a period of internal division. While the party was characterized by a devotion to liberty, abstract principles of democracy, and a "strict construction" of the constitution to prevent the aggrandizement of national power at the expense of citizens or states, these distinctions were not consistently maintained.

Following the War of 1812, "nationalistic" elements from the Federalist party—specifically those favoring commerce and manufactures—gradually joined the Republicans. This created a group of "broad-constructionists" within the Republican ranks. These groups eventually fused to form an independent party known as the National Republicans. Those who remained as Jacksonian Republicans subsequently became known simply as Democrats. Some historians date the origin of the modern Democratic party specifically to the period of 1828–1829.

## Political Developments in Kentucky

In Kentucky, the political environment was shaped by conflicts between 1820 and 1826 regarding the relief of delinquent debtors and the need for a more adequate currency system. This divided the state into "relief" and "anti-relief" parties. 

The relief party, or "new court" party, followed the leadership of Martin Van Buren and Andrew Jackson, eventually becoming Democrats. Conversely, the anti-relief or "old court" party followed John Quincy Adams and Henry Clay, becoming National Republicans and later Whigs. In the election of 1828, the electoral vote of Kentucky was cast for Jackson.

## Gubernatorial Transitions of 1828

The year 1828 saw transitions in the governorships of several states:

*   **Kentucky:** Joseph Desha's term ended in 1828, and Thomas Metcalfe, a National Republican, took office for the term 1828–1832.
*   **Louisiana:** Henry S. Johnson, a Democratic Republican, served until 1828, at which point Pierre Derbigny, also a Democratic Republican, took office. Derbigny subsequently died in office in 1829.
