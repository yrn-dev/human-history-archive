# Vincent Stuckey Lean and the Bristol Free Libraries

## The Bequest of Vincent Stuckey Lean
Vincent Stuckey Lean, who died in 1899, provided a significant financial bequest to the corporation of Bristol. He left the sum of £50,000 to the corporation for the purpose of further developing the city's free libraries. Lean's bequest was made with a particular emphasis on the sustenance and formation of a general reference library that would be scientific in character and of a high standard.

## Development of Bristol's Public Libraries
The city of Bristol maintains a history of providing free library services, with its city free library dating back to 1613 and being considered the original of its kind. In 1893, the corporation took over the Bristol museum and reference library. Following the financial support provided by Lean's bequest, the central library was opened in 1906. Additionally, an art gallery was opened in 1905, which had been presented by Sir William Henry Wills.

## Other Public Buildings in Bristol
The ecclesiastical buildings of the city often overshadow the interest of the public buildings. Notable structures include:
* **The Council House:** Located at the "Cross" where four main thoroughfares meet, this building dates from 1827 and was enlarged in 1894. It houses many portraits, including works by Kneller and Van Dyck, as well as the city archives.
* **The Guildhall:** A building designed in a modern Gothic style.
* **The Exchange:** A noteworthy building constructed in 1743 by John Wood, a famous architect of Bath, which serves as a corn-market.
* **Colston Hall:** Used for meetings and concerts, this institution commemorates Edward Colston (d. 1721), a benefactor of the city and revered citizen whose name appears on several institutions and buildings.

Other city landmarks include a bank located near St Stephen's church, which claims to be the origin of the first savings-bank established in England in 1812.
