# Daniel Garrison Brinton

## Early Life and Education
Daniel Garrison Brinton was born on May 13, 1837, in Thornbury, Pennsylvania. He pursued his higher education at Yale, from which he graduated in 1858. Following his graduation, Brinton spent two years studying at the Jefferson Medical College. He further expanded his academic training by traveling in Europe for one year, where he continued his studies in Heidelberg and Paris.

## Military Service during the Civil War
From 1862 to 1865, Brinton served as a surgeon in the Union army during the American Civil War. During this period of service, he held a leadership role for one year, from 1864 to 1865, acting as the surgeon in charge of the U.S. Army general hospital located at Quincy, Illinois.

## Medical and Academic Career
After the conclusion of the war, Brinton practiced medicine for several years in Westchester, Pennsylvania. He transitioned into medical publishing from 1874 to 1887, serving as the editor of a weekly periodical in Philadelphia titled the *Medical and Surgical Reporter*.

Brinton eventually moved into the fields of archaeology and ethnology. In 1884, he became a professor of archaeology and ethnology at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia. Subsequently, from 1886 until his death, he served as a professor of archaeology and American linguistics at the University of Pennsylvania.

## Professional Affiliations and Leadership
Brinton was a member of various learned societies across Europe and the United States. He held the presidency of several organizations at different times, including:
* The American Association for the Advancement of Science
* The American Folk-Lore Society
* The Numismatic and Antiquarian Society of Philadelphia

## Published Works
Between 1859, the year he published his first book, and 1899, Brinton authored a score of books, as well as numerous brochures, magazine articles, pamphlets, and addresses. His primary works include:
* *The Myths of the New World* (1868), which represented the first attempt to correlate and analyze American Indian mythology using true scientific principles.
* *The Religious Sentiment: Its Sources and Aim: A Contribution to the Science and Philosophy of Religion* (1876).
* *American Hero Myths* (1882).
* *Essays of an Americanist* (1890).
* *Races and Peoples* (1890).
* *The American Race* (1891).
* *The Pursuit of Happiness* (1893).
* *Religions of Primitive People* (1897).

Additionally, Brinton edited the *Library of American Aboriginal Literature*, an eight-volume set published between 1882 and 1890, which is considered a valuable contribution to American anthropology. Brinton edited six of these eight volumes.

## Death
Daniel Garrison Brinton died in Philadelphia on July 31, 1899.
