# Industrial Competition and Native Manufacturing in Barcelona

## Industrial Pre-eminence of Barcelona
Barcelona has served as the commercial and industrial center of eastern Spain for a significant period, with a pre-eminence that dates back to the 12th and 13th centuries. While the city experienced a temporary check resulting from the disasters of the Spanish-American War of 1898, it demonstrated rapid recovery. Less than a year after those events, Barcelona paid approximately L550,000 in commercial and industrial taxes, representing more than 11% of the total amount collected throughout the kingdom. Within five years, the city established itself as a port of regular call for thirty-five major shipping companies. Additionally, Barcelona housed the head offices of thirteen other lines, most notably the Transatlantic Mail Company, which operated a fleet of twenty-five steamships.

## Manufacturing and Labor
The industrial sector in Barcelona provides employment to more than 150,000 workers of both sexes. The primary industries are the weaving and spinning of silk, cotton, and wool. Driven by an enterprising spirit, the Catalans have attempted to establish themselves in nearly every industry where native capital could compete with foreign interests. This drive toward industrial diversification became especially prominent following the institution of protectionist tariffs in 1892. As a result of these measures, native manufacturers have proven quite able to compete with their foreign rivals within peninsular markets.

## Economic Infrastructure and Trade
The prosperity of the region has been supported in part by the significant development of communication means within the four Catalan provinces and around the city of Barcelona. The city's trade profile involves both the import of foreign goods and the export of local products. Imports consist largely of combustibles, raw materials, and comestibles, though the city also imports a considerable amount of foreign manufactured goods.

The principal exports from this industrial center include:
* Machinery
* Boots, shoes, and leather goods
* Furniture and barrels
* Cigarette-paper, soap, and cotton goods
* Cereals, olive-oil, and wines
