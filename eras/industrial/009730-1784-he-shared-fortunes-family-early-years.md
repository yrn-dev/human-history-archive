## Scientific and Mathematical Advancements
The year 1784 was a period of significant achievement in the field of astronomy and physics, particularly regarding the law of gravitation and the stability of the solar system.

Pierre Simon Laplace made two crowning contributions to gravitational astronomy in 1784. First, he provided the explanation for the "great inequality" of Jupiter and Saturn. This irregularity, previously noted by Edmund Halley, was attributed by Laplace to the near approach to commensurability in the periods of the two planets, with a disturbance cycle completing in approximately 929-1/2 years. Second, Laplace proved the existence of a specific relation between the inclinations and eccentricities of planetary orbits, demonstrating that an increase in either element could only occur to a very small extent. This work contributed to the conclusion that the solar system was constructed for indefinite permanence.

Additionally, Joseph Louis Lagrange contributed a set of five elaborate papers to the Berlin Memoirs between 1781 and 1784, which served to unify his perfected methods and results. In the realm of chemistry, 1784 saw Richard Kirwan, an Irish scientist, engage in a controversy with Cavendish regarding experiments conducted on air.
