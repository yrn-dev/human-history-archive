# Population of Baden

## Demographic Growth and Area
At the start of the 19th century, Baden was a margraviate with a population of 210,000 and an area slightly exceeding 1,300 square miles. Through the acquisition of additional territory over time, the area increased to 5,823 square miles. By 1905, the population had grown to 2,009,320. The population density of the region is approximately 330 people per square mile.

## Religious and Social Composition
As of 1905, the religious distribution of the population was approximately 60% Roman Catholics and 37% Protestants, with 1-1/2% identifying as Jews and the remainder belonging to other confessions. Socially, about one-half of the population is classified as rural, defined as those living in communities with fewer than 2,000 inhabitants.

## Regional Distribution and Urban Centers
The population is most densely clustered in the north and in the areas surrounding the Swiss town of Basel. The capital of the duchy is Karlsruhe. The region is divided into several districts with the following chief towns and populations as of 1905:
*   **Mannheim District:** Mannheim (162,607) and Heidelberg (49,439).
*   **Karlsruhe District:** Karlsruhe (111,200) and Pforzheim (59,307).
*   **Freiburg-im-Breisgau District:** Freiburg (74,102).
*   **Constance District:** Constance (24,818).

Other important towns in the region include Lahr, Bruchsal, Baden-Baden, and Rastatt.

## Ethnic Origins
The inhabitants of Baden are of diverse origins. Those living to the south of the Murg are descended from the Franks, while those to the north are descended from the Alemanni. Additionally, the population of the Swabian plateau is derived from another race.

## Notable Towns and Health Resorts
Within the grand-duchy of Baden, several towns serve as significant watering-places and health resorts:

**Baden-Baden (or Baden):** This fashionable town is located in the Black Forest valley, 23 miles south by west of Karlsruhe. Its resident population grew from 12,779 in 1885 to 14,862 in 1895, and reached 16,238 by 1905. The town attracts over 70,000 annual visitors. While French nationals were the prevailing visitors until the war of 1870, English, Russian, and American visitors have become more numerous in recent years.

**Badenweiler:** Located at the western edge of the Black Forest, 28 miles north by east of Basel, this health resort has a permanent population of approximately 600. It is visited by 5,000 people annually for its whey cure, equable climate, and warm mineral springs.
