# Linz

Linz is a town in Austria that has served as a center for scientific publication, religious activity, and literary residence.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
The railway connecting Budweis to Linz is noted for its historical significance in Austrian infrastructure. Laid in 1827 for the use of horse-cars, this railway was the first line constructed in Austria.

## Scientific and Literary History
Linz was a site of significant astronomical work by Kepler. In 1617, Kepler published the first of a series of ephemerides at Linz, which were calculated based on the laws regulating the motions of the planets. Between 1618 and 1621, the *Epitome Astronomiae Copernicanae*, a textbook of Copernican science emphasizing physical astronomy and the Jovian system, was published in Linz and Frankfort.

The town also served as the place of residence for the Hungarian poet Janos Bacsanyi (1763–1845). After translating a proclamation by Napoleon in 1809—an act considered anti-Austrian—Bacsanyi sought refuge in Paris. Following the fall of Napoleon, he was returned to the Austrians, who permitted him to live in Linz on the condition that he never leave the town. While residing there, he edited the poetical works of Faludi and Anyos. Bacsanyi died in Linz on May 12, 1845.

## Religious History
In 1799, the German Roman Catholic theologian Martin Boos (1762–1825) moved to Linz. He was welcomed by Bishop Gall and began his work first at Waldneukirchen and then at Leonding. Boos eventually became the pastor at Gallneukirchen in 1806. He led a pietistic movement that gained significant traction among the Catholic laity and attracted approximately fifty to sixty priests. However, following the death of Bishop Gall and other supporters, Boos faced persecution and bitter enmity starting around 1812, resulting in numerous accusations in the consistorial courts.
