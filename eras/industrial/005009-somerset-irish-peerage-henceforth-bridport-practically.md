# Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport

## Early Naval Career
Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport (1727–1814), was a British admiral and the younger brother of Samuel, Lord Hood. He also shared a familial connection as a cousin to Captain Alexander Hood and Sir Samuel. Hood began his naval service in January 1741. Six years after entering the navy, he was appointed lieutenant of the "Bridgewater," a rank he held for ten years while serving on various ships.

Following this period, Hood was posted to the "Prince," which served as the flag-ship for Rear-Admiral Saunders. In this capacity, he served for a time in the Mediterranean. Upon returning home, he was appointed to the "Minerva" frigate. While commanding the "Minerva," Hood was present at Hawke's victory in Quiberon Bay on November 20, 1759. In 1761, the "Minerva" engaged in a long struggle to recapture the "Warwick," a ship of equal force. Later that same year, Captain Alexander Hood served in the Mediterranean aboard the "Africa" until peace was concluded.

## Professional Advancement and Command
From the conclusion of that peace, Hood remained in continuous employment both ashore and afloat. In 1778, while aboard the "Robust," he was present at the battle of Ushant. This action was followed by a court-martial of Admiral Keppel (who later became a Viscount). Hood was involved in the proceedings and took a course in Keppel's defence that aroused adverse popular feeling; however, this did not appear to hinder his professional progress.

Two years after the battle of Ushant, Hood was promoted to rear-admiral of the white. He succeeded Kempenfeldt as one of the flag-officers for Howe, and in 1782, he was present at the relief of Gibraltar while aboard the "Queen" (90). During his career, Hood also spent time as a member of the House of Commons. He was promoted to vice-admiral in 1787 and became a K.B. in 1788. He briefly flew his flag again in 1790 during the Spanish armament.

## The French Revolutionary Wars and Peerage
With the outbreak of war against France in 1793, Sir Alexander Hood returned to sea as the second in command for Howe. He participated in the operations that led to the "Glorious First of June." In recognition of his services during this conflict, he was created Baron Bridport of Cricket St Thomas in Somerset within the Irish peerage. Following this elevation, Bridport operated practically in independent command. In 1795, he led a partial action off Belle-Ile on June 23, an event that was regarded unfavourably in some quarters and remained subject to criticism.
