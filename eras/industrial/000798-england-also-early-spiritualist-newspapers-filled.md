# Early Spiritualist Publications and Phenomena in England

## Spiritualist Newspapers and Automatic Writing
In England, early newspapers dedicated to spiritualism were frequently filled with "inspirational" writing, including works such as *Pages of Ike Paraclete*. A significant aspect of this movement was the practice of automatic writing. Among the most notable English examples of this phenomenon are the *Spirit Teachings* produced by the Rev. W. Stainton Moses.

To facilitate the process of automatic writing during the early years of the spiritualist movement, practitioners employed a "planchette," which was a small, heart-shaped board equipped with wheels. While the phenomenon was susceptible to deception, records suggest that in the vast majority of cases, the writing was produced without the deliberate volition of the individual.

## Evolution of Automatic Writing Theory
Over time, the theory that external inspiration caused automatic writing became generally discredited. Consequently, the practice shifted toward a method for the experimental investigation of subconscious mental processes. This method frequently revealed knowledge that had lapsed from primary consciousness, such as foreign languages or forgotten fragments of poetry.

Parallel experimental work was conducted by Edmund Gurney. Gurney demonstrated that information provided to a subject while in a hypnotic trance could later be reproduced through handwriting, even while the subject's attention was fully occupied by reading aloud or conversing. Similarly, arithmetical problems set during a trance could be solved under these conditions without the subject's apparent consciousness.

## Daniel Dunglas Home
A prominent figure in the spiritualist movement was the Scottish spiritualist Daniel Dunglas Home (1833–1886). Born near Edinburgh, Home's mother belonged to a family credited with second sight. After spending time in the United States, where he emerged as a spiritualistic medium without seeking financial gain from his exhibitions, he arrived in England in 1855.

Home conducted numerous seances attended by well-known figures. The poet Robert Browning attended one such session; however, Browning maintained his contempt for spiritualism and later wrote the unflattering poem "Sludge the Medium" (1864) based on his impressions of Home. Despite this, Home gained many disciples and performed seances at various European courts.

## Legal and Scientific Controversies
Home's life was marked by several notable conflicts. Although he became a Roman Catholic, he was eventually expelled from Rome on the grounds that he was a sorcerer. In 1866, a wealthy widow named Mrs. Lyon adopted Home as her son and settled £60,000 upon him. Mrs. Lyon later sued for the return of the funds, claiming they were obtained through "spiritual" influence. The court ruled against Home because he failed to establish the validity of the gift.

Despite these setbacks, Home continued his work on the Continent. In 1871, he appeared before two Russian scientists and the tsar of Russia, who attested to the phenomena he evoked. Upon returning to England, Home underwent a series of experiments conducted by Professor (later Sir William) Crookes, who declared the pretensions to be thoroughly genuine. Professor von Boutlerow of the Russian Academy of Science expressed a similar opinion after witnessing similar experiments. Home authored two volumes titled *Incidents of my Life* and *Lights and Shadows of Spiritualism* before his death in Auteuil, France, on June 21, 1886.
