# Boiler Brickwork and Construction

In the construction of various boiler types, brickwork serves as a critical structural and functional component, providing containment for the fire and directing the flow of combustion gases to maximize heating efficiency.

## Brickwork in Specific Boiler Types

In three specific types of boilers, the brickwork is constructed to create a system of flues consisting of one central flue that runs along the bottom of the boiler and two side flues that extend upward along the sides, reaching nearly to the water-level. The standard arrangement for these boilers involves dividing the flue gases as they exit the internal flue; half of the gases return along each side flue to the front of the boiler, after which the entire volume passes downward into the central flue. These gases travel under the bottom of the boiler until they reach the back end and exit into the chimney. 

In some instances, this arrangement is reversed, with gases first passing through the bottom flue and then returning via the side flues. While this reversed configuration promotes more rapid water circulation, it is unsuitable for boilers where heavy deposits form on the bottoms and requires the use of two dampers.

## Water-Tube Boiler Enclosures and Baffles

Water-tube boilers, where water is primarily contained in small tubes, typically feature an outer casing made of brickwork or other fire-resisting substances to enclose both the fire and the tubes. In certain designs, the casing is not exposed to direct fire because the fire is entirely surrounded by water-tubes.

Specific water-tube models utilize brickwork for structural support and gas regulation:

*   **The "Stirling" Boiler:** The entire boiler is enclosed in brickwork, which also incorporates the supporting girders and columns. This boiler employs brickwork baffles to force furnace gases to follow specified paths among the tubes. This includes a large combustion chamber between the boiler front and the tubes, where a baffle-bridge is occasionally constructed, and another chamber located between the first and second sets of tubes.
*   **The "Babcock & Wilcox" Boiler:** For land-based applications, the back and sides of this boiler are enclosed in brickwork up to the height of the center of the horizontal cylinder. The front utilizes an iron casing that is lined with brick at the lower section. Brickwork baffles are positioned between the tubes themselves, as well as between the cylinder and the nests of tubes, to ensure the products of combustion circulate properly, passing between the tubes three times.

## Other Brickwork Applications

Brickwork is also utilized in auxiliary systems associated with boilers. For example, a dust-catching apparatus designed by the Horsfall Furnace Syndicate in Edinburgh consists of a large circular block of brickwork measuring 18 feet in diameter and 13 feet 7 inches in height. This structure is situated between the boiler and the destructor cells. Internally, it features a spiral flue and an interior chamber with a domed top. This brickwork assembly acts as a heat reservoir, maintaining temperatures between 1500º and 1800º F, which helps maintain equable steam pressure in the boiler.

Additionally, in the "Cochran" boiler, the combustion chamber is designed with a "dry" back, utilizing doors lined with firebrick instead of a water space to provide easier access to the tube ends.
