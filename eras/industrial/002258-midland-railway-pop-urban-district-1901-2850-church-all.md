# Bakewell

Bakewell is a market town located in the western parliamentary division of Derbyshire, England. It is situated on the river Wye, 25 miles north-north-west of Derby, and is served by the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of its urban district was 2,850.

## The Church of All Saints
The town's church, All Saints, is mentioned in Domesday. Tradition attributes the construction of the nave to King John, although the western side of the tower is believed to be older. The interior of the church contains several monuments dedicated to the Manners and Vernon families, as well as some admirable examples of encaustic tiles. In the churchyard, an ancient runic rood-stone is located.

## Local Industry and Economy
Bakewell is known for several distinct trades and products. Sir Richard Arkwright established cotton manufacture within the town, and the surrounding neighborhood is involved in the working of marble and zinc. The town has gained fame for a specific type of jam-cake known as a "Bakewell pudding." Additionally, the town is noted for a chalybeate spring used for cases of chronic rheumatism, which features attached baths.

## Education and Institutions
The town's institutional history includes the founding of almshouses in 1602, known as St John's hospital. In 1637, Lady Grace Manners endowed a free grammar school. Modern additions to the town's infrastructure include a museum, a town hall, and the Bakewell and High Peak Institute.

## Geography and Landmarks
The scenery surrounding Bakewell is described as very beautiful, particularly within the Derwent and Wye valleys. Nearby landmarks include:

*   **Castle Hill:** Located in the vicinity, this site contains the remains of an earthwork said to have been raised by Edward the Elder in 924.
*   **Mansions:** The parish includes the mansions of Castle Hill and Burton Closes.
*   **Haddon Hall:** Situated two miles from the town, surrounded by meadows and gardens.
*   **Chatsworth:** A magnificent domain located to the east.
*   **Eyam:** A specially picturesque village near the Derwent. This village was almost depopulated by the plague of 1665, which was carried there from London. The rector of Eyam, William Mompesson, gained wide notice for his brave efforts to combat the outbreak.
