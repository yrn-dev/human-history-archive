# Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton

Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton (1827– ) was a French painter born on May 1, 1827, in Courrières, Pas de Calais, France. His early aptitude for art led him to study under the historical painter de Vigne in Ghent in 1843, followed by studies under Baron Wappers in Antwerp in 1846. He eventually worked in Paris under Drolling.

## Artistic Evolution and Subjects
Breton's initial artistic efforts focused on historical subjects, including "Saint Piat preaching in Gaul." Following the revolution of 1848, he produced a work titled "Misery and Despair." However, Breton eventually determined that he was not suited for historical painting and shifted his focus toward the memories of nature and the countryside from his youth.

By 1853, he began exhibiting works reflecting rustic life, such as the "Little Gleaner" in Brussels and the "Return of the Harvesters" at the Paris Salon. He became primarily a painter of rustic life, focusing specifically on the province of Artois. He left this region only three times for brief excursions: to Provence in 1864, and to Brittany in 1865 and 1873. His time in Brittany provided him with some of his most successful studies of religious scenes.

Breton's extensive body of work is generally categorized into four main classes:
* Labour
* Rest
* Rural festivals
* Religious festivals

## Notable Works
Breton received several accolades for his paintings. "The Day after St Sebastian's Day" (1855) earned him a third-class medal, while "Blessing the Fields" (1857) received a second-class medal. In 1861, he was awarded a first-class medal for "Evening" and "Women Weeding." He also received a medal of honour for "The Fountain" (1872).

His significant works include:
* **Early and Mid-Career:** "Erecting a Calvary" (1859), located in the Lille gallery; "The Return of the Gleaners" (1859), located in the Luxembourg; "Grandfather's Birthday" (1862); "The Close of Day" (1865); "Harvest" (1867); and "Potato Gatherers" (1868).
* **Brittany and Later Works:** "A Pardon, Brittany" (1869); "The Bonfires of St John" (1875); "Women mending Nets" (1876), located in the Douai museum; and "A Gleaner" (1877), located in the Luxembourg.
* **Late Career:** "Evening, Finistère" (1881); "The Song of the Lark" (1884); "The Last Sunbeam" (1885); "The Shepherd's Star" (1888); "The Call Home" (1889); "The Last Gleanings" (1895); "Gathering Poppies" (1897); "The Alarm Cry" (1899); and "Twilight Glory" (1900).

## Professional Recognition
In 1886, following the death of Baudry, Breton was elected to the Institut. In 1889, he was made com[mandeur].
