# Henri Alexis Brialmont

Henri Alexis Brialmont (1821–1903) was a Belgian military engineer and general who served as the inspector-general of fortifications and of the corps of engineers. Born on May 25, 1821, in Venlo, Limburg, he was the son of General Laurent Mathieu Brialmont. Brialmont was educated at the Brussels military school and began his military career in 1843 as a sub-lieutenant of engineers.

## Military Career and Promotions
Brialmont rose through the ranks of the Belgian army over several decades. He became a lieutenant in 1847 and served as the private secretary to General Baron Chazal, the war minister, from 1847 to 1850. He entered the staff corps in 1855, achieving the rank of major in 1861, lieutenant-colonel in 1864, colonel in 1868, and major-general in 1874. 

Upon reaching the rank of major-general, he initially served as the director of fortifications in the Antwerp district starting in December 1874. Nine months later, he was appointed inspector-general of fortifications and of the corps of engineers. He was promoted to lieutenant-general in 1877. Brialmont retired from service in 1886, though he continued to supervise defenses in Rumania after his retirement. He died on September 21, 1903.

## Evolution of Fortification Principles
Brialmont's approach to engineering evolved significantly throughout his career. In his early stages, his plans closely followed the ideas of Vauban; for example, his original scheme for the fortification of Antwerp utilized a bastioned trace for both the forts and the enceinte. By 1859, during the implementation of the great entrenched camp at Antwerp, Brialmont had shifted toward the ideas of Montalembert and the school of polygonal fortification.

Following the events of 1870-71 and the introduction of long-range guns, Brialmont developed his own distinct types and plans. His final principles of fortification were applied to the fortifications on the Meuse at Namur and Liége, as well as the extreme detached forts of the Antwerp region. These principles were characterized by:
* The multiplication of intermediate batteries.
* The suppression of all artillery positions that were open to overhead fire.
* The lavish use of armour to protect artillery located inside the forts.

## National and International Works
In his capacity as inspector-general, Brialmont drafted and carried out the entire scheme for the defences of Belgium. While his far-reaching schemes for Belgian fortifications faced significant opposition, which caused him disappointment, his expertise was sought internationally.

In 1883, Brialmont traveled to Rumania to advise on the country's required fortification works. He presided over the elaboration of a scheme to transform Bucharest into a first-class fortress. This project, which was constructed between 1885 and 1896, embodied the "system of Brialmont." The resulting defenses featured a perimeter of approximately 48 miles, defended by 36 batteries and armoured forts, with the forts situated at a mean distance of 4 miles from the city.

Brialmont's work in Rumania led to a period where he was placed *en disponibilité* in his own service, as he had undertaken the Bucharest works without the authorization of his sovereign. This action was partly influenced by Austria, which viewed the Bucharest fortifications as a menace. Brialmont returned to Belgium in 1884 to resume command of the Antwerp military district. Additionally, at the request of the Hellenic government, he prepared a scheme for the defence of Greece while he was in eastern Europe.

## Literary Contributions
Brialmont was an indefatigable writer who produced forty-nine pamphlets and twenty-three important works, in addition to various reviews, essays, and papers. In 1850, he originated the *Journal de l'armée Belge*. His most significant publications include:
* *La Fortification du temps présent* (1885)
* *Influence du tir plongeant et des obus-torpilles sur la fortification* (1888)
* *Les Régions fortifiées* (1890)
* *La Défense des états et la fortification à la fin du XIX^e siècle* (1895)
* *Progrès de la défense des états et de la fortification permanente depuis Vauban* (1898)
