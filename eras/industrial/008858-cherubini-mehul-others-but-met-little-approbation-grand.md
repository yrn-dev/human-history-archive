# Musical Figures and Influences: Cherubini and Mehul

## Professional Interactions and Influence
The composers Cherubini and Mehul were prominent figures in the musical landscape of Paris during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. Their influence extended to emerging composers, though not always with immediate success. For instance, Francois Adrien Boieldieu, a French composer of comic opera born in Rouen in 1775, moved to Paris in 1795 seeking success beyond his local achievements. Upon his arrival, Boieldieu submitted his scores to Mehul, Cherubini, and others, but these works met with little approbation. At the time, grand opera was the prevailing trend, and Boieldieu was forced to support himself as a pianoforte-player.

Boieldieu eventually achieved fame through his association with the fashionable singer P.J. Garat, who employed him as an accompanist in the drawing-rooms of the Directoire. Following the enormous success of his 1800 work, *Le Calife de Bagdad*, Boieldieu sought formal musical training and took lessons from Cherubini. The influence of Cherubini, described as a great master, is clearly evident in the higher artistic finish of Boieldieu's later compositions.

## Theoretical Contributions of Cherubini
Beyond his role as a teacher and critic, Cherubini held specific doctrines regarding musical composition, particularly concerning the fugue. He maintained the doctrine that a fugue cannot possess more than one subject. Consequently, Cherubini insisted on applying the term "subject" to the less prominent of the subjects in compositions that are commonly referred to as double, triple, or quadruple fugues (those beginning simultaneously with two or more subjects).

## Artistic Representation
The prominence of Cherubini and Mehul extended into the visual arts. The Italian sculptor Lorenzo Bartolini, who studied in Paris starting in 1797, executed several minor pieces for Denon, which included busts of both Mehul and Cherubini.

## Other Musical Connections
Cherubini continued to be a point of contact for international musicians. In 1825, the Irish composer Michael William Balfe was taken to Rome by Count Mazzara and was introduced to Cherubini during the journey. Balfe, who had begun his career as a violinist and operatic singer, went on to compose various dramatic works and operas in Italy and England.
