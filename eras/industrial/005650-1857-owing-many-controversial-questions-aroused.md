# 1857

The year 1857 is associated with several distinct events across the fields of literature, religion, politics, and academia.

## Literary Publications and Controversies
In 1857, Mrs. Gaskell first published the *Life of Charlotte Brontë*. The work sparked numerous controversial questions, specifically regarding the identity of Cowan Bridge school in relation to Lowood in *Jane Eyre*, the nature of the relationship between Branwell Brontë and the wife of his employer, and the supposed peculiarities of Mr. Brontë. Due to these controversies and other minor points, the third edition of the biography was considerably changed. 

Additionally, 1857 saw the publication of an English translation of Kuno Fischer's *Fr. Bacon* (translated by John Oxenford) and the publication of Ch. de Remusat's *Bacon, sa vie ... et son influence*.

## Religious and Ecclesiastical Activity
Thomas Binney, an English Congregationalist divine and opponent of the state church principle, traveled to the Australian colonies between 1857 and 1859. Binney had previously served as a popular minister at the Weigh House chapel in London and later at a larger chapel on Fish Street Hill.

## Political Appointments and Terms
In the political sphere, John Evelyn Denison served as the speaker of the House of Commons starting in 1857, a position he held until 1872. He was the brother of George Anthony Denison, an English churchman and champion of the ritualistic school.

In the United States, 1857 marked the end of the tenure of General Cass in the United States Senate, where he had served from 1849 to 1857. During his political career, Cass had been a member of the Senate from 1845 to 1848 as well, and had advocated for the Kansas-Nebraska Bill of 1854 and the Compromise Measures of 1850.
