# Sir William Lockhart and the Exploration of Wakhan

## The Mission of Sir William Lockhart
During the late 19th century, geographical knowledge of Central Asia was expanded through various missions and commissions. Among these was a mission led by Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart. Lockhart's expedition crossed the Hindu Kush mountains to enter Wakhan. Following this crossing, the mission returned to India via the Bashgol valley of Kafiristan. 

In addition to the general crossing, Lockhart's mission is credited with the first exploration of the Wakhijrui Pass. This pass serves as the junction where the Pamir-i-Wakhan meets the Little Pamir at Bozai-Gumbaz.

## Geography of Wakhan and Badakshan
Wakhan is a dependent district of Badakshan, a province located on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan. According to an Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873, Badakshan and Wakhan were acknowledged as fully belonging to the amir of Kabul, with boundaries limited to the southern or left bank of the Oxus.

The province of Badakshan is characterized by a "bottle-shaped" extension into the Pamirs. It is narrow at the neck, represented by the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, and swells eastward to include portions of the great and little Pamirs. The southern boundary of the province generally follows the crest of the Hindu Kush. This boundary is definite at the Dorah pass—which connects Ishkashim and Zebak with the Lutku valley leading to Chitral—and at the Khawak pass, which leads into the Panjshir valley. Between these two passes, the boundary is considered indefinite.

## Regional Topography and Subdivisions
Badakshan is divided into several provincial subdivisions. The eastern subdivisions include Wakhan, Zebak, Ishkashim, and Charan. The central region comprises Kishm, Minjan, Farkhar, and Faizabad. To the north lie Shiwa, Ragh, and Darwaz, while the western subdivisions include Anderab, Narin, Ghori, Kataghan, and Rustak.

The region's physical conformation is defined by the Hindu Kush, which acts as the southern edge of a great plateau. This plateau slopes northward toward the Oxus, descending from an average altitude of 15,000 feet to 4,000 feet at Faizabad and further down to 1,100 feet at Kunduz. To the south, the Hindu Kush breaks into long spurs that hide the isolated valleys of Kafiristan.

## Natural History and Trade Routes
The mountains of Wakhan are specifically noted for producing a breed of white hawks or falcons. These birds are caught among the cliffs by the hardy Wakhanis and are highly esteemed for their swiftness by the chiefs of Bokhara and Badakhshan. Additionally, the ramifications of the Hindu Kush are home to the *Kachkár* (or *Ovis Vignei*), a gregarious species of wild sheep that congregates in herds of several hundreds.

Historically, the region served as a vital link for continental travel. A significant trade route connecting China and Kashgar with Afghan Turkestan ran from Ishkashim through the Pamirs via the Panja. Another route, traced by comparing the itinerary of Captain Younghusband with the travels of Marco Polo, leads from Wakhan through the Wakhijrui Pass, the old fort of Kurgan-i-Ujadbai, the Tagh-dum-bash Pamir, Tásh Kurgán, Bulun Kul, and the Gez Defile before reaching Kashgar.
