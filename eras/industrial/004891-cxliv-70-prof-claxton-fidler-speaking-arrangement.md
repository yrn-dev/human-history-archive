## Civil Engineering and the Niagara Falls Bridge
The Niagara Falls and Clifton steel arch bridge was constructed to replace an older suspension bridge designed by Roebling. This structure features a two-hinged parabolic braced rib arch as its center span, supported by side spans measuring 210 and 190 feet. In terms of weight, the main span totaled 1629 tons, while the side spans weighed 166 and 154 tons. The bridge is designed to accommodate two footways, two roadways, and two electric-car tracks.

Prof. Claxton Fidler noted that the bridge represented the most significant advance to date in its type of construction, specifically regarding the arrangement used to apply initial stress to the top chord. Fidler explained that when a rib is erected on centering without this initial stress, the arch's own weight causes subsequent compression, which results in an excess of compression in the upper member at the crown and inflicts a bending stress. The engineer utilized "bold expedients" to annul this bending action.

## Paleoanthropology and the Neanderthal Men
Ethnologists have identified a species of extinct proto-men, known as Neanderthal Men or Neanderthalers, whose remains were discovered at Neanderthal and other locations. These creatures are classified as a different species within the same genus as humans, rather than being ancestral to the human line. 

Physically, Neanderthalers were distinguished by low foreheads, great brow ridges above the eyes, and heavy protruding jaws. Their jaw-bones are chinless and resemble the Heidelberg jaw-bone, differing markedly from human jaw-bones. Additionally, their teeth differed from the human pattern; their cheek teeth were more complicated in structure and lacked the long fangs of human cheek teeth, and they lacked the marked canines (dog teeth) found in ordinary humans. 

Their physical limitations included necks poised in a way that prevented them from looking up at the sky or turning their heads back, leading them to likely slouch forward with their heads down. Furthermore, their thumbs were not opposable to their fingers. While their skull capacity was quite human, the brain was lower in the front and bigger in the rear, indicating a different arrangement of intellectual faculties. These beings endured in Europe for hundreds or even thousands of years.

## Higher Education at Cornell University
The university's reputation is notably high in mechanical engineering, with Sibley College being developed primarily under the direction of Prof. Robert Henry Thurston from 1885 to 1903. Sibley College is divided into departments including electrical engineering, power engineering, experimental engineering, and machine design and construction.

Other prominent departments and schools include:
* **The Susan Linn Sage School of Philosophy**: Established via a $200,000 gift from Henry W. Sage in 1891, it is known for psychological laboratory investigations under Prof. E. B. Titchener and publishes *Cornell Studies in Philosophy* and *The Philosophical Review*.
* **Agriculture**: Led by Prof. Liberty Hyde Bailey.
* **Entomology**: Under Prof. J. H. Comstock.
* **Physics**: Under Prof. E. L. Nichols.
* **Latin Grammar**: The "Cornell School," led sequentially by Prof. W. G. Hale and Prof. C. E. Bennett.

The university produces several academic publications, such as the *Journal of Physical Chemistry*, *Physical Review*, *Cornell Studies in Classical Philology*, and *Studies in History and Political Science*. Student-led publications include the comic tri-weekly *The Cornell Widow* (1892), the literary monthly *The Cornell Magazine*, *The Cornell Daily Sun* (1880), *The Cornell Era* (1868), and *The Sibley Journal of Engineering* (1882).

Tuition fees are generally $100 annually, though they increase to $150 for architecture, medicine, and civil and mechanical engineering. Residents of New York state pay no tuition in the agricultural and veterinary colleges. Financial aid includes 150 free-tuition state scholarships (one per state assembly district), 36 undergraduate scholarships valued at $200 annually for two years, 17 graduate scholarships, and 23 fellowships (valued between $300 and $600).

## Biblical Criticism and Philology
In the study of the Old Testament, "higher criticism" is described as a method with various interrelated or independent conclusions. Generally accepted conclusions include that the Priestly Code is post-exilic, the book of Daniel was written in the 2nd century B.C., Isaiah did not author Isaiah xl.-lxvi., and Moses did not author the entire Pentateuch. Prof. Sayce, while opposing some aspects of higher criticism, has asserted that the Pentateuch is a compilation of older materials and likely reached its final form through Ezra.

Regarding biblical instruments, the *asor* is a "ten-stringed" instrument mentioned three times in the Bible (Psalm xxxiii. 2, Psalm xcii. 3, and Psalm cxliv.). It typically follows the word *nebel* and is likely a variant of the *nebel* with ten strings instead of the twelve attributed to it by Josephus.
