# Express Boilers of Messrs John I. Thornycroft & Co.

Messrs John I. Thornycroft & Co. produce two distinct forms of express boiler: the Thornycroft boiler and the Thornycroft-Schulz boiler. Both designs utilize a system of chambers connected by tubes to facilitate the heating process.

## The Thornycroft Boiler
The Thornycroft boiler is constructed with three chambers connected by tubes. For the majority of their length, these tubes are straight, though they are bent at each end to allow them to enter the water- and steam-chambers normally. 

The boiler employs a specific arrangement of "water-walls" to manage the flow of combustion gases. The outer rows of tubes function as water-walls at their lower sections, while their upper ends are designed to permit the passage of gases between them. Conversely, the inner rows of tubes form water-walls at their upper sections and remain open at their lower ends. This configuration compels the products of combustion to pass over the entirety of the heating surfaces. Regarding specifications, the fire-rows of tubes possess an outside diameter of 1-3/8 in., and the remaining tubes are also made with a 1-3/8 in. diameter. To assist with operation, large outside circulating pipes are situated at the front end of the boiler.

## The Thornycroft-Schulz Boiler
The Thornycroft-Schulz boiler differs from the standard Thornycroft model by utilizing four chambers and a fire-grate arranged in two separate portions. 

The tube arrangement in this model is as follows:
* **Outermost Rows:** On each side, the two outermost rows of tubes form water-walls at their lower parts and allow gases to pass between them at the upper part.
* **Inner Rows:** The rows located nearest to the fires are arranged in a manner similar to those found in the standard Thornycroft boiler.

Circulation within the outer sets of tubes is managed by large-diameter outer circulating pipes that connect the water- and steam-chambers. Additionally, the middle water-chamber is equipped with several nearly vertical down-comers located in the center of the boiler. Due to their design, boilers of the Thornycroft-Schulz type are used extensively in the German and British navies.
