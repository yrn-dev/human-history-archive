# Maria Constantinovna Bashkirtseff

## Early Life and Education
Maria Constantinovna Bashkirtseff, also known as Marie, was a Russian writer and artist born on November 23, 1860, at Gavrontsi in the government of Pultowa, Russia. Her father served as the marshal of the nobility at Pultowa. When Marie was seven years old, her parents became unable to live together due to incompatibility. Consequently, Madame Bashkirtseff and her daughter left Russia. They spent their winters in Italy or Nice and their summers at German watering-places.

During her youth, Marie received an education that was superior to that typically provided to most girls of her rank. She developed a high level of linguistic and classical proficiency, gaining the ability to read Virgil and Plato in the original and write in four different languages with almost equal facility.

## Musical and Artistic Career
Marie was a gifted musician and initially pursued a career as a singer, undertaking serious study in Italy for this purpose. However, her voice lacked the strength required to withstand hard work and eventually failed her. During the period she was studying music, she was also learning to draw. Following the loss of her singing voice, she devoted herself entirely to painting.

In 1877, Marie settled in Paris, where she maintained a steady practice in the studio of Tony Robert-Fleury. Her work was exhibited in the salon on several occasions:
*   1880: A portrait of a woman.
*   1881: "Atelier Julian".
*   1882: "Jean et Jacques".
*   1884: "Meeting" and a pastel portrait of her cousin.

The pastel portrait of her cousin was placed in the Luxembourg gallery and earned her a *mention honorable*.

## Later Life and Death
Despite her professional success, Marie's health was always delicate. She imposed a heavy labor upon herself, which, combined with the life of fashion she entered following her success as an artist, proved unsustainable. She died of consumption on October 31, 1884, leaving behind a small series of works described as having remarkable promise.

## Literary Legacy
Throughout her childhood, Marie kept an autobiographical journal. These writings were published in 1890 as *Journal de Marie Bashkirtseff*, described as "brilliant confessions." The editors of the journal sought to captivate readers by highlighting Marie's precocious gifts and the distinguished individuals she encountered. However, the treatment of certain portions of the text drew vehement protest, which has led some to question the truthfulness of the book, despite it being considered extraordinarily interesting.
