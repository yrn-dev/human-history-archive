# The Determination of Longitude at the Tashkent Observatory

## Geodetic Surveys in Russia
The Russian Empire established a highly precise system of geodetic surveys across its European territories. This system utilized direct triangulation to connect St Petersburg with Tiflis, located on the southern slopes of the Caucasus. Furthermore, St Petersburg was connected to Greenwich through European triangulation systems. Consequently, Russia adopted the Greenwich meridian as the zero point for all of its longitude values.

## Challenges in Central Asia
While rigorous geodetic methods were successful in Europe, they encountered significant obstacles beyond the eastern shores of the Caspian Sea. The arid nature of the trans-Caspian deserts proved to be an insuperable barrier to the first-class triangulation methods used in the European half of the empire. Because the surveys of Asiatic Russia were separated from those of Europe by the Caspian Sea, Russian geography in Central Asia had to rely on means other than direct measurement to establish coordinate values for latitude and longitude.

## The Tashkent Observatory
To address these geographical challenges, the astronomical observatory at Tashkent was adopted as the initial starting point for the trans-Caspian triangulation of Russia. This specific triangulation is classified as second-class and has extended to the Pamir frontier beyond Osh.

The longitude of the Tashkent observatory was determined differentially with Pulkova using the telegraph.
