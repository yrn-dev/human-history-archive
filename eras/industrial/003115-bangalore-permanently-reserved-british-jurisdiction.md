# Bangalore

## Overview and Geography
Bangalore is a city in India that serves as the capital of the native state of Mysore. It is situated 3,113 feet above sea level and is located 219 meters west of Madras by rail. As of 1901, the city had a population of 69,447. The broader district of Bangalore borders the Madras district of Salem and covers an area of 3,079 square miles. The district's geography is primarily defined by the valley of the Arkavati river, which joins the Cauvery river on the southern frontier. In 1901, the population of the district was 789,664, reflecting a 10% increase over the preceding decade.

## Historical Foundations and Early Rule
The present fort of Bangalore was established by a descendant of Kempe-Goude. Kempe-Goude was a husbandman from the neighboring country who, likely during the 16th century, settled a few miles north of Bangalore to escape the tyranny of the local *wadeyar*. Transitioning from a farmer to a warrior, he conquered Bangalore and subsequently erected fortresses there and at Savendrug.

Control of Bangalore later shifted as it was wrested from Kempe-Goude's descendants by Bijapur. It was eventually listed among the *jagirs* of Shahji, the father of Sivaji (the founder of the Mahratta sway), who appears to have resided there during his early career in the service of the Bijapur state. Possession later passed to Venkaji, one of Shahji's sons. However, because Venkaji occupied Tanjore, he considered Bangalore too distant to be safe given the circumstances of the time.

## Transitions of Power
In 1687, Venkaji attempted to sell Bangalore to Chikka Deva, the raja of Mysore, for three lakhs of rupees. Before this transaction could be finalized, Kasim Khan, the commander of Aurangzeb's forces, marched upon and entered the city with almost no resistance. Kasim Khan did not wish to keep the city and immediately delivered it to Chikka Deva upon the payment of the three lakhs of rupees.

Later, in 1758, Nanjiraj, a powerful minister to the raja, granted Bangalore as a *jagir* (or fief) to Hyder Ali. Hyder Ali, who later became the usurper of Mysore, strengthened and enlarged the fort. This fort served as a refuge for Hyder Ali in 1760 following his expulsion from Seringapatam. Additionally, the fort is known as the traditional site where Sir David Baird was first held captive after the defeat at Perambakam in 1780.

## Modern Infrastructure and Economy
Bangalore is the largest British cantonment in southern India. The district is served by several railway lines. Economically, the region supports industrial activity; outside the city of Bangalore, there is a woollen mill that produces woollen stuffs, cloth for greatcoats, and blankets.

The state of Mysore also includes the town and district of Kolar, located 43 miles east of Bangalore. Kolar, which has an ancient foundation, has been almost completely modernized and is known for the breeding of turkeys for export and the weaving of blankets.
