# Essling

Essling (also referred to as Esslingen) is a location situated close to the Danube river. It is historically significant as a site of military conflict during the Napoleonic campaigns and as a subject of literary interest for the author Balzac.

## The Battle of Aspern-Essling

The Battle of Aspern-Essling took place on May 21 and 22, 1809, between the French forces and their allies, led by Napoleon, and the Austrian army commanded by Archduke Charles. At the start of the engagement, Napoleon held Vienna, but the bridges over the Danube had been broken. To initiate the battle, the French utilized Lobau—one of the islands dividing the river—as a crossing point. After bridging the channels on the night of May 19–20, Napoleon collected 40,000 men on the Marchfeld, a broad open plain on the river's left bank.

The French forces were positioned on the Marchfeld facing north. Their line was anchored with the village of Aspern (Gross-Aspern) on the left and the village of Essling on the right. Because both villages were located close to the Danube, they could not be turned.

### Military Engagements at Essling

During the first day of fighting, Essling was the site of desperate combat. French cuirassiers launched repeated charges against the flank of the force commanded by Rosenberg, which served to delay the Austrian assault. Within the village, Lannes led a single division in a successful and heroic resistance that lasted until the end of the day.

The battle resumed at dawn on May 22. Rosenberg eventually succeeded in storming Essling, but Lannes, reinforced by the division of St Hilaire, resisted desperately and managed to drive Rosenberg out. Later in the day, Essling fell again to another assault by Rosenberg. Although a portion of the French Guard drove the Austrians out once more, Rosenberg redirected his efforts toward the flank of the French centre.

### Conclusion of the Battle

The battle ended in a costly retirement for the French. The situation was exacerbated when the Austrians set heavy barges adrift downstream, which cut the Danube bridges. The French suffered heavy losses, with 44,000 men lost out of 90,000 engaged; among the killed were St Hilaire and Lannes. The Austrians, with a force of 75,000, lost 23,360 men. This engagement is noted as the first great defeat of Napoleon. Following the battle, the French forces were withdrawn to the island of Lobau, where the great bridge was repaired on the night of May 22.

## Literary Connection

The battle of Essling was the intended subject of a considerable book titled *La Bataille*, written by Balzac. To prepare for this work, Balzac actually visited the ground. In his early letters to Madame Hanska, he frequently referred to the book as being in progress. However, the work has never been found, remaining one of the titles of his production that never moved from his head into print, or for which only fragmentary manuscript sketches exist.
