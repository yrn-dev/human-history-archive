# Courier

The *Courier* was the first daily newspaper established in Buffalo, beginning publication in 1831. By 1908, the city of Buffalo had eleven daily papers, including two in Polish and three in German. Other publications in the city included several weekly papers in German, three in Polish, and one in Italian. Additionally, a weekly paper called the *Gazette* was established in 1811, which later became a daily known as the *Commercial* in 1835.

## Other Publications Named Courier

In Louisville, a newspaper called the *Courier* was established in 1843 and owned by Walter N. Haldeman. In 1868, the *Courier* was consolidated with the *Democrat* (established 1844) and the *Journal* (established 1830). This consolidation resulted in the creation of the *Courier Journal*, a morning Democratic newspaper that is considered one of the most influential newspapers in the South. Henry Watterson, who had become editor of the *Journal* in 1867, became the editor of the *Courier Journal* upon its formation in 1868.

In Charleston, South Carolina, a publication called the *Courier* existed in 1823, where James Gordon Bennett served for a few months as a translator of Spanish.

## Related Journalistic Figures

James Gordon Bennett, the founder and editor of the *New York Herald*, had a varied career in journalism before establishing his own paper on May 6, 1835. Between 1827 and 1832, he served as the Washington correspondent for the *New York Enquirer* and later for the *Courier and Enquirer*. 

Another American journalist, Gamaliel Bailey (1807–1859), edited several publications. After a period editing a religious journal called the *Methodist Protestant* in Baltimore, he later edited a daily paper called the *Herald* starting in 1843. He also held editorial control of the *Philanthropist* from 1836 to 1847 and later assumed control of the *National Era* in Washington, D.C., in 1847.
