# 1821

The year 1821 is marked in the archive by the deaths of several prominent figures across Europe and the births of individuals who would later achieve distinction in philanthropy, literature, and education.

## Notable Deaths of 1821

Several figures from the arts and royalty passed away in 1821. In England, Mrs. Elizabeth Inchbald died on August 1. Born in 1753 in Standingfield, Suffolk, she was a playwright, novelist, and actress. Despite an impediment in her speech that hindered her success on the stage, she had a career that began in 1772 when she left home for London. She made her debut in Bristol as Cordelia and performed various roles in the provinces, including Juliet, Desdemona, and Anne Boleyn, before making her first London appearance at Covent Garden in 1780. She eventually retired from acting in 1789 to pursue her success as an author.

In the French theater, the first Madame Dugazon died in 1821. The daughter of a Berlin dancing master, she began her stage career at age twelve as a dancer. She debuted as an actress "with songs" at the Comedie Italienne in 1774 in the play *Sylvain*. She became a *societaire* in 1776 and was highly celebrated in Paris for over twenty years. She was specifically identified with roles of young mothers and women past their first youth, types of parts that subsequently became known as "*dugazons*" and "*meres dugazons*."

The year also saw the death of Caroline Amelia Augusta, the queen of George IV of Great Britain. Born in 1768 as the second daughter of the duke of Brunswick-Wolfenbuttel, she had a tumultuous marriage to the prince of Wales, from whom she separated after the birth of a daughter in 1796. After years of residence on the continent, she returned to England in June 1820 to claim her rights. This led to a high-profile trial in the House of Lords beginning in August 1820 on grounds of adultery, though the bill to dissolve the marriage was abandoned in November.

Additionally, the archive records the death of Lorenzo Chiappini in 1821. Upon his death, he left a letter to Maria Stella, his putative daughter, confessing that she was not his biological child but the daughter of a French nobleman. This revelation prompted Maria Stella to spend the remainder of her life attempting to establish her true identity.

## Births and Early Life

The year 1821 saw the birth of several women who would make significant contributions to their respective fields.

Lucie Duff-Gordon was born on June 24, 1821, to John and Sarah Austin in London. A woman of letters, she was known for her social gifts and her work as a hostess. She translated several works from German and French, including *Ancient Grecian Mythology* (1839) and *The Amber Witch* (1844). She also authored *Letters from the Cape* and *Letters from Egypt*.

Clara Barton, an American philanthropist, was born in 1821 in Oxford, Massachusetts. Educated at the Clinton Liberal Institute, she initially worked as a teacher before serving as a clerk in the Patent Office in Washington from 1854 to 1857. During the American Civil War, she provided supplies for wounded soldiers and organized a bureau of records to locate missing men. She later became the first president of the American Red Cross, holding the office until 1904.

## Educational and Professional Developments

In the field of education, 1821 was a pivotal year for Mary Mason Lyon. An American educationalist born in 1797, Lyon had spent years supporting her own education through spinning, weaving, and teaching in district schools. In 1821, she attended the girls' school in Byfield, which had been established by Joseph Emerson in 1819. This period of study and teaching contributed to her later development of a plan for a permanent institution to train young women for usefulness, which culminated in the 1837 opening of the Mount Holyoke Female Seminary.

In the performing arts, 1821 marked a milestone for Augustine Susanne Brohan. Having entered the Conservatoire at age eleven, she won the first prize for comedy in 1821, following a second prize in 1820. This achievement preceded her apprenticeship in the provinces and her eventual debut at the Odéon in 1832.
