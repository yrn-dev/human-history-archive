# Bombay

## Geography and History
The Bombay Presidency comprises a long strip of land along the Indian Ocean, extending from the north of Mysore to the south of the Punjab. The coastline is characterized as regular and little broken, with the exception of the Gulfs of Cutch and Cambay, which surround the peninsula of Kathiawar. Because the coast is rock-bound and difficult to access, only a few locations—specifically Karwar, Marmagoa, Karachi-in-Sind, and Bombay—possess harbours sufficiently land-locked to protect shipping during the south-west monsoon.

The city and island of Bombay derive their name from Mumba, a form of Parvati, the goddess of the Kolis. The Kolis, a race of fishermen and husbandmen, are the earliest known inhabitants, likely occupying the island around the beginning of the Christian era. Originally, Bombay consisted of seven islands, referred to by Ptolemy as the *Heptanesia*. The area was part of the dominions of various dynasties in western India, including the Rashtrakutas, Chalukyas, Mauryas, and Satavahanas. Between 450 and 750, during the Chalukya and Maurya periods, the principal location in the harbour was the city of Puri on Elephanta Island. 

The first town established on Bombay Island was Mahikavati (Mahim), founded by King Bhima (likely of the house of the Yadavas of Deogiri) following a raid into the Deccan by Ala-ud-din Khilji in 1294. Mahikavati remained under Hindu rule until 1348, when it was captured by a Mahommedan force from Gujarat. The islands remained part of the kingdom or province of Gujarat until 1534, when Sultan Bahadur ceded them to the Portuguese.

## Government and Administration
The government of Bombay is administered by a governor in council. This body consists of the governor, who serves as president and is appointed from England, and two ordinary members. These members are selected from the Indian civil service and are appointed by the crown; together with the governor, they serve as the executive members of the government. Each executive council member is responsible for one or two government departments. Each department is staffed by an assistant secretary, an under-secretary, a secretary, and a numerous staff of clerks.

Legislative functions are handled by a legislative council. This council includes the governor and his executive council, along with between eight and twenty other persons, at least half of whom must be non-officials. 

At the district level, administration is carried out by assistant collectors, collectors, and a varying number of supernumerary assistants. The political administration of native states is supervised by British agents stationed at the principal native courts. The specific position of these agents varies depending on the relationship between the paramount power and the principalities.

## Justice and Judiciary
The administration of justice throughout the presidency is conducted by a high court located at Bombay. This court consists of seven puisne judges and a chief justice. Supporting this high court are assistant judges and district judges located throughout the districts of the presidency.

## Local Government and Education
The municipal government of Bombay city was established by an act of the Bombay legislative council in 1888. The governing structure consists of a town council and a municipal corporation. The corporation is composed of 72 members:
* 16 are nominated by the government.
* 36 are elected by ratepayers.
* 16 are elected by justices of the peace.
* 2 are elected by the chamber of commerce.
* 2 are elected by the senate of the university.

The members of the corporation include Parsees, Mahommedans, Hindus, and Europeans. The town council serves as the standing committee for the corporation and consists of 12 members; 4 are nominated by the government, while the remaining 8 are elected by the corporation. Additionally, a port trust manages the warehouses, docks, and the port of Bombay, with members nominated by the government from the commercial community.

Educational institutions in Bombay City include the government law school, the government Elphinstone College, the Grant medical college, the Victoria Jubilee technical institute, the Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy school of art, and two missionary colleges, St Xavier and Wilson. The Bombay University was established in 1857 as an examining body, modeled after the university of London.
