# Heinrich Brockhaus

## Life and Career
Heinrich Brockhaus (1804–1874) was a German publisher who played a significant role in the expansion of the publishing business established by his father, Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus. Alongside his brother Friedrich Brockhaus (1800–1865), Heinrich managed the family firm, under whose leadership the business was considerably extended.

Due to his contributions to science and literature, the university of Jena recognized Heinrich Brockhaus in 1858 by awarding him an honorary doctorate of philosophy. He died in Leipzig on November 15, 1874, and had been made an honorary citizen of that city in 1872.

## Political Activity
Between the years 1842 and 1848, Heinrich Brockhaus served as a member of the Saxon second chamber. In this legislative capacity, he acted as the representative for the city of Leipzig.

## Family Background and the Brockhaus Firm
Heinrich was the son of Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus (1772–1823), a publisher born in Dortmund. Friedrich Arnold had an early career in mercantile trade, serving an apprenticeship in Düsseldorf from 1788 to 1793 and later operating emporiums for English goods in Dortmund, Arnheim, and Amsterdam. In 1805, he transitioned into publishing.

Friedrich Arnold is most noted for purchasing the copyright to the *Konversations-Lexikon* (originally started in 1796) and completing its first edition between 1810 and 1811. He began a second edition under his own editorship in 1812. In 1818, he moved his operations to Leipzig to establish a large printing-house. His literary undertakings included the *Zeitgenossen*, *Hermes*, and the *Literarisches Konversationsblatt* (which later became the *Blätter für literarische Unterhaltung*), as well as bibliographical and historical works such as Ebert's *Allgemeines bibliographisches Lexikon* and Raumer's *Geschichte der Hohenstaufen*.

Heinrich also had a brother, Hermann Brockhaus (1806–1877), who pursued an academic path rather than the family business. Hermann was a German Orientalist and professor of ancient Semitic at Leipzig starting in 1848. He was known for his expertise in various Eastern languages, including Persian, Arabic, Hebrew, Pali, Zend, and Sanskrit. His most significant scholarly contribution was the *editio princeps* of the *Katha-sarit-sagara* ("The Ocean of the Streams of Story"), a 12th-century collection of Sanskrit stories by Soma Deva.
