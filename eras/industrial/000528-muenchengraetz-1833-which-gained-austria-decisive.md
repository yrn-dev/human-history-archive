# Muenchengraetz (1833)

## Overview and the Holy Alliance
Muenchengraetz was the site of a diplomatic gathering in 1833 where the "Holy Alliance" was recemented. This alliance consisted of three autocratic northern powers. The agreement reached at Muenchengraetz served as a critical diplomatic instrument for Austria, as it later secured the decisive intervention of the tsar in 1849.

## Decline of the Alliance
Despite the stability provided by the recementing of the Holy Alliance in 1833, the partnership was eventually destroyed. The alliance was hopelessly shattered due to Austria's diplomatic attitude during the Crimean War. This breakdown led to a shift in European power dynamics, as Russia, feeling justly offended, strengthened its ties with Prussia. This shift occurred while Bismarck was developing plans that would eventually mature in 1866.

## Consequences of Austrian Isolation
The collapse of the alliance contributed to a period of conspicuous isolation for Austria. This isolation was evident during the congress of Paris, where the Sardinian plenipotentiary, Cavour, exposed the scandal of Austrian rule in Italy to the rest of Europe. 

The subsequent campaign of 1859, involving an alliance between Sardinia and France, further undermined Austrian influence and laid the foundations for a united Italy. While Prussia threatened to intervene during the provisions of the armistice of Villafranca, this was motivated by a fear of France becoming unduly aggrandized rather than a desire to support Austria.

## Results of the Diplomatic Shift
The long-term results of these diplomatic failures were two-fold for Austria:
* **External Losses:** Austria lost all of its Italian possessions, with the exception of Venice.
* **Internal Changes:** The failure of its external position necessitated the conciliation of public opinion through the granting of constitutional concessions.
