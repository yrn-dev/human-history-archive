# Queen Mary's Charters and the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885

During the mid-16th century, Queen Mary granted several charters of incorporation and corporate status to various English boroughs. These grants established local governance structures, market rights, and parliamentary representation, many of which remained in effect until the Municipal Corporations Act of 1835 or the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885.

## Banbury
In 1553, Queen Mary granted the first charter of incorporation to Banbury. This charter established a court of record, one justice of the peace, a Thursday market, and two annual fairs. It also instituted a common council composed of 12 chief burgesses, 12 aldermen, and a bailiff. While this charter was later confirmed by James I in 1608 and replaced by a new charter from George I in 1718, the parliamentary status of the borough remained consistent for centuries. From the date of Queen Mary's charter until the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885, Banbury was represented in parliament by one member.

## Buckingham
Buckingham was created a free borough corporate by Queen Mary in 1554. The charter defined the town's boundaries, extending in length from Padbury Mill bridge to Chackmore bridge and in width from Thornborowe bridge to Dudley bridge. The governance structure established by Mary included a steward, twelve principal burgesses, and a bailiff. Although a charter from Charles II was issued in 1684, it was quickly abandoned in favor of Queen Mary's original grant, which remained in force until 1835. Regarding parliamentary representation, Buckingham returned two members starting in 1529 and from 1545 onwards; however, the Representation of the People Act of 1867 deprived the borough of one member, and the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885 deprived it of the other.

## Aylesbury
Queen Mary granted a charter to Aylesbury in 1554 as a reward for fidelity during the rebellion of the duke of Northumberland. This charter constituted Aylesbury as a free borough corporate and established a common council consisting of 12 chief burgesses, 10 aldermen, and a bailiff. Additionally, the charter instituted fairs at the Invention of the Holy Cross and the Annunciation, as well as a Wednesday market. From the date of this charter until the Redistribution Act of 1885, the borough returned two members to parliament, though other privileges appear to have lapsed during the reign of Elizabeth.

## Barnstaple
Barnstaple, one of the most ancient royal boroughs, received a charter from Queen Mary in 1556. This charter added new privileges and specified that the common council should be composed of twenty-four chief burgesses, two aldermen, and a mayor. The borough had sent two members to parliament since 1295; this representation continued until the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885, at which point the representation was merged into that of the county.

## Other Impacts of the 1885 Act
The Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885 served as a major turning point for several other boroughs and counties:
* **Bury St Edmunds:** The borough's representation was reduced to one member.
* **Durham:** The boroughs of Hartlepool, Stockton, and Darlington, which had each returned one member since 1868, saw their representation affected by the Act.
* **Buckinghamshire:** The boroughs were deprived of representation, and the county returned three members for three divisions.
* **Macclesfield:** After being disfranchised for bribery in 1880, the borough was merged into the county division of Macclesfield in 1885.
