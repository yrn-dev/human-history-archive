# Condensed Milk and Related Agricultural Trade

The manufacture and trade of condensed milk are integrated into various regional agricultural economies and are subject to specific legal standards regarding purity and labeling, particularly within the United Kingdom and the United States.

## Regional Production and Trade

In England, several market towns maintain an agricultural trade that includes the manufacture of condensed milk. Buckingham, the county town of Buckinghamshire, is served by a branch of the Grand Junction Canal and produces condensed milk alongside artificial manure, maltings, and flour-mills. Similarly, Aylesbury, a market town in the Vale of Aylesbury, is connected by a branch to the Grand Junction canal and carries on the manufacture of condensed milk, printing works, and straw-plaiting, while being noted for rearing ducks.

In Ireland, the market town of Carrick-on-Suir in County Tipperary maintains a thriving export trade in agricultural produce and the manufacture of condensed milk. The town's navigable river channel, which is tidal to that point, has been improved through dredging and is lined with quays.

## Legal Regulations and Standards

The trade of dairy produce in the United Kingdom is governed by the Sale of Food and Drugs Act 1899, which took effect on January 1, 1900. Under Section 1, condensed separated or skimmed milk must be contained in receptacles with labels printed in large, legible type stating "machine-skimmed milk" or "skimmed milk." An article is considered adulterated or impoverished if any part of it has been abstracted or if it has been mixed with another substance to injuriously affect its quality, nature, or substance, though the addition of certain preservatives or coloring matters that are not injurious to health is permitted.

In 1900, a departmental committee was appointed to report on regulations for the board of agriculture under the Sale of Food and Drugs Act 1899. The committee's recommendations included:

*   **Purity Standards:** For condensed milk not labeled as "skimmed" or "machine-skimmed," a presumption that the milk is not genuine is raised if the milk-fat is less than 10% or if non-fatty milk-solids are less than 25%.
*   **Labeling:** It was suggested that labels for such condensed milk should state the dilution required to make the milk-fat proportion equal to that of uncondensed milk containing at least 3.25% milk-fat.
*   **Additives:** The committee expressed a desire to limit the addition of sugar in condensed whole milk and exclude it entirely from condensed machine-skimmed milk.
*   **Uniformity:** The committee recommended uniform procedures for collecting, forwarding, and retaining samples, as well as uniform methods of analysis for milk and cream.

Further regulations, cited as the Sale of Milk Regulations 1901, came into operation on September 1, 1901, extending to Great Britain. These regulations specify that for milk not sold as condensed, separated, or skimmed, a lack of 3% milk-fat or 8.5% milk-solids other than milk-fat raises a presumption that the milk is not genuine. For skimmed or separated milk that is not condensed, the threshold for genuine status is at least 9% milk-solids.
