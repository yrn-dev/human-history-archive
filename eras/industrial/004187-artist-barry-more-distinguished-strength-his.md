# BARRY (ARTIST)

The artist Barry was characterized more by the strength of his conceptions and his resolute, persistent determination to focus exclusively on great subjects than by his beauty in colouring or his skill in designing. His colouring was frequently described as wretched, and his drawing was rarely considered good. Personally, he was viewed as unequal and impulsive, oscillating between being sociable and urbane and being morose. While he was capable of delivering a splendid eulogy for Reynolds, he remained jealous of his contemporaries.

## Education and Travels
In the latter part of 1765, Barry was able to travel abroad thanks to the liberality of his friends, including Burke. His journey began in Paris and continued to Rome, where he stayed for more than three years. He also visited Bologna and Florence before returning home via Venice. During this period, Barry wrote letters to the Burkes regarding Leonardo da Vinci, Titian, Michelangelo, and Raphael, which demonstrated a remarkable insight. While abroad, he painted two works—a *Philoctetes* and an *Adam and Eve*—though neither was considered to be of any merit.

## Major Works and Style
Following his return to England in 1771, Barry produced a painting of *Venus*. This work was compared to the *Venus de Medici*, the *Venus* of Titian, and the *Galatea* of Raphael, although such comparisons were viewed as having little justice. In 1773, he exhibited *Jupiter and Juno on Mount Ida*. 

Barry's *Death of General Wolfe* was seen as a decline from his great style of art, partly because the French and British soldiers were depicted in very primitive costumes. His admirers attributed his reluctance to paint portraits to his fondness for Greek costume.

Other notable works include:
* A pair of classical subjects: *Narcissus looking at himself in the water* (suggested by Burke) and *Mercury inventing the lyre*.
* A historical painting of *Chiron and Achilles*.
* A painting of the story of *Stratonice*, for which the duke of Richmond paid him one hundred guineas.

## Professional Relationships and Failures
Barry had an early patron in Burke, but a misunderstanding arose between them when Barry failed to complete a portrait of Burke. This rift was said to have widened due to Barry's jealousy of the fortune and fame of Sir Joshua Reynolds—whom Barry viewed as a rival in a "humbler walk of the art"—and Burke's increasing intimacy with Reynolds.

In 1773, a proposal was made to decorate the interior of St Paul's with sacred and historical subjects. However, the plan failed because it did not receive the concurrence of the archbishop of Canterbury and the bishop of London. Barry was greatly mortified by this failure, as he had already decided on the subject he intended to paint: the rejection of Christ by the Jews during Pilate's proposal for his release.

In 1773, Barry also published a work titled *An Inquiry into the real and imaginary Obstructio*.
