# Richard Grenville, 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos

Richard Grenville (1797–1861) was the 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos. He was the only child of the 1st duke and was educated at Eton and Oriel College, Oxford. During his life, he was known by the titles Earl Temple and subsequently the Marquess of Chandos.

## Political Career
Grenville served as a member of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1818 to 1839. During his tenure in parliament, he was responsible for the "Chandos clause" within the Reform Bill of 1832. He later held the position of lord privy seal from September 1841 to January 1842. Due in part to his opposition to the repeal of the corn laws, he earned the moniker "the Farmers' Friend."

## Financial Decline and Later Life
Upon succeeding to the dukedom in 1839, Grenville found that the estates were heavily encumbered. His financial situation reached a climax due to his own luxurious and generous tastes. In 1847, his creditors seized his residences, forcing the duke to leave England. Following the sale of many of his landed estates and personal property, he eventually returned to England and dedicated himself to the study of literature.

## Literary Works and Death
Grenville authored several publications focusing on the English court and cabinets. His chief works include:
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of George III* (published in London, 1853–1855)
* *Memoirs of the Court of England, 1811–1820* (London, 1856)
* *Memoirs of the Court of George IV* (London, 1859)
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of William IV. and Victoria* (London, 1861)

He died in London on July 29, 1861.

## Family
In 1819, Richard Grenville married Mary, the daughter of John, 1st Marquess of Breadalbane. Mary obtained a divorce from the duke in 1850 and died in 1862. The couple had one son, Richard Grenville (1823–1889), who became the 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos.
