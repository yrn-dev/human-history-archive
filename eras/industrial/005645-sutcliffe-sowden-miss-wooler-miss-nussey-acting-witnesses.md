# The Marriage of Charlotte Brontë and Arthur Bell Nicholls

## The Marriage Ceremony
On June 29, 1854, Charlotte Brontë married Arthur Bell Nicholls (1817–1906) at Haworth church. The wedding ceremony was performed by the Reverend Sutcliffe Sowden. Acting as witnesses for the occasion were Miss Wooler and Miss Nussey. Following the ceremony, the couple spent their honeymoon in Ireland before returning to Haworth.

## The Groom and Domestic Arrangements
Arthur Bell Nicholls had been a persistent suitor for Charlotte's hand for a long period, though he had previously been discouraged by Mr. Brontë. Mr. Nicholls served as the curate to Mr. Brontë; upon the marriage of the couple, he pledged himself to continue in this position as curate to his father-in-law. The couple established their home with Mr. Brontë in Haworth.

## The Witnesses: Miss Wooler and Miss Nussey
The witnesses at the wedding, Miss Wooler and Miss Nussey, shared a long history with Charlotte Brontë dating back to her youth. In 1831, at the age of sixteen, Charlotte became a pupil at the school of Miss Margaret Wooler (1792–1885), located at Roe Head, Dewsbury. 

During her time at the school, Charlotte formed devoted friendships with two of her ten fellow-pupils: Ellen Nussey (1817–1897) and Mary Taylor (1817–1893). Charlotte maintained a correspondence with Miss Nussey and Miss Taylor for the rest of her life. The letters written to Ellen Nussey, in particular, provide a significant portion of the revealed story of Charlotte's life.

The connection to the Nussey family extended beyond friendship; in 1838, Charlotte received her first offer of marriage from Henry Nussey, who was the brother of Ellen Nussey. Charlotte refused this offer.

## Context of the Period
The marriage occurred during a productive period of Charlotte Brontë's literary career. Following the deaths of her siblings Branwell and Emily, she worked on the novel *Shirley*, which was published in October 1849. In the following years, she visited London as a guest of her publisher, Mr. George Smith, where she was introduced to Thackeray and sat for a portrait by George Richmond.

In 1851, she received a third offer of marriage from Mr. James Taylor, an employee of her publishers, though she did not accept. During 1852, she worked on the novel *Villette*, which was published in January 1853. In the years leading up to her marriage, she maintained a friendship with Mrs. Gaskell, visiting her in Manchester in May 1854 to plan the details of her upcoming marriage to Mr. Nicholls.
