# Catalogue of 2881 Stars and the Reform of the Nautical Almanac

The Catalogue of 2881 stars, published in the *Memoirs of the Royal Astronomical Society* (vol. ii), was prepared by the English astronomer Francis Baily. This work was recognized for its significance in the field of astronomy, and Baily was awarded the gold medal of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1827 for its preparation.

## Francis Baily
Francis Baily (1774–1844) was born on April 28, 1774, in Newbury, Berkshire. His early life included a tour of the unsettled regions of North America between 1796 and 1797. In 1799, he entered the London Stock Exchange. Baily initially established a high reputation as a writer on life-contingencies through the publication of several works: *Tables for the Purchasing and Renewing of Leases* (1802), *The Doctrine of Interest and Annuities* (1808), and *The Doctrine of Life-Annuities and Assurances* (1810).

After amassing a fortune through integrity and diligence, Baily retired from business in 1825 to dedicate himself entirely to astronomy. He had previously played a leading role in the foundation of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1820.

## Astronomical Contributions and Reforms
Beyond the Catalogue of 2881 stars, Baily was instrumental in the reform of the *Nautical Almanac* in 1829, a process initiated by his own protests. In 1837, he recommended the reduction of the catalogues of Nicolas de Lacaille and Joseph de Lalande, which contained approximately 57,000 stars; Baily executed a great part of this reduction. He also superintended the compilation of the British Association's Catalogue of 8377 stars, which was published in 1845.

Baily's scholarly work extended to the revision of several historical catalogues, including those of Hevelius, Edmund Halley, Tycho Brahe, Ulugh Beg, Ptolemy, and Tobias Mayer, as recorded in the *Memoirs of the Royal Astronomical Society* (vols. iv and xiii). Additionally, his 1835 work, *Account of the Rev. John Flamsteed*, is considered of fundamental importance to scientific history and included a republication of the British Catalogue.

## Other Scientific Work
Baily contributed to various other areas of physical science:
* **Solar Eclipses:** He described the phenomenon of "Baily's Beads" during an annular eclipse of the sun on May 15, 1836, at Inch Bonney in Roxburghshire. This description attracted significant attention to the totality of the eclipse on July 8, 1842, which Baily observed at Pavia, and started the modern series of eclipse-expeditions.
* **Earth Measurements:** Baily used Henry Cavendish's method between 1838 and 1842 to determine the mean density of the earth, arriving at the authoritative value of 5.66. He also completed and discussed pendulum-experiments by H. Foster, from which he deduced an earth ellipticity of 1/289.
* **Standards of Length:** In 1843, Baily was entrusted with the reconstruction of the standards of length. He also corrected the length of the seconds-pendulum by introducing a reduction element that had been previously neglected.

Francis Baily died in London on August 30, 1844.
