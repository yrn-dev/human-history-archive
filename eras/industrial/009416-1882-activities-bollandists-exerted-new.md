# The Bollandists

The Bollandists are the creators of a vast collection of the Lives of the Saints. This work was intended to encompass the entire year, though it only extended to the nine months from January to October, filling fifty-three large volumes. For example, the three volumes dedicated to the month of April contain the lives of 1,472 saints.

## Institutional History and Relocations
Following the dispersion of the Jesuits, the Bollandists received authorization to continue their work. They remained in Antwerp until 1778, at which point they were transferred to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. During their time there, they published volume iv. of October in 1780 and volume v. of October in 1886. However, the monastery of Coudenberg was suppressed in 1886, and the work of the Bollandists ceased in 1788.

The remains of the Bollandist library were subsequently acquired by the Premonstratensians of Tongerloo. This group attempted to continue the project, publishing volume vi. of October in 1794 within their abbey.

The work was resumed in 1837 after the Society of Jesus was re-established in Belgium. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and at the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were then installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels. In 1845, the first volume of a new series appeared (vol. vii. of October), which eventually reached vol. xiii. of October in 1883. Collaborators in this series included the Jesuit fathers Benjamin Bossue, Joseph van Hecke, Joseph van der Moere, Remi and Victor de Buck, Edu. Carpentier, Ant. Tinnebroeck, and Henr. Matagne. Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan edited the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which was included at the beginning of vol. xi. of October in 1864.

## Methodological Shift and the Analecta Bollandiana
In 1882, the Bollandists shifted the direction of their activities to better align their work with the progress of historical methods. As part of this effort, Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt established a quarterly review titled *Analecta Bollandiana*. By 1906, this review had reached its 25th volume and was edited by P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt.

The *Analecta Bollandiana* served several purposes:
* It provided dissertations, inedited texts, and studies intended to remould and continue the *Acta Sanctorum*.
* Since 1892, it included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which offered criticisms of recent works regarding hagiographic questions.
* It published numerous library catalogues, including those from Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres.

## Hagiographic Research and Publications
Beyond the quarterly review, the Bollandists analyzed hagiographic manuscripts (MSS.) in principal libraries. This resulted in several separate volumes:
* Latin MSS. in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (two volumes, 1886–1889).
* Latin and Greek MSS. in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (five volumes, 1889–1896).
* Greek MSS. in the Vatican (1899).
* Latin MSS. in the libraries of Rome (beginning in 1905).

The Bollandists also created inventories of previously published hagiographic texts, resulting in the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*. While these works delayed the publication of the principal collection, they provided a strictly scientific stamp and a more solid basis for the project.

Subsequent publications included volume i. for November in 1887 and volume ii. in 1894. The latter was preceded by the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* by the abbe Louis Duchesne and J.B. de Rossi. In 1902, the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* was published, which included the *Synaxarium ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae*.
