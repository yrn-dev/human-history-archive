# John Rennie the Elder

John Rennie the elder was a prominent engineer responsible for the design of several significant infrastructure projects in the 19th century, including bridges, piers, and consulting work on maritime safety structures.

## London Bridge
Rennie designed the present London Bridge, a masonry arch structure that was begun in 1824 and completed in 1831. The project was executed with his son, Sir John Rennie, serving as the acting engineer. The bridge is characterized by a slight curvature of the roadway, bold architectural details, and semi-elliptical arches with varying spans. 

The bridge has a total length of 1005 ft and a width of 56 ft from outside to outside. Its height above low water is 60 ft. The structure consists of several arches:
* A centre arch with a span of 152 ft, rising 29 ft 6 in above the Trinity high-water mark.
* Two arches on either side of the centre with spans of 140 ft.
* Abutment arches with spans of 130 ft.

The construction utilized granite for the exterior stones and the voussoirs of the centre arch. The interior of the two centre piers, which are 24 ft thick, consists of half Bramley Fall and half stone from Painshaw, Derbyshire. The granite voussoirs of the centre arch measure 4 ft 9 in deep at the crown and increase to at least 9 ft at the springing. Foundations for the bridge were laid at a general depth of approximately 29 ft 6 in below low water. While the contractor's tender for the bridge alone was £425,081, the total cost of the project was £1,458,311.

The waterway of this bridge is 690 ft, a significant increase from the 337 ft waterway of the Old London Bridge. This change resulted in a deepening of the river-bed and a lowering of the low-water level by 5 ft.

## Other Bridge and Pier Works
Rennie's engineering work extended to various other locations and materials:
* **Southwark Bridge:** Designed by Rennie and erected between 1814 and 1819 over the Thames, this bridge utilized cast iron ribs and featured a rise of 24 ft with a centre span of 240 ft.
* **Kelso Bridge:** In 1803, Rennie constructed a bridge of five arches to cross the Tweed at Kelso.
* **Margate Pier:** Rennie constructed a pier at Margate in 1815, which is primarily used by colliers and fishermen.

## Consulting and Other Projects
Rennie was associated as a consulting engineer with Robert Stevenson for the construction of the Bell (or Inchcape Rock) lighthouse. The project aimed to protect shipping near a sandstone reef in the North Sea, 11 m S.E. of Arbroath, following a 1799 gale that wrecked seventy sail. Although Rennie served as a consulting engineer, the design and details of the 100 ft high tower were entirely the work of Stevenson.
