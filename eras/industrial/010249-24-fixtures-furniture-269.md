## Legal Definitions and Classifications
Under the Bills of Sale Acts of 1878 and 1882, "personal chattels" are defined as furniture, goods, and other articles that are capable of complete transfer by delivery. Within this legal framework, fixtures are specifically identified as personal chattels when they are charged or assigned separately. 

The Bills of Sale Act 1878 governs both absolute bills of sale—where chattels are sold absolutely to a purchaser—and bills of sale used as security for the payment of money. A bill of sale is defined broadly to include assignments, transfers, inventories of goods with attached receipts, and other assurances of personal chattels.

## Historical Collections
Collections of historic furniture are maintained in several prominent museums. In France, the Louvre, Cluny, and the Garde Meuble have historically been the most famous repositories. The Louvre's Bureau du Roi is noted for possessing the most magnificent individual example of French furniture in existence.

In London, these are rivaled by the Wallace collection at Hertford House and the Victoria and Albert Museum at South Kensington. The Wallace collection, combined with the Jones bequest, is considered the finest gathering of French furniture from the great periods. In the United States, there are admirable collections of "colonial furniture" from the Georgian and Queen Anne periods, which was produced in both England and the United States.
