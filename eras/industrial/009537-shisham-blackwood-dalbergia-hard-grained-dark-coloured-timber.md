# Shisham or Blackwood (Dalbergia)

Shisham, also known as blackwood, is a hard-grained, dark-coloured timber belonging to the genus *Dalbergia*. It is recognized as one of the few trees in India that produce economically useful timber, a rarity caused by the general lack of durability in woods found in the region's extremely hot and moist climate.

## Botanical Varieties and Distribution
In the Bombay Presidency, blackwood is found in two primary varieties: *Dalbergia sisu* and *Dalbergia latifolia*. Additionally, *Dalbergia ujainensis* is listed among the principal timber trees of the region's forests. The forests of the Bombay Presidency are divided into two main classes: those of the alluvial plains and those of the hills. The hill forests are scattered across a wide area from 14 to 23 degrees North latitude, primarily located within the Western Ghats or the Sahyadri hills. In the plains, the sissoo (*Dalbergia*) is noted as one of the useful woods available.

## Bombay Furniture
The timber is the primary material used in the manufacture of "Bombay blackwood furniture." This class of articles is produced in the city of Bombay as well as in the towns of Ahmedabad and Surat in India. When treated properly, Shisham assumes a beautiful natural polish.

The furniture produced from this wood varies significantly in quality and style:
*   **Form and Design:** Some pieces are described as inelegant and clumsy in form. Experts, including Sir George Birdwood, suggest these defects stem from the use of original Dutch models. Conversely, smaller items—such as ornamental stands, small tables, and flower stands—are highly prized by collectors for their exceedingly graceful contours.
*   **Carving:** At its highest quality, the carving is lace-like. It is noted for its inherent beauty and the ingenuity of the craftsmen, who adapt detailed designs to the specific purpose of the object.
*   **Production Traditions:** The most artistic furniture is created by a special class of workmen who follow inherited traditions. In many cases, a craftsman knows only a single design, which was transmitted to him by his father, and to his grandfather before him.
*   **Modernization:** Under European auspices, there have been efforts to modernize the industry by integrating native workmanship into furniture of Western design, though conventional patterns generally remain the standard.

## Bombay Boxes and Inlay Work
A specific application of this timber is found in "Bombay boxes," which feature wood inlaid in geometrical patterns. The distinctive and peculiar effect of these boxes is achieved by combining minute pieces of various inlaying materials, including:
*   Sandal wood
*   Sapan wood
*   Ebony
*   Ivory
*   Stags' horns
*   Wire
