# Alexander I and the Events of 1803

The year 1803 was marked by significant geopolitical shifts and diplomatic interventions, particularly involving the Russian Emperor Alexander I and the expansionist activities of Napoleon Bonaparte.

## Diplomatic Influence in Baden
During the Napoleonic wars, the territory of Baden, which had been united under a single ruler in 1771, struggled with territorial fragmentation. Under the rule of Charles Frederick—who began his reign in 1738 and is regarded as the most notable of Baden's rulers—the state sought territorial unity. After fighting for Austria in 1792, Baden suffered devastation and was forced in 1796 to cede territories on the left bank of the Rhine to France.

The fortunes of Charles Frederick shifted in 1803. Largely due to the good offices of Emperor Alexander I of Russia, the ruler of Baden received several additions to his land, including the bishopric of Constance and part of the Rhenish Palatinate, along with other smaller districts. Additionally, he was granted the dignity of a prince elector. This period of aggrandizement continued as Charles Frederick later changed sides in 1805 to fight for Napoleon, eventually quadrupling the area of Baden before his death in June 1811.

## Russian Naval and Academic Appointments
Emperor Alexander I also utilized 1803 to advance Russian scientific and educational interests. He appointed Adam Ivan Krusenstern, a Russian navigator and admiral, to lead a voyage to the east coast of Asia. The objective of this project was to establish direct communication between Russia and China via the Cape of Good Hope and Cape Horn. In August 1803, the expedition departed Kronstadt using two English ships. The voyage proceeded through the Sandwich Islands and Cape Horn to Kamchatka and Japan, eventually returning to Kronstadt in August 1806. This marked the first Russian expedition to circumnavigate the world.

In the realm of academia, the emperor acted in 1803 to appoint Friedrich Maximilian von Klinger as the curator of the university of Dorpat. Klinger, a German dramatist and novelist who had previously served as an officer in the Russian army and director of the corps of cadets, held this curatorship until 1817.
