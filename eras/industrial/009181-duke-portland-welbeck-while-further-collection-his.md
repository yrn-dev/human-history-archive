# Duke of Portland and Welbeck

## Welbeck Abbey and the Dukeries
Welbeck Abbey serves as the seat of the dukes of Portland. The estate was acquired by the dukes of Portland from the Cavendish family, who were the dukes of Newcastle. The mansion is primarily classic in style and dates from the early 17th century, though it has undergone many subsequent additions. A notable feature of the grounds is a curious series of chambers and subterranean corridors constructed by the fifth duke of Portland, who died in 1879.

Welbeck Abbey is located within a district in the northwest of Nottinghamshire, England, known as the Dukeries. This district is situated within the ancient Sherwood Forest and derives its name from the presence of several adjacent noblemen's demesnes. Other estates in this district include Clumber House (the seat of the dukes of Newcastle), Thoresby House (the seat of the earls Manvers), and Worksop Manor (formerly belonging to the dukes of Norfolk).

## Political and Public Life
The dukes of Portland have held significant roles in English governance and politics. One duke of Portland served as the first minister of the crown and was a leader of the Whig party. In a different political era, the duke of Portland, along with Lord Fitzwilliam, led a movement in the House of Lords. This occurred as a portion of the opposition moved toward the government under the pretext of the spread of "French principles" or "Jacobinism," a shift that contributed to the decline of the Whigs and created difficulties for the career of Grey.

## Family and Influence
The influence of the duke of Portland extended to the professional advancement of family members. For example, the interest of the duke of Portland secured the clerkship of the council's reversion and the secretaryship of the island of Jamaica for his grandson, Charles Cavendish Fulke Greville. The secretaryship of Jamaica was a sinecure office where duties were handled by a deputy.

Additionally, the duke of Portland was involved in the sporting world. Charles Greville, who was the son of Lady Charlotte Bentinck (daughter of the duke of Portland), trained as an active member of the turf with the duke of Portland.

## Other Affiliations and Records
The duke of Portland has been associated with the Kennel Club, the governing body of the canine world. He served as one of the vice-presidents of the club, alongside H. Richards, J. H. Salter, Lord Algernon Gordon Lennox, and the president, the duke of Connaught and Strathearn.

Regarding historical records, the *Hist. MSS. Comm.* contains manuscripts of the Duke of Portland at Welbeck.
