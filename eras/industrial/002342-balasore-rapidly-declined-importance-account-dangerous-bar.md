# Balasore

Balasore is a town and district located in the Orissa division of Bengal in British India. The town serves as the administrative headquarters of the district and is situated on the right bank of the river Burabalang. Geographically, the town is approximately 16 miles from the sea-coast via the river and about 7 miles away as the crow flies.

## Early History and British Settlement
The foundation of British influence in India was based on the English settlement of Balasore, established in 1642, and a nearby settlement at Pippli, which had been formed seven years prior. Servants of the East India Company established themselves in a strong, fortified position and engaged in a brisk investment of country goods, with a primary focus on muslins and cottons. Despite facing oppressions from Mahommedan governors, the Company's servants flourished and used arms to assert their claims to respect when necessary. This period of tension reached a crisis in 1688, resulting in the bombardment of the town by Captain William Heath, the commander of the company's ships.

## Decline and the River Bar
During the 18th century, Balasore experienced a rapid decline in importance. This was caused by the formation of a dangerous bar across the mouth of the river. The silting-up of the river mouth continues to affect the area; currently, the bar has between 12 and 15 feet of water during spring-tides, but this drops to no more than 2 or 3 feet during low water in the dry season. Consequently, large ships are required to anchor in the open roadstead outside the bar. Despite these conditions, the town maintains a large maritime trade.

## Geography and Climate
The Balasore district is watered by six distinct river systems: the Jamka, the Burabalang, the Subanrekha, the Dhamra, and the Kansbans. The region's climate varies by season:
* **Hot Season (March to June):** Tempered by cool sea-breezes.
* **Oppressive Season (June to September):** The weather is close and oppressive.
* **Cold Season (October to February):** Characterized by north-easterly winds and cool evenings and mornings.

The country is subject to cyclonic storm-waves and destructive floods originating from hill-rivers.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The primary crop grown in the district is rice, which is largely exported by sea to Mauritius, the Maldives, and Ceylon. Imports to the region include metals, mineral oils, salt, betel-nuts, and piece goods and cotton twist. The district's coasting trade is conducted through the seaports of Dhamra, Chandbali, and Balasore.

Infrastructure in the district includes the East Coast railway, which runs from Madras to Calcutta, and the navigable Orissa coast canal, which traverses the entire length of the district. The town of Balasore itself is served by a station on the East Coast railway.

## Population
As of 1901, the population of the town of Balasore was 20,880. The population of the wider Balasore district in 1901 was 1,071,197, representing an increase of 9% over the previous decade.
