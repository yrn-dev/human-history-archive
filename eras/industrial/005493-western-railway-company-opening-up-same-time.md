# Great Western Railway Company

The Great Western Railway Company is a significant railway operator with extensive infrastructure across several regions, managing main lines, branch lines, and associated maritime facilities. Its network facilitates both passenger travel and the transport of industrial goods, particularly in Wales and England.

## Operations in Wales and the West
In Glamorganshire, Wales, the Great Western Railway Company maintains a presence in the seaport of Briton-Ferry, located on the eastern bank of the Neath river estuary in Swansea Bay. The town is 174 miles from London by rail. In 1861, the company constructed a 13-acre dock at Briton-Ferry. This development, alongside the opening of the South Wales mineral railway, provided the primary impetus for industrial growth in the district, enabling the export of coal from the mining districts of Maesteg and Glyncorrwg. The area has since become a center for the manufacture of tinplates and steel, as well as home to a foundry and iron-works.

In other parts of Wales, the Great Western main line has junctions with the Barry railway at St Fagans and Peterstone.

In Cornwall, the Great Western railway provides the primary means of communication, with its main line passing through the county and terminating at Penzance. The company operates several branch lines serving Helston in the south, and Newquay, St Ives, and Bodmin and Wadebridge. To reach locations beyond the reach of the railway, such as the area between Helston and the Lizard and the neighborhood of Penzance, the company provides a motor-car service.

## English Network and Joint Ventures
The Great Western company operates a main line that crosses the extreme south of the county (referenced in the archive's communications section) via Taplow and Slough. In this region, the company previously provided the sole rail service to Prince's Risborough and High Wycombe via a branch from Maidenhead to Oxford. However, in 1906, a new line was opened which is jointly owned by the Great Western and Great Central companies; this line passes through High Wycombe, Beaconsfield, and Prince's Risborough, connecting northward to the Great Central system.

Additionally, the Great Western system includes the old Monks Ferry station, which was superseded in 1878 by the opening of the Woodside passenger station. On the Cheshire side, the Great Western maintains connections with the North-Western, Wirral, and various local lines, extending to the low-level at Central Station in Liverpool.
