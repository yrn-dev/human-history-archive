# Toledo, Walhonding Valley & Ohio (Pennsylvania system), and the Ohio

## Railroad Systems in Ohio
The Toledo, Walhonding Valley & Ohio railway is part of the Pennsylvania system. This rail line serves the city of Bucyrus, the county-seat of Crawford county, Ohio. Bucyrus is also served by the Pennsylvania and the Ohio Central railways, as well as interurban electric lines. The Ohio Central railway maintains shops in Bucyrus, where the city serves as a division terminal.

Other rail networks operate across various Ohio municipalities. In Athens, the county-seat of Athens county, the village is served by the Hocking Valley, the Toledo & Ohio Central (Ohio Central Lines), and the Baltimore & Ohio Southwestern railways. In Coshocton, the county-seat of Coshocton county, rail service is provided by the Pennsylvania, the Wheeling & Lake Erie, and the Pittsburg, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis railways, the latter of which is controlled by the Pennsylvania.

## The Ohio River and Associated Transport
The Ohio river serves as a critical geographic and transport artery for several cities. Bellaire, located in Belmont county and situated five miles south of Wheeling, West Virginia, is served by the Pennsylvania, the Baltimore & Ohio, and the Ohio River & Western railways. Bellaire functions as the shipping center for the Belmont county coalfield.

Marietta, the county-seat of Washington county, is located on the Ohio river at the mouth of the Muskingum. It is served by the Marietta, Columbus & Cleveland railways, the Pennsylvania (Marietta Division), and the Baltimore & Ohio (which includes the Ohio River, Marietta & Zanesville, and Marietta & Parkersburg divisions). Additionally, Marietta is connected to several river ports via steamboat lines and is linked to Williamstown, West Virginia, by a bridge across the Ohio river.

In Pennsylvania, the borough of McKees Rocks in Allegheny county is also situated on the Ohio river, approximately three miles northwest of Pittsburg. It is served by the Pittsburg & Lake Erie and the Pittsburg, Chartiers & Youghiogheny railways.

## Regional Geography and Industry
The areas surrounding these transport hubs are characterized by diverse industrial and agricultural activities. Bucyrus is situated at an elevation of about 1000 feet above sea-level in a region suited for stock-raising and agriculture; its manufactures include structural steel, machinery, furniture, copper products, clay-working machines, wagons, carriages, ploughs, interior woodwork, and heating and ventilating apparatus.

Coshocton, located at the confluence of the Walhonding, Tuscarawas, and Muskingum rivers, serves as a commercial center for an extensive agricultural district. Its manufactures include glass, paper, flour, china-ware, cast-iron pipes, and advertising specialities.

In the vicinity of Athens, there are many coal mines, and the local manufactures include shirts, furniture, bricks, and veneered doors. Bellaire's industry is heavily tied to the Belmont county coalfield, which produced 19.3% of the state's total coal output in 1907. Bellaire also produces glass, iron and steel, stoves, agricultural implements, and enamelled and galvanized ware, utilizing local fireclay, limestone, and iron.

Marietta is located in a hilly region where iron-ore, coal, and petroleum abound. The city manufactures boilers, glass, bricks, boats, wagons, carriages, flour, leather, and chairs.

## Historical and Social Context
The regions of Ohio and Pennsylvania have been sites of significant historical activity. The "Underground Railroad" was a system that reached from Virginia and Kentucky across Ohio, and from Maryland across Pennsylvania and New York, extending to Canada and New England. In southern Ohio, anti-slavery men helped as many as 1000 slaves reach Canada as early as 1817. Levi Coffin, who settled in Wayne County, Ohio, in 1826, established a meeting point for three "lines" from Kentucky at his home in New Garden (now Fountain City) before moving to Cincinnati in 1847.

The area of Coshocton was originally the site of the chief village of the Turtle tribe of the Delawares, which was destroyed by whites in 1781. In Marietta, there are 18th-century buildings and earthworks created by "mound-builders." Similarly, an ancient Indian mound is located in McKees Rocks, Pennsylvania.
