# Daniel Garrison Brinton

Daniel Garrison Brinton (1837–1899) was an American ethnologist and archaeologist. Born on May 13, 1837, in Thornbury, Pennsylvania, he pursued an extensive education across North America and Europe before establishing himself as a scholar of linguistics and anthropology.

## Education and Early Career
Brinton graduated from Yale in 1858. Following his graduation, he spent two years studying at the Jefferson Medical College. He then traveled in Europe for one year, where he continued his academic studies in Heidelberg and Paris.

During the American Civil War, from 1862 to 1865, Brinton served as a surgeon in the Union army. Between 1864 and 1865, he held the position of surgeon in charge of the U.S. Army general hospital located at Quincy, Illinois. After the conclusion of the war, he spent several years practicing medicine in Westchester, Pennsylvania.

## Professional Appointments
Brinton's career transitioned from medicine to academia and journalism. From 1874 to 1887, he served as the editor of a weekly periodical in Philadelphia titled the *Medical and Surgical Reporter*. In 1884, he was appointed professor of archaeology and ethnology at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia. 

From 1886 until his death on July 31, 1899, in Philadelphia, he served as a professor of archaeology and American linguistics at the university of Pennsylvania. Throughout his career, he was a member of various learned societies in Europe and the United States. He also held the presidency of several organizations, including the American Association for the Advancement of Science, the American Folk-Lore Society, and the Numismatic and Antiquarian Society of Philadelphia.

## Published Works
Between 1859, the year he published his first book, and 1899, Brinton authored a score of books, as well as numerous brochures, magazine articles, pamphlets, and addresses. 

His primary works include:
* *The Myths of the New World* (1868), which represented the first attempt to correlate and analyze American Indian mythology using true scientific principles.
* *The Religious Sentiment: Its Sources and Aim: A Contribution to the Science and Philosophy of Religion* (1876).
* *American Hero Myths* (1882).
* *Essays of an Americanist* (1890).
* *Races and Peoples* (1890).
* *The American Race* (1891).
* *The Pursuit of Happiness* (1893).
* *Religions of Primitive People* (1897).

Additionally, Brinton edited and published the *Library of American Aboriginal Literature*, an eight-volume set published between 1882 and 1890. This work is noted as a valuable contribution to the science of anthropology in America; Brinton edited six of the eight volumes.
