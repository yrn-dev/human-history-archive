# John M^cGilchrist

John M^cGilchrist is the author of a biographical work titled *The Life of John Bright, M.P.*, which was published in 1868. This work appeared as part of a series titled *Cassell's Representative Biographies*.

## Subject of Biography
M^cGilchrist's work focuses on the life of John Bright, M.P., a public figure recognized for his mastery of English oratory. Bright was described by the 3rd marquess of Salisbury as the greatest master of English oratory of his generation, and perhaps several generations. Salisbury noted that Bright maintained a vigorous, powerful, and robust style of speaking that allowed him to express noble and burning thoughts during a period when eloquence had been largely depressed or exterminated.

In his later years, Bright received significant social and literary recognition. In 1882, he was elected lord rector of the university of Glasgow, though he expressed to Dr. Dale that he was weary of public speaking and felt his mind was "almost a blank." He was further honored with an honorary degree from the university of Oxford in 1886, and a statue was erected in his honor at Birmingham in 1888.

## Publication Context
The biography by M^cGilchrist was published through Cassell's. John Cassell (1817–1865) was a British publisher born in Manchester who was self-educated in French and English literature. Originally apprenticed to a joiner and an active worker for the cause of temperance, Cassell established himself as a tea and coffee merchant in 1847 before starting a publishing business. 

Cassell's primary goal in publishing was to provide quality literature to the working classes. His firm, which became Messrs. Cassell, Petter, Galpin & Co. in 1859 and later Cassell & Company, Limited in 1883, produced several notable publications, including *Cassell's Magazine* (starting in 1852), the *Popular Educator* (1852–1855), the *Technical Educator* (1870–1872), and the *Magazine of Art* (1878–1903). A defining feature of the books produced by Cassell was their use of illustration; for example, during the Crimean War, Cassell used cuts from *L'Illustration* in Paris for his *Family Paper*.
