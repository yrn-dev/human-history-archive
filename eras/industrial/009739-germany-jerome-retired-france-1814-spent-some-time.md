# Jerome Bonaparte

## Early Life and Naval Career
Jerome Bonaparte was born on November 15, 1784, in Ajaccio. During the early years of the French Revolution, he shared the fortunes of his family and received an education at Juilly. In 1800, he was called to join his brother, who was then the First Consul of France. While serving in the Consular Guard, Jerome was wounded during a duel with the younger brother of General Davout.

Following this, Jerome was transferred to the navy, where he cruised in the West Indies. During this period, a British cruiser blockaded him, prompting Jerome to leave his ship and travel through the United States. While in Baltimore, he married Miss Elizabeth Patterson, despite being a minor at the time. This marriage and his disregard for the laws of France and discipline greatly annoyed Napoleon. When Jerome brought his wife to Europe in 1805, the emperor ordered her exclusion from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but these efforts were in vain.

## Military Commands and Marriage
In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. Meanwhile, his wife resided in Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son. By November, Jerome was sailing in a squadron under Admiral Willaumez intended to ravage the West Indies, though the squadron was eventually scattered by a storm. After damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic, Jerome returned to France safely in August 1806.

Napoleon subsequently named Jerome a prince of France and appointed him to command a division of South Germans during the 1806 campaign. Following the battle of Jena, Jerome received the surrender of several Prussian towns. During this time, an imperial decree annulled Jerome's marriage to Elizabeth Patterson, and the emperor united him with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg.

## Kingdom of Westphalia
Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed on July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia. His tenure was marked by a fondness for luxury, numerous *amours*, and significant debt, which led to frequent rebukes from the emperor.

Despite these personal tendencies, the kingdom saw some benefits from its connection to France. The *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, and education received an impulse in its higher departments. Additionally, repressive laws affecting the Jews were removed.
