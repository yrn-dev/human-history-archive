# The 1817 British Mission to Kumasi

## The Mission to Kumasi
In 1817, the British government dispatched a mission to Kumasi in response to repeated invasions of Fantiland by the Ashanti. The mission was composed of T.E. Bowdich, W. Hutchinson, and Frederick James, who served as the commandant of Accra fort.

## Diplomatic Conflict and the Rent Dispute
The mission began with one or two interviews that were described as harmonious. However, the diplomatic atmosphere shifted when the king advanced a claim regarding the payment of quit rents for Cape Coast castle and Anamabo fort. The majority of these rents had been paid by the British to the Fanti, who had induced them to do so, leaving only a nominal sum to be transmitted to Kumasi.

The head of the mission, Mr. James, failed to volunteer a satisfactory explanation regarding these payments. This lack of explanation caused the king to enter an uncontrollable rage, during which he characterized the British emissaries as liars and cheats.

## Intervention by Bowdich and Hutchinson
Following the king's outburst, T.E. Bowdich and W. Hutchinson concluded that the safety of the mission and British interests were endangered. Consequently, they took the negotiations into their own hands. This shift in leadership led to the recall of Mr. James.

The mission eventually concluded with a treaty. Under the terms of this agreement, the demands of the king were satisfied, and the British secured recognition of their right to control the natives located in the coast towns.

## Historical Documentation
The events of this mission were documented in early works on the region, most notably in the 1819 publication *A Mission from Cape Coast Castle to Ashantee* written by T.E. Bowdich. Other general histories of the Gold Coast of West Africa include works by Col. A.B. Ellis (1893) and C.C. Reindorf (1895).
