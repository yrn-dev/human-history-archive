# Politico-Military Expeditions of 1890-1891

## Military Operations and Territorial Occupation
In 1890 and 1891, a series of politico-military expeditions were conducted that led to several strategic territorial gains and the establishment of military infrastructure. These operations resulted in the occupation of the Zhob valley and the foundation of Fort Sandeman, which served as a central cantonment. Additionally, a line of outposts was extended from Quetta, reaching as far as Wana, located north of the Gomal.

## Impact on Regional Stability
The extension of military occupation and the results of these expeditions had a direct effect on the local populations and security of the region. Specifically, these actions reduced the independent Pathan tribes of the Suliman mountains to effective order. Furthermore, the military presence put a stop to border raiding that had been occurring on the Indus plains south of the Gomal.

## Administration of Baluchistan
Following these expeditions, administrative shifts occurred within Baluchistan. In 1893, serious differences developed between Sir James Browne—who had succeeded Sir Robert Sandeman as the agent to the governor-general in Baluchistan—and the khan of Kalat. These conflicts were the result of the outrageous conduct of Mir Khodadad Khan regarding the treatment of his officials and the management of his own court. Consequently, the khan was deposed and succeeded by his son, Mir Mahmud Khan, in November 1893.

A significant change in the administration of the region took place in July 1899, involving the Nushki district and Niabat. The management and perpetual lease of this area, including all administrative power, jurisdiction, and rights, were transferred to the British agency. In exchange, a perpetual rent of Rs.9000 per annum was established. This transfer ensured direct control over the great highway to Seistan, which had been opened for railway traffic and khafila.
