# North-Western and North Staffordshire Railways

The London & North-Western and the North Staffordshire railways are key transport systems serving various urban districts and market towns across England, particularly within the counties of Staffordshire, Derbyshire, and Warwickshire.

## The London & North-Western Railway
The London & North-Western railway provides critical connectivity between London and several major industrial and market centers. In Warwickshire, it serves the city of Birmingham, which is located 113 miles north-west of London on the railway's loop line between Stafford and Rugby. In Staffordshire, the railway serves the market town of Bilston via the Ettingshall Road station; Bilston is situated 124 miles north-west of London.

Further north, the railway reaches the market town of Ashbourne in the western parliamentary division of Derbyshire. In the Gorton parliamentary division of Lancashire, the railway serves Denton, an urban district located 4½ miles north-east of Stockport. Additionally, the London & North-Western railway operates branches that serve Market Harborough in Leicestershire. In the county of Derbyshire, a branch of the London & North-Western railway serves the western region.

## The North Staffordshire Railway
The North Staffordshire railway operates in several key areas of the Midlands. In Staffordshire, it serves the municipal and county borough of Burton-upon-Trent, a purely industrial town located 127 miles north-west of London. The railway also extends into Shropshire; at Market Drayton, a branch of the North Staffordshire railway enters Staffordshire to join the Wellington-Crewe line of the Great Western railway. In the county of Derbyshire, the North Staffordshire railway serves the western portion of the region.

## Urban Populations and Industrial Centers
The areas served by these railways include a variety of urban districts with differing population sizes and economic bases as of the late 19th and early 20th centuries:

*   **Birmingham:** A metropolis of one of the greatest industrial districts in England, with a population of 522,204 in 1901.
*   **Burton-upon-Trent:** A municipal and county borough with a population of 46,047 in 1891, which grew to 50,386 by 1901.
*   **Bilston:** A market town in the Black Country with an urban district population of 24,034 in 1901. It is known for its productive mines of ironstone and coal, as well as factories for heavy iron goods, brass-work, and japanned goods.
*   **Denton:** An urban district in Lancashire with a population of 14,934 in 1901, where coal mining and the manufacture of felt hats are primary industries.
*   **Market Drayton:** A market town in Shropshire with a population of 5,167 in 1901 (civil parish of Drayton-in-Hales), serving as a center for agricultural trade and iron foundries.
*   **Ashbourne:** A market town in Derbyshire with an urban district population of 4,039 in 1901, characterized by a manufacture of corsets and a large agricultural trade.

## Regional Connectivity
In Derbyshire, the railway networks are complemented by canal systems. The Trent & Mersey canal crosses the southern part of the county, while the Derby canal connects Derby to the Trent & Mersey and the Erewash canal. The Erewash canal runs north from the Trent through the Erewash valley, with a smaller, little-used branch known as the Cromford canal leading to Matlock.
