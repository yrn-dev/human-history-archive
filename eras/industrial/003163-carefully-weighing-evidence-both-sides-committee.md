# Committee Recommendations on Bankruptcy and Debtors

## Inquiry into Bankruptcy Law
In 1906, the Board of Trade appointed a committee to investigate and report on the laws in force within the United Kingdom regarding bankruptcy, composition by insolvent debtors with their creditors, and deeds of arrangement. The inquiry also focused on the prevention and punishment of frauds committed by debtors against their creditors, as well as any areas where existing laws required amendment. 

The scope of this inquiry was international, embracing the laws and procedures of Ireland, Scotland, Australia, France, and Germany, in addition to English law. To conduct this study, the committee received documents, memoranda, and a vast amount of evidence from influential public bodies, trade protection societies, and chambers of commerce. The resulting report was issued in 1908 (Cd. 4068).

## Findings on Commercial Law
The committee reported that the commercial community did not express dissatisfaction with the main features of the existing procedure and law. However, the committee did identify specific branches of administration and incidents of the law that required recommendations. 

One primary concern was the prosecution and punishment of debtors who caused loss to creditors through improper and reckless trading or the commission of fraud. The committee noted that the existing procedure for these cases was expensive, cumbersome, and dilatory. Consequently, the committee opined that when a debtor committed an offence for which they ought to be prosecuted, conviction and adequate punishment should follow decisively and speedily. While the committee suggested leaving the existing procedure untouched, they recommended that such offences also be punishable on summary conviction before justices and magistrates, applying the provisions of the Summary Jurisdiction Acts.

## Recommendations on Bookkeeping and Insolvency
After weighing the evidence on both sides, the committee addressed the failure or omission of a debtor to maintain proper books of account in a trade or business during the two years preceding their bankruptcy. The committee recommended that, if such an omission occurred without excuse, it should be made a legal offence punishable by imprisonment upon summary conviction. 

This recommendation was subject to four specific limitations:
* The law must define the specific books of account that a person in trade or business is required to keep, following the examples of the laws in Germany and France.
* Punishment for the failure to keep these books should only occur if the debtor becomes bankrupt and the proved liquidated debts in that bankruptcy exceed £200.
* No prosecution for this failure should take place until two years have passed since the law was enacted.
* A debtor should not be punished if they can demonstrate that the omission was excusable and honest, did not contribute to their insolvency, and provided that no prosecution is instituted without an order from the bankruptcy court.

## Additional Committee Reports
The committee provided recommendations of a similar character regarding other delinquencies. Furthermore, the committee issued recommendations concerning:
* The realization of estate upon bankruptcy.
* The law relating to the after-acquired property of an undischarged bankrupt and dealings with said property.
* Trading by undischarged bankrupts.
* The operation of the law of relation back of a bankruptcy trustee's title.
* Liabilities of married women under bankruptcy law.

Additionally, the committee reported on the compulsory regulation of agreements for the hire and purchase of chattels, the compulsory regulation of assignments of book debts, and the law and practice regarding voluntary deeds of arrangement between creditors and a debtor.
