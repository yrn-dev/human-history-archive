# Equal Stresses + and -

In the study of structural engineering and material science, specifically concerning the design of bridges and the behavior of iron and steel, the condition of "equal stresses + and -" refers to a state where a bar is subjected to alternate tension and compression of equal magnitude.

## Material Breaking Strengths and Vibration Strength
The analysis of materials subjected to repeated loading involves several distinct measures of breaking strength. The statical breaking strength, denoted as *t*, is the strength of a bar loaded once gradually until it fractures. The primitive strength, denoted as *u*, is the breaking strength of a bar that is loaded and unloaded an indefinitely great number of times, with the stress varying alternately between *u* and 0.

When a bar is subjected to an indefinitely great number of repetitions of stresses that are equal and opposite in sign—meaning the stress ranges alternately from *s* to *-s*—the resulting breaking strength is termed the vibration strength, denoted as *s*.

## Stress Range and Working Stress
For a bar subjected to alternate tension and compression of equal amount, the range of stress ($\Delta$) is equal to $2 f_{max.}$. In such cases, the maximum stress $k_{max.}$ is $0.33 K$, where $K$ is the statical breaking stress. The safe working stress for this condition is determined by dividing $k_{max.}$ by the factor of safety.

The determination of working stress can also be approached using the methods of J.J. Weyrauch and W. Launhardt, which are based on an empirical expression of Wöhler's law. This law suggests that a bar will ultimately break if $f_{max.} = F\Delta$. For stresses of opposite signs, Weyrauch found that $F = (u-s)/(2u-s-f_{max.})$.

## Calculations for Iron and Steel
Using the Launhardt and Weyrauch formulas and a factor of safety of 3, the working stress ($f$) for tension or thrust is calculated based on the value of $\phi$. For iron, the equation is $f = 4.4 (1+½\phi)$, and for steel, it is $f = 5.87 (1+½\phi)$.

In the specific case of "Equal stresses + and -", the value of $\phi$ is -1.00. Applying this to the working stress calculations yields the following values:
*   **Iron:** 2.20 tons per sq. in.
*   **Steel:** 2.93 tons per sq. in.

The value for $\phi$ in this context is 0.500.

## Application in Bridge Work
In bridge construction, the occurrence of limiting stresses of opposite signs is rare and typically only occurs in some of the bracing bars. This differs from other loading scenarios, such as "all dead load" or "all live load," where the stresses are of the same sign.
