# Robert Michael Ballantyne

Robert Michael Ballantyne (1825–1894) was a Scottish writer of fiction known for his series of adventure stories written for young audiences. Born in Edinburgh on April 24, 1825, he belonged to the same family as the famous publishers and printers.

## Early Life and Career
At the age of sixteen, Ballantyne traveled to Canada, where he spent six years employed by the Hudson's Bay Company. Following his return to Scotland in 1847, he published his first book, *Hudson's Bay: or, Life in the Wilds of North America*, in 1848. For a period, he worked for the publishers Messrs Constable. However, in 1856, he left the business world to pursue a professional career in literature.

## Literary Works and Style
Ballantyne is popularly associated with a series of adventure stories for the young. His bibliography includes a vast number of works—upwards of a hundred in total—published in regular succession. Notable titles include:
* *The Young Fur-Traders* (1856)
* *Ungava: a Tale of Eskimo Land* (1857)
* *The Coral Island* (1857)
* *The World of Ice* (1859)
* *The Dog Crusoe* (1860)
* *The Lighthouse* (1865)
* *Deep Down* (1868)
* *Erling the Bold* (1869)
* *The Pirate City* (1874)
* *The Settler and the Savage* (1877)

Ballantyne adhered to a personal rule of writing his descriptions based as far as possible on personal knowledge of the scenes he depicted. His writing was characterized by a thoroughly healthy tone and considerable graphic force. In addition to his fiction, he wrote *Personal Reminiscences of Book-making*, published in 1893.

## Other Talents and Later Life
Beyond his writing, Ballantyne was a skilled artist who exhibited several of his water-colours at the Royal Scottish Academy. In his later years, he resided at Harrow. He died in Rome on February 8, 1894, having traveled there in an attempt to recover from the effects of overwork.
