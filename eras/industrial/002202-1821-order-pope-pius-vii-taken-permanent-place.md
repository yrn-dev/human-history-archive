# 1821 in the Papal and Italian Context

The year 1821 was marked by significant cultural, political, and personal developments within the Italian peninsula and the administration of the Papacy, particularly involving the influence of Pope Pius VII and the resulting instability in Rome.

## Musical and Cultural Contributions
Under the direction of Pope Pius VII, 1821 saw the creation of a notable work of ecclesiastical music. Giuseppe Baini (1775–1844), an Italian priest and composer who had been appointed musical director to the choir of the pontifical chapel in 1814, composed a ten-part *Miserere* for Holy Week. This composition, written in the severe ecclesiastical style, established a permanent place within the services of the Sistine chapel during Passion Week.

## Political Disturbances and Consequences
The year 1821 was characterized by political disturbances that affected various figures in Rome. Bartolommeo Borghesi (1781–1860), an Italian antiquarian and numismatist who had been tasked by Pius VII with the arrangement and cataloging of the Vatican's coin collections, was forced to retire to San Marino as a consequence of the disturbances of 1821. 

Outside of the immediate papal administration, the year was also a period of political activity for Lord Byron. In January 1821, Byron began work on his historical drama *Sardanapalus*, though progress was hindered by his political involvement. Having been elected *capo* of the "Americani" (a branch of the Carbonari), Byron spent his time in consultations with conspirators and the procurement of arms and ammunition. Additionally, in 1821, Byron published the first of two letters entitled "Strictures on the Life and Writings of Pope," a work written to exalt the merits of Pope and decry the "Lakers."

## The Influence of Pope Pius VII
While 1821 represents a specific point of instability, it occurred within the broader tenure of Pope Pius VII. His papacy was marked by complex interactions with Napoleon Bonaparte, including the 1804 coronation in Paris where Napoleon seized the crown and waved the Pope aside. Pius VII was later carried off from Rome in 1809, an event that led Bartolommeo Alberto Cappellari (the future Gregory XVI) to withdraw to Murano. 

Pius VII's influence extended to the intellectual and legal spheres; he founded the Academy of the Catholic Religion, of which Cappellari became a member in 1800. In legal matters, Pius VII refused to declare void the marriage between Jerome Bonaparte and Elizabeth Patterson, despite the wishes of Napoleon. Following the fall of Napoleon and the return of Pius VII to Rome, the Vatican received special envoys, such as Cesare d'Azeglio, who brought his son Massimo to Rome as an extra attache.
