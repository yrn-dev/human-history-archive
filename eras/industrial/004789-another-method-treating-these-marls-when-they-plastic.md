# Treatment of Marls in Plastic Condition

Marls, also referred to as lime clays, are a class of clays containing a high percentage of limestone or chalk. These materials are typically found in connection with limestone or chalk formations and usually contain between 15% and 30% calcium carbonate. In some instances, the limestone content may exceed 40%.

## Processing Methods for Marls
There are several methods for treating marls depending on their state and the desired outcome of the brickmaking process. When marls are in a plastic condition, one specific method of treatment involves squeezing the material through iron gratings using machinery. The purpose of this process is to arrest and remove pebbles from the clay.

Alternatively, marls may be processed through a grinding-mill. This machinery features heavy iron rollers and a solid bottom, which serves to crush limestone pebbles sufficiently so that they are mixed thoroughly throughout the entire mass of the clay.

## Importance of Pebble Removal
The removal or crushing of limestone pebbles is of great importance during the preparation of the clay. If these pebbles remain in the clay during the firing process, they are converted into quicklime. This chemical change creates a tendency for the finished brick to shatter when it is exposed to the weather.

## Other Preparation Techniques
In different districts, other methods are employed to treat clays containing limestone:
* **Wash-pan Method:** The marl is mixed with water on a wash-pan to create a creamy fluid. This fluid is then passed through coarse sieves and placed on a drying-bed. Because washed clays are generally very fine in grain, coarse sand is often added in the wash-pan.
* **Artificial Malm:** In areas like London, where natural deposits of fine chalk-clay (locally called "malm") have been exhausted, an artificial version is created. Ordinary brick-clay and ground chalk are mixed in water. The chalk is ground on grinding-pans, and the clay is worked with water until it reaches the consistency of cream. These pulps are run through a coarse sieve or grating onto a drying-bed. Once the mass is stiff enough to walk on, a layer of fine ashes is spread over it; the mass is then turned over, mixed by spade, and tempered with additional water.

## Characteristics of Burnt Marls
The high calcium carbonate content of marls affects the final appearance of the product. Marls typically burn to a distinctive yellow colour.

## Comparison with Other Clay Types
Marls differ from ordinary brick clays (class 1), which are more widely distributed and have a more extensive geological range. While marls are limited to limestone or chalk formations, ordinary brick clays vary more in composition. The strength of these clays is generally linked to the percentage of hydrated aluminium silicates, while the colour of the burnt brick is primarily determined by the presence of oxide of iron, which typically ranges from 2% to 10%.
