# Thermal Storage

In industrial and power-generation contexts, thermal storage refers to systems designed to manage the output of energy and the supply of materials to maintain efficiency during periods of fluctuating demand.

## Application in Boiler Systems
Thermal storage is employed in cases where the required work is very intermittent. In such systems, a large cylindrical storage vessel is positioned above the boiler. This vessel is designed with sufficient capacity to hold enough feed-water to supply the boiler during periods of maximum required output.

The operation of this system relies on the following mechanisms:
*   **Communication and Flow:** The upper portion of the storage vessel remains in free communication with the steam space of the boiler. Feed-water is delivered into the upper part of the vessel and can be run into the boiler from the lower part when needed.
*   **Temperature Regulation:** Before the feed-water reaches the bottom of the chamber, it is directed over extended surfaces exposed to steam. This process raises the temperature of the water to that of the steam.
*   **Accumulation and Usage:** During periods when the engine requires less than the normal supply of steam, a quantity of feed-water greater than the average is pumped into the chamber. This excess water accumulates at the evaporation point. When an extra supply of steam is necessary, the feed-pump is stopped, and the boiler is fed using the hot water stored in the chamber.

Beyond the storage effect, these chambers serve a secondary purpose by allowing many feed impurities to be deposited. These impurities are comparatively harmless in the chamber and can be readily removed.

## Use in Destructor Stations
Modern destructor stations utilize various accessory plants to manage operations. Among these are thermal storage arrangements, which are used specifically for equalizing the output of power from the station over a 24-hour day. Other related machinery at these stations includes air heaters or regenerators for producing hot-air blasts for furnaces, as well as superheaters.

## Other Thermal and Storage Contexts
While "thermal storage" specifically refers to the energy management systems described above, the archive contains references to other forms of thermal treatment and storage:

*   **Balneotherapeutic Treatment:** Thermal waters are used for medical purposes. Hot thermal treatment, including hot sulphur springs and hot muriated waters, is used to relieve chronic rheumatism, particularly when there is incipient stiffness of a joint or much exudation. Thermal gaseous saline water from Nauheim, combined with Schott's resistance movements, is used for patients with dilated hearts and painful joints following rheumatic fever.
*   **Cold Storage:** Cold storage facilities are used in the design of specific buildings and in the transit of perishable goods. In the trade of flowers and fruit, cold storage by rail and sea allows produce from regions such as California to be sold in England while remaining fresh.
*   **Temperature Regulation in Apparatus:** Certain medical apparatus, such as Dr. Tarnier's, utilize warm water vessels. However, without a temperature regulator, these can be subject to internal temperature fluctuations caused by external thermal variations.
