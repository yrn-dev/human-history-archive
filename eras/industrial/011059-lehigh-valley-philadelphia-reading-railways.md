# Lehigh Valley and Philadelphia & Reading Railways

The Lehigh Valley and Philadelphia & Reading railways are transportation systems that serve various municipalities and industrial centers across Pennsylvania and New York. These lines provide critical infrastructure for regions characterized by the mining of anthracite coal and the production of various manufactured goods.

## Service in Pennsylvania

In Pennsylvania, the Lehigh Valley railway serves several boroughs across different counties. In Luzerne county, it provides service to Kingston, a borough located on the North Branch of the Susquehanna river opposite Wilkes-Barré. In this location, the Lehigh Valley railway operates alongside the Delaware, Lackawanna & Western railway. Kingston is a site for the mining of anthracite coal and maintains machine-shops and railway repair facilities.

The Lehigh Valley railway also serves Ashland, a borough in Schuylkill county situated on the slope of Locust Mountain. Ashland is located approximately 100 miles northwest of Philadelphia and 50 miles northeast of Harrisburg. In Ashland, the Lehigh Valley railway operates in conjunction with the Philadelphia & Reading railway. The primary industry in Ashland is the mining of anthracite coal at various nearby collieries.

The Philadelphia & Reading railway provides service to Danville, the county-seat of Montour county. Located on the North Branch of the Susquehanna river, Danville is served by both the Philadelphia & Reading and the Delaware, Lackawanna & Western railways. The region surrounding Danville is rich in limestone, coal, and iron, supporting large manufactories of iron and steel, as well as furnaces and stoves.

## Service in New York

The reach of the Lehigh Valley railway extends into the state of New York, specifically serving the city of Cortland. Cortland is the county-seat of Cortland county and is situated in the central part of the state on the Tioughnioga river. In this city, the Lehigh Valley railway operates alongside the Delaware, Lackawanna & Western railway. Cortland is a significant manufacturing center, producing items such as cabs, carriages, wagons, iron, steel, clothing, and wallpaper, as well as wire-cloth and netting.
