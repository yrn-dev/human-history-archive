# Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky

Prince Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky (1814–1879) was a Russian soldier and governor of the Caucasus. His military career was characterized by extensive service in the Caucasus region and the implementation of systematic tactics in mountain warfare.

## Early Career and Education
Baryatinsky was privately educated and entered the school of the ensigns of the Guard at the age of seventeen. He received his commission as a cornet in the Life Guards of the cesarevich Alexander on November 8, 1833. His early military service included a period in the Caucasus in 1835, where he served with great gallantry. Upon returning to St Petersburg, he was awarded a gold sword "for valour." On January 1, 1836, he was attached to the suite of Alexander.

## Military Operations in the Caucasus
Baryatinsky returned to the Caucasus in 1845, where he distinguished himself particularly during an attack on the stronghold of Shamyl, an action for which he was awarded the order of St George. Between 1848 and 1856, he played a leading role in the primary military events of the region. 

Among his most notable exploits during this period were his operations against Shamyl at Chechen and his victory at Mezeninsk in 1850. Baryatinsky is credited with inaugurating a new era of mountain warfare through the use of tactics that were described as both energetic and systematic. In 1854, he participated in the Kuruk Dere campaign.

## Administrative and Command Roles
Baryatinsky's rise in rank continued with his appointment as adjutant-general on January 6, 1853, followed by his appointment as chief of the staff on July 5 of that same year. On January 1, 1856, he became the commander-in-chief of the Caucasian army and subsequently served as the governor of the Caucasus.

As an administrator, Baryatinsky was regarded as fully worthy of his reputation. During the first three years of his appointment, he succeeded in subduing the entirety of the eastern Caucasus and capturing the long elusive Shamyl. Additionally, he conquered various tribes in the western Caucasus located between the rivers Byelaya and Laba. In recognition of these services, he was created a fieldmarshal.

## Later Life and Death
The strenuous nature of Baryatinsky's labors eventually broke his health. At his own request, he was relieved of his post on December 6, 1862. He spent the final days of his life abroad and died in Geneva after a career consisting of forty-eight years of active service.
