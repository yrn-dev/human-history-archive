# Hewitt's Nitration of Para-Oxyazobenzene

## Overview of Oxyazo Compounds
The chemical constitution of oxyazo compounds has been a subject of significant scientific debate. Some chemists argue that these substances are true azophenols, characterized by the type $\text{R.N}_2\text{.R}_1\text{.OH}$. Conversely, other researchers propose a quinonoid structure, suggesting they are quinone hydrazones of the type $\text{R.NH.N:R}_1\text{:O}$.

Early chemical investigations into this matter were conducted by R. Meldola and Th. Zincke. Zincke observed that the products resulting from the condensation of phenyl-hydrazine with $\alpha$-naphthoquinone were identical to those obtained by coupling a diazonium salt with $\alpha$-naphthol. Meldola attempted to resolve the issue by acetylating azophenols and subsequently splitting the acetyl products through reduction in an acid solution, though these efforts did not yield satisfactory results.

## Physico-Chemical and Structural Theories
Further analysis was provided by K. Auwers, who utilized the determination of freezing-point depressions to examine the compounds. Auwers found that ortho-oxyazo compounds produced normal depressions, while para-oxyazo compounds produced abnormal depressions. Based on these findings, Auwers concluded that the ortho compounds are quinone hydrazones (or behave as such), whereas the para compounds are phenolic.

A different perspective was offered by A. Hantzsch, who classified oxyazo compounds as pseudo-acids. According to Hantzsch, these compounds possess the configuration of quinone hydrazones in their free condition, but their salts exhibit a normal phenolic type.

## Research by J. T. Hewitt
J. T. Hewitt contributed to this debate through specific chemical reactions involving nitration and bromination. Hewitt nitrated para-oxyazobenzene using dilute nitric acid. This process resulted in the production of a benzene azo-ortho-nitrophenol. Hewitt noted the significance of this result by pointing out that quinones are not attacked by dilute nitric acid, thereby providing evidence against a quinonoid structure for the para-oxyazobenzene in this context.

Hewitt further investigated the problem by brominating oxyazobenzenes. His experiments revealed that the outcome of the reaction depended on the presence of mineral acid:
*   **With mineral acid:** When the hydrobromic acid produced during the reaction remained in the system, a brombenzene-azo-phenol was formed. Hewitt concluded that the presence of mineral acid imparts a pseudo-quinonoid character to the azo compound.
*   **Without mineral acid:** When the hydrobromic acid was removed from the sphere of the reaction (achieved through the addition of sodium acetate), bromination occurred within the phenolic nucleus.

These results indicated that the azo compound does not possess a pseudo-quinonoid character if the mineral acid is removed.
