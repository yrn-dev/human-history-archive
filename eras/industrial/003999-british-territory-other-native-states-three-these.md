# British Territorial Administration and Native States

The British Empire is organized for political administration into three primary sections: the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland (including the Isle of Man and the Channel Islands), the colonial empire (consisting of all other dependencies and colonies), and the Indian empire. The Indian empire is specifically composed of British India and feudatory native states.

## The Bombay Presidency
The Bombay Presidency is a province of British India administered by a governor. Its territory is composed of both British districts and native states. Geographically, it is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, Indore, Hyderabad, and the Central Provinces to the east, and Rajputana, the Punjab, and Baluchistan to the north. Politically, the presidency also includes the settlement of Aden.

The total area of the presidency, including Sind but excluding Aden, is 188,745 sq. m. Of this, 65,761 sq. m. are under native rule and 122,984 sq. m. are under British rule. As of 1901, the population was 25,468,209, with 18,515,587 residents living in British territory and 6,908,648 in native states.

The presidency is divided into twenty-six districts across four divisions: Sind, the southern (Carnatic), the central (Deccan), and the northern (Gujarat). Native states within this presidency total 353 separate units. These are managed either by collectors of the districts where smaller states are located or by political agents. Major groups of states include:
* **North Gujarat:** Cambay, Palanpur agency, Mahi Kantha agency, Rewa Kantha agency, Cutch, and Kathiawar agency.
* **South Gujarat:** Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.
* **North Konkan, Nasik, and Khandesh:** Surgana, Jawhar, and the Khandesh political agency.
* **South Konkan and Dharwar:** Savanur, Sawantwari, and Janjira.
* **Deccan Satara Jagirs:** Jath, Phaltan, Aundh, Bhor, Akalkot, and Daphlapur.
* **Other:** Khairpur in Sind and the southern Mahratta states, including Kolhapur.

## Native States of the Bombay Region
Certain native states exhibit complex territorial arrangements with British districts. Baroda, located within the Gujarat province of Bombay, maintains direct relations with the governor-general. It comprises four isolated divisions: Nausari, Baroda, and Kadi (all in Gujarat proper), and Amreli with Okhamandal (in the Kathiawar peninsula). These divisions are interlaced in an intricate fashion with other native states or British territory. The total area of Baroda is 8,099 sq. m. In 1901, its population was 1,952,692, a 19% decrease from the previous decade attributed to epidemics of fever and cholera, the plague, and famines occurring in 1896-1897 and 1900-1901.

In the Deccan division of Bombay, the native state of Kuruntwad (or Kurandvad) forms part of the Southern Mahratta jagirs. Created in 1772 by a grant from the peshwa, it was divided in 1855 between a junior and senior branch. The territory of both branches is widely scattered among British districts and other native states. The senior branch covers 185 sq. m. with a 1901 population of 42,474, while the junior branch covers 114 sq. m. with a 1901 population of 34,003.

## Other British Territories and States
In Bengal, the native state of Kuch Behar (or Cooch Behar) is entirely surrounded by British territory. It is a triangular level plain of 1,307 sq. m. with a 1901 population of 566,974. The state is intersected by numerous rivers flowing south to the Brahmaputra.

In Burma, the northern Shan States include a district and ferry known as Kunlong. While the village was burnt by Kachins in 1893, the location is noted as the nominal terminus in British territory for the railway crossing the northern Shan States toward the borders of Yunnan. Additionally, the hills overlooking the Nam Ting valley are inhabited by the Wa, the majority of whom live in British territory that is unadministered.

In Baluchistan, the territory is split into two main divisions. British Baluchistan, a portion of British India under a chief commissioner, covers 9,403 sq. m. and consists mostly of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the 1879 treaty of Gandamak. The remaining 79,382 sq. m. belongs to the native states of Las Bela and Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran). There are also tribal areas held by the Bugti and Marri tribes covering 7,129 sq. m., and directly administered districts (including the Bolan Pass and the Zhob and Chagai political agencies) covering 36,401 sq. m.
