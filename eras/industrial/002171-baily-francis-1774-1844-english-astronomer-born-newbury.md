# BAILY, FRANCIS (1774-1844)

Francis Baily was an English astronomer born on April 28, 1774, in Newbury, Berkshire. His early life included a tour of the unsettled regions of North America between 1796 and 1797; a journal of this journey was later edited by Augustus de Morgan in 1856.

## Business Career and Financial Writings
In 1799, Baily entered the London Stock Exchange. During this period, he established a high reputation as a writer on life-contingencies through the publication of several works: *Tables for the Purchasing and Renewing of Leases* (1802), *The Doctrine of Interest and Annuities* (1808), and *The Doctrine of Life-Annuities and Assurances* (1810). Through his integrity and diligence, he amassed a fortune, which allowed him to retire from business in 1825 to devote himself entirely to the study of astronomy.

## Contributions to Astronomy and Catalogues
Baily was active in the astronomical community prior to his retirement, having taken a leading role in the foundation of the Royal Astronomical Society in 1820. In 1827, he was awarded the society's gold medal for preparing the Astronomical Society's Catalogue of 2881 stars. 

His work on stellar catalogues was extensive. He revised the catalogues of Hevelius, Edmund Halley, Tycho Brahe, Ulugh Beg, Ptolemy, and Tobias Mayer. In 1837, he recommended the reduction of the catalogues of Nicolas de Lacaille and Joseph de Lalande, which contained approximately 57,000 stars, a task he largely executed. He also superintended the compilation of the British Association's Catalogue of 8377 stars, which was published in 1845. Additionally, Baily's protests in 1829 led to the reform of the *Nautical Almanac*.

## Solar Eclipses and "Baily's Beads"
Baily is noted for his observations of solar eclipses. During an annular eclipse on May 15, 1836, at Inch Bonney in Roxburghshire, he provided a vivid description of a phenomenon caused by the inequalities of the moon's limb, known as "Baily's Beads." This observation initiated the modern series of eclipse-expeditions and drew significant attention to the totality of the eclipse on July 8, 1842, which Baily observed at Pavia.

Furthermore, Baily contributed to historical astronomy. In 1811, in his first contribution to the Transactions of the Royal Society, he settled the date of a specific eclipse to September 30, 610 B.C. Sir John Herschel later approved these calculations, noting that Baily was the first to solve this disputed question.

## Geophysics and Other Scientific Work
Baily's scientific interests extended to the physical properties of the earth. Between 1838 and 1842, he used Henry Cavendish's method to determine the mean density of the earth, arriving at the authoritative value of 5.66. He also completed and discussed the pendulum-experiments of H. Foster, from which he deduced an earth ellipticity of 1/289. In this process, he introduced a neglected element of reduction to correct for the length of the seconds-pendulum. Because of his expertise, he was entrusted with the reconstruction of the standards of length in 1843.

In 1835, he published an *Account of the Rev. John Flamsteed*, a work of fundamental importance to the scientific history of that era, which included a republication of the British Catalogue.

## Death and Legacy
Francis Baily died in London on August 30, 1844. His life and works were detailed in a memoir by J. Herschel published in 1845, which was also prefixed to Baily's *Journal of a Tour*.
