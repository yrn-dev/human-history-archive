# Joseph Dobrovsky and the Bohemian Language Revival

## The Decline of the Bohemian Language
Following the deaths of Comenius, Slavata, and Skala, the Bohemian language entered a period of significant decline. For a considerable amount of time, no works of importance were produced in the language. This stagnation was further exacerbated by the new Austrian government, which actively sought to discourage the use of the Bohemian language in every way possible.

## The 19th-Century Romantic Movement
A shift in the linguistic landscape occurred at the beginning of the 19th century with the onset of the romantic movement. The initial efforts to revive the Bohemian language were modest in scope. During this early phase of revival, the published material consisted almost exclusively of translations from foreign languages.

## The Contributions of Joseph Dobrovsky
Joseph Dobrovsky (1753-1829) was the first writer to draw attention back to the Bohemian language, which had been almost forgotten. While he is credited with sparking this renewed interest, the majority of his own scholarly output was written in Latin or German. His primary contributions to the field included a history of Bohemian literature and a grammar of the Bohemian language.

Dobrovsky's direct contributions to Bohemian-language writing were limited to a collection of letters and several essays. These essays were contributed to the early issues of the *Casopis Musea Kralovstvi Ceskeho* (Journal of the Bohemian Museum).
