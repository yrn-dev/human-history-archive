# Major-General H. G. Barry

Major-General H. G. Barry was a resident of Ballyclough, Co. Cork. He is noted in the archive's records as the father of Sir Redmond Barry (1813–1880), a British colonial judge.

## Family and Legacy
The son of Major-General Barry, Sir Redmond Barry pursued a career in law and colonial administration. After receiving his education, Redmond Barry was called to the Irish bar in 1838. He later emigrated to Australia, where he spent a significant portion of his life in Melbourne.

Sir Redmond Barry held numerous high-ranking positions in the colony of Victoria, including:
* Solicitor-general (the first to hold the position with a seat in the executive and legislative councils following the creation of the colony in 1851).
* Judge of the Supreme Court.
* Administrator of the government.
* Acting chief-justice.

Sir Redmond Barry was knighted in 1860 and was created a K.C.M.G. in 1877. He was also a representative for Victoria at the Philadelphia Exhibition of 1876 and the London International Exhibition of 1862.

## Contributions to Education and Culture
Sir Redmond Barry is credited as the first person in Victoria to take an interest in higher education. Through his efforts, he induced the local government to provide large sums of money for this purpose. His primary achievements in this field include:
* The founding of the university of Melbourne in 1853, where he served as the first chancellor.
* Serving as the president of the national gallery, the museum, and the Melbourne public library (beginning in 1854).

Additionally, Sir Redmond Barry was among the first individuals to foster the volunteer movement within Australia.
