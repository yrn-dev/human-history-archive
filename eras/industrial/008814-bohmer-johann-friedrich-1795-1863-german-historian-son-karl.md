# BOHMER, JOHANN FRIEDRICH (1795-1863)

Johann Friedrich Bohmer was a German historian born on April 22, 1795, in Frankfort-on-Main. He was the son of Karl Ludwig Bohmer, who died in 1817.

## Education and Career
Bohmer received his education at the universities of Gottingen and Heidelberg. During his early years, he demonstrated an interest in art, which led him to visit Italy. Upon returning to Frankfort, he shifted his focus toward the study of history. 

His professional career in Frankfort included serving as the librarian and archivist for the city. Additionally, he held the position of secretary for the *Gesellschaft fur altere deutsche Geschichtskunde*.

## Personal Views and Affiliations
Bohmer held strong personal and political convictions. He possessed a great dislike for the Protestant faith and Prussia. Conversely, he felt a corresponding affection for the Roman Catholic Church and Austria. Despite his affinity for the Roman Catholic Church, he did not belong to it. It has been noted that his critical sense may have been somewhat warped, though his research remains of great value to students.

## Historical Works and Research
Bohmer's historical contributions were primarily centered on the tabulation and collection of imperial documents and charters from the middle ages. His work often included explanatory passages and introductions.

His major publications include:
*   ***Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313*** (Frankfort, 1831), which served as an abstract.
*   ***Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Karolorum. Die Urkunden samtlicher Karolinger in kurzen Auszugen*** (Frankfort, 1833).
*   A series of ***Regesta imperii***, which covered the period 1314-1347 (Frankfort, 1839), followed by three supplementary volumes, and the period 1246-1313 (Frankfort, 1844), followed by two supplementary volumes. He also edited the *Regesta* for the period 1198-1254 (Stuttgart, 1849).
*   ***Fontes rerum Germanicarum*** (Stuttgart, 1843-1868), a collection of original authorities regarding German history in the 14th and 13th centuries. Following Bohmer's death, A. Huber edited the fourth and final volume of this work.

Other edited collections by Bohmer include *Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400* (Frankfort, 1832), *Wittelsbachische Regesten von der Erwerbung des Herzogtums Bayern bis zu 1340* (Stuttgart, 1854), and the *Codex diplomaticus Moeno-Francofurtanus. Urkundenbuch der Reichsstadt Frankfurt* (Frankfort, 1836), which saw a new edition by F. Law in 1901.

## Death
Johann Friedrich Bohmer died unmarried in Frankfort on October 22, 1863.
