# BALL, SIR ALEXANDER JOHN, BART. (1759-1809)

Sir Alexander John Ball was a British rear-admiral and the governor of Malta. Born into a family from Gloucestershire, he began his career in the navy and achieved the rank of lieutenant in 1778.

## Naval Career and Association with Nelson
In 1781, Ball began a close association with Rodney. Following Rodney's victory on April 12, 1782, Ball was promoted to commander two days later, and he attained the rank of captain in 1783. During this period, he spent one year in France to live economically and to learn the French language.

Ball's relationship with Lord Nelson was initially strained; while Nelson was a captain, he viewed Ball unfavorably, describing him as a "great coxcomb." Ball did not receive a command until 1790, though he remained continuously employed from that year forward. The animosity between the two men ended in 1798, when Ball provided assistance to Nelson's ship during heavy weather. This event led to a close friendship that lasted until their deaths.

Under the command of Nelson, Ball participated in the battle of the Nile. During this engagement, Ball commanded the ship "Alexander," which faced the "L'Orient," the flagship of Brueys, which subsequently blew up.

## The Blockade and Governance of Malta
Two months after the battle of the Nile, Ball was ordered to lead the blockade of Malta, a mission he maintained without interruption for two years. During the siege, Ball delegated the blockade duties to his first lieutenant, while he personally led the local militia and the marines to conduct the siege from the land side. His care for his men during this operation established a popularity with the Maltese people that persisted until his death.

Following the fall of Malta, Ball practically retired from active naval service, despite urgent requests from Nelson for him to remain afloat. In 1801, Ball was made a baronet and appointed governor of Malta. He served in this capacity until 1809. As governor, he became well-regarded by the local population due to his regard for their interests and his opposition to policies that would treat the island as a conquered dependency.

## Death and Legacy
Sir Alexander Ball died on October 20, 1809, and was buried in Malta. His high regard for Lord Nelson is evidenced in Nelson's letters, and Ball is mentioned numerous times in the edition of Nelson's *Despatches* by Sir H. Nicolas. Additionally, Ball was known to be kind to Coleridge, who praised him in "The Third Landing Place" of *The Friend*.
