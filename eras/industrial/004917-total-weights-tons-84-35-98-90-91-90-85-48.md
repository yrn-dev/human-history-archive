# Analysis of Weights and Tonnage in Engineering and Commerce

The measurement of weight in tons is applied across various disciplines, including locomotive engineering, bridge construction, maritime trade, and agricultural production. These measurements are used to determine structural stresses, vessel capacities, and commodity yields.

## Locomotive Weights and Loads
Locomotives are categorized by their function, with distinct weight profiles for express passenger, goods, and tank engines. 

### Engine Weight Specifications
Data for various locomotives indicates the following weight distributions:
*   **Express Passenger Engines:** Total weights range from 84.35 to 98.90 tons, with tons per foot over all ranging from 1.58 to 1.71 and maximum axle loads between 16.00 and 19.00 tons.
*   **Goods Engines:** Total weights range from 75.65 to 78.80 tons. Their maximum axle loads vary from 13.65 to 16.00 tons.
*   **Tank Engines:** Total weights range from 47.00 to 60.80 tons, with maximum axle loads between 15.29 and 17.54 tons.

### Bending Moment and Uniform Loads
To analyze the impact of heavy locomotives on tracks, equivalent uniform loads are determined for different spans. For a span of 5.0 feet, the equivalent load is 7.6 tons per foot run; this decreases as the span increases, reaching 1.97 tons per foot run for a span of 100.0 feet.

## Bridge Engineering and Load Calculations
In bridge construction, weights are divided into live loads and dead loads to ensure structural integrity.

### Load Definitions
The **live or temporary load** refers to the weight of a dense crowd or heavy wagon for road bridges, and the heaviest likely train for railway bridges. The **dead load** comprises the weight of the superstructure, including wind-bracing, flooring, and main girders. In railway bridges, the weight of rails and sleepers is estimated at 0.2 to 0.25 tons per foot run per line, while cross girders and rail girders weigh 0.15 to 0.2 tons. Adding a footway adds approximately 0.4 ton per foot run.

### Stress and Weight Formulas
Impact stresses are calculated as a percentage of live load stresses: 20% for floor stringers, 15% for floor cross girders, 10% for 40-ft. spans of main girders, and 5% for 100-ft. spans. Additionally, longitudinal drag from braking trains is estimated at one-seventh of the train's weight.

Specific bridge examples illustrate these weights:
*   **Douro Viaduct:** The center span weighs 727 tons, with a rolling load of 1.2 ton per foot.
*   **Ohio Bridge:** The total weight of steel and iron across three spans is approximately 5000 tons.
*   **Riga Pontoon Bridge:** Each iron pontoon has a weight of 22 tons and a displacement of 180 tons.

## Maritime Tonnage and Trade
Tonnage is used to measure the capacity and volume of shipping traffic, particularly in the Danube region.

### Vessel Capacity and Traffic
Historically, sailing ships of 200 tons register were replaced by steamers of up to 4000 tons register, which could carry a deadweight of nearly 8000 tons. Between 1847 and 1860, British ships entering the Danube averaged 193 tons each. By 1893, 905 vessels entered with a total of 1,287,762 tons. Between 1861 and 1902, the total tonnage of ships frequenting the Danube increased five-fold, while the mean size of individual ships increased ten-fold.

## Agricultural and Assay Measurements
The ton is also utilized as a standard for measuring agricultural output and mineral purity.

### Dairy Production
In dairy records from 1890 to 1899, milk production is measured in tons. For example, in 1890, the estimated total quantity of milk produced was 7,487,640 tons. This milk is used to calculate the production of butter (estimated at 49 cwt. or 531 lb. per ton of milk) and cheese (estimated at 220 lb. per ton of milk).

### Mineral Assaying
In the assaying of gold and silver ores, results are expressed in ounces to the ton (using the troy ounce and a 2000-pound avoirdupois ton). To facilitate metric calculations, the "assay ton" (A.T. = 29.166 grammes) is used. In this system, one milligram of gold or silver found in one assay ton of ore represents one ounce per ton.
