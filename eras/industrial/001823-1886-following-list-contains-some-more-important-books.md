# 1886

The year 1886 is associated with various academic publications, professional transitions, and the passing of notable figures across several fields of study and the arts.

## Academic and Scientific Publications
In the field of bacteriology, the New Sydenham Society published *Microparasites in Disease: Selected Essays* in 1886. This work consisted of translated essays from German and French sources providing accounts of classical researches.

Within the study of religion and ancient texts, 1886 saw the publication of *Sumangala Vilasin[i]*, edited by J. E. Carpenter and T. W. Rhys Davids and published in London by the Pali Text Society. Additionally, the *Journal of the Pali Text Society* published "Gandhavaramsa" (p. 59) during this year.

In the realm of biblical scholarship, C.H. Cornill published *Das Buch des Propheten Ezechiel* in 1886, which serves as a model for applying Old Testament textual criticism to the restoration of text.

## Historical and Literary Works
The year 1886 was significant for the study of the Domesday Book. A Domesday Commemoration was held in 1886, which led to the subsequent publication of two volumes of *Domesday Studies* (published in 1888 and 1891) by various writers.

In Portuguese literature, the novelist Camillo Castello Branco was the subject of a study by J. Pereira de Sampaio, published in *A Geracao Nova* in Oporto in 1886.

In the United States, scholar Hiram Corson published *Introductions* to the study of Browning in 1886. This period also marked a transition in his professional career at Cornell University; he served as the professor of Anglo-Saxon and English literature until 1886, at which point he became the professor of English literature and rhetoric.

## Notable Deaths
The English artist and illustrator Randolph Caldecott died on February 12, 1886, in Florida. Caldecott had previously worked as a bank clerk before becoming a prolific illustrator for works such as *Old Christmas* and *Bracebridge Hall*, as well as a contributor to *Punch* and the *Graphic*. Following his death, Henry Blackburn published *Randolph Caldecott, Personal Memoir of his Early Life* in London in 1886.
