# Racking and Storing in Brewing

In the process of brewing, racking and storing constitute the final main stage of production. Brewing is composed of seven distinct main processes: grinding, mashing, boiling, cooling, fermenting, cleansing, and finally, racking and storing.

## The Racking Process
Racking occurs after the fermentation and cleansing operations have been completed. During this stage, the beer is "racked off" into trade casks or storage vessels. In some instances, the beer may spend a few hours in a settling tank before being racked. 

In modern lager brewing, such as the process employed by Messrs Allsopp, the beer is conveyed via filtered air pressure to store tanks for a secondary fermentation lasting three weeks. Following this, the beer is cooled, filtered, and racked. To ensure that no gas escapes and the beer does not come into contact with outer air, these operations—including bottling—are performed under counter pressure.

## Storage and Conditioning
The duration of storage varies significantly depending on the type of beer produced:
* **Stock and Pale Ales:** These finest ales are stored for a period ranging from six weeks to three months before they are sent out.
* **Running Beers:** These include mild ales and other varieties that are frequently sent out of the brewery within ten days or a week of the mashing process.

To achieve the desired "condition," brewers employ several techniques during or after storage:
* **Dry Hopping:** For many of the better beers, it is customary to add hops directly into the cask.
* **Priming:** This is used for beers that have become flat or running beers that require rapid conditioning. Priming involves adding a small quantity of sugar solution to the beer in the cask, which ferments rapidly to produce condition.
* **Carbonating:** In the "chilling and carbonating" system used for light and bright bottled beers, beer is first chilled to a very low temperature to cause the immediate deposition of matter. After filtration, the beer is impregnated under pressure with carbonic acid gas (carbon dioxide) to produce immediate condition.

## Context within the Brewing Cycle
Racking is the culmination of a sequence that begins with malt being crushed in a malt mill to create "grist." This grist is mixed with hot water in a mashing machine and passed to a mash-tun to make the starch soluble. The resulting clear wort is boiled with hops in a copper, passed through a hop back to separate insoluble constituents, and then cooled via a cooler and refrigerator. 

Before reaching the fermenting vessels, the wort passes through a collecting vessel to allow revenue officers to assess the duty. In the fermenting vessels, the wort is transformed into "green" beer. Depending on the system used—such as the Skimming, Dropping, or Burton Union systems for top fermentation, or the closed cylindrical steel vessels for lager—the beer is cleansed of yeast before it is finally racked and stored.
