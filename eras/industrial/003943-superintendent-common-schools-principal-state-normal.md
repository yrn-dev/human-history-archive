# Superintendent of Common Schools and Principal of the State Normal

In the history of American educational reform, the roles of "superintendent of common schools" and "principal of the State Normal" were key administrative positions used to reorganize and professionalize public instruction. These titles are notably associated with the career of Henry Barnard, an American educationalist whose work in the mid-19th century helped shape the structure of state school systems.

## Henry Barnard and the Connecticut System
Henry Barnard (1811–1900) was born in Hartford, Connecticut, and graduated from Yale in 1830. After being admitted to the Connecticut bar in 1835, he served as a member of the Connecticut legislature from 1837 to 1839. During this tenure, Barnard framed and introduced a bill in 1838 designed to provide for "the better supervision of the common schools." This legislation established a board of "commissioners of common schools" within the state.

Barnard served as the secretary of this board from 1838 until it was abolished in 1842. Throughout this period, he worked to reform and reorganize the state's common school system, which earned him a national reputation as a reformer. His commitment to the common schools continued through his editorial work; he edited the *Connecticut Common School Journal* from 1838 to 1842, and again from 1851 to 1854.

## Administration in Rhode Island and Connecticut
Following his early work in Connecticut, Barnard was appointed by the governor of Rhode Island in 1843 as an agent to examine and recommend improvements for the state's public schools. This effort led to a reorganization of the Rhode Island school system two years later. From 1845 to 1849, Barnard served as the first commissioner of public schools in Rhode Island, an administration characterized by a decided step in educational progress. During this time, he also edited the *Journal of the Rhode Island Institute of Instruction* (1846–1849).

Upon returning to Connecticut, Barnard held two concurrent leadership roles from 1851 to 1855: he served as the "superintendent of common schools" and the principal of the State Normal School located at New Britain, Conn.

## Subsequent Educational Leadership
Beyond his roles in Connecticut and Rhode Island, Barnard held several other high-level academic and governmental positions:
* **University of Wisconsin:** From 1859 to 1860, he served as chancellor and as the agent of the board of regents of the normal school fund.
* **St John's College:** He was president of this institution in Annapolis, Maryland, in 1866.
* **United States Commissioner of Education:** From 1867 to 1870, he served as the first commissioner in this role, where he established the foundation for the Bureau of Education.

Barnard's most significant contribution to the field is considered to be his editorship of the *American Journal of Education* from 1855 to 1881. The thirty-one volumes of this journal serve as a comprehensive compendium of educational information.

## Context of Normal Schools and Superintendents
The titles held by Barnard reflect a broader trend in the establishment of state-led educational oversight and teacher training. "Normal schools" were institutions dedicated to the training of teachers. Examples of such institutions appearing in various regions include:
* **Georgia:** The State Normal School (co-educational) was established in 1891 in Athens, and the Georgia Normal and Industrial College for Girls was established in 1889 in Milledgeville.
* **Kentucky:** The state established two State Normal Schools for whites in 1906 (at Richmond and Bowling Green) and a Kentucky Normal and Industrial School for negroes in 1886 at Frankfort.
* **Illinois:** The state maintained several normal colleges, including the Southern Illinois State Normal College at Carbondale and the Eastern Illinois State Normal School at Charleston.
* **Minnesota:** A state normal school was erected in Duluth in 1902.

Similarly, the role of the superintendent varied by jurisdiction. In British Columbia, a superintendent of education acts under the provincial secretary to oversee common, graded, and high schools. In Kentucky, rural schools are supervised by a superintendent in each county. In Illinois, the State Superintendent of Public Instruction serves as one of the trustees of the University of Illinois.
