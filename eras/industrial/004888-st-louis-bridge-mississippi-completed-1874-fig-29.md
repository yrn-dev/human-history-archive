# St Louis Bridge over the Mississippi

The St Louis bridge is a significant example of a large-span bridge utilizing steel arches. Completed in 1874, the structure spans the Mississippi River at St Louis, where the river is confined to a single channel measuring 1,600 feet in width.

## Engineering and Construction
The bridge was designed by engineer Captain J.B. Eads. During the planning phase, it was noted that a freshet in 1870 had caused the scour to reach a depth of 51 feet. To ensure stability, Eads determined that the abutments and piers must be established on rock. For the east abutment and the east pier, this required reaching a depth of 136 feet below high water. This objective was achieved through the use of air locks and caissons with air chambers, a method described as an unprecedented feat in the history of engineering.

## Structural Design
The bridge consists of three spans, each constructed from arches of cast steel. The dimensions of these spans are as follows:
*   **Centre Span:** 520 feet in the clear, with a rise of 47½ feet.
*   **Side Spans:** 502 feet in the clear, with a rise of 46 feet.

Each of these spans is supported by four double ribs made of steel tubes. These tubes are clasped and butted using couplings made of wrought iron. To consolidate the upper and lower members of each rib—which are positioned 12 feet apart from centre to centre—vertical bracing is employed, turning them into a single arch. Unlike some later bridge constructions, the St Louis bridge is not hinged at the crown or the springings.

## Usage and Capacity
The bridge was designed for multi-modal transport. It carries a double railway track, and situated above the tracks is a roadway with a width of 54 feet.
