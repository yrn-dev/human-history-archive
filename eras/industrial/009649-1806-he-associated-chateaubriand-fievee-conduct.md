# Louis Gabriel Ambroise Bonald

Louis Gabriel Ambroise Bonald, Vicomte de (1754–1840), was a French politician and philosopher. He was born on October 2, 1754, at Le Monna, located near Millau in Aveyron.

## Early Life and Political Exile
Bonald emigrated from France in 1791 due to his dislike of the principles of the Revolution. During this period of exile, he joined the army of the prince of Conde and eventually settled in Heidelberg. While in Heidelberg, he authored his first significant work, *Theorie du pouvoir politique et religieux*. Published in three volumes in 1796 (with a new two-volume edition appearing in Paris in 1854), this highly conservative text was condemned by the Directory.

## Career and Public Service
Upon returning to France, Bonald lived in retirement as he was viewed as an object of suspicion. In 1806, he became associated with Fievee and Chateaubriand in the conduct of the *Mercure de France*. Two years later, he was appointed as a councillor of the Imperial University, an institution he had frequently attacked.

Following the restoration, Bonald served as a member of the council of public instruction. Between 1815 and 1822, he sat in the chamber as a deputy. His speeches during this time were characterized by extreme conservatism, and he went so far as to advocate for literary censorship. In 1822, he was appointed minister of state and presided over the censorship commission. He was made a peer in 1823, though he lost this dignity in 1830 after refusing to take the oath.

## Later Years and Death
Bonald was a member of the Academy starting in 1816. After 1830, he ceased taking part in public affairs and retired to his seat at Le Monna. He died there on November 23, 1840.
