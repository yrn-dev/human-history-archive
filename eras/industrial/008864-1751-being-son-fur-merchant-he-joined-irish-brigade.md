# Benoit de Boigne

## Early Life and Military Beginnings
Benoit de Boigne, Count, was born on March 8, 1751, in Chambery, Savoy. He was the son of a fur merchant. Boigne's military career began in 1768 when he joined the Irish Brigade in France. Following this period, he entered the service of Russia, during which time he was captured by the Turks.

## Career in India
Motivated by reports of the wealth found in India, Boigne traveled to the country. He initially served for a short duration with the East India Company before resigning his position. In 1784, he joined Mahadji Sindhia to provide training to his troops using European methods of warfare.

Boigne and his two battalions demonstrated their effectiveness during the battles of Chaksana and Lalsot, where they held the field despite the defeat of the rest of the Mahratta army by the Rajputs. In 1788, Boigne participated in the battle of Agra, where he restored the fortunes of the Mahratta and established Mahadji Sindhia as the undisputed master of Hindostan.

## Command and Later Military Successes
As a result of his success at Agra, Boigne was granted command of a brigade consisting of ten infantry battalions. With this force, he achieved victories at Merta and Patan in 1790. These achievements led to Boigne being appointed commander-in-chief of Sindhia's army and being permitted to raise two additional brigades of disciplined infantry. In 1793, he defeated the army of Holkar at the battle of Lakhairi.

Following the death of Mahadji Sindhia in 1794, Boigne remained loyal to Daulat Rao Sindhia, despite having the opportunity to make himself the master of Hindostan.

## Retirement and Final Years
In 1795, Boigne's health began to decline, leading him to resign his command. He returned to Europe the following year, possessing a fortune of L400,000. He spent the era of Napoleon's lifetime living in retirement. Later, he was greatly honoured by Louis XVIII. Benoit de Boigne died on June 21, 1830.
