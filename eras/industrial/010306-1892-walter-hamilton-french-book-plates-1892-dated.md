# Walter Hamilton and Book-plate Literature

## Contributions to Book-plate Studies
Walter Hamilton is an author noted for his contributions to the study of book-plates (ex-libris). In 1892, he published a work titled *French Book-plates*. He followed this in 1895 with another publication titled *Dated Book-plates*. 

His works are listed among the authorities for those interested in the composition of book-plates, alongside other significant texts in the field. These include the *Ex-libris Series* from London, A. Poulet-Malassis's *Les Ex-libris francais* (1875), and the *Guide to the Study of Book-plates* (1880) by the Hon. J. Leicester Warren, also known as Lord de Tabley. Other contemporary authorities in this subject include Sir A.W. Franks, who wrote *Notes on Book-plates, 1574-1800* (1887), and Friedrich Warnecke, author of *Die deutschen Bucherzeichen* (1890).

## Related Bibliographic Works
The study of book-plates and related illustrative arts was supported by various other publications during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Henri Bouchot published *Les Ex-libris et les marques de possession du livre* in 1891, and Egerton Castle released *English Book-plates* in 1892. Later works in the field include H.W. Fincham's *Artists and Engravers of British and American Book-plates* (1897) and *German Book-plates* by Count K.E. zu Leiningen-Westerburg, which was translated by G.R. Denis in 1901.
