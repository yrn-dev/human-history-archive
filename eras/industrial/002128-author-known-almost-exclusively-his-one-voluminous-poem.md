# Philip James Bailey

Philip James Bailey (1816–1902) was an English poet best known for his voluminous work, *Festus*. Born in Nottingham on April 22, 1816, Bailey was the son of a man who published both verse and prose and served as the editor and owner of the *Nottingham Mercury*, a primary journal in Nottingham, from 1845 to 1852.

## Education and Early Career
Bailey received his education locally until the age of sixteen, at which point he matriculated at Glasgow University. He did not complete his degree. In 1835, he moved to London and entered Lincoln's Inn. Although he entered the legal field, he did not make a serious practice of the law. He eventually settled at Basford, where he spent three years composing the poem *Festus*.

## Festus
First published anonymously in 1839, *Festus* achieved immediate success in both the United States and England. The work was highly popular, passing through twelve editions in England and nearly three times that number in the United States. By 1889, Bailey published a "Jubilee Edition" of the poem, which remained known to both younger and older generations.

The poem is described as a vast pageant of philosophy and theology. Organized into approximately twelve divisions, the work attempts to represent the relationship between man and God, preach the immortality of the soul, emphasize the benignity of Providence, and postulate a "gospel of faith and reason combined." While the work contains dignified thought and fine lines, its ambitious theme and a certain incoherency in its execution make it difficult to read for anyone other than the most sympathetic student.

## Literary Legacy
Bailey is essentially regarded as a man of one book; although he published other verses, he is known almost exclusively by *Festus*. The poem is noted as a singular example of a work that was virtually completed during the author's youth and was never reinforced or supplanted by his later achievements. Despite undergoing various incorporations and changes, it remained his defining work.

Philip James Bailey died on September 6, 1902.
