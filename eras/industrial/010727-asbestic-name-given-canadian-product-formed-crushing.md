# Asbestic

Asbestic is a specific Canadian product created through the processing of serpentine rock. The production process involves crushing serpentine rock that contains thin seams of asbestos and mixing the resulting material with lime to create a plaster.

## Properties and Industrial Applications of Asbestos
The economic value of asbestos is derived from several key physical properties: its ability to withstand high temperatures, its low thermal conductivity, and its partial resistance to the attack of acids. Because of these characteristics, it is utilized in a wide range of industrial arts, including:

*   **Insulation and Filtering:** It is used as an electric insulator and for the jacketing of steam-pipes and boilers. It also serves as a filtering medium for liquids that are corrosive.
*   **Manufacturing and Machinery:** Asbestos is processed into felt, millboard, and yarn. It is extensively employed as packing for glands, stopcocks, and joints in machinery.
*   **Construction and Safety:** It is used in the production of fire-proof paints, plasters, and cements. Additionally, it is used for roofing and flooring in the form of fire-proof felt and sheathing. It has also been used to create clothing for firemen and curtains for the stage.
*   **Specialized Uses:** Asbestos is mixed with fire-clay to create balls for gas-stoves and is used for packing safes.

In trade, various preparations of asbestos mixed with other materials are sold under names such as gypsine, asbestolith, salamandrite, and uralite.

## Historical and Rare Uses
Historically, asbestos was treated as a rare curiosity and woven into fabrics. It is said that Charlemagne owned a tablecloth made of this material, which could be purified by being thrown into a fire when it became soiled. In 1676, a merchant from China presented a handkerchief to the Royal Society known as *linum asbesti*, or "salamander's wool." Other applications include its use as a lamp-wick by the Eskimos of Labrador, a practice mirrored in some of the sacred lamps of antiquity.

## Production and Occurrence
### Canadian Production
Canadian asbestos is currently the most industrially important variety. It is found in the province of Quebec within a small belt of serpentine, primarily near Thetford and Black Lake. It was first recognized as having commercial value around 1877.

The production process typically involves the following steps:
1.  **Quarrying:** The rock is generally quarried and then cobbed by hand.
2.  **Processing:** The material is dried if necessary and crushed using rock-breakers.
3.  **Refining:** The crushed rock is passed between rollers and further reduced in division by "fiberizers."
4.  **Grading:** The loosened fibres are sorted on a shaking screen.

### Other Occurrences
In the United States, asbestos is worked to a limited extent. It is found in the serpentine of Vermont and is obtained as amphibole-asbestos from Sall Mountain, Georgia. It also occurs in Massachusetts, Virginia, Arizona, and South Carolina. Research by Dr. G.P. Merrill indicates that some asbestos is the result of a shearing process within the rocks.
