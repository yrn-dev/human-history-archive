# The Skimming System of Fermentation

The Skimming System is a specific method of applying top fermentation used in England for the production of beer. It is categorized as one of the methods within the broader Cleansing System, alongside the Dropping System and the Burton Union System.

## Process and Vessel Design
In the Skimming System, the fermentation process occurs from start to finish within a single set of wooden vessels. These vessels are referred to as either "rounds" or "squares." To manage the environment and the final product, these vessels are equipped with specific hardware:
*   **Attemperators:** A system of pipes through which cold water is run to prevent the temperature from becoming excessive.
*   **Skimming Devices:** The vessels are fitted with a "parachute" or other similar device used for the removal, or "skimming," of the yeast once fermentation has concluded.

## The Fermentation Cycle
The process begins with wort, which is generally maintained at a temperature of approximately 60° F. This wort is "pitched" with liquid yeast, also known as "barm," at a rate of 1 to 4 lb per barrel, depending on the strength and type of beer being produced. 

The biological action of the living yeast cells splits the sugar in the wort into carbonic acid and alcohol, accompanied by various subsidiary reactions. This process manifests visually on the surface of the liquid in several stages:
1.  **Initial Stage:** After a few hours, a slight scum or froth appears.
2.  **Cauliflower Stage:** This develops into a light, curly mass known as a "curly head" or "cauliflower."
3.  **Rocky Head Stage:** The mass becomes more solid and lighter in appearance.
4.  **Yeasty Head Stage:** The mass shrinks into a compact "yeasty head" that emits hissing sounds and large bubbles of gas.

## Temperature Control and Cleansing
As fermentation progresses, the temperature of the liquid rises considerably. To ensure the temperature does not exceed a maximum of 70-75° F, the attemperators are utilized. 

The appearance of the yeasty head signals that the "cleansing" of the beer—the process of separating the yeast from the liquid—has fairly commenced. Unlike the Dropping System, where beer is moved to second vessels (such as pontos or "backs") for the completion of fermentation and cleansing, the Skimming System completes the entire process within the original wooden vessel. The yeast is then removed using the aforementioned parachute or skimmer.

## Post-Fermentation
Once the fermentation and cleansing operations are finished, the beer is racked off into trade casks or storage vessels, sometimes after spending a few hours in a settling tank. Depending on the type of beer, the storage time varies:
*   **Stock and Pale Ales:** These are stored for periods ranging from six weeks to three months.
*   **Running Beers:** Mild ales and other running beers are often sent out within ten days or a week of the initial mashing.

To achieve "condition" rapidly, running beers or flat beers may be "primed" by adding a small quantity of sugar solution to the cask, which ferments quickly. Additionally, some higher-quality beers undergo "dry hopping," where hops are added directly into the cask.
