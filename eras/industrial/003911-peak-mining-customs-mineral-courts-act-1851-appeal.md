# High Peak Mining Customs and Mineral Courts Act 1851

The High Peak Mining Customs and Mineral Courts Act 1851 is the legislation used to regulate the powers and procedures of the Barmote courts. These courts are specialized judicial bodies located in the lead-mining districts of Derbyshire, England.

## The Barmote Courts
Barmote courts—also referred to as Barghmote, Bargemote, Barmoot, or Berghmote—are institutions of very ancient origin, with records of their existence dating back to the reign of Edward I. These courts were established to determine customs specific to the lead-mining industry and to settle disputes arising in connection with those customs.

The jurisdiction of the Barmote courts is extensive, covering seven clearly defined districts. This authority extends to lands under individual ownership as well as crown lands within the duchy of Lancaster. Historically, the miners of Derbyshire functioned as an independent community under the authority of barmasters and a steward, who convened two Barmote courts annually.

## Regulation and Appeals
As modern mining progressed, many of the traditional procedures and customs associated with these courts became obsolete. Consequently, the High Peak Mining Customs and Mineral Courts Act 1851 was enacted to regulate their powers.

For parties seeking to challenge the decisions of these courts, an appeal from the jurisdiction of the Barmote courts is available by way of *certiorari*.

## Historical Context of Derbyshire Mining Jurisdiction
The legal landscape of Derbyshire has evolved through various administrative divisions. Early divisions were known as wapentakes; while Domesday mentions five, 13th-century documents list seven. By the 14th century, most were described as hundreds, though Wirksworth maintained the designation of wapentake into modern times.

Other historical jurisdictions in the region included the forests of Duffield and Peak, which maintained their own officers and separate courts. The justice seat for the Forest of Peak was located in an extra-parochial area situated at equal distances from Bowden, Tideswell, and Castleton. Both the Peak and Duffield forests were disafforested during the 17th century. Additionally, the court of the Honour of Peverel, which once held jurisdiction in the Peak, Wirksworth, and Scarsdale hundreds, was abolished in 1849.
