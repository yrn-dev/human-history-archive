# Bohemian Literature and Language

The development of Bohemian literature and language is characterized by a significant revival in the 19th century and a diverse body of work spanning from ancient genuine documents to modern prose and poetry.

## Early Language and Literature
The earliest genuine documents of the Bohemian language consist of several legends—most notably those of St Dorothy and St Catherine—and various hymns. Ancient epic fragments have also been preserved, including *Tandarias a Floribella* and the *Alexandreis*. Another document of considerable historical value is the rhymed chronicle commonly, though incorrectly, called the chronicle of Dalimil. Likely written during the reign of King John (1310-1346), this chronicle records Bohemian history up to the reign of King Henry of Carinthia and is permeated by a strong feeling of racial antipathy toward the Germans.

While the so-called MSS. of Kralodvur (Koniginhof) and Zelena Hora (Gruneberg) are considered forgeries from the early 19th century, the genuine early writings were printed following the 19th-century revival of the literature.

## The 16th Century and the Bohemian Revival
During the 16th century, a period of comparative tranquillity, native literature developed further. This era produced writers from the community of the Bohemian Brethren and three notable historians. Wenceslas Hajek of Libocan (died 1553) wrote the *Kronika ceska*, a work dedicated to Emperor Ferdinand I. Although inaccurate and partial, it was translated into several languages. Other historians of this period include Sixt of Ottersdorf (1500-1583), who wrote *Memorials of the Troubled Years 1546 and 1547* regarding the unsuccessful rising against Ferdinand I, and Bartos (died 1539), whose *Chronicle of Prague* possesses great historical value.

The broader revival of the Bohemian language and literature is mainly attributed to four men: Palacky, Safarik, Kolar, and Jungmann. Francis Palacky is regarded as the greatest of Bohemian historians, particularly for his history of Bohemia from the earliest period to 1526. Paul Joseph Safarik wrote *Starozitnosti Slovanske* (Slavic antiquities), recording the history and literature of early Slavs. John Kolar, the greatest poet of the revival, wrote *Slavy dcera* (daughter of Slavia), a personification of the Slavic race. Joseph Jungmann (1773-1847) contributed a monumental German and Bohemian dictionary and the *Dejepes literatury ceska* (history of Bohemian literature).

## 19th Century Poetry and Prose
In the earlier part of the 19th century, famous writers included the poets Macha, Tyl, Erben, and Francis Ladislav Celakovsky, the author of *Ruze stolistova* (the hundred-leaved rose). Karel Havlicek is noted as the founder of Bohemian journalism.

By the latter part of the 19th century, particularly after the 1882 foundation of the national university, literature expanded rapidly. Notable older writers include Benes Trebizky, an author of historical novels, and Bozena Nemceva, whose work *Babicka* was translated into many languages. 

Other significant figures include:
* **John Neruda (1834-1891):** A talented writer of prose and poetry whose philosophical epic *Kosmicke basne* (cosmic poems) is considered among his most valuable works.
* **Julius Zeyer (1841-1901):** Author of the epic poem *Vysehrad*, which celebrates the Prague acropolis. Among his many novels, *Jan Maria Plojhar* has achieved the greatest success.
* **Svatopluk Cech:** A poet inspired by national enthusiasm, known for *Basne otroka* (the songs of a slave) and *Vaclav z Michalovic, Lesetinsky Kovar* (the smith of Lesetin).
* **Jaroslav Vrchlicky (b. 1853):** Born Emil Frida, he is described as one of the best later Bohemian poets and a brilliant dramatist. His lyric collections include *Sonety Samotare* (sonnets of a recluse), *Poute k Eldoradu* (pilgrimages to Eldorado), and *Rok na jihu* (a year in the south).
* **Adolf Heyduk:** Also cited as one of the best later Bohemian poets.

## Modern Novelists and Dramatists
Bohemian novelists became very numerous in the later period. Alois Jirasek is noted as both a novelist and a distinguished dramatic author. Jacob Arbes wrote the *Romanetta*, and Vaclav Hladik wrote *Evzen Voldan*, which provides a striking representation of modern life in Prague. Hladik, like many of his contemporaries, was also a prolific writer of dramas.
