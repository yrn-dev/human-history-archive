# Deductions for Repairs and Renewals in General Average

In the adjustment of claims for General Average (G.A.), the cost of repairs is subject to specific deductions based on the principle of "new for old." These regulations are designed to ensure that the allowance for repairs accounts for the age and condition of the vessel. The York-Antwerp Rules established a definite scale for these deductions to replace previous customary rules that varied by location and to adapt the process to modern shipbuilding conditions.

## General Principles of Deduction
Deductions for repairs and renewals are generally regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired. There are several exceptions to this rule, including boilers, machinery, and provisions and stores. 

Certain costs are allowed in full without deduction. These include:
*   The expense of straightening bent ironwork, including the labor required to remove and replace it.
*   Graving dock dues, which encompass the use of stages, shears, graving dock materials, and expenses related to cartages and removals.
*   Temporary repairs of damage allowable as G.A.

Conversely, no deduction is made for provisions and stores that have not been in use, nor for old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material. Additionally, painting of the bottom is not allowed if the bottom had not been painted within the six months preceding the date of the accident.

## Deductions for Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel ships, the deductions are determined by the time elapsed from the date of the original register to the date of the accident:

*   **Up to 1 year (A):** All repairs are allowed in full, with the exception of the coating or painting of the bottom, from which one-third is deducted.
*   **Between 1 and 3 years (B):** One-third is deducted from repairs and renewals of the hull's woodwork, masts, spars, furniture, upholstery, crockery, glassware, metal, sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers (excluding chain and wire), covers, awnings, and painting. A deduction of one-sixth is applied to chain cables, chain, wire ropes, wire hawsers, wire rigging, steam winches, donkey engines, and steam cranes, including their connexions. All other repairs are allowed in full.
*   **Between 3 and 6 years (C):** Deductions follow the same pattern as the 1-to-3-year bracket, except that a one-sixth deduction is applied to machinery (including mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 6 and 10 years (D):** Deductions follow the pattern of the 3-to-6-year bracket, but a one-third deduction is applied to all hawsers, rigging, ropes, sheets, and the repairs or renewal of all machinery (including boilers and mountings) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 10 and 15 years (E):** A one-third deduction is applied to all repairs and renewals. However, a one-sixth deduction is applied to chain cables and the ironwork of the hull and cementing. Anchors are allowed in full.
*   **Over 15 years (F):** A one-third deduction is applied to all repairs and renewals. Chain cables are subject to a one-sixth deduction, and anchors are allowed in full.

## Deductions for Wooden or Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, no "new for old" deduction is made if the ship is under one year old from the date of the original register at the time of the accident. After one year, a deduction of one-third is applied, with specific exceptions:
*   **Anchors:** Allowed in full.
*   **Chain cables:** Subject to a deduction of one-sixth.
*   **Metal sheathing:** The cost of a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing is allowed in full, minus the proceeds of the old metal.
*   **Nails, felt, and labour metalling:** Subject to a one-third deduction.

## Related General Average Provisions
Beyond repair deductions, G.A. covers other specific losses and expenses. When a ship is detained in a port for repairs, the maintenance and wages of the crew, officers, and master are admitted as G.A. until the ship is ready to proceed. However, if the voyage is abandoned or the ship is condemned, these costs are not admitted after the date of condemnation or abandonment.

Damage or loss of cargo caused by discharging, storing, reloading, and stowing is made good as G.A. only when the costs of those specific measures are admitted as G.A. Similarly, loss of freight resulting from cargo loss or damage is made good as G.A. The amount for sacrificed goods is based on the market value at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival.
