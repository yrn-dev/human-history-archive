# The Stone Square System

The Stone Square System, also referred to as the Yorkshire Stone Square System, is a specific method used in England for the top fermentation of beer. This system is utilized only to a certain extent and is used exclusively in the north of England.

## Technical Operation
The system consists of two square vessels built of stone, which are superimposed and kept very cool. These two vessels are connected to one another via a valve and a man-hole. The process involves pumping the fermenting wort from one of these vessels to the other. Once the fermentation process has concluded, the man-hole is closed, and the yeast is removed from the top square.

## Classification within Fermentation Methods
In England, the methods for applying the top fermentation system are divided into two primary classifications: the Cleansing System and the Yorkshire Stone Square System. The Cleansing System is further subdivided into the Skimming System, the Dropping System (which includes the ordinary dropping system and the pontos system), and the Burton Union System.

## Post-Fermentation Processes
Following the completion of fermentation and cleansing operations, the beer undergoes a process known as racking. This may occur after the beer has spent a few hours in a settling tank. The beer is then racked off into trade casks or storage vessels. The duration of storage depends on the type of beer:
* **Stock and Pale Ales:** These finest ales are stored for a period ranging from six weeks to three months before they are sent out.
* **Running Beers:** These include mild ales and are frequently sent out of the brewery within ten days or a week of mashing.

## Conditioning and Finishing
Certain beers undergo additional treatments to achieve the desired condition:
* **Dry Hopping:** For many of the better beers, it is customary to add some hops directly into the cask.
* **Priming:** This process is used for beers that have become flat or for running beers that require rapid conditioning. Priming involves adding a small quantity of sugar solution to the beer in the cask, which ferments rapidly to produce "condition."
