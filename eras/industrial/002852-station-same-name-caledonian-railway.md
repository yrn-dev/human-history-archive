# Balquhidder

## Geography and Location
Balquhidder is a village and parish located in Perthshire, Scotland. The name is derived from Gaelic, meaning "the farm in the back-lying country." Geographically, the village is situated at the east end of Loch Voil, a lake positioned at the foot of the Braes of Balquhidder. In terms of regional distance, it lies 27-3/4 miles northwest of Stirling. The village is located 2 miles west of the station of the same name, which is situated on the Caledonian railway line running from Callander to Oban. As of 1901, the population of the parish was 605.

## History and Clan Conflict
The district of Balquhidder was acquired by the Maclaurins as early as the 9th century. The Maclaurins occupied the area for several hundred years; however, they were eventually ousted by the Macgregors. The Macgregors were a neighbouring clan that had repeatedly raided Maclaurin lands, culminating in 1558 when they slew the chief of the Maclaurins along with many of his followers.

## Notable Figures and Landmarks
Balquhidder is associated with several historical and traditional figures:

*   **Rob Roy:** The village was the scene of some of the exploits of Rob Roy, who died there in 1734. His grave is located in the old kirkyard and is marked by a stone featuring rude carving, which was likely executed centuries before his time.
*   **Angus:** Tradition states that another ancient stone in the area covers the grave of Angus, a Columban missionary. Angus is credited as the first person to carry out Christian work in this part of the Highlands.
