# Hosea Ballou

## Early Life and Education
Hosea Ballou was an American Universalist clergyman born on April 30, 1771, in Richmond, New Hampshire. He was the son of Maturin Ballou, who served as a Baptist minister. Ballou was self-educated and dedicated himself to the ministry early in his life. In 1789, he became a convert to Universalism.

## Ministerial Career and Locations
Ballou's career as a pastor spanned several states and cities over several decades. In 1794, he became the pastor of a congregation located at Dana, Massachusetts. Between 1801 and 1807, he preached in the surrounding towns of Barnard, Vermont. Following this period, he served in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, from 1807 to 1815. He then moved to Salem, Massachusetts, where he preached from 1815 to 1817. 

In December 1817, Ballou began his tenure as the pastor of the Second Universalist Church in Boston. He remained in this position until his death in Boston on June 7, 1852.

## Theological Contributions and Works
Ballou is recognized as a principal American expositor of Universalism. His theological perspective differed from that of John Murray—another figure referred to as "the father of American Universalism"—in that Ballou opposed trinitarian views and legalism, and removed every trace of Calvinism from Universalism.

Throughout his life, Ballou was a prolific writer, producing approximately 10,000 sermons, numerous essays, polemic theological works, and many hymns. He is best known for the following publications:
*   *Notes on the Parables* (1804)
*   *A Treatise on Atonement* (1805)
*   *Examination of the Doctrine of a Future Retribution* (1834)

His most significant contribution to his Church is considered to be the extensive body of denominational literature he left behind.

## Editorial Work
In addition to his writing and preaching, Ballou founded and edited two significant publications:
*   *The Universalist Magazine*, founded in 1819 and later renamed *The Trumpet*.
*   *The Universalist Expositor*, founded in 1831 and later renamed *The Universalist Quarterly Review*.
