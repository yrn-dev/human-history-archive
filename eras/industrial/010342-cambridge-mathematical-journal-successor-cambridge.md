# Cambridge Mathematical Journal and the Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal

The *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* and its successor, the *Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal*, served as venues for the publication of mathematical research during the 19th century. These journals provided a platform for the dissemination of abstruse papers on mathematical topics and the publication of early research by notable mathematicians.

## Origins and Editorial Leadership
The *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* was originated and, for a period of time, edited by D.F. Gregory. Under Gregory's editorship, the journal published a variety of mathematical works, including the earliest published paper by the mathematician Boole.

## Contributions of Boole
The mathematician Boole was a frequent contributor to both the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* and its successor, the *Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal*. In total, Boole contributed twenty-two articles across these two publications. 

One of his most significant early contributions was a paper titled "Theory of Analytical Transformations," which was printed in the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* in 1839. The publication of this paper led to the establishment of a friendship between Boole and the journal's editor, D.F. Gregory, a relationship that continued until Gregory's premature death in 1844.

## Context within Mathematical Literature
The journals existed alongside other scientific and mathematical publications of the era. While the *Cambridge* journals focused on mathematical topics, other researchers of the time published their memoirs and papers in a variety of other venues, including:
* The *Philosophical Magazine* (specifically the third and fourth series).
* The *Philosophical Transactions* of the Royal Society.
* The *Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh*.
* The *Transactions of the Royal Irish Academy*.
* *Crelle's Journal*.
* The *Bulletin de l'Academie de St-Petersbourg*.
* The *Mechanic's Magazine*.

The *Catalogue of Scientific Memoirs*, published by the Royal Society, and a supplementary volume on *Differential Equations* edited by Isaac Todhunter, also served as records for detached papers and memoirs on logical and mathematical subjects.
