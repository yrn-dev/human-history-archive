# Machines for Blocking Designs upon Book-Covers

The machines used for blocking designs upon book-covers (fig. 14) have remained practically the same for many years, though several small improvements have been implemented. These enhancements primarily concern the heating of blocks used for gold work and the inking of rollers used for colour work. Due to the size of many modern blocks, the blocking press is now a large and cumbersome piece of equipment.

## Mechanical Operation
The blocking press consists of a strong metal bed where the block is firmly fixed. In front of this bed is a movable table equipped with gauges designed to keep the book cover exactly in the correct position. 

The process varies depending on whether the design is rendered in gold or colour:

*   **Gold Work:** The block is maintained at a specific temperature using gas jets. The cover, which has been overlaid with gold leaf, is passed on the table directly under the block. It is then pressed steadily upwards against the block, lowered, and drawn out. Finally, any superfluous gold is rubbed off.
*   **Colour Work:** The process is similar to gold blocking, but the block does not require heating. Instead, it is inked by a roller for each impression. Because a separate printing is required for every individual colour, these operations demand great care from the operator. The operator must closely monitor each pull and make immediate readjustments as needed, making it difficult to estimate the rate at which these printings can be completed.

## Technical Requirements and Precision
Precision is critical in the blocking process, particularly regarding heat and pressure. In gold blocking, the temperature of the block must be carefully managed; if the block is too hot, gold will adhere to areas where it is not wanted, and if it is too cool, it will fail to adhere where required. 

Furthermore, the operator must exercise "great nicety" regarding the exact amount of pressure applied and the precise number of moments the block remains in contact with the gold. The gold leaf is fastened to the leather or cloth through the use of egg albumen, which solidifies by heat.

## Industry and Application
Blocking presses are primarily of German manufacture, although English and Scottish presses are also used extensively. While large metal blocks or dies have been successfully used for decorating covers measuring approximately 8 by 11-1/2 inches, the use of such machinery varies by the type of binder. 

Larger trade binders utilize these machines extensively and advantageously. However, exclusively high-class trade binders do not yet materially depart from older, manual methods. Private binders generally have no reason to employ these machines.

## Context within Machine-Binding
The blocking press is part of a broader development in technical processes that has allowed work previously done laboriously by hand to be executed by ingenious machinery. This includes the folding of printed sheets, sewing them on bands, rounding the backs, making outer cases, and covering them with leather or cloth. 

Most modern bookbinding machines are driven by power and must be accurately set to a particular book size. Consequently, they are not suitable for binding books of varying sizes; their full advantage is realized only when producing a large edition of a single book.
