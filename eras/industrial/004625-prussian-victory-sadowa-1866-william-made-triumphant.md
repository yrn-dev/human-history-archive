# The Prussian Victory at Sadowa (1866)

The Battle of Sadowa, also known as the Battle of Königgrätz, was the decisive engagement of the Austro-Prussian War in 1866. The conflict saw Prussia and Italy attack Austria, which was supported by an alliance of south German states.

## The Battle and Immediate Outcome
The battle took place on July 3, 1866, near the town of Königgrätz (Czech: *Hradec Králové*), an episcopal see in Bohemia. The Prussian army crushed the Austrian army so severely that Austria was forced into an abject surrender. Following this victory, King William I of Prussia made a triumphant and complimentary entry into Breslau.

In the aftermath of the conflict, Prussia expanded its territorial and political influence. The city of Cassel was occupied by the Prussians in 1866 and subsequently became the capital of the newly formed Prussian province of Hesse-Nassau in 1867. Additionally, the region of Hanover passed to Prussia in 1866, including the town of Duderstadt.

## Political Repercussions in Germany
The victory at Sadowa fundamentally altered the political landscape of the German states. Prussia became the head of the North German Confederation. This new entity excluded Austria, as well as the states of Baden, Hesse, Württemberg, and Bavaria.

Bavaria, under King Louis, had sided with Austria during the war. Following the peace concluded on August 22, 1866, Bavaria was forced to surrender small portions of its territory and agree to the foundation of the North German Confederation under Prussian leadership. This defeat highlighted the necessity for Bavarian army reforms, which were carried out under the Liberal ministry of Hohenlohe and Minister of War Prauckh between December 1866 and February 1870.

## Impact on the Austrian Empire
The defeat of "old Austria" by Prussia at Sadowa served as a starting point for a new era of Austro-Hungarian history. The natural political map of Europe insisted upon the unity of German-speaking peoples, and this recognition manifested in the restructuring of the Austrian Empire. 

Hungary, which had previously been subordinated to Austria, was elevated to a kingdom on an equal footing with Austria. This transformation established the Dual Monarchy of Austria-Hungary in 1867.

## Honors and Later Developments
To commemorate the military achievements of 1866, a special grand cross of the *Order for Merit* (*Ordre pour le Mérite*) was instituted for Prince Frederick Charles and the crown prince, who later became Frederick III.

The geopolitical shifts initiated at Sadowa paved the way for further unification. In 1871, following a war with Napoleon III of France, the King of Prussia, William I, became the "German Emperor."
