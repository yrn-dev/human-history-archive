# Edwin Booth

Edwin Booth was a prominent American actor who achieved an unassailable position on the stage following the death of his father. He was described as having a lithe and graceful figure, buoyant spirits, luminous and expressive eyes, and dark hair that fell in waving curls across his brow.

## Early Career and Development
Booth made his first regular appearance on September 10, 1849, at the Boston Museum. He performed as Tressel in Colley Cibber's version of *Richard III*, acting alongside his father, who played the title role. While his father watched his progress with great interest, he also showed evident disappointment. Similarly, other members of the theatrical profession, who held the elder Booth's acting in great reverence, generally believed that the son had not inherited his father's genius.

On September 27, 1850, Booth made his first appearance in New York at the National theatre in Chatham Street, playing the character of Wilford in *The Iron Chest*. A year later, due to his father's illness, he stepped into the role of Richard III.

## International Success and Triumphs
Between 1852 and 1856, Booth traveled and performed in Australia, California, and the Sandwich Islands. His brilliant acting during this period captivated his audiences, which surprised those who had known him previously in the east. Following these performances, his dramatic triumphs were warmly acknowledged.

Booth's interpretations of several major roles were highly praised. His performances as Richelieu, Richard, and Hamlet were pronounced to be superior to those of Edwin Forrest. Additionally, his success in the role of Sir Giles Overreach in *A New Way to Pay Old Debts* was noted to have surpassed that of his father.

## Management and Later Career
In 1862, Booth became the manager of the Winter Garden theatre in New York. Between 1864 and 1867, he presented a series of Shakespearian productions characterized by unexampled magnificence, which included *The Merchant of Venice*, *Othello*, and *Hamlet*.

The height of this career period was interrupted for many months in 1865 when his brother, John Wilkes Booth, assassinated President Lincoln. Prior to this event, in the autumn of the previous year, the three Booth brothers—Edwin, John Wilkes (1839–1865), and Junius Brutus (1821–1853)—had performed together in *Julius Caesar*. This specific production was remembered both for its excellence and for the subsequent tragedies that befell two of the principal performers.

## Professional Relations
Booth's career intersected with other notable actors of the era. Lawrence Barrett, an American actor born in New Jersey, performed as Cassius to Booth's Brutus and as Othello to Booth's Iago. Barrett later wrote a sketch of Booth titled *Edwin Booth and his Contemporaries*, published in Boston in 1886.
