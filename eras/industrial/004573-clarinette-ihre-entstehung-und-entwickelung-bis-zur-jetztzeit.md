# Clarinette, ihre Entstehung und Entwickelung bis zur Jetztzeit

The study of the clarinet, specifically its origin and development up to the modern era in acoustic, technical, and musical terms, is documented across several specialized historical and instructional texts.

## Technical and Instructional Literature
Significant contributions to the understanding of the clarinet and its related instruments have been made through instructional manuals and treatises. J. G. H. Backofen published *Anweisung zur Klarinette, nebst einer kurzen Abhandlung ueber das Basset-Horn* in 1803 (Leipzig, Breitkopf & Haertel), which included illustrations. Later, in 1826, Iwan Mueller provided further technical guidance in *Anweisung zu der neuen Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto, nebst einigen Bemerkungen fuer Instrumentenmacher*, also published in Leipzig by Freidrich Hofmeister and featuring illustrations. Additionally, Gottfried Weber wrote on the clarinet and basset horn in *Cacilia*, Band xi, published in Mainz in 1834.

## Historical Development and Analysis
A comprehensive examination of the instrument's evolution is found in the work of Wilhelm Altenburg, titled *Die Clarinette, ihre Entstehung und Entwickelung bis zur Jetztzeit in akustischer, technischer u. musikalischer Beziehung*, published in Heilbronn in 1904. This work specifically addresses the instrument's development from an acoustic, technical, and musical perspective.

## Related Instruments and Documentation
The basset horn is frequently associated with the development of the clarinet. Visual records of early basset horns are preserved in the *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments at the Royal Military Exhibition, London* (1890), compiled by Capt. C. R. Day and published in 1891. Furthermore, the collection of the Rev. F. W. Galpin of Hatfield, Broad Oak, contains an instrument of this type stamped "H. Grenser, S. Wiesner, Dresden."

## Chronology and General History
General histories of instrumentation provide varying perspectives on the timeline of the instrument. Henri Lavoix, in *Histoire de l'instrumentation depuis le seizieme siecle jusqu'a nos jours* (Paris, 1878), cites the date 1777. Other references regarding the construction and capabilities of musical instruments are noted in the *Cantor Lectures on Musical Instruments* by A. J. Hipkins.
