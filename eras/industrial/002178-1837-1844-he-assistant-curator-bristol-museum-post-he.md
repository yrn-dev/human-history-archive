# William Hellier Baily

## Early Life and Education
William Hellier Baily was an English palaeontologist born in Bristol on July 7, 1819. He was the nephew of the sculptor E. H. Baily.

## Professional Career in Museums and Surveys
Baily began his professional career in his hometown, serving as the Assistant Curator in the Bristol Museum from 1837 to 1844. He eventually relinquished this position to join the staff of the Geological Survey located in London. His career progressed in 1854 when he was appointed as an assistant naturalist, serving first under Edward Forbes and subsequently under Huxley.

In 1857, Baily was transferred to the Irish branch of the Geological Survey. He took on the role of acting palaeontologist, a post he held for the remainder of his life.

## Publications and Scientific Contributions
Baily contributed extensively to the field of palaeontology through various writings. He authored numerous papers on palaeontological subjects and provided notes on fossils for the explanatory memoirs of the Geological Survey of Ireland. 

Between 1867 and 1875, he published a work titled *Figures of Characteristic British Fossils, with Descriptive Remarks*. Only the first volume of this work was issued, and it focused on palaeozoic species. Baily personally drew all the figures for this publication on stone.

## Death
William Hellier Baily died on August 6, 1888, at Rathmines, which is located near Dublin.
