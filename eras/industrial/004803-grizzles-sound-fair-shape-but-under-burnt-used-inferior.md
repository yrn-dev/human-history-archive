# Grizzles

In the classification of bricks used throughout Great Britain, "grizzles" refer to a specific quality of brick characterized by being sound and possessing a fair shape. Despite these attributes, they are categorized as under-burnt. Due to this state of burning, grizzles are utilized for inferior work and are specifically employed in construction cases where the bricks are not liable to be heavily loaded.

## Comparison with Other Brick Qualities

The terminology for bricks in Great Britain is noted as being most confusing, with various names assigned to different qualities. Grizzles exist within a spectrum of burnt and shaped materials:

*   **Sound and Well-Burnt:** "Stocks" are hard, sound, and well-burnt bricks used for all ordinary purposes. "Shippers" are also sound and hard-burnt, though they possess an imperfect shape and were frequently used as ballast for ships.
*   **Over-Burnt:** "Hard Stocks" are sound but over-burnt; these are used in positions where a good appearance is not required, such as the footings to walls.
*   **Under-Burnt and Defective:** "Place-bricks" are under-burnt and defective, used for temporary work. "Chuffs" are badly burnt, cracked, and defective in shape.
*   **Specialized Bricks:** Other varieties include "Rubbers" or "Cutters," which are sandy and suitable for rubbing to shape or cutting with a wire saw. "Pressed bricks" are moulded under hydraulic pressure for facing work and typically feature a deep frog or hollow on one or both horizontal faces to reduce weight and provide a key for mortar.

## Specialized Engineering and Decorative Bricks

Beyond the standard categories like grizzles and stocks, there are bricks designed for specific industrial or aesthetic functions:

*   **Blue Bricks:** Chiefly produced in North Wales and South Staffordshire, these are vitrified throughout, heavy, hard, and impervious. Because they are very durable and offer great compressional resistance, they are used in engineering work. Special shapes are available for coping, channelling, and paving.
*   **Fire-bricks:** These are used in connection with furnaces because they can withstand great heat. They are laid with fire-clay instead of cement mortar or lime.
*   **Glazed Bricks:** These may be enamelled (dipped in a slip of color before burning) or salt-glazed (brown in color, created by throwing salt on the bricks in the kiln). They are used for sanitary and decorative purposes, as well as where reflected light is needed.
*   **Moulded Bricks:** Used for labels, plinths, string courses, and cornices. These are often preferred over rubbers because they save the labor of cutting and offer greater durability. For arches and sewer work, bricks shaped as voussoirs are supplied.

## History of Brick Manufacture

The first brick buildings in America were erected in 1633 on Manhattan Island by a governor of the Dutch West India Company. These initial bricks were imported from England and Holland, where the industry had already achieved great excellence. While burnt bricks began to be produced domestically in America around 1650 at New Haven, the home-made bricks remained inferior to European imports for many years.

Historically, the Germans and Dutch were the primary brickmakers of Europe during the middle ages. Between the 14th and 15th centuries, the Italians developed the art of terra-cotta (decorative brick-work) and discovered how to apply coloured enamels to these materials. This was exemplified in the 15th century by the work of the Della Robbias.
