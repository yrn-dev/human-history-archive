# Mansel and Liddon: Bampton Lecturers of 1858 and 1866

The academic and theological contributions of H. L. Mansel in 1858 and H. P. Liddon in 1866 are situated within the history of the Bampton lectures. These lectures are a series of annual addresses delivered at the University Church in Oxford, established through the will of John Bampton.

## The Bampton Lectures
John Bampton (c. 1690–1751) was an English divine and a member of Trinity College, Oxford, who served as a canon of Salisbury. Upon his death on June 2, 1751, his will provided for the establishment of eight annual lectures to be delivered on Sunday mornings during the full term. The timing of these lectures is specified to occur between the commencement of the last month in Lent term and the end of the third week in Act term.

The purpose of the lectures is to address specific theological subjects, which include:
* Confirming and establishing the Christian faith and confuting schismatics and heretics.
* The divine authority of the Holy Scriptures.
* The authority of the writings of the primitive fathers regarding the faith and practice of the primitive Church.
* The divinity of the Holy Ghost.
* The divinity of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.
* The articles of the Christian faith as they are contained in the Nicene and Apostles' Creeds.

## Lecturer Requirements and Administration
To be eligible for the position of lecturer, a candidate must hold at least a Master of Arts degree from either Cambridge or Oxford. Historically, the lecturer was chosen annually on the fourth Tuesday in Easter term by the heads of colleges. A strict rule prohibits any individual from being chosen for the role a second time.

The endowment for the position provides £120 for each lecturer. Furthermore, the terms of the endowment require that the lectures be published within two months after they have been delivered. While the series began in 1780, the frequency of elections changed after 1895 to alternate years due to a depreciation of the fund's revenue.

## Mansel and Liddon
Within the history of these lectures, H. L. Mansel served as the lecturer in 1858. His contribution to the series was titled *The Limits of Religious Thought*. 

H. P. Liddon served as the lecturer in 1866. His work, titled *The Divinity of our Lord*, aligned with the Bampton mandate to address the divinity of Jesus Christ.

Both men are part of a broader lineage of lecturers that includes other figures such as Heber (1815), R. Whately (1822), R. D. Hampden (1832), E. M. Goulburn (1850), E. Hatch (1880), C. Bigg (1886), C. Gore (1891), W. Sanday (1893), J. R. Illingworth (1894), and W. R. Inge (1899).
