# Prof. H.T. Eddy

Prof. H.T. Eddy is an engineer who published a method for investigating the maximum shear at a section resulting from any distribution of a travelling load. This work was detailed in the *Transactions of the American Society of Civil Engineers* (Trans. Am. Soc. C.E.) volume xxii, published in 1890.

## Eddy's Method of Shear Investigation

The method developed by Prof. Eddy utilizes a graphical approach to determine shear on a girder. In this system, a girder AB with a span of length $l$ is analyzed with a load $W$ positioned at a distance $x$ from the left abutment. This load is represented in magnitude and position by $hk$ (as seen in fig. 56).

To execute the method, horizontal lines $kf$ and $hg$ are laid off, both equal to the span $l$. The points $f$ and $g$ are then joined to $h$ and $k$. Vertical lines are drawn at points A and B, and these are joined by a line $no$. The line $no$ is horizontal and equal to $l$, and is referred to as the datum line of shear.

## Calculation of Reactions and Shear

The method allows for the determination of reactions at the abutments, which correspond to the shear at specific points of the girder:

*   **Reaction at A:** The ratio $mn/mf$ is equal to $hk/kf$. This results in the formula $mn = W(l-x)/l$, which represents the reaction at A due to the load at C and the shear at any point of AC.
*   **Reaction at B:** Similarly, the line $po$ represents the reaction at B and the shear at any point of CB.

The distribution of shear due to the load at C is represented by shaded rectangles.

## Analysis of Moving Loads

The method can be adapted to account for the movement of the load. For example, if the load moves to a position D such that its distance from the left abutment becomes $x+a$, a vertical line is drawn at D. This line intersects $fh$ and $kg$ at points $s$ and $q$, respectively.

Under these new conditions:
*   The ratio $qr/ro$ equals $hk/hg$.
*   The reaction at A (and the shear at any point of AD) is $ro = W(l-x-a)/l$.
*   The shear on DB is $rs = W(x+a)/l$.

The distribution of shear for this new position is indicated by partially shaded rectangles. For the application of this specific method to a series of loads, the archive directs the reader to Prof. Eddy's original paper.
