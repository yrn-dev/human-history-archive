# William Ballantine

## Early Life and Education
William Ballantine was born in London on the 3rd of January 1812. He was the son of a London police-magistrate. His early education took place at St Paul's school. During the early stages of his life, Ballantine developed a varied acquaintance with literary and dramatic society.

## Legal Career
Ballantine was called to the bar in 1834. He utilized his acute intellect and a "pushing character," combined with his early social experiences, to quickly establish a large legal practice. He became particularly noted for his work in criminal cases and earned a reputation as a formidable cross-examiner. In this professional capacity, his great rival was Serjeant Parry (1816-1880). In 1856, Ballantine achieved the rank of serjeant-at-law.

## Notable Cases
The career of Serjeant Ballantine was marked by three significant cases:
* **The Prosecution of Franz Mueller (1864):** Ballantine successfully prosecuted Mueller for murder.
* **The Tichborne Claimant (1871):** Ballantine provided a skilful defence for the claimant.
* **The Gaekwar of Baroda (1875):** Ballantine defended the gaekwar in a case for which his fee was one of the largest ever known.

## Later Life and Death
Outside of his professional achievements, Ballantine's private life is described as decidedly Bohemian. Although he earned large sums of money throughout his career, he died very poor. Before his death, he published more than one volume of reminiscences. William Ballantine died at Margate on the 9th of January 1887.
