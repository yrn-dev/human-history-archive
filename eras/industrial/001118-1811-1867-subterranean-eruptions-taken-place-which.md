# Subterranean Eruptions of 1811 and 1867

In the history of volcanic activity, subterranean eruptions have occurred on various occasions, specifically noted in the years 1811 and 1867. These events are characterized by their occurrence beneath the surface and have, in some instances, resulted in the emergence of temporary islands.

## Volcanic Activity in the Archipelago
The volcanic nature of the archipelago—which consists of islands with undulating outlines, high and precipitous sea-coasts, and peaks ranging from 350 ft. at Corvo to 7,612 ft. at Pico—is evidenced by numerous eruptions and earthquakes since its discovery. The primary materials erupted in this region are scoria and basalt.

While certain islands such as Corvo, Flores, and Graciosa have remained exempt from such activity, and St Mary has escaped entirely, other islands have experienced significant volcanic events. Fayal suffered one eruption in 1672, and Pico experienced an eruption in 1572. St George was the site of numerous outbursts in 1580, and a town in Terceira was destroyed in 1614.

## The Role of St Michael's
The island of St Michael's has served as the primary center of volcanic activity. Its history includes a great eruption in 1444-1445, though preserved accounts may exaggerate its importance. A violent convulsion in 1522 buried the town of Villa Franca, which was the capital of the island at the time, along with its 6,000 inhabitants. St Michael's was further visited by successive earthquakes and eruptions of varying violence in 1630, 1652, 1656, 1755, and 1852.

## Subterranean Eruptions and Temporary Islands
Subterranean eruptions have occurred on several occasions, including 1638, 1720, 1811, and 1867. These specific types of eruptions have sometimes been accompanied by the appearance of temporary islands.

The most remarkable of these temporary landmasses was created in June 1811. This island was thrown up approximately half a league from the western extremity of St Michael's. It was named Sabrina by the commander of the British man-of-war Sabrina, who witnessed the phenomenon.
