# Antoine Louis Barye

Antoine Louis Barye (1796–1875) was a prominent French sculptor recognized as the creator of a new class of art and the greatest artist of animal life within the French school. His work influenced subsequent artists such as Gardet, Cain, Peter, and Fremiet, who are considered his most worthy followers.

## Early Life and Education
Born in Paris on September 24, 1796, Barye began his professional life as a goldsmith, a path similar to many sculptors of the Renaissance. His formal artistic education included studies under the painter Gros and the sculptor Bosio. In 1818, he was admitted to the Ecole des Beaux Arts.

## Artistic Development
Barye's specific artistic direction became clear in 1823 while he was employed by the goldsmith Fauconnier. During this period, he began observing wild beasts at the Jardin des Plantes. He produced vigorous pencil drawings of these animals—works compared to those of Delacroix—which he then translated into sculptures on both small and large scales.

He began exhibiting his animal studies in the early 1830s, showcasing "Tiger devouring a Crocodile" in 1831 and "Lion and Snake" in 1832, the latter of which demonstrated that he had mastered his own distinct style. Despite a perpetual struggle with financial want, Barye continued to exhibit animal groups that blended a spirit of true romance with a feeling for the beauty of the antique.

## Major Works
Barye's portfolio included both large-scale public monuments and smaller, highly valued works.

### Large-Scale Sculpture
His significant larger works include:
*   The Lion of the Column of July (plaster model cast in 1839).
*   Various tigers and lions located in the gardens of the Tuileries.
*   Four groups created in 1854: Order, Strength, Peace, and War.

### Small-Scale and Romantic Works
Barye excelled at representing animals in familiar attitudes and creating dramatic scenes. Notable examples include:
*   "Jaguar devouring a Hare" (cast in bronze in 1852).
*   "Lapitha and Centaur" (1848).
*   "Theseus and the Minotaur" (1847).

## Professional Recognition and Death
Recognition for Barye's contributions came late in his career. In 1854, he was appointed as a professor at the museum, and in 1868, he was elected to the Academy of Fine Arts. He died on June 25, 1875.
