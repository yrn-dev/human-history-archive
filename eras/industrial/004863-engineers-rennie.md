# Rennie

The name Rennie is associated with several figures in the fields of engineering, architecture, and scholarship, most notably the family of engineers responsible for significant infrastructure projects in Great Britain.

## John Rennie the Elder
John Rennie was a celebrated engineer whose work spanned the late 18th and early 19th centuries. His projects included both masonry and cast iron structures. In 1803, he constructed a five-arch bridge crossing the Tweed at Kelso. Between 1814 and 1819, he designed and erected the Southwark bridge over the Thames, which featured cast iron ribs, a rise of 24 ft., and a centre span of 240 ft.

Rennie the elder also provided the design for the present London Bridge. Begun in 1824 and completed in 1831, this masonry arch structure is characterized by a slight curvature of the roadway, semi-elliptical arches, and bold architectural details. The bridge has a total length of 1005 ft. and a width of 56 ft. from outside to outside. Its centre arch spans 152 ft. and rises 29 ft. 6 in. above the Trinity high-water mark; the side arches span 140 ft., and the abutment arches span 130 ft. The foundations were laid approximately 29 ft. 6 in. below low water. The construction utilized granite for the exterior stones and the voussoirs of the centre arch, while the interior stones were sourced from Painshaw, Derbyshire, and Bramley Fall. The total cost of the project was £1,458,311, though the contractor's tender for the bridge itself was £425,081.

## Sir John Rennie and Other Family Members
Sir John Rennie, the son of John Rennie the elder, served as the acting engineer for the construction of the present London Bridge. Other members of the Rennie family contributed to various fields of study and improvement; G. B. Rennie authored a work in 1856 titled *Suggestions for the Improvement of the Danube*.

## Professional Influence and Associations
The influence of John Rennie extended to other engineers of the era. Henry Bell, a Scottish engineer born in 1767, found employment under Rennie in London before returning to Scotland in 1790. Bell later became known for placing the steamboat "Comet" on the Clyde in January 1812.

In the field of maritime safety, John Rennie was associated as a consulting engineer with Robert Stevenson for the construction of a tower on the Bell (or Inchcape) Rock, a sandstone reef in the North Sea. Although Rennie was involved in a consulting capacity, the design and details of the 100 ft. high tower—which was completed at a cost of £61,300—were entirely the work of Stevenson.
