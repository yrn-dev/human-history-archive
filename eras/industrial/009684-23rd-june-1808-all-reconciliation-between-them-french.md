# Joseph Bonaparte and the Spanish Crown

## Accession and the Proclamation of Bayonne
In May 1808, Napoleon called upon Joseph to take the crown of Spain. Upon his appointment, Joseph issued a proclamation dated June 23, 1808, at Bayonne. Although this proclamation announced benevolent intentions toward the Spaniards, reconciliation between the Spanish people and the French proved impossible. This failure was attributed to the treatment Napoleon had dealt to Ferdinand VII, the *de facto* king of Spain.

## Challenges of Sovereignty
Joseph's reign was characterized by significant difficulties, and his sovereignty remained largely titular. His authority was severely limited, and he was described by his trusted adviser, Miot de Melito, as being unable to function as a constitutional king. Instead, following the events of 1808 and 1809, Joseph could only succeed in the Peninsula by acting as "the mere instrument of a military power."

The instability of his position was evidenced by several key events:
* **The Flight from Madrid:** In August 1808, Joseph was compelled to leave Madrid hastily following a Spanish success at Baylen.
* **Reinstatement:** Napoleon reinstated him at the close of 1808, though Joseph was kept in a subordinate position.
* **Attempts to Abdicate:** Due to his diminished role, Joseph offered to abdicate on four separate occasions. Napoleon ignored these offers and commanded him to govern with more energy.

## Erosion of Authority and Financial Strain
Between February and May 1810, Napoleon further undermined Joseph's power by placing the north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals. These areas were organized as military districts that were virtually independent of Joseph's authority. Despite Joseph's protests, these changes remained in place.

By the time he seriously considered departing, Joseph's treasury was exhausted, leaving him in a state of extreme financial distress. In April 1811, he traveled to Paris to either offer his abdication or extort better terms. He returned to Spain with a promise that the army of the centre—the smallest of the five French armies—would be under his control, along with a monthly subsidy of 500,000 francs.

## Final Years in Spain
In late 1811, Napoleon united Catalonia to France. Joseph's tenure as the ruler of the capital ended on July 22, 1812, when a victory by Wellington at Salamanca compelled him to leave.
