# Duchy of Lancaster

The Duchy of Lancaster is an English entity with origins dating back to the 14th century. Its history is characterized by its association with the English monarchy and various high-ranking nobles, as well as its role in the administration of specific lands and political offices.

## Origins and Early History
The title of duke was unknown in England until the 14th century. While the title "ealdorman" (later "earl") was sometimes rendered in Latin as *dux* during Saxon times, and English kings until the time of John styled themselves dukes of Normandy (and later dukes of Aquitaine), the first formal English duke was Edward the Black Prince, for whom King Edward III erected the county of Cornwall into a duchy in 1337.

The second person to be created a duke in England was Henry, who held the earldoms of Lancaster, Derby, Lincoln, and Leicester. He was created duke of Lancaster in 1351.

## Landholdings and Estates
The Duchy of Lancaster encompassed various manors and lordships over the centuries. In the case of Belper (also known as Beaurepaire), the manor was held by Edmund Crouchback, earl of Lancaster, by 1296. Crouchback is credited with building a hunting seat and enclosing a park there, giving the site the name Beaurepaire. Consequently, the manor became part of the duchy of Lancaster and is said to have served as a residence for John of Gaunt.

Similarly, the lordship and castle of Knaresborough eventually became part of the Crown as parcel of the duchy of Lancaster. This occurred after the property had passed through several owners during the 13th and 14th centuries, including Hubert de Burgh, Richard, earl of Cornwall, and Piers Gaveston, before finally being granted to John of Gaunt, duke of Lancaster.

## The Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster
The office of chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster has been held by various political figures throughout English history. Notable individuals who served in this capacity include:

*   **Robert Hobart**: The 4th earl of Buckinghamshire, who previously served as secretary for war and the colonies, became chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster under Pitt in 1805.
*   **Matthew Talbot Baines**: The son of Edward Baines and former president of the Poor Law Board, he entered the cabinet as chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster in 1856.
*   **The Earl of Dufferin**: Created earl of Dufferin in 1871, he served as chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster from 1868 to 1872 under Mr. Gladstone, though he held this position outside the cabinet.
