# Independent Values and Ranges in Geodetic and Scientific Measurement

The concept of independent values falling within a specific range is utilized across various scientific disciplines to determine the probability of error, the behavior of mathematical functions, and the characteristics of biological and physical systems.

## Geodetic Measurements and Error Probability
In the field of geodetic surveys, independent values are used to assess the accuracy of mean values. For example, measurements taken in 1875 via Omsk and Ekaterinburg (2 35 52.151), in 1891 via Orenburg and Saratov (2 35 52.228), and in 1895 via Baku and Kiev (2 35 51.997) provided three independent values. Because these values all fall within a range of 0^S.25, it is considered improbable that the mean value possesses an error as large as 0^S.10.

Such precision is part of broader exact surveys based on triangulation. In Russia, these surveys extend east to Chinese Turkestan (approximately 75 deg. E. of Greenwich), while in India, geodetic triangulation extends to the eastern boundaries of Burma (approximately 100 deg. E.). By the close of the 19th century, the final links of the geodetic triangulation of the Indian peninsula were forged, though connection with European systems remained to be completed. Additionally, the telegraphic method for determining longitudes differentially with Greenwich was refined after 1890, resulting in a correction of about 2" of arc to previous determinations made through Suez via four arcs: Greenwich–Potsdam, Potsdam–Teheran, Teheran–Bushire, and Bushire–Karachi.

## Mathematical Functions and Variables
In mathematics, independent and dependent variables may be expressed as single valued functions of a single variable, $\tau$. For instance, the differential equation $d^2y/dx^2 + [(\gamma - (\alpha + \beta + 1)x)/x(1 - x)]dy/dx - [\alpha\beta\gamma]y = 0$ can be solved by expressing both variables as single valued functions of $\tau$, where the independent variable is $x = \lambda(\tau)$.

Jacobi expressed functions such as $\lambda^{1/4}$, $(1 - \lambda)^{1/4}$, and $[\lambda(1 - \lambda)]^{1/4}$—which have singular points only at $\lambda = 0, 1, \infty$—as power series in $q = e^{i\pi\tau}$. Consequently, for a limited range of values of $\tau$, these are single valued functions of $\tau$.

## Biological and Environmental Ranges
In biology, living matter is characterized by three properties: it proceeds from pre-existing living matter, it possesses the power of propagating itself via offshoots, and it eventually ceases to live and resolves into highly oxidated compounds. Living matter is further distinguished by its dependence on moisture and heat, specifically requiring heat within a limited range of temperature.

Environmental ranges also define the habitability and characteristics of specific regions. In Barotseland, the healthy zone includes the Mashikolumbwe and Batoka plateaus (with extreme altitudes of 4150 and 4400 ft. respectively) and the Congo-Zambezi watershed, which reaches 5000 ft. in many places. The region's temperature shows a mean annual range of 68deg to 70deg F, with an extreme range between 100deg and 35deg F. Rainfall in the area is stable, varying by district from 22 to 32 inches per year.

## Physical and Artistic Ranges
Physical ranges are observed in maritime and artistic contexts. In the Baltic region, tides at Copenhagen are about one foot, while ordinary tides within the Baltic proper are scarcely perceptible. However, meteorological influences cause an annual rise and fall with a mean range of 13.9 cm. (0.46 ft.) at Swinemuende and 11.4 cm. (0.37 ft.) at Travemuende, peaking in August.

In the study of painting, specifically the "spectral palette" of pointillisme, colors are resolved into six bands of the rainbow. This method relies on the Helmholtz hypothesis of three primaries. However, the use of pigments limits the palette considerably in its upper range, and the exclusion of black causes the lower ranges to suffer.
