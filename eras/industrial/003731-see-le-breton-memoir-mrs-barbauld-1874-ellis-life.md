# Anna Letitia Barbauld

Anna Letitia Barbauld (1743–1825) was an English miscellaneous writer and poet. Born on June 20, 1743, in Kibworth-Harcourt, Leicestershire, she was the daughter of the Rev. John Aikin. A schoolmaster and Presbyterian minister, Mr. Aikin provided his daughter with an education that included the study of Greek and Latin. In 1758, the family relocated to Warrington, where Mr. Aikin served as a theological tutor within a dissenting academy.

## Literary Career and Early Works

Miss Aikin first achieved significant success in 1773 with the publication of a volume of *Poems*. During this period, she also collaborated with her brother, Dr. John Aikin, to produce a volume titled *Miscellaneous Pieces in Prose*. 

Following her marriage in 1774 to Rochemont Barbauld—a minister of a Presbyterian church at Palgrave, Suffolk, and a former student of the Warrington academy—she assisted her husband in establishing a boarding school. For the pupils of this institution, she authored *Early Lessons* and *Hymns in Prose*.

## Later Life and London Literary Circles

The Barbaulds left England for the continent in 1785 due to the seriously impaired health of Mr. Barbauld. Upon their return approximately two years later, Mr. Barbauld was appointed to a church in Hampstead. The couple eventually moved to Stoke Newington in 1802.

During her time in London, Mrs. Barbauld became a well-known figure in literary circles and engaged in various editorial and collaborative projects:
* **Collaborations:** She worked with Dr. Aikin on *Evenings at Home*.
* **Editorial Work:** In 1795, she published a critical essay and edition of Akenside's *Pleasures of Imagination*, followed by an edition of Collins's *Odes* in 1797.
* **Collections:** In 1804, she published a selection of correspondence from Samuel Richardson (including a biographical notice) and a selection of papers from the English Essayists. In 1810, she produced a 50-volume collection of *British Novelists*, which featured critical and biographical notices.

## Major Poetry and Death

In 1811, Barbauld published her longest poem, *Eighteen Hundred and Eleven*. The work presented a gloomy perspective on the future prospects and existing state of Britain. The poem is noted for anticipating Macaulay by imagining a future visitor from the antipodes observing the ruins of St Paul's from a broken arch of Blackfriars Bridge.

Anna Letitia Barbauld died on March 9, 1825. Her husband had previously passed away in 1808. In 1825, her niece, Lucy Aikin, published a two-volume collected edition of her works, which included a memoir.
