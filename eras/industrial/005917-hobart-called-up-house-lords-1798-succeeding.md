# Robert Hobart, 4th Earl of Buckinghamshire

Robert Hobart (1760–1816) was a British soldier and statesman who served in various high-ranking administrative and political roles across England, Ireland, and India. Born on May 6, 1760, he was the eldest son of the 3rd Earl of Buckinghamshire.

## Early Career and Irish Administration
Before ascending to the peerage, Hobart served as a soldier and held seats in both the Irish and English Houses of Commons. Between 1789 and 1793, he held the position of chief secretary to the lord-lieutenant of Ireland. During this tenure, he utilized his influence within the country to oppose and prevent any concessions being made to the Roman Catholics.

## Governorship of Madras
In 1793, while known by the courtesy title of Lord Hobart, he was appointed as the governor of Madras. His tenure there ended in 1798 when he was recalled following serious differences with Sir John Shore (later Lord Teignmouth), the governor-general of India.

## Political Career in England
Upon his return to British politics in 1798, Hobart was called up to the House of Lords. He later succeeded to the earldom of Buckinghamshire in 1804. His political activities included favoring the union between Ireland and England. 

Hobart held several significant government offices:
* **Secretary for War and the Colonies:** He served in this capacity from March 1801 to May 1804. His family name was used for the naming of Hobart Town in Tasmania.
* **Chancellor of the Duchy of Lancaster:** He assumed this role in 1805 under Pitt.
* **Joint Postmaster-General:** He held this position for a short duration.
* **President of the Board of Control:** He served in this role from 1812 until his death on February 4, 1816, a position for which his previous experience in India had prepared him.

## Lineage and Succession
The 4th Earl did not leave any sons. Consequently, his titles passed to his nephew, George Robert Hobart (1789–1849), who was the son of George Vere Hobart (1761–1802), the lieutenant-governor of Grenada. In 1824, George Robert Hobart (the 5th Earl) inherited the Buckinghamshire estates of the Hampden family and adopted the name Hampden. This inheritance stemmed from a marriage around 1655 between Mary Hampden and the 3rd baronet, Sir John Hobart.

Following the death of the 5th Earl in February 1849, he was succeeded by his brother, Augustus Edward Hobart (1793–1884), who became the 6th Earl and took the name Hobart-Hampden in 1878. The 6th Earl was survived by his grandson, Sidney Carr Hobart-Hampden (born 1860), who became the 7th Earl of Buckinghamshire and added the name Mercer-Henderson to his own. The 6th Earl's sons, Frederick John Hobart (1821–1875) and Vere Henry, Lord Hobart (1818–1875)—who served as governor of Madras from 1872—both predeceased their father. Another son of the 6th Earl was Augustus Charles Hobart-Hampden, known as Hobart Pasha.

## History of the Earldom
The earldom of Buckinghamshire was first held by John Hobart (c. 1694–1756), a descendant of Sir Henry Hobart (died 1625), who had been chief justice of the common pleas and attorney-general under James I. The Hobart family had been established in Suffolk and Norfolk for many years. John Hobart was created Baron Hobart of Blickling in 1728, became lord-lieutenant of Norfolk in 1740, and was created earl of Buckinghamshire in 1746.

The 1st Earl was succeeded by his eldest son, John (1723–1793), the 2nd Earl, who had previously served as the comptroller of the royal household, a member of parliament for Norwich, and ambassador to Russia (1762–1766). He also served as lord-lieutenant of Ireland from 1776 to 1780. Upon the 2nd Earl's death without sons in 1793, his half-brother George (c. 1730–1804) became the 3rd Earl. The 2nd Earl's daughter, Henrietta (1762–1805), inherited the Norfolk estates and Blickling Hall, which she brought into the Kerr family through her marriage to William Kerr, the 6th marquess of Lothian.
