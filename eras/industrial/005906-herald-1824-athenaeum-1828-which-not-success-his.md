# The Oriental Herald and The Athenaeum

## James Silk Buckingham
James Silk Buckingham (1786–1855) was an English author, traveller, and journalist born near Falmouth on August 25, 1786. The son of a farmer, Buckingham spent his youth at sea and engaged in years of wandering before establishing the *Calcutta Journal* in 1818. While this venture was initially highly successful, the paper's outspoken criticisms of the East India Company led to its suppression by the acting governor-general, John Adam, in 1823. Consequently, Buckingham was expelled from India. Following a case brought before parliament, the East India Company awarded him a pension of £200 a year as compensation.

## Journalistic Ventures in England
Upon returning to England, Buckingham continued his career in journalism. In 1824, he started the *Oriental Herald*. He later founded the *Athenaeum* in 1828, though this particular venture was not a success in his hands. 

Beyond his journalistic work, Buckingham was a voluminous writer who produced many useful books of travel based on his extensive travels in the East, America, and Europe. In 1851, he was granted a civil list pension of £200 a year in recognition of his literary and travel works. Buckingham also entered politics, serving as the member for Sheffield in parliament from 1832 to 1837, where he strongly advocated for social reform. He died in London on June 30, 1855, while working on his autobiography; two of the intended four volumes were published in the year of his death.

## The Athenaeum Periodical
The *Athenaeum* is a title used by several literary periodicals, most notably the London literary weekly founded in 1828. Over time, the publication incorporated other papers, such as *The Literary Chronicle*, to which the author John Doran had contributed journalistic sketches while travelling on the continent. 

The periodical served as a platform for literary and artistic critique. For example, the *Athenaeum* expressed a preference for the actor Ludwig Dessoir's interpretation of the role of Othello over those of Macready or Brooks. John Doran, an English author and former tutor, became a regular contributor to the *Athenaeum* and briefly served as its editor in 1869, succeeding Hepworth Dixon before moving on to edit *Notes and Queries* in 1870.

## Etymology and Historical Context of "Athenaeum"
The name *Athenaeum* (from the Greek *Athaenaion*) was originally used in ancient Greece to describe buildings dedicated to Athena. Specifically, it designated a temple in Athens where men of learning and poets gathered to read their productions. 

The term was later adopted for an academy built by Emperor Hadrian around A.D. 135 in Rome near the Forum. This institution provided regular courses of instruction in philosophy, grammar, jurisprudence, and rhetoric through a staff of professors. Though later called the Schola Romana, it maintained a high reputation until the 5th century. Similar academies were established in Constantinople by Emperor Theodosius II, as well as in various provinces. In modern times, the term has been used for Dutch high schools, academies in Marseilles and Lyons, and as a general designation for literary clubs.
