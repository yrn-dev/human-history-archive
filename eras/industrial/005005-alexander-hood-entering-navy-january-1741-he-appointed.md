# Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport

Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport (1727–1814), was a British admiral and the younger brother of Samuel, Lord Hood. He was also a cousin to Captain Alexander Hood and Sir Samuel Hood.

## Early Naval Career
Hood entered the navy in January 1741. Six years after his entry, he was appointed as a lieutenant of the "Bridgewater." He served in this rank for a period of ten years across various ships. Following this, he was posted to the "Prince," which served as the flagship of Rear-Admiral Saunders, an officer under whom Hood had previously served as a lieutenant. In command of the "Prince," Hood served for some time in the Mediterranean.

Upon returning home, Hood was appointed to the "Minerva" frigate. While serving on this vessel, he was present at Hawke's victory in Quiberon Bay on November 20, 1759. In 1761, the "Minerva" engaged in a long struggle to recapture the "Warwick," a ship of equal force. Later that same year, Hood transitioned to the "Africa," returning to the Mediterranean where he served until peace was concluded.

## Mid-Career and the Battle of Ushant
From the conclusion of that peace, Hood remained in continuous employment, serving both afloat and ashore. In 1778, while serving in the "Robust," he was present at the battle of Ushant. This action was followed by a court-martial of Admiral (later Viscount) Keppel. Hood was involved in the proceedings and took a course in Keppel's defence that aroused adverse popular feeling; however, this conduct did not appear to hinder his professional advancement.

## Rise to Admiral and the Relief of Gibraltar
Two years after the battle of Ushant, Hood was promoted to rear-admiral of the white. He succeeded Kempenfeldt as one of the flag-officers under Howe. In 1782, while in the "Queen" (90), he was present at the relief of Gibraltar. During this period of his life, Hood also served as a member of the House of Commons.

In 1787, he was promoted to vice-admiral, and the following year he became a K.B. During the Spanish armament of 1790, he flew his flag again for a brief period.

## The Revolutionary War and Peerage
With the outbreak of the war with France in 1793, Sir Alexander Hood returned to sea as the second in command under Howe. He participated in the operations that led to the "Glorious First of June." In recognition of these services, he was made Baron Bridport of Cricket St Thomas in Somerset within the Irish peerage.

Following this promotion, Bridport operated primarily in independent command. On June 23, 1795, he fought a partial action off Belle-Ile, an event that was regarded unfavourably in some quarters and became a subject of criticism.
