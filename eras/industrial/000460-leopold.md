# LEOPOLD I

Leopold I was the first King of the Belgians, recognized for his role in consolidating the young Belgian kingdom from both an interior and exterior perspective. He was described by Disraeli as a wise man who was "virtually a British prince."

## Accession and Early Diplomacy
The establishment of the Belgian state involved complex negotiations between the Belgians and other powers. King Leopold was instrumental in these proceedings, persuading the Conference of London to replace its protocols with a declaration of eighteen articles. This allowed for direct negotiations between Leopold and William of Holland. However, on August 2, twelve days after Leopold's accession to the throne, the Dutch King invaded Belgium. 

Despite displaying courage and military skill, Leopold's ill-trained troops suffered defeats at Hasselt and Louvain. Disaster was averted when Leopold appealed to Louis-Philippe for assistance, leading to the arrival of a French army that repulsed the Dutch. This French intervention alarmed England and other powers, who feared a return of French influence. Consequently, a new protocol known as the Twenty-four Articles was drafted, which stripped Belgium of most disputed areas except for the district of Arlon in Luxemburg. Faced with the threat of a German army invasion, the Belgians acceded, and the Treaty of the Twenty-four Articles was signed on November 15, 1831, by Belgium, France, and England, with later assent from Austria, Prussia, and Russia.

## Domestic Governance and Honors
Leopold I founded the Order of Leopold in 1832 to recognize military and civil merit. Originally established with four classes, a fifth class was added in 1838. The order's badge consists of a white enamelled cross with gold balls and borders, suspended from a royal crown and resting on an oak and laurel green wreath. Its center features a medallion with a golden Belgian lion on a black field, surrounded by a red fillet bearing the motto *L'union fait la force*.

During his reign, the political landscape of Belgium shifted. The union between Liberals and Catholics that had existed in 1828 disappeared. Starting in 1847, ministries were composed of only one party at a time. The Liberals held power from 1847 to 1855, followed by the Catholics, who then retired following a street riot in 1857. The Liberals remained in power for the remainder of Leopold I's reign.

## Family and Succession
In 1832, Leopold I married Louise-Marie, the daughter of French King Louis-Philippe. The couple had three children: Charlotte, Philippe (the Count of Flanders), and Leopold (the Duke of Brabant). Queen Louise-Marie died in 1850. 

The young prince Leopold, the Duke of Brabant, was appointed a member of the Belgian Senate on April 9, 1853. Upon this appointment, King Leopold I stated that he had taught his son everything essential and useful for political affairs, noting the prince's common sense and discretion. On August 22, 1853, the Duke of Brabant married Archduchess Henriette of Austria, a union that linked the Belgian dynasty with the heirs of Charles V and Maria Theresa.

## Death and Legacy
King Leopold I died on December 10, 1865. He was succeeded by his son, Leopold II. The successor inherited from his father a respect for the mission of a constitutional sovereign, sound common sense, unusual tact, and an acute knowledge of things and men.
