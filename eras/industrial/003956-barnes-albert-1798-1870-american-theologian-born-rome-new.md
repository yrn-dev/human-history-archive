# BARNES, ALBERT (1798-1870)

## Early Life and Education
Albert Barnes was an American theologian born on December 1, 1798, in Rome, New York. He pursued his higher education at Hamilton College in Clinton, New York, from which he graduated in 1820. Following his undergraduate studies, he attended the Princeton Theological Seminary, graduating in 1823.

## Ministerial Career
Barnes entered the ministry in 1825, when he was ordained as a Presbyterian minister by the presbytery of Elizabethtown, New Jersey. His pastoral career began in Morristown, New Jersey, where he served as pastor of the Presbyterian Church from 1825 to 1830. In 1830, he moved to Philadelphia to become the pastor of the First Presbyterian Church, a position he held for thirty-seven years until 1867.

## Theological Views and Controversy
Barnes was a prominent figure within the New School branch of the Presbyterian denomination, a faction he continued to adhere to following the denomination's division in 1837. His theological positions led to significant conflict within the church; in 1836, he was tried for heresy. The charges against him specifically concerned views he expressed in his 1835 work, *Notes* on Romans, regarding the atonement, original sin, and the imputation of the sin of Adam. Although he was not convicted, the bitterness resulting from the trial served to widen the gap between the progressive and conservative elements of the church.

## Literary Works and Influence
While recognized as an eloquent preacher, Barnes' reputation is primarily based on his expository writings. These works achieved a wider circulation in America and Europe than any other works of their kind. His *Notes on the New Testament* were particularly successful, with more than one million volumes issued by the year 1870. He also wrote *Notes* on Daniel, Isaiah, the Psalms, and Job, which were similarly well-accepted.

The primary merit of these expository works was their ability to present the results of the criticism of other scholars in a popular form accessible to general readers, though it is noted that this was not always done accurately and that Barnes displayed no original critical power. In addition to these, he authored several devotional and practical works. A collection titled *Theological Works* was published in Philadelphia in 1875.

## Death
Albert Barnes died in Philadelphia on December 24, 1870.
