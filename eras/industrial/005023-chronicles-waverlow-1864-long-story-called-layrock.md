# Chronicles of Waverlow and The Layrock of Langley Side

## Benjamin Brierley
Benjamin Brierley (1825–1896) was an English writer and weaver based in Lancashire. Born near Manchester to humble parents, Brierley began his professional life working in a textile factory. During his spare time, he pursued an education. Around the age of thirty, he started contributing articles to local newspapers. He gained attention in 1859 through the republication of several of his sketches featuring Lancashire characters in a work titled *A Summer Day in Daisy Nook*.

By 1863, Brierley transitioned fully into the fields of literature and journalism. In that same year, he published *Chronicles of Waverlow*. This was followed in 1864 by a long story titled *The Layrock of Langley Side*, which was later adapted into a drama.

## Later Career and Public Life
In 1869, Brierley established *Ben Brierley's Journal*, a weekly publication that ran until 1891. He was also known for giving public readings of his own literary works. His travels included visits to America in both 1880 and 1884. Among his popular writings were his pictures of common life in Lancashire and his *Ab-o'-th'-Yate* sketches, which focused on subjects such as London and America; these works were collected after his death.

In 1884, Brierley suffered a financial loss when a building society failed, resulting in the loss of his savings. In response, a fund was created to provide him with support. Benjamin Brierley died on January 18, 1896. Two years after his death, a statue was erected in his honor in Manchester's Queen's Park.
