# A Winning Hazard at the Prince of Wales's Theatre

The Prince of Wales's theatre, located off Tottenham Court Road in London, was a venue that became famous for its contributions to the development of the English stage and the revival of interest in modern drama. During the mid-to-late 19th century, the playhouse was associated with several influential actors, managers, and playwrights.

## Management and the Bancroft Era
The theatre was under the management of Effie Marie Wilton (born 1840). In 1865, she was joined in the management of the theatre by the English playwright Henry James Byron. This partnership lasted for two years before Byron severed the connection to pursue management on his own account in the provinces.

Effie Marie Wilton later married Sir Squire Bancroft (1841– ), an English actor who had first appeared on stage in Birmingham in 1861. Sir Squire Bancroft made his first London appearance in 1865 at the Prince of Wales's theatre, performing in the production *A Winning Hazard* by Wooler. Under the management of Mr. and Mrs. Bancroft, the theatre entered a new era. The couple was associated with the production of all the Robertson comedies, including *Society* (1865), *Ours* (1866), *Caste* (1867), *Play* (1868), *School* (1869), and *M.P.* (1870). Following the death of Robertson, the Bancrofts continued to produce revivals of old comedies with an admirable company.

## Notable Productions and Performers
The fame of the "little playhouse" was further established through several premieres, including *Money* (1872) by Lytton, *London Assurance* (1877) by Boucicault, and *Diplomacy*, which was an adaptation of Sardou's *Dora*. 

The theatre also hosted other prominent theatrical figures. William Hunter Kendal (born 1843) and his wife, Margaret "Madge" Shafto Robertson (born 1849), performed short seasons at the Prince of Wales's. During their time there, the Kendals joined the Bancrofts in productions such as *Diplomacy* and various other plays.

## Transition and Legacy
The Bancroft management remained at the Prince of Wales's until 1879, when they moved to the Haymarket. At the Haymarket, they produced works such as *Fedora* and *Odette* by Sardou (engaging Madame Modjeska), *Sweethearts* by W. S. Gilbert, and *Lords and Commons* by Pinero. After accumulating a considerable fortune, the Bancrofts retired in 1885. Sir Squire Bancroft was later knighted in 1897 and returned to the stage in 1889 to play the abbe Latour in a revival of *Dead Heart* by Watts Phillips, alongside Sir Henry Irving.
