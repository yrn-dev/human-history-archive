# Furnaces and Boiler Systems

Furnaces are utilized across a diverse range of applications, from industrial manufacturing and waste disposal to the propulsion of maritime vessels and the heating of water in various boiler designs.

## Vertical Boiler Furnaces
In installations where floor space is limited or for small-scale setups, vertical cylindrical boilers are frequently employed. The simple vertical boiler features a plain vertical fire-box that is slightly tapering in diameter, with an internal smoke stack that traverses the steam space. 

For applications requiring more heating surface than a standard cross-tube boiler can provide, specialized vertical boilers are used:
* **The "Tyne" Boiler:** Produced by Messrs Clarke, Chapman & Co., this model utilizes a hemispherical furnace. The products of combustion are directed into an upper combustion chamber, which is traversed by several vertical water-tubes of smaller diameter and four or more inclined water-tubes approximately 9 inches in diameter.
* **The "Victoria" Boiler:** Also manufactured by Messrs Clarke, Chapman & Co., this boiler features a hemispherical furnace. Furnace gases are led to an internal combustion chamber and then passed through numerous horizontal smoke-tubes into a smoke-box located on the side of the boiler.
* **The "Cochran" Boiler:** This design is similar to the Victoria but utilizes a "dry" back for the combustion chamber. Instead of water space at the rear, it employs doors lined with firebrick to allow easy access to the tube ends.

## Boiler Furnace Connections and Designs
There are two primary methods of connecting furnaces to combustion chambers. In one design, the furnace is riveted to the "wrapper" or side plate of the combustion chamber, with the crown portion flanged for riveting to the tube plate. The second method, known as the "Gourlay back end," involves contracting the end of the furnace into an oval conical form and flanging it outwards around its entire circumference; the furnace is then riveted to the tube plate, which extends to the bottom of the combustion chamber. The Gourlay design allows a damaged furnace to be replaced without disturbing the end plates, though it is less stiff and requires more longitudinal stays.

Other boiler furnace configurations include:
* **Galloway Boiler:** This system uses two furnaces that extend approximately 9 to 10 feet into the boiler, leading into a large flue or chamber containing "Galloway" tubes.
* **Cornish Boiler:** Used when boilers less than about 6-1/2 feet in diameter are required, this is a similar type to the Lancashire boiler but contains only one furnace.

## Maritime and Liquid Fuel Furnaces
Modern maritime propulsion has seen a shift toward liquid fuel. In large passenger steamers (those of 20 knots and upwards in the Atlantic trade), oil fuel offers advantages in cleanliness, bunkering time, and speed. By substituting coal with oil, a medium-sized Atlantic steamer can increase dead weight cargo by 1,000 tons and measurement cargo by approximately 100,000 cubic feet, as oil is stored in previously unutilized double bottom spaces.

The installation of oil-fuel systems is costly, requiring burners, pipes, pumps, valves, and oil-tight tanks. On the ss. "Murex," the furnace gear includes a steam-pipe, oil-pipe, injector, and a swivel to move the injector clear of the furnace. The furnace section of the ss. "Murex" features a fire-brick baffling bridge and a fire-brick pier. Experience indicates that removing the fire-bars typically used for coal produces better results with liquid fuel than protecting those bars with broken fire-brick. For boilers designed for liquid fuel, it is recommended to use longer, narrower boiler tubes and larger combustion chambers.

Various navies have adopted these technologies:
* **The Italian Admiralty:** Several large warships use boiler apparatus for petroleum.
* **The German Admiralty:** Liquid fuel is used regularly on the China station.
* **The Dutch Navy:** Furnaces are fitted for both coal and liquid fuel in combination, using coal for smaller power requirements and supplementing with oil for larger power needs, which has increased some destroyer speeds by nearly two knots.
* **The British Admiralty:** Experiments with liquid fuel have been conducted, with potential supply sources identified in Trinidad and Burma.

## Industrial and Specialized Furnaces
Furnaces are also employed in non-propulsion industrial contexts:
* **Waste Disposal:** Alfred Fryer patented a destructor in 1876 consisting of cells or furnaces, usually arranged in back-to-back pairs within a rectangular brick block where house refuse is tipped.
* **Blast Furnaces:** At the end of the 19th century, developments allowed the use of waste gas from blast furnaces. When mixed with air, this explosive mixture can drive the piston of an actuating cylinder, similar to a gas engine, providing a cost-effective way to power blowing engines.
* **Ancient Industry:** Archaeological evidence suggests the historical use of dyers' furnaces, as well as silver refineries and bakeries.
* **Metallurgy:** Reverberatory furnaces are utilized in industrial processes.
