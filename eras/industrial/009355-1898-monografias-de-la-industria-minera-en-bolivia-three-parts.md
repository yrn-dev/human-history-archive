# Monografias de la Industria Minera en Bolivia

The *Monografias de la Industria Minera en Bolivia* is a three-part work published in La Paz between 1899 and 1900. It is listed among the authorities and references for the study of the Republic of Bolivia, alongside other statistical, geographical, and industrial records of the period.

## Context of Bolivian Industrial and Geographical Study
The *Monografias* exists within a broader body of late 19th and early 20th-century documentation regarding Bolivia's resources and administration. Other contemporary authorities include M.V. Ballivian’s *Apuntes sobre la industria de goma elastica, &c.* (1896) and the *Noticia Politica, Geografica, Industrial, y Estadistica de Bolivia* (1900). These works, along with the *Sinopsis estadistica y geografica de la Republica de Bolivia* (1902-1904) and the *Diccionario Geografico de la Republica de Bolivia* (1900), provide a comprehensive look at the nation's industrial and territorial state.

## Geological Foundations of Mining
The mining industry in Bolivia is tied to the country's diverse geological composition. The Western Cordillera is primarily composed of Jurassic and Cretaceous beds, as well as ashes and lavas from great volcanoes. In contrast, the eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes consist of Palaeozoic rocks with other intrusions of granite. The intervening plateau features terrestrial and freshwater deposits, including ridges of Palaeozoic rock and gypsiferous marls and red sandstones.

The Palaeozoic beds have provided fossils from the Carboniferous, Devonian, Ordovician, and Cambrian ages. In the north-eastern ranges, the Devonian and Carboniferous beds are extensively developed, while the Cambrian and Ordovician beds are more prevalent in the eastern Andes of southern Bolivia.

## Territorial and Economic Factors
The industrial development of Bolivia has been heavily influenced by its territorial disputes and treaties, particularly regarding access to the sea and the extraction of minerals. In 1866, a treaty between Bolivia and Chile established the 24th parallel as the boundary of Chilean territory. Under this agreement, Chile was granted full facilities for trading on the coast between the 23rd and 24th parallels, a half share of the customs, and the right to export products and mine in the district without hindrance or tax from Bolivia.

By 1870, Bolivia further granted an Anglo-Chilean company the right to work specific nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel in exchange for $10,000. The transfer of this wealth to Chilean hands caused significant discontent within Bolivia. This tension contributed to a secret agreement signed between Bolivia and Peru on February 6, 1873, aimed at mutual defense and the preservation of territorial integrity against possible further Chilean encroachment.

## Political Transitions and Diplomatic Efforts
The period surrounding the publication of the *Monografias* saw various presidential administrations managing the recovery from war and the pursuit of commercial stability. Following the presidency of Dr. Pacheco, the office was held by Dr. Aniceto Arce (until 1892) and Dr. Mariano Baptista. In 1896, Dr. Severe Alonso became president.

Under Dr. Alonso's tenure, Bolivia resumed diplomatic relations with Great Britain. In July 1897, Senor Aramayo was sent to London as minister plenipotentiary, resulting in an extradition treaty concluded in March 1898.

Efforts to secure a seaport for commercial prosperity continued through treaties with Chile. On May 18, 1895, a treaty was signed in Santiago where Chile pledged that if it acquired permanent sovereignty over Arica and Tacna, it would transfer them to Bolivia for an indemnity of $5,000,000 silver. If this failed, Chile bound itself to cede $5,000,000 silver and the roadstead (caleta) of Vitor, or another analogous port, to satisfy Bolivia's commercial requirements. However, as of May 23, 1895, when further treaties of peace and commerce were signed, the provisions regarding the cession of a seaport remained unfulfilled.
