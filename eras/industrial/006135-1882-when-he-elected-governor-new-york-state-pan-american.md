# New York State History and Figures

The history of New York state is characterized by its development in jurisprudence, education, and political administration, as well as the contributions of various legal and academic figures.

## Legal and Jurisprudential Development
James Kent (1763–1847) was a pivotal figure in American jurisprudence. Born in Philippi, New York, Kent graduated from Yale College in 1781 and began practicing law in Poughkeepsie in 1785. His political career included serving as a representative of Dutchess county in the state Assembly during 1791 and 1702–93. Due to his Federalist sympathies, Governor Jay appointed him a master in chancery for the city of New York in 1793.

Kent held several high-ranking judicial positions, serving as the recorder of New York in 1797, judge of the state's supreme court in 1798, chief justice in 1804, and chancellor of New York in 1814. In 1822, he participated in the convention to revise the state constitution. After resigning his office the following year, he returned to teaching at Columbia College. His lectures formed the basis of the *Commentaries on American Law* (1826–1830), a work highly regarded by both American and English jurists. Kent is credited with forming the basis of American equity jurisprudence through his thoroughly developed judgments in chancery law, which had previously been unpopular and underdeveloped during the colonial period.

## Political Figures and Administration
New York has been the site of various political movements and the careers of several prominent administrators. Chauncey Mitchell Depew (born 1834) entered politics as a Whig and served in the New York Assembly from 1861 to 1862, later becoming the secretary of state of New York from 1864 to 1865. Though he was nominated for lieutenant-governor of New York in 1872 under the Liberal-Republican movement, he was defeated. Depew later served as a United States senator from New York state, elected in 1899 and re-elected in 1904.

Seth Low (1850– ) served as the mayor of Brooklyn from 1882 to 1886, winning twice on an independent ticket. His administration is noted for implementing the first municipal civil-service rules in the United States in September 1884, demonstrating the practicability of a rigid "merit" system. Low was also a key figure in the 1897 chartering of Greater New York. After an unsuccessful independent bid for mayor of New York City in 1897, he was elected mayor on a fusion ticket in 1900, serving from 1901 to 1903.

## Education and Academic Institutions
Columbia University (formerly Columbia College) has been a central institution in New York. Nicholas Murray Butler (born 1862) graduated from the college in 1882 and later served as a professor and dean of the faculty of philosophy. From 1887 to 1891, Butler served as the first president of the New York college for the training of teachers. In 1901, he succeeded Seth Low as president of Columbia University.

Under Seth Low's presidency (1890–1901), Columbia underwent significant transformation. Low provided $1,000,000 for a library and oversaw the university's move to Mornside Heights. During this period, the institution's name was changed to Columbia University, and it integrated the Medical School and the Teachers' College of Columbia. The university also established relations with Cooper Union, the Metropolitan Museum of Fine Arts, the American Museum of Natural History, and various theological seminaries (General, Union, and Jewish) in New York City.

## Colonial History and Documentation
The study of New York's early history was advanced by John Romeyn Brodhead (1814–1873). Admitted to the bar in New York City in 1835, Brodhead focused on colonial history and served as an attaché of the American legation at the Hague in 1839. His discovery of rich Dutch archives led the state legislature to fund the collection of transcripts regarding New York's history. Appointed by Governor William H. Seward in 1841, Brodhead gathered approximately eighty manuscript volumes from Holland, France, and England. These were later published as *Documents relating to the Colonial History of New York* (15 vols., 1853–1883). Brodhead also authored a two-volume *History of the State of New York* covering the period from 1609 to 1690.

## Electoral Processes
The electoral system in New York state is characterized by the large number of officials chosen simultaneously. During quadrennial presidential elections, electors vote for thirty-six presidential electors, a state-governor, lieutenant-governor, one congressman, five other state officers, members of both houses of the state legislature, several judges, a sheriff, and county-clerk. This results in "blanket" ballots that can reach 2 to 3 feet in length and 18 to 20 inches in width to accommodate the various party tickets.
