# Bander Abbasi

Bander Abbasi (also known as Bender Abbas) is a Persian town located on the northern shore of the Persian Gulf at 27deg 11' N. and 56deg 17' E. It is part of the administrative division of the "Persian Gulf ports," which is overseen by a governor residing at Bushire. The town has a population of approximately 10,000 people and is characterized by bad water and an insalubrious climate.

## History and Naming
Until 1622, the town was known as Gombrun (also referred to as Gombroon, Gamaroon, or Cambarao and Comorao by Portuguese writers). In 1612, the Portuguese built a fort in the area, but they were expelled in 1614 by Shah Abbas I. The town received its current name, meaning the "port of Abbas," in honor of the reigning shah in 1622. 

Following the capture of the island of Hormuz (Ormus) in 1622 by Persians and the English, a significant portion of the island's trade was transferred to Bander Abbasi. The Persians established their mainland operations at Gombroon, situated about 12 miles north-west of Hormuz, which the king designated as a royal port.

## European Presence and Trade
The English were permitted to establish a factory in the town, and the Dutch obtained similar privileges around 1620. Under the stipulations for providing aid, the English were granted an equal division of customs duties. Although trade with Persia became precarious and decayed, the English company maintained its factory at Gombroon to preserve its claim to this revenue. By 1683–1684, the debt owed to the company in Persia, including customs duties, was estimated at one million sterling.

The English factory remained in operation until 1759, when it was destroyed by the French. While it was later re-established, it has since been abandoned; the ruins of the factory and other buildings are located to the west of the current town.

## Political Control and Administration
In approximately 1740, Nadir Shah granted the town and district—including the town of Minab, the fort of Shamil, and the islands of Larak, Hormuz, and Kishm—to the Arab tribe of the Beni Ma'ini in exchange for a yearly tribute or rent. Roughly 40 years later, Sultan bin Ahmad, the ruler of Muscat, occupied the town after the Arab inhabitants appealed to him for assistance against Persian misrule. The Persian government issued a firman confirming Sultan bin Ahmad's possession, provided he paid a yearly rent of several thousand tomans. During this period, the islands were viewed as the property of Muscat.

Tensions continued into the 19th century. In 1852, Persians expelled the Muscat authorities from the district, though they later retired when Muscat agreed to pay an increased rent. A treaty concluded in 1856 stipulated that the islands and the town and district of Bander Abbasi were Persian territory, leased to Muscat for an annual rent of 14,000 tomans (L6000). Although intended to last twenty years, the Persians utilized the assassination of the sultan of Muscat, Seyed Thuweini, in 1866 to install their own nominee as governor of the district.

## Commerce and Infrastructure
Bander Abbasi maintains a lively trade, serving as a supplier of imports to Khorasan and central and south-eastern Persia, while exporting produce from those same regions. The port is served weekly by mail steamers from the British India Steam Navigation Company and hosts consuls from Russia and Great Britain. The town is equipped with post and telegraph offices.

Between 1890 and 1905, the average annual value of imports and exports was approximately L660,000. This consisted of L260,000 (L155,000 British) in exports and L400,000 (L340,000 British) in imports. Shipping data from 1905 shows that of the 255,000 tons of shipping that entered the port, 237,000 tons were British.

## Security
The region has historically faced threats from robbers. In 1721, 4000 horsemen of the Mekrani and Biluchi description plundered Bander Abbasi while Captain Alex. Hamilton was in the port. These raids were part of a long-standing pattern of incursions by Biluch robbers.
