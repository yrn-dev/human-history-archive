# Academic and Literary Developments (1856-1861)

The period between 1856 and 1861 was marked by significant contributions to philology, the natural sciences, and the publication of major literary and historical works across Europe and the United States.

## Philology and Linguistics
A major development in comparative grammar occurred through the work of Bopp. Between 1856 and 1861, a second German edition of his *Comparative Grammar* was published. This thoroughly revised edition expanded the scope of the work to include Old-Armenian. This specific edition later served as the basis for an excellent French translation produced by Professor Michel Breal in 1866. Bopp's broader objective in his *Comparative Grammar* was threefold: to investigate the origin of grammatical forms, to trace phonetic laws, and to provide a description of the original grammatical structure of languages based on their intercomparison.

In the field of classical studies, Conrad Bursian, a German philologist and archaeologist, began his professional ascent during this time. After completing his university studies at Leipzig in 1852 and traveling through Greece, Italy, France, and Belgium, he became a *Privat-docent* in 1856. He later became an extraordinary professor at Leipzig in 1858 and a professor of philology and archaeology at Tübingen in 1861. In 1856, Bursian published an edition of Julius Firmicus Maternus' *De Errore Profanarum Religionum*.

## Natural Sciences and Geology
In France, Eugène Eudes-Deslongchamps, a palaeontologist, succeeded his father around 1856 as the professor of zoology at the faculty of sciences at Caen. By 1861, his roles expanded to include dean and professor of geology. During this period, he contributed several memoirs to the Société Linnéenne de Normandie, including works on the geology of Calvados (1856-1863) and the department of La Manche (1856).

## Literary and Historical Publications
The late 1850s saw the release of various scholarly and biographical works. In Stockholm, J.G. Carlen edited a five-volume edition of the works of Bellman, published between 1856 and 1861, which included music, illustrations, and biographical notes. In England, Alexander Dyce published *Recollections of the Table Talk of Samuel Rogers* in 1856.

In the United States, Horace Greeley published *History of the Struggle for Slavery Extension* in 1856 and *Overland Journey to San Francisco* in 1860. He also assisted John F. Cleveland in editing *A Political Text-book* in 1860.

In Germany, the historian Klopp published *Geschichte Ostfrieslands* between 1854 and 1858. In 1861, he released a revised edition of *Tilly im dreissigjährigen Kriege* and began editing the works of Leibnitz in an eleven-volume set that continued until 1884.

## Political and Cultural Transitions
The period concluded with significant transitions in leadership and the arts. In the United States, James Buchanan's term as president expired on March 4, 1861, after which he retired to Wheatland, Pennsylvania.

In Denmark, the dramatic arts continued to flourish, providing a venue for experimental talent. This era saw the emergence of figures such as Oskar Benzon (born 1856), Gustav Wied (born 1858), and Einar Christiansen (born 1861).
