# Urban and Industrial Lighting Systems

The implementation of lighting systems in various cities and maritime applications has evolved through the use of animal fats, candles, gas, and electricity. These systems vary by region, with some municipalities managing their own utility plants while others rely on external natural resources or private infrastructure.

## Evolution of Street Lighting
In certain urban centers, the transition to modern lighting followed a specific progression. Before the introduction of gas lighting in 1856, street illumination was achieved first through the use of lamps burning tallow candles and, prior to that, lamps burning mares' grease. Over time, these cities integrated both gas and electricity into their lighting infrastructure.

## Municipal Gas and Electric Systems
Different cities have adopted various methods for generating and sourcing their light and heat:

*   **Buffalo:** This city utilizes electricity generated by the water power of Niagara Falls. It also employs manufactured gas, which is obtained via pipe lines from the Canadian (Welland) and Ohio-Pennsylvania natural gas fields. In Buffalo, gas is used extensively for both heating and lighting purposes.
*   **Bellefontaine:** In this Ohio city, the municipality owns and operates its own electric-lighting and gas plants.
*   **Cordoba:** Located in the Argentine Republic, the streets of Cordoba are lighted by a combination of electricity and gas.
*   **Kingston:** The capital of Jamaica maintains a supply of both electric light and gas.

## Specialized Lighting and Industrial Applications
Beyond general street lighting, gas and electricity are utilized in specialized maritime and industrial equipment:

### Maritime Buoys
London Trinity House employs steel buoys to mark sea positions. In 1878, gas buoys were introduced, featuring occulting lights of 10-candle power. A later design developed in 1896 by engineer-in-chief T. Matthews features a buoy capable of holding 380 cubic feet of gas. This system produces an occulting light with an intensity of 50 candles, visible for 8 miles. The light is positioned 10 feet above the sea and operates on a cycle of seven seconds of visibility and three seconds of obscuration. The gas chamber, charged to five atmospheres, is replenished by a steamer using transport receivers charged to ten atmospheres.

### Industrial and Laboratory Equipment
Electricity and gas are also used as heating and regulating agents in various technical apparatuses:
*   **Incubators:** Modern incubators from manufacturers such as Lautenschläger of Berlin, Lequeux of Paris, and Hearson of London use electric radiators for heating and regulation. Conversely, large bacteriological incubators at the Pasteur Institute in Paris utilize a large central boiler heated by gas-burners.
*   **Metallurgical Furnaces:** Heating agents for furnaces are classified by the nature of the agent used, which may include electricity, gas, oil, or coal (or similar combustibles).
*   **Manufacturing:** In the German town of Furstenwalde, local industries include the manufacture of gas-fittings and electric lamps.
