# 1865

## Notable Deaths and Legacies

Several prominent figures from various fields of study and governance passed away in 1865.

**P.B.J. Buchez** died on August 12, 1865. Buchez had a career shaped by his publications and his connections to the *National* newspaper and secret societies that opposed the government of Louis Philippe. These associations led to his appointment as the president of the Constituent Assembly following the Revolution of 1848, though he held this position for only a short time as he lacked the qualities required for such a tempestuous period. After the assembly was dissolved and he was not re-elected, Buchez returned to private life and continued his studies. His most significant late-life work was the *Traité de politique*, published in 1866, which completed his *Traité de philosophie*. He also wrote numerous brochures covering philosophical, political, medical, and historical subjects. Buchez was succeeded in his principles by a disciple of considerable ability, M.A. Ott, who applied and advocated for them in various writings, including a piece titled "P.B.J. Buchez" in the 1865 *Journal des économistes*.

**Thomas Corwin** (1794–1865) was an American orator and statesman who died in Washington, D.C., on December 18, 1865. Born in Kentucky and later based in Ohio, Corwin was admitted to the bar in 1817. His political career spanned several roles: he served in the Ohio legislature (1821, 1822, 1829), the national House of Representatives (1831–1840 and 1859–1861), as governor of Ohio (1840–1842), and in the United States Senate (1845–1850). He also served as the secretary of the treasury under President Fillmore from 1850 to 1853 and as the U.S. minister to Mexico from 1861 to 1864. Corwin, who shifted his affiliation from the Whig party to the Republican party after 1858, is remembered for his eloquence, humor, and kindliness. One of his most famous speeches was delivered on February 11, 1847, in opposition to the Mexican War. In 1860, he chaired the House "Committee of Thirty-three," which sought a scheme to reconcile the North and South.

**C. J. Thomsen** (1788–1865), a figure noted for his work in antiquarianism, also died in this year. Additionally, the Dutch critic of the romantic movement, **Reinier Cornelis Bakhuizen van den Brink** (1810–1865), passed away in 1865.

## Scientific and Academic Publications

The year 1865 saw the release of several specialized academic and scientific texts. Pierre Jean Édouard Desor, a Swiss geologist who studied glacial phenomena, palaeontology, and Jurassic Echinoderms, published two works in 1865: *Aus Sahara* and *Der Gebirgsbau der Alpen*.

In the field of historical documentation, 1865 was a year of publication for works regarding the reign of Louis IV. These include the *Regesta imperii* 1314-1347, edited by J. F. Böhmer and J. Ficker in Innsbruck, and the *Urkunden zur Geschichte des Römerzuges Königs Ludwigs des Bayern*, also edited by J. Ficker in Innsbruck.

## Educational Developments

In the United States, 1865 marked the founding of Des Moines College. Located in Des Moines, Iowa—the capital and largest city of the state—the college was established as a co-educational Baptist institution.
