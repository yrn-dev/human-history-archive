# Micro-organisms and Disease

The study of micro-organisms and their relationship to disease has generated an extensive body of bacteriological literature. This field encompasses the research of numerous authors and the publication of specialized manuals and textbooks focusing on pathogenic bacteria, bacteriological technique, and the mechanisms of immunity.

## Bacteriological Literature and Authorities
The volume of research in bacteriology is such that comprehensive references often rely on specialized texts. Among the most significant works is the *Handbuch der pathogenen Mikroorganismen* by Kolle and Wassermann (Jena, 1904), which is regarded as the most important work on the subject and continues to be updated with supplements. Other notable contributions include Klein's *Micro-organisms and Disease* (3rd ed., London, 1896) and Crookshank's *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* (4th ed., London, 1896).

Further foundational texts include *Principles of Bacteriology* by Abbott (7th ed., 1905) and Hueppe (translation, 1899), as well as *Manual of Bacteriology* authored by Hewlett (2nd ed., 1902), Sternberg (2nd ed., 1896), and Muir and Ritchie (4th ed., 1908). Specialized research is also found in works such as Woodhead's *Bacteria and their products* (1891) and Park's *Pathogenic Micro-organisms* (1906). For a comprehensive overview of the bacteriology of infective diseases, the *System of Medicine*, edited by Clifford Allbutt (2nd ed., 1907), provides detailed information.

## Pathogenic Effects and Specific Diseases
Micro-organisms are central to the development of various specific disorders. In the context of the respiratory system, bronchitis is categorized as either non-specific or specific. Specific bronchitis is that which occurs during infectious disorders—such as pneumonia, measles, influenza, and diphtheria—and is caused by the micro-organisms observed in those diseases.

In other cases, micro-organisms may cause infective diseases by germinating in damaged bone. In septic osteomyelitis, these organisms can enter the blood-stream and spread to other tissues, potentially leading to blood-poisoning. This condition can pose a severe risk to surgeons; for example, a surgeon who pricks his finger while operating on a case of septic osteomyelitis may suffer blood poisoning, risking the loss of the finger, hand, or life. In infants, these germs frequently migrate to neighboring joints, resulting in acute arthritis of infants.

## Septic Micro-organisms and Embolism
The presence of septic micro-organisms can complicate vascular issues, particularly in the kidneys. While some necrosis in the kidney is caused by clots detached from the heart, other plugs are infective due to the presence of septic micro-organisms. These infective plugs are likely to lead to the formation of small pyaemic abscesses. Additionally, septic conditions such as puerperal pyaemia can involve the formation of a clot in the pelvic veins, which may then spread up the vena cava and block the renal veins, causing extreme congestion of the kidney.

## General Bacteriological Research
Beyond disease, bacteriological study extends to the structure and function of bacteria, their classification, and their presence in the environment. Research has been conducted on the biology of *B. ramosus* and the bacteriology of water, including studies on the river Thames. Technical investigations have focused on the cell-membrane, cilia, the nucleus (including its division), and the nature of spores. Scholars such as Fischer and Migula have contributed extensively to the understanding of the structure and functions of bacteria.
