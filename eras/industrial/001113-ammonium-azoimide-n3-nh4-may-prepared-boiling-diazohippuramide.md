# Ammonium Azoimide

Ammonium azoimide, with the chemical formula $\text{N}_3\text{NH}_4$, is an extremely explosive salt. It is characterized by its solubility in water and is described as being only feebly alkaline.

## Preparation
Ammonium azoimide may be prepared by boiling diazohippuramide with alcoholic ammonia. This process is continued until no more ammonia escapes. The chemical reaction for this preparation is:
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO.NHCH}_2\text{CONH.N}_2\text{OH} + 2\text{NH}_3 = \text{N}_3\text{NH}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO.NH.CH}_2\text{CO.NH}_2$

Following the reaction, the liquid is left to stand for twelve hours. The precipitated hippuramide is then removed by decanting the clear alcoholic solution. To precipitate the ammonium salt, a volume of ether four times that of the alcoholic solution is added. The resulting salt is filtered, washed with ether, and air-dried.

## Related Compounds and Chemical Context
Ammonium azoimide is a salt of azoimide (also referred to as hydrazoic acid). The free acid is a colorless liquid with a very unpleasant smell that boils at $30^\circ\text{C}$ and is extremely explosive. It is a "weak" acid, ionizing only to a very slight extent in dilute aqueous solutions. When the acid dissolves metals such as iron and zinc, it liberates hydrogen and forms salts, including azides, azoimides, or hydrazoates, all of which are explosive.

Other related nitrogen-based compounds include:
*   **Hydrazine azoimide**: Known as $\text{N}_5\text{H}_5$.
*   **Chloroazoimide**: A highly explosive colorless gas ($\text{Cl.N}_3$) obtained by F. Raschig by acidifying a mixture of sodium azide and hypochlorite using boric or acetic acid.
*   **Diazoimino benzene**: A yellow oil ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_3$) with a stupefying odour that boils at $59^\circ\text{C}$ (at 12 mm.) and explodes when heated.
