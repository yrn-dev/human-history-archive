# Horatius Bonar

## Early Life and Education
Horatius Bonar (1808–1889) was a Scottish Presbyterian divine. He was born in Edinburgh on December 19, 1808. His education took place within his native city, where he attended both the university and the high school.

## Ecclesiastical Career
Bonar began his professional religious work with a term of mission work at Leith. In 1837, he was appointed as the parish minister of Kelso. Following the Disruption of 1843, he became a minister of the newly formed Free Church. He served in this capacity until 1866, at which point he moved to the Chalmers memorial church in Edinburgh.

Throughout his career, Bonar achieved several academic and leadership milestones. In 1853, he received the D.D. degree from Aberdeen University. Later, in 1883, he served as the moderator of the general assembly of his church. He died on July 31, 1889.

## Literary Works and Hymnology
Bonar was a prolific author of religious literature and served as an editor for several journals, including the *Presbyterian Review*, the *Quarterly Journal of Prophecy*, and the *Christian Treasury*. 

While he wrote extensively, his most significant contributions were in the field of hymnology. Between 1857 and 1866, he published three series titled *Hymns of Faith and Hope*, which were later released in a new edition in 1886. His influence on modern hymnody is substantial, as nearly every modern hymnal contains approximately twenty of his hymns. Notable examples of his work include:
* "I heard the voice of Jesus say"
* "Go, labour on"
* "When the weary, seeking rest"
* "Here, O my Lord, I see Thee face to face"
