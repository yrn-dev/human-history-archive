# Robert Williams Buchanan

## Early Life and Education
Robert Williams Buchanan (1841–1901) was a British dramatist, novelist, and poet. He was born on August 18, 1841, at Caverswall, Staffordshire. He was the son of Robert Buchanan (1813–1866), who worked as a journalist and Owenite lecturer. Buchanan's father was a native of Ayr and had lived in Manchester for several years before moving to Glasgow. 

Buchanan received his education in Glasgow, attending both the university and the high school. During his time as a student, he formed a close friendship with the poet David Gray. This relationship and their subsequent journey to London in 1860 in pursuit of fame were later detailed in an essay by Buchanan originally published in the *Cornhill Magazine*.

## Poetic Works
Following a period characterized by disappointment and struggle, Buchanan published his first volume, *Undertones*, in 1863, which he described as "tentative." He followed this with several other poetic collections: *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). In these works, Buchanan demonstrated a sympathetic insight into the humbler conditions of life and a faculty for poetic narrative.

In 1870, he attempted a more ambitious work titled *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic*, which served as a study in mysticism.

## Literary Controversy
Buchanan was a frequent contributor to periodical literature. He gained notoriety in October 1871 when he wrote an article for the *Contemporary Review* titled "The Fleshly School of Poetry," published under the pseudonym Thomas Maitland. This critique was expanded into a pamphlet in 1872.

The article provoked responses from other literary figures, including Mr. Swinburne in *Under the Microscope* (1872) and D.G. Rossetti, who wrote a letter to the *Athenaeum* on December 16, 1871, titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism." Buchanan later expressed regret regarding the violence of his attack; the "old enemy" to whom he dedicated *God and the Man* was Rossetti.

## Novels and Drama
In 1876, Buchanan began a long series of novels with the publication of *The Shadow of the Sword*, which is considered one of his best. In addition to his novels and poetry, he authored several successful plays, including:
* *Lady Clare* (produced in 1883)
* *Sophia* (1886), which was an adaptation of *Tom Jones*
* *A Man's Shadow* (1890)
* *The Charlatan* (1894)
