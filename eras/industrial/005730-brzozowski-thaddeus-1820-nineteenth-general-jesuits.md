# BRZOZOWSKI, THADDEUS

Thaddeus Brzozowski (d. 1820) was the nineteenth general of the Jesuits. He was appointed to this position on September 2, 1805, succeeding Gabriel Gruber. Brzozowski is noted primarily for serving as the general of the Society of Jesus during the period of its restoration across Europe.

## Administrative Achievements and Challenges
During Brzozowski's tenure, the Society of Jesus sought to re-establish its presence in various regions. In 1801, Pius VII had granted the Jesuits the liberty to reconstitute themselves in north Russia. In 1812, Brzozowski successfully secured the recognition of the Jesuit college of Polotsk as a university. 

However, Brzozowski faced limitations in his efforts to expand the Society's recognition in other territories. Specifically, he was unable to obtain the necessary permission to travel to Spain to advocate for the recognition of the Spanish Jesuits.

## Papal Authority and Russian Opposition
In 1814, Pius VII issued the bull *Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum*. Through this decree, the Pope granted Brzozowski full authority, among other powers, to receive individuals who wished to enter the society.

Despite this ecclesiastical support, the Russian government became alarmed by the growth of the Jesuits. This concern culminated in an edict published on December 20, 1815, which expelled the Jesuits from St Petersburg.

## Death
Following the expulsion and the challenges facing the Society in Russia, Brzozowski requested permission to retire to Rome. This request was vainly pursued, and he did not obtain the permission. Thaddeus Brzozowski died on February 5, 1820.
