# Bankura

## Geography and Administration
Bankura is a town and district located within the Burdwan division of Bengal in British India. The town has a population of 20,737, while the district covers an area of 2,621 square miles. As of 1901, the district's population was 1,116,411, which represented a 4% increase over the previous decade. 

The district is bordered by the Manbhum district to the west, the Midnapur district to the south, and the Burdwan district to the north and east. Geographically, Bankura serves as a connecting link between the mountainous highlands of Chota Nagpur in the west and the Ganges delta in the east. The landscape varies significantly across the district; the eastern boundary adjoining Burdwan is flat and alluvial, resembling the typical paddy lands of Bengal. Moving toward the north and west, the terrain rises into long undulating tracts where swamps and rice lands are replaced by forest trees or low thorny jungle. In these western wild forests, hamlets become more scattered and nearly disappear.

## Economy and Industry
The district possesses various local industries and natural resources. In the hilly tracts, tussur silk and lac are gathered in large quantities. While minerals and stone quarries are little worked, the district contains two coal-mines and indigo factories. Local manufacturing includes the weaving of silk and cotton, as well as the carving of plates and other items from soap-stone. Bishnupur, the old capital of the country, remains the primary center for these local industries.

Historically, the Bishnupur raj was one of the largest estates in Bengal during the late 18th century. However, shortly after the permanent settlement of 1793, it was sold due to arrears of revenue.

## Infrastructure and Transport
Bankura is served by several railway lines. The East Indian railway skirts the north-east part of the district beyond the Damodar river. Additionally, the district is traversed by the Midnapur-Jherria line of the Bengal-Nagpur railway, and a railway line connects Bankura to Howrah.

## Climate and Health
The climate of Bankura is generally considered healthy. The air is wholesome and dry, and fogs occur rarely, while the cold season is described as bracing. However, the district is susceptible to destructive floods and drought. Specifically, Bankura suffered during the famines of 1866, 1874-1875, and 1896-1897. During the hot season, the temperature is described as very relaxing and oppressive.
