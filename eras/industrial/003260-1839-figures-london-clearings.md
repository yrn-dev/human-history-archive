# The London Clearings in 1839

## Overview of the London Clearing House
The London Clearing House served as a central mechanism for the settlement of cheques among banks. The earliest authentic records regarding these clearing operations are found in the *Appendix to the Second Report, Committee of House of Commons, Banks of Issue*, published in 1841. These records provide specific data on the volume of clearings and the number of participating banks during the mid-19th century.

## Figures for 1839
In 1839, the figures for the London clearings totaled £954,401,600. During this year, there were 29 banks participating in the clearing process. 

## Comparative Clearing Data
The volume of clearings saw a slight increase in the following year; in 1840, the figures rose to £978,496,800, while the number of participating banks remained at 29. Over the subsequent decades, the figures grew substantially, reaching £9,150,269,000 in 1899 (with 19 banks) and £8,960,170,000 in 1900 (with 19 banks). By 1906, the clearings reached £12,711,334,000, involving 18 banks.

## Total Yearly Turnover in Cheques
The figures registered by the London Clearing House represent only a portion of the total movement of cheques. To determine the full scale of the yearly turnover, the returns of the London Clearing House must be combined with several other sources:
* Clearing houses located in large towns across Scotland, Ireland, and England.
* Numerous daily exchanges.
* Settlements made between the various offices of banks that maintain many branches.
* Cheques drawn by one bank customer and paid to another.

Because of these additional factors, the total yearly turnover in cheques is roughly estimated to be at least twice as great as the amount officially registered by the London Clearing House.
