# Barbados

Barbados (also rendered as Barbadoes) is an island located in the British West Indies, situated 78 miles east of St Vincent at 13deg 4' N. and 59deg 37' W. The island measures 21 miles in length and 14-1/2 miles at its broadest point, covering an extent of 166 square miles (106,470 acres), which is roughly equal to the Isle of Wight.

## Geography and Climate
The island's coasts are encircled by coral reefs that extend up to 3 miles seaward in certain areas. While elevated, the island is not mountainous; its apex is Mount Hillaby, which reaches 1100 feet. From this central point, the land descends toward the sea in a series of terraces. The incline of these hills is gentle enough that ascents are scarcely noticeable when driving on the well-constructed roads. The only natural harbour is Carlisle Bay on the south-western coast, though it is a shallow roadstead accessible only to light draught vessels.

The climate is described as pleasant, with seasons divided into wet and dry. The dry season, which is also the cold season, lasts from December to the end of May. Temperatures typically range between 70deg F. and 86deg F., rarely dropping below 65deg F. The average annual rainfall is approximately 60 inches, with September being the wettest month. For eight months of the year, the tropical heat is tempered by invigorating N.E. trade winds. Due to the porous nature of the soil, the extent of cultivation, and an absence of swamps, the island is free from miasma and fever is unknown. The climate is noted for arresting the decay of vital power associated with old age and having a beneficial effect on pulmonary diseases, particularly in early stages. However, elephantiasis is frequent enough to be termed "Barbados leg," and leprosy occurs among the negro population.

## History
The origin of the name "Barbados" is subject to differing opinions; it may derive from a species of bearded fig-tree or the Spanish word for hanging vine branches that strike root in the ground. Early 16th-century maps list the island under various names, including Barnodos, Barnodo, Barbudoso, Bernados, and St Bernardo. The island contains more traces of Carib Indians than any other of the Antilles.

Barbados is believed to have been first visited by the Portuguese, but it was acquired by the British without conquest. The ship "Olive Blossom" touched the island in 1605, and the crew took possession for James I. after finding it uninhabited. The first actual settlement occurred in 1625 under the direction of Sir William Courteen, via a patent from Lord Leigh (later the earl of Marlborough). By 1627, a grant was obtained by the earl of Carlisle. In 1628, Charles Wolferstone of Bermuda was appointed governor, and the present capital was founded when sixty-four settlers arrived at Carlisle Bay.

During the English Civil War, Royalists sought refuge on the island under Lord Willoughby, who leased the island from the earl of Carlisle. Despite offering stout resistance to the forces of the Commonwealth, Willoughby was eventually exiled. Following the Restoration, the crown took over the government and abolished the proprietary interest to appease planters. To satisfy the claims of the patentees, a 4-1/2% duty was imposed on all exports. A census conducted in 1684 under Governor Sir Richard Dutton recorded a population of 20,000.

## Population and Administration
Barbados is one of the most densely populated areas in the world. In 1901, the population was 195,588 (1178 per sq. m.), increasing to 196,287 by 1906. The majority of the population consists of negroes, who outnumber whites by 9 to 1. This population is described as having a well-marked dialect and physiognomy of their own and being more intelligent than other West Indian negroes.

The island is administered by a governor, supported by an executive council, a house of assembly consisting of 24 members elected on a limited franchise, and a legislative council of 9 nominated members. While the colony has representative institutions, it does not have responsible government; the home government appoints public officials (excluding the treasurer), and the crown maintains a veto on legislation.

The majority of inhabitants belong to the Church of England, which is the largest denomination. The government pays the clergy of all creeds and the bishop of the see. Educational institutions include:
* **Codrington College**: Founded via a 1710 bequest of two estates by Colonel Christopher Codrington to the Society for the Propagation of the Gospel, it is affiliated with the university of Durham and trains men for holy orders.
* **Secondary Schools**: Harrison College and The Lodge (for boys) and Queen's College (for girls).
* **Other**: Various primary and second-grade schools.

Additionally, the island serves as the headquarters for the Imperial Agricultural Department of the West Indies. Under Sir Daniel Morris, this department facilitated the development of cotton growing.

## Legal and Land Status
There are no forests or crown lands on the island. In terms of legislation, Barbados was among the colonies that passed statutes legalizing marriage with a deceased wife's sister.
