# Bernhard Bolzano

## Life and Academic Career
Bernhard Bolzano (1781–1848) was an Austrian philosopher and priest. Born in Prague on October 5, 1781, he showed distinction at an early age. Following his ordination to the priesthood in 1805, he was appointed as the professor of the philosophy of religion at Prague University.

In his capacity as a lecturer, Bolzano sought to demonstrate that reason was in complete harmony with Catholic theology. While these lectures were viewed with eager interest by younger thinkers, his views also encountered significant opposition. Bolzano was only able to retain his academic chair through the protection provided by the archbishop, Prince Salm-Salm.

## Resignation and Later Years
Bolzano's tenure at the university ended in 1820 when he was accused of having connections to several revolutionary societies composed of students, an event that compelled his resignation. Following this, Rome condemned several doctrines that had been extracted from his written works. Consequently, Bolzano was suspended from his priestly functions. He spent the remainder of his life dedicated to literary work until his death in Prague on December 18, 1848.

## Philosophical and Literary Works
Bolzano authored numerous works, with three being of particular importance:
*   ***Wissenschaftslehre, oder Versuch einer neuen Darstellung der Logik*** (Sulzbach, 1837): A four-volume work in which Bolzano advocated for a scientific method in the study of logic.
*   ***Lehrbuch der Religionswissenschaft*** (Sulzbach, 1834): A four-volume philosophic representation covering all the dogmas of Roman Catholic theology.
*   ***Athanasia, oder Grunde fur die Unsterblichkeit der Seele***: A work for which a second edition was published in Mainz in 1838.

In the field of philosophy, Bolzano was influenced by Kant and followed the monadology of Leibnitz as well as Reinhard in ethics.
