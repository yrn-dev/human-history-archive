# Condensation Processes and Products

Condensation occurs across various scientific and industrial contexts, ranging from the atmospheric formation of clouds to the chemical manufacture of elements and the production of food products.

## Industrial Manufacture of Bromine
Bromine is primarily manufactured in the central district of Michigan and Stassfurt from magnesium bromide found in "bittern," which is the mother liquor of the salt industry. There are two primary methods of production: the continuous process and the periodic process.

In the continuous process, chlorine is generated in special stills. A stream of steam mixed with chlorine is introduced at the bottom of a tall tower filled with broken bricks, where it meets a descending stream of hot bittern. This liberates bromine, which is swept out of the tower by the steam current along with some chlorine and subsequently condensed in a worm. Any bromine vapour that remains uncondensed is absorbed by moist iron borings to create iron bromide, which is then used to manufacture potassium bromide.

The periodic process involves the interaction of a bromide, sulphuric acid, and manganese dioxide (pyrolusite) within sandstone stills heated to 60° C. In this method, the product is condensed in the same manner as in the continuous process. If calcium chloride is present in the bittern, it is recommended to use potassium chlorate instead of pyrolusite.

## Chemical Condensation in Organic Chemistry
In organic chemistry, condensation refers to specific reactions involving ketones and aldehydes. Ketones may condense with aldehydes under the influence of sodium ethylate or alkalis. While ketones do not polymerize like aldehydes, they yield condensation products under the influence of bases and acids; for example, acetone can produce mesitylene, phorone, and mesityl oxide.

Specific compounds are also produced through condensation:
* **Acetyl acetone**: This liquid, with a boiling point of 136° C, can be prepared by condensing acetone with ethyl acetate in the presence of sodium. It further condenses readily with aniline to produce [alpha][gamma]-dimethyl quinoline.
* **Acetonyl acetone**: A liquid boiling at 194° C, this can be obtained by condensing mono-chloracetone with sodium aceto-acetate.

## Atmospheric Condensation
Water vapour in the atmosphere is invisible until it is cooled by natural processes, at which point it tends to condense into liquid cloud particles. Research by J. Aitken demonstrated that vapour molecules do not simply combine with one another. Instead, condensation requires a solid or liquid nucleus, such as atmospheric dust, upon which the molecules can combine.

The presence of these dust nuclei allows condensation to occur once air is cooled to the saturation point. Without dust, the air would become highly supersaturated, and condensation would only occur once the air was cooled far below the saturation point. Such an event would be violent, resulting in heavy rain-drops rather than clouds, and the supersaturated air would condense on all available solid and liquid surfaces, including the walls of dwellings and clothing.

To count dust particles, Aitken developed a method in 1888 where air was placed in an air-tight receiver, saturated with water vapour, and then expanded by an air-pump to produce cooling and condensation. The resulting cloud particles were counted using a short-focussed lens and a micrometer. This process requires that there be no more than 500 particles per cubic centimetre of air; if the count is higher, the air must be mixed with dustless air to ensure all particles form nuclei.

## Other Condensation Products and Processes
### Food Industry
The manufacture of condensed milk is a significant agricultural industry. In the United Kingdom, the quantity of imported condensed milk grew continuously after 1889, increasing from 389,892 cwt. in 1889 to 986,741 cwt. in 1900. During this same period, the annual value of these imports rose from L704,849 to L1,405,033. There is also a trade in home-made condensed milk in the UK; exports of this product rose from 61,442 cwt. (valued at L133,556) in 1892 to 209,447 cwt. (valued at L390,559) in 1900. In the United States, the manufacture of condensed milk, butter, and cheese increased by 60% between 1890 and 1900, with the 1905 product valued at $13,276,533.

### Thermodynamics and Gas Production
In the combustion of hydrogen, the product is water (H2O). The calorific value is higher when the water is condensed to liquid (34,500 calories) than when it remains as vapour (29,650 calories). This difference is due to the latent heat of volatilization, which reappears as heat when the vapour is condensed.

Regarding water gas, pure water gas is not produced by a continuous process because the endothermic reaction must alternate with the exothermic combustion of carbon by atmospheric air to balance energy. However, a continuous operation can be achieved by simultaneously carrying out both reactions using a feed of atmospheric air and steam (preferably superheated).
