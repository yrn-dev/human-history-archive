# Heinrich Barth

## Expedition to Central Africa
Heinrich Barth was a German explorer who participated in a significant mission to open commercial relations with the states of the central and western Sudan. This expedition was organized at the instance of scientists, including Bunsen, and the British government. Barth was appointed as a colleague to James Richardson, an explorer of the Sahara, and Adolf Overweg, a Prussian astronomer.

The party departed from Tripoli early in 1850. However, the mission suffered losses as James Richardson died in March 1851 and Adolf Overweg died in September 1852. These deaths left Barth to continue the mission alone.

## Travels and Discoveries
Barth's expedition is described as one of the most fruitful ever undertaken in inner Africa. His travels included journeys across the Sahara and traverses of the country from Cameroon in the south to Timbuktu in the west, and from Bagirmi and Lake Chad in the east. During his travels, he made prolonged sojourns in Timbuktu and the ancient emirates or sultanates of Sokoto, Nupe, Kano, and Bornu. In 1852, while in Bagirmi, Barth was imprisoned by the Bagirmese for a period of time.

Throughout these journeys, Barth conducted minute studies of the resources, civilizations, history, and topography of the regions he visited. He returned to Europe in September 1855.

## Publications and Later Career
Between 1857 and 1858, Barth published the account of his travels in five volumes, titled *Travels and Discoveries in North and Central Africa*, which was released simultaneously in German and English. The work is regarded as a standard authority on these regions—much of which later came under British rule as Nigeria—and is noted for its extent of information, variety, interest, and accuracy. A two-volume edition of *Travels* was later published in London in 1890.

Despite the success of the expedition, the British government provided Barth with no recognition for his services, with the exception of a C.B. After returning to Germany, Barth prepared a collection of Central African vocabularies, published between 1862 and 1866.

In his later years, Barth undertook further travels, visiting Turkey in Europe in 1862 and Asia Minor in 1858. He was appointed president of the Geographical Society and a professor of geography at Berlin University in 1863. He died in Berlin on November 25, 1865.
