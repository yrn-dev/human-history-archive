# BROOKE, SIR JAMES (1803-1868)

Sir James Brooke was an English traveller, soldier, and the raja of Sarawak. Born on April 29, 1803, at Coombe Grove near Bath, he was the son of a man who had lived for a long time in Bengal as a member of the East India Company's civil service. Brooke's early training was overseen by his mother, whom the archive describes as a woman of superior mind.

## Early Career and Military Service
After receiving a standard school education, Brooke entered the service of the East India Company and was sent to India around 1825. During the outbreak of the Burmese War, he and his regiment were dispatched to the valley of the Brahmaputra. While serving there, Brooke was dangerously wounded during an engagement near Rungpore, an event that forced his return to England in 1826. Following his recovery, he spent time travelling on the continent before returning to India. Shortly after this return, circumstances led him to leave the service of the East India Company.

## Visions for the Indian Archipelago
In 1830, Brooke undertook a voyage to China. During this passage, he travelled among the islands of the Indian Archipelago. He observed that while the region possessed magnificence, fertility, and natural beauty, it was inhabited by savage tribes who were engaged in relentless ferocity and piracy on a vast scale, frequently warring with one another. These observations led Brooke to conceive a design to bring these populations within the pale of civilization and rescue them from barbarism. This purpose was further confirmed during a second visit to China.

## Preparation and Expedition
Upon returning to England, Brooke began making earnest preparations for his mission. Having inherited a large property following the death of his father, he purchased and equipped a 140-ton yacht named the "Royalist." For three years, primarily in the Mediterranean, Brooke trained his crew of twenty men and tested the capacities of the vessel. On October 27, 1838, he sailed from the Thames to begin his adventure.

## Establishment as Raja of Sarawak
After experiencing various delays, Brooke reached Borneo. There, he encountered the raja Muda Hassim, who was the uncle of the reigning sultan. At the time, raja Muda Hassim was engaged in a war in the province of Sarawak against several Dyak tribes that had revolted against the sultan. 

Brooke offered his assistance to the raja. Alongside his crew and several Javanese who joined the effort, Brooke participated in a battle against the insurgents, resulting in the defeat of the rebels. In recognition of these services, Brooke was conferred the title of raja of Sarawak.
