# North Somerset Island and the Boothia Peninsula

## Discovery and Geography
North Somerset Island was discovered in 1819 by Sir E. Parry. This island is separated from the Boothia Peninsula by a narrow channel known as the Bellot Strait. 

The Boothia Peninsula (also referred to as Boothia Felix) is a peninsula of British North America located within the Franklin district. It is situated between 69 deg. 30' and 71 deg. 50' N. latitude and 91 deg. 30' and 97 deg. W. longitude, covering an area of 13,100 sq. m. The northernmost point of the American mainland is Murchison Point, which serves as the northernmost promontory of the peninsula. 

The peninsula was discovered during an expedition from 1829-1833 by Captain (later Sir James) Ross and was named in honor of Sir Felix Booth, who was primarily responsible for fitting out the expedition. Geographically, the peninsula is almost separated from the main mass of the continent by inlets and lakes. It forms the western side of the Boothia Gulf, which serves to separate the Boothia Peninsula from the Melville Peninsula and the north-western portion of Baffin Land.

## Regional Connections and Navigation
The Boothia Peninsula is linked to several other geographical features via various straits and inlets:
* **Prince Regent Inlet:** Connects the peninsula to Lancaster Sound and Barrow Strait.
* **Bellot Strait:** Connects the peninsula to Franklin Strait.
* **Fury and Hecla Strait:** Connects the peninsula to Fox Channel.

The western portion of the peninsula features Lord Mayor bay, while the southern portion contains Pelly and Committee bays.

## Scientific and Historical Importance
The Boothia Peninsula is noted for its connection to the Franklin search and the Franklin expedition. It also holds significant scientific value; Sir James Ross first distinctly localized the north magnetic pole on the western side of the peninsula at 70 deg. 5' N., 96 deg. 47' W.

Additionally, the broader region of the Parry Archipelago contains some Devonian forms. Specifically, Nathorst has demonstrated the existence of Devonian Old Red Sandstone facies on Gauss Peninsula, Ymer Island, Geographical Society Island, and Traill Island.

## Arctic Exploration and Mapping
Arctic voyagers, including Sir J. Ross, Sir F. L. MacClintock, and Sir E. W. Parry, have utilized rough maps drawn for them by Eskimos to aid their travels. Such practices of indigenous map-making are noted in various regions, including the Labrador Peninsula, where the Nasquapee and Montagnais Indians drew maps on bark.
