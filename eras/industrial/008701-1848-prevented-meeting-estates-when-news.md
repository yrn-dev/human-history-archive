# The Events of 1848 in Bohemia and Europe

The year 1848 was marked by significant political upheaval across Europe, characterized by revolutionary outbreaks and demands for liberal reform. These events disrupted established political processes and shifted the relationship between governing bodies and the populations they ruled.

## National Aspirations in Bohemia
Following the re-establishment of European peace in 1815, national aspirations in Bohemia began to revive, initially manifesting through the revival of Bohemian literature. Political action in the lands ruled by the house of Habsburg was rendered impossible by the absolutist and arbitrary government of Prince Metternich. Despite this pressure, the estates of Bohemia adopted an oppositional stance toward the government of Vienna starting in 1845.

The estates asserted their right to vote on the country's taxes, citing the constitution of 1627. By 1847, in an effort to gain support from wider classes of the population, the estates planned to propose several measures at their session the following year:
* The introduction of the Bohemian language into all higher schools.
* More extensive representation for towns at the diet.
* More stringent control over the country's finances by the estates.

## The Revolutionary Outbreak of 1848
The planned meeting of the estates was prevented by the revolutionary outbreak of 1848. When news of the February revolution in Paris reached Prague, it caused great excitement. On March 11, a large public meeting voted for a petition to the government of Vienna. This petition demanded liberal reforms, the convocation of a general diet for all Bohemian lands elected via extensive suffrage, and that the Bohemian language be granted equal rights with German in all government offices.

The response from Vienna was initially equivocal. However, after a second deputation, Emperor Ferdinand declared on April 8 that equality of rights would be secured for both nationalities in Bohemia. He further stated that a new meeting of the estates of Bohemia, including representatives from principal towns, would be convoked shortly. Additionally, the question of reuniting Silesia and Moravia with Bohemia was to be left to a general meeting of representatives from all parts of Austria.

## Unrest in Other German States
The February 1848 revolution in Paris also catalyzed agitation in other German states. In one instance, the Offenburg programme was adopted during numerous public meetings. On March 4, driven by popular excitement, the lower chamber accepted this programme almost unanimously. 

The government responded by proclaiming an amnesty, promising reforms, and remodeling the ministry in a Liberal direction. A new delegate was sent to the federal diet at Frankfort with the power to vote for the establishment of a parliament for a united Germany. Despite these concessions, republican agitators continued to foment disorder, leading to armed insurrection. These efforts were suppressed by federal troops; insurgents were defeated at Kandern on April 20, Freiburg fell on April 24, and a Franco-German "legion" that had invaded Baden from Strassburg was routed at Dossenbach on April 27.

## Other Notable Events of 1848
The year 1848 also saw significant personal and political milestones in the Americas. John Jacob Astor, an American merchant who amassed one of the largest fortunes of any American through the fur trade and New York real estate, died on March 29, 1848. His fortune was estimated at approximately $30,000,000.

In the United States government, Horace Greeley served a three-month term in Congress during 1848-1849 to fill a vacancy. During this time, he published an exposure of abuses regarding mileage allowances for members and introduced the first bill to provide small tracts of government land free to actual settlers.
