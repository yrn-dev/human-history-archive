## Richard Brocklesby
Richard Brocklesby (1722–1797) was an English physician born on August 11, 1722, in Minehead, Somersetshire. His education took place at Ballitore in Ireland, where he was a schoolfellow of Edmund Burke, followed by medical studies at Edinburgh and graduation from Leiden in 1745. In 1758, he was appointed as a physician to the army and served in Germany during a portion of the Seven Years' War. Upon returning from this service, he established a medical practice in London.

In 1764, Brocklesby published *Economical and Medical Observations*, a work that offered suggestions for the improvement of hygiene within army hospitals. In his later years, he transitioned into private life, maintaining a circle of friends that included distinguished literary figures. He was warmly attached to Dr. Johnson, attending him on his death-bed and offering him a life annuity of £100 around 1784. He also maintained an intimate friendship with Burke, presenting him with £1000 in 1788 and offering to repeat the gift annually until Burke's merit was properly rewarded at court. Brocklesby died on December 11, 1797, leaving his fortune in part and his house to his grand-nephew, Dr. Thomas Young.

## George Macartney, Earl Macartney
George Macartney (1737–1806) was born on May 14, 1737, in Lissanoure, Antrim, Ireland, descending from the Macartneys of Auchinleck, a Scottish family that had settled in Ireland in 1649. After graduating from Trinity College, Dublin, in 1759, he became a student of the Temple in London. He was taken up by Lord Holland through Stephen Fox.

Macartney's career was marked by extensive diplomatic and gubernatorial roles:
* **Diplomacy:** In 1764, he was appointed envoy extraordinary to Russia, where he successfully negotiated an alliance with England. Later, he served as the first British envoy to China.
* **Government:** He held a seat in the English parliament and was returned for Antrim in the Irish parliament in 1769 to serve as chief secretary for Ireland, a role after which he was knighted. He served as governor of the Caribbee Islands in 1775 (becoming an Irish baron in 1776) and governor of Madras in 1780, though he declined the governor-generalship of India.
* **Later Appointments:** After being created Earl Macartney in the Irish peerage in 1792, he undertook a confidential mission to Italy in 1795. He was raised to the English peerage as a baron in 1796 and appointed governor of the Cape of Good Hope.

Macartney resigned from his final post in November 1798 due to ill health and died at Chiswick, Middlesex, on May 31, 1806. His title became extinct, and his property passed to his niece's son following the death of his widow, the daughter of the 3rd earl of Bute.

## Charles Bulfinch
Charles Bulfinch (1763–1844) was a prominent American architect born in Boston, Massachusetts, on August 8, 1763, to Thomas Bulfinch, a wealthy physician. Educated at Harvard (graduating in 1781) and the Boston Latin school, Bulfinch spent several years studying and traveling in Europe before settling in Boston in 1787, where he became the first professional architect to practice.

Bulfinch was a central figure in the modernization of Boston, serving as chairman of the board of selectmen for twenty-one years (1797–1818). In this capacity, he acted as chief magistrate and influenced the city's infrastructure, including the reorganization of fire and police departments, the implementation of street-lighting and drainage systems, and the widening and straightening of streets. His architectural works in Boston included the "new" State House (1798) and the old Federal Street theatre (1793). 

Beyond architecture, Bulfinch helped promote the 1787 voyage of the ship *Columbia*, commanded by Captain Robert Gray, which was the first American-flagged vessel to sail around the world. In 1818, he became the architect of the National Capitol in Washington, succeeding B.H. Latrobe. Bulfinch designed the new portico and western approach and completed the central portion and unfinished wings, constructing the rotunda based on his own plans and suggestions from Latrobe. He returned to Boston in 1830 and died there on April 15, 1844. His son, Stephen Greenleaf Bulfinch (1809–1870), was an author and Unitarian clergyman.

## Other Notable Figures and Events
* **Francesco Bartolozzi (1725–1815):** An Italian engraver born in Florence. After studying painting and engraving in Venice and Rome, he moved to London in 1764, where he spent nearly forty years producing numerous engravings, including works based on the art of Angelica Kauffmann and Cipriani, as well as plates for Boydell's *Shakespeare Gallery*. He ended his career as director of the National Academy in Lisbon, Portugal.
* **Downing:** A diplomat and official who served under Charles II. He was knighted in May 1660 and rewarded with land that became Downing Street. He was known for his hostility toward the Dutch and his support of the Navigation Act of 1660. He was later sent to Holland to incite war between England and Holland to further French policy, though he eventually fled back to England due to extreme unpopularity.
* **The Geraldines:** In 16th-century Ireland, conflict existed between the Earl of Desmond and the Earl of Ormonde. Desmond was detained in England until 1564; upon his return, he and Ormonde engaged in open war, culminating in a battle at Affane where Desmond was defeated and captured. Later, James Fitzmaurice Fitzgerald led a rebellion with an ultra-Catholic character to seek foreign assistance.
