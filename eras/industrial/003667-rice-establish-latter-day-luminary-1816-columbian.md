# Rice and the Baptist Missionary Enterprises

## The Role of Luther Rice
Luther Rice was a central figure in the development of American Baptist missionary efforts during the early 19th century. In 1812, Rice, along with Adoniram Judson and others, was sent by the newly-formed American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions to establish missionary work in India. During this period, Rice and Judson converted to Baptist views. Following this conversion, Judson called upon his American brethren to support missionary work among the heathen, while Rice returned to the United States to organize missionary societies and generate interest in Judson's mission.

## Organization of Missionary Societies
The efforts of Rice led to the formation of "The Baptist Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in India and other Foreign Parts" in Boston in January 1813. This was quickly followed by the establishment of similar societies across the Southern, Middle, and Eastern states. 

The growing need for a national organization resulted in a meeting in Philadelphia in May 1814. Thirty-three delegates from eleven states gathered to organize the "General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions." Because the convention's meetings were scheduled to occur every three years, the organization became known as the "Triennial Convention." The convention appointed a Board of Commissioners, which was headquartered in Philadelphia until 1826, when the headquarters were transferred to Boston.

## Educational and Home Mission Expansion
The Triennial Convention recognized a profound need for a larger supply of educated ministers to serve both in home and foreign mission work. This necessity led to the establishment of Columbian College in Washington in 1821. Intended as a national Baptist institution, the college included a theological department. (The institution is now known as George Washington University).

In addition to foreign missions, the Triennial Convention began engaging extensively in home mission work from 1817 onward due to destitution found on the frontiers. This work was further formalized in 1832 with the constitution of the American Baptist Home Mission Society.
