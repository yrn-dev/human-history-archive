# Richard Grenville, 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos

Richard Grenville, the 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos (1823–1889), was a British politician and peer who held several high-ranking administrative and parliamentary positions throughout his career. He was the only son of the 2nd Duke.

## Early Life and Education
Grenville received his education at Eton and Christ Church, Oxford. Before succeeding to the dukedom, he held the title of Marquess of Chandos. In this capacity, he served as a representative for the borough of Buckingham in parliament from 1846 until 1857.

## Professional and Political Career
Grenville's early professional experience included a leadership role in the transport sector, serving as the chairman of the London & North-Western railway between 1853 and 1861. 

Following his succession to the dukedom, he entered the Conservative government of 1866–1868, where he held two significant positions: first as the lord president of the council, and subsequently as the secretary for the colonies. Later in his career, from 1875 to 1880, he served as the governor of Madras. In 1886, he was chosen as the chairman of committees in the House of Lords.

## Personal Life and Succession
The Duke was married twice and had three daughters. Because he left no son, the dukedom became extinct upon his death in 1889. However, several other titles were passed to his relatives:
*   The Scottish barony of Kinloss, for which he established his title in 1868, passed to his eldest daughter, Mary, who was the wife of Captain L. F. H. C. Morgan.
*   The earldom of Temple passed to his nephew, William Stephen Gore-Langton.
*   The viscounty of Cobham passed to his kinsman, Charles George, the 5th Baron Lyttelton.

Following the Duke's death, his widow married the 1st Earl Egerton of Tatton in 1894.
