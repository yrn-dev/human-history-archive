# Banking Inquiry of 1875 and the Provincial Banks

## The House of Commons Banking Inquiry
In 1875, a select committee of the House of Commons conducted a banking inquiry. As part of this process, Sir R. H. Inglis Palgrave provided evidence regarding the status of provincial banks in England and Wales. The resulting report included an Appendix 14, which provided statistics on the proportion of bank deposits relative to notes in circulation across various districts of the country.

## Impact of the Act of 1844
Prior to 1844, the majority of provincial banks in England and Wales operated as banks of issue. However, the Act of 1844 restricted their power to issue notes. Sir R. H. Inglis Palgrave testified during the 1875 inquiry that, due to these restrictions, these banks possessed a property in their issues that they were able to use, but were unable to sell.

## Trends in Circulation and Deposits
The 1875 inquiry examined data dating back to 1844, utilizing statements supplied by twenty-one banks. These institutions were located in a variety of environments, including agricultural districts, industrial and manufacturing areas, and mixed commercial and agricultural districts.

The data revealed a significant decline in the proportion of circulation relative to banking deposits in recent years. While the circulation diminished, deposits grew substantially in several instances:
* In one case, deposits increased three-fold.
* In other cases, deposits became five times, nearly seven times, or nearly twelve and a half times larger.

Specific examples of the diminishing proportion of circulation to deposits include:
* One instance where the proportion remained at about one-third of deposits for five consecutive years before falling to 9% by the end of the term.
* Another instance where the proportion dropped from 22% to 1-1/2% of the total.

The inquiry concluded that in all cases where detailed information was provided, the proportion of circulation to deposits had diminished greatly.

## Related Parliamentary Reports
The 1875 report on banks of issue was part of a broader history of parliamentary oversight regarding banking. Other relevant House of Commons reports and committees include:
* The Select Committee on the High Price of Gold Bullion (June 8, 1810).
* The Committee of Secrecy on the Bank of England Charter (1832).
* Select Committees on Banks of Issue in 1840 and 1841.
* The Secret Committee on Commercial Distress (1848).
* Select Committees on Bank Acts in 1857 and 1858.

Additionally, the House of Lords held a Secret Committee during the 1847-1848 session to investigate the causes of distress among commercial classes and the influence of laws regulating the issue of bank notes payable on demand.
