# Sir Joseph Barnby

## Early Life and Education
Sir Joseph Barnby (1838–1896) was an English conductor and musical composer. Born on August 12, 1838, in York, he was the son of Thomas Barnby, who served as an organist. Barnby's musical training began at a young age; he was a chorister at York Minster starting at age seven. He later pursued further education at the Royal Academy of Music, where he studied under Charles Lucas and Cipriani Potter.

## Professional Career and Appointments
Barnby's professional career included several prominent appointments in London and beyond. In 1862, he was appointed as the organist of St Andrew's on Wells Street in London, a position where he is credited with raising the services to a high level of excellence. In 1864, he became the conductor of "Barnby's Choir." 

His leadership in choral music expanded in 1871 when he succeeded Gounod as the conductor of the Albert Hall Choral Society, a role he maintained until his death. In 1875, he served as the director of music and precentor at Eton. Later, in 1892, he became the principal of the Guildhall School of Music. In July of that same year, he was granted the honor of knighthood.

## Musical Works and Conducting
As a composer, Barnby produced a wide variety of works. These include the oratorio *Rebekah*, *Ps. xcvii.*, numerous anthems and services, and pieces for the organ. He also composed part-songs, including the popular "Sweet and Low." Additionally, he wrote two hundred and forty-six hymn-tunes, which were published in a single volume in 1897.

As a conductor, Barnby was noted for his precision and rectitude, qualities he instilled in the singers under his direction, though he was described as lacking higher ideality or imagination. He had little practical sympathy for opera, yet he organized a notable concert performance of *Parsifal* at London's Albert Hall in 1884. He also conducted the Cardiff Festivals in 1892 and 1895. Furthermore, he played a significant role in encouraging the less educated members of the London public to appreciate the sacred music of Gounod.

## Death and Legacy
Sir Joseph Barnby died in London on January 28, 1896. Following a special service held at St Paul's Cathedral, he was buried in Norwood Cemetery.
