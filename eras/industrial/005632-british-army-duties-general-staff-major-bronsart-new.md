# The Duties of the General Staff and the British Army

The organization and operational guidance of the British army have been influenced by comprehensive treatises on military administration, most notably the work of the Prussian general Paul Bronsart von Schellendorf.

## The Influence of Paul Bronsart von Schellendorf
Paul Bronsart von Schellendorf (1832–1891) was a Prussian general born in Danzig. His military career included service in the Prussian Guards starting in 1849 and multiple appointments to the general staff, beginning as a captain in 1861. He served as a lecturer at the war academy and held the rank of major in 1865 and lieutenant-colonel in 1869. During the war of 1870, he served as the chief of a section on the Great General Staff and managed the preliminary negotiations for the French surrender at Sedan.

Following the war, Bronsart rose through the ranks to become colonel and chief of staff of the Guard army corps, major-general in 1876, and lieutenant-general in 1881. As war minister from 1883 to 1889, he oversaw significant Prussian army reforms, including the introduction of the magazine rifle.

Bronsart authored two highly influential military works: *Ein Rückblick auf die taktischen Ruckblicke* (1870) and *Der Dienst des Generalstabes* (first published in 1876). The latter is a comprehensive treatise on the duties of the general staff. The third edition of this work was translated into English and officially issued to the British army under the title *The Duties of the General Staff*. A subsequent new edition produced by the author's son, Major Bronsart von Schellendorf, in 1904 was also reissued in English by the General Staff in 1905.

## British Brigade Organization
In the British military structure, a brigade is a unit commanded by a colonel, brigadier-general, or major-general. It is composed of two or more regiments of artillery, cavalry, or infantry. 

The specific composition of these brigades varies by branch:
*   **Infantry Brigades:** Typically consist of four battalions (approximately 4,000 bayonets) with attached medical, transport, and supply units.
*   **Cavalry Brigades:** Comprised of two or three cavalry regiments.
*   **Artillery Brigades:** These are smaller units in Great Britain, commanded by a lieutenant-colonel and consisting of two or three batteries.

The staff of a cavalry or infantry brigade generally includes the commanding brigadier, a brigade-major, and an aide-de-camp. The brigade-major serves as a staff officer with duties that fall between those of a general staff officer and an adjutant.

## Staff Roles and Historical Precedents
The British army has utilized various specialized staff roles to manage complex operations. Historically, the "corporal of the field" was a title used in the 16th and early 17th centuries for a superior officer acting as a staff officer to the sergeant-major-general.

Prominent figures in British military history have held critical staff positions:
*   **William Cadogan, 1st Earl Cadogan:** Served as a confidential staff officer and right-hand man to Marlborough. As quartermaster-general, he organized the allies' march to the Danube and the subsequent return march. He also acted as Marlborough's chief of staff at Blenheim.
*   **Sir Benjamin d'Urbán:** Served in various staff offices and was appointed quartermaster-general during the reorganization of the Portuguese army by General Beresford. He later served as deputy quartermaster-general at the Horse Guards and colonel of the royal staff corps.
*   **Sir John Fox Burgoyne:** Served on the staff of General Fox in Sicily and later became the commanding engineer on Lord Wellington's staff following the battle of Vittoria.
*   **Sir James Kempt:** Served as an aide-de-camp to Sir Ralph Abercromby in Holland and Egypt, and later served on the staff in North America from 1807 to 1811.
*   **Viscount Horatio Herbert Kitchener:** Served on the staff of the British expeditionary force on the Nile and later served as adjutant-general of the army from 1889 to 1892.
