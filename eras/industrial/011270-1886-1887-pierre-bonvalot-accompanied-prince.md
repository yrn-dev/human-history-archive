# Exploration of Tibet and Central Asia (Late 19th Century)

The late 19th century was marked by numerous attempts by European and American explorers to penetrate the Tibetan plateau and reach the city of Lhasa. These journeys contributed significantly to the fields of geography and Asiatic research, though many explorers were unable to enter the city of Lhasa due to its jealously-guarded portals.

## Early Expeditions and the Quest for Lhasa
Between 1886 and 1887, Pierre G. Bonvalot, accompanied by Prince Henri d'Orleans, crossed the Tibetan plateau from north to south. Despite this crossing, they failed to enter Lhasa. Bonvalot later documented his travels in works including *Through the Heart of Asia* (translated by Pitman, 1889) and *From Paris to Tonkin* (translated by Pitman, 1891). In a work titled *De Paris au Tonkin, à travers le Tibet inconnu* (1892), the itinerary of Bonvalot and Prince Henri d'Orléans is detailed as following a path through Semipalatinsk, Kulja, Korla, Lob-nor, Charkalyk, and Altyn Tagh, moving in almost a straight line to Tengri Nor, and continuing to Batang, Ta Tsien lu, Ning-yuan, Yun-nan-fu, Mong-tsŭ, and Tung-King.

Following these efforts, the American traveller W.W. Rockhill undertook Tibetan journeys from 1889 to 1891. Like Bonvalot, Rockhill attempted to reach Lhasa without success, though he earned a great reputation in the annals of Asiatic research through both his explorations and his writings.

## Mapping and Fieldwork (1891–1895)
In 1891, Hamilton Bower traveled from Leh to Peking. While he also failed to penetrate Lhasa, he succeeded in securing a splendid addition to existing Tibetan mapping with the help of a native surveyor. During the period of 1891 to 1893, the French explorer Dutreuil de Rhins worked in the field of Tibet, where he eventually sacrificed his life to his work. He is also credited with the work *L'Asie Centrale* (1889).

Other notable figures in the region during these years included George N. Curzon (later Lord Curzon), who was in the Pamirs, and St George Littledale. Littledale, accompanied by his wife, conducted his first great Tibetan journey ending at Peking. His second journey, occurring between 1894 and 1895, brought him almost within sight of the sacred walls of Lhasa, though he remained unable to pass inside.

## Major Contributions to Geography
Professor Sven Hedin, a Swede described as one of the greatest modern Asiatic explorers (excluding Prjevalsky), conducted extensive investigations from 1894 to 1896. His monumental record includes travels through Tibet and the deserts of Takla Makan, as well as investigations into the glacial regions of the Sarikol mountains. His findings were published in *Die Geog. wiss. Ergebnisse meiner Reisen in Zentralasien* (1900).

From 1896 to 1898, two British cavalry officers provided valuable service to geography by striking out new lines of exploration: Captain M.S. Wellby of the 18th Hussars and Captain H. Deasy of the 16th Lancers. Captain Deasy continued the Pamir triangulation into the sources of the Zarafshan and the plains of Kashgar. This triangulation had previously been carried across the Hindu Kush in 1895 by R.A. Wahab and Sir T.H. Holdich, Colonels of the Pamir Boundary Commission.
