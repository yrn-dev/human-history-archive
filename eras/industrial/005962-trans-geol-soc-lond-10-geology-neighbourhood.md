## Contributions of Dr. Buckland
Dr. Buckland provided numerous original contributions to the fields of Palaeontology and Geology. His documented works include studies on the structure of the Alps and their relationship to the Transition and Secondary Rocks of England (1821), as well as an account of fossil bones and teeth of the rhinoceros, hippopotamus, and elephant found in a Yorkshire cave at Kirkdale in 1821.

His research extended to specific fossil species and formations, including:
*   The "Great Fossil Lizard" known as Megalosaurus from Stonesfield.
*   The Cycadeoideae plant family discovered in the Oolite Quarries of the Isle of Portland.
*   The discovery of coprolites (fossil faeces) and a new species of Pterodactyle, both found in the Lias of Lyme Regis.
*   The quartz rock of Lickey Hill in Worcestershire.

Additionally, Buckland wrote on the evidence of glaciers in the North of England and Scotland. He also co-authored papers in the *Trans. Geol. Soc. Lond.*, one regarding the South-Western Coal District of England (with the Rev. W.D. Conybeare) and another regarding the geology of the coast of Dorset and the neighbourhood of Weymouth (with Sir H. De la Beche).

## Geological Exploration in South Africa
Andrew Geddes Bain (1797–1864), a Scottish native, contributed significantly to the geology of South Africa. After emigrating to Cape Colony in 1820, Bain initially worked as a saddler at Graaf Reinet and later commanded a provisional battalion during the Kaffir War of 1833–34. His transition to geology began while he was employed as a surveyor of military roads under the Royal Engineers, specifically while constructing a road through the Ecca Pass. This interest was furthered in 1837 when he was loaned Lyell's *Elements*.

Bain discovered various reptilia remains, including the *Dicynodon* from the Karroo Beds near Fort Beaufort, which was subsequently described by Owen. In 1852, Bain prepared the first comprehensive geological map of South Africa, which the Geological Society of London published in 1856.

## Regional Geological Surveys
The archive lists several authorities and publications focusing on the geology of specific nations and territories:

### Ireland and Belgium
Geological works on Ireland include an *Outline of the Geology of Ireland* (1838) and studies on the Silurian and Carboniferous Limestone fossils of Ireland (1844, 1846), the latter produced in collaboration with F. McCoy. There is also a 1842 notice comparing the fossils of the Mountain Limestone of Ireland with the Devonian System and those of Great Britain.

In Belgium, geological descriptions were provided by C. Dewalque (1880) and M. Mourlon (1880–1881). Other research includes J. Gosselet's *L'Ardenne* (1888), M. Bertrand's studies on the Boulonnais and the northern basin houiller (1894), and work by F.L. Cornet and A. Briart regarding the soil relief in Belgium following paleozoic times.

### Asia and the West Indies
Geological data for Asia Minor includes works by P. Tchihatcheff (1867–1869), as well as contributions by Schaffer, Philippson, and English. In Burma, W. Theobald wrote on the geology of Pegu (1874), while F. Noetling published on the technical exploitation of petroleum (1898) and the subdivision of the Tertiary System (1895).

Regarding Barbados, the *Quart. Journ. Geol. Soc. London* published research by A. J. Jukes-Browne and J. B. Harrison (1891–1892), J. W. Gregory (1895), G. F. Franks and J. B. Harrison (1898), and J. W. Spencer (1902), the latter of whom also included notes on Trinidad.
