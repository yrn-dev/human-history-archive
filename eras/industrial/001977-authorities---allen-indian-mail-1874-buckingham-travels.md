## Regional Administration and Conflict
Records concerning the pashalic of Baghdad indicate that the region remained nominally part of the Turkish empire for a period. During the first half of the 18th century, as Turkish power declined and the empire disintegrated, a governor-general named Ahmed Pasha established it as an independent pashalic. This independence was challenged by Nadir Shah, a usurper of the Persian throne, who attempted to annex the province back to Persia. Nadir Shah besieged the city, but Ahmed Pasha defended it with sufficient courage to force the invader to raise the siege after suffering great loss. Turkish authority over the pashalic was eventually restored during the first part of the 19th century.

## Notable Travellers and Authors
The archive contains detailed accounts of individuals who documented their travels and professional observations in the 19th century.

### James Silk Buckingham
Born near Falmouth on August 25, 1786, James Silk Buckingham was an English author and traveller who spent his youth at sea. In 1818, he established the *Calcutta Journal*. While initially successful, the publication's outspoken criticisms of the East India Company led to the suppression of the paper by acting governor-general John Adam and Buckingham's expulsion from India in 1823. Following a parliamentary case, the East India Company awarded him a pension of £200 a year as compensation. 

Upon returning to England, Buckingham launched the *Oriental Herald* (1824) and the *Athenaeum* (1828), though the latter was not successful. He served as a member of parliament for Sheffield from 1832 to 1837, where he advocated for social reform. A voluminous writer, he produced many books of travel based on his journeys through the East, America, and Europe. He received a civil list pension of £200 a year in 1851. He died in London on June 30, 1855, while working on a four-volume autobiography, of which two volumes were published.

### Sir Clements Robert Markham
Born on July 20, 1830, Sir Clements Robert Markham was an English geographer, author, and traveller. After entering the navy in 1844, he served on the Franklin search expedition in the Arctic regions (1850-1851) before retiring in 1852. His subsequent travels included Peru and the eastern Andes (1852-1854), and a return to South America in 1860-1861 to arrange for the introduction of the cinchona plant into India. 

Markham visited India and Ceylon in 1865-1866 to report on cinchona plantations and the Tinnevelly pearl-fishery. He served as a geographer on the Abyssinian expedition of 1867-68 and accompanied Sir George Nares to Greenland in 1874. Professionally, he was in charge of the geographical department of the Indian Office from 1867 to 1877 and served as secretary to the Royal Geographical Society from 1863 to 1888, later becoming its president in 1893. He was instrumental in securing funds for the National Antarctic Expedition under Captain Robert Scott in 1901.

## Linguistic and Scholarly Works
The archive notes the contributions of Arthur Coke Burnell (1840-1882), an English Sanskrit scholar. Burnell joined the Indian civil service in 1860, stationed in Madras, where he collected Sanskrit manuscripts, presenting 350 of them to the India library in 1870. His major works include the *Handbook of South Indian Palaeography* (1874) and the *Classified Index to the Sanskrit MSS. in the Palace at Tanjore* (1880). Burnell also collaborated with Sir Henry Yule to create *Hobson-Jobson*, a dictionary of Anglo-Indian colloquial words and phrases.
