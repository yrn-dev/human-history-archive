# Circulation in Water-Tube Boilers

In the design of water-tube boilers, where water is primarily contained within numerous small tubes rather than a large tank, special provisions must be made to maintain the circulation of water through the tubes. This is a critical distinction from "tank" boilers, where the outer shell is cylindrical and heating surfaces are surrounded by water, generally presenting no difficulty in maintaining contact between the water and the heating surfaces.

## The Thornycroft and Thornycroft-Schulz Boilers
The Thornycroft boiler utilizes three chambers connected by tubes that are straight for most of their length but bent at the ends to enter the water- and steam-chambers normally. In this design, large outside circulating pipes are provided at the front end of the boiler. The outer rows of tubes are arranged to form "water-walls" at their lower parts, while their upper ends permit the passage of gases.

The Thornycroft-Schulz boiler features four chambers and a fire-grate divided into two separate portions. Similar to the Thornycroft model, the two outermost rows of tubes on each side form water-walls at their lower sections and allow gases to pass between them at the upper sections. In this specific type, circulation in the outer sets of tubes is arranged for by outer circulating pipes of large diameter that connect the steam- and water-chambers. Additionally, the middle water-chamber is equipped with several nearly vertical down-comers located in the center of the boiler. These boilers are used extensively by the German and British navies.

## The Normand Boiler
The Normand boiler employs three chambers, similar to the Yarrow boiler, which are connected by a large number of bent tubes that serve as the heating surface. Circulation is facilitated by large outside circulating tubes connecting the chambers at each end. The two outer rows of heating tubes on each side are arranged to touch one another for nearly their entire length, creating a "water-wall" to protect the outer casing. These tubes enter the steam-chest at approximately the water-level.

## The Babcock & Wilcox Marine Boiler
Used in the British and American navies, as well as in merchant steamers and yachts, the Babcock & Wilcox marine boiler consists of a horizontal cylindrical steam-chest positioned transversely over a group of elements. Each element is composed of a front and back header connected by water-tubes. To facilitate circulation, these tubes have a considerable inclination. 

The front headers are connected to the steam-chest at their upper ends via short nipples and connected at the bottom to a square-section pipe extending across the boiler's width. Circulation is further aided by nearly vertical tubes connecting this cross-pipe to the bottom of the steam-chest. The back headers are connected to the steam-chest at their upper ends by two long horizontal tubes, while their bottom ends remain closed.

## Other Water-Tube Circulation Methods
Different boiler designs employ various mechanical means to ensure water movement:

*   **Durr Boiler:** Circulation is provided for by inner tubes (similar to the Niclausse boiler) and a diaphragm located in the water-chamber.
*   **Locomotive Boilers:** Some locomotive boilers incorporate water-tubes extending across the fire-box. For example, a boiler used by the London & South-Western Railway Co. utilizes 112 seamless steel water-tubes (2-3/4 in. diameter) arranged in two clusters of 56.
