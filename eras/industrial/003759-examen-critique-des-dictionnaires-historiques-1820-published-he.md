# Examen critique des dictionnaires historiques

The *Examen critique des dictionnaires historiques* is a work authored by the French librarian and bibliographer Antoine Alexandre Barbier. Published in 1820, only the first part of this specific work saw publication.

## Author Biography
Antoine Alexandre Barbier was born on January 11, 1765, in Coulommiers, Seine-et-Marne. He initially took priest's orders, though the pope eventually released him from these orders in 1801. Barbier's career was closely tied to the administration of libraries and the arts in France. In 1794, he served as a member of the temporary commission of the arts, where he was tasked with distributing books confiscated during the Revolution to various libraries in Paris. During this process, he discovered the manuscripts of the works of Fenelon as well as the letters of the bishop of Avranches, Huet.

Barbier held several prestigious positions as a librarian, serving successively the Conseil d'Etat, the Directory, and Napoleon in 1807, for whom he executed various commissions. He was also involved in the foundation of the libraries at Saint-Cloud, Compiegne, Fontainebleau, and the Louvre. Under the reign of Louis XVIII, he was appointed administrator of the king's private libraries. However, in 1822, he was deprived of all his offices. Barbier died in Paris on December 5, 1825.

## Other Works by Barbier
Beyond the *Examen critique des dictionnaires historiques*, Barbier produced a standard work titled *Dictionnaire des ouvrages anonymes et pseudonymes*. This work was published in four volumes between 1806 and 1809, with a third edition appearing between 1872 and 1879.

## Context of Historical and Biographical Dictionaries
The *Examen critique* exists within a broader tradition of biographical and historical dictionaries in Europe. Early contributions to the genre include the *Acta Sanctorum*, compiled by the Bollandists (first volume 1653), which focused on martyrs and saints and is generally considered the first biographical dictionary in Europe. In 1674, Louis Moreri's *Grand Dictionnaire* introduced a biographical section of a general character.

A significant milestone in the development of the modern biographical dictionary was Pierre Bayle's *Dictionnaire historique et critique*, published in 1696. While Bayle often used biographical entries as a means to develop his own controversial and philosophical views, the work was based on genuine research. This *Dictionnaire* was translated into English in 1734 and influenced the interest in biography in England.
