# MICHAEL ANDREAS BARCLAY DE TOLLY

Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly, known to the Russians as Michael, Prince Bogdanovich (1761–1818), was a Russian field marshal born in Livonia in 1761. He was a descendant of a Scottish family that had settled in Russia during the 17th century.

## Early Military Career
Barclay de Tolly entered the Russian army at an early age. His early service included campaigns against the Turks from 1788 to 1789, and subsequent actions against the Poles and Swedes in 1794 and 1790. He rose through the ranks of the military, achieving the rank of colonel in 1798 and major-general in 1799.

## Napoleonic Wars and Promotions
During the war of 1806 against Napoleon, Barclay de Tolly played a distinguished role in the battle of Pultusk. He was wounded at Eylau, and his conduct during that engagement earned him a promotion to lieutenant-general. 

In 1808, he held command against the Swedes in Finland. In 1809, he executed a daring and rapid march across the frozen Gulf of Bothnia, which allowed him to seize and surprise Umeo. Following these achievements, he was appointed minister of war in 1810, a position he held until 1813.

## Command and Controversy
In 1812, Barclay de Tolly was given command of one of the armies operating against Napoleon. His appointment as commander-in-chief met with keen opposition due to his status as a foreigner. Following a defeat at Smolensk, public outcry became so significant that he resigned his command. He subsequently took a subordinate position under the veteran Kutusov and was present at the battle of Borodino, though he left the army shortly thereafter.

## Later Campaigns and Honors
Barclay de Tolly returned to field employment in 1813, participating in the campaign in Germany. After the battle of Bautzen, he was reinstated as the commander-in-chief of the Russian forces. In this capacity, he served at Leipzig, Kulm, and Dresden. He was made a count following the battle of Leipzig.

In 1814, he participated in the invasion of France and was awarded the baton of a field marshal at Paris. In 1815, he again served as commander-in-chief of the Russian army that invaded France. At the conclusion of the war, he was granted the title of prince.

## Death
Prince Bogdanovich died on May 14th (16th), 1818, at Insterburg in Prussia.
