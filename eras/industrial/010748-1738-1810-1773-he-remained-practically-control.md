# Francis Asbury

## Early Life and Conversion
Francis Asbury was an American clergyman born on August 20, 1745, at Hamstead Bridge in the parish of Handsworth, near Birmingham, in Staffordshire, England. Born to poor parents, Asbury received a brief period of education at the village school of Barre. At the age of fourteen, he was apprenticed to a maker of tongues, also known as "buckle chapes." 

Asbury's entry into Methodism occurred during his youth; he became converted to the movement in his thirteenth year. It is considered probable that his parents were also among the early converts of Wesley. By the age of sixteen, Asbury had become a local preacher. He was characterized as a fluent and simple speaker, a style that led to his success and his subsequent enrollment as a regular itinerant minister by John Wesley in 1767.

## Missionary Work in America
In 1771, Asbury volunteered for missionary work in the American colonies. Upon his arrival in Philadelphia in October of that year, Methodism had only been introduced to the colonies for three years, and the number of converts was fewer than 300. Asbury provided new life to the movement, and within one year, the membership across several congregations more than doubled. 

Due to this success, John Wesley appointed Asbury as "general assistant" in 1772 to oversee the work in America. Although he was superseded in 1773 by an older preacher named Thomas Rankin (1738-1810), Asbury remained practically in control of the operations.

## The War of Independence and Exile
The outbreak of the War of Independence brought challenges to the Methodist movement, which by that time consisted of several thousand members. The group fell under suspicion of Loyalism, an accusation described as unjust, primarily because the Methodists refused to take the prescribed oath. This political climate led many ministers, including Thomas Rankin, to return to England.

Asbury chose to remain at his post, feeling that his duties and sympathies lay with the colonies. Despite being threatened and arrested once, he continued his itinerant preaching. Eventually, hostility from the authorities in Maryland forced him into exile in Delaware. He remained there for two years, during which time he continued to be active.

## Later Appointments and Leadership
In 1782, Asbury was reappointed to supervise the affairs of the Methodist congregations in America. In 1784, John Wesley took a radical step by disregarding the authority of the Established Church and appointing Francis Asbury and the Rev. Thomas Coke (1747-1814) as "bishops" or superintendents of the church in the United States. Asbury continued his work in this capacity until his death in 1816.
