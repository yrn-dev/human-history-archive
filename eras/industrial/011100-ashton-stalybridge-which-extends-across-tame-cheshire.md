# Ashton and Stalybridge

Ashton-under-Lyne and Stalybridge are located in England, situated along the river Tame, which is a tributary of the Mersey. The area is characterized by its extension across the Tame into the county of Cheshire.

## Ashton-under-Lyne
Ashton-under-Lyne is a market-town and a municipal and parliamentary borough of Lancashire. It is located 6-1/2 miles east of Manchester and 185 miles north-west by north from London. The town covers an area of 1,346 acres, with a recorded population of 40,486 in 1891 and 43,890 in 1901.

The town's infrastructure includes several railway connections: the London & North-Western and the Lancashire & Yorkshire railways (via Charlestown station), and the Great Central railway (via Park Parade station). Public amenities maintained in the town include a free library, a school of art, a technical school, and several hospitals.

## Local Landmarks and Governance
The town features the church of St Michael, which is built in the Perpendicular style, though it has been almost entirely rebuilt. Other notable sites include barracks and the Old Hall, which served as the manor house for the Asshetons. The Old Hall remains in an altered state and is joined by an ancient prison. The area known as Gallows Meadow serves as a reminder of the summary execution of justice carried out by the lords of the manor. While many modern residences have been built, a few picturesque old houses still remain.

The parliamentary borough returns one member and extends into Cheshire. The local corporation is composed of a mayor, 24 councillors, and 8 aldermen.

## Industry and Shared Spaces
The primary industries of Ashton-under-Lyne include iron-founding, machinery works, hat-making, and cotton-spinning. Additionally, there are large collieries located in the surrounding neighborhood.

A notable shared feature between the towns of Ashton and Stalybridge is Stamford Park, which was presented by Lord Stamford. This park extends across the river Tame into Cheshire.

## Related Regional Areas
In the vicinity of these towns lies Dukinfield, a municipal borough of Cheshire located 6 miles east of Manchester. Dukinfield is situated between Ashton-under-Lyne and Stalybridge and falls within the parliamentary borough of Stalybridge. Incorporated in 1899, Dukinfield covers 1,405 acres with a population of 18,929 as of 1901. 

Dukinfield's industrial landscape includes extensive collieries, engineering, iron-founding, cotton manufactures, calico-printing, hat-making, and the production of tiles and firebricks. It is served by the Great Central and London & North Western railways. A remnant of the old timbered Dukinfield Hall exists; its chapel was where Samuel Eaton (who died in 1665) taught the first congregational church in the north of England. This chapel has been enlarged and continues to be used by the same denomination. The borough's government consists of a mayor, 18 councillors, and 6 aldermen.
