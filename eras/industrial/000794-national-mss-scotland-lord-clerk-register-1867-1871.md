# National MSS. of Scotland (Lord Clerk Register), 1867-1871

The *Facsimiles of National MSS. of Scotland* is an official publication issued between 1867 and 1871. It was produced under the authority of the Lord Clerk Register.

## Context and Related Publications
The publication of the Scottish national manuscripts was part of a broader effort to produce official facsimiles of national documents across the British Isles. Similar official series include the *Facsimiles of National MSS., from William the Conqueror to Queen Anne*, published by the Master of the Rolls between 1865 and 1868, and the *Facsimiles of National MSS. of Ireland*, published by the Public Record Office in Ireland from 1874 to 1884.

These official works exist alongside various private collections of autograph facsimiles published in England. Examples of such private collections include *British Autography* by J. Thane (1788-1793), *Autographs of Royal, Noble, Learned and Remarkable Personages in English History* by J. G. Nichols (1829), and several works by J. Netherclift and his son, such as *Autographs of the Kings and Queens and Eminent Men of Great Britain* (1835). Other notable private publications include *Facsimiles of Original Documents of Eminent Literary Characters* (1852) and the British Museum's *Facsimiles of Autographs* series (1896-1900).

## The Office of the Lord Clerk Register
The *Facsimiles of National MSS. of Scotland* were issued by the Lord Clerk Register. This office has been held by various figures in Scottish history. For example, Sir James Balfour (d. 1583 or 1584), a Scottish judge and politician who served as a secretary to Mary, was appointed lord-clerk-register in 1566 before later resigning the post to become president of the court of session. 

In the 18th century, the office was held by Alexander Campbell (formerly Hume), the 2nd Earl of Marchmont (1676-1740), who served as lord clerk register from 1716 to 1733. His son, Hugh Hume, the 3rd Earl of Marchmont (1708-1794), also later served as the lord clerk register of Scotland.
