# Ludwig van Beethoven

Ludwig van Beethoven (1770–1827) was a German musical composer. He was baptized in Bonn on December 17, 1770, likely the day after his birth. His family origins can be traced back to the 17th century in a village near Louvain, Belgium, with a lineal ancestor settling in Antwerp in 1650.

## Early Life and Education in Bonn
Beethoven's grandfather, Louis, arrived in Bonn in 1732 and served as a court musician for the archbishop-elector of Cologne. Although Louis died when Ludwig was only four years old, Beethoven cherished his portrait throughout his life. His father, a tenor singer at the same court, was characterized by a rough and violent temper and a passion for drink, and the family lived in dire poverty. His mother, Magdelina Leim (or Laym), was the daughter of the chief cook at Ehrenbreitstein and the widow of a *valet-de-chambre* of the elector of Trier.

Beethoven's father began a severe musical training for his son, focusing especially on the violin, when he was five years old. Around this time, the family moved from the house of his birth, located at 515 Bonngasse. By age nine, Beethoven began clavier lessons with a singer named Pfeiffer and received some general education from a man named Zambona. He also studied the pianoforte and organ under Van den Eeden, the court organist. Beethoven's progress was so rapid that when C.G. Neefe took over the post in 1781, Beethoven was soon able to act as his deputy. In 1783, with Neefe's permission, he published his first extant composition, a set of variations on a march by Dressler; the title page indicates these were written in 1780 when he was ten years old.

## Development and Move to Vienna
In the spring of 1787, Beethoven visited Vienna for three months, where he received a few lessons from Mozart and impressed him with his extemporizations. He returned to Bonn due to the illness of his mother, who died in July; a baby girl born to her died in November. For the next five years, Beethoven supported his family and became the practical head of the household at age fifteen, as his father's bad habits worsened. In 1789, Ludwig was officially entrusted with his father's salary. During this time, he formed lifelong friendships with Stephan Breuning and Count Waldstein.

By 1792, the archbishop-elector provided for Beethoven's second visit to Vienna. He was introduced to the Viennese aristocracy by the archbishop-elector and Count Waldstein. His playing, extemporization, and the use of the prefix "van" in his name—though not a title of nobility—helped him secure the respect and affection of the musical aristocracy, who viewed his rough manners as signs of a noble and passionate nature.

## Professional Studies and Relationships
In July 1792, Haydn passed through Bonn and was so struck by Beethoven that he likely instigated the archbishop's decision to send Beethoven to Vienna to study under him. However, the relationship was strained; Beethoven found Haydn perfunctory in correcting exercises, while Haydn disliked Beethoven's manners and the audacity of his free compositions. Consequently, Haydn did not take Beethoven to England in 1794. Beethoven also studied strict counterpoint with Albrechtsberger, a grammarian who held a poor opinion of him but was diligent in his work.

## Later Life and Health
Beethoven suffered from deafness, though it affected his conversation before it impacted his music. He was still able to conduct his opera *Fidelio* in 1806 and performed as a player as late as 1814 with his B flat trio, *op.* 97. However, an attempt to conduct in November 1822 proved disastrous. He used ear-trumpets to play for himself, though his friends noted that he sometimes played notes too softly to be heard or used an out-of-tune pianoforte. In 1817-1818, he received a pianoforte from Broadwood, which brought him great pleasure.

In his final years, Beethoven faced straitened circumstances. The London Philharmonic Society, for whom he wrote his 9th symphony and projected a 10th, sent him £100 in advance of a benefit concert. He refused to use money he had deposited in the bank for his nephew. This nephew, the son of Beethoven's brother Kaspar Karl, was a source of great distress for the composer during the last twelve years of his life. Beethoven fought the boy's mother in court for guardianship, but the nephew proved unworthy of this devotion, failing his examinations and eventually joining the army after an attempted suicide.

## Major Works
Beethoven's compositions include:
*   **Symphonies:** The 9th symphony (*op.* 125), which involved a project to set Schiller's *Freude* that began before he left Bonn.
*   **Operas:** *Fidelio*.
*   **Choral Works:** The mass in D (*Missa Solemnis*), *op.* 123, begun in 1818 and finished in 1826.
*   **Pianoforte Works:** The last sonata, *op.* 111 (1823); 33 variations on a waltz by Diabelli; and 6 bagatelles, *op.* 126.
*   **String Quartets:** *Op.* 127 (1824), *op.* 130 (1825) with finale *op.* 133 (grand fugue), *op.* 132 (1825) featuring a *Heiliger Dankgesang*, *op.* 131 (1826), and *op.* 135 (1826). His final composition was a new finale to *op.* 130.

## Legacy in Bonn
The city of Bonn maintains several memorials to the composer. A statue of Beethoven was erected in the Munster-platz in 1845. The house where he was born (515 Bonngasse) was opened as a museum of Beethoven relics in 1889, containing a collection of manuscripts and relics. Additionally, a collection of his ear-trumpets is preserved in Bonn.
