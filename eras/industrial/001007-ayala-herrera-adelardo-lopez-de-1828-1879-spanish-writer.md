# AYALA Y HERRERA, ADELARDO LOPEZ DE (1828-1879)

## Early Life and Education
Adelardo Lopez de Ayala y Herrera was a Spanish politician and writer born on May 1, 1828, in Guadalcanal. His interest in the theatre began at a very early age, where he started writing plays for the theatre in his native town. Because traveling companies did not visit Guadalcanal and women did not participate in theatrical representations, his early juvenile performances were written exclusively for men. These early works included *La Corona y el Punal*, *Me voy a Sevilla*, and *Salga por donde saliere*. 

Ayala later introduced a change to these local traditions by persuading his sister to play the heroine in his comedy *La primera Dama*. While this innovation scandalized some residents of his town, it allowed him to develop his talent more freely. In his twentieth year, he matriculated at the university of Seville, though his time as a student there was undistinguished. During his stay in Seville, he met Garcia Gutierrez, a playwright who is reported to have provided Ayala with the benefit of his own experience and encouraged his ambitions in drama.

## Professional Career in Madrid
In early 1850, Ayala left the university and moved to Madrid to pursue a career as a professional dramatist. Despite lacking influence or friends in the city, he quickly found success. On January 25, 1851, the managers of the Teatro Espanol produced *Un Hombre de Estado*, a four-act play in verse, which became a remarkable success. This production secured Ayala's popularity and position.

Within a year, he increased his visibility with the production of *Castigo y Perdon* and a more humorous work titled *Los dos Guzmanes*.

## Political Activity and Later Works
Ayala's professional life was intertwined with the political climate of Spain. He was appointed to a post in the home office by the *Moderado* government, a position he held until 1854, when he lost the post following the accession of the Liberal party to power. Between 1854 and 1856, he was an active participant in a political campaign conducted through the journal *El Padre Cobos*. During this period, Ayala shifted his political allegiance from the Moderates to the Progressives.

In 1854, he produced *Rioja*, which is considered perhaps the most admirable and most admired of all his works. Additionally, Ayala collaborated with Emilio Arrieta on *zarzuelas*, producing *El Agente de Matrimonios* and *Guerta a muerte* in 1855.
