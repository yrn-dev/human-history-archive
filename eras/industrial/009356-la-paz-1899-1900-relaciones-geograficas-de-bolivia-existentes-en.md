# La Paz and Bolivian Geographic Records (1898–1900)

During the turn of the 20th century, La Paz emerged as the commercial metropolis of Bolivia and served as the national capital following the revolution of 1898, replacing Sucre. The city functioned as a central hub for the republic's administration, commerce, and the publication of extensive geographic and statistical records.

## Geographic and Statistical Publications
Between 1896 and 1900, La Paz was the site of significant scholarly and administrative output regarding the nation's territory and resources. Key works published in the city during this period include:
*   *Apuntes sobre la industria de goma elastica, &c.* (1896) by M.V. Ballivian.
*   *Relaciones Geograficas de Bolivia existentes en el Archivo de la Oficina Nacional de Inmigracion, &c.* (1898).
*   *Breves Indicaciones para el Inmigrante y el Viajero a Bolivia* (1898).
*   *Monografias de la Industria Minera en Bolivia*, published in three parts between 1899 and 1900.
*   *Noticia Politica, Geografica, Industrial, y Estadistica de Bolivia* (1900).
*   *Diccionario Geografico de la Republica de Bolivia* (1900), authored by M.V. Ballivian and Eduardo Idiaquez.

Additionally, the city hosted the *Boletin de la Oficina Nacional de Inmigracion, Estadistica y Propaganda Geografica*. These records contributed to a broader body of knowledge that included the "Map of the Andes of La Paz" (1:600,000) produced by Sir Martin Conway in 1900.

## Regional Geography and Climate
La Paz is situated near the headwaters of the Beni River, which begins as the Rio de La Paz in the elevated Cordilleras. The region surrounding the city is characterized by diverse climatic zones. The *yungas* valleys of La Paz are tropical, humid lowlands and mountain valleys below 5,000 feet. Above these lie the *valle* (5,000 to 9,500 feet), the temperate *cabezera de valle* (9,500 to 11,000 feet), and the *puna* (11,000 to 12,500 feet), which comprises the great central plateau. The highest reaches, the *puna brava*, extend from 12,500 feet to the snow limit at approximately 17,500 feet.

## Economic Resources and Agriculture
The departments of La Paz and Cochabamba are noted for the cultivation of specific agricultural products. Tobacco of fair quality and superior grade cacao are grown in the warm Andean valleys of La Paz. Furthermore, coca—a leaf highly esteemed by natives for mastication and exported for medicinal purposes—is extensively cultivated in the province of Yungas within the department of La Paz.

## Transportation and Communications
At the turn of the century, La Paz faced significant isolation, which the government sought to remedy through railway construction. A short line of 54 miles was constructed by the Peruvian Corporation from Guaqui on Lake Titicaca to Alto de La Paz. From Alto de La Paz, the city of La Paz—located 1,493 feet below—is connected by a 5-mile electric line. This network provided La Paz with access to the Peruvian port of Mollendo, located 496 miles away.

Following a treaty ratified in 1905, the government of Chile agreed to construct a railway at its own cost from Arica to La Paz, covering a distance of 295 miles. By 1907, further railway projects were envisioned, including lines from La Paz to Cochabamba and from La Paz to the navigable waters of the Beni.

## Religious Administration
In the ecclesiastical organization of the republic, La Paz serves as the seat of one of the three bishoprics. The other two bishoprics are located in Cochabamba and Santa Cruz, while the archbishopric of La Plata is seated at Sucre.
