# BRIZEUX, JULIEN AUGUSTE PÉLAGE (1803-1858)

## Early Life and Education
Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux was a French poet born on September 12, 1803, at Lorient (Morbihan). He came from a family of Irish origin that had been settled in Brittany for a long period. Although he was educated for a career in law, Brizeux pursued literary interests, producing a one-act verse comedy titled *Racine* in 1827. This work was created in collaboration with Philippe Busoni and presented at the Théâtre Français.

## Italian Influence and Translations
Brizeux's work was significantly shaped by his travels to Italy. He made a journey to the country in the company of Auguste Barbier, which left a great impression on him. He returned for a second visit in 1834. These experiences led to the 1841 publication of a complete translation of the *Divina Commedia*, written in *terza rima*. In 1852, he published *Primel el Nola*, which included a collection of poems written under Italian influence titled *Les Ternaires* (1841).

## Breton Themes and Folklore
Brizeux drew extensive inspiration from his native province of Brittany. In 1836, he wrote the rustic idyl *Marie*, which focused on country life in Brittany. He further explored the legends and folklore of the region in his 1845 work, *Les Bretons*. Additionally, he utilized the Breton dialect in his 1844 work, *Telen-Aroor*.

## Academic Contributions and Later Work
Beyond his poetry, Brizeux was an ardent student of archaeology and philology regarding Brittany. He engaged in the collection of materials intended for a dictionary of Breton place-names. His *Histoires poétiques*, published in 1855, was crowned by the French Academy. His overall body of work is described as small in bulk but characterized by sincerity and simplicity.

## Death
Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux died at Montpellier on May 3, 1858.
