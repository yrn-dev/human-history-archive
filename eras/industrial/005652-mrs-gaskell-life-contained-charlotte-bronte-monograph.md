# The Life of Charlotte Brontë by Mrs Gaskell

The *Life of Charlotte Brontë* is a biography written by Mrs Gaskell and first published in 1857. It is recognized as one of the chief literary lives of its subject. The work presents the story of the Brontës in a manner described as being as interesting as any novel, capturing the pathos and romance of their forlorn lives.

## Publication and Revisions
Following its initial release in 1857, the *Life* aroused several controversial questions. These disputes concerned the identity of the school "Lowood" in the novel *Jane Eyre* (specifically its connection to Cowan Bridge school), the nature of the relationship between Branwell Brontë and the wife of his employer, and supposed peculiarities regarding Mr Brontë, among other minor points. Due to these controversies, the third edition of the biography was considerably changed.

The work has been reprinted many times. The Haworth edition of 1902, issued by the original publishers Smith, Elder & Co., is considered the most satisfactory form of the text. This specific edition includes a large number of letters written by Charlotte Brontë to her publisher, George Smith.

## Relationship between Mrs Gaskell and Charlotte Brontë
Mrs Gaskell and Charlotte Brontë shared a personal acquaintance that developed in the mid-19th century. They first met in August 1850 when Charlotte visited the English lakes as a guest of Sir James Kay-Shuttleworth. Later, in 1851, Charlotte visited Manchester and spent two days with Mrs Gaskell.

Their connection continued through mutual visits. In September 1853, Mrs Gaskell visited Charlotte at Haworth. In May 1854, Charlotte returned the visit, staying for three days in Manchester. During this visit, Charlotte and her hostess planned the details of Charlotte's marriage to Arthur Bell Nicholls, the curate to Mr Brontë.

## Subsequent Biographical Works
While Mrs Gaskell's *Life* was the primary biography, other works later supplemented or expanded upon the record of Charlotte Brontë's life:

*   **T. Wemyss Reid**: Published *Charlotte Brontë: a Monograph* in 1877, which provided the first new material to supplement Mrs Gaskell's work. This monograph subsequently inspired A.C. Swinburne to write *A Note on Charlotte Brontë* (1877).
*   **Augustine Birrell**: Published *Life of Charlotte Brontë* in 1887, providing further interesting details.
*   **Clement Shorter**: Produced several works, including *Charlotte Brontë and her Circle* (1896), *Charlotte Brontë and her Sisters* (1906), and *The Brontës: Life and Letters* (1907), the latter of which contains the complete letters of Charlotte Brontë in chronological order.
*   **William Wright, D.D.**: Wrote *The Brontës in Ireland* in 1893.
*   **Butler Wood**: Edited the Brontë Society publications between 1895 and 1907.
