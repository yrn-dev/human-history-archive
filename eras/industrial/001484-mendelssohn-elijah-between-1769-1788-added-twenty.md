# Historical Figures and Events (1769–1788)

The period between 1769 and 1788 was marked by significant political transitions in the Americas, the careers of various European intellectuals and artists, and the publication of influential academic works.

## Political and Territorial Developments
In the Americas, the late 18th century saw a shift in colonial governance. In 1769, Spanish domination began in certain territories, with Alejandro O'Reilly serving from 1769 to 1770, followed by Luis de Unzaga (1770–1777) and Bernardo de Galvez (1777–1785). 

In the newly formed United States, Rufus King emerged as a prominent political leader. Born in 1755, King was admitted to the bar in 1780 and served in the Confederation Congress from 1784 to 1787. He was instrumental in calling for the Philadelphia constitutional convention on February 21, 1787. In 1788, King was an influential member of the Massachusetts convention that ratified the Federal Constitution. That same year, he moved to New York City, where he had married Mary Alsop (born 1769) in 1786. By 1789, King was elected to the New York Assembly and subsequently became one of the first representatives of New York in the United States Senate.

## European Intellectuals and Artists
The era saw the contributions of several notable figures in science, music, and the arts. George Louis Leclerc, Comte de Buffon, a French naturalist born in 1707, spent his later years as a private individual after the death of his wife, Marie Françoise de Saint-Belin, in 1769. Buffon died in Paris on April 15, 1788, at the age of eighty-one.

In music, Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714–1788), the third son of Johann Sebastian Bach, was a renowned clavier-player and composer. During his time in Berlin, he produced various works, including *Oden mit Melodien* (1762) and *Sing-Oden* (1766). He also authored a masterly treatise, *Versuch ueber die wahre Art das Clavier zu spielen*, which reached its third edition by 1780. Bach died in 1788.

In the performing arts, Magdeleine Marie Louise Desgarcins (1769–1797) became a leading French tragédienne. She made her debut at the Comédie Française on May 24, 1788, in the production *Bajazet*, achieving immediate success and becoming a *sociétaire*.

Other notable figures of the period include the adventurer Casanova, who published *Confutazione della storia del governo Veneto d'Amelot de la Houssaye* in 1769 and the *Histoire* of his escape from prison in 1788. Additionally, Paul d'Albert de Luynes, a cardinal and astronomer, died in 1788, and Michel Ferdinand d'Albert d'Ailly, a writer on mathematical instruments, died in 1769.

## Biblical Narratives of Elijah
Archive records concerning the biblical figure Elijah focus on narratives of the northern kingdom. ## Academic Publications
The late 18th century also saw the progression of major reference works. The second edition of the Encyclopædia Britannica was published in ten volumes between 1777 and 1784. This was followed by a third edition, consisting of eighteen volumes, published between 1788 and 1797.
