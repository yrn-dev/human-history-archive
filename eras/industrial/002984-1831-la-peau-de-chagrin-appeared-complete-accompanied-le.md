# La Peau de chagrin

*La Peau de chagrin* is a literary work characterized by some estimates as one of the greatest, though also described as one of the crudest. It is noted for its romantic extravagance and surplusage, centered around the powerfully worked-out idea of the Nemesis of accomplished desire.

## Publication History
The work was not published in full immediately. In 1830, which is described as the epoch year, part of *La Peau de chagrin* was released. It appeared in its complete form in 1831.

## Associated Works of 1831
Upon its complete appearance in 1831, *La Peau de chagrin* was accompanied by several other titles:
* *Le Requisitionnaire*
* *Les Proscrits*
* *Le Chef-d'oeuvre inconnu* (described as a masterpiece that was fortunately not unrecognized)
* *Jesus Christ en Flandre*
* *Maitre Cornelius*

## Context of the Author's Later Life
The author's life was marked by enormous labors that weakened his health, which was further ruined by his journeyings across Europe and the Russian cold. In his personal life, he had a long-term engagement with Madame Hanska, who had formally promised to marry him in 1833 provided her husband died. Following her husband's death at the end of 1841, the engagement was renewed after a fashion during a trip to St Petersburg, though its fulfillment was indefinitely postponed for several years. During this time, the author met her in various cities, including Paris, Rome, Brussels, Wiesbaden, and Baden.

In September 1847, he was invited to her estate of Wierzschovnia in the Ukraine as her future husband. This visit lasted more than two years. The wedding finally took place on March 14, 1850.

## Death and Burial
Following the wedding, the author and his wife moved to a house in Paris on the rue Fortunee, which the author had purchased for her and filled with his collections. By this time, the author's own "Peau de chagrin" was reduced to its last morsel. 

He died at half-past eleven on the night of Sunday, August 17, attended by his mother but not by his wife; Victor Hugo found him dying. He was buried on August 20 in Pere La Chaise. His pall-bearers included Dumas, Hugo, the statesman Baroche, and Sainte-Beuve, the latter of whom was an enemy but acted as a generous one in this instance. Hugo delivered a speech at the burial.
