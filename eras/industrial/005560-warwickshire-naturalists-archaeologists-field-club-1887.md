# Warwickshire Naturalists' and Archaeologists' Field Club

## Foundation and Leadership
The Warwickshire Naturalists' and Archaeologists' Field Club was established in 1854. The chief founder of the organization was Peter Bellinger Brodie (1815–1897), an English geologist. Brodie was a man of diverse professional and academic backgrounds; he had attended Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and entered the church in 1838. His ecclesiastical career included roles as a curate at Steeple Claydon in Buckinghamshire and Wylye in Wiltshire, and as the rector of Down Hatherley in Gloucestershire. By 1855, he became the rural dean and vicar of Rowington in Warwickshire.

## Connection to Regional Natural History
The Field Club existed alongside other scientific and historical organizations in the region. Peter Bellinger Brodie, in addition to founding the Field Club, was an active member of the Warwickshire Natural History and Archaeological Society, as well as the Cotteswold Naturalists' Club. 

Brodie's contributions to the field of geology were extensive, and he published records of his geological observations across the various districts where he served. His specific interests included the Lias and Oolites in the Vale of Gloucester, as well as the outliers of Portland and Purbeck Beds in Buckinghamshire. He also discovered the isopod *Archaeoniscus Brodiei* in the Purbeck Beds of the Vale of Wardour and obtained fossil shells from the Pleistocene deposit at Barnwell while at Cambridge.

## Specialized Studies and Recognition
While involved with the Warwickshire Naturalists' and Archaeologists' Field Club, Brodie maintained a specialized focus on fossil insects, which was the subject of his 1845 work, *History of the Fossil Insects of the Secondary Rocks of England*. Many of his published papers were dedicated to this subject.

His lifelong commitment to geological science was recognized by the Geological Society of London, which awarded him the Murchison medal in 1887. Brodie remained in Rowington until his death on November 1, 1897.
