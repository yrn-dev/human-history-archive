# Dr. Tomas Frias and the Bolivian Presidency

## Succession and Early Presidency
Dr. Tomas Frias ascended to the presidency of Bolivia following the death of Adolfo Ballivian. His tenure began immediately after the conclusion of a treaty between Bolivia and Peru. During his time in office, Dr. Frias sought to resolve ongoing disputes with Chile. He signed a treaty in which Chile agreed to withdraw its claim to half of the duties levied in Bolivian ports. In exchange for this concession, the treaty stipulated that all Chilean industries established within Bolivian territory would be exempt from duty for a period of twenty-five years. However, this specific treaty was never ratified.

## Transition to General Hilarion Daza
In 1876, Dr. Frias was succeeded as president by General Hilarion Daza. The transition of power occurred during a period of heightened tension regarding the economic interests of Chilean capital in Bolivian territory. Previous agreements, such as a treaty from 1866, had established the 24th parallel as the boundary of Chilean territory and granted Chile a half share of customs and trading facilities on the coast between the 23rd and 24th parallels. Additionally, Chile had been permitted to mine and export products from this district without hindrance or tax. By 1870, Bolivia had further granted an Anglo-Chilean company the right to work nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel for a sum of $10,000.

## The Nitrate Dispute and Outbreak of War
Four years after taking office in 1876, General Hilarion Daza demanded a new price for Bolivia's consent regarding these arrangements. He required that a tax of 10 cents per quintal be paid on all nitrates exported from the country. Daza further declared that the Bolivian government would seize nitrates held by exporters if this levy was not paid.

Chile responded to these demands by sending its fleet to blockade the ports of Tocapilla, Cobija, and Antofagasta to protect the property of Chilean subjects. On February 14, 1879, Colonel Sotomayor of Chile occupied Antofagasta. A fortnight later, on March 1, the Bolivian government declared war.

## Consequences of the Conflict
The ensuing conflict saw Bolivia allied with Peru, based on a secret agreement signed on February 6, 1873, intended for mutual defense and the preservation of territorial integrity against exterior aggression. Following a series of revolutions in both nations, General Narciso Campero was elected president of Bolivia in June of a subsequent year.

While the war was primarily maintained at sea between Chile and Peru during 1880, with Bolivia playing little to no part, the conflict eventually led to the surrender of Lima and Callao. On December 11, a peace treaty was signed between Chile and Bolivia. Under the terms of this agreement, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, including the port of Cobija.

## Later Settlements and Territorial Loss
Following the war, Bolivia's presidency was held by Dr. Pacheco, Dr. Aniceto Arce (until 1892), and Dr. Mariano Baptista. In 1896, Dr. Severe Alonso became president. Despite further treaties of peace and commerce signed with Chile on May 23, 1895, the provision to cede a seaport back to Bolivia remained unfulfilled.

The dispute over the provinces of Arica and Tacna continued under the Pacto de Tregna of April 4, 1884. Although there was a prospect in 1895 that Chile might concede a sea outlet in exchange for recognition of Chilean ownership of Tacna and Arica, the discovery of secret negotiations between Bolivia and Argentina caused Chile to abandon this conciliatory stance.

Ultimately, under President Pando and his successor, Ismail Montes (who took office in 1904), Bolivia yielded to Chilean terms. A treaty ratified in 1905 required Bolivia to cede the maritime provinces occupied by Chile since the war of 1881, leaving the republic without a seaport. In return, the Chilean government agreed to:
* Construct a railway from Arica to the Bolivian capital, La Paz, at Chile's own expense.
* Provide Bolivians with free transit through Chilean territory to specific coastal towns.
* Pay a cash indemnity to Bolivia.
* Provide pecuniary assistance for the construction of other necessary railways to open the country.
