# Political and Economic Developments of 1812 and 1826

The years 1812 and 1826 were marked by significant legislative actions, political transitions, and institutional growth across England and the United States. These periods saw shifts in trade policy, the regulation of financial instruments, and the administration of various states.

## Financial and Trade Legislation
In England, the early 19th century was a period of contention regarding trade and currency. Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton, an English politician and financier who became head of the firm Baring Brothers & Co. in 1810, was a notable figure in these debates. Baring viewed politics through the lens of a businessman and opposed the restrictions on trade with the United States in 1812. Later, in 1826, he opposed the act for the suppression of small bank-notes.

The year 1826 also saw a major shift in government revenue and public gambling. Since 1709, the English government had raised substantial sums through lotteries authorized by acts of parliament, which provided prizes in the form of perpetual or terminable annuities. Between 1793 and 1824, these lotteries generated an average yearly profit of £346,765. However, following the development of strong public opinion against the practice—led by figures such as Lord Lyttleton—lotteries were finally prohibited in 1826.

## State Administration and Governance
During this era, the United States experienced various transitions in state leadership. In the state of Kentucky, the governorship transitioned in 1812 from Charles Scott to Isaac Shelby, who served as a Democratic-Republican from 1812 to 1816. In another state, the administration of Ninian Edwards continued from 1809 through 1818.

The mid-1820s also saw shifts in diplomatic and state roles. John Alsop King, who had served as a lieutenant of a cavalry company during the war of 1812, served as the secretary of the American legation in Great Britain starting in 1825. Following the return of his father due to ill health, King remained as chargé d'affaires until August 1826.

## Institutional and Religious Growth
The period starting in 1812 marked a significant era of expansion for American Baptists. In 1812, the denomination's population was fewer than 200,000. At that time, the group possessed only one degree-conferring college with a small faculty and student body, and they had no theological seminary. The value of church property was described as small, and their periodical literature consisted of a single monthly magazine with a circulation of a few hundred.

By comparison, later records show this grew to over 5,000,000 members, with the establishment of 11 theological seminaries and more than 100 universities and colleges by 1906.

## Cultural and Professional Developments
The year 1826 was also a milestone for the American arts. Asher Brown Durand, an American painter and engraver who had been apprenticed to Peter Maverick in 1812, became one of the founders of the National Academy of Design in 1826. Durand, who later became known as the father of the Hudson River School, had previously established his reputation by engraving Trumbull's "Declaration of Independence."
