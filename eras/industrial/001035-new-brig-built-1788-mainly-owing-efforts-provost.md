# New Brig

The New Brig is a bridge located in Ayr, a royal, municipal and police burgh and seaport serving as the county town of Ayrshire, Scotland. Situated at the mouth of the river Ayr, the town is positioned on a fine bay known for attracting thousands of summer visitors to its sands.

## Construction and History
The New Brig was built in 1788. Its construction was primarily the result of the efforts of Provost Ballantyne. It is one of three bridges that cross the river Ayr, alongside the railway viaduct and the Victoria Bridge, the latter of which was erected in 1898. The New Brig is distinguished from the Auld Brig, a structure said to date back to the reign of Alexander III, who died in 1286.

The New Brig's history is linked to a prophecy attributed to Burns, which was placed in the mouth of the older structure. This prophecy was realized in 1877 when the New Brig yielded to floods. Following this event, the bridge had to be rebuilt in 1879.

## The Town of Ayr
The New Brig serves a town that has seen significant expansion, particularly on the southern side of the river toward the racecourse, an area now featuring numerous fine villas. Ayr proper is located on the south bank of the river.

The town contains several notable buildings and landmarks:
* **County Buildings:** Designed after the temple of Isis in Rome, these buildings house various local authorities as well as provincial and circuit courts.
* **Town Buildings:** These are topped by a spire reaching 226 feet in height and house reading and assembly rooms.
* **Educational Institutions:** The Academy, rebuilt in 1880, is the most notable school; it superseded the burgh's grammar school in 1764, an institution that had existed since the 13th century.
* **Wallace Tower:** A Gothic structure located on High Street. It stands where an older building of the same name existed until it was taken down in 1835. The clock and bells of the Dungeon steeple were transferred to the tower. A statue of the Scottish hero Sir William Wallace, created by the self-taught sculptor James Thorn (1802-1850), is located in a niche in front of the tower.

Other statues in the town include those of Burns, General Smith Neill, and the 13th earl of Eglinton. The town's cultural and social infrastructure includes the Carnegie free library, established in 1893, and the *Ayr Advertiser*, which began publication on August 5, 1803, as the first newspaper in Ayrshire.

The town also provides various charitable institutions, including a district asylum, a county hospital, St John's refuge, a deaf and dumb home, the Kyle combination poor-house, and industrial schools for girls and boys.
