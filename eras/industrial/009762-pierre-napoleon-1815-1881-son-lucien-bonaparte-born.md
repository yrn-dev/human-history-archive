# Pierre Napoleon (1815-1881)

Pierre Napoleon was the son of Lucien Bonaparte. Born in Rome on September 12, 1815, he led a life characterized by early political adventure, a period of republican activism in France, and a later descent into personal and political obscurity.

## Early Adventures and Travels
Pierre began a life of adventure at the age of fifteen. Between 1830 and 1831, he joined insurrectionary bands in the Romagna. Following this, he traveled to the United States to join his uncle, Joseph, and later visited Colombia in 1832, where he was with General Santander. Upon returning to Rome, he was taken prisoner by order of the pope from 1835 to 1836. He eventually sought refuge in England.

## Political Career in France
Pierre returned to France during the revolution of 1848. He was elected to the Constituent Assembly as the deputy for Corsica. During this period, he identified as an "out-and-out republican" and aligned his voting record with the socialists. His political stances included support for the national workshops and opposition to the *loi Falloux*.

His republican alignment was noted to have contributed significantly to the popular confidence placed in his cousin, Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III). However, Pierre disapproved of the *coup d'etat* carried out by Louis Napoleon on December 2, 1851. Despite this disapproval, Pierre was soon reconciled with the emperor and accepted the title of prince. This acceptance led the republicans to abandon him.

## Later Life and the Victor Noir Incident
Following his reconciliation with the emperor, Pierre lost his political importance and led a debauched life. He turned his attention to literature, though the poems he published were described as mediocre.

In January 1870, Pierre returned to public prominence due to a violent controversy with Paschal Grousset. Grousset sent two journalists to provoke Pierre into a duel. During a violent discussion that followed, Pierre drew his revolver and killed one of the journalists, Victor Noir. This crime caused significant excitement within the republican press, which demanded that Pierre be put on trial. He was ultimately acquitted by the High Court, an outcome that led to criticism of the government.

## Personal Life and Death
Pierre married Justine Eleanore Ruffin, the daughter of a working-man from Paris. Before their marriage, he had two children with her:
* **Roland Napoleon**: Born May 19, 1858. He entered the army but was excluded from it in 1886, subsequently dedicating himself to scientific explorations and geography.
* **Jeanne**: Who became the wife of the marquis de Vence.

Pierre Napoleon died in obscurity at Versailles on April 7, 1881.
