# Satara Jagirs

The Satara Jagirs are a group of native states located within the Deccan division of the Bombay Presidency. This group of states comprises Akalkot, Bhor, Aundh, Phaltan, Jath, and Daphlapur.

## Administrative Context
The Satara Jagirs exist as part of the broader administrative structure of the Bombay Presidency, a province of British India. The presidency is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, Indore, the Central Provinces and Hyderabad to the east, and Baluchistan, the Punjab, and Rajputana to the north. 

The Bombay Presidency consists of both British districts and native states. As of 1901, the total population of the presidency was 25,468,209, with 6,908,648 residents living within native states. The native states are composed of 353 separate units, which are managed either by collectors of the districts where smaller states are located or by political agents.

## Regional Geography and Governance
The Satara Jagirs are situated in the Deccan division, one of four commissionerships in the province (the others being Gujarat in the north, Carnatic in the south, and Sind). The presidency's twenty-six districts include Satara, as well as others such as Poona, Sholapur, and Ahmednagar.

Within the political landscape of the region, the Satara Jagirs are distinguished from other groups of native states, such as the North Gujarat states (including Cutch and Cambay), the South Gujarat states (including Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur), and the southern Mahratta states, which include Kolhapur.

## Related Historical Annexations
While the Satara Jagirs comprise the aforementioned states, the broader history of the region includes the annexation of Satara itself. In January 1849, Lord Dalhousie recommended the annexation of Satara based on the "doctrine of lapse." This policy allowed for the annexation of Hindu native states that were created or revived by British government grants if there was a failure of male lineal descendants.
