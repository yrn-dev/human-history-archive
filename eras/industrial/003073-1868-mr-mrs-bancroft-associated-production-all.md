# Sir Squire Bancroft and Effie Marie Wilton

## Early Career and Marriage
Sir Squire Bancroft (1841– ) was an English manager and actor born on May 14, 1841, near London. He began his theatrical career in 1861 with a first appearance on the stage in Birmingham, subsequently spending several years playing with success in the provinces. In 1865, Bancroft made his first appearance in London at the Prince of Wales's theatre, located off Tottenham Court Road, in the production *A Winning Hazard* by Wooler. At that time, the theatre was under the management of Effie Marie Wilton (born 1840). Bancroft and Wilton married in 1868.

## The Prince of Wales's Theatre
Following their marriage, Mr. and Mrs. Bancroft became associated in the production of all the Robertson comedies. This series of productions included *Society* (1865), *Ours* (1866), *Caste* (1867), *Play* (1868), *School* (1869), and *M.P.* (1870). After the death of Robertson, the Bancrofts continued to produce revivals of the old comedies, employing an admirable company.

The couple's premieres at the Prince of Wales's theatre, which contributed to the fame of the small playhouse, included *London Assurance* by Boucicault (1877), Lytton's *Money* (1872), and *Diplomacy*, which was an adaptation of Sardou's *Dora*. The management provided by the Bancrofts at this venue is credited with reviving London's interest in modern drama and establishing a new era in the development of the English stage. During their tenure, they were joined by Mr. and Mrs. Kendal in *Diplomacy* and other plays.

## Later Career and Retirement
In 1879, the Bancrofts moved their operations to the Haymarket. Their productions there included W. S. Gilbert's *Sweethearts*, Pinero's *Lords and Commons*, and Sardou's *Fedora* and *Odette*; for the latter, they engaged Madame Modjeska. They also produced revivals of their previous successes.

The Bancrofts retired in 1885 after having made a considerable fortune. Sir Squire Bancroft was knighted in 1897. In 1889, he returned to the stage to join Sir Henry Irving in a revival of Watts Phillips's *Dead Heart*, in which he played the role of the abbe Latour.

## Published Memoirs
The professional and personal lives of the couple are documented in two works written by themselves: *Mr and Mrs Bancroft, on and off the Stage* (1888) and *The Bancrofts: Recollections of Sixty Years* (1909).
