# Sheridan Wrappering Machine

The Sheridan wrappering machine is a piece of industrial equipment used in the production of printed materials. Specifically, it is designed to cover pamphlets and magazines. The machine is capable of processing materials ranging from 5 to 12 inches in length, operating at a production rate of 40 units per minute.

## Related Bookbinding Machinery
The Sheridan wrappering machine is mentioned alongside other specialized binding equipment, such as the Smyth casing-in machine. The Smyth machine is used to paste the sides of a book and attach a cover over the entire work. It utilizes a system of cleverly arranged rollers and regulated pressure to fix the cover in the correct position, as well as a "jointing-in" device that forces the joints of the cover into the joints of the book. While the Sheridan machine focuses on smaller formats like pamphlets, the Smyth machine handles books between 1/4 and 3 inches in thickness and 4 to 22 inches in length, covering between 10 and 15 books per minute.

## Other Historical Uses of Wrappers
Outside of industrial machinery, wrappers have been used historically for the protection of military equipment. In ancient Greece, the Roman soldiers utilized leathern wrappers to carry their shields while on the march. Additionally, the Syracusan Agathokles is noted for employing a curious stratagem involving the wrappers or cases of shields.
