# BARRY

Barry is a seaport and urban district located in Glamorganshire, Wales, situated on the Bristol Channel. It is positioned 8 miles southwest of Cardiff and is 153 miles from London by rail.

## Geography and Demographics
The urban district of Barry comprises the ecclesiastical parishes of Merthyr-Dovan, Cadoxton, Barry, and a portion of Sully. This latter area includes Barry Island, which covers 194 acres and is currently joined to the mainland. A small brook, known as the Barri, flows into the sea at this location.

The district has experienced significant population growth. In 1881, the total population of the area was approximately 500, with Barry village alone accounting for only 85 residents. By 1901, the population of the urban district had increased to 27,030.

## History and Etymology
The location was formerly known in Welsh as Aber-Barri. It is believed that the names of both the island and the river are derived from Baruch, a 7th-century Welsh saint who maintained a cell on the island. During the middle ages, his chapel served as a place of pilgrimage; records indicate the chapel still existed during the time of Leland. Giraldus noted that his own family took the name "de Barri" from the island, which they once owned.

At the end of the 11th century, a follower of Fitzhamon settled in Barry and constructed a castle. Of this structure, only a gateway remains today.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
Barry serves as a terminus on the Barry railway. This line begins at Hafod in the Rhondda Valley, where it connects with the Taff Vale railway. The railway system includes several junctions:
* At Treforest, it connects with the Taff Vale line for Merthyr and Aberdare.
* At Cogan, it connects with the Taff Vale line for Penarth and Cardiff.
* At St Fagans and Peterstone, it connects with the Great Western main line.

Additionally, a branch from the main line at Tyn-y-caeau provides connections to the Brecon & Merthyr railway, the London & North-Western railway, and the Rhymney railway. 

The Vale of Glamorgan railway, which is operated by the Barry company, provides a direct route to Barry from the Garw, Ogmore, and Llynvi coalfields. This line also maintains a junction with the Great Western railway at Bridgend.

## Religious and Public Buildings
The district contains a variety of religious institutions. These include the rebuilt parish church of Barry and the small, old parish churches of Cadoxton and Merthyr-Dovan. There are four modern churches, one of which conducts services in Welsh. 

Nonconformist presence is significant, with approximately thirty chapels, nearly a third of which hold Welsh services. Other religious facilities include a Roman Catholic church and a church dedicated to Scandinavian and German seamen. Public buildings in the district also include a county intermediate school.
