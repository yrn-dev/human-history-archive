# A Guide to the Study of Book-plates

## Overview and Impact
*A Guide to the Study of Book-plates* is a seminal work authored by Lord de Tabley, who was known as the Hon. Leicester Warren at the time of its publication in 1880. The text provided the first real impetus for the study and collection of book-plates, which are private tokens of book-ownership. While the taste for collecting these items and their formal study hardly date back further than the year 1875, Lord de Tabley's work helped establish the field.

## Classification of Styles
The *Guide to the Study of Book-plates* is noted for being highly interesting from many perspectives and for establishing the general classification of styles that is currently accepted. These classifications include:

*   **Early Armorial**: This style refers to plates created previous to the Restoration. An example of this style is the Nicholas Bacon plate.
*   **Jacobean**: Although described as a somewhat misleading term, it is distinctly understood to include the heavy decorative manner associated with the early Georgian days, Queen Anne, and the Restoration. The Lansanor plate is cited as a typical example of the Jacobean style.
*   **Chippendale**: This style is identified as the one described as rococo. The French plate of Convers serves as a representative example.
*   **Wreath and Ribbon**: This style belongs to the period characterized as that of the urn.

## Development of the Field
Following the publication of Lord de Tabley's guide, literature regarding book-plates grew considerably. This growth led to the foundation of societies for collectors in several countries:

*   **England**: The Ex-libris Society (London) publishes *The Journal of the Ex-libris Society*, a monthly publication.
*   **France**: The societe francaise de collectionneurs d'ex-libris (Paris) publishes a monthly journal titled *Archives de la societe francaise de collectionneurs d'ex-libris*.
*   **Germany**: The *Ex-libris Zeitschrift* (Berlin) is published as a quarterly.
*   **United States**: Societies of collectors were also established in the United States.
