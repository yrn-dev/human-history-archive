# Royal Military Exhibition, London 1890

The Royal Military Exhibition was held in London in 1890. The event featured a wide array of musical instruments, which were documented in a *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments exhibited at the Royal Military Exhibition* compiled by Captain C. R. Day and published in 1891.

## Woodwind Instruments

The exhibition included several early examples of woodwind instruments, specifically those related to the development of the clarinet and the flute.

### Early Bass Clarinets
One of the earliest attempts to create a bass for the clarinet was exhibited at the event. This instrument, which took the form of a fagotto and was stamped "A. Grenser, Dresden," featured nine square-flapped brass keys that operated on knobs. It is held in the Grossherzogliches Museum at Darmstadt and was lent to the 1890 exhibition. This instrument originated from the work of Heinrich Grenser of Dresden, a renowned instrument-maker who produced the first bass clarinet in 1793. Grenser's work was likely influenced by the popularity of the tenor clarinet or basset horn.

### Other Woodwinds
The exhibition's catalogue also included a reproduction of a small instrument in F. This specimen was one of only three known "curious instruments" of its type in existence—the others being located in Brussels and the museum of the Conservatoire in Paris—all of which are marked with a trefoil. Additionally, the catalogue compiled by Captain C. R. Day provided heliogravures of early basset horns.

## Brass and Keyed Instruments

The exhibition showcased instruments that demonstrated the evolution of the bugle through the application of keys and valves.

### The Royal Kent Bugle
Col. Shaw-Hellier exhibited a Royal Kent bugle in C at the 1890 exhibition. This specific instrument was made by P. Turton of 5 Wormwood Gate, Dublin, and bore the name of its inventor, Joseph Halliday. Halliday, a bandmaster of the Cavan militia, had patented the keyed bugle in 1810, naming it the "Royal Kent Bugle" in honor of the duke of Kent, who served as commander-in-chief. 

The exhibited instrument measured 17 inches in length, with total tubing (including the mouthpiece) measuring 50½ inches. Its diameter was ½ inch at the mouthpiece and 5¾ inches at the bell. The instrument featured a chromatic compass of two octaves.

## Bag-pipes

The 1890 exhibition featured historical specimens of bag-pipes, including a Highland bag-pipe from the 15th century.

### 15th-Century Highland Bag-pipe
Dated MCCCCIX (1409), this instrument was in the possession of Messrs J. & R. Glen of Edinburgh. The bag-pipe consisted of a chaunter with a thumb-hole at the back and seven finger-holes in front. It featured two drones tuned to A, which were inserted into a single stock designed as a wide-spread fork.
