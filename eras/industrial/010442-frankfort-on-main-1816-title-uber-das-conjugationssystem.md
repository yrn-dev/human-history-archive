# Uber das Conjugationssystem

*Uber das Conjugationssystem der Sanskritsprache in Vergleichung mit jenem der griechischen, lateinischen, persischen und germanischen Sprache* is a philological work published in 1816 at Frankfort-On-Main. The book was authored by the German philologist Franz Bopp (1791–1867) and featured a preface written by Karl J. Windischmann.

## Author Background and Education
Franz Bopp was born on September 14, 1791, in Mainz. Due to political instability during that period, his parents moved to Aschaffenburg, Bavaria. Bopp received a liberal education at the Lyceum in Aschaffenburg, where his interest in the literature and languages of the East was first sparked by the lectures of Karl J. Windischmann. Windischmann, along with the brothers Schlegel, J.J. Gorres, and G.F. Creuzer, held a strong enthusiasm for Indian philosophy and wisdom. 

Bopp's interest in the sacred language of the Hindus was further stimulated by the 1808 book *Uber die Sprache und Weisheit der Indier*, written by Fr. Schlegel in Heidelberg, which had a significant impact on German historians and philosophers of the time.

## Studies in Paris
In 1812, the Bavarian government funded Bopp's travel to Paris to engage in the vigorous study of Sanskrit. During his four years in the city, Bopp associated with several eminent figures, including L.M. Langles, S. de Sacy, and A.L. Chezy. He was particularly influenced by Alexander Hamilton (1762–1824), who had gained knowledge of Sanskrit while in India and had collaborated with Langles to produce a descriptive catalogue of Sanskrit manuscripts for the Imperial library.

While at the Imperial library, Bopp utilized two primary types of resources:
*   A rich collection of Sanskrit manuscripts, many of which had been brought from India by Father Pons in the early 18th century.
*   Sanskrit books published by the Serampore and Calcutta presses.

## Scope and Objectives of the Work
The publication of *Uber das Conjugationssystem* in 1816 represented the first result of Bopp's four years of study in Paris. The work focused on the conjugation system of the Sanskrit language, comparing it with the systems of the German, Persian, Latin, and Greek languages.

The primary objective of the book was not to prove that Sanskrit shared a common parentage with the other mentioned languages, as that fact had already been established. Instead, Bopp sought to trace the common origin of their grammatical structures. This publication established the trajectory for the philological research that would occupy the remainder of Bopp's professional life.
