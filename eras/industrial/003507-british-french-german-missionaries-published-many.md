# Missionary Activities and Contributions of British, French, and German Societies

Missionary societies from Great Britain, Germany, and France have played significant roles in education, linguistics, and the exploration of various regions across Africa, Asia, and the Pacific. Their activities have ranged from the establishment of schools and the study of indigenous languages to the early exploration of uncharted territories.

## Linguistic Research and Documentation
Missionaries have contributed extensively to the documentation of African languages through the publication of dictionaries, grammars, and vocabularies. In East Africa, Dr. L. Krapf published a *Swahili-English Dictionary* (1882) that provides invaluable information on the Swahili language as spoken on the coast, including words that were becoming obsolete. Bishop Steere also published numerous handbooks and vocabularies on the languages of the East African coast-lands, which are noted for preserving prefix forms that have since passed out of use.

In British Central Africa, the Rev. D. C. Scott produced *An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mananja (Mang'anja) Language* (1891). Further linguistic work includes the *Dictionary of the Congo Language* (1891) by the Rev. Holman Bentley, as well as a *Grammar of Kimbundu* and *The Folklore of Angola* authored by Dr. Heli Chatelain.

## Exploration and Education
In the mid-19th century, German missionaries Johannes Rebmann and Ludwig Krapf, along with Baron Karl von der Decken, were the first to make the interior of what is now British East Africa known. 

Beyond exploration, missionary societies have been the primary providers of education in certain regions. These societies maintain numerous schools that provide instruction in ordinary branches of elementary education as well as handicrafts.

## Activities in the Pacific and Asia
In the Loyalty Islands, the London Missionary Society established missions at Maré in 1854, Lifu in 1859, and Uea in 1865. These efforts to introduce Christianity faced opposition from French officials; a commandant formally instituted by the French in 1864 attempted to stop the English missions through violence. This led to protests in England and Australia, eventually resulting in a commission of inquiry ordered by Emperor Napoleon to secure free liberty of worship for Protestant missions.

In India, the presence of both English and German missionaries is noted. During periods of conflict, German missionaries in India have been subject to arrest.

## Political and Diplomatic Contexts
Missionary activities have sometimes intersected with colonial tensions and diplomatic disputes. In Uganda, French missionaries were described as hostile to Great Britain. To consolidate their position in the region and counter French intrigues, the British company sent Captain F.D. Lugard to the capital, Mengo, in December 1890.
