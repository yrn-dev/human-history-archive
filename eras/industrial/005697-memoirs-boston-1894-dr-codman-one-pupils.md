# Brook Farm: Historic and Personal Memoirs

## Overview of the Work
*Brook Farm: Historic and Personal Memoirs* is a historical account published in Boston in 1894. The work was authored by Dr. J.T. Codman, who had a personal connection to the subject as one of the pupils in the school.

## Context of Brook Farm
The memoirs contribute to the documentation of the Brook Farm settlement. Other accounts of the community include *Brook Farm, Its Members, Scholars and Visitors*, published in New York in 1900 by Lindsay Swift, which is described as the best account of the settlement. Additionally, the history of the community is touched upon in Morris Hillquit's *History of Socialism in the United States*, published in New York in 1903.

## Notable Figures Associated with the Settlement
Among those connected to Brook Farm was John Sullivan Dwight (1813–1893). Born in Boston and educated at Harvard, Dwight originally became a Unitarian minister. However, he abandoned that career to join the Brook Farm settlement, where he served as a teacher of other subjects as well as music. 

Following his time at the settlement, Dwight settled in Boston in 1848 as a musical critic. He is most recognized as the editor and founder of the *Journal of Music*, which ran from 1852 to 1881 and is considered the most important musical periodical published in America. Following his death on September 5, 1893, G.W. Cooke edited his letters in 1898 and authored a memoir in 1899.
