# Barnsley

Barnsley, also known as Black or properly Bleak Barnsley, is a municipal borough and market town located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is situated 15 miles north of Sheffield on rising ground to the west of the river Dearne. The town is part of the Barnsley parliamentary division and is located within the parish of Silkstone.

## Population and Governance
The population of Barnsley grew from 35,427 in 1891 to 41,086 by 1901. The borough covers an area of 2,385 acres. It is governed by a corporation consisting of a mayor, six aldermen, and eighteen councillors.

## Industry and Economy
The town's economy is driven primarily by two main industries: the weaving of linen and other cloth, and the manufacture of steel and iron. Additionally, Barnsley supports a variety of other industrial works, including:
* Dyeworks, printfields, and bleachfields
* Glass, wire, and needle manufacture
* Cornmills and sawmills
* Malt-houses

The region is characterized by large coalfields that extend directly under the town. Coal and coke produced in these fields are exported in large quantities to Hull and London.

## Infrastructure and Transport
Barnsley is well-connected via several railway lines, including the Great Northern, Great Central, Midland, Hull & Barnsley, and Lancashire & Yorkshire railways. In addition to rail transport, the town is connected to the Aire and Calder Navigation system of canals.

## Institutions and Landmarks
Despite the presence of numerous factories, the surrounding neighbourhood possesses considerable natural beauty. The town's principal buildings and institutions include:
* **Religious Sites:** Several churches are present, the oldest being the parish church of St Mary, which was constructed in 1821 on an earlier site.
* **Public Buildings:** The town features a public hall, a court house, an institute, and a free library.
* **Education and Culture:** A free grammar school dating from 1665 serves the area, and a philosophical society was established in 1828.
* **Parks and Monuments:** A park was presented to the town in 1862 by the widow of Joseph Locke, M.P. In 1905, a monument was erected to honor prominent members of the Yorkshire Miners' Association.

## Surrounding Area
In the vicinity of Barnsley are two notable historical sites:
* **Monk Bretton Priory:** A Cluniac foundation established in 1157. Remaining structures include fragments of the church, Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse.
* **Wentworth Castle:** Built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford, this site is situated in a singularly beautiful park and houses a fine collection of portraits of historical interest.
