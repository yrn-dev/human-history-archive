# Bolton Abbey and Ilkley

## Bolton Abbey
Bolton Abbey is a village located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. It is situated 22 miles northwest of Leeds and 5-1/2 miles from Ilkley via the Midland railway. The village takes its name, though inaccurately, from the great foundation of Bolton Priory. The ruins of the priory are considered among the most exquisitely situated in England, located near the right bank of the upper Wharfe in a valley that is closely enclosed by hills and beautifully wooded.

The architecture of the church reflects several periods: the earliest section is of transitional Norman date, while the nave is Early English and Decorated. The choir and transepts are ruined, and the remaining domestic buildings are slight.

## History and Ownership
The manor of Bolton Abbey, along with the rest of the district of Craven, was granted to Robert de Romili by William the Conqueror. While Robert evidently held the manor in 1086, the Domesday survey contains no mention of it. In 1120, Cicely de Romili (heiress of Robert) and her husband, William de Meschines, founded and endowed a priory at Emmesay (or Embsay) near Skipton. This priory was moved to the current site in 1151 by their daughter, Alice de Romili, who was the wife of William FitzDuncan; she gave the manor to the monks in exchange for other lands.

Following the dissolution of the monasteries, the manor was sold in 1542 to Henry Clifford, the 2nd earl of Cumberland. The manor is currently held by his descendants, the dukes of Devonshire.

## Ilkley
Ilkley is an urban district in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England, located 16 miles northwest of Leeds on the North-Eastern and Midland railways. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 7,455. Situated in the upper part of the Wharfe valley, the area is a favorite health resort due to its fine scenery and the bracing air of the high moorlands. Hydropathic establishments are located here and at Ben Rhydding, which is 1 mile to the east.

The town's institutions include a grammar school, the Ilkley Bath Charitable Institution, the Siemens Convalescent Home, and a museum of local antiquities. The church of All Saints is primarily Decorated in style and underwent extensive restoration in 1860; its churchyard preserves three ancient sculptured crosses.

## Antiquities and Roman History
Ilkley has been identified as the *Olicana* of Ptolemy, serving as one of the towns for the Brigantes, a British tribe. A Roman fort was located near the present church of All Saints. This site has yielded small remains and inscriptions, and numerous relics are currently preserved in the local museum.
