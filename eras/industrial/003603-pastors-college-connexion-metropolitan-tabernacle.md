# Pastors' College in connexion with the Metropolitan Tabernacle

## Establishment and Purpose
The Pastors' College in connexion with the Metropolitan Tabernacle was instituted in 1856. Its creation was part of a broader historical trend among Baptists, who early in their history recognized the necessity of providing an educated ministry for their congregations.

## Context of Baptist Education
The drive for an educated ministry led various Baptist leaders to seek education at English universities, while others pursued learning independently. An example of such self-directed scholarship was Dr. John Gill, who was eminent for his knowledge of Hebrew, as evidenced in his nine-volume folio work, *Exposition of the Holy Scriptures*, published between 1746 and 1766.

Formal training for the ministry was further supported by private bequests. Edward Terrill, who died in 1685, left a significant portion of his estate to instruct young men training for the ministry. This was managed under the superintendence of the pastor of the Broadmead Church in Bristol. Following other similar bequests, the Baptist Academy began receiving students for the Baptist ministry in 1720.

## Related Institutions
The Pastors' College existed alongside several other educational institutions for the Baptist ministry:

*   **Bristol:** The Bristol Education Society was formed in 1770 to expand the existing academy, and the Bristol Baptist College was erected around 1811.
*   **North of England:** An education society was established in 1804 at Bradford, Yorkshire, which later moved to Rawdon, near Leeds.
*   **London:** A college was formed in Stepney in 1810 and subsequently moved to Regent's Park in 1856.
*   **Manchester:** The Baptist College at Manchester was instituted at Bury in 1866 to serve the interests of "Strict" Baptist views.
*   **Midland Baptist College:** Located in Nottingham, this college was maintained by the General Baptists since 1797. While previously supported by the General Baptist Association, it became a voluntary institution following the amalgamation of the two Baptist bodies.

Other theological colleges for Baptists were established in Glasgow, Ireland (one college), and Wales (two colleges). The combined total of students across these various institutions was approximately 210.

## Institutional Status
The Pastors' College, along with several other mentioned institutions, was categorized as a voluntary college that operated without denominational control.
