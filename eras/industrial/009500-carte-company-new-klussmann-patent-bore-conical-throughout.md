# Carte & Company's New (Klussmann's Patent) Bore

The new bore developed by Rudall, Carte & Company, utilizing Klussmann's patent, represents a significant advancement in the construction of brass wind instruments. This specific design is characterized by a bore that is conical throughout, encompassing both the open tube and the additional lengths of tubing.

## Context of Valve Instrument Intonation
The development of Klussmann's patent bore addresses an inherent defect found in the valve systems of instruments such as the cornet and the bombardon. To achieve a complete chromatic scale, these instruments require various lengths of tubing, similar to the seven positions available on a slide-trombone. In standard valve instruments, these lengths are obtained through combinations of pistons.

However, using combinations of pistons often fails to produce intervals with absolutely correct intonation. This occurs because the length of tubing opened by the pistons is not the theoretical length required to produce the specific interval. For example, in a B$\flat$ cornet with a tube approximately 4 feet long, a semitone requires an additional length of about 3 inches. If the instrument has already been lowered one tone to the key of A$\flat$, the tube length increases by some 6 inches; at this point, the 3-inch semitone piston no longer provides sufficient tubing to maintain correct intonation. Historically, performers had to compensate for these shortcomings by varying their lip tension.

## Evolution of Technical Remedies
Throughout the history of the cornet and similar instruments, various makers attempted to overcome these construction defects. Early efforts included the use of numerous crooks for various keys (such as A, A$\flat$, G, F, E, E$\flat$, and D), though most were abandoned as impractical for valve instruments.

Other ingenious contrivances were developed to remedy intonation issues, including:
*   **Adolphe Sax's six-valve independent system:** Designed around 1850, this system used a separate valve for each position to avoid piston combinations. It was eventually abandoned due to practical difficulties, as the valves were ascending rather than descending.
*   **The Besson *Registre*:** A system providing eight independent positions.
*   **The Besson compensating system *Transpositeur*.**
*   **D.J. Blaikley's Boosey automatic compensating piston.**
*   **V. Mahillon's automatic regulating pistons.**
*   **The Besson enharmonic valve system:** This system utilizes three pistons and six independent tuning slides.

## The Klussmann Patent Bore
Among the most recent improvements, the bore patented by Klussmann and produced by Rudall, Carte & Company is noted for its effectiveness. By maintaining a conical shape throughout the open tube and the additional lengths, this design produces instruments that leave nothing to be desired regarding their intonation. 

The application of this patent is evidenced in specific instruments, such as the B$\flat$ cornet, which is produced by Rudall, Carte & Co. with a strictly conical bore throughout.
