# Marie Pauline Borghese

## Early Life and First Marriage
Marie Pauline was born on October 20, 1780, in Ajaccio. Described as the most beautiful and gayest member of her family, she married General Leclerc, a staff officer of Napoleon, when she was seventeen years of age. Pauline accompanied her husband to St Domingo, where General Leclerc died of yellow fever in 1802.

## Marriage to Prince Camillo Borghese
Following her return to Paris, Marie Pauline married Prince Camillo Borghese on August 23, 1803. Camillo Filippo Ludovico, born in 1775, belonged to a noble Italian family of Sienese origin first mentioned in 1238. The family had established itself among the higher Roman nobility through the 1614 marriage of Paolo Borghese to Olimpia, the heiress of the Aldobrandini family. A previous member of the family, Marcantonio Borghese, had settled in Rome and fathered Camillo Borghese (1550–1620), who was elected pope as Paul V in 1605.

After their marriage, Marie Pauline and Prince Camillo resided in Rome. In 1806, she received the title of duchess of Guastalla, a title also held by her husband.

## Life at Court and Later Years
Marie Pauline eventually tired of her husband and returned to Paris. During this time, she gratified her whims in a manner that caused some scandal. Her presence at court ended in 1810 after she treated the new empress, Marie Louise, with offhandness, leading to her removal.

In 1814, she traveled to Elba with "Madame Mere." It is said that she expressed a desire to share the exile of Napoleon in St Helena. Marie Pauline died of cancer in 1825. She is remembered in art through a well-known statue by Canova, which depicts her as Venus reclining on a couch.

## Prince Camillo Borghese
Prince Camillo Borghese, in addition to his marriage to Napoleon's sister, served for several years as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces. Following the fall of Napoleon, he established his residence in Florence, where he died in 1832. 

The Borghese family's palace in Rome is noted as one of the city's most magnificent buildings. It once housed a splendid gallery of pictures, the majority of which were transferred to the Villa Borghese outside the Porto del Popolo (now the Villa Umberto I), which is owned by the Italian government.
