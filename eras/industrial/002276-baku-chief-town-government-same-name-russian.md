# BAKU

Baku is the chief town and capital of the government of the same name, located in Russian Transcaucasia. It is situated on the south side of the Apsheron peninsula at 40deg 21' N. and 49deg 50' E.

## Geography and Climate
The town is positioned on a peninsula characterized as dry and bare of vegetation. The climate is described as extreme, with an annual rainfall of 9.4 inches. The mean temperature for the year is 58deg F., with January averaging 38deg and July averaging 80deg. During the winter, winds of exceptional violence occasionally blow from the N.N.W.

## Urban Layout and Landmarks
Baku is divided into a new town and an old town. The new town climbs the slopes behind a long stone quay located next to the harbour. To the west lies the old town, which possesses a decidedly oriental appearance with narrow, winding, and steep streets. 

Notable landmarks within the old town include:
* Ruins of a 16th-century palace belonging to the native khans.
* Mosques built by Persian shahs in 1078, which have been converted into an arsenal.
* The "maidens' tower," which serves as a lighthouse.
* Remains of ancient walls projecting above the sea, featuring Arabic architecture from the 9th and 10th centuries.

Additionally, the town's harbour area contains stores, barracks, dry docks, and engineering works belonging to the Russian Caspian fleet. Approximately 13 miles east of the town is the now disused temple of the Parsee fire-worshippers at Surakhani.

## Economy and Industry
Baku serves as a chief depot for Russian manufactured goods and merchandise arriving from Transcaspia and Persia, including timber, dried fruit, fish, wine, rice, silk, and raw cotton. The town's industrial base includes tobacco factories, sulphuric acid works, flour-mills, oil-works for fuel, and petroleum refineries.

The Apsheron peninsula is home to famous petroleum wells reaching depths between 700 and 1700 feet. These wells yield crude naphtha used to distill kerosene and petroleum. The heavier residue, known as *mazut*, is used as fuel (such as for Transcaspian railway locomotives) and lubricating oil. 

The output of crude naphtha grew from 5,500 tons in 1863 to 9,833,600 tons in 1904, though a serious fire in 1905 injured business. Major oil-fields include:
* Balakhany-Sabunchi-Romany: The largest field (6 sq. m.), located 8-1/2 miles north of the town.
* Bibi-Eybat: Located 3-1/2 miles south.
* The "black town" (Nobel's): Located 2 miles south-east.
* The "white town" (Rothschild's): Located beyond the black town.

Lighter oil is transported via pipes to Batum on the Black Sea for export, while heavier oils are sent via tank railway-cars to Poti, Batum, and Novorossiysk.

## Transport and Infrastructure
Baku is connected to the south Russian railway system at Beslan. From this junction, it connects to Vladikavkaz (400 m.), and via Tiflis to Poti (536 m.) and Batum (560 m.) on the Black Sea, as well as to Petrovsk and Derbent.

## History and Population
The town was mentioned in the 10th century by the Arab geographer Masudi. It came into Persian possession in 1509. The Russians captured the town in 1723, restored it to the Persians in 1735, and finally incorporated it into the Russian empire in 1806.

The population grew significantly over the late 19th and early 20th centuries:
* 1860: 13,381
* 1897: 112,253
* 1900: 179,133

Between 1904 and 1905, general political anarchy led to serious conflicts between Armenians and Tatars, resulting in the burning of two-thirds of the Bibi-Eybat and Balakhani oil-works.

## The Government of Baku
The broader government of Baku covers 15,172 sq. m. and stretches along the west coast of the Caspian Sea from 38deg 30' to 41deg 50' N. lat. It is bounded by Persia to the south, the province of Daghestan and the government of Elisavetpol to the west. The region includes the Apsheron peninsula, the Kuba plain, the steppes of the lower Aras and Kura, and the eastern Caucasus range, including the Bazardyuz (14,727 ft.) and the Shad-dagh (13,960 ft.).

The government is divided into six districts with the following chief towns:
* Baku (the capital)
* Shemakha (pop. 20,008 in 1897)
* Kuba (pop. 15,346 in 1897)
* Salyany (pop. 10,168 in 1897, in the district of Jevat)
* Lenkoran (pop. 8,768 in 1897)
* Geok-chai (pop. 2,247 in 1897)

The total population of the government in 1897 was 828,511. The majority were Tatars, with other populations including the Tates (89,519), Armenians (52,233), Talysh (34,994), Russians, and Caucasian mountaineers known as Kurins. Agriculture in the region includes cattle-breeding and the extensive growth of mulberry and vine. The slopes of the Caucasus are fertile and well-irrigated with gardens, cereals, and rice, while the Kura steppes require artificial irrigation for cotton.
