# Ashbourne

Ashbourne is a market town located in the western parliamentary division of Derbyshire, England. It is situated 13 miles west-north-west of Derby and is served by the North Staffordshire and London & North-Western railways. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 4,039.

## Geography and Landscape
The town is positioned on rising ground between two small valleys that open into the valley of the Dove. The surrounding area is noted for its beauty, with the scenery of Dovedale located not far from the town. Additionally, the local streams are considered favorable for trout fishermen.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town's church, St Oswald, is a fine building featuring a cruciform design. Its architecture consists of Early English and later styles, characterized by a central tower and a lofty octagonal spire. The church is also noted for its brasses and monuments, which are of significant interest.

Ashbourne Hall is an ancient mansion with historical associations involving "Prince Charlie," who occupied the residence both before and after his advance on Derby in 1745. The town also has connections to Dr. Johnson, who frequently visited his friend Dr. Taylor; Taylor's house was located opposite the grammar school.

## Economy and Trade
Ashbourne maintains a large agricultural trade and is a center for the manufacture of corsets. The town's agricultural significance is further highlighted by the holding of cheese fairs.
