# On the Adaptation of External Nature to the Physical Condition of Man

## Overview
*On the Adaptation of External Nature to the Physical Condition of Man* is a scientific work authored by John Kidd, M.D., published in 1833. The text was produced as one of the Bridgewater treatises, a series of publications appearing between 1833 and 1840 (and later in Bohn's Scientific Library) that explored various scientific disciplines with reference to natural theology. While the merit of the treatises in the series is described as unequal, several, including Kidd's contribution, attained a high rank within apologetic literature.

## Author Biography
John Kidd (1775–1851) was an English geologist, chemist, and physician. Born in Westminster on September 10, 1775, he was the son of Captain John Kidd, a naval officer. Kidd received his education at Westminster and Bury St Edmunds before attending Christ Church, Oxford, where he earned his B.A. in 1797 and his M.D. in 1804. Between 1797 and 1801, he studied at Guy's Hospital in London under the tutelage of Sir Astley Cooper.

Kidd's academic career at Oxford was distinguished by several appointments:
* **Chemistry:** He became a reader in chemistry in 1801 and was elected the first Aldrichian professor of chemistry in 1803.
* **Medicine:** In 1822, he succeeded Sir Christopher Pegge as the regius professor of medicine.
* **Library Science:** He was appointed keeper of the Radcliffe Library in 1834.

Beyond his official titles, Kidd was a popular and instructive lecturer. He voluntarily provided courses on geology and mineralogy in the dark chambers beneath the Ashmolean Museum. These lectures provided the foundational geological instruction for several scholars, including W. Buckland, C. G. B. Daubeny, and J. J. and W. D. Conybeare. His efforts were instrumental in the establishment of the geological chair, which was first held by Buckland. In 1818, Kidd became a F.R.C.P., and in 1834, he delivered the Harveian oration before the Royal College of Physicians. He died at Oxford on September 7, 1851.

## Context of the Bridgewater Treatises
Kidd's work on the physical condition of man was part of a broader collection of treatises that addressed the intersection of nature and theology. Other volumes in this series included:
* Thomas Chalmers, D.D., who wrote on the moral and intellectual condition of man.
* William Whewell, D.D., who focused on general physics and astronomy.
* Sir Charles Bell, who examined the mechanism and vital endowments of the hand.
* Peter Mark Roget, who wrote on animal and vegetable physiology.
* William Buckland, D.D., who addressed geology and mineralogy.
* William Kirby, who studied the instincts and habits of animals.
* William Prout, M.D., who covered chemistry, meteorology, and the function of digestion.
