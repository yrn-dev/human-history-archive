# Baring

The Barings are a family of English bankers and financiers. The family's presence in England began with John Baring, the son of a Lutheran minister from Bremen, Germany. After arriving in England, John Baring established a cloth manufactory at Larkbear, located near Exeter.

## Francis Baring and the Founding of the Firm
Francis Baring (1740–1810) was born at Larkbear and eventually entered a commercial firm in London. In 1770, Francis and his brother John established a banking-house in London. By the time of his death in 1810, Francis Baring had developed the business to such an extent that he was considered the first merchant in Europe.

Beyond his banking interests, Francis Baring was involved in governance and corporate leadership. He served as a director of the East India Company for many years and held the position of chairman from 1792 to 1793, for which he received a baronetcy. Additionally, he sat in parliament as a Whig almost continuously between 1784 and 1806.

## Alexander Baring (Lord Ashburton)
Francis Baring had five sons. While the eldest, Sir Thomas Baring (1772–1848), became a collector and patron of art, the control of the family business passed to the second son, Alexander Baring (1774–1848), better known as Lord Ashburton.

Alexander Baring expanded the firm's operations in America. His influence within the American commercial community was bolstered by his marriage to the daughter of William Bingham, a United States senator and wealthy resident of Philadelphia. Alexander's political career included representing various constituencies in parliament from 1806 to 1835, during which time he was a strong opponent of reform. 

In 1834, during the first administration of Sir Robert Peel, Alexander Baring served as the master of the mint and president of the Board of Trade. He was raised to the peerage as Baron Ashburton in 1835. Due to his familiarity with American institutions and customs, he was appointed as a commissioner to the United States in 1842 to negotiate disputes and the north-eastern boundary question. This resulted in the 1842 treaty concluded at Washington, known as the Ashburton treaty, which fixed the frontier between Canada and Maine.

## Later Management
Following the death of Lord Ashburton in 1848, the management of the house was taken over by Thomas Baring (1799–1873), who was the son of Sir Thomas Baring. Thomas Baring served in parliament as the representative for Huntingdon starting in 1844.
