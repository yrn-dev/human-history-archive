# Cast Iron Bridges of Paris

## Overview of Cast Iron Arch Construction
During the late 18th and early 19th centuries, engineers constructed cast iron arches capable of considerable spans. Because casting heavy arch ribs presented significant difficulties, builders often utilized cast voussoirs, a method similar to the construction of masonry bridges. Examples of this engineering approach outside of France include the Wearmouth bridge (1793-1796) with a span of 235 ft, and the Southwark bridge over the Thames (1814-1819), which featured a rise of 24 ft and a centre span of 240 ft.

## Cast Iron Bridges in Paris
In Paris, this architectural method was applied to the construction of the Austerlitz and Carrousel bridges. The Austerlitz bridge was constructed between 1800 and 1806, while the Carrousel bridge was built later, between 1834 and 1836. Both of these structures featured arches made of cast iron.

## Related Engineering Developments
The use of cast iron for arched structures extended to other functional designs during the 19th century. In 1858, M.C. Meigs (1816-1892) erected an aqueduct bridge in Washington. This specific structure utilized two arched ribs formed by cast iron pipes with an internal diameter of 4 ft and a thickness of 1½ in. To prevent the water from freezing, the pipes were lined with pine staves 3 in. thick. The total span of this aqueduct bridge was 200 ft. Other iron bridge constructions of the era included wrought iron arches used for a bridge over the Rhine at Coblenz, which consisted of three spans of approximately 315 ft each.
