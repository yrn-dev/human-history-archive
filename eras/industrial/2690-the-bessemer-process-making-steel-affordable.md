# The Bessemer Process: Making Steel Affordable

## Overview
Before the mid-19th century, steel was a luxury material. While stronger and more flexible than cast iron (which is brittle) and wrought iron (which is soft), steel was produced in small batches via the crucible process, making it prohibitively expensive for large-scale infrastructure. This changed in 1856 with the introduction of the Bessemer process.

Developed by Sir Henry Bessemer in England, this method revolutionized metallurgy by introducing the concept of "oxidation." Bessemer discovered that blowing air through molten pig iron removed impurities—specifically carbon, silicon, and manganese—through oxidation. This reaction was exothermic, meaning it generated its own heat, eliminating the need for additional fuel to keep the metal molten. By controlling the amount of carbon remaining in the metal, manufacturers could produce high-quality steel in massive quantities and at a fraction of the previous cost.

This shift catalyzed the Second Industrial Revolution. Affordable steel enabled the construction of expansive railway networks, the rise of skyscrapers in cities like Chicago and New York, and the creation of more durable machinery and naval vessels.

## Key Facts
*   **The Converter:** The heart of the process was the "Bessemer Converter," a large, pear-shaped vessel that could tilt to pour out the finished steel.
*   **The Phosphorus Problem:** Early Bessemer steel often suffered from "cold shortness" (brittleness) because the original process could not remove phosphorus. Historians and engineers note that this limited the types of iron ore that could be used until Sidney Gilchrist Thomas developed a basic lining (using limestone) for the converter in 1878.
*   **Economic Impact:** The cost of steel plummeted. In the United States, the price of steel rails dropped from roughly \$100 per ton to about \$12 per ton within a few decades.
*   **Historical Debate:** There is a long-standing historical discussion regarding the "invention" of the process. William Kelly in the United States had developed a similar pneumatic process independently around the same time. While Bessemer received the primary fame and patents, Kelly is recognized by many historians as a co-innovator.

## Did You Know?
1. **Self-Heating:** The Bessemer process is one of the few industrial processes where the chemical reaction itself provides the energy required to maintain the temperature of the material.
2. **The "Steel Age":** The Bessemer process is credited with transitioning the world from the "Age of Iron" to the "Age of Steel."
3. **Railroad Revolution:** Before this process, rails were made of iron and wore out every few years; steel rails lasted ten times longer, drastically reducing maintenance costs for railroads.
