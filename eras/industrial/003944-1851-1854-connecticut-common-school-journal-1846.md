# Educational and Professional Activities (1846–1855)

## Henry Barnard and the Connecticut Common School Journal
Henry Barnard (1811–1900) was a prominent American educationalist born in Hartford, Connecticut. His career was marked by extensive efforts to reform and reorganize the common school system. Between 1838 and 1842, he served as the secretary of the board of "commissioners of common schools" in Connecticut, a board established by a bill he himself framed and introduced in 1838 to ensure the better supervision of common schools.

Barnard's editorial work was central to his influence in the field of education. He edited the *Connecticut Common School Journal* during two separate periods: first from 1838 to 1842, and again from 1851 to 1854. Additionally, from 1846 to 1849, he served as the editor of the *Journal of the Rhode Island Institute of Instruction*.

## Administrative Roles in Rhode Island and Connecticut
Following his early work in Connecticut, Barnard was appointed by the governor of Rhode Island as an agent to examine and recommend improvements for the state's public schools in 1843. This work led to a reorganization of the Rhode Island school system two years later. From 1845 to 1849, Barnard served as the first commissioner of public schools in Rhode Island, an administration characterized by significant educational progress.

Upon returning to Connecticut, Barnard held two major positions from 1851 to 1855: he served as the "superintendent of common schools" and as the principal of the State Normal School located at New Britain, Connecticut.

## Perspectives on the Kindergarten System
In 1854, the kindergarten system was introduced into England. Henry Barnard provided a report on this development in his 1854 *Report to Governor of Connecticut*, in which he described the kindergarten as the most philosophical, attractive, and original form of infant development the world had seen to that point. Barnard later aided Mrs. Horace Mann and Miss Elizabeth Peabody in beginning the kindergarten movement in the United States in 1867.
