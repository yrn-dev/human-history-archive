# Fort House and Bleak House

## Overview of Broadstairs
Broadstairs is a watering-place located in the Isle of Thanet parliamentary division of Kent, England. Situated on the South-Eastern & Chatham railway, it lies 3 miles south-east of Margate. As of 1901, the population of the urban district of Broadstairs and St Peter's was 6,466. The village is characterized by a "semicircular sweep of houses" and has evolved into a considerable town due to the arrival of summer visitors. To accommodate these guests, the town provides numerous boarding-houses, hotels—including the "Albion"—libraries, a fine promenade, and a bathing establishment.

## Charles Dickens in Broadstairs
Between 1837 and 1851, Broadstairs served as a favorite summer resort for Charles Dickens. In a sketch titled "Our English Watering-Place," Dickens described the location as a place that the "tide of years" had left "high and dry." 

During his time there, Dickens maintained a residence known as Fort House. Although the house was originally called Fort House, it eventually became known as Bleak House. This name change occurred through an association with his novel of the same name, though the novel was written after Dickens' final visit to Broadstairs in 1851.

## Local Geography and Landmarks
Broadstairs features a small pier used for fishing-boats, which was first constructed during the reign of Henry VIII. An archway that leads to the shore contains an inscription indicating it was erected in 1540 by George Culmer. Nearby is the site of a chapel of the Virgin; historically, ships passing by would lower their top-sails in respect to this chapel.

The urban district also includes St Peter's parish, located on the landward side, which contains a church with origins dating from the 12th century to the end of the 16th century. To the north of Broadstairs on the coast is Kingsgate, located on the North Foreland. This area was formerly called St Bartholomew's Gate but was renamed in honor of the Duke of York and Charles II, who landed there in 1683 while traveling from London to Dover. Additionally, Stonehouse, located close by, served as the residence of Archbishop Tait and is currently a preparatory school for boys; it was here that the Archbishop's wife established an orphanage.
