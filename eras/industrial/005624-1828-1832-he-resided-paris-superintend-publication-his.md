# Peter Oluf Bröndsted

Peter Oluf Bröndsted (1780-1842) was a Danish traveller and archaeologist. Born on November 17, 1780, in Fruering, Jutland, he pursued studies at the university of Copenhagen before beginning a career defined by the study of antiquities and extensive European travel.

## Early Travels and Archaeological Work
In 1806, Bröndsted visited Paris accompanied by his friend Georg Koes. Following a two-year stay in Paris, the two travelled to Italy. Driven by a shared zeal for antiquities, Bröndsted and Koes joined an expedition to Greece in 1810. During their three years of active research in Greece, they conducted excavations at the temple of Apollo at Bassae in Arcadia and the temples of Zeus in Aegina.

## Academic and Diplomatic Career
Upon his return to Copenhagen, Bröndsted was appointed professor of Greek at the university as a reward for his archaeological labours. While he began preparing the vast materials collected during his travels for publication, he found that Copenhagen lacked the necessary facilities for his work. Consequently, in 1818, he exchanged his professorship for the role of Danish envoy at the papal court, which led him to reside in Rome.

Bröndsted continued to expand his research materials, visiting the Ionian Isles and Sicily in 1820 and 1821. In 1826, he travelled to London to study other remains of antiquity and the Elgin marbles at the British Museum, where he established connections with the leading archaeologists of England.

## Publication and Later Years
From 1828 to 1832, Bröndsted resided in Paris for the purpose of superintending the publication of his work, *Travels*. Following this period, he returned to Copenhagen to serve as the director of the collection of coins and medals as well as the museum of antiquities. 

His principal scholarly contribution was the *Travels and Archaeological Researches in Greece*, published between 1826 and 1830 in French and German. Of this work, only two volumes were published, focusing on the metopes of the Parthenon and the island of Ceos.

In 1842, Bröndsted attained the position of rector of the university. His life ended on June 26, 1842, following a fall from his horse.
