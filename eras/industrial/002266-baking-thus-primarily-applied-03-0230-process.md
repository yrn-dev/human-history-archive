# Baking

## Definition and Etymology
Baking is the action of the verb "to bake," a term common to Teutonic languages (such as the German *backen*) that means to cook by dry heat. While the term is primarily applied to the process of preparing bread, it is also used to describe the "firing" or hardening of materials such as bricks, earthenware, or pottery through the application of heat.

## Applications in Food Preparation
In the context of food, baking is used for various staples and delicacies:
*   **Bread and Biscuits:** Baking is the primary method for preparing bread. A specific form of unvesiculated bread, made into thin cakes of various shapes and baked to be short and crisp, is known as a biscuit (from the Latin *bis coctum*, meaning "twice cooked"). In the north of England and the United States, the term "biscuit" is also used for vesiculated bread baked in small cakes or flat loaves; in the United States, the former crisp variety is typically called a cracker.
*   **Botanical Foods:** Certain plant products are prepared via baking. The fleshly fruit of *Melocanna*, a plant native to India and sometimes cultivated in Mauritius, is baked and eaten.
*   **Confectionery:** The Dutch term *banket* refers to a sweetmeat resembling almond hard-bake. This term is the root for the South African mining term "banket," used to describe beds of auriferous conglomerate in the Witwatersrand gold-fields due to their physical resemblance to the sweetmeat.

## Industrial and Material Applications
Baking is a critical process in the production of ceramics, construction materials, and specialized industrial linings.

### Ceramics and Pottery
The process of firing pottery, porcelain, or earthenware is referred to as baking. Earthenware or porcelain that has undergone its initial baking and is prepared for glazing is termed "bisque" or "biscuit."

### Construction and Furnace Linings
Baking is used to produce bricks and refractory materials for furnaces:
*   **Firebricks:** Siliceous bricks containing ferric oxide are used in brewers' coppers and boiler flues. Red firebricks, known as Windsor bricks, share a composition similar to soft red sandstone.
*   **Electric Furnace Materials:** In 1899, Engels applied carborundum to furnace linings by washing firebricks with carborundum paste and then baking them.

### Metallurgy and Mineral Processing
Baking is employed in the extraction and refinement of minerals. In Kermán, tutia is produced by kneading ore with water and baking it in crucibles within a potter's kiln. Other accounts describe tutia as being made from an argillaceous ore of zinc that is moulded into tubular cakes and baked until it reaches a moderate hardness.

## Specialized Technical Uses
Baking is utilized in precision medical and dental prosthetics. In the "continuous gum method" of denture construction—perfected by Dr. John Allen of New York around 1846-1847—porcelain teeth are attached to a platinum base-plate. The interstices between the teeth are filled with a porcelain paste called the "body," which is then baked in a muffle furnace until it becomes vitrified. Subsequently, a vitreous enamel is applied and fired to imitate the color of natural gums.
