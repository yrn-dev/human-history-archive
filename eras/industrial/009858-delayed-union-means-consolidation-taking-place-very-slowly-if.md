# Delayed Union and Consolidation

In various fields of study, the terms "delayed union" and "consolidation" describe processes of joining, strengthening, or unifying that occur over time. These processes range from biological healing and chemical transitions to political unification and the growth of civilizations.

## Medical Definition of Delayed Union
In the context of orthopedics, specifically regarding fractures in young people, delayed union refers to a state where the consolidation of a bone is taking place very slowly, or perhaps not at all. This is distinct from a "special fracture" known as diastasis, where the end of a bone becomes detached from the shaft in children or growing persons. While diastasis can lead to stiffness in neighboring joints or an arrest of growth, delayed union is a specific rate of healing.

Delayed union may be caused by constitutional factors or local causes. If the bones are maintained in a good position, the recommended treatment involves patience, general health-measures, and massage. This condition differs from an "ununited fracture," which is a state where nature has made no attempt to consolidate the parts after several months or weeks. Ununited fractures may result from constitutional defects, the interposition of a tendon or muscle between broken ends, constant disturbance of the fracture site, or the failure to bring the bone ends close enough together. Treatment for ununited fractures—unless caused by constitutional defects—requires surgical intervention to freshen the broken ends by sawing off a thin slice and fixing them with a screw or wire. In children, ununited fractures of the leg-bones are considered a rebellious and unsatisfactory condition.

## Chemical and Geological Consolidation
Consolidation and transition also occur in the physical sciences, often characterized by slow rates of change. In the solid state, a transition from one crystalline form to another may occur at a definite temperature. This process is typically slower than the transition from solid to liquid due to the small molecular mobility of solids. For example, rhombic sulphur passes slowly into a monosymmetric form at 95.6 deg. C. Similarly, a solid solution of carbon in iron passes into a soft form called "pearlite" at approximately 700 deg. C. when cooled slowly.

In geology, consolidation refers to the hardening of rock. Recent investigations into coral reefs indicate that changes are occurring in rocks that have not long consolidated. One such process is the "metasomatic" substitution of calcite by the double salt dolomite. In the Carboniferous limestones of Britain, limestones pass into dolomites along faults, fissures, or joints. This process alters the minute structure of the rock, effaces fossils, and creates cavities due to the higher specific gravity of dolomite compared to limestone.

## Political and State Consolidation
Consolidation is frequently used to describe the unification of political entities or the strengthening of a party's position.

### European and Asian States
In 1397, Margaret (1375-1412) consolidated the work of Valdemar by establishing the Union of Kalmar. This effort sought to combine three northern kingdoms into a single state dominated by Denmark. While the system functioned during Margaret's life, it was unpopular in Sweden and Norway because Denmark ignored stipulations that each country retain its own customs and be ruled by natives.

In Asia, the consolidation of China presented a significant obstacle to Hunnish peoples. The establishment of a firm government, disciplined armies, and a wall prevented the Hunnish population from moving southward into the disorders of a divided China. Following this consolidation, the Chinese civilization spread slowly and continuously for three thousand years, extending into Tibet, Manchuria, Siberia, and toward the Gobi desert.

### Greek and Macedonian Ambitions
Early city-states in Mesopotamia and the Nile valley were consolidated into empires by god kings and priest kings over long ages. In contrast, the Greeks in the seventh century B.C. built their civilization upon the ruins of existing systems. By the fourth century B.C., the philosopher Isocrates and the historian Herodotus developed ideas regarding the possibility of a consolidated Greece conquering Asia—an ambition later pursued by Philip of Macedonia.

### Parliamentary Consolidation
In a political context, consolidation may refer to the internal unification of a political party. In one instance, the Unionists, finding themselves a miserable minority in parliament (157 members compared to 379 Liberals, 83 Nationalists, and 51 Labour members), sought consolidation. This resulted in an agreement between Mr. Chamberlain and Mr. Balfour, confirmed at Lansdowne House, which established fiscal reform as the party's primary constructive work.
