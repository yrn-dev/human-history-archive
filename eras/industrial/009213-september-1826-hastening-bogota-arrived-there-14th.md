# Bolivar and the Political Crisis of 1826-1827

## Travels and Assumption of Power
In September 1826, Simon Bolivar departed from Lima. Before his departure, he had nominated a council to manage the government, placing Santa Cruz at its head. Bolivar traveled with haste toward Bogota, arriving there on November 14. Upon his arrival, he immediately assumed extraordinary powers; these were powers that the constitution authorized the president to exercise in the event of a rebellion.

Following a brief period in the capital, Bolivar moved forward to Venezuela to attempt to stop the effusion of blood, as the situation there had deteriorated beyond what he had contemplated. He reached Puerto Cabello on December 31 and issued a decree the following day offering a general amnesty. After a friendly meeting with Paez, Bolivar entered Caracas. He established his headquarters there to monitor the northern departments, which had served as the primary theater for the disturbances.

## Presidential Transitions and Resignation
During this period, Bolivar and Santander were re-elected to the offices of president and vice-president, respectively. According to the law, they were meant to qualify for these positions in January 1827. However, in February, Bolivar formally resigned from the presidency of the republic. In doing so, he expressed a desire to retire into private life on his patrimonial estate, stating his determination to refute accusations of ambition that had been freely cast upon him.

Santander opposed this resignation, urging Bolivar to resume his role as constitutional president. Santander argued that the personal influence and authority of the liberator were the only means by which the agitations and troubles of the country could be appeased. This position was confirmed by a resolution of congress, though the resolution was not unanimous. Consequently, Bolivar decided to resume his functions and returned to Bogota to take the necessary oaths.

## Restoration of Order
Prior to his return to Bogota, Bolivar issued three simultaneous decrees:
1. A grant of general amnesty.
2. The convocation of a national convention at Ocana.
3. The establishment of constitutional order throughout Colombia.

His return to the capital was accelerated by events occurring in the southern departments and Peru, which threatened the foundation of his power.

## Legacy and Contributions
Bolivar is credited with achieving the independence of three states and introducing a new spirit to the southern portion of the New World. His efforts included the purification of the administration of justice, the fostering of national interests, and the encouragement of the sciences and arts. He also worked to ensure that other countries recognized the independence he had helped secure.

Despite having unlimited control over the revenues of Bolivia, Peru, and Colombia for a considerable time, Bolivar died without any public money in his possession, having spent nine-tenths of his own splendid patrimony in service to his country.

His memory has been honored with several monuments:
* A monument in Caracas, where his remains were moved in 1842.
* A statue in Bogota, erected in 1846.
* An equestrian statue in Lima, erected by Peruvians in 1858.
* A statue in New York's Central Park, erected in 1884.

Documentation of his career includes twenty-two volumes of official documents published in Caracas between 1826 and 1833, as well as two volumes of correspondence published in New York in 1866. Biographies have been written by Rojas (1883), Ducoudray-Holstein (1831), and Larrazabal (1866).
