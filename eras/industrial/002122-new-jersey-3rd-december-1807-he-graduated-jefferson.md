# Gamaliel Bailey

## Early Life and Education
Gamaliel Bailey was an American journalist born on December 3, 1807, in Mount Holly, New Jersey. He pursued medical studies and graduated from the Jefferson Medical College in Philadelphia in 1827.

## Medical and Early Editorial Career
Following his graduation, Bailey spent a brief period editing a religious journal based in Baltimore titled the *Methodist Protestant*. In 1831, he relocated to Cincinnati, Ohio. Upon arriving in Cincinnati, he initially focused almost exclusively on the practice of medicine. During this time, he also served as a lecturer on physiology at the Lane Theological Seminary.

## Abolitionism and Journalism
Bailey's political views shifted during the February 1834 debates at Lane Seminary, which took place between students who were anti-slavery and those who were pro-slavery. Following the withdrawal of the anti-slavery students, Bailey became an ardent abolitionist.

In 1836, he entered the field of editorial control for the *Philanthropist*, joining James G. Birney. Bailey succeeded Birney as the editor the following year. He continued to conduct the paper until 1847, despite facing acts of violence and threats; during his tenure, a mob wrecked the printing office on three separate occasions. Additionally, starting in 1843, Bailey edited a daily publication called the *Herald*.

## The National Era
In 1847, Bailey assumed control of the *National Era*, a new abolitional organ located in Washington, D.C. This publication also became a target for pro-slavery mobs. In 1848, the printers and the editor were besieged within their office for a period of three days. The *National Era* achieved considerable circulation and is notable for being the first to publish Mrs. H. B. Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* between 1851 and 1852.

## Death
Gamaliel Bailey died on June 5, 1859, while at sea during a trip to Europe.
