# C-shaped and Curved Tubes in Mechanical and Musical Instruments

The use of curved, U-shaped, and bent tubing is a common feature across various mechanical systems and musical instruments to manage space, facilitate fluid or air movement, and adjust technical performance.

## Fluid and Gas Regulation Systems
In specific gas regulation apparatus, such as the regulator designed by R. A. Buddicom, U-shaped glass tubes are utilized to maintain constant pressure. In this system, a U-shaped tube contains mercury, with one limb receiving gas from a meter through a glass tube (G) and the other limb interacting with a counter-poising weight. As gas pressure rises, a bell is raised, lowering the weight and forcing mercury up the limb of the U-tube. This action diminishes the size of the oblique orifice in tube G, cutting off some of the gas to maintain a constant pressure.

## Musical Instrument Construction
Curved and bent tubing is essential for the structural design and tuning of various wind instruments.

### Woodwinds and Brass
The bassoon features a wooden tube approximately 8 feet long that is doubled back upon itself to reduce the instrument's height to about 4 feet. This design necessitates a double bore in the butt (the lower end when played) due to the abrupt bend of the tube. While older models used a flat oval cork pad to stop the prolongation of the double tube, modern bassoons employ a U-shaped tube. Additionally, the bassoon uses a "crook," which is a narrow curved metal tube roughly 12 inches long that connects to the double reed mouthpiece.

In the family of obsolete woodwind instruments known as cornets or cornetts, the curved cornet (referred to as *krummer Zinck* or *Stadtkalb* in German and *cornetto curvo* in Italian) required a specific construction method. Because the bore could not be pierced through a single piece of wood, the channel for the air was hollowed out of two pieces of wood.

In brass instruments, U-shaped slides are utilized as a means of compensating for slight errors in intonation. This is particularly relevant when valves are used in combination; for example, when valves 1 and 3 are depressed, the additional length supplied by valve 3 may be too short to produce a minor third strictly in tune, resulting in notes that are too sharp.

## Industrial Boiler Systems
Water-tube boilers utilize complex tubing arrangements to facilitate the circulation of water and the flow of combustion products.

### Babcock & Wilcox Boilers
In the land-based Babcock & Wilcox boiler, a sloping nest of tubes is placed under a horizontal cylinder. These tubes are connected by front and back headers made of wrought steel that are sinuous in form. In the marine version of the Babcock & Wilcox boiler, the elements also consist of front and back headers connected by numerous water-tubes with a considerable inclination to facilitate circulation. The back headers in the marine model are connected to the steam-chest via two long horizontal tubes.

### Belleville Boilers
The Belleville boiler, used in the British navy, employs elements consisting of straight seamless steel tubes. These tubes are connected at their ends by malleable cast iron junction-boxes arranged vertically. The lower front box of each element is connected to a horizontal cross-tube of square section known as a "feed-collector."

## Other Tubular Applications
Other historical applications of bent or shaped tubing include the diagonal barometer, suggested by Sir Samuel Morland and Bernardo Ramazzini, where the upper part of the tube is inclined to the lower part. Even when the tube is bent through 45 degrees, the readings are only increased in a ratio of 7 to 5.

In bicycle construction, tubular portions of the frame are made of weldless cold-drawn steel. To ensure strength at junctions, such as the steering post and fork sides, some makers use internal liners or butt-ended tubes that are drawn thicker at the ends than in the middle.
