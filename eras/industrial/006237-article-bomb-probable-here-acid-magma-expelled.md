# BOMB

In the fields of geology and military history, the term "bomb" refers to distinct phenomena involving the expulsion of materials or the use of explosive projectiles.

## Geological Bombs
In geology, a bomb is defined as a mass of lava that has been hurled from a volcanic vent through explosive action. These masses vary significantly in size, ranging from the size of a walnut to masses that weigh several tons. Their structure can be solid, hollow, or more or less cavernous, and they typically appear in discoidal, ellipsoidal, or spheroidal shapes.

It is generally believed that the shape of a bomb is partly the result of the mass rotating during its flight through the air; in some instances, a gyratory movement causes the bomb to become twisted. However, some experts contest this classification. Professor J.D. Dana demonstrated that many objects regarded as bombs in Hawaii are actually lava-balls that were not hurled through the air. Similarly, Dr. H.J. Johnston-Lavis argued that many so-called bombs from Vesuvius are merely globular masses formed within a lava stream rather than projectiles.

### Specialized Types of Geological Bombs
Certain masses of pumice ejected from Vulcano are referred to by Johnston-Lavis as "bread-crust bombs." These are characterized by a coating of obsidian that has been cracked and bent, resembling the crust of a roll. This occurs because acid magma was likely expelled in a very viscous condition; as it cooled, a crust formed which was subsequently burst by steam from occluded water.

Other examples include bombs from recent eruptions of Etna, which consist of white granular quartz encased in a black scoriaceous crust, where the quartz is an altered sandstone. Additionally, bombs of granular olivine have been found in some tuffs in the Eifel and are common in geological collections.

## Military Bombing
In a military context, bombing refers to the use of explosive projectiles delivered by aircraft. Historical perspectives on the evolution of this practice indicate a shift from early "prentice days" to more advanced warfare. 

Sir Louis predicted that the Air Force Estimates would become a primary part of war preparations, discussing the conversion of commercial flying machines for reconnaissance and bombing. He suggested that bombers in future conflicts would move away from targets located near the front lines of armies to achieve better results. Instead, they would target centers where troops are trained and stores are manufactured. In practice, as seen in the east of England and London between 1917 and 1918, this resulted in the promiscuous bombing of population centers. Predictions for subsequent wars suggested the use of a vastly larger number of aeroplanes and the deployment of "bigger and much nastier bombs."
