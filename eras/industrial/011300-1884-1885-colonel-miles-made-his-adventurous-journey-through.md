# Colonel S.B. Miles and the Exploration of Oman

## Exploration of the Arabian Peninsula
During the late 19th century, research into the physiography of the great oases and deserts of Arabia expanded significantly. Following the work of Wilfrid S. Blunt and Charles Doughty in northern Arabia between 1877 and 1878, further investigations were conducted in the south-west approximately ten years later by E. Glaser and G. Schweinfurth. 

Within this context of geographical discovery, Colonel S.B. Miles undertook an adventurous journey through Oman during the years 1884-1885. His travels contributed to the broader effort to bring additional light to the western fields of research regarding the Arabian region.

## Contemporary Geographical Research
The journey of Colonel Miles occurred alongside other significant investigations into ancient Semitic history and geography. In 1888, Theodore Bent conducted investigations in the Bahrein Islands, and later, between 1894 and 1895, Bent performed further research in Hadramut. 

These efforts were part of a wider era of exploration across Asia. During the same period as Miles' journey, other explorers were active in different regions:
* **Central Asia:** Between 1884 and 1886, the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission mapped the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan. This commission included Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich. Simultaneously, Ney Elias crossed from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs, identifying the "Dragon Lake," known as Rangkul.
* **The Hindu Kush:** Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart led a mission across the Hindu Kush into Wakhan. This provided an opportunity for Colonel Woodthorpe to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush and verify previous explorations conducted by W.W. M'Nair.
* **Other Asian Expeditions:** In 1885, Andrew Dalgleish and Arthur Douglas Carey contributed new data to the map of Asia by following the tracks of Prjevalsky. In 1886, Captain (later Sir Francis) Younghusband crossed the Muztagh—the mountain barrier between Kashmir and China—to travel across the heart of the continent.
