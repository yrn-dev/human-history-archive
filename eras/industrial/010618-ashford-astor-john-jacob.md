# ASHFORD

The name Ashford refers to several distinct locations and geological sites across England, including a market town in Kent and areas of industry and quarrying in Derbyshire.

## Ashford, Kent
Ashford is a market town situated in the Southern or Ashford parliamentary division of Kent, England, located 56 miles southeast of London. It is positioned on a gentle eminence near the confluence of the upper branches of the river Stour. As of 1901, the urban district had a population of 12,808.

The town is served by the South-Eastern & Chatham railway, which maintains large carriage and locomotive works there. Other local industries include breweries and agricultural implement works. The town features a Perpendicular church dedicated to St Mary, characterized by a well-proportioned, lofty tower and various monuments. Additionally, a grammar school was established in the town during the reign of Charles I by Sir Norman Knatchbull. On the south-eastern outskirts of the town lies the populous village of Willesborough, which had a population of 3,602.

Historically, Ashford (also referred to as Esselesford, Asshatisforde, or Essheford) was held by Hugh de Montfort during the Domesday survey; de Montfort had arrived in England with William the Conqueror. The town has never been incorporated. Its commercial history is marked by several royal grants:
* **1243:** Henry III granted the lord of the manor an annual fair and a Saturday market.
* **1349 and 1466:** Edward III and Edward IV, respectively, granted further annual fairs.
* **1607:** At the petition of the inhabitants, James I granted Sir John Smith, Kt., the right to hold a court of record every third Tuesday.
* **1672:** Charles II granted a market to be held every second Tuesday, including a court of pie-powder.

In the latter half of the 18th century, the cattle trade grew due to the fertile pasture-land of Romney Marsh to the east and south, leading to the creation of a stock market in 1784. Nearby locations of note include Charing, where the archbishops of Canterbury maintained a residence from pre-Conquest times (with ruins of a Decorated period palace remaining), and Bethersden, where marble quarries once extensively supplied local churches and the cathedrals of Rochester and Canterbury.

## Ashford, Derbyshire
In Derbyshire, Ashford is noted for its mineral resources and historical industry. The Domesday Survey records the presence of lead mines at Ashford, along with other sites such as Bakewell, Matlock, Crich, and Wirksworth.

The area is also a source of high-quality Carboniferous limestone. The finest kind of black marble is obtained from near Ashford in Derbyshire. Additionally, the region produces "rosewood marble," a dense brown laminated limestone that, when polished, resembles the grain of rosewood. Due to its limited quantity, this stone is primarily used for inlaid work.
