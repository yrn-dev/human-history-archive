# British Constitutional Theory of Colonial Dependence

## Theory of Direct Dependence
Within the working of the British constitution, there exists a recognized theory regarding the direct and exclusive dependence of colonial possessions on the crown. While it has not been found practicable to carry out this theory in its full extent, the principle is recognized as far as possible. Under this constitutional framework, it is understood that the principal sections of the empire are not subordinate to one another, but instead enjoy equal rights under the crown.

## Role of Parliament and the Crown
The intervention of the imperial parliament in the affairs of the colonies is admitted only in a theoretical capacity. Specifically, such intervention is recognized only to the extent that the constitutional advisers of the crown require the support of parliament. 

To bring the actual practice of the empire into complete harmony with this theory, it would be necessary to establish a council to advise the crown on imperial affairs. Such a council would require representation from all the important parts of the empire.

## Evolution of Self-Governance
The way has been cleared for further development of the supreme headship of the empire to align more closely with modern ideas. This progress has been facilitated by two primary factors:
1. The gradual recognition of the constitutional theory of the British empire.
2. The assumption of full self-governing responsibilities by the principal colonies.
