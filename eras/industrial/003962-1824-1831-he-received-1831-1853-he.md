# Sir Edward Barnes

## Military Career and Early Promotions
Sir Edward Barnes (1776–1838) was a British soldier who began his military service in 1792 upon entering the 47th regiment. He experienced a rapid ascent through the field ranks, achieving the rank of lieutenant-colonel in 1807 and colonel in 1810. Two years after his promotion to colonel, Barnes was deployed to the Peninsula to serve on the staff of Wellington. His contributions during this period led to further promotion to major-general, a rank with which he led a brigade during the Pyrenean battles and at Vittoria. For his service in the Peninsula, he was awarded three clasps and the cross.

Barnes continued his service as adjutant-general during the campaign of 1815, during which he was wounded at the Battle of Waterloo. His military achievements were recognized with several honors; in addition to being a K.C.B., he received the Russian order of St Anne and the Austrian order of Maria Theresa.

## Governance of Ceylon
In 1819, Barnes established a connection with Ceylon, subsequently serving as the island's governor from 1824 to 1831. During his tenure, he focused on infrastructure and administration. He directed the construction of several lines of communication, most notably the great military road connecting Kandy and Colombo. Barnes was also responsible for conducting the first census of the population and, in 1824, introduced coffee cultivation based on the West Indian system.

## Commander-in-Chief in India and Later Life
Following his governorship of Ceylon, Barnes received the G.C.B. in 1831. From 1831 to 1853, he served as the commander-in-chief in India, holding the local rank of general.

Upon returning to his home country, Barnes sought entry into politics. After two unsuccessful attempts to secure a seat, he became the M.P. for Sudbury in 1837. He died in the following year.

## Legacy and Commemoration
Sir Edward Barnes is commemorated in Ceylon through a memorial statue erected in Colombo. Additionally, a portrait of him was painted for Ceylon by the artist John Wood.
