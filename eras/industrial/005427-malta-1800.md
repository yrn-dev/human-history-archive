# Malta

## British Acquisition and Governance
Malta came under British control in 1800 through capitulation. Following the fall of the island, Sir Alexander John Ball, a British rear-admiral, served as the governor of Malta from 1801 until his death on October 20, 1809. Ball had previously established a strong rapport with the Maltese people during a two-year blockade of the island, during which he led the local militia and marines in a siege on the land side. 

As governor, Ball was noted for his regard for the interests of the local population and his opposition to policies that would treat Malta as a conquered dependency. He was buried on the island upon his death in 1809.

## Strategic Military Importance
Malta has historically served as a vital Mediterranean base. Its strategic value was such that Britain fought Napoleon for many years to secure it; the renewal of war in 1803 was specifically pretexted by the issue of Malta, summarized by the phrase "*Malta ou la guerre!*" 

In later conflicts, the island continued to be a critical naval asset. During the crisis involving Germany, the British ship *Dublin* was sent to Malta to coal before taking destroyers to Troubridge. Military records indicate that Malta provided "certain and instant" coaling facilities, making it a preferred location for battle cruisers to refuel and reposition to close exits from Messina or join naval forces in the Adriatic. During the cooperation between French and British fleets in the Mediterranean, French Admirals spoke of basing the French Fleet on Malta, with instructions given to use the base as if it were Toulon.

## Historical and Notable Events
The archive records various individuals who visited or resided on the island. In 1808, the duke of Orleans took the comte de Beaujolais to Malta while the comte was suffering from consumption; the comte subsequently died there on May 29.

In the broader historical context, the island is associated with the Hospitallers, who emigrated to Malta and Rhodes. The *Histoire de Chevaliers de Malthe* by l’Abbe de Vertot provides a depiction of the order's activities, including its role in the defense of Palestine prior to its emigration.
