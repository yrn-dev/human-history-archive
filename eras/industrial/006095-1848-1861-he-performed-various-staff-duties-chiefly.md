# Don Carlos Buell

## Early Life and Education
Don Carlos Buell was an American soldier born on March 23, 1818, near Marietta, Ohio. He attended West Point, from which he graduated in 1841.

## Early Military Career
Following his graduation, Buell served as a company officer of infantry. During this period, he participated in the Seminole War of 1841-42 and the Mexican War. In the latter conflict, he was present at nearly all the battles led by Generals Scott and Taylor. His service in the Mexican War earned him the brevet of captain at Monterey and the brevet of major at Contreras-Churubusco, where he sustained a wound.

## Staff Duties and the Civil War
From 1848 to 1861, Buell performed various staff duties, serving chiefly as assistant-adjutant-general. Upon the outbreak of the Civil War, he received several rapid appointments: he was named lieutenant-colonel on May 11, 1861, brigadier-general of volunteers a few days later, and major-general of volunteers in March 1862.

Buell provided efficient aid in the organization of the Army of the Potomac. In November 1861, at the request of General McClellan, Buell was sent to Kentucky to take over command from General William T. Sherman. In this role, he focused on the training and organization of the Army of the Ohio (which later became the Army of the Cumberland). This army maintained a level of efficiency and discipline that was surpassed only by the Army of the Potomac throughout its existence.

## Major Campaigns and Command
In the spring of 1862, Buell followed the retreating Confederates under the command of Sidney Johnston. He arrived on the field of Shiloh at the conclusion of the first day of fighting; on the second day, Grant was able to carry all before him with the assistance of Buell's well-trained and fresh army. Buell later served under Halleck during the advance on Corinth.

In the autumn, Buell commanded the campaign in Kentucky against Bragg. This period involved maneuvering in which Buell barely held his own, eventually resulting in the indecisive battle of Perryville.

## Removal from Command and Professional Reputation
Buell was eventually removed from command due to his objection to a campaign plan ordered by the authorities in Washington and the alleged tardiness of his pursuit. 

While recognized for his gifts as a disciplinarian and organizer, Buell was described as haughty in his interactions with civil authorities. In high command, he demonstrated an utter disregard for the requirements of the political situation and a general, unnecessary tardiness of movement. He was a friend of General McClellan and shared similar views.
