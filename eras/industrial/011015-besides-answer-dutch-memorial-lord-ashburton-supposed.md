# Lord Ashburton

The title of Baron Ashburton has been held by different individuals in English history, most notably John Dunning and Alexander Baring.

## John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton
John Dunning (1731–1783) was an English lawyer born in Ashburton, Devonshire, the second son of an attorney. Educated at the local free grammar school, he entered the Middle Temple at age nineteen and was called to the bar in 1756. His early career was slow, and he spent several years on the western circuit without receiving a single brief.

Dunning's reputation was established in 1762 when he was employed to draft a defense for the British East India Company against a memorial of grievances submitted to the crown by the Dutch East India Company. The "masterly style" of this document brought him both emolument and fame. He further distinguished himself in 1763 as counsel for Wilkes, specifically through his powerful arguments against the validity of general warrants in the case of *Leach v. Money*. By 1776, his legal practice had grown significantly, with reported annual receipts of nearly £10,000.

His political career included serving as the recorder of Bristol in 1766 and as solicitor-general from December 1767 until May 1770. A member of parliament for Calne starting in 1768, Dunning became a prominent member of the opposition. In 1780, he successfully carried a motion stating that the influence of the crown had increased and ought to be diminished. While he strongly opposed pensions and sinecure officers, he later accepted a rich sinecure himself in 1782 when he was appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster under the marquis of Rockingham.

Beyond his legal and political work, Dunning is supposed to have assisted in writing a pamphlet regarding the law of libel. He is also credited as the author of the 1764 work, *A Letter to the Proprietors of East India Stock, on the subject of Lord Clive's Jaghire, occasioned by his Lordship's Letter on that Subject*. Additionally, he was once suspected of being the author of the *Letters of Junius*.

## Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton
Alexander Baring (1774–1848) was an English financier and politician, the second son of Sir Francis Baring, founder of Baring Brothers & Co. Baring was raised in the family business and spent time in the United States, where he married Anne Bingham of Philadelphia and established extensive connections with American commercial houses. He became the head of the firm upon his father's death in 1810.

Baring served in parliament for several constituencies between 1806 and 1835, including Taunton, Callington, Thetford, and North Essex. In politics, he operated from a businessman's perspective, opposing restrictions on trade with the United States in 1812 and the 1826 act for the suppression of small bank-notes. He was a staunch opponent of Reform. Although he accepted the post of chancellor of the exchequer in a projected 1832 ministry under the duke of Wellington, he later withdrew, stating he would "face a thousand devils" rather than the House of Commons of that time.

In 1834, Baring served as master of the mint and president of the board of trade in Sir Robert Peel's government. He was created Baron Ashburton on April 10, 1835, a title previously held by his aunt's husband, John Dunning. In 1842, he was sent to America as a commissioner to resolve disputes, resulting in the Ashburton (or Webster-Ashburton) treaty. This agreement settled the north-east boundary of Maine, arranged for the extradition of certain criminals, and committed both states to maintaining squadrons of at least eighty guns off the coast of Africa to suppress the slave trade.

## Associated Locations and Entities
The town of Ashburton in Devonshire is a market-town and ancient stannary town. It was historically governed by a bailiff and portreeve and was once held by the bishop of Exeter. The town's church, St. Andrew, contains a tablet in memory of John Dunning with an inscription written by Dr. Johnson. The town is known for the manufacture of serge, as well as its breweries, saw-mills, and paint factories.

Separately, the Ashburton River in Western Australia flows 400 miles north-westward into Exmouth Gulf. It passes through a gold-bearing district and pastoral country, with the port of Onslow serving as the outlet for these regions. The river is not navigable.
