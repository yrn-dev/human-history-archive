# Louis Lucien (1813-1891)

Louis Lucien was a member of the Bonaparte family, the son of Lucien Bonaparte. He was born on January 4, 1813, at Thorngrove, Worcestershire, England.

## Early Life and Political Career
Louis Lucien spent his youth in England and did not travel to France until 1848. Following the revolution of that year, he entered French politics and was elected as the deputy for Corsica on November 28, 1848. Although this election was later invalidated, he was returned to office in June 1849 as the deputy for the Seine.

During his time in the Legislative Assembly, he sat on the right. He did not have a direct role in the *coup d'etat* carried out by his cousin on December 2, 1851. Subsequently, Napoleon III granted him the titles of prince and senator. Despite these honors, Louis Lucien took very little part in political affairs during the period of the Second Empire.

## Later Life and Academic Pursuits
Following the proclamation of the Third Republic in 1870, Louis Lucien withdrew from France and returned to England. During this final period of his life, he dedicated himself to the study of philology. He became particularly interested in the Basque language, producing and publishing several notable works on the subject:

*   *Grammaire basque* (1876)
*   *Remarques sur plusieurs assertions concernant la langue basque* (1876)
*   *Observations sur le basque Fontarabie* (1878)

Louis Lucien died on November 3, 1891. He left no children.
