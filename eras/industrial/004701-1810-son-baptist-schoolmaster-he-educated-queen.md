# John Sherren Brewer

## Early Life and Education
John Sherren Brewer was an English historian born in Norwich in 1810. He was the son of a Baptist schoolmaster. Brewer received his higher education at Queen's College, Oxford.

## Ecclesiastical and Academic Career
In 1837, Brewer was ordained in the Church of England. Following his ordination, he served as a chaplain for a workhouse located in central London. His career transitioned toward academia in 1839 when he was appointed as a lecturer in classical literature at King's College, London. 

Brewer's academic standing grew over the following decades. In 1855, he succeeded F.D. Maurice to become a lecturer in modern history and a professor of English language and literature. Parallel to his academic roles, Brewer engaged in journalistic work starting in 1854, contributing to the *Standard*, the *Morning Post*, and the *Morning Herald*.

## Historical Research and State Papers
In 1856, Brewer was commissioned by the master of the rolls to create a calendar of the state papers of Henry VIII, a project that required a vast amount of research. In connection with this work, he was appointed as a preacher and a reader at the Rolls.

In 1877, Disraeli secured a crown living for Brewer at Toppesfield, Essex. This position provided him with the time necessary to continue his work on the *Letters and Papers of the Reign of King Henry VIII*. The introductions to this work were published separately in 1884 under the title *The Reign of Henry VIII*, which is regarded as an authoritative and scholarly history of the reign of the king. Additionally, Brewer directed the production of new editions of several standard historical works.

## Death
John Sherren Brewer died in February 1879 at Toppesfield.
