# Deductions for Repairs to and Renewal of Woodwork

In the adjustment of claims for General Average (G.A.), specific rules govern the deductions made from the cost of repairs and renewals. These deductions, often referred to as "new for old," are designed to account for the age and condition of the vessel's components. Under the York-Antwerp Rules, these deductions have been standardized to provide uniformity and certainty, replacing previous customary rules that varied by location and adapting the scale to modern shipbuilding conditions.

## General Principles of Deductions
Deductions for repairs are generally regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired, with exceptions for boilers, machinery, and provisions and stores. No deduction is applied to old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material, nor to provisions and stores that have not been in use. Additionally, no "new for old" deductions are made for temporary repairs of damage that is allowable as G.A.

Certain expenses are always allowed in full, regardless of the ship's age. These include the cost of straightening bent ironwork (including the labor for removal and replacement) and graving dock dues, which encompass the use of stages, shears, graving dock materials, and expenses related to cartages and removals.

## Deductions for Wooden and Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, the deduction scale is based on the date of the original register at the time of the accident:
*   **Under one year old:** No "new for old" deduction is made.
*   **Over one year old:** A deduction of one-third is applied, with specific exceptions. Anchors are allowed in full, and chain cables are subject to a deduction of only one-sixth. 
*   **Metal Sheathing:** The cost of a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing is allowed in full, minus the proceeds from the old metal.
*   **Other Materials:** Nails, felt, and labour metalling are subject to a one-third deduction.

## Deductions for Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel ships, the deductions are categorized by the age of the vessel from the date of original register to the date of the accident:

**Up to 1 Year Old (A)**
All repairs are allowed in full, except for the painting or coating of the bottom, which is subject to a one-third deduction.

**Between 1 and 3 Years (B)**
A one-third deduction is applied to the repairs and renewal of:
*   Woodwork of the hull, masts, and spars.
*   Furniture, upholstery, crockery, metal, and glassware.
*   Sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, and hawsers (excluding chain and wire).
*   Painting, covers, and awnings.

A one-sixth deduction is applied to chain cables, chain, wire ropes, wire hawsers, wire rigging, steam winches and their connexions, donkey engines, and steam cranes and their connexions. All other repairs are allowed in full.

**Between 3 and 6 Years (C)**
The deductions remain the same as in category B, except that a one-sixth deduction is applied to machinery (including mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.

**Between 6 and 10 Years (D)**
The deductions follow category C, with the following modifications: a one-third deduction is applied to all hawsers, rigging, ropes, and sheets, as well as to the renewal and repair of all machinery (including boilers and mountings) and the ironwork of masts and spars.

**Between 10 and 15 Years (E)**
A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs, with two exceptions: ironwork of the hull and cementing, as well as chain cables, are subject to a one-sixth deduction. Anchors are allowed in full.

**Over 15 Years (F)**
A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs. Chain cables are subject to a one-sixth deduction, and anchors are allowed in full.

## Additional Regulations and Limitations
Regarding the painting of the bottom, no allowance is made if the bottom had not been painted within the six months prior to the date of the accident. 

In broader maritime contexts, "bulwark" refers to the woodwork running around a ship above the deck level. In urgent naval conversions, such as those noted in October 1914, woodwork, canvas, and paint are categorized as tasks that can be completed quickly, potentially within two to three weeks.
