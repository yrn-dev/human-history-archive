# Academic Appointments in Political Economy and Philosophy

The history of higher education in the 19th century is marked by the establishment and movement of professors across various international institutions, particularly in the fields of political economy, philosophy, and the sciences.

## Professors of Political Economy in Britain and Ireland

Several prominent economists held chairs at universities in Britain and Ireland. John Elliott Cairnes, a British political economist born in Ireland, began his academic career at Trinity College, Dublin, where he earned a B.A. in 1848 and an M.A. six years later. In 1856, Cairnes was appointed to the chair of political economy at Dublin, a position founded by Archbishop Whately. He later served as professor of political economy and jurisprudence at Queen's College, Galway, starting in 1861. In 1866, Cairnes moved to University College, London, as professor of political economy. He continued lecturing there until 1872, during his final session of which he admitted ladies to his lectures. Upon his resignation in 1872, he was granted the honorary title of emeritus professor of political economy.

John Ramsay M'Culloch, a British economist and statistician, also held a significant appointment in London. After delivering lectures in Edinburgh and providing private instruction in London, M'Culloch was appointed professor of political economy at London University in 1828. This followed a failed 1825 attempt by Jeffrey and others to establish a separate chair of political economy at the university of Edinburgh. In Dublin, Charles Francis Bastable served as the Regius Professor of Laws and Professor of Political Economy in the University of Dublin.

## North American Academic Appointments

The late 19th century saw a frequent exchange of scholars between European and North American universities. William James Ashley, an English economist educated at Balliol College, Oxford, and a fellow of Lincoln College, was appointed professor of constitutional history and political economy at Toronto University in 1888. He resigned this post in 1892 to become the professor of economic history at Harvard University.

Similarly, Edward Webster Bemis, an American economist educated at Johns Hopkins University and Amherst, held the professorship of history and political economy at Vanderbilt University from 1887 to 1892. He subsequently served as associate professor of political economy at the university of Chicago from 1892 to 1895.

## Philosophy and Science in College Administration

Beyond economics, other disciplines shaped the administration and instruction of colleges. James Mccosh, a Scottish philosophical writer, was appointed professor of logic and metaphysics at Queen's College, Belfast, in 1852. In 1868, he became the professor of philosophy and president of the college of New Jersey, at Princeton. Although he resigned the presidency in 1888, he remained a lecturer on philosophy until his death in 1894. Mccosh was noted for his success in college administration and his Hamiltonian philosophical method.

In the United States, Albert Hopkins had a long tenure at Williams College, where he graduated in 1826. His roles evolved from tutor (1827–1829) to professor of mathematics and natural philosophy (1829–1838), then professor of natural philosophy and astronomy (1838–1868), and finally professor of astronomy (1868–1872). Hopkins is credited with organizing a Natural History Expedition to Nova Scotia in 1835, which is said to be the first of its kind from an American college. Additionally, he directed the construction of an astronomical observatory at Williams College in 1837, reported to be the first observatory in the United States built at a college specifically for instructional purposes.

## European Continental Appointments

In Italy, Luigi Cossa, an economist educated at the universities of Leipzig, Vienna, and Pavia, was appointed professor of political economy at Pavia in 1858. He maintained a high reputation through the publication of several works, including *Scienza delle finanze* and *Guida allo studio dell' economia politica*.
