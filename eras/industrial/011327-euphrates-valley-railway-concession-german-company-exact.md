# Euphrates Valley Railway Concession

The Euphrates Valley railway, also referred to as the Bagdad railway scheme, was a major infrastructure project intended to develop material progress in Mesopotamia, Syria, and Asia Minor. The project was characterized by international competition and complex financial arrangements between several European powers and the Ottoman Empire.

## Development and Diplomatic Competition
The railway scheme was brought forward prominently in 1899. During its early stages, the project became a point of contention between various nations. Russian proposals to undertake the railway were rejected. While British proposals followed, they were opposed by Germany. The Germans claimed preference in the matter based on their control of the existing line to Konia in Asia Minor.

Following this competition, the Porte granted a provisional convention to a German company, and an irade was obtained in 1902. In 1903, discussions occurred regarding the possibility of placing the line under international control. This prospect generated specific interest in England because the railway would have provided a short route to India when used in conjunction with fast steamship services in the Persian Gulf and the Mediterranean. However, the British government ultimately decided that the proposals for international control did not offer sufficient security.

## Financial Structure and Control
The final financial arrangement for the railway involved a distribution of capital among several international interests. The control of the capital was divided as follows:
*   **German financiers:** 40%
*   **French interests:** 30% (managed through the Imperial Ottoman Bank)
*   **Austrian, Swiss, Italian, and Turkish interests:** 20%
*   **The Anatolian Railway Company:** 10%

## Construction and Extension
The project progressed in stages across the region. By 1904, the line had been completed from Konia, passing through Eregli to Bulgurli. This section, running from Eski-shehr to Afium-Kara-hissar, Konia, and Bulgurli (east of Eregli), is identified as the first section of the Bagdad railway. These lines were worked by the German *Gesellschaft der anatolischen Eisenbahnen*.

Further expansion occurred in 1908, when an irade sanctioned the extension of the line across the Taurus mountains to Adana, continuing onward to Helif near Mardin, covering a distance of 522 miles.

## Regional Context
The Euphrates Valley railway represented one of the few promises of material development in Mesopotamia, Syria, and Asia Minor. In the broader region of Asia Minor, the Germans maintained a virtual monopoly of the central plateau, while the right to construct all railways in north-eastern Asia Minor and Armenia had been conceded to Russia. Other lines in the region were worked by different interests, including a French company operating the line from Smyrna to Manisa, Ala-shehr, and Afium-Kara-hissar, and an English company operating the line from Smyrna to Aidin and Dineir.
