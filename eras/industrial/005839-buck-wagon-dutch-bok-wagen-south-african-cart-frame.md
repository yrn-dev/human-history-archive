# Buck-wagon

The buck-wagon is a specific type of cart utilized in South Africa. It is characterized by a frame that projects over the wheels and is designed for the transport of heavy loads.

## Etymology and Terminology
The term "buck-wagon" is derived from the Dutch word *bok-wagen*. The English component "buck" in this context may originate from an obsolete word meaning "body," or it may be derived from the sense of bouncing or jumping. This is similar to the etymology of the "buck-board," which is a light four-wheeled vehicle featuring one or more seats on a springy board that joins the front and rear axles, serving as both the body and the springs.

## Linguistic Context
The use of the term reflects the linguistic history of South Africa. In the 17th century, Dutch settlers from Holland and Zeeland, led by Jan van Riebeek, established a port in Cape Town. Over time, these settlers were joined by other groups, including English settlers (who became numerous in the Cape Colony after 1820), Portuguese, Malay people, and French emigrants. The majority of the French emigrants were Huguenots who left France around 1688 and adopted the Dutch language. 

The blending of these various linguistic influences with Dutch idioms led to the emergence of a language known in the first half of the 18th century as South African Dutch. While the present Dutch language has been used more frequently in official writings since 1880, it has been adapted to suit the native idiom.
