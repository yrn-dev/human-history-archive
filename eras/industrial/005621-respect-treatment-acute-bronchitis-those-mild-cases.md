# Treatment of Mild Acute Bronchitis

Acute bronchitis is defined as the inflammation of the mucous membrane of the bronchial tubes. It generally arises from exposure to cold—particularly when accompanied by dampness—or from a sudden transition from a heated atmosphere to a cool one. While the condition can be a severe ailment, it often manifests initially as a common cold or catarrh.

## Management of Mild Cases
In mild cases of acute bronchitis, which are more similar in nature to a simple catarrh, the necessary treatment is relatively limited. The primary requirements for recovery include:
*   **Environment:** Confinement in bed or a warm room for a few days.
*   **Diet:** The use of a light diet.
*   **Hydration:** The consumption of warm diluent drinks.

## Treatment for Developed Cases
When the disease becomes more markedly developed, additional medical measures are required. In the earlier stages of the illness, medicines that promote perspiration and allay fever are considered highly serviceable. 

As the disease progresses, the focus of treatment shifts toward favoring expectoration and soothing the pain associated with the cough. For this purpose, mixtures of tolu may be advantageously employed. These mixtures may include the addition of an opiate, such as ordinary paregorics.

## Precautions Regarding Opium
The use of opium in any form requires strict caution, particularly regarding young children. Medical advice must be sought before administering opium to children because its action is less under control and more potent in them than it is in adults. Furthermore, caution is necessary when using "soothing mixtures," as some have been found to contain quantities of opium sufficient to be dangerous when given to children.

## Supplementary and Alternative Therapies
Various other methods may be used to provide relief from the outset of an attack:
*   **External Applications:** The use of fomentations or, specifically, a turpentine stupe can provide great relief. In non-specific forms of the disease, this may be combined with salts and a good dose of calomel to potentially render the attack abortive.
*   **Inhalations:** Steam atomizers filled with guaiacol, creosote, or an aqueous solution of compound tincture of benzoin can offer relief. 
*   **Nebulizers:** A globe nebulizer can be used to introduce volatile antiseptic oils in a fine fog for deep inhalation. Remedies that may be dissolved in benzoinol for this purpose include white pine extract, eucalyptol, and menthol. If the cough is troublesome, opium or cocaine may be added to these solutions.

## Risk Factors and Complications
While mild cases are managed with the measures above, acute bronchitis is pre-eminently dangerous at the extremes of life (infants and the aged), where it may assume the capillary form and prove quickly fatal. It is also apt to be very severe in individuals addicted to intemperance. Additionally, the supervention of acute bronchitis is an alarming complication for those with heart disease, consumption, or children who have suffered from whooping-cough or measles, as it increases the embarrassment of breathing.
