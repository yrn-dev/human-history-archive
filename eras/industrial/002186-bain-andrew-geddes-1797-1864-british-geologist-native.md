# BAIN, ANDREW GEDDES (1797-1864)

## Early Life and Emigration
Andrew Geddes Bain was a British geologist born in Scotland. In 1820, he emigrated to Cape Colony. For several years following his arrival, he operated a business as a saddler located at Graaf Reinet.

## Military and Engineering Career
Bain's career shifted toward military and civil service during the Kaffir War of 1833-34, during which he took command of a provisional battalion that had been raised to defend the frontier. Following this period, he was hired to construct a military road through the Ecca Pass. His performance during this project demonstrated engineering talents that resulted in his permanent employment as a surveyor of military roads under the corps of Royal Engineers.

## Geological Studies and Discoveries
Bain's work as a road surveyor sparked an interest in geology. This interest was further developed in 1837 when he was loaned a copy of Lyell's *Elements*. He dedicated all of his spare energy to the study of geology, leading to the discovery of the remains of various reptilia. Among these discoveries was the *Dicynodon*, which was described by Owen after being obtained from the Karroo Beds near Fort Beaufort.

## Major Contributions and Death
In 1852, Bain prepared the first comprehensive geological map of South Africa. This work was regarded as being of great merit and was subsequently published in 1856 by the Geological Society of London. 

Andrew Geddes Bain died in Cape Town in 1864. Following his death, an obituary was written by Dr R. N. Rubidge and published in *Geol. Mag.* in January 1865. A portrait of Bain also appears in volume ii, part v of the *Trans. Geol. Soc. S. Africa*, published in June 1896.
