# The Penny Cyclopaedia

The *Penny Cyclopaedia* was a large-scale composite work started in 1833 by the English publisher and author Charles Knight. Knight, the son of a Windsor printer and bookseller, had previously served as the superintendent of publications for the Society for the Diffusion of Useful Knowledge before resuming his own business. 

## Publication and History
The *Penny Cyclopaedia* was launched alongside *The Penny Magazine*, both of which achieved large circulation. However, the *Cyclopaedia* faced significant financial challenges due to a heavy excise duty. As a result, the work was not completed until 1844, a process that caused Knight a great pecuniary sacrifice. In 1853, Knight became the editor of *The English Cyclopaedia*, which served primarily as a revision of the original *Penny Cyclopaedia*.

## Contributors and Content
The *Penny Cyclopaedia* relied on a variety of specialized contributors to provide its content across numerous fields of study.

### Science and Mathematics
Augustus De Morgan was a prolific contributor to the first edition between 1833 and 1843. His contributions were extensive, numbering no fewer than 850 articles of various lengths. De Morgan wrote primarily on biography, physics, astronomy, and mathematics. It has been estimated that his work constituted one-sixth of the entire *Cyclopaedia* and perhaps represented its most valuable portion.

In the field of natural history, William John Broderip—an English naturalist and metropolitan police magistrate—authored the zoological articles. Broderip's contributions to the *Penny Cyclopaedia* included specific observations on fauna; for example, in 1842, he noted that the New Zealand Company had presented two specimens of *Apteryx* to the Zoological Society, while Lord Derby had obtained two more.
