## Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry
Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry (1836– ) was an eminent engineer who was educated at Glenalmond. He began his professional development as an engineering pupil under Sir John Hawkshaw. During this association, he was involved in the construction of railway bridges across the Thames, specifically those at Cannon Street and Charing Cross. 

In 1867, Wolfe-Barry began practicing independently. He quickly established extensive connections with railway companies across Great Britain and internationally. His professional portfolio included the construction of the St Paul's station and bridge for the London, Chatham & Dover railway, as well as extensions for the Metropolitan District railway. Beyond London, he was engaged in the creation of the Barry Docks for the Barry railway company located near Cardiff.

## Engineering Works and Honors
Wolfe-Barry was responsible for several significant structures over the Thames, including the new Kew bridges and the Tower Bridge. Following the completion of the Tower Bridge in 1894, he was appointed a C.B., and he was later made a K.C.B. three years following that appointment.

In addition to his structural projects, he contributed to technical literature. In 1874, he published a book titled *Railway Appliances*, and in 1882, he co-authored *Railways and Locomotives* with Sir F. J. Bramwell. His professional standing was further recognized in 1896 when he was elected president of the Institution of Civil Engineers.

## Royal Commissions and Public Service
Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry served on various royal commissions throughout his career. These appointments covered a range of public works and infrastructure issues:
* **Irish Public Works**: 1886–1890
* **Highlands and Islands of Scotland**: 1889–1890
* **Accidents to Railway Servants**: 1899–1900
* **Port of London**: 1900–1902
