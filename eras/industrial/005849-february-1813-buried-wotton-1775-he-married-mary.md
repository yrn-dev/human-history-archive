# February 1813

The month of February 1813 is associated with several significant events in political succession and literary publication, as well as the death of a prominent British statesman.

## Political Transitions in the Buckingham Lineage
In February 1813, a transition of titles occurred within the family of the marquesses of Buckingham. George Nugent Temple Grenville, the 1st marquess of Buckingham, died on February 11, 1813, at his residence, Stowe House, located in Buckingham. He was the second son of George Grenville and had been educated at Christ Church, Oxford, and Eton. 

Following the death of the 1st marquess, his elder son, Richard Grenville, succeeded him as the marquess of Buckingham in February 1813. Richard Grenville had previously served as one of the members of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1797 to 1813 and had been active in politics as Earl Temple. He later became the 1st duke of Buckingham and Chandos in 1822 after marrying the only child of the 3rd duke of Chandos.

## Literary Publications of Lord Byron
February 1813 marked a period of high poetic productivity for the author Byron. During this month, he published a work titled *The Waltz*, which was released anonymously. This publication was part of a series of works produced in 1813, which included *The Giaour* (published June 5) and *The Bride of Abydos* (published November 29). Byron's "Turkish Tales" gained significant popularity, and he was noted for his efforts to ensure the accuracy of the "costume" and accessories in his work, despite his own views on the artistic or ethical value of these experiments.

## Career of George Nugent Temple Grenville
The death of George Nugent Temple Grenville in February 1813 concluded a long career in English and Irish politics. Born on June 17, 1753, he entered parliament as a member for Buckinghamshire ten years after being appointed a teller of the exchequer in 1764. He was known in the House of Commons as a sharp critic of the American policy pursued by Lord North.

Grenville held several high-ranking positions, including:
* **Earl Temple:** Succeeded his uncle in September 1779.
* **Lord-Lieutenant of Buckinghamshire:** Appointed in 1782.
* **Lord-Lieutenant of Ireland:** He served two terms in this role. The first began in July 1782 under the ministry of the earl of Shelburne. During this tenure, he advised the passing of the Renunciation Act of 1783 and created the order of St Patrick in February 1783, serving as its first grand master. His second tenure, beginning in November 1787 under Pitt, was less successful; he was censured by the Irish Houses of parliament and denounced by Grattan for extravagance. He eventually resigned in September 1789.
* **Secretary of State:** Appointed in December 1783 by the younger Pitt, though he resigned two days later.

Grenville also held the confidence of George III. He was authorized by the king to warn that anyone voting for Fox's East India Bill would be considered an enemy of the king, a message that led to the bill's defeat.
