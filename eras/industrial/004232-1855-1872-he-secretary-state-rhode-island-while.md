# John Russell Bartlett

John Russell Bartlett (1805–1886) was an American linguistic and historical student. Born in Providence, Rhode Island, on October 23, 1805, he spent the first eighteen years of his life living in Kingston, Canada.

## Early Career and Professional Transitions
Between 1824 and 1836, Bartlett worked in Providence in several different capacities, serving as a book-keeper, a bank cashier, and a clerk in a dry goods store. Following this period, he moved to New York City, where he operated as a bookseller for more than ten years. He eventually returned to Providence in 1850.

## Government Service and Explorations
From 1850 to 1853, Bartlett served as a commissioner for the United States tasked with surveying the boundary between Mexico and the United States. This work remained unfinished due to a lack of funds. After President Franklin Pierce took office, Bartlett was superseded by another commissioner. 

Following this experience, he published a two-volume work in 1854 titled *A Personal Narrative of Explorations and Incidents in Texas, New Mexico, California, Sonora and Chihuahua*. This publication is noted for containing significant historical and scientific material regarding the south-west.

## Secretary of State of Rhode Island
From 1855 to 1872, Bartlett held the office of secretary of state of Rhode Island. During his tenure in this role, he performed extensive work on the state's records, which he thoroughly classified and re-arranged. Additionally, he produced various compilations and bibliographies that focused primarily on the history of the state.

## Linguistic Contributions and Death
Bartlett is most remembered for his pioneer work, the *Dictionary of Americanisms*, published in 1848. Although subsequent changes in dialect have affected its final authoritativeness or completeness, the work remains a valuable resource for students of language and is considered his chief contribution to the field.

John Russell Bartlett died in Providence on May 28, 1886.
