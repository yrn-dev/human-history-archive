# Forests of the Bombay Presidency

The Bombay Presidency contains two primary categories of forests: those located in the alluvial plains and those found in the hills.

## Distribution and Geography
The hill forests are spread across a broad region, extending from 14 degrees to 23 degrees North latitude. A majority of these forests are situated within the Western Ghats, also known as the Sahyadri hills. In contrast, the alluvial forests are located in Sind, positioned on or near the banks of the Indus river, covering a total area of 550 square miles.

## Timber and Botanical Species
The forests of the presidency are home to a wide variety of principal timber trees. These include:
* Teak
* Two varieties of blackwood (*Dalbergia Sisu* and *Dalbergia latifolia*)
* *Dalbergia ujainensis*
* *Pterocarpus Marsupium*
* *Terminalia glabra*
* *Acacia arabica* and *Acacia Catechu*
* *Nauclea cordifolia* and *Nauclea parvifolia*
* *Bidelia spinosa*
* *Hardwickia binata*
* *Juga xylocarpa*
* *Populus euphratica*
* *Tamarindus indica*

## Non-Timber Resources and Palms
Beyond timber, the forests contain numerous trees valued for their berries, nuts, or fruits. Notable examples include the jack (*Artocarpus integrifolia*), the mango (*Mangifera indica*), *Zizypkus Jujuba*, *Aegle Marmelos*, *Terminalia Chebula*, *Calophyllum Inophyllum*, *Bassia latifolia*, and *Pongamia glabra*.

The region's palms consist of the areca catechu, palmyra, date, and cocoa-nut. Additionally, jungle tribes engage in the collection of gum from various tree species. In the region of Sind, the Forest Department collects a small amount of revenue from lac.
