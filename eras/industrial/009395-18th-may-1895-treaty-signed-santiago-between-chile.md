# Treaty of Santiago (1895)

The Treaty of Santiago was a diplomatic agreement signed on May 18, 1895, between Chile and Bolivia. The treaty was established with the intent of strengthening friendship between the two nations and addressing the "higher necessity" of Bolivia's requirement for free access to the sea to ensure its future commercial prosperity and development.

## Terms of the 1895 Agreement
Under the terms of the treaty, Chile committed to a specific course of action regarding the territories of Arica and Tacna. Chile declared that if it acquired permanent sovereignty and dominion over these territories—either through a direct arrangement or as a result of a plebiscite conducted under the treaty of Ancon with Peru—it undertook to transfer those territories to Bolivia in the same extent and form as they were acquired. In exchange for this transfer, the republic of Bolivia agreed to pay Chile an indemnity of $5,000,000 silver.

The treaty further stipulated that if this cession occurred, Chile would move its own frontier from north of Camerones to Vitor, extending from the sea to the existing frontier separating that district from Bolivia. Chile also pledged to use its utmost efforts, whether acting alone or jointly with Bolivia, to obtain possession of Arica and Tacna.

## Contingencies and Supplementary Protocols
The agreement included provisions for the event that Chile failed to obtain the aforementioned territories. In such a case, Chile bound itself to provide Bolivia with $5,000,000 silver and the roadstead (caleta) of Vitor, or another analogous port. Supplementary protocols added to the treaty specified that any port ceded to Bolivia must fully satisfy both the present and future requirements of Bolivian commerce.

While further treaties regarding commerce and peace were signed between the two nations on May 23, 1895, the specific provisions concerning the cession of a seaport to Bolivia remained unfulfilled.

## Context of the Boundary Dispute
The 1895 treaty was part of a long-standing dispute regarding Chile's occupation of the former Bolivian provinces of Arica and Tacna, which had been held under the Parto de Tregna of April 4, 1884. While there was a prospect in 1895 that Chile might concede an outlet to the sea in exchange for Bolivia recognizing Chilean ownership of those provinces, the situation changed when Chile discovered secret negotiations between Bolivia and Argentina. This discovery led Chile to abandon its conciliatory stance.

Bolivia's position was further weakened when the boundary dispute between Argentina and Chile was settled, removing the hope that Bolivia could obtain Argentine support. Consequently, Bolivia was not in a position to compel concessions from Chile or venture into hostilities.

## Final Settlement and the 1905 Treaty
The diplomatic impasse eventually led to a final settlement under the presidencies of Pando and his successor, Ismail Montes (who took office in 1904). A treaty was ratified in 1905 which required Bolivia to cede the maritime provinces that Chile had occupied since the war of 1881, leaving Bolivia without a seaport.

Despite the loss of the coast, the 1905 treaty provided several advantages for Bolivia:
*   The Chilean government undertook to construct a railway from Arica to the Bolivian capital, La Paz, at its own expense.
*   Chile granted Bolivians free transit through Chilean territory to specific coastal towns.
*   Chile agreed to provide a cash indemnity to Bolivia and offer pecuniary assistance for the construction of other railways necessary to open up the country.
