# Charles Lucien Jules Laurent

Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803–1857), the prince of Canino and son of Lucien Bonaparte, was a man primarily dedicated to science rather than politics. In 1822, he married his cousin Zenaide Bonaparte, who was the daughter of Joseph.

## Scientific Career and Publications
Laurent established his scientific reputation starting at the age of twenty-two when he began publishing *American Ornithology*, a four-volume work published in Philadelphia between 1825 and 1833. Following this, he produced a variety of other zoological works, including:
* *Iconographia della fauna Italica* (3 vols., Rome, 1832–1841)
* *Catalogo metodico degli uccelli europei* (1 vol., Bologna, 1842)
* *Catalogo metodico dei pesci europei* (1 vol., Naples, 1845)
* *Catalogo metodico dei mammiferi europei* (1 vol., Milan, 1845)
* *Telachorum tabula analytica* (Neufchatel, 1838)

Later in his life, after returning to science in France, he published *Conspectus systematis ornithologiae, mastozologiae, erpetologiae et amphibologiae, Ichthyologiae* in Leiden (1850), *Tableau des oiseaux-mouches* in Paris (1854), and *Ornithologie fossile* in Paris (1858).

## Academic Honors
Laurent's contributions to science led to several prestigious appointments. He was elected as an honorary member of the academy of Upsala in 1833 and the academy of Berlin in 1843. Additionally, he became a correspondent of the Institute of France in 1844.

## Political Activity
Around 1847, Laurent became involved in political agitation within Italy. He presided over scientific congresses, most notably in Venice, where he expressed his support for the expulsion of the Austrians and the independence of Italy. In 1848, he joined the Junto of Rome and was elected as a deputy for Viterbo to the national assembly. However, the failure of the revolution necessitated his departure from Italy in July 1849. He traveled through Holland before arriving in France, where he resumed his scientific pursuits.
