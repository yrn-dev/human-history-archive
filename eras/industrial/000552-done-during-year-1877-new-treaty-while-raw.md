# Commercial and Diplomatic Developments of 1877

The year 1877 was marked by significant shifts in international trade policy, colonial administration, and diplomatic relations, most notably within the dual state of Austria-Hungary and the expanding British Empire.

## The Austria-Hungary Customs Union
In the dual state of Austria-Hungary, the year 1877 served as a pivotal point for the renewal of the Customs Union, which had originally been established in 1867. Prior to this period, the customs tariff was governed by various commercial treaties with nations including Great Britain, France, Italy, and Germany. These treaties featured "most favoured nation" clauses and maximum duty limits, resulting in a system that closely resembled an absence of customs duties.

By 1873, Austrian manufacturers—facing competition from Belgian, English, and German goods—began pushing for protective duties, reflecting a broader economic reaction against free trade doctrines. Conversely, Hungary remained in favor of free trade to ensure a secure market for its agricultural produce, as it lacked significant manufacturing industries.

Following the expiration of commercial treaties after 1875, Hungary gave notice to terminate the commercial union with Austria. Negotiations to renew the union were concluded during 1877. The resulting new treaty introduced several key changes:
*   **Duties:** While raw materials continued to be imported free of duty, low duties were placed on corn and textile goods.
*   **Excises:** The excise taxes on brandy and sugar were increased.
*   **Payment:** All duties were required to be paid in gold, which led to a considerable increase in costs.

This shift in policy coincided with the breakdown of agreement attempts with Germany due to the policies of Bismarck. Consequently, tariff treaties with France and Great Britain were not renewed, allowing Austria-Hungary to establish a fresh tariff based on legislation rather than commercial treaties.

## British Colonial Administration
In 1877, the British Empire expanded its administrative oversight in the Western Pacific. An order in council created a High Commission that granted jurisdiction over various island groups, including the Ellice, Gilbert, and Southern Solomon islands, as well as other groups not under the jurisdiction of other civilized powers or colonial governments. This administrative move preceded the establishment of protectorates over all these islands by 1900.

## Diplomatic and Social Events of 1877
The year 1877 also saw significant transitions in diplomacy and labor:
*   **Russian Diplomacy:** Count Nicholas Pavlovich Ignatiev, who had served as the Russian ambassador to Constantinople since 1864, concluded his tenure in that post in 1877. His efforts to bring Christian nationalities, particularly the Bulgarians, under Russian influence culminated in the Russo-Turkish war of 1877-1878.
*   **Labor in Grenada:** In the colony of Grenada, where cocoa served as the staple crop, it was found necessary to introduce East Indian labour by 1877.
*   **Education:** Jutaro Komura, a Japanese statesman who later served as the minister in Washington, St Petersburg, and Peking, graduated from Harvard in 1877.
