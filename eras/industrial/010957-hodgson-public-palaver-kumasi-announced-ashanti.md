# The 1900 Ashanti Rebellion

The 1900 rebellion in Ashanti was a serious conflict that erupted following a period of British administrative control in Kumasi. After the deportation of Prempeh, no successor was appointed to the throne of Ashanti, and the "golden stool"—the symbol of authority and sovereignty among the Ashanti—remained hidden by the Kumasi since 1896. While other states within the Ashanti confederacy maintained their tribal systems and kings, the affairs of Kumasi were administered by chiefs under the guidance of a British resident, Captain Donald W. Stewart.

## Outbreak of Hostilities
Although the Kumasi chiefs appeared outwardly submissive, they were not reconciled to British rule. Tensions were further heightened by a desire among the Kumasi to erase the dishonor of allowing Prempeh to be deported without a fight. 

The immediate catalyst for the rebellion occurred on March 28, 1900. During a public palaver at Kumasi, Sir F. Hodgson, the governor of the Gold Coast, announced that the Ashanti chiefs were required to pay the British government 4,000 oz. of gold annually. Additionally, Sir F. Hodgson reproached the chiefs for failing to deliver the golden stool to him. 

Three days after this announcement, Kumasi warriors attacked a party of Hausa who had been sent with the primary objective of locating the hidden golden stool. This followed a failed secret attempt to seize the stool in the preceding January.

## The Siege of Kumasi
Following the attack on the Hausa party, the Kumasi warriors threatened the fort at Kumasi. This fort, built in 1896, served as the residence of the chief commissioner and was capable of housing several hundred men. Sir F. and Lady Hodgson, along with other Basel missionaries including Mr. Ramseyer, took refuge within the fort and requested urgent reinforcements.

The rebellion involved the Kokofu, Adansi, and Kumasi tribes, while other tribes of the Ashanti confederation remained loyal to the British. The rebel forces were reported to number 40,000.

## British Military Response
Reinforcements arrived in stages to secure the fort:
* **April 18:** 100 Gold Coast constabulary arrived.
* **April 29:** The Kumasi launched a forceful attack but were repulsed. On this same day, 250 Lagos constabulary reached the town, though they arrived with little ammunition after fighting their way up.
* **May 15:** Major A. Morris arrived from the British territory north of Ashanti with an additional 250 men.

By the time these reinforcements arrived, the garrison totaled 700 men. The Europeans inside the fort numbered 29, which included four women.

## Aftermath and Economic Impact
Following the suppression of the 1900 revolt, the British took measures to improve trade between Cape Coast and Kumasi. Kumasi served as the distributing center for the hinterland and the whole of Ashanti. The town experienced a marked revival in trade and rapid expansion, particularly after the railway from Sekondi was completed in 1903. By 1906, Kumasi had supplanted the coast towns as the primary distributing center.

The region's economy remained heavily tied to gold mining, particularly in the Obuassi mines. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti reached 68,259 oz., valued at £254,790. Other commercial products included rubber, gum copal, tobacco in the north, and cocoa plantations, which became established especially in the eastern districts after 1905.
