# The Retail Bookseller as the Third Partner in Publishing

## Role and Interests of the Retail Bookseller
In the publishing industry, the retail bookseller is identified as the third partner and its ramification. To safeguard the interests of this partner, a London booksellers' society was established in 1890. The primary objectives of this society were to control all details related to the trade, arrange prices generally, and restrict discounts to 25%. A few years after its founding, the society expanded its operations to cover the entire United Kingdom, at which point it was renamed "The Associated Booksellers of Great Britain and Ireland."

## The Second-Hand Book Trade
The trade in second-hand books, also referred to as old books, is considered a higher class of business. This sector requires a specific knowledge of bibliography because transactions involve individual books rather than large numbers of copies. Dealers in this field occasionally increase their stock by purchasing "remainders"—books that have stopped selling with the publisher—which are then offered to the public as bargains.

## Development of the Periodical Trade
The periodical trade emerged during the 19th century. Early examples of publications from this era include *Chambers's Journal* and the *Penny Magazine*. Several factors contributed to the growth of this sector of the business:
* The introduction of attractive illustrations.
* The abolition of the duty upon paper and the newspaper stamp.
* The availability of facilities that allowed for the purchase of books by instalments.
