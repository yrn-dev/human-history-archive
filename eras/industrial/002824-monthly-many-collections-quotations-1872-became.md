# Maturin Murray Ballou

## Career in Journalism
Maturin Murray Ballou (1820–1895) was a prominent figure in the development of American illustrated journalism. Throughout his career, he edited several publications, including *Ballou's Monthly* and *Gleason's Pictorial*. In addition to these periodicals, he edited numerous collections of quotations. 

In 1872, Ballou became the editor-in-chief of the *Boston Daily Globe*. He was not only the leader of the publication but also one of its founders.

## Literary Works and Family
The son of the first Hosea Ballou, Maturin Murray Ballou authored several books. In 1854, he wrote a *History of Cuba*, and in 1860, he wrote a biography of his father.

## Family Background
Maturin Murray Ballou was the son of Hosea Ballou (1796–1861). The elder Hosea Ballou was born in Halifax, Vermont, on October 18, 1796. He served as a preacher to Universalists in several locations: Stafford, Connecticut (1815–1821), Roxbury, Massachusetts (1821–1838), and Medford, Massachusetts (1838–1853). 

In 1847, Hosea Ballou urged the necessity of a Universalist denominational college, an effort that contributed significantly to the establishment of Tufts College at Medford. He was elected the first president of Tufts College in 1853 and served in that capacity until shortly before his death on May 27, 1861, in Somerville, Massachusetts.

The elder Hosea Ballou was also an editor and author. He worked with another Hosea Ballou (the elder) to edit *The Universalist Quarterly Review*. His written works include the *Ancient History of Universalism*, which covered history down to A.D. 553 (first published in 1829, with a second edition in 1842), and he edited an 1833 edition of Sismondi's *History of the Crusades*.
