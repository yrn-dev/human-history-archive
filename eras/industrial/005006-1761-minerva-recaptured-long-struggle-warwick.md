# The Recapture of the Warwick (1761)

## Naval Engagement of 1761
In 1761, a naval engagement occurred involving the British frigate "Minerva." During this encounter, the "Minerva" recaptured the "Warwick." The process was described as a long struggle, and the "Warwick" was noted to be of equal force to the "Minerva."

## Command of the "Minerva"
The "Minerva" was commanded by Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport. Hood had entered the navy in January 1741 and served as a lieutenant of the "Bridgewater" for ten years starting six years after his entry. Prior to his appointment to the "Minerva," Hood had been posted to the "Prince," which served as the flag-ship of Rear-Admiral Saunders, and had served in the Mediterranean. 

While in command of the "Minerva," Hood was present at the victory of Hawke in Quiberon Bay on November 20, 1759. Following the recapture of the "Warwick" in 1761, Hood later that same year transitioned to the "Africa" for service in the Mediterranean, where he remained until peace was concluded.

## Career of Alexander Hood, Viscount Bridport
Following his service in the Mediterranean, Alexander Hood remained in continuous employment both ashore and afloat. In 1778, while serving in the "Robust," he was present at the battle of Ushant. This action was followed by a court-martial of Admiral (later Viscount) Keppel; Hood participated in Keppel's defence, an action that aroused adverse popular feeling but did not appear to harm his professional career.

Hood's subsequent promotions and appointments included:
* **1780**: Appointed rear-admiral of the white and became one of the flag-officers for Howe, serving in the "Queen" (90) during the relief of Gibraltar in 1782.
* **1787**: Promoted to vice-admiral.
* **1788**: Became K.B.
* **1790**: Flew his flag for a short period during the Spanish armament.
* **1793**: Returned to sea during the outbreak of war with France as Howe's second in command, participating in the operations of the "Glorius First of June."

For these services, he was made Baron Bridport of Cricket St Thomas in Somerset in the Irish peerage. He later held independent command and fought a partial action off Belle-Ile on June 23, 1795. At one time, Hood also served as a member of the House of Commons.
