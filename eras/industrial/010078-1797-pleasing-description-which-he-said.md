# Events and Figures of 1797

The year 1797 was marked by significant literary publications, the deaths and births of notable figures, and political trials in France.

## Literary and Academic Publications
Several scholarly and descriptive works were published in 1797. Charles Victor de Bonstetten, a Swiss writer and liberal patrician who had served as the governor of the Italian-speaking districts of Mendrisio, Locarno, Lugano, and Val Maggia for the Swiss Confederation from 1795 to 1797, published a pleasing description of these districts in 1797. Bonstetten is also said to have introduced the cultivation of the potato into these regions.

In the field of numismatics, Jos. Eckhel, a Professor of Antiquities and Keeper of the Cabinet of Medals in Vienna, published the eight-volume work *Doctrina veterum Nummorum* in Vindobona in 1797. Additionally, the work *Mahommedan Coinage* was published in 1797, edited by O. G. Tychsen in Rostock, with a French translation by S. de Sacy published in Paris that same year.

## Political Trials and Executions in France
In France, the year 1797 saw the conclusion of the trial of the socialist Babeuf and his accomplices. The trial began at Vendome on February 20, 1797, and lasted for two months. Although other important individuals were implicated, the government designated Babeuf as the leader of the conspiracy. On Prairial 7 (April 26, 1797), Babeuf and Darthe were condemned to death. They were executed at Vendome on Prairial 8 (1797). Other prisoners involved in the case, including Buonarroti, were exiled, while others, such as Vadier and fellow-conventionals, were acquitted.

## Notable Births and Deaths
The year 1797 recorded the birth of Gérard Paul Deshayes, a French conchologist and geologist. Born in Nancy on May 13, 1797, Deshayes was the son of a professor of experimental physics at the École Centrale of the department of la Meurthe. He would later become a professor of natural history in the Muséum d'Histoire Naturelle and a founder of the Société Géologique de France.

Conversely, 1797 marked the death of Magdeleine Marie [Louise] Desgarcins, a prominent French actress and tragédienne. After a successful career at the Comédie Française and the Théâtre de la République—where she performed in works such as *Othello* and *King Lear*—her health failed. She died insane in Paris on October 27, 1797.
