# Blackwood

## The Blackwood Family
The Irish Blackwoods are of old Scottish stock, with a lineage tracing back to the 14th century. The ancestor of the Irish line was John Blackwood of Bangor (1591–1663), who acquired landed property in County Down and accumulated a fortune. His great-grandson, Robert, was created a baronet in 1763. 

The family's holdings expanded when Sir John Blackwood (who died in 1799) married the heiress of the Hamiltons, the viscounts of Clandeboye and earls of Clanbrassil. This marriage brought the barony of Dufferin and the borough of Killyleagh in County Down into the family. While Sir John and his heir, James, declined peerages, the title of Baroness Dufferin was conferred in 1800 upon Sir John's widow. James succeeded as the second baron in 1808 and died in 1836. He was succeeded by his brother Hans (d. 1839), the third baron, who married Miss Temple. Their son, Price Blackwood, became the 4th baron and served in the Navy. Price Blackwood married Helen Selina Sheridan in 1825, the granddaughter of the politician and dramatist Richard Brinsley Sheridan.

Other notable members of the family include Admiral Sir Henry Blackwood, Bart. (1770–1832), a brother to James and Hans and one of Nelson's captains, who commanded the *Euryalus* at the Battle of Trafalgar. The 1st Marquess of Dufferin and Ava, Frederick Temple Hamilton-Temple-Blackwood (1826–1902), was a British diplomatist born in Florence, Italy, and the son of Price Blackwood.

## Blackwood's Magazine and Publishing
Blackwood is also identified as a publisher and the producer of *Blackwood's Magazine*. The magazine served as a venue for various writers and scholars. William Edmonstoune Aytoun, a Scottish poet and professor of rhetoric and *belles lettres* at Edinburgh University, began contributing translations from Uhland to *Blackwood's Magazine* in 1836 and remained on the staff from 1839 until his death.

Additionally, the publishing house of Blackwood released technical and academic works. In 1851, Blackwood published *Fortification for Officers of the Army and Students of Military History*, written by Lieutenant H. Yule. This work was later translated into French seven years after its publication. H. Yule also contributed writings on Tibet to *Blackwood's Magazine*.

## Academic Disputes and Samuel Bailey
The name Blackwood is associated with the publication of academic rejoinders involving the philosopher Samuel Bailey (1791–1870). Bailey, a former Sheffield merchant and chairman of the Sheffield Banking Company, was a prolific author known for his lucidity and originality. 

In 1842, Bailey published a Review of *Berkeley's Theory of Vision*. This work prompted responses from other intellectuals. While J. S. Mill replied in the *Westminster Review*, J. F. Ferrier published a rejoinder in *Blackwood*. Ferrier's response was later reprinted in the second volume of *Lectures and Remains*. Samuel Bailey subsequently addressed his critics, including Ferrier, in a publication titled *Letter to a Philosopher* in 1843.
