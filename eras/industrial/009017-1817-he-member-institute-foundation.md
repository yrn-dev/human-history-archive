## Notable Deaths and Transitions
The year 1817 marked the death of Francis Horner (1778–1817), a British economist born in Edinburgh. After studying at the university and high school of his native city, Horner was called to the Scottish bar in 1800. He later moved to London in 1802, where he conducted research in political economy, philosophy, and law before being admitted to the English bar in 1807. Horner served in parliament as a member for St Ives (starting in November 1806), Wendover (the following year), and St Mawes (from 1812) under the patronage of the marquis of Buckingham. Despite an offer for a treasury secretaryship in 1811 from Lord Grenville, Horner refused to accept office until he was financially independent. Due to a fragile constitution, he traveled to Italy in October 1816 on physician's orders and died in Pisa on February 8, 1817. He was buried at Leghorn, and a marble statue by Chantrey was later erected in his memory at Westminster Abbey.

Following this period, Biddle continued his career in law and finance, eventually being appointed by President Monroe in 1819 as one of five government directors of the Bank of the United States, later becoming its president in 1823.

## Travel and Commerce
The year 1817 was a period of international business travel for John Lowell (1799–1836). The son of Francis Cabot Lowell and born in Boston, John Lowell traveled to the East Indies and India on business during 1816 and 1817. He later embarked on a trip around the world in 1832 and died in Bombay in 1836. Lowell is remembered for leaving $237,000 via a will—described by Edward Everett as being written "on the top of a palace of the Pharaohs"—to establish the Lowell Institute.
