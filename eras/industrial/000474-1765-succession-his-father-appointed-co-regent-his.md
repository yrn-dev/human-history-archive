# Events and Figures of 1765

The year 1765 is associated with significant political transitions in the Habsburg Monarchy and the passing of high-ranking nobility in Great Britain.

## Joseph II and the Habsburg Monarchy
In 1765, Joseph II was elected emperor. He succeeded his father and was appointed as co-regent alongside his mother. Joseph II's tenure was later marked by territorial ambitions in southern Germany, specifically regarding Bavaria. 

Bavaria was ruled by the house of Wittelsbach, which served as a secular rival to the house of Habsburg. Austrian statesmen had long considered the annexation of Bavaria—either through exchange or conquest—to eliminate a perpetual threat to Austrian peace and to establish overwhelming Habsburg strength in South Germany. This ambition came to a head in 1777 following the death of elector Maximilian III. While the heir was the elector palatine Charles Theodore, Joseph II claimed the inheritance and prepared to use force to assert this claim. 

This led to the War of Bavarian Succession. Although the armies of Joseph II and Frederick were positioned face to face in the field, no actual fighting occurred. Maria Theresa, fearing another struggle with Prussia, overruled her son. The conflict was resolved via the treaty of Teschen, in which Austria accepted the cession of some other districts and the Quarter of the Inn (Innviertel).

## Lionel Cranfield Sackville
In Great Britain, 1765 marked the death of Lionel Cranfield Sackville, the 1st Duke of Dorset, who died on October 10 of that year. Born on January 18, 1688, as the only son of the 6th earl, he succeeded his father as the 7th earl of Dorset in January 1706 and was created duke of Dorset in 1720.

The Duke held numerous positions of trust and high office throughout his life, including:
* **Lord Steward of the Royal Household:** Served from 1725 to 1730, and again from 1737 to 1745.
* **Lord-Lieutenant of Ireland:** Served from 1730 to 1737, and was appointed a second time in 1750, though he was dismissed from this office in 1755 following a stormy viceroyalty.
* **Lord President of the Council:** Served from 1745 to 1751.

He also served several times as one of the lords justices of Great Britain. The Duke left three sons: George (who became Viscount Sackville in 1782 and took the name Germain in 1770), Charles (the 2nd duke), and John Philip, who also died in 1765.
