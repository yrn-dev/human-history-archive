# Captain William Gill

Captain William Gill, R.E., was an officer of the Royal Engineers and a member of the Indian survey. He is recognized as one of the pioneering explorers who contributed to the geographical research of the Far East during the late 19th century.

## Explorations in China and Tibet
Captain Gill was the first to make his way across China to Burma and eastern Tibet. His travels took place during a period of increasing geographical investigation into the route connections between China and Burma, a movement that followed the 1874 expedition of Augustus Margary. Gill's explorations were part of a broader tide of inquiry by experts seeking to unlock doors to trade and promote economic and commercial development within China.

## The River of Golden Sand
The results of his travels were published in his work, *The River of Golden Sand*, first published in London in 1880. The text is a narrative of his journey through China and eastern Tibet to Burmah and included ten maps based on original surveys as well as illustrations. An introductory essay for the work was provided by Colonel H. Yule.

Following the death of Captain Gill, a condensed version of *The River of Golden Sand* was published in 1883. This edition was condensed by Edward Colborne Baber, who served as the Chinese Secretary to H.M.’s Legation at Peking, and was edited by Colonel H. Yule, who also included a memoir of the Captain. A separate memoir and introductory essay by Colonel Yule were published in 1884.

## Context of Asiatic Surveys
Gill's work was situated within a larger framework of Indian triangulation and exploratory surveys. During this era, surveys were being pushed outwards from India through Afghanistan and Baluchistan, and branching toward Siam and western China. These efforts aimed to raise the standard of accuracy in Asiatic geographical surveys.

Gill was among several explorers who mapped the region. He was followed by E.C. Baber, who investigated the geographic mysteries of the western provinces of the "Celestial empire" between 1877 and 1878. Other notable explorers of the continent and its trade routes included Archibald Colquhoun, who traveled from Peking to Talifu in 1881, and Mark Bell, who crossed the continent in 1887.
