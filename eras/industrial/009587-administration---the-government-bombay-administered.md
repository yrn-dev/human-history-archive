# Administration of the Bombay Presidency

The Bombay Presidency was a province of British India consisting of British districts and native states. Geographically, the territory extended from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. and from 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. It was bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, Indore, the Central Provinces and Hyderabad to the east, and Baluchistan, the Punjab and Rajputana to the north. The presidency included the settlement of Aden, though the Portuguese settlements of Goa, Damaun, and Diu, as well as the native state of Baroda, remained within its limits while maintaining different administrative relations.

## Executive and Legislative Government
The government of Bombay was administered by a governor in council. The governor, who served as president, was appointed from England. The council consisted of the governor and two ordinary members selected from the Indian civil service and appointed by the crown. These individuals functioned as the executive members of the government.

For the purpose of making laws, a legislative council was established. This body included the governor and his executive council, along with between eight and twenty other persons, at least half of whom were non-officials.

Administrative duties were divided among the executive council members, with each member overseeing one or two government departments. Each department was staffed by a secretary, an under-secretary, an assistant secretary, and a large number of clerks.

## Judicial and District Administration
Justice throughout the presidency was administered by a high court located at Bombay, which comprised a chief justice and seven puisne judges. This was supported by assistant and district judges across the presidency's districts.

The presidency was divided into twenty-six districts and four commissionerships: Sind, the southern or Carnatic, the central or Deccan, and the northern or Gujarat. District administration was carried out by collectors, assistant collectors, and various supernumerary assistants.

## Administration of Native States
The presidency included 353 separate native state units. These were administered either by collectors of the districts where smaller states were located or by political agents. Political administration of these states was supervised by British agents stationed at principal native courts; their specific positions varied based on the relationship between the principality and the paramount power.

Chief groups of native states included:
* **North Gujarat:** Cambay, Palanpur agency, Mahi Kantha agency, Rewa Kantha agency, Cutch, and Kathiawar agency.
* **South Gujarat:** Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.
* **North Konkan, Nasik and Khandesh:** Surgana, Jawhar, and the Khandesh political agency.
* **South Konkan and Dharwar:** Savanur, Sawantwari, and Janjira.
* **Deccan Satara Jagirs:** Jath, Phaltan, Aundh, Bhor, and Akalkot.
* **Southern Mahratta states:** Kolhapur and other states.
* **Sind:** Khairpur.

## Local and Educational Administration
The municipal government of Bombay City was established by an 1888 act of the legislative council. It consisted of a town council and a municipal corporation. The corporation had 72 members: 16 nominated by the government, 36 elected by ratepayers, 16 elected by justices of the peace, two by the chamber of commerce, and two by the senate of the university. The corporation included Parsees, Mahommedans, Hindus, and Europeans. The town council served as the corporation's standing committee and consisted of 12 members (four nominated by the government and eight elected by the corporation). Additionally, a port trust nominated by the government from the commercial community managed the warehouses, docks, and port of Bombay.

Education was overseen by a director of public instruction, who managed the department according to state policy. The University of Bombay, established in 1857 as an examining body based on the London university model, consisted of fellows, a vice-chancellor, and a chancellor (a role held *ex officio* by the governor). While native states generally adopted the government system, the Kathiawar states and Baroda employed their own inspectors.

## Historical Evolution
The English presence began in 1618 with a factory at Surat. Bombay was ceded to the English crown in 1661 as part of the dower of the infanta Catherine of Portugal. Due to unsuccessful administration by crown officers, the city was transferred to the East India Company in 1668 for an annual payment of £10. 

The governor's seat moved from Surat to Bombay after 1708. While Bombay was the head of all Company possessions in India in 1687, it became subordinate to Calcutta in 1753. Territorial expansion occurred through conflicts with the Mahratta power; the treaty of Salbai in 1782 ceded Salsette to the British, and the second Mahratta war ending in 1803 resulted in the acquisition of districts such as Kaira and Broach, following the 1800 annexation of Surat.
