# Dulcigno

Dulcigno (known in Servian as *Ultsin* and in Turkish as *Olgun*) is a seaport of Montenegro located on the Adriatic Sea, situated approximately 8 miles west of the Albanian frontier. The town is characterized by its picturesque setting, built partly along the shore and partly on a promontory overlooking its bay, and is shut in by forests and hills.

## Geography and Urban Character
The town possesses a decidedly Turkish air, evidenced by its mosques, minarets, bazaars, and narrow, crooked lanes. The old quarter, located on the promontory, is walled and contains a medieval castle that was once of great strength. While the town is considered the prettiest of the Montenegrin towns, its harbor is liable to deposits of silt and lacks adequate shelter.

## History
The history of Dulcigno is marked by various occupations and strategic shifts:
*   **Roman and Medieval Periods:** The Romans captured the town in 167 B.C., referring to it as *Olcinium* or *Ulcinium*. During the middle ages, the location was noted as a haunt for pirates.
*   **17th and 18th Centuries:** In the 17th century, Dulcigno served as the residence of Sabbatai Zebi (who died in 1676), a person who declared himself the Messiah before embracing Islam. In 1718, the town was the site of a significant defeat for the Venetians.
*   **Turkish and Montenegrin Rule:** The town belonged to the Turks until 1880. Its cession to Montenegro was mandated by the terms of the Treaty of Berlin (1878). This transfer was enforced through the "Dulcigno demonstration," a naval action involving the fleets of Russia, Austria, Germany, France, and Great Britain.

## Population and Economy
As of 1900, the population of Dulcigno was approximately 5,000. In 1880, the population was higher, numbering about 8,000, though the number of Turks—who form the bulk of the inhabitants—decreased steadily after that year. Albanians and Italians are also fairly numerous in the town.

The local economy includes fishing and some shipbuilding. The town is also a stop for Austrian Lloyd steamers, which call at intervals. Religious diversity is reflected in the town's architecture, as it contains an ancient Latin church and a Roman Catholic cathedral alongside its mosques.
