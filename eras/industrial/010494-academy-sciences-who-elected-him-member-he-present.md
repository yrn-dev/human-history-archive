# Academy of Sciences

The Academy of Sciences, particularly in France and other European nations, has served as a primary institution for the recognition of scientific achievement, the publication of research, and the election of distinguished scholars across various disciplines.

## The French Academy of Sciences
The French Academy of Sciences has elected members from a wide array of scientific fields, including astronomy, physics, mathematics, and naval architecture. Election to the Academy often followed the presentation of significant research or memoirs.

### Astronomers and Physicists
Jean Sylvain Bailly, a French astronomer and orator, was elected a member of the Academy of Sciences in 1763. His election followed his work calculating the orbit of the 1759 comet (Halley's) and reducing the observations of 515 zodiacal stars made by Nicolas de Lacaille. Bailly further contributed to the Academy with a memoir in 1763, which he later expanded into the 1766 work *Essai sur la theorie des satellites de Jupiter*.

Jean Baptiste Biot, a physicist born in Paris, was elected to the Academy of Sciences at an unusually early age in 1803. Biot's scientific contributions were extensive, particularly in the field of optics and the polarization of light, though he failed in his ambition to become the Academy's perpetual secretary.

### Mathematicians and Engineers
Jean Charles Borda, a mathematician and nautical astronomer, was elected a member of the Academy of Sciences in 1756 after presenting a *Memoire sur le mouvement des projectiles*. Borda provided the Academy with a long series of valuable memoirs and invented the majority of the instruments used in the attempt to determine an arc of the meridian, a project in which he was associated with P.F.A. Mechain and J.B.J. Delambre.

Stanislas Charles Henri Laurent Dupuy de Lome, a naval architect, was elected to the Academy of Sciences in 1866. He is noted for his work in iron shipbuilding, including the construction of the steam line-of-battle ship "Napoleon" and the armored frigate "Gloire." In July 1875, he exhibited models of "bateaux porte-trains" and plans for an improved harbour at Calais to the Academy of Sciences.

## Other National Academies of Science
Beyond France, various national academies have recognized both domestic and international scholars.

### The Portuguese Academy of Sciences
The Portuguese Academy of Sciences was founded with the assistance of the duke of Alafoes and Jose Francisco Correa da Serra. Correa da Serra served as the institution's perpetual secretary and held the privilege of publishing its transactions without the oversight of a censor.

### The Berlin and Prussian Academies
The Berlin Academy of Sciences (also referred to as the Prussian Academy of Sciences) has elected several international members:
* **Charles Darwin:** Elected as a corresponding member in 1863 and became a fellow in 1878.
* **Sir Charles Lyell:** Elected as a corresponding member of the Royal Academy of Sciences at Berlin.
* **Ingram Bywater:** An English classical scholar who was elected as a corresponding member of the Prussian Academy of Sciences.

## Related Academic Institutions
In addition to the scientific academies, other specialized bodies exist. For example, the Academie des Sciences Morales elected a member in 1875 whose career was devoted to teaching and historical research at the Sorbonne and the Ecole Normale. Furthermore, the French Institute has served as a body for corresponding members, including Charles Darwin (botanical section) and Sir Charles Lyell.
