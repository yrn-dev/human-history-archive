# Bottomry Bonds

A bottomry bond is a specific type of contract of hypothecation used in maritime affairs. Under this agreement, the owner of a ship, or the ship's master acting as the owner's agent, borrows money to address an emergency, such as the need for necessary repairs for the use of the ship.

## Security and Repayment
To secure the repayment of the borrowed funds, the borrower pledges the ship as security. This pledge may be for the entire vessel or specifically for the keel or bottom of the ship, a practice referred to as *partem pro toto*.

The repayment of the bond is contingent upon the outcome of the ship's journey:
* **Successful Voyage:** If the ship safely accomplishes her voyage, the obligee is entitled to the return of the principal sum along with the agreed-upon interest.
* **Total Loss:** If the ship is totally lost, the obligee loses the money altogether.

## Context within English Law
In the broader context of English law, a bond is defined as an obligation by deed. Its primary purpose is to ensure that the obligor (the person giving the bond) either performs a specific act, refrains from an act, or pays a sum of money. To achieve this, the obligor binds himself to a penalty toward the obligee. 

Most bonds include a condition known as a "defeasance," which renders the bond void if the obligor fulfills the specified obligation—such as paying a sum that is usually half of the penalty. If the condition is not met, the bond becomes absolute or forfeit at law, charging both the obligor and his estate.

## Other Types of Bonds
The archive distinguishes bottomry bonds from several other legal and financial instruments:
* **Post Obit Bonds:** Bonds given by a legatee or expectant heir, payable on or after the death of the person from whom they expect an inheritance.
* **Lloyd's Bonds:** Instruments under a railway company's seal that admit indebtedness for goods supplied or work done, allowing companies to increase debt without technically violating their charter.
* **Debenture Bonds:** Bonds secured only by a company's covenant, lacking a fixed or floating charge on assets.
* **Heritable Bonds:** A term from Scots law describing a bond for money joined with a conveyance of land held as security.
* **Recognizance:** An obligation of record entered into before a court of record.

## Legal Restrictions and Remedies
English law deems bonds void if they are used to secure actions contrary to the policy of the law, such as marriage brocage bonds (used to procure a marriage) or bonds given to a woman for future cohabitation.

For bonds involving special conditions, the act of 1696 and the Judicature Act prescribe the remedy for the obligee. The obligee assigns the breaches, damages are assessed, and a stay of execution is entered once the obligor pays those damages into court. Courts frequently must determine if the sum in such bonds represents liquidated damages or a true penalty; while courts may interfere with penalties, they will not interfere if the parties have agreed upon the damages for a breach.
