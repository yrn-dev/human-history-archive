# Newmilns, Darvel, and Beith

Newmilns, Darvel, and Beith are towns located in the county of Ayrshire, Scotland. These locations are noted for their specific industrial contributions to the region, particularly in the production of textiles and furniture.

## Industrial Activity
The towns of Newmilns and Darvel are characterized by the presence of large lace curtain factories. In Beith, the economy is further diversified by a considerable industry in cabinet-making. Additionally, Beith is one of several towns in Ayrshire—alongside Dalry, Kilbirnie, Kilmaurs, and Stewarton—where hosiery and other fabrics, including cotton and woollens, are manufactured.

Administratively, Beith serves as a site for legal and educational services. Resident sheriffs-substitute at Ayr and Kilmarnock also sit at Beith. The town provides secondary education through its secondary schools, and the county and borough councils provide funding for technical education. These technical classes, which are subsidized at various local centres, offer instruction in subjects such as mining, engineering, gardening, science, art, and agriculture.

## Regional Context
These towns are part of Ayrshire, which is the principal mining county in Scotland and possesses the second largest coalfield. Beith is specifically listed as one of the chief coal districts in the county. The broader region is characterized by a wide array of heavy and light industries, including the production of iron ore, pig iron, and fire-clay. Other significant regional activities include shipbuilding at ports such as Troon, Ayr, Irvine, and Fairlie, as well as the manufacture of explosives near Stevenston.

## Cabinet-Making and Furniture
The cabinet-making industry found in Beith and Dalry relates to the work of the "cabinet-maker," an artificer who constructs furniture. While the manufacture of cabinets in the strict sense—pieces typically supported by a stand and containing pigeon-holes and drawers—has become an occasional part of the work, the term now applies to various furniture used for the exhibition or safe-keeping of valuable objects. Historically, the cabinet evolved from a rudimentary oblong box used for storing money and precious stones, originating in Italy at the start of the 16th century, before becoming a sumptuous household adornment during the Renaissance.
