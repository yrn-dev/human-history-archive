# Shipping and Transport of the Canadian Pacific Railway Company

The Canadian Pacific Railway Company (C.P.R.) operates an extensive network of transportation infrastructure, integrating transcontinental rail services with international and regional shipping lines. The company's activities have played a significant role in the development of the Dominion of Canada, specifically within the province of British Columbia.

## International Shipping Lines
The Canadian Pacific Railway Company maintains several lines of mail steamers that operate from the ports of Victoria and Vancouver. These include:

*   **The Empress Line:** This service runs to China and Japan once every three weeks.
*   **The Australian Line:** This service runs once a month, with routes to Sydney, Fiji, and Honolulu.

In addition to these international mail lines, the company operates a fleet of coasting steamers and a dedicated line of steamers serving Alaska. The establishment of the first Canadian steamship lines to Australia, as well as to Japan and China, is noted as a chief event in the history of British Columbia.

## Railway Infrastructure in British Columbia
The C.P.R. manages a vast portion of the rail network in British Columbia. By 1905, there were 1,627 miles of railway in the province, with 1,187 miles owned or controlled by the Canadian Pacific Railway.

### The Main Line
The Pacific division of the railway enters British Columbia from the east through the Rocky Mountains. It traverses approximately 500 miles across the province before reaching its terminus at Vancouver. The main line crosses the Rocky Mountains via the Kicking Horse (or Wapta) Pass, which sits at an elevation of 5,300 feet. This line also intersects the Columbia River at Revelstoke and again at Donald.

### Southern Branch and Regional Lines
A southern branch of the railway departs from the main line at Medicine Hat. This branch crosses the Rocky Mountains through the Crow's Nest Pass (5,500 feet), entering British Columbia just north of the boundary with the United States. The line runs approximately parallel to the boundary as far as Midway in the Boundary district. This specific route has been instrumental in opening up productive mining districts and extensive coal fields.

## Impact on Urban and Regional Development
The construction of the Canadian Pacific Railway has been a primary driver of urban growth and political stability. The city of Vancouver was founded by the C.P.R., and the city of Calgary was created by the building of the railway around 1883. Calgary serves as an important point on the line and maintains a resident general superintendent for the company.

The railway's construction was also a critical political condition for British Columbia's entry into the confederation in 1871. The failure to fulfill the promise of speedy construction by 1876 led to strained relations between the province and the government in Ottawa, which were only resolved once the transcontinental road began construction.

## Interaction with Natural Geography
The C.P.R. lines navigate some of the most challenging terrain in North America. The railway must traverse the Rocky Mountains, which feature peaks such as Mount Robson (13,700 feet) and various passes. While the province possesses numerous rivers—including the Fraser, Columbia, and Skeena—these are often interrupted by rapids, rendering them of indifferent value for commercial communication. Consequently, wherever the Canadian Pacific Railway has constructed lines, the previous importance of these rivers as means of transport has been diminished.
