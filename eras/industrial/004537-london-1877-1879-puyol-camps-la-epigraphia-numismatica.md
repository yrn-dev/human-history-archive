# London (1877 and 1879)

The years 1877 and 1879 in London were marked by significant activity in the fields of academic publishing, legal appointments, historical scholarship, and the arts. During this period, the city served as a hub for the publication of diverse international research, ranging from Arctic exploration and linguistic studies to the documentation of ancient texts.

## Academic and Linguistic Publications
London was a center for the dissemination of specialized scholarly works during these years. In 1877, the city saw the publication of *Essai sur la langue basque* by C. Ribary, which was translated from Hungarian by Julien Vinson. Additionally, Wentworth Webster published *Basque Legends* in London in both 1877 and 1879.

The study of ancient and foreign languages was further supported by the London Pali Text Society, which published the first volume of the *Jataka*, edited by V. Fansböll, in 1877.

## Exploration and Geographical Records
The late 1870s saw the publication of several key accounts of polar and global exploration in London. In 1877, Nares published *Voyage to the Polar Sea* in two volumes, and the Blue Book containing the journals of the Nares Expedition (1875-1876) was also issued. That same year, H. Rink published *Danish Greenland*.

Other geographical and biographical records appearing in London included a memorial for Maj.-Gen. W. W. H. Greathed, C.B., of the Royal Engineers (Bengal), which was printed for private circulation in 1879.

## Legal and Political Figures
The London legal and political landscape experienced several transitions during this timeframe. In 1877, Edward Fry was knighted and raised to the bench; as a chancery judge, he became known for his elucidations and interpretations of the Judicature Acts as they first came into operation.

In the realm of the peerage, 1879 was a year of succession for the earls of Durham. George Frederick d'Arcy, the 2nd earl of Durham, died in 1879 and was succeeded by his son, John George Lambton, who became the 3rd earl.

## Arts and History
The London arts scene was influenced by figures such as Randolph Caldecott, who had migrated to the city in 1872 to study at the Slade School. By 1877, Caldecott had produced illustrations for Mrs. Comyns Carr's *North Italian Folk* and Washington Irving's *Bracebridge Hall*. In 1879, he provided illustrations for Henry Blackburn's *Breton Folk*.

In the field of history, John Sherren Brewer served as a professor of English language and literature and a lecturer in modern history at King's College, London. Although he was secured the crown living of Toppesfield, Essex, by Disraeli in 1877, Brewer's career had been deeply rooted in London's academic and journalistic circles. He died in February 1879.

## Other Notable Works and Events
Various other publications were released in London during this period, including Harriet Martineau's four-volume *History of the Thirty Years' Peace* in 1877. In 1878, Bickers and Son published a record of a protest regarding Mr. Henry M. Stanley and the Royal Geographical Society, authored by Col. H. Yule and H. M. Hyndman.

Additionally, the city's consular and military connections were evident in the career of Mr. Annesley, who, after serving in the Turkish army during the war of 1877, entered the British consular service in 1879.
