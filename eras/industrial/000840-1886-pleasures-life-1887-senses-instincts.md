## Political and Public Life of John Lubbock
During this period, John Lubbock, 1st Baron Avebury, was a highly active figure in English public life. An English banker, naturalist, and politician, Lubbock served as the president of the Linnaean Society from 1881 until 1886. In 1886, he also served as the Rede lecturer at Cambridge University. Lubbock's influence extended to parliament, where he was a member of the Unionist party and had previously carried enactments such as the Bank Holidays Act 1871. His career was characterized by a balance between public affairs—including serving on commissions regarding financial questions and coinage—and his scientific interests, which led him to serve as president of both the Anthropological Institute and the Entomological Society.
