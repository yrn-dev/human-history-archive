# Corpus Christi College, Oxford

Corpus Christi College is an academic institution at the University of Oxford. The college has served as a center for scholarship across various disciplines, including mathematics, history, and theology, and maintains a collection of historical manuscripts.

## Academic Appointments and Fellows
The college has been home to numerous scholars and fellows over several centuries. In the late 18th century, Thomas Burgess was educated at the college and became a fellow in 1783. In the early 19th century, William Buckland was elected as a scholar of the college in 1801, earned his B.A. in 1804, and was subsequently elected a fellow in 1809.

## Notable Alumni and Affiliations
The college has provided the foundation for various professional careers in the church and sciences. William Buckland, after his time as a fellow, was presented by the college in 1825 to the living of Stoke Charity near Whitchurch, Hants. Buckland's career extended into geology and divinity; he became the first holder of a readership in geology endowed by the treasury in 1819 and was later appointed to the deanery of Westminster in 1845.

Other figures associated with the college include Thomas Burgess, who later became the Bishop of St David's in 1803 and the Bishop of Salisbury in 1823. Additionally, in 1630, the college provided the curacy of St Benet's, Cambridge, to Thomas Fuller.
