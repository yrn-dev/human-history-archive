# Cesare Balbo

## Early Life and Family
Cesare Balbo, a statesman and writer of Italy, was born in Turin on November 21, 1789. He was born into a noble Piedmontese family. His father, Prospero Balbo, held a high-ranking position within the Sardinian court and served as the mayor of the capital city at the time of Cesare's birth. His mother belonged to the Azeglio family and passed away when Cesare was three years old. Following her death, he was raised in the household of his great-grandmother, the countess of Bugino. In 1798, Balbo joined his father in Paris.

## Political and Military Career
Between 1808 and 1814, Balbo served the Napoleonic empire in various roles across Paris, Rome, Florence, and Illyria. Following the fall of Napoleon, he returned to the service of his native country. During this period, while his father served as the minister of the interior, Cesare entered the army and conducted political missions to London and Paris.

In 1821, a revolution broke out. Although Balbo disapproved of the revolution, he was suspected of sympathizing with it, which resulted in his forced exile. While he was eventually permitted to return to Piedmont, he was denied any further public service.

## Literary Work and Political Ideals
Denied political appointments despite frequent efforts to obtain one, Balbo turned to literature as his sole remaining method for influencing the destiny of his country. This transition resulted in literary works that were often incomplete or fitful, characterized by a practical and sometimes temporary element.

The primary objective of Balbo's writing was to assist in securing Italian independence from foreign control. He did not desire or expect true Italian unity; instead, he was devoted to the house of Savoy, believing they were destined to change Italy's fate. Balbo's genuine ideal was a confederation of separate states under the supremacy of the pope. In his work *Speranze* (Hopes of Italy), he addressed the essential requirements for independence, suggesting that Austria should seek compensation in the Balkans for the inevitable loss of its Italian provinces.
