# Jorgen Engebretsen Moe

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813–1882) was a collector of Norwegian folklore who worked in close partnership with Peter Christen Asbjornsen. The two men were so united in their professional efforts that they are rarely named separately in historical records.

## Early Life and Education
Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at the Mo i Hole parsonage, located in Sigdal Ringerike. He first met Peter Christen Asbjornsen when Moe was fourteen years of age, initiating a close friendship that continued until the end of their lives. 

Moe attended Christiania University, which he left in 1839. Following his departure from the university, he devoted himself to the study of theology and supported himself by working as a tutor in Christiania.

## Collection of Folklore
In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. This discovery led the two friends to compare their findings and decide to work together in concert. 

While Asbjornsen pursued zoology and conducted investigating voyages along the coasts of Norway—specifically in the Hardanger fjord—Moe utilized his holidays to wander through the mountains. He traveled into the most remote districts to collect stories.

## Published Works
The collaboration between Moe and Asbjornsen resulted in a major work titled *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*. The first installment of this work appeared in 1842–1843 and was recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology. A second volume was published in 1844, followed by a new collection in 1871. 

In 1859, Sir George Dasent translated many of the *Folkeeventyr* into English. While the two worked closely, some projects were independent; for example, Asbjornsen published a collection of Norwegian fairy tales (*huldreeventyr og folkesagn*) in 1845 without Moe's assistance.
