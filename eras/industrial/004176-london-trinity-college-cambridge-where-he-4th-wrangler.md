# Trinity College, Cambridge

Trinity College, Cambridge, is an academic institution associated with numerous scholars, theologians, and scientists. The college has produced individuals who achieved high rankings in the university's mathematical examinations, known as wranglers, and has served as a foundation for figures who later held prominent positions in the church, law, and science.

## Academic Achievements and the Wrangler System
The college is noted for its students' performance in the mathematical tripos. Notable individuals who achieved wrangler status at Trinity include:
* **Alfred Barry**, who was the 4th wrangler and also earned a first-class in the classical tripos in 1848.
* **John MacGregor** (known as "Rob Roy"), who attended Trinity in 1844 and was a wrangler.
* **Thomas Hewitt Key**, who graduated as the 19th wrangler in 1821.

## Notable Fellows and Leadership
Trinity College has been home to various distinguished fellows and masters. Dr. John Wilkins served as master of the college and later became the bishop of Chester. Dr. Barrow, a fellow of Trinity, was appointed master of the college by king's patent on February 13, 1672, and later served as vice-chancellor of the university in 1775. 

Other fellows of the college include:
* **Sir Joseph John Thomson**, the Cavendish Professor of Experimental Physics.
* **John Percival Postgate**, a professor of Latin at the University of Liverpool.
* **W. Aldis Wright**, who served as the secretary for a company appointed to work on the Old Testament.
* **The Rev. Frederick Field**.
* **Aubrey Stewart** and **George Long**, both of whom were fellows and translated the works of Plutarch.

## Influence in Theology and the Church
Many members of Trinity College transitioned into high-ranking ecclesiastical roles. Alfred Barry, after his time at Trinity and King's College, London, served as the bishop of Sydney and primate of Australia, as well as assistant bishop in the diocese of Rochester and assistant bishop in West London. He also held canonries at Worcester, Westminster, and Windsor.

Samuel Horsley entered Trinity College, Cambridge, and became LL.B. in 1758. He later became the bishop of St David's, Rochester, and St Asaph, and held the deanery of Westminster. Horsley is remembered for his polemical controversy with Joseph Priestley regarding the doctrine of the Trinity.

## Connections to London and Other Institutions
The archive records several links between Trinity College and institutions in London. Alfred Barry served as the principal of King's College, London, from 1868 to 1883. Thomas Hewitt Key, after his education at Trinity and St John's, became a professor of Latin and later a professor of comparative grammar at the newly founded university of London.

Additionally, several Trinity-affiliated individuals held roles in London's civic and scientific life. John MacGregor was elected to the London school board in 1870 and 1873. Charles Chree, a former fellow of King's College, Cambridge, served as the President of the Physical Society of London.
