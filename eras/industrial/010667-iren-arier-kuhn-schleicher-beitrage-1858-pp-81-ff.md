# Iren and Arier

"Iren and Arier" is the title of an article written by Pictet, published in 1858 within the first volume of *Kuhn and Schleicher's Beiträge* (pp. 81 ff.). The work focuses on establishing a linguistic and historical connection between the term *Erin* (referring to Ireland) and the term *Arier*.

## Philological Analysis and Critiques
The central thesis of Pictet's article sought to establish a connection between *Erin* and *Arier*. However, this connection has been characterized as impossible by Whitley Stokes, as noted in Max Muller's *Lectures on the Science of Language* (1891). 

Despite the rejection of the link to *Erin*, some philological possibilities remain regarding the root of the term. It is suggested that the word may share the same origin as the *Ario-* found in names such as *Ariovistus*, a form present in both Germanic and Celtic words, according to Uhlenbeck's *Kurzgefasstes etymologisches Worterbuch der altindischen Sprache*.

## The Concept of the Aryan
The broader context of the term *Arier* (Aryan) is heavily associated with the works of Max Muller on comparative philology. Muller's research illustrates that the word was used as a national name in several regions, including:
* India (Sanskrit *arya-*)
* Persia (Old Persian *ariya-*)
* Bactria (Sanskrit *arya-* and Zend *airya-*)

There are ongoing scholarly debates regarding the exact etymology of the word. Max Muller asserted a connection to *ira*, a Sanskrit word for earth, though this is considered far from certain. Spiegel, in *Die arische Periode*, observed that while the word can be connected to a root *ar-*, there are multiple roots of that form with different meanings, leaving no certain criterion to decide the specific relationship.

## Distinctions within Sanskrit
Within the Sanskrit language, it is probable that two distinct words must be identified:
1. *Arya*: The origin of the term "Aryan," from which the common term *arya* is derived.
2. *Arya*: An epithet for deities that frequently appears in the *Rig Veda*.

Additionally, in various passages, *aryas* may be interpreted as the genitive of *ari*, which is explained as meaning "pious, devoted, active."

Other connections to the term *Arier* remain doubtful or uncertain. For instance, the name of Armenia (Old Persian *Armina-*) has often been connected to this group, but its origin is considered uncertain.
