## Chronological Calculations and the Hegira
In the study of the Hegira calendar, specific mathematical formulas are used to determine the nature of years and the days of the week. A year is classified as intercalary when the value of R is less than 11. 

To calculate the number of intercalary years from year 1 up to a specific year Y (inclusive), the formula used is ((11 Y + 14) / 30)_w. For the period up to year Y - 1, the formula is (11 Y + 3 / 30)_w. 

The determination of the day of the week on which any year of the Hegira begins is based on the fact that year 1 began on a Friday. Following a common year of 354 days (which consists of 50 weeks and 4 days), the day of the week is postponed by 4 days, in addition to the extra day provided by each intercalary year. Using a scale where w = 1 through 7 represents Sunday through Saturday, the commencement day for year Y is calculated as w = 2 + 4(Y / 7)_r + ((11 Y + 3) / 30)_w (rejecting sevens). 

Further simplification of this formula results in w = 6(Y / 7)_r + 3((11 Y + 3) / 30)_r (rejecting sevens). These values circulate in a period of 210 years (7 times 30). If C represents the number of completed cycles and y represents the year of the cycle, then Y = 30 C + y, and the formula becomes w = 5(C / 7)_r + 6(y / 7)_r + 3((11 y + 3) / 30)_r (rejecting sevens).

## Engineering and Bridge Stress Determination
In the context of bridge engineering, specifically regarding beam girder or truss bridges, the determination of stresses in main girders is a primary requirement. A main girder is composed of a vertical web and an upper and lower flange (also referred to as a chord or boom). 

Loading forces are categorized as follows:
*   **Vertical forces:** These are the primary loading forces considered for the girders.
*   **Horizontal forces:** These are caused by wind pressure and are managed separately via a horizontal system of bracing.

For practical application, the web is considered to resist all vertical forces and equal horizontal shearing forces (in the case of a plate web), while the chords or booms are considered to carry exclusively horizontal compression and tension.

When analyzing a beam with a system of loads (W_1, W_2, ... W_n), the reaction at the right abutment (R_2) is calculated as W_1x_1/l + W_2x_2/l... while the reaction at the left abutment (R_1) is the sum of all loads minus R_2. The total shear (S) at any section a b is R minus the summation of all loads to the left of that section. The bending moment (M) at section a b is calculated as R_1p minus the summation of (W_1p_1 + W_2p_2 ...), where p is the distance of R_1 from the section and p_1, p_2 are the distances of the loads from the section.

## Greatest Shear and Concentrated Loads
When concentrated loads at fixed distances travel over a bridge, the greatest shear at a specific section C is determined by the position of the loads. If W_1 is at C, and R is the resultant of the loads on the bridge, the shear at C and the reaction at B is Rn/l. 

If the loads advance a distance a so that W_2 reaches C, the shear at C becomes R(n+a)/l - W_1, plus any reaction d at B caused by additional loads entering the girder. The shear increases by moving W_2 to C if Ra/l + d > W_1.

If the action of the load is distributed to bracing intersections by cross girders and rail, the loads at A and B are W(p-x)/p and Wx/p. In this scenario, if W_2 is at C, the shear at C is:
*   R(n+a)/l + d - W_1 (if a > p)
*   R(n+a)/l + d - W_1a/p (if a < p)

Neglecting d, the shear increases by moving W_2 to C if Ra/l > W_1 (in the first case) or if Ra/l > W_1a/p (in the second case).
