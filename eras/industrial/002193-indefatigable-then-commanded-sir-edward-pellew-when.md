# H.M.S. Indefatigable

The name *Indefatigable* has been borne by multiple vessels in the service of the British Royal Navy across different eras, appearing in archival records during the late 18th century and the early 20th century.

## Service under Sir Edward Pellew
During the period of conflict between Great Britain and France, H.M.S. *Indefatigable* was commanded by Sir Edward Pellew. During this time, the vessel was involved in encounters with American trading ships. In one specific instance, the *Indefatigable* was responsible for the impressment of an American seaman. This action later prompted a retaliatory response from William Bainbridge, a merchant sailor who would later become a commodore in the United States navy; Bainbridge is said to have taken a man from a British ship in retaliation for the impressment carried out by Pellew's command.

## 20th Century Operations
In August 1914, a battle-cruiser named *Indefatigable* operated in the Mediterranean under the direction of the Admiralty and the Commander-in-Chief, Mediterranean, Sir Berkeley Milne. The vessel was tasked with the critical objective of shadowing the German battle-cruiser *Goeben* and the fast light cruiser *Breslau*.

On the morning of August 4, the Commander-in-Chief reported that the *Indefatigable*, alongside the *Indomitable*, was shadowing the *Goeben* and *Breslau* at coordinates 37.44 North and 7.56 East. The Admiralty's primary concern was to prevent the *Goeben* from interfering with French transports. While Winston Churchill and Sir Edward Grey agreed that the *Indefatigable* and *Indomitable* should engage the *Goeben* if the French transports were attacked, the British Cabinet formally maintained that no act of war should be committed before the expiration of the ultimatum, prioritizing the moral integrity of the British Empire over the sinking of a single ship.
