# Joseph Dobrovsky and the Bohemian Language Revival

## Introduction to the Bohemian Language Revival
The Bohemian language experienced a period of significant decline following the deaths of Comenius, Slavata, and Skala. During this time, no works of importance were written in the language, and the Austrian government actively sought to discourage its use. This trend shifted at the beginning of the 19th century with the onset of the romantic movement. The initial stages of the revival were modest, characterized primarily by the publication of translations from foreign languages.

## The Role of Joseph Dobrovsky
Joseph Dobrovsky (1753–1829) was the first writer to draw attention back to the Bohemian language, which had become almost forgotten. Recognized as the greatest Czech philologist of his era, Dobrovsky produced a history of Bohemian literature and a grammar of the Bohemian language. Despite his role in the revival, most of his works were written in Latin or German. His contributions specifically in the Bohemian language were limited to a collection of letters and several essays written for the early issues of the *Casopis Musea Kralovstvi Ceskeho* (Journal of the Bohemian Museum).

## The Kralodvorsky Rukopis Controversy
Dobrovsky played a critical role in the scholarly evaluation of the *Kralodvorsky Rukopis* (the manuscript of Königinhof). In 1817, Wenceslas Hanka, who later served as the librarian of the Bohemian museum, claimed to have discovered an ancient manuscript containing lyric and epic poems in the church tower of Dvur Kralove. While the document was long accepted as genuine, frequently translated, and printed across Europe, Dobrovsky expressed suspicions regarding its authenticity from the beginning. These doubts were eventually validated; after Hanka was convicted of fabricating other Bohemian documents, professors such as Masoryk, Gebauer, and Goll proved the manuscript was a forgery.

## Subsequent Figures in the Language Revival
While Dobrovsky initiated the process, the main credit for the revival of Bohemian literature and language is attributed to four subsequent men:

*   **Joseph Jungmann (1773–1847):** He published numerous translations of English and German writers. His most significant contributions include a monumental German and Bohemian dictionary and his *Dejepis literatury ceska* (history of Bohemian literature).
*   **John Kolar (1793–1852):** Regarded as the greatest poet of the revival, Kolar is best known for *Slavy dcera* (daughter of Slavia), a personification of the Slavic race. While its artistic value is debated, it was highly important to the literary revival. He also produced philological studies.
*   **Paul Joseph Safarik (1795–1861):** A prolific writer whose work *Starozitnosti Slovanske* (Slavic antiquities) attempted to record the literature and history of the early Slavs.
*   **Francis Palacky (1798–1876):** Considered the greatest of Bohemian historians, his most important work is the history of Bohemia covering the period from the earliest times to 1526.

## Later Challenges to the Language
The struggle for the national language continued into the mid-19th century. By the end of 1849, constitutional government in Bohemia had ceased. A severe reaction followed in which the Austrian monarchy attempted to exterminate the national language. During this period, Bohemian newspapers were suppressed, and the German language was mandated for use in all government offices and schools. Even the society of the Bohemian museum, consisting of scholars and noblemen, was forced to hold meetings under police supervision.
