# Districts of the Bombay Presidency: Kaira, Panch Mahals, Surat, Thana, Ahmednagar, and Khandesh

The districts of Kaira, Panch Mahals, Surat, Thana, Ahmednagar, and Khandesh are administrative divisions within the Bombay Presidency, a province of British India. The presidency is governed by a governor and consists of both British districts and native states. These specific districts are distributed across the presidency's divisions, primarily within the northern (Gujarat) and central (Deccan) commissionerships.

## Historical Acquisition and Administration
The territorial framework of the Bombay Presidency evolved significantly between 1803 and 1827. Surat was one of the earliest English interests, with the East India Company establishing a factory there in 1618 under a charter from Emperor Jahangir. Surat was later annexed in 1800. Following the second Mahratta war, which ended in 1803, the British received the districts of Kaira and others. The Gujarat districts were taken over by the Bombay government in 1805 and further enlarged in 1818.

The Panch Mahals were added to the presidency in 1853 through a lease from Sindhia. In the Deccan, the defeat and capture of Baji Rao, the last of the peshwas, between 1817 and 1818 led to the inclusion of Ahmednagar, along with Poona, Nasik, and other regions. The settlement of these areas was completed by Governor Mountstuart Elphinstone (1819–1827), who sought to rule on native lines while codifying laws and establishing schools and courts.

## Regional Profiles and Geography

### Khandesh
Khandesh, located in the central division, was originally a single district but was partitioned into East and West Khandesh in 1906. East Khandesh has its headquarters at Jalgaon and covers 4,544 sq. m., while West Khandesh is headquartered at Dhulia and covers 5,497 sq. m. The population of the old single district was 1,427,382 in 1901.

The region is defined by the Tapti river, which flows from east to west, dividing the districts. The landscape varies from an alluvial plain containing rich tracts to the rugged, wooded country of the Satpura hills to the north. This wooded terrain is inhabited by the Bhils, an aboriginal tribe numbering 167,000. Under British rule, many Bhils have been transitioned from a disorderly life to employment as village watchmen and in police duties. The primary crops of the region include wheat, pulse, oilseeds, cotton, and millets.

### Surat and Thana
Surat is situated in the Gujarat region and is crossed by the Tapti Valley line and the Tapti river, which falls into the Gulf of Cambay near the town of Surat. Thana is located in the presidency and is noted for the use of the Mahratti language. Both districts are centers for the manufacture of silk goods.

### Ahmednagar and Kaira
Ahmednagar, located in the Deccan, was once the seat of a new kingdom after the weakness of the Delhi sovereigns in the middle of the 14th century. It was reconquered by Akbar in 1600, after which it became a center of frequent rebellions. Kaira is part of the Gujarat districts acquired by the British in the early 19th century.

## Economy and Industry
The industrial life of these districts is heavily characterized by the growth and manufacture of cotton. Large steam mills have been established in Khandesh, as well as in Ahmedabad and Bombay City. In Khandesh specifically, there are numerous factories for pressing and ginning cotton, including a cotton-mill at Jalgaon.

Silk manufacturing is a notable industry in Surat, Thana, and other nearby areas, though this sector has declined due to competition from European goods. Additionally, Surat is famous for its carved wood-work, with excellent examples of this craft existing within the town.

## Demographics and Culture
The linguistic and religious landscape of these districts varies by region:
*   **Languages:** In Thana and the central division, Mahratti is the chief language. In Khandesh, both Gujarati and Mahratti are spoken.
*   **Religion:** In the Gujarat districts, including Surat and Kaira, Hinduism is the predominant religion, though the influence of former Mahommedan kingdoms remains. The Deccan, where Ahmednagar is located, is the home of the Mahrattas, who make up 30% of the presidency's population.
