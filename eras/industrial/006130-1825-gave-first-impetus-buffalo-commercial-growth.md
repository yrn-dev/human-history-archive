# Buffalo

Buffalo is a major commercial and industrial center in the Union, situated almost equidistant from New York, Boston, and Chicago. Its development has been driven by its favorable location for lake transportation and its position on the primary northern trade route connecting the East and West.

## Early History and Settlement
The site of Buffalo was first visited by Jesuit missionaries and French trappers. In 1679, La Salle built a ship named the "Griffin" on the east bank of the Niagara river at the mouth of Cayuga Creek, where he also constructed Fort Conti; the fort was burned that same year. In 1687, the marquis de Denonville built a fort at the mouth of the river, which served as the predecessor to the fortifications later known as Fort Niagara. The area remained a site for military operations until the end of the War of Independence.

By 1784, the area was referred to as the "Buffalo Creek region," a name derived either from an Indian chief or from the bison and buffalo that Indian tradition suggests frequented the creek's salt licks. The first permanent white resident was Cornelius Winney, an Indian trader who built a cabin near the mouth of the creek around 1788-1789. 

The land was originally part of the Phelps-Gorham Purchase and was acquired around 1793 by the Holland Land Company as part of the Holland Purchase. Joseph Ellicott, the company's agent and known as the "Father of Buffalo," laid out a town in 1801-1802. Though called New Amsterdam on company books until approximately 1810, the name Buffalo or Buffalo Creek remained more popular. The village became the county-seat of Niagara county in 1808, and the town of Buffalo was officially erected two years later.

During the second war with Great Britain, the region became a center for military activity, with British Fort Erie located directly across the Niagara river. In October 1812, Lieutenant Jesse D. Elliott captured the "Caledonia" and "Detroit" from Buffalo. Later, on December 30 and 31, 1813, the settlement was captured, sacked, and attacked.

## Commerce and Transportation
Buffalo serves as a critical distributing center for raw products moving from the West to the East and manufactured goods moving from the East to the West. It is a primary global market for flour and grain. In 1843, Joseph Dart built the first grain elevator; by 1906, these elevators had an annual receipt of more than 200,000,000 bushels and a capacity between twenty and thirty million bushels. Yearly flour receipts are approximately 10,000,000 barrels.

Other significant commercial activities include:
* **Livestock:** Extensive stock-yards covering 75 acres at East Buffalo handle over 10,000,000 head of livestock annually. The city also hosts the largest horse market in America.
* **Other Goods:** Annual receipts include 200,000,000 feet of lumber and 15,000,000 pounds of fish. Coal and iron ore are also handled, partly at Tonawanda.
* **Trade Value:** In 1908, the Buffalo Creek customs district reported imports valued at $6,708,919 and exports valued at $26,192,563.

Transportation is facilitated by several modes:
* **Rail:** More than 500 miles of track exist within city limits, including a belt line transfer company and terminals for some fourteen trunk lines.
* **Water:** The Erie Canal's western terminus is in Buffalo; its completion in 1825 provided the first impetus for the city's commercial growth. The port of Buffalo handles over 5,000,000 tons annually. The Federal government improved the harbor with piers, inner breakwaters, and a 4-mile outer breakwater of cement and stone costing over $2,000,000. The city has 8 to 10 miles of wharfs and a waterfront of about 20 miles.
* **Lake Steamships:** Most Great Lakes steamship lines have an eastern terminus here, connecting Buffalo to Milwaukee, Detroit, Chicago, Cleveland, and the "Head of the Lakes" (Duluth-Superior). The Great Northern Steamship Company, a subsidiary of the Great Northern railway, operates some of the finest and largest inland passenger steamships.
* **International Links:** Buffalo is connected to the Canadian shore via ferry and the International bridge from Squaw Island, which was completed in 1873 at a cost of $1,500,000.

## Government and Infrastructure
Buffalo is governed by a mayor elected for a four-year term and a bicameral city council under an amended charter from 1896. The mayor appoints the heads of the police, fire, parks, civil service, and health departments, while the city council elects the city clerk.

The city's infrastructure includes:
* **Utilities:** Water is obtained from Lake Erie via a municipal supply system. Lighting is provided by manufactured gas and electricity generated from the water power of Niagara Falls. Natural gas for heating and lighting is piped in from Canadian (Welland) and Ohio-Pennsylvania fields.
* **Public Works:** The municipality maintains several well-equipped public baths.
* **Media:** The first newspaper, the *Gazette*, began as a weekly in 1811 and became the daily *Commercial* in 1835. The first daily, the *Courier*, was established in 1831. As of 1908, there were eleven daily papers (including two in Polish and three in German) and several weekly papers in Italian, Polish, and German.
