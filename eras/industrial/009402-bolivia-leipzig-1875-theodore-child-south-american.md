# Bolivia

## Ethnography and Indigenous Peoples
The Aymara, also known as the Colla, are a tribe of South American Indians who formerly lived in the valleys of the Andes and the region surrounding Lake Titicaca. Currently, they constitute the primary ethnical element in Bolivia, though they are of very mixed blood. In Bolivia, the population of Aymaras, including half-breeds, is approximately half a million.

Historically, the Aymaras possessed a significant culture prior to being conquered by the Incas during the 13th and 14th centuries; the megalithic ruins of Tiahuanaco serve as evidence of this period. For the Incas, the Aymara home by Lake Titicaca was considered a "holy land," and Inca national legends attributed the origin of all Quichua (Inca) civilization to that area. Following the arrival of the Spaniards, the Aymaras had been under Inca domination for a long time and were in a decadent state. Despite this, they maintained certain privileges, including the use of their own language. The treatment they received from their conquerors suggested that the conquerors believed themselves to be of Aymara blood.

Physically, a pure Aymara is characterized by a broad face, rounded forehead, black eyes, and a reddish complexion. They are typically short and thick-set with significant chest development. Their disposition is described as apathetic and sullen, similar to that of the Quichuas.

## Flora and Fauna
Bolivia possesses a diverse array of birds and insects. The common vulture is found throughout the country, while the high sierras are home to eagles and condors of the largest size. The tropical valleys and plains are inhabited by the American ostrich (*Rhea americanus*) and a large species of stork known as the *jaburu* or *bata* (*Mycteria americana*), which can reach a maximum height of 8 feet and a wingspan of 8 feet 6 inches. 

The forests of the warm valleys contain song-birds and birds with beautiful plumage, while humming-birds are found even at high mountain altitudes. The lowland forests are populated by toucans, araras, and parrots, which are noted for their beautiful feathers and harsh voices.

The country has benefited from the introduction of European animals, including poultry, swine, goats, sheep, cattle, and horses, which provide wealth and food for much of the population. Mules are widely used as pack animals, though they are imported from Argentina. Additionally, the cochineal insect is found where cactus conditions are favorable, and silkworms have been bred successfully in some departments.

## Historical and Archaeological Research
Archaeological and historical investigations in Bolivia have been conducted by figures such as Adolph Francis Alphonse Bandelier. An American archaeologist born in Switzerland, Bandelier began working in Ecuador, Bolivia, and Peru after 1892. His work was connected to the American Museum of Natural History in New York and the Hemenway Archaeological Expedition. Bandelier's research led him to conclude that various historical myths were false, specifically regarding the Inca civilization of Peru.

## Bibliographic Sources and Authorities
A wide range of literature exists regarding the geography, industry, and history of the Bolivian Republic. Key historical and administrative works include the *Archive Boliviano* by Vicente de Ballivian y Rizas (1872) and Ramon Sotomayor Valdes's study on the administration of General don Jose Maria Acha (1874), which includes a compendium of the governments of the Republic until 1861 and the War of Independence.

Other significant authorities and publications include:
* **Geography and Statistics:** *Nociones de geografia de Bolivia* by J.L. Moreno (1889), *Bosquejo estadistico de Bolivia* by M. Dalence (1878), and the *Diccionario Geografico de la Republica de Bolivia* (1900) by M.V. Ballivian and Eduardo Idiaquez.
* **Industry and Resources:** M.V. Ballivian's works on the rubber industry (*Apuntes sobre la industria de goma elastica*, 1896) and mining (*Monografias de la Industria Minera en Bolivia*, 1899-1900).
* **Travel and Exploration:** G.E. Church's accounts of routes to Bolivia via the River Amazon (1877) and the Rio de la Plata (1902), Sir W.M. Conway's *Climbing and Exploration in the Bolivian Andes* (1903), and Edward D. Mathews's travels through Bolivia and Peru (1879).
* **General Studies:** E. Mossbach's *Bolivia* (1875), Theodore Child's *The South American Republics* (1801), and Charles Wiener's *Perou et Bolivie* (1880).
