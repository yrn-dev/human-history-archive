# Autographic Collections and Related Publications (1860s)

The mid-19th century saw a proliferation of published collections of facsimiles of autographs from various nations, with a significant number produced in England. These works served to preserve the handwriting of royal, noble, and eminent figures of history.

## English Autograph Collections
Among the notable collections published in England during and around the 1860s were several works associated with the Netherclift family. F. G. Netherclift published *The Handbook of Autographs* in 1862. Later, in 1865, *The Autograph Souvenir* was published by R. Sims and F. G. Netherclift. Another significant publication from this period was *The Autographic Mirror*, which was published between 1864 and 1866. 

These works were part of a broader tradition of autograph collections in England, which included earlier efforts such as J. Thane's *British Autography* (1788-1793, with a 1854 supplement), J. G. Nichols' *Autographs of Royal, Noble, Learned and Remarkable Personages in English History* (1829), and J. Netherclift's *Autographs of the Kings and Queens and Eminent Men of Great Britain* (1835). Other related works included *One Hundred Characteristic Autograph Letters* (1849) and *The Autograph Miscellany* (1855), both by Netherclift and Son. Later publications in this genre included L. B. Phillips' *The Autograph Album* (1866) and a five-series set of *Facsimiles of Autographs* published by the British Museum between 1896 and 1900.

## Official National Facsimiles
In addition to private publications, official government records also produced facsimile collections of national manuscripts. These included:
* *Facsimiles of National MSS., from William the Conqueror to Queen Anne*, published by the Master of the Rolls (1865-1868).
* *Facsimiles of National MSS. of Scotland*, published by the Lord Clerk Register (1867-1871).
* *Facsimiles of National MSS. of Ireland*, published by the Public Record Office, Ireland (1874-1884).

## Other Handbooks and Manuals of the Era
The term "handbook" was frequently used for instructional or reference texts across various disciplines during this period. In the field of philosophy, Henry Calderwood, a Scottish philosopher and divine who was made LL.D. of Glasgow in 1865, authored *A Handbook of Moral Philosophy*. In the realm of linguistics and literature, American scholar Hiram Corson wrote a *Hand-Book of Anglo-Saxon and Early English* (1871) and *An Elocutionary Manual* (1864). 

Reference works also appeared in specialized regional and technical studies. For example, J. H. Collins published a *Handbook to the Mineralogy of Cornwall and Devon* in 1871. In the banking sector, J. George Kiddy produced *The Country Banker's Handbook* (4th edition, 1903).
