# Board of Control for India

The Board of Control for India was a British administrative body established in 1784. It functioned as part of the home government's mechanism for managing colonial affairs and overseeing the administration of India.

## Establishment and Early Administration
The Board of Control was created in 1784 under the leadership of William Pitt. Its establishment occurred during a period of shifting colonial policy; previously, the "Council of Trade and Plantations" had managed colonial affairs until it was suppressed in 1782. Following this, there was a reaction against the liberal system of colonial self-government, and an attempt was made to govern colonies simply as dependencies. 

During the late 18th century, the presidency of the Board of Control was held by various statesmen. Baron William Wyndham Grenville became president of the board around the time he was appointed secretary of state for the home department in 1789. He retained this post until 1793, even after being transferred to the foreign office in 1791.

## Later Leadership and Personnel
In the early 19th century, the Board of Control continued to be led by figures with specific administrative or regional experience. Robert Hobart, the 4th Earl of Buckinghamshire, served as president of the Board of Control from 1812 until his death on February 4, 1816. Hobart's appointment to this role was considered a result of his previous experience in India, where he had served as governor of Madras starting in 1793, though he was recalled in 1798 following serious differences with the governor-general of India, Sir John Shore (later Lord Teignmouth).

Additionally, Thomas Grenville served as president of the Board of Control for a few months during 1806 and 1807, while simultaneously serving as first lord of the admiralty.

## Abolition and Transition to the India Council
The Board of Control remained in place until 1858. At that time, the territories of the East India Company were completely transferred to the crown. Consequently, the Board of Control was abolished and replaced by the India Council. This new body was placed under the presidency of a secretary of state for India. A specific provision was included in the creation of the India Council to ensure that its members were not permitted to sit in parliament.

## Context of Colonial Governance
The creation and operation of the Board of Control existed alongside other developments in the British Empire. In 1791, shortly after the Board's creation, Pitt granted representative institutions to Canada, though the home government maintained responsible control. Similar institutions were later granted to South Africa and Australia. By 1840, Canada was granted fully responsible government, a status that was gradually extended to other colonies. By 1854, a separate secretary of state for the colonies was appointed, and the colonial office was established on its modern footing.
