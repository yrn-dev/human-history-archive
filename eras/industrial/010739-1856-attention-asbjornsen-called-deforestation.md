# Peter Christen Asbjornsen

## Life and Early Work
Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812–1885) was a collector of Norwegian folklore. Born in Christiania on January 15, 1812, he was a member of an ancient family from the Gudbrandsdal, a lineage believed to have ended with his death. Asbjornsen's pursuit of folklore began early; by 1832, at the age of twenty, he had started writing down and collecting legends and fairy stories. He later expanded these collections by traveling on foot throughout Norway.

Asbjornsen entered the university as a student in 1833. Professionally, he became a zoologist and conducted a series of investigating voyages along the Norwegian coasts, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord, supported by the university.

## Partnership with Jorgen Moe
Asbjornsen's life's work was closely linked with that of Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813–1882). The two had known each other since Moe was fourteen years old, establishing a close friendship that lasted until their deaths. In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had also independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. This discovery led the two friends to compare their findings and decide to work together.

While Asbjornsen focused on zoology, Moe left Christiania University in 1839 to study theology and worked as a tutor in Christiania. During his holidays, Moe collected stories while wandering through remote districts and mountains.

## Literary Contributions
The collaboration between Asbjornsen and Moe resulted in the publication of *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*, the first installment of which appeared in 1842–1843. This work was recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology. A second volume followed in 1844, and a further collection was published in 1871. These stories were later translated into English by Sir George Dasent in 1859.

In addition to their joint efforts, Asbjornsen published a separate collection of Norwegian fairy tales, titled *huldreeventyr og folkesagn*, in 1845.

## Environmental Advocacy
In 1856, Asbjornsen's attention was drawn to the issue of deforestation in Norway. Following this, he induced the government to take up the matter.
