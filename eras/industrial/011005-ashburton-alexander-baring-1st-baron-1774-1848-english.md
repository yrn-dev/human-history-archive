# ASHBURTON, ALEXANDER BARING, 1ST BARON

Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton (1774–1848), was an English financier and politician. He was the second son of Harriet, daughter of William Herring, and Sir Francis Baring, the founder of the banking house Baring Brothers & Co. Born on October 27, 1774, Baring was raised within his father's business.

## Financial Career and American Connections
Baring's early professional life involved being sent to the United States by his father. While there, he married Anne, the daughter of William Bingham, a United States senator and wealthy resident of Philadelphia. This marriage allowed Baring to form wide connections with American houses and secured him considerable influence within the American commercial community. Following the death of his father in 1810, Alexander Baring became the head of the Baring Brothers firm.

## Political Career
Baring served in parliament for several decades, representing various constituencies between 1806 and 1835, including Taunton (1806–1826), Callington (1826–1831), Thetford (1831–1832), and North Essex (1832–1835). In his political approach, he viewed matters from the perspective of a businessman. His parliamentary record includes opposition to the restrictions on trade with the United States in 1812, the orders in council, and the 1826 act for the suppression of small bank-notes. He was also a strong antagonist of Reform.

In 1832, Baring accepted a position as chancellor of the exchequer in the projected ministry of the duke of Wellington. However, he later advised the recall of Lord Grey, stating that he "would face a thousand devils rather than such a House of Commons," having been alarmed by the scene in parliament. In 1834, during Sir Robert Peel's government, he served as master of the mint and president of the board of trade.

## Peerage and Later Public Life
On April 10, 1835, upon the retirement of Sir Robert Peel, Baring was created Baron Ashburton. He took this title, which had previously been held by John Dunning, the husband of his aunt. Despite his previous associations, Baring later disapproved of Peel's free-trade projects and opposed the Bank Charter Act of 1844.

Beyond his political and financial roles, Lord Ashburton was a privy councillor and a D.C.L. of Oxford. He also served as a trustee for the National Gallery and the British Museum. He published several speeches and a work titled *An En*.

## The Ashburton Treaty
In 1842, the British government despatched Baring to America as a commissioner. His appointment was based on his business capacity and his intimate acquaintance with American institutions and customs. He was tasked with negotiating disputes between the two countries, specifically the north-eastern boundary question.

That same year, he concluded the Webster-Ashburton treaty (also known as the Ashburton treaty) in Washington. The treaty established a compromise regarding the north-east boundary of Maine and fixed the frontier between Canada and Maine. Additionally, the treaty arranged for the extradition of certain criminals. The two governments also agreed to unite in efforts to persuade other powers to close slave markets within their territories, and each state agreed to maintain a squadron of at least eighty guns on the coast of Africa to suppress the slave trade.
