# Wilson Barrett

Wilson Barrett (1846–1904) was an English playwright, manager, and actor known for his work in melodrama and dramas of religious emotion. Born on February 18, 1846, in Essex to a farmer, Barrett began his professional stage career in 1864 at Halifax. His early career involved performing in the provinces, sometimes alongside his wife, Caroline Heath, in the production *East Lynne*.

## Managerial Career and Early Successes
Barrett gained managerial experience in Leeds and other locations before taking over the management of the old Court theatre in 1879. During his tenure there, he introduced Madame Modjeska to the London stage in several plays, including *Adrienne Lecouvreur*, *La Dame aux camelias*, and an adaptation of Schiller's *Maria Stuart*.

In 1881, Barrett took over the Princess's theatre, which brought him significant public recognition through the emotional drama *The Lights o' London*, written by G. R. Sims. However, it was the 1882 production of Henry Arthur Jones's *The Silver King* that established him as a favorite with the public. This production is regarded as perhaps the most successful melodrama ever staged. In this play, Barrett performed as Wilfred Denver, appearing alongside his brother George, a comedian, and E. S. Willard. The role of the "Spider" in this production was the part that first brought E. S. Willard to prominence. Barrett played his part in *The Silver King* for three hundred nights without a break.

## Later Works and International Tours
Following the success of *The Silver King*, Barrett appeared in W. G. Wills's *Claudian* in London and took on the role of Hamlet in 1884. Despite these ventures, he primarily returned to melodrama and spent much of his time acting in the provinces, though he maintained occasional seasons in London.

Barrett expanded his career internationally, making his first visit to America in 1886, with subsequent visits in later years. He also visited Australia in 1898.

As the London stage began to be influenced by new trends, Barrett's popularity in traditional melodrama declined. He found a new vein of success in 1895 with *The Sign of the Cross*, a drama of religious emotion. This play attracted audiences that largely existed outside the typical circle of playgoers. Barrett attempted to follow this success with other plays of a religious nature, but these did not achieve the same effect, and several of his later works were failures.

## Personal Characteristics and Death
Wilson Barrett is described as a robust actor of a sterling type with a striking physique. While he was not noted for intellectual finesse, he was an excellent performer. He died on July 22, 1904.
