# Perfect Confidence in Results

In the history of mathematical discovery and symbolic analysis, the capacity for absolute confidence in the outcomes of formal processes has been identified as a defining trait of certain eminent thinkers. Specifically, this characteristic is noted in the work of Boole, who contributed significantly to the fields of mathematics and the philosophy of the subject.

## The Mathematical Contributions of Boole

Boole completed two systematic treatises on mathematical subjects during his lifetime. The first, the *Treatise on Differential Equations*, was published in 1859. This was followed in the next year by the *Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences*, which was intended to serve as a sequel to the first work. These treatises are regarded as valuable contributions to their respective branches of mathematics, as Boole combined profound philosophical investigation with elementary exposition.

A significant portion of Boole's discoveries is embodied in these works. For example, the 16th and 17th chapters of the *Treatise on Differential Equations* provide a lucid account of a general method in analysis—originally described in a famous memoir printed in the *Philosophical Transactions* for 1844—and the general symbolic method.

## Symbolic Analysis and Confidence

Boole was among the most eminent figures to recognize that symbols of operation could be treated as distinct objects of calculation, separate from the symbols of quantity. His approach to mathematics was defined by two primary strengths:

1.  **Perfect Confidence:** His principal characteristic was a state of perfect confidence in any result that was obtained by treating symbols in accordance with their primary conditions and laws.
2.  **Analytical Skill:** He possessed an almost unrivalled power and skill in tracing out these results.

The bold and skilful employment of the general symbolic method was the catalyst for Boole's chief discoveries.
