# Women Weeding and Grandfather's Birthday

"Women Weeding" (1861) and "Grandfather's Birthday" (1862) are works by the French painter Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton.

## The Artist
Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton was born on May 1, 1827, in Courrières, Pas de Calais, France. His artistic talent became evident at a young age, leading him to study under the historical painter de Vigne in Ghent in 1843, and later under Baron Wappers in Antwerp in 1846. He eventually worked in Paris under Drolling. 

While Breton's initial artistic efforts focused on historical subjects—such as "Saint Piat preaching in Gaul" and "Misery and Despair," the latter created under the influence of the 1848 revolution—he eventually determined that he was not suited for historical painting. Consequently, he returned to the memories of the country and nature that had been impressed upon him during his youth.

## Artistic Style and Subjects
Breton became essentially a painter of rustic life. He focused primarily on the province of Artois, leaving the region only three times for short excursions: to Provence in 1864, and to Brittany in 1865 and 1873. His time in Brittany provided him with some of his most successful studies of religious scenes.

His body of work is generally categorized into four classes:
* Labour
* Rest
* Rural festivals
* Religious festivals

## Notable Works and Recognition
Breton began exhibiting his work at the Paris Salon and in Brussels, starting with "Return of the Harvesters" and "The Little Gleaner" in 1853. Over the following decades, he produced a wide array of paintings depicting rural and religious life. 

Among his significant works are:
* "The Day after St Sebastian's Day" (1855), which earned him a third-class medal.
* "Blessing the Fields" (1857), which earned him a second-class medal.
* "Erecting a Calvary" (1859), currently located in the Lille gallery.
* "The Return of the Gleaners" (1859), currently located in the Luxembourg.
* "Evening" and "Women Weeding" (1861), the latter of which earned him a first-class medal.
* "Grandfather's Birthday" (1862).
* "The Close of Day" (1865), "Harvest" (1867), and "Potato Gatherers" (1868).
* "A Pardon, Brittany" (1869).
* "The Fountain" (1872), for which he received a medal of honour.
* "The Bonfires of St John" (1875).
* "Women mending Nets" (1876), located in the Douai museum.
* "A Gleaner" (1877), located in the Luxembourg.
* "Evening, Finistère" (1881), "The Song of the Lark" (1884), "The Last Sunbeam" (1885), "The Shepherd's Star" (1888), "The Call Home" (1889), "The Last Gleanings" (1895), "Gathering Poppies" (1897), "The Alarm Cry" (1899), and "Twilight Glory" (1900).

In 1886, following the death of Baudry, Breton was elected to the Institut.
