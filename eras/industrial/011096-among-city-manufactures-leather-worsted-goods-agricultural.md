# Manufacturing of Leather and Agricultural Implements

Across various global regions, the production of leather and agricultural implements has served as a cornerstone of local industry, often coinciding with the development of commercial centers and seaports.

## North American Industrial Centers

In the United States, several cities have developed significant manufacturing bases for these goods. In Ashtabula, Ohio—a city situated on the Ashtabula river and Lake Erie—leather, worsted goods, and agricultural implements are listed among the city's manufactures, alongside machine shop and foundry products. By 1905, the total value of factory products in Ashtabula reached $1,895,454, representing a 114.3% increase over the previous five years.

Louisville, Kentucky, serves as a larger commercial and manufacturing hub due to its transport facilities and the richness of the surrounding country in iron, coal, timber, and agricultural produce. Its leather industry is specifically noted for the production of sole leather. Additionally, the city manufactures agricultural implements, with a particular emphasis on ploughs. Other related transport manufactures in Louisville include carriages, wagons (especially farm wagons), and the materials used to build them.

In Wisconsin, the city of Kenosha is known for its extensive sole-leather tannery. Leather and brass goods are listed among its manufactures, which also include automobiles, typewriters, mattresses, and brass and iron beds.

## European and Russian Production

In Europe, the manufacture of these goods is found in both specialized towns and broader regional industries. In Bohemia, the town of Kuttenberg (Kutná Hora) maintains an industry that includes the production of agricultural implements and leather goods, as well as brewing, sugar-refining, and the manufacture of woollen and cotton stuffs.

In Russia, the seaport of Mariupol, located on the north shore of the Sea of Azov, has inhabitants engaged in the manufacture of agricultural implements and leather, in addition to the production of bricks and iron goods. The city's economy is further supported by fishing, cattle-breeding, and agriculture.

On a broader regional scale, the area of Baden reports that its industrial output now equals or surpasses its agricultural industry. Leather is listed as one of the chief articles of manufacture for the region, alongside chemicals, glass, china, paper, tobacco, and machinery.

## Historical and Specialized Leatherwork

Certain cities have gained historical renown for specific types of leather production. Cordova was celebrated during the time of the Moors for exporting a peculiar kind of leather that gave the city its name and provided the origin for the word *cordwainer*. While the original Moorish leather handicraft almost disappeared by the 15th century, it is still often skillfully imitated today.

## Trade and Economic Context

The production of leather and agricultural machinery is often linked to the broader export and import economies of their respective regions. For instance, in certain areas, leather and hides are listed as principal exports alongside other goods such as silk, mohair, and cotton. In the South-west of the United States, Dallas, Texas, has established itself as the largest distributing point for agricultural machinery in the region, serving as a shipping center for areas raising cotton, fruit, and wheat.
