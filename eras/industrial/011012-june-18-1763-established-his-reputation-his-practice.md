# John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton

John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was a prominent English lawyer. Born on October 18, 1731, at Ashburton, Devonshire, he was the second son of an attorney, also named John Dunning. He received his early education at the free grammar school in his hometown.

## Legal Career and Rise to Reputation
Dunning's entry into the legal profession began with articles under his father. At the age of nineteen, he was admitted to the Middle Temple and was subsequently called to the bar in 1756. His early professional years were marked by a slow start; for several years, he worked the western circuit without receiving a single brief.

His trajectory changed in 1762 when he was hired to draft a defense for the British East India Company against the Dutch East India Company, which had submitted memorials to the crown regarding various grievances. The "masterly style" of this document provided Dunning with immediate emolument and reputation.

In 1763, Dunning further distinguished himself as counsel for Wilkes, managing the cause throughout. A pivotal moment in his career occurred on June 18, 1763, in the case of *Leach v. Money*. His powerful argument against the validity of general warrants in this case established his reputation, leading to a gradual increase in his practice. By 1776, it is reported that his annual income had reached nearly £10,000.

## Public Appointments and Political Life
Dunning's professional success led to several high-profile appointments. He was chosen as the recorder of Bristol in 1766 and was appointed solicitor-general in December 1767. He held the position of solicitor-general until May 1770, at which point he retired alongside his friend Lord Shelburne. In 1771, he was granted the freedom of the city of London.

Entering the political sphere, Dunning was first elected as the member for Calne in 1768, representing the borough until his promotion to the peerage. He became known as a regular member of the opposition and was recognized for delivering many able speeches in parliament.

## Parliamentary Contributions and Later Years
In 1780, Dunning introduced a motion asserting that the influence of the crown had increased, was continuing to increase, and ought to be diminished; this motion passed by a majority of eighteen. He was a strong opponent of the system of pensions and sinecure officers. However, the archive notes that his probity was not sufficient to prevent him from benefiting from this system himself. In 1782, following the appointment of the marquis of Rockingham as prime minister, Dunning was appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster, which was a rich sinecure. Around this same time, he was advanced to the peerage.
