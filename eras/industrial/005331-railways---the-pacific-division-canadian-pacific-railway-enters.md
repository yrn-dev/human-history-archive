# Railways

## The Canadian Pacific Railway in British Columbia
The Canadian Pacific railway's Pacific division enters British Columbia from the east through the Rocky Mountains. From this point of entry, the line runs for approximately 500 miles across the province before reaching its terminus at Vancouver. The construction of this transcontinental road was a critical political issue; the colony of British Columbia entered the confederation in 1871 under the condition that the railway be constructed speedily. When this promise was not fulfilled by 1876, relations between British Columbia and the government at Ottawa became strained.

The main line of the Canadian Pacific railway traverses the Rocky Mountains via the Kicking Horse (or Wapta) Pass, which sits at an elevation of 5,300 feet. In addition to the main line, a southern branch leaves the railway at Medicine Hat and runs south-west. This branch crosses the Rocky Mountains through the Crow's Nest Pass (5,500 feet), entering British Columbia a short distance north of the United States boundary. This line continues across the province, running roughly parallel to the boundary as far as Midway in the Boundary district. This specific branch has opened up productive mining districts and extensive coal fields.

The railway's impact on the region has been significant. The city of Vancouver was founded by the Canadian Pacific Railway Company, and the city of Calgary was created by the building of the railway around 1883. Calgary serves as an important point on the line and is the residence of a general superintendent.

## Regional Railway Infrastructure
By 1905, the province of British Columbia contained 1,627 miles of railway, with 1,187 miles owned or controlled by the Canadian Pacific railway. Other rail services in the province include:
* **Vancouver Island:** Two railways operate here: the 78-mile Esquimalt & Nanaimo railway, which connects southern ports with coal fields, and the Victoria & Sydney railway, which is about 16 miles long.
* **Southern British Columbia:** The Great Northern operates several short lines in the southern portion of the province that connect to its system in the United States.
* **Other Projects:** The Yellow Head Pass (3,733 feet) is expected to be used by the Grand Trunk Pacific railway.

The introduction of railways has diminished the commercial importance of the province's rivers. While rivers such as the Fraser, Columbia, and Skeena exist, they are often interrupted by rapids, making them indifferent as means of communication for commercial purposes compared to railway lines.

## Railway Hubs and Systems in the United States
In the United States, several cities and states serve as major railway centers:
* **Denver, Colorado:** Known as the "Queen City of the Plains," Denver is a major railway center served by nine railways. The primary lines include the Union Pacific, the Denver & Rio Grande, the Atchison, Topeka & Santa Fé, the Chicago, Burlington & Quincy, the Chicago, Rock Island & Pacific, and the Denver, North-Western & Pacific.
* **Louisiana:** By the end of 1908, the state had 4,943.55 miles of railway. Major roads include the Southern Pacific, the Missouri Pacific, the Texas & Pacific, the Kansas City Southern, the Illinois Central, and the Vicksburg, Shreveport & Pacific, among others. The Illinois Central provided the first northern connection for Louisiana and is vital to the trade of New Orleans.
* **Other States:** In another unnamed state, railway mileage grew from 206 miles in 1880 to 2,022.04 miles by January 1909. This region is served by the Northern Pacific, the Great Northern, the Oregon Railway & Navigation, the Oregon Short Line, and the Union Pacific.

## International and Industrial Connections
Railways often intersect with other forms of transport and industry. The Canadian Pacific Railway Company operates mail steamer lines from Victoria and Vancouver, including the Empress line to China and Japan and an Australian line serving Sydney, Fiji, and Honolulu.

In England, the London & North-Western railway serves the urban district of Denton in Lancashire, an area where coal is extensively mined and felt hat manufacture is the leading industry.

## Historical Development and Advocacy
The resolve to construct the Canadian Pacific railway was largely due to the energy and optimism of Sir Georges Etienne Cartier. A Canadian statesman and former joint prime minister (1858–1862), Cartier was a strong advocate for the construction of railways and also played a major role in the success of the Grand Trunk railway.
