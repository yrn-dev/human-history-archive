# The Forth Bridge

The Forth Bridge is a cantilever bridge constructed across the Forth, situated at the only narrowing of the waterway within a distance of 50 miles. At this specific location, the channel is approximately one mile in width and is divided by the island of Inchgarvie.

## Design and Construction
The bridge was begun in 1882 and reached completion in 1889. While the original design for the structure was a stiffened suspension bridge, this plan was abandoned following the fall of the Tay bridge in 1879. 

The final construction utilized 38,000 tons of steel, a figure that does not include the approach viaducts. The bridge features an internal viaduct composed of lattice girders, which serves to carry a double line of rails.

## Technical Specifications
The total length of the cantilever bridge is 5,330 feet. This length is comprised of the following components:
*   A central tower located on Inchgarvie measuring 260 feet.
*   Piers at Queensferry and Fife, each measuring 145 feet.
*   Two central girders positioned between cantilevers, each measuring 350 feet.
*   Six cantilevers, each measuring 680 feet.

The bridge consists of two main spans, each 1,710 feet in length. The extreme height of the towers above high water is 361 feet, providing a clear headway of 157 feet.

## Engineering and Stability
To ensure structural balance, the outer ends of the shore cantilevers are loaded to balance 200 tons in addition to the rolling load and half the weight of the central girder. 

The engineering of the bridge includes specific provisions to account for environmental factors and physical stresses, including:
*   Longitudinal expansion resulting from changes in temperature.
*   Distortion caused by the sun acting on one side of the structure.
*   The effects of wind acting on one side of the bridge.
