# John Romeyn Brodhead

## Early Life and Education
John Romeyn Brodhead was an American historical scholar born on January 2, 1814, in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. He was the son of Jacob Brodhead (1782-1855), who was a prominent clergyman within the Dutch Reformed Church. Brodhead pursued his higher education at Rutgers College, from which he graduated in 1831. Following his graduation, he moved to New York City, where he was admitted to the bar in 1835.

## Colonial History Research
After 1837, Brodhead shifted his primary focus toward the study of American colonial history. To facilitate his research into the early Dutch settlements in America, he secured an appointment in 1839 as an attaché of the American legation at the Hague. While stationed there, his investigations revealed that the Dutch archives contained a wealth of material regarding the early history of New York.

These findings prompted the state legislature to provide funds for the systematic collection of transcripts of documents related to New York from various archives across Europe. In 1841, Governor William H. Seward appointed Brodhead to lead this effort. Over the course of several years, Brodhead gathered approximately eighty manuscript volumes of transcriptions from Holland, France, and England. Many of these documents had not previously been utilized by historians. These materials were later edited by Berthold Fernow (volumes xii through xv) and Edward O'Callaghan (volumes i through xi) and were published by the state between 1853 and 1883 under the title *Documents relating to the Colonial History of New York*, totaling 15 volumes.

## Diplomatic and Public Service
Brodhead held several official positions throughout his career. Between 1846 and 1849, he served as the secretary of legation under George Bancroft, who was then the minister to Great Britain. Later, from 1853 to 1857, he served as the naval officer of the port of New York.

## Publications and Later Life
In addition to his work gathering colonial records, Brodhead published several addresses. He is also the author of a scholarly work titled *History of the State of New York*, published in two volumes between 1853 and 1871. This work covers the period from 1609 to 1690 and is generally regarded as the best history for that specific timeframe.

John Romeyn Brodhead died in New York City on May 6, 1873.
