# London & North-Western Railway and Bletchley

## The Bletchley Junction
Bletchley serves as an important junction on the system of the London & North-Western railway. From this central point, several railway branches diverge. To the east, lines lead to Cambridge, Bedford, and Fenny Stratford. To the west, branches extend toward Banbury and Oxford, with the western branch also providing service to Buckingham.

## Connection to Banbury
The town of Banbury, a municipal borough and market-town in Oxfordshire, England, is connected to Bletchley via a branch line of the London & North-Western railway. Banbury is situated on the river Cherwell and the Oxford canal, located 86 miles north-west of London. In addition to the London & North-Western connection, Banbury is served by the northern line of the Great Western railway and branch lines of the Great Central railway, which connect to the main line at Woodford.

## Banbury's Economy and Industry
Banbury is the centre of a rich agricultural district. This agricultural focus is reflected in the town's industry, which includes a large manufacture of agricultural implements. Other local industries include brewing as well as leather and rope works. The town is also known for Banbury cakes—pastry cases containing a mixture of currants—which have maintained a reputation for three centuries.

## Regional Railway Infrastructure
The broader railway network in the region involves several intersecting systems. The Metropolitan-Great Central joint line serves Aylesbury, Chesham (via a branch), and Amersham, eventually joining the North-Western Oxford branch at Verney Junction. This joint line is utilized by the Great Central railway, whose main line continues north-westward from Quainton Road. 

Other regional connections include a branch from Cheddington to Aylesbury and a light railway connecting Quainton Road to the large village of Brill to the south-west. Furthermore, the Great Central and Great Western companies jointly own a line passing through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield.
