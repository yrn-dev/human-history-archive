# Bierbrauerei and the Works of J.E. Thausing

## Overview of Brewing Literature
The study of brewing and malting at the turn of the 20th century was supported by a variety of technical manuals and scientific texts. Among the notable German contributions to this field was the work *Grundriss der Bierbrauerei*, published in Berlin in 1904 by E.J. Lintner. Additionally, J.E. Thausing contributed to the technical literature of the era with the publication *Die Theorie und Praxis der Malzbereitung und Bierfabrikation*, which was released in Leipzig in 1898.

## Technical Resources and Journals
Beyond individual textbooks, the brewing industry relied on a network of technical journals to disseminate information. In Germany, these included the Berlin-based *Wochenschrift für Brauerei* and the Munich-based *Zeitschrift für das gesammte Brauwesen*. These publications complemented English-language resources such as *The Brewers' Journal* (published in both London and New York), *The Brewing Trade Review* (London), and *The Journal of the Institute of Brewing* (London).

## Comparative Brewing Science
The scientific approach to brewing was further detailed in other contemporary texts, such as E. Michel's *Lehrbuch der Bierbrauerei* (Augsburg, 1900) and E. Prior's *Chemie u. Physiologie des Malzes und des Bieres* (Leipzig, 1896). English-language authorities on the subject included Julian L. Baker's *The Brewing Industry* (1905), Frank Thatcher's *Brewing and Malting* (1898), H.E. Wright's *A Handy Book for Brewers* (1897), and W.J. Sykes' *Principles and Practice of Brewing* (1897), as well as the work *A Text-book of the Science of Brewing* (1891) by Moritz and Morris.

## Industry Standards and Practices
Historical records indicate specific standards regarding the classification of materials used in the industry. In 1896, classifications were set at 28 lb, but this standard was updated in 1897 to a rate of 32 lb to the bushel. In certain contexts, these figures were inclusive of maize and rice, while in others, they were exclusive of these ingredients. Regarding the physical process of production, it is noted that, as a general rule, there is no separate "collecting vessel," as duty is instead assessed within the fermenting vessels.
