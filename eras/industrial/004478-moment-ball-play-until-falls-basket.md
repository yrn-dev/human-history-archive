# Basket-ball

Basket-ball is a fast-paced game characterized by a high degree of team-play and combination. It is regarded as excellent winter training for outdoor games and is extremely popular with young women, though the rules for women are somewhat modified.

## Court and Equipment
The game is played on a marked-off space measuring 40 ft. by 60 ft., although dimensions may be greater when played in the open air. The goal consists of a basket—a net suspended from a metal ring 18 in. in diameter—placed 10 ft. above the floor or ground in the middle of each short side. This ring is backed by a back-board measuring 6 ft. long and 4 ft. high, positioned at a distance of 6 in. from the ring. The ball used is leather-covered, inflated, and 30 in. in circumference.

## Gameplay and Objectives
The objective of the game is to propel the ball into the opponents' basket by striking it with the open hands. A match consists of two periods of play separated by an interval of rest; the side that scores the most goals wins.

The game begins with the two opposing centres standing within a 4-foot ring in the middle of the floor. The referee tosses the ball into the air over their heads, and the centres jump for possession or attempt to bat the ball toward the opposing goal. Once the referee puts the ball in play, it remains in play until one of three things occurs: it falls into a basket, it passes the boundary-lines, or a foul is made. After a goal is scored, the referee puts the ball back in play at the centre.

If the ball is thrown across the boundary, a player from the opposing side stands on the line at the point of exit and puts the ball in play by throwing or passing it to a teammate in any direction. There is no off-side rule, a point of similarity to the game of lacrosse. Opponents attempt to intercept these throws or prevent the pass to secure the ball.

## Rules and Scoring
Players must throw the ball from the spot where they receive it. Running with the ball is prohibited, except when the player is continuously bounding it on the floor. A player may interfere with an opponent who possesses the ball, provided they use only one arm and do not hold the opponent.

Scoring is divided into two categories:
* **In-play goals:** A goal scored while the ball is in play counts for two points.
* **Free throws:** A goal scored from a free throw counts for one point.

A "free throw" is awarded to a player of the opposing side when a foul has been called. The throw is taken from a mark 15 ft. distant from the basket without interference. Fouls include hacking, kicking, holding, and striking.

## Team Structure and Officiating
While there is practically no limit to the number of players on a side, indoor matches are played by teams of five. These players occupy positions that oppose one another, similar to lacrosse: two guards (or backs) on the right and left, and forwards on the right, left, and centre.

The game is managed by several officials:
* **Referee:** Provides general supervision and decides if goals have been properly scored.
* **Umpire:** Watches for infringements of the rules (fouls).
* **Scorer and Timekeeper:** Maintain the game's record and duration.

The principal qualifications for a successful player include endurance, coolness, accuracy, self-control, and quickness of judgment and movement. Important requisites for the game include passing, throwing, dodging, and team-play.
