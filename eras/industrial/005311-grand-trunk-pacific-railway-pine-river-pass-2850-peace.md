# Grand Trunk Pacific Railway and the Geography of the Peace Region

## The Grand Trunk Pacific Railway
The Grand Trunk Pacific railway is a projected rail line in Canada. Its planned route is situated near the Bulkley Valley, south of the Skeena river, an area characterized by immense beds of semi-anthracite and high-grade bituminous coal. The railway's Pacific terminus is intended to be located in the vicinity of Port Simpson. Meteorological data for Port Simpson indicates a mean annual temperature of 45.1° Fahr., with the warmest month being August (56.9°) and the coldest being January (34.9°).

To traverse the Rocky Mountains, the Grand Trunk Pacific railway will probably utilize the Yellow Head Pass, which stands at an elevation of 3,733 ft.

## Mountain Passes and Elevations
The Rocky Mountains, composed primarily of Carboniferous to Cambrian palaeozoic sediments with subordinate Cretaceous areas containing coal, feature several passes. In addition to the Yellow Head Pass, these include:
*   **The Pine River Pass:** Located at an elevation of 2,850 ft.
*   **The Peace River Pass:** Located at 2,000 ft., serving as the passage through which the Peace river flows.
*   **Other notable passes:** The South Kootenay or Boundary Pass (7,100 ft.), the Crow's Nest Pass (5,500 ft.), the Kicking Horse or Wapta Pass (5,300 ft.), and the Athabasca Pass (6,025 ft.).

The range reaches its highest Canadian peak at Mount Robson, which rises to 13,700 ft., though the highest peak in British Columbia is Mount Fairweather at 15,287 ft.

## The Peace River System
The Peace river is a major tributary of the Mackenzie river. It originates in the mountains of British Columbia under the name of the Finlay, flowing south-east and north-east through a great intermontane valley that bounds the Rocky Mountains to the west. After its confluence with the Parsnip, the combined waters are known as the Peace. 

The river flows east through the Rocky Mountains and then north-east to join the river discharging the waters of Lake Athabasca; from that point to Great Slave Lake, the system is known as the Slave river. The Peace is navigable for approximately 220 m. from its junction with the Slave river up to Vermilion Falls.

The Peace river system includes several principal tributaries:
*   North Pine and South Pine rivers
*   Omineca, Nation, Parsnip, Halfway, Smoky, Battle, and Loon rivers

Including the Peace, the total length of the Mackenzie river system is approximately 2,350 m.
