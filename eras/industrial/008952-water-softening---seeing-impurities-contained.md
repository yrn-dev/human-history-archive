# Water-softening

Water-softening is a purification process used to treat feed-water before it is pumped into a boiler. This process is necessary because impurities contained in the feed-water do not evaporate in the steam; instead, they become concentrated in the boiler water.

## Effects of Impurities in Boilers
Impurities in feed-water typically precipitate within the boiler, appearing either as mud or as scale that forms on heating surfaces. While some mud and soluble impurities can be removed using blow-off cocks, scale requires periodical cleaning to be removed. 

The presence of incrustations on heating surfaces creates two primary issues:
1. **Efficiency Loss:** They obstruct the transmission of heat through the tubes and plates, thereby lessening the efficiency of the boiler.
2. **Structural Danger:** If incrustations become excessive, they can cause plates to become weakened by overheating, which presents a considerable danger.

Because of these risks, when feed-water is very impure, certain boiler types that allow for easy cleaning—specifically the Cornish, Galloway, and Lancashire types—are used. Multitubular or water-tube boilers are excluded in such cases because thorough cleaning is more difficult in those designs.

## The Softening Process
To avoid the problems associated with "hard" feed-water, the water is passed through a "softener." Most impurities in ordinary feed-water consist of magnesia salts or lime, which are soluble in cold water but significantly less soluble in hot water.

The softening process involves the following steps:
* **Mixing:** Measured quantities of chemical reagents are thoroughly mixed with the feed-water. The specific kind and quantity of chemicals used are determined by the amount and nature of the impurities present.
* **Heating:** The temperature of the mixture is raised using other means or exhaust steam.
* **Precipitation:** This combination of chemicals and heat causes most of the impurity to precipitate. Any remaining impurities are converted into salts that are more soluble, allowing them to remain in solution in the boiler until they are blown out.
* **Filtration:** The water is filtered before it is finally pumped into the boiler.

## Related Systems for Impurity Management
In some instances, "thermal storage" is utilized to manage feed-water. This involves placing a large cylindrical storage vessel above the boiler. Feed-water is delivered into the upper part of the vessel and runs over extended surfaces exposed to steam, raising its temperature to that of the steam. This system allows for the accumulation of hot water during periods of low steam demand, which can then be fed into the boiler during periods of maximum output. Additionally, many feed impurities become deposited in this storage chamber, where they are readily removable and comparatively harmless.
