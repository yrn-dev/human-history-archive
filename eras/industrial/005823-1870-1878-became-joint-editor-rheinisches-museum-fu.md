# Events and Appointments of 1870

The year 1870 marked several significant transitions in academia, journalism, politics, and professional administration across Europe and North America.

## Academic and Educational Developments
In the field of classical scholarship, Franz Bücheler, a German scholar educated at Bonn, began a professorship at Bonn in 1870. Bücheler had previously held professorships at Freiburg in 1858 and Greifswald in 1866. He was noted for his success as both a commentator and teacher, producing various editions including *Frontini de aquis urbis Romae* and *Q. Ciceronis reliquiae*.

In the United States, Seth Low graduated from Columbia University in 1870. Following his graduation, he began working as a clerk in 1870 for A. A. Low & Brothers, a tea and silk-importing house owned by his father. Low would later serve as the president of Columbia University from 1890 to 1901 and as the mayor of New York City from 1901 to 1903.

Additionally, the Rev. Andrew Hollingsworth Frost served as the Principal of the Church Missionary College in Islington starting in 1870, a position he held until 1874.

## Journalism and Editorial Appointments
The year 1870 saw several changes in the leadership of various publications. John Doran, an English author and translator, became the editor of *Notes and Queries* in 1870. This appointment followed a brief period in 1869 where he served as the editor of *The Athenaeum* after succeeding Hepworth Dixon.

In the religious press, Edward Beecher became an associate editor of the *Christian Union* in 1870. Beecher, who had previously served as the president of Illinois College and held various pastorates, had also been the senior editor of the *Congregationalist* between 1849 and 1855.

## Political and Diplomatic Activity
In Canada, the year 1870 represented a turning point for statesman Sir Richard John Cartwright. By 1870, the breach between Cartwright and the Conservative party—which he had entered in 1863—became complete. This conflict had begun after the federation of 1867, stemming from Cartwright's view that the party's financial policy was extravagant. Following this separation, he later became the finance minister of the Liberal ministry under the Hon. Alexander Mackenzie in 1873.

## International Correspondence
During 1870 and 1871, James Macdonell, a British journalist, served as a special correspondent in France. Macdonell had previously worked for the *Daily Telegraph* from 1865 to 1875 before becoming a leader-writer for *The Times* in 1873.
