# Manufactures

The development of manufacturing industries varies significantly across global regions, ranging from the transition of domestic crafts to large-scale industrialization and the specialization of cities in specific staples.

## Textile Industries
Textile production is a dominant feature in several industrial centers. In Bombay, the growth and manufacture of cotton is the chief feature of modern industrial life. The industry is centered in Bombay City and Island, which houses nearly two-thirds of the mills. By 1905, there were 432 factories in the presidency, most engaged in cotton. While the period between 1891 and 1901 saw a depression caused by famine and plague—which disorganized labor and caused a population exodus—the trade later expanded with improved goods. In 1901, the cotton industry employed 131,796 people. Additionally, the province had 178,000 hand-loom weavers as of 1901. Silk goods are also produced in Bombay, Thana, Nasik, Yeola, Surat, and Ahmedabad, though competition from European goods has caused most branches of this industry to decline.

In Dundee, jute is the primary industry, with 300,000 tons of raw material imported annually from India. Dundee merchants also own many factories in Bengal. The city's textile output includes carpets, sackings, sheetings, ropes, canvas (largely for the British navy), ducks, and drills. Linen is another staple industry in Dundee; the first significant work, Bell Mill, was built in 1806, followed by the first power-loom factory in 1836. The jute, flax, and hemp industries combined employ approximately 50,000 workers with an annual output value exceeding £6,000,000.

In Belgium, the cloth industry faced a crisis that led to the rise of the linen industry in Flanders and Brabant. As large-scale medieval manufacturing forms became unsustainable, linen production shifted to the homes of operatives in the country. By 1480, the cloth industry sought to survive in Verviers, near Liège. In Cordova, modern industries include the manufacture of linen, silken, and woollen goods.

## Specialized Crafts and Regional Manufactures
Various regions are noted for specific artisanal and industrial products:
*   **Wood-work and Ornaments:** Ahmedabad and Surat are famous for carved wood-work, with elaborate carving found on houses in Ahmedabad and examples in Baroda, Nasik, Yeola, Surat, and Broach. In the Black Forest, inhabitants are celebrated for making musical boxes, organs, toys, and wooden ornaments.
*   **Metal and Jewelry:** In Bombay, goldsmiths are employed to create gold and silver ornaments using metal supplied by customers. Cordova was historically celebrated for silversmiths from Damascus and continues to produce fine silver filigree.
*   **Leather:** Cordova was famous for a specific leather that gave rise to the word "cordwainer," though this handicraft nearly disappeared in the 15th century. Leather manufacture is also a prominent industry in Autun and Dundee.
*   **Other Goods:** Salt is produced in large quantities at government works in Udu and Kharaghoda in Ahmedabad for export to central India and Gujarat. Autun engages in marble-working, metal-founding, furniture and machinery manufacture, and oil extraction from bituminous schist. Baden produces chemicals, glass, china, tobacco, paper, silk ribbons, cotton and woollen goods, machinery, clocks, and jewelry, as well as beet sugar.

## Industrialization in Japan and Birmingham
Japan has experienced a remarkable shift from a purely domestic manufacturing character before the Meiji era to a modern industrial state. While agriculture—specifically intensive rice cultivation—remains vital, manufacturing has grown rapidly. Japan now has approximately 11,000 factories employing 700,000 operatives and 9,000 joint-stock companies. Despite the advantage of easily trained labor and proximity to Oriental markets, Japan faces challenges including labor that is less efficient than that of America and Europe, and wages that have nearly doubled in the last fifteen years.

In Birmingham, the industrial structure is characterized by a numerous class of rich men who are actively engaged in trade or manufactures. The "system of small masters" persists in Birmingham's manufactures, which has resulted in infrequent strikes and disputes between capital and labor. The city is also noted for the housing of its workmen; it was the birthplace of freehold land and building societies. A well-known example of industrial housing is Bourneville, occupied by employees of the chocolate manufacturers, Messrs Cadbury.

## Miscellaneous Industries
Other industrial activities include the production of explosives, such as mercuric fulminate used as a detonator. In Dundee, the city is celebrated for preserves and confectionery, particularly marmalade, and is involved in shipbuilding, engineering, tanning, breweries, and the construction of motor-cars. In Bombay, besides cotton, there are workshops and flour mills to meet local needs, and a brewery exists at Dapuri near Poona.
