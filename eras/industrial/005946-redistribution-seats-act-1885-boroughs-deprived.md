# Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885

The Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885 was a legislative measure that significantly altered the parliamentary representation of various boroughs and counties across England. The Act resulted in the disfranchisement of numerous boroughs, the reduction of members for others, and the reorganization of county divisions.

## Impact on Borough Representation

The Act of 1885 led to the total loss of independent parliamentary representation for several boroughs. In Dorsetshire, the remaining boroughs were disfranchised by the Act, following the earlier disfranchisement of Corfe Castle in 1832 and Lyme Regis in 1868. In Buckinghamshire, the boroughs were similarly deprived of representation.

Individual towns also saw their representation end or change under the Act:
* **Buckingham:** Having returned two members from 1545 onwards, the borough was deprived of one member by the Representation of the People Act of 1867 and lost its final member through the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885.
* **Banbury:** This borough had been represented by one member from the date of Queen Mary's charter until the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885.
* **Aylesbury:** The borough returned two members to parliament from 1554 until the Redistribution Act of 1885.
* **Barnstaple:** A royal borough that had sent two members to parliament since 1295, Barnstaple saw its representation merged into that of the county in 1885.
* **Darlington, Stockton, and Hartlepool:** These three boroughs each returned one member from 1868 until the Redistribution Act of 1885.

In Cornwall, the Act ended the separate representation of several boroughs that had survived previous reforms. Until 1885, Bodmin, Helston, Launceston, Liskeard, and St Ives were separately represented, while Truro and Penryn with Falmouth each returned two members.

## Changes to County Divisions and Member Counts

The Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885 reorganized how counties returned members to parliament, often shifting power from specific boroughs to broader county divisions.

In Buckinghamshire, the loss of borough representation was accompanied by a change in county returns; the county returned three members for three divisions. In Kent, the Act increased the county's representation to eight members across eight divisions. However, the representation for the boroughs of Canterbury, Maidstone, and Rochester was reduced to one member each.

In Cornwall, the Act significantly reduced the density of representation. In one area near Liskeard, which had been represented by nine parliamentary boroughs returning eighteen members prior to 1832, only one member remained after 1885.

## Related Legislative Context

The 1885 Act followed a series of previous reforms that gradually reduced the number of "rotten boroughs" and shifted representative rights. For example, in Cornwall, the number of members had reached forty-four between 1584 and 1821. The Reform Act of 1832 had already removed fourteen Cornish boroughs, including Bossiney, Camelford, Lostwithiel, East Looe, West Looe, and Fowey.

In other regions, such as Durham, the movement for representation had been historically contested. The county and city of Durham, along with the borough of Barnard Castle, sought representation in 1614, though the county was not first summoned to return members until 1654. By 1832, the boroughs of South Shields, Sunderland, and Gateshead had acquired representation.
