# Sabine Baring-Gould and Selected Works

Sabine Baring-Gould (1834– ) was an English novelist and clergyman born in Exeter on January 28, 1834. His academic background included graduation from Clare College, Cambridge, followed by several years of travel. 

## Ecclesiastical Career
Baring-Gould entered the ministry in 1864, serving as the curate of Horbury, Yorkshire. In 1867, he became the perpetual curate of Dalton in the same county. By 1871, he had moved to Essex to serve as the rector of East Mersea. Following the death of his father in 1872, Baring-Gould inherited the Lew Trenchard estate in North Devon, a location where his family had been established for nearly three centuries. In 1881, he exchanged his living in Essex for the rectory of Lew Trenchard.

## Literary Contributions
Baring-Gould was a prolific writer with a "ready pen," publishing works starting in 1854. His writing spanned a diverse range of subjects, including religion, mythology, history, travel, folk-lore, and fiction. 

In the realm of fiction, he authored several novels, including:
* *Mehalah* (1880), which is set on the east coast of England.
* *John Herring* (1883), a story centered on the west country.
* *Court Royal* (1886).
* *Red Spider* (1887).
* *The Pennycomequicks* (1889).
* *Cheap Jack Zita* (1893).
* *Broom Squire* (1896), which is a tale of Sussex.

Beyond his novels, Baring-Gould contributed to the study of antiquities, topography, and folk-lore. While these works were written for a popular audience, they were characterized by real learning and serious research. Notable examples include *Book of Were-wolves* (1865), *Curious Myths of the Middle Ages* (1866), and *Curious Survivals* (1892).

## Other Works and Editorial Roles
In addition to his research and fiction, Baring-Gould produced numerous volumes of popular theology and sermons. Between 1871 and 1873, he served as the editor of *The Sacristy*, which was a quarterly review dedicated to ecclesiastical literature and art.
