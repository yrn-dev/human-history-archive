# E.B. Eastwick

E.B. Eastwick was a translator active during the mid-19th century, known for his work in bringing significant linguistic and literary texts from Oriental and comparative grammar sources into the English language.

## Translation of Bopp's Comparative Grammar
Eastwick is noted for his 1845 English translation of the *Comparative Grammar* authored by Bopp. The original work, titled *Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen*, was published in six parts between 1833 and 1852. 

The objective of Bopp's research in this text was threefold: to investigate the origin of grammatical forms, to trace phonetic laws, and to provide a description of the original grammatical structure of the languages based on their intercomparison. The first two goals served the third. Bopp's work was preceded by a series of monographs in the *Transactions of the Berlin Academy* from 1824 to 1831, titled *Vergleichende Zergliederung des Sanskrits und der mit ihm verwandten Sprachen*. While Eastwick's translation appeared in 1845, a subsequent second German edition of the work was produced between 1856 and 1861; this revised edition was thoroughly revised and expanded to include Old-Armenian.

## Translation of the Anvar-i Suheili
In addition to his work on comparative grammar, E.B. Eastwick translated the *Anvar-i Suheili*. This work is a Persian version of the Fables (associated with the *Pancha Tantra* and the *Kalilah and Dimna*). Eastwick's translation of the *Anvar-i Suheili* was published in Hertford in 1854.
