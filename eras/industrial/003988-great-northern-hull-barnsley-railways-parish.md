# Great Northern and Hull & Barnsley Railways in Barnsley

## Railway Infrastructure and Connectivity
The town of Barnsley, a municipal borough and market town located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England, is served by several railway lines. Among these are the Great Northern and the Hull & Barnsley railways, which operate alongside the Midland, Great Central, and Lancashire & Yorkshire railways. In addition to these railway communications, the town maintains connectivity via the Aire and Calder Navigation system of canals.

## Geographic and Administrative Context
Barnsley is situated on rising ground to the west of the river Dearne, approximately 15 miles north of Sheffield. Administratively, the town is located in the parish of Silkstone. This parish is also the namesake for important collieries in the area. The borough covers an area of 2,385 acres and is governed by a mayor, eighteen councillors, and six aldermen.

## Industrial Importance and Trade
The presence of railway infrastructure supports the town's significant industrial output. Barnsley is characterized by large coalfields that extend directly under the town; coal and coke from these fields are exported in large quantities to Hull and London. 

The town's primary industries include the weaving of other cloth and linen, as well as the manufacture of steel and iron. Other industrial activities carried out in the borough include:
* The manufacture of wire, needles, and glass.
* The operation of cornmills, sawmills, and malt-houses.
* The operation of dyeworks, printfields, and bleachfields.

The importance of the town grew significantly following the introduction of linen manufacture in 1744, which superseded the earlier chief industry of wire-drawing that had begun to decline toward the end of the 18th century.

## Town Features and Institutions
Despite the presence of numerous factories, the neighborhood of Barnsley is noted for having considerable natural beauty. The town's population was recorded at 35,427 in 1891 and grew to 41,086 by 1901.

Key buildings and institutions within the borough include:
* **Religious Sites:** Several churches, including the parish church of St Mary, which was constructed in 1821 on an earlier site.
* **Civic and Cultural Buildings:** A public hall, a court house, an institute, and a free library.
* **Education:** A free grammar school dating from 1665 and a philosophical society established in 1828.
* **Public Spaces:** A park presented in 1862 by the widow of Joseph Locke, M.P.
* **Monuments:** A monument erected in 1905 dedicated to prominent members of the Yorkshire Miners' Association.

## Regional Landmarks
In the vicinity of Barnsley are several sites of historical interest. These include Wentworth Castle, built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford, which is set in a beautiful park and houses a collection of historical portraits. Additionally, the area contains Monk Bretton Priory, a Cluniac foundation from 1157; remaining structures include fragments of the church, Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse.
