# M.V. Ballivian and Apuntes sobre la industria de goma

## Overview of M.V. Ballivian's Works
M.V. Ballivian was a prolific author of geographical, industrial, and statistical texts concerning Bolivia during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Among his notable contributions is the work *Apuntes sobre la industria de goma elastica, &c.*, published in La Paz in 1896. 

Ballivian's bibliography reflects a broad focus on the administration and resources of Bolivia. His other publications include *Noticia Politica, Geografica, Industrial, y Estadistica de Bolivia* (1900) and *Breves Indicaciones para el Inmigrante y el Viajero a Bolivia* (1898). He also authored *Relaciones Geograficas de Bolivia existentes en el Archivo de la Oficina Nacional de Inmigracion, &c.* (1898) and a three-part series titled *Monografias de la Industria Minera en Bolivia*, published between 1899 and 1900. Additionally, in collaboration with Eduardo Idiaquez, he produced the *Diccionario Geografico de la Republica de Bolivia* in 1900.

## Context within Bolivian Bibliography
The works of M.V. Ballivian are situated among a wider array of authorities and explorers who documented the region. Other significant sources on Bolivia include the *Archive Boliviano. Collecion de documentes relativos a la historia de Bolivia* (1872) by Vicente de Ballivian y Rizas. 

The study of Bolivia's geography and resources was furthered by various international and local authors, such as:
* **Explorers and Travelers:** G.E. Church, who wrote *The Route to Bolivia via the River Amazon* (1877) and "Bolivia by the Rio de la Plata Route" (1902); Edward D. Mathews, author of *Up the Amazon and Madeira Rivers, through Bolivia and Peru* (1879); and Sir W.M. Conway, who wrote *Climbing and Exploration in the Bolivian Andes* (1903).
* **Regional Analysts:** Carlos Matzenauer (*Bolivia in historischer, geographischer und cultureller Hinsicht*, 1897), Charles Wiener (*Perou et Bolivie*, 1880), and M. Dalence (*Bosquejo estadistico de Bolivia*, 1878).
* **Official Records:** The International Bureau of the American Republics published *Bolivia, Geographical Sketch, Natural Resources, &c.* (1904), and the *Sinopsis estadistica y geografica de la Republica de Bolivia* was released in three volumes between 1902 and 1904.

## Historical and Political Context of Bolivia
The intellectual and industrial documentation provided by authors like Ballivian occurred against a backdrop of significant political volatility in Bolivia. The country experienced repeated risings following its independence, including a revolution led by General Blanco that overturned the government shortly after Marshal Santa Cruz became president in 1828. 

Santa Cruz eventually brought financial order to the country and established a treaty of commerce with Peru. However, his involvement in Peruvian internal conflicts led to a war with Chile, resulting in his defeat at the battle of Jungay in June 1839 and his subsequent exile. This period of instability saw the rise of General Ballivian, who took chief power after the Santa Cruz party revolted against General Velasco. General Ballivian's presidency was marked by a conflict with Peru's General Gamarra, who attempted to annex the province of La Paz in August 1841 but was routed and killed in battle. General Ballivian remained president until 1848.

Later political tensions involved Adolfo Ballivian and Dr. Tomas Frias. A treaty with Chile regarding duties in Bolivian ports remained unratified, leading General Hilarion Daza (president in 1876) to demand a tax of 10 cents per quintal on exported nitrates. This demand led to a Chilean blockade of the ports of Cobija, Tocapilla, and Antofagasta, and the occupation of Antofagasta on February 14, 1879, resulting in a declaration of war by the Bolivian government on March 1.
