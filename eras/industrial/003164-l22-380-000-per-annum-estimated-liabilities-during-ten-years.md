# Trading Insolvency and Bankruptcy Liabilities in England and Wales

## Overview of Bankruptcy Statistics
According to annual reports from the Board of Trade, which are published as parliamentary papers and include detailed statistics and illustrations regarding the causes of insolvency, there has been a significant shift in the financial liabilities of bankrupt estates in England and Wales. In the ten-year period ending December 31, 1905, the total number of insolvencies processed under the bankruptcy acts was 43,141. These cases involved estimated assets totaling L26,001,417 and estimated liabilities amounting to L61,685,678.

## Comparative Analysis of Liabilities
Data from the Board of Trade reports allow for a comparison between two different ten-year periods. For the ten years ending in 1883, the estimated liabilities of bankrupt estates averaged L22,380,000 per annum. By contrast, for the ten years ending in 1905, the average annual estimated liabilities had decreased to L6,168,567.

## Private Arrangements and Trading Trends
While liabilities under the Bankruptcy Acts decreased, a separate category of financial arrangements occurred outside of these acts. During the period ending in 1905, there was an annual average of 3,426 private arrangements made by insolvent debtors, which involved an additional estimated annual liability of L4,166,354. 

A complete comparison with the period prior to 1883 is impossible because there are no means of ascertaining the amount of liabilities for private arrangements during that earlier time. However, the available data indicates a very great diminution in trading insolvency within England and Wales. Furthermore, it is recognized as a matter of general knowledge in commercial circles that the period was marked by a great decrease in reckless speculation and the proportion of fraudulent trade.

## Administration and Efficiency
The official figures indicate that the cost of administering bankruptcy under the current system has decreased considerably when compared to the system under the act of 1869. Additionally, estates are now closed at much shorter intervals.
