# The Cauvery and Related Southern Districts

## The District of Bangalore
The district of Bangalore is bordered by the Madras district of Salem. A significant portion of the district consists of the valley of the Arkavati river, which joins the Cauvery on the southern frontier. The district covers an area of 3,079 sq. m. According to 1901 data, the population was 789,664, which represented a 15% increase over the previous decade. 

The region is served by several railway lines. Industrial activity outside of Bangalore city includes a woollen mill that produces woollen stuffs, cloth for greatcoats, and blankets.

## The District of Kolar
The district of Kolar occupies a portion of the Mysore table-land and borders the Eastern Ghats. It has an area of 3,180 sq. m. The geography of the district is characterized by numerous hill ranges, with the principal watershed located in the north-west around the hill of Nandidrug, which reaches an elevation of 4,810 ft. 

The district's chief rivers include the North Pinakini, the South Pinakini (or Pennar), the Papagani, and the Palar. These rivers are used for irrigation through the use of tanks and anicuts. The soil in the higher levels consists of gravel and sand, while the valleys contain fertile loam. The hills are covered by brushwood, jungle, and scrub. The rocks are primarily granite or syenite, with small amounts of feldspar and mica.

In 1901, the population of Kolar was 723,600, an increase of 22% over the decade. The district is traversed by the Bangalore line of the Madras railway, which includes a 10 m. branch known as the Kolar Goldfields railway.

## Gold Mining and Industry in Kolar
Gold prospecting in the Kolar region began in 1876. By the end of 1904, the industry had produced gold with a total value of 21 million sterling. This resulted in 9 million sterling paid in dividends and 1 million sterling paid in royalty to the Mysore state. The region hosts the mines of the Nandidrug, Ooregum, Champion Reef, and Mysore companies.

The municipality of Kolar Gold Fields had a population of 38,204 in 1901, though it suffered severely from plague. The mines utilize electricity as motive power, which is sourced from the falls of the Cauvery, located 93 m. away. Other primary industries in the district include silk and cotton weaving, as well as sugar manufacture.

## Historical Sites
The hill fort of Nandidrug is a point of modern historical interest. In 1791, the British stormed the fort following a bombardment that lasted 21 days.
