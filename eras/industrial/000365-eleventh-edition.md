# ELEVENTH EDITION

## Overview and Publication
The Eleventh Edition of the Encyclopædia Britannica, subtitled "A Dictionary of Arts, Sciences, Literature and General Information," was published between 1910 and 1911. This edition consists of twenty-nine volumes. Copyright for the work is held in all countries subscribing to the Bern Convention by the Chancellor, Masters and Scholars of the University of Cambridge. In the United States of America, the copyright was held by The Encyclopædia Britannica Company, located at 342 Madison Avenue, New York, as of 1910.

## Historical Context of Editions
The Eleventh Edition is part of a long history of publications. The first edition was published in three volumes from 1768 to 1771. This was followed by a second edition of ten volumes (1777-1784), a third edition of eighteen volumes (1788-1797), and a fourth edition of twenty volumes (1801-1810). The fifth and sixth editions both consisted of twenty volumes, published from 1815 to 1817 and 1823 to 1824, respectively. The seventh edition contained twenty-one volumes (1830-1842), and the eighth edition contained twenty-two volumes (1853-1860). The ninth edition comprised twenty-five volumes, published between 1875 and 1889. The tenth edition consisted of the ninth edition along with eleven supplementary volumes, published from 1902 to 1903.
