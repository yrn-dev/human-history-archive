# Ashburton

Ashburton is a market-town located in the Ashburton parliamentary division of Devonshire, England. It is situated in a valley surrounded by hills, a short distance from the river Dart, and is positioned 24 miles northwest by west of Plymouth. The town is served by a branch of the Great Western railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 2,628.

## History and Governance
Historically known as Essebretona, Asperton, or Ashperton, Ashburton is an ancient stannary town and a borough by prescription. In the Domesday Book, it was recorded as being held in chief by Osbern, the bishop of Exeter, and it rendered geld for six hides. The town was governed by a bailiff and a portreeve, who were elected annually at the court leet held by the lord of the manor.

In 1552, the bishop sold the two manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough, after which they became crown property. The manors were eventually acquired in moieties by the Clinton family; the joint lords of the manor are currently Sir Robert Jardine and the present Lord Clinton.

The town's parliamentary representation has fluctuated over the centuries. Ashburton returned two members in 1298 and 1407. From 1407 until 1640, it returned only one member, before returning to two members. Following the Reform Act of 1832, it was deprived of one member, and the Reform Act of 1885 deprived it of the remaining member.

## Commerce and Industry
Ashburton has a history as a center for trade and manufacturing. In the reign of Edward II, Bishop Stapledon obtained a Saturday market and two annual fairs that lasted three days, held on the feasts of St. Martin in winter (November 11) and St. Laurence (August 10). Later, in 1672, John Ford was granted a Tuesday market specifically for the sale of woollen goods and wool made from English yarn. In 1705, Andrew Quicke obtained two additional annual fairs for the sale of merchandise, corn, and cattle, held on the first Thursdays of June and March.

The town's industrial output includes the manufacture of serge. Other local industries consist of saw-mills, paint factories, and breweries. In the surrounding neighborhood, a large deposit of umber is worked. While copper and tin mines and slate quarries were valuable in the past, the serge industry continues in the nearby urban district of Buckfastleigh, located 3 miles south-southwest of Ashburton.

## Landmarks and Education
The town contains the church of St. Andrew, a cruciform building with a lofty tower constructed early in the 15th century. The church features a fine old oak roof over its north aisle and a tablet dedicated to the 1st Baron Ashburton and solicitor-general John Dunning (1731-1783), with an inscription written by Dr. Johnson.

Education in the town is represented by the grammar school, which was founded in 1314 as a chantry.

Between Ashburton and Buckfastleigh lies Buckfast Abbey. This site is said to have been a Benedictine house before the Conquest and was refounded for Cistercians in 1137. In 1882, a French Benedictine community restored the abbey to use; while the Perpendicular abbot's tower remains, other sections were rebuilt according to the original lines.
