# Giuseppe Bonomi and the Duke of Argyll

## Giuseppe Bonomi
Giuseppe Bonomi (1739–1808) was an English architect born in Rome on January 19, 1739. After establishing a significant reputation in Italy, he moved to England in 1767, where he eventually settled into professional practice. Bonomi is credited with playing a large role in the revival of classical architecture within England.

During his career, Bonomi was involved in a dispute regarding the Royal Academy. Sir Joshua Reynolds, the president, desired for Bonomi to be appointed as a full Academician, as Reynolds viewed him as a suitable candidate for the vacant chair of perspective. However, the majority of the Academicians opposed this appointment. Consequently, Bonomi was elected only as an associate, a decision reached via the president's casting vote. This disagreement served as the innocent cause for Sir Joshua Reynolds' retirement from the presidency.

## Architectural Works and Honors
Bonomi's most renowned architectural achievement was the design of an Italian villa located at Roseneath in Dumbartonshire, which was designed for the duke of Argyll. Later in his career, in 1804, Bonomi received the appointment of honorary architect to St Peter's in Rome. He died in London on March 9, 1808.

## The Duke of Argyll and Associated Interests
The duke of Argyll is associated with various historical, literary, and geographic interests across Scotland and beyond. In the field of cartography, an atlas by Dowet was produced at the expense of the duke of Argyll. The duke also authored a work titled *India under Dalhousie and Canning*. ## Argyll Family Estates and History
The family of the duke of Argyll has long-standing ties to several Scottish sites:

*   **Dunoon:** Located in Argyllshire on the western shore of the Firth of Clyde, Dunoon features the remains of Dunoon Castle on a conical hill. Robert Bruce conferred the hereditary keepership of this castle upon the family of Sir Colin Campbell of Loch Awe, an ancestor of the duke of Argyll.
*   **Kilmun:** Situated on the northern shore of Holy Loch, Kilmun contains the ruins of a Collegiate chapel. Founded in 1442 by Sir Duncan Campbell of Loch Awe, this site served as the burial-ground for the Argyll family.
*   **Dunstaffnage:** This 13th-century ruined castle in Argyllshire is a quadrangular structure with walls 60 feet high and 10 feet thick. Originally held by the MacDougalls, it was captured by Robert Bruce in 1308 and given to the Campbells. While the castle is currently the property of the crown, the duke of Argyll serves as its hereditary keeper. The adjoining chapel was the burial-place of the Campbells of Dunstaffnage.

## Geography of Dumbartonshire
Dumbartonshire, where Bonomi's villa at Roseneath was located, is geologically divided into two areas. The boundary is defined by a line extending from Rossdhu on Loch Lomond, passing through Row and Roseneath, to Kilcreggan. 

The region north of this line is mountainous, consisting of rocks from the Eastern Highlands metamorphic series, including the Aberfoyle slates and grits and the Ben Ledi grits and schists. To the south of this boundary lies a belt of Upper Old Red Sandstone strata, which extends from the shores of Loch Lomond through Helensburgh and Roseneath Castle to Kilcreggan.

As of 1901, the population of Dumbartonshire was 113,865. The county town is Dumbarton, which is the only royal burgh in the area. Other municipal and police burghs include Helensburgh, Kirkintilloch, Milngavie, Clydebank, and Cove and Kilcreggan.
