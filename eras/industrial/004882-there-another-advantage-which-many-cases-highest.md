# Cantilever Bridges

## Overview and Structural Design
Cantilever bridges represent a method of bridging that is understood in many positions to be the simplest and most economical. This system is available for spans that exceed those practicable when using independent girders. In terms of stresses, the cantilever system virtually reduces spans to smaller ones.

The adoption of the cantilever system was notably influenced by the exceptional local conditions found at the site of the Forth bridge, a system that had been little considered prior to that instance. In certain designs, such as those of American origin, the shore ends of the cantilevers are anchored to the abutments.

## Advantages in Construction
A significant advantage of the cantilever system, which is of the highest importance in many cases, relates to the method of assembly. Cantilevers can be constructed member by member, building out from the piers. This process eliminates the need for temporary scaffolding below the structure, which provides several specific benefits:
* Navigation is not interrupted during construction.
* The costs associated with scaffolding are saved.
* The difficulties inherent in building in deep water are obviated.

Regarding the center girder, it may be lifted from the water-level or rolled into place after being built on the cantilevers.

## Alternative Erection Methods
J.A.L. Waddell has demonstrated that it is convenient in some instances to erect simple independent spans by first building them out as cantilevers and then converting them into independent girders after the erection is complete. In this process, the side spans are anchored to the piers and erected first on staging. The center span is then built out from both sides with the assistance of temporary members, which are removed once the process is finished.

## Comparison with Girder Bridges
In girder bridges consisting of three or more spans, making the girders continuous would result in an important economy of material. However, this is generally avoided due to the danger of support settlement. Such settlement would seriously alter the distribution and magnitude of stresses, as well as the points of contrary flexure (where the bending moment changes sign). To resolve this ambiguity regarding stresses, hinges or joints can be introduced at the points of contrary flexure, effectively making those points where the bending moment is zero.
