# BAILY, WILLIAM HELLIER (1819-1888)

## Early Life and Education
William Hellier Baily was an English palaeontologist born in Bristol on July 7, 1819. He was the nephew of the sculptor E. H. Baily.

## Professional Career
Baily began his professional career in Bristol, where he served as the Assistant Curator in the Bristol Museum from 1837 until 1844. Following his tenure in Bristol, he relinquished his post to join the staff of the Geological Survey located in London. 

His career progressed within the survey, and by 1854, he attained the position of assistant naturalist. In this capacity, he served under Edward Forbes and subsequently under Huxley. In 1857, Baily was transferred to the Irish branch of the Geological Survey. He took on the role of acting palaeontologist, a position he held for the remainder of his life.

## Publications and Contributions
Baily contributed extensively to the field of palaeontology through the authorship of numerous papers. His professional writing also included notes on fossils, which were featured in the explanatory memoirs produced by the Geological Survey of Ireland.

Between 1867 and 1875, he published a work titled *Figures of Characteristic British Fossils, with Descriptive Remarks*. This publication was intended to be a useful resource, though only the first volume was issued; this specific volume focused on palaeozoic species. Baily personally drew all the figures for this work on stone.

## Death
William Hellier Baily died on August 6, 1888, at Rathmines, which is located near Dublin.
