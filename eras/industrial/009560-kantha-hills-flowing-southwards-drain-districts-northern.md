# Mahi Kantha Hills and Northern Gujarat Drainage

## Geography and Hydrography
The Mahi Kantha hills serve as the source for two significant rivers: the Sabarmati and the Mahi. These rivers flow in a southward direction, providing the primary drainage for the districts of Northern Gujarat. After their southward course, both the Sabarmati and the Mahi eventually fall into the sea near the head of the Gulf of Cambay.

## Regional Context: The Bombay Presidency
The districts of Northern Gujarat are part of the Bombay Presidency, a province of British India. This presidency is a territory extending from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. latitude and from 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. longitude. It is bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, Indore, the Central Provinces and Hyderabad to the east, and Baluchistan, the Punjab and Rajputana to the north.

The presidency is divided into four commissionerships: Sind, the southern or Carnatic, the central or Deccan, and the northern, which is known as Gujarat. The total area of the presidency, including Sind but excluding Aden, is 188,745 sq. m. Of this total, 122,984 sq. m. are under British rule, while 65,761 sq. m. are under native rule.

## Administrative Divisions and Native States
The Bombay Presidency is organized into twenty-six districts. Those located within the broader region include Ahmedabad, Broach, Kaira, Panch Mahals, and Surat. Additionally, the presidency contains 353 separate native state units. These are administered by collectors of the districts in which smaller states are located or by political agents.

The native states are organized into several chief groups. The North Gujarat group specifically comprises the following agencies and areas:
* Mahi Kantha agency
* Rewa Kantha agency
* Palanpur agency
* Cutch
* Kathiawar agency
* Cambay

## Other Major Rivers of Western India
While the Sabarmati and Mahi drain Northern Gujarat, other major river systems characterize the region:
* **The Indus:** The chief river of western India, it enters the presidency from the north of Sind and flows south in a tortuous course before falling into the Arabian Sea through several mouths, including the Khori, Sir, Bhitiara, Wari, Mall, Vatho, Hajamro, Sisa, Pitiani, Khudi, and Ghizri creeks.
* **The Nerbudda:** Rising in the Central Provinces and traversing the dominions of Holkar, it enters the presidency at the north-western extremity of the Khandesh district. It flows eastward for 700 m. before falling into the Gulf of Cambay, creating the alluvial plain of Broach.
* **The Tapti:** This river enters the presidency south of the town of Burhanpur and flows eastward through the district of Surat, the district of Khandesh, and the native state of Rewa Kantha. It falls into the Gulf of Cambay a few miles west of Surat and is considered the most useful of the Gujarat rivers from a commercial perspective, draining approximately 250 m. of country.
* **The Banas and Saraswati:** These streams rise in the Aravalli hills and flow eastward through the native state of Palanpur to fall into the Runn of Cutch.
