# The Vicious System of Trafficking in Advances

## Overview of Building Society Practices
In the history of financial institutions, certain building societies exhibited signs of unsubstantial prosperity despite a period of steady progress. One such instability arose from the practice of receiving deposits that were repayable at call, creating potential embarrassment for societies that had invested those funds into long-term loans. Furthermore, it was suspected or known that many of these societies held large amounts of property that had been reduced into their possession following the default of borrowers on their instalments.

During this period, "mushroom societies" were established, which functioned primarily to pay fees to their promoters rather than conducting genuine business. Within many of these societies, a vicious system of trafficking in advances prevailed. These advances had been awarded by ballot, and the trafficking of them was considered to be near akin to gambling. These weaknesses were noted by well-informed observers and the crisis was accelerated by the disastrous failure of the Liberator, a large society incorporated under the act of 1874 that had long ceased to perform genuine building society business.
