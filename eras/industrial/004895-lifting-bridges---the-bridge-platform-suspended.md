# Lifting and Movable Bridges

Movable bridges are structures designed to be closed to facilitate the passage of railways, roads, or aqueducts, while possessing the ability to open to allow for free navigation. These bridges are categorized into several distinct types based on their mechanism of movement, including lifting, rolling, draw or bascule, and swing or turning bridges.

## Lifting Bridges
Lifting bridges are a specific type of movable bridge where the platform and the bridge itself are suspended from girders located above. The system utilizes chains and counterweights positioned at the four corners to facilitate movement. When the bridge is opened, it is lifted vertically to the required height. According to the archive's records, bridges of this specific type are not particularly numerous or important.

## Rolling and Bascule Bridges
Rolling bridges utilize girders that are longer than the actual span. The portion of the bridge overhanging the abutment is counter-weighted, ensuring that the center of gravity remains over the abutment as the bridge is rolled forward. To address the gap in the approaches created by this movement, a frame carrying that section of the road is moved sideways into place. In some instances, such as at Sunderland, a hydraulic press is used to lift the bridge to clear the roadway behind it before it is rolled back.

Draw or bascule bridges evolved from the original fortress draw-bridge, which featured a single leaf (bascule) turning on a horizontal hinge at one abutment and being raised by counterweights and chains. A more common modern variation employs two bascules, one hinged at each abutment, which lock at the center when closed. These bascules are prolonged backwards beyond the hinge to maintain balance; this prolongation sinks into the piers during the opening process.

In certain bascule constructions, each leaf consists of four main girders. For river crossings, these may be lattice girders with transverse girders spaced 12 feet apart. The floor is divided into rectangles of 3 by 3½ feet using longitudinal and subsidiary transverse girders, covered with buckled plates and a roadway of dowelled pine blocks. Such bascules may rotate through an angle of 82°, utilizing steel shafts 48 feet long and 21 inches in diameter. These structures can be operated by hydraulic machinery, typically taking one to one and a half minutes to lift or lower.

## Swing or Turning Bridges
The largest movable bridges are those that revolve around a vertical axis. These bridges are supported by a circular base plate featuring a central pivot and a circular track for conical rollers and a live ring. A revolving platform rests on these components, and motion is provided by a toothed arc fixed to either the live ring or the platform. The main girders rest on this revolving platform, with the ends of the bridge shaped as circular arcs to fit the fixed roadway.

There are three primary arrangements for swing bridges:
1. The axis of rotation is located on a pier in the center of the river, with an equal-armed bridge that opens two navigation passages simultaneously.
2. The axis of rotation is on one abutment, usually resulting in an unequal-armed bridge where the shorter arm is over land.
3. In smaller bridges, the shorter arm is vertical, and the bridge turns on a vertical crane post at the abutment.

An example of this system is the bridge between Duluth and Superior over the St Louis river. This turning bridge is 58 feet wide with a center opening of 500 feet. Its 2000-ton girders support a double track for trains, while cantilevers on each side carry a footway, roadway, and trolley track. It is operated by two large electric motors with a speed reduction of 1500 to 1. When closed, massive eccentrics with a 4-inch throw transfer the bridge's weight to the piers, creating a 2-inch lift that results in a load of 50 tons per eccentric.

## Engineering Requirements for Movable Bridges
Movable bridges must be proportioned like fixed bridges when in the closed position, but they must satisfy additional mechanical conditions. If a bridge revolves around a vertical axis, its center of gravity must always lie within that axis. If the bridge rolls, the center of gravity must remain over the abutment. Furthermore, the structure must possess sufficient strength to safely support its own overhanging weight while in motion.
