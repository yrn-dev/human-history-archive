# Western India and the Works of Sir William Lee-Warner

## Development of Bombay and Western India
The region of Western India, specifically the city of Bombay, entered a period of unrestricted development following the annexation of the Deccan and the British victory over the Mahrattas. During this era, the fortunes of the city were vigorously fostered by Mountstuart Elphinstone around 1819. The population of Bombay grew significantly during the 19th century; by 1838, the population had reached 236,000, and by 1891, it had more than doubled to 821,000.

This rapid expansion was attributed to several key factors: the foundation of cotton factories, the opening of the Suez Canal, and the influence of railways, with the first railway line being completed in 1853.

## Economic Fluctuations and Crisis
The prosperity of the region faced a significant interruption between 1866 and 1867. A financial crisis occurred due to a fall in the price of cotton, which resulted from the termination of the American war. Despite this setback, Bombay recovered and reached a state of greater prosperity by 1891 than it had ever known previously.

However, the decade following 1891 was marked by great havoc caused by plague, which negatively impacted both trade and the population. During this period, the population declined by 6% and exports fell by 7%. In contrast, the exports of Calcutta rose by 38% during the same timeframe.

## Sir William Lee-Warner
Sir William Lee-Warner was an author who produced works concerning the administration and geography of British India. In 1904, he published *The Presidency of Bombay* through the Society of Arts. Additionally, he wrote the *Life of the Marquis of Dalhousie, K.T.*, which provides detailed events regarding the period of the Marquis of Dalhousie's life and administration.
