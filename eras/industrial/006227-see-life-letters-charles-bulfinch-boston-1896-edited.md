# Charles Bulfinch

Charles Bulfinch (1763–1844) was a prominent American architect born in Boston, Massachusetts, on August 8, 1763. He was the son of Thomas Bulfinch, a wealthy and prominent physician. Bulfinch received his education at Harvard and the Boston Latin school, graduating from Harvard in 1781. Following his graduation, he spent several years in Europe engaged in study and travel.

## Professional Career in Boston
Bulfinch settled in Boston in 1787, where he became the first person to practice as a professional architect. For more than twenty-five years, he remained the most active architect in the city. His early architectural contributions included the "new" State House, completed in 1798, and the old Federal Street theatre, which was established in 1793 as the first play-house in New England.

## Public Service and Civic Influence
Parallel to his architectural career, Bulfinch held a leading role in the public life of Boston. From 1797 to 1818, he served as the chairman of the board of selectmen for twenty-one years. In this capacity, which functioned practically as the chief magistrate, he exerted significant influence over the modernization of the city. His civic contributions included the reorganization of the fire and police departments, the implementation of new systems for street-lighting and drainage, and the widening and straightening of Boston's streets.

## National Capitol and Later Life
In 1818, Bulfinch was appointed as the architect of the National Capitol in Washington, succeeding B.H. Latrobe. During his tenure, he designed the new portico and western approach and completed the central portion and unfinished wings of the building. He constructed the rotunda based on his own plans, incorporating suggestions from his predecessor, Latrobe.

Bulfinch returned to Boston in 1830 and remained there until his death on April 15, 1844. His architectural style was characterized by simplicity, refinement of taste, sincerity, and a total freedom from affectation, which left a great influence on American architecture during its early formative period.

## Other Interests and Family
Beyond architecture and governance, Bulfinch was involved in maritime exploration. In 1787, he was one of the promoters of the voyage of the ship "Columbia." Under the command of Captain Robert Gray (1755–1806), the "Columbia" became the first vessel to carry the American flag around the world.

Bulfinch's son, Stephen Greenleaf Bulfinch (1809–1870), was an author and a well-known Unitarian clergyman.
