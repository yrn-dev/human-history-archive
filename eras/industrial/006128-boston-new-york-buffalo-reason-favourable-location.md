# Buffalo, New York

## Geography and Status
Buffalo is a city and port of entry located in the United States, serving as the county-seat of Erie county, New York. It is situated at the upper end of the Niagara river and at the eastern extremity of Lake Erie. In terms of population, it is the eighth largest city in the United States and the second largest in the state of New York. Its distance by rail from other major hubs is 423 miles from New York City, 499 miles from Boston, and 540 miles from Chicago.

## Commerce and Transportation
Buffalo has developed into one of the Union's most significant industrial and commercial centers due to its position on the principal northern trade route between the West and the East, as well as its favorable location for lake transportation. The city is situated almost equidistant from New York, Boston, and Chicago.

The city's commercial infrastructure is heavily reliant on rail and water:
*   **Railways:** Approximately fourteen trunk lines either pass through or have terminals in Buffalo. The city contains more than 500 miles of track, including tracks of a belt line transfer company that encircle the city.
*   **Lake Commerce:** Buffalo serves as the eastern terminus for nearly all great steamship transportation lines of the Great Lakes. This provides direct freight and passenger connections to Milwaukee, Detroit, Cleveland, Chicago, and the "Head of the Lakes" (Duluth-Superior). The connection to the latter is maintained by the Great Northern Steamship Company, a subsidiary of the Great Northern railway, which utilizes some of the finest and largest inland passenger steamships in existence.
*   **Port Facilities:** The port of Buffalo handles more than 5,000,000 tons annually. It possesses a waterfront of approximately 20 miles and 8 to 10 miles of wharfs. The Federal government has increased shipping facilities through harbor improvements, including piers, a series of inner breakwaters, and a 4-mile outer breakwater made of cement and stone, which cost over $2,000,000.
*   **Canals and Bridges:** The Erie Canal, which has its western terminus in Buffalo, provided the first impetus for the city's commercial growth upon its completion in 1825. Additionally, Buffalo is connected to the Canadian shore via ferry and the International bridge from Squaw Island; the bridge was completed in 1873 at a cost of $1,500,000.

## Etymology and Related Terms
The name "Buffalo" properly refers to an aberrant species of cattle native to India, which has been domesticated in Egypt and India since time immemorial and was introduced into southern Europe. These animals are characterized by massive angulated horns, broad muzzles, long tufted tails, large ears, and sparsely haired skin. They resemble typical oxen in that they possess only 13 pairs of ribs. The term is now used to include all closely related animals, including African buffaloes, which are distinguished by the hair on their backs being directed backwards.

Separately, the term "buff" (derived from the French *buffle*) refers to a dull pale yellow leather. While originally made from buffalo skin, it is now also produced from other animals' skins and is used for jerkins or buffcoats (leathern military coats). This color is associated with certain British army regiments: the old 3rd Foot regiment of the line (now the East Kent Regiment) and the old 78th Foot (now the 2nd battalion Seaforth Highlanders) are known as the "Buffs" and "Ross-shire Buffs" because of the buff-colored facings of their uniforms.
