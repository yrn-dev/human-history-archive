# Mapping Chinese Turkestan and the 1870 Mission

## The Mission of Sir Douglas Forsyth
In 1870, a mission led by Sir Douglas Forsyth traveled to Yarkand. This expedition occurred during a period when Indian viceroys had long dreamed of establishing lucrative trade between Central Asia and India. Prior to this mission, very little was known regarding the regions of the great central depression or the highlands of the Pamirs, despite efforts to improve the northern approaches to Simla.

The Forsyth mission was preceded by the work of European geographical pioneers Robert Barkley Shaw and George Hayward, who had arrived at Yarkand within a few weeks of each other in 1868, venturing into the central dominion of Kashgar. Robert Barkley Shaw subsequently joined the 1870 mission led by Forsyth.

## Contributions of Henry Trotter
During the 1870 mission to Yarkand, Henry Trotter was responsible for producing the first maps of Chinese Turkestan. His work contributed to the broader scientific enterprise of Asiatic inquiry, which sought to define the geographical exactness of the great highland plateau stretching from the frontier of Kashmir eastwards to China, and from the Himalaya northwards to Chinese Turkestan.

## Subsequent Geographical Developments
Following the 1870 mission, further advancements in the knowledge of Central Asiatic geography were achieved through several key expeditions and commissions:

*   **Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission (1884–1886):** During this period, the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan were mapped by Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich. Simultaneously, Ney Elias traveled from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs to reach the commission's camp, identifying Rangkul (the "Dragon Lake") during his journey.
*   **Explorations of the Hindu Kush:** A mission led by Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart crossed into Wakhan via the Hindu Kush and returned to India through the Bashgol valley of Kafiristan. This allowed Colonel Woodthorpe to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush and verify previous explorations conducted by W.W. M'Nair.
*   **Late 19th Century Surveys:** In 1885, Andrew Dalgleish and Arthur Douglas Carey contributed new data to the map of Asia by following the tracks of Prjevalsky. In 1886, Captain (later Sir Francis) Younghusband crossed the Muztagh—the mountain barrier separating Kashmir and China—to travel across the heart of the continent.

## Related Cartographic and Academic Works
The mapping of the region was often subject to academic debate. For example, Baron von Richthofen and Prjevalsky disagreed on the situation of Lob-nor. Richthofen argued that Prjevalsky had traveled by an ancient road to a point south of the true Lop-noor, reaching two sweet-water lakes instead. Richthofen compared Prjevalsky's findings with Chinese topography from the time of Kien-long to illustrate these differences.

Other significant contributions to the region's documentation include the works of Sven Hedin (1905–1907), who produced a general map of Tibet on a 1:1,000,000 scale, and H.H.P. Deasy, who published a map in *In Tibet and Chinese Turkestan* (1901).
