# BRIDGEPORT

Bridgeport is a city, a port of entry, and one of the county-seats of Fairfield county, Connecticut, U.S.A. It is co-extensive with the town of Bridgeport and is situated in the southwestern part of the state on Long Island Sound, at the mouth of the Pequonnock river, approximately 18 miles southwest of New Haven.

## History and Governance
The area was originally part of the township of Stratford, with the first settlement established in 1659. The settlement was known as Pequonnock until 1695, at which point the name was changed to Stratfield. During the War of Independence, the location served as a center for privateering. The borough of Bridgeport was chartered in 1800, followed by the incorporation of the township in 1821. The city itself was not chartered until 1836.

## Demographics and Transportation
The population of Bridgeport grew steadily between 1880 and 1910, recorded as follows:
*   1880: 27,643
*   1890: 48,866
*   1900: 70,996
*   1910: 102,054

Of the population in 1900, 22,281 residents were foreign-born. This group included 5,974 individuals from Ireland, 3,172 from Hungary, 2,854 from Germany, 2,755 from England, and 1,436 from Italy.

The city is served by the New York, New Haven & Hartford railway. Maritime transportation includes lines of coast steamers and steamers providing direct access across Long Island Sound to Port Jefferson and to New York City. The harbor is described as excellent, formed by an inlet called Yellow Mill Pond and the estuary of the Pequonnock river.

## Geography and City Layout
The city is divided by its harbor and river. A peninsula known as East Bridgeport lies between the pond and the estuary and contains some of the city's largest manufacturing establishments. The main portion of the city is located west of the river and harbor. This area includes a wholesale section along the bank, a retail section located further back, and numerous factories situated far to the westward along the railway line.

The city features two large parks:
*   **Beardsley:** Located in the extreme north part of the city.
*   **Seaside:** Located along the Sound and west of the harbor entrance. Seaside Park contains a soldiers' and sailors' monument and statues of P.T. Barnum, a showman who lived in Bridgeport after 1846 and contributed significantly to the city (particularly East Bridgeport), and Elias Howe, who established a large sewing-machine factory in the city in 1863. The vicinity of Seaside Park is home to many fine residences.

## Economy and Industry
By 1905, Bridgeport had become the principal manufacturing center in Connecticut, with $49,381,348 of invested capital and products valued at $44,586,519. The city was the leading city in the United States for the manufacture of corsets in 1905, producing 19.9% of the national total. Other major industries include:
*   Sewing machines (including a factory of the Singer Manufacturing Co.)
*   Typewriters, phonographs, and gramophones
*   Automobiles, brass goods, and cartridges (including the Union Metallic Cartridge Co. factory)
*   Steam-fitting and heating apparatus
*   Large machine shops and foundries

Additionally, the city serves as the winter headquarters for "Buffalo Bill's Wild West Show" and "Barnum and Bailey's circus." As a port of entry, Bridgeport recorded imports valued at $656,271 in 1908.
