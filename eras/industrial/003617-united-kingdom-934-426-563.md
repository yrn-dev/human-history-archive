# United Kingdom

## Brewing Industry
The brewing trade in the United Kingdom has reached vast proportions. In 1900, production reached a maximum of 37,090,986 barrels. Although a steady decline followed that peak, production for 1905-1906 remained at 34,109,263 barrels, a figure higher than any year prior to 1897. This growth is notable when compared to 1875, when the industry was producing approximately 25,000,000 barrels.

By the year ending September 30, 1905, the exchequer received £13,156,053 in beer duty. There were 5,180 brewers for sale during this period. The largest brewery in the world was owned by the firm Messrs Guinness, which brewed more than two million barrels and paid roughly one million sterling to the revenue. Three other firms each brewed close to or more than one million barrels.

The materials used in production for the 1905-1906 period included:
*   Malt: 51,818,697 bushels
*   Unmalted corn: 125,671 bushels
*   Sugar: 2,746,615 cwt.
*   Hops: 62,360,817 lb
*   Hop substitutes: 49,202 lb
*   Rice, flaked maize, and similar materials: 1,348,558 cwt.

The average specific gravity of the beer produced in 1905-1906 was 1053.24. Regarding trade, 520,826 barrels of beer were exported and 57,194 barrels were imported; these figures remained almost stationary for thirty years. The majority of beer is consumed within England, which accounted for 28,590,563 barrels of the total consumption in 1905-1906, compared to 3,265,084 barrels in Ireland and 1,648,463 barrels in Scotland.

Financial investment in the liquor trade was calculated by Professor Leone Levi to be £117,000,000 in 1871, a figure that may have safely doubled by 1908. Additionally, a 1906 *Brewers' Almanack* writer estimated that capital invested in limited liability breweries alone was £185,000,000.

## Dairy Imports and Exports
Between 1886 and 1900, the United Kingdom imported significant quantities of butter, margarine, and cheese. Total imports of butter and margarine rose from 2,431,540 cwt. in 1886 to 4,298,932 cwt. in 1900. Cheese imports grew from 1,734,890 cwt. in 1886 to 2,711,805 cwt. in 1900.

Condensed milk imports were not separately distinguished until 1888, when they totaled 352,332 cwt. These imports increased continuously after 1889, growing from 389,892 cwt. in 1889 to 986,741 cwt. in 1900. This represents a nearly threefold increase in quantity. The annual value of these imports also advanced steadily from £704,849 in 1889 to £1,405,033 in 1900.

The United Kingdom also engaged in the export of condensed milk. Imported condensed milk was exported in the following quantities:
*   1897: 143,932 cwt. (£274,578)
*   1898: 133,596 cwt. (£256,525)
*   1899: 118,394 cwt. (£228,446)
*   1900: 164,602 cwt. (£309,460)

Furthermore, there was a trade in home-made condensed milk. Exports of this variety were 61,442 cwt. (valued at £133,556) in 1892, increasing to 111,959 cwt. (valued at £224,831) by 1896. By 1900, home-made condensed milk exports reached 209,447 cwt. with a value of £390,559.
