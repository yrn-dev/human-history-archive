# Bahia

## Geography and Topography
Bahia is an Atlantic state located in Brazil. It is bounded to the north by the states of Pernambuco, Piauhy, and Sergipe; to the east by the Atlantic and Sergipe; to the south by Minas Geraes and Espirito Santo; and to the west by Goyaz and Minas Geraes. The state covers an area of 164,650 square miles.

A significant portion of the state consists of a barren, arid plateau known as a *chapada*. This region is traversed from the northeast and south to the north by the drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river. The general elevation of the *chapada* ranges from 1,000 to 1,700 feet above the river, or 2,300 to 3,000 feet above sea level. On the western boundary, the *chapada* reaches an elevation of 2,300 feet and a breadth of 60 miles, serving as the water-parting between the Tocantins and the Sao Francisco.

East of the Sao Francisco, the state is divided into three distinct regions:
1. A rough limestone plateau that rises gradually toward the culminating ridges of the Serra da Chapada.
2. A gneissose plateau characterized by extensive exposures of bare rock that dip slightly toward the coast.
3. A narrower plateau with compact sandy soil that descends toward the coastal plain.

The first two regions, each approximately 200 miles wide, are inhospitable, barren, and arid, except at the dividing ridges where sea clouds provide some moisture. The third zone becomes less arid as it nears the coast and is better clothed with vegetation. The coastal plain varies in character, appearing in some areas as sandy, swampy, or low-lying—filled with intersecting canals and lagoons—while other areas are more elevated, rolling, and very fertile.

## Climate and Environment
The climate of Bahia closely follows its surface features. The interior is characterized as being hot and dry, while the coastal regions are hot and humid, and in some places, unhealthy.

## Economy and Industry
In the interior of the state, cattle-raising was previously the primary industry. However, this has been almost extinguished due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts. These conditions were caused by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve the grass.

Agricultural production is concentrated in the agricultural regions, where the following are produced:
* Cacao, coffee, and tobacco.
* Sugar, cotton, and mandioca.
* Tropical fruits.

The state's exports include rum, diamonds, cabinet woods, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, and hides.

## Population
The population of Bahia was recorded as 1,919,802 in 1890. The population is described as being largely of an unprogressive and mixed character.
