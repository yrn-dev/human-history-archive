# Cochran Boiler

The "Cochran" is a type of vertical boiler used in installations where floor space is limited. It is characterized by a specific design of its combustion chamber, which distinguishes it from similar models such as the "Victoria" boiler.

## Design and Combustion Chamber
The defining feature of the Cochran boiler is that its combustion chamber is constructed with a "dry" back. In contrast to other designs that maintain a water space at the rear of the chamber, the Cochran boiler utilizes doors lined with firebrick. The primary purpose of these firebrick-lined doors is to provide easy access to the tube ends.

## Context within Vertical Boilers
The Cochran boiler belongs to a broader category of cylindrical vertical boilers. These are often employed for small installations or as auxiliary equipment on board ships. Other vertical boiler types include:

*   **Simple Vertical Boilers:** These feature a plain vertical fire-box that tapers slightly in diameter, with an internal smoke stack traversing the steam space. The area between the fire-box and the shell is filled with water. Except in the smallest sizes, these typically include cross tubes (approximately 9 inches in diameter and made of 3/8-inch plate) that are flanged and riveted to the fire-box plates. These tubes are usually inclined to help water circulation and feature hand-holes in the outer shell for cleaning.
*   **The "Tyne" Boiler:** This model utilizes a hemispherical furnace. The products of combustion are directed into an upper combustion chamber that is traversed by several vertical water-tubes of varying diameters and four or more inclined water-tubes of about 9 inches in diameter.
*   **The "Victoria" Boiler:** Produced by Messrs Clarke, Chapman & Co., this boiler also features a hemispherical furnace. Its gases are led to an internal combustion chamber and then passed through numerous horizontal smoke-tubes into a smoke-box located on the side of the boiler.

## General Construction and Staying
In boilers of this general class, the flat backs and sides of combustion chambers are secured to each other or to the boiler shell using numerous screw stays. These stays are screwed through the connected plates and typically feature nuts inside the combustion chambers. 

The tops of these chambers are generally supported by strong girders resting on the chamber back plates and tube plates. In some instances, vertical stays attached to T bars riveted to the boiler shell are used, or the tops are strengthened with heavy transverse girder plates. To secure the end plates in the steam space and below the combustion chambers, longitudinal stays are used, passing through the full length of the boiler and secured with double nuts at each end.

## Tube Specifications
Tube plates in these systems are strengthened by stay tubes. The dimensions of the tubes vary based on the type of draught used:
*   **Natural or Chimney Draught:** Tubes are rarely longer than 7 feet and generally have an outside diameter of 3 or 3-1/4 inches.
*   **Forced Draught:** Tubes are typically 8 to 8-1/2 feet long with a diameter of 2-1/2 inches.

Regardless of the tube size, a clear space of 1-1/4 inches is almost always maintained between the tubes.
