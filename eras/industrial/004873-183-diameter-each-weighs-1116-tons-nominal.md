# The Williamsburg Bridge

The Williamsburg Bridge is a significant suspension bridge located in New York. Construction of the bridge began in 1897, and it was opened for traffic in 1903.

## Design and Dimensions
The bridge features a span of 1600 ft. and a width of 118 ft., with a versed sine of 176 ft. The structure is designed with two decks to accommodate a wide variety of transportation. These include two carriageways, two footways, two bicycle paths, four electric tramcar lines, and two elevated railway tracks.

## Cable System and Specifications
The bridge is supported by four cables, with one cable positioned on each side of the two main trusses or stiffening girders. While these cables support the girders over the centre span, they do not do so in the side spans; instead, the trusses in the side spans are supported by intermediate piers.

The cables have a diameter of 18¾ in. and each weighs approximately 1116 tons. Each cable is composed of 7696 parallel steel wires (No. 8 B.W.G.), organized into 37 strands of 208 wires each. These wires are approximately 3/16 in. in diameter. The steel used for the wires was required to have an elongation of 5% in 8 in. and 2½% in 5 ft., with a tensile strength of 89 tons per sq. in.

The nominal breaking strength of each cable is 22,320 tons, though the actual breaking strength is likely higher. Collectively, the four cables support a live load of 4017 tons and a dead load of 7140 tons.

## Construction and Materials
The cables are held together by cast steel clamps, which also serve as the attachment points for the suspending rods. For protection, the cables are sheathed in sheet iron and wrapped in cotton duck that has been soaked in varnish and oxidized oil. The saddles for the cables are situated 332 ft. above the water.
