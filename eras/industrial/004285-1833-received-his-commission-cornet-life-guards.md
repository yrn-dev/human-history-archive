# Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky

Prince Alexander Ivanovich Baryatinsky (1814–1879) was a Russian governor of the Caucasus and a soldier. He received a private education and entered the school of the ensigns of the Guard during his seventeenth year. On November 8, 1833, he received his commission of cornet in the Life Guards of the cesarevich Alexander.

## Military Career in the Caucasus
Baryatinsky's military service was heavily defined by his operations in the Caucasus. In 1835, he served with great gallantry in the region, an effort for which he was awarded a gold sword "for valour" upon his return to St Petersburg. He was later ordered back to the Caucasus in 1845, where he distinguished himself brilliantly, particularly during the attack on the stronghold of Shamyl. For these actions, he received the order of St George.

Between 1848 and 1856, Baryatinsky played a leading role in the primary military events of the Caucasus. His most notable exploits during this period included his operations against Shamyl at Chechen and his victory at Mezeninsk in 1850. He is credited with inaugurating a new era of mountain warfare through the use of systematic and energetic tactics. In 1854, he participated in the Kuruk Dere campaign.

## Administrative Leadership and Later Years
Baryatinsky rose through the ranks of the military administration, being appointed chief of the staff on July 5, 1853, and adjutant-general on January 6 of that same year. On January 1, 1856, he became the governor of the Caucasus and the commander-in-chief of the Caucasian army.

As an administrator, Baryatinsky maintained a high reputation. Within three years of his appointment, he subdued the entirety of the eastern Caucasus and took the long elusive Shamyl captive. He also conquered various tribes of the western Caucasus located between the rivers Byelaya and Laba. Due to these services, he was created a fieldmarshal.

Baryatinsky's strenuous labors eventually broke his health. At his own request, he was relieved of his post on December 6, 1862. He spent the final days of his life abroad and died in Geneva after forty-eight years of active service.

## Other Engagements
Outside of his service in the Caucasus, Prince Baryatinsky assisted Fieldmarshal Paskievich in 1846 to suppress the rising in Cracow. He had also been attached to the suite of Alexander starting on January 1, 1836.
