# 1873: Political and Legal Developments

The year 1873 was marked by significant legislative shifts in electoral processes and governance across various regions, most notably in Austria, Canada, and Scotland, alongside the deaths of several prominent public figures.

## Electoral Reform in Austria
In 1873, a new electoral law was passed that dealt a substantial blow to the Federalists. This measure transferred the right of electing members of the Reichsrath from the diets to the direct vote of the people. The primary effect of this change was to deprive Federalists of their chief weapon; it became impossible to take a formal vote of legal representatives in any territory to refuse the appointment of deputies. Consequently, if a Slovene or Czech member failed to take their seat, the result was limited to a single unrepresented constituency, which served to weaken the opposition.

The law was met with strong opposition, including a petition from Bohemia containing 250,000 names. Additionally, the Poles withdrew from the Reichsrath upon the law's introduction. Despite this, the measure passed with a vote of 120 to 2, as enough members remained to maintain the legal quorum. Alongside this reform, the number of members in the Reichsrath was increased to 353.

Following the reform bill, the Liberals secured an increased majority in the Reichsrath. The political landscape at the time was characterized by divisions among the Czechs; the "Young Czechs" (or Radicals) sought universal suffrage and political reform in alliance with Germans, while the "Old Czechs," led by Rieger, maintained a policy of passive opposition and alliance with Feudalists. In the resulting political environment, the real opposition consisted of 40 representatives of other Slav races and Clericals under the leadership of Hohenwart, facing 227 Constitutionalists.

## Canadian Political Transitions
In Canada, 1873 saw the transition of several key political figures. Sir Georges Etienne Cartier, a Canadian statesman and former joint prime minister (1858–1862), died on May 20, 1873. Cartier had been a leading member of the Liberal-Conservative party and was instrumental in the codification of civil law in Lower Canada and the promotion of the Grand Trunk and Canadian Pacific railways. He had also played a critical role in bringing his native province into federation between 1864 and 1867.

During the same year, Dorion accepted the position of minister of justice in the Mackenzie government. During his six-month tenure in office, he passed the Controverted Elections Act and the Electoral Law of 1874.

## Other Regional Developments
In Scotland, the first School Board election took place in 1873 under the Education (Scotland) Act 1872. This election adopted the code of procedure prescribed by W. E. Forster's Ballot Act of 1872, which had introduced the ballot in municipal and parliamentary elections (excluding universities), abolished public nominations of candidates, and addressed election expenses and the offense of personation.
