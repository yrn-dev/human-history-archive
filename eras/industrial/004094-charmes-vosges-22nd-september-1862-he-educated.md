# Maurice Barres

## Early Life and Education
Maurice Barres was a French politician and novelist born on September 22, 1862, in Charmes, located in the Vosges region. He received his education at the *lycée* of Nancy. In 1883, Barres moved to Paris to pursue further studies in law.

## Literary Career and Individualism
During his early years, Barres contributed to the monthly periodical *Jeune France* and established his own periodical titled *Les Taches d'encre*, though it only lasted for a few months. Following four years spent in journalism, he traveled to Italy. This period resulted in the publication of *Sous l'oeil des barbares* (1888), which served as the first volume of a *trilogie du moi*. This trilogy was later completed by *Le Jardin de Berenice* (1891) and *Un Homme libre* (1889).

In these works, Barres presented a worldview divided between *moi* (the self) and "the barbarians," a category he used to describe anyone antipathetic to his own individuality. He further expanded these apologies for individualism in *L'Ennemi des lois* (1892) and a volume of travel impressions titled *Du sang, de la volupte et de la mort* (1893). His early literary output is characterized by an elaborate style that was frequently very obscure.

## Political Activity
Barres applied his theories of individualism to the political sphere as an ardent partisan of General Boulanger. While in Nancy, he directed a Boulangist newspaper. His political career led to his election as a deputy in 1889, a seat in the legislature he held until 1893. In 1894, his play *Une Journee parlementaire* was produced at the Comedie Francaise.

## Later Works and National Themes
In 1897, Barres began a new trilogy titled *Le Roman de l'energie nationale*, starting with the publication of *Les Deracines*. This series served as a plea for the preservation of the distinctive qualities of old French provinces and for local patriotism. The first novel in the series follows the adventures of seven young Lorrainers who travel to Paris to seek fortune. The second novel, *L'Appel au soldat* (1900), focuses on the history of Boulangism and features six of the original characters. The final sequel, *Leurs figures* (1902), addresses the Panama scandals.

Barres continued to write on themes of nationalism and patriotism in subsequent works, including:
* *Scenes et doctrines du nationalisme* (1902)
* *Les Amities francaises* (1903), in which he argued that patriotism should be inculcated through the early study of national history.
* *Ce que j'ai vu a Rennes* (1904)
* *Au service de l'Allemagne* (1905), which details the experiences of an Alsatian conscript serving in a German regiment.
* *Le Voyage de Sparte* (1906)
