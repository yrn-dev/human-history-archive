# Manufactures and Commerce of Bohemia

From an industrial perspective, Bohemia is one of the greatest manufacturing centres in Europe and holds the first rank among the Austrian provinces. The region's industrial growth was characterized by enormous strides during the last half of the 19th century, a development fueled by an abundance of coal and iron, as well as water supplies that could be converted into motive power.

## Glass and Porcelain Industries
The glass industry in Bohemia was introduced from Venice during the 13th century and quickly became of vast importance. Factories are primarily located near the mountains to take advantage of plentiful fuel and minerals, specifically silica. The highest quality product, crystal-glass, is produced around Steinschonau and Haida. Additionally, a very extensive porcelain industry is concentrated in and around the area of Carlsbad.

## Textiles and Specialized Manufactures
The textile industry is a leading sector, mostly concentrated in the valley of the Lower Elbe and the north-east corner of Bohemia around Reichenberg. Specific textile specializations include:
* **Cloth:** Manufactured at Reichenberg.
* **Linen:** Centered in Trautenau and Rumburg.
* **Woollen yarns:** Produced at Asch and Aussig.
* **Lace:** Pursued as a home-industry in the Erzgebirge region, with its main centre at Weipert.

Other specialized manufactures include the production of red fezes (Turkish caps) in Strakonitz and the manufacture of pencils in Budweis.

## Metallurgy and Heavy Industry
Favoured by rich deposits of iron and coal, metallurgic industries are concentrated around the mines. The production of industrial and agricultural machinery takes place in Prague, Pilsen, and Reichenberg. Prague also hosts a large establishment dedicated to the production of railway rolling-stock.

## Diversified Industries and Urban Centers
Bohemia maintains several other important industrial branches, including the production of leather, paper, dyeing, and calico-printing. Chemical manufacturing is centered in Aussig and Prague. The region is also known for its breweries, producing a beer appreciated globally. Sugar refining is a more recent industry that has seen very great development.

Specific towns contribute significantly to this industrial landscape:
* **Budweis:** A principal commercial centre of South Bohemia and an important river port and railway junction. Its varied industry includes the manufacture of machinery, bricks, tiles, black-lead pencils, chemicals, matches, and paper. It also features corn and saw mills, bell-founding, and boat-building. Budweis carries on a large trade in salt, lignite, timber, corn, industrial products, and beer, the latter of which is mostly exported to America.
* **Kuttenberg:** The industry in this town includes brewing, sugar-refining, the manufacture of agricultural implements, leather goods, and woollen and cotton stuffs.

## Commerce and Infrastructure
The commercial development of Bohemia has progressed hand-in-hand with its industrial activity. This growth has been stimulated by a system of good roads and an extensive railway network. For example, the railway from Budweis to Linz, constructed in 1827 for horse-cars, was the first line built in Austria.
