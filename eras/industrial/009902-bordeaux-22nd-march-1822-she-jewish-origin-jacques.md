# Rosa Bonheur

## Early Life and Family
Rosa Bonheur (born Marie Rosalie) was a French painter born in Bordeaux on March 22, 1822. She was of Jewish origin. According to the Belgian medallist Jacques Wiener, a native of Venloo, he and Rosa's father, Raymond Bonheur, used to attend synagogue in Bordeaux. Another source indicates that Rosa was referred to in common parlance as Rosa Mazeltov, utilizing the Hebrew term for "good luck," which corresponds to the French name Bonheur.

Rosa was the eldest of four children, all of whom pursued careers as artists. Her siblings included Auguste (1824–1884), who painted landscapes and animals; Isidore (born 1827), a sculptor of animals; and Juliette (1830–1891), who received an "honourable mention" at the exhibition of 1855.

## Artistic Development and Career
Rosa was taught to draw at an early age by her father, who died in 1849. Recognizing her remarkable talent, he allowed her to abandon the business of dressmaking—a pursuit she had been placed in against her will—to devote herself entirely to art. 

Between 1840 and 1845, Bonheur exhibited her work at the salon, where she received a prize five times. In 1848, she was awarded a medal. Her fame increased significantly following the exhibition of 1855, after which her works became highly sought after by public galleries and collectors in England.

## Style and Notable Works
Bonheur is noted for representing animals as they truly appear, based on her observations of them in the country, a trait she shared with her contemporary Jacques Raymond Brascassat (1804–1867). While her work is praised for having faultlessly true anatomy and a "manly and firm" treatment devoid of femininity, some critics noted a dryness of style and a frequent failure to convey a perfect sense of atmosphere.

Among her many works, the following are highlighted:
*   "Hay Harvest in Auvergne" (1835)
*   "Ploughing in the Nivernais" (1848), located in the Luxembourg gallery
*   "The Horse Fair" (1853), for which there are two replicas, one of which is in the National Gallery in London and the original of which is in the United States.

## Honors
For her contributions to art, Bonheur was decorated with the Legion of Honour by Empress Eugenie. She was later promoted within the order to the rank of "officer."
