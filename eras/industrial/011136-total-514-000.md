# Hydrographic Basins of Asia

The hydrographic basins of Asia are categorized into three primary divisions: the northern division, the southern division, and the interior or inland basins. These divisions are defined by their drainage patterns and the specific seas or depressions into which their waters flow.

## The Northern Division
The northern division of Asia's hydrographic basins encompasses an area of approximately 6,394,500 square miles. This division is composed of three distinct drainage areas:
*   **Arctic river basins:** The largest portion of this division, covering 4,367,000 square miles.
*   **Aralo-Caspian basin:** Covering 1,759,000 square miles.
*   **Mediterranean:** Covering 268,500 square miles.

## The Southern Division
The southern division is nearly equal in extent to the northern division, totaling 6,514,000 square miles. This area is divided between two major drainage systems:
*   **Pacific drainage:** Covering 3,641,000 square miles.
*   **Indian Ocean:** Covering 2,873,000 square miles.

## Interior and Inland Basins
The interior or inland basins cover an area of 3,141,500 square miles, which is approximately half the extent of the northern and southern divisions. These basins include several specific regions:
*   The Gobi depression.
*   The Tibetan plateau.
*   The Iranian (or Perso-Afghan) uplands.
*   The Syro-Arabian inland basin.
*   The basin of Asia Minor.
*   Lacustrine regions located south of the Arctic watershed.

## Major Asiatic River Basins
Among the river basins in Asia, the Ob is the largest, with an extent that exceeds 1,000,000 square miles. Other significant river basins include:
*   **Amur:** 776,000 square miles (located on the east and south).
*   **Yang-tsze-kiang:** 685,000 square miles.
*   **Ganges:** 409,500 square miles.
*   **Indus:** 370,000 square miles.
