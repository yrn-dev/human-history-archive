# Communications

The development of communications, encompassing the construction of roads, railways, and maritime systems, has historically served as a primary means of opening up territories for occupation, commerce, and administration.

## Railway and Road Development in East Africa
In the protectorate of East Africa, significant efforts were made to open the country through the construction of roads and railways. A trunk road was established from Mombasa, passing through Lake Naivasha and Kibwezi in the upper Sabaki basin, reaching Berkeley Bay on Victoria Nyanza. 

The most critical engineering project in the region was the Uganda railway, which connects Mombasa to Victoria Nyanza. A preliminary survey for this line was conducted in 1892, with construction beginning in 1896. The railway is a mountain line of metre (3.28 ft.) gauge, totaling 584 miles in length. Its route roughly follows the road until the equator, after which it crosses the Mau plateau to reach Port Florence on the Kavirondo Gulf. 

The line features significant elevation changes:
* It begins at Mombasa, crossing to the mainland via a half-mile bridge.
* At the Kikuyu Escarpment, 346 miles from the start, it reaches 7,600 ft. above sea level.
* It descends via viaducts to a valley floor of 6,011 ft.
* It ascends the Mau escarpment to its highest point of 8,321 ft.
* It concludes at Port Florence at an altitude of 3,738 ft.

Built by the British government at a cost of £5,331,000 (approximately £9,500 per mile), the first locomotive arrived at Victoria Nyanza on December 26, 1901. The permanent way was largely finished by March 1903, coinciding with the resignation of the lead engineer, Sir George Whitehouse. This railway facilitated the development and occupation of Uganda—previously nearly impossible due to goods carriage costs of £60 per ton—and effectively ended the slave trade in that part of East Africa by eliminating the need for human carriage of goods.

## Internal Communications in Burma
In Burma, water was historically the primary means of internal communication. The Irrawaddy Flotilla Company operated steamers on the Irrawaddy to all points as far north as Bhamo, and to Myitkyina during dry weather, as well as on the Chindwin to Homalin (during rains) and Kindat. Additionally, the Arakan Flotilla Company assisted in opening the Arakan division.

Road construction was initially limited in Lower Burma, where there were 814 miles of road in 1871-1872. However, Upper Burma saw more extensive development. By the end of 1904-1905, the province had 7,486 miles of road, consisting of 1,516 miles of metalled roads, 3,170 miles of unmetalled roads, and 2,799 miles of other tracks.

Railway construction provided the most significant advance in Burmese communications. Key lines include:
* Rangoon to Prome (161 m.), opened in 1877.
* Rangoon to Toungoo (166 m.), opened in 1884.
* Extensions from Toungoo to Mandalay, and the Mu Valley railway from Sagaing to Myitkyina (752 m. from Rangoon).
* Mandalay to Lashio (178 m.).
* Branch lines opened in 1900 from Meiktila to Myingyan and Sagaing to Mônywa-Alôn.
* A line from Henzada to Bassein (1902) and a link from Henzada to Letpadan (1903).
* Pegu to Martaban (121 m.) and Henzada to Kyang-in (66 m.).

By 1904-1905, 1,340 miles of railway were open. While five of the eight commissionerships and Lashio were connected, the southern Shan States and Taung-gyi remained accessible only via difficult hill-roads.

## Maritime and Urban Communications
In ancient Greece, the empire of Athens established a maritime system that controlled the transit and commerce of the Aegean. This system excluded irregular force and kept Persian tribute-officers and ships of war away from the seaboard.

In more modern contexts, governments have focused on public works to stimulate industry, such as the extension of railways and the opening of internal communications. For example, in 1863, the navigation of the Scheldt was made free. In urban environments, the width of communications—such as streets—has been a point of contention. In Antwerp's *Marché aux Souliers*, a dispute between house-owners, the commune, and the city over widening a narrow street was eventually resolved by the destruction caused by German heavy artillery.

## Strategic Communications in Warfare
Communications can be a decisive factor in military sieges. During the operations of Imilkon against Syracuse, the city's communications with the surrounding country remained partially open on two sides despite the siege. Supplies reached the city via the Hexapylon (the principal gate in the northern cliff fortification) and westward through the Euryalus. Despite Imilkon's superior fleet, the Syracusans utilized armed squadrons to protect store-ships and ensure the arrival of provisions by sea.
