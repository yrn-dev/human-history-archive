# African Lakes Trading Corporation

The African Lakes Trading Corporation was a trading company that emerged from missionary societies in South Central Africa. Its development and activities were closely linked to the exploration of the region and the subsequent competition for influence between European powers and local entities.

## Origins and Context
The foundation of the corporation followed the exploration of South Central Africa by David Livingstone. In 1859, Livingstone penetrated the Shiré river and discovered Lake Nyasa. He later conducted journeys to Lake Mweru, Lake Bangweulu (where he died in 1873), and the southern end of Tanganyika. These explorations established British interests in the region.

Following Livingstone's death, various missionary societies entered the area to begin evangelization. These missionaries, along with a small number of Scottish settlers, consistently opposed efforts by the Portuguese to expand their control into the region from the Zambezi and the adjoining provinces of Moçambique. The African Lakes Trading Corporation grew out of these missionary societies.

## Conflict and Unofficial Warfare
The African Lakes Trading Corporation entered into a conflict with a number of Arabs who had established themselves at the northern end of Lake Nyasa. This rivalry escalated into a struggle between Britons and Arabs for the possession of the country, beginning around 1885.

During this period, the corporation engaged in an unofficial war, enlisting volunteers to fight. Notable participants in these operations included Mr. (later Sir) Alfred Sharpe and Captain (later Sir F.D.) Lugard. Both men were wounded during the conflict, and the operations they led were not crowned with complete success. The struggle for the territory continued until 1896.

## Diplomatic Resolution and Protectorate
In 1889, Mr. (later Sir) H.H. Johnston was dispatched to the region with two primary objectives. First, he was tasked with attempting to effect an arrangement to resolve the dispute between the African Lakes Trading Corporation and the Arabs. Second, he sought to ensure that friendly native chiefs were protected from Portuguese aggression beyond a specific point.

The treaties and efforts led by Johnston resulted in the creation of a British sphere of influence and protectorate located north of the Zambezi.
