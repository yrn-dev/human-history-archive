# Buckingham and Chandos

The dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos, as held by the Grenville family, was a title derived from the town of Buckingham. It originated in 1784 when the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham." This title was later elevated into the dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos in 1822 for his son.

## Richard Grenville, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Chandos
Richard Grenville (1776–1839) served as a member of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1797 to 1813. During this period, while known as Earl Temple, he was an active participant in politics. He succeeded his father as marquess of Buckingham in February 1813. Following his marriage to the only child of the 3rd duke of Chandos, he was created duke of Buckingham and Chandos in 1822. 

The 1st duke died in 1839. Due to financial embarrassments, he spent a period of time living outside of England. An account of these travels was published in 1862 under the title *The Private Diary of Richard, Duke of Buckingham and Chandos*.

## Richard Grenville, 2nd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos
The 1st duke was succeeded in 1839 by his only child, Richard Grenville (1797–1861). Educated at Oriel College, Oxford and Eton, he was known as Earl Temple and later as marquess of Chandos. He represented Buckinghamshire in parliament from 1818 to 1839 and was the individual responsible for the "Chandos clause" within the Reform Bill of 1832. From September 1841 to January 1842, he served as lord privy seal. He earned the nickname "Farmers' Friend," partly because of his opposition to the repeal of the corn laws.

Upon succeeding to the dukedom in 1839, the 2nd duke found the estates heavily encumbered. His own luxurious and generous tastes eventually brought the financial situation to a climax, leading his creditors to seize his residences in 1847. Consequently, the duke left England, and many of his landed estates and personal property were sold. After returning to England, he turned his attention to literature. He died in London on July 29, 1861.

The 2nd duke married Mary, daughter of John, 1st marquess of Breadalbane, in 1819; she obtained a divorce from him in 1850 and died in 1862. His primary publications include:
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of George III.* (1853–1855)
* *Memoirs of the Court of England, 1811–1820* (1856)
* *Memoirs of the Court of George IV.* (1859)
* *Memoirs of the Court and Cabinets of William IV. and Victoria* (1861)

## Richard Grenville, 3rd Duke of Buckingham and Chandos
The 3rd duke, Richard Grenville (1823–1889), was the only son of the 2nd duke. He was educated at Christ Church, Oxford and Eton. While known as marquess of Chandos, he represented the borough of Buckingham in parliament between 1846 and 1857. He also served as the chairman of the London & North-Western railway from 1853 to 1861.

Following his succession to the dukedom, he held several high offices in the Conservative government of 1866–1868, serving as secretary for the colonies and lord president of the council. He was the governor of Madras from 1875 to 1880 and was chosen as chairman of committees in the House of Lords in 1886.

The 3rd duke married twice and had three daughters, but no son. Upon his death, the dukedom became extinct. Other titles were passed to different heirs: the earldom of Temple went to his nephew, William Stephen Gore-Langton; the viscounty of Cobham went to his kinsman, Charles George, 5th Baron Lyttelton; and the Scottish barony of Kinloss, to which he had established his title in 1868, passed to his eldest daughter, Mary. His widow married the 1st Earl Egerton of Tatton in 1894.
