# Sand-lime Bricks

Sand-lime bricks are a type of building block produced through a specific chemical hardening process involving sand and lime. Patented in the early 1880s by Dr. Michaelis of Berlin, the process utilizes high-pressure steam to harden the materials, leading to the extensive production of these bricks across many countries.

## Manufacturing Process
While specific details of manufacture may vary, the general method remains consistent. The process begins by intimately mixing dry sand with powdered slaked lime. On average, the proportion used is one part lime to ten parts sand, though this ratio may be adjusted based on the purity and character of the sand and the nature of the lime.

Once mixed, the materials are slightly moistened with water and placed into powerful presses. These presses exert a pressure of approximately 60 tons per square inch to mould the mixture into bricks. Immediately following their removal from the press, the bricks are placed into large steel cylinders, typically 7 feet in diameter and 60 to 80 feet in length. Inside these cylinders, the bricks are subjected to high-pressure steam, ranging from 120 to 150 lb per square inch, for a period of ten to fifteen hours.

## Chemical Composition and Properties
A typical German sand-lime brick consists of the following chemical analysis:
*   Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$): 84%
*   Lime ($\text{CaO}$): 7%
*   Alumina and oxide of iron: 2%
*   Water, magnesia, and alkalis: 7%

The application of high-pressure steam causes the lime to attack the sand particles, producing a chemical compound of silica, lime, and water. This resulting bond of hydrated calcium silicate creates a strong connection between the larger sand particles. This bond is of a better type and differs from the calcium carbonate filling found in mortar-bricks, making the sand-lime brick significantly stronger than any ordinary mortar-brick.

In terms of appearance, sand-lime bricks are usually light-grey. However, they can be stained using pigments or colouring oxides that are unaffected by the manufacturing conditions and the lime. The manufacturing process is considered simple and, with reasonable care, produces bricks of constant quality.

## Comparison with Mortar Bricks
Sand-lime bricks differ fundamentally from mortar bricks, which are unburnt blocks of artificial stone. Mortar bricks are created by moulding a mixture of sand and slaked lime and allowing them to harden naturally in the air. This natural hardening occurs through the evaporation of water and the conversion of slaked lime (calcium hydrate) into calcium carbonate via atmospheric carbonic acid.

The natural hardening process for mortar bricks is lengthy, taking between six and eighteen months. While some hydrated calcium silicate and hydrated basic carbonate of lime form as cements in mortar bricks, the process is slower and results in a weaker product than the steam-treated sand-lime brick. Although various improvements were introduced in the latter half of the 19th century—such as using hydraulic presses, adding cement or ground blast-furnace slags, and treating blocks with pressurized carbon dioxide—the newer type of mortar brick has practically superseded the old versions.
