# Cheese: Production, Varieties, and Trade (1890-1900)

Cheese is a dairy product produced through various processes of fermentation and ripening involving myriads of bacteria. While no milk is free from bacteria, the forms typically present in normal fluid induce changes such as "turning" or souring. Research conducted between 1890 and 1900 led to practical applications in cheese production, such as in the case of Edam cheese. Additionally, the Japanese produce cheeses through the bacterial fermentation of boiled Soja beans.

## Regional Varieties and Characteristics

Cheese exists in numerous forms across different nations, varying in shape, weight, and flavor:

*   **Holland:** Common varieties include Gouda and Edam. Gouda is a flat cheese with convex edges weighing up to 20 lb. Edam is spherical, weighs between 3 to 4 lb., is usually dyed crimson on the outside, and is noted for having a finer flavor than Gouda.
*   **Switzerland:** The Emmenthaler is a well-known variety, roughly the size of a cart-wheel and weighing between 150 and 300 lb. It is characterized by regularly distributed small holes and shares the same flavor and color as Gruyere.
*   **Italy:** Parmesan is a round, flat cheese approximately 5 inches thick, weighing 60 to 80 lb. Gorgonzola, named after a town near Milan, is made in the Cheddar shape, weighs 20 to 40 lb., and is permeated by a blue mould when ripe, resembling a rich old Stilton.
*   **Germany:** The leading variety is Limburger, though Backstein and Munster are also produced; all are described as strong-smelling.
*   **United Kingdom:** Cheddar is a substantial type of cheese. Cheshire cheese is primarily produced in the county of its name and adjoining districts, with extensive consumption in Liverpool, Manchester, and other parts of the densely populated county of Lancaster.

## Production and Manufacturing

The manufacture of pressed cheese, specifically Cheddar, requires a specific volume of milk. Data from the Bath and West of England Society cheese school for 1899 and 1900 shows that 53,028 gallons of milk yielded 50,168 lb. of saleable cheese. This equates to approximately 1.05 gallons of milk per 1 lb. of cheese, or roughly 10 lb. of milk per 1 lb. of pressed cheese.

In the United States, the manufacture of cheese, butter, and condensed milk saw a 60% increase between 1890 and 1900, though the growth rate slowed to 3.1% between 1900 and 1905.

## United Kingdom Trade and Consumption

Between 1886 and 1900, the United Kingdom experienced shifts in its dairy imports. While cheese imports increased, they did so at a slower rate than butter imports. In 1886, 1887, 1888, 1890, and 1892, cheese imports exceeded those of butter; however, after 1892, butter imports became the larger of the two. By 1899, butter imports were a million cwt. higher than cheese imports. It is suggested that the growth in cheese demand among industrial classes may have been checked by the cheapness of imported fresh meat.

### Import Sources and Proportions
For the ten years ending June 30, 1900, the average supply of cheese in the United Kingdom was 55.3% home-made, 24.2% colonial, and 20.5% foreign. Between 1891 and 1900, home-made production diminished by 17,000 tons, while colonial imports—chiefly from Canada—increased. By 1900, the proportions shifted to 50.3% home-made, 28.9% colonial, and 20.8% foreign.

Over 80% of imported cheese comes from North America, with Canada supplying nearly 60% of the total import. In 1900, the value of Canadian cheese exported to the UK was nearly L3,800,000. Other contributors include:
*   **Holland:** Imports remained mostly stationary, though they reached 17,000 tons in 1900 due to high prices stimulating manufacture.
*   **France:** France lost market share to Belgium; imports fell from over 3,000 tons in 1891 to below 2,000 tons in 1900. French shipments are dominated by "fancy" or soft cheeses, which commanded a higher average price (61s. per cwt. in 1900) compared to other sources (50s. per cwt.).
*   **Belgium:** Imports increased from less than 1,000 tons in 1891 to 2,600 tons in 1900.
*   **Others:** Australasia (mostly Cheddar type), Germany, Italy, and Russia.

### Export and Market Value
The United Kingdom maintains a very small export trade in cheese. In 1900, the UK exported 9,356 cwt. of cheese with a value of L36,691.
