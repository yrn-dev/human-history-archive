# Brussels

Brussels is a city in Belgium that has served as a center for education, religious scholarship, military administration, and the arts.

## Education and the Arts
In February 1842, Charlotte Brontë and her sister Emily became pupils at the Pensionnat Héger in Brussels. The establishment was headed by the wife of the principal teacher, M. Héger. Both sisters worked hard during their time there, earning the admiration and goodwill of M. Héger. Their studies were cut short before the year ended, as they were called back to England due to the critical illness of their aunt, Miss Branwell, who died on October 29, 1842.

The city also attracted international musical talent. In 1864, the Bohemian composer Karel (or Karl) Bendl traveled to Brussels, where he served for a short period as the second conductor of the opera before moving on to Paris and Amsterdam.

## Religious Scholarship
Brussels was a significant site for the work of the Bollandists. After being authorized to continue their work following the dispersion of the Jesuits, the Bollandists moved from Antwerp to Brussels in 1778, settling at the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg. During their tenure there, they published volume iv of *October* in 1780 and volume v in 1786. 

The work of the Bollandists ceased in 1788 after the suppression of the Coudenberg monastery. However, the project was revived in 1837 at the suggestion of the Belgian government and the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were then installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels. This new series of publications began with volume vii of *October* in 1845 and continued through volume xiii in 1883, featuring collaborations from Jesuit fathers including Joseph van der Moere, Joseph van Hecke, Benjamin Bossue, Victor and Remi de Buck, Ant. Tinnebroeck, Edu. Carpentier, and Henr. Matagne. Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan edited the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which appeared in volume xi of *October* in 1864.

## Military and Strategic Importance
Brussels is a key city in the defensive geography of Belgium. During the events of August 1914, the Belgian field army was posted on the river Gette, providing cover for both Antwerp and Brussels. Prior to this, the inspector-general Brialmont had drafted and executed the comprehensive scheme for the defenses of Belgium. Brialmont was a prolific writer who published several important works in Brussels, including *La Fortification du temps présent* (1885), *Influence du tir plongeant et des obus-torpilles sur la fortification* (1888), *Les Régions fortifiées* (1890), *La Défense des états et la fortification à la fin du XIX^e siècle* (1895), and *Progrès de la défense des états et de la fortification permanente depuis Vauban* (1898).

## Diplomatic Events
Brussels served as the site for critical diplomatic communications during the onset of war in August. On August 2, at 7:00 in the morning, the German Minister in Brussels received a formal answer to a German ultimatum. The response was a categorical refusal to allow the German army to pass through Belgian territory.
