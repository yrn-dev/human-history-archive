# Charlotte Brontë

Charlotte Brontë (1816–1855) was an English novelist, recognized for the intensity and rough individuality of her work. She was one of the three daughters of Patrick Brontë, a Church of England clergyman, and Maria Branwell.

## Early Life and Education
Charlotte was born in Thornton, some three miles from Bradford. Along with her sisters Emily and Anne, she was raised in Haworth, West Riding of Yorkshire, where her father served as the perpetual incumbent of the parish for the final forty-one years of his life. Following the death of her mother, the children were cared for by their aunt, Elizabeth Branwell.

In 1824, Charlotte and her sisters Maria and Emily were sent to the Clergy Daughters' school at Cowan Bridge. While Maria and Elizabeth died shortly after returning home in 1825, Charlotte and Emily returned to a prolonged home life at Haworth. During this time, the surviving children engaged in study and developed precocious literary aspirations, producing small booklets of essays, verses, and stories. 

At age sixteen, in 1831, Charlotte attended Miss Margaret Wooler's school at Roe Head, Dewsbury. She left in 1832, having gained proficiency in composition, French, and drawing, and formed lifelong friendships with Ellen Nussey and Mary Taylor. In 1835, she returned to Roe Head as a governess. After returning to Haworth in 1838, she declined two marriage proposals—one from Henry Nussey and another from a curate named Bryce—and worked as a nursery governess for the Whites at Rawdon and the Sidgwicks of Stonegappe.

In February 1842, seeking independence and the ability to open her own school, Charlotte and Emily studied at the Pensionnat Héger in Brussels. They were recalled to England before the year ended due to the illness of their aunt, Miss Branwell, who died on October 29, 1842. Miss Branwell's bequest provided the sisters with the financial means to reconsider their life plans.

## Literary Career
The Brontë sisters first attempted to publish a volume of poetry in 1846 under the pseudonyms Currer, Ellis, and Acton Bell. The collection, published by Aylott & Jones, was a commercial failure, selling only two copies. Charlotte contributed nineteen poems to the volume.

Charlotte's first novel, *The Professor* (originally titled *The Master*), was submitted to the firm Smith, Elder & Co. In August 1847, the firm's reader, R. Smith Williams, informed her that while the work had merit, it was too short for the required three-volume format. In response, Charlotte submitted a longer novel, *Jane Eyre*, which was published in October 1847 to wild acclaim, though it later received a critical review in the *Quarterly* in December 1848 by Miss Rigby (Lady Eastlake).

Following the deaths of her brother Branwell and sister Emily in 1848, Charlotte worked on *Shirley*. She paused the writing to nurse her sister Anne, completing the novel after Anne's death in May 1849. *Shirley* was published in October 1849. In 1852, she wrote *Villette*, which was published in January 1853.

## Personal Life and Later Years
Between 1849 and 1851, Charlotte visited London as a guest of her publisher, George Smith. During these visits, she was introduced to Thackeray (to whom she dedicated *Jane Eyre*) and sat for a portrait by George Richmond. In August 1850, while visiting the English lakes as a guest of Sir James Kay-Shuttleworth, she met Matthew Arnold, Miss Martineau, and Mrs Gaskell. In 1851, she received a marriage offer from Mr. James Taylor, an employee of her publishers, but she did not marry him.

In 1854, Charlotte promised to marry Arthur Bell Nicholls (1817–1906), her father's curate, who had been a persistent suitor despite the discouragement of Mr. Brontë. They were married at Haworth church on June 29, 1854, with the ceremony performed by the Rev. Sutcliffe Sowden. The couple spent their honeymoon in Ireland before returning to Haworth to live with Mr. Brontë, where Mr. Nicholls continued his role as curate.

## Biographies and Legacy
The first major biography, *Life of Charlotte Brontë* by Mrs Gaskell, was published in 1857. The third edition was revised due to controversies regarding the identity of "Lowood" in *Jane Eyre* (linked to Cowan Bridge school), the relationship between Branwell Brontë and his employer's wife, and the peculiarities of Mr. Brontë. The 1902 Haworth edition is considered the most satisfactory version and includes letters from Charlotte to George Smith.

Other biographical works include T. Wemyss Reid's *Charlotte Brontë: a Monograph* (1877), Augustine Birrell's *Life of Charlotte Brontë* (1887), and William Wright's *The Brontës in Ireland* (1893). Clement Shorter also published *Charlotte Brontë and her Circle* (1896), *Charlotte Brontë and her Sisters* (1906), and *The Brontës: Life and Letters* (1907), the latter of which contains Charlotte's letters in chronological order.
