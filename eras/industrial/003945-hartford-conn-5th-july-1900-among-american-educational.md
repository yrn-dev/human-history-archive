# Henry Barnard and Educational Reform in Connecticut

## Early Life and Legal Career
Henry Barnard was an American educationalist born in Hartford, Connecticut, on January 24, 1811. He pursued his higher education at Yale, from which he graduated in 1830. Following his studies, he entered the legal profession and was admitted to the Connecticut bar in 1835.

## Legislative Work and State Reform
Between 1837 and 1839, Barnard served as a member of the Connecticut legislature. During his tenure in 1838, he framed and introduced a bill that was subsequently passed to provide for "the better supervision of the common schools." This legislation established a board of "commissioners of common schools" within the state. Barnard served as the secretary of this board from 1838 until it was abolished in 1842. Throughout this period, he worked indefatigably to reform and reorganize the state's common school system, a pursuit that earned him a national reputation as an educational reformer.

## Regional and National Educational Leadership
Barnard's influence extended beyond Connecticut. In 1843, the governor of Rhode Island appointed him as an agent to examine the state's public schools and recommend improvements, leading to a reorganization of the school system two years later. He served as the first commissioner of public schools in Rhode Island from 1845 to 1849, an administration characterized by a decided step in educational progress.

Upon returning to Connecticut, Barnard held the position of "superintendent of common schools" from 1851 to 1855 and served as the principal of the State Normal School at New Britain, Connecticut. His career later included several high-level administrative roles:
* **University of Wisconsin:** Chancellor and agent of the board of regents of the normal school fund (1859–1860).
* **St John's College, Annapolis, Maryland:** President (1866).
* **United States Commissioner of Education:** As the first person to hold this office from 1867 to 1870, he established the foundation for the future work of the Bureau of Education.

## Editorial Contributions
Barnard's most significant service to education was his work as an editor. From 1855 to 1881, he edited the *American Journal of Education*. The resulting thirty-one volumes are described as a veritable encyclopaedia of education and one of the most valuable compendiums of information on the subject ever assembled by a single individual. Additionally, he edited the *Journal of the Rhode Island Institute of Instruction* from 1846 to 1849, and the *Connecticut Common School Journal* during two separate periods: 1838 to 1842 and 1851 to 1854.

## Death
Henry Barnard died in Hartford, Connecticut, on July 5, 1900.
