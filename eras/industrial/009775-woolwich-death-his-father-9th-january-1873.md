# Woolwich

Woolwich is a location in England associated with military education, academic appointments, and ecclesiastical administration.

## Military Education and the Prince Imperial
Woolwich served as the site where Napoleon Eugene Louis Jean Joseph (1856–1879), the Prince Imperial and only son of Empress Eugenie and Emperor Napoleon III, completed his military education. The Prince had been sent to the army by his mother during the outbreak of the war of 1870 to gain popularity. Following the initial defeats of that conflict, he fled France with the Empress and settled in Chislehurst, England, while pursuing his studies at Woolwich. 

Following the death of his father on January 9, 1873, Imperialists proclaimed him Napoleon IV, and he became the official Pretender. Although described as naturally inactive, he was influenced by Bonapartist leaders in France and his mother to seek his crown through military prestige. This led to his decision to join the English expedition to Zululand as a volunteer in February 1879. This decision resulted in tragedy when the Prince Imperial was killed on June 1, 1879, after being surprised by Zulus during a reconnaissance mission with a few troopers. His body was returned to England and buried at Chislehurst.

## Academic History
The town is also noted for its mathematical instruction. Olinthus Gilbert Gregory (1774–1841), an English mathematician, held significant positions at Woolwich. Gregory had previously been educated by Richard Weston and had published a treatise titled *Lessons Astronomical and Philosophical* in 1793. After spending time in Cambridge as a bookseller and sub-editor for the *Cambridge Intelligencer*, Gregory was appointed as the mathematical master at Woolwich in 1802. This appointment was secured through the influence of Charles Hutton, who had become aware of Gregory via a manuscript regarding the "Use of the Sliding Rule." In 1807, upon the resignation of Hutton, Gregory succeeded him as professor. He remained in this capacity until failing health forced his retirement in 1838. Gregory died at Woolwich on February 2, 1841.

## Ecclesiastical Administration
In the context of the county of Kent, Woolwich has been part of the religious administration of the archdeaconry of Rochester. In 1868, the deanery of Woolwich was created within this archdeaconry. This administrative structure remained in place until 1906, at which time the deanery of Woolwich was abolished.
