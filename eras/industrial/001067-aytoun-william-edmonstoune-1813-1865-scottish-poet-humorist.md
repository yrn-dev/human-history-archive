# AYTOUN, WILLIAM EDMONSTOUNE (1813-1865)

William Edmonstoune Aytoun was a Scottish miscellaneous writer, humorist, and poet. Born in Edinburgh on June 21, 1813, he was the only son of Roger Aytoun, who served as a writer to the signet. He belonged to the same family stock as Sir Robert Aytoun. Aytoun's early tastes in literature, his political sympathies, his admiration for the Stuarts, and his love for ballad poetry were derived from his mother, a woman of considerable culture and marked originality of character.

## Education and Legal Career
Aytoun began his formal education at the Edinburgh Academy at the age of eleven, later progressing to the university. In 1833, he spent several months in London to study law. Following this, in September 1833, he traveled to Aschaffenburg to study German, where he remained until April 1834. 

Upon his return, Aytoun resumed his legal studies in the chambers of his father. He was admitted as a writer to the signet in 1835 and was called to the Scottish bar five years later. Despite these qualifications, Aytoun confessed that although he followed the law, he "never could overtake it."

## Literary Works and Journalism
Aytoun's first publication appeared in 1832, a volume titled *Poland, Homer, and other Poems*, which reflected his eager interest in the state of Poland. During his time in Germany, he produced a translation of the first part of *Faust* in blank verse; however, this work was never published as other translations had forestalled it.

In 1836, Aytoun began contributing to *Blackwood's Magazine* through translations from Uhland. He remained a member of the *Blackwood* staff from 1839 until his death. Around 1841, he formed an association with Mr. (later Sir) Theodore Martin. Together, they wrote a series of light, humorous papers concerning the follies and tastes of the era. These papers included verses that were later published in 1855 as the *Ban Gaultier Ballads*.

Aytoun's primary reputation as a poet is based on the *Lays of the Scottish Cavaliers*, published in 1848 (with a 29th edition appearing in 1883).

## Academic and Political Life
In 1845, Aytoun was appointed as the professor of *belles lettres* and rhetoric at Edinburgh University. His lectures were considered very attractive, which led to a corresponding increase in the number of students. Beyond his academic and literary pursuits, Aytoun provided services in support of the Tory party, particularly during the Anti-Corn Law period.
