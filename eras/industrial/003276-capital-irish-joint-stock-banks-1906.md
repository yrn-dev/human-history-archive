# Capital of Irish Joint-Stock Banks in 1906

The banking landscape in Ireland during the early 20th century was characterized by a mix of joint-stock banks, some of which held the privilege of issuing notes. Unlike other regions in the three kingdoms, Ireland maintained the limit of authorized circulation established by Peel's Act of 1845. As of 1906, none of the six banks that possessed the privilege of issue at the time of that Act had lost it.

Of these institutions, the Hibernian Bank, Royal Bank, and Munster Bank, Ltd. were not banks of issue.

## Banking Infrastructure and Circulation
The number of banking offices in Ireland grew significantly between the mid-19th century and the early 20th century. In 1844, there were 180 offices, resulting in one office for every 45,417 inhabitants. By 1906, the number of offices had increased to 777, reducing the ratio to one office for every 5,738 inhabitants.

Regarding note circulation, the average circulation in Ireland for 1906 was L6,452,000. This contributed to the total United Kingdom circulation of L43,372,000.

## Historical Context of Irish Banking Statistics
The availability of trustworthy statistics for Irish banking has historically been limited. While total deposits and cash balances for Irish joint-stock banks were published collectively starting in 1840 by Dr. Neilson Hancock, a general position based on more than an estimate only became possible in recent years. This was due to a long-standing reluctance by many banks to allow the publication of their balance sheets.
