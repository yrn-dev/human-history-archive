# Events and Figures of 1837-1838

The years 1837 and 1838 were marked by significant developments in the fields of medicine, literature, science, and the arts, as well as the administrative growth of American settlements.

## Medical and Scientific Developments
In the United States, the practice of homoeopathy continued to expand during this period. The system became known in Connecticut, Maryland, Delaware, and Kentucky in 1837, and spread to Massachusetts between 1837 and 1838, and to Vermont in 1838.

In the realm of astronomy, 1837 saw the construction of an astronomical observatory at Williams College. This project was built under the direction and suggestion of Albert Hopkins, who had served as a professor of mathematics and natural philosophy until 1838, at which point he became a professor of natural philosophy and astronomy. The observatory is noted as being the first in the United States built at a college specifically for instructional purposes.

## Literature and Military History
The late 1830s saw the publication of various literary and historical works. The author Hook published the novel *Jack Brag* in 1837, followed by *Gurney Married* in 1838. 

In military history, General von Grolmann provided much of the material for von Damitz's *Gesch. des Feldzugs 1815*, which was published in Berlin between 1837 and 1838. Additionally, the military figure Marmont published a four-volume work titled *Voyage en Hongrie* in 1837 and *Voyage en Sicile* in 1838.

## Performing Arts and Opera
The period was a time of activity for various operatic figures. Giulia Grisi, a brilliant dramatic soprano and prima donna, was known for her associations in London with singers such as Rubini, Tamburini, Mario, and Lablache. 

The Irish composer Michael William Balfe was also active during this era. Having returned to England in 1833, he achieved success with *The Siege of Rochelle* (1835) and *The Maid of Artois* (1836).

## Regional Development in the United States
The settlement of Burlington, Iowa, underwent several administrative changes during these years. Originally laid out as a town called Flint Hills in 1834, it was incorporated as a town in 1837. In 1837, a newspaper called the *Wisconsin Territorial Gazette* was established there. By 1838, the population of the settlement had reached 1,200, and it was chartered as a city by the territory of Wisconsin. Furthermore, the territorial legislature of Wisconsin met in Burlington from 1836 to 1838, after which the territorial legislature of Iowa met there from 1838 to 1840.
