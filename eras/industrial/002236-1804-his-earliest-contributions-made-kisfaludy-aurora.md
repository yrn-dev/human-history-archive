# Joseph Bajza

Joseph Bajza (1804–1858) was a Hungarian critic and poet born at Szucsi. He is noted for his contributions to Hungarian literature, dramatic criticism, and historical writing, as well as his involvement in political journalism.

## Literary Career and Editing
Bajza began his literary career by contributing to *Aurora*, a literary paper. He eventually served as the editor of *Aurora* from 1830 to 1837. In addition to this role, he wrote extensively for several other publications, including the *Athenaeum*, the *Figyelmezo* (or *Observer*), and the *Kritische Blaetter*. Among his various miscellaneous writings, his criticisms regarding dramatic art were regarded as his best work.

In 1830, Bajza published *Auslaendische Buehna*, a collection of translations of foreign dramas. He later published a collection of his own poems in 1835.

## Theatre and Historical Works
In 1837, Bajza was appointed as the director of the national theatre at Pest, which had been newly established. Following this period, he spent several years focusing on historical writing. During this time, he published three major works:
* *Historical Library* (*Tortereti Konyvtar*), published in six volumes between 1843 and 1845.
* *Modern Plutarch* (*Uj Plutarch*), published between 1845 and 1847.
* *Universal History* (*Vilagtoretet*), published in 1847.

These historical publications were, to some extent, translations of authors from Germany.

## Political Journalism and Later Life
Bajza was involved in the political press of the late 1840s. In 1847, while in Leipzig, he edited the opposition journal *Ellenoer*. Subsequently, in March 1848, he was appointed by Kossuth to serve as the editor of the paper *Kossuth Hirlapja*.

In 1850, Bajza was afflicted with brain disease. He died in 1858.
