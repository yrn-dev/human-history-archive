# Attempt at a Mineralogical Description of Landeck

The *Attempt at a Mineralogical Description of Landeck* is the English translation of a scientific work authored by Christian Leopold von Buch, Baron. Published in Edinburgh in 1810, the text originated from von Buch's earlier German publication, *Versuch einer mineralogischen Beschreibung von Landeck*, which was first released in Breslau in 1797. Prior to the English edition, the work had also been translated into French and published in Paris in 1805.

## Author Biography
Christian Leopold von Buch (1774–1853) was a German geographer and geologist born on April 26, 1774, at Stolpe in Pomerania. He was a member of a noble and ancient Prussian family. His academic training began between 1790 and 1793 at the mining school of Freiberg, where he studied under Werner. During this period, Alexander von Humboldt was one of his fellow students. Von Buch later completed his higher education at the universities of Göttingen and Halle.

## Scientific Context and Evolution
At the time of his early publications, von Buch was a zealous supporter of the Neptunian theory championed by his master, Werner. In his earlier works, including the descriptions of Landeck, he advocated for the aqueous origin of other formations and basaltic ones.

However, von Buch's scientific convictions shifted following extensive field research. In 1797, he explored the Alps and the geological formations of Styria with Humboldt. In the following spring, he traveled to Italy, where his belief in the Neptunian theory began to be shaken. This transition continued in 1802 when he explored the extinct volcanoes of Auvergne. Specifically, the strata of basaltic lava and the trachyte cone of the Puy de Dôme led him to conclude that Werner's doctrines regarding the formation of these rocks were untenable.

## Related Works and Research
In addition to the Landeck description, von Buch published *Entwurf einer geognostischen Beschreibung von Schlesien (Geognostische Beobachtungen auf Reisen durch Deutschland und Italien, Band i.)* in 1802. He later embodied the results of his various investigations in *Geognostische Beobachtungen auf Reisen durch Deutschland und Italien*, published in Berlin between 1802 and 1809.

Von Buch's research also extended to volcanology and climatology. He visited Vesuvius in 1799 and returned in 1805 with Gay Lussac and Humboldt. During this second visit, they witnessed a remarkable eruption that provided von Buch with the necessary data to refute several erroneous ideas regarding volcanoes. Furthermore, he spent two years among the Scandinavian islands, where he conducted important observations on geology, climatology, and the geography of plants. Through this work, he demonstrated that many erratic blocks found on the North German plains must have been moved.
