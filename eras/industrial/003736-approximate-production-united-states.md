# APPROXIMATE PRODUCTION FOR THE UNITED STATES

## Dairy Production
At the close of the 19th century, it was estimated that the United States had approximately 17,500,000 milch cows, averaging one cow for every four persons. These animals were distributed unevenly, with a high concentration in the great dairy states; Iowa led this group with 1,500,000 cows, followed closely by New York. To maintain the supply of cheese, butter, and milk for the country and for export, between 23 and 27 cows per 100 people were required. While the export trade fluctuated, it never exceeded the produce of 500,000 cows.

In a typical year, the production and value of dairy products were as follows:
*   **Butter:** Produced by 11,000,000 cows at a rate of 130 lb. per cow, totaling 1,430,000,000 lb. with a value of $257,400,000 (at 18 cents per lb.).
*   **Cheese:** Produced by 1,000,000 cows at a rate of 300 lb. per cow, totaling 300,000,000 lb. with a value of $27,000,000 (at 9 cents per lb.).
*   **Milk:** Produced by 5,500,000 cows at a rate of 380 gallons per cow, totaling 2,090,000,000 gallons with a value of $167,200,000 (at 8 cents per gallon).

The grand total for these three products represented a value of $451,600,000. When including calves dropped yearly and the feeding value of whey, butter-milk, and skim-milk, the annual aggregate value of dairy cow produce exceeded $500,000,000, or more than one hundred million pounds sterling. Geographically, milk production largely supplied large cities and towns in the eastern and middle states, while butter was the primary dairy product in the north-west, west, and central regions.

## Fencing Production
The United States saw a significant increase in the production of wire fencing between 1874 and 1907. Barbed wire was initially popular, particularly with railroads, but meshed wire field fencing saw increased demand beginning in 1899 due to the high cost and scarcity of labour and the decline in rail fence construction. Meshed wire was viewed by many as a superior enclosure for hogs and sheep compared to barbed wire.

Production statistics for the period include:
*   **1874:** 5 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1875:** 300 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1876:** 1,500 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1877:** 7,000 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1878:** 13,000 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1879:** 25,000 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1880:** 40,000 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1890:** 125,000 tons of barbed wire.
*   **1900:** 200,000 tons of barbed wire and 50,000 tons of meshed field fencing.
*   **1907:** 250,000 tons of barbed wire and 425,000 tons of meshed field fencing.

## Hop Cultivation
The distribution of hop-cultivation areas in the United States underwent great changes during the first decade of the 20th century and the last decades of the 19th century. New York served as the chief hop-growing state during the earlier part of this period. However, toward the end of this timeframe, hop-growing extended greatly onto the Pacific coast, specifically in the states of Washington, California, and Oregon, where the mild climate and richness of the soil favored the bines.

## Beer Production
In terms of liquid bulk, the United States is the second-greatest beer-producing nation, following Germany and preceding the United Kingdom. American beers of the lager type (bottom fermentation products) are very similar in composition to German beers, while American ales are significantly heavier than the general run of corresponding British products. Despite its high production rank, the consumption of beer per head in the United States is very much lower than in both Germany and the United Kingdom.
