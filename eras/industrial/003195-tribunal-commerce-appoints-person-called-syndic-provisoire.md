# Bankruptcy and the Tribunal of Commerce in France

In the French legal system, bankruptcy is governed by the Commercial Code of 1807, which was subsequently supplemented and amended by the law of 9th June 1838. Under Article 437 of this code, bankruptcy is defined as the state of a trader who is unable to meet his commercial engagements.

## Classifications of Insolvency
The French system distinguishes between different types of insolvency. Simple insolvency is referred to as *faillite*. When insolvency is accompanied by fraud or circumstances of misconduct, it is categorized as either *banqueroute frauduleuse* or *banqueroute simple*. 

The laws regulating bankruptcy are designed exclusively for the protection of commerce; consequently, only a trader can be declared bankrupt, and the debt required for adjudication must be a commercial debt. A trader does not necessarily need to be insolvent to be made a bankrupt; it is sufficient that the individual has suspended payment. This liability extends to commercial companies of all kinds, which are declared bankrupt in the same manner as individual traders.

## Adjudication Process
A trader-debtor may be adjudicated bankrupt through three different avenues:
1. Upon the debtor's own petition.
2. Upon the petition of a creditor.
3. By the court itself, acting *proprio motu*.

If a debtor petitions for bankruptcy, they are required to file a declaration of suspension at the office of the district's Tribunal of Commerce within fifteen days. This filing must include a true account of the state of their affairs and their conduct, specifically detailing personal expenses, debts, assets, profits, and losses.

## Role of the Tribunal of Commerce
Upon the adjudication of bankruptcy, the Tribunal of Commerce performs specific appointments to manage the legal proceedings. The Tribunal appoints a person known as a *syndic provisoire*, whose responsibility is to manage the estate of the bankrupt party. To ensure oversight, a *juge commissaire* is also named to supervise the *syndic provisoire*.

Tribunals of commerce are established in various French cities; for example, such tribunals are located in Avignon and Bordeaux. In these cities, the tribunals of commerce exist alongside other legal and commercial entities, such as tribunals of first instance, chambers of commerce, councils of trade-arbitrators, and branches of the Bank of France.

## Termination and Rehabilitation
A bankruptcy proceeding can terminate in one of three ways: through a sale of the debtor's assets (*union*), an ordinary composition (*concordat*), or a composition by relinquishment of assets.

A distinct feature of the French system is the treatment of remaining debts. Even if a discharge in bankruptcy is granted—and even when accompanied by a *declaration d'excusabilite*—any unpaid balance remains a debt of honour. This reflects a spirit originating from a resolution passed by the National Convention during the French Revolution, which stated that any man who contracted a debt should never be free from the liability to pay it. Consequently, a trader is not rehabilitated and continues to suffer various disabilities until every penny owed has been paid.
