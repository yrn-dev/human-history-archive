# Cantilever Bridges

## Overview and Engineering Principles
Cantilever bridges are a system of bridging that is often the simplest and most economical method for certain positions, particularly for spans greater than those practicable with independent girders. In this system, spans are virtually reduced to smaller spans regarding stresses. A primary advantage of the cantilever design is the method of construction; cantilevers can be built out from the piers member by member. This eliminates the need for temporary scaffolding below, which saves costs, avoids the difficulties of building in deep water, and ensures that navigation is not interrupted. The center girder may be lifted from the water level or rolled into place once the cantilevers are established.

While economical, the cantilever system is unstable unless it is rigidly fixed at the piers. In some designs, such as the Forth bridge, stability is achieved through the great width of the river piers and a significant excess of dead load over live load.

## The Forth Bridge
Located at the only narrowing of the Forth in a distance of 50 miles, the Forth bridge was begun in 1882 and completed in 1889. The original design for a stiffened suspension bridge was abandoned following the fall of the Tay bridge in 1879. The bridge is situated where the channel, approximately a mile wide, is divided by the island of Inchgarvie.

The cantilever bridge has a total length of 5330 ft., consisting of:
*   A central tower on Inchgarvie: 260 ft.
*   Fife and Queensferry piers: 145 ft. each.
*   Two central girders between cantilevers: 350 ft. each.
*   Six cantilevers: 680 ft. each.

The two main spans each measure 1710 ft., with an extreme tower height of 361 ft. above high water and a clear headway of 157 ft. To balance the rolling load, the central girder's half weight, and an additional 200 tons, the outer ends of the shore cantilevers are loaded. The structure utilizes 38,000 tons of steel (excluding approach viaducts) and features an internal viaduct of lattice girders for a double line of rails. The design includes provisions for distortion caused by the sun acting on one side, wind acting on one side, and longitudinal expansion due to temperature changes.

## Other Notable Cantilever Bridges
Various other cantilever structures have been implemented globally for railway and road transport:

*   **Lansdowne Bridge:** Completed in 1889 at Sukkur over the Indus for a single line of railway (5 ft. 6 in. gauge). It has a clear span of 790 ft. and a suspended girder 200 ft. in length. The span between the centers of the end uprights is 820 ft. Its anchors are buried in concrete and made of angle bars and steel plates. The most strained parts are the back guys, which are designed for a stress of 1200 tons resulting from wind pressure, the rolling load on half the bridge, the weight of the cantilever, and half the weight of the center girder.
*   **Quebec Bridge:** A bridge over the St Lawrence designed for a highway, electric railway, and two lines of rails. It collapsed during construction in 1907. It had a total length between abutments of 3240 ft., a channel span of 1800 ft., a suspended span of 675 ft., and shore spans of 562½ ft., with a total metal weight of about 32,000 tons.
*   **Jubilee Bridge:** Located over the Hugli and designed by Sir Bradford Leslie, this bridge carries a double line of railway. It features a central double cantilever 360 ft. long and two side span girders 420 ft. long. The cantilever rests on two river piers 120 ft. apart (center to center).
*   **Poughkeepsie Bridge:** Built between 1886 and 1887 over the Hudson, this bridge consists of two shore spans and five river spans. It features girder spans of 525 ft., cantilever spans of 547 ft., and shore spans of 201 ft.
*   **Red Rock Bridge:** A cantilever bridge over the Colorado river with a center span of 660 ft.
*   **Roseburgh Bridge:** A combination bridge in Oregon utilizing both timber and steel. The total distance between anchor piers is 584 ft., with river arms of 105 ft., shore arms of 147 ft., and a suspended girder of 80 ft. Compression members are generally timber, though the bottom chord panels next the river piers and the struts are steel.

## Hybrid and Specialized Applications
Cantilever elements are sometimes integrated into other bridge types. The Tower Bridge in London is primarily a suspension bridge, but its high-level footways use girders of the suspended girder and cantilever type. These cantilevers are fixed to the shore side of the towers, with middle girders 120 ft. in length attached via links.

Additionally, some independent spans are erected by building them out as cantilevers and then converting them into independent girders after the erection is complete.
