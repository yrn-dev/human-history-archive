# Single-Ended Large Tube Boilers and Related Water-Tube Systems

The design and implementation of large tube boilers vary significantly depending on their application, whether for naval vessels, merchant steamers, or land-based installations. These systems are characterized by their configuration of steam chests, water chambers, and the arrangement of tubes used to create heating surfaces.

## The Yarrow Boiler
The Yarrow boiler is utilized extensively in the British navy and several other navies. Its structure consists of two lower water-chambers and a large cylindrical steam chest, which are linked by numerous straight tubes. The diameter of these tubes varies based on the boiler's purpose:
* **Large vessels:** All tubes have an external diameter of 1-3/4 inches.
* **Large express boilers:** The two rows nearest the fire on each side are 1-1/4 inches in diameter, while the remaining tubes are 1 inch in diameter.

The tubes are spaced with their centers forming equilateral triangles, allowing for external cleaning from the front of the boiler and cross-ways in two directions. To improve circulation, some models connect the lower part of the steam-chest to the water-chambers via large pipes located outside the casings.

Regarding scale, the largest single-ended large tube boiler in use features a steam drum with a diameter of 4 feet 2 inches, a heating surface of 3,750 square feet, and a grate area of 73.5 square feet. However, double-ended boilers, which are fired from both ends, have been constructed in much larger sizes.

## Access and Maintenance
Maintenance access depends on the size of the boiler. In most Yarrow boilers, manholes in the water-chamber ends and the steam-chest provide internal access. In smaller versions used in torpedo boats, the water-chambers are too small for manholes; instead, they are constructed in two parts joined by a bolted joint to make the tube ends accessible.

## Comparative Water-Tube Designs
Other water-tube boilers employ different configurations of chambers and tubes to manage combustion and circulation:

* **Normand Boiler:** Similar to the Yarrow in having three chambers, the Normand boiler uses bent tubes for its heating surface and large outside circulating tubes at each end. It features "water-walls" created by the two outer rows of tubes (which touch for nearly their whole length) and two inner rows of bent tubes to force combustion products through a definite course.
* **Blechynden and White-Foster Boilers:** These also utilize three chambers connected by bent tubes. In the Blechynden model, tubes can be removed through small doors in the upper steam-chest; in the White-Foster model, they are removed via the steam-chest end manhole.
* **Reed Boiler:** This design uses longer, more curved tubes than the Normand boiler and lacks water-walls, allowing combustion products to pass directly from the fire-grate to the chimney. A distinguishing feature is the use of screw and nut connections at each tube end rather than expansion into the tube plate, allowing for quick replacement.
* **Thornycroft Boiler:** This express boiler consists of three chambers connected by tubes that are straight for most of their length but bent at the ends. It employs water-walls at the lower parts of the outer rows and at the upper parts of the inner rows to ensure gases pass over all heating surfaces.

## Industrial and Marine Applications
Different boiler types are tailored for specific environments:

**Babcock & Wilcox Boilers**
For land purposes, this boiler consists of a horizontal cylinder steam chest with dished ends and two riveted cross-boxes at the bottom. A sloping nest of tubes is placed under the cylinder, with the fire located under the upper end. Brickwork baffles ensure combustion products pass between the tubes three times. These boilers can reach pressures of 500 lb. per sq. in. in special cases, though 180 lb. is more common.

The marine version of the Babcock & Wilcox boiler is used in British and American navies, as well as in merchant steamers and yachts. It features a horizontal cylindrical steam-chest placed transversely over elements. These elements consist of front and back headers made of wrought steel connected by inclined water-tubes.

**Multitubular Return Tube Boilers**
Common in merchant steamers, these cylindrical boilers range up to 17 feet in diameter. They are available in double-ended and single-ended types. Single-ended boilers, the most common variety, have furnaces at one end only, ranging from one to four furnaces (three being the most common). Working pressures typically range from 160 to 200 lb. per sq. in., though some reach 265 lb. per sq. in.
