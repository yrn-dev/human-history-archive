# BADHAM, CHARLES (1813-1884)

Charles Badham was an English scholar and textual critic who spent the latter part of his career in Australia. Born on July 18, 1813, in Ludlow, Shropshire, he was the son of Charles Badham, a regius professor of physic at Glasgow and an excellent classical scholar who translated Juvenal. His mother was a cousin of the poet Thomas Campbell.

## Education and Early Academic Career
Badham's education began in Switzerland, where he was a pupil of Pestalozzi starting around the age of seven. He later attended Eton and was elected to a scholarship at Wadham College, Oxford, in 1830. He obtained a third class in classics in 1836; this result may have been influenced by his dislike of the study methods fashionable at Oxford during a period when classical scholarship was in an unsatisfactory state.

After receiving his degree in 1837, Badham traveled to Italy to study ancient manuscripts, focusing specifically on those held in the Vatican library. During this period, he established a lifelong friendship with G. C. Cobet. Following his time in Italy, he spent time in Germany. Upon returning to England, he was incorporated as an M.A. at Peterhouse, Cambridge, in 1847.

## Professional Appointments and Degrees
Badham entered holy orders and served as a headmaster in two different locations: first at Louth grammar school in Lincolnshire from 1851 to 1854, and subsequently at the Edgbaston proprietary school near Birmingham. 

His academic credentials expanded throughout his career. He earned a D.D. from Cambridge in 1852 and was awarded an honorary degree of doctor of letters from the university of Leiden in 1860. In 1866, he left England to accept the professorship of classics and logic at Sydney University, a position he held until his death on February 26, 1884.

## Scholarship and Reputation
Dr. Badham was recognized as a scholar of European reputation and eminence, particularly in the field of textual criticism. His classical attainments were acknowledged by several prominent European critics, including Tischendorf, A. Ritschl, J. A. F. Meineke, F. W. Schneidewin, W. Dindorf, Ludwig Preller, and G. C. Cobet.

Despite his academic standing and his ability as a teacher, Badham did not achieve professional success. It is noted that his advancement may have been hindered by a hasty temper and a dislike of anything that approached disingenuousness. Consequently, despite his international reputation, he made a comparatively small mark in his native country.

## Personal Life
Charles Badham was married twice.
