# The Bessemer Process: Making Steel Affordable

## Overview
Before the mid-19th century, steel was a luxury material. While stronger and more flexible than cast iron, it was produced in small batches via the crucible process, making it prohibitively expensive for large-scale infrastructure. The landscape of the Industrial Revolution shifted dramatically in 1856 with the introduction of the Bessemer process.

Developed by Sir Henry Bessemer in England, this method revolutionized metallurgy by introducing a way to remove impurities from molten pig iron through oxidation. By blowing air through the molten metal in a pear-shaped vessel known as a "converter," the oxygen reacted with impurities—primarily carbon, silicon, and manganese—burning them off as gas or slag. This process not only accelerated production from days to mere minutes but also slashed the cost of steel, enabling the construction of skyscrapers, massive bridges, and expansive railway networks that defined the modern urban landscape.

Historians often debate the extent to which Bessemer "invented" the process. While he patented the converter, evidence suggests that William Kelly in the United States had developed a similar pneumatic process independently around the same time. The subsequent legal and professional disputes highlight the collaborative yet competitive nature of Victorian-era innovation.

## Key Facts
*   **Oxidation:** The core mechanism was the use of air to burn out carbon, which previously required expensive fuel and manual labor.
*   **Infrastructure Boom:** The affordability of steel allowed for the transition from timber and iron to steel-frame construction, facilitating the growth of cities like Chicago and New York.
*   **Economic Shift:** Steel production shifted the global economic balance, fueling the rapid industrialization of the United States and Germany.
*   **Material Properties:** Unlike brittle cast iron, Bessemer steel possessed the tensile strength necessary for high-pressure boilers and heavy-load rails.

## Did You Know?
*   **The "Air" Secret:** The Bessemer process is exothermic; the reaction between the oxygen and the impurities generates so much heat that no external fuel is needed once the process begins.
*   **The Phosphorus Problem:** Early Bessemer converters couldn't remove phosphorus, meaning only specific "low-phosphorus" ores could be used until the Gilchrist-Thomas lining (basic lining) was invented in 1878.
*   **Railway Revolution:** Before Bessemer, iron rails wore out every few years under heavy loads; steel rails extended that lifespan significantly, drastically lowering maintenance costs for railroads.
