# A Poet's Sketch Book and Related Works of Robert Buchanan

## Overview of Robert Buchanan's Literary Contributions
Robert Buchanan was a prolific author whose work spanned poetry, novels, and periodical literature. His poetic output was organized into three volumes in 1874, later consolidated into a single volume in 1884, and eventually published as the two-volume *Complete Poetical Works* in 1901. 

Among his notable individual poems is "The Drama of Kings" (1871) and "Balder the Beautiful" (1877). He also authored "The City of Dream" (1888), "The Outcast: a Rhyme for the Time" (1891), and "The Wandering Jew" (1893). Additionally, he published "St Abe and his Seven Wives" in 1872, which is described as a lively tale set in Salt Lake City and was released anonymously.

## Periodical Literature and Essays
Buchanan made significant contributions to periodical literature through several key works. These include *David Gray and other Essays, chiefly on Poetry* (1868) and *Master Spirits* (1873). 

In 1883, he published *A Poet's Sketch Book*, a work that is noted for reprinting an interesting essay on Gray. His contributions to this genre also include *A Look round Literature*, published in 1887.

## Novels and Other Prose
Buchanan's early novels, *The Shadow of the Sword* and *God and the Man* (1881), are characterized by a simplicity and breadth of treatment. *God and the Man* specifically tells the story of a family feud. This style is less apparent in his later novels, which include *The Martyrdom of Madeline* (1882), *Foxglove Manor* (1885), *Effie Hetherington* (1896), and *Father Anthony* (1898).

Beyond his fiction and essays, Buchanan wrote *The Land of Lorne* (1871), a two-volume work that provides a vivid record of his experiences yachting along the west coast of Scotland.

## Biographical References
Further information regarding the life of Robert Buchanan can be found in the account written by Harriett Jay, titled *Robert Buchanan; some Account of his Life*, published in 1903.
