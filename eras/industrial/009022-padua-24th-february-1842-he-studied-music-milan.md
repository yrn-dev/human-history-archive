# Arrigo Boito

Arrigo Boito (1842– ) was an Italian musical composer and poet. He was born in Padua on February 24, 1842.

## Education and Early Career
Boito pursued his musical education at the Milan Conservatoire. During his time as a student, he demonstrated a divided allegiance between music and literature, devoting an equal amount of his time to both fields. This duality would become a primary characteristic of his life's history. While studying at the Conservatoire, Boito collaborated with Franco Faccio to write and compose a cantata titled *Le Sorelle d'Italia*, which was performed successfully.

Following the completion of his studies, Boito spent several years traveling. Upon his return to Italy, he settled in Milan, where he balanced his professional life between the pursuits of music and journalism.

## Musical Compositions and Operas
In 1868, Boito conducted the first performance of his opera *Mefistofele* at the Scala theatre in Milan. The production was a complete failure and was withdrawn after its second performance. However, the work was revived in Bologna in 1875 in an abbreviated and much altered form, achieving unquestionable success. It was later performed with success in London in 1880, though it never firmly established itself in popular favor despite frequent revivals.

In *Mefistofele*, Boito approached the Faust legend with a spirit more closely aligned with the conception of Goethe than the approach found in Gounod's *Faust*. While the opera contains many isolated beauties, it is noted for a lack of dramatic interest and cohesion.

## Libretti and Literary Works
Boito later focused much of his energy on the composition of libretti. His principal works in this area include:
* *Otello* and *Falstaff*, which were set to music by Verdi.
* *La Gioconda*, set by Ponchielli.
* *Amleto*, set by Faccio.
* *Ero e Leandre*, set by Mancinelli and Bottesini.

These libretti are characterized by uncommon literary value and a rare knowledge of the requirements of dramatic poetry. In addition to his musical and dramatic contributions, Boito published a novel titled *L'Alfier Meno* and a book of poems.

## Later Life and Honors
Boito's life included a period of military service; in 1866, he fought under Garibaldi. In 1893, the university of Cambridge conferred upon him the degree of doctor of music.
