# BROCKHAUS, FRIEDRICH ARNOLD (1772-1823)

Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus was a German publisher born on May 4, 1772, in Dortmund. He received his early education at the gymnasium in his hometown before entering a mercantile house in Düsseldorf, where he served an apprenticeship from 1788 to 1793. Following this period, Brockhaus spent two years in Leipzig studying literature and modern languages.

## Early Business Ventures
After completing his studies, Brockhaus returned to Dortmund to establish an emporium for English goods. He later relocated this business to Arnheim in 1801 and subsequently to Amsterdam in the following year. In 1805, he abandoned his initial trade to begin a career as a publisher. His early efforts in this field included the projection of two journals, though neither was permitted by the government to survive for a significant length of time. Due to complications regarding the affairs of Holland, Brockhaus returned home in 1810.

## Publishing Career and the Konversations-Lexikon
In 1811, Brockhaus settled in Altenburg. Approximately three years prior to this move, he had acquired the copyright for the *Konversations-Lexikon*, a work that had been started in 1796. Between 1810 and 1811, he completed the first edition of this celebrated work. He later began a second edition in 1812, serving as its editor, which was met with universal favour.

As his business grew rapidly, Brockhaus moved to Leipzig in 1818, where he established a large printing-house. His literary undertakings expanded to include several large historical and bibliographical works, such as Ebert's *Allgemeines bibliographisches Lexikon* and Raumer's *Geschichte der Hohenstaufen*. He also produced critical periodicals, including the *Zeitgenossen*, the *Literarisches Konversationsblatt* (which later became the *Blätter für literarische Unterhaltung*), and *Hermes*.

## Death and Succession
Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus died in Leipzig on August 20, 1823. The publishing business was continued by his sons, Heinrich and Friedrich. Friedrich Brockhaus (1800-1865) remained with the business until his retirement in 1850. Heinrich Brockhaus (1804-1874) considerably extended the firm and provided significant services to science and literature. In recognition of these contributions, the university of Jena named Heinrich an honorary doctor of philosophy in 1858. Additionally, between 1842 and 1848, Heinrich served as a representative for Leipzig in the Saxon second chamber. He was made an honorary citizen of Leipzig in 1872 and died in that city on November 15, 1874.

## Family and Other Descendants
Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus had another son, Hermann Brockhaus (1806-1877), who was born in Amsterdam on January 28, 1806. Unlike his brothers, Hermann pursued an academic career as a German Orientalist. He served as an extraordinary professor in Jena starting in 1838 and moved to Leipzig in 1841 in a similar capacity, eventually becoming an ordinary professor of ancient Semitic in 1848.

Hermann Brockhaus was a scholar of the East, acquiring familiarity with its principal literature and languages, including Persian, Arabic, and Hebrew. He was also able to lecture on Pali, Zend, Chinese, and his specialty, Sanskrit. His most significant work was the *editio princeps* of the *Katha-sarit-sagara* ("The Ocean of the Streams of Story"), a 12th-century collection of Sanskrit stories by Soma Deva. This publication provided the first impetus for the scientific study of the spread and origin of popular tales, allowing others, such as Prof. Benfey, to trace many Western and Eastern stories to a Buddhistic and Indian source. Hermann's other publications included a critical edition of the "Songs of Hafiz," the text of the "Zend-Avesta" in Latin letters, and an edition of the philosophical play *Prabodhachandrodaya* ("The Rise of the Moon of Intelligence"). He died in Leipzig on January 5, 1877.
