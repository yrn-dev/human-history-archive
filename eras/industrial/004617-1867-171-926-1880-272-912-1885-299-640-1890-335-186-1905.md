# Breslau

## Overview and Geography
Breslau (known in Polish as Wraclaw) is a German city that serves as the episcopal see and the capital of the Prussian province of Silesia. The city is situated on a wide, fertile plain on both banks of the navigable Oder river, located 350 meters from the river's mouth and at the point where the Ohle flows in. In terms of transportation, it is 202 meters from Berlin via the railway to Vienna.

The Oder river divides the city into two unequal halves, which are connected by numerous bridges. The larger portion of the city is located on the left bank and contains the inner or old town. This area is surrounded by beautiful promenades that were built on the site of ramparts dismantled after 1813. Within this area is the Liebichs Höhe, which provides a fine view of the surrounding country. The new town, featuring extensive suburbs, is located across the Oder and outside the inner town. This area includes spacious squares and handsome streets, particularly in the northern Oder quarter and the southern Schweidnitz quarter.

## Demographics
The population of Breslau saw steady growth between the mid-19th century and the early 20th century. Recorded population figures are as follows:
* 1867: 171,926
* 1880: 272,912
* 1885: 299,640
* 1890: 335,186
* 1905: 470,751

As of 1905, the religious composition of the city was approximately 60% Protestant, 35% Roman Catholic, and nearly 5% Jews.

## Architecture and Religious Sites
The inner town is characterized by narrow streets and several medieval secular and religious buildings.

### Roman Catholic Churches
The city contains several notable Roman Catholic churches:
* **The Cathedral**: Dedicated to St John the Baptist, construction began in 1148 and was completed by the end of the 15th century. It underwent enlargements in the 17th and 18th centuries and was restored from 1873 to 1875. The cathedral houses sculptures, paintings, and a high altar made of beaten silver.
* **Kreuzkirche**: A brick building dating from the 13th and 14th centuries (the church of the Holy Cross). It is noted for its historical monuments, including the tomb of Henry IV, duke of Silesia, as well as its stained glass.
* **Sandkirche**: Dedicated to Our Lady on the Sand, this 14th-century church served as the church of the Augustinian canons until 1810.
* **Dorotheenor Minoritenkirche**: This church is distinguished by its high-pitched roof and was founded in 1351 by Emperor Charles IV.
