# Floyer, Unexplored Baluchistan and T. Thornton, Life of Sandeman

The works *Unexplored Baluchistan* (London, 1882) by Floyer and *Life of Sandeman* (London, 1896) by T. Thornton are cited as authorities regarding the region of Baluchistan and the administration of the khan of Kalat.

## Context of Baluchistan and Kalat
Baluchistan is described as a region that cannot be regarded as a distinct entity among Asiatic nations in the same manner as Afghanistan. While some areas exist in various degrees of independence, the region is subject to British control. British Baluchistan officially comprises the districts of Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali. Although these districts had been nominally handed over to Kalat forty years prior to 1879, they were transferred to British authority via the treaty of Gandamak in 1879. These districts now function as an official province of British Baluchistan within the Baluchistan Agency, which extends from the Persian frontier and the Gomal to the Arabian Sea.

The political status of the province is compared to that of the native states of the Indian peninsula, with supreme control practically exercised by the agent to the governor-general of India and his staff of political assistants.

## Revenues and Economy of the Khan of Kalat
The financial standing of the khan of Kalat is derived from agricultural revenue and subsidies, totaling approximately Rs.500,000 per annum. Specific components of this revenue include:
* **Quetta District:** Since 1882, the khan has received Rs.25,000 as government rent for this district.
* **The Boian:** The khan received Rs.30,000 in lieu of transit duties, a sum that was later increased by Rs.9,000.

Trade statistics for Kalat in 1899 indicate that total imports were valued at Rs.700,000, while exports were valued at Rs.505,000.

## Related Historical Documentation
In addition to the works of Floyer and T. Thornton, other sources provide ethnographic, historical, and boundary information for the region:
* **Boundary and Survey Reports:** Sir F. Goldsmid authored the Seistan Boundary Report of 1873. Sir T. Holdich produced "Ethnographic and Historical Notes on Makran" (1892) and "Antiquities, Ethnography, &c., of Las Bela and Makran" (1894).
* **Regional Memoirs and Journals:** G. P. Tate wrote *Kalat, a Memoir* (1896). The *R.G.S. Journal* contains entries on "Ancient and Medieval Makran" (1896) and the "Perso-Baluch Boundary" (1897), while McMahon contributed "The Southern Borderland of Afghanistan" (1897).
* **Biographical and Cultural Notes:** Notes regarding the tours of Sir R. Sandeman in Baluchistan are recorded in volumes v., xii., xiii., and xiv. of the *R.G.S. Proceedings*. Cultural insights are provided in M. Longworth-Dames' *Popular Poetry of the Baloches* (1907).
