# Robert Williams Buchanan

Robert Williams Buchanan (1841–1901) was a British dramatist, novelist, and poet. He was born on August 18, 1841, in Caverswall, Staffordshire. He was the son of Robert Buchanan (1813–1866), who served as a journalist and Owenite lecturer.

## Early Life and Education
Buchanan's father, a native of Ayr, lived in Manchester for several years before moving to Glasgow. It was in Glasgow that Buchanan received his education, attending both the university and the high school. During his time as a student, he formed a close friendship with the poet David Gray. This relationship and their subsequent journey to London in 1860 in pursuit of fame were later detailed in an essay by Buchanan originally published in the *Cornhill Magazine*.

## Poetic Works
Following a period of disappointment and struggle, Buchanan published his first volume, *Undertones*, in 1863, which he described as "tentative." He followed this with several other collections: *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). These works demonstrated his sympathetic insight into the humbler conditions of life and his skill in poetic narrative. In 1870, he attempted a more ambitious work of mysticism titled *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic*.

## Literary Controversies
Buchanan was a frequent contributor to periodical literature and gained notoriety through an article published in the October 1871 issue of the *Contemporary Review*. Writing under the pseudonym Thomas Maitland, he authored "The Fleshly School of Poetry," which he later expanded into a pamphlet in 1872. 

The article provoked strong responses, including a letter to the *Athenaeum* on December 16, 1871, from D.G. Rossetti titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism," as well as a response from Mr. Swinburne in *Under the Microscope* (1872). Buchanan eventually regretted the violence of his attack. He later dedicated the work *God and the Man* to Rossetti, whom he referred to as the "old enemy."

## Novels and Drama
Buchanan began a long series of novels in 1876 with the publication of *The Shadow of the Sword*, which is considered one of his best. 

In addition to his novels, Buchanan wrote several successful plays. These include:
* *Lady Clare* (produced in 1883)
* *Sophia* (1886), which was an adaptation of *Tom Jones*
* *A Man's Shadow* (1890)
* *The Charlatan* (1894)
