# 10th of September 1849: Edwin Booth's Debut

## The Performance at Boston Museum
On the 10th of September 1849, Edwin Booth made his first regular appearance as an actor at the Boston Museum. He performed the role of Tressel in a production of *Richard III*, specifically using the version written by Colley Cibber. In this production, he acted alongside his father, who played the title role of Richard.

## Physical and Personal Attributes
At the time of this debut, Edwin Booth was described as having a lithe and graceful figure and being buoyant in spirits. His physical appearance was characterized by soft, luminous, and highly expressive eyes, and dark hair that fell across his brow in waving curls.

## Critical and Familial Reception
The reception of Booth's first regular appearance was mixed. While his father watched the performance with great interest, he did so with evident disappointment. Similarly, members of the theatrical profession—who held the acting of the elder Booth in great reverence—seemed to agree that the son had not inherited the genius of his father.

## Subsequent Career Development
Following his debut, Edwin Booth made his first appearance in New York on the 27th of September 1850, playing the character of Wilford in *The Iron Chest* at the National theatre in Chatham Street. A year later, he took over the role of Richard III due to his father's illness. 

Booth did not achieve an unassailable position on the stage until after the death of his parent. Between 1856 and 1852, he performed in the Sandwich Islands, Australia, and California. His brilliant acting in these locations surprised those who had known him in the east, and from that point forward, his dramatic triumphs were warmly acknowledged.

## Later Triumphs and Management
In the later stages of his career, Booth's performances as Richelieu, Richard, and Hamlet were considered superior to those of Edwin Forrest. Additionally, his portrayal of Sir Giles Overreach in *A New Way to Pay Old Debts* was noted to have surpassed that of his father.

From 1862, Booth served as the manager of the Winter Garden theatre in New York. Between 1864 and 1867, he produced a series of Shakespearian productions of unexampled magnificence, which included *The Merchant of Venice*, *Othello*, and *Hamlet*.

## Family and Tragedy
Edwin was one of three Booth brothers, including Junius Brutus (1821–1853) and John Wilkes (1839–1865). In the autumn of 1864, the three brothers performed together in *Julius Caesar*, a production remembered for its excellence. However, the splendor of Edwin's career was dashed for many months in 1865 when his brother, John Wilkes Booth, assassinated President Lincoln.
