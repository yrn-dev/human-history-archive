# Graving Dock Dues and Infrastructure

## Financial Allowances for Graving Dock Dues
In the context of ship repairs and accidents, specific financial regulations govern the reimbursement of expenses. Graving dock dues are allowed in full. This comprehensive allowance includes the expenses associated with removals, cartages, and the use of graving dock materials, as well as the use of stages and shears. Similarly, the full expense for the labor of taking out and replacing bent ironwork, including the cost of straightening it, is allowed for all ships.

## Graving Dock Infrastructure and Technology
Graving docks are specialized facilities used for the maintenance and repair of vessels. To close these docks, sliding or rolling caissons are generally employed. Closed iron caissons, also known as ship-caissons due to their vessel-like shape, are particularly common because they are simple to use and can be readily floated into and out of position. 

Sliding caissons are sometimes utilized as alternatives to lock-gates. These require a side chamber to receive them when they are drawn back. A significant advantage of these caissons, especially in naval dockyards where heavy weights are moved, is that they provide a strong movable bridge, which removes the need for a swing-bridge across the opening. Additionally, the term caisson may refer to flat, air-tight constructions used to raise vessels for repairs or cleaning by sinking them under the ship and floating them; these are more commonly referred to as pontoons or floating dry-docks when air-chambers are added to the sides.
