# William Booth

## Early Life and Religious Development
William Booth, the founder and "general" of the Salvation Army, was born in Nottingham on April 10, 1829. At fifteen years of age, Booth experienced a strong religious turn under the influence of the Wesleyan Methodists. Within this religious body, he served as a local preacher.

## Ministerial Career and Transitions
In 1849, Booth moved to London. According to his own account, his passion for preaching in the open air led to his severance from the Wesleyans. He subsequently joined the Methodist New Connexion, where he was ordained as a minister. However, Booth left this organization in 1861 because he was not employed in the active "travelling evangelization" he desired.

During this period, in 1855, Booth married Catherine Mumford. The couple had four children, and both William and Catherine engaged in preaching. Their work took them first to Cornwall, and later to Walsall and Cardiff. While in Walsall, a "Hallelujah band" was first organized, consisting of others and converted criminals who provided public testimony regarding their conversions.

## Foundation of the Salvation Army
Booth returned to London in 1864, where he continued his services in the open air and in tents. He founded an organization that underwent several name changes before becoming the Salvation Army in 1878; its previous names included the Christian Mission, the East London Christian Mission, and the East London Revival Society.

The Salvation Army is a quasi-military organization, and Booth based its "Orders and Regulations" on the regulations of the British army. The organization operates through several specific methods:
* Holding processions and outdoor meetings.
* Visiting private houses, prisons, and public-houses.
* Conducting meetings in factories, theatres, and other unusual buildings.
* Utilizing the language of everyday life and popular song-tunes.
* Encouraging every convert to act as a daily witness for Christ in both private and public settings.

## Opposition and Global Expansion
The early campaigns of the Salvation Army met with violent opposition. A group known as the "Skeleton Army" was formed specifically to break up the Army's meetings. Consequently, for many years, the followers of Booth faced imprisonment and fines for being breakers of the peace, though these disorders had largely ceased by 1889.

Under the leadership of "General" Booth, who was described as an organizer, speaker, and indefatigable traveller, the operations of the army expanded globally. The organization extended its reach to the United States in 1880 and to Australia in 1881. It further spread to India, Ceylon, and the European continent.
