# Geological Survey of India and Related Geological Studies

The Geological Survey of India has produced a significant body of literature concerning the geological composition, mineral distribution, and stratigraphic development of various regions within India and neighboring territories. These records include detailed memoirs and records focusing on specific geographical areas and mineral occurrences.

## Geological Studies in Baluchistan and the Frontier
Research conducted through the Geological Survey of India has extensively covered the regions of Baluchistan and the frontier between Quetta and Dera Ghazi Khan. W. T. Blanford provided geological notes on the hills in the neighborhood of the Sind and Punjab Frontier in 1883. Further exploration of the Baluchistan Desert and portions of Eastern Persia was documented by E. Vredenburg in a geological sketch published in 1901. Additionally, Vredenburg contributed to the *Records of the Geological Survey of India* in 1904, specifically detailing the occurrence of a species of halorites within the Trias of Baluchistan.

## Geology of Burma
The Geological Survey of India has also documented the geology of Burma. W. Theobald published work on the geology of Pegu in 1874. Later research by F. Noetling focused on the Tertiary System in Burma, specifically its subdivision and development, in 1895. Noetling also authored a memoir in 1898 regarding the technical exploitation and occurrence of petroleum within Burma.

## Economic Geology and Mineral Distribution
Beyond regional surveys, the archive notes works on the economic geology of India, such as the second edition of T. H. Holland's *Economic Geology of India* (1898). Other related geological interests include the distribution and occurrence of corundum, as detailed in the 1906 *Bulletin of the U.S. Geological Survey* by Joseph Hyde Pratt.
