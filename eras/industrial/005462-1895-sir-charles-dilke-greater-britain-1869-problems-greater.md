# Sir Charles Dilke

Sir Charles Dilke, Bart., was a British author and amateur sportsman who contributed to the literature of British imperialism and European politics.

## Literary Contributions to Imperialism
Sir Charles Dilke authored several works concerning the British Empire and the subject of British imperialism. His writings on these topics include *Greater Britain*, published in 1869, and *Problems of Greater Britain*, published in 1890. He later wrote *The British Empire* in 1899. These works are cited as important literature for those consulting topics related to British imperial policy.

## Political and Biographical Writings
Beyond his focus on the British Empire, Dilke wrote on continental affairs, producing *The Present Position of European Politics*, which was published in London in 1887. He also engaged in biographical writing; he authored a memoir of his grandfather, C. W. Dilke, which was a friend of Thomas Hood. This memoir serves as a prefix to the work *Papers of a Critic*.

## Sporting Interests
Sir Charles Dilke was among the active English amateurs who were early to recognize the possibilities of epee-play. He is listed alongside other notable figures, such as Captain Hutton and Lord Desborough, as one of the individuals who perceived the potential of this form of fencing.
