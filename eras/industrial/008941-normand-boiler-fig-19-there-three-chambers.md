# Normand Boiler

The Normand boiler is a type of water-tube boiler characterized by a specific arrangement of chambers and tubes designed to manage heating surfaces and the flow of combustion products.

## Design and Construction
The Normand boiler features three chambers, a configuration similar to that of the Yarrow boiler. These chambers are linked by a large number of bent tubes that serve as the heating surface. Additionally, large outside circulating tubes connect the chambers at each end.

The tubes within the Normand boiler are arranged in specific rows to create "water-walls," which serve to protect the outer casing of the boiler. The two outer rows of heating tubes on each side are positioned so that they touch one another for nearly their entire length. These tubes enter the steam-chest at approximately the water-level. 

Furthermore, the two inner rows of tubes are bent into a specific form that creates a water-wall for a larger portion of the boiler's total length. This structural arrangement is designed to compel the products of combustion to follow a definite course as they pass among all the tubes.

## Comparison with Related Boilers
The Normand boiler shares similarities with several other boiler designs, though it differs in specific technical details:

*   **Yarrow Boiler:** The Normand boiler utilizes three chambers, similar to the Yarrow. However, in the "express" version of the Yarrow boiler—used in cruisers and torpedo-boat destroyers to maximize power relative to weight—the tubes are smaller in diameter and more closely arranged than in other Yarrow models.
*   **Blechynden and White-Foster Boilers:** Like the Normand, these boilers also employ three chambers connected by bent tubes. They differ in the curvature of these tubes to facilitate maintenance; in the Blechynden boiler, tubes can be removed via small doors in the upper part of the steam-chest, while in the White-Foster boiler, they are removed through a manhole at the end of the steam-chest.
*   **Reed Boiler:** The Reed boiler differs from the Normand in that its tubes are more curved and longer. Unlike the Normand, the Reed boiler does not utilize "water-walls," allowing combustion products to pass directly from the fire-grate to the chimney. The Reed boiler also uses specially designed screw and nut connections for the tubes instead of expanding them into the tube plate, allowing for quicker replacement.
