# The Railway Tunnel beneath the Lötschen Pass

The railway tunnel beneath the Lötschen Pass (also known as the Lötschberg) is a significant engineering project designed to provide direct communication between the Bernese Oberland and the Valais region.

## Geography and History of the Lötschen Pass
The Lötschen Pass is an easy glacier pass reaching an elevation of 8,842 feet. It serves as a route from Kandersteg in the Bernese Oberland to the Lötschen valley in the Valais. The pass is of great antiquity; while it was first mentioned distinctly in 1352, it was likely crossed earlier by Valaisans who colonized various areas of the Bernese Oberland. 

The pass has a history of conflict and strategic caution. Battles were fought on the pass between the Valaisans and the Bernese in 1384 and again in 1419. In 1698, a mule path was constructed on the Bernese slope, though traces of this path are all that remain because the project was not continued. This cessation was due to fears among the Valaisans that the Bernese would use the path to cross over and alter their religion. The Lötschen Pass is distinct from the Lötschenlücke, which is another easy glacier pass at 10,512 feet leading from the head of the Lötschen valley to the Great Aletsch glacier.

## Construction of the Tunnel
The piercing of the railway tunnel beneath the pass began in 1906. The tunnel spans a length of 8½ miles, starting slightly above Kandersteg and terminating at Goppenstein, located near the mouth of the Lötschen valley. The financial support for the project was provided through subsidies granted by the canton of Bern and the confederation.

## Impact on Brieg
The opening of the Lötschen Pass tunnel is calculated to further contribute to the prosperity of Brieg (also spelled Brig), a picturesque small town in the Swiss canton of the Valais. Brieg is situated on the right bank of the Saltine stream, just above its junction with the Rhone, at the foot of the northern slope of the Simplon Pass.

The town's importance and size had already been increased by other developments, including the 1807 opening of the carriage road across the Simplon and the 1906 opening of the tunnel beneath the Simplon Pass. Additionally, Brieg's location below the steeper and less fertile portion of the Upper Valais—an area frequently visited by tourists—has aided its growth. 

The town's development is reflected in its layout, with a new town extending below the old one, positioned closer to the right bank of the Rhone. As of 1900, Brieg had a population of 2,182. The demographic composition at that time consisted of 1,316 German-speaking residents, 719 Italian-speaking residents (who were workmen for the Simplon tunnel), and 142 French-speaking residents, with one person speaking Romonsch. Almost all residents were Romanists.
