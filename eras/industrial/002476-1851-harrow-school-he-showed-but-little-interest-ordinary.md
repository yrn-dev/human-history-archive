# Francis Maitland Balfour

## Early Life and Education
Francis Maitland Balfour was a British biologist born in Edinburgh on November 10, 1851. He was the younger brother of Arthur James Balfour. From an early age, his mother cultivated in him a taste for natural science, with a particular interest in geology. 

Balfour attended Harrow school, where he demonstrated very little interest in the ordinary routine of the institution. However, he found a mentor in one of the school's masters, Mr. George Griffith, who provided the aid and encouragement Balfour needed to pursue his interests in natural science.

## Academic Career at Cambridge
In 1870, Balfour entered residence at Trinity College, Cambridge. By the following year, he was elected a natural science scholar of the college. Although his approach to reading was not aligned with the usual lines for the Schools, he achieved second place in the Natural Science Tripos in December 1873.

His academic focus shifted toward animal morphology after he attended a course of lectures on embryology delivered by Sir Michael Foster in 1871. Following his tripos, Balfour was selected to occupy one of the two seats allocated to the University of Cambridge at the Naples zoological station.

## Research and Publications
The research Balfour conducted in Naples was instrumental in his election as a fellow of Trinity in 1874. This work also provided the material for a series of papers, later published as a monograph in 1878, regarding Elasmobranch fishes. This research offered new insights into the development of several organs in Vertebrates, specifically the nervous and uro-genital systems.

Balfour subsequently authored a comprehensive two-volume treatise titled *Comparative Embryology*. The first volume, published in 1880, focused on Invertebrates, while the second volume, published in 1881, dealt with Vertebrates. The work was recognized as an admirable digest of animal development observations from the preceding twenty-five years and incorporated a significant amount of original research. The text was noted for a vigorous scientific imagination that remained controlled by a logical sense, allowing Balfour to rigidly distinguish between mere hypothesis and proved fact.

## Professional Reputation
Due to the success of his publications and research, Balfour gained wide recognition in his field. His reputation grew to the point that other universities sought his services, leading to an invitation to succeed a predecessor in a professional capacity.
