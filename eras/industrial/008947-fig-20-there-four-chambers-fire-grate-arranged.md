# Boiler and Furnace Systems

The archive contains detailed specifications for various types of boilers and combustion systems used in naval, locomotive, stationary, and industrial applications. These systems vary significantly in their internal architecture, tube arrangements, and methods of fuel delivery.

## Naval and Marine Boilers

Several specialized boiler designs are employed by various navies, including the British and German navies.

### The Thornycroft-Schulz Boiler
The Thornycroft-Schulz boiler is characterized by a four-chamber design. Its fire-grate is divided into two separate portions. The boiler utilizes two outermost rows of tubes on each side that function as water-walls at their lower sections, while allowing gases to pass between them at the upper sections. The rows located nearest to the fires follow an arrangement similar to that of the Thornycroft boiler. To manage circulation, large-diameter outer circulating pipes connect the water-chambers and steam-chambers, while several nearly vertical down-comers are situated in the center of the boiler for the middle water-chamber.

### The Yarrow Boiler
Commonly used in the British navy and several others, the Yarrow boiler consists of two lower water-chambers and a large cylindrical steam chest, all connected by numerous straight tubes. In large vessels, these tubes have an external diameter of 1-3/4 inches. However, in large express boilers, the diameter varies: the two rows closest to the fire on each side are 1-1/4 inches, while the remaining tubes are 1 inch in diameter. These tubes are spaced to form equilateral triangles, allowing for external cleaning from the front and cross-ways in two directions. 

Some models utilize large pipes outside the casings to connect the water-chambers to the lower part of the steam-chest to improve circulation. The largest single-ended large tube boiler recorded has a heating surface of 3750 sq. ft., a grate area of 73.5 sq. ft., and a steam drum diameter of 4 ft. 2 in. Double-ended boilers, which are fired from both ends, can be even larger. Access for maintenance is typically provided via manholes in the water-chamber ends and steam-chest, though smaller versions used in torpedo boats utilize a bolted joint to divide the water-chambers into two parts for tube-end access.

### Other Marine Systems
The Reed boiler features tubes that are more curved and longer than those in the Normand boiler and lacks "water-walls," allowing combustion products to pass directly from the fire-grate to the chimney. A distinguishing feature of the Reed boiler is the use of screw and nut connections at each end of the tubes, allowing for quick replacement. These tubes are reduced in diameter at their lower ends to permit the use of smaller chambers.

## Vertical and Stationary Boilers

Vertical boilers are typically used for small installations or where floor space is limited, often serving as auxiliary equipment on ships.

*   **Simple Vertical Boiler:** This type features a plain vertical fire-box and an internal smoke stack that traverses the steam space. The fire-box tapers in diameter, with water filling the space between the fire-box and the shell. Most versions include cross tubes (approximately 9 inches in diameter) fitted with a slight inclination to aid water circulation.
*   **Tyne Boiler:** This design employs a hemispherical furnace. Combustion products enter an upper combustion chamber that is traversed by several vertical water-tubes of smaller diameter and four or more inclined water-tubes of about 9 inches in diameter.
*   **Victoria Boiler:** Produced by Messrs Clarke, Chapman & Co., this boiler also uses a hemispherical furnace. Gases are led to an internal combustion chamber and then through horizontal smoke-tubes into a smoke-box located on the side of the boiler.
*   **Cochran Boiler:** Similar to the Victoria, but featuring a "dry" back combustion chamber. Instead of water space at the rear, it uses firebrick-lined doors to provide access to tube ends.

## Locomotive and Industrial Combustion

### Express Locomotive Boilers
An example of an express locomotive boiler features a barrel 5 ft. 6 in. in diameter and 16 ft. long between tube plates, with a heating surface of 2500 sq. ft. and a grate area of 30.9 sq. ft. The fire-box crown is supported by vertical stays extending to the shell crown, except for the three rows nearest the tube plates, which are supported by cross girders on brackets secured to the outer shell.

### The Beaman and Deas Destructor
Used by public authorities in locations such as Bolton, Wimbledon, and Canterbury, this destructor features a level-fire grate with ordinary bars and a high-temperature combustion chamber at the back of the cells. The hearth is made of firebrick and slopes at an angle of approximately 52°. Material is fed via an 18-inch square hopper mouth and slides down the hearth to the grate bars. 

The system utilizes a hermetically closed ash-pit under the grate area, supplied by 12-inch diameter air blast pipes. Air is provided by Schiele's patent fans at a pressure of 1½ to 2 inches of water. The combustion chamber can reach temperatures approaching 2000° F, allowing for the disposal of bad meat and infected articles.

## Fueling and Stoking Methods

### Mechanical Stoking
While many boilers are hand-fired, the "chain-grate" stoker is a common mechanical alternative. This system uses an endless chain of short cast-iron bars that rotate slowly from front to back. Coal is fed from a hopper across the grate's width. This allows volatile portions of the coal to distill at the front and pass over incandescent fuel at the back, ensuring combustion is nearly complete by the time the fuel reaches the rear.

### Liquid Fuel
In vessels such as the ss. "Murex," liquid fuel is used. Evidence suggests that omitting the fire-bars typically used for coal produces better results with liquid fuel than protecting those bars with broken fire-brick. For boilers designed for liquid fuel, it is recommended to use longer and narrower boiler tubes and larger combustion chambers than those used for coal-only combustion.
