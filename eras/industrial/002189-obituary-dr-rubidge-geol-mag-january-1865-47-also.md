# Andrew Geddes Bain

## Early Life and Emigration
Andrew Geddes Bain (1797–1864) was a British geologist born in Scotland. In 1820, he emigrated to the Cape Colony. For a period of several years, he operated a business as a saddler located at Graaf Reinet.

## Military and Engineering Career
During the Kaffir War of 1833–34, Bain took command of a provisional battalion that had been raised for the purpose of defending the frontier. Following this, he was engaged to construct a military road through the Ecca Pass. In this project, he demonstrated engineering talents that resulted in his permanent employment as a surveyor of military roads under the corps of Royal Engineers.

## Geological Contributions
Bain's professional occupation as a surveyor created an interest in geology. This interest was further fostered in 1837 when he was loaned a copy of Lyell's *Elements*. He dedicated all of his spare energies to the study of geology, leading to the discovery of the remains of many reptilia. Among these was the *Dicynodon*, which was obtained from the Karroo Beds near Fort Beaufort and subsequently described by Owen.

In 1852, Bain prepared the first comprehensive geological map of South Africa. This work was considered to be of great merit and was published in 1856 by the Geological Society of London.

## Death and Legacy
Andrew Geddes Bain died in Cape Town in 1864. His passing was noted in an obituary written by Dr. R. N. Rubidge, published in *Geol. Mag.* in January 1865 on page 47. Further information, including a portrait, was published in the *Trans. Geol. Soc. S. Africa*, vol. ii. part v., in June 1896.
