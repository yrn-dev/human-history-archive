# General Ballivian and the Presidency of Bolivia

## Rise to Power
General Ballivian ascended to the chief power in Bolivia following a period of significant political instability. This instability began in the late 1820s, characterized by repeated risings and the 1827 expulsion of General Sucre and Colombian troops from La Paz. While Marshal Santa Cruz had previously brought some order to the country's financial affairs and established a code of laws, his subsequent involvement in a conflict in Peru led to his defeat at the battle of Jungay in June 1839. Following Santa Cruz's exile, General Velasco served as the provisional chief of Bolivia. However, the Santa Cruz party remained a strong force within the country and successfully revolted against Velasco's government, ultimately installing General Ballivian in the chief power.

## Conflict with Peru
During Ballivian's tenure, Bolivia faced external aggression from Peru. In August 1841, General Gamarra, seeking to annex the rich province of La Paz, invaded the region and besieged its capital. General Ballivian led the Bolivian forces in a battle against Gamarra's army, resulting in a total rout of the Peruvian forces and the death of Gamarra. Following this victory, Ballivian prepared to invade Peru; however, this action was prevented by the interference of Chile.

## Retirement and Succession
General Ballivian remained in the presidency until 1848, at which time he retired to Valparaiso. His departure was followed by a successful military revolution led by General Belzu at the end of 1848.

## Related Figures and Later Developments
The archive notes a later president, Colonel Adolfo Ballivian, who succeeded Colonel Augustin Morales in 1872 and served until his death in 1874. Under Adolfo Ballivian's presidency, Bolivia entered into a secret agreement with Peru on February 6, 1873. The ostensible purpose of this agreement was mutual defense against exterior aggression and the preservation of territorial integrity, specifically regarding potential encroachments by Chile. 

Following the death of Adolfo Ballivian, Dr. Tomas Frias succeeded to the presidency. Dr. Frias signed a treaty with Chile in which Chile agreed to withdraw its claim to half the duties levied in Bolivian ports, provided that Chilean industries in Bolivian territory remained duty-free for twenty-five years. This treaty was not ratified. By 1876, General Hilarion Daza succeeded Dr. Frias and demanded a tax of 10 cents per quintal on all exported nitrates. This led to a Chilean blockade of the ports of Cobija, Tocapilla, and Antofagasta, and the occupation of Antofagasta on February 14, 1879, prompting Bolivia to declare war on March 1.
