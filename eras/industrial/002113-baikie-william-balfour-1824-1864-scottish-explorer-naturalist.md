# BAIKIE, WILLIAM BALFOUR (1824-1864)

William Balfour Baikie was a Scottish naturalist, philologist, and explorer. Born on August 21, 1824, in Kirkwall, Orkney, he was the eldest son of Captain John Baikie of the Royal Navy.

## Education and Early Career
Baikie pursued medical studies at Edinburgh, where he earned his M.D. degree. Following his medical education, he joined the royal navy in 1848. His early professional work brought him to the attention of Sir Roderick Murchison, which led to his appointment as a naturalist and surgeon for a Niger expedition in 1854. This expedition was sent out by Macgregor Laird and received government support.

## The First Niger Expedition
During the 1854 expedition, the senior officer, Consul Beecroft, died at Fernando Po, and Baikie succeeded him in command. Utilizing a small steamer named the "Pleiad," Baikie led the voyage, ascending the Benue approximately 250 miles beyond the limits reached by previous explorers. The expedition lasted 118 days and returned to the mouth of the Niger without the loss of any men.

A specific objective of the mission was to attempt to provide assistance to Heinrich Barth, who had crossed the upper course of the Benue in 1851; however, Baikie was unable to find any trustworthy information regarding Barth's location. Upon his return to England, Baikie published the details of his journey in his 1856 work, *Narrative of an Exploring Voyage up the Rivers Kwora and Binue...*

## Second Expedition and Settlement at Lokoja
In March 1857, Baikie embarked on another expedition in the "Pleiad," this time holding the rank of British consul. He spent two years exploring the Niger, but the mission faced a significant setback when the navigating vessel was wrecked while passing through river rapids. This disaster made it impossible for Baikie to keep his party together, and all members of the expedition returned home except for Baikie.

Undeterred, Baikie decided to continue the purposes of the expedition single-handedly. Accompanied by one or two native followers, he landed from a small boat at the confluence of the Benue and Niger rivers. He established his base of operations at Lokoja, a site that had previously been the location of a model farm established by a British government expedition in 1841. That earlier settlement had been abandoned within a year due to the deaths of most of the white settlers.

After purchasing the site of the former farm, Baikie concluded a treaty with the Fula emir of Nupe. He then began the process of clearing the ground and constructing houses.
