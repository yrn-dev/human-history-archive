# History, Directory and Gazetteer of the County of York

The *History, Directory and Gazetteer of the County of York* is a published work authored by Edward Baines. It is recognized as one of the best-known writings of Baines, who was a politician and newspaper proprietor in England.

## Author Biography
Edward Baines (1774–1848) was born in Walton-le-Dale, located near Preston, Lancashire. His early education took place at the grammar schools of Preston and Hawkshead. At sixteen years of age, Baines began an apprenticeship with a printer in Preston. After four and a half years in that town, he moved to Leeds to complete his apprenticeship, after which he established his own business.

Baines was characterized as an assiduous student with great ability and practical shrewdness. He maintained a keen interest in social and political movements. His personal political views led him to join the Independents and sympathize with nonconformity.

## Career in Journalism and Politics
In 1801, with the help of party friends, Baines purchased the *Leeds Mercury*. At the time of his acquisition, provincial newspapers lacked significant influence, reporting systems were defective, and editors did not typically provide "leading articles." Baines implemented a complete change in the *Mercury* by introducing able political articles. This transformed the publication into the organ of Liberal opinion in Leeds, establishing a powerful influence for the Baines family for many years.

Baines became a prominent figure in politics and an ardent advocate for parliamentary reform. His influence was primarily responsible for the return of Macaulay for Leeds in 1832. Baines succeeded Macaulay as a member of parliament in 1834 and was subsequently re-elected in 1835 and 1837, eventually resigning in 1841. While supporting the Liberal party in parliament, he maintained independent views. He and his son, Edward, strongly opposed government interference in national education and advocated for the separation of state and church. In 1846, Baines wrote letters to Lord John Russell regarding national education, which had a powerful influence on government action.
