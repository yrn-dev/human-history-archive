# Literary and Historical Events of 1847

The year 1847 was marked by significant developments in English literature, the publication of historical research, and the establishment of national honors in Europe.

## The Brontë Sisters and the Publication of Jane Eyre
During this period, Charlotte, Emily, and Anne Brontë were attempting to find publishers for their respective novels. Emily Brontë wrote a story entitled *Wuthering Heights*, and Anne Brontë wrote *Agnes Gray*. Charlotte Brontë's novel, originally titled *The Master*, was retitled *The Professor* before being sent to London.

After the works traveled between various publishers, *The Professor* was submitted to the firm Smith, Elder & Co. of Cornhill. The firm's reader, R. Smith Williams (1800-1875), and his employers were impressed by the work. However, in August 1847, Charlotte Brontë received a letter informing her that regardless of the merits of *The Professor*—though it was hinted that the work lacked "varied interest"—the novel was too short to meet the then-imperative three-volume form. 

The publishers informed the author that they would gladly consider a longer novel. Charlotte Brontë replied in August of the same year with a longer work, which was published in October 1847 as *Jane Eyre*. The novel received wild acclaim on every hand, although this enthusiasm was countered in December 1848 when Miss Rigby (later Lady Eastlake) reviewed it in the *Quarterly*.

## Historical and Biographical Publications
Several notable volumes were published in 1847 across different genres:

*   **Biographies and Memoirs:** Two volumes of *Memoirs, with Extracts from her Journals and Letters*, edited by two of her daughters, were published in 1847 regarding Elizabeth Fry. Additionally, a short memoir written by the son of the author of *Ingoldsby* was included as a prefix to a new edition of that work in 1847.
*   **Greek History:** A scholar who had previously served as a bank manager and a leader among city Radicals devoted himself to literature after 1842. Following the publication of the first two volumes of his *History* in 1846, the remaining ten volumes were published between 1847 and the spring of 1856.
*   **Political Observations:** In July 1847, the formation of the Sonderbund prompted the aforementioned historian to visit Switzerland to study conditions analogous to those of ancient Greek states. This visit resulted in seven weekly letters published in the *Spectator*, which were collected into a book at the end of 1847.

## European Honors
In 1847, Oscar I founded the Order of St Olaf in Norway. Established in honor of St Olaf, the founder of Christianity in Norway, the order serves as a general order of merit for both civil and military service. The order consists of three classes, with the latter two being subdivided into two grades each in 1890 and 1873. The order's ribbon and badge feature the motto *Ret og Sandhed*, which translates to "Right and Truth."
