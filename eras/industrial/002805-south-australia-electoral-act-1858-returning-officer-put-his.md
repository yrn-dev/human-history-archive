# South Australia (Electoral Act of 1858)

## Electoral Procedures and the Ballot
The introduction of the ballot in South Australia was established by the Constitution Act of 1856, occurring at the same time it was introduced in other colonies. Under the subsequent Electoral Act of 1858, specific procedures were mandated for the handling of voting cards to ensure the secrecy and validity of the vote.

The returning-officer was required to place his initials on the voting card. The voter was then directed to fold the card in a manner that prevented the officer from seeing the vote, which was indicated by a cross. Failure to follow these folding directions resulted in the nullity of the vote. 

Furthermore, the process in South Australia featured a specific security measure regarding the submission of the ballot: the voter did not place the card directly into the ballot box. Instead, the card was handed to the officer, who then placed it in the box. This method was employed as a security measure to prevent the use of non-official cards, to stop voters from carrying their cards away, and to guard against double-voting.

## Legal and Social Legislation
South Australia was a pioneer in the legalization of marriage with a deceased wife's sister, being the first to adopt an act to this effect. However, the process was marked by legislative persistence, as the parliament of the state had to pass the bill five times before royal assent was granted.

In the realm of social welfare, the state took measures to prevent the "evils of baby-farming" through the State Children Act of 1895. This act established the State Children's Council, which held extensive powers to oversee infants under two years of age who were boarded out by their mothers. Women acting as "foster-mothers" were required to be licensed, with the license fixing the number of children they were authorized to keep. These licensed foster-mothers were mandated to maintain a register including:
* The name, age, and place of birth of every child.
* Descriptions, names, and addresses of the parents or any other person from whom the child was received or to whom they were delivered.
* The dates of receipt or delivery.
* Details of any illness or accident involving the child.
* The name of any medical practitioner who attended the child.

## Economic and Administrative Systems
The legal framework for bankruptcy in South Australia was modeled after the English system, following the lines of existing English acts.

Regarding geography and land management, South Australia has conducted trigonometrical and cadastral surveys via surveyors-general for many years. These efforts have resulted in the publication of county, township, and parish maps that keep pace with the settlement of the region. While South Australia possesses general maps on a scale of 1:506,930, it does not possess a topographical map with the same level of accuracy as those published in Europe (a distinction shared by all Australian states except Victoria).

## Regional Geography: Kooringa
Within South Australia, the town of Kooringa (also known as Burra) serves as a center for an agricultural and mining district. Located on Burra Creek, it is 101 miles by rail North by East of Adelaide. As of 1901, the population was 1,994. The district is noted for large areas of wheat-growing and the famous Burra Burra copper mine, which was discovered by a shepherd in 1844. The vicinity is also known for the presence of lead and silver ore.
