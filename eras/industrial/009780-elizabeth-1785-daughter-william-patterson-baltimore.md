# Elizabeth Patterson

Elizabeth Patterson (b. 1785) was a Baltimore resident and the daughter of William Patterson, a merchant in Baltimore. William Patterson was likely a descendant of the Robert Paterson who served as the inspiration for the character in Sir Walter Scott's "Old Mortality."

## Marriage to Jerome Bonaparte
Elizabeth married Jerome Bonaparte, the brother of Napoleon, on December 24, 1803, in Baltimore. Jerome had traveled through the United States after leaving his ship while blockaded by a British cruiser in the West Indies. Although Jerome was a minor at the time of the union, the marriage was considered valid under American law.

The marriage was greatly disliked by Napoleon, who refused to recognize its legality. This disregard for the laws of France and military discipline caused significant annoyance to the emperor. Despite Napoleon's opposition, Pope Pius VII refused to declare the marriage void.

## Life in Europe and Separation
In 1805, Jerome brought Elizabeth to Europe. However, Napoleon ordered that she be excluded from his states. While Jerome was commanding a small squadron in the Mediterranean in May 1805, Elizabeth went to Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son.

Eventually, Jerome was forced by his brother to separate from his wife. Following a period of residence in England, Elizabeth returned to Baltimore. During this time, she was commonly referred to as either Madame Patterson or Madame Bonaparte. She remained in Baltimore until her death in 1879.

## Descendants
The marriage between Elizabeth Patterson and Jerome Bonaparte produced one child, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870). He was born in England but spent the majority of his life residing in Baltimore. It was noted that he bore a marked resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte maintained a good relationship with his father, and the two met occasionally; for a period, Jerome provided his son with a large allowance.

Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte had an elder son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893). This grandson of Elizabeth entered the French army and served in Italy and the Crimea.
