# Cotton Mills

The establishment and growth of cotton mills have been central to the industrial development of various global regions, ranging from the early industrialization of England and the United States to the commercial expansion of Bombay. These mills, often integrated with other milling industries such as flour or corn mills, have shaped local populations and economies.

## Development in Bombay
In Bombay, the milling industry is a primary feature of the city's commercial success, second only to the docks. The staple manufacture in this region is cotton-spinning. The industry is concentrated in Bombay City and Island, which houses nearly two-thirds of the mills. Large steam mills have also developed rapidly in Khandesh and Ahmedabad.

The scale of the industry grew significantly in the late 19th century, with the number of factories increasing from fifty-three in 1881 to eighty-three by 1890. This growth attracted a large industrial population from surrounding districts. However, the period between 1891 and 1901 saw a temporary setback and a period of depression caused by famine and the ravages of plague. The plague not only caused mortality but also led to an exodus of the population from the island, which disorganized labor in both the mills and the docks. Additionally, the municipality had to spend large sums on sanitary improvements and plague operations. By 1901, the cotton industry employed 131,796 persons, and both trade and population began to revive after that year. By 1905, there were 432 factories in the presidency, the vast majority of which were dedicated to the manufacture and preparation of cotton.

While the steam mills expanded, hand-loom weavers continued to maintain a position in the province, numbering 178,000 in 1901 due to their skill in manipulating designs woven into cloth.

## Early Industrialization in England
In Derbyshire, the rise of cotton mills began in the late 18th century. Sir Richard Arkwright established one of his first cotton mills in Cromford in 1771. The industry expanded quickly in the county, with twenty-two cotton mills recorded by 1787.

Other regions of England also developed specialized cotton industries. In the Vale of Leven, cotton-printing began at Levenfield in 1768. The area became a center for the bleaching, dyeing, and printing of yarns, calicoes, and other cotton cloths on a large scale, with establishments located in towns such as Renton, Jamestown, Bonhill, and Alexandria. The first power-loom mill was erected at Milton.

## North American Establishments
In the United States, cotton mill development is noted in the South. In Greensboro, one of the first cotton mills in the state and likely one of the first in the South was established in 1832. Although it closed approximately 20 years later, new mills were constructed in 1889. Following 1895, three very large mills were built, leading to the creation of three mill villages: White Oak, Revolution, and Proximity. By 1908, the population of these villages was estimated at 8,000. In these communities, mill owners provided "welfare work" and maintained schools for the children of the operatives.

## Trade and Related Industries
Cotton goods often intersect with port commerce. In Grimsby, cotton yarn and cotton goods are listed as chief exports, while cottons are among the principal imports. 

In various industrial centers, cotton mills exist alongside other milling operations. For example, Bombay's commercial landscape includes flour mills alongside its cotton-spinning factories. Similarly, Grimsby maintains corn and bone mills, and Buffalo's industrial output includes flour and grist mill products.
