# The Documentary History of the Revolution

## Overview and Publication
"The Documentary History of the Revolution" is an article written by Bancroft and published in 1835 within the *North American Review*. This work served as a supplement to the first volume of Bancroft's *History of the United States*, which had been released in 1834.

## Impact and Methodology
The publication of the article was significant for its introduction of a new method of historical study to the reading public. Through this work, Bancroft revealed the extensive wealth of available material regarding the Revolution. The "philosophic spirit" and solidity of his work, along with the nature of his studies, contributed to the enthusiasm with which his earlier volumes were received.

## Context of Bancroft's Work
Bancroft produced this article during a period of prolific academic activity. While managing the severe and exacting routine of the Round Hill school, he contributed frequently to Walsh's *American Quarterly* and the *North American Review*. His other scholarly contributions included a translation of Heeren's work, *The Politics of Ancient Greece*.

Following the first volume of his *History of the United States* (1834) and the supplementary 1835 article, a second volume was published in 1837, with subsequent volumes appearing as the requirements of his public life allowed. These works achieved significant international success, seeing large sales in the United States and being reprinted in England. They were also translated into French, German, Italian, and Danish.

## Professional Recognition
Bancroft's academic standing was recognized by several European institutions. Upon his preparation to leave Berlin, the Royal Academy honored him as a guest at a public dinner, an event described as an unprecedented honor. Additionally, the universities of Munich, Heidelberg, and Berlin united in a testimonial of regard for him. In Washington, Bancroft served as the unofficial dean of the best society and acted as a confidential advisor to statesmen throughout his life.
