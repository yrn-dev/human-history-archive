# The Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature

*The Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature* is a foundational work of English bibliography. First published in 1834, it is recognized as the first systematic work of its kind.

## Authorship and Early History
The manual was written by William Thomas Lowndes (1798–1843), an English bibliographer and the son of a London bookseller. Lowndes spent fourteen years compiling the work, which was originally published in four volumes in 1834. Despite the merits of the publication, it did not bring Lowndes money or fame, and he was eventually reduced to poverty. 

Following this period, Lowndes became the cataloguer for the publisher and bookseller Henry George Bohn. In 1839, Lowndes began publishing *The British Librarian*, which was intended to serve as a supplement to the original manual. However, he was unable to complete this project due to failing health and died on July 31, 1843.

## The Bohn Revision (1857–1864)
The work was later revised by Henry George Bohn (1796–1884), a British publisher born in London to a German bookbinder. Bohn's revision of the manual was a laborious and useful undertaking, resulting in a six-volume set completed in 1864. 

Bohn's edition expanded significantly upon the original 1834 work. The plan for this revision included the addition of critical notices, bibliographical details, and particulars regarding prices. This version of the manual remained a primary resource for collectors; for example, it was used to catalogue numerous books on husbandry produced by the writer Markham.

## Role in Book Collecting and Bibliography
For "old-fashioned" collectors, the *Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature* (alongside Brunet's *Manuel du libraire*) provided nearly all the necessary information required for their pursuits. It is cited as one of the sufficient records available for books of great rarity that are not represented in the catalogues of the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris or the British Museum.

The manual served the needs of the "omnivorous collector." However, as the output of the press increased and the ideals of collectors shifted, general manuals like Lowndes's were superseded by more specialized handbooks and detailed bibliographies. While the archive notes that new editions of the manual would still be useful to booksellers and librarians, no publisher has produced further editions beyond Bohn's 1857–1864 revision.
