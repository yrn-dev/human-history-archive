# Sympiezometer

The sympiezometer is a scientific instrument used for measuring atmospheric pressure. Invented in 1818 by Adie of Edinburgh, the device represents a revived version of the marine barometer originally developed by Hooke.

## Design and Mechanism
The construction of the sympiezometer consists of a glass tube featuring an open cistern at the bottom and a small chamber located at the top. The instrument utilizes two different substances: the lower part of the tube and the cistern are filled with glycerin, while the upper portion of the tube is filled with air.

The device operates based on the compression and expansion of the contained air in response to atmospheric changes. When atmospheric pressure increases, the glycerin rises, which in turn compresses the air. Conversely, when atmospheric pressure diminishes, the fluid falls, allowing the contained air to expand.

## Temperature Adjustment and Accuracy
Because variations in temperature can cause the pressure of the contained air to increase—thereby creating an error in measurement—the sympiezometer includes specific corrective components. A sliding-scale and a thermometer are added to the instrument, allowing the user to adjust the device to the current temperature at the time of each observation.

In terms of performance, the sympiezometer is characterized as a sensitive instrument. Due to its design, it is well-suited for traveling and for rough purposes at sea. However, it is not considered suitable for exact observation.

## Obsolescence
The sympiezometer has long since been superseded by the Aneroid barometer. The Aneroid is noted for far exceeding the sympiezometer in terms of handiness.
