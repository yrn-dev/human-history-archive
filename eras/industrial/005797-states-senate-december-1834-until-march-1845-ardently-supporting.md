# James Buchanan

## Early Life and Education
James Buchanan was born on April 23, 1791, near Foltz in Franklin County, Pennsylvania. He was of Scottish-Irish Presbyterian descent through both of his parents. He attended Dickinson College in Carlisle, Pennsylvania, from which he graduated in 1809. Following his graduation, Buchanan studied law in Lancaster between 1809 and 1812, and he was admitted to the bar in 1812.

## Early Political Career
Buchanan's entry into legislative politics began in the lower house of the state legislature, where he served from 1814 to 1816. He later transitioned to national politics, serving as a representative in Congress from 1821 to 1831. During his tenure in the House, he served as the chairman of the judiciary committee. In this capacity, he conducted the 1830 impeachment trial of Judge James H. Peck. 

While in the judiciary committee, Buchanan led a movement to relieve Supreme Court judges of their circuit duties and to increase the number of judges on the Court, though this movement was unsuccessful. He did, however, succeed in defeating an effort to repeal the twenty-fifth section of the Judiciary Act of 1789. This specific section ensured that the Supreme Court maintained appellate jurisdiction by writ of error over state courts in instances where treaties and federal laws were in question.

## Diplomatic and Senate Service
Originally a member of the Federalist party, Buchanan shifted his support to the Jackson-Van Buren faction following the dissolution of the Federalists, eventually becoming definitely associated with the Democrats. In 1832 and 1833, he represented the United States at the court of St Petersburg, where he negotiated a commercial treaty of importance.

Buchanan served as a Democratic member of the United States Senate from December 1834 until March 1845. During his time in the Senate, he was an ardent supporter of President Jackson.

## Secretary of State and Later Career
From 1845 to 1849, Buchanan served as the secretary of state in the cabinet of President Polk. This period of his career was characterized by the Mexican War, the annexation of Texas, and negotiations with Great Britain regarding the Oregon question.

Following his time as secretary of state, Buchanan retired for four years to practice his profession. His return to public service occurred in 1853, when President Pierce appointed him as the minister to Great Britain. He eventually became the fifteenth president of the United States, passing away in 1868.
