# Friedrich Karl Christian Ludwig Büchner

Friedrich Karl Christian Ludwig Büchner (1824–1899) was a German physician and philosopher. Born in Darmstadt, he pursued his studies across several cities, including Vienna, Würzburg, Strassburg, and Giessen.

## Academic Career and Kraft und Stoff
In 1852, Büchner was appointed as a lecturer in medicine at the university of Tübingen. It was during his tenure there that he published his major work, *Kraft und Stoff*, in 1855. According to Lange, this work was the product of a "fanatical enthusiasm for humanity." In the text, Büchner attempted to demonstrate the finality of physical force and the indestructibility of force and matter.

The extreme materialism presented in *Kraft und Stoff* generated significant opposition, which ultimately forced Büchner to resign from his position at Tübingen. Following his departure, he returned to Darmstadt to practice as a physician. During this period, he remained an active contributor to physiological and pathological magazines.

## Philosophical Views and Materialism
Büchner continued to write in defense of materialism throughout his life. His philosophical framework was primarily informed by his background as a physiologist rather than a metaphysician. He posited that matter and force (or energy) are infinite, and that the imperishability of matter serves as the ultimate basis for all science; from this, the conservation of force follows.

Büchner's theories regarding the relationship between matter and force were not always clear. While he occasionally refused to explain the connection, he generally assumed that spiritual and natural forces dwell within matter. In *Kraft und Stoff*, he used the analogy of a steam-engine producing motion to explain that the "intricate organic complex of force-bearing substance in an animal organism" produces a sum of effects. When these effects are bound in unity, they are referred to as thought, soul, and mind. This perspective represents a materialistic monism, where mind and force emanate from original matter. However, in other writings, he suggested a different form of monism—not necessarily materialistic—where mind and matter are viewed as two distinct aspects of the basis of all things.

## Later Works and Death
Following *Kraft und Stoff*, Büchner published several other philosophical works, including:
* *Natur und Geist* (1857)
* *Aus Natur und Wissenschaft* (Volume I in 1862; Volume II in 1884)
* *Fremdes und Eigenes aus dem geistigen Leben der Gegenwart* (1890)
* *Darwinismus und Socialismus* (1894)
* *Im Dienste der Wahrheit* (1899)

Büchner died in Darmstadt on May 1, 1899.
