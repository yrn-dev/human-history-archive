# Eton, Fenny Stratford, and Linslade

The towns of Eton, Fenny Stratford, and Linslade are located within the south midland county of Buckinghamshire, England. This county is bounded by Northamptonshire to the north, Oxfordshire to the west, Surrey and Berkshire to the south, and Bedfordshire, Hertfordshire, and Middlesex to the east.

## Eton
Eton is an urban district in Buckinghamshire with a population of 3,301. The town is home to Eton College, a famous public school founded by Henry VI, which is noted as the most notable institution within the county. The town's educational influence extended to figures such as William Johnson Cory (1823–1892), an author and schoolmaster who was educated at Eton and later served as an assistant master there for twenty-six years. Cory was regarded as one of the most brilliant Eton tutors of his era and was a defender of the Etonian system.

## Fenny Stratford
Fenny Stratford is an urban district with a population of 4,799. It serves as a point of connectivity for the London & North-Western railway, with branches diverging from the important junction at Bletchley toward Fenny Stratford, Cambridge, and Bedford. 

Historically, Fenny Stratford is associated with the boundary of the Danelagh—the districts of north and north-east England settled by Scandinavian invaders and Danes. Following the peace of Alfred and Guthrum (ratified approximately in the year 880), the boundary between the kingdom of Guthrum and the lands of Alfred the Great was determined. This boundary ran from the Thames estuary, up the Lea to its source near Leighton Buzzard, north to Bedford, and then eastwards up the Ouse to Watling Street in the vicinity of Stony or Fenny Stratford.

## Linslade
Linslade is an urban district with a population of 2,157. It is situated on the Ouzel, positioned opposite Leighton Buzzard in the neighboring county of Bedfordshire. The Ouzel serves as the principal tributary of the river Ouse within Buckinghamshire. The Grand Junction Canal reaches the valley of the Ouse by passing through the Ouzel valley from the south.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure
The areas surrounding these towns are divided between the basins of the Thames and Ouse rivers. While the Thames forms the entire southern boundary of the county, none of the rivers in Buckinghamshire are continuously navigable except for the Thames.

The administrative county of Buckinghamshire covers 479,358 acres and is divided into thirteen petty sessional divisions and 230 civil parishes. It is almost entirely within the diocese of Oxford. The county is represented in parliament by three divisions: Southern (Wycombe), Mid (Aylesbury), and Northern (Buckingham), each of which returns one member.
