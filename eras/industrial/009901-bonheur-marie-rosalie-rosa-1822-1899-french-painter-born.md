# BONHEUR, ROSA

## Early Life and Family
Rosa Bonheur (1822–1899) was a French painter born in Bordeaux on March 22, 1822. She was of Jewish origin; the Belgian medallist Jacques Wiener, a native of Venloo, noted that he and Rosa's father, Raymond Bonheur, attended synagogue in that town. Additionally, another authority states that she was known in common parlance as Rosa Mazeltov, using the Hebrew term for "good luck," which translates in French to *Bonheur*.

Rosa was the eldest of four children, all of whom pursued careers as artists. Her siblings included Auguste (1824–1884), who painted landscapes and animals; Isidore (born 1827), a sculptor of animals; and Juliette (1830–1891), who received an "honourable mention" at the exhibition of 1855.

## Artistic Development and Career
Rosa's father, who died in 1849, taught her to draw at an early age. Recognizing her remarkable talent, he allowed her to abandon the business of dressmaking—a pursuit she had been placed in against her will—to devote herself entirely to art.

Between 1840 and 1845, Bonheur exhibited her work at the salon, where she received a prize five times. She was further recognized in 1848 with the award of a medal. Her fame increased significantly following the exhibition of 1855, after which her works became highly sought after in England by public galleries and collectors.

## Style and Technique
Bonheur is noted for representing animals as they really are, based on her observations of them in the country, a quality she shared with her contemporary Jacques Raymond Brascassat (1804–1867). Her work is characterized by a faultlessly true anatomy of animals and a handling that is described as firm and manly, lacking feminine qualities. However, her gift for accurate observation was sometimes paired with a dryness of style, and she occasionally failed to convey a perfect sense of atmosphere.

## Notable Works and Honors
Among her many works, the archive notes the following:
* "Hay Harvest in Auvergne" (1835)
* "Ploughing in the Nivernais" (1848), located in the Luxembourg gallery
* "The Horse Fair" (1853), for which there are two replicas, one of which is in the National Gallery, London, while the original is in the United States.

For her achievements, she was decorated with the Legion of Honour by Empress Eugenie and was later promoted to the rank of officer within the order.
