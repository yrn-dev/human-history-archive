# Historical Events and Publications (1858–1861)

The period between 1858 and 1861 was marked by significant contributions to geography, literature, science, and political administration across Europe and North America.

## Geographical Discoveries and Exploration
In February 1858, the explorer Burton and his companion Speke discovered Lake Tanganyika during a commission by the foreign office to search for the sources of the Nile in the lake regions of equatorial Africa. While Burton suffered an illness, Speke continued forward based on Burton's indications and found Victoria Nyanza. This expedition provided the incentive for subsequent explorations by Stanley, Livingstone, Baker, and Grant. Burton later published *Lake Regions of Equatorial Africa* in 1860. Additionally, Burton visited the United States in 1860, which resulted in the 1861 publication of *The City of the Saints*.

## Literary and Academic Works
The late 1850s and early 1860s saw a prolific output of diverse texts:

### General Literature and History
John Doran, an English author, published *The History of Court Fools* and an edition of the *Bentley Ballads* in 1858. In 1860, he published *The Princess of Wales* and his most elaborate work, *Their Majesties' Servants*, a history of the English stage from Kean to Betterton. He followed these with the *Memoirs of Queen Adelaide* in 1861. 

Maxime Du Camp, a French writer, published *Convictions* in 1858. After serving as a volunteer with Garibaldi in 1860, he wrote *Expedition des deux Siciles* (1861). He also produced art criticism works titled *Les Salons* in 1857, 1859, and 1861.

Charles Kingsley produced several works during this window, including *Andromeda and other Poems* (1858), *Miscellanies* (1859), and *The Good News of God* (1859). In 1860, he wrote *Limits of Exact Science applied to History* (Inaugural Lectures), followed by *Town and Country Sermons* in 1861.

### Science and Specialized Studies
In the field of geology, Jules Marcou published *Geology of North America* in 1858. Between 1857 and 1860, he wrote *Lettres sur les roches du Jura et leur distribution géographique dans les deux hémisphères*. In 1861, Marcou published the second edition of his *Geological Map of the World*.

Other specialized publications included the *Narrative of Services in the Liberation of Chili, Peru and Brazil* (1858) by the 10th earl of Dundonald, and the first two volumes of the 10th earl's *Autobiography of a Seaman*, published between 1860 and 1861. In the realm of musicology, Bock's "Gebrauch der Horner im Mittelalter" appeared in Gustav Heider's *Mittelalterliche Kunstdenkmaler Osterreichs* between 1858 and 1860.

## Political and Military Developments
In the United States, the political landscape shifted as Thomas Corwin identified himself with the Republican party after 1858. In 1860, Corwin served as the chairman of the House "Committee of Thirty-three," a body composed of one member from each state tasked with devising a scheme to reconcile the North and the South. Corwin later served as a member of the national House of Representatives from 1859 to 1861 before becoming the U.S. minister to Mexico from 1861 to 1864.

Military activity increased in 1861 with the start of the American Civil War. Charles Russell Lowell entered the Union army in June 1861, having been commissioned on May 14 as a captain of the 3rd (later 6th) U.S. cavalry. Lowell had previously served as the local treasurer of the Burlington & Missouri river railroad from 1858 to 1860 and took charge of the Mount Savage Iron Works in Maryland in 1860.
