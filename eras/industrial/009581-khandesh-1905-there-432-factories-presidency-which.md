# Khandesh

Khandesh is a region located within the Bombay Presidency of British India. It was originally a single district, though it was partitioned into two districts in 1906. Politically, the area includes the Khandesh political agency, as well as Surgana and Jawhar.

## Geography and Natural Features
The primary natural feature of Khandesh is the Tapti river, which flows from east to west through both of its districts, dividing each into two unequal parts. The Tapti receives thirteen principal tributaries during its course through the region. While the district is generally well-supplied with surface water, the Tapti flows in a bed too deep to be useful for irrigation, and none of the rivers in the area are navigable.

The larger portions of the districts lie to the south and are drained by the Panjhra, Bori, and Girna rivers. To the north, beyond an alluvial plain containing some of the richest tracts in Khandesh, the land rises toward the Satpura hills. The eastern and central parts of the country are level, characterized by an arid and unfertile appearance with some low ranges of barren hills. In the north and west, the terrain becomes rugged, difficult, and thickly wooded.

Khandesh is not rich in minerals. Although a large area is covered by forest, most of the valuable timber has been removed from the jungles. Wild beasts are numerous in the region.

## Population and Aboriginal Tribes
In 1901, the population of the old single district was 1,427,382, reflecting an increase of less than 1% over the previous decade. The most important of the aboriginal tribes are the Bhils, numbering 167,000. Formerly described as a lawless and wild robber tribe, the Bhils have been successfully won from this disorderly life through the offer of suitable employment and kindly treatment under British rule. Many Bhils are currently employed as village watchmen and in police duties. In the rugged, wooded areas of the north and west, the Bhils support themselves primarily through wood-cutting and the fruits of the forests.

## Agriculture
The staple food grains of Khandesh and the Deccan are bajra (*Holcus spicatus*) and joar (*Sorghum vulgare*). Other principal crops grown in the region include oilseeds, wheat, pulse, and millets. 

Cotton is a major fiber produced in the area. The indigenous plant, which gave its name to one of the lowest classes of cotton in the Bombay market, has been almost entirely replaced in Khandesh by the superior Hinganghat variety.

## Industry and Infrastructure
Khandesh is a significant center for the growth and manufacture of cotton. The region contains many factories for the ginning and pressing of cotton, and it hosts a cotton-mill at Jalgaon. These developments are part of a wider industrial trend in the Bombay Presidency; by 1905, there were 432 factories in the presidency, the vast majority of which were dedicated to the manufacture and preparation of cotton.

The region is served by several railway lines. The Tapti Valley line from Surat crosses both districts. Additionally, the Great Indian Peninsula railway traverses the eastern district, with Bhusawal serving as an important center of trade and a branching point toward Nagpur and Jubbulpore.

## History of Famine
The region has been impacted by periods of food scarcity, suffering from famine in 1896-1897 and experiencing a more severe famine during 1899-1900.
