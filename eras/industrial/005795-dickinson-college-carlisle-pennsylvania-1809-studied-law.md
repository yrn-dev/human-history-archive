# Dickinson College

Dickinson College is an educational institution located in Carlisle, Pennsylvania. The archive's records indicate that the college has served as a site for undergraduate education and professional academic instruction across various disciplines, including the sciences, mathematics, and classical languages.

## Notable Alumni and Students

The college has graduated several individuals who achieved prominence in American government and science. James Buchanan, the fifteenth president of the United States, graduated from Dickinson College in 1809. Following his graduation, Buchanan studied law in Lancaster from 1809 to 1812 before being admitted to the bar in 1812. 

Another notable alumnus is Spencer Fullerton Baird, an American naturalist born in Reading, Pennsylvania. Baird graduated from the college in 1840. After his graduation, he conducted an ornithological excursion through the mountains of Pennsylvania, covering 400 miles in twenty-one days.

## Academic Faculty and Instruction

The college has employed a variety of scholars in its faculty. In the mid-19th century, John McClintock, a Methodist Episcopal educationalist and theologian, held several positions at the institution. Between 1836 and 1837, he served as assistant professor of mathematics, and from 1837 to 1840, he was professor of mathematics. He subsequently served as professor of Latin and Greek from 1840 to 1848.

In 1845, Spencer Fullerton Baird returned to the college as a professor of natural history. During his tenure, he also assumed the duties of the chair of chemistry and provided instruction in mathematics and physiology.
