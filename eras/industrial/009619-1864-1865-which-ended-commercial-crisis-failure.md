## The United States National Banking System
In the United States, the financial requirements of the Federal government during the Civil War led to the creation of a national banking system. Secretary of the Treasury Mr. Chase developed a scheme to create a compulsory market for government bonds by granting special privileges to banks organized under Federal charters; these banks could issue circulating notes only when secured by the deposit of government bonds. This plan was authorized by an act on February 25, 1863, and was further supplemented by an act on June 3, 1864.

To ensure the predominance of national banks over established state banking systems, a 10% tax was imposed on the face-value of state bank notes in circulation after July 1, 1866. This drove state banks out of the note-issuing business, leading some to convert into national banks. This period saw rapid growth: in 1864, there were 453 national banks with an aggregate capital of $79,366,950, which increased by 1865 to 1,014 banks with an aggregate capital of $242,542,982.
