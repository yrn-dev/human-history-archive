# Bacup

## Geography and Location
Bacup is a market town and municipal borough located in England within the Rossendale parliamentary division of Lancashire. It is situated on the river Irwell and is characterized by its location in a narrow valley, surrounded by high-lying, wild moorland. In terms of distance and direction, Bacup is positioned 203 miles north-northwest from London and 22 miles north by east from Manchester. The town is served by the Lancashire & Yorkshire railway.

## Population and Development
The town is described as being wholly of modern growth. In 1841, the population of the chapelry was only 1,526 inhabitants, but by 1901, the population of the borough had grown to 22,505. The total area of the borough is 6,120 acres.

## Industry and Economy
Bacup possesses numerous manufacturing industries, with the chief being power-loom weaving and cotton spinning. Additionally, there are large collieries located in the vicinity of the town. The principle of co-operation is strongly developed in Bacup; this is evidenced by a large and handsome store which includes a free library for its members.

## Governance and Institutions
The borough was incorporated in 1882. Its corporation is composed of a mayor, 17 councillors, and 6 aldermen. 

The town contains several other buildings and institutions, including:
*   **Religious Buildings:** Several handsome churches.
*   **Education and Literature:** A library and a mechanics' institute, the latter of which is cited as the chief institution in the town.
*   **Recreation:** Beautifully laid out recreation grounds, which were presented to the town in 1893 by Mr. J. H. Maden, M.P.

## Local Landmarks
Beyond the modern developments of the town, the surrounding landscape contains an ancient earthwork and ditch used to fortify one of the nearby hills.
