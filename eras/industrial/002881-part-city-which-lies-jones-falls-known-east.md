# East Baltimore

East Baltimore is the portion of the city located to the east of Jones's Falls. This area is nominally divided into two distinct sections: Old Town to the north and west, and Fells' Point to the south and east.

## Regional Characteristics and Architecture
Fells' Point currently serves as a quarter for manufacturing and shipbuilding. In contrast, Old Town retains several examples of architecture from the eighteenth century. These include old-fashioned post-houses that previously provided entertainment for travelers. These travelers were typically journeying toward the Middle West via the old Cumberland Road, which began at Fort Cumberland; the route from Baltimore to Fort Cumberland was served by a much older turnpike.

The district is characterized by several wide streets featuring squares in the middle, which are decorated with fountains, flower-beds, and lawns. Broadway is a primary example of such a street, running north and south through the center of East Baltimore. Additionally, North Gay Street, which runs northeast through the district, contains many busy small retail shops.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city as a whole covers an area of approximately 32 square miles, extending nearly 6-1/2 miles from east to west and slightly more than 5 miles from north to south (except on the western side). The terrain is generally gently rolling, though some areas were originally marked by precipitous heights or were swampy; the heights have since been rounded off and the swamps filled and drained.

Jones's Falls is a small stream contained between granite walls several feet high. It enters the city at the northern boundary line, a short distance west of the middle, and flows southeast to the southeast corner of the main business quarter. At this point, it meets the north-west branch of the Patapsco, which contains the harbour.

The city's water supply is sourced from the Gunpowder river and Jones's Falls, located a few miles north of the city limits. This water is transported through tunnels and stored in eight reservoirs with a total capacity of 2275 million gallons. The municipality owns the system, which is capable of providing about 300 million gallons daily.

## Commerce and Maritime Access
The harbour is divided into three parts. Its entrance is located at Fort McHenry, which is built at the southeast extremity of Locust Point—an irregular peninsula featuring wharves, including those of the Baltimore & Ohio railway, and grain-elevators. The entrance channel is 600 feet wide with a minimum draft of 31 feet (as of 1907).

From the entrance, the channel continues for a mile and a quarter to near Fells' Point, where it narrows to one-fourth of a mile with a depth of 16 feet. Beyond this, it widens into an ellipse half a mile broad, a mile long, and 15 feet deep. The inner harbour, which penetrates deep into the city, has a depth of 14 feet. Vessels of 4000 tons are able to use the inner harbour west of the mouth of Jones's Falls.

By 1908, the city was ranked fourth among the Atlantic ports of the United States in terms of imports ($23,722,045) and exports ($82,113,496). Since a great fire, $5,000,000 had been appropriated for new docks by 1905.

## City Layout and Administration
The city's central intersection is formed by Baltimore Street (running east and west) and Charles Street (running north and south), located a little south of the city center. This intersection serves as the point from which buildings on these two streets are numbered north, south, east, and west. Buildings on other streets are numbered north and south from Baltimore Street and east and west from Charles Street.

Baltimore Street serves as the chief thoroughfare for business. The shipping, financial, and wholesale districts are located slightly to the north and south of this street. Retail activity is concentrated on West Lexington Street to the north, as well as on North Eutaw and North Howard Streets between Franklin and Fayette Streets.
