## Industrial Development in Budapest
In the Austro-Hungarian monarchy, Budapest emerged as a primary center for commerce and industry, second only to Vienna. The city's progress was heavily supported by its location on the Danube, particularly following the introduction of steamboats in 1830, which stimulated the corn trade. Budapest became one of the world's leading milling centers, utilizing local inventions and machinery manufactured within the city.

The growth of the city's industrial base is evidenced by the rise of joint-stock companies. In 1873, there were 28 such companies with a total capital of £2,224,900. This expansion continued into the next decade, reaching 242 companies with a total capital of £31,378,655 by 1899.
