# Revivals of the Latter Half of the 18th Century

The latter half of the 18th century served as a pivotal period for the restoration and revival of various cultural, social, and architectural interests across Europe. These revivals ranged from the restoration of ancient thermal baths in France to the emergence of specific artistic styles in furniture.

## Thermal Springs of Bagneres-de-Luchon
In south-western France, specifically in the department of Haute-Garonne, the town of Bagneres-de-Luchon is noted for its thermal springs. Situated in a valley at the confluence of the Pique and the One at the foot of the central Pyrenees, the town is a fashionable resort celebrated for its bathing establishments. The antiquity of these baths is evidenced by numerous Roman remains, and they are identified as the *Onesiorum Thermae* mentioned by Strabo. 

The modern revival of these baths began in the latter half of the 18th century. This resurgence was due to the efforts of Antoine Megret d'Etigny, who served as the *intendant* of Auch. The springs, which number 48, vary in composition—primarily impregnated with sulphate of sodium—and maintain temperatures ranging from 62 to 150 degrees. The town's bathing establishment, known as the Thermes, is located at the southern end of the Allees d'Etigny, an avenue of lime-trees.

## Architecture and Furniture
The latter half of the 18th century also saw the construction of imposing architectural works and the development of distinct furniture styles. In north-eastern France, within the department of Aube, the château of Brienne-le-Château was built during this period by the cardinal de Brienne. This building overlooks the town and houses a significant collection of pictures, including historical portraits from the 18th and 17th centuries.

In the realm of decorative arts, the second half of the 18th century was characterized by the use of mahogany in furniture. Specifically, carved mahogany chairs produced in the style of Chippendale date from this era. This period of design transitioned into the late 18th century with the emergence of the Hepplewhite style, featuring carved and inlaid mahogany.

## Contrasting Trends and Later Revivals
While some interests were revived in the late 18th century, others experienced decline. In England, horse-racing declined significantly during the latter half of the 18th century, leading to the discontinuation of many meetings due to the wars of the period. Racing did not rapidly revive until the beginning of the 19th century, particularly after 1815 following the conclusion of the French war.

Similarly, other forms of cultural revival occurred in different timeframes. The revival of interest in various forms of English dialects followed the revival of Lowland Scots, which took place across the 18th and 19th centuries. In the religious sphere, the "artificial modern revival" of carol-singing produced new carols often derived from medieval Latin Christmas hymns, though this is distinct from the 18th-century religious carols such as those by Charles Wesley.
