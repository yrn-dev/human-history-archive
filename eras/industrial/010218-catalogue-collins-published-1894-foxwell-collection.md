# Historical Collections and Catalogues

The acquisition and cataloguing of works with historical value serve as a bridge between private collecting and formal historical research. Collectors who focus on original editions or works that have not been reprinted often act as both rivals and servants to historical students, preserving authentic materials that promote academic study.

## Notable Intellectual Collections
Several significant collections illustrate the impact of focused gathering on historical and professional research. Lord Crawford is cited for his extensive collections of papal bulls and proclamations from Ireland, Scotland, and England. In the realm of philology, Prince Lucien Bonaparte assembled a library that was the subject of *An Attempt at a Catalogue* by V. Collins, published in 1894. Additionally, the Foxwell collection, which focuses on early books regarding political economy, was presented to the University of London by the Goldsmiths' Company. While libraries associated with special studies and professions now conduct much of this work, opportunities remain for private collectors to pursue similar lines of inquiry.

## Hagiographic Research and the Bollandists
The Bollandists shifted their activities in 1882 to align more closely with historical methods, establishing a quarterly review titled *Analecta Bollandiana*. Founded by Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt, the review reached its 25th volume in 1906. Under the editorship of P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt, the publication provided inedited texts, dissertations, and studies intended to remould and continue the *Acta Sanctorum*. Since 1892, it has also included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques* for the criticism of recent hagiographic works.

The Bollandists also analyzed hagiographic manuscripts in major libraries, producing several specialized catalogues:
* **Bibliotheque Nationale (Paris):** Five volumes on Latin and Greek manuscripts (1889-1896).
* **Bibliotheque Royale (Brussels):** Two volumes on Latin manuscripts (1886-1889).
* **Vatican:** Greek manuscripts (1899).
* **Rome:** Latin manuscripts in various libraries (beginning in 1905).

Furthermore, they created inventories of published hagiographic texts, including the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*. These efforts provided a scientific basis for the publication of the principal collection, including volume ii. (1894) and the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* (1902).

## Commercial Rare Book Cataloguing
The intersection of commerce and bibliography is exemplified by Henry George Bohn (1796-1884). A British publisher and son of a German bookbinder, Bohn began dealing in "remainders" and rare books in 1831. In 1841, he produced a "monumental" "Guinea" Catalogue containing 23,208 items. Bohn was also known for high-profile auction sales; a notable 1848 sale lasted four days and featured a twenty-folio-page catalogue that included a schedule for dinner, dessert, tea, and supper.

Beyond dealing, Bohn inaugurated the *Libraries* in 1846, a series of 766 volumes consisting of translations and standard works on archaeology, classics, theology, science, and history. He also performed a laborious revision of W.T. Lowndes' *The Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature* in 1864, expanding the 1834 original with critical notices and price particulars. Bohn sold the *Libraries* to G. Bell & Sons (originally Messrs. Bell and Daldy) in 1864.

## Regional and Specialized Bibliographies
Cataloguing is also employed to preserve regional history and specific academic fields. In Cornwall, various authorities have compiled records, such as G. C. Boase and W. P. Courtney's *Bibliotheca Cornubiensis* (1864-1881), which catalogues printed works and manuscripts by Cornishmen or works relating to Cornwall. Other regional records include the *Collectanea Cornubiensia* (1890) by G. C. Boase.

In the field of astronomy, the constellation Draco has been the subject of various catalogues over time, including those by Hevelius (40 stars), Tycho Brahe (32 stars), and Ptolemy (31 stars).
