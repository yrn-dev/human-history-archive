# Bolton and the Industrial Revolution

## Early Woollen Industry
Bolton was recognized for its woollen manufactures from its early days. During the reign of Richard I, the town served as a center for the woollen cloth trade, as evidenced by the appointment of an aulneger responsible for stamping and measuring all bundles of woollen goods. The industry experienced a significant impulse in 1337 following the settlement of a party of Flemish clothiers. By 1558, Leland noted in his *Itinerary* that Bolton produced "cottons," though these were actually woollen goods. The growth of the trade was such that in 1566, an act of parliament was required to appoint deputies to assist the aulnegers, with Bolton specifically named as a location for these deputies.

## Transition to Cotton and Fustians
Actual cotton goods were not manufactured in Lancashire until 1641. At that time, Bolton was identified as the primary seat for the manufacture of dimities, vermilions, and fustians. This industry was further stimulated by the arrival of French refugees following the revocation of the edict of Nantes. Innovation continued into the 18th century; muslins and cotton quiltings were produced in 1763, and velvets were first manufactured around 1756 by Jeremiah Clarke.

## Technological Advancements and Factory Growth
The cotton trade experienced an astonishing impetus due to the inventions of Samuel Crompton (1780) and Sir Richard Arkwright (1770), both of whom were born in the parish. Following the introduction of machinery, spinning factories were established, with the first in Bolton reportedly set up in 1780. The number of these facilities grew rapidly; by 1851, there were 66 cotton mills operating with 860,000 throstle spindles.

## Related Industries and Infrastructure
Beyond cotton spinning, the cognate industry of bleaching had been practiced since the early 18th century. In the latter half of the 19th century, large ironworks were developed in the area. Infrastructure improvements supported this industrial growth, including the construction of a canal from Manchester to Bolton in 1791. Additionally, an act of parliament in 1792 led to the enclosure of Bolton Moor.
