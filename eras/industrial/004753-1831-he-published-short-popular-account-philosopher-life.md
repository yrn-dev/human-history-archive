# Brewster's Biography of Sir Isaac Newton

## Literary and Editorial Career
Brewster began his literary pursuits in 1799 as a regular contributor to the *Edinburgh Magazine*, serving as its editor when he was twenty years old. His editorial experience expanded in 1807 when he undertook the editorship of the *Edinburgh Encyclopaedia*; the first part of this work was published in 1808, and the final part appeared in 1830. The scientific department was the strongest portion of the encyclopedia, with Brewster authoring many of its most valuable articles.

Brewster also became a leading contributor to the seventh and eighth editions of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, writing articles on subjects including Magnetism, Optics, Hydrodynamics, Stereoscope, Microscope, Electricity, and Voltaic Electricity. 

In 1819, in conjunction with Robert Jameson (1774-1854), Brewster established the *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* to replace the *Edinburgh Magazine*. Brewster and Jameson jointly edited the first ten volumes from 1819 to 1824, after which Jameson edited the remaining four volumes (1825-1826) alone. Following his separation from Jameson, Brewster started the *Edinburgh Journal of Science* in 1824, editing sixteen volumes between 1824 and 1832 and writing numerous articles for the publication. Additionally, Brewster contributed between three and four hundred papers to the transactions of various learned societies and wrote extensively for various reviews, including seventy-five articles for the *North British Review*.

## Works on Sir Isaac Newton
Among Brewster's larger separate works, his biography of Sir Isaac Newton is regarded as the most important. In 1831, Brewster published a short popular account of the philosopher's life within Murray's *Family Library*. 

This initial account was followed by a much more comprehensive work issued in 1855 titled *Memoirs of the Life, Writings and Discoveries of Sir Isaac Newton*. This later publication was the result of more than twenty years of patient investigation, during which Brewster studied all available sources and original manuscripts.
