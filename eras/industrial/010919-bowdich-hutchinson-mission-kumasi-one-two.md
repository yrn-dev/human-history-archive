# British Mission to Kumasi (1817)

## Background and Objectives
In 1817, the British government dispatched a diplomatic mission to Kumasi, the capital of Ashanti. The mission was prompted by repeated invasions of Fantiland by the Ashanti. The delegation consisted of Frederick James, who served as the commandant of Accra fort, along with T.E. Bowdich and W. Hutchinson.

## Diplomatic Conflict and the Quit Rents
The mission began with one or two harmonious interviews between the emissaries and the king. However, the atmosphere shifted when the king advanced a claim for the payment of quit rents regarding Cape Coast castle and Anamabo fort. The majority of these rents had been paid by the British to the Fanti, who had induced them to do so, leaving only a nominal sum to be transmitted to Kumasi.

When the head of the mission, Mr. James, failed to volunteer a satisfactory explanation regarding these payments, the king reacted with uncontrollable rage. He characterized the British emissaries as liars and cheats.

## Resolution and Treaty
Concerned that the safety of the mission and broader British interests were being endangered by the breakdown in communication, Bowdich and Hutchinson took control of the negotiations. Following this shift in leadership, Mr. James was recalled. 

The mission eventually concluded with a treaty. Under the terms of this agreement, the demands of the king were satisfied, and the British right to control the natives in the coast towns was formally recognized.

## Historical Documentation
The details of this early diplomatic effort are preserved in historical records, most notably in the work *A Mission from Cape Coast Castle to Ashantee*, published in 1819 by T.E. Bowdich, which is cited as one of the most valuable early works on the country.
