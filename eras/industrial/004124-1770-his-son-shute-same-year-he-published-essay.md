# The Barrington Family

The Barrington family includes several prominent English figures across the 18th and 19th centuries, notably the 1st and 2nd Viscounts Barrington and the Bishop Shute Barrington. Their history is characterized by involvement in law, theology, politics, and the church.

## John Shute, 1st Viscount Barrington
John Shute (1678–1734) was an English theologian and lawyer born at Theobalds, Hertfordshire, to Benjamin Shute, a merchant. His education included time at the university of Utrecht, and upon his return to England in 1698, he studied law at the Inner Temple. A member of the Protestant dissenters, Barrington published pamphlets in 1701 advocating for their civil rights.

Barrington's political career included a role in Scotland, where, following a recommendation from Lord Somers, he worked to encourage Presbyterians to support the union of the two kingdoms. For this service, he was appointed commissioner of the customs in 1708, though he was removed from this post in 1711 due to a change in administration. His financial standing was bolstered by bequests of two estates from John Wildman of Becket and Francis Barrington of Tofts; the latter's name he assumed via an act of parliament.

During the reign of George I, Barrington represented Berwick-upon-Tweed in parliament. In 1720, he was granted the title of Viscount Barrington of Ardglass in the Irish peerage. However, his parliamentary career was interrupted in 1723 when he was expelled from the House of Commons for engaging in the Harburg lottery, a bubble speculation. This punishment was viewed by some as excessively severe and attributed to the personal malice of Walpole.

In 1725, he published his primary work, *Miscellanea Sacra or a New Method of considering so much of the History of the Apostles as is contained in Scripture*, originally in two volumes. He also published *An Essay on the Several Dispensations of God to Mankind*. He died on December 14, 1734.

## William Wildman Shute, 2nd Viscount Barrington
The eldest son of the 1st Viscount, William Wildman Shute (1717–1793), was born on January 15, 1717. After succeeding to the title in 1734, he traveled for a period before being returned to parliament as the member for Berwick-upon-Tweed in March 1740. 

His career was marked by various high-ranking government offices:
* **Admiralty:** Appointed one of the lords commissioners of the admiralty in 1746.
* **Parliamentary Roles:** Member of parliament for Plymouth starting in 1754 and a member of the Irish House of Lords from 1745.
* **Government Offices:** He served as secretary at war (1755–1762 and 1765–1778), chancellor of the exchequer (1761), and treasurer of the navy (1762). In 1782, he served as joint postmaster-general for four months.
* **Other Duties:** He was a privy councillor and one of the managers involved in the impeachment of Simon, Lord Lovat.

He married Mary Lovell in 1740 but had no children. He died at Becket on February 1, 1793, and was buried in Shrivenham church.

## Shute Barrington, Bishop of Durham
The youngest son of the 1st Viscount, Shute Barrington (1734–1826), pursued a career in the church. Educated at Oxford and Eton, he held several minor dignities before being appointed bishop of Llandaff in 1769. He was later translated to Salisbury in 1782 and finally to Durham in 1791.

A vigorous Protestant, Bishop Barrington advocated for Roman Catholics to receive all degrees of toleration, provided it did not include political establishment or power. His literary contributions included various tracts and volumes of sermons, as well as a biography detailing the political life of his brother, the 2nd Viscount Barrington.

## Legacy and Associated Figures
The influence of the Barrington family extended to other professionals. Thomas Burgess, an English divine and later bishop of St David's and Salisbury, served as chaplain to Shute Barrington while the latter was bishop of Salisbury. Through Barrington's influence, Burgess obtained a prebendal stall. In 1791, Burgess accompanied Barrington to Durham, where he performed evangelistic work among the poorer classes.

Additionally, the works of the 1st Viscount were preserved by his descendants; in 1770, his son Shute reprinted *Miscellanea Sacra* in three volumes, incorporating corrections and additions.
