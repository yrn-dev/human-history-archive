# Victoria Terminus and the Taj Mahal Hotel

## Overview of Bombay's Architecture
The city of Bombay is noted for having one of the finest water-fronts in the world. Along the esplanade, facing Back Bay, there is a great line of public offices. These buildings are constructed in a style that mixes Gothic and Saracenic elements; while they are not individually distinguished for their architectural merit, they possess a cumulative effect of great dignity. Among the most notable buildings in the city are the Taj Mahal hotel and the Victoria terminus of the Great Indian Peninsula railway.

## The Great Indian Peninsula Railway
The Great Indian Peninsula is one of the chief railway systems in the province. Within the Bombay Presidency, the province is well supplied with railways, the majority of which concentrate at Bombay City. By 1905, the total length of railway open for traffic under the Bombay government was 7,980 miles, though this figure does not include the railway system in Sind.

The Great Indian Peninsula railway serves as a primary artery for the region. For example, it connects with a light railway that serves Barsi, a town in the Sholapur district of Bombay. Barsi is a flourishing center of trade that exports large quantities of oil-seeds and cotton to Bombay, utilizing several factories for pressing and ginning cotton.

## Comparison with Other Regional Systems
The railway infrastructure in Bombay differs from other regions such as Bengal. In Bengal, the system is centered on the seaport of Calcutta. Major lines there include the East Indian, which has its terminus at Howrah, and the Eastern Bengal, which has its termini at Sealdah. While the Bombay system concentrates largely at Bombay City, the North-Western line is a notable exception in the province, as it enters Sind from the Punjab and terminates at Karachi.
