# The Belleville Water-Tube Boiler

The Belleville boiler is identified as the earliest form of water-tube boiler to come into general use within the British navy. It represents a specific design within the broader category of water-tube boilers, which are characterized by containing water mainly in numerous comparatively small tubes, with the fire typically placed beneath these tubes to form the majority of the heating surface.

## Design and Components
The Belleville boiler is composed of two primary sections: the boiler proper and the economizer. Both of these sections consist of several sets of elements arranged side by side. The elements of the boiler proper are positioned directly over the fire, while the economizer elements are located in the uptake above the boiler. The space between these two sections is designed to function as a combustion chamber.

Each element is constructed from a series of straight tubes made of seamless steel. These tubes are connected at their ends via screwed joints to junction-boxes made of malleable cast iron. These boxes are arranged vertically; except for the boxes at the very front and back, each junction-box connects the upper end of one tube to the lower end of the next tube in the element.

## Technical Specifications and Maintenance
The tubes used in the Belleville boiler vary in thickness based on their position. They generally have an external diameter of approximately 4-1/2 inches. The two lowest rows of tubes are 3/8 inch thick, the following two rows are 5/16 inch thick, and the remaining tubes are approximately 1/5 inch thick. The economizer follows a similar construction but utilizes shorter and smaller tubes, which generally measure about 2-3/4 inches in diameter.

For maintenance and inspection, the junction-boxes at the back of the boiler are close-ended, while those at the front feature small oval holes. These openings are secured by an internal door with a cross-bar and bolt, allowing the interior of the tubes to be cleaned and examined. Additionally, the design allows for flexibility in repair; any element can be disconnected and replaced with a spare when the boiler is not in use.

## Water Circulation and Feed System
The Belleville boiler employs a specific system for water distribution and sediment management. The lower front box of each element in the boiler proper is connected to a "feed-collector," which is a horizontal cross-tube of square section extending across the full width of the boiler. 

The feed-collector is connected to the lower part of the steam-chest via vertical pipes at both ends of the boiler. These pipes extend below the level of the feed-collector to form closed pockets, which serve to collect sediment. The economizer's lower boxes are connected to a horizontal feed pipe that is supplied with water by a feed-pumping engine.

## Context of Water-Tube Technology
The Belleville boiler operates on the principle of the water-tube class, which differs from "tank" boilers. While tank boilers use a cylindrical outer shell to contain large quantities of water and are generally internally fired, water-tube boilers enclose both the fire and the tubes within an outer casing made of fire-resisting substance or brickwork. Because water is contained in tubes rather than a large shell, special design provisions—such as those found in the Belleville—are necessary to maintain the circulation of water through the tubes. In naval applications, such as the Belleville, seamless steel tubes are invariably used.
