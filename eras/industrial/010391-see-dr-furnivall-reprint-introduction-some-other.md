# Frederick James Furnivall

Frederick James Furnivall (1825–1910) was an English editor and philologist who spent half a century promoting the study of early English literature. Born on February 4, 1825, in Egham, Surrey, he was the son of a surgeon. Although he was called to the bar in 1849, his professional focus shifted toward social problems and philological studies.

## Social and Educational Contributions
Furnivall was involved in the Christian Socialist movement, providing valuable assistance to Frederick Denison Maurice. He was also among the founders of the Working Men's College.

## Philological Work and Societies
Furnivall's primary legacy lies in his promotion of early and middle English studies. He achieved this through his own editorial work and by founding and directing several learned societies. Most notably, he founded the Early English Text Society in 1864, an organization described as being of inestimable service to the field. Additionally, he established and conducted the following societies:
* The Chaucer Society
* The Ballad Society
* The New Shakespeare Society
* The Wyclif Society
* Societies dedicated to the special study of Shelley and Browning

Furnivall served as the honorary secretary of the Philological Society. He was also one of the original promoters of the *New English Dictionary* at Oxford. During the preliminary period of material collection, he co-operated with the first editor, Herbert Coleridge, and served as the principal editor for a time following Coleridge's death.

## Editorial Labours
Furnivall edited texts for the Rolls Series, the Roxburghe Club, and the Early English Text Society. His most significant editorial contributions were devoted to Chaucer; he specifically assisted the study of the poet through other publications of the Chaucer Society and his "Six-Text" edition of the *Canterbury Tales*. 

His work with the Early English Text Society included the publication of *Political Poems*. In 1870, he produced a reprint of the *Introduction* and other selections for the society's new series. He also co-edited volume ii (1867) of Bishop Percy's Folio MS, which includes a version of *Lanval* featuring Sir Lambewell.

## Personal Interests and Later Life
Outside of his academic and social pursuits, Furnivall was an enthusiastic oarsman. In 1845, alongside John Beesley, he introduced a new type of narrow sculling boat. His interest in rowing continued throughout his life, and in 1886, he started sculling eights and sculling fours races on the Thames.

In 1900, his fifty years of labor were recognized with a handsome testimonial. This included a considerable donation to the Early English Text Society and a volume of philological essays dedicated to him titled *An English Miscellany*, published in Oxford in 1901.

Frederick James Furnivall died on July 2, 1910.
