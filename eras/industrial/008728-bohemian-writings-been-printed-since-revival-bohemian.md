# Bohemian Literature and the 19th-Century Revival

The written history of the Bohemian (Czech) language is ancient, though some early records, such as the manuscripts of Kralodvur and Zelena Hora, are considered 19th-century forgeries. Genuine early documents include legends—most notably those of St. Dorothy and St. Catherine—as well as hymns and epic fragments like *Tandarias a Floribella* and the *Alexandreis*. Another significant early work is the rhymed chronicle often called the chronicle of Dalimil; likely written during the reign of King John (1310-1346), it records Bohemian history up to the reign of King Henry of Carinthia and is characterized by a strong racial antipathy toward Germans.

## Medieval and Reformation Era Writings
By the 14th century, Bohemia's high intellectual level produced writers of philosophy and religion using the national language. Thomas of Stitny (c. 1331-1401), a forerunner of Huss, wrote the valued *Besedni reci* ("learned entertainments") and *O obecnych vecech Krestanskych* (on general Christian matters). John Huss also contributed significantly to the language, writing in both Latin and Bohemian. His most important Bohemian work, written in 1412, is the *Vyklad viry, desatera Boziho prikazani, a patere* (exposition of the creed, the ten commandments and the Lord's Prayer), alongside his *Postilla* (sermons), letters written in prison, and treatises on simony and the road to salvation.

Following Huss's death, the Hussite movement utilized the national language due to its democratic nature. While the writings of the extreme Taborite party were entirely destroyed, some works from the moderate Calixtines (Utraquists) survived, including *Lives of the Priests of Tabor* and *Of the Great Torment of the Holy Church*. Archbishop John of Rokycan also produced a *Postilla*.

## The Bohemian Brethren
The community of the Bohemian Brethren, indirectly founded by Chelcicky, exerted a profound influence on the refinement of the Bohemian language, writing nearly all their theological and historical works in the national tongue. Their most famed achievement was the *Bible of Kralice*, first printed in 1593. Other notable figures include Brother Gregory, who wrote *On good and evil priests* and *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan*, and Bishop Augusta, whose captivity was recorded by Jan Bilek.

The brotherhood's greatest writer was John Amos Comenius (1592-1670), who authored 142 works, including many educational and devotional texts in his native language. His most famous Bohemian work is the allegorical tale *Labyrinth of the World*. Many other historical works of the brotherhood, such as Blahoslav's *History of the Unity* and Wenceslas Brezan's *History of the House of Rosenberg*, were largely destroyed in the 17th century when anti-Roman books were purged.

## The 19th-Century Revival
Following the deaths of Comenius, Slavata, and Skala, the Bohemian language saw a period of decline, exacerbated by an Austrian government that discouraged its use. A revival began in the early 19th century driven by the romantic movement. Initially, this revival was modest and focused primarily on translations from foreign languages. Joseph Dobrovsky (1753-1829) was the first to draw renewed attention to the language; although he wrote a history of Bohemian literature and a grammar, these were mostly in Latin or German. His Bohemian contributions were limited to a collection of letters and essays in the *Casopis Musea Kralovstvi Ceskeho* (Journal of the Bohemian Museum).

The revival's primary success is attributed to four men:
*   **Joseph Jungmann (1773-1847):** He published numerous translations of English and German writers and created a monumental German and Bohemian dictionary and a history of Bohemian literature (*Dejepes literatury ceska*).
*   **John Kolar (1793-1852):** The revival's greatest poet, known for *Slavy dcera* (daughter of Slavia), a personification of the Slavic race.
*   **Paul Joseph Safarik (1795-1861):** Author of *Starozitnosti Slovanske* (Slavic antiquities), which recorded the history and literature of early Slavs.
*   **Francis Palacky (1798-1876):** Regarded as the greatest Bohemian historian, specifically for his history of Bohemia from the earliest period to 1526.

## Later Historical and Literary Works
Since the revival, early Bohemian writings have been printed, and the field of history has remained fruitful. Wenceslas Tomek (1818-1905) wrote the *Dejepis miesta Prahy* (history of the town of Prague). Jaroslav Goll (b. 1846) focused on the Bohemian Brethren and co-founded the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* (Bohemian Historical Review) in 1895 with Dr. Anton Rezek. Other contributors include Professor Joseph Kalousek, who studied the ancient constitution (*Statni pravo*), Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, who wrote on John Huss, and Professors Pic and Niederle, who conducted archaeological studies. Modern histories of Bohemian literature in the national language have been produced by Mr. Jaroslav Vlaek, Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, and Dr. Karel Tieftrunk.
