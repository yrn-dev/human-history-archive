# J. P. A. Mery

## Collaboration with Auguste Barthelemy
J. P. A. Mery (1798-1866) was a French writer and compatriot of the satirical poet Auguste Marseille Barthelemy (1796-1867). The two maintained a collaboration so intimate that it is considered impossible to distinguish their individual personalities within their joint works. 

Their partnership produced a series of immediate and pronounced successes in the form of political satires and squibs. Notable joint productions include *La Villeliade ou la prise du chateau de Rivoli* (1827), which required fifteen editions within a single year, as well as *Les Sidiennes* (1825), *La Corbiereide* (1827), and *La Peyronneide* (1827). Other significant works authored by the pair include *Biographie des quarante de l'academie francaise* (1826) and *Napoleon en Egypte* (1828), the latter of which saw nearly a dozen editions in one year.

## Political Activity and Legal Consequences
The writings of Barthelemy and Mery often targeted political figures and governments. In 1829, the publication of their poem *Fils de l'homme*, which concerned Napoleon's son, the duke of Reichstadt, resulted in Barthelemy being fined 1000 francs and imprisoned. 

Following the Revolution of 1830, which liberated Barthelemy, Mery and his collaborator celebrated the triumph of the people with a work titled *L'Insurrection*. Between March 1831 and April 1832, they published a weekly series of verse satires called the *Nemesis*, which were used to attack the ministers and government of Louis Philippe.

## Historical Disputes
The work of Barthelemy and Mery also drew responses from military figures. Marshal Grouchy wrote *Fragments historiques relatifs a la campagne et a la bataille de 1815* between 1829 and 1830 specifically in reply to the writings of Mery and Barthelemy, as well as Marshal Gerard.
