# Headers in Boiler Construction

In the context of water-tube boiler engineering, headers are critical components used to connect water-tubes to the steam-chest. They are typically constructed from wrought steel and are designed to facilitate the circulation of water and the passage of furnace gases.

## Babcock & Wilcox Boiler Headers

The Babcock & Wilcox boiler, utilized in merchant steamers, yachts, and the British and American navies, employs a system of elements consisting of front and back headers. These headers are connected by numerous water-tubes that are inclined to assist circulation. 

### Design and Configuration
Except for the outer pairs, which feature flat outer portions, the headers are sinuous on both sides, allowing the sinuosities to fit into one another. The front headers are positioned immediately under the steam-chest and are connected to it via short nipples. At the bottom, they connect to a square-section pipe that spans the width of the boiler. The back headers are connected to the steam-chest at their upper ends by two long horizontal tubes, while their bottom ends remain closed.

### Tube Arrangement and Access
Tubes are utilized in two different sizes: 1-13/16 inches and 3-15/16 inches. The larger diameter is used for the return tubes between the back headers and the steam-chest, as well as for the two lower rows. The smaller tubes are arranged in groups of two or four at nearly all the header sinuosities to ensure furnace gases are well mixed and maintain contact with heating surfaces.

To allow for the securing of tubes, headers are furnished with holes on opposite sides. For tubes grouped in fours, a hole is formed on the side opposite each tube. For groups of two, a larger oval hole is provided. These oval holes are closed with fittings similar to those found in land boilers. Smaller conical holes, which are wider on the inside, are closed by special conical fittings consisting of a single forging of a bolt and conical portion, paired with a close-ended nut.

## Land-Based Babcock & Wilcox Boilers
In versions of the Babcock & Wilcox boiler fitted for land purposes, the nest of tubes consists of separate elements formed by sinuous wrought steel front and back headers. The front headers connect to the front cross-box of the horizontal cylinder at the top and are closed at the bottom. The back headers connect to the back cross-box at the top and to a horizontal mud drum at the bottom via short pipes; this mud drum includes a pipe and blow-off cock.

The headers in these land boilers feature holes on two opposite sides. One side provides the connection between the headers and the tubes, while the opposite side allows for cleaning and the fixing of tubes in position. The outer holes are oval and secured by special fittings and outer cover plates. These oval fittings are designed to be placed from the outside and are constructed to cover the opening to prevent a significant outrush of water or steam if a bolt should break.

## Other Boiler Header Types

### Niclausse Boiler
The Niclausse boiler, used in the British navy and electric-light installations on land, utilizes headers with a rectangular cross-section. These are typically made of malleable cast iron with cast-in diaphragms, though steel is sometimes used. In steel versions, the diaphragms and the closed bottoms are made of riveted steel plate. 

The tube-holes in Niclausse headers are accurately bored through the front, back, and diaphragm at an angle that matches the 6-degree inclination of the tubes. The holes in the header walls are slightly conical. These headers are bolted to socket-pieces riveted to the bottom of the steam-chest to allow for easy removal of any element.

### Durr Boiler
The Durr boiler, used in the German navy and some British vessels, differs from the Niclausse by replacing separate headers with a single large water-chamber made of steel plates with welded joints. In this design, tubes are secured to the inner plate using conical joints. The holes in the outer plate are closed by small round doors that are fitted from the inside.
