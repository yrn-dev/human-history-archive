# Thomas Banks

Thomas Banks (1735–1805) was an English sculptor born in London on December 29, 1735. The son of a surveyor who served as a land steward to the duke of Beaufort, Banks received his initial instruction in drawing from his father. In 1750, he began an apprenticeship with a wood-carver, though he continued to practice sculpture during his spare time.

## Education and Early Career
Before 1772, Banks had already exhibited several fine works. In that year, he obtained a travelling studentship, which allowed him to proceed to Rome. He returned to England in 1779, but found that the taste for classic poetry—which had always served as his source of inspiration—no longer existed. Consequently, he spent two years in St Petersburg. During this period, he was employed by Empress Catherine, who purchased his work titled "Cupid tormenting a Butterfly."

## Major Works and Recognition
Upon returning to England, Banks modeled a colossal work titled "Achilles mourning the loss of Briseis," a piece described as being full of passion and force. This work led to his election as an associate of the Royal Academy in 1784, and he became a full member the following year.

Banks's most famous work is perhaps the colossal group "Shakespeare attended by Painting and Poetry," which is currently located in the garden of New Place in Stratford-on-Avon. His other notable contributions include:
* **St Paul's Cathedral:** Monuments to Captain Burges and Captain Westcott.
* **Westminster Abbey:** A monument to Sir Eyre Coote.
* **National Portrait Gallery:** A bust of Warren Hastings.

Thomas Banks died in London on February 2, 1805.
