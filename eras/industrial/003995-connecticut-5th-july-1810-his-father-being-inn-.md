# Phineas Taylor Barnum

## Early Life and Early Business Ventures
Phineas Taylor Barnum was born on July 5, 1810, in Bethel, Connecticut. His father worked as a store-keeper and an inn-keeper. Barnum's own professional beginnings were as a store-keeper, and he was also involved in the lottery mania that was prevalent in the United States at the time. Following failures in business, Barnum transitioned to publishing in 1829, starting a weekly newspaper in Danbury called *The Herald of Freedom*. His time as a publisher was marked by several libel suits and a prosecution that led to his imprisonment.

## Career as a Showman
In 1834, Barnum moved to New York, and by 1835, he began his career as a showman. His first major venture involved the purchase and exploitation of Joyce Heth, a coloured woman who was reputed to be over one hundred and sixty years old and the nurse of George Washington. Barnum conducted successful and well-advertised tours across America with Heth and a small company until 1839. However, Heth died in 1836, at which point it was proved that her age was no more than seventy.

After a period of failure, Barnum purchased Scudder's American Museum in New York in 1841. He expanded the museum considerably, turning it into one of the most popular shows in the United States. A significant success occurred in 1842 with the exhibition of Charles Stratton, known as "General Tom Thumb." Barnum subsequently toured England with the dwarf in 1844.

## Major Productions and Later Career
Barnum demonstrated significant enterprise in 1850 when he engaged the singer Jenny Lind to perform in America. The arrangement provided Lind with $1000 a night for one hundred and fifty nights, with the entrepreneur paying all expenses.

Although Barnum retired from the show business in 1855, he was forced to return to his career as a museum proprietor and showman after having to settle with his creditors in 1857. In 1871, he established the "Greatest Show on Earth," which was a travelling amalgamation of a museum of "freaks," a menagerie, and a circus. This venture was incorporated as "Barnum, Bailey & Hutchinson," and later as "Barnum & Bailey's," and it toured globally. The business was eventually sold to Ringling Brothers in 1907.

## Literary Works and Death
In addition to his career in entertainment, Barnum authored several books. These include *Autobiography* (first published in 1854, with later editions), *The Humbugs of the World* (1865), and *Struggles and Triumphs* (1869). Phineas Taylor Barnum died on April 7, 1891.
