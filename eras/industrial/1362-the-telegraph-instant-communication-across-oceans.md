# The Electric Nerve: The Telegraph and the Shrinking World

## Overview
The invention and deployment of the telegraph during the Industrial Revolution marked the first time in human history that information could travel faster than a physical messenger. Before the mid-19th century, communication across oceans relied on ships, meaning a message from London to New York took weeks to arrive. The introduction of electrical telegraphy decoupled communication from transportation, effectively "shrinking" the planet and revolutionizing global diplomacy, finance, and journalism.

While Samuel Morse is often credited with the breakthrough in the United States, the telegraph was a global effort involving figures like William Cooke and Charles Wheatstone in Britain. The true zenith of this era was the laying of the transatlantic cable, a feat of engineering that faced immense technical hurdles and repeated failures before finally establishing a permanent link between the Old and New Worlds.

## Key Facts
*   **Economic Impact:** The telegraph birthed the modern financial market. For the first time, commodity prices (like cotton or wheat) could be synchronized between exchanges in Liverpool and New York, reducing arbitrage and increasing market efficiency.
*   **Political Control:** Imperial powers, particularly Great Britain, used the telegraph to manage distant colonies more tightly. The "All Red Line" (a network of cables connecting the British Empire) allowed London to issue orders to India or Canada in hours rather than months.
*   **The Technical Struggle:** The early transatlantic cables suffered from "signal attenuation." Historians and scientists of the era debated the nature of electricity in underwater cables; some believed the signal slowed down or "smeared," leading to the development of more sophisticated relays and insulation using gutta-percha (a natural latex).

## Did You Know?
1. **The Gutta-Percha Secret:** The transatlantic cables would have been impossible without gutta-percha, a resin from Malaysian trees that provided the only effective underwater insulation available at the time.
2. **The 1858 Failure:** The first transatlantic cable of 1858 worked for only three weeks. The chief electrician, Wildman Whitehouse, attempted to increase the voltage to boost the signal, which inadvertently fried the cable's insulation.
3. **The Birth of the "News Cycle":** The telegraph led to the creation of the Associated Press (AP), as newspapers needed a way to share expensive telegraphic reports to make the costs viable.
