# Blue Bricks

Blue bricks are a variety of very strong, vitreous bricks characterized by a dark, slaty-blue colour. They are primarily manufactured in North Wales and South Staffordshire. Due to their specific physical properties, they are utilized extensively in engineering work and in any application where impermeability or great compressional resistance is required.

## Composition and Manufacture
Blue bricks are produced from clay that contains between 7% and 10% oxide of iron. The initial stages of manufacture follow ordinary brick-making processes. However, during the later stages of firing, the bricks are subjected to a smoky atmosphere created by damping down the admission of air and throwing small amounts of bituminous coal upon the fire-mouths. 

This strongly reducing action transforms the red ferric oxide into metallic iron or blue-green ferrous oxide. These elements combine with the silica in the clay to create a fusible ferrous silicate. This "slag" fills the pores of the brick and combines with other silicates to produce a vitreous, impermeable layer. The thickness of this layer depends on the texture of the brick, the finishing temperature of the kiln, and the character and duration of the smoking process. Additionally, particles of carbon penetrate the surface during the early stages of smoking, which contributes to a darker colour and a harder surface.

## Physical Properties and Applications
Because they are vitrified throughout, blue bricks are heavy, hard, durable, and impervious. These characteristics make them suitable for specialized construction needs. Beyond general engineering work, blue bricks of special shapes are produced for the following purposes:
*   Coping
*   Channelling
*   Paving

## Strength and Performance
Blue bricks exhibit significantly higher crushing loads and structural strength compared to other brick types. In terms of crushing load per square inch, Staffordshire blue bricks range from 2.8 to 3.3 tons, which is substantially higher than common hand-made bricks (0.4 to 0.9 tons), machine-made bricks (0.9 to 1.2 tons), or London stock bricks (0.7 to 1.3 tons). They are comparable to sand-lime bricks, which range from 2.9 to 3.4 tons per square inch.

When used in brickwork, the strength of Staffordshire blue varies based on the mortar used. Experiments conducted by the Royal Institute of British Architects in 1896-1897 found that Staffordshire blue brickwork crushed under a pressure of 114.34 tons per square foot when using lime mortar, and 135.43 tons per square foot when using cement mortar. This performance far exceeds that of other brickwork types, such as Leicester red (45.36 to 83.36 tons per sq. ft.), Fletton (30.68 to 56.25 tons per sq. ft.), and Gault (31.14 to 51.34 tons per sq. ft.).
