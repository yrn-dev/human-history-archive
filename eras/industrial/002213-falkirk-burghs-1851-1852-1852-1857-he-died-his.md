# James Baird

## Early Life and Business Career
James Baird (1802–1876) was a Scottish iron-master born on December 5, 1802, at Kirkwood, Lanarkshire. He was the son of a coal-master. In 1826, Baird entered into a lease for coalfields at Gartsherrie and the surrounding area alongside his father and two brothers. This venture expanded in 1828 when they leased nearby iron mines, followed by the construction of blast furnaces in 1830.

Following the retirement of his father in 1830, the firm of William Baird & Co. was organized, and James Baird took over active control of the operation. Through the implementation of machinery improvements, Baird significantly increased the output of his furnaces. By 1864, the number of furnaces had grown to nearly fifty, employing 10,000 workers and producing 300,000 tons annually. In addition to their industrial success, the brothers became great landowners.

## Political Career
James Baird entered politics and served as the Member of Parliament (M.P.) for the Falkirk burghs. He held this position during two consecutive terms: from 1851 to 1852 and from 1852 to 1857.

## Philanthropy and Later Life
During his lifetime, Baird was recognized as a great public benefactor. His philanthropic efforts included the founding of schools and the establishment of the Baird Lectures in 1871, which were intended for the defence of orthodox theology. In 1873, he created the Baird Trust with a sum of £500,000 to assist the Established Church of Scotland in meeting the spiritual needs of the masses.

James Baird married twice, though he left no children. He died on June 20, 1876, at his estate located near Ayr. At the time of his death, his property was valued at three million pounds.
