# Edwin Forrest and the American Actors Series

## Literary Contributions of Lawrence Barrett
Lawrence Barrett (1838–1891) was an American actor born in Paterson, New Jersey, to Irish parents. Throughout his career, Barrett contributed to the documentation of the American stage. In 1881, he wrote a life of Edwin Forrest, which was published in Boston as part of the *American Actors Series*. Additionally, in 1886, Barrett authored a sketch of Edwin Booth for the publication *Edwin Booth and his Contemporaries*, also published in Boston.

## Professional Career of Lawrence Barrett
Born with the family name Brannigan, Barrett began his acting career in 1853 in Detroit, appearing as Murad in *The French Spy*. He debuted in New York in December 1856 at the Chambers Street theatre in the role of Sir Thomas Clifford in *The Hunchback*. By 1858, he was a member of the stock company at the Boston Museum. 

Beyond the stage, Barrett served as a captain in the 28th Massachusetts infantry regiment during the Civil War. Between 1867 and 1870, he co-managed the California theatre in San Francisco with John McCullough. Barrett's repertoire was extensive, including roles such as Hamlet, Macbeth, Lear, Richard III, Shylock, Richelieu, Wolsey, and Benedick. He also performed as David Garrick, Hernani, Alfred Evelyn, and James Harebell in *The Man o' Airlie*. In George Henry Boker's *Francesca da Rimini*, he played Lanciotto. He performed alongside Booth, playing Cassius to Booth's Brutus and Othello to Booth's Iago. Barrett also acted in London during 1867, 1881, 1883, and 1884; his portrayal of Richelieu in the drama by Bulwer Lytton was regarded as his best part.

## Edwin Forrest and Associated Figures
Edwin Forrest was a significant figure in the American theatrical landscape, serving as a subject for both literature and art. In addition to the biography written by Lawrence Barrett, Forrest's influence extended to other actors and artists. John Edward McCullough (1837–1885), an Irish-born American actor, played second roles in tragedies in support of both Edwin Booth and Edwin Forrest. Upon his death, Forrest left all of his prompt books to McCullough.

Forrest was also immortalized in sculpture by Thomas Ball (1819–1897). Ball, a Massachusetts-born sculptor who studied in Florence, created a work of Edwin Forrest as "Coriolanus," which is located in the Actors' Home in Philadelphia.
