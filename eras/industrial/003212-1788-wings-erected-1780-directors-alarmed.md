# Bank of England Building and Development

## Early History and Locations
The Bank of England's operations began in the Mercers' chapel following the election of the first twenty-four directors on Wednesday, 11th July 1694. These directors were men of high mercantile standing, including two brothers of the first governor, Sir John Houblon. Sir John Houblon was a descendant of James Houblon, a Flemish refugee who had escaped persecution from Alva. 

The bank's business remained at the Mercers' chapel until 28th September, at which point the operations moved to Grocers' Hall. The bank continued as tenants of Grocers' Hall until 1732.

## Construction of the Permanent Site
The first stone of the bank's current building was laid on 1st August 1732, and the institution has remained on that site since that time. The building was constructed on land previously occupied by the gardens and house of the first governor, Sir John Houblon, which had been purchased for this specific purpose.

The structure underwent several phases of expansion and modification over the following decades:
* **1764–1788:** The wings of the building were erected.
* **1780:** The directors became concerned that the adjacent church of St Christopher le Stocks provided dangerous facilities that a mob could utilize. Consequently, they obtained parliamentary powers to acquire the fabric of the church. Much of the present building stands on the site of that former church.
* **19th Century:** The structure was developed into its present form around the commencement of the 19th century.
