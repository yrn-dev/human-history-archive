# The Durr Boiler

The Durr boiler is a type of marine boiler utilized in several vessels within the German navy, as well as in a small number of vessels in the British navy. In its general design, it bears some resemblance to the Niclausse boiler, though it incorporates several distinct structural and mechanical differences.

## Design and Construction
The Durr boiler differs from the Niclausse boiler primarily in its header configuration. While the Niclausse uses separate headers, the Durr boiler employs a single large water-chamber. This chamber is constructed from steel plates featuring welded joints. 

The method of securing the tubes also differs from the Niclausse system. In the Durr boiler, tubes are not secured to two plates by "lanternes"; instead, they are secured only to the inner plate using conical joints. To maintain the integrity of the outer plate, the holes are closed by small round doors that are fitted from the inside. During the installation process, each tube is forced into its specific position individually using a small portable hydraulic jack. The caps' lower ends are sealed with cap-nuts manufactured from a specific heat-resisting alloy consisting of manganese and copper.

## Circulation and Gas Flow
To facilitate the movement of water, the Durr boiler utilizes inner tubes and a diaphragm located within the water-chamber, a feature similar to that found in the Niclausse boiler. To ensure that furnace gases circulate effectively among the tubes, baffle plates are installed.

## Superheating System
The boiler includes a supplementary set of tubes located above the main set. These tubes are arranged horizontally and connect directly to the steam receiver. This section is equipped with an internal diaphragm and internal tubes, allowing steam from the chest to circulate through them before reaching the stop-valves. Although this arrangement is intended to function as a superheater, the available surface area is insufficient to produce more than a very small amount of superheat.
