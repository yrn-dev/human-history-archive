# Joanna Baillie

## Early Life and Education
Joanna Baillie was a British dramatist and poet born on September 11, 1762, at the manse of Bothwell, located on the banks of the Clyde. She was a member of an old Scottish family that claimed Sir William Wallace as one of its ancestors. Baillie received an excellent education and began writing poetry early in her life. At a young age, she moved to London with her sister, Agnes, to join their brother, Dr. Matthew Baillie. Following an inheritance of a small competence from their uncle, Dr. William Hunter, the two sisters settled in Hampstead, which was then on the outskirts of London, where they lived for the remainder of their lives.

## Literary Career and "Plays on the Passions"
Baillie's first published work was a volume titled *Fugitive Verses*, released anonymously in 1790. However, she is most noted for her "plays on the passions," the first volume of which, *A Series of Plays*, was published in 1798. Her objective with these works was to illustrate the strongest and deepest passions of the human mind—including love, fear, jealousy, and hate—by utilizing both a comedy and a tragedy to exhibit how an individual acts under the influence of these specific passions.

Although the first volume was published anonymously and its authorship was initially attributed to Sir Walter Scott, Baillie was soon discovered as the author. The work achieved considerable success, leading to the publication of a second volume in 1802 and a third in 1812. In 1836, she published three volumes of *Dramas*. Other works include *Miscellaneous Plays*, published in 1804, and *The Family Legend*, published in 1810.

## Theatrical Intent and Reception
Joanna Baillie intended for her plays to be performed on the stage rather than remaining as "closet" dramas. Some of her works saw varying degrees of success in the theater:
* ***The Family Legend***: Produced in Edinburgh in 1810 under the enthusiastic patronage of Sir Walter Scott, it enjoyed a brief but brilliant success.
* ***De Monfort***: This play had a short run in London, largely due to the performances of Mrs. Siddons and John Kemble.
* ***Henriquez*** and ***The Separation***: These productions were received coldly.

Despite her intentions, most of Baillie's plays are considered unsuited for stage exhibition. Critics point to a flaw in her fundamental concept—the idea of an individual serving as the embodiment of a single passion. Additionally, the lack of incident and the focus of attention on a single point are cited as insuperable obstacles to their success as acting pieces.

## Death and Legacy
Joanna Baillie died in 1851. Her collected works were published in London that same year under the title *Dramatic and Poetical Works*.
