# Basset Horn and Related Clarinet Developments

The basset horn is a musical instrument that flourished primarily in Germany. During the late 18th century, it served as a favorite solo instrument for several celebrated instrumentalists, including Lotz, Springer, David, and Czerny.

## Origins and Development
The basset horn was developed from the same archetypes as the clarinet. Its creation was driven by a desire prevalent during the 17th and 16th centuries to establish complete families of instruments capable of playing in concert. The invention of the instrument is attributed to a clarinet maker from Passau named Horn in 1770, from whom the instrument derived its name. 

Due to a misnomer, the instrument became known as the *cor de basset* in France and the *corno di bassetto* in Italy. The instrument underwent several modifications over the following decades:
* In 1782, Theodore Lotz of Pressburg introduced modifications.
* Further improvements were made by Johann and Anton Stadler, two instrumentalists based in Vienna.
* In 1812, the famous clarinettist Iwan Mueller further evolved the instrument. Mueller invented the alto clarinet in Eb by applying a construction and fingering to the basset horn that was analogous to the clarinet in Bb, which served as his model rather than the clarinet in C.

## Use by Composers
The basset horn was highly appreciated by several great masters of music. Mozart was foremost in his appreciation of the instrument, utilizing it in various capacities:
* **Operas:** In *La Clemenza di Tito*, Mozart wrote a fine obbligato for the instrument in the aria "Non piu di Flori." He also employed it in *Zauberfloete*.
* **Chamber Music:** Mozart composed a short adagio for bassoon and two basset horns, as well as another piece for three basset horns and two clarinets.
* **Sacred Music:** In his *Requiem*, the reed family is represented by two bassoons and two basset horns, which are given independent parts.

Other composers who employed the instrument include Beethoven, who used it in his *Prometheus* overture, and Mendelssohn. Mendelssohn utilized the basset horn in military music and in two concerted pieces (opp. 113 and 114 in D min. and F) for basset horn and clarinet with pianoforte accompaniment, which were dedicated to Carl and Heinrich Baermann.

## Technical Literature and Documentation
Various instructional texts and catalogs have documented the instrument and its relatives. J. G. H. Backofen published *Anweisung zur Klarinette, nebst einer kurzen Abhandlung ueber das Basset-Horn* in 1803 (Leipzig, Breitkopf & Haertel), which included illustrations. In 1826, Iwan Mueller published *Anweisung zu der neuen Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto, nebst einigen Bemerkungen fuer Instrumentenmacher* (Leipzig, Freidrich Hofmeister), also featuring illustrations.

Other scholarly contributions include Gottfried Weber's "Ueber Clarinette und Bassethorn" in *Cacilia* (1834) and Wilhelm Altenburg's 1904 work, *Die Clarinette, ihre Entstehung und Entwickelung bis zur Jetztzeit in akustischer, technischer u. musikalischer Beziehung*. Visual records of early basset horns are found in the *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments at the Royal Military Exhibition, London* (1890), compiled by Capt. C. R. Day. Additionally, an instrument stamped "H. Grenser, S. Wiesner, Dresden" is noted as being part of the collection of the Rev. F. W. Galpin of Broad Oak, Hatfield.
