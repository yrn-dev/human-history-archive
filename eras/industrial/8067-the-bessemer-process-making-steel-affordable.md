# The Bessemer Process: Making Steel Affordable

## Overview
Before the mid-19th century, steel was a luxury material. While stronger and more flexible than cast iron, the process of creating it—primarily through the crucible method—was slow, labor-intensive, and prohibitively expensive. Steel was reserved for high-end cutlery and swords, while the burgeoning Industrial Revolution relied on brittle iron for rails and bridges.

This changed in 1856 when Sir Henry Bessemer, an English inventor, patented a method for the mass production of steel. The Bessemer process utilized a pear-shaped container called a "converter." By blowing air through molten pig iron, Bessemer triggered a chemical reaction where the oxygen reacted with impurities (such as silicon, manganese, and carbon). This oxidation process released immense heat, keeping the metal molten without additional fuel, and burned off the excess carbon to transform iron into steel.

The result was a dramatic reduction in production time—from several days to roughly 20 minutes—and a collapse in the price of steel. This technological leap enabled the construction of skyscrapers, massive suspension bridges, and thousands of miles of durable railway tracks, effectively fueling the Second Industrial Revolution.

## Key Facts
*   **The Phosphorus Problem:** Early Bessemer steel was often brittle because the process could not remove phosphorus. Historians and engineers note that this limited the process to specific "low-phosphorus" ores until Sidney Gilchrist Thomas introduced a basic lining (dolomite) to the converter in 1878.
*   **Economic Impact:** The cost of steel plummeted from roughly £40 per ton to £7 per ton shortly after the process was perfected, democratizing the material for urban architecture.
*   **The "Bessemer Debate":** There is a long-standing historical discussion regarding the "originality" of the invention. William Kelly in the United States had developed a similar "air-boiling" technique independently around the same time. While Bessemer is credited with the commercialization and refinement, legal battles over priority were common in the late 19th century.

## Did You Know?
1. **Self-Heating:** The Bessemer process is exothermic; the oxidation of impurities generates so much heat that the metal stays liquid without needing an external furnace.
2. **Railroad Revolution:** Before Bessemer, iron rails had to be replaced every few years due to wear; steel rails lasted ten times longer.
3. **The Skyline:** Without the affordability of Bessemer steel, the "Chicago School" of architecture and the birth of the skyscraper would have been physically and financially impossible.
