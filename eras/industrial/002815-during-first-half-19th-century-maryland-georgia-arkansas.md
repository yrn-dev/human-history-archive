# Maryland, Georgia, and Arkansas in the First Half of the 19th Century

During the first half of the 19th century, the states of Maryland, Georgia, and Arkansas underwent specific transitions regarding their electoral processes and maintained various natural resource industries.

## Electoral Transitions
At the time the United States constitution was adopted, viva voce voting (voting by voice) was the prevailing method for public elections in Georgia, Maryland, and Virginia. This stood in contrast to several other colonies and states, such as those in New England, Pennsylvania, Delaware, and the Carolinas, which had already provided for the use of printed or written ballots by 1775 or 1778.

As the 19th century progressed, these states moved away from viva voce voting. During the first half of the century, Maryland and Georgia adopted the ballot system. Arkansas also adopted the ballot in 1846. These changes were part of a broader trend among states that had entered the Union without an initial ballot system, a group that also included Illinois (which adopted the ballot in 1848) and Missouri.

Despite the adoption of ballots, the process remained primitive. There was no official requirement for any entity to print or distribute ballots at polling places. In town meetings, voters wrote their own ballots or used those furnished by candidates. Eventually, political committees for various parties took over the preparation and distribution of ballot-tickets. Because these tickets were prepared for specific party lists, voting a mixed ticket was difficult. Furthermore, since voters received their ballots within a few feet of the polls, secrecy was nearly impossible, which facilitated frequent bribery and intimidation.

## Natural Resources and Industry
The regions of Maryland, Georgia, and Arkansas are noted for specific geological deposits and manufacturing outputs.

### Mineral and Earth Deposits
Georgia and Arkansas both possess deposits of fuller's earth. In Georgia, this appears as a brittle, whitish earth of Oligocene age. Arkansas also works deposits of this material.

Additionally, these states are producers of marble. Georgia is listed as one of the primary American states producing marble by value. Maryland also produces marble, specifically a fine brecciated marble found on the Maryland side of the Potomac, below Point of Rocks.

### Manufacturing in Maryland
Maryland is recognized for the production of whisky made from rye. This distinguishes it from the production in Kentucky, where whisky is made from Indian corn.
