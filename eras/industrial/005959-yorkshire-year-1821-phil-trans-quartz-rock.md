## Geological Context of the Region
While not specific to the year 1821, the archive provides extensive data on the geological formations of Yorkshire. The region contains Corallian rocks, which differ in several respects from their equivalents found in the south of England. These rocks are subdivided into several layers:
*   **Upper Calcareous Grit**
*   **Coral Rag and Upper Clay** (containing *A. plicatilis*)
*   **Middle Calcareous Grit**
*   **Lower Limestone**
*   **Passage Beds** (containing *A. perarmatus*)
*   **Lower Calcareous Grit**

These Corallian strata are instrumental in the formation of the Howardian Hills, the Hambleton Hills, and the Vale of Pickering. Furthermore, these formations are well exposed in Gristhorpe Bay.
