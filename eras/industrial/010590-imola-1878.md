# Imola

Imola, known in antiquity as Forum Cornelii, is a town and episcopal see located in the province of Bologna in Emilia, Italy. Situated 140 feet above sea level, it is located 21 miles southeast of Bologna by rail. As of 1901, the town had a population of 12,058, while the commune's population was 33,144.

## History
The ancient settlement of Forum Cornelii served as a station on the Via Aemilia. While the 5th-century writer Prudentius attributed its founding to Sulla, the town's membership in the Tribus Pollia indicates that it possessed Roman citizenship prior to the Social war. During the Roman era, the poet Martial published his third book of epigrams while residing there. The name "Imolas" began to appear during the Lombard period.

In later history, the town experienced a chequered trajectory. In 1480, it became the possession of Girolamo Riario, the lord of Forli, as part of the dowry for his wife, Caterina Sforza. By 1500, the town was incorporated into the States of the Church by Caesar Borgia.

## Architecture and Landmarks
Imola features several notable religious and civic structures:
*   **The Cathedral of S. Cassiano:** A modernized cathedral containing interesting reliquaries and the tomb of Petrus Chrysologus, a native of Imola who served as the archbishop of Ravenna and died in 451.
*   **S. Domenico:** Notable for its fine Gothic portal.
*   **S. Maria in Regola:** Characterized by an old campanile.
*   **The Citadel:** A square structure with round towers at the angles dating from 1304, which currently serves as a prison.
*   **Madonna del Piratello:** Located 2 miles northwest of the town, this early Renaissance structure dates to 1488. Its campanile was likely built in 1506 based on plans by Bramante.

The town also contains various fine palaces and a large lunatic asylum housing over 1,200 inmates.

## Culture and Education
The communal library of Imola holds several manuscripts, including a miniatures-decorated psalter that formerly belonged to Sir Thomas More. The town is also the birthplace of Innocenzo Francucci (Innocenzo da Imola), a painter of the Bolognese school who lived from 1494 to 1549; two of his works are kept in the Palazzo del Comune.

## Notable Figures and Literature
Imola is associated with several historical figures. Pope Honorius II (Lamberto Scannabecchi), who served as pope from December 15, 1124, to February 13, 1130, was a native of Fagnano, located near Imola.

In the field of historical literature, the town is the site of publication for the 1878 work *Cesare Borgia, Duca di Romagna*, written by E. Alvisi. This text is cited as an authority on the life of Cesare Borgia, an unscrupulous and clever Renaissance adventurer whose success was largely dependent on the support of the papacy.
