# BARTHELEMY SAINT-HILAIRE, JULES (1805-1895)

Jules Barthélemy Saint-Hilaire was a French statesman and philosopher who lived from 1805 to 1895. Born in Paris on August 19, 1805, he maintained a career that spanned political journalism, academic scholarship in ancient philosophy, and high-level government service.

## Early Political Journalism and Academic Transition
In his early years, Saint-Hilaire was an active political journalist. Between 1826 and 1830, he wrote for *Le Globe*, where he opposed the reactionary policies of the king. During the revolution of 1830, he signed the journalists' protestation on July 28. He continued to contribute to various newspapers, including *Le National*, *Le Constitutionnel*, and the *Courrier francais*, until 1833.

In 1833, Saint-Hilaire shifted his focus away from politics to devote himself to the history of ancient philosophy. This transition led to a translation of Aristotle that occupied the majority of his life from 1837 to 1892. His scholarly reputation earned him a seat at the Academy of Moral and Political Science in 1839 and the chair of ancient philosophy at the College de France in 1838. In his scholarly analysis of Aristotle, Saint-Hilaire proposed a reading of "ἐν Χαλκηδόνι" instead of "ἐν Καρχηδόνι" to address contradictory statements regarding the Carthaginian government.

## Political Career and Diplomatic Missions
Saint-Hilaire returned to active politics following the revolution of 1848, when he was elected as a republican deputy. However, he was forced to withdraw from this position after the *coup d'etat* of Louis Napoleon. 

In 1855, he served as a member of an international commission sent to Egypt to report on the feasibility of the proposed Suez canal. Through the articles he wrote regarding this mission, he contributed significantly to the popularity of the project within France.

He was elected as a deputy again in 1869, at which time he joined the opposition to the Empire. In 1871, he focused his efforts on the election of Thiers as president of the republic and subsequently served as Thiers' secretary.

## Later Government Service and Statecraft
In 1875, Saint-Hilaire was appointed senator for life, aligning himself with the moderate republicans. He reached a peak of political influence from September 1880 to November 1881, when he served as the minister of foreign affairs in the cabinet of Jules Ferry. The most significant event during his administration was the annexation of Tunis as a French protectorate, a move which Saint-Hilaire actively promoted.

## Literary and Scholarly Works
Beyond his extensive translation of Aristotle and related studies, Saint-Hilaire authored several works on Eastern religions and philosophy, including:
* *Des Vedas* (1854)
* *Du Bouddhisme* (1856)
* *Mahomet et le Coran* (1865)

Additionally, he wrote a notice on the works of Burnouf, which was prefixed to the 1876 second edition of *Introd. à l'histoire du Bouddhisme indien*.

Jules Barthélemy Saint-Hilaire died on November 24, 1895.
