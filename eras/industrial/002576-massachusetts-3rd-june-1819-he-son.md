# Thomas Ball

## Early Life and Education
Thomas Ball was an American sculptor born on June 3, 1819, in Charlestown, Massachusetts. He was the son of a house-and-sign-painter. Ball began his artistic career as a self-taught portrait painter before shifting his professional focus to sculpture in 1851. His earliest work in this medium was a bust of Jenny Lind.

## Artistic Development and International Study
At the age of thirty-five, Ball traveled to Florence to pursue further study. He remained there for more than thirty years, though his time in Italy was interrupted by a period of work in Boston, Massachusetts, between 1857 and 1865. While residing in Florence, he was part of an artistic colony that included Hiram Powers and the Brownings. Ball eventually returned to the United States in 1897, where he established a studio in New York City and lived in Montclair, New Jersey.

## Major Works and Musical Interests
Ball was an accomplished musician and was the first person in America to sing "Elijah." This interest is reflected in his early work, which included many cabinet busts of musicians. 

His later career was defined by significant monumental sculptures, including:
*   The equestrian statue of Washington in the Boston public gardens, which is considered probably his best work.
*   The statue of Josiah Quincy in Boston's City Hall Square.
*   The statue of Charles Sumner in the public gardens of Boston.
*   The statue of Daniel Webster in New York City's Central Park.
*   The Lincoln Emancipation group located at Washington.
*   The Washington monument in Methuen, Massachusetts.
*   A sculpture of Edwin Forrest as "Coriolanus," located in the Actors' Home in Philadelphia.

## Legacy and Publications
The work of Thomas Ball had a marked influence on monumental art within the United States, with a particular impact in New England. In 1891, he published an autobiographical volume titled *My Three Score Years and Ten*.
