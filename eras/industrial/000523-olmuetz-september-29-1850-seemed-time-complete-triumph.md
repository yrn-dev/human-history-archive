# Convention of Olmuetz

The Convention of Olmuetz, signed on September 29, 1850, was a diplomatic agreement that appeared at the time to be a complete triumph for Austria over Prussia. This event occurred following the capitulation of Vilagos, which brought an end to the Hungarian insurrection and provided Schwarzenberg with the opportunity to restore the influence of Austria in Germany and the *status quo ante*.

## Political Implications and Analysis
While the terms of the convention seemed to favor Austria, the actual nature of the agreement has been characterized differently by historians. Count Beust described the convention not as a "Prussian humiliation," but rather as a sign of "Austrian weakness." 

The archive indicates that Austria possessed the power to crush Prussia and eliminate the dual influence within the Confederation, a system that experience had demonstrated to be unworkable. However, Austria chose instead to re-establish a discredited system. This decision provided Prussia with the time and opportunity to increase its strength for an inevitable future conflict.
