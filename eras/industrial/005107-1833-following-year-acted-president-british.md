# Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane

Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane (1773–1860) was a Scottish astronomer and soldier who held significant military, colonial, and scientific leadership roles throughout his career. Born on July 23, 1773, at Brisbane House near Largs, Ayrshire, he entered the army in 1789. His military service included deployments to the Peninsula, the West Indies, and Flanders. In 1814, he was sent to North America, though he did not participate in the battle of Waterloo because he did not arrive in time following his recall during Napoleon's return from Elba.

## Colonial Administration in New South Wales
In 1821, Brisbane was appointed governor of New South Wales. He held this office for four years. While the finances of the colony fell into confusion during his tenure, Brisbane sought to improve the region's condition through the encouragement of land reclamation and the breeding of horses. He also introduced tobacco plants, sugar-cane, and the vine. Under his instigation, exploring parties were dispatched, one of which discovered the Brisbane river, which was subsequently named in his honor.

## Astronomical and Scientific Contributions
Brisbane established an astronomical observatory at Paramatta in 1822. The observations conducted there between 1822 and 1826 resulted in the *Brisbane Catalogue*, which was printed in 1835 and contained 7,385 stars. Although the Paramatta observatory was discontinued in 1855, Brisbane continued his scientific pursuits after returning to Scotland. He maintained a large, well-equipped observatory at Makerstoun in Roxburghshire. 

Beginning in 1841, he conducted important magnetic observations at Makerstoun. These results were published in the *Transactions* of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and earned him the Keith prize of that society in 1848. To further the encouragement of scientific research, Brisbane founded two gold medals: one for the Scottish Society of Arts and another for the Royal Society of Edinburgh.

## Professional Honors and Leadership
Brisbane's ascent in military and social rank included being made a baronet in 1836, a G.C.B. in 1837, and attaining the rank of general in 1841. 

His leadership in the scientific community was marked by two significant presidencies. Following the death of Sir Walter Scott in 1833, Brisbane was elected president of the Royal Society of Edinburgh. In the following year, he acted as president of the British Association.

Sir Thomas Makdougall Brisbane died on January 27, 1860, at Brisbane House.
