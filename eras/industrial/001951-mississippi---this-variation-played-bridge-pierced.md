# Mississippi

The term Mississippi refers to both a major river system in the United States and a specific variation of a game.

## The Mississippi River and Drainage
The Mississippi River serves as a primary outlet for various regional water systems. In the state of Illinois, the river receives three-fourths of the state's waters. Important rivers that empty into the Mississippi include the Rock in the north and the Kaskaskia in the south. Additionally, the Chicago river, which originally flowed into Lake Michigan, had its waters turned in 1900 via the construction of the Chicago Drainage Canal, ultimately flowing into the Mississippi.

## Engineering and Infrastructure
Several significant bridges and structures span the Mississippi River:

*   **The St Louis Bridge:** Completed in 1874, this is one of the most important large-span bridges with steel arches. Engineered by Captain J.B. Eads, the bridge's piers and abutments were established on rock 136 ft. below high water using air locks and caissons with air chambers. The bridge consists of three spans made of cast steel arches; the center span is 520 ft. and the side spans are 502 ft. in the clear. The center arch rises 47½ ft., while the side arches rise 46 ft. Each span utilizes four steel double ribs of steel tubes. The bridge carries a roadway 54 ft. wide above a double railway track.
*   **Keokuk Infrastructure:** In Lee county, Iowa, the city of Keokuk features a bridge across the Mississippi approximately 2200 ft. long. The Federal Government also constructed a navigable canal approximately 9 m. long to bypass the Des Moines Rapids, which opened in 1877. This system includes a dam 38 ft. high and 1½ m. long.
*   **Davenport Connections:** The city of Davenport, Iowa, is connected to Rock Island, Illinois, by two bridges and a ferry. Additionally, the Hennepin Canal (also known as the Illinois and Mississippi Canal) connects the Mississippi and Illinois rivers.

## Cities of the Mississippi
Several cities are situated along the river:

*   **Davenport, Iowa:** Founded in 1835 and chartered as a city in 1851, Davenport is the third largest city in Iowa. It is a center for commerce in grain and coal and manufactures products including locomotives, steel cars, washing machines, and agricultural implements.
*   **Keokuk, Iowa:** Located at the mouth of the Des Moines river, Keokuk was laid out as a town in 1837 and chartered as a city in 1848. It was named after Keokuk (1780-1848), a chief of the Sauk and Foxes known as "the watchful" or "he who moves alertly," who remained neutral during Black Hawk's war policy of 1832.

## Mississippi (Game Variation)
Mississippi is also the name of a game variation played with a bridge pierced with arches (numbered from 1 upwards), the number of which depends on the size of the table. Usually, nine balls are used, though the black ball is occasionally omitted. 

The objective is to send the balls through the arches. To score, a ball must strike a cushion first; if a ball is played through an arch without striking a cushion, the score is awarded to the adversary. However, if a ball is lying in front of the bridge, the cue-ball may send it through provided the cue-ball has struck a cushion. If the black ball is used, it counts for double the value of the arch. Further points are scored if a ball falls into a cup, in which case the striker receives the value of both the cup and the arch.

This game is similar to *Trou Madame*, except that in *Trou Madame*, balls do not need to strike a cushion, and the opponent—rather than the striker—scores the value of any ball that falls into a cup.
