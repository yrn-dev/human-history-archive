# Thornycroft-Schulz Boiler

The Thornycroft-Schulz boiler is a specific type of water-tube boiler produced by Messrs John I. Thornycroft & Co. as one of their express boiler forms. It is characterized by its internal chamber arrangement and specialized tube configuration designed to optimize heat transfer and water circulation.

## Design and Structure
The Thornycroft-Schulz boiler consists of four chambers. The fire-grate within the system is arranged in two separate portions. The boiler utilizes a series of tubes to facilitate the heating process, with specific arrangements for different rows:

*   **Outermost Rows:** On each side, the two outermost rows of tubes are designed to form water-walls at their lower sections. At their upper sections, these tubes permit the passage of gases between them.
*   **Inner Rows:** The rows of tubes located nearest to the fires are arranged in a manner similar to those found in the standard Thornycroft boiler.

## Water Circulation
Maintaining the circulation of water is a critical requirement for water-tube boilers. In the Thornycroft-Schulz model, this is achieved through two primary mechanisms:

1.  **Outer Circulation:** Large diameter outer circulating pipes connect the water-chambers to the steam-chambers to manage circulation in the outer sets of tubes.
2.  **Middle Circulation:** For the middle water-chamber, the boiler is equipped with several nearly vertical down-comers located in the center of the unit.

## Applications
Due to its design and efficiency, the Thornycroft-Schulz boiler is used extensively within the German and British navies.
