# Private Banks

Private banks are financial institutions that operate independently of state-run central banks, often issuing their own notes or providing credit and deposit services. Their prevalence and influence vary significantly by region and historical period, ranging from dominant local currency issuers in some territories to marginalized entities in others.

## Private Banking in the United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, the role of private banks has evolved alongside joint-stock banks and the Bank of England. By 1906, the banking business in England was primarily conducted by approximately sixty joint-stock banks and ten private banks, though some joint-stock organizations were essentially private firms in structure.

### England and Ireland
In England, the average circulation for private banks in 1906 was £124,000, which was significantly lower than that of joint-stock banks (£429,000) and the Bank of England (£28,890,000). In Ireland, the landscape shifted in May 1907, when the number of private banks was reduced to 12, with an authorized issue of £482,744 and an actual issue of £122,536. 

The disparity between authorized and actual issues in Ireland was attributed to the limited geographic reach of these banks. Notes issued by country banks were largely unknown outside their immediate neighborhoods. While these notes were payable in London, individuals often faced considerable difficulty when attempting to cash them.

### Scotland
Private banking had little impact in Scotland. While eight private banks existed in 1819, they had all disappeared by 1844. By 1906, the Scottish banking sector was dominated by ten banks of issue, which operated 1,180 branches and handled nearly all the country's banking business, supplemented by two recently established small banks.

## International Private Banking and Colonial Interests

British financial interests extended into various colonies and foreign territories through both joint-stock banks and private firms.

### British Colonies
In the Dominion of Canada, 29 banks—many of which were large and managed according to Scottish principles—held deposits of approximately £140,000,000 and operated over 1,200 offices. In Australia and New Zealand, 24 banks held deposits of about £130,000,000 across nearly 1,700 offices. In India, approximately 15 banks (including three Presidency banks) operated over 210 offices, sometimes connecting to locations such as Zanzibar, Hong Kong, and Bangkok. In South and West Africa, at least eight banks operated nearly 370 offices.

In total, more than 80 joint-stock British banks worked in the colonies, while many private firms also conducted considerable business in these regions.

### Foreign Countries
Approximately 20 British banks operated in foreign countries, with total capitals of £15,200,000 and deposits of £135,000,000. This included six banks primarily in South America and six large banks operating in the East. Other institutions included Barings and six other banks with combined capitals of about £4,500,000.

## Private Banking in Other Nations

### China
In certain Chinese cities, private and local promissory notes were used as currency despite the disuse of government paper money. In Fuchau, banking-houses were counted by the hundreds and operated without government control. Any company or individual with sufficient credit or capital could establish a bank and issue bills ranging from 100 cash to 1,000 dollars, effectively displacing bullion.

However, private banking could lead to severe instability. The Regent Sushun issued a vast number of notes through his own banks in Peking but failed to redeem them. This resulted in the failure of all those banks and caused great commotion in the city.

### United States
In the United States, the national banking system allowed for the conversion of private and state institutions. By October 31, 1907, 2,389 national banks had been incorporated with capitals of less than $50,000 (following an act in 1900 that lowered the minimum capital to $25,000 for towns with populations under 3,000). Of these, 272 banks were conversions of private and state institutions.

### Bulgaria
Bulgaria's banking sector included several private banks alongside state institutions like the National Bank. To protect the peasantry from usurers, agricultural chests (later the Bulgarian Agricultural Bank) were established. These chests accepted deposits at interest and provided loans to peasants on mortgage or the security of two solvent landowners at a rate of 8%.
