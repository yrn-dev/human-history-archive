# 1845 in Literature and Scholarship

The year 1845 saw the publication of various scholarly works, historical novels, and academic dissertations across Europe and the Americas, reflecting a period of active literary production and the continuation of long-term archival projects.

## The Bollandists and the "October" Series
A significant milestone in hagiographical scholarship occurred in 1845 with the publication of volume vii. of *October*. This volume marked the beginning of a new series produced by the Bollandists. The work of the Bollandists had faced several interruptions: following the dispersion of the Jesuits, they remained at Antwerp until 1778, after which they moved to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. There, they published volume iv. of *October* in 1780 and volume v. in 1886, before the monastery was suppressed and their work ceased in 1788.

The Premonstratensians of Tongerloo later acquired the Bollandist library and published volume vi. of *October* in 1794. The work was officially resumed in 1837 at the college of St Michael in Brussels, supported by the Belgian government and the Academie Royale of Belgium. The new series initiated in 1845 eventually reached volume xiii. by 1883. Collaborators in this series included the Jesuit fathers Benjamin Bossue, Joseph van Hecke, Joseph van der Moere, Remi de Buck, Victor de Buck, Edu. Carpentier, Ant. Tinnebroeck, and Henr. Matagne.
