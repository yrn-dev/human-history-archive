# JEROME BONAPARTE (1784-1860)

Jerome Bonaparte was born on November 15, 1784, at Ajaccio. During the early years of the French Revolution, he shared the fortunes of his family and received an education at Juilly. In 1800, he was called to join his brother, who was then the First Consul of France.

## Early Military Career and American Marriage
While serving in the Consular Guard, Jerome fought a duel with the younger brother of General Davout, during which he was wounded. He was subsequently transferred to the navy and cruised in the West Indies. After being blockaded by a British cruiser, he left his ship and traveled through the United States. In Baltimore, he met and fell in love with Miss Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a Baltimore merchant named William Patterson. Despite her being a minor, they married on December 24, 1803.

This marriage was greatly disliked by Napoleon, who refused to recognize its legality and viewed it as a disregard for the laws of France and military discipline. Although the marriage was valid under American law and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void, Napoleon ordered Elizabeth to be excluded from his states when Jerome brought her to Europe in 1805. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but he was unsuccessful.

## Naval Commands and Rise to Power
In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. Meanwhile, his wife resided in Camberwell, where she gave birth to their son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870). In November of that year, Jerome sailed with a squadron under Admiral Willaumez intended to ravage the West Indies, though the fleet was scattered by a storm. After damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic, Jerome returned to France in August 1806.

Napoleon subsequently named Jerome a prince of France and placed him in command of a division of South Germans during the 1806 campaign. Following the battle of Jena, Jerome accepted the surrender of several Prussian towns.

## Kingdom of Westphalia
Following an imperial decree that annulled his marriage to Elizabeth Patterson, Jerome was united with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia.

During his reign, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for running deeply into debt. However, the kingdom saw some benefits from its connection to France: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, education received an impulse in its higher departments, and repressive laws affecting the Jews were removed.

## Later Life and Descendants
In his later years, Jerome served as the governor of the Invalides. During this time, he commissioned the historian Pierre Emmanuel Albert Ducasse, Baron Ducasse, to write *Memoires pour servir a l'histoire de la campagne de 1812 en Russie* (1852).

Jerome had one child with Elizabeth Patterson, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870), who was born in England but lived primarily in Baltimore. This son is said to have strongly resembled his uncle, the emperor. Jerome maintained good terms with his son, providing him with a large allowance, and the two met occasionally. The grandson of Jerome, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832-1893), served in the French army in Italy and the Crimea.
