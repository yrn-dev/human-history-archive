# The National Guard and the French Revolution

The National Guard was an armed force established in Paris and other large cities during the early stages of the French Revolution. It was created by municipal bodies as a provisional force designed primarily and plainly to resist the forces of the crown.

## Origins and Purpose
The formation of the National Guard followed a period of intense political conflict regarding the states-general. After the Third Estate declared that it alone represented the nation and refused to be levied of taxes without its consent, the king closed their meeting hall. In response, deputies met at a tennis court and took the Oath of the Tennis Court, pledging not to separate until a constitution had been established for France.

Although the king initially accepted the principle of a National Assembly, he later prepared to reverse these concessions, bringing up foreign regiments in the French service under the Marshal de Broglie. This led to a revolt in France and Paris. In the wake of this unrest, provisional city governments were established, which in turn brought the National Guard into existence.

## Role in Paris and the Mayoralty of Bailly
The National Guard operated under the authority of city leadership. Between July 15, 1789, and November 16, 1791, the mayor of Paris was a man who had previously served as the president of the Third Estate and had led the proceedings at the Tennis Court on June 20. 

On July 17, 1791, the mayor ordered the National Guard to disperse a riotous assembly in the Champ de Mars. This action made the mayor obnoxious to the infuriated populace, leading to his retirement to Nantes. While in Nantes, he composed his *Memoires d'un temoin*, an incomplete narrative of his public life. He eventually left Nantes in late 1793 to join Pierre Simon Laplace at Melun, where he was arrested on November 10 and brought before the Revolutionary Tribunal. He was guillotined on November 12 amid a howling mob.

## Other Figures and Activities
Various individuals associated with the military and political upheavals of the era were linked to the National Guard. Joseph Marie, Count Dessaix, a French general born in Savoy, joined the National Guard in Paris in 1789. Following this, in 1791, he attempted to raise an *émeute* in Savoy, though he was unsuccessful.

During this same period, the French monarchy continued to struggle with the revolutionary tide. King Louis XVI, who had sworn to maintain the constitution at the festival of the federation on July 14, 1790, attempted to flee on June 20, 1791. Despite leaving documents that proved his opposition to the Revolution, he was arrested at Varennes and brought back to Paris, where he took an oath on September 13, 1791, to remain a constitutional king.
