# Baltimore College of Dental Surgery and the Maryland College of Pharmacy

## Baltimore College of Dental Surgery
Established in November 1840, the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery was the first college in the world dedicated to the systematic education of dentists. Its founding occurred during a pivotal era for the profession; until well into the 19th century, the only method for acquiring knowledge of dentistry was through apprenticeship. This apprenticeship system had fostered quackery and secrecy among many early practitioners, leading better-educated and more liberal-minded members of the craft to oppose these narrow methods.

The creation of the college was part of a broader professional movement that began in 1837 with the formation of a local association of dentists in New York, followed by the 1840 establishment of a national association known as The American Society of Dental Surgeons. This national body sought to advance dental science through the interchange of sentiments and free communication. Additionally, the world's first dental periodical, *The American Journal of Dental Science*, was issued in June 1839.

The birth of the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, alongside the national association and the professional journal, provided the three essential factors for the growth of dentistry as a profession. The college's emergence was further necessitated by the refusal of medical schools to provide the facilities required for dental instruction, which initially placed dentistry on a footing entirely separate from general medicine. Over time, the preparatory curriculum for dental practice increased in length and content until its fundamental principles became practically equal to those required for medical specialists, while maintaining the technical subjects specific to dentistry.

## Maryland College of Pharmacy
The Maryland College of Pharmacy is one of the professional schools located in Baltimore. In 1904, the institution became part of the University of Maryland.

## Educational Context in Baltimore
Baltimore is recognized as a high-ranking educational center. In addition to the Maryland College of Pharmacy and the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, the city's professional landscape includes the University of Maryland and Baltimore University—both of which provide courses in dentistry, medicine, and law. Other medical institutions in the city include the College of Physicians and Surgeons, the Baltimore Medical College, and the Woman's Medical College.

The city's broader educational infrastructure includes a Polytechnic Institute, high schools for coloured and white pupils, and several seminaries, including St. Joseph's and St. Mary's. St. Mary's Seminary, established by the Society of St Sulpice in 1791, is noted as the oldest Catholic theological seminary in the United States. Other notable institutions in the city include Johns Hopkins University, a leading center for graduate study, the Peabody Institute, and Goucher College for women.
