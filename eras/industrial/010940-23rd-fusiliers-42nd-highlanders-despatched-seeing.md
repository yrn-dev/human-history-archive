# Military Units and Designations: Fusiliers and Highlanders

## The Fusiliers
The term "fusilier" originated in the late 17th century (approximately 1670 in French and 1680 in English). It originally referred to soldiers armed with the fusil, a light flintlock musket. The development of these units was driven by the technical requirements of 17th-century artillery; because artillery used open powder barrels, it was unsafe to allow the lighted matches of standard muskets near the guns. Consequently, companies of "firelocks"—the term then used for flintlock weapons—were organized to provide both protection and surveillance for artillerymen.

The French Royal Fusiliers, organized by Vauban in 1671, served as the European model for these units. In the British service, the (7th) Royal Fusiliers (City of London Regiment) was formed in 1685 based on this French model. While the 5th foot (later the Northumberland Fusiliers) was senior to the 7th in the army, it was not a fusilier regiment at the time of the 7th's formation.

As the flintlock musket became generally adopted and the pike was suppressed across European armies, the specific duties of fusiliers evolved. They were frequently employed in light infantry work, a transition attributed to the individual aptitude for detached duties possessed by soldiers who were not restricted to a fixed position in the line of battle.

## Uniforms and Equipment
The distinctive head-dress of British fusiliers is a fur cap, which is smaller and differs in detail from those worn by the Foot Guards. This type of head-dress is colloquially referred to as a "busby." In a broader military context, the busby can take several forms:
* **Hussar busby:** A cylindrical fur cap with a bag, worn by hussars and the Royal Horse Artillery.
* **Rifle busby:** A folding astrachan cap, taller than a "Glengarry."
* **General usage:** The term also describes the tall bear-and-raccoon-skin caps of fusiliers and foot-guards, as well as the full dress feather bonnet used by Highland infantry.

Regarding weaponry, the ring bayonet was invented by Mackay. This invention was prompted by the failure of the plug-bayonet to stop the rush of Highlanders during the battle of Killiecrankie.

## Notable Personnel and Units
Various fusilier and Highland units appear in historical records across different conflicts:

**Dixon Denham (1786-1828):** An English traveller and soldier born in London. He joined the army in 1811, serving first in the 23rd Royal Welsh Fusiliers and later in the 54th foot. He participated in campaigns in Belgium, France, Spain, and Portugal, and was a recipient of the Waterloo medal. Denham later served as the superintendent of liberated Africans in Sierra Leone (1826) and as the governor of Sierra Leone (1828), where he died of fever at Freetown.

**Highland and Infantry Engagements:**
* **Dunkeld (1689):** Following the battle of Killiecrankie, the Cameronian regiment (later the 26th Foot) under Colonel William Cleland was sent to hold Dunkeld. On August 26, a force of 5,000 Highlanders assaulted the position held by 1,200 Covenanters.
* **The Somme (1918):** During the offensive by Debeney's Army on August 8, the 42nd Division recaptured the village of Mézières.
* **Gallipoli (Beach V):** During a landing operation at Sedd-el-Bahr, the collier *River Clyde* carried 2,000 men, including units from the 1st Dublin Fusiliers and 1st Munster Fusiliers.
