# Victor Balaguer

Victor Balaguer (1824–1901) was a Spanish author and politician who played a significant role in the political and cultural life of Barcelona and Madrid. Born in Barcelona on December 11, 1824, he was educated at the university in his native city.

## Early Life and Literary Career
Balaguer demonstrated remarkable precocity in his youth. At fourteen years of age, his first dramatic essay, *Pepin el jorobado*, was staged in Barcelona. By the age of nineteen, he was publicly "crowned" following the production of his second play, *Don Enrique el Dadivoso*. 

As a poet, Balaguer is described as imitative, with his lyrical poems showing reminiscences of Byron, his historical ballads reflecting Zorrilla, and his patriotic songs showing the influence of Quintana. While he wrote too hastily to satisfy artistic canons, he is regarded as a pioneer in Catalonia.

## Political Activity and Catalan Patriotism
From 1843 to 1868, Balaguer served as the chief of the Liberal party in Barcelona. During this time, he promoted the growth of local patriotism in Catalonia as the editor and proprietor of *El Conseller*. 

His engagement with Catalan culture extended to poetry and tradition. In 1857, he wrote his first poem in Catalan, which consisted of verses to the Virgin of Montserrat. He subsequently used the pseudonym "lo Trovador de Montserrat." In 1861, he was proclaimed *mestre de gay saber*, having helped restore the "Juegos Florales" in 1859.

## National Politics and Government Service
Balaguer eventually moved to Madrid, where he became a prominent figure in political life. In 1867, he emigrated to Provence, but returned to Spain following the expulsion of Queen Isabella. Upon his return, he represented Manresa in the Cortes.

Between 1871 and 1872, Balaguer held two high-ranking government positions, serving successively as the minister of finance and the minister of the colonies. Although he resigned from office during the restoration, he eventually rallied to the dynasty alongside his party. This led to his appointment as a senator and vice-president of congress.

## Historical Works and Later Life
Balaguer authored two major historical works: *Historia de Cataluna y de la Corona de Aragon* (1860–1863) and *Historia politica y literaria de los trovadores* (1878–1879). While these books are noted for being attractively written and serving the cause Balaguer once supported, they are characterized as partial, unscientific, and inaccurate. In his later years, Balaguer became alienated from the advanced school of Catalan nationalists and attempted to explain away the severe criticisms of Castile found in his earlier work on Catalonia and the Crown of Aragon.

Victor Balaguer died in Madrid on January 14, 1901.
