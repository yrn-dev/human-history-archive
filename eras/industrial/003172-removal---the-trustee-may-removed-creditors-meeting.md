# Removal of the Trustee

In the administration of a bankrupt estate, the trustee is subject to various mechanisms of removal to ensure the faithful management of assets and the protection of creditors. The authority to remove a trustee is shared between the creditors, the Board of Trade, and the legal consequences of specific judicial or administrative actions.

## Removal by Creditors and the Board of Trade
The creditors possess the power to remove a trustee at a meeting specifically summoned for that purpose. In such instances, the creditors may remove the trustee without being required to assign a reason. However, this removal is subject to appeal at the instance of the creditors.

The Board of Trade also maintains the power of removal. The Board may remove a trustee on the grounds of misconduct, incapacity, or a failure to perform his duties. Additionally, a trustee may be removed based on any of the personal grounds of objection that would have applied to his initial appointment. The Board of Trade is tasked with the general cognizance of trustee conduct and is required to inquire into complaints made by creditors; if a trustee is found to be not faithfully performing his duties, the Board may take any action it deems expedient, including removal.

## Financial and Security-Based Removal
A trustee's tenure is contingent upon the maintenance of financial security and the proper handling of estate funds. Before confirmation of appointment, a trustee-elect must provide security to the satisfaction of the Board of Trade. This security must be maintained at the originally fixed amount, or a lesser amount if required by the department; failure to keep up this security renders the trustee liable to be removed from office.

Furthermore, the trustee's accounts are subject to audit by the committee of inspection at least once every three months. If it is discovered that a trustee has retained more than £50 in hand for more than ten days without providing a satisfactory explanation, he may be removed from office. In such a case, he may also lose all claims to remuneration and be surcharged with interest at a rate of 20% per annum.

## Vacating Office and Resignation
Certain legal and administrative events result in the automatic vacation of the trustee's office. If a receiving order is made against a trustee, he thereby vacates his office. Additionally, the granting of a release—which occurs after the property has been realized and distributed and the Board of Trade has considered the final accounts—operates as a removal from office.

A trustee may also resign from his position, provided he obtains the consent of a general meeting of creditors. It is noted, however, that resignation does not serve as a release from the trustee's liability to account for his administration of the estate.

## Continuity of the Trusteeship
The archive's sources emphasize the continuity of the office to ensure the bankrupt's property remains vested. Because the property passes from trustee to trustee by operation of law without the need for formal acts of conveyance, the office is secured. When a trustee is removed from office, dies, resigns, or is released, the official receiver again becomes the trustee.
