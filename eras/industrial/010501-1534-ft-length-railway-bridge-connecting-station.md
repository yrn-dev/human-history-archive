# Railway Bridges and Infrastructure

The development of railway infrastructure involves the construction of specialized bridges and stations to facilitate the movement of trains across geographical obstacles and through urban centers. These structures vary significantly in design, material, and scale depending on the requirements of the railway system and the environment.

## Bridge Design and Engineering
In the United States, railway companies typically do not design or build their own bridges. Instead, they provide general specifications regarding loading and span to bridge-building companies. These companies employ expert engineers to create designs, including detail drawings and strain sheets, which are then submitted to the railway engineer with estimates. This system results in the use of well-settled types and uniform member designs, allowing for more systematic workshop manufacture.

The choice of bridge type often depends on the span length:
*   **Riveted plate girders:** Used for spans up to 50 ft.
*   **Riveted braced girders:** Used for spans between 50 ft. and 75 ft.
*   **Pin-connected girders:** Used for spans longer than 75 ft.

Additionally, cantilever bridges have been used extensively since the erection of the Forth bridge, and various steel suspension and arch bridges have been constructed. Overhead railways are characterized as virtually continuous bridge constructions. The substructure of these bridges—consisting of foundations, abutments, and piers—is usually made of concrete, brickwork, or stone masonry, though woodwork or metal work is occasionally used for intermediate piers.

## Notable Railway Bridges
Several significant railway bridges have been constructed globally, utilizing various engineering techniques:

*   **The Quebec Bridge:** Spanning the St Lawrence, this bridge was designed to carry two lines of rails, an electric railway, and a highway on each side. It has a total length between abutments of 3240 ft., a channel span of 1800 ft., a suspended span of 675 ft., and shore spans of 562½ ft. The total weight of the metal is approximately 32,000 tons. The bridge collapsed during construction in 1907.
*   **The Poughkeepsie Bridge:** Built between 1886 and 1887 over the Hudson, this bridge features five river spans and two shore spans. It utilizes cantilever spans of 547 ft., girder spans of 525 ft., and shore spans of 201 ft.
*   **The Ohio Bridge:** Located on the Cincinnati & Covington railway, this is likely the largest girder span constructed. Built in 1888 by the Phoenix Bridge Company, it features a center girder 545 ft. in length and 84 ft. in depth. The bridge carries two lines of railway, two footways, and two carriageways.
*   **The Lansdowne Bridge:** Completed in 1889 at Sukkur over the Indus, this bridge was designed by Sir A. Rendel. It is intended for a single line of railway with a 5 ft. 6 in. gauge. It has a clear span of 790 ft. and a suspended girder 200 ft. in length.
*   **Other Structures:** The Niagara bridge has a total length of 910 ft. for two lines of railway, while the Red Rock cantilever bridge over the Colorado river features a center span of 660 ft.

## Railway Stations and Urban Integration
Railway stations often serve as central hubs for towns and cities. In Bristol, England, the central station, Temple Meads, is used by both the Midland railway and the Great Western railway. In the town of Ciney, the station building is described as unusually handsome and is a center of activity. In Kotri, British India, the town serves as a junction for branches of the North-Western railway, with a railway bridge crossing the Indus. Kotri previously served as the station for Hyderabad.

In other regions, railway infrastructure is integrated with industrial and civic centers. For example, in the town of Kilbarchan, Scotland, the Milliken Park station on the Glasgow & South-Western railway is located 1 m. from the burgh. Nearby, Bridge of Weir and Kilmalcolm also possess stations on the same railway line. In Egypt, the Central railway station is located on the west side of the Ismailia canal, while the Bulak quarter houses railway works, a foundry, and an arsenal.

## Professional Contributions
The field of railway engineering has been advanced by individuals such as Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry. He was associated with Sir John Hawkshaw in building railway bridges across the Thames at Cannon Street and Charing Cross. His independent work included the bridge and St Paul's station of the London, Chatham & Dover railway, as well as the Barry Docks for the Barry railway company. He also authored texts on *Railways and Locomotives* (1882) and *Railway Appliances* (1874).
