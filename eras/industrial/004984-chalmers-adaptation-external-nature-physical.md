# The Adaptation of External Nature to the Physical Condition of Man

## Overview and Publication
*The Adaptation of External Nature to the Physical Condition of Man* is a scientific work authored by John Kidd, M.D. It was published in 1833 as part of a series known as the Bridgewater treatises. These treatises first appeared between the years 1833 and 1840 and were later included in Bohn's Scientific Library.

The work is one of eight treatises in the collection, which collectively addressed various aspects of natural theology. Other volumes in the series included:
* *The Adaptation of External Nature to the Moral and Intellectual Condition of Man* by Thomas Chalmers, D.D.
* *Astronomy and General Physics considered with reference to Natural Theology* by William Whewell, D.D.
* *The Hand, its Mechanism and Vital Endowments as evincing Design* by Sir Charles Bell.
* *Animal and Vegetable Physiology considered with reference to Natural Theology* by Peter Mark Roget.
* *Geology and Mineralogy considered with reference to Natural Theology* by William Buckland, D.D.
* *The Habits and Instincts of Animals with reference to Natural Theology* by William Kirby.
* *Chemistry, Meteorology, and the Function of Digestion, considered with reference to Natural Theology* by William Prout, M.D.

## Author Biography
The author, John Kidd (1775–1851), was an English geologist, chemist, and physician. Born in Westminster on September 10, 1775, he was the son of Captain John Kidd, a naval officer. Kidd received his education at Westminster and Bury St Edmunds before attending Christ Church, Oxford, where he graduated with a B.A. in 1797 and an M.D. in 1804. Between 1797 and 1801, he studied at Guy's Hospital in London under the tutelage of Sir Astley Cooper.

Kidd's academic career at Oxford was extensive. He became a reader in chemistry in 1801 and was elected the first Aldrichian professor of chemistry in 1803. He provided voluntary lectures on geology and mineralogy in the dark chambers beneath the Ashmolean Museum; these lectures provided the first lessons in geology for several notable figures, including W. Buckland, C. G. B. Daubeny, and J. J. and W. D. Conybeare. His efforts contributed to the establishment of the geological chair, which was first held by Buckland.

In addition to his teaching, Kidd held several prestigious appointments:
* Fellow of the Royal College of Physicians (F.R.C.P.) in 1818.
* Regius professor of medicine in 1822, succeeding Sir Christopher Pegge.
* Keeper of the Radcliffe Library in 1834.

In 1834, he also delivered the Harveian oration before the Royal College of Physicians. He died at Oxford on September 7, 1851.

## Other Publications
Beyond his Bridgewater Treatise, John Kidd authored other significant scientific texts, including *Outlines of Mineralogy* (published in two volumes in 1809) and *A Geological Essay on the Imperfect Evidence in Support of a Theory of the Earth* (1815).
