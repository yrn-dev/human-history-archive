# Sing-Oden (1766) and Related Works of Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach

The *Sing-Oden* (1766) is a collection of songs composed by the German musician and composer Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714–1788). Bach, the third son of Johann Sebastian Bach, was a prominent figure in European music, particularly noted for his mastery of the clavier.

## Context and Composition
Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach's musical output during his residence in Berlin included a variety of vocal works. The *Sing-Oden* was published in 1766 as part of a broader effort to produce song collections. This work followed two previous volumes of songs: *Geistliche Oden und Lieder* (1758), which featured words by Gellert, and *Oden mit Melodien* (1762). 

In addition to these song collections, Bach composed other vocal pieces during his time in Berlin, including an Easter cantata written in 1756 and a setting of the *Magnificat* in 1749. The *Magnificat* is noted for showing more traces of the influence of his father than is usual for his work.

## Secular Cantatas and Occasional Pieces
Alongside his structured song collections and sacred works, Bach composed a few secular cantatas and other *pieces d'occasion*. While these vocal works were significant, they existed alongside a massive body of instrumental music. During the same period, Bach focused heavily on the clavier, producing nearly two hundred sonatas and other solos, including the set *mit veraenderten Reprisen* (1760–1768) and several pieces intended *fuer Kenner und Liebhaber*.

## Professional Background
Bach's ability to compose such diverse works was supported by a rigorous education and a high-profile career. Born in Weimar on March 14, 1714, he attended the Thomasschule at Leipzig starting at age ten. He later studied jurisprudence at the universities of Leipzig (1731) and Frankfort on the Oder (1735), receiving his degree in 1738. However, he abandoned a legal career to devote himself to music.

In 1740, upon the accession of the crown prince of Prussia, Bach became a member of the royal household. He was recognized as one of Europe's leading clavier-players. His reputation was further established through sets of sonatas dedicated to the grand duke of Wuerttemberg (1744) and Frederick the Great (1742). In 1746, he was promoted to the position of *Kammermusikus*, a post he held for twenty-two years.

## Theoretical Contributions
Beyond his compositions like the *Sing-Oden*, Bach contributed to musical theory with his treatise *Versuch ueber die wahre Art das Clavier zu spielen*. The first part was published in 1753, and a second part followed in 1762. This systematic work was influential enough to reach a third edition by 1780.
