# Larrazabal and Rojas

The works of Larrazabal and Rojas are recognized in the historical record as biographical accounts of the life and career of Bolivar.

## Biographical Works on Bolivar
Bolivar was a figure of significant influence in the southern portion of the New World, credited with achieving the independence of three states: Bolivia, Peru, and Colombia. His efforts were directed toward the fostering of national interests, the encouragement of the arts and sciences, and the purification of the administration of justice. He spent nine-tenths of a splendid patrimony in the service of his country and, despite having unlimited control over the revenues of the three aforementioned countries for a considerable period, he died without possessing any public money.

Among the biographical literature documenting his life are the "lives" written by Larrazabal, published in New York in 1866, and Rojas, published in Madrid in 1883. Another biography was authored by Ducoudray-Holstein and published in Paris in 1831. Additionally, official documentation regarding Bolivar's career was published in Caracas between 1826 and 1833, consisting of twenty-two volumes, and two volumes of his correspondence were published in New York in 1866.

## Commemorations of Bolivar
The legacy of the liberator is marked by several monuments and statues across different nations:
* **Caracas:** His remains were moved here in 1842, and a monument was erected to his memory.
* **Bogota:** A statue was installed in 1846.
* **Lima:** The Peruvians erected an equestrian statue of Bolivar in 1858.
* **New York:** A statue was placed in Central Park in 1884.
