# Shoe Manufacturing Establishments of Brockton

The W.L. Douglas, the Ralston, the Walkover, the Eaton, the Keith, and the Packard establishments were among the shoe factories operating in Brockton, Massachusetts, in 1908. These factories were part of a larger industrial landscape that established Brockton as a primary center for the manufacture of boots and shoes in the United States.

## Industrial Significance of Brockton
Brockton is a manufacturing city located in Plymouth County, Massachusetts, approximately 20 miles south of Boston. The city occupies an area of 21 square miles of rolling surface. By 1900 and 1905, Brockton ranked first among all cities in the United States for the manufacture of boots and shoes. 

The scale of this industry is reflected in the city's production numbers. In 1905, the city produced 14,000,000 pairs of shoes, a figure that rose to approximately 17,000,000 pairs by 1907. The economic impact of this sector was substantial; in 1905, the boot and shoe product was valued at $30,073,014. When combined with boot and shoe findings ($2,435,177) and boot and shoe cut stock ($1,344,977), these three categories represented 89.6% of the total manufactured product of the city.

## Factory Landscape and Related Products
As of 1908, there were 35 shoe factories located within the city, including the aforementioned W.L. Douglas, Ralston, Walkover, Eaton, Keith, and Packard establishments. Beyond the primary production of footwear, the city's industrial base included the manufacture of related goods such as shoe fittings and tools, blacking, lasts, and wooden and paper packing boxes, as well as spikes and nails.

The overall value of the city's factory product grew significantly during the early 20th century, rising from $24,855,362 in 1900 to $37,790,982 in 1905, marking a 52% increase over five years.

## City Development and Demographics
Brockton's growth as an industrial center is mirrored in its population and valuation. The population grew from 8,007 in 1870 to 56,878 by the 1910 census. The 1900 census recorded 40,063 residents, of whom 9,484 were foreign-born; this group included 2,667 Irish, 2,199 English Canadians, and 1,973 Swedes. The assessed valuation of the city also saw a sharp increase, rising from $6,876,427 in 1881 to $37,408,332 in 1907.

The city serves as the industrial hub for several surrounding populations, including Avon, Randolph, Holbrook, Whitman, North Easton, and East and West Bridgewater.

## History and Infrastructure
Originally part of Bridgewater, the area was incorporated as the township of North Bridgewater in 1821. It adopted the name Brockton in 1874 and was chartered as a city in 1881. The city is served by the New York, New Haven & Hartford railway. In 1896, Brockton became the first city in Massachusetts to abolish all grade crossings within its limits.

Other civic features of the city include a public library, which held 54,000 volumes in 1908. Additionally, the city maintained a low death rate for a manufacturing city of its size, recorded at 13.18 in 1907. Between 1886 and 1998 (with the exception of 1898), the liquor traffic was prohibited annually by popular vote.
