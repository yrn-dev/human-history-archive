# Edinburgh Philosophical Journal

The *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* was a scientific publication established in 1819. It was created to take the place of the *Edinburgh Magazine*.

## Editorial History and Publication
The journal was established through the joint editorial efforts of Brewster and Robert Jameson (1774–1854). For the first ten volumes, published between 1819 and 1824, Brewster and Jameson served as joint editors. Following this period, the remaining four volumes, published from 1825 to 1826, were edited by Jameson alone.

## Relation to Other Publications
The *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* emerged from a lineage of literary and scientific work associated with its founders. Prior to the establishment of the journal, Brewster had been a regular contributor to the *Edinburgh Magazine* starting in 1799, serving as its editor at the age of twenty. 

After Brewster parted company with Robert Jameson, he moved on to start another publication in 1824 called the *Edinburgh Journal of Science*. This subsequent journal saw sixteen volumes appear under Brewster's editorship between 1824 and 1832, featuring numerous articles written by Brewster himself.

## Context of the Editor's Work
The establishment of the *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* was part of a broader career of scientific and editorial contributions by Brewster. In addition to his work with the *Edinburgh Philosophical Journal* and the *Edinburgh Journal of Science*, Brewster undertook the editorship of the *Edinburgh Encyclopaedia* in 1807 (with the first part appearing in 1808 and the last in 1830), a work noted for its strength in the scientific department. He was also a leading contributor to the seventh and eighth editions of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, authoring articles on subjects including Magnetism, Optics, Hydrodynamics, Stereoscope, Microscope, and Electricity (including Voltaic Electricity).
