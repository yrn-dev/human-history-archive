# Briton-Ferry

Briton-Ferry is a seaport located in Wales within the mid-parliamentary division of Glamorganshire. It is situated on the eastern bank of the estuary of the Neath river in Swansea Bay. As of 1901, the population of its urban district was 6,973.

## History and Nomenclature
The town has been known by the Welsh name Llansawel since at least the 16th century. The name La Brittone was assigned to the ferry crossing the Neath estuary by Norman settlers during the 12th century. This ferry remains in use; historical records note that Giraldus and Archbishop Baldwin crossed at this point in 1188.

## Transport and Infrastructure
Briton-Ferry is connected to London by rail, situated at a distance of 174 miles. It is served by stations on the Rhondda & Swansea Bay railway and the Great Western railway. Additionally, a tram-line links the seaport with Neath, which is 2 miles away. The Vale of Neath Canal, constructed in 1797, has its terminus at Briton-Ferry.

## Industrial Development
While the district was previously noted for its scenery, industrial growth has considerably marred the landscape. This development was primarily driven by the construction of a 13-acre dock in 1861, which is owned by the Great Western Railway Company. Around the same time, the South Wales mineral railway opened up the mining districts of Maesteg and Glyncorrwg. This railway connects these districts to the dock, providing coal as the primary export.

The town's industrial capacity includes the large-scale manufacture of tinplates and steel. Furthermore, the area contains a foundry and iron-works.

## Regional Geology
In the broader Vale of Neath in South Wales, a substance known as Dinas clay is found. This material is a loose disintegrated sandstone consisting of nearly pure silica, containing no more than approximately 2-1/2% of bases. The clay is processed by crushing it between rollers, mixing it with roughly 1% lime, and firing it in kilns at very high temperatures to create bricks. These bricks are specifically utilized for fire arches, roofs, and other components of reverberatory steel-melting furnaces that are subjected to intense heat.
