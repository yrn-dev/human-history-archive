# Count Eduard Taaffe

Count Eduard Taaffe was a prominent Austrian statesman who served as the Austrian prime minister, maintaining his position for fourteen years. His political career was characterized by a strategy of "unpolitical politics," through which he sought to govern by balancing various party interests and avoiding contentious legislation.

## Early Political Career and Rise to Power
Taaffe first emerged as a significant figure in the Austrian government in early 1870, serving as the minister of the interior in a reconstructed cabinet presided over by Stremayr. During this period, Taaffe and Count Alfred Potocki, the minister of agriculture, advocated for a policy of conciliation toward the Slav races, a direction supported by Beust and likely the emperor. However, this approach clashed with other members of the ministry who wished to weaken the opposition by removing Reichsrath elections from the diets. Consequently, Taaffe and his allies resigned in January 1870.

Following a period of political instability and the failure of subsequent attempts to reconcile with the Slavs—including a failed effort by Potocki to persuade the Czechs to accept the constitution—Taaffe and Potocki formed another government to pursue reconciliation.

## Premiership and the "Iron Ring"
Taaffe eventually became the first minister (prime minister) in 1879. Upon entering office, he initially attempted to govern with the assistance of moderates from all parties, including representatives from nearly every party in his cabinet. However, after the Liberals voted against the government on a critical military bill, Taaffe was forced to seek support from the Right.

This shift led to the resignation of the German members of the government, who were replaced by Czechs, Poles, and Clericals. This coalition created an "iron ring" of three parties that left the German Liberals in a minority and helpless within the parliament. Taaffe, who was not a party man and had previously served in a Liberal government, utilized his position between the Right and the constitutionalists to maintain power. The Right supported him primarily to prevent the return of a Liberal government.

## Relations with Bohemia and the Czechs
One of Taaffe's primary achievements was persuading the representatives of Bohemia to end their policy of "passive resistance" and participate in the deliberations of the parliament in Vienna. This followed a period of tension starting in 1872 when a government with a pronounced German tendency had taken office.

The Bohemians agreed to take their seats in the Vienna parliament, though they did so with the formal reservation that Bohemia, along with Moravia and Silesia, constituted a separate state under the same sovereign as Austria and Hungary. While they took the oath without reservation upon entering the House, the emperor stated in the speech from the throne that they had entered without prejudice to their convictions.

In recognition of this concession, Taaffe's government implemented several measures:
* **Electoral Reform:** The government removed some of the most gross anomalies in the Bohemian electoral system, which had previously been heavily biased toward the German minority.
* **Education:** The government consented to the foundation of a Bohemian university at Prague, which significantly aided the intellectual development of the region.
* **Political Negotiation:** Through private negotiations, Taaffe also persuaded Bohemian feudal proprietors to grant a certain number of seats to the Feudalists.

## The Reform Bill and Resignation
In October 1893, Taaffe departed from his cautious approach by introducing a reform bill regarding the franchise. This bill was a response to demands for universal suffrage from Socialists, working men, and the Young Czechs. Taaffe's proposal suggested granting the franchise in rural districts and towns to anyone who could read and write and had resided in one place for six months, while maintaining the existing *curiae* for the chambers of commerce and feudal proprietors.

The bill faced strong opposition:
* **Liberals:** Opposed the measure fearing that the extension of the franchise would destroy their influence due to the rise of anti-Semitism and socialism.
* **Conservatives:** Despite the potential to ruin the Liberals, they refused to support a measure that transferred power from the well-to-do to the indigent.
* **Poles:** Opposed the bill because it would increase the influence of the Ruthenes.

Having failed to consult key allies such as Hohenwart, Taaffe found the position of his government hopeless and resigned.
