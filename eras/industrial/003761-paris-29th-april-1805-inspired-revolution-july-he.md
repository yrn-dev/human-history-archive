## Henri Auguste Barbier
Born in Paris on the 29th of April 1805, Henri Auguste Barbier became a poet and dramatist. His literary output was significantly influenced by the revolution of July, which inspired him to write a series of vigorous and eager poems. These works, which denounced the evils of the time in a crude manner, are referred to collectively as the *Iambes* (1831), although this designation does not strictly apply to every poem in the series. 

The *Iambes* were modeled on the verse of Andre Chenier and included the following titles:
* *La Curee*
* *La Popularite*
* *L'Idole*
* *Paris*
* *Dante*
* *Quatre-vingt-treize*
* *Varsovie*

While Barbier's other poems have been forgotten, he did collaborate with Leon de Wailly on the libretto for the opera *Benvenuto Cellini* by Berlioz. Additionally, he produced two series of poems focusing on social and political troubles in England and Italy, which were included in later editions of *Iambes et poemes*.

Barbier's professional recognition was long delayed; he was admitted to the Academy in 1869. This admission prompted Montalembert to remark, "Barbier? mais il est mort!" It was also claimed, though without foundation, that Barbier was not the actual author of the *Iambes*. He died on the 13th of February 1882 in Nice.

## Other Events of 1805
Beyond the birth of Barbier, the year 1805 saw developments regarding the Legion of Honour, the only order of France. Instituted by Napoleon Bonaparte on the 19th of May 1802, the order served as a general civil and military order of merit. In 1805, the rank of "Grand Eagle" (later known as the Grand Cordon or Grand Cross) was instituted, which took precedence over the grand officers.
