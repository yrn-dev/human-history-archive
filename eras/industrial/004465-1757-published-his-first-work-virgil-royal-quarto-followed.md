# John Baskerville

## Early Life and Career
John Baskerville (1706–1775) was an English printer born on January 28, 1706, in Wolverley, Worcestershire. Before entering the printing trade, Baskerville established himself in Birmingham around 1726, where he worked as a writing master. He demonstrated a significant talent for calligraphy and the cutting of inscriptions in stone. During his time in Birmingham, he also acquired a considerable fortune through important improvements he made to the process of japanning.

## Innovations in Typography
Around the year 1750, Baskerville began conducting experiments in type-founding. His efforts resulted in the production of types that were superior in elegance and distinctness compared to any previously employed. Following these developments, he established his own printing-house.

Baskerville is ranked among the foremost figures who advanced the art of printing, and his works are considered admirable specimens of typography. While some of his contemporaries claimed that the quality of his books was due more to the ink and paper than the type itself, the high estimation of his work is evidenced by the difficulty of obtaining specimens from his press today.

## Major Publications
Baskerville published his first work in 1757, which was a *Virgil* produced in royal quarto. This was followed in 1758 by his famous edition of Milton. In that same year, he was appointed as the printer to the university of Cambridge, a role that led him to undertake editions of the Book of Common Prayer and the Bible.

In 1762, he published a *Horace* that is noted even among his own productions for its beauty of type, correctness, and the quality of the paper. The success of a second *Horace*, published in quarto in 1770, prompted Baskerville to produce a series of quarto editions of various Latin authors. This series included the works of:
* Florus
* Sallust
* Terence
* Lucretius
* Propertius
* Tibullus
* Catullus

The publication of these classical texts serves as a counterpoint to allegations that Baskerville lacked an education.

## Death and Legacy
John Baskerville died on January 8, 1775. Following his death, his wife, Sarah Baskerville, continued to manage the printing business for a period of time.
