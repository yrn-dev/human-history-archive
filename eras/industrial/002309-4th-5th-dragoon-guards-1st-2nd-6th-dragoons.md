# 4th and 5th Dragoon Guards, 1st, 2nd and 6th Dragoons

The 4th and 5th Dragoon Guards, along with the 1st, 2nd, and 6th Dragoons, constituted the Heavy Brigade of the British cavalry division during the action of Balaklava.

## Role in the Battle of Balaklava
On October 25, 1854, during the Crimean War, a Russian field army under General Liprandi advanced to attack allied Turkish, French, and English forces besieging Sevastopol. After Russian forces cleared the Vorontsov ridge and captured several guns, a heavy mass of Russian cavalry descended into the Balaklava plain.

The British cavalry division, commanded by the earl of Lucan, was positioned in the plain at this time, though the commander was restricted from engaging the Russians by the tenor of his orders. The Heavy Brigade—comprising the 4th and 5th Dragoon Guards and the 1st, 2nd, and 6th Dragoons—was under the command of Brigadier-General J. Y. Scarlett. These regiments were noted to be very weak in numbers.

As the Russian cavalry mass moved toward Balaklava, it was fired upon by a Turkish battery. Shortly thereafter, the Heavy Brigade launched a charge. Although the attack was initially impeded by obstacles on the ground, the weight of the British troopers eventually broke the enemy during the ensuing melee. The charge of the 4th Dragoon Guards, which was delivered against the flank of the Russian mass, proved decisive. This action caused the Russian cavalry to break and flee back to the ridge; the entire charge lasted less than five minutes.

## Classification of Dragoons
The regiments mentioned above belong to a broader history of the "dragoon" soldier. Originally, in the 16th and 17th centuries, a dragoon was a mounted infantryman trained to fight only on foot. The name derived from the "dragon," a short musket or species of carbine. During this period, dragoons were organized into companies rather than squadrons, and their non-commissioned and commissioned officers held infantry titles.

Because they were the most economical and efficient form of cavalry for guerrilla warfare and police work, they were frequently used for such tasks. An example of this was the "Dragonnades," operations involving mainly mounted troops used to enforce the decrees of Louis XIV against Protestants following the revocation of the edict of Nantes.

Over time, dragoons improved their armament and horsemanship to meet cavalry standards. By the early wars of Frederick the Great, the term "dragoon" evolved to mean medium cavalry. In the British service, the classification is further divided: the Dragoon Guards (consisting of seven regiments) are medium cavalry, while the dragoons—specifically the 1st Royals, 2nd Scots Greys, and 6th Inniskillings—are classified as heavy cavalry.
