# The Stirling Water-Tube Boiler

The Stirling boiler is a specific type of water-tube boiler utilized for stationary purposes. It is characterized by a design consisting of four or five horizontal drums. Within this configuration, the three upper drums serve as the steam-space, while the one or two lower drums are used to contain water.

## Structural Design and Connectivity
The internal connectivity of the Stirling boiler varies based on the number of lower drums fitted. When two lower drums are used, they are connected to one another via horizontal tubes at approximately the middle of their height. These lower drums are further connected to the upper drums by numerous nearly vertical tubes, which constitute the major portion of the boiler's heating surfaces.

The upper drums are arranged with the central drum positioned at a slightly higher level than the others. This central drum communicates with the drum nearest the back of the boiler through a set of curved tubes located entirely above the water-level. It communicates with the front drum via two separate sets of tubes: one set positioned above the water-level and another set positioned below it.

## Combustion and Casing
The entire boiler assembly is enclosed in brickwork, which also incorporates the supporting girders and columns. To ensure the furnace gases follow specified courses among the tubes, brickwork baffles are employed. 

The design creates a large combustion chamber in the space between the tubes and the boiler front; all furnace gases must pass through this chamber before entering the spaces between the tubes. In some instances, a baffle-bridge is constructed within this chamber. Additionally, a second chamber is formed between the first and second sets of tubes.

## Water Circulation and Sediment Management
The feed-water enters the boiler through the back upper drum. It is required to pass down through the third set of tubes into the lower drum before it can reach other sections of the boiler. This arrangement ensures that the coldest water is positioned where the temperature of the furnace gases is at its lowest.

Because the current through the lower drum is slight, solid matters that separate from the feed-water as its temperature rises have the opportunity to settle at the bottom of the drum. This area is characterized by lower heating levels, ensuring that the presence of these solids is not injurious.

## Superheaters
When superheaters are required for the Stirling boiler, they are constructed using two drums connected by numerous small tubes, making their construction somewhat similar to the boiler itself. The superheater is positioned between the first and second sets of tubes to expose it to furnace gases before too much heat has been extracted from them. The system includes arrangements for draining the superheater and for flooding it while steam is being raised.
