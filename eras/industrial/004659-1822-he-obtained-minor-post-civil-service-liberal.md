# Manuel Bretón de los Herreros

Manuel Bretón de los Herreros (1796–1873) was a Spanish dramatist noted for his comic power, festive invention, and unique metrical dexterity. Born on December 19, 1796, in Quel (Logroño), he received his education in Madrid.

## Military and Early Civil Service
Bretón de los Herreros began his adult life in military service, enlisting on May 24, 1812. He served in Catalonia and Valencia in the conflict against the French. He eventually retired from military duty on March 8, 1822, holding the rank of corporal. Following his retirement, he obtained a minor post in the civil service under the liberal government.

## Literary Career and Success
Upon his discharge, Bretón de los Herreros decided to pursue a living as a writer for the stage. His debut piece, *Á la vejez viruelas*, was produced on October 14, 1824, and established him as the legitimate successor to the younger Moratin. He demonstrated astonishing industry during his early career; between October 1824 and November 1828, he composed thirty-nine plays. Of these, six were original works, while the remainder were recasts of classic masterpieces or translations.

His body of work eventually grew to include approximately three hundred and sixty original plays, twenty-three of which were written in prose. Several of his works continued to be performed long after their debut, including *Marcela o a cual de los trés?* (1831), *Muérete; y verás!* (1837), and *La Escuela del matrimonio* (1852). While he wrote a few pieces in the fashionable romantic vein, such as *Elena*, his career was largely defined by a long series of successes in comedy.

## Scholarship and National Library
In 1831, Bretón de los Herreros published a translation of Tibullus. This work earned him a reputation for scholarship—which the archive describes as unmerited—and consequently secured him an appointment as a sub-librarian at the national library.

## Political Shift and Later Years
Although he had previously been a liberal, Bretón de los Herreros became conservative as he aged. This shift in political leaning led to a significant professional setback in 1840 when he wrote *La Ponchada*, a play in which he ridiculed the National Guard. As a result of this work, he was dismissed from his position at the national library. During this period of unpopularity, he seriously considered emigrating to America.

However, his professional standing recovered, and within two years he regained his supremacy on the Spanish stage. He later became the secretary to the Spanish Academy, though he eventually quarrelled with his fellow members. Manuel Bretón de los Herreros died in Madrid on November 8, 1873.
