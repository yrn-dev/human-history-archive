## Revenue Sources
The state's revenues are derived from a variety of sources, principally including:
*   **Trade and Imports:** Duties and fees on imports, as well as export duties on rubber, coca, and minerals (with the exception of silver).
*   **Excise Taxes:** Taxes levied on sugar, tobacco, wines, and spirits.
*   **Corporate and Legal Fees:** Taxes on the profits of stock companies, stamp taxes, and fees for patents and licences.
*   **Other Income:** General mining taxes and revenues generated from the postal and telegraph services.

While Bolivia is sometimes referred to as a "free-trade country" due to nominally moderate import duties, this is considered a misnomer. In addition to schedule rates of 10 to 40% *ad valorem* on imports, several other charges apply. These include a consumption tax of 10 centavos per quintal (46 kilogrammes), a consular fee of 1-1/2% for the registration of invoices that exceed 200 bolivianos, and fees for viseing certificates for merchandise in transit. Furthermore, there are import and consumption taxes levied by municipalities and departments, as well as special "octroi" taxes on merchandise controlled by monopolies, such as tobacco and spirits.
