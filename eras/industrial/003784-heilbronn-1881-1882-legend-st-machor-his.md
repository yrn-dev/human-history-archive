# Heilbronn (1881-1882)

The city of Heilbronn served as a site of publication for several scholarly and critical works between 1881 and 1882, primarily focusing on the fields of national literature, hagiography, and historical criticism.

## Literary and Hagiographic Publications
During this period, C. Horstmann published significant texts in Heilbronn concerning the works of the Scottish national poet, Barbour. In 1881 and 1882, Horstmann released a two-volume set titled *Barbours des schottischen Nationaldichters Legendensammlung nebst den Fragmenten seines Trojanerkrieges*. This work included a collection of legends and fragments from the Trojan War. Horstmann used these publications to uphold the claim of Barbour's authorship of the texts.

Additionally, in 1881, Horstmann published *Altenglische Legenden. Neue Folge* in Heilbronn. This volume contains the legend of St Machor, located specifically on pages 189 to 208. The authorship of these legends remained a point of scholarly contention; while Horstmann supported Barbour, a later 1896 edition by W. M. Metcalfe disputed this claim.

## Historical and Critical Works
Heilbronn was also the location of publication for critical analysis regarding English history and biography. In 1882, Thum published *Anmerkungen zu Macaulay's History of England* in the city. This work contributed to the broader body of German criticism concerning the history written by Lord Macaulay.

## Other Related Publications
Beyond the specific works of 1881-1882, Heilbronn had previously been a site for the publication of Spanish literary works, such as the 1877 edition of *El Mágico prodigioso* edited by Alfred Morel-Fatio.
