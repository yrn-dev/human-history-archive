# Ludwig van Beethoven

Ludwig van Beethoven is described as the greatest musician, and perhaps the greatest artist, to have ever lived, characterized by his absolute conscientiousness of execution, a perfect sense of beauty, and depth and truth of thought. His influence on subsequent music is considered immeasurable and universal, affecting every school of composition.

## Artistic Development and Style
Beethoven's work is proverbially divided into three styles, though these periods are not rigidly separated chronologically or internally. His first manner is often popularly described as "Mozartesque," though this is considered an injustice as his style differed more radically from Mozart's than Mozart's did from Haydn's. 

In his second manner, as seen in the "Waldstein" sonata (op. 53), the newer elements of his art became familiar, displacing the first manner. In this period, the need for a jocular or paradoxical manner disappeared, replaced by an orderly sequence in the bass and a "calm diurnal energy of nature." His third period is no longer viewed as being obscured by his deafness, nor is his later treatment of form regarded as merely revolutionary.

Beethoven is noted as being unique among artists for his ability to revolutionize his means of expression while maintaining the power to construct perfect works of art. The transition between his periods was smooth, covering an emotional range as vast as that between Bach and Mozart.

## Key Works and Musical Devices
Beethoven's pianoforte works are considered a key to his intellectual development and a way to trace the growth of his style more readily than his larger compositions. Notable works include:
* **The 2nd Symphony:** Described as the most brilliant and triumphant piece written up to that time.
* **Sonata, op. 101:** The first work indisputably in his "third manner."
* **Sonata, op. 106 (B-flat):** The largest extant symphonic pianoforte work, surpassed in length only by Beethoven's own 33 variations on Diabelli's waltz and Bach's *Goldberg* variations.
* **Overture, *Die Weihe des Hauses* (op. 124):** A magnificent essay in orchestral free fugue.
* **25 Scotch songs (op. 108):** A collection showing Beethoven's artistic impulse prevailing over sympathetic insight into folk-music.

The influence of Beethoven's artistic devices was later developed *pari passu* with those of Bach by the composer Brahms. This synthesis resulted in a complete unification of the opposition between form and polyphony, a conflict that had previously threatened the art of the sonata during its infancy.

## Early Life and Education
Born in Bonn, Beethoven became the practical head of his family by age fifteen. In 1787, he visited Vienna, where he gave lessons and astonished Mozart with his extemporizations. He later studied under Haydn in 1792, though the relationship was strained; Haydn found Beethoven's manners poor and his free compositions audacious. Beethoven also studied strict counterpoint with Albrechtsberger, a grammarian who held a poor opinion of him but provided the necessary academic rigor.

Beethoven's footing in Vienna was secured by the impressiveness of his playing and the support of the musical aristocracy, who respected him despite his rough manners and temper, viewing them as signs of a noble and passionate nature.

## Personal Life and Character
Beethoven's personal life was marked by significant struggle and a "grapple with fate." He was devoted to his mother and later fought a legal battle with his brother Kaspar Karl's widow for the guardianship of his nephew. This nephew became a source of great anxiety and distress during the final twelve years of Beethoven's life.

Despite his "artistic temperament," Beethoven is described as having an utterly simple nature and a passionate affection that often lacked a natural outlet. His romantic attachments, though often with women above his social rank, were honorable and respected. He maintained a level of orthodoxy that led him to object to Mozart's *Don Giovanni* and influenced his selection of the subject for his opera, *Fidelio*.
