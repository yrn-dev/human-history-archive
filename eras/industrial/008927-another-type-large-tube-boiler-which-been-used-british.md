# The Niclausse Boiler

The Niclausse boiler is a type of large-tube boiler utilized in several electric-light installations on land, as well as within the British and other navies. It is characterized by a specific arrangement of elements and a specialized tube construction designed for heating.

## Design and Structure
The boiler consists of a horizontal steam-chest. Positioned beneath this chest is a series of elements arranged side by side over the fire. To protect the structure from the direct action of the fire, the entire assembly is enclosed in an iron casing lined with firebrick.

Each individual element is composed of a header with a rectangular cross-section. These headers are typically made of malleable cast iron with cast-in diaphragms, although steel is sometimes used; in steel versions, the diaphragms and the closed bottoms are made of riveted steel plate. To allow for easy removal of any single element, the headers are bolted to socket-pieces that are riveted to the bottom of the steam-chest.

## Tube Construction and Arrangement
The heating surface of the Niclausse boiler is formed by two rows of inclined, close-ended tubes within each element. These tubes slope downwards toward the back of the boiler at an inclination of 6 degrees relative to the horizontal. The tubes are manufactured from seamless steel and feature a peculiar construction:

*   **The Back End:** The lower or back ends of the tubes are reduced in diameter, screwed, and sealed with cap-nuts.
*   **The Front End:** The front ends are thickened through upsetting. The sections fitting into the diaphragm and header walls are turned to gauge. The upper and lower portions of the tubes between these fitting points are cut away, leaving only the side portions, a feature termed a "lanterne."
*   **Circulation:** Inside each generating tube is a small water-circulating tube made of thin sheet steel. This inner tube is open at the lower end and secured at the other end to a smaller "lanterne" that extends from the front of the header to the diaphragm, effectively closing the front end of the generating tube.

The tube-holes through the diaphragm and the front and back of the headers are accurately bored at an angle to match the inclination of the tubes; those in the header walls are slightly conical.

## General Boiler Classification
The Niclausse boiler falls under the broader category of "water-tube" boilers. In this class of boiler, the water is primarily contained within numerous comparatively small tubes, and the fire is usually placed beneath these tubes. This differs from "tank" boilers, which contain larger quantities of water in a cylindrical outer shell. 

A primary design requirement for water-tube boilers, including the Niclausse type, is the implementation of special provisions to maintain the circulation of water through the tubes. In naval applications, such as the Niclausse, seamless steel tubes are invariably employed.
