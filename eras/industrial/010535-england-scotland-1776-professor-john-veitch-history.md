# England and Scotland (1776) and Professor John Veitch

## Historical Literature of the Borders
The border regions between England and Scotland have been the subject of various historical and literary studies. George Ridpath published a work titled *Border History of England and Scotland* in 1776. Later, in 1877, Professor John Veitch contributed to the scholarship of the region with his work, *History and Poetry of the Scottish Border*.

The literature of this area is notably characterized by border ballads. Sir Walter Scott is credited with rescuing many of these ballads from oblivion through his research in the district, which resulted in the publication of *Minstrelsy of the Scottish Border* in 1802 and 1803. Other authors who were drawn to the folklore, traditions, and stirring incidents of the border country include John Mackay Wilson (1804-1835), who published *Tales of the Borders* in 1835, as well as John Wilson ("Christopher North") and James Hogg, known as "the Ettrick Shepherd."

## Scottish Dynastic and Political History
The history of Scotland includes the influence of the Baliol family. The family was founded in England by Guy (or Guido) de Baliol, a Norman baron who arrived with William the Conqueror and received lands in northern England from William II. His descendant, Bernard de Baliol, established Castle Barnard in Durham. A later Bernard de Baliol is noted for taking William the Lion, king of Scotland, prisoner in July 1174.

John de Baliol, who married Dervorguila, daughter of Alan, earl of Galloway, served as one of the regents of Scotland during the minority of Alexander III, though he was deprived of this office and his lands in 1255 due to treason. His son, also named John de Baliol, eventually became king of Scotland. There exists a tradition, though described as dubious, that the family name was changed to Baillie because the conduct of Edward and John Baliol was considered pusillanimous.

Other significant figures in Scottish history include Margaret (1283-1290), known as the "maid of Norway." The daughter of Eric II of Norway and Margaret (daughter of Alexander III of Scotland), she became queen in March 1286 after the death of Alexander. A marriage was arranged between the infant queen and the son of the English king Edward I (the future Edward II), but Margaret died in the Orkneys in September 1290.

## Scottish Banking and Institutional History
The financial history of Scotland has been documented by several authors. W. H. Logan wrote *The Scottish Banker* (1847), and Robert Somers published *The Scotch Banks and System of Issue* (1873). Further analysis of the system is found in W. Mitchell's *Scotch Banks and Limited Liability* (1879), as well as A. W. Kerr's *History of Scotch Banking* (1884) and *Scottish Banking, 1865-1896* (1898). Additionally, Boase provided a history of banking in Dundee covering a century, published in 1867.
