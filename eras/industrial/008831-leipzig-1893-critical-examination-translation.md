# Leipzig

Leipzig is a city in Germany that has served as a significant center for higher education, academic publishing, and scholarly research. The city's universities have attracted international students and faculty, contributing to its reputation as a hub for various disciplines, including political economy, biblical criticism, and philology.

## Academic and Educational Influence
The universities of Leipzig have played a role in the education of prominent figures across different fields. Luigi Cossa, an Italian economist born in Milan in 1831, was educated at the university of Leipzig, among other institutions in Vienna and Pavia. In the field of biblical criticism, Johann Jakob Griesbach (1745–1812) was educated at the university of Leipzig, as well as at the universities of Tubingen and Halle. Griesbach became a disciple of J. S. Semler, who encouraged his focus on the textual criticism of the New Testament.

## Publishing and Philological Works
Leipzig has been a major site for the publication of critical editions and scholarly translations. In the field of Sanskrit and comparative philology, Otto von Bohtlingk resided in Leipzig from 1885 until his death in 1904. In 1887, he published *Paninis Grammatik mit Ubersetzung* in Leipzig, which was a republication of his earlier work on Panini's *Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln* (1839–1840), adding a complete translation. Additionally, an index to Bohtlingk's *Indische Spruche* was published in Leipzig in 1893 by Blau.

The city's publishing industry also extended to classical and legal texts. For example, the edition of C. Sintenis (1839) and the edition of G.H. Schaefer (1826) of Plutarch's works were both produced in Leipzig. In the realm of Attic Law, several Greek texts were published there, including I. B. Telfy's *Corpus juris Attici* (1868), the 1903 edition of Aristotle's *Constitution of Athens* by Blass, and the editions of Lysias by Frohberger (1866–1871). Furthermore, J. H. Lipsius published *Das attische Recht und Rechtsverfahren* in Leipzig in 1905.

## Biblical and Theological Scholarship
Leipzig has been a center for the publication of critical examinations of religious and ancient texts. C.H. Cornill published *Prolegomena to Ezechiel* (1886) and H.L. Strack published *Einleitung in das alte Testament, Prolegomena Critica in Vet. Test.* (1873) in the city. Additionally, E. Nestle's *Urtext und Uebersetzungen der Bibel* was published in Leipzig in 1897.
