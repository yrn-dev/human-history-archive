# Bancroft

The name Bancroft is associated with several distinct historical figures across the fields of history, the arts, religion, and science.

## George Bancroft
George Bancroft (1800–1891) was an American statesman and historian born on October 3, 1800, in Worcester, Massachusetts. His father, Aaron Bancroft, was an author, clergyman, and revolutionary soldier. George Bancroft received an extensive education at Harvard University and Phillips Academy, Exeter, as well as at the universities of Berlin, Goettingen, and Heidelberg. While at Goettingen, he studied natural science with Blumenbach, New Testament Greek with Eichhorn, and Plato with Heeren. The latter, a leading historical critic, influenced Bancroft's commitment to painstaking erudition.

Following a European tour where he met figures such as Hegel, Byron, Humboldt, Goethe, and Manzoni, Bancroft returned to the United States in 1822. Although his father, a Unitarian, intended for him to enter the ministry, Bancroft's early attempts at preaching were unsatisfactory. He subsequently served as a tutor at Harvard, where he felt out of place due to the pedantic spirit of classical studies and the narrow curriculum of the time. In 1823, he published a volume of original pieces, translations, and poetry, though it did not bring him fame.

Bancroft is best known for his *History of the United States*, the first volume of which appeared in 1834, followed by a second in 1837. His work was characterized by a philosophic spirit and solidity, leading to significant enthusiasm and large sales at home. His volumes were translated into French, German, Italian, and Danish, and were reprinted in England. In 1835, he published an article in the *North American Review* titled "The Documentary History of the Revolution," which introduced the reading public to a new method and the wealth of available material. He also translated Heeren's work on *The Politics of Ancient Greece* and contributed to Walsh's *American Quarterly* and the *North American Review*.

Throughout his life, Bancroft served as an unofficial dean of the best society and a confidential advisor to statesmen in Washington. His international reputation was evidenced by a public dinner hosted by the Royal Academy in Berlin and a testimonial of regard from the universities of Munich, Heidelberg, and Berlin.

## Sir Squire Bancroft
Sir Squire Bancroft (1841– ) was a prominent English manager and actor born near London on May 14, 1841. He began his stage career in 1861 in Birmingham and spent several years performing in the provinces. He made his London debut in 1865 at the Prince of Wales's theatre in *A Winning Hazard*. 

Bancroft married Effie Marie Wilton (born 1840) in 1868. Together, they managed the Prince of Wales's theatre, where they produced all the Robertson comedies, including *Society* (1865), *Ours* (1866), *Caste* (1867), *Play* (1868), *School* (1869), and *M.P.* (1870). Their management is credited with reviving London's interest in modern drama and establishing a new era for the English stage. Other notable premieres at the playhouse included Boucicault's *London Assurance* (1877), Lytton's *Money* (1872), and *Diplomacy* (an adaptation of Sardou's *Dora*).

In 1879, the Bancrofts moved to the Haymarket, producing works such as Pinero's *Lords and Commons*, W. S. Gilbert's *Sweethearts*, and Sardou's *Fedora* and *Odette* (the latter featuring Madame Modjeska). After accumulating a considerable fortune, they retired in 1885. Sir Squire Bancroft, who was knighted in 1897, returned to the stage in 1889 to play the abbe Latour in a revival of Watts Phillips's *Dead Heart* alongside Sir Henry Irving.

## Richard Bancroft
Richard Bancroft (1544–1610) served as the Archbishop of Canterbury. Born in Farnworth, Lancashire, he was educated at Cambridge's Jesus College and Christ's College, earning his B.A. in 1567 and M.A. in 1570. His early career included serving as chaplain to Bishop Richard Cox and holding rectories in Holborn and Teversham. He earned his B.D. in 1580 and D.D. in 1585.

Bancroft was known as a champion of the church and a critic of the Puritans. In a sermon preached at Paul's Cross on February 9, 1589, he attacked Puritan motives and the right of private judgment, asserting the divine right of bishops. He held various positions, including treasurer of St Paul's cathedral and canon of Westminster. 

In June 1597, he was consecrated bishop of London. Due to the incapacity of Archbishop Whitgift, Bancroft effectively managed ecclesiastical affairs as primate. He directed proceedings against John Penry, Thomas Cartwright, and "Martin Mar-Prelate." In 1600, he led a failed embassy to Embden to resolve disputes between the Danes and the English. He was present at the death of Queen Elizabeth.
