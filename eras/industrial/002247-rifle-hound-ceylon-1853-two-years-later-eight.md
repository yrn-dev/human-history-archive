# The Rifle and the Hound in Ceylon and Eight Years' Wanderings in Ceylon

## Overview of the Works
*The Rifle and the Hound in Ceylon*, published in 1853, and *Eight Years' Wanderings in Ceylon*, published in 1855, are works authored by the English explorer Sir Samuel White Baker. These publications were the result of numerous adventurous hunting expeditions conducted by Baker during his residence in Ceylon.

## Author Biography
Sir Samuel White Baker was born in London on June 8, 1821. He received an education divided between Germany and England. Although his father, a West India merchant, intended for him to pursue a commercial career, Baker found himself entirely unsuited for office work. 

Following his marriage to Henrietta Biddulph Martin on August 3, 1843, and a two-year stay in Mauritius, Baker traveled to Ceylon in 1846. Driven by a desire for travel, he established an agricultural settlement at Nuwara Eliya, which served as a mountain health-resort. With the assistance of his brother, Baker brought choice breeds of cattle and emigrants from England to the settlement, which eventually became a success.

## Subsequent Career of Sir Samuel White Baker
After his time in Ceylon, Baker's restless energy led him to various international endeavors. In 1856, he traveled to the Crimea and Constantinople. He later supervised the construction of a railway across the Dobrudja to connect the Black Sea with the Danube. Following the completion of this project, he toured Asia Minor and south-eastern Europe. During this period, he met Florence, the daughter of Finnian von Sass, in Hungary; she became his second wife in 1860 after the death of his first wife in 1855.

In March 1861, Baker began his first exploration of central Africa. His stated goal was to discover the sources of the Nile and to potentially meet Captains Grant and Speke, who were leading an East African expedition near Victoria Lake. Baker spent a year on the border of Abyssinia and the Sudan, where he explored Nile tributaries, including the Atbara, and learned Arabic. During this time, he proved that the sediment of the Nile originated in Abyssinia. He reached Khartum and departed in December 1862 to follow the White Nile, eventually meeting Speke and Grant at Gondokoro two months later.
