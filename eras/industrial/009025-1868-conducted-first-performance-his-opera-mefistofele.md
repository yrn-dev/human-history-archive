# Arrigo Boito and the Opera Mefistofele

## Biography of Arrigo Boito
Arrigo Boito (1842– ) was an Italian musical composer and poet born in Padua on February 24, 1842. His early education took place at the Milan Conservatoire, where he demonstrated a divided allegiance between the study of music and literature, a characteristic that would define his life's history. During his time at the Conservatoire, Boito collaborated with Franco Faccio to write and compose a cantata titled *Le Sorelle d'Italia*, which was performed successfully.

Following the completion of his studies, Boito spent several years traveling. Upon returning to Italy, he settled in Milan, where he balanced his time between the fields of music and journalism. In 1866, he participated in the conflict under Garibaldi. Later in his career, in 1893, the university of Cambridge conferred upon him the degree of doctor of music.

## Mefistofele
In 1868, Boito conducted the first performance of his opera *Mefistofele* at the Scala theatre in Milan. The initial reception of the work was poor; it failed completely and was withdrawn after its second performance. However, the opera was revived in 1875 at Bologna. This version was abbreviated and much altered, and it achieved unquestionable success. The work was subsequently performed in London in 1880 with success. Despite these revivals, *Mefistofele* never firmly established itself in popular favor.

In composing the work, Boito approached the Faust legend with a spirit more closely aligned with the conception of Goethe than the approach found in Gounod's *Faust*. While the opera contains many isolated beauties, it is noted for a lack of dramatic interest and cohesion.

## Literary and Operatic Contributions
Beyond *Mefistofele*, Boito's energies were primarily focused on the composition of libretti. His principal works in this area include:
* *Otello* and *Falstaff*, which were set to music by Verdi.
* *La Gioconda*, set by Ponchielli.
* *Amleto*, set by Faccio.
* *Ero e Leandre*, set by Mancinelli and Bottesini.

These libretti are recognized for their uncommon literary value and a rare understanding of the requirements of dramatic poetry. In addition to his musical and operatic contributions, Boito authored a novel titled *L'Alfier Meno* and published a book of poems.
