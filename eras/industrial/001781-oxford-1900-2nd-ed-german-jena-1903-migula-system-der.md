# BACTERIOLOGICAL AND SCIENTIFIC AUTHORITIES

The study of bacteria and related microorganisms has produced an extensive body of literature, encompassing general structural analysis, classification, and the study of pathogenic organisms. Due to the volume of original articles, scholarly reference is often directed toward comprehensive textbooks and specialized treatises.

## General Bacteriology and Structure
Several key authorities provide foundational knowledge on the structure and functions of bacteria. Fischer's *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (Oxford, 1900, 2nd ed.), also published in German (Jena, 1903), and Migula's *System der Bakterien* (Jena, 1897) are cited as general authorities. Other significant works include Lafar's *Technical Mycology* (London, 1898) and Mace's *Traite pratique de bakteriologie* (5th ed. 1904).

Research into specific cellular components has been detailed by various authors:
*   **Cell-membrane:** Studied by Butschli (Leipzig, 1896), Fischer (Jena, 1897), and Rowland (1899).
*   **Cilia:** Investigated by Fischer in *Pringsh. Jahrb.* vol. xxvii, as well as in the works of Migula and Fischer.
*   **Nucleus:** Research includes contributions by Wager, Migula, Fischer, and Vejdovsky, the latter of whom published on the nucleus and its division in *Cent. f. Bakt.* (1904) and on bacteria in the Prager water supply (1905). Mencl also provided additions to the structural relations of *Bacterium gammari* in 1907.
*   **Spores:** Documented by Marshall Ward (1895), Sturgis, and L. Klein (1889).

## Specialized Bacteriological Studies
The archive identifies several specialized areas of bacteriological research:
*   **Fossil Bacteria:** Renault's "Recherches sur les Bacteriacees fossiles" (1896).
*   **Bacteria in Water:** Frankland and Marshall Ward provided reports on the bacteriology of water, and Marshall Ward specifically examined the biology of *B. ramosus*. Additionally, papers have been published regarding the bacteria found in the river Thames.
*   **Classification:** Marshall Ward (1892) wrote on the characters used for classifying Schizomycetes, while Lehmann and Neumann contributed to the field via their *Atlas and Essentials of Bac*.

## Pathogenic Bacteria and Immunity
Literature regarding bacteria in the context of disease is vast. The New Sydenham Society published *Microparasites in Disease: Selected Essays* in 1886, which translated classical researches from German and French. 

Among the most important modern works on pathogenic microorganisms is the *Handbuch der pathogenen Mikroorganismen* (Jena, 1904) by Kolle and Wassermann, which continues to be updated with supplements. Other notable texts include:
*   Abbott, *Principles of Bacteriology* (1905).
*   Crookshank, *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* (1896).
*   Duclaux, *Traite de microbiologie* (1899-1900).
*   Eyre, *Bacteriological Technique* (1902).
*   McFarland, *Text-book upon the Pathogenic Bacteria* (1906).
*   Park, *Pathogenic Micro-organisms* (1906).

The bacteriology of infective diseases is also detailed in the *System of Medicine* edited by Clifford Allbutt (1907). Regarding immunity, the primary works cited are those by Ehrlich (*Studies in Immunity*, 1906) and Metchnikoff.
