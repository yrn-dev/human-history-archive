# Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider

## Early Life and Education
Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider (1776–1848) was a German theologian and scholar born in Gersdorf, Saxony. His academic pursuits began in 1794 when he entered the university of Leipzig to study theology, a course of study that lasted four years. Following a period of hesitation regarding his professional path, he decided to seek ordination. In 1802, he passed the examination for *candidatus theologiae* with great distinction. This achievement brought him to the attention of F.V. Reinhard, the author of *System der christlichen Moral* (1788–1815) and a court-preacher at Dresden, who became a patron and close friend to Bretschneider for the rest of his life.

## Academic Career at Wittenberg
Between 1804 and 1806, Bretschneider served as a *Privat-docent* at the university of Wittenberg. During his tenure in this role, he delivered lectures on the subjects of theology and philosophy. This period was marked by significant literary output; in 1805, he wrote *Systematische Entwickelung aller in der Dogmatik vorkommenden Begriffe nach den symbolischen Schriften der evangelisch-lutherischen und reformirten Kirche*, a work focusing on the development of dogma (the 4th edition of which was published in 1841). He also produced an edition of Ecclesiasticus featuring a Latin commentary.

Bretschneider's university career was interrupted by geopolitical events. Upon the advance of Napoleon's French army into Prussia, he chose to leave Wittenberg and abandon his career at the university.

## Ecclesiastical Appointments
Following his departure from Wittenberg, Bretschneider entered the ministry through the assistance of Reinhard. In 1807, he became the pastor of Schneeberg in Saxony. His career progressed in 1808 when he was promoted to the office of superintendent of the church of Annaberg. In this capacity, he was responsible for deciding various matters of ecclesiastical law in accordance with the canon law of Saxony.

However, Bretschneider found that his official duties interfered with his theological studies and that the local climate did not agree with him. Seeking a change, he returned to the university of Wittenberg to obtain a degree of doctor of theology, which he received in August 1812.

## Later Life and Literary Activity
In 1816, Bretschneider was appointed as the general superintendent at Gotha. He remained in this position until his death in 1848. This final period of his life is noted as the great period of his literary activity.
