# United Kingdom

## Early Political Organization
The early history of the region was characterized by the existence of numerous separate kingdoms, each with its own royal family. In the districts north of the Humber, the kingdoms of Bernicia and Deira eventually united to form Northumbria. South of the Humber, the kingdom of Mercia formed around the upper basin of the Trent, while East Anglia was located further down the east coast. Between these two was a territory known as Middle Anglia. Other ancient kingdoms included Essex, Kent, and Sussex, as well as the kingdom of the Hwicce, which likely coincided with the area of the old diocese of Worcester. The south of England, between Sussex and "West Wales" (which was eventually reduced to Cornwall), was occupied by Wessex, which initially held territory north of the Thames. Even the Isle of Wight appears to have had its own dynasty.

These kingdoms were not always independent. At various times, certain kings held supremacy over others based on the principle of personal allegiance. Examples include Æthelberht, king of Kent, who was supreme over all kings south of the Humber; the East Anglian king Raedwald; and a series of Northumbrian kings. Earlier, the West Saxon king Ceawlin and the first king of Sussex, Ella (or Ælle), held similar positions of supremacy.

In Scotland, the kingdom of Dalriada was founded in Argyllshire around A.D. 500 by emigrants from the Irish Dalriada (the northern part of county Antrim). While initially dependent on the Irish kingdom, King Aidan secured its independence around 575. By approximately 843, Kenneth, a successor of Aidan, became king of the Picts, and the name Dalriada eventually fell into disuse.

## Military Sanitary Reform
In 1857, a commission was appointed to improve the sanitary conditions of hospitals and barracks in the United Kingdom. This commission reported its findings in 1861 and established general sanitary principles for the construction and arrangement of military buildings. Prominent contributors to this work included John Sutherland, M.D., Captain (later Sir Douglas) Gallon, R.E., and Sidney Herbert (later Lord Herbert of Lea).

The commission found significant discrepancies in living space. Although a 1845 general order required 450 to 500 cubic feet per man in new home station barracks, inspections revealed that some men had less than 250 cubic feet. Out of a nominal accommodation for 76,813 soldiers, only 2,003 had 600 cubic feet per man, which was the new minimum scale established by the 1857 commission. Implementing this 600 cubic feet standard would have reduced total barrack accommodation by nearly one-third. Consequently, many buildings were condemned as unsuitable, while others were improved through site opening and alterations. In 1862, the commission became a standing body known as the Army Sanitary Committee, which continues to advise the secretary of state for war on type plans, sanitary fitments, and sites for new hospitals and barracks. This work formed the basis for the first issue of the *Barrack Synopsis* in 1865.

## Industrial Development
### Dairy Farming
The dairying industry in the United Kingdom underwent a revolution during the final quarter of the 19th century. Beginning around 1875, economic causes led agriculturists to focus more on the production of cream, milk, cheese, and butter. A pivotal event occurred on October 24, 1876, with the first London dairy show. During this event, Professor J. Prince Sheldon proposed the creation of the British Dairy Farmers' Association, an organization that became closely linked to the industry's development.

Two major technological advancements drove this modification: the introduction of the centrifugal cream-separator around 1880, which replaced the slow process of raising cream in pans, and the invention of a quick method to determine the fat content of milk samples approximately ten years later, removing the need for tedious chemical analysis. By 1901, the United Kingdom's butter imports reached a record high of 3,702,810 cwt., valued at £19,297,005. Additionally, a special committee of the Royal Statistical Society began an inquiry into the home production of meat and milk in 1901.

### Carpet Manufacturing
In the early 19th century, English carpet makers used the Jacquard apparatus for weaving patterns by hand, following French examples. The industry accelerated when English enterprise perfected steam-worked Jacquard weaving carpet looms, utilizing inventions from Mr. William Wood of England and Erastus B. Bigelow of America. This led to the establishment of many power-loom carpet manufactories in the United Kingdom. This technological lead was significant enough that French pile carpet manufacturers did not begin adopting similar power-looms from England until 1880.

## Beer Production and Consumption
The United Kingdom is the third-largest beer-producing nation in terms of liquid bulk, following Germany and the United States. However, the consumption of beer per head is slightly higher in the United Kingdom than in Germany and significantly higher than in the United States. In terms of composition, British-type ales are noted to be much heavier than the corresponding products found in the United States.
