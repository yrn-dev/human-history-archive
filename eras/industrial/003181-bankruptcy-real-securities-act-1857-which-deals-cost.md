# Bankruptcy and Real Securities Act 1857

The Bankruptcy and Real Securities Act 1857 is a legislative measure that served as an amendment to the laws governing sequestration and bankruptcy in Scotland. Its primary function was to address the costs associated with the competition for trusteeships.

## Context of Scottish Sequestration
The Act operated within a broader legal framework where sequestration—the process of administering a bankrupt's estate—could be awarded by either the sheriff or the court of session. Under this system, a sequestration could be recalled if a majority in number and four-fifths in value of the creditors preferred to wind up the estate through a private arrangement.

When sequestration proceeded, creditors held a meeting to elect a trustee to administer the estate via a majority in value. To assist and control this administration and to declare dividends, three commissioners (who were either creditors or their mandatories) were also elected.

## Obligations of the Bankrupt
Under the laws amended by the 1857 Act, the bankrupt was required to provide all available information regarding their estate, under the threat of imprisonment. The bankrupt was subject to a public examination on oath before the sheriff. Furthermore, individuals described as "conjunct and confident persons" could also be examined.

The law provided several paths for a bankrupt to be discharged:
*   **By composition:** An agreement reached between the parties.
*   **Without composition:** This could occur through a petition with the concurrence of all creditors; after six months with the concurrence of a majority and four-fifths in value of creditors; after eighteen months with the concurrence of a bare majority in number and value; or after two years without any concurrence. 

In the case of discharge without concurrence after two years, a judge maintained the authority to refuse the application if the bankrupt had willfully failed to comply with the law or fraudulently concealed effects.

## Related Legislative Amendments
The Bankruptcy and Real Securities Act 1857 was part of a series of amendments to Scottish bankruptcy law. These included:
*   **The Bankruptcy Amendment (Scotland) Act 1860:** This allowed the court to recall a sequestration if it was deemed more convenient to wind up the estate in Ireland or England.
*   **The Bankruptcy Amendment Act (Scotland) 1875:** This act established preferential claims for wages. Claims for servants, shopmen, and clerks were prioritized for an amount not exceeding £50 and a period not exceeding four months. Workmen were placed on a similar footing for a period not exceeding two months.
*   **The Debtors (Scotland) Act 1880:** This act introduced a principal change by abolishing imprisonment for debt, though it maintained provisions for punishing fraudulent bankrupts.

## Comparison with Other Jurisdictions
While the 1857 Act addressed Scottish procedures, bankruptcy in Ireland was regulated by different statutes, specifically the leading Irish statutes of 1857 and 1872, and the Irish Debtors Act 1872. Although the Irish system shared main features with older English enactments and modifications from the English act of 1869, it notably never imported the system of liquidation by arrangement and composition without the control or approval of the court.
