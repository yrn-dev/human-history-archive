# Nathaniel Prentiss Banks

## Early Life and Education
Nathaniel Prentiss Banks was an American soldier and politician born on January 30, 1816, in Waltham, Massachusetts. His early education was limited to common school. At a young age, Banks entered the workforce as a bobbin-boy in a cotton factory, where his father served as the superintendent.

## Legal and Journalistic Beginnings
Following his early employment in the factory, Banks edited a weekly newspaper in Waltham. He subsequently pursued the study of law and was admitted to the bar. Banks gained distinction during this period through his ability as a public speaker and his energy.

## Political Career
Banks began his political service as a Free Soiler, serving in the Massachusetts house of representatives from 1849 to 1853. During this tenure, he held the position of speaker in 1851 and 1852. In 1853, he served as the president of the state Constitutional Convention. That same year, he was elected to the national House of Representatives as a coalition candidate representing both Free Soilers and Democrats.

Although he was re-elected in 1854 as an American (also known as a "Know-Nothing"), he departed from that party shortly thereafter. In 1855, he presided over a Republican convention in Massachusetts. At the start of the Thirty-Fourth Congress, Banks was supported for the speakership by anti-Nebraska men. After a protracted and bitter contest that lasted from December 3, 1855, to February 2, 1856, he was chosen as speaker on the 133rd ballot. This event has been described as the first national victory for the Republican party.

Banks was re-elected as a Republican in 1856 but resigned his seat in December 1857. He served as the governor of Massachusetts from 1858 to 1861, a term characterized by educational and administrative reforms. Following his governorship, he succeeded George B. McClellan as the president of the Illinois Central railway.

## Military Service
Despite having been a strong advocate for peace during his time as governor, Banks was among the first to offer his services to President Lincoln. In 1861, he was appointed major-general of volunteers and became one of the most prominent volunteer officers.

In 1862, during George B. McClellan's Peninsular Campaign, Banks's corps was tasked with the important responsibility of defending Washington from the army of "Stonewall" Jackson. In the spring of that year, Banks was ordered to move against Jackson in the Shenandoah Valley; however, he was defeated at Winchester, Virginia, on the 25th by Jackson's superior forces.
