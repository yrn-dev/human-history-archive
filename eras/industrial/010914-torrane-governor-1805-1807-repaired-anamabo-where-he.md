# Colonel G. Torrane and the Ashanti Conflict

## Overview of the Ashanti Empire
The Ashanti first drew the attention of Europeans in the early 18th century, following a series of successful wars against kingdoms that bordered maritime territory. The power of the Ashanti is attributed to its real founder, Osai Tutu, who is credited with either building or significantly extending the city of Kumasi. Under his leadership, the Ashanti subdued the neighboring state of Denkera in 1719, as well as the Banna and Gaman (Mahommedan) countries, expanding the empire through conquests to the east and west. Although Osai Tutu was defeated and slain in 1731, his successor, Osai Apoko, continued to make acquisitions moving toward the coast.

## The Reign of Osai Tutu Quamina
In 1800, Osai Tutu Quamina became king. Described as an ambitious and enterprising leader, he sought to open communications with white nations. Around 1807, a conflict arose involving two chiefs of the Assin who had been defeated in battle by the king. These chiefs sought refuge among the Fanti, the ruling people on the coast. When the Fanti refused to deliver the fugitives to the Ashanti, Osai Tutu invaded their country, defeated them, and drove them toward the sea.

## The Invasion of Anamabo
The Ashanti forces reached the coast near Anamabo, a location where a British fort was situated. The British governor attempted to mediate the situation, exhorting the townsmen to come to terms; however, the townsmen resolved to continue the contest. This resulted in the slaughter of 8,000 inhabitants and the destruction of the town. The Ashanti suffered over 2,000 casualties and were unable to storm the English fort, though the fort's garrison was reduced from twenty-four men to eight.

## The Administration of Colonel G. Torrane
Colonel G. Torrane served as the governor of Cape Coast from 1805 to 1807. Following the conflict at Anamabo, a truce was established. Because the Ashanti king refused to negotiate with anyone other than the governor of Cape Coast, Torrane repaired to Anamabo, where he was received with great pomp.

During these negotiations, Torrane determined to surrender the fugitive Assin chiefs. While one chief managed to escape, the other was handed over and subsequently put to death by the Ashanti. Torrane concluded a formal agreement with the Ashanti that included the following terms:
* An acknowledgment of the Ashanti conquest of Fantiland.
* The delivery of half of the fugitives located in the Anamabo fort to the Ashanti.
* An agreement by the governor to pay rent to the Ashanti for both Cape Coast castle and Anamabo fort.

Regarding the remaining fugitives in the Anamabo fort, most were sold as slaves by Torrane and the members of his council. Colonel G. Torrane died on the coast in 1808.
