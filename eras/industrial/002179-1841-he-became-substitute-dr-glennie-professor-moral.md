# Alexander Bain

Alexander Bain (1818–1903) was a Scottish educationalist and philosopher. Born on June 11, 1818, in Aberdeen, he received his initial schooling in his hometown. In his early life, Bain worked as a weaver, a fact that led to the punning description of him as *Weevir, rex philosophorum*.

## Academic Career and Early Appointments
Bain entered Marischal College in 1836. During his time there, he was influenced by several professors: William Knight (natural philosophy), Thomas Clark (chemistry), and John Cruickshank (mathematics). His academic performance was distinguished, particularly in the fields of physics, mathematics, and mental philosophy.

Toward the conclusion of his arts course, Bain began contributing to the *Westminster Review*. His first article, titled "Electrotype and Daguerreotype," was published in September 1840. This professional engagement initiated a lifelong friendship and connection with John Stuart Mill.

In 1841, Bain was appointed as the substitute for Dr. Glennie, the professor of moral philosophy, who was unable to perform the active duties of the chair due to ill-health. Bain held this position for three successive sessions. During this period, he continued his writing for the *Westminster Review* and assisted Mill in 1842 with the revision of the manuscript for *System of Logic*. Following this, in 1843, Bain wrote the first review of that book for the *London and Westminster*.

## Later Professional Transitions
In 1845, Bain was appointed to the Andersonian University of Glasgow as the professor of natural philosophy and mathematics. However, he resigned from this post a year later, seeking a wider field of activity and choosing to devote himself to literary work.

In 1848, Bain moved to London to take a position on the board of health under Edwin Chadwick. While in London, he became a prominent member of a distinguished social and intellectual circle that included John Stuart Mill and George Grote.

## Published Works and Later Roles
Bain established his reputation as an independent thinker through the publication of two large treatises: *The Senses and the Intellect* (1855) and *The Emotions and the Will* (1859).

In addition to his literary work, Bain served as an examiner for several institutions. Between 1857 and 1862, and again from 1864 to 1869, he was an examiner in moral and logical philosophy for the University of London. He also served as an examiner in moral science for the Indian Civil Service examinations.
