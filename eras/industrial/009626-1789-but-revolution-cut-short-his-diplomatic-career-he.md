# 1789 and the French Revolution

The year 1789 marked the beginning of a transformative period in France, characterized by the rise of revolutionary ideas and the eventual establishment of a "crowned republic" between 1789 and 1791. During this early phase, France functioned as a limited monarchy where the National Assembly ruled a country at peace and the king maintained a diminished state within the Tuileries.

## Political and Literary Developments
The revolutionary atmosphere of 1789 was fueled by public literary contributions and political activism. Camille Desmoulins began a public literary career during this time; in May and June 1789, he wrote *La France libre*. Although his publisher initially refused to print it, the work was issued on July 18 following the taking of the Bastille. The text was noted for its courageous and erudite examination of the rights of the people, the clergy, the nobles, and the king, and it pronounced in favor of a republic. Desmoulins later published *Discours de la lanterne aux Parisiens*, which referenced the excesses of the mob, earning him the nickname "Procureur-général de la lanterne." By November 1789, he transitioned into journalism with the launch of the weekly publication *Les Révolutions de France et de Brabant*.

Other figures also emerged as prominent voices. Brissot, who edited the *Patriote français* starting in 1789, became a leading spirit of the Girondists (also known as Brissotins). Upon the demolition of the Bastille, the keys were presented to him. Brissot later used his knowledge of foreign affairs as a member of the diplomatic committee of the Legislative Assembly to direct French foreign policy.

## Impact on Diplomacy and State Service
The upheaval of 1789 significantly disrupted the careers of those in the French diplomatic service. Marc Marie, Marquis de Bombelles, had served as the ambassador of France to Portugal starting in 1786. He was transferred to Vienna in early 1789, but the Revolution cut short his diplomatic career, leading to his removal from his post in September 1790. Despite this, he remained attached to Louis XVI and conducted secret missions to other sovereigns to secure aid for the king.

In contrast, Guillaume de Bonne-Carrere, a diplomat who had entered service under Vergennes, became very active during the Revolution. A friend of Dumouriez and Mirabeau, Bonne-Carrere was eventually re-established as the director-general of the department of foreign affairs in March 1792. He maintained the diplomatic habits of the old regime until December 1792, when he was sent to Belgium as an agent of the republic.

## Military and International Context
While France experienced internal revolution, other European powers were occupied elsewhere. Russia, Prussia, and Austria were involved in the final division of the "crowned republic of the east." In the Austrian military, Heinrich Joseph Johannes, Count von Bellegarde, distinguished himself as a colonel of dragoons during the Turkish War of 1788-1789.

Within France, the Revolution caused deep divisions among the military and nobility. Guy Pierre, who had served in the navy and participated in the American war of independence, emigrated because he did not accept the principles of the Revolution.

## Evolution of Revolutionary Thought
The period following 1789 saw the emergence of various political trajectories. While the early years were defined by the limited monarchy, the movement eventually shifted toward the Revolution of the Jacobins and the subsequent Jacobin republic from 1792 to 1794. This era also saw the rise of early socialist thought; Gracchus Babeuf is identified as the first to propound socialism as a practical policy, which later influenced movements in 1848 and 1871.
