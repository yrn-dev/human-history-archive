# Combustion Chamber Stays and Boiler Construction

In the construction of boilers, the structural integrity of the combustion chambers is maintained through various staying methods designed to support the flat surfaces against internal and external pressures.

## Staying of Combustion Chambers
The flat backs and sides of combustion chambers are stayed using numerous screw stays. These stays are screwed through the two plates they connect, either staying the plates to one another or connecting them to the shell of the boiler. These screw stays are nearly always fitted with nuts on the inside of the combustion chambers.

The tops of these chambers are typically supported by strong girders that rest upon the chamber back plates and the tube plates. In some instances, vertical stays are used instead, which are attached to T bars riveted directly to the boiler shell. Additionally, certain boilers utilize heavy transverse girder plates to strengthen the chamber tops.

## Additional Boiler Staying and Strengthening
Beyond the combustion chambers, other areas of the boiler require specific staying:
*   **End Plates:** The plates at the end of the boiler, located in the steam space and below the combustion chambers, are supported by longitudinal stays. These pass through the entire length of the boiler and are secured at each end by double nuts.
*   **Tube Plates:** These are strengthened through the use of stay tubes that are screwed into them.

## Tube Specifications and Draught
The dimensions of the tubes within the boiler vary based on the type of draught employed:
*   **Natural or Chimney Draught:** Tubes generally have an outside diameter of 3 or 3-1/4 inches and are rarely longer than 7 feet.
*   **Forced Draught:** Tubes are usually 2-1/2 inches in diameter and range from 8 to 8-1/2 feet in length.

Regardless of the tube size, a clear space of 1-1/4 inches is almost always maintained between the tubes.

## Furnace and Combustion Chamber Connections
There are two primary methods for connecting furnaces to combustion chambers:
1.  **Standard Riveted Connection:** The furnace is riveted to the side plate (or "wrapper") of the combustion chamber, with the crown portion flanged for riveting to the tube plate.
2.  **Gourlay Back End:** In this design, the furnace end is contracted into an oval conical form and flanged outwards around its entire circumference. The furnace is then riveted to the tube plate, which extends to the bottom of the combustion chamber. This design allows a damaged furnace to be replaced without disturbing the end plates. However, the Gourlay back end is less stiff than the standard design and requires a greater number of longitudinal stays within the boiler.

## Vertical Boiler Variations
In installations where floor space is limited, vertical cylindrical boilers are often used. 
*   **Simple Vertical Boiler:** Features a plain vertical fire-box (slightly tapering in diameter) and an internal smoke stack. Except in small sizes, these include cross tubes (approximately 9 inches in diameter) riveted to the fire-box plates and inclined to help water circulation.
*   **Tyne Boiler:** Utilizes a hemispherical furnace where combustion products enter an upper combustion chamber. This chamber is traversed by several vertical water-tubes and four or more inclined water-tubes of about 9 inches in diameter.
*   **Victoria Boiler:** Also employs a hemispherical furnace, but the gases are led to an internal combustion chamber and then through horizontal smoke-tubes to a side-mounted smoke-box.
*   **Cochran Boiler:** This variation features a "dry" back for the combustion chamber. Instead of water space at the rear, it uses firebrick-lined doors to provide easy access to the tube ends.
