# Sir Bartle Frere

Sir Bartle Frere was a British official and figure of note in the 19th century, serving as a governor in India and maintaining various intellectual and social connections throughout his career.

## Administration of the Bombay Presidency
Sir Bartle Frere served from 1862 to 1867. During his tenure, agricultural prosperity in the region reached its highest point. This economic peak was the result of the American Civil War, which created an enormous demand for Indian cotton in Europe. The influx of money into the country during this period led to an epidemic of speculation.

## Personal and Professional Associations
Frere was known for his friendships with several notable figures. He shared a "wholehearted chivalrous devotion" with Col. H. Yule, who regarded Frere as one of his few close friends in later days. Yule's academic work also intersected with Frere; for instance, Yule's translation of the *Travels of Friar Jordanus* included a dedication written to Sir Bartle Frere during a halt in Genoa.

Frere also maintained a relationship with the Agha Khan. In 1875, Frere wrote of the Agha Khan, noting that he kept his court in a "grand and noble style" similar to his ancestor from the time of Marco Polo. Frere observed that the Agha Khan's sons, known as "The Persian Princes," were active sportsmen and that the Agha Khan himself continued to enjoy horse-racing, utilizing the skill of English jockeys and trainers regardless of race or religion. Frere further noted that the Agha Khan was often among the first to receive news of disturbances or war in Persia or Central Asia, frequently visiting government officials to offer his services as an experienced leader and "tried sword."

## Interests and Collections
Sir Bartle Frere possessed an interest in Persian crafts. Col. H. Yule recorded seeing a genuine Kermán carpet in Frere's house. This specific carpet was characterized by a very dense and even short pile, featuring a design of floral tracery, birds, and vases that resembled the illuminated frontispiece of certain Persian manuscripts.
