# Para-oxyazobenzene

Para-oxyazobenzene, also known as benzene-azo-phenol, is an organic compound with the chemical formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:N(1).C}_6\text{H}_4\text{.OH(4)}$. It is characterized as an orange-red crystalline substance with a melting point of 154°C.

## Preparation and Chemical Properties
Para-oxyazobenzene is prepared by coupling diazotized aniline with phenol within an alkaline solution. It belongs to the broader category of oxyazo compounds, which are generally produced by adding a diazonium salt solution to a cold, slightly alkaline solution of a phenol. In these reactions, the diazo group typically occupies the para position relative to the hydroxyl group; if this position is unavailable, the group enters the ortho position. The diazo group never enters the meta position directly.

## Related Oxyazo Compounds
Two other isomers of oxyazobenzene exist with different properties and methods of synthesis:

*   **Ortho-oxyazobenzene** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:N(1)C}_6\text{H}_4\text{.OH(2)}$): This compound is obtained in small quantities simultaneously with the para compound. It can be separated via distillation in a current of steam, as the ortho compound passes over with the steam. It forms orange-red needles that melt at 82.5–83°C. When reduced with zinc dust in a dilute sal-ammoniac solution, it yields aniline and ortho-aminophenol.
*   **Meta-oxyazobenzene** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:N(1)C}_6\text{H}_4\text{.OH(3)}$): Obtained in 1903 by P. Jacobson, this compound is synthesized by condensing ortho-anisidine with diazo benzene. The resulting substance is diazotized and reduced by alcohol to benzene-azo-meta-anisole, which is then hydrolyzed with aluminium chloride to produce meta-oxyazobenzene. It melts at 112–114°C and is easily reduced to a corresponding hydrazo compound.

## Theoretical Constitution
The chemical constitution of oxyazo compounds has been a subject of significant debate among chemists. Some argue they are true azophenols (type $\text{R.N}_2\text{.R}_1\text{.OH}$), while others suggest a quinonoid structure, viewing them as quinone hydrazones (type $\text{R.NH.N:R}_1\text{:O}$).

Various researchers have investigated this:
*   **Th. Zincke** observed that products from coupling a diazonium salt with $\alpha$-naphthol were identical to those produced by condensing phenyl-hydrazine with $\alpha$-naphthoquinone.
*   **K. Auwers** used physico-chemical analysis of freezing-point depressions. He found that ortho-oxyazo compounds give normal depressions while para-oxyazo compounds give abnormal depressions, leading him to conclude that para compounds are phenolic and ortho compounds are quinone hydrazones.
*   **A. Hantzsch** proposed that these compounds are pseudo-acids that possess the configuration of quinone hydrazones in their free condition, but their salts are of the normal phenolic type.
*   **J. T. Hewitt** nitrated para-oxyazobenzene with dilute nitric acid, producing benzene azo-ortho-nitrophenol. Because quinones are not attacked by dilute nitric acid, this suggested a phenolic nature. Hewitt further found that bromination of oxyazobenzenes produces a brombenzene-azo-phenol if hydrobromic acid remains in the system, but bromination occurs in the phenolic nucleus if the acid is removed (using sodium acetate). He concluded that mineral acid gives the azo compound a pseudo-quinonoid character.

## General Context of Azo Compounds
Para-oxyazobenzene is a member of the azo compounds ($\text{R.N:N.R}'$), which are intensely coloured organic substances. These compounds can be prepared through the reduction of nitro compounds in alkaline solution, the oxidation of hydrazo compounds, or the coupling of a diazotized amine with a phenolic or aminic compound. 

While azo compounds are coloured, they only function as dyestuffs if they contain basic, acid, or salt-forming groups. In the context of colour theory, the azo group ($\text{--N:N--}$) is considered a chromophorous group (a colour-bearing group) that turns a colourless hydrocarbon like benzene into a chromogen. The introduction of auxochromous groups, such as $\text{OH}$, then transforms these chromogens into dyestuffs.
