# Publications and Literary Works of 1902

The year 1902 saw the release of several significant literary, historical, and scientific publications across various fields, ranging from biographical editions and regional histories to linguistic studies and personal correspondence.

## Biographical and Literary Editions
A notable publication of 1902 was the Haworth edition of *The Life of Charlotte Brontë*, written by Mrs. Gaskell. Originally published in 1857, the work had undergone considerable changes in its third edition due to controversies regarding the identity of Lowood in *Jane Eyre* (specifically its connection to Cowan Bridge school), the relationship between Branwell Brontë and his employer's wife, and the perceived peculiarities of Mr. Brontë. The 1902 Haworth edition, issued by the original publishers, Smith, Elder & Co., is considered the most satisfactory form of the biography. This specific edition is distinguished by the inclusion of numerous letters written by Miss Brontë to her publisher, George Smith.

Additionally, 1902 marked the publication of a fuller edition of *Letters from Egypt*. Edited by Mrs. Janet Ross and featuring an introduction by George Meredith, this version provided a more complete form of the correspondence than had previously been possible.

## Regional and National Histories
In the field of regional history and geography, 1902 saw the publication of *The Rise of Bombay* by S.M. Edwardes. This work contributes to the historical record of Bombay, appearing alongside other regional studies such as James Douglas's *Bombay and Western India* (1893) and Sir William Lee-Warner's *The Presidency of Bombay* (1904).

## Linguistic and Scientific Works
The year 1902 also produced contributions to the study of language and colonial administration. Sir H. H. Johnston published *The Uganda Protectorate* (1902-1904), a work that included illustrative vocabularies. In the realm of Scottish linguistics, Gregory Smith published *Specimens of Middle Scots* in 1902.
