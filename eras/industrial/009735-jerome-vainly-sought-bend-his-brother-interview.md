# Jerome Bonaparte

## Early Life and Military Career
Jerome Bonaparte was born on November 15, 1784, in Ajaccio. During the early years of the French Revolution, he shared the fortunes of his family. He received an education at Juilly and was called to join his brother, who was then the First Consul of France, in 1800. 

Jerome's early career was marked by an affectionate but impetuous nature. While serving in the Consular Guard, he was wounded during a duel with the younger brother of General Davout. He was subsequently transferred to the navy, where he cruised in the West Indies. During this period, a British cruiser blockaded him, prompting Jerome to leave his ship and travel through the United States.

## Marriage to Elizabeth Patterson
While in Baltimore, Jerome fell in love with Miss Elizabeth Patterson and married her, despite her being a minor. This union took place on December 24, 1803. The marriage was valid under American law, and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void; however, Napoleon greatly disliked the union and refused to recognize its legality, viewing the marriage as a disregard for the laws of France and military discipline.

When Jerome brought his wife to Europe in 1805, the emperor ordered her to be excluded from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but he vainly sought to bend his brother's will. Consequently, Jerome was forced by Napoleon to separate from his wife. Madame Patterson (or Madame Bonaparte) stayed in England for a period before returning to Baltimore, where she eventually died in 1879.

## Military Commands and Political Rise
In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. His wife proceeded to Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870). In November of that year, Jerome sailed with a squadron under Admiral Willaumez intended to ravage the West Indies, though the fleet was scattered by a storm. After damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic, Jerome returned to France in August 1806.

Napoleon subsequently named Jerome a prince of France and placed him in command of a division of South Germans during the 1806 campaign. Following the events at Jena, Jerome received the surrender of several Prussian towns.

## Kingdom of Westphalia
Following an imperial decree that annulled his marriage to Elizabeth Patterson, the emperor united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia.

During his reign in Westphalia, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor. He was known for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for running deeply into debt. Despite these personal tendencies, the kingdom saw some benefits from its connection to France: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, education received an impulse in its higher departments, and repressive laws against the Israelites were removed.

## Descendants in America
The union between Jerome and Elizabeth Patterson produced a branch of the family known as the Bonapartes of Baltimore. Their only child, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870), was born in England but lived primarily in Baltimore. He was noted for having a marked resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. Jerome remained on good terms with his son, occasionally meeting him and providing him with a large allowance for some time. This Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte had an elder son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832-1893), who served in the French army in Italy and the Crimea.
