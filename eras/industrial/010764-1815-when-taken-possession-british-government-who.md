# British Territorial Acquisitions and Global Position (1815)

The year 1815 marked a significant period of territorial transition and geopolitical consolidation for the British government, following the defeat of Napoleon and the general pacification of Europe. During this time, Great Britain expanded its influence through the acquisition of strategic islands and the formalization of protectorates.

## Possession of Ascension Island
Following the arrival of Napoleon at St Helena in 1815, the British government took possession of Ascension Island. Prior to this, the island had remained uninhabited since its discovery in 1501 by the Portuguese navigator Joao da Nova. Upon taking possession, the British government dispatched a small garrison to the island. 

A settlement known as George Town (referred to locally as Garrison) was established on the north-west coast. The settlement relied on "Dampier's" springs, located in the Green Mountain six miles away, which had been discovered in 1701 by William Dampier after he was wrecked on the coast. Under the rule of the admiralty, the island served primarily as a sanatorium and maintained a depot of stores for the navy. It was governed by a naval captain listed on the books of the flagship of the admiral superintendent at Gibraltar. The population of approximately 300 people consisted of marines, seamen, and Krumen from Liberia.

## The Ionian Islands and Corfu
Under the treaty of Paris dated November 5, 1815, the Ionian Islands were placed under the protectorate of Great Britain. As part of this arrangement, Corfu became the seat of the British high commissioner. Although a native assembly and senate were retained, the British commissioners acted as practical autocrats. They implemented a strict method of government that resulted in a decided improvement in the island's material prosperity, though the strictness of these measures displeased the native population. Corfu remained under this administration until 1864, when it and the other Ionian Islands were ceded to the kingdom of Greece.

## The British Empire in 1815
By 1815, the British Empire comprised a diverse array of global territories:
*   **North America:** The coastal river and lake regions of Canada, which were thinly populated, and a vast wilderness hinterland containing fur-trading stations operated by the Hudson Bay Company.
*   **South America and the Caribbean:** British Guiana in South America, Jamaica, and several minor slave-labour possessions in the West Indies.
*   **Africa:** The coast districts of the Cape of Good Hope (inhabited by blacks and rebellious-spirited Dutch settlers) and several trading stations on the coast of West Africa.
*   **Asia:** Approximately one-third of the Indian peninsula, governed by the East India Company.
*   **Europe:** The rock of Gibraltar and the island of Malta.
*   **Oceania:** Two convict dumps located in Tasmania and at Botany Bay in Australia.

During this period, the East India Company was the only entity showing a spirit of expansion.

## Other 1815 Geopolitical Events
The year 1815 was defined by the final defeat of Napoleon at Waterloo, where the British under Wellington and the Prussians under Blücher secured a victory that left Napoleon without support. Following this, Napoleon was treated as a prisoner by the British and transported from Plymouth to the tropical island of St Helena. Additionally, in the settlement of 1815, the island of Corsica—which had been briefly occupied by Great Britain in 1814—was restored to the French crown.

In other regions, 1815 saw the city of Cartagena taken by Bolivar, though it was surrendered to royalists within the same year. In Bikanir, the tyranny of Maharaja Surat Singh led to a general rising of his thakurs in 1815. While Surat Singh had previously applied for British protection in 1802 and again in 1816, the British government had initially refused.
