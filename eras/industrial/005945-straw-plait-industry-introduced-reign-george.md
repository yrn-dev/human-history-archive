# The Straw-Plait Industry

The straw-plait industry, involving the manufacture of straw-plait and related straw goods, has been a significant economic activity across various regions of Europe and the Atlantic islands, often intersecting with agricultural pursuits.

## Development in England

In England, the straw-plait industry established strongholds in the counties of Buckinghamshire and Bedfordshire. In Buckinghamshire, the industry was introduced during the reign of George I and previously provided employment for a large portion of the population. Local straw-plaiting continues to be carried on in the county, particularly in the district around Aylesbury, a market town noted for its agricultural trade and the rearing of ducks.

In Bedfordshire, the town of Luton serves as the principal seat of straw-plait manufacture in England. The industry in Luton originated with a colony of straw-plaiters who were transplanted from Scotland by James I; these individuals had previously been brought from Lorraine by Queen Mary. Luton has exported large quantities of straw goods and hats, although the industry has suffered in recent years due to increased competition from foreign sources. Additionally, the market town of Biggleswade in Bedfordshire was formerly engaged in straw-plaiting as well as lace manufacture.

## European and Atlantic Production

Beyond England, the manufacture of straw-plait and straw hats is a chief occupation in certain Italian regions, ranking just after pastoral pursuits and agriculture. This industry exists alongside other activities such as the rearing of cocoons and silkworms, silk industries, and the manufacture of terra-cotta and bricks.

In France, the city of Grenoble produces straw hats in addition to carved furniture, liqueurs, artificial cements, and kid gloves.

In the Azores, straw production varies by district. In the district of Ponta Delgada, straw hats are produced. In the district of Horta, the population creates various ornamental articles, mats, and baskets using straw, osier, and the pith of dried fig-wood.

## Materials and Applications

While straw is a primary material for plaiting, it is often used in conjunction with other substances. Since 1880, plaited straw—which is frequently coloured—has been used alongside enamelled skains of cane and the central pith of *Calamus viminalis* (known as cane-pith or cane-pulp) to manufacture finer classes of work and furniture. This development in basket-work has encroached upon the field of cabinet-making, leading to the construction of tables, chairs, and other furniture using a combination of wood, nails, and plaited materials.
