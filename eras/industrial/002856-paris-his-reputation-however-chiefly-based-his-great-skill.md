# Paris

Paris is a major center of art, medicine, diplomacy, and publishing, serving as a residence for international diplomats and a site for the education of specialists in various fields.

## Arts and Architecture
The city has been a destination for artists and architects seeking professional development and fame. Louis Pierre Baltard (1764–1846), born in Paris, originally practiced as a landscape painter. After traveling through Italy and becoming impressed by Italian buildings, he transitioned to architecture. Baltard achieved significant success in this field and was chosen to create plans for several of the largest public edifices in Paris. However, he is most renowned for his skill as an engraver. His notable works include portrait engravings and the two-volume folio *Paris et ses monuments* (1803), which contains drawings of the city.

Similarly, the Italian sculptor Lorenzo Bartolini traveled to Paris in 1797. While there, he studied sculpture under F. F. Lemot and painting under Desmarets. Bartolini established his reputation as a sculptor in 1803 after winning the second prize of the Academy for his bas-relief "Cleobis and Biton," an achievement that attracted numerous influential patrons.

## Medicine and Science
Paris served as a hub for medical advancement through institutions such as the Ecole de Medecine. Guillaume, Baron Dupuytren (1777–1835), a French surgeon and anatomist, studied medicine at this newly established school. He entered the professional field early, being appointed prosector via competition at the age of eighteen. Dupuytren's career was centered in Paris, where he served as a professor of operative surgery starting in 1811 and later as the head surgeon and chair of clinical surgery at the Hotel-Dieu. He was noted for his dexterity and resourcefulness in surgery and authored a *Treatise on Artificial Anus*.

## Diplomacy and Political History
Paris has frequently been a focal point for international diplomatic relations and political transitions. During the 19th century, it served as a residence for diplomats such as Bulwer, who regarded the city as his "choicest place of residence" and spent time on its boulevards. In June 1839, Bulwer was dispatched to Paris as secretary of embassy. Between 1839 and 1840, he served as the *ad interim* charge d'affaires at the court of France during a period of tension between England and France regarding the feud between the Porte and Mehemet Ali.

The city was also the site of significant political settlements following the Napoleonic era. After the Battle of Waterloo, Bignon served as the minister of foreign affairs under the executive commission and signed the convention of July 3, 1815, which resulted in Paris being handed over to the allies.

## Publishing and Literature
Paris has been a center for the publication of historical and political texts. Various works concerning Louis XVIII were published in the city, including the *Mémoires de Louis XVIII* (1832–1833), compiled by Lamothe-Langon, and the *Journal de Marie-Thérèse de France, duchesse d'Angoulême* (1896). Other Parisian publications include the *Lettres d'Artwell* (1830) and the works of E. Daudet, such as *La Terreur Blanche* (1878) and *Hist. de la restauration 1814-1830* (1882). Additionally, the city saw the publication of Bignon's critiques of the continental allies, including *Du Congres de Troppau* (1821) and *Les Cabinets el les peuples depuis 1815 jusqu'a la fin de 1822* (1822).
