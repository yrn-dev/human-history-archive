# Louis Gabriel Ambroise Bonald

Louis Gabriel Ambroise, Vicomte de Bonald (1754–1840), was a French politician and philosopher known for his extreme conservative views and his involvement in the French government during the early 19th century.

## Early Life and Emigration
Bonald was born on October 2, 1754, at Le Monna, located near Millau in Aveyron. Due to his dislike of the principles associated with the Revolution, he emigrated from France in 1791. During this period, he joined the army of the prince of Conde and eventually settled in Heidelberg.

## Philosophical Works and Return to France
While in Heidelberg, Bonald authored his first significant work, *Theorie du pouvoir politique et religieux*. Published in three volumes in 1796 (with a new two-volume edition released in Paris in 1854), this highly conservative text was condemned by the Directory. Upon his return to France, Bonald was viewed as an object of suspicion, which forced him to live in retirement.

His professional associations resumed in 1806 when he joined Fievee and Chateaubriand in the conduct of the *Mercure de France*. Two years later, he was appointed as a councillor of the Imperial University, an institution he had frequently attacked.

## Political Career and Censorship
Following the restoration, Bonald served as a member of the council of public instruction. Between 1815 and 1822, he sat in the chamber as a deputy. During his tenure in the chamber, his speeches were characterized by extreme conservatism, and he went so far as to advocate for the implementation of literary censorship.

In 1822, Bonald was appointed minister of state. In this capacity, he presided over the censorship commission. His political ascent continued the following year when he was made a peer. However, he lost this dignity in 1830 after refusing to take the oath.

## Later Life and Death
Bonald was a member of the Academy starting in 1816. After 1830, he ceased participating in public affairs and retired to his seat at Le Monna. He died there on November 23, 1840.
