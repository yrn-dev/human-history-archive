# EXAF

In the study of braced structures and frames, EXAF refers to a specific polygon of forces that serves as the reciprocal figure for the lines meeting at a particular point within a frame. This geometric construction is used to determine the direction and magnitude of forces acting upon the members of a structure.

## Theory of Reciprocal Figures
The concept of reciprocal figures was defined by J. Clerk Maxwell in 1864. According to this definition, two plane figures are considered reciprocal when they contain an equal number of lines, such that corresponding lines in both figures are parallel. Furthermore, lines that converge to a single point in one figure must form a closed polygon in the other.

When applied to a frame without redundant members and the external forces maintaining its equilibrium, one plane figure represents the frame and its external forces, while the reciprocal figure represents the stresses on each member. Specifically, the lines in the reciprocal figure indicate the direction and magnitude of the forces between the joints of the frame.

## Construction of the Polygon EXAF
The polygon EXAF is developed through a graphic method of finding stresses in braced structures. This process begins by analyzing the points of support and the loads applied to the frame. For example, at a point of support, two members are sufficient to balance the reaction. By drawing triangles where one side is the reaction (such as YX) and the other two sides are parallel to the members of the frame, a polygon of forces in equilibrium is created.

When passing to the point XEFA in a frame, the analysis incorporates known forces, such as a downward load (XA) and a push from a strut (XE). Because a strut in compression pushes at both ends, its force is represented as an upward thrust (EX) in the reciprocal diagram. To complete the polygon of forces EXAF:
1. The process begins with the upward thrust EX.
2. It continues downward with the load XA.
3. A line AF is drawn parallel to the member AF in the frame.
4. The polygon is completed by drawing a line EF parallel to the member EF in the frame.

The point F is established by the intersection of the line beginning at A and the line beginning at E. The resulting closed polygon, EXAF, is the reciprocal figure of the lines meeting at that point in the frame and represents the forces acting at the point EXAF.

## Application to Frame Analysis
The use of reciprocal figures provides a simple method for computing stresses on the members of a frame. By determining the direction of forces—such as those on EH and XA—the nature of the member's function is revealed. For instance, if a force acts away from a point, the member acts as a tie (pulling); if it acts toward the point, the member acts as a strut (pushing).

This method is often applied to structures such as the Warren girder, a common form of bridge truss. In such cases, external forces are represented by lines outside the frame, and the polygon of external forces is constructed such that its sides are arranged in the same order as the joints on which the forces act. This ensures that passing around the polygon corresponds to the direction of the forces. While the method of reciprocal figures is effective, other techniques, such as the method of sections or the method of influence lines, are sometimes more convenient for dealing with braced girders.
