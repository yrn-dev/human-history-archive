# Sir John Barrow

## Early Life and Education
Sir John Barrow (1764–1848) was an English statesman born on June 19, 1764, in the village of Dragley Beck, located in the parish of Ulverston in Lancashire. His early professional career began in Liverpool, where he served as the superintending clerk of an iron foundry. Following this role, he transitioned into education, teaching mathematics at a school in Greenwich.

## Embassy to China
Barrow's career shifted toward diplomacy through the interest of Sir George Staunton, to whose son Barrow had taught mathematics. This connection led to his appointment to the first British embassy to China. During this mission, he served as the comptroller of the household to Lord Macartney. 

While attached to the embassy, Barrow acquired a proficient knowledge of the Chinese language. This expertise allowed him to contribute several interesting articles regarding China to the *Quarterly Review*. Additionally, the account of the embassy authored and published by Sir George Staunton documents many of Barrow's valuable contributions to science and literature related to China.

## Literary Works and Publications
Sir John Barrow contributed to the historical record of British diplomacy in Asia. In 1807, he published *The Life and Writings of Lord Macartney*. His work complements other accounts of the same period, such as the account of Macartney's embassy to China published by Sir George Staunton in 1797.
