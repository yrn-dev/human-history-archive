# Malzbereitung und Bierfabrikation (Leipzig, 1898) and E. Michel, Lehrbuch

## Overview of Brewing Literature
The study of brewing and malting is supported by a variety of technical texts and journals. Among the recognized works in this field is *Die Theorie und Praxis der Malzbereitung und Bierfabrikation*, published in Leipzig in 1898 by J.E. Thausing. Additionally, E. Michel authored a textbook on the subject titled *Lehrbuch der Bierbrauerei*, which was published in Augsburg in 1900.

## Related Technical Works
Other significant contributions to the science of brewing and malting include:

*   *A Text-book of the Science of Brewing* by Moritz and Morris (London, 1891).
*   *A Handy Book for Brewers* by H.E. Wright (London, 1897).
*   *Brewing and Malting* by Frank Thatcher (London, 1898).
*   *The Brewing Industry* by Julian L. Baker (London, 1905).
*   *Grundriss der Bierbrauerei* by E.J. Lintner (Berlin, 1904).
*   *Chemie u. Physiologie des Malzes und des Bieres* by E. Prior (Leipzig, 1896).

## Professional Journals
The industry is further documented through several technical journals. In London, these include *The Journal of the Institute of Brewing*, *The Brewing Trade Review*, and *The Brewers' Journal*. A corresponding *Brewers' Journal* is also published in New York. German-language publications include the *Wochenschrift für Brauerei* based in Berlin and the *Zeitschrift für das gesammte Brauwesen* based in Munich.

## Production Standards and Regulations
Historical data regarding brewing standards indicates that in 1896, classifications were set at 28 lb. However, starting in 1897, the standard was adjusted to a rate of 32 lb to the bushel. 

In terms of ingredients and taxation, some measurements are inclusive of maize and rice, while others are exclusive of these materials. Regarding the physical infrastructure of production, it is noted that, as a general rule, there is no separate "collecting vessel" used, as duty is instead assessed within the fermenting vessels.
