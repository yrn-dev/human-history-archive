# Christian Treasury, the Presbyterian Review and the Quarterly

## The Editorial Work of Horatius Bonar
The *Christian Treasury*, the *Presbyterian Review*, and the *Quarterly Journal of Prophecy* are journals that were edited by Horatius Bonar. Bonar (1808–1889) was a Scottish Presbyterian divine born in Edinburgh. His education took place at the university and high school of his native city. 

Bonar's professional career included a period of mission work at Leith before he was appointed as the parish minister of Kelso in 1837. Following the Disruption of 1843, he became a minister of the newly formed Free Church, a position he held until 1866, when he transitioned to the Chalmers memorial church in Edinburgh. In 1853, he was awarded a D.D. degree from Aberdeen University, and he later served as the moderator of the general assembly of his church in 1883. While he was a prolific author of religious literature and editor of the aforementioned journals, his most noted contributions were in the field of hymnology. Between 1857 and 1866, he published three series titled *Hymns of Faith and Hope* (with a new edition released in 1886). His hymns, such as "I heard the voice of Jesus say," "Go, labour on," "When the weary, seeking rest," and "Here, O my Lord, I see Thee face to face," appear in nearly every modern hymnal.

## The Quarterly Review and Related Periodicals
The *Quarterly Review* is a publication that has featured a wide array of critical and biographical content. Its archives include:
* **Literary and Artistic Criticism:** The journal published a critique by Roger Fry titled "A Genre Painter and his Critics" in April 1908, regarding the *Life and Works of Vittorio Carpaccio*. It also featured articles on the author Rudyard Kipling in July 1892, and pieces concerning the novelist and dramatist John Lyly, including a work by H. Morley on "Euphuism" (1861) and an article by R. W. Bond titled "John Lyly, Novelist and Dramatist" (January 1896).
* **Biographical and General Interest:** The *Quarterly Review* published an article by Yule regarding Lord Lawrence in April 1883. Other entries include a January 1898 piece regarding the Villiers line and excerpts from a Common place Book.
* **Historical and Religious Notes:** The publication has been cited in relation to historical accounts of sacrifices during the pilgrimage to Mecca by the Khalif Mokteder in the year of the Hejira 350.

## Other Presbyterian and Quarterly Publications
Beyond the journals edited by Bonar, other specialized reviews appear in the archive's records. *The Presbyterian and Reformed Review* (Philadelphia) published articles by John D. Davis in 1900 (vol. xi, pp. 377-396). 

Additionally, the *Jewish Quarterly Review* has been a source for scholarly analysis on religious and historical texts. In 1893, Kohler used the *Jewish Quarterly Review* to demonstrate that the book "The Rest of the Words of Baruch" (also known in the Abyssinian Church as "the Rest of the Words of Jeremiah") was originally written by a Jew but later revised by a Christian. The *Jewish Quarterly Review* is also cited in discussions regarding the Maranos—Jewish Christians in Spain who were converted to Roman Catholicism under compulsion.
