# Hop Back

The hop back is a specialized vessel used in the brewing process to separate insoluble hop constituents from the wort. It serves as a critical transition point between the boiling stage and the cooling stage of beer production.

## Function and Placement in Brewing
Brewing is comprised of seven main processes: grinding, mashing, boiling, cooling, fermenting, cleansing, and racking and storing. The hop back is utilized immediately following the boiling process. After the clear wort has been boiled with hops in the copper, it is turned into the hop back to settle. The primary purpose of this stage is to ensure that the insoluble constituents of the hops are separated from the wort before the liquid proceeds to the cooler or refrigerator.

## Design and Operation
A hop back is constructed as a vessel made of either metal or wood. Its defining structural feature is a false bottom consisting of perforated plates. These plates act as a filter, retaining the spent hops while allowing the "bright wort" to be drawn off. After the wort has rested for a brief period within the hop back, it is run into the coolers.

## Integration with Cooling Systems
Following its time in the hop back, the wort typically enters a cooler, which is a shallow vessel with a large surface area. This exposure to a large volume of air causes other substances and hop constituents in the wort to precipitate and become insoluble. 

However, modern brewing practices have seen variations in this sequence. Because the exposure of hot wort in a cooler carries a significant risk of wild yeast and bacterial infection, some breweries now bypass the cooler entirely, running the wort directly from the hop back to the refrigerator. To mitigate infection risks while maintaining the benefits of aeration, some suggest placing the cooler in an air-tight chamber with sterilized and purified air. In the United States, cooling tanks—enclosed vessels capable of artificial aeration—are frequently used as replacements for the cooler.

Regardless of whether a cooler is used, the wort cannot reach the necessary temperature for fermentation through these methods alone; therefore, the wort must eventually pass through a refrigerator, where it trickles over pipes containing circulating cold water.

## Subsequent Processing
Once the wort has passed through the hop back and the cooling stages, it is moved to a collecting vessel. This step is specifically designed to allow revenue officers to assess the duty. Finally, the wort is transferred to fermenting vessels, where it is transformed into "green" beer before being cleansed, racked, and stored.
