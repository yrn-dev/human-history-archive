# Secondary Roads

In the context of global infrastructure and engineering, secondary roads vary significantly in their classification, construction, and physical condition depending on the region and the standards applied by local authorities.

## Engineering Standards and Load Capacities
In Switzerland, roads are categorized into three distinct classes based on their importance to determine the strength required for bridge design. Designers must ensure that bridges can support either a uniformly distributed crowd or a heavy wagon positioned anywhere on the roadway. For "Secondary Roads," the prescribed load for a crowd is 72 lb per sq. ft., and the prescribed load for a wagon is 6 tons per axle with a 10 ft. wheel base. 

These standards differ from "Main Roads," which require a crowd load of 92 lb per sq. ft. and a wagon load of 10 tons per axle with a 13 ft. wheel base, and "Other Roads," which require 51 lb per sq. ft. for crowds and 3 tons per axle with an 8 ft. wheel base. In England, provisions are made for even larger loads, such as traction engines with boiler trolleys or trucks loaded with granite.

## Regional Conditions in Korea
In Korea, the state of internal communications has historically been poor. While main roads centering in Seoul are often unfit for the passage of ox-carts, the secondary roads are described as bad bridle-tracks. These tracks frequently degenerate into "rock ladders." 

Due to the poor condition of these roads and the risks associated with the "bridgeless season," trade is greatly hampered. Bridges in this region are constructed from posts with a framework covered in earth, pine branches, or timber; these are removed at the start of the rainy season and are not replaced for three months. Consequently, the inland transit of goods relies heavily on animals and people:
* Bulls carrying 450 to 600 lb.
* Ponies carrying 200 lb.
* Men carrying 100 to 150 lb.

This method of transport results in an average cost of slightly over 8d. per mile per ton. Some improvements to these communications have been implemented under Japanese direction.

## Materials and Maintenance
Certain types of igneous rocks, specifically dolerites, are commonly used for kerbstones and road-mending. Dolerites are widely distributed wherever basalts occur. However, they are considered unsuitable for high-quality architectural work because of their dark color and a tendency to weather with a dingy brown crust.
