# Bangor

The name Bangor refers to several distinct geographical locations, including cities in the United States and Wales, a commune in France, and historical ecclesiastical institutions.

## Bangor, Maine
Bangor is a city, port of entry, and the county-seat of Penobscot county in Maine, USA. It is situated at the head of navigation on the Penobscot river, approximately 60 miles from the ocean and 75 miles northeast of Augusta, at the confluence of the Penobscot river and the Kenduskeag stream. The city serves as a trade center for an extensive agricultural district and is one of the largest lumber depots in the United States, also shipping significant quantities of ice.

### History and Development
Some antiquarians have identified Bangor as the site of the mythical city of Norumbega; reports from 1656 indicated that a French-built Fort Norombega stood there. However, the city's authentic history began in 1769 with the arrival of the first settlers. The settlement was initially called Conduskeag and was briefly known locally as Sunbury. It was incorporated as a town in 1791. Under the influence of the first pastor, Rev. Seth Noble, the name was changed to Bangor, after one of his favorite hymn-tunes.

During the war of 1812, a British force occupied Bangor for several days in September 1814, during which time they destroyed cargoes and vessels. Bangor was later chartered as a city in 1834.

### Infrastructure and Institutions
Bangor has been a pioneer in transportation within the state; the first railway in Maine was completed in 1836, running from Bangor to Old Town. In 1889, the city established the first electric street-railway in Maine. The city is served by the Eastern Steamship line and the Maine Central railway, the latter of which connects with the Washington County railway and the Bangor & Aroostook railway (which maintains its general offices in the city). A bridge approximately 1,300 feet long connects Bangor with Brewer.

The city is the seat of several state institutions: the law school of the University of Maine, the Eastern Maine insane hospital, and the Eastern Maine general hospital. It is also home to the Bangor Theological Seminary (Congregational), which was incorporated in 1814, opened at Hampden in 1816, moved to Bangor in 1819, and was empowered to confer degrees in divinity in 1905. Other city features include a public library, several public parks, the city hall, the opera house, the Federal building, and the county court house. Various charitable institutions operate here, including homes for aged men, aged women, a children's home, and a deaconesses' home. The city also hosts the Eastern Maine State Fair and the annual Eastern Maine Music Festival in October.

## Bangor, Wales
Bangor (formerly Bangor Fawr) is a seaport, market-town, and city in Carnarvonshire, North Wales, located 240 miles northwest of London. It serves as a municipal (since 1883) and contributory parliamentary borough within the Carnarvon district. The city is divided into Upper and Lower Bangor, with the Lower portion consisting practically of one street. Situated near the northern entrance of the Menai Straits, it is a destination for many visitors.

The city's architecture includes a small cathedral, a deanery, a small Roman Catholic church, and a disused bishop's palace. The cruciform cathedral stands on the site of a church founded around 525 by St Deiniol, which the English destroyed in 1071. The cathedral suffered various damages over the centuries: it was severely handled in 1102 and 1212, destroyed in 1211, and burned during the time of Owen Glendower. It was rebuilt in parts by Bishop Skevyngton (who added the nave and tower in 1532) and Bishop Dean (who rebuilt the choir during the time of Henry VII). Sir G. Scott restored the cathedral between 1866 and 1875.

Educational institutions in the city include the University College of N. Wales (established 1883) with a female students' hall, as well as Normal, Baptist, Independent, and N. Wales Training Colleges. The region's staple export is slate from Lord Penrhyn's quarries at Bethesda, shipped via Port Penrhyn.

## Other Locations and Terms
### Belle-Ile-en-Mer
Bangor is one of the four communes on Belle-Ile-en-Mer, an island off the west coast of France in the department of Morbihan. The island is a treeless plateau with a rugged coast and a mild climate.

### Ecclesiastical and Historical Terms
In the *Myvyrian Archaeology*, the term *bangor* refers to college institutions. Three principal *bangor* are listed: that of Emrys (Ambrosius) at Caer Caradawg, that of Illtud Farchawg at Caer Worgorn (Wroxeter), and the *bangor wydrin* (glass) in the glass isle, Afallach. A fourth, *bangor Illtud* (or Llanilltud), is also mentioned. In the first three, 420 saints succeeded each other by hundreds in pious offices.

Additionally, "Bangor" refers to a specific liturgical use (the use of Bangor) regarding the censing of the altar and relics during religious services, distinct from the uses of Sarum, York, and Hereford.
