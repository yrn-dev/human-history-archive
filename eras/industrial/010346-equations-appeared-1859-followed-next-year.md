# Boole's Mathematical Treatises

## Overview of Boole's Systematic Works
During his lifetime, Boole completed only two systematic treatises on mathematical subjects. The first of these, the _Treatise on Differential Equations_, was published in 1859. This work was followed the next year by a second publication, the _Treatise on the Calculus of Finite Differences_, which was intended to serve as a sequel to the first. These two works are considered valuable contributions to their respective branches of mathematics.

## Methodology and Philosophy
In composing these treatises, Boole combined profound investigation into the philosophy of the subject with elementary exposition. These works embody many of his most important discoveries. A primary characteristic of Boole's approach was his perfect confidence in results obtained by treating symbols in accordance with their primary conditions and laws, as well as an unrivalled power and skill in tracing those results.

Boole was among the most eminent mathematicians to recognize that symbols of operation could be treated as distinct objects of calculation, separate from symbols of quantity.

## The General Symbolic Method
The _Treatise on Differential Equations_ contains a lucid account of the general symbolic method in its 16th and 17th chapters. The bold and skilful application of this method led to Boole's chief discoveries. Additionally, this treatise includes a general method in analysis that Boole had originally described in a famous memoir printed in the _Philosophical Transactions_ for 1844.
