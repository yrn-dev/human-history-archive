# Vermont and Portsmouth (1801-1807)

## Religious Activity and Hosea Ballou
During the years 1801 to 1807, Hosea Ballou, an American Universalist clergyman, preached in Barnard, Vermont, as well as in the towns surrounding it. Ballou was born in Richmond, New Hampshire, in 1771 and was the son of a Baptist minister named Maturin Ballou. A self-educated man, Ballou converted to Universalism in 1789 and had previously served as a pastor in Dana, Massachusetts, starting in 1794.

Following his time in Vermont and the surrounding areas, Ballou moved his ministry to Portsmouth, New Hampshire, where he preached from 1807 to 1815. Ballou is recognized as a principal American expositor of Universalism, having divested the faith of trinitarian views, legalism, and any traces of Calvinism. His literary contributions during and after this era include *Notes on the Parables* (1804) and *A Treatise on Atonement* (1805).

## Natural Resources of Vermont
Vermont is noted for its extensive deposits of marble, which are found in several distinct varieties across the state:

*   **Statuary Marble:** A beautiful material suitable for statuary work is quarried near Rutland.
*   **Variegated and Coloured Marbles:** Some of the most beautiful variegated marbles are located in the northern part of the state, specifically in the neighbourhood of Lake Champlain.
*   **Black Marble:** Shoreham is cited as one of the principal localities for the production of black marble.

By 1908, Vermont was the leading state in the U.S. in terms of the value of marble produced.

## Regional Geography and Settlements
The archive provides details on several key locations within the region:

**Burlington**
Located in the northwestern part of Vermont on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain, Burlington serves as the county-seat of Chittenden county. It is the largest city in the state and is situated 300 miles north of New York and 90 miles southeast of Montreal. The city is built on a strip of land extending approximately six miles south from the mouth of the Winooski river. Burlington is the site of the University of Vermont, which was founded in 1791.

**Bennington**
Situated in the southwestern part of the state, Bennington is a village and county-seat of Bennington county, located about 30 miles east-northeast of Troy, New York. Settled in 1761 and organized as a township in 1762, it was named for Governor Benning Wentworth of New Hampshire. Bennington was a center for the "Green Mountain Boys," led by Seth Warner and Ethan Allen, during a period when both New York and New Hampshire claimed jurisdiction over the "New Hampshire Grant" towns. The area is also the site of the Bennington Battle Monument, which commemorates a victory on August 16, 1777, where forces including New Hampshire and Massachusetts militia, under General John Stark, defeated detachments of General Burgoyne's army.
