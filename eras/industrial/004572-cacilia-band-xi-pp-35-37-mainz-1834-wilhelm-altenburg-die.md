# Musical Instruments and Literature of the 19th Century

The 19th century saw significant developments in the study and construction of wind instruments, as well as the publication of various technical manuals and historical analyses. Much of this documentation was preserved in specialized journals and treatises focusing on the evolution of instruments such as the clarinet, basset horn, and bassoon.

## The Clarinet and Basset Horn
Research into the clarinet and its related instruments is documented across several key texts. Gottfried Weber contributed to the journal *Cacilia* (Mainz, 1834), specifically in Band xi, pp. 35-37, where he wrote "Ueber Clarinette und Bassethorn." Further technical and historical analysis is found in the work of Wilhelm Altenburg, titled *Die Clarinette, ihre Entstehung und Entwickelung bis zur Jetztzeit in akustischer, technischer u. musikalischer Beziehung* (Heilbronn, 1904), specifically pages 16-32, as well as his subsequent work *Die Klarinette* (Heilbronn, 1904-1905).

Instructional guides from the period include J. G. H. Backofen's *Anweisung zur Klarinette, nebst einer kurzen Abhandlung ueber das Basset-Horn* (Leipzig, 1803) and Iwan Mueller's *Anweisung zu der neuen Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto, nebst einigen Bemerkungen fuer Instrumentenmacher* (Leipzig, 1826). Both of these works featured illustrations. Additionally, the *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments at the Royal Military Exhibition, London* (1890), compiled by Capt. C. R. Day, contains heliogravures of early basset horns. One such instrument, stamped "H. Grenser, S. Wiesner, Dresden," is held in the collection of the Rev. F. W. Galpin of Hatfield, Broad Oak.

## The Bassoon
The bassoon was utilized by many great masters, though it was an exception for Handel. Beethoven frequently employed the instrument in his symphonies, creating original and beautiful independent parts. Bach included parts for two bassoons in his mass in B min., while Mozart composed a concerto in Bb for bassoon with orchestra (Kochel, No. 191). Weber also composed a concerto for bassoon in F (op. 75) for full orchestra.

Technical improvements to the instrument were noted by Gottfried Weber in "Verbesserungen des Fagotts," published in *Cacilia* (Mainz, 1825), vol. ii. Furthermore, the wind-instrument-maker W. Heckel of Biebrich-am-Rhein, acting on the instigation of Wagner, produced bassoons with an additional key that extended the instrument's compass downwards to A.

Educational resources for the bassoon include:
* Etienne Ozi's *Nouvelle Methode du Bassoon* (Paris, 1788 and 1800).
* J. B. J. Willent-Bordogny's *Gran Methodo completo per il Fagotto* (Milan, 1844), which includes illustrations of early bassoons.
* Joseph Froehlich's *Vollstaendige Musikschule fuer alle beym Orchester gebrauchliche wichtigere Instrumente* (Cologne, Bonn, 1811), which provides practical illustrations.

## Related Musical and Historical Studies
Other musical studies from the era include the *Cantor Lectures on Musical Instruments, their Construction and Capabilities* by A. J. Hipkins and Henri Lavoix's *Histoire de l'instrumentation depuis le seizieme siecle jusqu'a nos jours* (Paris, 1878). In the realm of percussion, Boracchi described and illustrated an invention in *Manuale pel Timpanista* (Milan, 1842).
