# The Bollandists: Cessation of Work and Library Transition

## Cessation of Work and Library Transition
The Bollandists, who had been authorized to continue their work following the dispersion of the Jesuits, were based in Antwerp until 1778. At that time, they were transferred to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. During their tenure at Coudenberg, they published volume iv. of October in 1780 and volume v. of October in 1786. Following the suppression of the monastery of Coudenberg, the work of the Bollandists ceased in 1788.

Upon the cessation of their activities, the remains of the Bollandist library were acquired by the Premonstratensians of Tongerloo. This group attempted to continue the work, resulting in the publication of volume vi. of October in 1794 at their abbey.

## Re-establishment and New Series
The work was resumed in 1837 after the Society of Jesus was re-established in Belgium. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and at the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were subsequently installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels.

In 1845, the first volume of a new series was published: volume vii. of October. This series continued until 1883, reaching volume xiii. of October. Several Jesuit fathers collaborated on this series, including Joseph van Hecke, Joseph van der Moere, Benjamin Bossue, Remi de Buck, Victor de Buck, Edu. Carpentier, Ant. Tinnebroeck, and Henr. Matagne. Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan was tasked with editing the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which appeared at the beginning of volume xi. of October in 1864.

## Methodological Shift and Analecta Bollandiana
In 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align more closely with the progress of historical methods. This led to the establishment of a quarterly review titled *Analecta Bollandiana*, founded by Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt. By 1906, the review had reached its 25th volume; its editors included P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt.

The *Analecta Bollandiana* served several purposes:
* It provided dissertations, inedited texts, and studies intended for the remoulding and continuation of the *Acta Sanctorum*.
* Since 1892, it included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which offered criticisms of recent works regarding hagiographic questions.
* It published numerous library catalogues, including those from Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres.

## Hagiographic Analysis and Inventories
Beyond the quarterly review, the Bollandists conducted analyses of hagiographic manuscripts (MSS.) in major libraries. This resulted in several separate publications:
* Latin MSS. in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (two volumes, 1886-1889).
* Latin and Greek MSS. in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (five volumes, 1889-1896).
* Greek MSS. in the Vatican (1899).
* Latin MSS. in the libraries of Rome (starting in 1905).

The Bollandists also created inventories of previously published hagiographic texts. These included the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*. While these scientific endeavors delayed the publication of the principal collection, they provided a more solid basis for the work.

## Later Publications
Following these preparatory works, volume i. for November was published in 1887, followed by volume ii. in 1894. Volume ii. was preceded by the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* by the abbe Louis Duchesne and J.B. de Rossi. In 1902, the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* was published, which contained the *Synaxarium ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae*.
