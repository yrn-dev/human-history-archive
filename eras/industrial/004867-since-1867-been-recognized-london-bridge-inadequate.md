# London Bridge

London Bridge has a long history of evolution, transitioning from early timber structures to stone constructions and eventually undergoing modern expansions to accommodate increasing urban demands.

## Early History and Old London Bridge
The earliest bridges crossing the Thames at London were constructed from timber, with William of Malmesbury noting the existence of a bridge in 994. Around the year 1176, a stone bridge, commonly referred to as Old London Bridge, began to be founded by Peter of Colechurch. This structure was built near the existing timber bridge, though positioned further to the west.

Old London Bridge was characterized by the presence of timber houses built upon it, which were subject to frequent fires. Despite these incidents, the main structure remained in place until the beginning of the 19th century. The bridge's arches varied in span from 10 to 33 feet, and its total waterway measured only 337 feet.

## The Present London Bridge
The current London Bridge features a significantly larger waterway of 690 feet. The removal of the obstructions created by the previous stone bridge resulted in a considerable deepening of the river-bed and a lowering of the low-water level by 5 feet.

By 1867, it was recognized that the bridge was inadequate for the volume of traffic passing over it. Consequently, a scheme to widen the bridge was adopted in 1900. This widening project was executed between 1902 and 1904. The work involved the use of granite corbels to carry the footways, which are further adorned with open parapets and cornices. Following these improvements, the width between the parapets became 65 feet, consisting of a 35-foot roadway and two footways measuring 15 feet each. The project was overseen by engineer G. E. W. Cruttwell and architect Andrew Murray.

## Related Engineering and Infrastructure
The development of London's bridges and transit has been influenced by several eminent engineers. Sir John Wolfe Wolfe-Barry, an engineer who began practicing on his own account in 1867, was involved in the construction of railway bridges across the Thames at Cannon Street and Charing Cross. He was also engaged in the construction of the new Kew bridges and the Tower Bridge, the latter of which was completed in 1894. Wolfe-Barry later served on a royal commission regarding London Traffic from 1903 to 1905.

Additionally, Sir Benjamin Baker, an English engineer, contributed to London's infrastructure through his role in the construction of the Metropolitan railway and his work in introducing the system of constructing intra-urban railways using deep tubular tunnels made of cast iron segments.
