# The Modern Series of Eclipse-Expeditions

## Origins at Inch Bonney
The modern series of eclipse-expeditions was initiated following the observations made on the 15th of May 1836. During an annular eclipse of the sun at Inch Bonney in Roxburghshire, the astronomer Baily provided a notice of a phenomenon he termed "Baily's Beads." This effect is caused by inequalities in the limb of the moon.

## Impact on Astronomical Observation
Baily's description of the "beads" was so vivid that it generated an unprecedented level of attention toward the totality of the eclipse that occurred on the 8th of July 1842, which Baily observed at Pavia.

## Other Scientific Contributions of Baily
Beyond his work with eclipses, Baily contributed to several other areas of physics and astronomy:
*   **Earth's Density and Shape:** Between 1838 and 1842, Baily utilized Henry Cavendish's method to determine the mean density of the earth, establishing an authoritative value of 5.66. He also discussed the pendulum-experiments of H. Foster, from which he deduced an ellipticity for the earth of 1/289.
*   **Standards and Measurement:** Baily introduced a neglected element of reduction to correct the length of the seconds-pendulum. In 1843, he was entrusted with the reconstruction of the standards of length.
*   **Scientific History:** In 1835, Baily published an *Account of the Rev. John Flamsteed*, a work of fundamental importance to the scientific history of that era, which included a republication of the British Catalogue.

Baily died in London on the 30th of August 1844. His work was later detailed in J. Herschel's *Memoir of F. Baily, Esq.* (1845), which was prefixed to Baily's *Journal of a Tour*.
