# Constitutional and Political Developments of 1849

The year 1849 was marked by significant constitutional transitions and political upheavals across various regions, ranging from the German states and Denmark to the United States and Italy. These events often involved conflicts between radical and moderate factions, the restructuring of governmental bodies, and the influence of military mutinies.

## Unrest in Baden and the German States
At the start of 1849, the pursuit of a new constitution based on the resolutions of the Frankfort parliament triggered serious instability. Radical factions were angered when the second chamber refused a proposal on February 10, 1849, to summon a constituent assembly. This led to a formidable insurrection characterized by military sympathy for the revolution.

A military mutiny occurred at Rastatt on May 11, followed by a proclamation of revolution at Offenburg two days later. On May 13, a mutiny at Karlsruhe forced the grand-duke to flee, with his ministers following the next day. In the interim, a committee of the diet led by Lorenz Brentano—who represented moderate Radicals in opposition to republicans—attempted to manage affairs. A provisional government was established on June 1, and a "constituent diet" composed of "advanced" politicians assembled on June 10. However, this body had little practical power as the country was controlled by mutinous soldiers and an armed civilian mob. Consequently, the grand-duke of Baden, in coordination with Bavaria, requested Prussian armed intervention. Prussia granted this request on the condition that Baden join the League of the Three Kings.

## Constitutional Changes in Denmark
Denmark also underwent a process of establishing a parliament on modern lines. A constitutional rescript from January 28, 1848, was replaced by an electoral law for a national assembly. This assembly was designed to have 152 members: 38 nominated by the king to form an Upper House (*Landsting*), and the remainder elected by the people to form a popular chamber (*Folketing*).

Political tension arose between the National Liberals and the *Bondevenlige* (philo-peasant party), the latter of which opposed the king's right of nomination and advocated for a one-chamber system. The National Liberals won the general election, and the Radicals eventually acceded to the majority's wishes due to fears of reactionary tendencies. The new constitution received royal sanction on June 5, 1849.

## Political Transitions in the United States
In the United States, constitutional and electoral shifts occurred at the state level. In Illinois, the Constitutional Convention of 1848 ordered a new election of state officials, which resulted in Governor French being re-elected to a new term beginning in 1849. Illinois politics during this era were heavily influenced by sectionalism and immigration; for instance, from 1818 to 1838, the first six governors and six of the eight U.S. senators were born in Southern states.

## Political Stability in Piedmont
In Italy, the year 1849 saw a transition of power in Sardinia. Following the defeat of Novara on March 23, 1849, King Charles Albert abdicated and was succeeded by Victor Emmanuel II. D'Azeglio was called upon to form a cabinet, which he accepted despite difficult conditions. He dissolved the Chamber and summoned a new one to ratify a treaty of peace. While other parts of Italy experienced despotism, the king in Piedmont maintained the constitution intact against a general wave of reaction.

## Banking Constitutions and Legislation
The term "constitution" also applied to the structural organization of financial institutions in England and Wales. The Bank Charter Acts of 1844 and 1845 significantly altered the banking system. The act of 1844 divided the Bank of England into two departments: one for the transaction of ordinary banking business and one for the issue of notes. 

Regarding the constitution of private banks, the number of partners was restricted to six until 1862, when an act allowed for up to ten partners (though this did not extend to issuing private banks). The 1844 act influenced the system by eventually leading to the formation of large, powerful banks after years of favoring small banks.
