## Bridge Engineering and Girder Weights
In the design of bridges, the weight of main girders ($W_g$) is determined by the live load ($W_l$) and the weight of the platform ($W_f$). The relationship is expressed as $W_g = (W_l + W_f)k / (1 - k)$. To determine the necessary section of all members, a provisional design is created where $W_g$ is neglected, and the actual bridge members are then increased in the ratio $k / (1 - k)$.

Empirical relations provided by Waddell allow for the comparison of girder weights per foot run ($w_1, w_2$) for different spans ($l_1, l_2$) and live loads ($p, p'$). The ratio of weights for different spans is $w_2/w_1 = ½ [l_2/l_1 + (l_2/l_1)^2]$. When live loads vary, the ratio is $w_2'/w_2 = 1/5(1 + 4p'/p)$.

A partially rational approximate formula for the weight of main girders ($w_3$) is given as $w_3 = (w_1 + w_2)l^2 / (Cds - l_2)$ or $w_3 = (w_1 + w_2)lr / (Cs - lr)$, where:
* $w_1$ is the total live load per foot run.
* $w_2$ is the weight of the platform per foot run.
* $l$ is the span in feet.
* $s$ is the average stress in tons per square inch on the gross section of metal.
* $d$ is the depth of the girder at the center.
* $r$ is the ratio of span to depth ($l/d$).
* $C$ is a constant based on the girder type.

For iron braced parallel or bowstring girders, $C$ varies from 1500 to 1800, and for steel girders, it ranges from 1200 to 1500. A related value, $K$, ranges from 6000 to 7200 for iron and 7200 to 9000 for steel bridges.

## Bending Moments and Shear Stress
The analysis of beams involves calculating the greatest bending moment and shear when concentrated loads travel across a bridge. For a series of loads moving from the right, the greatest bending moment occurs when a specific condition is met regarding the position of the loads ($W_1$ through $W_n$) relative to the section being considered.

When loads $W_1, W_2, W_3$ traverse a girder from the left at fixed distances $a$ and $b$, the distribution of the bending moment for each load is represented by triangles. As these loads move, the middle ordinates describe parabolas ($M_1, M_2, M_3$) with values of $¼W_1l, ¼W_2l,$ and $¼W_3l$. The total bending moment under a load is the sum of the intercepts of these triangle sides.

To find the greatest shear at a section $C$, loads are advanced from the left abutment. If $R$ is the resultant of the loads on the bridge when $W_1$ is at $C$, the reaction at $B$ and shear at $C$ is $Rn/l$. The shear may increase if $W_2$ is moved to $C$, provided that $Ra/l + d > W_1$ (where $d$ is a generally negligible reaction). If the load is distributed via flooring to points $A$ and $B$, the loads become $W(p-x)/p$ and $Wx/p$.

## Determination of Relative Density
The archive details several methods for determining the relative density or specific gravity of solids and liquids.

### Hydrostatic Weighing
This method involves weighing a solid in air ($W$) and then in water ($W_1$). The relative density is the quotient $W / (W - W_1)$. For solids lighter than water, a sinker is used; if $w$ is the weight of the sinker in water, the relative density is $W / (W + w - W_1)$. 

Practical application requires suspending the solid by a fibre (such as platinum or silk) and using a beaker of distilled water. To ensure accuracy, air bubbles are removed with a camel-hair brush, and porous solids are boiled to expel air. For high accuracy, the formula is adjusted to $\Delta(0°/4°) = (\rho\alpha W - \delta W_1) / (W - W_1)$, accounting for the density of air ($\delta$), the density of water ($\rho$), and the coefficient of cubical expansion of the body ($\alpha$).

### Other Methods
* **Specific Gravity Bottle:** Used for small, insoluble fragments. The relative density is calculated as $W / (W + W_1 - W_2)$, where $W$ is the weight of the solid in air, $W_1$ is the bottle full of liquid, and $W_2$ is the bottle containing the solid and liquid.
* **Say's Stereometer:** This device determines volume based on Boyle's law, though it is susceptible to errors from temperature, atmospheric pressure, and moisture.
* **Equal Volume Weighing:** Used for liquids by weighing a glass vessel empty ($w$), filled with the liquid ($W$), and filled with a standard substance ($W_1$). The relative density is $(W - w) / (W_1 - w)$.
