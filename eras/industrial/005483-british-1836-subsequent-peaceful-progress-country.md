# British Activities and Administration in 1836

During the year 1836, British interests and activities spanned various global regions, ranging from territorial claims in Central America to naval surveying in the Pacific and legislative developments within the United Kingdom.

## Territorial Claims in Central America
In 1836, the British claimed the territories located between the Sarstoon and Belize rivers. The legal status of British sovereignty in this region had been a subject of historical debate. While some argued that the defeat of a Spanish attempt in 1798 established permanent sovereignty as an act of conquest, others noted that a new treaty with Spain in 1814 revived earlier provisions. Furthermore, between 1817 and 1819, acts of parliament continued to describe Belize as a "settlement, for certain purposes, under the protection of His Majesty."

Following the independence of Central America between 1819 and 1822, Great Britain worked to secure its position through various treaties. This included incorporating the 1786 treaty provisions into a new agreement with Mexico in 1826, as well as drafts for treaties with the United States of Central America (1831) and New Granada (1825). 

The sovereignty of Great Britain over the whole colony, including the territories claimed in 1836, was eventually confirmed by several factors: the peaceful progress of the country under British rule, the settlement of boundary disputes with Guatemala in 1859, and the 1850 Clayton-Bulwer Treaty. The latter treaty forbade the United States and Great Britain from colonizing or fortifying any point on the Central American mainland, but Belize was specifically excepted from this provision.

## Naval Exploration and Surveying
In 1836, Sir Edward Belcher, a British naval officer who had entered the navy in 1812, began a significant surveying mission. On board the "Sulphur," Belcher took up the work on the Pacific coast of South America that had been left unfinished by Frederick William Beechey. 

Belcher's mission involved visiting numerous islands and making various observations. Although he was later delayed by the war in China between 1840 and 1841, he eventually returned to England in 1842 via the Trans-Pacific route. His experiences during this period were later detailed in his 1843 publication, *Narrative of a Voyage round the World performed in H.M.S. "Sulphur," 1836-1842*.

## Domestic Legislation and Publications
Within the United Kingdom, 1836 saw the enactment of the Marriage Act. This act established the framework for the solemnization of marriages in registered places of worship. Later legislation, specifically the Marriage Act of 1898, would authorize the celebration of marriages under the 1836 Act without the presence of a registrar, provided a person authorized by the governing body of the place of worship was present.

Additionally, 1836 was the year of publication for the *Life* of John Mackay of Rockville. Mackay was the inventor of the ring bayonet, a design he developed after the plug-bayonet failed to stop the rush of the Highlanders at Killiecrankie.
