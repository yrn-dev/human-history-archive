# Skimmer, Parachute, and Attemperator

The terms skimmer, parachute, and attemperator refer to various tools and mechanisms used across different industries and biological contexts, most notably in brewing, dairy production, and zoology.

## Brewing Equipment
In the context of the top fermentation system used in England, several specialized devices are employed to manage the conversion of wort into beer and the subsequent "cleansing" process, which is the separation of yeast from the liquid.

### The Attemperator
During fermentation, the temperature of the liquid rises considerably. To prevent the temperature from exceeding a maximum of 70-75° F, fermenting vessels are equipped with attemperators. An attemperator is a system of pipes through which cold water is run to regulate the heat of the vessel. These are used in various methods, including the Skimming System, the ordinary dropping system, and the Burton Union System.

### The Parachute and Skimmer
In the Skimming System, fermentation occurs in wooden vessels known as "rounds" or "squares." These vessels are fitted with an attemperator and a skimming device, such as a parachute, used to remove or "skim" the yeast at the conclusion of the fermentation process. 

Similarly, in the ordinary dropping system, partly fermented beer is moved from rounds or squares into large vessels called skimming or dropping "backs." These backs are also fitted with attemperators and parachutes for yeast removal; in this specific application, the parachute typically covers the entire width of the back.

## Dairy Production
In butter dairies where a centrifugal separator is not utilized, a skimmer (also referred to as a skimming-dish) is used. This tool, made of tin, is employed to collect cream from the surface of milk that has been "set" for cream-raising in a milk-pan. The collected cream is then transferred to a cream-crock, where it remains for one to three days before churning.

## Biological and Zoological Applications
The term "parachute" is used to describe the physical adaptations of certain reptiles that allow them to glide or leap.

### Pterodactyls
The Pterodactyls, a vanished tribe of Mesozoic reptiles with bat-like fore limbs, were the first vertebrate creatures to fly. Their development allowed them to leap and parachute among the branches and fronds of forest trees.

### Flying Dragons
In zoology, the genus *Draco* consists of small flying lizards found in Indo-Malayan countries, including *D. dussumieri* in Madras. These lizards possess wing-like cutaneous processes supported by elongated ribs. These processes form a sort of parachute that enables the animal to make extensive leaps and glide from branch to branch. These organs are independent of the fore limbs and are not true wings; when not in use, they are folded by the side like a fan.

## Other Uses of "Skimmer"
The term "skimmers" has also been used metaphorically in historical contexts. The Barbary pirates, who operated along the coast of northern Africa and infested the commerce of the Mediterranean and the ocean, were referred to as "skimmers of the sea." The town of Bougie was noted as a particularly notorious haunt for these pirates during the 14th century.
