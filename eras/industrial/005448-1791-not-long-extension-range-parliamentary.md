# Events and Developments of 1791

The year 1791 was marked by significant shifts in colonial administration, political ruptures within the British government, and industrial and social developments across various regions.

## Colonial Administration and Representative Institutions
In 1791, William Pitt took a step forward in the administration of the empire by granting representative institutions to Canada. While these institutions were established, the home government maintained responsible control over them. This move followed a period of reaction against the liberal system of colonial self-government, during which an attempt had been made to govern colonies simply as dependencies. 

This development occurred not long after the range of parliamentary authority had been extended in another part of the empire through the creation of the Board of Control for India in 1784. Prior to these changes, the "Council of Trade and Plantations" had managed colonial affairs, but it was suppressed in 1782 after it was judged that separate provision in the home government for colonial administration was unnecessary. Following the patterns established in Canada, similar representative institutions were later granted to South Africa and Australia.

## Political Ruptures in the House of Commons
The period of 1791-1792 was characterized by intense political disagreement regarding parliamentary reform and the Revolution in France. While figures such as Charles Fox, Grey, Windham, Sheridan, Francis, and Lord Fitzwilliam—most of the Whig leaders—welcomed the French Revolution, others held opposing views. Fox described the revolution as "the best" and "much the greatest event it is that ever happened in the world."

In contrast, Burke viewed projects for parliamentary reform during the agitation of 1791-1792 as "downright crimes." Burke's horror of Jacobinism led to a divergence of opinion that overrode party associations. Although a breach between Sheridan and Burke had appeared in the House of Commons as early as February 1790, a full public rupture between Burke and Fox occurred in the House of Commons in May 1791.

## Industrial and Social Developments
The year 1791 also saw specific advancements in infrastructure and industry:

*   **Shipbuilding in Ireland:** In 1791, William Ritchie, an energetic Scotsman, established shipbuilding on an extensive scale. This, combined with existing rope and canvas manufacture and the earlier introduction of cotton manufacture by Thomas M'Cabe and Robert Joy in 1777, increased the demand for skilled labour and led to rapid population growth through immigration from rural districts and ordinary extension.
*   **Healthcare in England:** In Margate, the royal sea-bathing infirmary was established in 1791. This institution was later enlarged in 1882 through the munificence of Sir Erasmus Wilson.
