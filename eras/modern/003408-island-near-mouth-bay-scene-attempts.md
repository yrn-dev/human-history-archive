# Bear Island

## Geography and Location
Bear Island is one of the principal islands located in Bantry Bay, situated off the town of Bantry in County Cork, Ireland. The island is positioned near the mouth of the bay, where it sits in front of the harbour at Castletown Bearhaven on the northern shore. 

Bantry Bay itself is described as a picturesque inlet, measuring approximately 22 miles in length and between 3 to 6 miles in breadth. The waters of the bay range from 12 to 32 fathoms in depth and provide excellent anchorage.

## Military and Strategic Significance
Due to its location and the quality of the anchorage, the bay serves as one of the headquarter stations for the Channel Squadron, which utilizes the harbour at Castletown Bearhaven located behind Bear Island.

The area has been the site of several significant military events:
* **French Invasions:** The bay was the scene of attempts by the French to invade Ireland in 1689 and 1796.
* **William of Orange:** In 1697, troops belonging to William of Orange were landed at this location.

## Regional Context
Bear Island is part of a group of islands in the vicinity, with Whiddy being another of the principal islands off the town of Bantry. Bantry itself is a seaport, market-town, and seaside resort that serves as a terminus for the railway and a coaching station on the "Prince of Wales" route (named for King Edward VII) connecting Cork to Killarney and Glengarriff. 

The surrounding region is also known for its sea fisheries, although ruins of "fish palaces" in the area indicate that the pilchard fishery failed during the 18th century.
