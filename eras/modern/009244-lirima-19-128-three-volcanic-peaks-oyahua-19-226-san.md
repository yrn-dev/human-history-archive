# Lirima and the Volcanic Peaks of the Bolivian Tableland

## Geography of the Bolivian Andean System
The Andean cordilleras cross the south-west corner of Bolivia and project east toward the Brazilian highlands, forming a great obtuse angle. Roughly two-fifths of Bolivia's total area is comprised within these cordilleras. The system is divided into two great parallel chains, featuring flanking ranges and spurs to the east, and is characterized by parallel ranges that enclose deep ravines and great elevated plateaus. To the north-east of Lake Titicaca, the Nudo de Apolobamba consists of a confused mass of lofty intersecting ridges, which include some of the highest peaks in South America.

## The Bolivian Tableland
Below the mountainous area of the Nudo de Apolobamba, the ranges open to enclose extensive plateaus. The western range, known as the Cordillera Occidental, serves as part of the boundary between Bolivia and the northern provinces of Chile. This range forms the western rampart of the *alta-planicie*, or the great Bolivian tableland. 

The *alta-planicie* extends from the Vilcanota knot in Peru in the north to the Serrania de Lipez on the Argentine frontier in the south. It measures approximately 500 miles in length and 80 miles in breadth, covering about 40,000 square miles. The plateau's northern region is commonly referred to as the *puna*, while the southern region is known as the "desert of Lipez," which is similar in appearance and character to the Puna de Atacama.

## Topography and Elevation
The tableland is a great lacustrine basin that once held an inland sea, which had an outlet to the east via the La Paz gorge. The average elevation of the plateau is approximately 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca, though it descends by about 1,000 feet toward its southern extremity. The environment is described as bleak and inhospitable in the north and barren and arid toward the south. The region contains great saline depressions that are covered with water during the rainy season.

The plateau is broken by various peaks and ridges. The highest peak within the plateau itself is the Cerro de Tahua, reaching an altitude of 17,454 feet.

## Western Peaks and Volcanic Formations
Overlooking the *alta-planicie* from the west are several snow-clad peaks. These include:
*   Sajama (21,047 ft.)
*   Huallatiri (21,654 ft.)
*   Parinacota (20,918 ft.)
*   Pomarape (20,505 ft.)
*   Lirima (19,128 ft.)

Additionally, there are three specific volcanic peaks located in this western overlooking range:
*   Licancaur (19,685 ft.)
*   San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.)
*   Oyahua (19,226 ft.)
