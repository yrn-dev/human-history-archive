# Austrian Duchies

The administration and territorial consolidation of the Austrian duchies underwent significant development under the House of Habsburg, particularly through the efforts of Maximilian I. His governance focused on establishing organic unity and structured administration across the various lands under his sway.

## Administrative and Political Development
Maximilian I, the son of Frederick and a key figure in the expansion of the House of Habsburg, implemented several reforms to improve the government of the Austrian duchies. To ensure more effective oversight, he established specific bodies dedicated to judicial, financial, and executive purposes. 

In 1512, the Austrian lands were constituted as one of the imperial circles. Further progress toward political integration occurred in 1518, when representatives from the various diets, known as *Landtage*, convened at Innsbruck. This meeting is recognized as the beginning of an organic unity within the Austrian lands. Despite these administrative successes, Maximilian's attempts to elevate Austria to the status of an electorate or a kingdom were unsuccessful.

## Territorial Expansion and Consolidation
The greatness of the house of Habsburg was expanded through a combination of treaties, inheritance, and military action. Maximilian I recovered his hereditary lands in 1490 after driving the Hungarians out of Austria. In the same year, he brought the county of Tirol under his rule through an arrangement with his kinsman, Sigismund of Tirol. Following the death of Emperor Frederick in 1493, Maximilian united all Austrian lands.

Additional territories were acquired through the following means:
* **Inheritance:** In 1500, Maximilian inherited the possessions of the counts of Goerz.
* **Intervention:** He added districts to Tirol by intervening in a succession war located in Bavaria.
* **Conflict:** Gradisca was acquired in 1512 following a struggle with Venice.

The duchy of Carinthia serves as a regional example of the Austrian territories. Its capital, Klagenfurt (also known as Celovec), is situated on the river Glan. The city features several historical and administrative structures, including the *Landhaus* (house of assembly), which dates from the end of the 14th century.
