# The Second Civil War and the July 1648 Uprising in Surrey

## The July 1648 Uprising
During the Second Civil War, a military movement occurred in July 1648 in Surrey. George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, and his younger brother, Lord Francis, joined the earl of Holland in this region. This participation in the rebellion led to significant consequences for the Duke of Buckingham. Although his lands had been restored to him in 1647 due to his youth, they were confiscated again following these events. A considerable portion of these lands passed into the possession of Fairfax, and the duke refused to compound.

The uprising resulted in military clashes and casualties. Lord Francis was killed near Kingston. On the 10th of the month, the earl of Holland and the Duke of Buckingham were surprised at St Neots; while Holland was captured, Buckingham succeeded in escaping to Holland.

## George Villiers, 2nd Duke of Buckingham
George Villiers (1628–1687) was an English statesman and the son of the 1st duke. Born on January 30, 1628, he and his brother Francis were brought up by King Charles I alongside the king's own children. Villiers was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he earned an M.A. in 1642.

His involvement in the various conflicts of the era began in the first Civil War, where he fought for the king and participated in the attack on Lichfield Close in April 1643. Following this, he and his brother lived in Rome and Florence under the care of the earl of Northumberland. 

After his escape to Holland following the 1648 uprising, Villiers continued his service to the royal cause. On September 19, 1649, Charles II conferred the Garter upon him, and on April 6, 1650, he was admitted to the privy council. During this period, Villiers opposed Hyde and supported an alliance with the Scottish presbyterians. He accompanied Charles to Scotland in June 1650 and allied himself with Argyll. In this capacity, he dissuaded Charles from joining a royalist plot in October 1650, leading to suspicions that he had betrayed the plan to the convenanting leaders.

Villiers held several military commissions: he was appointed general of the eastern association in England in May and was commissioned to raise forces abroad. In the following year, he was chosen to command the Scottish royalists and lead a projected movement in Lancashire. He was present with Charles at the battle of Worcester on September 3, 1651, and escaped alone to Rotterdam in October. His influence later diminished due to his readiness to sacrifice the interests of the church during negotiations with Cromwell's government and his audacious courtship of the king's sister, a widowed princess.

## Other Figures in the Civil War Era
The Civil War period involved various figures with differing loyalties and fortunes:

*   **Sir John Denham:** A poet born in Dublin in 1615, Denham served as the high sheriff for Surrey and governor of Farnham Castle at the start of the Civil War. Lacking military ability, he quickly surrendered the castle to the parliament. After being sent to London as a prisoner, he was eventually permitted to join the king at Oxford.
*   **Robert Pierrepont:** The earl of Kingston-upon-Hull (1584–1643) initially remained neutral during the outbreak of the Civil War. He later joined the king and was appointed lieutenant-general for several counties, including Norfolk, Cambridge, Huntingdon, Rutland, and Lincoln. He was taken prisoner while defending Gainsborough and was accidentally killed on July 25, 1643, during his transport to Hull. His sons took opposing sides: Francis Pierrepont was a colonel in the parliamentary army and a member of the Long Parliament, while William Pierrepont was a leading member of the parliamentary party.
*   **Murrough O'Brien:** The 1st Earl of Inchiquin (c. 1614–1674) was an Irish soldier and statesman. During the Civil War, the English parliament appointed him president of Munster. However, in early 1648, he declared for his former master, Charles I, and spent approximately two years attempting to uphold the royalist cause in Ireland.
