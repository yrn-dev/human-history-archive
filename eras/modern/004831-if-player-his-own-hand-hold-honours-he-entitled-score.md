# Scoring Honours in Bridge

In the game of bridge, honours are specific high-value cards that provide additional points to a player's score. In a suit declaration, honours consist of the ace, king, queen, knave, and ten. The scoring of these cards is based on whether they are held individually or conjointly by partners.

## Suit Declaration Honours
When a suit is declared, the scoring for honours depends on the number of these cards held by a partnership:
* **Three honours:** If a player and their partner conjointly hold three (also referred to as "simple") honours, they score twice the value of a trick.
* **Four honours:** If the partnership conjointly holds four honours, they score four times the value of a trick.
* **Five honours:** If the partnership conjointly holds five honours, they score five times the value of a trick.

## Individual Hand Bonuses
Additional points are awarded if a significant number of honours are concentrated in a single hand rather than split between partners:
* **Four honours in one hand:** A player who holds four honours in their own hand is entitled to score four honours in addition to the score for conjoint honours. For example, if one player holds four honours and their partner holds the fifth, the total score for honours is nine.
* **Five honours in one hand:** A player who holds five honours in their own hand is entitled to score ten by honours.

## No-Trump Honours
The scoring system for honours differs when a no-trump hand is played. In this instance, the score is based specifically on the number of aces held by the partnership:
* **Three aces:** If partners conjointly hold three aces, they score 30 for honours.
* **Four aces:** If partners conjointly hold four aces, they score 40 for honours.
* **Four aces in one hand:** If all four aces are held in a single hand, they count for 100.

## Integration into Total Score
The score for honours does not contribute toward winning or losing the rubber. Instead, it is added to the trick score after the rubber is completed to determine the final value. Other scores are treated on the same footing as honours, including the *chicane* (scored if a player holds no trump, in an amount equal to simple honours), a *grand slam* (40 points for winning all tricks), and a *little slam* (20 points for winning 12 tricks).

At the conclusion of the rubber, all points—whether derived from honours, tricks, slam, chicane, or rubber points—are summed. The final number of points won is the difference between the two totals.

## Relative Value of Honours
The perceived value of honour points fluctuates depending on the stage of the rubber. In the first and second games, a point scored for honours is generally worth about half of a point scored for tricks. This value may be slightly less in a close game or slightly more in a one-sided game. In the deciding game of the rubber, because winning the game is of primary importance, the value of an honour point sinks to one-third of a point scored for tricks.
