# Korotnev and the Fauna of Lake Baikal

## Biological Exploration of Lake Baikal
The fauna of Lake Baikal has been the subject of exploration by several researchers, including Dybowski and Godlewski. Between 1900 and 1902, further exploration was conducted by Korotnev. These studies revealed that the fauna is much richer than it was previously supposed to be and possesses a quite original character.

## Marine and Freshwater Species
While the zoological groups in the lake exhibit a relative poverty, there is a great wealth of species within those specific groups. For example, there are as many as 300 species of gammarids. Those gammarids living at great depths—between 330 and 380 fathoms—tend to assume abyssal characters that are similar to the deep-sea fauna found in the ocean.

The lake's waters swarm with fish, specifically salmonidae and sturgeons. The chief product of the fisheries is the herring, known as *Salmo omul*, although the amount taken has been notably fewer within the last forty or fifty years. Additionally, plankton is very abundant in the lake.

Lake Baikal is home to a seal (*Phoca vitulina* or *Phoca baikalensis* of Dybowski) that is quite akin to the seals of Spitsbergen. Other marine-like organisms present in the lake include polychaetes, marine sponges, and some marine gammarids, as well as a marine mollusc called *ancilodoris*.

## Related Water Bodies
Close to the northern extremity of Lake Baikal is the little Lake Frolikha. This lake communicates with Lake Baikal via a river of the same name. Lake Frolikha contains a peculiar species of trout, *Salmo erythreas*, which is not known to exist anywhere else.

## Geological Hypotheses
The presence of these species has led to hypotheses regarding a direct communication between the Arctic Ocean and Lake Baikal during the Tertiary or Post-Tertiary ages; however, these hypotheses have not been proved.
