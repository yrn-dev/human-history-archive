# River Systems and Geography of Bolivia

The river systems within Bolivia are naturally categorized into three distinct regions: the Central Plateau, La Plata, and the Amazon. The Amazon region is the most extensive, with a drainage basin that covers more than one-half of the republic. This system consists of rivers that flow either indirectly or directly into the Madeira, which is one of the great tributaries of the Amazon. This region also includes several small tributaries of the Purus and the Acre in the north.

## The Mamore River
The Mamore and the Beni are the two principal rivers of the Amazon system in Bolivia. These two rivers unite at latitude 10 deg. 20' S. to form the Madeira. The Mamore originates on the north-east slopes of the Sierra de Cochabamba, situated a little south of the 17th parallel. Its upper section is known as the Chimore. 

The river follows a serpentine course toward the north, primarily located between the 65th and 66th meridians. The Mamore has a total length of approximately 600 m. A significant portion of this length—fully three-fourths—is navigable and passes across a level plain, extending from Chimore (which is 925 ft. above sea level) to the rapids located near the river's mouth.

## The Guapay and Its Tributaries
The Guapay, also referred to as the Grande, is the principal Bolivian tributary of the Mamore. Above the point where they unite, the Guapay is longer and larger than the Mamore and is considered the main stream. It rises in the Cordillera Oriental, east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas, and flows east until it reaches the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones, where it enters the Bolivian lowlands.

The Guapay follows a magnificent curve to the north, passing around the south-east extremity of the Sierra de Cochabamba and skirting the Llanos de Chiquitos. It then turns north-west to join the Mamore at Junta de los Rios (15 deg. 20' S. lat. and 64 deg. 40' W. long.). The Guapay's course is described as tortuous, spanning over 700 m., and is noted as not being navigable. Its primary tributaries include the Piray (or Sara), the Mizque, and the Yapacani. The Yapacani rises on the east slopes of the Cordillera Real and flows east through Cochabamba to the sierras of the same name, where it makes a great bend to the north.

## Other Tributaries of the Mamore
Several other large Bolivian tributaries flow into the Mamore, all of which originate on the north-east flanks of the Andes. These include:
* The Secure
* The Chapare
* The Manique (or Apere)
* The Yacuma

The Yacuma is specifically noted for draining a region characterized by swamps and lakes located north of the Sierra Chamaya.
