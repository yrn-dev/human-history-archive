# Hydrographic Basins of Asia

The hydrographic basins of Asia are categorized into three primary divisions based on their drainage patterns: the northern division, the southern division, and the interior or inland basins.

## The Northern Division
The northern division of Asia's hydrographic basins encompasses an area of approximately 6,394,500 square miles. This division includes drainage that falls into the Mediterranean, the Aralo-Caspian depression, and the Arctic Sea. The distribution of this area is as follows:
*   **Arctic river basins:** 4,367,000 sq. m.
*   **Aralo-Caspian basin:** 1,759,000 sq. m.
*   **Mediterranean:** 268,500 sq. m.

The Ob river basin is the largest river basin in Asia, with an extent that exceeds 1,000,000 sq. m.

## The Southern Division
The southern division is nearly equal in extent to the northern division, totaling 6,514,000 sq. m. This division is composed of two main drainage areas:
*   **Pacific drainage:** 3,641,000 sq. m.
*   **Indian Ocean:** 2,873,000 sq. m.

Other significant river basins in the east and south include the Amur, which embraces 776,000 sq. m., the Yang-tsze-kiang at 685,000 sq. m., the Ganges at 409,500 sq. m., and the Indus at 370,000 sq. m.

## Interior and Inland Basins
The interior or inland basins cover an area of 3,141,500 sq. m., which is approximately half the size of the other two divisions. These basins include several distinct regions:
*   The Gobi depression.
*   The Tibetan plateau.
*   The Iranian (also referred to as Perso-Afghan) uplands.
*   The Syro-Arabian inland basin.
*   The basin of Asia Minor.
*   Lacustrine regions located south of the Arctic watershed.
