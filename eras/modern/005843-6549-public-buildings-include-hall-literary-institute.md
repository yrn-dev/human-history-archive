# Buckie

Buckie is a police burgh and fishing town located in Banffshire, Scotland. It is situated on the Moray Firth at the mouth of the Buckie burn, approximately 17 miles west of Banff. The town is served by a station on the Great North of Scotland railway.

## Population and Geography
The population of Buckie grew from 5,849 in 1891 to 6,549 by 1901. The town is divided into two sections, Eastern Buckie and Nether Buckie, by the Buckie burn. This burn originates near the Hill of Clashmadin, which is located about 5 miles to the south-west.

## Public Buildings and Institutions
The public buildings of the town include a hall and a literary institute. This institute is equipped with recreation rooms and a library.

## Industry and Economy
Buckie is a significant center for the fishing industry, serving as the chief seat of line fishing in Scotland and attracting one of the largest Scottish fleets during the herring season. The town's harbour consists of an inner and an outer basin, covering an area of 9 acres and providing half a mile of quayage.

Beyond the fisheries, the town's economy is supported by several other industrial works, including:
* Engineering works
* Distilleries
* Facilities for the production of sails, ropes, and oil

## Surrounding Localities
Near Buckie are other notable settlements. Portgordon, located 1½ miles to the west, is described as a thriving fishing village. To the east, approximately 2 miles away, is Rathven. Rathven is situated in a fertile district that contains other relics of the remote past, specifically several interesting Danish cairns.
