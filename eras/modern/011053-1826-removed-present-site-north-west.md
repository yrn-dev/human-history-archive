# Asheville

## Geography and Location
Asheville is a city located in the western part of North Carolina, U.S.A., serving as the county-seat of Buncombe county. It is situated in the mountainous Blue Ridge region, approximately 210 miles west of Raleigh. The city is positioned on a high terrace on the east bank of the French Broad river, at the mouth of the Swannanoa, at an elevation of about 2,300 feet above the sea. Its location is further defined by the junction of three branches of the Southern railway.

## Demographics and Climate
The population of Asheville has shown steady growth over several decades. In 1890, the population was 10,235; by 1900, it grew to 14,692 (including 4,724 negroes); and by the 1910 census, the population reached 18,762. The city is characterized by beautiful scenery and a dry, equable climate. Due to these features, it is recognized as one of the most popular pleasure and health resorts in the south, serving as a winter resort for those from the north and a summer resort for southerners.

## City Infrastructure and Social Life
Asheville features an extensive system of suburban and city parks connected by beautiful drives, with great forests located in the vicinity. The city's public buildings include an auditorium, a public library, a Federal building, a court house, and the city hall. Social life is supported by several clubs, including the Swannanoa Country club, the Tahkeeostee, the Elks, the Asheville, and the Albemarle.

## Educational Institutions
Several educational facilities are located in or near the city:
* **Presbyterian Church Institutions:** Under the control of the Woman's Board of Home Missions of the Presbyterian Church, the city hosts a collegiate and normal institute for young women (established 1892) and a home industrial school for girls (established 1887), both of which share the same campus.
* **Industrial Schools:** There is an Asheville farm school for boys, which serves as an industrial school for negroes.
* **The Asheville School for Boys:** Located 5 miles west of the city.
* **The Bingham School:** Founded in 1793 by William Bingham (who died in 1826) at Pittsboro, N.C., this school was removed to its current site, 3 miles north-west of Asheville, in 1891.

## The Biltmore Estate
Located approximately 2 miles south-east of Asheville is Biltmore, the estate of George W. Vanderbilt. Spanning 125,000 acres, it is considered likely the finest country place in the United States. The estate's central feature is a French Renaissance design chateau, measuring 375 by 150 feet, modeled after the famous chateau at Blois in France. 

Mr. Vanderbilt also maintains a model village in the neighborhood, which includes a church, a hospital, an industrial school for whites, and an elementary school. Both the village and the chateau were designed by Richard M. Hunt.
