# The Southern Division of Asia

In the study of the hydrographic basins of Asia, the continent is categorized into three primary divisions. The southern division is nearly equal in extent to the northern division.

## Hydrographic Extent and Basins
The southern division of Asia encompasses a total area of 6,514,000 square miles. This total is comprised of two primary drainage areas:
*   **Pacific drainage:** 3,641,000 square miles.
*   **Indian Ocean drainage:** 2,873,000 square miles.

For comparison, the northern division—which includes the Mediterranean, the Aralo-Caspian depression, and drainage falling into the Arctic Sea—embraces an area of approximately 6,394,500 square miles. The interior or inland basins, which include the Syro-Arabian inland basin, the Tibetan plateau, the Gobi depression, the Iranian (or Perso-Afghan) uplands, the lacustrine regions south of the Arctic watershed, and the basin of Asia Minor, total 3,141,500 square miles, which is about half the extent of the northern and southern divisions.

## Major River Basins
While the Ob is the largest river basin in Asia, exceeding 1,000,000 square miles, several significant basins are located in the east and south. These include:
*   **The Amur:** 776,000 square miles.
*   **The Yang-tsze-kiang:** 685,000 square miles.
*   **The Ganges:** 409,500 square miles.
*   **The Indus:** 370,000 square miles.

## Geographic Outline of Asia
The overall form of Asia can be viewed as a great isosceles spherical triangle. Its north-eastern apex is located at East Cape (Vostochnyi) in the Bering Strait. The two equal sides of this triangle are approximately 6,500 miles in length; one extends to the extremity of the Malay peninsula in the east, and the other extends to the southern point of Arabia in the west. 

The base of this triangle, located between the Malay peninsula and the southern point of Arabia, occupies about 60 degrees of a great circle, or 4,500 miles. This base is deeply indented on either side of the Indian peninsula by the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea.

## Regional Geography and Boundaries
The north-western boundary of Asia is indicated with fair accuracy by a great circle drawn from the southern point of Arabia to East Cape. This line passes through the western part of the Caspian, over the Ural Mountains, and nearly along the coast-line of the Arctic Ocean. However, the north-western half of Arabia and Asia Minor lie outside of this great circle. Similarly, a great circle drawn from East Cape to the extremity of the Malay peninsula follows the eastern boundary, passing nearly over the coasts of China, Manchuria, and Cochin-China.
