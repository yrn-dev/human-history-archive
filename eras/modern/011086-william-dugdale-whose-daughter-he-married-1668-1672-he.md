# Sir William Dugdale

Sir William Dugdale was an English antiquary and author known for his extensive research into the history and records of England. His scholarly contributions are characterized by a focus on monastic history, heraldry, and regional antiquities.

## Literary Works and Contributions
Dugdale produced several significant works of historical research. His most important publications include *Antiquities of Warwickshire*, published in 1656, and the *History of St Paul's Cathedral*, published in 1658. He also authored the *Baronage of England*, which was published between 1675 and 1676.

One of his most enduring contributions is the *Monasticon Anglicanum*, a three-volume work published between 1655 and 1673. This work is cited as a source for the history of various religious foundations, such as Bolton Priory. The *Monasticon* was a collaborative effort in some respects; for instance, the Scottish annalist and antiquary Sir James Balfour contributed to it.

## Professional Life and Honors
Dugdale's expertise in heraldry and history led to his appointment to high office. He served as the Garter king-at-arms, a position he obtained after Elias Ashmole refused the office in his favor. 

## Personal Life and Records
Dugdale's personal history is preserved through a *Life* which he wrote himself up to the year 1678. This autobiographical account, along with his correspondence, diary, and an index to his manuscript collections, was edited by William Hamper and published in 1827. In 1668, his daughter married Elias Ashmole.
