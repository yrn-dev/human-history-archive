# The Kingdom of Holland under Louis Bonaparte

## Establishment of the Kingdom
Following the victory of Austerlitz on December 2, 1805, Napoleon began planning a ring of states in close alliance with the French Empire. As part of this strategy, Napoleon destined his brother, Louis, for the throne of Holland. Louis was proclaimed king of the country on June 6, 1806.

## Relations with Napoleon
The relationship between the two brothers was characterized by tension and eventual rupture. From the beginning, Napoleon reproached Louis for courting popularity too much and for being too lenient with his subjects. These frictions were further exacerbated by a violent jealousy Louis felt toward his wife.

Political disputes also arose regarding the continental system; the increasing rigour of this system led to an open rupture between the brothers. In 1808, Napoleon offered Louis the vacant throne of Spain, but Louis refused to accept it, and the position was instead given to Joseph.

## Annexation to the French Empire
By the latter part of 1809, Napoleon virtually resolved to annex Holland to stop secret trade being carried on by the Dutch with England. At the end of that year, Louis traveled to Paris in an attempt to gain better terms for Holland and to obtain a divorce from Hortense, though he failed in both objectives.

In January 1810, Napoleon annexed the island of Walcheren, claiming that Louis had failed to defend French interests during the British Walcheren expedition of 1809. French troops also occupied Bergen-op-Zoom and Breda. Although Louis acquiesced to these points, the crisis continued. Following the collapse of negotiations with Great Britain in the spring of 1810, Napoleon pressed Louis further and sent French troops against the Dutch capital.

Despairing of any ability to offer resistance, Louis fled his kingdom and eventually settled at Toplitz in Bohemia. On July 9, 1810, Napoleon formally annexed Holland to the French Empire.

## Later Life of Louis
Louis spent the remainder of his life separated from his wife. In 1815, he gained custody of his elder son. He lived primarily in Rome, where he focused on philosophic and literary studies and the fortunes of his sons. He found great pleasure in his sons' devotion to the democratic and national cause in Italy between 1830 and 1831, though this was overshadowed by the death of his elder son, Napoleon Louis, during the spring campaign.

## Other Historical Contexts of Holland
The archive contains other references to the region's governance. In the 16th century, the Prince of Orange initiated the United Provinces of Holland during a revolt against Spain and Philip II. The Prince served as the governor of Zeeland and Holland until his assassination at Delft in 1584 by Balthazar Gérard.

In the 19th century, after the fall of Napoleon and the first peace of Paris on May 30, 1814, Belgium was united with Holland to create the kingdom of the Netherlands. This state's sovereignty was granted to the Prince of Orange, who became William I on March 23, 1815. This union later faced instability, leading to the Belgian provinces detaching themselves by force from Holland to form an independent state, a process that culminated in the London protocol of October 15, 1831.
