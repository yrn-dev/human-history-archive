# Austrian Political History and Bibliography (1801-1810)

The study of Austrian political history during the first decade of the nineteenth century, specifically the period from 1801 to 1810, is documented through several specialized historical works and diplomatic analyses. This era is characterized by complex diplomatic maneuvers and the evolving nature of the Austrian state's foreign and internal policies.

## Diplomatic and Foreign Policy
The diplomatic landscape of Austria between 1801 and 1810 is a primary subject of research in the archive's diplomatic records. A significant work on this specific decade is A. Beer's *Zehn Jahre oesterreichischer Politik, 1801-1810*, published in Leipzig in 1877. Further insight into the early part of this period is provided by A. Fournier in *Gentz und Cobenzl: Gesch. der oest. Politik in den Jahren 1801-1805*, published in Vienna in 1880.

Beyond the immediate decade, the broader scope of Austrian foreign policy is explored in Beer's *Die orientalische Politik Oesterreichs seit 1774* (published in Prague and Leipzig in 1883), which examines the state's "oriental policy" from 1774 onward. Other related diplomatic studies include F. von Demelitsch's work on Metternich and his foreign policy between 1809 and 1812, and H. Uebersberger's analysis of the relations between Austria and Russia from the end of the 15th century through 1605.

## General and Constitutional History
The broader historical context of the House of Austria is anchored by Archdeacon William Coxe's *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792*, which was later continued by W. Kelly. While this remains the only general history of Austria in the English language, it has been largely superseded by more recent research.

Constitutional developments and the legal history of the empire are detailed in several texts, including E. Werunsky's *Oesterreichische Reichs- und Rechtsgeschichte* and A. Bechmann's *Lehrbuch der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch*. Other scholars, such as A. Huber and A. Luschin von Ebengreuth, have contributed to the understanding of the imperial history (*Reichsgesch*), with Luschin von Ebengreuth's work being noted as of first-class importance.

## Regional and Internal Dynamics
The Austrian empire's nucleus is the original archduchy, which includes Upper Austria and represents the oldest possession of the House of Habsburg within its current dominions. The internal political climate of the monarchy also involved significant regional tensions. For example, in Hungary, the diet was not summoned between 1811 and 1825, and in Transylvania, it remained unsumnoned between 1811 and 1834.

The archive also notes a vast body of literature concerning the "racial question," specifically regarding languages and race in Austria. Key legal perspectives on these issues are found in the *Oesterr. Staatswoerterbuch* by Josef Ulbrith and Ernst Mischler.
