# Scriptores rerum Austriacarum veteres et genuini

## Overview and Publication
The *Scriptores rerum Austriacarum veteres et genuini* is a collection of early authorities regarding the history of Austria. The work was published between 1721 and 1725 in three folio volumes by Hieronymus Pez in Leipzig. 

## Subsequent Editions
Following the original publication, the work saw further editions in later years. A new edition was printed in Regensburg in 1745. Later, between 1793 and 1794, A. Rauch published the collection in Vienna under the modified title *Rerum Austriacarum scriptores*.

## Evolution of Austrian Historical Sources
While the *Scriptores rerum Austriacarum veteres et genuini* served as an early authority, the systematic exploitation of private and public archives did not begin until the latter half of the 19th century. This led to the creation of more comprehensive collections.

One of the most significant developments was the *Fontes rerum Austriacarum*, published by the Historical Commission of the Imperial Academy of Sciences at Vienna. Beginning with its first volume in 1855, this series covers both modern and medieval Austrian history and is divided into two distinct parts:
*   ***Scriptores***: The 9th volume of this section was published in 1904.
*   ***Diplomataria et Acta***: The 58th volume of this section was published in 1906.

Other notable contributions to the study of Austrian history include the *Quellen und Forschungen zur Geschichte, Literatur und Sprache Oesterreichs und seiner Kronlaender*, edited by J. E. Wackernagel and J. Hirn in Graz (1895), with its 10th volume appearing in 1906. Additionally, Dahlmann-Waitz's *Quellenkunde* (1906 edition and 1907 supplement) provides various inventories and accounts of private and public archives.

## Treaty Collections
The archive of Austrian history also includes specialized collections of treaties. L. Neumann published a six-volume set in Leipzig (1855) titled *Recueil des traites conclus par l'Autriche avec les puissances etrangeres depuis 1763*. This work was later continued by A. de Plason in an 18-volume series published in Vienna between 1877 and 1905. In 1907, the Imperial Commission for the Modern History of Austria began a new series titled *Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege*, which organizes treaties by the specific states with which they were entered.
