# Commerce and Trade in West Africa

The commercial landscape of West Africa is characterized by a diverse array of agricultural exports, specialized mining industries, and strategic trading hubs that facilitate the movement of goods between the interior and the coast.

## The Kola-Nut and Agricultural Trade
The kola nut is a significant commercial product across several West African regions. In Ashanti, the town of Kintampo—located approximately 130 miles north-east of the capital, Kumasi—has evolved from a former mart for ivory and slaves into a center for the kola-nut commerce and a depot for government stores. The nut of the kola tree is noted for being in great demand. In the province of Illorin, a British West African protectorate in Nigeria, kolas are listed among the primary agricultural products alongside tobacco, rice, peppers, cotton, and ground-nuts.

In Dahomey proper, the economy is heavily dependent on the export of palm-kernels and palm-oil, the latter of which is produced in large quantities, particularly in the district of Toffo. Other exported vegetable products from Dahomey include rubber, copra, and kola-nuts. The region also produces a wide variety of fruits and vegetables, such as plantains, coco-nuts, yams, cassava, maize, and the African apple. While palm-wine is manufactured, the practice is discouraged because it destroys the tree.

## Mining and Manufacturing
The Ashanti region is a major center for the gold-mining industry, specifically in Obuassi, located in the south-west. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti reached 68,259 oz., with a value of £254,790. The railway completed in 1903 from Sekondi to Kumasi passes through this auriferous region.

The Ashanti people are also recognized for their skill in manufacturing. They are particularly adept at weaving cotton and producing pottery. Their gold work is characterized by considerable artistic elaboration; in 1874, the king's palace at Kumasi yielded valuable goldsmiths' work and masks of beaten gold, showing a perceptible influence of Moorish art. Similarly, in Illorin, various manufactures are carried out, including the production of pottery, carved wooden vessels, finely plaited mats, embroidered work, and leather goods, such as shoes made of red and yellow leather.

## Trading Centers and Distribution
Strategic towns serve as vital links for the distribution of merchandise. Kumasi acts as the distributing center for the hinterland and the entirety of Ashanti. Illorin is another great trading center where Hausa caravans bring goods from central Africa and merchandise from the Mediterranean coast. These goods are then distributed from Illorin to Benin, Dahomey, and the Lagos hinterland. Conversely, trade from the Guinea coast is primarily managed by the Yoruba and flows through Lagos.

Historically, Illorin operated under a system where middlemen transacted all business between northern and southern traders, as southern traders were generally prohibited from entering the city. Following the establishment of British authority, the city was opened, leading to an influx of petty traders from Lagos and the yearly issuance of 4,000 to 5,000 trade licenses. By 1904, the British resident estimated that at least 3,000 loads of British cotton goods, valued at £5 per load, were imported.

## Historical and Coastal Commerce
Ancient commerce along the western coast of Africa involved Phenician settlements. The colony of Gadês served as a center for extensive trade that reached south along the African coast for at least thirty days' sail. The island of Kernê, located twelve days' sail from the strait of Gibraltar, functioned as an established dépôt for Phenician merchandise traded with the interior. In this early trade, inhabitants of central regions brought elephants' teeth and leopard skins to coastal towns of Ethiopians or Libyans to exchange them for pottery from Athens and unguents from Tyre.

In more recent history, the trade of Ashanti was temporarily diverted to the French port of Assini due to wars between England and the Ashanti. However, following the suppression of a 1900 revolt, measures were implemented to improve trade routes between Cape Coast and Kumasi.
