# BONE DISEASES AND INJURIES

Bone is the hard tissue that serves as the framework for the animal skeleton. In the human body, specific diseases affecting this tissue include inflammation of the bone and various types of fractures.

## Ostitis and Periostitis
Chronic ostitis and periostitis are characterized by an increased and long-continued vascular supply. These conditions primarily affect the shafts of the bones and may be caused by rheumatism, syphilis, or injury. Patients typically experience a dull pain in the bone that worsens at night. The affected area becomes tender and thickened, forming a lump known as a "hard node," which is clearly visible via X-rays.

Treatment for these conditions involves keeping the affected limb elevated and rested. To ease pain, fomentations and leeches may be used, while iodide of potassium is cited as the most useful medicinal treatment.

## Septic Osteomyelitis
Septic osteomyelitis is an infective disease. It typically begins in the delicate growing tissue located between the cartilaginous end of the bone and the main part of the shaft, known as the diaphysis. Consequently, the earliest reports of pain often occur near the wrist, elbow, ankle, or just above or below the knee. In infants, the germs may migrate into a neighboring joint, resulting in acute arthritis of infants.

The disease is dangerous because micro-organisms can enter the blood-stream from the damaged bone, potentially causing blood-poisoning. This risk extends to surgeons; if a surgeon pricks their finger during an operation on a case of septic osteomyelitis, they risk blood-poisoning, which could lead to the loss of a finger, a hand, or life.

## Surgical Intervention and Complications
Once the disease is recognized, surgical treatment involves making an incision down to the bone to scrape out and disinfect the affected area using a solution of corrosive sublimate. In some instances, the shaft of the bone must be opened for a significant portion of its length to clear it of pus and germs. 

Complications can include:
* **Sub-periosteal abscess:** This may strip a considerable area of the bone bare, making necrosis likely.
* **Multiple necrosis:** Inflammatory engorgement and devastating abscesses may kill large pieces of several bones simultaneously.
* **Amputation:** If the diaphysis is found loose within a large abscess cavity, immediate amputation may be necessary to save the patient's life. Amputation may also be required later due to grave constitutional disturbance or long-continued suppuration.

Septic ostitis can be distinguished from rheumatism and erysipelas by the presence of central tenderness and thickening.

## Systemic Relations and Other Bone-Related Conditions
Chronic bone disease can have systemic effects on other organs. For example, albuminoid disease of the kidney—a condition where the kidneys are irritated by toxic products—is seen as a result of chronic bone disease.

Additionally, certain constitutional causes produce lesions in the oral cavity and teeth. These include rickets, scurvy, and poisoning from mercury or lead.

## Historical Perspectives on Bone Health
In the feudal age, physicians in China classified diseases by the four seasons. While they did not categorize bone diseases under a specific season, they utilized a system of drugs derived from minerals, grains, trees, herbs, and living creatures. Within these classes, medicines were categorized by flavor; specifically, drugs with a "sour" flavor were used for the purpose of nourishing the bones.
