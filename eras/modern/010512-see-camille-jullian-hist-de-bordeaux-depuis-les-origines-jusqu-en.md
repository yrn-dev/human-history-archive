# Bordeaux

Bordeaux is a French city that has experienced significant religious, political, and architectural transformations from the early modern period through the late 19th century.

## Religious and Political Unrest
The city was a site of religious tension during the era of the reformed religion, which attracted numerous adherents in Bordeaux. Following the massacre of St Bartholomew, nearly three hundred inhabitants of the city lost their lives. 

The 17th century was characterized by further disturbance. Bordeaux served for a period as the primary support for the Fronde. Royal measures were met with insurrections on two separate occasions, in 1653 and 1675, necessitating the deployment of troops to repress the uprisings.

## Urban Development and Commerce
In the middle of the 18th century, Bordeaux entered a phase of architectural and commercial activity. The marquis de Tourny, acting as the *intendant* of Guienne, implemented improvements to the city's infrastructure by laying out public squares and widening the streets.

The city's commercial fortunes fluctuated in later years. Under the rule of Napoleon I, the commerce of Bordeaux was greatly reduced.

## Revolutionary and Imperial Era
During the French Revolution, Bordeaux served as the headquarters for the Girondists. The city subsequently suffered during the Reign of Terror, experiencing severity comparable to that of Marseilles and Lyons.

In 1814, the city declared its support for the house of Bourbon. Following this, Louis XVIII granted the title of duc de Bordeaux to his grand-nephew, who is more commonly known as the comte de Chambord.

## Modern History
In 1870, as German forces approached Tours, the French government was transferred to Bordeaux from that city.
