# Ceylon

## Population and Demographics
Ceylon is categorized within the archive's records as having a population of "high type, brown and mixed," totaling 3,568,824. This population is part of a larger grouping of Ceylon and the Eastern Colonies, which together number 5,144,954. The most significant racial groups inhabiting the island are the Sinhalese, numbering 2,000,000, and the Tamil, numbering 954,000. These two groups constitute the greater part of the population. Other inhabitants include Malays, Arabs, Chinese, Eurasians, and various other groups.

## Geography and Urban Centers
Kurunegala serves as the chief town in the north-western province of Ceylon. The town has a population of 6,483, while the surrounding district contains 249,429 people. Kurunegala is situated under Adagalla, known as the rock of the Tusked Elephant, which reaches a height of 600 feet. The town is a center for the cultivation of cocoa, coffee, tea, coco-nut, and rice.

## History and Infrastructure
Between A.D. 1319 and 1347, Kurunegala served as the residence for the kings of Ceylon. In terms of transportation, the Northern railway reached Kurunegala in 1902, located 59 miles from Colombo. This railway was subsequently extended another 200 miles to reach the northernmost coast of the Jaffna Peninsula.

## Religion and Chronology
The religion of the great bulk of the people in the region extending from the borders of the Sind desert and the river Sutlej through to Ceylon is Hindu or Brahminical, although Mahommedans are often numerous and may be the majority in certain areas.

The archive contains specific chronological data regarding the Buddha as recorded in the Ceylon Chronicles. According to these chronicles, the interval between the death of the Buddha and the accession of Asoka to the throne was 218 years. Based on the date of Asoka's accession (approximately 270 B.C.), this places the death of the Buddha at 488 B.C. and his birth at 568 B.C. However, the dates accepted in Ceylon, Siam, and Burma are approximately half a century earlier, citing 623 B.C. for his birth and 543 B.C. for his death.
