## Political Transitions in Denmark
The year 1670 saw a change in leadership in Denmark following the death of Frederick III on February 9th. Prior to his passing, Frederick III had entrusted his royal counsellor, Schumacher, with several secrets. These included the location of a new regalia and a throne made of walrus ivory embellished with three silver life-size lions, both of which were concealed in a vault beneath the royal castle. 

On his death-bed, Frederick III recommended Schumacher to his son, advising that he "Make him a great man, but do it slowly!" On February 9, 1670, Schumacher delivered a sealed packet known as the *Kongelov* to the successor, Christian V. After a private meeting lasting approximately one hour, Christian V appointed Schumacher as his "Obergeheimesekreter." This began a period of rapid promotion for Schumacher; by May 1670, he was named privy councillor and an excellency, and by July 1670, he was ennobled as Griffenfeldt, a title derived from the gold griffin on his escutcheon.
