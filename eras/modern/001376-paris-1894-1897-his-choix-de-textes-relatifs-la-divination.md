# A. Boissier and Works on Assyro-Babylonian Divination

## Overview of A. Boissier's Contributions
A. Boissier is a scholar whose work focuses on the study of ancient Mesopotamian texts, specifically those concerning omens and divination. His contributions to the field include the publication of specialized documents and curated selections of texts that analyze the practices of the Assyrian and Babylonian civilizations.

## Documents Assyriens Relatifs aux Presages
Between 1894 and 1897, A. Boissier published *Documents assyriens relatifs aux presages* in Paris. This work is categorized among the monographs and special texts bearing on the religion of the ancient Babylonians and Assyrians. It serves as a primary resource for understanding the "presages" (omens) utilized in these ancient societies.

## Choix de Textes Relatifs a la Divination Assyro-Babylonienne
Following his earlier work on omens, Boissier published *Choix de textes relatifs a la divination assyro-babylonienne* in Geneva between 1905 and 1906. This publication provides a selection of texts specifically focused on the methods and theories of divination practiced by the Assyro-Babylonian people.

## Context within Mesopotamian Religious Studies
The works of A. Boissier exist alongside a broader body of research into the religious and mystical practices of the region. Other related studies include:
*   **Magic and Sorcery:** L. W. King's *Babylonian Magic and Sorcery* (1896) and Ch. Fossey's *La Magie assyrienne* (1902).
*   **Astrology and Astronomy:** The study of "Chaldean" astrology is explored in the works of Ch. Virolleaud (*L'Astrologie chaldeenne*) and the reports of magicians and astrologers from Nineveh and Babylon compiled by R. C. Thompson (1900).
*   **General Religion:** Comprehensive surveys of the period include Morris Jastrow's *Religion Babyloniens und Assyriens* (1904) and A. H. Sayce's *Religions of Ancient Egypt and Babylonia* (1902).
