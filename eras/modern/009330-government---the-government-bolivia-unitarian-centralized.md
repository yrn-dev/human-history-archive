# Government of Bolivia

## National Foundation and Early Governance
The establishment of Bolivia as a distinct and independent nation occurred in August 1825. Following the supreme command of General Sucre in Upper Peru, an assembly of fifty-four deputies from various provinces met at the capital, Chuquisaca. After deciding that Upper Peru should remain separate from the Argentine provinces, the assembly issued a formal declaration of independence and gave the country the name Bolivia.

Early governance was marked by instability and constitutional shifts. In December 1824, Bolivar convoked a constituent congress, which invested him with dictatorial power for one year due to the unsettled state of the country. On May 25, 1826, Bolivar presented a project for a constitution. This code was controversial for proposing a president for life who would hold executive authority without responsibility and possess the power to nominate a successor. This proposal caused alarm among republicans in Chile and Buenos Aires, while in Peru, Bolivar was accused of attempting to unite Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia into a single state under his perpetual dictatorship.

Following this period, repeated risings occurred, including the 1827 expulsion of General Sucre and his Colombian troops from La Paz. A new congress formed at Chuquisaca in April 1828, which modified Bolivar's constitution and appointed Marshal Santa Cruz as president. After a brief period of disorder led by General Blanco, Santa Cruz restored quiet by 1831, promulgated a code of laws bearing his name, and organized the country's financial affairs.

## Structure of the Republic
The government of Bolivia is defined as a "unitarian" or centralized republic. While representative in form, it is described as autocratic in several important particulars. The constitution in force in 1908 was originally adopted on October 28, 1880.

### The Executive Branch
The executive branch is presided over by a president and two vice-presidents. These officials are elected via direct popular vote for four-year terms and are ineligible for re-election for the immediately succeeding term. The president is supported by a cabinet consisting of five ministers of state:
* Foreign relations and worship
* Finance and industry
* Interior and fomento
* Justice and public instruction
* War and colonization

Every act of the executive must be countersigned by a minister of state. The minister is held responsible for the enforcement and character of the act and may be prosecuted before the supreme court if the act is illegal.

### The Legislative Branch
Legislative power is held by a national congress composed of two houses:
* **The Senate:** Consists of 16 members (two from each department) elected by direct popular vote for six-year terms, with one-third of the members retiring every two years.
* **The Chamber of Deputies:** Consists of 72 members elected for four-year terms, with one-half retiring every two years.

Congress meets annually for sessions lasting sixty days, which can be extended to ninety days. The two chambers possess separate and concurrent powers as defined by the constitution. In the event of impeachment trials, the Chamber of Deputies acts as the prosecutor while the Senate serves as the court. Additionally, the Chamber of Deputies is responsible for electing the justices of the supreme court.

## Suffrage and Political Control
The right to vote is restricted to male citizens. To exercise suffrage, a citizen must be at least twenty-one years old if single, or eighteen years old if married. Furthermore, the citizen must be able to read and write and must either own real estate or possess an annual income of 200 bolivianos (excluding income earned as a servant). Because of these requirements, the electoral body is small and remains under the control of a political oligarchy that effectively rules the country regardless of which party holds power.

## Territorial and Diplomatic Relations
Bolivian governance has been heavily influenced by conflicts and treaties with neighboring states, particularly Chile. Following a war from 1878 to 1881, Bolivia lost control of the railways in its Litoral department. A peace treaty signed on December 11, 1883, resulted in Bolivia ceding its entire sea-coast, including the port of Cobija, to Chile.

Long-standing disputes continued regarding the Chilean occupation of the former Bolivian provinces of Arica and Tacna. While there was a prospect in 1895 for Chile to grant Bolivia an outlet to the sea in exchange for recognition of Chilean ownership of those provinces, secret negotiations between Bolivia and Argentina caused Chile to withdraw its conciliatory stance. 

Under the leadership of President Pando and his successor, Ismail Montes (who took office in 1904), Bolivia ratified a treaty in 1905. Under this agreement, Bolivia ceded the maritime provinces occupied by Chile since 1881, leaving the republic without a seaport. In exchange, Chile agreed to provide free transit through Chilean territory to certain coastal towns, pay a cash indemnity, and construct a railway at its own expense from Arica to the capital, La Paz.
