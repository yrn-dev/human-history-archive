# The Bonapartes of Baltimore

The Bonapartes of Baltimore represent a branch of the Bonaparte family that settled in the United States. This lineage descended from Jerome Bonaparte and his union with Elizabeth Patterson.

## Jerome Bonaparte and Elizabeth Patterson
Jerome Bonaparte was born on November 15, 1784, at Ajaccio. In his early years, he shared the fortunes of his family during the French Revolution and received an education at Juilly. In 1800, he was called to join his brother, the First Consul of France. While serving in the Consular Guard, Jerome fought a duel with the younger brother of General Davout, during which he was wounded. He was later transferred to the navy and cruised in the West Indies.

While blockaded by a British cruiser, Jerome left his ship and traveled through the United States. In Baltimore, he fell in love with Miss Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of William Patterson, a Baltimore merchant. Elizabeth was born in 1785 and was likely descended from Robert Paterson, the figure who served as the original for Sir Walter Scott's "Old Mortality." Despite being a minor at the time, Jerome married Elizabeth in Baltimore on December 24, 1803.

## Conflict with Napoleon
The marriage was greatly disliked by Napoleon, who was annoyed by Jerome's disregard for the laws of France and military discipline. Napoleon refused to recognize the legality of the union. However, the marriage was valid under American law, and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void.

In 1805, Jerome brought his wife to Europe, but the emperor ordered her to be excluded from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but these efforts were in vain. In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. Meanwhile, Elizabeth proceeded to Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son. 

By August 1806, Jerome returned to France after damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic. Napoleon subsequently made him a prince of France and placed him in command of a division of South Germans during the 1806 campaign. Following the events at Jena, Jerome received the surrender of several Prussian towns.

## The Kingdom of Westphalia and Later Life
An imperial decree eventually annulled the marriage to Elizabeth Patterson. Napoleon then united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the new kingdom of Westphalia. 

During his reign in Westphalia, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for running deeply into debt. Despite these personal issues, the kingdom saw some benefits from its connection to France: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, education received an impulse in its higher departments, and repressive laws against the Jews were removed.

Jerome died in 1860.

## Descendants in America
Jerome and Elizabeth Patterson had one child, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870). Born in England, he resided primarily in Baltimore. It was noted that he bore a marked resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. He maintained a good relationship with his father, from whom he received a large allowance for some time, and the two met occasionally.

The younger Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte had a son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893). This grandson of the original Jerome entered the French army and served in Italy and the Crimea.

Elizabeth Patterson, often referred to as Madame Bonaparte or Madame Patterson, returned to Baltimore after a stay in England following her forced separation from Jerome. She died in 1879.
