# The Triumvirate

The "triumvirate" was a political group active during the French Constituent Assembly. It consisted of three members: Antoine Pierre Joseph Marie Barnave, Adrien Duport, and Alexandre de Lameth. The group was initially popular during its time in the Assembly.

## Members of the Group

### Antoine Pierre Joseph Marie Barnave
Born on October 22, 1761, in Grenoble, Dauphine, Barnave was a member of a Protestant family. He was educated by his mother and trained in law, gaining early recognition at the age of twenty-two for a discourse on the division of political powers delivered before the local parlement. Barnave was a prominent voice in the early stages of the revolution, authoring a pamphlet in May 1788 titled *Esprit des edits enregistres militairement*. 

He served as a deputy for the *tiers etat* of his native province in the states-general convoked at Versailles on May 5, 1789. Within the Assembly, Barnave was a friend to many leaders of the popular party. His contributions included drawing up the first address to the king, participating in the conference regarding the claims of the three orders, and supporting Sieyes' proposal for the Assembly to declare itself National. Until 1791, he was a primary member of the club that later became known as the Jacobins, for which he drafted the first rules and manifesto. While Barnave was a passionate lover of liberty, he sought to maintain the French monarchy alongside that freedom, though he participated in attacks on the clergy, church property, the monarchy, and provincial parlements.

### Adrien Duport
Born in Paris in 1759, Adrien Duport was a learned jurist and an influential advocate in the parlement. He rose to prominence through his opposition to Lomenie de Brienne and Calonne, the ministers. In 1789, the *noblesse* of Paris elected him to the states-general. 

During the Constituent Assembly, Duport focused on the organization of the French judiciary. He is particularly noted for a report delivered on March 29, 1790, in which he advocated for the implementation of trial by jury, although he failed to secure the jury system for civil cases. 

### Alexandre de Lameth
Alexandre de Lameth was the third member of the group known as the triumvirate during the period of the Constituent Assembly.

## Political Shift and Dissolution
The unity of the triumvirate and its members' political alignments shifted following the flight of the king to Varennes. Adrien Duport sought to defend the king, serving as a member of the commission tasked with questioning him and attempting to provide excuses for his actions. On July 14, 1791, Duport opposed the formal accusation against the king. This position led Duport to leave the Jacobins and join the Feuillant party.

## Later History of Adrien Duport
Following the Constituent Assembly, Duport served as the president of the criminal tribunal of Paris. He was arrested during the insurrection of August 10, 1792, but escaped, likely due to the complicity of Danton. He returned to France after the 9th of Thermidor of the year II, but was forced into exile again following the republican *coup d'etat* of the 18th of Fructidor of the year V. Duport died in 1798 in Appenzell, Switzerland.
