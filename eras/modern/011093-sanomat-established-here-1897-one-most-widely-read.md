# Amerikan Sanomat and Ashtabula

## Overview of Ashtabula
Ashtabula is a city located in Ashtabula township within Ashtabula county, Ohio, U.S.A. Situated on the Ashtabula river and Lake Erie, the city is positioned 54 miles northeast of Cleveland. The city is built on the high bank of the river, approximately 75 feet above the lake, providing views of diversified scenery. 

The city's history dates back to the first settlement around 1801. Ashtabula township was created in 1808, later leading to the formation of the townships of Sheffield, Plymouth, and Kingsville. The village of Ashtabula was incorporated in 1831 and subsequently received a city charter in 1891. The name "Ashtabula" is derived from an Indian word meaning "fish river," which was first applied to the river.

## Population and Culture
The population of Ashtabula has seen steady growth over the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1890, the population was 8,338; by 1900, it had grown to 12,949, including 3,688 foreign-born residents. By the 1910 census, the population reached 18,266. 

The city and the surrounding county are home to a large population of Finnish birth. This community is served by the *Amerikan Sanomat*, a Finnish weekly newspaper established in 1897. The publication is noted as being one of the most widely read Finnish weeklies in the country. Additionally, the city provides a public library for its residents.

## Economy and Industry
Ashtabula possesses an excellent harbour that serves as a major hub for the shipment of coal and iron ore. The port is particularly significant for iron ore; it receives more iron ore annually than any other port in the country, and potentially the world. This ore is transported by rail to iron manufacturing centres such as Youngstown and Pittsburg. In 1907, the port recorded the receipt of 7,542,149 gross tons of iron ore and the shipment of 2,632,027 net tons of soft coal.

The city's manufacturing sector includes:
* Foundry and machine shop products
* Agricultural implements
* Worsted goods
* Leather

In 1905, the total value of factory products was $1,895,454, representing a 114.3% increase over the previous five years. Furthermore, the region is known for large greenhouses located in and near the city. Under glass, quantities of tomatoes, cucumbers, and lettuce are raised and shipped to large cities, including Pittsburg.

## Transportation
The city is well-connected via several railway systems, including:
* The New York, Chicago & St Louis railways
* The Lake Shore & Michigan Southern
* The Pennsylvania railway

Additionally, Ashtabula is served by inter-urban electric lines.
