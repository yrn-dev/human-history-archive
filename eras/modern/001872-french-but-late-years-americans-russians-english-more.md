# Baden-Baden

## Overview and Geography
Baden-Baden (distinguished from other places of the same name) is a fashionable watering-place and town located in the grand-duchy of Baden, Germany. It is situated on a hill 600 feet high within the valley of the Black Forest, approximately 23 miles south by west of Karlsruhe. The town is connected to Karlsruhe via a branch of the Mannheim and Basel railway. While the town proper is located on the right bank of the Oos, the primary resorts for visitors are situated on the left bank.

## Tourism and Demographics
The town is renowned for its gardens, extensive pleasure-grounds, promenades, and the brilliance of the life led during the season, which attracts crowds of visitors from across the globe. The resident population has grown over several decades, recorded at 12,779 in 1885, 14,862 in 1895, and 16,238 in 1905. Despite the size of the resident population, the number of annual visitors exceeds 70,000. 

Historically, the prevailing nationality of these visitors was French until the war of 1870. In more recent years, however, English, Russian, and American visitors have become more numerous.

## Thermal Springs and Health
Baden-Baden is noted for its twenty-nine hot springs, which flow from the castle rock at a rate of 90 gallons per minute. The temperature of these waters varies from 37° to 54° R. (115° to 153° Fahr.). The water is transported through the town via pipes to supply various baths, including two chief bathing-establishments that are considered the most elegant in Europe. The waters are used as specifics for various internal complaints, such as uric acid and stone, as well as for neuralgia, paralysis, skin diseases, and chronic gout and rheumatism.

## Architecture and Culture
The town features several significant buildings, including a library, a reading-room, a theatre, a picture-gallery, a *Trinkhalle* (pump-room), and a *Conversationshaus*. While public gaming-tables were once a striking feature of the town, they have since been abolished.

The town's religious architecture reflects its international visitors:
* **The Parish Church:** Dating from the 15th century, this is the only building of significant antiquarian interest besides the castles and contains the tombs of several margraves.
* **Other Churches:** The town includes a Lutheran church, a Russian church featuring beautiful frescoes, and an English church built in the Norman style of architecture.
* **The Greek Chapel:** Located on the Michaelsberg, this chapel features a gilded dome and was erected over the tomb of a son of the Rumanian prince Michel Stourdza, who died in 1863.
