# Antoine Pierre Joseph Marie Barnave

## Early Life and Education
Antoine Pierre Joseph Marie Barnave was born on October 22, 1761, in Grenoble, located in Dauphine. He was born into a Protestant family; his father served as an advocate at the parlement of Grenoble, and his mother was characterized as a woman of noble character, superior ability, and high birth. Due to his Protestant faith, Barnave was unable to attend school and was instead educated by his mother. This upbringing produced a man who was studious, thoughtful, social, passionate, graceful in manners, and handsome in person.

## Early Legal and Political Career
Barnave pursued the study of law. At twenty-two years of age, he gained favorable recognition through a discourse he delivered before the local parlement regarding the division of political powers. As the revolution began to stir, Dauphine was among the first provinces to experience this excitement. Barnave became a leading voice for the general sentiment of the region, publishing a pamphlet titled *Esprit des edits enregistres militairement le 20 mai 1788*. Following this, he and his father were elected as deputies to the states of Dauphine, where Barnave played a prominent role in the debates.

## Role in the French Revolution
In May 1789, the states-general were convoked at Versailles. Barnave was chosen as a deputy of the *tiers etat* representing his native province. He quickly made an impression on the Assembly and established friendships with most of the leaders of the popular party. During the Constituent Assembly, he formed a group known as "the triumvirate" alongside Alexandre Lameth and Adrien Duport.

Barnave's contributions to the early revolutionary government were significant:
* He participated in the conference regarding the claims of the three orders.
* He drafted the first address to the king.
* He supported the proposal by Sieyes for the Assembly to declare itself National.
* He drafted the first rules and the manifesto for the club that later became known as the Jacobins, of which he was a principal member until 1791.

## Political Philosophy and Conflict
Barnave was a passionate lover of liberty, yet he maintained the hope of simultaneously securing the monarchy and the freedom of France. Despite these goals, he was carried away by the currents of the era and participated in attacks directed at the clergy, the monarchy, the provincial parlements, and church property.

## Legacy and Works
Barnave died in 1793. His *Oeuvres posthumes* were published in four volumes by Berenger (de la Drome) in 1842.
