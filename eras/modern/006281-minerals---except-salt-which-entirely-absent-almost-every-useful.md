# Minerals

The distribution and extraction of minerals vary significantly across different global regions, ranging from the metallic ores of Europe to the coalfields of India and the diverse chemical compositions of various salts.

## Regional Mineral Deposits

### Bohemia and the Carpathians
Bohemia possesses a wide array of useful metals and minerals, with the notable exception of salt, which is entirely absent. Lignite and coal are the most important in terms of value and quantity. Lignite fields are located in the north-east corner of Bohemia near Teplitz, Ossegg, Falkenau, Dux, and Brux. Coal is mined in several areas, including Pilsen, Kladno, Rakonitz, Buschtehrad, Nurschan, and Schlan; the oldest mines in the region are found at Radnitz, dating to the 17th century.

The region is also a center for the iron industry, with foundries located near Konigshof and Kladno and iron ores found at Nucic and Krusnahora. Other extracted metals include silver (at Joachimsthal and Pribram), lead (at Pribram), and tin (at Graupen in the Erzgebirge), the latter being the only location in Austria where tin is found. Additionally, Bohemia produces antimony near Tabor (Milleschau), uranium and radium near Joachimsthal, graphite near Budweis and Krumau, and porcelain-earth near Carlsbad. Other available minerals include cobalt, nickel, arsenic, copper, alum, sulphur, building stone, and precious stones such as the pyrope (Bohemian garnet). The region also yields large quantities of asphalt and peat, particularly in the south-west.

The Carpathian mountain system is described as the richest in metallic ores in Europe. It contains zinc, lead, iron, copper, silver, gold, coal, petroleum, and salt.

### India and Other Regions
In Bengal, coal is the primary mineral product, competing with the gold of Mysore for premier importance in India's mining industries. Major coalfields include Raniganj (500 sq. m.), Jherria (200 sq. m.), and Daltonganj (200 sq. m.), while the small Karharbari field (11 sq. m.) produces the best coal in the region. Twenty-five other coalfields exist in initial development stages.

In other districts, such as the Tamañ Peninsula and Maikop, petroleum wells are found, with those in Tamañ covering over 570 sq. m. This region also contains silver, zinc, iron ores, soda, salt, alabaster, and Epsom salts. Coal is also found on the Kubañ and its tributaries, though extraction is limited to less than 10,000 tons per year.

## Mineral Waters and Salts

### Therapeutic Springs
Mineral springs are frequently used for bathing and drinking. In Marienbad, Bohemia, there are eight springs. The Rudolfsbrunnen is an earthy-alkaline spring, while the Karolinenbrunnen and Ambrosiusbrunnen are among the strongest iron waters globally. The Ferdinandsbrunnen and Kreuzbrunnen contain cold alkaline-saline waters. These waters are used to treat obesity, diabetes, gout, and liver affections. Marienbad also utilizes mud-baths featuring peat that is the richest in iron in the world. Approximately 1,000,000 bottles of mineral water are exported from here annually. Other notable mineral waters are found at Psekup and Tamañ.

### Chemical Compositions of Salts
Various salts occur naturally in mineral forms:

*   **Epsom Salts:** Heptohydrated magnesium sulphate ($\text{MgSO}_4\cdot7\text{H}_2\text{O}$), also known as *magnesii sulphas*. It is found in sea water and mineral waters at Seidlitz, Pullna, and Epsom. In nature, it appears as hair-salt (fibrous excrescences) or reichardite (compact masses), and is associated with gypsum at Montmartre and limestone in the Mammoth Caves of Kentucky.
*   **Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$):** This constitutes limestone rocks and chalk deposits. It is found in the minerals calcite and aragonite, as well as in travertine and tuff in volcanic districts.
*   **Calcium Sulphate ($\text{CaSO}_4$):** This forms the minerals anhydrite and, in hydrated form, alabaster, gypsum, and selenite.
*   **Calcium Phosphates:** The normal salt ($\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$) is the main inorganic part of bones and is found in the mineral apatite and concretions called coprolites. It also constitutes the minerals sombrerite, osteolite, and ornithite.
*   **Calcium Silicates:** These are abundant and include wollastonite (tabular spar). They are also present in zeolites, felspars, amphiboles, pyroxenes, and olivine.

## Industrial and Medical Applications
Minerals are utilized across various sectors. Epsom salts are used in medicine as a hydragogue purgative for febrile diseases and painters' colic (often combined with potassium iodide to eliminate lead), and in industry for dyeing and weighting cotton fabrics. Calcium orthophosphate is used in the production of "superphosphate of lime," an artificial manure created by treating coprolites or ground bones with sulphuric acid.
