# Wohltemperirtes Klavier, the Solo Violin and Violoncello Sonatas

The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* (The Well-Tempered Clavier), along with the solo sonatas for violin and violoncello, are central instrumental works composed by Johann Sebastian Bach. These compositions are primarily associated with Bach's tenure as *Kapellmeister* to the duke of Coethen, a position he held between 1717 and 1723.

## Das Wohltemperirte Klavier
*Das wohltemperirte Klavier* is a collection written for the clavichord. It consists of two books, each containing 24 preludes and fugues. These pieces are composed in every major and minor key. The objective of this structure was to encourage the use of "equal temperament" tuning, which prevents the euphony of remoter keys from being sacrificed for the sake of more common keys.

The two books were composed at different times, reflecting a shift in Bach's style toward designs on a larger scale. The first book dates to 1722, while the second book was completed in 1744. In terms of scale, the projected *Das Orgelbuechlein* (a collection of short choral-preludes) would have been a larger volume than the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* had it been fully executed.

## Solo Violin and Violoncello Works
Bach composed a series of works for solo string instruments, which are categorized as chamber music. These include:
* **Solo Violin:** Six sonatas, which consist of three sonatas and three partitas.
* **Solo Violoncello:** Six suites.

These works, like the first book of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier*, were produced during the Coethen period.

## Editions and Scholarship
The *Bach-Gesellschaft* (B. G.) provides the only complete edition of Bach's works, though the quality of its volumes varies. The *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* appeared in *Jahrgang* xiv. of the B. G. and was edited by Kroll, who also made the text available in the *Edition Peters*. This was a significant development, as the previous *Edition Peters* version by Czerny was considered a result of corrupt tradition. Later, Kroll's text was supplemented using evidence from an autograph found in the British Museum.

The archive notes that the Steingraeber edition of the clavier works, edited by Dr. Hans Bischoff, is regarded as incomparably the best. This edition distinguishes the editor's phrasing signs and nuances from Bach's own rare indications and provides comprehensive explanations of ornaments and variants in footnotes.
