# Lake Balkhash

Lake Balkhash (also referred to as Balkhash) is a lake located in Asiatic Russia within the Kirghiz steppes. It is situated between the governments of Semiryechensk and Semipalatinsk, positioned between 45° to 47° N. latitude and 73° 30' to 79° E. longitude. The lake is located approximately 600 miles to the east of Lake Aral.

## Physical Characteristics
Lake Balkhash is the fourth largest lake in Eurasia, covering an area of 7,115 square miles. It sits at an altitude of 900 feet. The lake is shaped like a broad crescent, measuring about 430 miles in length from the west-south-west to the east-north-east, with its concave side facing south. Its width varies between 36 and 53 miles.

The lake's depth varies by location, with its greatest depth of 35 feet occurring along the north-west shore. While the lake has no outlet, its waters remain relatively fresh.

## Geography and Environment
The shores of the lake differ significantly in character. The north-western shore is bordered by a dreary plateau known as the Famine Steppe, or *Bek-pak-dala*. In contrast, the south-east shore is low; evidence suggests that this shore previously extended as far as the Ala-kul and the Sasyk-kul.

The lake generally freezes from November to April. Regarding its surface level, the Kirghiz declared in 1903 that the surface had been rising steadily for the ten years prior, although it had been dropping before that period.

## Hydrology and Fauna
The primary feeder for Lake Balkhash is the Ili, which originates in the Tian-shan Mountains' Khantengri group. Other rivers entering from the north-east include the Ayaguz, while the Lepsa, the Aksu, and the Karatal enter from the south-east. The Karatal, Aksu, and Lepsa rivers struggle to pass through reeds and sands that were covered by the lake in the recent past.

The fauna of the lake and its tributaries, which were explored by Nikolsky, is more similar to the fauna found in the rivers of the Tarim basin than to that of Lake Aral. Notably, the lake does not contain the common frog.

## Geological History and Research
Based on faunal evidence, it is considered probable that Lake Balkhash once communicated with the lake that filled the Lukchun depression (located at 42-1/2° N. lat and 89-1/2° E. long) via the lakes Ayar (Telli-nor) and Ebi-nor. However, research indicates that a connection with Lake Aral was improbable in recent times. The lake has been the subject of investigation by L. S. Berg, as noted in *Petermanns Mitteilungen* (1903).

The lake is known by different names among various peoples: the Chinese call it *Si-hai*, and the Kirghiz refer to it as *Ala-denghiz* or *Ak-denghiz*.
