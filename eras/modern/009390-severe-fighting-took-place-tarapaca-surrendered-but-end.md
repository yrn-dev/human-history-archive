# Military Conflicts and Surrenders in Global History

The historical record contains numerous instances of severe fighting preceding the surrender of strategic positions, ranging from ancient city-states to modern territorial disputes. These events often involve protracted sieges, sudden assaults, or hard-fought campaigns that result in the transfer of territorial control.

## The War for Tarapaca and Southern Peru
In the late 19th century, conflict erupted between Chile and Peru. The tensions were exacerbated by a secret treaty and a 1875 Peruvian law that created a monopoly over the nitrate beds of Tarapaca, which prejudiced Chilean enterprise. After Peru refused demands for the annulment of the secret treaty and an assurance of neutrality, Chile declared war.

Chilean naval superiority allowed for the landing of troops to confront allied forces at Arica and other southern points. Following the capture of Bolivian ports, a sea attack on Pisagua routed troops under the Peruvian general Buendia, opening a path into southern Peruvian territory. This defeat led General Daza to lead Bolivian troops in a disorderly retreat to Tacna. While Iquique was occupied without a struggle, the province of Tarapaca only surrendered after severe fighting. By the end of 1879, the Chileans were in complete possession of the province.

## Sieges and Battles in Europe
The town of Fuenterrabia, located in Spain, served as a strategic point frequently contested between France and Spain. It was the site of several unsuccessful French attempts to seize the town in 1476 and 1503, though the French were successful in 1521 before the fortress was retaken in 1524. In 1638, the prince of Conde suffered a severe repulse there, leading Philip IV to grant the town the rank of city. Fuenterrabia surrendered to French troops under the duke of Berwick in 1719 after a severe siege, and fell again to the French in 1794. Later, in 1813, the duke of Wellington forced a passage into France at the ford opposite the town. The location also saw severe fighting during the Carlist War in 1837.

In southern Spain, the town of Bailen was the site of a significant event during the Peninsular War. After several days of hard fighting, the French general Dupont signed the capitulation of Bailen on July 23, 1808. This resulted in the surrender of 17,000 men to the Spaniards and marked the first severe blow the French suffered in that war.

## Conflicts in East Asia
Japanese history records various instances of fierce combat and surrender. During the era of the Ashikaga, Takauji and Tadayoshi pushed toward Kyoto after shattering their opponents. This led to several days of fierce fighting on the east and south of the capital; however, while the Ashikaga held the advantage, the victory was not decisive. In an earlier period, the Yemishi exhibited varying levels of docility. In 655, a Japanese squadron of 180 vessels under Hirafu attacked the Yemishi at Akita. This campaign ended in a rehearsed surrender where the Yemishi claimed their bows and arrows were for hunting rather than fighting.

In more modern conflict, the siege of Port Arthur concluded on the last day of 1904. The Japanese investing force, under General Nogi, faced a determined defense by General Stossel. After a severe repulse in August, the Japanese utilized engineering arts to advance. The most critical point of the operation was the capture of 203-Metre Hill on November 30th. This followed several days of the most terrible fighting ever witnessed, resulting in ten thousand Japanese casualties. The hill was essential for guiding heavy artillery against Russian ships in the harbour.

## Ancient Greek and Roman Engagements
In ancient history, the city of Byzantium experienced a sudden assault by Alkibiades. While a fleet created a disturbance in the harbour, Alkibiades landed his soldiers and marched to the walls in silence. Despite the surprise, the town did not surrender without fighting. Peloponnesians, Megarians, and Boeotians drove the Athenians back to their ships before engaging the land forces in a severe battle. Alkibiades and Theramenes eventually emerged victorious, taking approximately three hundred prisoners.

Additionally, the area of Bailen in Spain is identified as the ancient Baecula, where P. Cornelius Scipio the elder achieved signal defeats over the Carthaginians in 206 and 209 B.C.
