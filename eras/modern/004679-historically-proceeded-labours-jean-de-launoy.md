# Jean de Launoy

Jean de Launoy (1603-1678) was a French scholar and critic whose historical and textual labors contributed to significant religious and political revisions in France and influenced historical interpretations of the early medieval period.

## Influence on the French Breviaries
During the 17th and 18th centuries, a movement of revision emerged in France that succeeded in modifying approximately half of the country's Breviaries. This movement proceeded historically from the labors of Jean de Launoy and Louis Sébastien le Nain de Tillemont. Launoy, referred to as "le dénicheur des saints," worked alongside Tillemont to demonstrate the falsity of numerous lives of the saints.

While the theological impetus for these changes came from the Port Royal school—which emphasized communion with God over the invocation of saints—the practical application involved adopting a rule that all responses and antiphons be written in the exact words of Scripture. This change effectively removed appeals to created beings. The resulting reformed French Breviaries, such as the 1736 version by Archbishop Charles Gaspard Guillaume de Vintimille and the 1680 version by Archbishop François de Harlay, were characterized by a deep knowledge of Holy Scripture and simplified, shortened services. These reforms also made the weekly use of the entire Psalter a reality, whereas it had previously been a mere theory in the Roman Breviary due to the frequency of saints' day services.

The influence of these French models, alongside Gallican and Jansenist influence, extended into Germany and Italy. In Germany, Breviaries based on these models were published in towns including Mainz, Münster, and Cologne. However, a strong Ultramontane movement later arose against these texts during the pontificate of Pius IX. Led by literary advocates such as Louis François Veuillot and Dom Gueranger, a Benedictine monk and abbot of Solesmes, this movement succeeded in suppressing the reformed Breviaries. The diocese of Orleans was the last to surrender in 1875.

## Historical Criticism and Political Analysis
Beyond his work on liturgical texts, Launoy is noted for his scholarly treatment of early historical events. He, along with Natalis Alexander and Pagi, applied learning and attention to the subject of the deposition of Childeric. In these analyses, Launoy and his contemporaries exhibited a strong bias toward preserving the independence of the crown. 

In examining the establishment of the Carlovingians, Launoy contended that the strong language used by Eginhard—specifically the claims that Childeric was deposed by the order (*jussu*) and authority (*auctoritate*) of the Roman Pontiff—was susceptible to a "very soft interpretation."
