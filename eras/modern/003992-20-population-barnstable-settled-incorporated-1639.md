# Barnstable

## Overview and Geography
Barnstable is a seaport township located in Massachusetts, U.S.A., where it serves as the county-seat for the county of the same name. The township is situated across Cape Cod, positioned between Nantucket Sound to the south and Cape Cod Bay to the north. The geography of much of the county is characterized by scattered woods, beach-grass, salt-marshes, and regions of sands.

## History and Population
Barnstable was settled and incorporated in 1639, while the county was created later in 1685. Notable natives of the township include Lemuel Shaw and James Otis. 

According to the 1900 census, the population of Barnstable was 4,364, which included 391 foreign-born residents. By 1910, the U.S. census recorded the population as 4,676. On a broader scale, the county experienced a population decline of 20.1% between the years 1865 and 1895.

## Economy and Industry
The economic basis of Barnstable has shifted over the centuries. During the 17th century, the economy relied on whale and mackerel fisheries. Over time, the whale fishery was replaced by cod and other fisheries, although the fishing industry is now considered relatively unimportant. Current fisheries include other shell fisheries and oysters.

The township's soil is well adapted to agriculture, distinguishing it from other parts of the county. The primary industry currently involves the growing of vegetables and the production of poultry and milk for its various villages. Additionally, cranberries are raised in large quantities. Many of the township's villages now function as summer resorts.

## Infrastructure and Education
Barnstable is served by the New York, New Haven & Hartford Railway. In the field of education, the township is home to a co-educational state normal school at Hyannis, which was established in 1897.
