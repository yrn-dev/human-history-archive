# Factors Influencing Summer Heat and Temperature

The variation of summer temperatures across different geographic regions is determined by a combination of solar positioning, atmospheric conditions, and the physical characteristics of the land surface.

## Solar Influence and Latitude
In areas north of the tropic of Cancer, very high summer temperatures are observed. These are accounted for by the increased length of the day in higher latitudes, which more than compensates for the loss of heat resulting from the smaller mid-day altitude of the sun. For example, the difference in the heating power of the sun's rays at noon on June 21st between latitude 20 deg. and latitude 45 deg. is approximately 2%. However, the accumulated heat received during the day is about 11% greater in the higher latitude, where the day lasts 15-1/2 hours compared to 13-1/4 hours in the lower latitude.

## Modifying Atmospheric and Environmental Factors
A significant modifying cause of temperature in the tropical region is the occurrence of periodical summer rains. These rains tend to greatly arrest the increase of summer heat in the areas where they prevail, giving the heat peculiar characteristics.

The physical nature of the terrain also plays a critical role in temperature regulation:
*   **Surface Sterility and Moisture:** In regions such as central Asia, Afghanistan, and Persia, the sterility of the surface—caused by small rainfall—operates to increase summer heat. Solar heat accumulates on dry surface soil and cannot be released upwards via evaporation, as would occur if the soil were moist or covered with vegetation. Similarly, this heat cannot be conveyed downwards as it is over the ocean.
*   **Oceanic Proximity:** The proximity of the ocean provides a moderating effect, particularly along the eastern and southern parts of Asia where the land consists of peninsulas or islands.
*   **Elevation:** Great elevations above sea level in central Asia, Persia, and Afghanistan tend to exaggerate winter cold.

## Regional Temperature Characteristics
In northern Asia, summers are hot but short, with maximum heat levels that are only slightly less than those found in tropical countries further south. In contrast, the soil in Siberia near Yakutsk remains permanently frozen to a depth of over 380 ft., where temperatures stay 5 or 6 deg. Fahr. below the freezing point; summer heat only thaws the surface to a depth of approximately 3 ft.

Other regional variations include:
*   **The Plateau and Coasts:** In certain plateau regions, summers are very hot with usually cool nights. On some south coasts, summer heat is very great, while on north coasts, summer heat can be damp and enervating.
*   **Wind Effects:** In some coastal areas, the *inbat* (north wind) blows almost daily from noon until near sunset during the summer.

## Atmospheric Pressure and Global Cycles
Summer heat affects barometric pressure by expanding air upwards, which disturbs the planes of equal pressure and causes the upper strata of air to flow out of the heated area. This contributes to a general diminution of barometric pressure over Asia during summer months.

On a broader scale, climatic contrast is influenced by three independent systems of change: the precession of the equinoxes, changes in the eccentricity of the orbit, and the change in the obliquity of the equator to the orbit. When the orbit is most eccentric, the equator most tilted, and summer occurs at aphelion or perihelion, climates reach their extremest. In such periods, the heat of a brief hot summer may be partly reflected back into space by white snow, making it unable to melt all the winter's ice.
