## Records on Frederick the Great and Prussian History
Documentation regarding the life and reign of Frederick the Great includes general works by v. Taysen, Preuss, and Carlyle, who are noted as being of particular importance. The archive also contains Frederick's own works and specialized military histories. These include the Prussian official *Die I. und II. schlesischen Kriege* (1890-1895), Bernhardi's *Friedrich der Grosse als Feldherr* (1881), and v. Schoening's *Die 5 erste Jahre der Regierung Friedrichs des Grossen*. 

Other relevant texts include Gruenhagen's *Gesch. des I. schlesischen Krieges* (1881-1882), Orlich's *Gesch. der schlesischen Kriege*, and Duncker's *Militaerische, &c., Aktenstuecke zur Gesch. des I. schles. Krieges*. For broader context on the era, the archive lists v. Arneth's *Maria Teresias ersten Regierungsjahre* (1863), the Austrian official *Kriege der Kaiserin Maria Theresia; Gesch. des oesterr. Erbfolgekrieges* (from 1895), and Deroy's *Beitraege zur Gesch. des oesterr. Erbfolgekrieges* (1883). Additional perspectives are provided by Crousse regarding the Belgian provinces (1885), and works by Francis Parkman (1892) and F. H. Skrine (1906).

## French Monarchy and Diplomacy
The archive contains significant records on the reigns of Louis XIV and Louis XV. For Louis XIV, the court life is documented through the memoirs of Saint-Simon and Madame de Motteville (covering the Fronde), as well as the letters of Madame de Sévigné. The king's own ideas are preserved in the *Mémoires de Louis XIV. pour l'instruction du dauphin*, while his private life is detailed in the letters of the Duchesse d'Orléans and Madame de Maintenon. Historians such as Michelet and Voltaire have also written on the king's private life and century.

Regarding Louis XV, the archive highlights the *Secret du roi*, a system of organized espionage and secret diplomacy. This is documented in Albert duc de Broglie's *Le Secret du roi. Correspondance secrète de Louis XV. avec ses agents diplomatiques 1752-1774* (1878). A general account of the reign is provided by H. Carré in *La France sous Louis XV.* (1891).

## Danish and Scandinavian History
The history of Denmark is categorized into general, early/medieval, and modern periods. General works include *Danmarks Riges Historie* (1897-1905), R. Nisbet Bain's *Scandinavia* (1905), and H. Weitemeyer's *Denmark* (1901). Early and medieval history is covered by Saxo's *Gesta Danorum* (1886) and the *Repertorium diplomaticum regni Danici mediaevalis* (1894).

Records from the 16th to 19th centuries include the works of Peter Edvard Holm, who wrote on the internal history of Denmark and Norway from 1660 to 1814. Other specific accounts include Marcus Rubin's work on Frederick VI and Christian Frederick von Holten's memories of the deutsch-dänische Krieg (1900).

## Intellectual and Philosophical Biographies
The archive preserves detailed bibliographies for several thinkers:

*   **René Descartes:** Biographical works include A. Baillet's *La Vie de M. Des Cartes* (1691), Elizabeth S. Haldane's *Descartes, his Life and Times* (1905), and J. P. Mahaffy's *Descartes* (1902). Primary sources include the *Lettres de M. Descartes* edited by Claude Clerselier (1657).
*   **Samuel Butler:** His works include *Hudibras* and a prose pamphlet against the Puritans (1672). The archive notes that Robert Thyer edited *Genuine Remains in Verse and Prose of Mr Samuel Butler* (1759), which includes *The Elephant in the Moon*. Dr. Zachary Grey's 1744 edition of *Hudibras* serves as the basis for later editions.
*   **Horace Bushnell:** Documentation includes *The Spirit in Man: Sermons and Selections* (1903), with a bibliography by Henry Barrett Learned. Biographies were written by Mrs. M.B. Cheney (1880) and Dr. Theodore T. Mungers (1899).
*   **Christian Ludwig Ideler:** A German astronomer and chronologist (1766-1846), Ideler published the *Handbuch der mathematischen und technischen Chronologie* (1825-1826) and *Die Zeitrechnung der Chinesen* (1839).
