# Harnai Loop of the Sind-Pishin Railway

The Harnai loop is a segment of the Sind-Pishin railway located in Baluchistan. It serves as an alternative route from Sibi to Quetta, starting in a direction exactly opposite to that of the Bolan route and entering the hills at Nari. Although the Harnai route is longer than the Bolan route, it is the alignment adopted for all ordinary traffic, while the Bolan loop is reserved for emergency use.

## Geography and Topography
The railway and the surrounding plains and passes divide Baluchistan into two distinct parts. To the south-west of this dividing railway lies the great block of Southern Baluchistan, where drainage generally trends toward the Arabian Sea or the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora. To the north of the railway, between the plains of the Indus and Afghanistan, are the long ridges of the Suliman system's central ranges.

The railway provides access to the Quetta-Pishin plateau, which acts as the central dominant water-divide of Baluchistan and the base of the Kandahar highway. This plateau is reached via the drainage of the Nari and Bolan basins, both of which drain south-eastwards from the plateau into the irrigated flats of the alluvial bay known as Kach Gandava. This bay extends 130 miles from the Indus to Sibi.

## Surrounding Peaks and Quetta
The Sind-Pishin railway's Harnai loop is flanked to the east of Quetta by Khalifat, one of the grandest peaks and precipices in Baluchistan. Quetta itself is situated on an open plain 5,500 feet above sea level, formed by high-level tributaries of the Lora. The city is overlooked by several prominent heights ranging from 10,500 to 11,500 feet:
* **Khalifat**: Located to the east.
* **Takatu**: Located to the north.
* **Chahiltan (Chiltan)**: Located to the south-west.
* **Murdar**: A great square-headed peak located to the south.

## Regional Demographics
The area characterized by curved wrinkles and twisted flexures at the end of the Suliman system is occupied by true Baluchis. Specifically, the Bugti and Marri sections of the great Rind confederation of tribes, who claim an Arabic origin, reside here. To the north of these groups are the Bozdars, another Rind clan. These Rind tribes are an exception to the general pattern of Pathan occupation in northern Baluchistan. In contrast, the most prominent Pathan tribal divisions in the region are the Dumars and Kakars of Pishin, along with the Mando Khel of Zhob.
