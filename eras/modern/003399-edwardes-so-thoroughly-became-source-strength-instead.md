# Administration of the Bannu District

## Geography and Demographics
The district of Bannu is located north of the Indus river. Since the constitution of the North-West Frontier Province in 1901, the district has consisted of the Marwat and Bannu tahsils, covering an area of 1680 sq. m. The valley of Bannu proper is shaped as an irregular oval, measuring approximately 40 m. from east to west and 60 m. from north to south, extending to the foot of the frontier hills.

The region is defined by its waterways; in addition to the Indus, the Gambila and the Kurram (which flows into the Indus) are the other streams passing through the district. The Indus is navigable for native boats throughout its 76 m. course within the district, though it is not bridged at any point there.

As of 1901, the population of the district was 231,485, with the great majority being Mahommedans. The primary tribes inhabiting the area include:
*   **Waziri Pathans**: Recent immigrants from the hills who are generally good cultivators and peaceable.
*   **Marwats**: A Pathan race residing in the lower, sandier portions of the valley.
*   **Bannuchis**: Described as a mongrel Afghan tribe characterized by mean vices and bad physique.

## Governance and Security
The inhabitants of the Bannu district have a history of stubborn resistance against their predecessors, specifically the Sikhs and Afghans, and have always been very independent. Following the annexation of the Punjab, the valley was administered by Herbert Edwardes. His administration was so thorough that the region became a source of strength rather than weakness during the Mutiny.

While the inhabitants of the valley itself are currently peaceful, the area remains subject to incursions from the neighbouring hills and the Waziri tribes located in the Tochi valley. The frontier border is primarily occupied by the Dawaris, Battannis, and Waziris.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The chief export of the Bannu district is wheat. The region also possesses mineral resources; alum is obtained in the neighbourhood of Kalabagh, where salt is also quarried on government account. 

In terms of infrastructure, a military road connects Bannu town to Dera Ismail Khan. Regarding regional administrative changes, the cis-Indus portions of Dera Ismail Khan and Bannu now make up the new Punjab district of Mianwali.
