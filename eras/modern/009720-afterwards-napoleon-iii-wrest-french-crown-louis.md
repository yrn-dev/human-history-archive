# The French Crown and the House of Bonaparte

The history of the French crown during the 19th century was marked by a succession of regimes, including the First Empire under Napoleon I, the reign of Louis-Philippe, and the subsequent rise of Napoleon III. These transitions involved complex familial dynamics, the creation of satellite kingdoms, and shifting political alliances across Europe.

## The First Empire and Louis Bonaparte
Following his victory at Austerlitz on December 2, 1805, Napoleon I sought to establish a ring of allied states surrounding the French Empire. As part of this strategy, he proclaimed his brother, Louis, as the King of Holland on June 6, 1806. However, the relationship between the two brothers was fraught with tension. Napoleon frequently criticized Louis for being too lenient with his subjects and for seeking excessive popularity.

The rupture between the brothers deepened due to the rigour of the continental system and Louis's jealousy regarding his wife. In 1808, Napoleon offered Louis the vacant throne of Spain, but after Louis refused, the position was given to Joseph. By late 1809, Napoleon resolved to annex Holland to stop secret trade with England. Despite Louis's attempts in Paris to secure better terms for Holland and a divorce from Hortense, he failed in both. In January 1810, Napoleon annexed the island of Walcheren, claiming Louis had failed to defend French interests during the British Walcheren expedition of 1809. French troops subsequently occupied Bergen-op-Zoom and Breda. 

The crisis culminated in 1810 when Napoleon sent troops against the Dutch capital. Louis fled his kingdom and eventually settled in Toplitz, Bohemia. On July 9, 1810, Napoleon formally annexed Holland to the French Empire. Louis spent his remaining years in Rome, focusing on philosophic and literary studies and the fortunes of his sons. He took pleasure in his sons' devotion to the democratic and national cause in Italy between 1830 and 1831, though this was marred by the death of his eldest son, Napoleon Louis.

## The Reign of Louis-Philippe
Following the abdication of Napoleon I in 1814 and the subsequent reigns of Louis XVIII and Charles X, Louis-Philippe came to power in 1830 after ousting Charles X. Louis-Philippe was described by Queen Victoria as possessing "vast knowledge" and "great activity of mind," noting that he was "thoroughly French" in character, characterized by liveliness and talkativeness. However, Victoria also noted his tendency toward "tricks and over-reachings," specifically citing his cunning in transactions regarding Spanish marriages, which ultimately led to his downfall and ruined his reputation across Europe.

During his reign, Louis-Philippe's influence extended to the formation of the new kingdom of Belgium. The Belgian congress initially offered the crown to the Duke of Nemours, the younger son of Louis-Philippe. However, England opposed this, fearing Belgium would fall under direct French influence, and threatened war if the offer was accepted. Consequently, Louis-Philippe declined on behalf of his son. To quiet the resulting jealousy and maintain ties, Louis-Philippe married his daughter, Louise of Orléans, to the elected King of the Belgians, Leopold I, in 1832.

## Napoleon III and the Second Empire
Napoleon III became Emperor of the French in 1852. His reign involved significant international interventions, including the patronage of Archduke Maximilian of Austria. Under Napoleon III's support, Maximilian served as Emperor of Mexico for several years, accompanied by 2,000 Belgian volunteers. However, Napoleon III abandoned Maximilian when political troubles erupted in Mexico. Maximilian was eventually captured and shot at Queretaro in 1867 by order of Juarez.

The era of Napoleon III also saw the continuation of the French ducal system. While the *ancien regime* had various classes of dukes, republican France recognized existing titles as badges of ancestry. New ducal families were created under the empires of Napoleon I and Napoleon III, with the latter creating titles such as Magenta, Morny, and Malakoff.

Napoleon III's reign ended following his declaration of war against Prussia in 1870. Paris surrendered in 1871, leading to the Peace of Frankfort and the establishment of the King of Prussia as the "German Emperor."

## Bonapartist Succession
Following the fall of the empire, the Bonapartist party continued. Prince Napoleon Victor Jerome Frederic (born 1862), the eldest son of Jerome, became the recognized head of the French Bonapartist party upon his father's death. Jerome's second son, Prince Louis Napoleon, served as an officer in the Russian army and was appointed governor of the Caucasus in 1906. Another relative, Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine (daughter of Jerome), resided in Paris after the election of Louis Napoleon to the presidency of the republic, where she served as a patron of letters and art until her death in 1904.
