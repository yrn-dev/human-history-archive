# BADAJOZ

Badajoz is a frontier province located in western Spain. Formed in 1833 from districts previously belonging to the province of Estremadura, it is the largest province in the kingdom. Its boundaries are defined by Caceres to the north, Cordova and Ciudad Real to the east, Seville and Huelva to the south, and Portugal to the west. As of 1900, the province had an area of 8,451 square miles and a population of 520,246.

## Geography and Climate
The landscape of Badajoz consists largely of a monotonous and desolate plain that is either flat or slightly undulating, though low ranges of hills exist in several districts. The primary river is the Guadiana, which flows from east to west across the north of the province. Fed by numerous tributaries, the river-beds only fill with significant volumes of water during certain seasons, often allowing the river to be forded with ease.

The climate is characterized by extreme temperatures, with fierce north and north-west winds blowing across the plains during the winter and great heat in the summer. In the Guadiana valley, intermittent fevers are prevalent during the hot months. Rainfall is generally scanty in average years, and only a small proportion of the land is irrigated. Much of the territory is covered by thin forest and bush, or is used as pasture.

## Economy and Industry
Agriculture and the cultivation of fruit, including olives and vines, are in a backward state. However, Badajoz possesses more livestock than any other province in Spain. The province is particularly noted for its acorn-fed swine, which are celebrated throughout Spain for their bacon and hams. Additionally, large herds of goats and sheep thrive in areas where the pasture is too meager for cattle.

Mining efforts are hindered by a lack of communication and water. In 1903, only 26 of nearly 600 registered mines were operational, producing lead and small amounts of copper. Local industries are of limited importance, producing oils, soaps, cork, leather, and coarse cotton and woollen stuffs. Commercial interests are more significant than industrial ones, driven by transit trade with Portugal through seven custom-houses. Transport is difficult, as many parts of the province are accessible only by roads that are in poor condition.

## The City of Badajoz
The capital city of Badajoz is situated on the left bank of the Guadiana river, close to the Portuguese frontier and the Madrid-Lisbon railway. In 1900, its population was 30,899. The city serves as the official residence of the captain-general of Estremadura and is the see of a bishop.

The city is built on a slight eminence overlooking the river, crowned by the ruins of a Moorish castle. Its appearance is one of great strength, featuring a broad moat, outworks, bastions, a strong wall, and forts on the surrounding heights. A magnificent granite bridge crosses the river between the castle-hill and the armed fort of San Cristobal; this bridge was originally built in 1460, repaired in 1597, and rebuilt in 1833. The cathedral, dating from 1258, also resembles a fortress with its massive embattled walls.

The city was the birthplace of the painter Luis de Morales (1509-1586)—whose work is preserved in the cathedral—and the statesman Manuel de Godoy, duke of Alcudia (1767-1851). Local industries in the city include pottery and the manufacture of leather, woollen, and linen goods.

## History
Badajoz is not mentioned by Roman historians and first gained importance under Moorish rule, becoming the capital of a small Moorish kingdom in 1031. Although the Portuguese held the city temporarily in 1168, it remained independent until 1229, when it was captured by Alphonso IX of Leon.

Due to its position as a frontier fortress, the city faced numerous sieges. It was beleaguered by the Portuguese in 1660 and by the Allies in 1705 during the War of the Spanish Succession. During the Peninsular War, the French attacked unsuccessfully in 1808 and 1809. However, on March 10, 1811, the Spanish commander Jose Imaz was bribed into surrendering the city to the French forces under Marshal Soult. A British army led by Marshal Beresford attempted to retake the city; although they defeated a relieving force at Albuera on May 16, the siege was abandoned in June.

## Other Localities
The province includes the town of Don Benito, located near the left bank of the Guadiana on the Madrid-Badajoz-Lisbon railway. Founded in the 15th century by refugees from Don Llorente who were fleeing Guadiana floods, Don Benito is a thriving, modern town. It maintains an active trade in fruit (especially melons), wine, and wheat, and manufactures oil, soap, flour, brandy, cloth, and linen.
