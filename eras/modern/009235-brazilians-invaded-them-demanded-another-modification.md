# Boundary Disputes and Territorial Modifications of Bolivia and Brazil

The boundary between Bolivia and Brazil was established based on the limits of the Portuguese and Spanish colonies. These limits were originally determined by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750), and were subsequently modified by treaties in 1867 and 1903.

## The Northern and Central Boundary
The boundary line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.). From this point, the line ascends the river to a point on the west bank, located 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra. It then moves inland 4 kilometres to a point at lat. 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and long. 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W.

From there, the line follows an irregular course North and East of North toward Lakes Uberaba, Gaiba (or Mandiore), and Mandiore. The boundary then proceeds up the San Matias river and North along the Sierra Ricardo Franco to the headwaters of the Rio Verde, which is a tributary of the Guapore. This specific section of the boundary was turned inland from the Paraguay river to ensure that settlements on the west bank, including Corumba and Fort Coimbra, remained within Brazilian jurisdiction. In 1903, this area was modified by a recession of approximately 1158 sq. m. to Bolivia to facilitate better commercial facilities on the Paraguay.

## The Southern Boundary and the Treaty of Petropolis
The boundary continues along the Madeira, Mamore, Guapore, and Verde rivers down to the mouth of the Abuna (approximately lat. 9 deg. 44' S.), as established by the treaty of 1903. This region was part of the original colonial frontier, which previously ran down the Madeira to a point midway between the Amazon and the Beni, then ran due West to the Javary.

A modification occurred via the treaty of 1867, which moved the starting point to the mouth of the Beni (lat. 10 deg. 20' S.) and established a straight line to the source of the Javary. This change granted a large area of territory to Brazil. However, once the valuable rubber forests of the upper Purus were discovered, Brazilians invaded these forests and demanded a further modification of the boundary line.

This dispute was finally resolved in 1903 through the treaty of Petropolis. The resulting boundary line ascends the Abuna river to lat. 10 deg. 20' S., then follows that parallel West to the Rapirran river. The line follows the Rapirran to its principal source, then proceeds due West to the Ituxy river, following it West to its source. From there, it goes to the source of Bahia Creek, follows it to the Acre (or Aquiry) river, and then proceeds up the Acre river to its source.
