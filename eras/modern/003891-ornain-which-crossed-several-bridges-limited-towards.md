# Bar-le-Duc

Bar-le-Duc is a town located in north-eastern France and serves as the capital of the department of Meuse. It is situated 50 miles east-south-east of Chalons-sur-Marne, positioned on the main Eastern railway line that connects Nancy and Chalons-sur-Marne. As of 1906, the town had a population of 14,624.

## Geography and Layout
The town is divided into two primary sections: a lower town and an upper town. The lower part of the town is more modern and busier, extending along a narrow valley that is enclosed by hills covered in vines or woods. This area is traversed throughout its entire length by the Ornain river, which is crossed by several bridges. 

The boundaries of the town are defined by water features: to the north-east, it is limited by the canal running from the Marne to the Rhine; to the south-west, it is limited by the Canal des Usines, which is a small arm of the Ornain. The upper town, known as the Ville Haute, is situated on the left bank of the Canal des Usines.

## The Ville Haute
The Ville Haute is accessed via steep, narrow thoroughfares and staircases. A long, quiet street intersects this quarter, featuring houses that date back to the 15th, 16th, and 17th centuries. Several historical landmarks are located here:
* **The Chateau of the Dukes of Bar:** Remains of this 16th-century structure exist, though it was dismantled in 1670.
* **The College and Clock-Tower:** Both were constructed during the latter half of the 16th century.
* **Church of St Pierre:** Dating from the 14th and 15th centuries, this church houses a white stone effigy of a half-decayed corpse. This work was created by Ligier Richier (1500-1572), a pupil of Michelangelo, and was erected in memory of Rene de Chalons, who died in 1544.

## Lower Town and Civic Administration
The lower town houses the official buildings and a few churches, though the latter are described as being of little interest. The hotel-de-ville is located in the former house of Charles Nicolas Oudinot, one of the town's distinguished natives who is also honored with a statue.

The town serves as a regional administrative and commercial center, containing the following institutions:
* Tribunals of commerce and first instance.
* A chamber of commerce and a board of trade arbitrators.
* A branch of the Bank of France.
* An art museum.
* Educational facilities, including a lycee and a training-college for girls.

## Industry and Commerce
Bar-le-Duc maintains a diverse industrial base. Its manufacturing capabilities include the production of machinery, hosiery, corsets, flannel goods, wall-paper, and jam. The town also engages in brewing, cotton weaving and spinning, and the dyeing and dressing of leather, as well as iron-founding. The primary articles of commerce for the town are iron, timber, and wine.
