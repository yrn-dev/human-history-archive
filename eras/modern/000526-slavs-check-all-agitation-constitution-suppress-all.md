# Slav National Movements and Austrian Governance

## National Agitation and the Habsburg Monarchy
During the 19th century, the Austrian Empire experienced significant political instability driven by the aspirations of various ethnic groups. Under the reign of Ferdinand I, who succeeded Francis I on March 2, 1835, the government was characterized by weakness and divided counsels due to the emperor's epilepsy, which necessitated a veiled regency. This administrative fragility allowed popular discontent to grow rapidly.

Slav populations across the empire became increasingly restive under the domination of Germans and Magyars. In Bohemia, the Czech literary movement evolved into an organized resistance against the established order. Nationalists in Bohemia adopted the watchword "Repeal," framing their struggle as a criticism of English administration in Ireland. Among the southern Slavs, the "Illyrian" movement emerged around 1836, promoted by Ljudevit Gaj through the *Illyrian National Gazette*. While initially seeking a vague Pan-Slav union, the movement shifted its focus in 1844—following interference from the Austrian government—toward the revival of the "Triune Kingdom" (comprising Dalmatia, Croatia, and Slavonia) independent of the Hungarian crown.

## The Revolutions of 1848 and Concessions
The revolutionary atmosphere of 1848 led to a period where the Austrian government adopted a yielding spirit toward various national claims. In Bohemia, a separate constitution was proclaimed on April 8 after attempts to hold elections for the Frankfort parliament failed. The emperor Ferdinand also declared that equality of rights would be secured for both nationalities in Bohemia and suggested that a general meeting of representatives would decide the reunion of Silesia and Moravia to Bohemia.

Other concessions were made to the southern Slavs, including the confirmation of Baron Joseph Jellachich as ban of Croatia on March 23. In Galicia, Count Stadion proclaimed a new constitution on March 18. Even for groups such as the Rumans and Serbs, who received no formal sanction for their claims, the emperor was viewed as the ultimate guarantee of their success.

## The Despotic Regime of 1851
By 1851, Austria appeared to have overcome its revolutionary difficulties. The revolutionary movements were suppressed, and the Federal Diet of 1815 was restored. Vienna became the center of a despotic, autocratic government. The primary objectives of this regime were to suppress all attempts to secure a free press, check all agitation for a constitution, and Germanize the Slavs and Magyars. For approximately ten years, the dominion existed under this autocratic rule.

The eventual failure of the Habsburg emperor to maintain this despotic system was attributed to three factors: the establishment of Italian unity, the successful claim of Prussia to leadership in Germany, and the Crimean War.

## Regional Variations Among Slav Races
The experience and goals of Slav groups varied by region. The Poles of Galicia remained distinct from other Slav races. While the province had a large minority of Ruthenes (approximately 43%), the Poles were primarily interested in gaining autonomy for their own province and the restoration of the Polish kingdom. They were little influenced by Pan-Slav agitation and preferred a strong, united Austria that provided them with power and freedom, provided the Ruthenes were surrendered to them.

## Religious and Political Intersections
The Roman Catholic Church generally exerted a favorable influence on the Slav races, partly because the Slavs provided more candidates for ordination than the Germans. Conversely, German nationalists in Austria tended to prioritize nationality over religion. This led to a conflict with the Church, which could not acquiesce to the absolute authority of nationality. By 1898, this tension resulted in the "Los von Rom" movement in Styria and other territories, where Germans left the Catholic Church for Old Catholicism or Protestantism. This movement was largely a political demonstration caused by the alliance between the Clerical party and the Slav parties.
