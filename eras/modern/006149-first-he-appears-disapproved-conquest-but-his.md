# Disapproval and Conquest in Historical Contexts

The concept of disapproval, particularly regarding military conquests, administrative decisions, and social institutions, appears across various eras of human history. These instances range from the strategic shifts of military leaders to the political tensions within imperial and national governments.

## Bugeaud and the Conquest of Algeria

In the history of French involvement in Africa, Bugeaud initially appeared to disapprove of the conquest of Algeria. However, his loyalty to Louis Philippe led him to align with the government's position. Bugeaud subsequently proposed the immediate use of all necessary forces to achieve a complete, lasting, and swift subjugation of the region.

Bugeaud was sent to Africa in a subordinate role, where he implemented a system of "flying columns." This strategy led to his first victory on July 7, 1836, followed by a six-week campaign that earned him the rank of lieutenant-general. On June 1, 1837, he signed the treaty of Tafna with Abd-el-Kader; while this act was justified by the political and military situation of the time, it resulted in attacks against Bugeaud in the chamber, which he spent 1839 refuting.

By 1840, Bugeaud was appointed governor-general of Algeria, and in 1841, he fully enforced his flying column system. His energy drove the forces of Abd-el-Kader back, and his leadership earned him the devotion of the rank and file, who called him "Père Bugeaud." He further secured French positions by constructing roads in 1842 and was named marshal of France in 1843. Following a major victory at Isly on August 14, 1844, he was granted the title of duke. Despite a disaster at Sidi Brahim on September 22, 1845, Bugeaud remained active in the field until his retirement in July 1846, which was caused by differences with the home government regarding the future government of the province. During his tenure, the number of French colonists increased from 17,000 to 100,000.

## Roman and Carthaginian Conquests

In the Roman Republic, the Anician family rose to prominence. One hundred and sixty-eight years before the Christian era, the family was ennobled when Anicius served as Prætor and ended the Illyrian war through the conquest of the nation and the capture of its king. Later, the Anician family became the first members of the Roman senate to embrace Christianity. A member of the family, Probus, served as Prætorian praefect four times and shared the consulship with Gratian. While the public may have disapproved of the methods used to acquire Probus's immense estates, he was admired by strangers and appreciated by his clients for his magnificence and generosity.

Regarding the conquest of Spain, the Carthaginians faced warlike nations that defended themselves with courage. The conquest was protracted and difficult because the various districts and peoples of Spain were detached from one another and lacked mutual correspondence. Had the Spaniards formed a single state and assisted one another, the Carthaginians might not have accomplished their design. While the Romans eventually invaded Spain as their first continental province, it was the last they subdued, requiring over 200 years of vigorous opposition before it was entirely subjected to Roman power.

In ancient Greece, the Scionaeans initially included individuals who disapproved of the actions being taken. However, they eventually gained confidence in Brasidas, welcoming him as the liberator of Hellas and crowning him with gold.

## Cultural and Philosophical Disapprovals

Disapproval also extended to intellectual and social practices in antiquity. Plato disapproved of tragedy on the same grounds that he disapproved of rhetoric. In ancient Chios, while the institution of slavery was universal and not disapproved of, the actual slave-trade—the buying and selling of slaves—was considered more or less odious.

In Ethiopia, the practice of electing a dog as king involved a unique form of governmental disapproval. The dog was kept in great state with guards and officers; if the dog growled, it was interpreted as disapproval of the manner in which the government was being conducted. These indications were either obeyed implicitly or interpreted according to the interests and caprice of the worshippers.
