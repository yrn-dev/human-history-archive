# BADMINTON

Badminton is a fast-paced game played with rackets and shuttlecocks. The game's name is derived from the Duke of Beaufort's seat located in Gloucestershire. While the game appears to have first been played in England around 1873, it was played in India prior to that time, where it remains very popular.

## History and Organization
The Badminton Association in England was established in 1895. The laws of the game were developed from a code of rules created in 1887 for the Bath Badminton Club, which were themselves based on the original Poona rules of 1876. 

Competitive championships were instituted in the All England championships starting in 1899 for mixed doubles, ladies' doubles, and gentlemen's doubles. In 1900, championships for ladies' singles and gentlemen's singles were added. The first championship between Ireland and England took place in 1904.

Regarding official publications, *Lawn Tennis and Badminton* served as the official organ of the Badminton Association until October 1907, after which the *Badminton Gazette* took over the role in November 1907. Additional information on the laws of the game is published annually in London as the *Laws of Badminton and the Rules of the Badminton Association*.

## Equipment and Court Specifications
The game is played in a hall that should be at least 18 feet high. In England, it is almost always played in a covered court and can be played using either artificial light or daylight.

The court features a net that is 30 inches deep and ranges from 17 to 24 feet in length, depending on the position of the posts. The top of the net is edged with a 3-inch wide white tape and stands 5 feet from the ground at the center and 5 feet 1 inch at the posts. Net posts are placed either on the side boundary lines or up to 2 feet outside them.

The dimensions of the court vary by game type:
*   **Four-handed (Double) game:** The distance between posts is 20 to 24 feet.
*   **Two-handed (Single) game:** The court width is reduced to 17 feet, and the distance between posts is 17 to 21 feet. In this version, long service lines are dispensed with, and the back boundary lines serve as the long service lines.

The shuttlecock (or shuttle) consists of 16 feathers, measuring between 2-1/2 and 2-3/4 inches in length, and weighs between 73 and 85 grains. The racket is strung with strong fine gut and typically weighs about 6 oz, though there are no specified requirements for its weight, shape, or size.

## Gameplay and Techniques
Badminton consists entirely of volleying. It is noted for being extremely fast, with a single game requiring more staying power than a single at lawn tennis. Players utilize skill and judgment through techniques such as "smashing" (a hard downward stroke) and "dropping" (hitting the shuttle gently just over the net).

A shuttle is considered "in play" from the moment it is struck by the server's racket until it touches the ground, touches the net without crossing over, or a "fault" occurs.

### Service and Scoring
In the four-handed game, the first server stands in the right-hand half court and serves to the opponent in the opposite right-hand half court. Once the shuttle is hit, players may stand anywhere on their side of the net. 

The "in" side (the serving side) is the only side that can score an ace. If the "out" side (non-serving side) makes a fault, the "in" side scores an ace and the players on the "in" side switch half courts. The server then serves from the left half court to the opponent in the opposite left half court. If the "in" side makes a fault, the server loses their "hand" (serve) to the player who was served to, but no score is recorded.

In the first innings of a game, the starting side has only one "hand." In all subsequent innings, every player on each side has a "hand," and partners serve consecutively.

### Lets and Faults
A "let" occurs if the server misses the shuttle entirely during a service attempt, or if the shuttle strikes the net during service but still falls into the proper half court. In these cases, the stroke does not count and the server must serve again.

A "fault" is called under several conditions:
*   **Service errors:** If the service is overhand (the shuttle is struck higher than the server's waist), if the shuttle does not fall into the diagonally opposite half court, or if the server or receiver has a foot on or outside the line of their respective half courts before the shuttle is struck.
*   **Play errors:** If the shuttle falls outside the court, passes under or through the net, hangs in the net, or touches the side walls, roof, or a player's person or dress.
*   **Technical errors:** If the shuttle is hit before it reaches the striker's side, if a player touches the net or its supports, if the shuttle is struck twice by the same player or successively by partners, or if the shuttle is "spooned" (caught on the racket rather than distinctly hit).
*   **Conduct:** If a player willfully obstructs an opponent.
