# F. S. Arnot and the Barotse Region

## Early Western Presence
The pioneers of Western civilization in the Barotse region were missionaries rather than agents of chartered companies. Among these early figures was F. S. Arnot, an Englishman who spent two years in the country between 1882 and 1884. Following this period, in 1884, a mission was established by the Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris, which yielded fruitful results.

## Missionary Work and Francois Coillard
The first agent of the Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris was Francois Coillard (1834-1904). Prior to his arrival in the Barotse region, Coillard had been involved in mission work in Basutoland. He dedicated the remainder of his life to the Barotse. While Coillard was an admirer of British institutions and desired that the country eventually fall under British jurisdiction, he initially sought to delay the arrival of white men in the country to protect the interests of his mission.

## British Suzerainty and Administration
The establishment of British rule in the region was formalized through a charter granted to the British South Africa Company in October 1889. This charter permitted the company to establish rule in regions north of the Middle Zambezi that were not part of Portuguese dominions. Subsequently, a treaty signed on June 11, 1891, between Great Britain and Portugal declared the Barotse kingdom to be within the British sphere of influence.

To manage the region, the British South Africa Company entered into friendly relations with Lewanika, the paramount chief of the Barotse. The company appointed an administrator to reside in the country and raised a native police force commanded by a British officer. Additionally, district commissioners and magistrates were appointed. Despite these administrative steps, the company did not interfere in the internal affairs of the Barotse, and relations between the British and the Barotse remained uniformly friendly.

## Territorial Boundaries and Protectorate
The western limits of Barotseland were the subject of a dispute between the contracting powers, which was eventually settled through the arbitration of the king of Italy. In an award dated May 30, 1905, the frontier was fixed at the Kwando river as far north as 22deg E., then following that meridian up to 13deg S., continuing east along that parallel to 24deg E., and finally following that meridian to the frontier of the Belgian Congo.

Regarding the political status of the region, Lewanika petitioned the "Great White Queen" to assume a protectorate over his dominions. This petition was made contrary to the advice of Francois Coillard.
