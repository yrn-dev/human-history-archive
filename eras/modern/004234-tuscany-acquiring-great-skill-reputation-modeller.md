# Tuscany

Tuscany is a region in Italy that has been associated with various political figures, religious leaders, and artists throughout history.

## Political Administration and Governance
In the Roman era, Tuscany was part of a larger administrative district. Historical records, specifically a two-column inscription, note an individual who served as the Corrector of Tuscany and Umbria. This official held numerous other civil and religious honors, including serving as the Præfect of Rome and the Prætorian præfect of Italy and Illyricum, before his death before the year 385.

In later history, the region was governed by the Grand-dukes. Leopold II served as the grand-duke of Tuscany, during which time he earned a reputation as a liberal and enlightened ruler. However, upon becoming the ruler of the Habsburg empire, Leopold II shifted his approach toward traditional Habsburg methods and a policy of stability, reverting to the supremacy of the Church and the old machinery of estates and diets.

## Religious Figures
Tuscany is the birthplace of Saint Bonaventura (also known as John of Fidanza), a Franciscan theologian born in 1221 at Bagnarea. Destined for the church by his mother, he is said to have received the name Bonaventura from St Francis of Assisi following a miraculous cure. Bonaventura entered the Franciscan order in 1243 and later studied in Paris. His reputation grew rapidly; by 1256, he was elected general of his order. He was later rewarded with the titles of bishop of Albano and cardinal by Gregory X and attended the great council of Lyons in 1274, where he died.

## Arts and Sculpture
The region has been home to notable artists, including the sculptor Lorenzo Bartolini (1777-1850), who was born in Vernio. Bartolini first established himself as a skilled modeller in alabaster before moving to Paris in 1797 to study painting under Desmarets and sculpture under F. F. Lemot. He gained fame and influential patrons after winning the second prize of the Academy in 1803 for his bas-relief titled "Cleobis and Biton."

Bartolini's most significant patron was Napoleon, who commissioned a colossal bust from him and sent him to Carrara to establish a school of sculpture. Following the fall of Napoleon, Bartolini resided in Florence until his death. His body of work consists of a vast number of busts and several highly regarded pieces, including "Faith in God," "Hercules and Lichas," and the group of Charity. In Italy, popular opinion often associates his skill as a sculptor with those of Canova and Thorwaldsen.
