# World War I: The War to End All Wars

## Overview
World War I, occurring between 1914 and 1918, was one of the deadliest conflicts in human history and a defining moment of the modern era. Often referred to as "The Great War," it signaled the collapse of the old world order—marked by the fall of four great empires—and the birth of modern industrial warfare.

The conflict was ignited by a complex web of mutual defense treaties, imperial competition, and fervent nationalism. While the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary acted as the immediate catalyst, the underlying tensions had been simmering for decades. The war is most infamous for the stalemate of trench warfare on the Western Front, where millions of soldiers endured horrific conditions for negligible territorial gains. By the time the Armistice was signed on November 11, 1918, the geopolitical map of the world had been permanently altered, setting the stage for the social and political upheavals of the 20th century.

## Key Facts
*   **The Belligerents:** The war was fought between the **Allied Powers** (primarily France, Great Britain, Russia, Italy, and later the United States) and the **Central Powers** (primarily Germany, Austria-Hungary, and the Ottoman Empire).
*   **Technological Leap:** WWI saw the first widespread use of chemical weapons (poison gas), tanks, airplanes for reconnaissance and combat, and submarines (U-boats).
*   **Total War:** This was a "total war," meaning nations mobilized their entire populations. Women entered the workforce in unprecedented numbers to support the war effort, which accelerated the movement for women's suffrage.
*   **Human Cost:** An estimated 9 to 11 million military personnel and millions of civilians perished, not only from combat but from the 1918 Influenza Pandemic that swept through exhausted populations.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Christmas Truce:** In December 1914, unofficial ceasefires occurred along the Western Front, where soldiers from both sides stepped into "No Man's Land" to exchange gifts, sing carols, and even play soccer.
2. **The War Horse:** Horses remained vital for transport and cavalry, with approximately 8 million horses dying during the conflict.
3. **A New Map:** The war resulted in the creation of several new sovereign nations, including Poland, Czechoslovakia, Yugoslavia, and the Baltic states, following the dissolution of the Austro-Hungarian, Ottoman, German, and Russian empires.
