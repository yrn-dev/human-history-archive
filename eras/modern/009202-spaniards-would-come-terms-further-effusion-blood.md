# The Republic of Colombia and the Conflict with Spain

## Formation of the Republic of Colombia
Following his return to Angostura, which was celebrated as a national festival, Bolivar was hailed as the father and deliverer of his country. During this period of high distinction, he secured the enactment of a fundamental law on December 17, 1819. This law united the republics of New Granada and Venezuela into a single state known as the Republic of Colombia, with Bolivar serving as president. The seat of government was provisionally moved to Rosario de Cucuta, located on the frontier of the two provinces.

## Military Campaigns and the Truxillo Armistice
Bolivar returned to the field leading the best-appointed and most numerous army the republicans had assembled to that date. This force allowed him to gain significant advantages over the Spaniards, who were under the command of Morillo. On November 25, 1820, an armistice of six months was concluded at Truxillo. This agreement was reached in the hope that the Spaniards would come to terms, thereby sparing further effusion of blood.

However, these hopes were disappointed. Morillo was recalled from his position and replaced by General Torre. As the armistice expired, a renewal of the conflict became inevitable.

## The Battle of Carabobo and Independence
Determined to deliver a decisive blow, Bolivar encountered General Torre at Carabobo. The resulting battle ended in a complete rout of the Spanish forces. The remains of the shattered Spanish army were forced to seek refuge in Puerto Cabello, where they eventually surrendered to Paez two years later. 

The victory at Carabobo is regarded as the event that ended the war in Venezuela. Bolivar entered Caracas on June 29, 1821, and by the end of that year, the Spaniards had been driven from every part of the province, with the exception of Puerto Cabello.

## Establishment of Political Institutions
To secure the independence that had been dearly purchased, permanent political institutions were established. On August 30, 1821, the constitution of Colombia was adopted with general approbation. Under this new political structure, Bolivar continued as president, and Santander served as vice-president.
