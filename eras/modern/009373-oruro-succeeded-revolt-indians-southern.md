# Oruro

Oruro is a city and department located within the republic of Bolivia. It has served as one of the four cities to function as the national capital, alongside Sucre, La Paz, and Cochabamba.

## Geography and Demographics
Oruro serves as the capital of the department of the same name. According to the census of 1900, the department of Oruro covers an area of 19,127 square miles. The total population of the department was recorded as 86,081, with the capital city of Oruro having a population of 13,575. These population figures include an estimated number of wild Indians and a 5% addition for sundry corrections and omissions.

## History and Political Conflict
Oruro was a site of significant military action during the War of Independence in Upper Peru (the region that later became Bolivia). Between July 1809 and August 1825, the region experienced nearly uninterrupted warfare between the Spanish royalist forces and the South American patriot forces. In 1815, four years of alternating victories ended with a total rout of the patriot forces in a battle that took place between Oruro and Potosi. This defeat was followed by a revolt of Indians from the southern provinces of Peru, which was joined by numerous Creoles seeking the independence of the entire country, though the royalists quickly suppressed the insurrection.

In the late 19th century, Oruro was again central to national political instability. Following an attempt in December to establish Sucre as the perpetual capital, La Paz rose in revolt. The insurgent leader, Colonel Pando, marched upon Oruro and entered the town on April 11, 1899, after achieving a complete defeat of government troops. Following this victory, Colonel Pando formed a provisional government and was subsequently elected president without opposition, taking office on October 26.

## Education and Infrastructure
Oruro is a center for specialized higher education. The city hosts a university that provides instruction in law. Additionally, Oruro is the site of a mining and civil engineering school.

In terms of transportation and commerce, Oruro is connected to other regions via railway. The Antofagasta and Oruro railway meets the Pulacayo branch at Uyuni. Furthermore, there is an important projected railway extending from Viacha to Oruro.

## Religion
Oruro is part of the archbishopric of La Plata, which has its seat at Sucre. This archbishopric encompasses several areas, including the departments of Potosi, Tarija, Chuquisaca, and Oruro, as well as the Chilean province of Antofagasta.
