# Llanos de Chiquitos

The Llanos de Chiquitos are a region of low alluvial plains located in Bolivia. Situated between the Andes and the Chiquitos sierras, this area is characterized by a more diversified surface and a higher general elevation than the plains located further north.

## Geography and Hydrography
The Chiquitos region is part of the three-fifths of Bolivia composed of gently undulating forest regions, great swamps, flooded bottomlands, and low alluvial plains. The area is influenced by the river systems of the Amazon, specifically the Guapay (also known as the Grande). The Guapay rises in the Cordillera Oriental, flows east to the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones, and then turns north in a magnificent curve that skirts the Llanos de Chiquitos before uniting with the Mamore river at Junta de los Rios.

## Cattle-Raising Industry
Stock-raising was one of the earliest industries established in the country following mining. The industry in the Chiquitos and Mojos llanos was initiated by Jesuit founders who brought cattle with them when they entered the region to work among the Indians. Consequently, these plains became well stocked and have since provided a consistent supply of beef for nearby inland markets.

While the soil and climate are well-suited for cattle, the development of the industry and the improvement of stock have been hindered by high transportation costs and the inaccessibility of the region. In other parts of Bolivia, such as the llanos of Tarija and Chuquisaca, large herds are also sustained on well-watered plains of the Chaco and elevated districts. To serve home markets, *saladeros* are used to prepare beef, typically through sun-drying and salting.

## Population and Social Structure
The population of the Chiquitos and Mojos regions consists of Indians who were organized into industrial communities by the Jesuits during the 17th century. These tribes are described as industrious and peaceable, serving as an important element of labor. 

Following the expulsion of the Jesuits, these populations became less prosperous, though they continued to live in organized communities, preserving the religion and industries taught to them. The archive notes that these groups often exist in a state of peonage and suffer from profound ignorance, which contributes to a lack of ambition to improve their condition. Additionally, there is a noted addiction to *chica*, a native beer produced from Indian corn.
