# Pennsylvania and West Virginia

## Colonial Origins of Pennsylvania
The colony of Pennsylvania was founded by the Quaker Penn, who maintained a positive relationship with Charles II. Penn established the colony to the north at Philadelphia. The geographical boundaries of Pennsylvania, specifically its borders with Virginia and Maryland, were delimited by two men, Mason and Dixon. This boundary became known as the "Mason and Dixon line," which later served as a significant line in the affairs of the United States.

## Political Development and Voting Systems
In the late 19th century, Pennsylvania was part of a broader movement toward the adoption of new ballot systems. In 1891, Pennsylvania was among seventeen states and two territories that adopted the Australian ballot system. While some states, such as Massachusetts, Arkansas, and Texas, utilized the Massachusetts arrangement, Pennsylvania adopted the system of party groups. Under this specific arrangement, Pennsylvania organized all candidates who were not part of a party-group in alphabetical order on the ballot.

## Agriculture and Natural Resources
Pennsylvania is one of the states in the temperate regions of the United States where apples, pears, and plums are grown with advantage. Additionally, the state is part of a strawberry-growing region that extends from Florida to Virginia, facilitating a natural succession of crops from south to north for various market centers.

The state also possesses valuable mineral deposits. As of 1908, Pennsylvania was listed as one of the American states producing marble, though it ranked behind states such as Vermont, Georgia, Tennessee, New York, and Massachusetts in terms of value.

## West Virginia and Regional Industry
West Virginia is mentioned in the context of regional geography and transportation. The city of Wheeling, West Virginia, is located approximately five miles north of Bellaire, Ohio. This region is served by several railway lines, including the Pennsylvania railway. 

The surrounding area is characterized by industrial activity; for example, the Belmont county coalfield, for which Bellaire serves as the shipping center, produced 19.3% of the total coal output for the state of Ohio in 1907. Other natural resources found in the vicinity of this border region include fireclay, limestone, and iron.

## Historical Context and the First Continental Congress
Pennsylvania played a central role in the early political organization of the American colonies. In September 1774, Philadelphia served as the meeting place for the first colonial Congress. Twelve colonies were represented at this gathering: Pennsylvania, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland, Virginia, and North and South Carolina (Georgia was absent). This Congress acted as an insurrectionary government and documented its position through a "Declaration of Rights."
