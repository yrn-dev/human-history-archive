# Mahabaleshwar

## Geography and Characteristics
Mahabaleshwar is a fine plateau located within the Sahyadri, also known as the Western Ghats, a great range of hills that runs parallel to the coast. The plateau is situated 37 m. from Poona and is characterized by its rich vegetation. It stands at an elevation of 4,500 ft., making it the best-known elevation of the Sahyadri range.

The broader Sahyadri range generally maintains an average elevation of about 1,800 ft., though individual peaks can rise to more than double that height. The range stretches southwards for over 500 m. with a breadth of 10 to 20 m. While the western declivity of these hills is abrupt, the landward slope is often very gentle, with the crest of the range sometimes only slightly raised above the Deccan plateau.

## Administrative Use
Due to its characteristics and location, the Bombay government utilizes Mahabaleshwar as its sanitarium and summer retreat.

## Regional Context
Mahabaleshwar is part of the rugged and mountainous country south of the Tapti, which forms the northern extremity of the Western Ghats. In the vicinity of these hills, particularly toward the northern end of the range, the terrain is broken and rugged, featuring spurs, masses of rock, and isolated peaks that serve as watersheds for the great rivers of the Deccan.

The surrounding region of the Bombay presidency is divided into several distinct tracts. While the plains of the Deccan and Khandesh are often bleak and devoid of vegetation for much of the year due to uncertain rainfall, the Western Ghats provide a contrasting landscape of bluffs, precipices, and fertile strips of land between the base of the hills and the sea.
