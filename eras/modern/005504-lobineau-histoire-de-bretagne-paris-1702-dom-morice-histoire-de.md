# Lobineau and Morice: Historians of Bretagne

The historical study of Bretagne has been shaped by several key scholarly works, among which the contributions of Dom Lobineau and Dom Morice are noted as significant authorities. These authors provided foundational narratives of the region's past, contributing to a broader body of literature that examines the political, social, and ecclesiastical development of the territory.

## Dom Lobineau and the Histoire de Bretagne
Dom Lobineau authored the *Histoire de Bretagne*, which was published in Paris in 1702. His work serves as one of the primary historical references for the region, appearing alongside other notable histories such as those written by Bertrand d'Argentré (1586) and later scholars. Lobineau's research contributed to the understanding of Bretagne's antiquity and its historical trajectory.

## Dom Morice and the Histoire de Bretagne
Following the work of Lobineau, Dom Morice produced his own *Histoire de Bretagne*. This work was published between 1742 and 1756. Together with Lobineau, Morice is cited as a principal authority for those researching the history of the region.

## Related Historical Perspectives
The works of Lobineau and Morice exist within a wider historiographical context. Other authors have explored specific aspects of Bretagne's history, including:

*   **Early Migration and Independence:** The independence of Bretagne during the period when insular Britons sought refuge in the country is a point of study. Authors such as Daru and Gallet have written on this subject, suggesting that the majority of these Britons arrived as fugitives rather than as conquerors.
*   **Ecclesiastical and Administrative History:** The region's religious and civic structures have been documented by various scholars. Anatole Jean-Baptiste Antoine de Barthelemy, for instance, collaborated with J. Geslin de Bourgogne to publish *Etudes sur la revolution en Bretagne* (1858) and produced a six-volume exhaustive work on the *Anciens eveches de Bretagne; histoire et monuments* between 1855 and 1879.
*   **Social and Rural Studies:** Later historians focused on the social stratification of the region. Henri Sée conducted studies on rural classes in Bretagne during the Middle Ages, as well as from the 16th century until the Revolution.
*   **Political Integration:** The process of Bretagne's union with France was analyzed by A. Dupuy in *La Réunion de la Bretagne à la France* (1880), while the administrative functions of the 18th century were examined in Dupuy's *Études sur l'administration municipale en Bretagne au XVIII^e siècle* (1891).

## Other Notable Works on Bretagne
Beyond the histories provided by Lobineau and Morice, the archive identifies several other significant texts:
*   **Daru:** Published a three-volume *Histoire de Bretagne* in Paris in 1826.
*   **Arthur de la Borderie:** Wrote an *Histoire de Bretagne* published in Rennes starting in 1896.
*   **J. Loth:** Focused on the migration of Bretons into Armorica between the 5th and 7th centuries in *L'Émigration bretonne en Armorique du V^e au VII^e siècle* (1883).
*   **Cultural and Folkloric Studies:** The region's legends and artistic nature were captured by F. M. Luzel in *Légendes chrétiennes de la Basse-Bretagne* (1881) and *Veillées bretonnes* (1879), and by H. du Cleuziou in *Bretagne artistique et pittoresque* (1886).
