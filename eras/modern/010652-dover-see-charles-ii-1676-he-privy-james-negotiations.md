# Dover

Dover, historically known as *Dubris*, is an English port and borough in Kent. Throughout history, it has served as a critical point for continental traffic and national defense.

## Early History and Infrastructure
In Roman times, Dover was one of the primary ports for traffic to the continent. By the 4th century, the harbor was protected by a fort that was part of the Saxon shore defenses (*Litus Saxonicum*). Following the decay of Richborough harbour, the passage from Dover to Whitsand, and later to Calais, became the standard route to France. This importance was codified by a 1465 statute which mandated that no one could ship for Calais except at Dover. In 1607, James I incorporated the guardians of the harbor.

## The Cinque Ports and Municipal Governance
As one of the Cinque Ports, Dover was required to provide twenty ships as part of the quota furnished by those ports. In exchange for this service, Edward the Confessor granted the ports a charter of liberties. This charter exempted townsmen from shires and hundreds and granted them the right to be impleaded only at Shepway. These privileges were confirmed and expanded by subsequent monarchs up until the reign of James II.

The town's municipal government was led by a mayor and twelve jurats, a system that first appeared around the middle of the 13th century. In terms of parliamentary representation, Dover returned two members starting with the parliament of 1265, a practice that continued until 1885, when the number was reduced to one.

## Dover Castle and Strategic Importance
Dover Castle was regarded as the key to England and was frequently a focal point of foreign invasions and civil wars. From the reign of John, the crown appointed the constable of the castle, who also served as the warden of the Cinque Ports. A notable event in the castle's history occurred in 1216, when Hubert de Burgh successfully defended it against the French forces led by the dauphin Louis. De Burgh also founded the Maison Dieu to accommodate pilgrims.

## Later Royal Grants and Activity
During the reign of Charles II, the town saw specific royal patronage. On November 11, 1685, Charles II confirmed the inhabitants' right to a "fair beginning," an event held traditionally in the town. Additionally, the king granted two other fairs to be held on April 23rd and 24th, and September 25th and 26th.

## Notable Figures Associated with Dover
The archive records several individuals with the name Dover or connections to the location:

*   **Robert Dover (1575–1641):** An English attorney and captain who founded the "Cotswold Games" around 1604. These "Olimpick Games" were a protest against growing Puritanism and featured activities such as wrestling, gymnastics, horse-racing, and cudgel-playing. The games continued until three years after his death in 1641 and were briefly revived during the reign of Charles II.
*   **Henry Jermyn, Earl of Dover (c. 1636–1708):** The second son of Sir Thomas Jermyn, he was a noted gambler and duellist at the court of Charles II. A Roman Catholic, he held influence with James II, who created him Baron Dover in 1685. After the Revolution, he followed James abroad and was created earl of Dover in July 1689 as part of the "Jacobite peerages," which the English government did not recognize. He fought at the battle of the Boyne before submitting to William III.
