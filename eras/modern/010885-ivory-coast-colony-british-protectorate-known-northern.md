# Ivory Coast Colony

The Ivory Coast colony is a territory located in French West Africa. Its geography and administration are characterized by diverse terrain and specific regional divisions, such as the circle of Kong.

## Geography and the Kong Region
In the northwest of the Ivory Coast colony lies Kong, which encompasses a town, a district, and a range of hills. These hills serve as part of a band of high ground that separates the coast regions from the inner plains of West Africa. The highest point in the Kong system is the Pic des Kommono, reaching an elevation of 4,757 feet, although most summits in the range remain below 2,000 feet and generally do not exceed 700 feet above the surrounding country's level.

Historically, maps from the first half of the 19th century depicted this range as part of a vast mountain chain stretching east and west across the African continent, suggesting it was a continuation of the snow-clad heights of Ruwenzori or the Mountains of the Moon.

## Administration and Demographics of Kong
The "circle of Kong" is one of the administrative divisions of the Ivory Coast colony. It covers an area of 46,000 square miles and has an estimated population of approximately 400,000. The inhabitants are negroes, primarily of Mandingo and Bambara ethnicity. Regarding religious practice, about one-fourth of the population professes Mahommedanism, while the remainder are spirit worshippers.

The town of Kong is located at 9° N., 4° 20´ W., though it is noted as not being of great importance in the current period.

## History of French Influence
European contact with Kong began in 1827 when René Caillié, who spent time in the western part of the country, became likely the first European to visit the town. In 1888, Captain L. G. Binger successfully induced native chiefs to accept the protection of France. By 1893, this protectorate was formally attached to the Ivory Coast colony.

The region experienced a period of instability when it was overrun by the armies of Samory. However, following the capture of Samory in 1898, France oversaw the peaceful development of the district.

## Regional Borders
The Ivory Coast colony shares a border to its east with Ashanti, a British possession in West Africa. Ashanti is described as an irregular oblong with a triangular projection to the south (the country of the Adansi) and an area of 23,000 square miles. To the north of Ashanti lies a British Protectorate known as the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast.
