# Cordillera Occidental

The Cordillera Occidental is a major mountain range in South America that serves as a part of the boundary between Bolivia and the northern provinces of Chile. It is one of two great parallel chains that comprise the Andean cordilleras crossing the south-west corner of Bolivia. Together with flanking ranges and spurs to the east, these cordilleras form the massif of the Andean system, reaching their greatest breadth in this region.

## Geography and Topography
The Cordillera Occidental closely follows the outline of the coast and acts as the western rampart for the great Bolivian tableland, also known as the *alta-planicie*. This tableland extends from the Vilcanota knot in Peru in the north to the Serrania de Lipez on the Argentine frontier in the south. The tableland measures approximately 500 meters in length and 80 meters in breadth, covering an area of about 40,000 square miles.

The elevation of the plateau averages about 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca, though it descends by approximately 1,000 feet toward its southern end. The northern section of the plateau is referred to as the *puna*, while the southern section is known as the "desert of Lipez," which is characterized as part of the great Puna de Atacama. This region is a large lacustrine basin that was once an inland sea with an eastern outlet through the La Paz gorge. The terrain is described as bleak and inhospitable in the north and barren and arid toward the south, featuring great saline depressions that fill with water during the rainy season. The highest peak of the plateau is the Cerro de Tahua, reaching 17,454 feet.

Overlooking the plateau from the west are several snow-clad peaks, including:
*   Huallatiri (21,654 ft.)
*   Sajama (21,047 ft.)
*   Parinacota (20,918 ft.)
*   Pomarape (20,505 ft.)
*   Licancaur (19,685 ft.)
*   San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.)
*   Oyahua (19,226 ft.)
*   Lirima (19,128 ft.)

## Geology
The geology of the Cordillera Occidental differs significantly from the eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes. While the eastern ranges are formed of Palaeozoic rocks with other intrusions, the Western Cordillera consists primarily of Cretaceous and Jurassic beds. Additionally, the range is characterized by the presence of ashes and lavas from great volcanoes; indeed, the volcanoes of Bolivia are located almost entirely within the Western Cordillera.

## Climate
The climate of the region is heavily influenced by the presence of the high mountain ranges. The Cordillera Occidental extracts moisture from the prevailing easterly winds, resulting in limited rainfall on the elevated plateaus to the west. This rainfall diminishes further toward the south, where the surface becomes absolutely barren. While the northern plateau occasionally experiences brief and furious rain-storms, these are infrequent and limited to a short season. Electrical wind storms are also frequent at these high altitudes.
