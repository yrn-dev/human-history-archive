# G. Vibrio form with spore

## Overview of the Vibrio Form
Within the classification of Schizomycetes, various elongated cellular forms exist. When an elongated form is slightly curved or sinuous, it is referred to as the *Vibrio* form. This is distinguished from the *Spirillum* or *Spirochaete* forms, where the sinuosity is more pronounced and the spiral winding is well marked. These terms may be applied to individual cells or to filaments composed of several cells.

## Spore-Formation in Schizomycetes
Spore-formation is a characteristic method of reproduction in many forms of Schizomycetes. These minute reproductive bodies serve as a resting stage for the organism. In the case of true spores, also known as endospores, the process begins with the appearance of a minute granule within the protoplasm of a vegetative cell. This granule enlarges over a few hours, absorbing all the protoplasm and secreting a resistive, thin envelope. The resulting ripe ovoid spore is smaller than the mother-cell and lies loosely within it.

The archive identifies specific instances of spore-formation in curved and spiral Schizomycetes. Specifically, the *Vibrio* form with spore is documented as a type of spore-formation, as noted by Prazmowski. Other related examples include *Spirillum*-like Schizomycetes that also undergo spore-formation.

## Characteristics of Ripe Spores
Ripe spores in Schizomycetes are typically colourless or very dark—the latter likely due to their minute size and high index of refraction—and are highly refractive. Their shapes can be spherical, ovoid, or long-ovoid. For example, spores of *Bacillus subtilis* are measured at 0.0006 mm broad by 0.0012 mm long. The membrane of the spore may be relatively thick and can exhibit strata or shells.

The process of spore formation usually occurs in a bacillar segment, and typically only one spore is formed per cell. The cell-wall may change its form to accommodate the contents. In certain instances, the protoplasm responsible for spore-formation produces a blue reaction when exposed to iodine solutions.

## Germination and Resistance
Spores are highly resistant to cold, heat, and desiccation. They may germinate immediately or remain dormant for months or even years. When placed in a proper temperature and a suitable medium, germination can be completed within a few hours. 

During germination, the spore elongates and swells. The contents grow forth into a cell similar to the one that originally produced the spore. This growth may occur by the surrounding membrane swelling and dissolving, or by the germinal rodlet clearly breaking through the membrane, leaving the remains of the membrane attached to the rodlet.

## Environmental Factors and Variations
The conditions required for spore-formation vary by species. Aerobic species require a free supply of oxygen, while anaerobic species require very little. Each species also has an optimum temperature and may require specific food-media. 

Bacteriologists have been able to induce "asporogenous races" by systematically interfering with these conditions—such as using high temperatures or adding minute traces of poisons and acids—which indefinitely postpones the formation of spores and can alter the virulence and vigour of the organism.
