# Bibliography and Authorities in the Dictionary of National Biography

The *Dictionary of National Biography* (often abbreviated as *Dict. of Nat. Biog.* or *Dict. Nat. Biog.*) serves as a primary reference point for historical research, frequently cited as a central repository for the lives of notable figures and the collection of their chief authorities. In various historical records, it is utilized alongside primary manuscripts, state papers, and specialized biographies to provide a comprehensive view of an individual's life and career.

## Use in Political and Noble Biographies

The *Dictionary of National Biography* is frequently cited as a foundational source for tracing the lives of English nobility and political figures. ## Integration with Primary Sources and State Papers

The authorities listed in the *Dictionary of National Biography* are often supplemented by extensive primary documentation. These include:

*   **State Papers and Calendars:** Researchers frequently cross-reference *Dictionary* entries with the *Calendars of State Papers* (Domestic, Irish, and Foreign), the *Register of Privy Council of Scotland*, and the *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* (edited by Brewer and Gardiner).
*   **Manuscripts (MSS):** The archive highlights the importance of the *Egerton MSS*, *Add. MSS* at the British Museum, and the *Colton MSS*. Specific collections, such as the *Stanley Papers* or the *Fairfax Correspondence*, provide granular detail that complements the broader summaries found in the *Dictionary*.
*   **Legal and Official Records:** Sources such as the *State Trials*, *Lords Journals*, and the *Council Book of Youghal* are used to verify the factual claims found in biographical dictionaries.

## Application to Intellectual and Academic Figures

## Comparison with Other Biographical Works

While the *Dictionary of National Biography* provides a standardized academic overview, the archive indicates it is often used in conjunction with other biographical formats:

*   **General Biographies:** Works such as *Biographia Britannica* (Kippis) and Wood's *Athenae Oxonienses* (Bliss) are frequently listed alongside the *Dictionary*.
*   **Personal Memoirs:** The *Dictionary* entries are often balanced against more personal or biased accounts, such as the *Lismore Papers* (diaries and autobiographical notes) or the "too laudatory" memoir by F. R. Haines in the *Stanley Papers*.
*   **Specialized Studies:** For religious figures like Cardinal Bellarmine, the archive suggests that while general biographies exist, the most exhaustive bibliographies are found in specialized works like Sommervogel's *Bibliotheque de la Compagnie de Jesus* or the *Dict. de theol. cat.*
