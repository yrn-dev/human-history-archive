# Legal and Administrative Terminology in the United States

The terminology used in the United States often differs in meaning and application from that used in other English-speaking regions, particularly in the fields of law and government administration.

## The Legal Concept of the Brief
In the United States, the term "brief" carries a different meaning than it does in England. In the English system, a brief is required; however, in the United States, the American attorney performs all the functions that are divided between solicitors and barristers in England.

There are different types of briefs used within the American legal system:

*   **Trial Briefs:** A lawyer may prepare a "trial brief" for their own use during a trial. This document corresponds in all essential particulars to the brief prepared by an English solicitor for counsel.
*   **Briefs in Error or Appeal:** This is a more distinctive American use of the term, referring to documents submitted to an appellate court. These written or printed documents embody the argument regarding the question affected. 

Most appellate courts require these printed briefs to be filed for the court and opposing counsel by a designated time before a hearing. According to the rules of the circuit courts of appeals and the United States Supreme Court, such a brief must include:
1. A concise statement of the case.
2. A specification of the errors relied upon, which includes the substance of evidence (the admission or rejection of which is to be reviewed) or any extract from a charge excepted to.
3. An argument that clearly exhibits the points of fact or law to be discussed.

In certain states of the Union, this specific form of printed brief is also adopted for use during trials.

## Administrative Terminology and the Bureau
The word "bureau" originates from the French word *burel* or *bureau*, referring to a coarse cloth used for coverings. While it generally refers to a writing-table, desk, or an office/place of business (particularly a government department), it has specific applications in the United States. In America, a bureau can refer to a low chest of drawers. 

In a governmental context, the United States uses the term to describe certain subdivisions of executive departments. An example of this is the bureau of statistics, which is a division of the treasury department. 

Related to this is the term "bureaucracy," which is often used to describe the concentration of administrative power within these departments or bureaux. It can also signify the undue interference of officials in government details or matters outside the scope of state interference, and is frequently used as a synonym for "red-tapism."
