# Common and Commercial Fruits

The study of fruits such as the orange, lemon, citron, banana, apricot, and Japanese medlar encompasses their botanical classification, their commercial value in global trade, and their distribution across various geographical regions.

## Botanical Classifications
Certain fruits are categorized by their specific biological structures. The *hesperidium* refers to indehiscent fleshy syncarpous fruits, including the lemon, orange, and shaddock. In these fruits, the endocarp forms triangular divisions with seeds attached to the inner angles, while the epicarp and mesocarp create a separable rind. The *pome* is another type of fleshy indehiscent syncarpous fruit—seen in the medlar, apple, pear, hawthorn, and quince—where the receptacle becomes the outer succulent part and the true fruit is a horny core. In the medlar, this core is of a stony hardness, and the succulent covering is open at the summit.

Within the genus *Citrus*, which includes the lemon and orange, anomalous appearances often occur. A separation of the carpels can result in "fingered citrons" or "horned oranges." Additionally, the navel orange is produced when a supernumerary row of carpels creates the appearance of small, imperfect oranges enclosed within the original fruit.

## Commercial Value and Trade
In the United Kingdom, fruits that cannot be grown on an industrial scale are imported. In 1908, the expenditure on imported fruits exceeded four million sterling per annum. Specifically, the outlay for bananas was £1,769,249, oranges were £2,269,651, and lemons were £471,713.

In the United States, the government provides aid and encouragement to fruit-growing through the U.S.A. department of agriculture and experiments at Cornell University. Oranges, citrons, and lemons are grown extensively in California and Florida, though frosts can severely injure these crops when they are in blossom. Lemons are described as a practical necessity for the American people due to the demand for refreshing, acidulous drinks during hot summers. The pomelo, or grape-fruit, is a type of lemon with a more acid flavor and a thicker rind; originally confined to Florida, it is now grown in Californian orchards.

## Geographical Distribution
These fruits are found in diverse climates and regions across the globe:

*   **The Americas:** Beyond Florida and California, the loquat is grown in Louisiana. In the Bahamas, fruits are very numerous, including citrons, lemons, oranges, bananas, and pomegranates. While oranges and pine-apples are important articles of commerce there, systematic cultivation is rare.
*   **Europe and the Mediterranean:** In Corfu, the Japanese medlar (also known as the loquat) and the banana are grown in some spots, alongside other southern European fruit trees.
*   **Other Regions:** In certain vilayets, the produce includes the banana, lemon, orange, and pomegranate. In the "Hot Region" of Kermán, the lemon-tree and orange flourish.

## Related Species and Varieties
The pomegranate is noted as a common fruit in several regions, including the Bahamas and certain vilayets. While not yet largely grown in the United States, it is possible that its culture will develop in Louisiana and southern Texas. 

Historical and regional notes also mention the "apples of Paradise." While some sources suggest this was a kind of *Citrus* or a beautiful fruit of the citron kind, other views suggest they were fruits of the Konár tree.
