The "Annual Reports of Minister of Mines and other Departmental Reports of the Provincial and Dominion Governments" serve as a primary source of administrative and economic data for the region of British Columbia. These reports are listed among the authorities used for documenting the history and physical characteristics of the province, alongside other records such as the Catalogue of Provincial Museum, the Annual Reports of the British Columbia Board of Trade, and reports from the Geological Survey of Canada (dating from 1871) and Canadian Pacific (Government) Surveys (1872-1880).

## Provincial Finance and Economy of British Columbia
The financial status of British Columbia is characterized by a combination of dominion contributions and provincial taxation. In 1905, the province received an annual contribution from the dominion government totaling $307,076. When combined with income tax, timber dues, sales of public land, and provincial taxes on personal and real property, the total revenue for 1905 reached $2,920,461, against an expenditure of $2,302,417.

The province's financial obligations in 1905 included a gross debt of $13,252,097. With assets valued at $4,463,869 (which excludes public works and new legislative buildings), the net debt stood at $8,788,228. The province utilizes a sliding scale for its income tax.

Regarding capital investment, an estimate from 1899 valued the total capital in the province at $307,385,000. This figure includes:
* Timber: $100,000,000
* Provincial assessments: $51,500,000
* Municipal assessments: $45,000,000
* Railways and telegraphs: $47,500,000
* Mining plant and smelters: $10,500,000

## Mining Production and State Reporting
The importance of state and departmental reporting is further evidenced in the records of Idaho. The State Mine Inspector reported that the total mineral product in 1906 was valued at $24,138,317. Gold production, which drove the settlement of the Idaho Territory around 1860, was estimated at $250,000,000 between 1860 and 1906. State reports indicate that gold production rose from $1,075,618 in 1905 to $1,373,031 in 1907.

Silver production in Idaho was valued at $5,242,172 in 1905 and $5,546,554 in 1907. The Coeur d'Alene district in Shoshone county was particularly productive, with a 1906 value of $5,322,706. Additionally, Shoshone county produced approximately half of the lead mined in the United States, with lead values reported at $9,851,076 in 1904 and $12,232,233 in 1907.
