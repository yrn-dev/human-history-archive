# ASBJORNSEN, PETER CHRISTEN AND MOE, JORGEN ENGEBRETSEN

Peter Christen Asbjornsen (1812–1885) and Jorgen Engebretsen Moe (1813–1882) were collectors of Norwegian folklore. Their professional and personal lives were so closely integrated through their shared work that they are rarely named separately.

## Early Lives and Education
Peter Christen Asbjornsen was born in Christiania on January 15, 1812. He was a member of an ancient family from the Gudbrandsdal, a lineage believed to have ended with his death. Asbjornsen began collecting and recording legends and fairy stories at the age of twenty, in 1832, and later became a student at the university in 1833. By profession, he became a zoologist and conducted several investigating voyages along the coasts of Norway, with a particular focus on the Hardanger fjord, supported by the university.

Jorgen Engebretsen Moe was born on April 22, 1813, at the Mo i Hole parsonage in Sigdal Ringerike. Moe first met Asbjornsen when he was fourteen years old, initiating a close friendship that lasted until the end of their lives. Moe attended Christiania University but left in 1839 to study theology. While working as a tutor in Christiania, he spent his holidays wandering through remote mountain districts to collect stories.

## Collaborative Work in Folklore
In 1834, Asbjornsen discovered that Moe had independently begun searching for relics of national folklore. The two friends decided to work in concert and compared their findings. Asbjornsen expanded his own collections by wandering on foot throughout the length and breadth of Norway.

Their joint efforts resulted in the publication of *Norwegian Popular Stories (Norske Folkeeventyr)*. The first instalment appeared in 1842–1843 and was recognized across Europe as a significant contribution to both literature and comparative mythology. A second volume followed in 1844, and they published a new collection in 1871. These works reached an English-speaking audience in 1859 through translations by Sir George Dasent.

## Independent Works and Other Activities
While much of their work was collaborative, Asbjornsen published a separate collection of Norwegian fairy tales, titled *huldreeventyr og folkesagn*, in 1845 without Moe's assistance. 

Beyond folklore, Asbjornsen became concerned with the deforestation of Norway in 1856, an issue for which he induced the government to take action.
