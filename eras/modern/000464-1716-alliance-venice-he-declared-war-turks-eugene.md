# The Austro-Turkish War of 1716-1718

## Outbreak and Military Campaigns
In 1716, Emperor Charles VI declared war on the Turks, acting in alliance with Venice. This conflict was part of a broader series of successes in eastern Europe that helped Charles VI compensate for the heavy drain on resources caused by the War of Spanish Succession. 

A central figure in the Austrian military effort was Prince Eugene of Savoy. In 1716, Eugene achieved a victory at Peterwardein, which led to the conquest of the banat of Temesvar. This success was followed in 1717 by the capture of Belgrade. Among the officers who distinguished themselves during these campaigns was Ludwig Andreas Khevenhüller, an Austrian field-marshal. By 1716, Khevenhüller had risen to command Prince Eugene's own regiment of dragoons and performed with distinction at both the battles of Belgrade and Peterwardein.

## The Treaty of Passarowitz
The conflict concluded with the signing of the treaty of Passarowitz on July 21, 1718. Under the terms of this treaty, the Habsburg monarchy annexed the northern districts of Servia, Belgrade, and the banat. This annexation served to round off the borders of Hungary. 

The treaty also had significant implications for Venice. It confirmed the delimitation of frontiers established by the earlier peace of Carlowitz (1699), which had given the whole of Dalmatia to Venice, including the coast of Herzegovina (though excluding the domains of Ragusa and the Ottoman territory surrounding them).

## Administration and Regeneration of the Banat
The Banat had been a Turkish sanjak (province) since its conquest in 1552. Following its liberation from the "Turkish yoke" by Prince Eugene of Savoy in 1716, it received the official title of Banat after the peace of Passarowitz. 

During the period of Turkish occupation, the district had become nearly depopulated and was largely desolate, consisting of forest, heath, and marsh. To remedy this, the region was placed under a military administration. In 1720, Count Claudius Mercy was appointed governor of Temesvar. Mercy implemented numerous measures for the regeneration of the region, including:
* The clearing of marshes near the Theiss and Danube.
* The construction of canals and roads, which required great expense of labour.
* The encouragement of trade and agriculture.
* The attraction of other settlers and German artisans to colonize the district.

Later, Maria Theresa took a further interest in the Banat. She developed Mercy's measures, founded many villages, and colonized crown lands with German peasants. She also encouraged the exploitation of the country's mineral wealth. Maria Theresa transitioned the region from military to civil administration in 1751.

## Subsequent Political Status of the Banat
The political status of the Banat shifted several times in the following century. In 1779, it was incorporated with Hungary. Following the revolution of 1848-1849, the Banat and the county of Bacs were separated from Hungary to become a distinctive Austrian crown land. However, by 1860, the Banat was definitely incorporated with Hungary once more.
