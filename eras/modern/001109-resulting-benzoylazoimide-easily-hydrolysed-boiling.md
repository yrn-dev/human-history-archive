# Benzoylazoimide

## Chemical Formation and Synthesis
Benzoylazoimide is an organic compound that can be synthesized through a specific chemical sequence involving nitrous acid. The process begins with a nitroso compound, which is produced when nitrous acid reacts with a precursor. This nitroso compound then undergoes a spontaneous change, resulting in the loss of water to form benzoylazoimide. The chemical progression is represented as:
C_6H_5CO.NH.NH_2 + HONO = H_2O + C_6H_5CO.N(NO).NH_2, followed by C_6H_5CO.N(NO).NH_2 = H_2O + C_6H_5CO.N_3.

In this reaction, benzoyl glycollic acid is typically used, although ethyl benzoate may be employed as an alternative.

## Hydrolysis and Resulting Products
Benzoylazoimide is characterized by its susceptibility to hydrolysis. This process is achieved by boiling the compound with alcoholic solutions of caustic alkalis. The hydrolysis of benzoylazoimide yields two primary substances:
1. A benzoate of the alkali metal.
2. An alkali salt of a new acid.

When left to stand, the alkali salt of the new acid precipitates in a crystalline condition.

## Related Chemical Processes and Compounds
The synthesis of benzoylazoimide shares similarities with other methods of producing acid salts. For instance, an improved preparation method involves the use of hippuric acid. This substance reacts with hydrazine hydrate to create hippuryl hydrazine (C_6H_5CONH.CH_2CONH.NH_2). This is then converted by nitrous acid into diazo-hippuramide (C_6H_5CONH.CH_2.CO.NH.N_2.OH). Like benzoylazoimide, diazo-hippuramide is hydrolysed by caustic alkalis, producing salts of hydrazoic acid.

Hydrazoic acid itself is a "weak" acid that is ionized only to a very slight extent in dilute aqueous solutions. It is a colorless liquid with a very unpleasant smell, boiling at 30 degrees C, and is extremely explosive. Its solutions can dissolve metals such as iron and zinc, liberating hydrogen and forming salts known as hydrazoates, azides, or azoimides. These metallic salts are explosive, crystallize in an anhydrous condition, and leave a residue of pure metal upon heating.
