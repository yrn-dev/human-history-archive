# Field Marshal Lord Raglan

## Family Origins and Lineage
Field Marshal Lord Raglan was the youngest son of the 5th duke of Beaufort. His family is associated with Badminton House, the seat of the dukes of Beaufort, located in the village of Badminton in the southern parliamentary division of Gloucestershire, England. The manor of Badminton had been acquired in 1608 by Thomas, Viscount Somerset, from Nicolas Boteler. It was later given to Henry Somerset, the 1st duke of Beaufort and 3rd marquess of Worcester, who constructed the current Palladian-style mansion in 1682.

## Military Career and the Crimean War
Lord Raglan served as the English commander during the Crimean War. During this conflict, he established headquarters in the East, which were accompanied by Sir John Fox Burgoyne, a British field marshal. Sir John Fox Burgoyne served as the principal engineer adviser to Lord Raglan during the initial phase of the siege of Sevastopol and was responsible for superintending the disembarkation at Old Fort.

## Burial and Memorials
Lord Raglan is buried in the parish church of St. Michael, which is situated close to Badminton House. The church is a Grecian building constructed in 1785, featuring an inlaid altar-pavement and a richly ornamented ceiling. The interior of the church contains various memorials to former dukes, including the burial site of Lord Raglan.
