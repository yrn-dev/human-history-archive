# BUKOVINA

Bukovina is a duchy and crownland of Austria. It is bounded to the north by Galicia, to the west by Hungary and Transylvania, to the south by Rumania, and to the east by Rumania and Russia. The total area of the province is 4,035 square miles.

## History and Administration
Originally, Bukovina was a part of the principality of Moldavia, and the ancient capital of that principality, Suczawa, was located within this province. The territory was occupied by the Russians in 1769 and subsequently by the Austrians in 1774. In 1777, the Porte, which held suzerainty over Moldavia, ceded the province to Austria. This acquisition followed a period of shifting diplomatic relations where Austria, under Maria Theresa and Kaunitz, had navigated tensions between the Ottoman power and the ambitions of Catherine of Russia.

Administratively, Bukovina was incorporated into a single province with Galicia in 1786. However, it was separated from Galicia in 1849 and established as a separate crownland. The official language used for instruction in the university, the law-courts, and the administration is German.

## Geography and Climate
The geography of Bukovina is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathians, particularly in the southern regions, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. The Borgo Pass and the Radna Pass serve as the principal passes of the region. Except for the Dniester river, which runs along the northern border, the province belongs to the Danube watershed. Its primary rivers include the Pruth and the Sereth, the latter of which is joined by the Bistritza, Moldava, and Suczawa affluents.

The climate is described as healthy but severe, particularly during the winter months, though it is generally milder than the climate of Galicia. In Czernowitz, the mean annual temperature is 46.9° F.

## Land Use and Economy
The name "Bukovina" is derived from the abundance of beech trees in the region. Woodland occupies 43.17% of the total area. Of the remaining land, 27.59% is arable land, 12.68% consists of meadows, 10.09% is pasture, and 0.78% is dedicated to gardens.

The soil is fertile, leading to significant progress in agriculture. The primary agricultural products include rye, wheat, barley, oats, maize, hemp, flax, and potatoes. Cattle-rearing is another significant source of revenue. The chief industries are milling, distilling, and brewing. The government operates a salt mine at Kaczyka, as salt is the principal mineral found in the region. Commerce is primarily handled by Armenians and Jews and is mostly limited to raw products, including wood, cattle, wool, and agricultural produce.

## Population and Demographics
In 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a population density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is ethnically diverse:
* Ruthenians: over 40%
* Rumanians: 35%
* Jews: 13%
* Others: Germans, Russians, Armenians, Poles, and Hungarians.

Religious affiliation is predominantly Greek Orthodox, with nearly 70% of the population belonging to this church under the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Additionally, 11% of the population are Roman Catholics, 3.25% belong to the Greek United Church, and 2.5% are Protestants.
