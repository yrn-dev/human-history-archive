# Ministry of War

The Ministry of War is one of the three joint ministries established for the administration of common affairs within the Dual Monarchy. Alongside the ministry of finance and the ministry of foreign affairs and of the imperial and royal house, it manages matters that affect both states of the monarchy.

## Role and Jurisdiction
The minister of war serves as the head for the administration of all military affairs. However, this authority is not absolute; the ministry does not oversee the Hungarian *Honveds* or the Austrian *Landwehr*, as these are managed by the respective ministries for national defence of each state. 

Furthermore, the joint ministers, including the minister of war, have restricted authority limited strictly to common affairs. They are prohibited from exercising influence or directing government affairs that affect only one of the two halves of the monarchy. The supreme command of the army remains vested in the monarch, who possesses the power to implement all measures regarding the entire army, ensuring that the total armed power of the Dual Monarchy operates as a single entity under the sovereign's command.

## Budgetary Structure and Revenue
The Ministry of War is funded through a common budget that covers the expenditures of the joint ministries, the army, and foreign affairs. This joint budget is supported by several revenue streams:
*   Revenues from the joint ministries.
*   Net proceeds from customs.
*   The quota, which consists of proportional contributions from the two states.

The quota is fixed for several years and typically aligns with the duration of the commercial and customs treaty. Historically, Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30% of the joint expenditure remaining after common revenues were deducted. This changed in 1897, with the quota set at 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary until July 1907. In 1907, Hungary's contribution was increased to 36.4%. Additionally, 2% of total charges are debited to Hungary due to the incorporation of the former military frontier into that state.

In 1905, the budget estimates for the common administration listed the revenue from the Ministry of War at 305,907 L.

## Expenditures
The expenditures of the Ministry of War are divided between the army and the navy, as well as extraordinary costs. In a detailed breakdown of expenditures, the Ministry of War's costs were listed as follows:
*   **Army:** 12,679,160 L
*   **Navy:** 2,306,100 L

There are also separate allocations for extraordinary military expenditures, which included 4,785,500 L for general extraordinary military costs and 315,920 L specifically for extraordinary military expenditure in Bosnia.

## Historical Expenditure Trends

The accounts of the Ministry of War, along with other joint ministries, are audited by a joint supreme court of accounts, which is appointed to control the common finances.
