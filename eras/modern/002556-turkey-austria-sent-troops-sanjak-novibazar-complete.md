# Sanjak of Novibazar

The sanjak of Novibazar is a political division located in the Balkan Peninsula. Geographically, it is situated in northern Albania and is part of the vilayet of Kossovo in European Turkey. The region is also considered part of the broader area known as Illyria, which extends along the eastern shore of the Adriatic from Fiume to Durazzo and inland toward the Servian Morava and the Danube.

## Area and Population
As of 1909, the sanjak of Novibazar covered an area of 2,840 square miles. Its population at that time was recorded as 153,000, with a population density of 53.5 people per square mile. The town of Novibazar served as a local center with a population of 12,000.

## Administration and Regional Context
The sanjak is one of the administrative divisions of the vilayet of Kossovo, which also includes the sanjak of Uskub in Macedonia and the sanjak of Prizren. The broader vilayet of Kossovo, as of 1905, had a population of approximately 1,100,000 and an area of 12,700 square miles. The inhabitants of this larger region are primarily Slavs and Albanians, though it also contains smaller communities of Turks, Greeks, Vlachs, and gipsies.

## Economic Features
The sanjak of Novibazar is part of a province noted for its unsurpassed agricultural trade among Turkish provinces. Exports from the vilayet of Kossovo, valued at approximately £950,000, include fruit, grain, livestock, skins, hemp, opium, vegetables, and tobacco. Sericulture is a growing industry in the region, supported by the Administration of the Ottoman Debt, and rice is grown for local use.

Imports to the region are valued at roughly £1,200,000 and consist of chemical products, metals, machinery, petroleum, groceries, and other manufactured goods. Petroleum is specifically utilized in factories and flour-mills due to the prohibitive cost of coal. Trade is primarily conducted via rail into Servia or through Salonica, with virtually no trade occurring through Adriatic ports.

## Austro-Turkish Relations and the Railway Concession
In the early 20th century, the sanjak of Novibazar became a point of geopolitical tension between the Habsburg monarchy and Russia. Following the retirement of Count Goluchowski in October 1906, his successor, Baron von Aerenthal, sought to advance the traditional policy of the Habsburg monarchy in the Balkans.

In January 1908, Baron von Aerenthal undermined an existing Austro-Russian agreement by securing a concession from the sultan for the construction of a railway. This railway was designed to run from the Turkish terminus at Mitrovitza, through the sanjak of Novibazar, to the Bosnian frontier. This action was taken despite the expressed opposition of Russia and caused resentment in Servia. Servia viewed the railway scheme as another link in a chain forged by the Habsburg empire, a sentiment compounded by Servia's economic dependence on the Dual Monarchy following a war of tariffs in 1906.
