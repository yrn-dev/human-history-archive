# Gracchus Babeuf and the Conspiracy for Equality

## Character and Historical Significance
Gracchus Babeuf was a representative type of the French revolutionists of his era, described as warm-hearted, excitable, and half-educated. During the chaos of the revolutionary period, he is noted to have lost his moral and mental balance. Despite this, his historical importance is significant as he was the first individual to propose socialism as a practical policy. Consequently, he is regarded as the father of the movements that became prominent during the revolutions of 1871 and 1848.

## The Conspiracy and Trial
Babeuf was involved in a conspiracy for which he was eventually made the leader by the government, although other more important individuals were implicated. The government's decision to cast him as the leader was aided by Babeuf's own vanity. 

The legal proceedings against Babeuf and his accomplices took place before a newly constituted high court of justice at Vendome. The trial began on February 20, 1797, and lasted for two months. A full report of these proceedings was published in Paris in 1797 in four volumes titled *Debats du proces, &c.*

## Events Surrounding the Arrests
The period surrounding the conspiracy was marked by political instability. Following a successful *coup*, the *Tribun* ceased publication on April 24. Simultaneously, Lebois used the *Ami du peuple* to attempt to incite soldiers to revolt, leading to rumors of a military rising.

When the prisoners were moved from Paris on Fructidor 10 and 11 (August 27 and 28), there were tentative attempts to start a riot to rescue them, though these efforts were easily suppressed. A further attempt on September 7, involving five or six hundred Jacobins who tried to rouse soldiers at Grenelle, also failed.

## Sentencing and Execution
The trial concluded with varying sentences for the participants:
* **Death Penalty:** On Prairial 7 (April 26, 1797), Babeuf and Darthe were condemned to death. They were executed at Vendome on Prairial 8, 1797.
* **Exile:** Several prisoners, including Buonarroti, were sentenced to exile.
* **Acquittal:** Other participants, including Vadier and his fellow-conventionals, were acquitted.
* **Escape:** Drouet managed to escape; according to Barras, this occurred with the connivance of the Directory.
