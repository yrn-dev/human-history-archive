# BORDEAUX

Bordeaux is a city located in south-western France and serves as the capital of the department of Gironde. It is described as one of the most extensive and finest cities in France. As of 1906, the city had a population of 237,707.

## Geography and Layout
The city is situated on the west (or left) bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea. It is positioned within a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district. At this location, the Garonne river forms a semicircle, which creates a physical separation between the city proper on the left bank and La Bastide, an important suburb located on the right bank.

The city is surrounded by a semicircle of boulevards. Beyond these boulevards are the suburbs of Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat. Within the city, the area containing most of the important buildings is characterized by narrow, winding streets. Outside of this central quarter, the streets are quiet and narrow, lined with low white houses, which differ from the high tenements typically found in other large French towns.

## Infrastructure and Transport
Bordeaux is connected to other major cities via railway. It is 359 miles south-south-west of Paris via a main line of the Orleans railway and 159 miles north-west of Toulouse on the main line of the Southern railway. 

The Garonne is crossed by two primary structures:
* **The Pont de Bordeaux**: A stone bridge from the early 19th century measuring 1,534 feet in length.
* **A railway bridge**: This structure connects the Southern company station on the left bank with the Orleans railway company station located in La Bastide.

The city features a crescent of busy quays. This area is characterized by a background of factories, lofty warehouses, and mansions, with various steeples and towers rising behind them.

## Public Spaces and Landmarks
The Place des Quinconces is located near the center of the line of quays and serves as a focal point for the city's concentrated life. This area is planted with plane trees and features two large statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne. The square terminates at the quays with two rostral columns that function as lighthouses. Additionally, the west side of the square contains a monument dedicated to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed in 1793 under the convention.

Other notable areas and landmarks include:
* **The Place de la Comedie**: Located at the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces, this square contains the 18th-century Grand Theatre.
* **Promenades**: The city's principal promenades are located near the center and include the Place des Quinconces, the allees de Tourny, and a beautiful public garden.

## Regional Significance
Bordeaux holds significant administrative and religious status in the region. It is the center of an *académie* and the province of the archbishop of Bordeaux. In terms of maritime importance, its port is ranked as one of the top three in France, a position it disputes with the seaport of Dunkirk.
