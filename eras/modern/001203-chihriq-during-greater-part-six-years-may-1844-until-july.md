# Chihriq

## Overview
Chihriq served as one of the final locations of captivity for the Bab during his career. He was held there for the greater part of a six-year period spanning from May 1844 until July 1850. His imprisonment at Chihriq followed previous periods of captivity in Shiraz and Maku.

## The Babi Movement and Revolts
While the Bab was imprisoned, his disciples maintained an active propaganda effort. This activity led to several serious revolts against the government, which intensified following the death of Muhammad Shah in September 1848.

Notable uprisings included:
*   **Mazandaran:** Occurring between December 1848 and July 1849, this revolt took place at the ruined shrine of Shaykh Tabarsi near Barfurush. Led by Mulla Husayn of Bushrawayh (identified as the first who believed) and Mulla Muhammad 'Ali of Barfurush, the Babis resisted the shah's troops for seven months before being subdued and put to death.
*   **Zanjan:** Located in the north-west of Persia, this revolt was headed by Mulla Muhammad 'Ali Zanjani and lasted for seven or eight months, from May to December 1850.
*   **Niriz:** A less protracted but serious struggle against the government was waged in Fars by Aga Sayyid Yahya of Niriz.

## Execution and Aftermath
The revolts at Zanjan and Niriz were still in progress when the Bab was removed from his prison at Chihriq. Accompanied by one of his devoted disciples, he was brought to Tabriz, where he was publicly shot in front of the citadel, or *arg*. 

Following the execution, his body was exposed for several days before being recovered by the Babis. They conveyed the body to a shrine near Tehran, and it was eventually moved to Acre in Syria, where it is buried.

## Later Persecutions
Following a period of two years with relatively little news of the movement, a new wave of persecution began after August 15, 1852. On this date, three Babis acting on their own initiative attempted to assassinate Nasiru'd-Din Shah as he returned to his palace at Niyavaran from the chase. Although the attempt failed, it resulted in the execution of approximately thirty Babis in Tehran on August 31, 1852, characterized by atrocious cruelty. Among the victims were Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, who wrote the oldest history of the movement from the Babi perspective, and the talented poetess Qurratu'l-'Ayn.
