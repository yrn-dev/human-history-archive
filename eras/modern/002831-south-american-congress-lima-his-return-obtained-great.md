# Jose Manuel Balmaceda and the South American Congress at Lima

## Early Life and Career
Jose Manuel Balmaceda was born in Santiago in 1838 to wealthy parents. During his early years, his primary interests and activities were centered on agricultural and industrial enterprise.

## The South American Congress and Political Rise
In 1865, Balmaceda served as one of the representatives of the Chilean government at the general South American congress held at Lima. Following his return from this congress, he achieved great distinction as an orator within the national assembly. His subsequent career included several diplomatic missions abroad. He eventually held high-ranking government positions, serving as the minister of the interior and the minister of foreign affairs under the presidency of Senor Santa Maria. While serving as minister of the interior, Balmaceda introduced several laws that were highly obnoxious to the clergy, including the implementation of compulsory civil marriage.

## Presidency and Conflict
Balmaceda was elected president of the republic of Chile in 1886. Although he possessed great capacity, his imperious temper made him poorly suited for the presidency. This temperament led to an irreconcilable variance between Balmaceda and the majority of the national representatives. 

The political situation became intolerable by the beginning of 1891. On January 1, 1891, Balmaceda attempted to resolve the conflict by refusing to convoke the assembly. Furthermore, he ordered that taxes continue to be collected under his own authority.

## The Chilean Civil War and Death
The actions taken by Balmaceda on January 1 led to the Chilean Civil War of 1891. The conflict resulted in the overthrow of his presidency. On September 18—the anniversary of the date he was elevated to the presidency—Balmaceda committed suicide.
