# Jura

The Jura is a geographical region and mountain range located in Europe, spanning portions of France and Switzerland. It is characterized by its distinct geological formations, its role as a political and administrative boundary, and its influence on the surrounding hydrology.

## Geology and Physical Geography
In the field of physical geography and geology, the Jura is classified as a "horst." Specifically, it is an example of a block of the earth's crust that has been crushed in a mountain range against a stationary block. The region is also known for its Jura-Cretaceous formations.

The Jura range influences the local topography of neighboring areas. For example, the town of Bienne in the Swiss canton of Bern is situated where the river Suze (or Scheuss) emerges from a deep cleft in the Jura range. Additionally, the slopes of the Jura are home to locations such as Macolin and Evilard, which are accessible from Bienne via funicular railways.

## Regional Geography and the Bresse District
The Jura serves as a boundary for several districts and departments. In eastern France, the Bresse district embraces portions of the departments of Ain, Saône-et-Loire, and Jura. The Bresse is a plain situated between 600 and 800 feet above sea level, extending from the Dombes in the south to the river Doubs in the north. Its eastern limit is the Jura; the Bresse measures approximately 20 miles in the direction of the Jura and 60 miles in the direction of the Saône.

## Hydrology
The Jura is the source of significant waterways, most notably the river Doubs. The Doubs rises in the Jura at the foot of the Noirmont ridge at an elevation of 3,074 feet. From its source, the river flows through wooded gorges and the lake of St Point, eventually forming a frontier between Switzerland and France for approximately 28 miles. After passing through the department of Jura and the town of Dole, the river leaves the mountainous region and enters a wide plain before joining the Saône at Verdun.

## Administrative and Political History
The Jura has historically functioned as a marker for political divisions. In the period following the reign of Charlemagne, the treaty of Verdun in 843 apportioned various territories; subsequently, the diocese of Besançon and the country located beyond the Jura were given to Lothair, king of Lorraine.

In modern administrative terms, the region includes the department of Jura in France. This department includes the town of Salins, the birthplace of the geologist Jules Marcou (1824–1898). Marcou, who was educated in Besançon and Paris, conducted early geological research on the Jura mountains alongside J. Thurmann.
