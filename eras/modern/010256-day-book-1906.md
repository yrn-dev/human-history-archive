# DAY BOOK

In the context of accounting and business record-keeping, a day book is a specific type of book of first-entry used to record transactions.

## Function and Usage
The day book, also referred to as a sales book or sales journal, is utilized specifically to record the particulars of goods sold. It is part of a broader system of accounting practiced more generally in North America and Great Britain. 

In these regions, rather than maintaining a single journal for all classes of transactions, businesses typically employ several specialized journals. In addition to the day book for sales, this system includes:
*   A purchases book (also known as an invoice book or bought journal) for recording goods purchased.
*   A returns inwards book for goods sold that were subsequently returned by customers.
*   A returns outwards book for goods purchased that were subsequently returned.
*   A bills receivable book for recording bills of exchange received from debtors.
*   A bills payable book for recording bills of exchange given to creditors.

This method of splitting the journal into numerous books of first-entry differs from the approach generally adopted in the continental countries of Europe, where the journal is typically treated as an intermediate book. In the European method, transactions are brought together and focused in the journal as a preliminary step before being recorded in the ledger.

## Evolution of Book-keeping
The transition toward using specialized books like the day book occurred in modern times due to the growth of business. It was found to be impracticable to maintain one single book of first-entry for all transactions, necessitating either the intermediate journal method or the division of the journal into multiple books, each dedicated to a particular class of transaction.
