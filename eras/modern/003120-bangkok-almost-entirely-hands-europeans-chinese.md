# Bangkok

Bangkok is the capital of Siam, situated on the river Me Nam approximately 20 miles from its mouth. The city is located at 13deg 45' N. and 100deg 30' E. Historically, the city was constructed largely on piles or floating pontoons along the edges of numerous water-courses and canals that served as thoroughfares. In modern times, these have been supplemented by well-planned roads and streets crossing the old canals, featuring brick houses where much of the former riparian population now lives.

## City Layout and Infrastructure
The city covers an area exceeding 10 square miles. At its center is the royal palace, which is enclosed by walls and situated in a bend of the river. A second wall, located at a radius of nearly a mile, encircles the closely-packed city proper. Beyond this wall, the town extends north toward the royal parks and south toward the docks, harbour, rice-mills, warehouses, and the business quarter.

The city's appearance is characterized by beautiful Buddhist temples with gilded spires and coloured tile roofs. Other notable structures include the mint, the arsenal, various public offices, schools, hospitals, markets, the palaces of various princes, and Christian churches, primarily of the Roman Catholic denomination. Infrastructure includes four railway stations connecting the capital to the provinces, streets lit by electricity and lined with shade-trees, and a complete system of electric tramways provided by two companies.

## Port and Commerce
The port of Bangkok is formed by a four-mile reach of the Me Nam immediately below the city proper. The river is of good depth up to the banks and ranges from 250 to 400 yards in width. While the river is convenient for loading and berthing, a bar at the mouth prevents vessels drawing more than 12 feet from passing; consequently, large ships must utilize lighters for partial loading and unloading outside the bar.

The port's banks are lined with wharves, warehouses, and offices of commercial houses, as well as timber yards and numerous rice-mills. The harbour master's office, the custom house, and various consulates and foreign legations are also located here.

Trade in Bangkok is almost entirely controlled by Chinese and Europeans. The primary exports are teak and rice, while the primary imports include gold-leaf and silk and cotton goods. Between 1900 and 1907, the value of trade more than doubled, reaching £7,100,000 in exports and £5,600,000 in imports by the latter year. Seventy-five percent of this total trade is conducted with the British empire, and many of the Far East's most well-known banks and mercantile firms maintain branches in the city. The unit of currency used is the *tical*.

Shipping activity in 1904 saw 750 steamships clear the port, with proportions consisting of three out of seven German, two Norwegian, and one British. However, the introduction of regular services by a Japanese and a British company in 1905 altered these proportions. While trade with Singapore has shown a tendency to decrease, direct trade with Europe has increased. Additionally, a fleet of junks, schooners, and small steamers operates trade with districts on the west and east coasts of the Gulf of Siam.

## Government and Administration
The government of the city is entrusted to a member of the cabinet known as the minister of the capital. This minister oversees the revenue, harbour master's, sanitary, and police offices. The police force consists of 3,000 well-organized men led by a European commissioner of police. The sanitary department, which includes an engineer's office, a bacteriological laboratory, and a board of health, is managed with expert European assistance. Following an act of 1905, the harbour master controls the city water-ways and the port.

Legal jurisdiction is split; while the ministry of justice controls ordinary law courts, foreign powers in Siam enjoy extra-territorial rights. This means each consulate has an attached court with jurisdiction over cases where the defendant is a subject of the power that the consulate represents.

## Population and Health
The population of Bangkok is estimated at 450,000 and is mixed in composition. The major part of the population consists of Chinese and Siamese, though persons of almost every race found between Japan and Bombay are present. Europeans of various nationalities number more than 1,000.

The city faces a high death rate, particularly among children, due to the prevalence of fevers, smallpox, and cholera during dry weather. Sanitation is improving, specifically through the boring of numerous artesian wells that provide good water.
