# Trinity College

The archive contains records regarding several institutions known as Trinity College, most notably those located in Cambridge, Dublin, and other regions. These institutions have served as centers for higher education, theology, and the development of academic professorships.

## Trinity College, Cambridge

Trinity College, Cambridge, has been associated with numerous scholars, mathematicians, and theologians. In the 17th century, the college was led by masters such as Dr. John Wilkins. Later, following the promotion of Dr. Pearson to the see of Chester, the mastership of Trinity College was granted to Dr. Barrow via a king's patent dated February 13, 1672. Dr. Barrow had previously been a fellow of the college and had held a mathematical chair at Cambridge—which he eventually resigned in 1669 to his pupil, Isaac Newton—before turning his studies toward divinity.

The college has hosted a wide array of fellows and students across different disciplines. Aubrey Stewart and George Long both served as fellows of Trinity College, Cambridge, and collaborated on the translation of *Plutarch's Lives*. Other notable figures associated with the institution include:
* **Duncan Farquharson Gregory**: Entered the college in 1833, where he focused primarily on mathematics and served for a period as an assistant professor of chemistry.
* **Alfred Barry**: Educated at the college, where he achieved a first-class in the classical tripos and was the 4th wrangler in 1848.
* **Edward Dodwell**: An English writer on archaeology and traveler who was educated at the college.

## Trinity College, Dublin

Trinity College, Dublin, is noted for its contributions to Anglican divinity. William King (1650–1729), an Anglican divine and later the archbishop of Dublin, was educated at this institution. In 1718, King founded a divinity lectureship at Trinity College, Dublin, which continues to bear his name. Additionally, Benjamin Williamson, a professor of Natural Philosophy and Fellow of the Royal Society, served as the Vice-Provost of Trinity College, Dublin, and authored works on differential calculus.
