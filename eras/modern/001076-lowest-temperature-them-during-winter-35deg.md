## Botanical and Biological Temperature Requirements

Certain plants and microorganisms require specific temperature ranges for growth and maintenance. The azalea, a genus of plants in the heath order (Ericaceae) native to the United States, Asia Minor, and the hilly regions of Japan and China, has distinct thermal needs. During the winter, the lowest temperature for azaleas is approximately 35deg. During their growth season, they require night temperatures between 55deg and 65deg and daytime temperatures of 75deg, provided the atmosphere is well charged with moisture. Additionally, when grafting half-ripened shoots, the plants are placed in a moist heat of 65deg.

In the study of bacteria, specifically the group Myxobacteriaceae, temperature plays a role in cultivation. Myxobacteria, which can be found in the air and the alimentary canal of herbivorous animals, are most easily grown in dark dung exposed to air for at least eight days. A temperature of 30-35deg C is maintained for this process, as high temperatures favor the growth of these bacteria while remaining inimical to common fungi.

## Extreme Cold and Arctic Climates

The archive documents some of the lowest temperatures on Earth in northern Asia and Greenland. In North Greenland, the lowest temperatures of the northern hemisphere may be found. In the interior of the inland ice, winter minimums may sink to -90 deg. F. This extreme cold is attributed to the radiation of heat from the snow-surface in rarefied air. On Nansen's expedition, night temperatures in early September were recorded at about -49 deg. F. The interior of Greenland is described as an elevated desert of snow, with daily temperature ranges sometimes reaching 40 deg.

Other cold regions include Melville Island, north of the American continent, where the lowest observed mean temperature is about 4 deg. Fahr. In eastern Siberia, the mean temperature of the north coast is only a few degrees above the Fahrenheit zero.

## Regional Climate Variations in the Americas

In the United States, states such as Idaho and Illinois exhibit significant temperature fluctuations. Idaho's mean annual temperature from 1898 to 1903 was 45.5° F. Within the state, the Snake river basin and the valleys of the Weiser, Payette, and Boisé rivers are the mildest regions, recording a mean annual temperature of 52° F. in 1902. Conversely, the Lost river regions and Camas prairie are much colder, with a recorded low of -35° F.

Illinois is characterized by extremes caused by Arctic winds from the north and warm winds from the Gulf of Mexico. The lowest temperature ever recorded in the state was -32° F. in February 1905 at Ashton. The state's mean annual temperature increases from north to south, measuring 47° F. at Winnebago, 52° F. at Springfield, and 58° F. in Cairo.

## Tropical and Subtropical Environments

Temperature patterns vary significantly in tropical and subtropical zones. In Barotseland, the plateauland is more temperate than expected for its latitude. The mean maximum and minimum temperatures are 80deg and 55deg F., with an extreme range spanning from 100deg down to 35deg.

In Keng Tung, the capital of the British Shan States, temperatures rise to nearly 100° F. during hot weather, often falling by 30° or more at night. During cold weather, the lowest experienced temperature is approximately 40° or a few degrees more or less.

Other subtropical regions show more stability. In Cadiz, the mean annual temperature is about 64° F., with summer and winter temperatures typically varying only 10° above or below that mean. In another subtropical colony, the highest recorded shade temperature was 98° F. and the lowest was 50°.
