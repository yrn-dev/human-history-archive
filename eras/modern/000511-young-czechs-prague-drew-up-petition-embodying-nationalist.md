# Young Czechs

The Young Czechs were a political faction within the nationalist movement of Bohemia, characterized by their impatience for national rights and their opposition to the more moderate approaches of the Old Czechs. They played a significant role in the political struggle for the recognition of the Bohemian kingdom and the expansion of Czech linguistic and administrative influence within the Austrian Empire.

## Early Nationalist Activity
The roots of the nationalist movement in Bohemia were tied to a romantic and nationalist surge that began around 1830. This movement was further bolstered by the introduction of parliamentary government and popular education. On March 11, 1848, a meeting of "young Czechs" in Prague drafted a petition that outlined liberal and nationalist demands. During this period of revolutionary unrest, the city of Prague saw significant conflict; on June 12, a rising involving the populace and Czech students occurred, which was subsequently suppressed by Prince Windischgraetz on June 16.

## Political Demands and the Bohemian Kingdom
The primary objective of the Czech nationalist movement was the full restoration of the Bohemian kingdom. This goal included the demand that the emperor be crowned as king of Bohemia in Prague, following the tradition of his predecessors. Leaders sought a position for Bohemia within the monarchy similar to the settlement achieved by Hungary.

In 1868, Czech leaders presented a "declaration" to the diet at Prague, which served as an official statement of their claims. This document argued that:
* The Bohemian kingdom should be fully restored.
* No foreign assembly possessed the qualification to impose taxes within Bohemia.
* The diet was not qualified to elect representatives to go to Vienna.

This declaration was signed by eighty-one members, including bishops and many feudal nobles. In response, the German majority declared that these members had forfeited their seats and ordered new elections, leading to serious riots and the decree of exceptional laws by the government.

## Conflict with the Old Czechs
By the late 19th century, a divide emerged between the established leadership and the Young Czechs. While the older leaders were potentially content with the influence they had already gained, the Young Czechs were more impatient for results. 

In January 1890, conferences were held in Vienna, chaired by Taaffe, to reconcile the German party, the Feudal party, and the Czechs. An agreement was reached regarding a revision of the electoral law and a separation between German and Czech districts. However, the Young Czechs were not invited to participate in these conferences. Consequently, they did not consider themselves bound by the decisions and utilized obstruction to oppose the resulting bills, arguing that the measures threatened the unity of the Bohemian country. This conflict culminated in the elections of 1891, where the Young Czechs achieved a complete victory over the Old Czechs.

## Influence on Language and Administration
The Young Czechs sought further concessions regarding the use of the Czech language in Bohemia. In the late 1890s, the government under Badeni sought support from the nationalities, including sixty Young Czechs. While the more moderate members of the party were willing to negotiate to gain influence over the administration, their primary requirement was linguistic concession.

This led to the publication of the language ordinances of 1897. These ordinances determined that:
1. All correspondence and documents brought before government officials in Bohemia should be conducted in the language in which they were first introduced, effectively introducing Czech into government offices throughout the kingdom.
2. After 1903, any individual appointed to a government post in Bohemia was required to pass an examination in Czech.

These measures, along with earlier ordinances from 1880 and 1886 and the introduction of Czech classes at the university of Prague in 1882, were viewed by German Nationalists and Radicals as a threat, leading to parliamentary obstruction and widespread disturbances.
