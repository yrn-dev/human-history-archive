# The Bohemians During the Ministries of Korber and Gautsch

## Political Opposition and National Rights
During the ministries of Korber and Gautsch, the Bohemians maintained a stance of opposition toward the central government located in Vienna. Their primary objective during this period was the assertion of their national rights. This continued friction followed a series of political instabilities and legislative reversals regarding the status of the Bohemian language and regional autonomy.

## Preceding Language Disputes
The tensions during the Korber and Gautsch administrations were rooted in earlier attempts by Count Badeni to conciliate Bohemia. In early 1897, Badeni issued a decree requiring government officials seeking employment in Bohemia to demonstrate knowledge of both the German and Bohemian languages. While Badeni did not believe reopening the question of Bohemian autonomy was feasible, he intended this measure to remedy serious grievances within the country.

This decree provoked violent opposition from the German inhabitants of Austria, leading to the collapse of Badeni's cabinet at the end of 1897. He was succeeded briefly by Count Thun and subsequently by Count Clary. The government under Count Clary repealed the decrees that had granted equal rights to the Bohemian language. These revocations resulted in outbreaks of trouble in Prague, which the Austrian authorities severely repressed.

## The Ministry of Baron Gautsch
Following the period of unrest, Baron Gautsch served as premier. His administration eventually ended in April when he fell from power due to a difference with the Poles. He was succeeded by Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst, who resigned six weeks later. This resignation served as a protest against the crown's consent to a customs tariff in Hungary that was distinct from the joint Austro-Hungarian tariff established in the Szell-Koerber compact and enacted by the Reichsrath.

## Subsequent Administrative Reforms
Following the resignation of Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst, a new cabinet was formed on June 2 by Baron von Beck. Baron von Beck, a former permanent under secretary of state in the ministry for agriculture and an instructor in administrative and constitutional law to Archduke Francis Ferdinand, utilized skillful negotiation with various races and parties to resolve political deadlocks. 

Supported by the emperor, who informed recalcitrant party leaders that reform "must be accomplished," Baron von Beck reached a final agreement in October 1906. This led to the adoption of a reform on December 1, which included raising the number of constituencies to 516, distributed according to the provinces.
