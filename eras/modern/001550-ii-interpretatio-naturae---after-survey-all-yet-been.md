# Interpretatio Naturae

*Interpretatio Naturae* is the second division of the *Instauratio*, a comprehensive scheme for the reorganization of the sciences designed to restore humanity's command over nature, which was lost following the fall. This specific division follows the *Partitiones Scientiarum* (represented by the *De Augmentis Scientiarum*), which served as an inventory of the human mind's possessions and a survey of existing or required sciences based on an analysis of the objects and faculties of human knowledge.

## Purpose and Methodology
The primary objective of *Interpretatio Naturae* is to establish a new method to train and direct the human mind toward the renovation of science. This phase begins after a survey of all previous discoveries and inventions has been completed. While the *Novum Organum* (specifically book ii) represents this division, the archive notes that it does so only imperfectly.

The methodology central to this division is the new method of induction, described as the *filum labyrinthi* (the thread of the labyrinth). This is necessary because nature appears to the mind as a "huge congeries of phenomena"—manifestations of simple, primitive qualities that are hidden by the complexity of the things themselves. To navigate this labyrinth and move from knowledge to power, the mind must follow this inductive thread.

## The Role of Observation and Natural History
A fundamental tenet of *Interpretatio Naturae* is that the new method cannot be applied until facts have been observed and collected. The philosophy posits that man, acting as the servant and interpreter of nature, can only understand or do that which he has observed in thought or fact regarding the course of nature.

The foundation of a "purer natural philosophy" is laid in natural history. This requires the preparation of a "natural and experimental history" that is both good and sufficient. To achieve this, the memory and the senses must be assisted through "ministration," ensuring that instances are arranged in tables and specific arrangements to prevent the mind from becoming confused or distracted.

## Requirements for the Interpreter
For the process of interpretation to be successful, the observer must adhere to strict mental discipline:
* **Receptivity:** The mind must be absolutely free from preconceived ideas.
* **Obedience:** Nature is conquered only through obedience.
* **Objectivity:** The observer must keep the eye steadily fixed on the facts of nature, receiving images simply as they are.

The goal of this disciplined approach is to avoid substituting a "dream of our own imagination" for the pattern of the world, and instead to write a "true vision" or "apocalypse" of the footsteps of the Creator.

## Context of the Author
The framework of *Interpretatio Naturae* is linked to the mental character of its architect, as seen in the fragment *De Interpretation Naturae Prooemium* (written approximately 1603). The author viewed himself as being born to be of advantage to mankind, combining the ideal of discovering truth for the good of the human race with a practical desire to serve his country and the church. His approach was characterized by a meditative, methodical, and far-seeing intellect that remained cautious and free from prejudice.
