# The Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies

The Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies is an educational network of five institutions located in Virginia, U.S.A. These five institutions are under the control of a single board of trustees, a structure designed to correlate the work of the system's preparatory schools with that of its colleges.

## Member Institutions
The system comprises a variety of colleges, academies, and institutes:

*   **Randolph-Macon College**: A Methodist Episcopal, South institution and one of the oldest Methodist Episcopal colleges in the United States. It received its charter in 1830 and opened in 1832 near Boydton in Mecklenburg county, Virginia. In 1868, the college was moved to Ashland, a village in Hanover county situated 17 miles northwest of Richmond. During the 1907-1908 period, the college maintained a faculty of 16 and an enrollment of 150 students. The college is also responsible for publishing *The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College*, an endowed historical series.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Bedford City)**: Established in 1890, this academy serves boys.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Front Royal)**: Established in 1892, this academy also serves boys.
*   **Randolph-Macon Woman's College**: Located in Lynchburg, Virginia, this college was established in 1893. In 1907-1908, it had an enrollment of 390 students.
*   **Randolph-Macon Institute**: Located in Danville, Virginia, this institute for girls was admitted into the "System" in 1897.

## Location and Context
The central college is located in Ashland, which was settled in 1845 and incorporated in 1856. The village is served by the Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac railway and is considered a favorite resort for those visiting from Richmond. The surrounding area is noted for the birthplaces of Patrick Henry (approximately 15 miles distant) and Henry Clay (approximately 7 miles out of Ashland).
