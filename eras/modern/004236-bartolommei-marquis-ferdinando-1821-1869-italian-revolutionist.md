# BARTOLOMMEI, MARQUIS FERDINANDO (1821-1869)

Marquis Ferdinando Bartolommei was an Italian statesman and revolutionist who played a significant role in the political developments of Tuscany between 1848 and 1860. Born into an old and noble Florentine family, Bartolommei distinguished himself from the moderately liberal aristocracy through his ardent Liberalism and his sympathies for the democratic party.

## Early Revolutionary Activity
During the period of 1847-1848, Bartolommei's home served as a center for revolutionary committees. He became a prominent figure during the brief era of the constitutional regime. However, following the return of Grand Duke Leopold II in 1849, which occurred under the protection of Austria, Bartolommei's political activities led to conflict with the authorities. In 1851, he attended a requiem service at the church of Santa Croce dedicated to those who had died in the campaign against Austria. Following disorders that broke out during this service, he was relegated to his country estate.

## Exile and International Influence
Shortly after his relegation, Bartolommei was implicated in the distribution of seditious literature, resulting in a one-year exile from Tuscany. During this period, he settled in Turin, where he established connections with Piedmontese liberals and Cavour. His travels subsequently took him to England and France; while abroad, he became enamoured of British institutions, a common sentiment among many Italian patriots of the time.

## Promotion of Italian Unity
Upon returning to Florence in 1853, Bartolommei dedicated himself to promoting the concepts of Italian unity and independence among the populace. Despite being carefully monitored by the police, he operated a secret printing-press within his palace in Florence. 

Recognizing that the nobility remained hesitant to adopt a stance of uncompromising hostility toward the house of Lorraine, Bartolommei allied himself more closely with the popular party. In this effort, he found an effective lieutenant in Giuseppe Dolfi (1818-1869), a whole-hearted enthusiast and baker who possessed significant influence over the common people.

## Military and Political Organization
As the possibility of war between Austria and Piedmont became imminent, Bartolommei took an active role in organizing Tuscan volunteers to join the Piedmontese army, financing these efforts with large sums of his own money. Additionally, he served as the president of the Societa Nazionale's Tuscan branch. He worked with great intensity to conspire for the overthrow of the grand duke.

## Biographical Sources
The most comprehensive biography of Bartolommei was written by his daughter, Matilde Gioli, in the 1905 work *Il Rivolgimento Toscano e l'azione popolare*. However, it is noted that Gioli may attribute an undue level of importance to her father's role in the Tuscan revolution and is not entirely fair in her depiction of other aristocratic leaders, such as Bettino Ricasoli. Other relevant documents include the *Lettere e documenti di B. Ricasoli* (1887-1896) and D. Zanichelli's *Lettere politiche di B. Ricasoli, U. Peruzzi, N. Corsini, e C. Ridolfi* (1898).
