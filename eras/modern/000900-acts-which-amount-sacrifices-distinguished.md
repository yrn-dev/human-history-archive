# General Average Sacrifices

General Average (G.A.) refers to a system of maritime law and adjustment used to calculate the amounts to be made good and paid by various interests involved in a maritime adventure. A central component of this system is the distinction between G.A. sacrifices and G.A. expenditures.

## Definition and Legal Framework
The York-Antwerp Rules provide definite provisions regarding acts that amount to G.A. sacrifices, particularly in areas where previous authority was doubtful or scanty. While these rules do not significantly alter English common law regarding the distinction between sacrifices and expenditures, they do introduce specific changes. For example, under Rule I, the jettison of deck cargo is not allowed as a G.A. sacrifice, even if the cargo was carried on deck according to an established custom of that particular trade.

## Compensation and Valuation
The rules establish specific protocols for making good losses resulting from G.A. acts:

*   **Cargo Loss:** According to Rule XVI, the amount to be made good for goods sacrificed is based on the loss sustained by the owner of the goods. This is calculated using market values at the termination of the adventure or at the date the vessel arrives.
*   **Loss of Freight:** Under Rule XV, loss of freight resulting from the loss of or damage to cargo is made good as G.A. if it was caused by a G.A. act, or when the loss or damage to the cargo itself is made good.
*   **Temporary Repairs:** Rule XIV stipulates that no "new for old" deductions are to be made from the cost of temporary repairs for damage that is allowable as G.A.

## Contributory Values and Adjustment
The adjustment of general average is the process of calculating the payments required from the several interests. The fundamental principle is that interests at risk contribute in proportion to the benefits they received upon the completion of the adventure.

### Calculation of Contributions
Contributions are not based on the amounts at stake at the moment the sacrifice occurred, but rather on the results at the end of the adventure. The liability to contribute is inchoate when the sacrifice is made and becomes complete upon the adventure's end—either through arrival at the destination or if the adventure is broken up at an intermediate point while the interest still survives. 

The following valuation rules apply:
*   **Depreciated or Lost Interests:** An interest that is lost after the sacrifice during the voyage pays nothing. An interest that has depreciated pays in proportion to its diminished value.
*   **Actual Values:** Under Rule XVII, contributions are made based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed property.
*   **Deductions:** Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight at risk, specifically port charges and crew's wages that would not have been incurred had the cargo and ship been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice (provided these were not already allowed as G.A.). Additionally, charges incurred for the property after the G.A. act are deducted, unless those charges are allowed in G.A.
*   **Exemptions:** Passengers' personal effects and luggage that were not shipped under a bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.

## Distinction from G.A. Expenditure
While G.A. sacrifices are settled based on the arrived or realized values at the end of the adventure, G.A. expenditures (often borne initially by the shipowner) follow a different rule if all interests are lost. In the event that all interests are lost, the burden of expenditure does not remain solely with the interest that first bore it; instead, contributions are made by all interests that were at stake when the expenditure was made, based on their values at that time.
