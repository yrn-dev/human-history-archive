## Geographical Contributions to Asiatic Inquiry
Kreitner was a participant in Szecheny's expedition during a period of significant scientific enterprise directed toward Asiatic inquiry. This era of research focused heavily on modifying previous understandings of the orography of the vast central region encompassing Mongolia and Tibet. Specifically, researchers worked to define the great highland plateau that extends from the frontier of Kashmir eastwards to China, and northwards from the Himalaya to Chinese Turkestan, with increased geographical exactness.

### Sachu (Saitu)
Sachu, located in Mongolia, is considered an obligatory point in modern map construction. While the currently adopted longitude value for Sachu is 94 deg. 54' E. of Greenwich (a revised value provided by Prjevalsky in the map accompanying his fourth exploration), various explorers provided differing measurements. Kreitner, as part of Szecheny's expedition, recorded the longitude of Sachu as 94 deg. 58'. Other recorded values for this point include:
*   Prjevalsky (second and third explorations): 94 deg. 26'
*   Krishna: 94 deg. 23'
*   Carey and Dalgleish: 94 deg. 48'
*   Littledale: 94 deg. 49'

### Darchendo (Tachienlu)
Darchendo, situated on the extreme east, is accepted as another obligatory point for geographical mapping. The Royal Geographical Society has adopted a value of 102 deg. 12" for this location. Kreitner's determination for the longitude of Darchendo was 102 deg. 5". Other values for this point were provided by Krishna (102 deg. 15") and Baber (102 deg. 18").
