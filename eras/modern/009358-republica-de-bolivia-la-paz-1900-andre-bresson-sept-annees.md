# Republica de Bolivia

The Republica de Bolivia is a South American nation with a total area of 515,156 square miles, according to Gotha computations (which include corrections for territory lost to Brazil in 1903). The country's administrative and political center has shifted between several provincial capitals, including Oruro, Cochabamba, Sucre (also known as Chuquisaca), and La Paz. While Sucre was previously chosen as the capital, the capital was located at La Paz following the revolution of 1898, as La Paz serves as the commercial metropolis and offers greater accessibility.

## Geography and Climate
Bolivia is characterized by a wide range of temperatures across different latitudes, which the natives categorize into five distinct climatic zones:

*   **Yungas:** Lowlands and mountain valleys up to 5,000 feet. This tropical region is exceedingly humid with a mean temperature of 70°F; malaria and fevers are common here.
*   **Valle:** Deep valleys between 5,000 and 9,500 feet. This sub-tropical region is described as a place of perpetual summer with moderate temperature variations.
*   **Cabezera de Valle:** The heads of deep valleys from 9,500 to 11,000 feet. This temperate zone has regular seasons.
*   **Puna:** The great central plateau between 11,000 and 12,500 feet. The air is cold and dry with a mean temperature of 54°F.
*   **Puna Brava:** Bleak territory from 12,500 feet up to the snow limit (approximately 17,500 feet), inhabited primarily by miners and shepherds.

The Beni river, a tributary of the Madeira, rises near La Paz (where it is known as the Rio de La Paz) and flows northeast. The lower river is navigable for 217 miles from Reyes to the Esperanza rapids. The department of Beni in the northeast is bounded by Brazil, Santa Cruz, Cochabamba, and La Paz. Its capital, Trinidad, was formerly a Jesuit mission.

## Economy and Resources
Agricultural development in Bolivia has been slow. In the temperate and warm Andean valleys, cereals, fruits, and vegetables are cultivated. Sugar-cane is grown primarily for the manufacture of rum, particularly in eastern Bolivia. Other crops include rice and fair-quality tobacco in the east and the *yungas* valleys of Cochabamba and La Paz. Superior grade cacao is grown in the department of Beni and the Andean valleys of Cochabamba and La Paz, while high-flavor coffee is produced in the eastern Andes. Indigenous staples include quinoa and coca; the latter is grown extensively in La Paz and Cochabamba (especially the province of Yungas) for medicinal export and native consumption.

Mining is a significant industry, exemplified by the Huanchaca silver mining company at Pulacayo, which employs 3,200 men.

## Infrastructure and Communications
Bolivia has historically been an isolated region, leading to various international treaties to develop its railways. A 1903 treaty with Brazil provided for a 180-mile railway around the falls of the Madeira to give north-eastern Bolivia access to the Amazon, along with L2,000,000 in cash for internal railway construction. 

Following a war with Chile (1878-81), Bolivia lost control of the railways in its Litoral department. However, a treaty ratified in 1905 saw Chile agree to construct a 295-mile railway from Arica to La Paz and provide pecuniary assistance for other interior lines. Argentina also undertook to extend its northern railway from Jujuy to the Bolivian town of Tupiza. Additionally, the Peruvian Corporation built a 54-mile line from Guaqui on Lake Titicaca to Alto de La Paz, which connects to the city of La Paz via a 5-mile electric line. This route provides access to the Peruvian port of Mollendo.

## Religion and Society
According to Article 2 of the constitution, the state recognizes and supports the Roman Apostolic Catholic religion. While the public exercise of other worship is prohibited, it is tolerated in colonies and tacitly extended to resident foreigners. A 1900 census recorded 1,609,365 Roman Catholics and 24,245 people of other creeds.

The religious structure consists of one archbishopric (La Plata, with its seat at Sucre) and three bishoprics (La Paz, Cochabamba, and Santa Cruz). The *Propaganda Fide* manages mission work among the Indians through five colleges and numerous missions, overseeing an estimated 20,000 Indians. The annual state appropriation for the Church is approximately L17,150.

## Historical and Cartographic Records
Knowledge of Bolivian geography has been augmented by various surveys and publications. Notable contributions include Sir Martin Conway's 1900 "Map of the Andes of La Paz" and Major P. H. Fawcett's 1906-1907 survey of the Brazilian boundary. Other significant archival sources include the *Diccionario Geografico de la Republica de Bolivia* (1900) by M.V. Ballivian and Eduardo Idiaquez, and the explorations of Andre Bresson detailed in *Sept annees d'explorations, de voyages et de sejours dans l'Amerique australe* (1886).
