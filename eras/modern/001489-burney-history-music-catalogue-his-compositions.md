# History of Music by Charles Burney

The *History of Music* is a comprehensive musical study authored by the English musical historian Charles Burney (1726–1814). The work was a long-projected project that Burney pursued alongside his various professional avocations.

## Research and Development
To collect materials that were unavailable in Great Britain, Burney traveled abroad on two separate occasions. In June 1770, he left London with numerous letters of introduction, visiting Paris, Geneva, Turin, Milan, Padua, Venice, Bologna, Florence, Rome, and Naples. These observations were first published in 1771 as *The Present State of Music in France and Italy*. 

Burney returned to the continent in July 1772 to gather further materials. Upon his return to London, he published the results of this second tour in 1773 under the title *The Present State of Music in Germany, the Netherlands and United Provinces*.

## Publication and Content
The first volume of the *History of Music* appeared in 1776 in quarto format. The second volume followed in 1782, and the third and fourth volumes were published in 1789. The first volume included a "Dissertation on the Music of the Ancients," which was translated into German by J.J. Eschenburg and printed in Leipzig in 1781. In producing the work, Burney utilized the first two volumes of Padre Martini's writings for aid.

The fourth volume is noted as the least satisfactory of the set, as the treatment of Bach and Handel is considered quite inadequate. Specifically, the fourth volume provides a full account of the career of Johann Christian Bach (1735–1782).

## Reception and Criticism
The *History of Music* was generally recognized as a work of great merit, though it faced severe criticism from certain figures. In Germany, it was criticized by Forkel, and in Italy, it was attacked by the Spanish ex-Jesuit Requeno. In his 1798 work *Saggi sul Ristabilimento dell' Arte Armonica de' Greci e Romani Cantori*, Requeno specifically targeted Burney's account of ancient Greek music and referred to him as *lo scompigliato Burney*.

## Contributions to Musical History
Beyond the *History of Music*, Burney is credited with recovering the composer Des Prés from oblivion, a task of rehabilitation that was later continued by Forkel. 

## Catalogue of Charles Burney's Compositions
In addition to his work as a historian, Charles Burney was a composer. His known compositions include:

*   **Keyboard Works:** Six Sonatas for the harpsichord; Six Lessons for the harpsichord; Three Concertos for the harpsichord; and Two Sonatas for pianoforte, violin, and violoncello.
*   **Instrumental Works:** Two Sonatas for the harp or piano (with violin and violoncello accompaniments); two sets of Sonatas for two violins and a bass; Six Duets for two German flutes; and Six Concertos for the violin, etc., in eight parts.
*   **Organ and Sacred Works:** Six concert pieces with an introduction and fugue for the organ; Anthems, etc.; and a Cantata.
*   **Other Works:** *XII. Canzonetti a due voci in Canone*, featuring poetry by Abate Metastasio.

Burney also produced operatic music, including a free English adaptation of J.J. Rousseau's operetta *Le Devin du village*, titled *The Cunning Man*, produced at Drury Lane in 1766. Earlier in his career, he wrote music for Thomson's *Alfred*, produced at Drury Lane theatre on March 30, 1745.
