# The Great Depression: The Decade of Economic Collapse

## Overview
The Great Depression was the most severe economic downturn in the history of the industrialized Western world, lasting roughly from 1929 to 1939. While the stock market crash of October 1929 is often cited as the starting gun, historians and economists argue that the collapse was the result of a systemic failure involving agricultural overproduction, unsustainable consumer debt, and a fragile international banking system.

The crisis was characterized by a catastrophic spiral: as banks failed, the money supply contracted, leading to deflation and massive unemployment—which peaked at nearly 25% in the United States. The era was defined by "Hoovervilles" (shanty towns) and the "Dust Bowl," a series of severe droughts and soil erosion that devastated the American Midwest.

A central point of historical debate remains the primary cause of the Depression. The **Monetarist school**, led by Milton Friedman, argues that the Federal Reserve exacerbated the crisis by failing to provide liquidity to banks. Conversely, **Keynesian economists** argue that a collapse in aggregate demand and a lack of government spending were the primary drivers.

## Key Facts
*   **The Banking Crisis:** Between 1929 and 1933, thousands of U.S. banks failed, wiping out the life savings of millions of depositors because deposits were not yet insured.
*   **The New Deal:** President Franklin D. Roosevelt implemented a series of programs (the "Alphabet Agencies" like the CCC and WPA) to create jobs and regulate the financial sector via the Glass-Steagall Act.
*   **Global Impact:** The depression was not limited to the U.S.; it triggered political instability worldwide, contributing to the rise of totalitarian regimes in Germany and Italy as democratic governments failed to solve economic misery.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Dust Bowl** was caused by a combination of severe drought and poor farming practices that stripped the prairie grasses, creating massive "black blizzards" of topsoil.
2. **The "Bank Holiday"** of 1933 saw FDR close every bank in the U.S. for four days to stop bank runs and restore public confidence.
3. **GDP Contraction:** Between 1929 and 1933, the U.S. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) fell by approximately 30%.
