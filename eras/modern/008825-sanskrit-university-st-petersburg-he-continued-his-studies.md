# Sanskrit Studies and Scholars

The study of Sanskrit and comparative philology in the 19th century involved a network of international scholars, with significant academic activity occurring in Germany, Russia, and the United States. This field focused on the grammatical structure of Sanskrit, its relationship to other languages, and the translation of ancient Indian texts.

## Otto von Bohtlingk and the University of St Petersburg

Otto von Bohtlingk (1815–1904) was a distinguished German Sanskrit scholar born in St Petersburg. Between 1833 and 1835, he studied Oriental languages at the university of St Petersburg, focusing particularly on Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic. Following his time in Russia, he continued his studies in Germany, spending time in Berlin and later in Bonn from 1839 to 1842.

Bohtlingk returned to St Petersburg in 1842, where he became attached to the Royal Academy of Sciences and was elected an ordinary member of the society in 1855. His career was marked by several high honors, including being named "Russian state councillor" in 1860 and later "privy councillor" with a title of nobility. He eventually moved to Jena in 1868 and later to Leipzig, where he resided until his death on April 1, 1904.

Bohtlingk's contributions to Indian and comparative philology were extensive. His early work included an edition of Panini's *Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln* (1839–1840), which served as a criticism of the philological methods used by Franz Bopp. He later republished this work in 1887 as *Paninis Grammatik mit Ubersetzung*, providing a complete translation. Other notable publications include:
* *Vopadevas Grammatik* (1847)
* *Uber die Sprache der Jakuten* (1851)
* *Indische Spruche* (1870–1873)
* A translation and critical examination of the *Chhandogya-upanishad* (1889)
* A translation of the *Brihadaranyaka-upanishad* (1889)
* A treatise on Sanskrit accents titled *Uber den Accent im Sanskrit* (1843)

His *magnum opus* was the *Sanskrit-Worterbuch*, a seven-volume dictionary published in St Petersburg between 1853 and 1875 (with a new edition from 1879 to 1889). This work took twenty-three years to complete and was produced with the assistance of Albrecht Weber and Rudolf Roth.

## Franz Bopp and Comparative Grammar

Franz Bopp (1791–1867) was a German philologist whose work was foundational to the field. His interest in the "sacred language of the Hindus" was stimulated by the lectures of Karl J. Windischmann and the writing of Fr. Schlegel. In 1812, Bopp traveled to Paris to study Sanskrit, utilizing the Imperial library's collection of manuscripts and books from the Serampore and Calcutta presses.

In 1821, Bopp was appointed to the chair of Sanskrit and comparative grammar at Berlin. His major works include the *Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache* (1827) and a Sanskrit and Latin glossary (1830). His primary focus was his *Comparative Grammar* (*Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen*), published in six parts between 1833 and 1852. Bopp's objective was to investigate the origin of grammatical forms, trace phonetic laws, and describe the original grammatical structure of these languages through intercomparison.

## Other Notable Sanskrit Scholars

Several other scholars contributed significantly to the study of Sanskrit and related philological fields:

**Theodor Benfey (1809–1881):** A German philologist who originally studied Hebrew. Benfey turned to Sanskrit after a wager that he could learn enough of the language in a few weeks to review a book. He produced a *Manual of Sanskrit* (1852–1854), a practical Sanskrit grammar (1858), and an edition of the *Pantscha Tantra* (1859). He is best known for his *Sanskrit-English Dictionary* (1866).

**Edward Washburn Hopkins (1857– ):** An American scholar who studied at Leipzig and later became a professor of Sanskrit and comparative philology at Yale University in 1895. He served as the editor and secretary of the American Oriental Society. His writings include *Caste in Ancient India* (1881), *Manu's Lawbook* (1884), *Religions of India* (1895), and *The Great Epic of India* (1901).

**Jan Hendrik Kern (1833– ):** A Dutch Orientalist who studied under Albrecht Weber in Berlin. Kern served as a professor of Sanskrit at Benares in 1863 and at Leiden in 1865. His work included the study of Malay languages and the publication of *Geschiedenis van het Buddhisme in Indië* (1881–1883).

**Carl Darling Buck (1866– ):** An American philologist who studied in Leipzig and became a professor of Sanskrit and Indo-European comparative philology at the University of Chicago in 1892, though his primary research focused on Italic dialects.
