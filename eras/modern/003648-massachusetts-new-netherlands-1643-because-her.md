# Lady Deborah Moody and the New Netherlands

## Migration to the New Netherlands
In 1643, Lady Deborah Moody departed from Massachusetts to settle in the New Netherlands (modern-day New York). Her decision to leave Massachusetts was driven by her antipaedobaptist views. During her journey, she stopped at New Haven, where she successfully won Mrs. Eaton, the wife of Governor Theophilus Eaton, to her principles.

## Settlement and Religious Liberty
Upon arriving in the New Netherlands, Lady Deborah Moody settled at Gravesend, a location that is now part of Brooklyn. Her settlement there was made possible after she received a guarantee of religious liberty from the Dutch authorities.

## Baptist Activity in the Region
Lady Deborah Moody's migration was part of a broader trend; from 1643 onward, antipaedobaptists from New England and other locations settled in the New Netherlands. Other English Baptists also labored in the region, though they faced varying degrees of acceptance:

*   **Francis Doughty:** An English Baptist who had spent time in Rhode Island, Doughty worked in the region in 1656. He baptized several converts, an action that resulted in his banishment.
*   **William Wickenden:** Hailing from Providence, Wickenden evangelized and administered ordinances at Flushing later in 1656. He was subsequently banished and heavily fined.
*   **Valentine Wightman:** A General Baptist from Connecticut (1681–1747), Wightman made occasional missionary visits to New York starting in 1711. He visited at the invitation of Nicolas Eyres, a businessman who had adopted Baptist views. In 1714, Wightman baptized Eyres and several others, assisting them in the organization of a church. This church nearly collapsed in 1730 due to debts incurred while erecting a meeting-house.

## Other Baptist Settlements
Beyond the New Netherlands, other Baptist communities formed in the surrounding areas during the 17th and 18th centuries. A number of Baptists settled on Block Island around 1663, and a Baptist church, which was probably Arminian, was established at Oyster Bay some time before 1724.
