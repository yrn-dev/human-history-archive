# Mineral Resources and Springs of Bohemia

Bohemia possesses a diverse array of mineral wealth and over two hundred mineral springs. While the region is rich in various metals and minerals, salt is entirely absent from its natural deposits.

## Mineral Springs and Watering-Places
Although Bohemia has more than two hundred mineral springs, only a few are utilized for medicinal purposes. Among the most celebrated globally are Carlsbad, Marienbad, Franzensbad, Teplitz-Schonau, and Bilin. Other springs of importance include Sedlitz, Pullna, and Seidschitz near Brux; Giesshubl near Carlsbad; and several located at the foot of the Schneekoppe, including Johannisbad, Neudorf, Sangerberg, Konigswart, Liebwerda, and Tetschen.

### Marienbad
Marienbad is a prominent European watering-place located 115 miles west of Prague by rail and 40 miles southwest of Carlsbad. Situated at an altitude of 2,093 feet on the outskirts of the Kaiserwald, the town is surrounded by pine forests and sloping hills. The town's mineral springs, which are owned by the adjoining abbey of Tepl, number eight. These are used for drinking and bathing, with the exception of the Marienquelle, which is used exclusively for bathing.

The springs vary in composition:
* **Alkaline-saline waters:** Found in the Ferdinandsbrunnen and Kreuzbrunnen, these resemble Carlsbad waters but are cold and contain nearly double the amount of purgative salts.
* **Iron waters:** The Karolinenbrunnen and Ambrosiusbrunnen are among the strongest iron waters in the world.
* **Earthy-alkaline waters:** Provided by the Rudolfsbrunnen.

These waters are used to treat obesity, diabetes, gout, and liver affections, requiring patients to follow a strictly regulated diet. Additionally, the town offers mud-baths (moor baths) using peat that is the richest in iron in the world. Approximately 1,000,000 bottles of mineral water are exported from Marienbad annually.

### Bilin
Bilin is a town located 90 miles north of Prague. It is famous for the *Biliner Sauerbrunnen* mineral springs, which have a temperature of 45.6 degrees Fahrenheit and contain a high proportion of bicarbonate of soda. The town exports approximately 4,000,000 bottles of this water annually, as well as salt recovered from the water through evaporation.

## Metallic and Non-Metallic Minerals
Bohemia contains nearly every useful mineral and metal except salt.

### Fuels and Industrial Minerals
Lignite and coal are the most important minerals in terms of value and quantity. Lignite fields are found in the north-east corner of Bohemia around Teplitz, Ossegg, Falkenau, Dux, and Brux. Coal is mined in several areas, including Pilsen, Kladno, Radnitz, Nurschan, Rakonitz, Schlan, and Buschtehrad; the mines at Radnitz are the oldest in Bohemia, dating to the 17th century.

Other industrial minerals include:
* **Iron:** Ores are found at Nucic and Krusnahora, with major foundries located around Konigshof and Kladno.
* **Silver and Lead:** Silver is extracted at Joachimsthal and Pribram; however, the once-famous silver mines of Kuttenberg are now abandoned. Lead is also extracted at Pribram.
* **Other Metals:** Tin is found at Graupen in the Erzgebirge (the only location for tin in Austria). Antimony is extracted near Tabor at Milleschau. Uranium and radium are found near Joachimsthal.
* **Miscellaneous:** Graphite is found near Budweis and Krumau; porcelain-earth is located near Carlsbad. Other minerals present include cobalt, nickel, sulphur, arsenic, alum, copper, and various building stones and precious stones, such as the pyrope (Bohemian garnet).

The region also collects large quantities of asphalt and peat, particularly in the south-west.

## Geological Formations and Rare Minerals
Bohemia's landscape includes various basaltic rocks. Leucite-basalts, characterized by small rounded crystals of leucite, are found in the region. Additionally, a rare group of basic rocks known as melilite-basalts—characterized by the lime-alumina-silicate melilite—occurs in Bohemia.

Regarding rare minerals, greenockite (cadmium sulphide) is found in Bohemia. In this region, the mineral appears only as a powder dusted over the surface of calamine and zinc blende minerals, which contain small amounts of cadmium.
