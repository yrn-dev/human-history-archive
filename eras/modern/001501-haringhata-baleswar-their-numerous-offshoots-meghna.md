# The Meghna, Haringhata, and Baleswar

The Meghna, the Haringhata (also known as the Baleswar), and the Arial Khan are the principal rivers of the district. These waterways, along with their numerous offshoots, define the region's geography and navigation.

## The Meghna
The Meghna is a noble river that represents the accumulated waters of the Ganges and the Brahmaputra. It flows in a southerly direction along the eastern boundary of the district for approximately 100 miles before debouching into the Bay of Bengal. In the latter part of its course, the river expands into a large estuary. This estuary contains many islands, with Dakshin Shahbazpur being the principal one. The islands located on the sea-front are subject to devastation caused by cyclonic storm-waves.

## The Haringhata and Baleswar
The Haringhata and Baleswar, along with the Garai and Madhumati, are various local names for the same river at different points of its course. This river is a great offshoot of the Ganges. It enters Backergunje near the north-west corner of the district, where it forms the western boundary. While its upper reaches are characterized by great windings, it runs south, eventually crossing the Sundarbans and falling into the Bay of Bengal via a large and deep estuary. This estuary is capable of receiving ships of considerable burden.

The river is navigable by native boats of large tonnage throughout its course in the district. Furthermore, large sea-going ships can navigate as far up as Morrellganj, located in the neighboring district of Jessore. One of the most important tributaries of the river in Backergunje is the Kacha, a considerable stream that flows south for 20 miles before falling into the Baleswar; the Kacha is navigable by large boats year-round.

## The Arial Khan and Other Waterways
The Arial Khan is a branch of the Ganges that enters the district from the north and generally flows in a south-easterly direction until it reaches the estuary of the Meghna. The width of the main channel of the Arial Khan varies by season, measuring about 1,700 yards in the dry season and between 2,000 and 3,000 yards during the rains. It is navigable throughout the year by the largest size of native cargo boats and features several offshoots and a number of tributaries.

Other rivers of minor importance in the region include the Khairabad, Kumar, Ghagar, Nihalganj, Bishkhali, and Barisal. All rivers in the district are subject to tidal action, originating from the Bay of Bengal to the south and the Meghna to the north.
