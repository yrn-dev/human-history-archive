# Barcelona and Margarita

The archive contains records regarding two distinct locations: the city of Barcelona in Spain and the island of Margarita in the Caribbean.

## Barcelona: History and Development
Barcelona, located in eastern Spain on the Mediterranean Sea, serves as the capital of the province of Barcelona and was formerly the capital of Catalonia. The city is situated on a small plain between the Llobregat and Besos rivers, bounded by the Montanas Matas to the north-east, the heights of Tibidabo to the west, and the fortified hills of Montjuich to the south-west.

Traditionally founded in the 3rd century B.C. by the Carthaginian Hamilcar Barca, the city was known as Barcino. Following the Roman conquest, Augustus renamed it Julia Faventia (later Augusta and Pia), granting it the status of a Roman colony. By the 2nd century A.D., it had become a leading mart of the western Mediterranean, competing with Massilia and Tarraco. The city's early history includes the founding of a bishopric in 343 and serving as the temporary capital for the Visigoths in 415 and 531.

After being captured by the Moors in 713, the city passed to the Franks in 801. Independent monarchs known as the counts of Barcelona ruled from 874. The city reached its zenith of wealth and fame following the 1149 union between Aragon and Catalonia. During this era, Barcelona's merchant ships traded as far as the Baltic and North Seas to the west and Alexandria to the east. In 1258, James I of Aragon authorized the *Consulado del Mar*, a maritime law code adopted by various European states.

The union of Aragon and Castile in 1479, combined with the 1492 discovery of America, shifted commercial supremacy to western Spanish ports. This led to internal unrest, and in 1640, Barcelona became the center of a rebellion against Philip IV, seeking French protection. Although it returned to its allegiance in 1652, it was captured by the duke of Vendome in 1697.

## Barcelona: Culture and Industry
By 1900, Barcelona had a population of 533,000 and was the principal seaport of Spain. The city is a hub for railways connecting to Saragossa, Madrid, and Perpignan. Its industrial landscape is characterized by huge factories and a busy port, contrasted by lush suburbs and gardens.

The city's intellectual history is documented in various works, including A. A. P. Arimon's *Barcelona antigua y moderna* and S. Sampere's *Topografia antigua de Barcelona*. Political and economic histories have been detailed by A. Capmany and T. Tomich. Additionally, M. Gil Maestre has traced the spread of criminality and the revolutionary anarchist movement within the city.

Notable figures associated with the city include Victor Balaguer (1824–1901), a politician and author born in Barcelona who led the Liberal party there from 1843 to 1868. Another figure, Ignatius, arrived in Barcelona during Lent in 1524 to study Latin under the guidance of a grammar school master and a pious lady named Isabella Roser.

## Margarita Island
Margarita is a Caribbean island belonging to Venezuela, located approximately 12 miles north of the Araya peninsula. It is roughly 40 miles long with an area of 400 square miles. The island's geography consists of two mountainous extremities—the peak of Macanao (4,484 ft.) in the west and the peak of Copei (4,170 ft.) in the east—connected by a narrow isthmus and separated by the Laguna Grande.

Discovered by Columbus in 1498, the island was granted to Marceto Villalobos by Charles V in 1524. It faced various raids, including one by Lope de Aguirre in 1561 and the destruction of the town of Pompatar by the Dutch in 1662. Administratively, Margarita was attached to Cumana for a long period before becoming independent in the eighteenth century.

The island's economy historically relied on pearl fisheries, which were highly productive in the 16th and 17th centuries but are no longer important. Current principal industries include salt making and fishing. Women on the island produce coarse straw hats for sale on the mainland. Despite a healthful climate, the island's products cannot fully support its population, leading to periodic emigration. As of 1904, the population was estimated at 40,000, consisting largely of half-caste Guayqueri Indians. The capital is Asunción, and the primary port is Pompatar.

## Military Conflicts and Independence
Margarita played a role in the wars of independence, with its sailors and traders providing invaluable assistance to revolutionists. In 1817, the Spanish general Morillo was driven from the island's shores; consequently, Margarita was renamed Nueva Esparta (New Sparta) and established as a separate state.

In December, Bolivar landed first in Margarita and then at Barcelona. A provisional government was established there to assemble troops to resist Morillo's advancing division. On February 16, 1817, the forces engaged in a three-day conflict resulting in the defeat of the royalists. Morillo's retreat was further compounded by an overthrow led by J.A. Paez and his *llaneros*.

Following this, Bolivar, recognized as commander-in-chief, established headquarters at Angostura on the Orinoco. In February 1819, he submitted his views on government to a congress and surrendered his authority, though he was requested to resume power until independence was fully established. Bolivar subsequently crossed the Cordilleras to join General Santander in New Granada. This campaign led to the entry into Tunja in July 1819 and a victory at Boyaca on August 7, granting Bolivar possession of Bogota and all New Granada.
