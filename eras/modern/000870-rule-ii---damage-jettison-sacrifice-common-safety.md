# RULE II.--DAMAGE BY JETTISON AND SACRIFICE FOR THE COMMON SAFETY

Rule II is one of the provisions established under the York-Antwerp Rules, which govern the adjustment of General Average (G.A.). These rules provide definite provisions on points where legal authority was previously doubtful or scanty, often adopting a broader view than the strict principles of English common law.

## Definition and Scope of Rule II
Under Rule II, damage caused to a ship, its cargo, or both, is made good as General Average if that damage occurs by or as a consequence of a sacrifice made for the common safety. This rule specifically includes damage caused by water that enters a ship through hatches that were opened, or other openings created, for the purpose of making a jettison for the common safety.

## General Average vs. English Common Law
The York-Antwerp Rules, including Rule II, differ in important respects from former English practice and common law. The strict principle of English common law requires that for a sacrifice or expenditure to be characterized as G.A., it must be made under the pressure of a common risk for the common safety of the several interests involved in the adventure. Under the English view, it is insufficient for an expenditure to be prudent, necessary for the completion of the adventure, or beneficial to both the shipowner and cargo owner; the safety of the interests must be at stake.

In contrast, the York-Antwerp Rules follow practices prevalent in foreign countries. While they do not significantly alter English common law regarding the distinction between G.A. sacrifices and expenditures, they do introduce specific changes. For example, Rule I stipulates that the jettison of deck cargo—including any structure not built into the vessel's frame—is not allowed as G.A., even if the cargo was carried on deck according to established trade customs.

## Related Acts of Sacrifice
Rule II exists alongside other rules that define various acts of sacrifice for the common safety:

*   **Fire Extinguishing (Rule III):** Damage to ship or cargo caused by water or other means, including scuttling or beaching a burning ship to extinguish a fire, is made good as G.A. However, compensation is not provided for portions of the ship, bulk cargo, or separate packages that were actually on fire.
*   **Voluntary Stranding (Rule V):** If a ship is intentionally run on shore for common safety, the resulting loss or damage is allowed as G.A., unless the ship would have inevitably sunk or driven onto rocks/shore regardless of the course taken.
*   **Sails and Spars (Rule VI):** Damage caused by forcing a ship off the ground or driving her higher up the ground for common safety is allowed as G.A. This does not apply to damage caused by carrying a press of sail while the ship is afloat.
*   **Machinery (Rule VII):** Damage to boilers and machinery occurring during efforts to refloat a ship in a position of peril is allowed as G.A., provided there was an actual intention to float the ship for common safety.
*   **Fuel Consumption (Rule IX):** Cargo, stores, or ship's materials burnt for fuel during a time of peril for common safety are admitted as G.A., provided an ample supply of fuel had been provided.

## Expenses and Port of Refuge
The rules also address expenditures associated with these sacrifices. Under Rule X, expenses for entering a port of refuge due to accident, sacrifice, or extraordinary circumstances necessary for common safety are admitted as G.A. If the ship later sails with all or part of its original cargo, the expenses of leaving the port are also allowed.

Furthermore, the cost of discharging cargo to enable repairs to the ship—whether those repairs were necessitated by accident or sacrifice—is allowed as G.A. if the repairs were necessary for the safe prosecution of the voyage. This applies to repairs at both ports of call and ports of refuge. Associated expenses, such as storing cargo on shore and reloading/stowing it after repairs, are also treated as G.A. (Rule X(c)), as is any incidental loss or damage during those operations (Rule XII).

## Valuation and Contribution
When damage or loss is made good as G.A. under these rules, the amount for sacrificed goods is based on the loss sustained by the owner, calculated using market values at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival.

Contributions to G.A. are based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good for sacrificed property. Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight, such as crew's wages and port charges that would not have been incurred had the ship and cargo been totally lost at the time of the sacrifice. Notably, personal effects and passengers' luggage not shipped under a bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.
