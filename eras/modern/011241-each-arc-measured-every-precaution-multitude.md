# Measurement of Arcs and Geodetic Applications

The measurement of arcs is a fundamental process utilized across various scientific, mathematical, and technical disciplines, ranging from global geodesy and cartography to structural engineering and the design of recreational equipment.

## Geodetic and Astronomical Arc Measurement
In the field of earth measurement, the determination of arcs is essential for establishing precise geographical positions. In one instance of high-precision measurement, every arc was measured using a multitude of observations and every available precaution. The primary source of uncertainty in such measurements can be the retardation of the current; for example, a current traveling between Teheran and Potsdam (a distance of 3000 m.) took 0.20 seconds, though the final value is likely correct within 0.05 seconds. Such determinations can result in the repositioning of astronomical sites, such as placing the Madras observatory 2' 27" west of its previously adopted position.

Historically, Eratosthenes (276-196 B.C.) introduced an effective mode of arc-measurement to determine the circumference of the globe. By knowing that Syene and Alexandria were situated 5000 stadia apart on the same meridian, he observed that the sun was 7 deg. 12' south of the zenith at the northern extremity of this arc while it was vertically overhead at the southern extremity. From this, he inferred the globe's entire circumference to be 252,000 stadia.

## Cartography and Map Projections
In cartography, the shortest distance between two points on a globe's surface is represented by the arc of a great circle. For points located on the equator or the same meridian, distances are found using tables of the lengths of arcs of the equator and meridian. In other cases, spherical trigonometry, globes, or maps are used.

Different map projections utilize arcs in various ways:
*   **Globular Projection:** This representation of a hemisphere uses a circular boundary. The meridians are arcs of circles that pass through the poles and divide the equator into equal parts. Similarly, the parallels are arcs of circles that divide the extreme and central meridians into equal parts.
*   **Field Sheets:** Topographical surveys often use conical or polyconic projections. To avoid the difficulty of plotting flat arcs for parallels, some surveys replace these arcs with polygons, where each side equals the length of the arc portion it replaces. This is particularly suitable for scales of 1:125,000 and larger.

## Engineering and Mathematical Applications
In structural engineering, specifically regarding the graphic method of finding deflection in a beam, arcs are used to represent radii of curvature. A span (L) is divided into equal parts (l), and radii of curvature ($R_1, R_2, R_3$) are computed for the sections. To make these drawable for a draughtsman, a constant (b) is used to create a series of radii ($r_1, r_2, r_3$). A curve is then drawn using arcs of length $l_1, l_2, l_3$ and radii $r_1, r_2$. To ensure accuracy, the value of $b$ must be regulated so that there is no sensible difference between the length of the arc and its chord.

In mathematical analysis, the concept of the arc of integration is used. For a continuous real function (U) on a circumference, a small circle centered at point $P_0$ can cut the given circle at points A and B, creating an arc $AP_0B$. Further circles can define a smaller arc $A'P_0B'$. Integrals can then be evaluated as the sum of two parts: one along the small arc $AP_0B$ and another along the greater arc $AB$.

## Technical Applications in Game Design
The geometry of the arc is also applied to the design of pockets in billiards, where the size and shape of the pocket affect the ease of scoring. The mouth of the pocket, referred to as the "drop" or "fall," is part of the arc of a circle. These circles are larger for corner pockets than for middle pockets. 

The measurement of these arcs differs by pocket location:
*   **Corner Pockets:** Measured by the length of the tangent drawn from the outside point of the arc to the cushion on either side. The fall is located halfway down the passage cut into the cushions.
*   **Middle Pockets:** Measured at the points where the arc terminates in the cushions. The fall (the outside point of the arc) lies on the line of the cushion's outside face.
