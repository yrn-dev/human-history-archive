# Late 19th Century Artistic and Academic Works

The final decade of the 19th century saw a diverse array of contributions across the fields of fine arts, philosophy, theology, and historical scholarship. This period was marked by a transition in artistic focus toward rustic life in France, the publication of significant philosophical treatises in Great Britain, and the refinement of historical chronologies regarding ancient dynasties.

## The Works of Jules Breton

Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton (born May 1, 1827, in Courrières, France) was a French painter who, after early studies in Ghent under de Vigne, in Antwerp under Baron Wappers, and in Paris under Drolling, shifted his focus from historical subjects to the memories of nature and the countryside. Breton became primarily known as a painter of rustic life, with a particular emphasis on the province of Artois. His work is generally categorized into four themes: religious festivals, rural festivals, rest, and labour.

During the late 19th century, Breton produced several notable works that continued his exploration of rural and natural themes. These include "The Last Gleanings" (1895), "Gathering Poppies" (1897), "The Alarm Cry" (1899), and "Twilight Glory" (1900). His career was marked by significant institutional recognition; he was elected to the Institut in 1886 following the death of Baudry.

## Philosophical and Theological Publications

The late 1890s were a productive period for British philosophy and biblical commentary. B. Bosanquet contributed extensively to various branches of philosophy during this time, publishing "Psychology of the Moral Self" (1897), "Philosophical Theory of the State" (1899), and "Aspects of the Social Problem" (1895). In the realm of logic, Bosanquet published "Essentials of Logic" in 1895. Other philosophical works of the era include D. G. Ritchie's "Natural Rights" (1895) and J. Royce's "The Conception of God" (1897).

In theological scholarship, the late 19th century saw the release of several important commentaries and investigations into the New Testament. T.K. Abbott published a commentary on Ephesians in 1897, and B. Weiss released the third edition of his New Testament Introduction in 1897 (with an English translation appearing in 1887). Similarly, G. Salmon's New Testament Introduction saw its eighth edition in 1897. F.J.A. Hort published "Prolegomena to St Paul's Epistles to the Romans and the Ephesians" in 1895, while T. Zahn produced work between 1897 and 1899.

## Historical and Scientific Scholarship

Academic efforts in the late 1890s also focused on the rigorous dating of ancient history and the study of marine biology. Various historians proposed different chronologies for the first three dynasties of ancient civilizations. For example, Maspero (1897) and Rost (1897) provided specific date ranges for these periods, while Sayce (1899) and Marquart (1899) offered alternative timelines.

In the field of zoology, specifically the study of Copepoda, several authorities published findings toward the end of the century. These include Hansen's work on "Choniostomatidae" (1897) and Sars's "Caspian Entomostraca" (1897). Other contributions were made by Giesbrecht, who published on "Asterocheriden" in 1899, and Bassett-Smith and Brady, who both published research on Copepoda in 1899.

## Academic Works on Austrian History

The mid-to-late 1890s saw the publication of several foundational texts regarding the constitutional and imperial history of Austria. A. Bechmann published "Lehrbuch der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch" between 1895 and 1896. A. Huber's "Oesterreichische Reichsgesch" saw a second edition in 1895. Additionally, A. Luschin von Ebengreuth published a two-volume work of "first-class importance" titled "Oesterreichische Reichsgesch" in 1895 and 1896, followed by "Grundriss der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch" in 1899. Regarding the relations between Austria and Hungary, J. Andrassy published "Ungarns Ausgleich mit Oesterreich, 1867" in 1897.
