# Bali

## Geography and Physical Characteristics
Bali is an island located in the East Indies, situated to the east of Java. It is separated from Java by the Bali Strait, which is shallow and reaches its narrowest point at slightly over one mile in width. The island measures 93 miles in length and has a maximum breadth of 50 miles, with a total area of 2,095 square miles.

Physically, Bali is associated with Java, sharing the same soil and climate. Its geological formation consists of three distinct regions: alluvial plains located between older formations, a southern peninsula composed of Tertiary limestone, and a central volcanic region. The island features mountains of proportionate height to those in Java, including several deep lakes and streams that the native population utilizes for irrigation. The central chain of mountains has an average altitude of 3,282 feet. The highest volcanoes on the island are Gunung Agung (also known as Bali Beak) at 10,497 feet, Tabanan at 7,545 feet, and Batur at 7,383 feet.

## Flora, Fauna, and the Wallace Line
The flora and fauna of Bali are associated with those of Java. To the east, Bali is separated from Lombok by a deep strait. This boundary was used by A. R. Wallace to establish "Wallace's Line," a demarcation used to separate Asiatic fauna from Australian fauna. In terms of ichthyology, this line separates the Cyprinoid and Acyprinoid divisions of the equatorial zone; while two species of Cyprinid fishes have been found in Bali, none are known from Lombok or islands further east.

## Administration and Politics
In 1882, for administrative purposes, Bali was combined with the island of Lombok to form the Dutch residency of Lombok and Bali, having been separated from Java. Politically, the island is divided into two categories:
1. Two districts located on Dutch territory: Jembrana and Buleleng. Buleleng, situated in the north-west, serves as the chief town.
2. Five autonomous states: Tabanan, Badung, Mengui, Bangli, and Klung Lung.

In the year 1905, the population of the whole residency residing on Dutch territory was 523,535.

## Economy and Trade
Agriculture serves as the primary means of subsistence for the population, with rice being a crop of particular importance. For the purpose of export, the island produces cocoa, tobacco, coffee, and indigo. Other significant economic activities include the making of musical instruments and arms, gold-working, wood-carving, and the weaving of silk, cotton, and gold thread.

The island's rivers are not navigable. The only roadstead that remains safe throughout the entire year is Temukus, located on the north coast. Trade is supported by numerous Chinese and Arab traders.
