# Rhodope and Northern Bulgarian Districts

The Rhodope mountains and specific districts in northern Bulgaria are characterized by a distinct intersection of geography, biodiversity, and ethnic composition, most notably regarding the presence of the Pomaks.

## The Pomaks
The Pomaks are Bulgarian Mahommedans who inhabit the valleys and highlands of Rhodope, as well as certain districts in northern Bulgaria, specifically in the neighborhood of Plevna (Pleven) and Lovtcha (Lovetch). These populations are described as some of the most genuine representatives of the Bulgarian race. Their conversion to Islam is noted as having preserved their women from the license of the Turkish conquerors. 

Despite their religious affiliation, the Pomaks have retained many ancient national usages and their Bulgarian speech. They are compared to indigenous Moslems found in Bosnia, Albania, and Crete. In the Bulgarian principality, the Pomak population is estimated at 26,000, though this number is reported to be declining. Within the broader context of the Balkan Peninsula, the Bulgarian Mahommedans are considered numerically insignificant.

## Geography and Geology
The Rhodope system is a great mountain chain that traverses the center of the Balkan Peninsula, extending spurs toward the Aegean and Black Seas. Its principal summits include Popova Shapka (8,855 ft.) and Musalla (9,631 ft.). The region's geology is characterized by an Archean formation composed of crystalline schists and gneiss, which is also found in the Rilska Planina and the Eastern Rumelian plain.

The area is known for its thermal springs, which are mostly sulphureous. These springs are found in Rhodope, as well as in the districts of Kiustendil and Sofia, and have been frequented since Roman times for their therapeutic qualities.

## Flora and Fauna
The flora of the Rhodope and Rila group is rich in indigenous types, combined with vegetation typical of the mountains of Asia Minor and the central European Alps. The southern mountain group, including Rhodope, presents several isolated types of flora that are unknown elsewhere in Europe and sometimes correspond to those found in the Caucasus. The forests of Rhodope are home to the cock of the woods (*Tetrao urogallus*).

The fauna of the higher mountain districts in Rhodope and the Rilska Planina includes:
*   **Mammals:** Abundant small brown bears (similar to those of the Carpathians), chamois in the higher regions, and numerous wolves.
*   **Other Wildlife:** The region is also noted for its various birds of prey, including eagles and vultures.

## Political and Territorial Context
The Rhodope region has been a point of territorial definition between Bulgaria and Turkey. The Berlin Treaty of 1878 originally set a boundary following the crest of Rhodope. However, the cantons of Rupchus and Krjali were restored to Turkey in 1886. The subsequent frontier passed north of these districts to reach the watershed of Rhodope, slightly north of the Dospat valley.
