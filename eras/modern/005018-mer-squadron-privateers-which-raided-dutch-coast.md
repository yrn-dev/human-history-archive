# Gueux sur Mer

The *Gueux sur Mer* was a squadron of privateers that operated under the commission of the prince of Orange. The squadron is primarily noted in history for its raids along the Dutch coast during the 16th century.

## Capture of Brielle
The most significant event associated with the *Gueux sur Mer* was the capture of Brielle on April 1, 1572. Brielle is a fortified seaport located in the province of South Holland on the north side of the island of Voorne, situated at the mouth of the New Maas. 

The seizure of this port by the privateer squadron is regarded as the first blow in the long war for Dutch independence. This action was subsequently followed by a general outbreak of the patriotic party.

## Historical Context of Brielle
Following the events involving the *Gueux sur Mer*, Brielle—referred to as "The Brill"—became one of four Dutch towns handed over to Queen Elizabeth in 1584. This transfer served as security for expenses the English had incurred while aiding the Dutch. 

The town is also recognized as the birthplace of two notable naval commanders: the famous admiral Martin van Tromp and Admiral van Almonde, who served as a distinguished commander during the early 18th century.

## Geography and Infrastructure
Brielle is characterized as a fortified place featuring a barracks, magazine, arsenal, and a good harbour. Its architectural landmarks include an orphanage dating from 1533 and a quaint town hall. The tower of the Groote Kerk of St Catherine functions as a lighthouse. 

While the town historically relied on its port, much of its trade was diverted to Hellevoetsluis following the cutting of the Voornsche Canal in 1829. Despite this, the town maintained business in fodder and corn, operated a few factories, and supported a population of 4,107 as of 1900, many of whom worked as pilots or in the fisheries.
