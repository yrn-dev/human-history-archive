# Avicenna in Hamadan

## Arrival and Early Service
Following a period of residence at Rai, where he composed approximately thirty of his shorter works, the scholar Avicenna was forced to leave due to ongoing feuds between the regent and her second son, Shams Addaula. After a short stay in Kazwin, Avicenna traveled south to Hamadan, the location where the prince Shams Addaula had established himself. Upon his arrival, Avicenna initially entered the service of a high-born lady. However, the amir soon learned of his arrival and summoned him to serve as a medical attendant, subsequently sending him back to his home with presents.

## Political Ascent and Conflict
Avicenna's standing in Hamadan grew until he was appointed to the office of vizier. This promotion caused friction with the turbulent soldiery, which consisted of Turks and Kurds. The soldiers mutinied against their nominal sovereign, demanding the death of the new vizier. In response, Shams Addaula agreed to have Avicenna banished from the country. Despite this order, Avicenna remained in hiding for forty days within the house of a sheik. He was eventually restored to his post as vizier after a new attack of illness affected the amir.

## Intellectual and Social Life
Even during this period of political instability, Avicenna continued his teaching and studies. Every evening, he dictated and explained extracts from his major works, the *Sanatio* and the *Canon*, to his pupils. Following these lessons, he spent the remainder of the night in festive enjoyment, accompanied by a band of players and singers.

## Concealment and Incarceration
Following the death of the amir, Avicenna's tenure as vizier ended. He went into hiding in the house of an apothecary, where he continued to compose his works with intense assiduity. During this time, he wrote to Abu Ya'far, the prefect of Isfahan, to offer his services. The new amir of Hamadan eventually discovered the location of Avicenna's concealment and learned of this correspondence. As a result, the amir incarcerated Avicenna in a fortress.

## Return to Hamadan
A state of war persisted between the rulers of Hamadan and Isfahan. In 1024, the ruler of Isfahan captured the towns and the city of Hamadan, expelling the Turkish mercenaries. Once the conflict had passed, Avicenna returned to Hamadan with the amir and resumed his literary labors. He eventually departed the city accompanied by two slaves, a favorite pupil, and his brother.
