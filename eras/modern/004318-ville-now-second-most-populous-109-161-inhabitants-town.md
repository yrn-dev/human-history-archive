# Basel (Bale Ville)

Basel, also referred to as Bale Ville (and incorrectly as Basle), is the capital of the Swiss half canton of Basel Stadt or Bale Ville. It is the second most populous town in the Swiss Confederation, with a population of 109,161 inhabitants, ranking only after Zuerich. Despite its population rank, it is reputed to be the richest town in Switzerland, with a number of resident millionaires (in francs) that exceeds those of any other Swiss town.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The wealth and population of Basel are largely attributed to the opening of the St Gotthard railway in 1882. This line allows merchandise collected from across central and north Europe to be stored in Basel before being redistributed. Consequently, the city maintains a flourishing and extremely large transit trade, although the older portions of the city have a rather dingy appearance.

The city is divided by the Rhine river into two sections: Klein Basel to the north and Gross Basel to the south, with the latter being significantly larger. The two areas are connected by several bridges; notably, a stone bridge was built in 1905 to replace an older wooden one. The city's transportation hubs are split between the two sections, with the Baden station located in Klein Basel and the main or central railway station situated in Gross Basel.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The most prominent building in Basel is the Muenster, or cathedral, which is constructed from deep red sandstone on a high terrace overlooking the Rhine. Although it was consecrated in 1019, the cathedral was mainly rebuilt following a disastrous earthquake in 1356 that nearly ruined the city. The cathedral's choir served as the site for public meetings of the great oecumenical council from 1431 to 1449, while the council's committees met in the chapter-house.

Other notable structures include the 16th-century Rathaus (town hall), which has undergone recent restoration. The city also houses a university, founded by Pius II in 1460, which is the oldest university in Switzerland.

## Culture and History
Basel has been home to several celebrated figures. Erasmus lived in the city from 1521 to 1529 and was buried in the cathedral upon his death in 1536. The cathedral cloisters also serve as the burial site for other noted men, including Grynaeus (d. 1541), Oecolampadius (d. 1531), and Buxtorf (d. 1732).

The city's museums preserve significant artistic and historical works. The museum contains a fine collection of art by Holbein, who resided in Basel between 1528 and 1531. Additionally, the historical museum, located in the old Franciscan church, houses various treasures, including fragments of the *Dance of Death*, which have been wrongly attributed to Holbein.

## Administration and Demographics
The half canton of Basel Stadt (Bale Ville) includes the city of Basel and three rural districts located north of the Rhine: Bettingen, Riehen, and Klein Hueningen, the latter of which has been united with the city. The total area of the half canton is 13.7 sq. m., with 11 sq. m. classified as "productive" and 1.5 sq. m. consisting of forests.

As of 1900, the total population of the half canton was 112,227, with 3,066 people living in the rural districts. The population is mainly German-speaking and consists of 73,063 Protestants, 37,101 Romanists (including Old Catholics), and 1,897 Jews.

The cantonal constitution was established in 1889. The government consists of an executive of seven members and a legislature known as the *Grossrat*, comprising 130 members. These officials, along with six members sent to the Federal *Nationalrat* and one member sent to the Federal *Staenderat*, are elected every three years via direct popular vote. Since 1875, the system has allowed for a *facultative Referendum* (claimable by 1,000 citizens) on all bills, as well as the right of *initiative* regarding laws or the revision of the cantonal constitution.
