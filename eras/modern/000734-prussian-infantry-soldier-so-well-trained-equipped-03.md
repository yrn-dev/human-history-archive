# The Prussian Infantry and Military Organization

## The Army of Frederick the Great
In 1740, Prussia was a small, compact, and thoroughly organized power possessing an army of 100,000 men. Although it was regarded as one of Europe's minor armies—having only seen desultory service in the Rhine campaign of 1733-35—it had developed the standing army theory to full effect during a period undisturbed by wars. This preparation allowed Prussian regiments to take the field immediately, whereas the Austrians were forced to wait for drafts to complete their field forces.

The Prussian infantry of this era was drilled to a level of perfection not previously attained. This superior training and equipment enabled a Prussian infantry soldier to fire five shots for every three fired by an Austrian soldier. While the artillery and cavalry were considered less efficient than the infantry, the overall organization allowed Frederick to overrun Silesia almost unopposed starting on December 16, 1740.

## Military Precision and Discipline
The effectiveness of the Prussian infantry was demonstrated through both mathematical precision in siege work and tenacity in open battle. In March 1741, during the storming of Glogau, Prussian forces under Prince Leopold of Anhalt-Dessau executed their task in one hour with a precision that excited universal admiration.

During the battle of Mollwitz on April 10, 1741—the first pitched battle fought by Frederick and his army—the Prussian right wing of cavalry was quickly routed. However, the day was retrieved by the tenacity and magnificent discipline of the infantry. The Prussian volleys caused the Austrian infantry to gradually melt away, and the Austrian cavalry was shattered in repeated attempts to ride down the Prussian lines. This victory signaled to Europe the emergence of a new military power and led France to negotiate an alliance with Frederick.

## Later Prussian Military History
Prussian military personnel continued to play significant roles in European conflicts through the 19th century. Karl Wilhelm Georg von Grolmann, born in Berlin in 1777, entered an infantry regiment at nearly thirteen years of age. Following the downfall of Prussia after the war of 1806, Grolmann served as an assistant to Scharnhorst in the reorganization of 1809. He later served in the defense of Cadiz and participated in the victory of Kulm and the battle of Leipzig. By 1815, as a major-general and quartermaster-general to Field Marshal Prince Blucher, Grolmann had a significant share in directing Prussian operations and concurred in the decision to press forward to assist Wellington on June 18, 1815.

Prussian forces remained active in various regions over the following century. In Montdidier, Prussian garrisons held the town after Easter in one instance, occupied it for three months in 1815, and appeared again before the town on October 15, 1870.

## Equipment and Field Logistics
In later conflicts, German infantry equipment evolved to provide better protection and mobility than that of their opponents. While French soldiers utilized small, low huts built of branches and boughs for protection against cold and rain, German infantry were equipped with shelter-tents. Each soldier carried a portion of a brown tarpaulin as part of his equipment; when joined together, these tarpaulins formed temporary shelters for groups of soldiers.
