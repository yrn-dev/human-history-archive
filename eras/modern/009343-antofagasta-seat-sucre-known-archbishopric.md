# Antofagasta

Antofagasta is a region associated with the history of Bolivia and Chile, serving as a port, a railway hub, and a territory of ecclesiastical significance.

## Ecclesiastical Administration
In the religious organization of Bolivia, the country is divided into three bishoprics and one archbishopric. The archbishopric, known as the archbishopric of La Plata, has its seat at Sucre. This ecclesiastical jurisdiction encompasses several departments, including Chuquisaca, Oruro, Potosi, and Tarija, as well as the Chilean province of Antofagasta.

## Conflict and Territorial Change
The status of Antofagasta was a central point of contention between Bolivia and Chile in the late 19th century. Following a period of treaty negotiations, General Hilarion Daza, who became president in 1876, demanded a tax of 10 cents per quintal on all nitrates exported from the country. He declared that nitrates held by exporters would be seized by the Bolivian government if this levy was not paid.

In response to these demands and to protect the property of Chilean subjects, the Chilean fleet blockaded the ports of Tocapilla, Cobija, and Antofagasta. On February 14, 1879, Chilean colonel Sotomayor occupied Antofagasta. Shortly thereafter, on March 1, the Bolivian government declared war. As a result of the war between 1878 and 1881, the railways of the Litoral department, totaling 282 miles in length, passed under Chilean control.

## Infrastructure and Communications
Antofagasta has played a role in the development of Bolivian transportation. Up until 1903, one of the only railways in Bolivia was the Antofagasta and Oruro line. This line had a total length of 574 miles, with 350 miles located within Bolivian territory. A private branch of this line, extending 26 miles, runs to the Pulacayo mines.

The town of Uyuni serves as a critical junction where the Antofagasta and Oruro railway meets the Pulacayo branch. Uyuni also functions as a converging point for several projected railways and important highways.

## Geography and Neighboring Provinces
In the context of Chilean geography, the province of Antofagasta is located to the north of the province of Atacama. The province of Atacama extends from the Pacific coast to the Argentine boundary line and is bounded to the north by Antofagasta and to the south by Coquimbo.
