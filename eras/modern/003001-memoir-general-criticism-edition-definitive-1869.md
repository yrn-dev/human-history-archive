# Memoir and General Criticism Before the Edition Definitive (1869)

The production of memoirs, critical notices, and definitive editions of literary and historical works often involves a complex transition from early, fragmented publications to comprehensive, scholarly collections. This process is exemplified by the bibliographic history of authors such as Balzac and the critical reception of figures like Lord Byron and Henry Mackenzie.

## The Works of Balzac and the Edition Definitive
Prior to the emergence of the *Edition definitive* starting in 1869, the works of Balzac were issued in various installments and forms during the author's lifetime. The earliest version of the *Comedie humaine* appeared between 1842 and 1846, spanning sixteen volumes. For a significant period, the most recognized edition consisted of fifty-five small, closely printed 24mos published by Michel (Calmann) Levy. This version, which Browning referred to as "all Balzac's novels fifty volumes long," included the *Oeuvres de jeunesse* but omitted miscellaneous works and did not follow the author's final disposition.

The *Edition definitive* (1869 onwards) shifted this arrangement. While it did not reprint the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*, it provided the miscellaneous works in four volumes, an invaluable volume of correspondence, and the *Histoire des oeuvres*. The *Histoire des oeuvres* (1875 and later), attached by M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul, offers a nearly complete account of Balzac's complicated bibliography. Further supplements by Lovenjoul include *Autour de Balzac* and *Une Page perdue de Balzac*. Additionally, the English translation edited by Saintsbury (1895-1898) contains a short Memoir and general criticism, with summaries of the bibliography appended to the introductory critical notices of each volume.

Later additions to the Balzac archive include the 1893 *Repertoire des oeuvres de Balzac* by MM. Cerfbeer and Christophe, which tracks the history of the *Comedie* personages. In 1899 and 1906, installments of *Lettres a l'etrangere* were published as part of the *Oeuvres posthumes*, the latter including illustrations and a portrait of Madame Hanska.

## Memoirs and Critical Notices of Lord Byron
The critical and biographical record of Lord Byron is extensive, consisting of numerous memoirs and journals published throughout the 19th century. Early accounts include J.C. Hobhouse's *Journey through Albania... with Lord Byron* (1812), and various 1824 publications such as the *Recollections of the Life of Lord Byron* by R.C. Dallas, the *Journal of the Conversations of Lord Byron* by Capt. T. Medwin, and *Last Days of Lord Byron* by W. Parry. Other early works include Dr. John Watkins's *Memoirs of the Life and Writings of... Lord Byron* (1822) and Sir E. Brydges's *Letters on the Character and Poetical Genius of Lord Byron* (1824).

Further critical perspectives were provided by W. Hazlitt in *The Spirit of the Age* (1825), George Clinton in his 1826 *Memoir of the Life and Writings of Lord Byron*, and Thomas Moore's *Letters and Journals of Lord Byron, with Notices of his Life* (1830). Later 19th-century contributions include T.B. Macaulay's *Critical and Historical Essays* (1843), the 1869 work *Lord Byron jugé par les témoins de sa vie*, and the Countess Guiccioli's *My Recollections of Lord Byron* (1869). Other notable biographies and studies include H. Beecher Stowe's *Lady Byron Vindicated* (1870), Karl Elze's *Lord Byron, a Biography* (1872), and J.C. Jeaffreson's *The Real Lord Byron* (1883).

## Other Memoirs and Biographical Works
The archive contains various other memoirs and critical works across different disciplines:

*   **Political and Historical Figures:** Joseph Bonaparte is the subject of *Memoires et correspondance politique et militaire du roi Joseph* (10 vols., 1854) by Baron A. du Casse, as well as the *Memoirs of Count Miot de Melito* (1881). Sir Clements Markham authored several memoirs, including a *Memoir of the Countess of Chinchon* (1875), a *Memoir of Sir John Harington* (1880), and a *Memoir on the Indian Surveys* (1871, 1878). Thomas D'Arcy McGee's legacy is documented in *Memoirs of the Life and Conquests of Art McMurrogh, King of Leinster* (1847) and a *Memoir of C. G. Duffy* (1849).
*   **Philosophical and Academic Works:** A memoir was prefixed to G. Croom Robertson's *Philosophical Remains* (1894). In the field of philology, a memoir of Klotz was written by his son Richard and published in the *Jahrbücher* for 1871.
*   **Literary Criticism:** Sir Walter Scott provided an "admiring but discriminating criticism" of Henry Mackenzie's work in the *Prefatory Memoir* prefixed to an edition of Mackenzie's novels in Ballantyne's *Novelist's Library* (1823).
