# Poetry

## Definitions and Forms
Epic poetry, also referred to as epos, represents the most dignified and elaborate forms of narrative poetry. The term is derived from the Greek *epos*, meaning a story, and *epikos*, meaning pertaining to a story. The term *epopee* is occasionally used to designate this form; in Greek, *epopoiios* refers to a maker of epic poetry, while *epopoiia* refers to the work produced.

## Notable Poets and Works

### Robert Williams Buchanan (1841-1901)
A British poet, novelist, and dramatist, Robert Williams Buchanan was born in Caverswall, Staffordshire. Educated at the university and high school in Glasgow, he maintained a close friendship with the poet David Gray, a relationship detailed in Buchanan's essay on Gray. 

Buchanan's early poetic output began with the "tentative" volume *Undertones* (1863), followed by *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). These works demonstrated his sympathetic insight into the humbler conditions of life and a faculty for poetic narrative. In 1870, he attempted a more ambitious study in mysticism titled *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic*. 

Buchanan also gained notoriety for his critical writing. Using the pseudonym Thomas Maitland, he wrote an article for the *Contemporary Review* in October 1871 titled "The Fleshly School of Poetry," which was later expanded into a pamphlet in 1872. This attack evoked replies from Mr. Swinburne in *Under the Microscope* (1872) and from D.G. Rossetti in a letter to the *Athenaeum* titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism." Buchanan later regretted the violence of this attack and dedicated *God and the Man* to Rossetti, whom he called his "old enemy."

### Paul Laurence Dunbar (1872-1906)
An American author of negro descent born in Dayton, Ohio, Paul Laurence Dunbar is regarded as pre-eminently the poet of his race in America. His poetry first gained the attention of American readers through an appreciative introduction by William Dean Howells for *Lyrics of Lowly Life* (1896). Dunbar subsequently published eleven additional volumes of verse.

While some of his poetry was written in literary English, his most enduring and best work utilized the dialect of his people. This poetry is noted for its lyrical grace, sweetness, rare humour, and pathos, preserving the outlook and temperament of his people with freshness of feeling and truth. These "poems of the soil" are considered a distinct contribution to American literature.

## Academic and Philosophical Studies of Poetry
In Scandinavia, Frederik Christian Sibbern (1785-1872), a learned philosophical writer, authored a three-volume dissertation titled *On Poetry and Art*, published between 1853 and 1869. Additionally, J. Girard published *Etudes sur la poesie grecque* in 1884.
