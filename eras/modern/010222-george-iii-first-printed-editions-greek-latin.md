# George III and the First Printed Editions of the Greek and Latin Classics

## Collecting Trends in the Era of George III
During the period associated with George III, a specific trend emerged among book collectors regarding the acquisition of the first printed editions of the Greek and Latin classics. These works were highly prized by collectors of that era, who paid high prices to acquire them.

## Value and Utility of Early Editions
The high valuation placed on these first printed editions of Greek and Latin classics has been characterized as fanciful. From a scholarly perspective, these editions are generally considered to be of no value as texts, with few exceptions. Furthermore, there are no possible associations that can link these specific printed volumes to the personalities of the authors of the classics.

## Evolution of Collector Interest
The desire to possess first editions is often driven by a combination of factors: the positive superiority of first editions over reprints for the purposes of study, and the associations—either proven or imagined—that the books possess. 

While the first editions of Greek and Latin classics were once highly sought after during the days of George III, it is doubted whether anyone continues to collect them today, except as specimens of printing. This contrasts with the prestige of first editions of French and English literary masterpieces, which has increased immensely. For example, a first folio of Shakespeare from 1623 was sold in 1906.

## General Principles of Book Collecting
The interest in specific books often stems from their association with famous events or persons, such as great poets, soldiers, statesmen, or royalty. Books that belonged to figures who appealed to popular sympathies, such as Mary queen of Scots, or those bearing marks of being valued by a famous owner, hold an interest that defies analysis. Additionally, collectors often value the "pedigree" of a work if it can be traced through a long series of book-loving owners. This interest in the history of book ownership has led to the production of works such as *English Book-Collectors* by W.Y. Fletcher (1902), *Great Book-Collectors* by Charles and Mary Elton (1893), and Guigard's *Nouvel armorial du bibliophile* (1890).
