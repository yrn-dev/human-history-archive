# La Plata Estuary

The La Plata estuary is a significant body of water located on the east coast of South America. It serves as a primary maritime gateway for Argentina and is the site of the capital city, Buenos Aires.

## Geography and Physical Characteristics
The estuary forms the eastern boundary of the maritime province of Buenos Aires, which possesses a frontage of over 900 miles along the Atlantic and the La Plata. At the point where the city of Buenos Aires is situated, the estuary is 34 miles wide. The waters are characterized by a brown color, which harmonizes with the appearance of the local fauna.

The estuary is notably shallow. In the vicinity of Buenos Aires, the water is so shallow that vessels can only enter the docks via artificial channels that require constant dredging to remain open. Prior to the development of a new port, ocean-going vessels with a draught exceeding 15 feet were required to anchor in the outer roads, approximately 12 miles from the city.

## Buenos Aires and Port Development
The city of Buenos Aires is located on the west shore of the estuary, approximately 155 miles above its mouth and 127 miles west-by-north from Montevideo. The city is built on an open grassy plain with an average elevation of 65 feet above sea level. It extends north from the Matanzas (or Riachuelo) river, where the "Boca" port is located.

To overcome the natural limitations of the shallow water frontage, a costly artificial port was constructed. This project, along with the reclamation of significant areas of shallow water, has extended the city's area below the original banks of the estuary.

## Regional Environment and Wildlife
The La Plata estuary is part of a larger geographical region characterized by a great treeless plain that extends westward to the Andes. The area between Buenos Aires and San Nicolas features a delta region and low river banks covered by a fringe of small, tangled wood.

The estuary is home to the La Plata dolphin (*Stenodelphis blainvillei*). This species is approximately 5 feet in length and possesses a palish brown color that matches the brown-colored water of the estuary.

## Historical Context
The area surrounding the estuary was the site of three Spanish attempts to establish a colony. The first occurred in 1535 under Don Pedro de Mendoza, but failed due to mismanagement and hostility from the Indians. A second attempt in 1542 by an expedition under Cabeza de Vaca was similarly unsuccessful. 

The third and successful attempt was led in 1580 by Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay. Garay sought to establish a port near the sea to allow Spanish colonists in Asunción to receive supplies and provision ships. He built a fort and laid out a town in the Spanish style, naming it "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad," while retaining the name Buenos Aires for the port due to the invigorating and agreeable atmosphere of the location. 

By 1620, the Spanish settlements of the La Plata region were divided into three provinces: Tucuman, Paraguay, and Buenos Aires. The city of Buenos Aires became the capital of its namesake province and the seat of a new bishopric. While Spain initially viewed the site as a small trading station—often considering one small shipment of supplies per year sufficient—the town's importance grew due to increasing trade and population. Additionally, the unprotected state of the coast and proximity to Portuguese settlements in Brazil facilitated smuggling.
