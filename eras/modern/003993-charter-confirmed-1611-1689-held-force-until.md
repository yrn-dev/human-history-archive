# Barnstaple

Barnstaple, also known as Berdestaple, Barnstapol, Barstaple, or Barum, is one of the most ancient royal boroughs. Its history is documented as far back as the Domesday survey, which mentions the town several times and records that there were forty burgesses within the town and nine without, who rendered 40s.

## Early History and Governance
Tradition attributes the construction of defensive earthworks in the town to King Athelstan. However, the existing castle is attributed to Joel of Totnes, who held the manor during the reign of William the Conqueror. Joel of Totnes also founded a Cluniac priory dedicated to St Mary Magdalene. The borough and the priory grew side by side, each maintaining independent rights of government and privileges until the priory was dissolved in 1535.

During the reign of Edward II, the burgesses petitioned for the restoration of rights that had been bestowed by a charter from Athelstan. Although the existence of this specific charter was denied, the requested privileges were granted, including the right to elect a mayor.

## Charters and Legal Evolution
The earliest authenticated charter for Barnstaple is that of Henry I, which was subsequently confirmed by a charter of Henry II. This later charter stipulated that the burgesses were to have customs similar to those granted to London, a right that was confirmed by further charters.

In 1556, Queen Mary issued a charter that added new privileges and specified the composition of the common council. This council was to consist of a mayor, two aldermen, and twenty-four chief burgesses. 

In 1610, James I issued a charter that increased the number of chief burgesses to twenty-five. This charter also instituted several official roles, including a recorder, a clerk of the market, and justices of the peace, among other officers. This specific charter was confirmed in 1611 and 1689, and it remained in force until the Municipal Corporations Act of 1835. Following this act, the government was restructured to establish eighteen councillors and six aldermen.

## Trade and Parliamentary Representation
Barnstaple was once renowned for its woollen trade, though this industry has entirely declined. As early as the reign of Edward III, the town served as an important naval port with an extensive shipping trade. However, this prosperity was not uninterrupted; during the time of the Armada, the mayor pleaded inabil.

In terms of political representation, the borough sent two members to parliament starting in 1295. This continued until the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885, at which point the representation was merged with that of the county.
