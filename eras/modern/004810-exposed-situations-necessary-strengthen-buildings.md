# Strengthening Buildings in Exposed Situations

In the field of construction—the branch of architectural science concerned with the practical execution of works to produce a structure—specific measures must be taken when a building is located in an exposed situation. To ensure the stability and integrity of the structure against environmental pressures, it is necessary to implement specific structural reinforcements.

## Structural Reinforcements
When buildings are situated in exposed positions, they must be strengthened through the following methods:
*   **Wall and Parapet Thickness:** The thickness of the walls and parapets must be increased.
*   **Copings and Flashings:** Heavier flashings and copings must be provided.
*   **Fixing Precautions:** Special precautions are required when fixing ridges, chimney pots, hips, and copings.

## Wind Pressure Considerations
The necessity for these reinforcements is driven by wind pressure. In England, the greatest wind pressure experienced is 56 lb on a square foot, though this level of pressure is only found on a sea front or in the most exposed positions in the country. 

For most other cases, an allowance of forty pounds is considered sufficient. In situations where the building is protected by surrounding buildings or trees, the requirement is reduced to 28 lb per square foot.

## General Principles of Successful Building
Beyond reinforcements for exposed sites, the planning of a successful building relies on several summary conditions:
1.  Ease of access.
2.  Good light.
3.  Good service.
4.  Approaches and environment that are pleasing.
5.  True economy with minimum cost.
6.  For office buildings specifically, the ability to rearrange the space easily to suit tenants.

To achieve these goals, an architect must be practically acquainted with all arts or modes of operation employed in building. They must be capable of ascertaining quantities and minutely estimating the absolute cost of executing a proposed structure beforehand. While a quantity surveyor usually measures work at an advanced stage, the architect typically cubes a building at a price per foot cube; mastery of the profession requires the architect to know how to prepare quantities and measure work.
