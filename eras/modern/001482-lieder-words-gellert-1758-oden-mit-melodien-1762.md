# Lieder, to words by Gellert (1758) and Oden mit Melodien (1762)

The *Geistliche Oden und Lieder* (1758), set to words by Gellert, and the *Oden mit Melodien* (1762) are song collections composed by the German musician and composer Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach.

## Composer Background
Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714–1788) was the third son of Johann Sebastian Bach. Born in Weimar on March 14, 1714, he entered the Thomasschule at Leipzig at the age of ten, where his father served as cantor starting in 1723. Bach pursued studies in jurisprudence at the universities of Leipzig (beginning in 1731) and Frankfort on the Oder (beginning in 1735), receiving his degree in 1738. Following his graduation, he abandoned a legal career to devote himself to music.

## Career in Prussia
Shortly after deciding to pursue music, Bach obtained an appointment in the service of the crown prince of Prussia. Upon the prince's accession in 1740, Bach became a member of the royal household. He established a reputation as one of Europe's premier clavier-players, producing approximately thirty sonatas and concerted pieces for the instrument by 1731. His standing was further solidified by two sets of sonatas dedicated to the grand duke of Wuerttemberg (1744) and Frederick the Great (1742).

In 1746, Bach was promoted to the position of *Kammermusikus*. For the next twenty-two years, he enjoyed the continued favor of the king, sharing this patronage with Johann Gottlieb Naumann, Johann Joachim Quantz, and Karl Heinrich Graun.

## Vocal Compositions
During his residence in Berlin, Bach composed a variety of vocal works. These included an Easter cantata (1756), several secular cantatas, and other *pieces d'occasion*. He also wrote a setting of the *Magnificat* in 1749, a work that displayed more influence from his father than was typical for his compositions.

Among his vocal output, Bach produced at least three volumes of songs. These include:
*   *Geistliche Oden und Lieder* (1758), which utilized words by Gellert.
*   *Oden mit Melodien* (1762).
*   *Sing-Oden* (1766).

## Other Works and Contributions
While Bach composed these song collections, his primary focus remained the clavier. During his time in Berlin, he wrote nearly two hundred solos and sonatas for the instrument, including the set *fuer Kenner und Liebhaber* and the *mit veraenderten Reprisen* (1760–1768).

Bach also contributed to musical theory as a European critic. He authored the *Versuch ueber die wahre Art das Clavier zu spielen*, a masterly and systematic treatise. The first part was published in 1753, and a second part was released in 1762 (which included a reprint of the first). By 1780, this work had reached its third edition.
