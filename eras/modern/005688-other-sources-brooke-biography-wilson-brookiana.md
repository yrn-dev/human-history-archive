# Brooke

The archive contains records pertaining to multiple individuals named Brooke, most notably a writer associated with the Elizabethan era and a later author of satirical and poetic works.

## The Elizabethan Writer Brooke
Brooke was a writer who addressed theological, philosophical, and current political topics. He is described by Milton as a friend of toleration. During his lifetime, he published only four poems and the *Tragedy of Mustapha*. His other works were published posthumously, including a volume of works in 1633, a volume of *Remains* in 1670, and a biography of Sidney in 1652. One of his poems, an elegy on Sidney, appeared in *The Phoenix Nest* in 1593.

### Dramatic and Poetic Works
Brooke wrote two tragedies based on the Senecan model: *Alaham* and *Mustapha*. *Alaham* is set in Ormuz and features a prologue where the ghost of a former king reveals a curse descending upon a doomed family. The theme of *Mustapha* is derived from Madeleine de Scudéry's *Ibrahim ou l'illustre Bassa* and focuses on the ambition of the sultana Rossa. 

Brooke stated that these tragedies were not intended for the stage. Charles Lamb characterized them as political treatises, suggesting Brooke was "nine parts Machiavel and Tacitus, for one of Sophocles and Seneca." Lamb further noted that Brooke's work, including his love poems, was "frozen and made rigid with intellect" and marked by an obscurity of expression resulting from the subtlety and intensity of his thought.

In these plays, the choruses function as philosophical dissertations with limited connection to the drama. For example, in *Mustapha*, the third chorus is a dialogue between Eternity and Time, and the fifth is an invective against superstition, followed by a chorus of priests that maintains an impression of scepticism.

### Personal Reputation and Legacy
A rhyming elegy published in Huth's *Inedited Poetical Miscellanies* accused Brooke of extreme penuriousness; however, there is abundant testimony regarding his generous treatment of writers of his time. 

Brooke left no sons. Consequently, his barony passed to his cousin, Robert Greville (c. 1608-1643), who became the 2nd Lord Brooke. Robert Greville was an active member of the parliamentary party during the Civil War and defeated the Royalists at Kineton in August 1642. After seizing Lichfield, he was killed there on March 2, 1643. A later descendant, Francis Greville, the 8th baron (1710-1773), was created earl of Warwick in 1746.

## Henry Brooke
Henry Brooke was an author whose *Poetical Works* were published in 1792 by his daughter, Charlotte Brooke, though she provided very little biographical information. Biographical sources for Henry Brooke include a preface by E. A. Baker in a 1906 edition of *The Fool of Quality* and a two-volume work titled *Brookiana* (1804) by C. H. Wilson.

### Literary Contributions
Henry Brooke's bibliography includes several tragedies, though only some were staged. His other works consist of:
* *Jack the Giant Queller* (1748), an operatic satire that was forbidden from repetition due to its political allusions.
* "Constantia, or the Man of Lawe's Tale" (1741), which was contributed to George Ogle's *Canterbury Tales modernized*.
* *Juliet Grenville; or the History of the Human Heart* (1773), a novel.
* Fables contributed to Edward Moore's *Fables for the Female Sex* (1744).
