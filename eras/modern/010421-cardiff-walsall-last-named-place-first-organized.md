# Cardiff and Walsall

## Cardiff: Geography and Infrastructure
Cardiff is a city in Wales that serves as a central transit point for several surrounding locations. It is situated 153 miles by rail from London and is located 7 miles from the market town of Caerphilly and 8 miles from the seaport of Barry. 

The city is connected to the region via an extensive railway network. A direct line from Caerphilly to Cardiff was established in 1871, utilizing a tunnel under Cefn Onn approximately 2000 yards in length. Additionally, the Rhymney railway provides a route from Rhymney through Caerphilly to Taff's Well, where it connects to the Taff Vale railway leading into Cardiff. The Barry railway also provides access to the city via junctions at Cogan.

## Cardiff: Culture and Military Presence
Cardiff has hosted significant cultural events, including the Cardiff Festivals of 1892 and 1895, which were conducted by the English musical composer and conductor Sir Joseph Barnby. In a military capacity, the city has served as the site of a United States naval base.

## Walsall and the Black Country
Walsall is one of the famous manufacturing towns located within a vast industrial district in South Staffordshire. This region is commonly referred to as the "Black Country." The district is characterized by its industrial nature and includes other manufacturing centers such as Dudley, Wolverhampton, and Wednesbury. While the city of Birmingham forms the south-eastern extremity of this industrial district, it is noted that Birmingham itself does not lie within the "Black Country" properly so-called.

## Religious Activity in Cardiff and Walsall
Both regions have been associated with various religious movements and figures. In the district near Cardiff, specifically around the town of Caerphilly, there was a significant Methodist revival during the 18th century. In 1743, the first synod of the Calvinistic Methodists was held at Watford farm. Two years prior to this, George Whitefield was married at Eglwysilan church.

Walsall is specifically noted as the location where William Booth and his wife, Catherine Mumford, conducted preaching services. It was in Walsall that a "Hallelujah band" was first organized. This group consisted of others and converted criminals who provided public testimony regarding their conversion. 

William Booth, born in Nottingham in 1829, had a religious awakening at age fifteen under the influence of the Wesleyan Methodists. After periods as a local preacher and a minister for the Methodist New Connexion, Booth's work in places like Walsall preceded his return to London in 1864. There, he founded a series of organizations—the East London Revival Society, the East London Christian Mission, and the Christian Mission—which eventually became the Salvation Army in 1878.
