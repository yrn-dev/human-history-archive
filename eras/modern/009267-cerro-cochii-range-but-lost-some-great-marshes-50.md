# Cerro Cochii Range

The Cerro Cochii range is a geographical feature located within the south-east drainage basin of Bolivia. This basin is smaller and of less economic importance than that of the Madeira. It is bounded to the north by the Sierras de Chiquitos, to the south by the Argentine frontier, to the west by the Cordillera Oriental, and to the east by the Paraguay.

## Hydrology and Drainage
The Cerro Cochii range serves as a drainage source for the Otuquis, which is the most southern group of the Bolivian tributaries of the upper Paraguay. The Otuquis is formed by the confluence of the Tucabaca and the San Rafael, both of which drain the slopes of the Cerro Cochii range.

The Otuquis river does not maintain a continuous course to its destination; it is lost in great marshes located 50 miles from the Paraguay.

## Regional Context
The Cerro Cochii range is situated in a region characterized by various water systems and topographical features:

*   **The Pilcomayo:** The only large river in Bolivia within this drainage basin, the Pilcomayo rises on the east slopes of the Cordillera Oriental, opposite the south end of Lake Pampa Aullaguas. It flows through the sierra region toward the Bolivian Chaco. Due to the nearly level terrain, the river has a sluggish current and its channels are frequently obstructed. Its largest tributaries in the sierra region include the Mataca, the Cachimayo, and the Pilaya (or Camblaya), the latter of which is formed by the San Juan and the Cotagaita.
*   **The Bermejo:** An Argentine river that receives the Tarija (or Rio Grande) as a large tributary from the Bolivian uplands, draining a small district to the south-east of the Santa Victoria sierra.
*   **The Parapiti:** Another significant stream in the region that rises on the eastern slopes of the Sierra de Misiones. It flows north-east through a low plain for approximately 150 miles before it is lost in the great marshy districts of the Bolivian plain.
