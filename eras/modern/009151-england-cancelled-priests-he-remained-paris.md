# Lord Bolingbroke and the Pretender

## Political Fall and Flight to Paris
Following the accession of George I, Lord Bolingbroke was immediately dismissed from his office. During this period, he retired to Bucklebury, where it is said he wrote a response to the *Secret History of the White Staff*, a work that had accused him of Jacobitism. In March 1715, Bolingbroke attempted to defend the previous ministry within the new parliament, but these efforts were unsuccessful. 

On March 28, 1715, upon the announcement that Walpole intended to attack the authors of the treaty of Utrecht, Bolingbroke fled to Paris in disguise. Before his departure, he sent a letter from Dover to Lord Lansdowne, in which he protested his innocence and challenged his "most inveterate of his enemies" to provide any evidence of criminal correspondence.

## Service to the Pretender
Upon arriving in Paris, Bolingbroke was well received. By July, he had fully identified himself with the interests of the Pretender and subsequently became the Pretender's secretary. However, on September 10, he was attainted. 

Despite his position, Bolingbroke found that his own counsel was neglected in favor of the advice given by Irish priests and ignorant refugees. This led to the resolution to launch the expedition of 1715, a move that Bolingbroke had advised against. While he was responsible for drawing up James's declaration, the priests cancelled the assurances he had included regarding the security of the Church of England.

## Dismissal and Renunciation
Bolingbroke remained in Paris, where he attempted to establish relations with the regent. However, due to jealousies and petty intrigues, he was eventually dismissed from his office upon the return of James. 

Following this dismissal, Bolingbroke renounced all further efforts on behalf of the Pretender. His break with the cause was marked by a stark response to Mary of Modena; when she sent a message deprecating his ill-will, Bolingbroke replied by wishing that his arm might rot off if he ever again used a sword or pen in their service.
