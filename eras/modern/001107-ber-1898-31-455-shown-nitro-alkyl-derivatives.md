# Nitro-Alkyl Derivatives and Mixed Azo Compounds

## Overview of Mixed Azo Compounds
Mixed azo compounds are organic substances characterized by an azo group (.N:N.) that is united with an aromatic radical on one side and a radical of the aliphatic series on the other. These compounds can be synthesized through the union of a diazonium salt with the sodium or potassium salt of a nitroparaffin. An example of this process is the reaction between $C_6H_5N_2.NO_3$ and $CH_3.CH(NO_2)K$, which results in $KNO_3$ and benzene-azo-nitro-ethane ($C_6H_5N_2.CH(NO_2)CH_3$). For mixed azo compounds that do not contain a nitro group, preparation is achieved by oxidizing corresponding mixed hydrazo compounds using mercuric oxide.

## Properties of Nitro-Alkyl Derivatives
Research conducted by E. Bamberger (published in *Ber.*, 1898, 31, p. 455) indicates that nitro-alkyl derivatives behave as though they possess the constitution of hydrazones. This behavior is evidenced by their reaction when heated with dilute alkalies, during which they split more or less readily into an acid hydrazide and an alkaline nitrite. The chemical equation for this reaction is:
$C_6H_5NH.N:C(NO_2)CH_3 + NaOH = NaNO_2 + C_6H_5NH.NH.CO.CH_3$.

## Related Azo-Alkyl Substances
Several specific azo-alkyl compounds exhibit distinct physical properties:
*   **Benzene-azo-methane ($C_6H_5.N_2.CH_3$):** A yellow oil that is readily volatile in steam and has a boiling point of 150° C.
*   **Benzene-azo-ethane ($C_6H_5.N_2.C_2H_5$):** A yellow oil with a boiling point of approximately 180° C., though it undergoes more or less decomposition at this temperature. When this substance is left with 60% sulphuric acid for a period of time, it is converted into the isomeric acetaldehyde-phenylhydrazone ($C_6H_5NH.N:CH.CH_3$).

## General Context of Azo Compounds
Azo compounds generally follow the type $R.N:N.R'$, where $R$ is an aryl radical and $R'$ is either a substituted alkyl or aryl radical. Beyond the methods used for mixed azo compounds, these substances can be prepared via:
*   The reduction of nitro compounds in alkaline solution using an alkaline stannite solution or zinc dust and alkali.
*   The oxidation of hydrazo compounds.
*   The coupling of a diazotized amine with any compound of an aminic or phenolic type, provided a free para position is available in the phenol or amine.
*   The molecular rearrangement of diazoamines when warmed with its hydrochloride and parent base.

Azo compounds are intensely coloured. However, they cannot be used as dyestuffs unless they possess basic, acid, or salt-forming groups. They can be converted into amines via reducing agents, or into azoxy compounds via oxidizing agents.
