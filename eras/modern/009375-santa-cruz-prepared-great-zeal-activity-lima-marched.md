# Marshal Santa Cruz and the History of Bolivia

## The War of Independence in Upper Peru
The region known as Upper Peru, which later became Bolivia, was a primary theater of conflict during the War of Independence from Spain. Between July 1809 and August 1825, the area experienced nearly continuous warfare characterized by alternating successes for the Spanish royalist forces and the South American patriot forces. The fighting occurred mainly between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta.

The conflict began with a successful invasion by the army of Buenos Aires under General Balcarce, who defeated Spanish troops twice and celebrated the first anniversary of independence near Lake Titicaca in May 1811. However, due to the negligence and dissolute conduct of its leaders, the patriot army became disorganized and was defeated in June 1811 by the Spanish army led by Gey fol Goyeneche. Following four years of fluctuating fortunes, the patriots suffered a total rout in 1815 in a battle between Oruro and Potosi.

Subsequent events included a revolt by Creoles and Indians of the southern provinces of Peru seeking total independence, though this was quickly suppressed by royalists. In 1816, the Spanish commander-in-chief of Upper Peru, General Laserna, attempted to invade the Argentine provinces to march on Buenos Aires, but he was forced to retire due to the activity of irregular *gaucho* troops from Jujuy and Salta. For the following six years, patriots in Upper Peru maintained a guerrilla war from the mountains, particularly in the province of Yungas. In June 1823, General Santa Cruz launched an expedition prepared with great zeal and activity.

## The Formation of Bolivia
The liberation of the region was finalized through the efforts of Bolivar and his colleague Sucre. After routing the Spaniards at Pichincha in June 1822 and entering Lima, Bolivar eventually defeated Canterac at the plains of Junin on August 6, 1824. Sucre followed the retreating royalists into Upper Peru, achieving a decisive victory at Ayacucho. 

In June 1825, Bolivar visited Upper Peru, which had detached itself from the government of Buenos Aires to form a separate state named Bolivia in his honor. The first congress of the republic met in August 1825, declaring Bolivar the perpetual protector and requesting a constitution.

## The Presidency of Santa Cruz
The early years of the republic were marked by instability. Following the departure of Bolivar from Lima in September 1826 and the subsequent driving of General Sucre and Colombian troops from La Paz in late 1827, a new congress formed at Chuquisaca in April 1828. This congress modified Bolivar's constitution and elected Marshal Santa Cruz as president.

Although a revolution led by General Blanco briefly overturned the government a year later, order was restored by 1831. During his tenure, Santa Cruz promulgated a code of laws bearing his name, organized the country's financial affairs, and established a treaty of commerce with Peru, leading to several years of peace.

## Intervention in Peru and Exile
In 1835, Santa Cruz intervened in a power struggle between two factions in the republic of Peru. He marched into the country, defeated General Gamarra, and completed the pacification of Peru by the spring of 1836. Santa Cruz named himself the protector of Peru with the intention of creating a confederation between the two nations.

This move prompted active interference from the government of Chile, which supported Gamarra and sent troops into Peru. After three years of fighting, Santa Cruz was defeated and exiled following a battle at Jungay in June 1839. In the aftermath, Gamarra became the president of Peru, while General Velasco served as the provisional chief in Bolivia.

## Subsequent Political Conflict
Despite the exile of Santa Cruz, his supporters remained strong in Bolivia and successfully revolted against Velasco, eventually installing General Ballivian in power. Taking advantage of this instability, Gamarra attempted to annex the province of La Paz in August 1841. However, Gamarra's army was totally routed in a battle with Ballivian, and Gamarra was killed. Ballivian remained president until 1848, when he retired to Valparaiso, followed by a successful military revolution led by General Belzu.

## Geography and Administration
