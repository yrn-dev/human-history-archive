# District of Bulandshahr

## Geography and Climate
The District of Bulandshahr encompasses an area of 1899 sq. m. The landscape is characterized as a level plain that slopes gently from the northwest to the southeast. There is a gradual, though very slight, elevation located approximately midway between the Jumna and Ganges rivers. These two rivers serve as the principal waterways of the district; the Ganges remains navigable throughout the entire year, whereas the Jumna is navigable only during the rainy season. Additionally, the district is intersected by the Ganges canal, which is utilized for both navigation and irrigation purposes. The headworks for the Lower Ganges canal are situated at Narora.

The climate of the region is subject to extremes, experiencing excessive heat during the summer months and very cold temperatures in the winter.

## Population and Demographics
As of 1901, the population of the District of Bulandshahr was 1,138,101. This figure represents a population increase of 20% over the preceding decade. The district is described as being thickly populated.

## Agriculture and Industry
The land in the district is very highly cultivated. Industrial activity includes several mills used for cleaning and pressing cotton, as well as several indigo factories. However, the indigo factories have suffered significant losses due to a decline in indigo in recent years.

## Trade and Transport
Khurja serves as the chief centre of trade for the district. Transport infrastructure is supported by the crossing of two major railway lines: the Oudh and Rohilkhand railway and the main line of the East Indian railway.
