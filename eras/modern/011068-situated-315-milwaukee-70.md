# United States of America: Regional Industrial and Urban Centers

The United States of America contains a diverse array of commercial and industrial centers, particularly within the Great Lakes region. In the North, the population of European blood and descent has reached approximately six million, with numbers continuing to increase.

## Commercial Hubs of New York and Michigan
Buffalo is a primary commercial and industrial center in the Union, situated almost equidistant from New York, Boston, and Chicago. Its growth was initially spurred by the 1825 completion of the Erie Canal, which terminates there. The city serves as a critical link between the East and West, featuring over 500 miles of track and terminals for fourteen trunk lines. Buffalo's lake commerce is significant, with an annual port tonnage exceeding 5,000,000 tons. The Federal government has improved shipping facilities through a 4-mile outer breakwater of cement and stone costing over $2,000,000. Additionally, the International bridge from Squaw Island, completed in 1873 at a cost of $1,500,000, connects the city to the Canadian shore.

In Michigan, Ludington serves as the county-seat of Mason county. Situated on Lake Michigan at the mouth of the Marquette river, it was settled around 1859 and chartered as a city in 1873. Originally named Père Marquette, it was renamed in 1871 after James Ludington, a local lumberman. While its reputation as a lumber center has declined, the city maintains a large grain trade and various manufactures. It is a transit point where the Père Marquette railway ferries cars across the lake to Two Rivers, Kewanee, Milwaukee, and Manitowoc, Wisconsin.

## Industrial Cities of Wisconsin
Wisconsin hosts several specialized industrial cities, many of which are integrated into the Great Lakes shipping network.

**Kenosha**, the county-seat of Kenosha county, is located on the southwest shore of Lake Michigan. Originally known as Southport (settled c. 1832), it was chartered as a city in 1850. It is a major manufacturing hub, ranking third in the state for product value in 1905 with a total of $12,362,600. Its industries include the production of automobiles (the "Rambler" by Thomas B. Jeffery and Co.), typewriters, wagons, leather and brass goods, and mattresses and brass and iron beds via the Simmons Manufacturing Co.

**Ashland**, the county-seat of Ashland county, is located at the head of Chequamegon Bay on Lake Superior. Settled around 1854 and chartered as a city in 1887, it serves as the primary port for iron ore from the Gogebic Range, shipping approximately 3,500,000 tons annually valued at $12,000,000. Its diverse economy includes lumber exports, brownstone quarrying, and the manufacture of dynamite, charcoal, wood-alcohol, and sulphite fibre.

**Marinette**, the county-seat of Marinette county, is situated on the west shore of Green Bay. The first white settlement was established in 1830 on the site of a Menominee Indian village; the city was named for Marinette (Jacobs), the daughter of an Indian chief. Its economy is centered on the manufacture of lumber, paper, flour, boats, and agricultural machinery.

**Beloit**, located in Rock county on the Rock river, was founded by New Englanders in 1838 and chartered in 1856. Its industries utilize river water-power to produce flour, cutlery, ladies' shoes, steam pumps, windmills, and wood-working machinery.

**De Pere**, in Brown county, is located on the Fox river. It was the site of a polyglot Indian settlement and was visited by Jean Nicolet in 1634-1635. In 1670, Father Claude Allouez established the mission of St Francis Xavier there. The city is a shipping point featuring paper mills, cheese factories, and a launch and pleasure-boat factory.

## Education and Culture
The region features several notable educational and cultural institutions:
* **Beloit College**: A non-sectarian, co-educational institution founded in 1847 under the auspices of the Presbyterian and Congregational churches.
* **Northland College and Academy**: A Congregational institution located in Ashland.
* **St Norbert's College**: A Roman Catholic college established in De Pere in 1902.
* **Kemper Hall**: A Protestant Episcopal school for girls in Kenosha, opened in 1870 as a memorial to Jackson Kemper, the first bishop of Wisconsin.

Cultural contributions include the work of Carl Marr, an American artist born in Milwaukee in 1858. Marr, who became a professor in the Munich Academy and a member of the Berlin Academy of Arts, produced works such as "The Flagellants," which is held in the Milwaukee public library.
