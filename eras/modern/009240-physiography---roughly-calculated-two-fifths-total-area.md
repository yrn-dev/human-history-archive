# Physiography and Regional Areas

The study of physiography and territorial area encompasses a wide range of global regions, from the mountainous cordilleras of South America to the vast continental expanses of Asia and the maritime basins of Europe.

## The Andean Cordilleras of Bolivia
Roughly two-fifths of the total area of Bolivia is comprised of the Andean cordilleras. These mountains cross the south-west corner of the country and project east toward the Brazilian highlands, forming a great obtuse angle. The system is divided into two great parallel chains with spurs and flanking ranges to the east, creating the massif of the Andean system. This region consists of parallel ranges that enclose deep ravines and great elevated plateaus.

North-east of Lake Titicaca, the Nudo de Apolobamba consists of a knot of lofty intersecting ridges containing some of South America's highest peaks. Below this, the ranges open into extensive plateaus. The Cordillera Occidental serves as part of the boundary between Bolivia and the northern provinces of Chile, acting as the western rampart for the Bolivian tableland, or *alta-planicie*. This tableland extends from the Vilcanota knot in Peru south to the Serrania de Lipez on the Argentine frontier, measuring approximately 500 m. long and 80 m. broad, covering about 40,000 sq. m.

The *alta-planicie* is divided into the *puna* in the north and the "desert of Lipez" in the south, the latter being part of the great Puna de Atacama. With an average elevation of about 12,650 ft. near Lake Titicaca, the plateau descends about 1000 ft. toward its southern end. It is a lacustrine basin that once held an inland sea with an eastern outlet through the La Paz gorge. The terrain is barren, arid, and inhospitable, featuring saline depressions and peaks such as the Cerro de Tahua (17,454 ft.). Other notable snow-clad peaks overlooking the plateau from the west include Sajama (21,047 ft.), Huallatiri (21,654 ft.), Parinacota (20,918 ft.), Pomarape (20,505 ft.), Lirima (19,128 ft.), and the volcanic peaks of Licancaur (19,685 ft.), San Pedro y Pablo (19,423 ft.), and Oyahua (19,226 ft.).

## Regional Studies: China and Burma
In the widest sense, "China" includes Tibet, Mongolia, Manchuria, Eastern Turkestan, and the Eighteen Provinces, totaling some five million square miles. However, "China Proper"—the Eighteen Provinces—occupies not quite two-fifths of that total, covering somewhat more than a million and a half square miles. Its boundaries are roughly marked by the Tibetan frontier in the west, Shanghai in the east, Peking (the capital) in the north, and Canton in the south.

Burma, a province of British India, has a total estimated area of 238,738 sq. m. This is divided between Burma proper (168,573 sq. m.), the Shan States (59,915 sq. m.), and the Chin hills (10,250 sq. m.). The province extends from the Pakchan river (9° 55' north latitude) to the Kachin, Chingpaw, or Naga hills (between 27° and 28° north latitude), and from the Bay of Bengal in the west to the Mekong river in the east.

## Maritime and Tribal Areas
The Baltic, including the Kattegat and Skagerrak, has a total area of 166,397 sq. m. and a volume of 6907 cub. m., with a mean depth of 36 fathoms. Its geography is defined by several ridges and depressions, including the Aland Haf, the Bothnian Sea, and the Gulf of Bothnia. The Gulf of Finland extends eastwards for about 250 m., separating Esthonia from Finland, while the Gulf of Riga is a shallow, roughly circular inlet about 100 m. in diameter.

In Baluchistan, the Marri-Bugti country is a tribal area with a total area of 7,129 sq. m.

## Land Use and Agriculture
