# Buenos Aires and Ensenada

The ports of Buenos Aires and Ensenada serve as the primary hubs for an immense volume of business in Argentina. Because the navigation of the Paraná is difficult and impossible for larger ocean-going steamers, the majority of trade from the western and northern provinces is concentrated at these two locations. The republic's great trunk railways converge at these ports, and they are further supported by a liberal supply of branch railways. From these points, transatlantic steamship lines transport the products of the region's fertile soil.

## The Province of Buenos Aires
Buenos Aires is the largest and most populous maritime province of Argentina, covering an area of 117,812 square miles. It is bounded to the north by Entre Rios and the province of Santa Fé, to the east by the Atlantic and the La Plata estuary, to the south by the Atlantic, and to the west by the provinces of Santa Fé and Córdoba, as well as the territories of Las Pampas and Rio Negro. According to an official estimate from 1903, the population was 1,251,000, excluding the federal district.

Despite having over 900 miles of frontage on the Atlantic and the La Plata, the province possesses few good natural ports. The most significant are Bahia Blanca, where the government has built a naval port, and Ensenada (La Plata), which features extensive artificial basins designed to receive ocean-going steamers. A costly artificial port has also been constructed at Buenos Aires, while San Nicolas provides a fairly good river port in the extreme north.

## History and Administration
The early history of the province was defined by a two-generation struggle for supremacy over other provinces. Its territory was expanded through the conquest of adjoining Indian territories to the south and west, with the final additions occurring as late as 1879. The province joined the Confederation in 1820 and adopted a constitution in 1854, establishing a government consisting of a legislature of two chambers and a governor, both elected by popular vote.

In 1880, an unsuccessful revolt against the national government resulted in the federalization of the city of Buenos Aires. Consequently, La Plata was selected as the provincial capital. While the republic assumed the public debts of the provinces as indemnification during this transition, the province subsequently incurred further liabilities of ten million sterling, which has hindered its development.

## The City of Buenos Aires
The foundation of the city followed three separate attempts at colonization. The first in 1535 by Don Pedro de Mendoza failed due to mismanagement and Indian hostility. A second attempt in 1542 under Cabeza de Vaca was similarly unsuccessful. The city was finally established in 1580 by Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay. Garay sought to create a port near the sea to provision ships and receive supplies from Spain for the colonists at Asunción. He built a fort and a town called "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad," though he kept Mendoza's descriptive name for the port due to the invigorating atmosphere.

Buenos Aires remained a dependency of Asunción until 1620, when it became the capital of its own province and the seat of a bishopric. Although initially viewed by Spain as a small trading station of little interest compared to the gold and silver of Peru, the city's importance grew due to increasing trade and its proximity to Portuguese settlements in Brazil, which facilitated smuggling.

## Urban Development and Culture
Known by its residents, the Porteños, as the "Paris of America," Buenos Aires is the principal manufacturing center of the republic, with thousands of industrial establishments. The city's architecture has evolved from the Spanish style—characterized by one-story buildings grouped around patios with flat roofs and barred windows—to a prevailing French Renaissance style. This is evident in the elegant private residences of Avenida Alvear and the ornamented business edifices of Avenida de Mayo. However, urban conditions vary, as one-fifth of the population lives in overcrowded tenement-houses known as *conventillos*.

Notable public buildings include:
* **Casa Rosada**: The government house, located on the site of Garay's 1580 fort.
* **Congress Hall**: Finished in 1906 at a cost of approximately £1,300,000.
* **Educational and Cultural Institutions**: The University of Buenos Aires (which had 3,562 students in 1901), a national library, a national museum, an aquarium, and a zoological garden.

The city's residents are noted for their interest in the drama, music, and various diversions, ranging from horse-racing and *pelota* to Italian opera.

## Other Provincial Centers
Beyond the capital and the suburbs of Flores and Belgrano, the province contains several important towns:
* **La Plata**: The provincial capital.
* **Bahia Blanca**: A prominent port in the far south used for exporting wheat and wool.
* **San Nicolas**: A river port on the Paraná.
* **Campana**: Site of Argentina's first mutton-freezing factory (1883).
* **Other towns**: These include Chivilcoy, Pergamino, Mar del Plata (a seaside resort), Azul, Tandil, Chascomús, Mercedes, and Barracas al Sud, the former center of jerked beef industries.
