# Wales

## Geography and Urban Centers
Bangor (formerly known as Bangor Fawr) is a city and seaport located in Carnarvonshire, North Wales. It serves as a market-town and is situated near the northern entrance of the Menai Straits. As of 1901, the population of Bangor was 11,269. The city is divided into Upper and Lower sections, with the Lower section consisting practically of one street. It is located 240 miles northwest of London, accessible via the London & North Western railway.

The city is a municipal and contributory parliamentary borough within the Carnarvon district. Its economy is supported in part by the export of slate from Lord Penrhyn's quarries at Bethesda, located 6 miles away, which are shipped from Port Penrhyn at the mouth of the stream Cegid.

## Architecture and Institutions
Bangor is home to several notable buildings and educational institutions. These include:
*   **The Cathedral:** A cruciform structure with a low pinnacled tower. It stands on the site of a church founded around 525 by St Deiniol, which was destroyed by the English in 1071. The cathedral suffered several periods of damage, being severely handled in 1102 and 1212, destroyed in 1211, and burned during the time of Owen Glendower. It was rebuilt in part by Bishop Dean during the reign of Henry VII, and Bishop Skevyngton added the nave and tower in 1532. Sir G. Scott restored the cathedral between 1866 and 1875.
*   **Other Buildings:** The city contains a deanery, a disused bishop's palace, a small Roman Catholic church, and other churches.
*   **Education:** Bangor hosts the University College of N. Wales (established 1883), which includes a female students' hall. Additionally, the city contains Normal, Baptist, Independent, and N. Wales Training Colleges.

## Baptist Influence and Education
Baptists have established a presence in Wales, both through educational institutions and migration. There are two Baptist theological colleges located in Wales. These are part of a broader effort by the Baptist community to provide an educated ministry, which includes various colleges in England, Ireland, and Glasgow.

Historically, the Quaker colonies attracted a considerable number of Baptists from Wales, as well as from England and New England, due to the large measure of religious liberty offered in those colonies.
