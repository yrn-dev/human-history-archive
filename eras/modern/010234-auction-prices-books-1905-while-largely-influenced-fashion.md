# Auction Prices of Books (1905)

The financial aspects of book collecting and the determination of auction prices are documented in several specialized texts, including Livingston's *Auction Prices of Books* (1905), as well as *American Book Prices Current* and Slater's *Book-Prices Current*, the latter of which has been published annually since 1887.

## Factors Determining Book Prices
While the prices assigned to books are largely influenced by fashion, they are generally not considered wholly unreasonable. The value of a book is determined by three primary factors:
1. **Interest**: The positive or associative interest found within the book itself.
2. **Availability**: The infrequency with which copies appear on the market relative to the number and wealth of potential buyers.
3. **Condition**: Except for books of extreme rarity and interest, the price is affected by the copy's freshness, size, completeness, and the absence of stains.

Imagination often reinforces the positive interest in certain volumes, particularly editions connected to the development, diffusion, or origin of printing. Local antiquaries frequently covet the first book printed in any given town. This desire to measure the quality and amount of work produced by early printers has ensured the preservation of thousands of books that might otherwise have been lost.

## Illustrated Books and Market Trends
Illustrated books from specific periods are highly sought after. Except for a few volumes whose early celebrity prevented them from becoming rare, these books have seen an inordinate increase in price. 

Primitive woodcuts in incunabula are currently viewed as being almost too highly appreciated. For example, the *Hypnerotomachia Poliphili* (Venice, 1499) seldom fetches more than L120, and the *Nuremburg Chronicle* (1493) seldom exceeds L30, though rarer illustrated books can be priced in the hundreds. 

Market trends also show a sudden fashion for English books featuring coloured illustrations. Conversely, copper-engraved book illustrations from England and the later Dutch works, as well as "pretty French books" from the middle of the 16th century, have been imperfectly appreciated.

## Historical Context of Book Values
The cost of books in the 15th century is difficult to determine due to changes in coinage and the value of money. However, some records exist:
* A rubricated and illuminated vellum copy of the first printed Bible (Mainz, c. 1455) in two large folio volumes was said to be worth 100 florins.
* In 1467, the bishop of Aleria noted that the introduction of printing had reduced prices to one-fifth of previous costs.
* Fifteen "Legends" bequeathed by Caxton to St Margaret's, Westminster, sold for prices between 5s. and 6s. 8d.

## Notable Auctions and Collections
The history of book collecting includes significant auction events and the formation of great libraries. Henry George Bohn, a British publisher and dealer in rare books, was noted for his auction sales; one sale in 1848 lasted four days.

High-profile competitions between collectors have also shaped the market. In 1812, the sale of the duke of Roxburghe's books created significant excitement, particularly the competition between the duke of Marlborough (then marquess of Blandford) and Lord Spencer over a 1471 Venice edition of Boccaccio printed by Valdarfer. The duke of Marlborough paid L2260 for the volume. In 1819, during the sale of the duke of Marlborough's books, the same Boccaccio was purchased by Earl Spencer for L750. This volume eventually passed to Mrs Rylands in 1892 and was gifted to the John Rylands library at Manchester in 1899.
