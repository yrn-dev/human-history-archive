# Bohemia

## Geography and Orography
Bohemia is shaped like an irregular rhomb. Its northernmost point is Buchberg, located above Hainspach, which is the farthest north point in the entire Austro-Hungarian monarchy. Geographically, Bohemia is a separate massif within the Austrian provinces, bordered on three sides by mountain ranges. To the northeast are the Riesengebirge (Giant Mountains) and other ranges of the Sudetes. To the northwest lie the Erzgebirge (Ore Mountains). To the southwest is the Bohmerwald, also known as the Bohemian Forest.

The Bohmerwald runs from southeast to northwest and is divided into two parts by the pass of Neumark. The northern section, known in Czech as *Cesky Les*, reaches an altitude of 3,300 ft. at the massif of Czerkov. The southern section, known as *Sumava*, is the most picturesque and highest part of the range. On the Bohemian side, this area includes the Plockenstein (4,513 ft.) and the Osser (4,053 ft.), though the highest peak of the range is the Arber (4,872 ft.), located in Bavaria. The region is characterized by blue lakes in its valleys, crystalline torrents, and extensive forests of pine and oak.

## Strategic Pass and Historical Conflicts
The pass of Neumark, also called the pass of Neugedein, has served as the primary approach to Bohemia from Germany. Located above the small town of Taus (Czech *Domazlice*, formerly *Tuhost*), this site has been the location of several significant battles. In the first half of the 7th century, Samo defeated invading Avar hordes here to protect the independence of newly-settled Slavonic inhabitants. In August 1040, Wratislas II defeated the German emperor Henry III in a battle lasting two days. Later, in 1431, the Hussites achieved a major victory against a German army of crusaders at this pass, and George of Podebrad similarly vanquished another German army in the same location.

## Ethnic and Political Tensions
Bohemia and Moravia were characterized by a vehement contest between two nearly equal races: the Germans and the Czechs. For twenty years, the Czechs consistently encroached upon the Germans, a process aided by government action. An 1880 ordinance required government offices and law courts to handle business in the language in which it was introduced, granting the Czech language a position in government service. In 1886, this was extended to the high courts of Bruenn and Prague to avoid frequent translations. 

Further shifts occurred with the founding of numerous Czech elementary and middle schools, the introduction of Czech classes in German schools, and the 1882 establishment of Czech classes at the university of Prague, which Germans viewed as a desecration of the oldest German university.

Politically, the Czechs were divided into several groups, including the Young Czechs, Old Czechs, Czech Agrarians, Czech Clericals, Realists, and Czech National Socialists. The Young Czechs were particularly impatient and opposed any measures that threatened the unity of the Bohemian country. In 1891, the Young Czechs emerged completely victorious in a struggle against the Old Czechs.

## Governance and Constitutional Struggles
The Czechs sought the recognition of the Bohemian kingdom. While some leaders were content with their influence, the Young Czechs pushed for more. In 1890, Taaffe attempted to reconcile the German party, the Czechs, and the Feudal party through conferences in Vienna. An agreement was reached to revise the electoral law and separate German and Czech districts, but the measures failed in the diet because the Young Czechs were excluded from the conference and used obstruction to block them.

Between 1902 and 1904, Dr. von Koerber attempted further compromises. He proposed dividing Bohemia into 10 districts (five Czech, three German, and two mixed) and allocating 234 district tribunals as 133 Czech, 94 German, and seven mixed. The Czechs rejected this, demanding that German and Czech be placed on equal footing for all official purposes throughout Bohemia. Because this implied recognizing Czech as a language of internal service, the Germans refused. Consequently, Koerber governed largely via imperial ordinance under paragraph 14 of the constitution, as the Czechs blocked parliament with "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction, including one sitting that lasted 57 hours. In response, various German parties—including the Christian Socialists, German Populists, German Progressists, and Constitutional Landed Proprietors—formed a joint executive committee to protect German racial interests.

## Czech Independence and Military Action
During the World War, Czechs among Austrian prisoners in Russia demonstrated a burning patriotism and an uncompromising stand for freedom from the Austrian yoke. Unlike many other Austrian prisoners who remained passive or sought to avoid military service, the Czechs took the initiative to fight for victory and France. 

This movement culminated in the seizure of Vladivostok. As a Slavic, Russian-speaking people, the Czechs were welcomed by the local population, some of whom were planning anti-Bolshevik demonstrations. The Czechs were able to free the city from Bolshevik oppressors a month after the initial demonstration. Their success in Russia was attributed to the fact that they had braved death rather than fight Russians, allowing them to fight oppressive Russian elements without their motives being misunderstood.
