# Briançon and the Geography of South-Eastern France

## The Town of Briançon
Briançon is a strongly fortified town located in the department of Hautes-Alpes in south-eastern France. The town is situated on a plateau at a height of 4,334 ft., a position that dominates the junction of the Guisane and the Durance. The layout of the town is characterized by picturesque, narrow, and very steep streets.

Due to its location at the foot of the descent from the Mont Genèvre Pass, which provides access to Turin, numerous fortifications have been built on the surrounding heights, particularly toward the east. Among these is Fort Janus, which stands 4,000 ft. above the town. Other notable landmarks include the parish church, built between 1703 and 1726 with two towers in a conspicuous position, and the Pont d'Asfeld. Built in 1734 to the east of the town, this bridge features an arch with a 131 ft. span, crossing the Durance at a height of 184 ft.

The modern section of the town is located in the plain at the south-western foot of the plateau. This area, known as the suburb of Ste Catherine, contains the railway station and an important factory for silk-weaving. Briançon is 51½ m. from Gap by rail. As of 1906, the commune had a total of 7,524 inhabitants, consisting of a civil population of 4,883 (with 3,130 being the urban population) and a permanent garrison of 2,641.

## The Department of Lozère
Lozère is a department of south-eastern France that belongs to the central plateau. It was formerly composed of almost the whole of Gévaudan and parts of the old dioceses of Alais and Uzès, all of which were previously included in the province of Languedoc. In terms of average elevation, Lozère is the highest of all French departments and is mountainous throughout. It is bounded by Cantal and Haute-Loire to the north, Ardèche and Gard to the east, Gard and Aveyron to the south, and Aveyron and Cantal to the west. In 1906, its population was 128,016 across an area of 1,999 sq. m.

The department is divided into three distinct regions:
*   **The Cévennes:** Located in the south-east, this region begins with Mont Aigoual, which rises above 5,100 ft. Nearby are the mountains of Bougès, which feature bare southern faces and wooded slopes falling toward the Tarn.
*   **The Causses:** Located in the south-west, these are arid, fissured calcareous plateaus separated by well-watered, deep gorges. The causse of Méjan and the causse of Sauveterre (the latter ranging from 3,000 to 3,300 ft.) are situated between the Tarn and the Lot.
*   **Mountain Tracts:** This area includes the range of Lozère, where the department's highest point, the peak of Finiels, reaches 5,584 ft. It also includes the broad, marshy plateau of Montbel and the mountains of La Margeride. The Margeride consists of undulating granitic tablelands covered in pastures and woods of fir, beech, and oak, with its highest point, Truc de Randon, reaching 5,098 ft. Adjoining these is the volcanic range of Aubrac, a pastoral district with a highest point of 4,826 ft.

## The Department of Aveyron
Aveyron is a department of southern France located on the southern border of the central plateau. It is bounded by Cantal to the north, Lozère and Gard to the east, Tarn and Tarn-et-Garonne to the south-west, and Lot to the west. It corresponds nearly to the old district of Rouergue. The earliest known natives of the region were the Celtic Rutheni, who are credited with the department's numerous megalithic monuments. In 1906, Aveyron had a population of 377,299 and an area of 3,386 sq. m.

The department is divided into four zones by its chief rivers—the Lot, the Aveyron, and the Tarn—all of which flow east to west as tributaries of the Garonne:
*   **North-east:** Between the Lot and the Truyère lies the pastoral plateau of the Viadene, dominated by the volcanic Aubrac mountains, which include the department's highest summit at 4,760 ft.
*   **The Causses:** Between the Lot and the Aveyron is a belt of monotonous limestone table-lands known as causses, characterized by profound gorges. This zone is watered by the Dourdou du Nord.
*   **The Segala:** Between the Tarn and the Aveyron is the plateau of the Segala, traversed by the deep valley of the Viaur and bordered to the east by the Palanges and Levezou heights.
*   **The South:** The region south of the Tarn is largely occupied by the huge plateau of Larzac, situated between the Causse St Affrique and the Causse Noir. The Causse Noir features Montpellier-le-Vieux, a chaos of precipices and rocks.

## The Lot River
The Lot is a river of southern France that flows westward across the central plateau through the departments of Lot, Lozère, Aveyron, and Lot-et-Garonne. It rises in the Cévennes on the Mont du Goulet at a height of 4,918 ft., approximately 15 m. east of Mende. The river is about 300 m. long with a basin area of 4,444 sq. m.

Its upper course flows through gorges between the Aubrac Mountains and the Causse of Mende to the north, and the tablelands (causses) of Comtal, Severac, and Sauveterre to the south. After crossing the plateau of Quercy, it enters a fertile plain and flows into the Garonne at Aiguillon. The Lot's largest tributary is the Truyère, which joins from the right at Entraygues. Other tributaries include the Célé (above Cahors) and the Dourdou de Bozouls. The river is canalized for 160 m. between the Garonne and Bouquiès.
