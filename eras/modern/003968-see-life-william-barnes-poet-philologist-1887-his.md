# William Barnes

William Barnes (1800–1886) was a poet and philologist from Dorsetshire. Born on February 22, 1800, at Rushay, near Pentridge in Dorset, he was the son of Grace Scott and John Barnes, both of the farmer class. While his forebears were described as a strong race, Barnes was a delicate child who inherited a love for books and a retiring, refined disposition from his mother.

## Early Education and Career
Barnes attended school at Sturminster Newton, where he was regarded as the cleverest boy. His professional life began when he was selected by a solicitor named Dashwood to serve as a pupil. During this period, Barnes utilized his spare time to study music under an organist and classics with the village parson. 

In 1818, he moved to Dorchester to work in the office of a man named Coombs, continuing his evening studies with the assistance of another clergyman. Barnes also developed a skill for wood-engraving. He used the funds earned from creating a series of blocks for a work titled *Walks about Dorchester* to publish his first book, *Orra, a Lapland Tale*, in 1822.

## Professional Life and Literary Development
In 1823, Barnes took over a school at Mere in Wiltshire. He married Julia Miles, the daughter of an excise officer, in 1827 and settled in Chantry House, a Tudor mansion in Mere. As his school grew, Barnes dedicated his remaining time to an assiduous study of diverse authors, including Ovid, Petrarch, Sallust, Herodotus, Burns, and Buffon.

During these years, he began writing poetry, publishing many of his verses in the *Dorset County Chronicle*. He also ventured into playwriting; in 1832, he wrote a comedy performed at Wincanton and a farce titled *The Honest Thief*, which was produced by a visiting company of strolling actors. Additionally, he authored educational texts, including *Outlines of Geography* and *Elements of Perspective*.

## Philological Work and Dialect Poetry
Barnes's primary academic interests were philological. In 1829, he published *An Etymological Glossary of English Words of Foreign Derivation*. Later, in 1886, he produced a *Glossary of Dorset Dialect*.

In 1833, Barnes began writing poems in the Dorsetshire dialect, first appearing in a local newspaper. These works included two eclogues: "A Bit o' Sly Coorten" and "The 'Lotments." His poetry is noted for providing an admirable illustration of the county's dialect, which, while distinct from the dialects of Somersetshire and Wiltshire, retains many common marks of Saxon origin.

## Legacy and Biographical Records
Barnes left Mere in 1835 and returned to his home region. His life and work were later documented by his daughter, Lucy E. Baxter (who wrote on art under the pseudonym Leader Scott), in the 1887 publication *The Life of William Barnes, Poet and Philologist*. Additionally, Thomas Hardy wrote a notice regarding Barnes in the *Athenaeum* on October 16, 1886.
