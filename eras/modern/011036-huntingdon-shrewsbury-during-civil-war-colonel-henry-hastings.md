# Huntingdon and Shrewsbury

## The Earldoms of Huntingdon and Shrewsbury
The earldoms of Huntingdon and Shrewsbury are distinguished as two of the three "catskin earls," alongside the earl of Derby. This designation is believed to be a corruption of the term *quatre-skin*, referring to ancient earls' robes that were decorated with four rows of ermine, similar to the robes of a modern duke, rather than the three rows used in later centuries. These three earldoms are the only ones currently in existence that were created prior to the 17th century.

## Robert of Belesme and the Earldom of Shrewsbury
In the early 12th century, Robert of Belesme served as the earl of Shrewsbury. He inherited the fief of Shrewsbury from his father, a companion of the Conqueror, and the fief of Belesme from his mother, Mabel Talvas. Because these were march-fiefs—one protecting England from the Welsh and the other guarding Normandy from Maine—the lord of these lands possessed significant independence and power.

Robert is described as a tall and strong man, an able military engineer, and a persuasive, eloquent, and politic noble. However, he was also characterized as a systematic traitor, a rebel, and an oppressor of the weak who was savagely cruel and greedy for land. His history was marked by rebellion: he supported a revolt against the Conqueror in 1077 and was an accomplice in a conspiracy against Rufus in 1088. Although he succeeded his brother Hugh in the earldom of Shrewsbury in 1098, he later revolted against Henry I in 1102. This led to his banishment and the loss of his English estates. After betraying Robert's cause at Tinchebrai, he was imprisoned for life by Henry I in 1112.

## Colonel Henry Hastings and the Civil War
During the Civil War, Colonel Henry Hastings played a role in the fortification and defense of Ashby-de-la-Zouch. He fortified the castle there and held it for the king; the castle was subsequently visited by Charles in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament. In 1645, Colonel Henry Hastings was created a baron. He is listed among the subsequent lords of the manor of Loughborough, which had previously been held by Sir Edward Hastings (created Baron Hastings of Loughborough in 1558) and later by the earls of Huntingdon.

## Regional Military Administration in Huntingdon
During the 17th-century Civil War, the county of Huntingdon was among several areas placed under the administration of Robert Pierrepont. Pierrepont, who had been created earl of Kingston-upon-Hull in 1628, initially remained neutral at the start of the conflict. He later joined the king and was appointed as the lieutenant-general for the counties of Norfolk, Cambridge, Rutland, Lincoln, and Huntingdon. Pierrepont was taken prisoner while defending Gainsborough and was accidentally killed on July 25, 1643, during his transport to Hull.
