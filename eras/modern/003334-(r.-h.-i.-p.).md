## Mathematical Computations for Easter
In the context of calculating the date of Easter Sunday for any year after the Reformation, specific variables and formulae are utilized. To determine the number of days from March 21st to Easter Sunday (represented as $p$), the formula $p = P + (L - l)$ is used. 

The values for $P$ and $l$ are determined by the value of $E$ (the epact):
*   If $E < 24$, then $P = 24 - E$ and $l = 27 - E$, or $((27 - E) / 7)_r$.
*   If $E > 23$, then $P = 54 - E$ and $l = 57 - E$, or $((57 - E) / 7)_r$.

To ensure $L - l$ is a positive whole number, 7 must be added to $L$ as often as necessary. Because $E > 23$, the values are $P = 28$ and $l = 3$, leading to $p = 29$.

### Imidazoles
Imidazole ($\text{C}_3\text{H}_4\text{N}_2$), also known as glyoxaline, was first prepared by H. Debus in 1858 via the action of ammonia on glyoxal. It crystallizes in thick prisms with a melting point of 88-89° C and a boiling point of 253° C. It is soluble in water and alcohol. While unaffected by chromic acid, it is oxidized to formic acid by potassium permanganate. Other compounds in this series, such as Lophine (triphenylglyoxaline), can be prepared through the condensation of ortho-diketones with ammonia and aldehydes.
