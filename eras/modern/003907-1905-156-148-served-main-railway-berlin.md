# Barmen

## Geography and Administration
Barmen is a town located in Germany within the governmental district of Duesseldorf and the Prussian Rhine province. The town is situated in a narrow valley along the river Wupper, stretching for approximately 4 miles. Within the municipal boundaries, the river is crossed by twenty bridges, and the town is surrounded by high wooded hills. Barmen is divided into three primary districts: Lower, Middle, and Upper Barmen. It lies immediately east of Elberfeld, and the two locations virtually form a single town.

Although Barmen was mentioned in chronicles as early as the 11th century, it did not receive civic rights until 1808, at which time it was formed into a municipality.

## Population and Infrastructure
The population of Barmen has seen steady growth over the 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1816, the population was 19,030; by 1890, it had grown to 116,144, and by 1905, it reached 156,148.

The town is served by the main railway line running from Berlin to Aix-la-Chapelle. Barmen is connected to Elberfeld throughout its entire length via a tramway, a railway, and a suspended trolley line that hangs over the bed of the river Wupper.

## Industry and Commerce
Barmen is recognized as one of Germany's most important manufacturing centres, though its rapid commercial development only began at the start of the 19th century. It serves as the chief seat of ribbon weaving in Germany. The town's diverse manufacturing output includes:
*   **Textiles:** Lace, thread, braids, cloth goods, cotton, and silks.
*   **Hardware and Goods:** Steel wares, machinery, plated goods, and buttons. The button industry is particularly significant, employing approximately 15,000 workers.
*   **Chemicals and Processing:** Soap works, chemical works, and dyeworks, the latter of which are famous for Turkey-red. There are also numerous print-fields and bleaching-fields.
*   **Other:** Potteries and extensive breweries.

Barmen maintains a considerable export trade, with a particular emphasis on trade with the United States.

## Public Institutions and Culture
The town possesses a variety of religious and civic buildings, including two Roman Catholic churches and nine Evangelical churches. Civic architecture includes a stately modern town hall and an exchange. 

Cultural and educational facilities include:
*   **Arts and History:** A theatre, a picture-gallery, and an ethnographical museum.
*   **Education:** A missionary theological seminary, academies of engineering, textile industry, and technical science, as well as several high-grade public schools.
*   **Monuments:** A Hall of Fame (_Ruhmeshalle_) featuring statues of Frederick III and William I (emperors). Other public monuments commemorate the Franco-German War of 1870-71, Bismarck, and the native poet Emil Rittershaus (1834-1897).

To the south of the town, the hills are utilized as public grounds. This area contains numerous villas, a tower providing an extensive view, and a health resort.
