# Local Government and Port Management of Bombay

## Port Administration and Infrastructure
The port of Bombay, which includes its warehouses and docks, is managed by a port trust. The members of this trust are nominated by the government from within the commercial community. Bombay is noted as the only important coastal location in India where the rise of the tide is sufficient to allow for the construction of docks on the largest scale; average tides reach 14 feet, while the highest spring tides reach 17 feet.

The city's dockyard was originally established in 1736. It covers an area of approximately 200 acres and possesses a sea-face of nearly 700 yards. This facility includes four building slips located opposite the Apollo Bandar landing-place on the south-east side and five graving docks. Two of these graving docks combine to form a single dock 582 feet long, while the other three together create a large dock 648 feet long. To facilitate continuous operation, the dockyard is equipped with electric lighting, allowing work to proceed during both day and night.

Other significant dock facilities include:
* **Prince's Dock:** Opened in 1879 after its foundation-stone was laid by the prince of Wales in 1875. It measures 1,460 feet in length by 1,000 feet in breadth, with a water area of 30 acres.
* **Victoria Dock:** Completed and opened between 1887 and 1888, featuring a water area of 25 acres.
* **Alexandra Dock:** The largest dock in India, for which the prince of Wales laid the foundation-stone in 1905. It is situated south of the Victoria dock.

## Municipal Government
The municipal government of Bombay was established by an act of the Bombay legislative council passed in 1888. The governing structure is divided between a town council and a municipal corporation.

The municipal corporation consists of 72 members. The composition of this body is as follows:
* 16 members nominated by the government.
* 36 members elected by ratepayers.
* 16 members elected by the justices of the peace.
* 2 members elected by the chamber of commerce.
* 2 members elected by the senate of the university.

The membership of the corporation is diverse, including Parsees, Mahommedans, Hindus, and Europeans.

The town council serves as the standing committee for the corporation. It is composed of 12 members, four of whom are nominated by the government, with the remaining eight elected by the corporation.

## Commercial and Educational Context
Alongside the docks, the milling industry—specifically cotton-spinning—is a primary driver of Bombay's commercial success. Other local industries include workshops and flour mills. The number of factories grew from 53 in 1881 to 83 by 1890, accompanied by an influx of industrial population from surrounding districts. However, the period between 1891 and 1901 saw a temporary decline due to over-production and the impact of the plague. The plague caused a population exodus from the island and disrupted labor in the mills and docks. Additionally, the municipality spent large sums on sanitary improvements and plague operations. Trade and population began to revive after 1901; as of that year, 131,796 people were employed in the cotton industry.

The city also hosts several major educational institutions. Bombay University was established in 1857 as an examining body, modeled after the university of London. Other prominent institutions include:
* The government law school and government Elphinstone College.
* The Grant medical college.
* The Victoria Jubilee technical institute.
* The Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy school of art.
* Two missionary colleges, namely St Xavier and Wilson.
