# Historiography and Literature of Austria

The study of Austrian history is supported by an extensive body of literature covering general histories, constitutional developments, diplomatic relations, and specific regional and racial inquiries.

## General Histories and Early Works
The only general history of Austria available in the English language is Archdeacon William Coxe's *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792*, published in three volumes in 1817. This work was later continued by W. Kelly in 1853, with a new edition appearing in 1873, though it has since been superseded by more recent research.

Other significant general works include J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates* (1834-1850) and the 17-volume series *Oesterreichische Gesch. fuer das Volk* (1864). Franz X. Krones (Ritter von Marchland) authored the five-volume *Handbuch der Gesch. Oesterreichs* (1876-1879) and *Gesch. der Neuzeit Oesterreichs vom 18ten Jahrhundert bis auf die Gegenwart* (1879), the latter written from a German-liberal perspective.

## Constitutional and Diplomatic History
Constitutional history is documented in several key texts, including E. Werunsky's *Oesterreichische Reichs- und Rechtsgeschichte* (1894), A. Bechmann's *Lehrbuch der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch* (1895-1896), and A. Huber's *Oesterreichische Reichsgesch* (1895), which saw a second edition by A. Dopsch in 1901. A. Luschin von Ebengreuth provided a work of "first-class importance" titled *Oesterreichische Reichsgesch* (1895, 1896), as well as *Grundriss der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch* (1899). G. Kolmer's *Parlament und Verfassung in Oesterreich* (1902-1905) covers the period from 1848 to 1885 across three volumes. Regarding relations with Hungary, sources include J. Andrassy's *Ungarns Ausgleich mit Oesterreich, 1867* (1897) and L. Eisenmann's *Le Compromis austro-hongrois de 1867* (1904).

Diplomatic history is explored through A. Beer's works on Austrian politics from 1801-1810 and oriental politics since 1774. Other diplomatic sources include A. Fournier's study of Gentz and Cobenzl (1801-1805) and F. von Demelitsch's volume on Metternich's foreign policy (1809-1812). H. Uebersberger's work examines the relationship between Austria and Russia from 1488 to 1605. Modern developments regarding the "Austrian question" are discussed by Andre Cheradame (1901, 1902), Rene Henry (1903), and "Scotus Viator" (1907).

## Regional, Racial, and Biographical Studies
The original archduchy, including Upper Austria, serves as the nucleus of the empire and the oldest Habsburg possession. This is detailed in F. Umlauft's *Das Erzherzogtum Oesterreich unter der Enns* and M. Vansca's *Gesch. Nieder- u. Ober-Oesterreichs*. Additionally, G. Juritsch and M. Schmitz have written on the Babenberger dynasty.

The "racial question" and issues of language are addressed in the *Oesterr. Staatswoerterbuch* by Josef Ulbrith and Ernst Mischler (1894-1907), which provides a statement on the legal questions involved. Other authors on this topic include Dummreicher, Hainisch, Herkner, L. Leger, Bressnitz von Sydacoff, and Bertrand Auerbach.

Biographical records are primarily found in C. von Wurzbach's 60-volume *Biographisches Lexikon des Kaisertums Oesterreich* (1856-1891) and the *Allgemeine deutsche Biographie*.

## Specialized Historical Accounts
Certain periods of upheaval have received specific attention. Count F. von Hartig's *Genesis der Revolution in Oesterreich im Jahre 1848* (1851) was noted for creating a great sensation. W. H. Stiles provided an eyewitness account in *Austria in 1848-1849* (1852). Other specialized works include A. von Arneth's 10-volume *Geschichte Maria Theresias* (1863-1879) and H. Bidermann's study of the "Gesamtstaatsidee" from 1526-1804.
