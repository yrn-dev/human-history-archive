# The Decision Between the Two Races and Feudal Voting

In the political history of the Austrian Empire, specifically within Bohemia, the balance of power between different ethnic groups was frequently determined by the structure of the electoral system and the influence of landed estates. During the ministry of Auersperg, which lasted eight years, the government pursued a vigorous Liberal centralizing policy. In Bohemia, this administration initially succeeded in nearly crushing opposition.

## The Role of Feudal Proprietors in Bohemia
The political struggle in Bohemia often centered on the "decision between the two races," where the outcome turned on the votes of the feudal proprietors. To ensure a German majority in the diet, a society of German capitalists from Vienna—popularly known as *Chabrus*—was formed. The purpose of this society was to acquire portions of estates that carried voting rights, whether through real or fictitious purchases. 

These efforts were successful in securing a large German majority. Consequently, individuals from Vienna replaced figures such as the Schwarzenbergs and the Thuns in the diet. For several years following these measures, the Czechs refused to sit in the diet, allowing the government to operate without difficulty.

## Electoral Laws and Political Shifts
The government further weakened the Federalists by passing a new electoral law in 1873. This law shifted the right to elect members of the *Reichsrath* (the parliament for the whole empire) from the diets to a direct vote of the people. This change deprived the Federalists of their primary weapon; it was no longer possible to use a formal vote of legal representatives in a territory to refuse the appointment of deputies. If a Slovene or Czech member declined to take their seat, it only resulted in a single unrepresented constituency, thereby weakening the opposition.

Despite strong opposition—including a petition with 250,000 names from Bohemia and the withdrawal of the Poles from the *Reichsrath*—the law passed by a vote of 120 to 2. This increased the total number of members to 353.

## The Structure of the Diets and Curiae
Under the February Constitution of 1861, the *Reichsrath* consisted of a Chamber of Deputies and a House of Lords (*Herrenhaus*). Members of the Chamber of Deputies were not chosen by direct election but by the diets. These diets were elected every six years and were composed of several groups:
* Bishops and university rectors who sat by virtue of their office.
* Members chosen by four electoral bodies, or *curiae*.

The first of these *curiae* consisted of the "great proprietors," defined as owners of estates that had enjoyed certain feudal privileges prior to 1848. The other three *curiae* included the towns, the rural districts, and the chambers of commerce.

## Conflict and Reconciliation Efforts
Prior to the combination of Federalists and Clericals, the ministry faced internal division. While some, including Alfred Potocki (the minister of agriculture) and Count Taaffe, sought to conciliate the Slav races—a policy likely supported by the emperor and recommended by Beust—others wanted to cripple the opposition by removing the diets' role in *Reichsrath* elections.

Following the resignation of Taaffe and his friends in January 1870, a new government was formed by Potocki and Taaffe to attempt reconciliation with the Slavs. Potocki traveled to Prague and summoned Thun and Rieger to Vienna, but the attempt failed. Both the Czechs and the feudals supported the declaration of 1868 and refused any compromise.

During the crisis of the war between France and Germany in July, the emperor dissolved the *Reichsrath* and all diets to avoid alienating either the Slavs or the Germans. In Bohemia, the Czechs, supported by the Clericals and the Feudals, won a large majority. However, they viewed the diet as an informal assembly rather than a legal representative of the Bohemian kingdom and refused to elect delegates for the *Reichsrath*.
