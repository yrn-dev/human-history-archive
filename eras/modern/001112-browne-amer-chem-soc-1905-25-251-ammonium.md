# Azoimides and Related Nitrogen Compounds

The study of nitrogen-based compounds includes various salts and acids characterized by high reactivity and, in several instances, extreme instability. Among these are the azoimides, including ammonium and hydrazine variants, as well as the related chloroazoimide.

## Ammonium Azoimide
Ammonium azoimide, with the chemical formula $\text{N}_3\text{NH}_4$, is a salt that is readily soluble in water and exhibits a feebly alkaline nature. It is characterized as being extremely explosive.

The preparation of ammonium azoimide involves boiling diazohippuramide with alcoholic ammonia. This process continues until ammonia no longer escapes, following the reaction:
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO.NHCH}_2\text{CONH.N}_2\text{OH} + 2\text{NH}_3 = \text{N}_3\text{NH}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO.NH.CH}_2\text{CO.NH}_2$.

Following the reaction, the liquid is left to stand for twelve hours, allowing the hippuramide to precipitate. The clear alcoholic solution is then decanted. To this solution, ether is added in a volume four times that of the solution, which causes the ammonium salt to precipitate. The final product is filtered, washed with ether, and air-dried.

## Related Azoimide Compounds
Other compounds in this group include hydrazine azoimide, which has the formula $\text{N}_5\text{H}_5$. Additionally, F. Raschig obtained chloroazoimide ($\text{Cl.N}_3$), the chloride corresponding to azoimide. This substance is a colorless gas that is highly explosive, produced by acidifying a mixture of hypochlorite and sodium azide using boric or acetic acid.

## Synthesis of Related Acids
Certain acids can be synthesized through various oxidative processes. For example, an acid can be obtained through the action of nitrous acid on hydrazine sulphate. Other methods include the oxidation of hydrazine using ammonium metavanadate, or by utilizing sulphuric acid and hydrogen peroxide.

Furthermore, W. Wislicenus prepared a sodium salt by passing sodamide over nitrous oxide at high temperatures.

## Other Nitrogenous Acids
Fulminuric acid, represented as $(\text{HCNO})_3$, was originally obtained by Liebig by boiling water with mercuric fulminate. In 1905, L. Bernardini and C. Ulpiani synthesized the compound, regarding its formula as $\text{NO}_2\text{CH(CN).CO.NH}_2$. Fulminuric acid forms a characteristic cuprammonium salt and deflagrates at 145 degrees.
