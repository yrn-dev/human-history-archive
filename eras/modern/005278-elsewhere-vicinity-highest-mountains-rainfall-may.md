# Rainfall and Orographical Influence on Precipitation

The relationship between mountainous terrain and precipitation varies significantly across different global regions, with high altitudes often serving as focal points for increased rainfall or influencing the distribution of water across plateaus and coastal belts.

## Precipitation in Central Africa
In certain parts of Africa, the landscape is characterized by an abundance of rainfall and a practical absence of desert country. However, precipitation levels vary based on geography. In the lower Shiré district and the land to the north-east and east of Lake Mweru, annual rainfall is relatively low, likely not exceeding an average of 35 inches. 

In contrast, rainfall increases significantly in the vicinity of the highest mountains. In these areas, the average may reach 75 inches, and in parts of Mount Mlanje, it may frequently reach 100 inches per year. The general average for the region is approximately 50 inches per annum, a figure mirrored in the Shiré Highlands of British Central Africa.

On a broader continental scale, eastern Africa is marked by parallel mountain ranges rising in tiers from the coast. The first line of hills is located ten to fifty miles from the Indian Ocean, followed by a second belt known in South Africa as the low veldt. The final mountain system, situated one hundred to one hundred and fifty miles from the ocean, supports a great elevated inland plateau between 4,000 and 6,000 feet above sea level. While the middle belt tends to be arid with very light rainfall, the plateau and its mountain flanks experience high or very high rainfall. Most great African rivers, excluding the Niger, originate on this plateau or its flanks.

## Rainfall in Asia and the Middle East
In the peninsula of Arabia and Syria, the region is generally among the hottest and driest, characterized by low mountains rising to 3,000 or 4,000 feet. In these areas, rainfall is greatest in the neighborhood of the coasts, where regions of greater fertility exist. In northern Syria, the mountains of Lebanon rise to approximately 10,000 feet; this higher elevation provides a more copious water supply, rendering the country more productive.

In Asia, the Himalaya ranges show a distinct precipitation gradient. The lower ranges in eastern India are wetter and support tropical flora. As one moves westward, rainfall diminishes and winter cold increases. The higher ranges of the Himalaya are less rainy and cooler, maintaining a more uniform character of vegetation. Upon reaching the Tibetan plateau, the environment becomes characterized by increased dryness.

In Siberia, the rainfall is not heavy, though it is sufficient to maintain vegetation compatible with the local temperatures. This region includes a high, broken plateau fringed by border ridges.

## Precipitation in the Caribbean and North America
In the British West Indies, the island of Grenada features a well-wooded mountain range with Mount Catharine as its highest point at 2,750 feet. The island experiences very heavy rainfall, which in some parts reaches 200 inches per year. The rainy season occurs from May to December. Specific measurements show an average annual rainfall of 79.07 inches at St Georges and 164 inches at Grand Etang.

In British Columbia, precipitation varies by location. While the station at Victoria represents the southern coast, rainfall is much greater on the exposed parts of the outer coast. Generally, precipitation increases as one moves northward, both in the interior and along the coast.

## Atmospheric Observations on Mountains
Observations at specific mountain stations have noted variations in air particles related to weather and rainfall. At the Ben Nevis observatory, A. Rankin observed a yearly variation in particle numbers, which peaked in March, April, and May. This phenomenon may be attributed to small rainfall during those months, though it is more likely caused by south-easterly winds bringing impure air from densely inhabited areas. Similarly, Aitken observed daily variations at Rigi Kulm, noting a maximum near the hottest part of the day.
