# BOLAS AND BONOMI, GIUSEPPI

## Bolas
Bolas (the plural of the Spanish word *bola*, meaning ball) are weapons used for war and the chase by South American Indians. These weapons consist of balls made of stone which are attached to the ends of a rope made from braided or twisted hemp or hide. They have been utilized for centuries within the forest regions of the Rio Grande and the South American pampas. F. Ratzel, in *History of Mankind*, suggests that the bolas are a form of lasso. A similar weapon is used by Eskimos for the purpose of killing birds.

According to Charles Darwin's *Voyage of the Beagle*, there are two primary types of bolas:
* **Simple Bolas:** Used primarily to catch ostriches, these consist of two round stones covered in leather and joined by a thin, plaited thong approximately 8 feet in length.
* **Three-ball Bolas:** These consist of three balls joined by thongs to a common center. A Gaucho (a native of Spanish descent) uses this version by holding the smallest ball in his hand and whirling the other two around his head. When thrown, the balls revolve through the air like chain shot; upon striking an object, they wind around it, cross each other, and become firmly hitched.

Additionally, there are *bolas perdidas* (lost bolas), which are stones that either have no thong or are attached to a very short one.

## Giuseppi Bonomi (1739–1808)
Giuseppi Bonomi was an English architect born in Rome on January 19, 1739. After establishing a significant reputation in Italy, he moved to England in 1767, where he eventually settled his practice. Bonomi is credited with being largely responsible for the revival of classical architecture in England. His most prominent work was the design of the Italian villa at Roseneath, located in Dumbartonshire, for the duke of Argyll. In 1804, he was named the honorary architect to St Peter's in Rome.

Bonomi's relationship with the Royal Academy was marked by a conflict involving Sir Joshua Reynolds. Sir Joshua desired for Bonomi to become a full Academician, as he viewed him as a suitable candidate for the vacant chair of perspective. However, the majority of the Academicians opposed this. Consequently, Bonomi was elected only as an associate, a result achieved only through the president's casting vote. This situation led to the retirement of Sir Joshua Reynolds from the presidency of the Royal Academy. Bonomi died in London on March 9, 1808.

## Giuseppi Bonomi (1796–1878)
The son of the architect, also named Giuseppi Bonomi, was a sculptor and illustrator who studied art at the Royal Academy in London. He is best recognized for his work as an illustrator for the primary Egyptological publications of his era. 

Between 1824 and 1832, Bonomi resided in Egypt, where he produced drawings of monuments while in the company of Wilkinson, Lane, and Burton. In 1833, he visited the mosque of Omar and produced detailed drawings of the site. He returned to Egypt from 1842 to 1844, during which time he was attached to a Prussian government exploration expedition led by Lepsius. In 1853, he assisted in arranging the Egyptian court at the Crystal Palace. His later career included an appointment as the curator of the Soane Museum in 1861. He died on March 3, 1878.

## Other References to Bola
In historical records from *Plutarch's Lives*, Bola is mentioned as a location and a people. Specifically, the town of Bola was one of several Latian cities—along with Pedum, Labici, and Tolerium—that were taken by storm by Marcius. During this campaign, the inhabitants of Bola were plundered and sold as slaves.
