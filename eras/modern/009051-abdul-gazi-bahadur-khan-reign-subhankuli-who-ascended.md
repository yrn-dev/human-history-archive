# Abdul-Gazi Bahadur Khan

## Overview and Reign
Abdul-Gazi Bahadur Khan (also referred to as Abulghazi Bahadur) was a descendant of Zingis who reigned over the Usbeks of Charasm, also known as Carizme, from A.D. 1644 to 1663. He is described as the Khan of Khowaresm.

## Literary Contributions
Abdul-Gazi Bahadur Khan is noted for authoring a genealogical history of the Tartars (the *Histoire Généalogique des Tatars*). This work is considered of significant value regarding the manners, pedigrees, and names of his nation. The history is divided into nine parts:
*   **Part I:** Traces descent from Adam to Mogul Khan.
*   **Part II:** Traces descent from Mogul to Zingis.
*   **Part III:** Details the life of Zingis.
*   **Parts IV through VII:** Provides a general history of the four sons of Zingis and their descendants.
*   **Parts VIII and IX:** Focuses on the particular history of the descendants of Sheibani Khan, who reigned in Charasm and Maurenahar.

The work was translated into French by Swedish prisoners in Siberia from the original Mogul manuscript.

## Historical Perspectives and Genealogies
In his writings, Abdul-Gazi Bahadur Khan provides accounts of early lineages. He refers to the "Zalzuts," and his evidence as a Tartar prince and descendant of Zingis is cited in relation to the figures Alancu (or Alankavah) and Oguz Khan.

## Regional Context
The territories associated with Abdul-Gazi Bahadur Khan, such as Charasm and Maurenahar (also known as Ma wara 'l Nahr or Transoxiana), are located in central Asia. This region, known to ancients as Sogdiana, was historically centered around the cities of Samarkand and Bokhara. While Samarkand often served as the seat of government, Bokhara maintained a high reputation as a center of learning and religion. The area was historically influenced by various powers, including the Seleucidae, the Yue-chi under King Kadphises I, the Sassanian dynasty of Persia, and later the conquests of Jenghiz Khan in 1220 and Timur (Tamerlane) in 1369.
