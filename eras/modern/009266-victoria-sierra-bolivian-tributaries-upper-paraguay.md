# Bolivian Tributaries of the Upper Paraguay

The drainage basin of the south-east in Bolivia discharges into the Paraguay river. This basin is smaller and considered less economically important than the basin of the Madeira. Geographically, it extends from the Cordillera Oriental in the west to the Paraguay river in the east, and from the Sierras de Chiquitos in the north down to the Argentine frontier in the south.

## The Pilcomayo River
The Pilcomayo is the only large river in Bolivia within this drainage basin. It originates on the east slopes of the Cordillera Oriental, opposite the south end of Lake Pampa Aullaguas. From its source, the river flows in an easterly and south-easterly direction through the sierra region toward the Bolivian Chaco. 

The Pilcomayo traverses a nearly level landscape. Because the current is so sluggish, its channels are greatly obstructed. While the tributaries of the Pilcomayo in the Chaco are not definitely known, the river has several small tributaries in the sierra region. The largest of these include the Mataca, the Cachimayo, and the Pilaya (also known as the Camblaya); the latter is formed by the confluence of the San Juan and the Cotagaita.

## Other Regional Streams and Tributaries
The Bolivian tributaries of the upper Paraguay are generally characterized as small and unimportant. Notable among them are:

*   **The Otuquis:** This is the most southern group of tributaries. It is formed by the Tucabaca and San Rafael, which drain both slopes of the Cerro Cochii range. The Otuquis is lost in great marshes approximately 50 miles from the Paraguay river.
*   **The Parapiti:** This considerable stream rises on the eastern slopes of the Sierra de Misiones. It flows north-east through a low plain for about 150 miles before it is lost in the great marshy districts of the Bolivian plain.
*   **The Tarija (Rio Grande):** While the Bermejo is an Argentine river, it receives the Tarija as a large tributary from the Bolivian uplands. The Tarija drains a small district located south-east of the Santa Victoria sierra.

## Geography and Drainage
The region is characterized by defective drainage, where streams possess currents too sluggish to effectively carry off water during the rainy season. This results in the formation of several large, shallow lakes that fill slight depressions and change in size and character according to the season. Near the Paraguay river, these include Lake Uberaba, Lake Gaiba, Lake Mandiore, Lake Caceres, and Bahia Negra. Some of these are deep enough for navigation by small craft. Additionally, the region contains the great Xarayes swamps, which are sometimes described as a lake.

Between November and March (and occasionally into May), the summer months bring periodical inundations. During this time, extensive areas of level country are flooded, making traffic possible only via boats.

## Commercial and Transport Links
Due to the loss of its maritime department, Bolivia relies on the ports of the Paraguay and Madeira rivers and their affluents for trade. Because these ports require transhipment in foreign territory, transport costs are increased, and neighboring nations can exclude Bolivia from direct commercial intercourse.

The primary connections to the upper Paraguay include:
*   **Puerto Suarez:** Located on Lake Caceres, this port handles the bulk of Bolivian trade in this direction and is approximately 1,600 miles from Buenos Aires by river.
*   **Puerto Pacheco:** Located on Bahia Negra.
*   **Puerto Quijarro:** A projected port on Lake Gaiba, which is suggested to be more accessible than other ports in the region.

Direct river communication with Montevideo and Buenos Aires would become possible if the trade of southern Bolivia grew sufficient to justify the expense of opening a navigable channel in the Pilcomayo.
