# The Geographical and Demographic Distribution of the British Empire

The British Empire is a vast congeries of states acquired through various methods and held under the supreme headship of the crown. Its structural basis is generally acknowledged as a triple principle of self-defence, self-support, and self-government, though the application of this principle varies across its territories. The home section of the British Isles serves as the residence of the crown and the place of origin of the British race.

## Territorial Extent and Hemispheric Division

The land surface of the earth is estimated to be approximately 52,500,000 square miles, of which the British Empire occupies nearly one-quarter, totaling about 12,000,000 square miles. This territory is divided almost equally between the northern and southern hemispheres. In the northern hemisphere, India (including native states), Canada, and the United Kingdom cover 5,271,375 square miles. In the southern hemisphere, South Africa and Australasia cover 5,308,506 square miles.

The division between the eastern and western hemispheres is less equal. Canada alone occupies 3,653,946 square miles in the western hemisphere. In the eastern hemisphere, the United Kingdom, India, South Africa, and Australasia together occupy 6,925,975 square miles. Because the eastern portions of Australasia border the western hemisphere so closely, the empire experiences a nearly complete distribution of day and night. Similarly, the hemispheric split ensures a complete alternation of seasons, allowing summer produce from one region to supply winter requirements in another.

## Climate and Settlement

A significant portion of the empire's land lies within temperate zones suitable for white settlement. However, there are notable exceptions where this is not the case, including:
*   The southern half of India and Burma.
*   Central, East, and West Africa.
*   The West Indian colonies.
*   New Guinea, British Borneo, and the northern portion of Australia.
*   Portions of North America extending into Arctic regions.

## Population and Demographics

The total population of the empire is estimated at approximately 400,000,000, which represents more than one-fourth of the world's total population (calculated at over 1,500,000,000). 

The white population, which reached over 53,000,000 in 1901, constitutes slightly more than one-eighth of the total population. This group is mainly of Anglo-Saxon race, though it includes Spanish, Dutch, and French peoples. Within the white races, particularly in the United Kingdom, there is a considerable sprinkling of Jewish blood. The white population is distributed as follows:
*   United Kingdom and home dependencies: 41,608,791
*   British North America: 5,500,000
*   Australasia: 4,662,000
*   Africa (British and Dutch): 1,000,000
*   West Indies and Bermuda: 100,000

The native population includes various black, yellow, and brown races. The population of India, described as mainly high-type brown, was 294,191,379 according to the 1901 census. Indian populations are divided into 118 linguistic groups, which can be collected into principal groups including Indo-European (Indo-Aryan sub-family), Dravido-Mu[n.][d.][=a] (Dravidian and Mu[n.][d.][=a]/Kolarian), Indo-Chinese (Tibeto-Burman, Mon-Khmer, and Siamese-Chinese), Malayo-Polynesian, Semitic, Hamitic, and unclassed groups such as Gipsy.

Other native populations include:
*   West Africa: 18,863,000
*   East Africa: 7,425,000
*   South Africa: 5,211,329
*   Ceylon and Eastern Colonies: 5,144,954
*   British Central Africa: 2,000,000
*   West Indies: 1,912,655
*   Australasia and Islands: 824,000
*   Canada (Red Indians): 100,000

## Political Structure and Connectivity

The empire is organized into several tiers of political importance. Central to the system is the United British Kingdom, a "crowned republic" whose ministry acts as the effective supreme government with powers of peace and war. Next are the "crowned republics" of Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, and Newfoundland, which are practically independent, self-governing states in alliance with Great Britain. The Indian Empire is an extension of the Empire of the Great Mogul, reaching from Burma to Beluchistan and including Aden.

The five principal divisions—the United Kingdom, Canada, India, Australia, and South Africa—are separated by the world's three great oceans. This geography necessitated the acquisition of intermediate ports for commercial and military purposes, such as coaling stations. These include Gibraltar, Malta, the Bermudas, Aden, Ceylon, Hong-Kong, the Straits Settlements, and Labuan on the eastern route, as well as St. Helena, Ascension, the Seychelles, the Mauritius, and the Falklands on other routes.
