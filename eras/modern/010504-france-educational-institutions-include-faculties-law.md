# France

## Educational Institutions in Bordeaux
Bordeaux serves as the center of an *academie* (educational division). The city's educational landscape is diverse, featuring faculties of law, science, letters, and medicine and pharmacy. Additionally, there is a faculty of Catholic theology. Other institutions of learning in Bordeaux include lycees, training colleges, a higher school of commerce, a school of fine art, a naval school of medicine, and a chair of agriculture. The city also maintains a library containing over 200,000 volumes, several museums—one of which holds a large collection of sculptures and pictures—and numerous learned societies.

## Educational Institutions in Lyons
Lyons is also the center of an *academie* (educational division). It possesses a university with faculties of medicine and pharmacy, science, letters, and law. The city further hosts Catholic faculties (*facultés libres*) of science, letters, theology, and law. Secondary and vocational education in Lyons includes three *lycées*, training collèges for teachers, and various minor educational establishments.

Lyons is noted for several specialized schools:
* **Fine Arts:** Founded in the 18th century, this school was established to train competent designers for textile manufactures, though it has contributed significantly to sculpture and painting.
* **Veterinary School:** Instituted in 1761, this was the first veterinary school of its kind in Europe and features an admirably equipped laboratory for the study of comparative physiology.
* **Other Specialized Schools:** The city hosts schools for agriculture, music, weaving, tanning, watch-making, applied chemistry, drawing, and commerce (*école supérieure de commerce*). It also includes an army medical school and the écoles La Martiniere, which provide free instruction in art and science as applied to industry.

Intellectual life in Lyons is supported by the *Académie des Sciences, Belles Lettres et Arts*, founded in 1700, as well as societies dedicated to horticulture, geography, natural history, and agriculture.

## Professional Training in Dentistry
In France, the professional training of dentists is based on the conception that dentistry is a department of general medicine. The country possesses two institutions devoted to this instruction.

## Academic and Historical Figures
The French historian Antoine Elisabeth Cleophas Dareste de la Chavanne (1820–1882) was born in Paris to a family from Lyons. He received his education at the Ecole des Chartes. His academic career included serving as a professor in the faculty of letters at Grenoble starting in 1844, and later at Lyons in 1849, where he remained for nearly thirty years. He is known for authoring several historical works, including a general *Histoire de France* (1865–1873), a *Histoire de la Restauration* (1880), and a *Histoire du Gouvernement de Juillet*. He also wrote histories regarding the administration in France since Philippe-Auguste and the agricultural classes in France from the time of saint Louis to Louis XVI.

## Administrative and Legal Infrastructure
In addition to educational centers, French cities such as Bordeaux and Lyons serve as significant administrative hubs. Both cities are seats of an archbishop and house a court of appeal and a court of assizes. Bordeaux is the headquarters of the XVIII. army corps and contains tribunals of commerce and first instance, a chamber of commerce, a council of trade-arbitrators, and a branch of the Bank of France. Lyons is the headquarters of the XIV. army-corps and the seat of a prefect. The archbishop of Lyons holds the title of archbishop of Vienne and primate of the Gauls. Lyons also maintains tribunals of first instance and commerce, as well as two boards of trade arbitration (*conseils de prud'hommes*).
