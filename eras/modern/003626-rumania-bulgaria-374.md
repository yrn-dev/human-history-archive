# Rumania and Bulgaria

## Geography and Demographics
The Danube River serves as a primary geographical marker between the two nations, separating Rumania from Bulgaria for a distance of 284 miles from its junction with the Timok down to Silistria. The Bulgarian bank of the river is characterized by low heights featuring the towns of Silistria, Rustchuk, Palanka, and Vidin.

Bulgaria's total area is 37,240 square miles, comprising northern Bulgaria (24,535 sq. m.) and Eastern Rumelia (12,705 sq. m.). In terms of size, Bulgaria ranks between Portugal and Rumania. As of a census on January 12, 1906, the population of united Bulgaria was 4,028,239, with 2,853,704 in the north and 1,174,535 in Eastern Rumelia. Population records show a steady increase from 3,154,375 in 1888 to 3,744,283 in 1901. In 1909, the population of the Dobrudja region under Rumanian control was 258,242.

## Territorial Disputes and the Dobrudja
The Dobrudja peninsula has been a point of contention between the two nations. Following the Treaty of San Stefano on March 3, 1878, the Dobrudja was reserved as compensation for Rumania due to the Russian annexation of Bessarabia. This transfer was later sanctioned by the Treaty of Berlin on July 13, 1878.

During the First World War, the Dobrudja became a central objective for Bulgaria. In 1913, the southern Dobrudja had been annexed by Rumania. When Bulgaria entered the war on the Teutonic side in 1915, the Germans promised the reclamation of the southern Dobrudja. Following Rumania's intervention on the Allied side in the autumn of 1916, Bulgarian troops defeated the Rumanians and overran the entire Dobrudja as far as the mouth of the Danube, eventually parading through the streets of Bukharest.

However, Germany later restricted Bulgaria's gains. Because the Dobrudja controlled the mouth of the Danube and the main railroad trunk-line from Central Europe to the Black Sea, Germany refused to place the entire peninsula in Bulgarian hands. Bulgaria was granted only the southern Dobrudja, while the remainder was held "in common." When Rumania made peace with the Central powers in the spring of 1918, the Dobrudja prize was ceded to the Central powers rather than to Bulgaria.

## Bulgaria in the First World War
Bulgaria joined the Teutonic alliance in the autumn of 1915 with the support of the majority of its people. The alliance offered several strategic advantages: the possession of Serbian Macedonia, the destruction of Serbia, and the prospect of reclaiming populations in Greek Macedonia and the southern Dobrudja. Additionally, the German government arranged for Turkey to cede the Demotika district of Thrace west of the Maritza River, providing Bulgaria with a railroad connection to Dedeagatch, its only practicable outlet on the Aegean Sea.

By the end of 1915, Serbia was crushed and Serbian Macedonia was under Bulgarian control. In the spring of 1916, Bulgaria occupied the Kavala, Serres, and Drama districts of Greek Macedonia. Bulgaria remained a loyal member of Mitteleuropa through the close of 1916.

The eventual capitulation of Bulgaria had significant implications for the region. Under the terms of its armistice, Bulgaria agreed to allow the Allies free passage and full use of its railways. This allowed Allied forces to move through Bulgaria into Turkish Thrace, threatening Constantinople and sealing the doom of Turkey.
