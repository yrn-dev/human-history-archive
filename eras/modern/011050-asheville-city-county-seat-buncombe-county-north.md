# ASHEVILLE

Asheville is a city in the United States and serves as the county-seat of Buncombe county, North Carolina. It is located in the western part of the state within the mountainous Blue Ridge region, situated approximately 210 miles west of Raleigh.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city is positioned at an elevation of about 2,300 feet above sea level on a high terrace on the east bank of the French Broad river, specifically at the mouth of the Swannanoa. Asheville serves as a junction for three branches of the Southern railway. The surrounding area is characterized by beautiful scenery and great forests.

## Demographics and Tourism
Asheville is recognized as one of the most popular pleasure and health resorts in the south, functioning as a winter resort for those from the north and a summer resort for southerners, aided by an equable and dry climate. 

Population records indicate steady growth at the turn of the century:
* 1890: 10,235
* 1900: 14,692 (of whom 4,724 were negroes)
* 1910: 18,762 (census)

The city's attractiveness is enhanced by an extensive system of suburban and city parks linked by a series of beautiful drives. Social life is supported by several clubs, including the Swannanoa Country club, the Tahkeeostee, the Elks, the Asheville, and the Albemarle.

## Public Buildings and Education
The city's public infrastructure includes an auditorium, a public library, a Federal building, the court house, and the city hall. 

Educational institutions in or near the city include:
* **The Bingham school**: Founded in 1793 at Pittsboro, N.C., by William Bingham (who died in 1826), it was moved to a site 3 miles north-west of Asheville in 1891.
* **The Asheville school for boys**: Located 5 miles west of the city.
* **The Asheville farm school for boys**: An industrial school for negroes.
* **Presbyterian Institutions**: Under the control of the Woman's Board of Home Missions of the Presbyterian Church, the city hosts a home industrial school for girls (established 1887) and a collegiate and normal institute for young women (established 1892), both of which occupy the same campus.

## The Biltmore Estate
Located approximately 2 miles south-east of Asheville is Biltmore, the estate of George W. Vanderbilt. Spanning 125,000 acres, it is considered likely the finest country place in the United States. The estate's central feature is a French Renaissance design chateau, measuring 375 by 150 feet, modeled after the famous chateau at Blois, France. 

Mr. Vanderbilt also maintains a model village in the neighborhood, which includes a church, a hospital, an industrial school for whites, and an elementary school. Both the village and the chateau were designed by Richard M. Hunt.
