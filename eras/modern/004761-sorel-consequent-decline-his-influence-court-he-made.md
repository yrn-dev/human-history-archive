# Agnes Sorel and the Influence of Pierre de Brézé

## Rise to Power
Pierre de Brézé was a member of a noble Angevin family and served as a trusted statesman and soldier for Charles VII. He first established his reputation during the English wars, specifically in 1433 when he collaborated with the constable Richmond, others, and Yolande, the queen of Sicily, to remove Charles VII's minister, La Trémoille, from power. Following this, Brézé was knighted by Charles of Anjou in 1434 and subsequently joined the royal council.

Brézé held several significant administrative roles, serving as the seneschal of Anjou in 1437 and the seneschal of Poitou in 1440. He also provided substantial service to the royal cause during the Praguerie, fighting against the revolted nobles and the dauphin Louis. While this service was initially valued, it was later remembered against him after Louis ascended to the throne.

## The Influence of Agnes Sorel
In 1443, Pierre de Brézé became the chamberlain to Charles VII. During this period, he attained the primary power within the state. This ascendancy was achieved through the influence of Agnes Sorel, which allowed Brézé to supersede his former allies, Charles of Anjou and Richmond. The period of Brézé's dominance, spanning from 1444 to 1450, is noted as the most prosperous era of the reign of Charles VII.

## Opposition and Decline
The most dangerous opponent to Brézé's power was the dauphin Louis. In 1448, Louis brought accusations against Brézé, leading to a formal trial. However, the trial resulted in Brézé's complete exoneration and his restoration to royal favor.

The turning point in Brézé's courtly influence occurred with the death of Agnes Sorel. Following her death, Brézé experienced a consequent decline in his influence at court. Despite this loss of power, he continued his military service, fighting in Normandy between 1450 and 1451, and eventually became the seneschal of that province.

## Later Career and Death
In 1457, Brézé led a descent on the English coast at Sandwich, though the effort proved ineffective. He later prepared an expedition to support Margaret of Anjou, but the accession of Louis XI resulted in his disgrace and a brief period of imprisonment.

Brézé's relationship with the crown shifted again in 1462 when his son, Jacques, married Charlotte de Valois, who was the daughter of Agnes Sorel and the half-sister of Louis XI. In the same year, Brézé accompanied Margaret to Scotland with a force of 2,000 men. After the battle of Hexham, he escorted her back to Flanders. Upon his return, he was reappointed as the seneschal of Normandy. Pierre de Brézé died on July 16, 1465, during the battle of Montlhéry. He was succeeded as seneschal of Normandy by his eldest son, Jacques de Brézé, the count of Maulevrier.
