# Ibrahim Khayru'llah and the English Baha'i Literature

## Overview of English Works for American Converts
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, several works were composed in English specifically for converts in America. Among the most significant of these is the book *Baha'u'llah* (The Glory of God), published in Chicago in 1900. This work was written by Ibrahim Khayru'llah, who was assisted in its production by Howard MacNutt. 

Following the publication of *Baha'u'llah*, Ibrahim Khayru'llah authored additional texts, including *Facts for Bahaists*, published in 1901, and a work titled *The Three Questions*, for which no date is provided in the archive.

## Context within Baha'i Literature
The literature of the sect is described as being very voluminous, though it exists mostly in manuscript form. Major public collections of these manuscripts in Europe are located in Paris (Bibliotheque Nationale), London (British Museum), and St Petersburg. The Paris collection includes rare manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, such as a history written by Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, a contemporary of the Bab.

Other significant scholarly and historical works regarding the movement include:
*   *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*: A Persian work by 'Abbas Efendi (the son of Baha), which was edited, translated, and annotated by E. G. Browne and published in 1891.
*   *The New History of the Bab*: Published by E. G. Browne in 1893.
*   *Kitab-i-Aqdas*: Identified as the most important of Baha's works, with an edition including a Russian translation and introduction provided by Captain A. G. Toumansky in 1899.
*   *Die Babi's in Persien*: Written by Andreas in 1896.
*   *Za'imu'd-Duwla*: An Arabic history of the Babis written by the Persian Mirza Muhammad Mahdi Khan and printed in Cairo between 1903 and 1904; this work is noted as being well-informed but unsympathetic.

## Other English-Language Contributions
Beyond the works of Ibrahim Khayru'llah, other authors contributed to the English-language literature for American converts. Isabella Brittingham published *The Revelations of Baha'u'llah, in a Sequence of Four Lessons* in 1902. Laura Clifford Burney authored *Some Answered Questions Collected*. Additionally, Myron H. Phelps wrote *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi*, published in New York in 1903, which featured a preface by E. G. Browne.
