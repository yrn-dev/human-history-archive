# First Methodist Episcopal Church and the Synagogues of Baltimore

The city of Baltimore contains several notable religious institutions, among which are the First Methodist Episcopal church and the synagogues of the Oheb Shalom Congregation and the Baltimore Hebrew Congregation.

## The First Methodist Episcopal Church
The First Methodist Episcopal church is listed as one of the prominent churches within the city of Baltimore. It belongs to the Methodist Episcopal Church of America, a denomination characterized by a specific system of episcopacy.

### Governance and Episcopacy
The episcopacy of the Methodist Episcopal Church is fundamentally distinct from that of the Catholic Church, as it denies any special *potestas ordinis* in the degree of bishop. The orders of the bishops in this church are derived from Thomas Coke, a presbyter of the Church of England. In 1784, Coke was ordained by John Wesley, with the assistance of two other presbyters, to serve as the "superintendent" of the Methodist Society in America.

### Historical Development in America
Methodism was introduced into the American colonies three years prior to October 1771. At that time, converts numbered fewer than 300. The movement gained significant momentum through the work of Francis Asbury (1745–1816), an English clergyman who volunteered for missionary work in the colonies in 1771. Within one year of his arrival in Philadelphia, membership in the various congregations more than doubled.

In 1772, John Wesley appointed Asbury as "general assistant" in charge of American operations. Although Thomas Rankin superseded him in 1773, Asbury remained practically in control. During the War of Independence, Methodists faced suspicion of Loyalism due to their refusal to take a prescribed oath, leading many ministers, including Rankin, to return to England. Asbury remained in the colonies despite threats and a period of arrest. He was eventually driven into exile in Delaware for two years by the hostility of Maryland authorities. In 1782, he was reappointed to supervise American Methodist affairs, and in 1784, John Wesley appointed both Asbury and Thomas Coke as "bishops" or superintendents of the church in the United States.

## Related Methodist Institutions
The influence of the Methodist Episcopal tradition extends to various educational institutions in the region and beyond. In Baltimore, this includes Goucher College for women, established in 1888, and Morgan College, which opened in 1876 to provide college education to coloured young people.

Outside of Baltimore, the Methodist Episcopal Church is associated with the Wilmington Conference Academy in Dover, Delaware. In Indiana, the de Pauw University in Greencastle is a Methodist Episcopal institution; it was founded in 1837 as Indiana Asbury University and renamed in 1884. The university has been led by several bishops of the Methodist Episcopal church, including Bishop Thomas Bowman, Bishop Matthew Simpson, and Bishop Edwin Holt Hughes.
