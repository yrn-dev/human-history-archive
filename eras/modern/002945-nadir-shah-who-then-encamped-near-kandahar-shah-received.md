# Nadir Shah

Nadir Shah was a Persian monarch whose reign was marked by extensive military campaigns, the expansion of his authority into neighboring regions, and significant architectural projects. His activities spanned from the heart of Persia to the plains of Hindustan.

## Military Campaigns and Expansion
Nadir Shah advanced from Persia to invade Hindustan, during which time he sacked the city of Delhi. While encamped near Kandahar, he extended his influence into Baluchistan by dispatching several detachments into the province to establish his authority. 

During his time in Baluchistan, Nadir Shah initially ordered that Abdulla Khan continue in the government of the country. However, following the death of Abdulla Khan in a battle against the nawabs of Sind, the government passed to Abdulla's eldest son, Haji Mahommed Khan. Haji Mahommed Khan's rule was characterized by oppressive taxation and a tyrannical and licentious lifestyle, which alienated the population. This led Nasir Khan, the second son of Abdulla Khan, to return to Kalat. Nasir Khan had previously acquired the confidence and favor of Nadir Shah while accompanying the monarch to Delhi. After Nasir Khan killed his brother and took power, he reported the events to Nadir Shah, who was then encamped near Kandahar. Nadir Shah received this news with satisfaction and issued a firman in 1739 appointing Nasir Khan as the *beglar begi* (prince of princes) of all Baluchistan.

## Architectural Works and Patronage
Nadir Shah was involved in the reconstruction of palaces in Persia. In the province of Mazandaran, in the town of Ashref, he rebuilt the Chehel Situn (forty pillars) palace in 1731. This palace had previously been destroyed by the Afghans in 1723, though by 1743 it was already in ruins again. Additionally, Nadir Shah is noted among the Persian tyrants who enriched the tomb of Ali with spoils taken from the people.

## Assassination and Death
Nadir Shah died in 1747. His death was the result of a conspiracy involving members of his own tribe and his inner circle. On the night of June 8, 1747 (11th of Jemady ul Sany, A.H. 1160), near the city of Khojoon—located three days' journey from Meshed—Mohammed Kuly Khan Ardemee and the *Kushukchee Bashee* (captain of the guard), along with seventy members of the *Kukshek* or guard, swore an oath to assassinate the shah.

## Legacy and Aftermath
Following the death of Nadir Shah, the political landscape of his former territories shifted. Nasir Khan of Kalat acknowledged the title of Ahmad Shah (Durani), the king of Kabul, in 1747. Later, in the mid-18th century, Mulla 'Ali Shah, who had served as an admiral under Nadir Shah, established himself as an independent chief on part of Kishm and the island of Hormuz.
