# Marianne Elisa Bonaparte

Marianne Elisa (1777–1820) was a member of the Bonaparte family and the sister of Napoleon. Born in Ajaccio on January 3, 1777, she was educated at the establishment of St Cyr near Paris, where she entered as a "king's scholar" through the efforts of her brothers. Following the disruption of this establishment by revolutionists in 1792, Napoleon took charge of her and returned her to Ajaccio.

## Marriage and Early Life
Elisa shared the fortunes of her family in the south of France during the revolutionary period. On May 5, 1797, she married Felix Bacciochi, a Corsican who was well-connected.

## Political Appointments and Administration
Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted Elisa the principality of Piombino and, shortly thereafter, Lucca. In 1808, through her own importunities, she secured the grand duchy of Tuscany. 

During her tenure in these regions, Elisa exerted a significant influence on Italian affairs in general and on the administration of her territories. This influence was attributed to her ability and pride, particularly as her husband, Bacciochi, was considered almost a nullity.

## Relations with Napoleon and Later Years
Elisa's relationship with her brother was frequently strained. Between 1813 and 1814, she abetted the enterprises of Murat. After the fall of Napoleon, she retired from public life under the title of countess of Compignano. 

She first resided in Bologna and later moved to Santo Andrea near Trieste. During her final years, she lived at Trieste with her sister, Maria Annunciata Caroline. Marianne Elisa died on August 6, 1820.
