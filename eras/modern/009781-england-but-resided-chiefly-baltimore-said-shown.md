# Baltimore

Baltimore is a seaport city and the metropolis of Maryland, U.S.A. It is situated at the head of tide-water on the middle and north-west branches of the Patapsco river, where they form an estuary 12 miles from the entrance into Chesapeake Bay. Geographically, it is located at latitude 39° 17' N and longitude 76° 37' W. The city is positioned approximately 172 miles by water from the Atlantic Ocean, 40 miles by rail northwest of Washington, 26 miles northwest of Annapolis, 97 miles southwest of Philadelphia, and 184 miles from New York.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Baltimore has shown steady growth over the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1890, the population was 434,439; by 1900, it grew to 508,957, and by the 1910 census, it reached 558,485. The 1900 census recorded that 79,258 residents were negroes and 68,600 were foreign-born. Among the foreign-born population, the largest groups were natives of Germany (33,208), Russia (10,493), Ireland (9,690), England (2,841), Poland (2,811), Bohemia (2,321), and Italy (2,042).

## City Geography and Layout
The city is divided by Jones's Falls. The area to the east is known as East Baltimore, which is further divided into Old Town to the northwest and Fells' Point to the south and east; the latter is a manufacturing and shipbuilding quarter. Old Town retains some eighteenth-century architecture, including old-fashioned post-houses that once served travelers using the old Cumberland Road and a turnpike leading to Fort Cumberland.

The western side of Jones's Falls is considered the more inviting portion of the modern city, with the principal residential districts located in the northern half. The city's central intersection is formed by Baltimore Street, which runs east and west, and Charles Street, which runs north and south. Baltimore Street serves as the chief business thoroughfare, with the shipping, financial, and wholesale districts located slightly to its north and south. Retail stores are concentrated on West Lexington Street, North Howard and North Eutaw Streets (between Fayette and Franklin Streets), and North Gay Street in East Baltimore. North Charles Street is lined with imposing residences of wealthy citizens. Wide streets featuring fountains, lawns, and flower-beds include Broadway, Eutaw Place, Washington Place, and Mount Vernon Place.

## Infrastructure and Education
Baltimore is a significant transportation hub served by several railways, including the Baltimore & Ohio, the Northern Central, the Western Maryland, the Maryland & Pennsylvania, the Baltimore & Annapolis Short Line, the Baltimore, Chesapeake & Atlantic, and the Philadelphia, Baltimore & Washington (Pennsylvania system). The city also maintains steamship lines connecting to Brazil, the West Indies, and various European ports, including London, Liverpool, Havre, Rotterdam, Bremen, Hamburg, Glasgow, Dublin, and others.

The city is a prominent educational center. Leading institutions include:
* **Higher Education:** Johns Hopkins University (a leader in graduate study), Goucher College (a Methodist institution for women), and Loyola College (for Catholic training).
* **Professional Schools:** The University of Maryland and Baltimore University (offering law, medicine, and dentistry), the Baltimore Medical College, the College of Physicians and Surgeons, the Woman's Medical College, the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, and the Baltimore Law School.
* **Specialized Institutions:** The Maryland College of Pharmacy, the Polytechnic Institute, and St. Mary's Seminary (established in 1791 by the Society of St Sulpice).
* **Other Education:** Morgan College (Methodist) for coloured young people, the state normal school opened in 1867, and the McDonogh farm school, which prepares poor boys for college.

The city's libraries include the Maryland Historical Society, the Bar Association, the Enoch Pratt, the New Mercantile, the Maryland Diocesan, and those of the Peabody Institute and Johns Hopkins University.

## Parks and Public Spaces
As of 1908, the department of public parks and squares had jurisdiction over 33 squares and 13 parks of 10 acres or more. The total area comprised 2,188 acres of parks and 86.53 acres of squares. Notable sites include Druid Hill Park (672.78 acres), Clifton Park (311.26 acres, formerly part of the Johns Hopkins estate), and Patterson Park (125.79 acres), which is a popular resort for East Baltimore residents.

## Notable Residents and Associations
The city has been home to various figures of historical note. George Peabody, founder of the Peabody Institute (1859), was a resident for several years. Frederick Douglass, the orator and journalist, lived in Baltimore in the family of Hugh Auld and was later apprenticed as a ship caulker before escaping to New York in 1838.

The city also has ties to the Bonaparte family. Jerome Bonaparte married Elizabeth Patterson, daughter of a Baltimore merchant, on December 24, 1803. Although the marriage was disliked by Napoleon, it was valid under American law. Their son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870), was born in England but resided primarily in Baltimore. His own son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832-1893), served in the French army in Italy and the Crimea.

Additionally, Baltimore served as the site of a Democratic National Convention where August Belmont, a banker and financier, joined those who withdrew from the Charleston convention and was subsequently chosen chairman of the National Democratic Committee.
