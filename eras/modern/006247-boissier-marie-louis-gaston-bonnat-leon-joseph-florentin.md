# BOISSIER, MARIE LOUIS GASTON AND BONNAT, LEON JOSEPH FLORENTIN

## Marie Louis Gaston Boissier
Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier (1823–1908) was a French classical scholar and the secretary of the French Academy. Born in Nimes on August 15, 1823, Boissier was drawn to the study of ancient history from an early age by the Roman monuments found in his native town. He focused his academic efforts on epigraphy.

At twenty-three years of age, Boissier began his professional career as a professor of rhetoric in Angouleme, a position he held for ten years. After a university travelling inspector heard him lecture, he was appointed as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne in Paris. His early literary contributions included a thesis on the poet Attius, published in 1857, and a 1861 study regarding the life and work of M. Terentius Varro.

In 1861, Boissier was appointed professor of Latin oratory at the College de France and became an active contributor to the *Revue des deux mondes*. He gained significant success with the publication of *Ciceron et ses amis* in 1865 (translated into English by A.D. Jones in 1897), a work noted for its erudition and Boissier's ability to reproduce the characteristics and society of ancient Rome with vivacity. 

His later major works include the two-volume *La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins* (1874), which analyzed the religious movements of antiquity prior to the acceptance of Christianity, and *L'Opposition sous les Cesars* (1875), which depicted the political decadence of Rome under the early successors of Augustus. Boissier was elected to the French Academy in 1876 following the death of H.J.G. Patin and was later appointed its perpetual secretary in 1895.

## Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat
Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat (1833– ) was a prominent French painter born in Bayonne on June 20, 1833. He received his education in Spain under Madrazo in Madrid. This training is evident in his extensive series of portraits, which reflect the influence of Spanish realists and Velasquez.

Bonnat established himself as a leading artist of his era in Paris, winning a medal of honour in 1869. His professional career in art education and administration included becoming a professor of painting at the Ecole des Beaux Arts in 1888. He eventually succeeded Paul Dubois as the director of the institution in May 1905.

While Bonnat is most characterized by his vivid portrait-painting, he is also recognized for his subject pictures. One of his most famous works is the "Martyrdom of St Denis," located in the Pantheon. Additionally, Bonnat served as a teacher to other artists; for example, the American landscape painter John Joseph Enneking studied under Bonnat in Paris during the periods of 1873–1876 and 1878–1879.
