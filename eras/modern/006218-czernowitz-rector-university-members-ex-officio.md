# Czernowitz and University Rectors in the Austrian Context

## Geography and Administration of Bukovina
Bukovina is a crownland and duchy of Austria. It is bordered by Russia and Rumania to the east and south, Galicia to the north, and Hungary and Transylvania to the west. The region covers an area of 4,035 square miles. Its landscape is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathians, particularly in the southern regions, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. The primary passes in the region are the Borgo Pass and the Radna Pass.

The watershed of the Danube encompasses Bukovina, with the exception of the Dniester river, which runs along the northern border. Other significant rivers include the Pruth and the Sereth, the latter of which is joined by the Bistritza, Moldava, and Suczawa affluents. The climate is described as healthy but severe, particularly during winter, though it remains generally milder than the climate of Galicia. In Czernowitz, the mean annual temperature is 46.9° F.

## Land Use and Economy of Bukovina
The name Bukovina is derived from the abundance of beech trees, which is reflected in the fact that woodland occupies 43.17% of the total area. The remaining land is distributed among arable land (27.59%), meadows (12.68%), pastures (10.09%), and gardens (0.78%). Due to fertile soil, agriculture has progressed significantly, producing crops such as barley, rye, maize, wheat, hemp, flax, potatoes, and oats. Cattle-rearing is another primary source of revenue.

The government owns the mine at Kaczyka, where salt—the principal mineral of the region—is extracted. The chief industries are milling, distilling, and brewing. Commerce is primarily managed by Armenians and Jews and is largely limited to raw products, including wood, cattle, wool, and agricultural produce.

## Demographics and Culture of Bukovina
As of 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is ethnically diverse: over 40% are Ruthenians, 35% are Rumanians, and 13% are Jews. The remaining population consists of Russians, Armenians, Poles, Hungarians, and Germans.

German serves as the official language for instruction in the university, the law-courts, and the administration. Religiously, nearly 70% of the population are members of the Greek Orthodox Church and fall under the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Other religious groups include Roman Catholics (11%), the Greek United Church (3.25%), and Protestants (2.5%).

## Austrian Governance and the Role of Rectors
Under the governance of the house of Habsburg, territories not included in the Hungarian crown after the settlement of 1867 were managed as separate political units. Following the February Constitution of 1861, a parliament known as the *Reichsrath* was established for the empire, consisting of a Chamber of Deputies and a House of Lords (*Herrenhaus*). The House of Lords included prince bishops, archbishops, members of the imperial family, and other hereditary or life-appointed members.

Local governance was conducted through *Landtage* or diets. These diets were generally elected every six years. A specific feature of this system was that certain individuals sat in the diets by virtue of their office (*ex officio*), specifically a certain number of rectors of universities and bishops.

In the province of Bohemia, this system is exemplified by the local diet, which consists of 242 members. The members who belong *ex officio* to the Bohemian diet include the three bishops, the archbishop, and the two rectors of the universities.
