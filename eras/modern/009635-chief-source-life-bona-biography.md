# Bona

## Biographical Sources
The primary source for the life of Bona is a biography written by the Cistercian abbot Bertolotti, published in Asti in 1677. In terms of modern scholarship, the most distinguished study on the subject was produced by A. Ighina in 1874, published in Mondovi.

## Geography and Administration
Bona (also referred to as Bone) is a seaport located in Algeria at 36 deg. 53' N. and 7 deg. 46' E. It is situated on a bay of the Mediterranean Sea at the base of the wooded heights of Edugh. Administratively, it serves as the chief town of an arrondissement within the department of Constantine. Its location is 136 miles northeast of Constantine and 220 miles west of Tunis by rail.

## Urban Development and Architecture
The town's layout features a modern rampart constructed outside an older Arab wall, which had become too small to accommodate the city's growth. While much of the old town has been demolished, the current city is a flourishing French center characterized by wide, though sometimes steep, streets.

The center of the town is defined by the Cours Jerome-Bertagna (formerly known as the Cours National), a broad promenade lined with trees. This area houses the city's principal buildings, including hotels, banks, and a theatre. Notable landmarks include:
* **The Cathedral of St Augustine:** A large church built in a quasi-Byzantine style, located at the northern end of the promenade. It houses a relic believed to be the right arm of St Augustine, which was brought from Pavia in 1842.
* **The Grand Mosque:** Situated on one side of the Place d'Armes, this structure was built using ruins from the ancient Hippo.
* **The Kasbah:** A citadel located on a hill to the northeast of the town.
* **Public Infrastructure:** The town contains civil and military hospitals, as well as barracks capable of accommodating 3,000 men.

## Maritime and Commercial Activity
Bona is a major center of commerce, ranking only behind Oran and Algiers in Algeria. It maintains direct steamship communication with Marseilles. The port consists of an inner harbour covering 25 acres with fine quays capable of mooring vessels drawing 22 feet. An outer harbour was constructed between 1857 and 1868, with further enlargements occurring between 1905 and 1907.

The city's economy is based on the following trade:
* **Imports:** Manufactures and general merchandise.
* **Exports:** Iron, zinc, phosphates, wool, sheep, barley, cork, and esparto.
* **Local Industry:** The town hosts manufactories producing leather, tapestry, and native garments.

## Environmental and Natural Resources
To improve sanitary conditions and provide an abundant water supply, a system of canals was used to drain the marshes at the mouths of the Bujema and Seybuse rivers, which enter the sea to the south of the town. The water supply is sourced from the Edugh hills. The surrounding region is noted for its marble quarries and cork woods.
