## Legal Interpretations of the Crime Victims' Rights Act
In 2010, the Department of Justice undertook efforts to update its 2005 Guidelines. As part of this process, the Office of the Deputy Attorney General convened a Victim of Crimes Working Group. This group requested that the Office of Legal Counsel (OLC) revisit a 2005 preliminary review regarding the definition of a "crime victim" under the Crime Victims' Rights Act of 2004 (CVRA). The OLC solicited input on this matter from federal law enforcement agencies and Department components.

On December 17, 2010, the OLC issued an opinion titled "The Availability of Crime Victims' Rights Under the Crime Victims' Rights Act of 2004." Based on memoranda opinions from Department components, relevant case law, and the language of the CVRA, the OLC reaffirmed its 2005 conclusion. The opinion stated that CVRA rights do not vest until a criminal charge has been filed via indictment, information, or complaint. Furthermore, these rights cease to be available if the government declines to bring formal charges after a complaint is filed, or if all charges are dismissed on the merits or voluntarily. The OLC noted that while the Department may not afford CVRA rights to victims before charges are filed, it may still provide certain services to victims that serve a similar function to some CVRA rights.
