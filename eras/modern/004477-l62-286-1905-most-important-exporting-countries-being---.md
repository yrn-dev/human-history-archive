# Foreign Willow and Basket Trade (1900–1905)

The trade of willows and basket-ware involves the import and export of raw materials and finished goods, with the United Kingdom maintaining detailed Board of Trade returns on these commodities starting in 1900.

## Willow Imports and Exporting Countries
Between 1900 and 1905, the import values of foreign willows saw a steady increase, rising from £52,219 in 1900 to £62,286 in 1905. During this period, three countries emerged as the most important exporters of willows:

*   **Germany:** This country saw the most significant growth, with export values increasing from £22,594 in 1900 to £34,752 in 1905, a gain of £12,158.
*   **Holland:** Exports from Holland rose from £9,771 in 1900 to £12,750 in 1905, an increase of £2,979.
*   **Belgium:** Conversely, Belgian exports declined from £18,800 in 1900 to £11,864 in 1905, a decrease of £6,936.

Additionally, the United Kingdom engaged in small-scale re-exports of willows to British possessions and other foreign parts. These re-exports were valued at £1,808 in 1900 and decreased to £371 by 1905.

## Basket-ware Trade and Supply Sources
The import of basket-ware from foreign countries was valued at £239,402 in 1900. This figure increased to £264,183 in 1901 before declining to £227,070 by 1905. 

A comparison of the main sources of supply between 1900 and 1905 reveals shifting trade patterns:
*   **Belgium:** Remained a primary source, with imports increasing from £72,031 to £77,766 (+£5,735).
*   **Japan:** Showed a remarkable increase in supply, rising from £8,140 in 1900 to £25,536 in 1905. (For 1904, the value was £52,377).
*   **Holland:** Experienced a decrease from £58,214 to £54,407 (-£3,807).
*   **France:** Showed a remarkable decrease, falling from £55,870 in 1900 to £27,910 in 1905 (-£27,960).
*   **Germany:** Imports declined from £33,155 to £22,892 (-£10,263).
*   **Portugal:** Imports decreased from £5,066 to £3,971 (-£1,095).

British re-exports of baskets to foreign parts and British possessions also occurred, with values rising from £3,785 in 1900 to £6,633 in 1905.

## Manufacturing Centers
Outside of London, the chief centers for English basket manufacture are located in Grantham, Basford (near Nottingham), and Thurmaston (near Leicester). 

International production is also significant. Great quantities of fancy and other basket-work are produced in the Netherlands, Belgium, and Germany—specifically in the Black Forest, at Sonnefeld in Saxony, and at Lichtenfels in Bavaria. France also produces light basket-work for the English market, notably in other parts of the Aisne department and in Verdun, though these quantities have been decreasing.
