# An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mang'anja Language

## Overview
*An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mang'anja Language, as spoken in British Central Africa* is a linguistic work authored by the Rev. D.C. Scott. Published in 1891, the text serves as a comprehensive resource for the Mang'anja language (also referred to in some sources as Mananja) as it was utilized within the region of British Central Africa.

## Scholarly Significance
The work is characterized as a "mine of information" for students and researchers of African languages. Within the broader context of Bantu speech and South African languages, it is categorized alongside other admirable linguistic contributions of the late 19th century, such as the *Dictionary of the Congo Language* by the Rev. Holman Bentley (1891).

## Context within Regional Bibliography
The dictionary is part of a larger body of literature concerning the geography, people, and languages of British Central Africa and the Zambezi region. It is cited alongside various other authoritative sources on the area, including:
* The writings of David and Charles Livingstone, specifically the *Narrative of an Expedition to the Zambezi* (1865) and the *Last Journals of David Livingstone* (1874).
* Works by Sir H.H. Johnston, including *British Central Africa* (2nd ed., 1898).
* Contributions by Miss A. Werner, such as *The Natives of British Central Africa* (1906), as well as other linguistic works by Werner, the Rev. Govan Robertson, Dr. R. Laws, A.C. Madan, Father Torrend, and Monsieur E. Jacottet.
* Other regional accounts by John Buchanan (*The Shiré Highlands*, 1885), Lionel Décle (*Three Years in Savage Africa*, 1898), and H.L. Duff (*Nyasaland under the Foreign Office*, 1903).
