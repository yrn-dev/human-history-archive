# BRITISH EAST AFRICA

## Definition and Scope
In its widest sense, British East Africa is a term used to describe all territory under British influence on the eastern side of Africa. Geographically, this region is situated between Abyssinia and the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan to the north, and German East Africa to the south. This broad designation comprises three protectorates: East Africa, Uganda, and Zanzibar.

## Geography and Topography
The region is characterized by a narrow belt of coastland, while the remaining continental area consists almost entirely of the great plateau of East Africa. Elevations in this area rarely fall below 2,000 feet, with extensive sections reaching heights between 6,000 and 8,000 feet. 

The terrain rises from the coastal lowlands in a series of steps and intervening plateaus, leading to a broad zone of high ground that shows abundant traces of volcanic action. This upland is furrowed by the eastern "rift-valley," which was formed by the subsidence of its floor and contains lakes that have no outlet. To the west, there is a basin of lower elevation partially occupied by Victoria Nyanza, which drains north toward the Nile. Further inland, the ground rises again to a second volcanic belt, which culminates in the Ruwenzori range.

## Linguistic Groups
The Bantu dialects of British East Africa are categorized into several distinct groups:

*   **The Kikuyu-Kamba group:** Located east of the Rift valley, this group includes the dialects of Ukambani and Kikuyu, as well as scattered fragments of Bantu speech found on the upper Tana river (Dhaicho) and Mount Kenya.
*   **The Kilimanjaro (Chaga-Siha) group:** This group encompasses the peculiar dialects of Mount Meru, Mount Kilimanjaro, and Ugweno.
*   **The Pokomo-Nyika-Giriama-Taveita group:** These represent the Bantu dialects of the coast province, situated between the frontier of German East Africa in the south and the Tana river in the north.

## Historical and Administrative Records
The administration and development of the East Africa protectorate have been documented in various official and academic works. The British government has issued annual reports by the governor detailing the progress of the protectorate since 1896. Other key records include the *Précis of Information* concerning the British East Africa Protectorate (1901), issued by the War Office, and the *Final Report of the Uganda Railway Committee* (1904), which summarizes the construction of the Uganda railway.

Comprehensive accounts of the protectorate's economic resources were provided by Sir Charles Eliot in *The East Africa Protectorate* (1905). Historical perspectives on the region are found in J.W. Gregory's *The Foundation of British East Africa* (1901), while the activities of the Imperial British East Africa Company are detailed in P.L. McDermont's *British East Africa or Ibea* (1895). Additionally, *Drumkey's Year Book for East Africa* was first issued in 1908.

Specialized studies of the region include Joseph Thomson's *Through Masai Land* (1886) and J.W. Gregory's *The Great Rift Valley* (1896) regarding geology. Other geological contributions include H.B. Muff's *Report relating to the Geology of the East Africa Protectorate* (1908) and Hans Meyer's *Across an East African Glacier* (1890). Missionary efforts are summarized in Bishop A.R. Tucker's *Eighteen Years in Uganda and East Africa* (1908), and A. Chapman's *On Safari* (1908) covers ornithology and big game.
