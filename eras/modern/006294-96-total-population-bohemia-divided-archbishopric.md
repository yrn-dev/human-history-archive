# Bohemia

## Population and Demographics
In 1900, Bohemia had a population of 6,318,280, which equated to 315 inhabitants per square mile. In terms of total numbers, it was the second most populous Austrian province, following Galicia; in terms of population density, it ranked third, behind Lower Austria (which includes Vienna) and Silesia. This represented a significant increase from 1800, when the population was slightly over 3,000,000.

The population is divided by nationality into approximately 65% Czechs and 35% Germans. Geographically, Czechs occupy the south, south-east, and middle regions of the country. Germans are primarily concentrated in the west and north near the borders, though they are also found throughout the country in large towns. There are numerous German-speaking enclaves within purely Czech districts, while Czechs have tended to move into manufacturing and mining districts that were purely German. Despite industrial development and rich natural resources, there is a steady flow of emigrants from Bohemia who settle in Russia, Germany, other provinces of the monarchy, or America.

## Religion and Ecclesiastical Administration
The Roman Catholic Church is the faith of 96% of the total population. For administrative purposes, Bohemia is divided into the archbishopric of Prague and three bishoprics: Leitmeritz, Koniggratz, and Budweis.

## Agriculture and Natural Resources
Bohemia is highly developed agriculturally, aided by a thriving rural population and a suitable climate. Over 50% of the total area is under cultivation. The most fertile regions include the "Garden of Bohemia" around Leitmeritz, the "Paradise" around Teplitz, and the "Golden Road" around Koniggratz. 

The primary agricultural products are wheat, barley, rye, and oats, although large areas have been converted to beetroot cultivation due to competition from Hungarian wheat. The staple food for the people is the potato. Other notable products include high-quality flax and hops, for which the Saaz district is celebrated. Plums and other fruits are abundant and serve as major export articles.

While cattle-rearing is less developed than agriculture, bee-cultivation is an important industry, and large flocks of geese are reared, particularly in the south. Bohemian peasants have successfully pursued pisciculture for centuries, and there has been fair success in recent attempts to rear silkworms.

## Political History and Governance
Bohemia is described as the richest part of the Austrian dominions. Historically, a great party led by Rieger and Palacky demanded the restoration of the Bohemian monarchy in its fullest extent—including Silesia and Moravia—and insisted that the emperor be crowned as king of Bohemia at Prague. This movement was supported by all Czechs and several great nobles of German descent, such as Count Heinrich Clam-Martinitz, Count Leo Thun, and Prince Friedrich von Schwarzenberg (the cardinal archbishop of Prague). Conversely, Germans in Bohemia advocated for centralization to avoid falling under Czech control.

In 1871, Count Karl Hohenwart, the Austrian prime minister, attempted to reach an agreement to establish a constitutional system that would satisfy Bohemian claims while maintaining a firm connection to the Habsburg empire. On September 14, 1871, an imperial message to the diet of Prague recognized the rights of the kingdom of Bohemia. However, this attempt failed. By 1872, a government with a strong German tendency took office in Vienna, leading Bohemians to temporarily refuse attendance at the parliamentary assemblies of Prague and Vienna.

In 1879, Count Eduard Taaffe became prime minister and persuaded Bohemian representatives to return to the parliament in Vienna. The Bohemians agreed on the condition that this did not prejudice their view that Bohemia, along with Silesia and Moravia, constituted a separate state under the same sovereign as Hungary and Austria. In return, the government of Count Taaffe removed some of the grossest anomalies in the electoral system, which had previously been heavily biased toward the German minority, and consented to the foundation of a Bohemian university at Prague.

## Industry
Along with Moravia and Lower Austria, Bohemia is in the front rank of Austrian provinces from an industrial point of view.
