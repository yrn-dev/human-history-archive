# The Republic of Colombia and the Conflict with Spanish Forces

## Formation of the Republic of Colombia
Following his return to Angostura, where he was celebrated as the father and deliverer of his country, Bolivar utilized the prevailing momentum to establish a new political entity. On December 17, 1819, a fundamental law was enacted that united the republics of New Granada and Venezuela into a single state. This new entity was titled the Republic of Colombia, with Bolivar serving as its president. To facilitate the administration of this union, the seat of government was provisionally moved to Rosario de Cucuta, located on the frontier of the two provinces.

## Military Campaigns Against Morillo
Bolivar returned to the field leading the best-appointed and most numerous army the republicans had assembled to date. During this period, he secured significant advantages over the Spanish forces commanded by Morillo. Earlier in the conflict, Bolivar had faced Morillo when the latter advanced with a strong division; however, on February 16, 1817, a three-day conflict resulted in the defeat of the royalists. Morillo's subsequent retreat was marked by a further and more complete overthrow at the hands of J.A. Paez and his *llaneros*.

## The Armistice of Truxillo
On November 25, 1820, Bolivar concluded an armistice at Truxillo. This agreement was set for a duration of six months. The armistice was entered into likely in the hope of sparing further effusion of blood and the possibility that the Spaniards would agree to terms. These hopes were not realized, as Morillo was recalled from his position and replaced in command by General Torre. Consequently, the armistice was allowed to expire, making a renewal of the military contest inevitable.

## The Battle of Carabobo and Spanish Defeat
Determined to deliver a decisive blow to the Spanish forces, Bolivar engaged General Torre at Carabobo. The result was a complete rout of the Spaniards. The remnants of the shattered Spanish army were forced to seek refuge in Puerto Cabello. Two years after the battle of Carabobo, these remaining forces surrendered to Paez. This engagement is regarded as the event that ended the war in Venezuela.

## Establishment of Independence
Following the victory at Carabobo, Bolivar entered Caracas on June 29, 1821. By the end of that year, the Spaniards had been driven from every part of the province, with the exception of Puerto Cabello. To secure the independence that had been purchased at a high cost, permanent political institutions were established. On August 30, 1821, the constitution of Colombia was adopted with general approbation. Under this constitutional framework, Bolivar continued as president, and Santander served as vice-president.
