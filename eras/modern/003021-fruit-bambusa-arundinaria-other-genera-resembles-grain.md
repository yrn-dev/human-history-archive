# Fruit and Characteristics of Bamboo

Bamboo plants exhibit a diverse range of fruit types depending on their genus. In the genera *Bambusa* and *Arundinaria*, as well as other genera, the fruit resembles the grain that is generally characteristic of grasses. However, this is not universal across all bamboo; in *Dendrocalamus* and other genera, the fruit is a nut. In rare instances, such as with *Melocanna*, the fruit is fleshy and resembles an apple in both its appearance and size.

## Cultivation and Propagation
A number of bamboo species are hardy enough for cultivation in the British Isles, primarily those native to Japan and China. These include species from the genera *Phyllostachys*, *Bambusa*, and *Arundinaria*, the latter of which includes some species from the Himalayas. 

Propagation of these plants can be achieved through cuttings, division, or seed. However, because the occurrence of fruit is rare, propagation by seed is seldom applicable. To flourish, these "hungry plants" require generous treatment, including rich loam that is not too stiff and thorough mulching for the first two years. They also require protection from winds and consistent watering during their growing period. When transplanting bamboo, it is necessary to disturb the roots as little as possible.

## Notable Species
Several specific species are recognized for their unique characteristics:
*   ***Arundinaria simoni***: A fine plant capable of reaching 18 feet in height; it is noted for flowering and fruiting in Britain.
*   ***Arundinaria japonica***: A tall, handsome plant often grown in gardens under the name *Bambusa metake*.
*   ***Arundinaria nitida***: Described as remarkably hardy and the most attractive and daintiest of its genus.
*   ***Bambusa palmata***: Characterized by leaves that are three inches broad and a foot or more in length.
*   ***Bambusa quadrangularis***: Notable for having square stems.
*   ***Phyllostachys mitis***: A species native to Japan and China that can grow to 60 feet high.
*   ***Phyllostachys nigra***: A handsome species named for its black stem.
*   ***Bambusa vulgaris***: A species that grows to a height of 20 to 50 feet.

## Uses and Products
In Oriental countries, the products and parts of the bamboo plant are applied to almost endless uses. The shoots are cut off when they first begin to spring and are served at the table like asparagus; they are succulent and soft. To keep them fit for consumption longer, they are earthed over, providing a supply throughout the year, though the supply is most abundant in autumn. Additionally, these shoots may be candied and preserved in sugar, salted and eaten with rice, or prepared as pickles.

As the bamboo plant ages, it secretes a fluid in its hollow joints. This fluid gradually develops into a concrete substance known as *tabascheer* or *tabaxir*. While this substance was once highly valued in the East for medicinal qualities and possesses peculiar optical properties, it has been found to be a purely siliceous concretion. In terms of medicinal application, bamboo is entirely inert and has not been included in the European materia medica.
