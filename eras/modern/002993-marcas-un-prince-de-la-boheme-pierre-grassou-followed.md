# Pierre de Marca

Pierre de Marca (1594–1662) was a French historian and prelate. Born on January 24, 1594, at Gan, near Pau, he belonged to a family known within judicial circles during the 16th century. Despite the official introduction of the Reformed religion into Navarre, his family maintained the Roman Catholic faith.

## Early Career and Political Rise
After studying law at the university of Toulouse and practicing in Pau, Marca sought a larger professional sphere. He advocated for the armed intervention of King Louis XIII in Béarn, publishing his first work in 1618, titled *Discours d'un Béarnais, très fidèle sujet du roi, sur l'édit du rétablissement de l'exercice de la religion catholique dans tout le Béarn*. Following the campaign of 1620, Marca supervised the return of possessions to the Roman Catholic church, a task he performed with moderation and judgment.

His activities during the siege of La Rochelle brought him into contact with Richelieu. Consequently, in 1631, he was nominated as *intendant de justice* in Béarn, and by 1639, he was summoned to Paris to serve as a counsellor of state.

## Theological Works and Ecclesiastical Appointments
In 1641, Marca published his celebrated treatise *De concordia sacerdotii et imperii, seu de libertalibus ecclesiae gallicanae* (also referred to as *De libertatibus Ecclesiae Gallicanae*), in which he defended the liberties of the Gallican Church in response to a pamphlet by Charles Hersent. 

Although the king nominated him bishop of Couserans on December 28, 1641, the pope initially refused to sanction the appointment. Marca was only proclaimed in the consistory on January 13, 1648, after he formally denied the propositions in *De concordia* that had displeased Rome.

## Administrative and Historical Contributions
Marca served as the governor of the French-occupied province of Catalonia until 1651. Following the Treaty of the Pyrenees, he directed the conference tasked with fixing the limits of Roussillon.

As an historian, Marca displayed an early interest in the history of his native land. Starting at age twenty-three in 1617, he researched archives, copied charters, and corresponded with scholars such as Jean Besly, André Duchesne, and the Dupuy brothers. His *Histoire de Béarn* was published in Paris in 1640; while less immediately well-received than *De concordia*, it is more highly regarded by posterity, though it remains incomplete as it does not extend beyond the year 1300.

During his time in Catalonia, Marca began preparations for a historical and geographical description of the province. This work was later completed and published in 1688 as *Marca hispanica* by his former secretary, Étienne Baluze, who added appendices to the text.

## Personal Life and Legacy
Marca married Marguerite de Forgues on June 4, 1618. They had three daughters and one son, Galactoire, who served as president of the parlement of Navarre and died on February 10, 1689.

Following his death, two intimate friends wrote biographies of Marca in Latin: his secretary, Étienne Baluze (1663), and his cousin, Paul de Faget (1668). The latter publication included four treatises on the sacrament of the Eucharist, the sacrifice of the Mass, and the erection of the patriarchate of Constantinople. These writings caused scandal and were suspected of containing heretical propositions, leading to a public conflict between Baluze and Faget as they both sought to defend the memory of the prelate.
