# Legal Regulations and Procedures in the United States

## State Laws on Infant Care and Adoption
In most states within the United States, the law is noticeably strict. Specific examples of these regulations can be found in Massachusetts and New York regarding the care of infants. 

In Massachusetts, a law enacted in 1891 requires any person who receives an infant under three years of age for board or for the purpose of procuring adoption to use diligence in determining if the infant is illegitimate. If the person knows or has reason to believe the infant is illegitimate, they must immediately notify the State Board of Charity. The board, including its agents or officers, is authorized to enter and inspect any building where they believe such an illegitimate infant is being boarded. If the board judges that removal is necessary due to abuse, neglect, or other causes to preserve the infant's life, they may remove the child, who then remains in the custody of the Board of Charity for provision according to the law.

In New York, the penal code (s. 288, subsec. 4) mandates that a license for baby-farming must be issued by the board of health of the town or city where the children are kept. Licensed individuals are required to maintain a register. This register must include the names and ages of all children boarded there, as well as children born on the premises. It must also record the residences and names of parents (as far as known), the time of reception and discharge of children and the reasons for such actions. Additionally, a correct register must be kept for every child under five years of age who is indentured, adopted, taken away, or given out, including the residence and name of the person adopting the child.
