# White Papers in United Kingdom Elections

In the United Kingdom, a white paper is utilized as a ballot during elections. This document serves as the primary means for a voter to cast their vote for a specific candidate.

## Ballot Structure and Voting Process
The white paper is printed with the names of the candidates listed in alphabetical order. To cast a vote, the voter fills in the blank space located on the right-hand side opposite the name of their chosen candidate with an "X". 

To maintain the integrity of the election, the presiding officer marks both sides of the paper with an official stamp before it is distributed. This stamp is kept secret and is restricted from being used for any second election for a period of seven years. Additionally, the back of the paper is marked with a number that corresponds to a counterfoil. This counterfoil remains with the officer and is marked with the voter's number from the register, allowing the vote to be identified during a scrutiny. The register itself is marked to indicate that the voter has received their ballot paper.

## Security and Submission
After marking the paper, the voter folds it to conceal the mark while keeping the official stamp visible to the officer. The paper is then deposited into a box that is locked and sealed. The box is specifically constructed to ensure that papers cannot be withdrawn unless it is unlocked.

## Special Provisions for Voters
Certain accommodations are made for voters who cannot mark the paper themselves. The presiding officer will cause the paper to be marked as the voter directs and will keep a record of the transaction in the following instances:
* The voter is incapacitated due to blindness or another physical cause.
* The voter makes a declaration to the officer regarding an inability to read.
* The poll takes place on a Saturday and the voter declares himself a Jew.

## Spoiled and Tendered Papers
If a voter inadvertently spoils their paper, it may be exchanged. The presiding officer is required to preserve these spoiled papers separately. 

In cases where a voter claims to vote after another person has already voted using the same qualification, the voter is given a green paper. This is known as a "tendered" paper; it is not placed in the ballot box but is preserved separately. To receive a tendered paper, the voter must declare their identity and affirm that they have not already voted.

## Polling Station Administration
Voting takes place in public rooms or schoolrooms. Regulations require that a separate compartment be provided for every 150 electors entitled to vote at a station. While agents of candidates are permitted to be present at the polling station, both these agents and the officials are sworn to secrecy regarding who has voted.

At the conclusion of the poll, the presiding officer must provide an account of all entrusted papers to the returning officer. This total is comprised of:
1. Papers contained in the box.
2. Spoiled papers.
3. Unused papers.
4. Tendered papers.
