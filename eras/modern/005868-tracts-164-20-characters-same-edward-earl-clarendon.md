# Tracts, 164 (20) and Characters by Edward, Earl of Clarendon

## Tracts, 164 (20)
The *Thomason Tracts*, specifically volume 164 (20), contains a work titled *A Parallel between Robert Earl of Essex and George late Duke of Buckingham*. This piece was written by Sir Henry Wotton and published in 1641. It serves as one of the various biographical and historical documents used to illustrate the life of the Duke of Buckingham.

## Characters by Edward, Earl of Clarendon
Edward, Earl of Clarendon, authored a work titled *Characters*, published in 1706. This text is listed among the bibliography of sources concerning George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham. Clarendon is noted in the archive as a writer of "subtle discrimination of character and political motive," and his broader work, the *History of the Rebellion* (printed 1702–1704), is described as one of the most vivid "portrait-galleries" and greatest of memoirs.

## Clarendon's Observations on Historical Figures
The archive provides examples of Clarendon's descriptive style and his assessments of contemporary figures:

*   **The Earl of Bristol:** Clarendon describes the Earl of Bristol as a man possessing "incomparable parts" who, despite these abilities, was "none the wiser for any experience or misfortune that befell him." He further records Bristol's "extraordinary facility" in the simultaneous making of friends and enemies. In another description, Clarendon characterizes him as a man of "grave aspect" and a presence that commanded respect, noting his "great parts and ability." However, Clarendon also critiques him as being "passionate and supercilious" and observes that he was "too voluminous a discourser in council."

## Related Biographical Context
The works of Clarendon and the *Thomason Tracts* exist within a wider body of literature documenting the Stuart era. Other related sources include the *Life of Buckingham* by Sir Henry Wotton (1642), the *Life of George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham, &c.* (1740), and the *Historical and Biographical Memoirs of George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham* (1819). These are supplemented by state papers, the *Cal. of State Papers*, and various manuscripts held in the British Museum.
