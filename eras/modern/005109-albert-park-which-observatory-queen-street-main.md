# Brisbane City Layout and Landmarks

## Geography and Urban Structure
Brisbane is the capital of Queensland, Australia, situated in Stanley county. The city is located on the banks of the river Brisbane, approximately 25 miles from where the river meets Moreton Bay. The city's construction is characterized by a series of hills rising from the river-banks, although certain areas, including South Brisbane and Woollongabba, are located on low-lying flats that have occasionally experienced disastrous floods.

The principal buildings and main streets of the city are positioned on a tongue of land created by a southward bend of the river. The extremity of this tongue is open and contains several adjacent sites: the Houses of Parliament, the botanical gardens, the grounds of Government House (the official residence of the colony's governor), and Queen's Park, which serves as a recreation ground.

## Major Thoroughfares and Parks
Two primary streets define the central layout of the city. Albert Street begins at Queen's Park and runs for approximately three-quarters of a mile through the heart of the city. This street leads to Albert Park, which houses the observatory.

Queen's Street serves as the main thoroughfare of Brisbane. It crosses Albert Street at the midway point between Queen's Park and Albert Park. Queen's Street continues across the Victoria Bridge, providing access to the separate city of South Brisbane located on the opposite side of the river. The Victoria Bridge is a steel structure that was built to replace a previous bridge that was swept away during floods in February 1893.

## Architectural Landmarks
Brisbane features numerous buildings of architectural merit, though the narrowness of some streets occasionally mars their effect. Prominent structures include:

*   **Government and Civic Buildings:** The Houses of Parliament, the lands office, the general post-office, the town halls of South Brisbane and Brisbane, and a great domed custom-house located on the river-bank.
*   **Religious Institutions:** The city is the seat of an Anglican bishop and a Roman Catholic archbishop. The Roman Catholic cathedral of St Stephen, located on Elizabeth Street, is an imposing structure featuring a detached campanile that houses the largest bell in Australia. The Anglican cathedral is situated on an elevated site in Ann Street; its foundation-stone was laid in 1901 by the prince of Wales (then the duke of York).
*   **Commercial and Private Buildings:** Notable examples include the massive treasury buildings and the Queensland National Bank, which is described as a classic Italian structure.
*   **Culture:** The city also contains an opera house.
