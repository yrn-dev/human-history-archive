# Cachimayo, Mataca and Pilaya or Camblaya

## Overview and Geographic Context
The Cachimayo, Mataca, and Pilaya (also referred to as Camblaya) are rivers located in the sierra region of Bolivia. These streams serve as tributaries to the Pilcomayo, which is the only large river in Bolivia within the south-east drainage basin.

## The Pilcomayo River System
The Pilcomayo rises on the east slopes of the Cordillera Oriental, positioned opposite the south end of Lake Pampa Aullaguas. From its source, the river flows in an easterly and south-easterly direction through the sierra region before reaching the Bolivian Chaco. The river traverses a nearly level landscape, resulting in a current so sluggish that its channels are frequently obstructed. 

While the tributaries of the Pilcomayo within the Chaco are not known in definite detail, the river possesses several small tributaries in the sierra region. Among these, the largest are the Cachimayo, the Mataca, and the Pilaya or Camblaya.

## Formation of the Pilaya or Camblaya
The Pilaya, which is also known as the Camblaya, is formed by the confluence of two other streams: the Cotagaita and the San Juan.

## Regional Drainage Basin
The south-east drainage basin, in which these rivers are situated, is smaller and holds less economic importance than the basin of the Madeira. This drainage system discharges into the Paraguay and spans a territory extending from the Cordillera Oriental in the east to the Paraguay, and from the Sierras de Chiquitos in the north down to the Argentine frontier in the south.
