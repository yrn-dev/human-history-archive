# Berlin School of Art and Hans von Bartels

## Hans von Bartels
Hans von Bartels (1856– ) was a German painter born in Hamburg. He was the son of Dr. N. F. F. von Bartels, who served as a Russian government official. Bartels' artistic education began in Hamburg under the marine painter R. Hardorff, followed by studies under C. Schweitzer in Duesseldorf and C. Oesterley in Hamburg. He completed his formal education at the Berlin School of Art.

Following a period of extensive travel, with a particular focus on Italy, Bartels settled in Munich in 1885. His professional standing grew within the academic community, leading to his appointment as a professor of painting in 1891.

## Artistic Style and Subjects
Bartels is recognized as an oil painter of great power and is considered one of the leading German water-colour painters. His work is characterized by a great display of technical skill and "rude vigour." He specialized primarily in marines and scenes depicting fishing life. He was noted for his excellence in painting storm scenes and his depictions of the strong, healthy fishing-folk found on northern coasts.

## Principal Works and Recognition
Bartels' work is represented in several prestigious galleries and collections:
*   **Berlin Gallery**: "Sturmflut"
*   **Hungarian National Gallery**: "Lonely Beach"
*   **Prague**: "Potato Harvest--Ruegen"
*   **German emperor's collection**: "Storm--Bornholm"
*   **New Pinakothek, Munich**: "Moonlight on the Zuyder Zee"

Due to his contributions to the arts, he was granted honorary membership in leading art societies across Austria, Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, and England.
