# Rechtsgeschichte and Legal History Literature

The study of legal history (*Rechtsgeschichte*) and constitutional development is documented across various regional and national contexts within the archive's bibliographic records, with particular emphasis on Austrian, German, and Swiss jurisdictions.

## Austrian Constitutional and Imperial History
Literature concerning the constitutional and imperial history of Austria includes several key texts from the late 19th and early 20th centuries. E. Werunsky published *Oesterreichische Reichs- und Rechtsgeschichte* in Vienna in 1894. A. Bechmann authored the *Lehrbuch der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch* (Prague, 1895-1896). Other significant works in this field include A. Huber's *Oesterreichische Reichsgesch* (Leipzig and Vienna, 1895), which saw a second edition by A. Dopsch in 1901.

A. Luschin von Ebengreuth produced a work of "first-class importance" titled *Oesterreichische Reichsgesch* in two volumes (Bamberg, 1895, 1896), as well as the *Grundriss der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch* (Bamberg, 1899). For the study of the constitution and parliament in Austria between 1848 and 1885, G. Kolmer's *Parlament und Verfassung in Oesterreich* (Vienna, 1902-1905) is cited in three volumes.

Regarding the specific relations between Austria and Hungary, the archive notes J. Andrassy's *Ungarns Ausgleich mit Oesterreich, 1867* (Leipzig, 1897) and L. Eisenmann's *Le Compromis austro-hongrois de 1867* (Paris, 1904). Additionally, legal questions pertaining to race and language in Austria are addressed in the *Oesterr. Staatswoerterbuch* (Vienna, 1894-1897; 2nd ed. 1904), authored by Josef Ulbrith and Ernst Mischler.

## German Legal History and the Mark System
The archive identifies R. Schröder as a source for German legal history, specifically in the *Lehrbuch der deutschen Rechtsgeschichte*, with editions published in Leipzig in 1902 and 1904. Other authorities on German legal and governmental structures include G. Stobbe, who wrote *Reichshofgericht und Reichskammergericht* (Leipzig, 1878), and J. N. Harpprecht's *Staatsarchiv des Reichskammergerichts* (1757-1785).

A specialized area of study is the mark system. G. L. von Maurer is cited as the chief authority on this subject, having written *Geschichte der Markenverfassung in Deutschland* (Erlangen, 1856) and *Einleitung zur Geschichte der Mark- Hof- Dorf- und Stadtverfassung und der öffentlichen Gewalt* (Munich, 1854; new ed., Vienna, 1896). Related research into the origin of property in land is found in the works of N. D. Fustel de Coulanges (1885), including a translation featuring an introductory chapter by Professor W. J. Ashley.

## Swiss and Regional Legal History
The legal and state history of Swiss democracies is documented in the three-volume work *Staats- und Rechtsgeschichte d. Schweiz. Demokratien* by J. J. Blumer (St Gall, 1850-1859). Specifically regarding the city and republic of Lucerne, A. P. von Segesser authored the four-volume *Rechtsgeschichte d. Stadt u. Republik Luzern* (1850-1858).
