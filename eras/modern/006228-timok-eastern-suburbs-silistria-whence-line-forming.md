# Bulgaria: Geography and Frontiers

Bulgaria is a kingdom located in south-eastern Europe on the Black Sea and in the north-east of the Balkan Peninsula. The kingdom covers an area of 37,240 sq. m. Its territory comprises the western highlands of Sofia, Samakov, Trn, and Kiustendil; the province of Eastern Rumelia, which lies south of the Balkans; and the lands situated between the river Danube and the Balkan chain.

## Northern and Eastern Boundaries
The northern border of Bulgaria is defined by the Danube. This boundary begins at the river's confluence with the Timok and extends to the eastern suburbs of Silistria. From Silistria, the Rumanian frontier is marked by a line drawn to a point on the Black Sea coast, located 10 m. south of Mangalia. To the east, the kingdom is bordered by the Black Sea.

The Danube serves as a separator between Rumania and Bulgaria for a distance of 284 m., starting from its junction with the Timok and continuing as far as Silistria. The Bulgarian bank of the river is characterized by low heights that are almost continuous, providing the sites for several considerable towns, including Silistria, Rustchuk, Palanka, Lom, and Vidin (Widdin). These towns are noted as memorable names in the Turko-Russian wars.

## Southern and Western Frontiers
The southern frontier with Turkey begins at a point on the coast approximately 12 m. south of Sozopolis. From there, the boundary runs in a south-westerly direction, crossing the river Maritza at Mustafa Pasha before reaching the Arda at Adakali. While the Berlin Treaty of 1878 originally extended the line up the Arda to Ishiklar and followed the crest of the Rhodope to the west, the districts of Rupchus and Krjali were restored to Turkey in 1886. Consequently, the current frontier passes north of those districts to reach the Rhodope watershed slightly north of the Dospat valley. It then follows the crest of the Rilska Planina to the summit of Tchrni Vrkh.

At Tchrni Vrkh, the Bulgarian, Turkish, and Servian territories meet. The western (Servian) frontier proceeds northwards from this point, leaving Pirot to the west and Trn to the east. This line reaches the Timok near Kula and follows the river's course until it joins the Danube.

## Ethnological and Political Context
The boundaries established by the Berlin Treaty of 1878 did not align with the ethnological limits of the Bulgarian race. These limits were more accurately defined by the treaty of San Stefano, which was later abrogated. According to impartial authorities, Bulgarian populations mainly inhabit large tracts of the Dobrudja, the northern half of the vilayet of Adrianople, the districts of Vranya and Pirot (which belong to Servia), and a considerable portion of Macedonia.

Politically, Bulgaria existed as a tributary and autonomous principality under the suzerainty of the sultan of Turkey from 1878 until October 5, 1908.

## Geology and Terrain
The stratified formation of Bulgaria exhibits significant variety. The Archean system, consisting of crystalline schists and gneiss traversed by eruptive veins, is found across the Rilska Planina, the Rhodope, the adjacent ranges, and the majority of the Eastern Rumelian plain; north of the Balkans, it is only present near Berkovitza. 

The Cretaceous system is found throughout Northern Bulgaria, extending from the Danube to the summits of the Balkans. In the western Balkans, the Carboniferous system appears with a continental facies (Kulm), containing beds of sandstone and argillite where anthracitiferous coal is located. The Permian system, represented by conglomerate and red sandstone, is especially present around the basin of Sofia. Mesozoic deposits from the upper Jurassic to the Trias occur in the central part of the range and the western Balkans.

Other geological features include:
* **Tertiary Formations:** The Eocene appears with nummulitic formations on both sides of the eastern Balkans, while the Oligocene is found near Burgas on the Black Sea coast. The Neogene's Sarmatian stage is present in the districts of Varna, Silistria, and the plain of the Danube.
* **Quaternary Era:** Typical loess covers most of the Danubian plain. Alluvial deposits in riparian districts contain bone-caverns with remains of *Equus* and *Ursus*.
* **Other Features:** Flysch is widely distributed, representing the Eocene and Cretaceous systems. Gosau beds are located on the southern declivity of the Balkan chain. Eruptive masses are found in the Sredna Gora and the Balkans.
