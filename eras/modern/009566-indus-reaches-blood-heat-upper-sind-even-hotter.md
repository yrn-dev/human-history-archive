# Sind

## Climate and Environment
The region of Sind is characterized by extreme heat and dryness. In Upper Sind, the climate resembles the sultry deserts of Africa due to its aridity and sandy soil; in this area, thermometers have been known to register 130 deg. in the shade. In Lower Sind, the mean maximum temperature during the six hottest months of the year is 98 deg. F. in the shade, and the water of the Indus reaches blood heat. 

While the south-west monsoon generally begins around the first week of June and brings heavy rain to the coast, it exerts little influence in Sind. Consequently, traveling in Sind remains feasible from June to October, a period when travel elsewhere is often difficult and unpleasant.

## Geography and Geology
The plain of Sind, along with eastern Gujarat, is covered by wind-blown sand and alluvium. In the native state of Khairpur, this manifests as a great alluvial plain that is very rich and fertile near the irrigation canals and the Indus. Other areas of Khairpur consist of continuous sand-hill ridges covered with stunted brushwood, rendering cultivation impossible. A limestone hill ridge, a continuation of the Ghar running southwards from Rohri, passes through the northern part of the state. In the desert tracts of this region, pits of natron are found.

The region's vegetation consists largely of plants also diffused over Baluchistan and Africa. 

## Hydrography
The Indus River is the central geographic feature of the region. In Khairpur, the state is watered by five canals drawn from the Indus, as well as the Eastern Nara, which follows an old bed of the river. 

On the right bank of the Indus lies Manchar Lake, which reaches a length of 20 m. and a breadth of 10 m. during inundations, covering an estimated total area of 180 sq. m. To the west of the Indus, there is a significant development of Tertiary sandstones, shales, and limestones.

## Settlements and Trade
Several key towns and ports are situated along the Indus:

*   **Kotri:** Located on the right bank of the Indus in the Karachi district, Kotri serves as a railway junction for branches serving both banks of the river via a railway bridge. It formerly served as the station for Hyderabad and the headquarters of the Indus steam flotilla. It maintains a considerable general transit trade by river. As of 1901, its population was 7,617.
*   **Keti:** A seaport in the Karachi district situated on the Hajamro branch of the Indus. It is an important trade center for transferring goods between river boats and seaborne vessels. Its population in 1901 was 2,127.
*   **Khairpur:** A native state with an area of 6,050 sq. m. and a population of 199,313 (1901). The town of Khairpur is located 15 m. east of the Indus on a canal and 20 m. south of Sukkur. The town produces arms, carpets, cloth, and goldsmiths' work, and exports oilseeds, grain, and indigo.

## Historical and Regional Nomenclature
The region has been referred to by various names. In former days, Lower Sind was often called *Diul-Sind*. Upper Sind is frequently referred to by Sepoys in India as *Rori-Bakkar*, named after two adjoining places on the Indus.
