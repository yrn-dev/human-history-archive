# Bretagne au XVIII^e siècle

The study of Brittany (Bretagne) during the 18th century is documented through various historical and administrative analyses. Research into this period includes examinations of municipal administration and the broader social structures of the region.

## Administrative and Social Studies
Academic works focusing on the 18th century include A. Dupuy's *Études sur l'administration municipale en Bretagne au XVIII^e siècle*, published in 1891. Additionally, the rural social hierarchy of the region leading up to the Revolution has been analyzed by Henri Sée in *Les Classes rurales en Bretagne du XVI^e siècle à la Revolution* (1906).

## Historical Context and Literature
The history of Brittany has been the subject of numerous texts. General histories include works by Dom Lobineau (1702) and Dom Morice (1742-1756). Other specialized studies address specific events and figures, such as J. Lemoine's 1898 work on the "Revolt of the stamped paper" (also known as the revolt of the *bonnets rouges*) in 1675, and works by M. Marion and B. Pocquet concerning the Duc d'Aiguillon and the Chalotais.

The intellectual and cultural landscape of the 18th century is further explored through the study of the French Academy. Rene Kerviler's *La Bretagne et l'Academie francaise du XVIII^e siecle* (1889) examines this connection, while the works and autobiography of Duclos, published by Auger in 1821, provide further insight into the era.

## Regional Origins and Early History
While 18th-century studies focus on administration and social class, the archive notes that the region's name, Bretagne, is derived from Britain due to migrations that occurred in the 5th and 6th century A.D. Earlier historical perspectives on the region's antiquities have been provided by authors such as the Abbe de Vertot in his 1720 *Hist. Critique de l’Etablissement des Bretons dans les Gaules*. Other historians, such as Gallet and Daru, have argued that Bretagne was independent at the time when insular Britons sought refuge there, suggesting that most arrived as fugitives rather than conquerors.

## Related Archaeological and Numismatic Research
The broader history of the region has been supported by the work of archaeologists and numismatists. Anatole Jean-Baptiste Antoine de Barthelemy, for instance, published *Anciens eveches de Bretagne; histoire et monuments* between 1855 and 1879, as well as *Choix de documents inedits sur l'histoire de la ligue en Bretagne* in 1880.
