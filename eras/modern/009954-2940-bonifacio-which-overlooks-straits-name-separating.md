# Bonifacio

Bonifacio is a maritime town located at the southern extremity of Corsica, within the arrondissement of Sartene. It is situated 87 miles south-southeast of Ajaccio by road. As of 1906, the town had a population of 2,940.

## Geography and Situation
The town occupies a remarkable position on the summit of a peninsula composed of white calcareous rock. This peninsula extends parallel to the coast and encloses a narrow and secure harbour. The town overlooks the straits of Bonifacio, which separate the island of Corsica from Sardinia. Below the town, the cliffs facing it feature rock hollowed into caverns that can only be accessed by boat.

## Architecture and Fortifications
The chief buildings of Bonifacio include the cathedral of Santa Maria Maggiore, which dates mainly to the 12th century, and St Dominic, a church built by the Templars in the 13th century. The town's citadel and fortifications date from the 16th and 17th centuries. Additionally, the town possesses a massive medieval tower that is used as a powder-magazine.

## Economy and Trade
The trade of Bonifacio is conducted primarily with Sardinia and focuses on fine quality olive-oil, wine, cork, and cereals. Local industries include tobacco-manufacture, coral-fishing, and cork-cutting. In the neighbourhood of the town, the olive is cultivated extensively, and the town itself contains oil-works.

## History
During a period of conflict, Alphonso of Aragon arrived with a large fleet to take possession of Corsica. While Calvi fell to him, Bonifacio resisted. This resistance provided the Corsicans time to organize a revolt against the exactions and tyranny of the Aragonese. Eventually, the siege of Bonifacio was raised. The town was confirmed in its privileges and became practically an independent republic under the protection of the Genoese.
