# Histoire de la Louisiane et la cession de cette colonie par la France aux États-Unis

The *Histoire de la Louisiane et la cession de cette colonie par la France aux États-Unis* is a work of significant importance in the field of diplomatic history. Written by François Barbé-Marbois, the text was published in Paris in 1829, with an English translation following in Philadelphia in 1830.

## Authorship and Context
The author, François Barbé-Marbois, was a career official who served in six different governments, a tenure characterized by servility. His professional life included serving as the president of the *cour des comptes*. In 1830, upon the assumption of government by Louis Philippe, Barbé-Marbois visited him to offer compliments and was subsequently confirmed in his position. He remained in office until April 1834 and passed away on February 12, 1837.

Beyond his work on the cession of Louisiana, Barbé-Marbois published several other titles, including *Reflexions sur la colonie de Saint-Domingue* (1794), *De la Guyane, &c.* (1822), and a two-volume account of his transportation following the 18th Fructidor titled *Journal d'un deporte non juge* (1834).

## Content and Historical Value
The *Histoire de la Louisiane* focuses on the transfer of the Louisiana colony from France to the United States. While the work is regarded as having great importance for diplomatic history, it has been subject to critical scrutiny regarding its accuracy. Specifically, the text contains numerous "sayings" attributed to Livingston and Napoleon, as well as supposed "speeches" by Napoleon. 

Critics note that these passages appear highly prophetic for the year 1803, though they lost such a quality by the time of the book's publication in 1829. Despite these concerns, the work has been used liberally and indiscriminately by prominent American historians.

## Relation to Other Louisiana Historiographies
The work of Barbé-Marbois exists within a broader body of literature concerning the history of Louisiana. Earlier foundational work was produced by Le Page du Pratz, who is credited as the first historian of Louisiana, authoring a three-volume *Histoire de la Louisiane* in Paris (1758) and a two-volume version in London (1763). Other early contributions include Judge F. X. Martin's *History of Louisiana* (1827-1829), which serves as a supplement to later scholarly works.

In the 19th century, Charles E. A. Gayarré produced a series of standard works based on deep scholarly research, including *Romance of the History of Louisiana* (1837, 1848) and *History of Louisiana: The French Domination* (1854). More recent accounts include Alcée Fortier's four-volume *A History of Louisiana* (1904), which focuses on American domination.
