# Carnivora, Insectivora, and Ruminants of the Western Archipelago and Asia

The distribution of mammal fauna across Asia and the surrounding archipelagos is marked by distinct regional boundaries. In the western part of the Archipelago, there is an abundance of mammals belonging to the orders Carnivora, Insectivora, and Ruminants. These groups are notably absent from the Molucca Islands and Celebes, a distribution that indicates the margin of the Australian region has been reached.

## Carnivora

The order Carnivora is numerous across several Asian regions, with specific characteristics varying by climate and geography.

### Indian Region
The Indian region is considered the true home of the tiger, although the animal has migrated north into the Palaearctic division of Asia. Other characteristic Carnivora of this region include various ichneumons, civets, and the benturong (*Arctictis*). Additionally, two species of bears are restricted specifically to the Indian region.

### Northern Asia (Palaearctic)
In northern Asia, Carnivora are also numerous, particularly species that frequent cold climates. These include foxes, wolves, weasels, and bears.

### Evolutionary History and Diversity
Modern Carnivora are descendants of the extinct early Tertiary suborder Creodonta. The transition toward "Carnivora Vera" is evident in the European Oligocene genus *Amphictis*, which possesses a sectorial character in the first lower molar and fourth upper premolar. From *Amphictis*, the *Hyaenidae* likely arose via the Lower Pliocene genus *Ictitherium*. The *Viverridae* likely descended from *Amphictis* through the African genus *Nandinia*. The *Canidae* are believed to have transitioned from the Oligocene *Cynodictis*, which shares a dental formula with *Canis* or *Cyon*. The *Felidae* may have sprung from *Amphictis* or the creodont *Palaeonictis*, the latter of which is regarded as the direct ancestor of the sabre-toothed cats (*Machaerodontinae*).

In other regions, such as north-western America, the order includes the kit-fox (*Canis [Vulpes] velox*), a small, swift animal typically grey in color. In South and Central America, the raccoon family (*Procyonidae*) includes the kinkajou (*Cercoleptes caudivolvulus*), a nocturnal, arboreal species that feeds on honey, fruit, eggs, and small mammals.

## Insectivora

The order Insectivora is well-represented in both the Indian and Palaearctic regions of Asia.

### Indian Region
This region contains very peculiar forms of Insectivora, specifically *Tupaia* and *Gymnura*.

### Northern Asia
In the cold and temperate regions of northern Asia, the Insectivora are characterized by a prevalence of shrews, hedgehogs, and numerous forms of moles.

## Ruminants and Ungulates

Ruminants, particularly the Bovidae (hollow-horned ruminants), are prominent in the western parts of the Asian regions.

### Indian Region
The Indian region features several genera of antelopes and species of true *Bos*, including *B. bubalus*, *B. frontalis*, and *B. sondaicus*. Deer are also numerous in this region, and the chevrotains (*Tragulus*) are a characteristic group. Other ungulates found here include one species of tapir, four species of rhinoceros, and several peculiar forms of the swine family.

### Northern Asia
The ungulate population in northern Asia includes various forms of sheep, with twelve or ten wild species of *Ovis* found in the mountain chains. Other peculiar antelopes include *Gazella gutturosa* (yellow sheep) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*). Deer are also present, especially those of the typical genus *Cervus*, while the musk deer (*Moschus*) is a peculiar type restricted to northern Asia.

### Regional Limits and Anomalies
The distribution of these animals is strictly bounded in certain areas; for example, deer do not extend into New Guinea. In New Guinea, the genus *Sus* appears to reach its eastern limit. In Celebes, a singular ruminant known as the *Anoa* is found; this animal, along with a peculiar form of baboon (*Cynopithecus*), appears to have no relation to Asiatic animals and is instead allied with those found in Africa.
