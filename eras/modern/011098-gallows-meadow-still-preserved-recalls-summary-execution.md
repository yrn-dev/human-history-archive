# Gallows Meadow

## Overview
Gallows Meadow is a location associated with the market-town of Ashton-under-Lyne in Lancashire, England. The name of the meadow has been preserved over time as a reminder of the summary execution of justice carried out by the lords of the manor.

## Geography and Local Context
Ashton-under-Lyne is situated on the river Tame, which is a tributary of the Mersey. The town is located 6-1/2 miles east of Manchester and 185 miles north-west by north from London. The municipal and parliamentary borough covers an area of 1,346 acres. According to records from 1891 and 1901, the population was 40,486 and 43,890, respectively.

The town is served by several railways, including the Lancashire & Yorkshire (via Charlestown station), the London & North-Western, and the Great Central (via Park Parade station). Local landmarks include the church of St Michael, which is Perpendicular in style though almost entirely rebuilt, and various barracks.

## The Manor and Gallows Meadow
The Old Hall served as the manor house for the Asshetons and remains today in an altered state. Adjoining this manor house is an ancient prison. It is in this vicinity that the name Gallows Meadow persists, recalling the historical practice of the lords of the manor administering summary justice.

## Regional Economy and Administration
Ashton-under-Lyne is characterized by several primary industries, including iron-founding, machinery works, hat-making, and cotton-spinning. Additionally, there are large collieries located in the surrounding neighborhood. The town maintains a free library, a school of art, a technical school, and several hospitals.

The parliamentary borough returns one member and extends into Cheshire. Administratively, the corporation is composed of a mayor, 24 councillors, and 8 aldermen. Nearby, Stamford Park—which was presented by Lord Stamford—is shared between Stalybridge and Ashton, extending across the river Tame into Cheshire.
