# Bonn

Bonn is a location characterized by its educational facilities and natural beauty, which attract a considerable number of foreign residents, particularly those from England. While the city's trade and commerce are of moderate dimensions, it maintains several principal industries, including the manufacture of beer, machinery, flags, and porcelain, as well as jute weaving and spinning. Additionally, the city engages in some trade in wine.

## The University of Bonn

The university is the primary source of importance for the city. It has served as a center for a wide array of academic disciplines and has hosted numerous scholars and students throughout the 19th century.

### Theology and Art History
The university has been a site for significant theological study. Gottfried Christian Friedrich Lücke, a German theologian, was appointed as a professor extraordinarius at the new university of Bonn in the spring of 1818, becoming a professor ordinarius by the following autumn. During his tenure, his colleagues included Karl Immanuel Nitzsch, J. K. L. Gieseler, and J. C. W. Augusti. 

Johann Gottfried Kinkel, a German poet born near Bonn in Obercassel, also studied theology at the university. After establishing himself there as a *privat docent* of theology in 1836 and serving as a master at the local gymnasium, Kinkel eventually shifted his focus toward the history of art following a journey to Italy in 1837. In 1846, he was appointed as the extraordinary professor of the history of art at Bonn University.

### Philology and Other Disciplines
The university has also attracted international scholars in the field of Oriental languages. Otto von Bohtlingk, a distinguished German Sanskrit scholar born in St Petersburg, continued his studies in Bonn from 1839 to 1842 after studying in St Petersburg and Berlin. During his time in Bonn, Bohtlingk produced his first major work, an edition of Panini's *Acht Bucher grammatischer Regeln* (1839-1840), which served as a criticism of the philological methods used by Franz Bopp.

Other notable figures associated with the university include:
* **Rudolph Albert von Kölliker**: A Swiss anatomist and physiologist who moved to the university of Bonn after spending two years at the university in Zürich.
* **Onno Klopp**: A German historian and author of *Der Fall des Hauses Stuart*, who received part of his education at the university of Bonn, alongside studies in Göttingen and Berlin.
* **Justus Hashagen**: A *Privatdozent* in Medieval and Modern History at the university and author of *Das Rheinland unter die franzosische Herrschaft*.
