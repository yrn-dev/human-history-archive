# Napoleon 1'er et le roi de Hollande (1806-1813) d'apres des documents authentiques et inedits

*Napoleon 1'er et le roi de Hollande (1806-1813) d'apres des documents authentiques et inedits* is a historical work authored by T. Jorissen. Published in 1868 in Paris and The Hague, the text examines the relationship and political dynamics between Napoleon I and the King of Holland during the period from 1806 to 1813.

## Historical Context
The period covered by Jorissen's work began in 1806, a year in which Napoleon I sought to revive ancient imperial traditions, such as crowning himself with the iron crown of Lombardy in Milan. As part of a broader strategy to reorganize Europe and influence western Germany, Napoleon transformed several daughter republics of France into kingdoms. In 1806, he established his brother, Louis, as the King of Holland.

## Academic Significance and Related Literature
Jorissen's work is cited as one of the chief scholarly contributions regarding the life and reign of King Louis. It exists alongside a body of literature that utilizes various archival and personal documents to reconstruct the era. Other significant works on the government of Holland and the reign of Louis Bonaparte include:

*   The three-volume *Documents historiques et reflexions sur le gouvernement de Hollande* (2nd ed., 1820) by le comte de Saint-Leu.
*   *Napoleon I^er et le Roi Louis, d'apres les documents conserves aux archives nationales* (1875) by F. Rocquain.
*   *La Cour de Hollande sous le regne de Louis Bonaparte, par un auditeur* (1823) by A. Garnier.
*   *Louis Bonaparte, Koning van Holland* (1888) by V. Loosjes.
*   *De Regeering van Koning Lodewijk Napoleon (1806-1810)* (1892) by L. Wichers.

Additionally, the broader context of Napoleon's siblings and their respective kingdoms is explored in *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883) by Baron A. du Casse and the four-volume *Napoleon et sa famille* (1897-1900) by F. Masson.

## Notable Figures of the Era
During the timeframe discussed in Jorissen's text, the Dutch intellectual and poet Willem Bilderdijk returned to Holland in 1806. Bilderdijk, a man of strong monarchical and Calvinistic convictions who had previously been forced to leave Holland in 1795, was received kindly by Louis Napoleon. The King appointed Bilderdijk as his librarian and a member of the Royal Institute, where Bilderdijk eventually served as president from 1809 to 1811.
