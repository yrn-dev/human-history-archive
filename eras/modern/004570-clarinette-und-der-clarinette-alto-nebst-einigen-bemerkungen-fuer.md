# Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto

The *Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto* refers to a body of instructional and technical literature concerning the clarinet and the alto clarinet, specifically including remarks intended for instrument makers.

## Historical Literature and Manuals
Several key texts have documented the instruction and development of these instruments. In 1803, J. G. H. Backofen published *Anweisung zur Klarinette, nebst einer kurzen Abhandlung ueber das Basset-Horn* in Leipzig through Breitkopf & Haertel, which included illustrations. Later, in 1826, Iwan Mueller published *Anweisung zu der neuen Clarinette und der Clarinette-alto, nebst einigen Bemerkungen fuer Instrumentenmacher* in Leipzig via Freidrich Hofmeister; this work also featured illustrations.

Additional scholarly contributions include Gottfried Weber's "Ueber Clarinette und Bassethorn," published in *Cacilia* (Band xi, pp. 35-37) in Mainz in 1834. In 1904, Wilhelm Altenburg provided a detailed account of the instrument's origin and development in *Die Clarinette, ihre Entstehung und Entwickelung bis zur Jetztzeit in akustischer, technischer u. musikalischer Beziehung*.

## Related Instruments and Collections
The study of the clarinet often extends to related instruments such as the basset horn. Heliogravures of early basset horns are preserved in the *Descriptive Catalogue of the Musical Instruments at the Royal Military Exhibition, London* (1890), compiled by Capt. C. R. Day. Furthermore, the collection of the Rev. F. W. Galpin of Hatfield, Broad Oak, contains an instrument of this type stamped "H. Grenser, S. Wiesner, Dresden."

## The Bass Clarinet
The bass clarinet (known in French as *clarinette basse*, German as *Bass-Klarinette*, and Italian as *clarinetto basso* or *darone*) functions essentially as an A, Bb, or C clarinet that speaks an octave lower. Consequently, the general history, acoustic properties, transposition, and fingering of the standard clarinet apply to the bass clarinet.

### Physical Construction
Due to its increased length, the bass clarinet's form differs from higher clarinets in two primary ways:
1. The bell joint is bent upward in front of the instrument and ends in a large bell shaped like a gloxinea.
2. The mouthpiece is secured to a serpent-shaped crook made of silver or brass using screws and a strong ligature.

### Compass and Notation
The modern orchestral bass clarinet generally shares the same compass as the higher clarinets in C, Bb, and A, but shifted one octave lower. The real sounds vary by instrument:
* **Bass clarinet in C:** Speaks an octave lower.
* **Bass clarinet in Bb:** Real sounds are one tone lower.
* **Bass clarinet in A:** Real sounds are 1-1/2 tone lower.

Despite these differences in sound, the notation remains the same for all three. In some instances, the treble clef is used; in such cases, the instrument in C is understood to speak an octave lower, the Bb a major ninth lower, and the A a minor tenth lower. The tenor clef is also frequently employed in orchestral works.
