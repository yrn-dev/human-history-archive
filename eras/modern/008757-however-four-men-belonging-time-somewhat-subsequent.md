# The Bohemian Revival

The revival of the language and literature of Bohemia is mainly attributed to four men who lived in a time subsequent to that of Dobrovsky. These figures are Joseph Jungmann, John Kolar, Paul Joseph Safarik, and Francis Palacky.

## Joseph Jungmann and John Kolar
Joseph Jungmann (1773-1847) contributed to the development of the Bohemian language through his monumental German and Bohemian dictionary. Early in his life, he also published numerous Bohemian translations of writers from England and Germany. His other most significant work is the *Dejepes literatury ceska*, which serves as a history of Bohemian literature.

John Kolar (1793-1852) is recognized as the greatest poet of the Bohemian revival. His primary poem, *Slavy dcera* (daughter of Slavia), serves as a personification of the Slavic race. While the artistic value of the work is noted, its principal importance currently lies in the role it played in reviving Bohemian literature. Outside of his poetry, Kolar's works consist mostly of philological studies.

## Paul Joseph Safarik and Francis Palacky
Paul Joseph Safarik (1795-1861) was a prolific writer. He authored *Starozitnosti Slovanske* (Slavic antiquities), a work that attempted to record the literature and history of the early Slavs, which were almost unknown at the time. This work continues to hold considerable value.

Francis Palacky (1798-1876) is considered the greatest of the Bohemian historians. He produced many works, the most important of which is his history of Bohemia, covering the period from the earliest times up to the year 1526.
