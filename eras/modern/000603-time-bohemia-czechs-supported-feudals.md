# Political and National Struggles in Bohemia

The territory of Bohemia, the wealthiest part of the Austrian dominions, was characterized by intense racial and political conflict between the Czech majority and the German minority. Over three-fifths of the population were Czech, and their national feeling was bolstered by an appeal to historic law and a desire for the restoration of the Bohemian monarchy.

## The Czech National Movement and the Feudal Alliance
Led by figures such as Palacky and Rieger, a significant Czech party demanded the full restoration of the Bohemian monarchy, including Silesia and Moravia. Their objectives included the requirement that the emperor be crowned as king of Bohemia in Prague, following the tradition of his predecessors, and the establishment of a position within the monarchy similar to that of Hungary.

This movement was not limited to the Czech population; it was supported by many great nobles of German descent. These feudal nobles, including Prince Friedrich von Schwarzenberg (the cardinal archbishop of Prague), Count Leo Thun, and Count Heinrich Clam-Martinitz, sought a self-governing kingdom of Bohemia to preserve their own power against the threats posed by German Liberals. The feudal nobility held substantial influence due to their wealth, family traditions, connections to the court, and the electoral laws that granted them numerous representatives in the diet.

## The Declaration of 1868 and Constitutional Conflict
In 1868, Czech leaders presented a "declaration" to the diet at Prague, which served as the official statement of their claims. This document asserted that no foreign assembly was qualified to impose taxes in Bohemia and that the diet was not qualified to elect representatives to Vienna. They demanded a separate settlement for Bohemia similar to the one granted to Hungary. This declaration was signed by eighty-one members, including bishops and many feudal nobles.

The German majority in the diet responded by declaring that the signatories had forfeited their seats and ordered new elections. This period was marked by serious riots and the subsequent decree of exceptional laws by the government to maintain order.

## Shifts in Government Policy and the Reichsrath
The struggle for control often depended on the alignment of the government and the feudal proprietors. Under the ministry of Auersperg, a vigorous Liberal centralizing policy was pursued. In 1872, the government used its influence to secure a German majority in the diet by suppressing Czech newspapers, transferring Czech journalists to German districts for trial, and confiscating manifestoes. To ensure the support of feudal proprietors, German capitalists from Vienna formed a society (popularly called *Chabrus*) to purchase estates that carried voting rights. This resulted in a German majority where representatives from Vienna replaced the Thuns and Schwarzenbergs.

A further blow to the Federalists came with the electoral law of 1873, which shifted the election of members of the Reichsrath from the diets to a direct vote of the people. This prevented territories from formally refusing to appoint deputies, as a vacancy would only leave a single constituency unrepresented.

Later, in 1870, a government formed by Potocki and Taaffe attempted to reconcile with the Slavs. However, the Czechs and Feudals remained committed to the declaration of 1868 and refused compromise. Following the dissolution of the diets during the war between Germany and France, the Czechs, supported by Clericals and Feudals, won a large majority in Bohemia. They took their seats only to declare the diet an informal assembly rather than the legal representative of the Bohemian kingdom, and they refused to elect delegates for the Reichsrath.

## Language Disputes and the Young Czechs
The conflict eventually shifted toward the use of language in administration. An 1880 ordinance stipulated that business brought before government offices or law courts be dealt with in the language in which it was introduced, granting Czech a position in government service. In 1882, Czech classes were introduced at the university of Prague, and by 1886, business introduced in Czech was handled in that language in the high courts of Bruenn and Prague.

Internal divisions emerged within the Czech movement between the "Old Czechs" and the "Young Czechs." The Young Czechs were more impatient and criticized agreements that they believed threatened the unity of the Bohemian country. In 1890, Taaffe attempted a reconciliation between the German party, the Czechs, and the Feudal party, proposing a separation of German and Czech districts and a revision of the electoral law. This failed because the Young Czechs were excluded from the conference and used obstruction to block the measures. By the 1891 elections, the Young Czechs were completely victorious over the Old Czechs.

Further tensions arose under Badeni, who published ordinances in 1897 requiring that all government correspondence in Bohemia be conducted in the language in which it was first introduced and requiring government appointees to pass an examination in Czech after 1903. These measures led to intense obstruction by German Nationalists and Radicals.

## Later Administrative Attempts
By the early 20th century, Dr. von Koerber attempted to promote a compromise in 1902 and 1903 by proposing that Bohemia be divided into ten districts (five Czech, three German, and two mixed). The Czechs rejected this, demanding that both Czech and German be placed on an equal footing throughout the entire territory for all official purposes. This demand was refused by the Germans, leading Koerber to govern largely without parliament through imperial ordinances until his fall on December 31, 1904.
