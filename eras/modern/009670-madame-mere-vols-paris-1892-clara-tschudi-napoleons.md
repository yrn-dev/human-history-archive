# Letizia Bonaparte (Madame Mere)

Letizia Bonaparte, known by the title *Madame Mere*, was the mother of Napoleon I. Her life spanned the rise and fall of her son's empire, and she survived him by sixteen years.

## Life During the Empire
Despite the immense wealth she possessed and her distinguished title, Letizia did not adapt to the role she was expected to perform during the Empire. She lived primarily in retirement, maintaining a strict domestic economy. This habit was a second nature to her, developed during her early privations; however, this frugality made her very unpopular in France and was a source of displeasure for Napoleon.

## Exile and Later Years
Following the events of 1814, Letizia joined the emperor on the island of Elba. During this time, she was privy to his plans for escape and returned to Paris during the period known as the Hundred Days. After the final defeat at Waterloo, she established her residence in Rome. There, she received protection and great consideration from Pope Pius VII, who shielded her from the suspicious attentions of the powers of the Grand Alliance.

In 1818, Letizia wrote a pathetic letter to the powers gathered at the congress of Aix. In this petition, she requested the release of Napoleon, arguing that his mortal illness meant he could no longer be a menace to world peace. The powers did not answer the letter, as they believed the terms had been concerted with Napoleon and that the request was a political move.

## Final Years and Death
The latter part of Letizia's life was marked by mournful seclusion, saddened by the deaths of Napoleon, her daughters Elisa and Pauline, and several of her grandchildren. In 1829, she suffered a serious fall that left her crippled. She became almost blind before her death in 1836.

## Family Relations
Letizia was the mother of Napoleon I and several siblings. Among her children was Marie Pauline (1780–1825), born in Ajaccio on October 20, 1780. Pauline, described as the most beautiful and gayest member of the family, married General Leclerc at seventeen and accompanied him to St Domingo, where he died of yellow fever in 1802. Pauline later married Prince Camillo Borghese on August 23, 1803, and lived in Rome. After receiving the title of duchess of Guastalla in 1806, Pauline was removed from court in 1810 due to her offhand treatment of the empress Marie Louise. In 1814, Pauline joined "Madame Mere" in traveling to Elba and expressed a desire to share Napoleon's exile in St Helena. She died of cancer in 1825.

Other siblings of Napoleon I included Joseph Bonaparte, Lucien Bonaparte, Caroline Murat, and Jerome Bonaparte.
