# Noverre, Lettres sur la danse and Castel-Blaze

## Jean Georges Noverre and the Ballet d'Action
Jean Georges Noverre is regarded as the father of ballet dancing in its modern form. He is credited with the invention of the *ballet d'action*. According to archival records, the ballet has not advanced beyond the stage of development to which Noverre brought it; rather, it is suggested that the art form has gone back.

The core of Noverre's theory was the belief that mere display was insufficient to provide life and interest to a ballet. To address this, Noverre perfected the art of using pantomime to present a story. He maintained a strict standard for choreography, never permitting dancing that did not serve as a direct expression of a specific attitude of mind. His influential work, *Lettres sur la danse*, was first published in 1760, with a new edition released in 1804.

## Related Figures in Ballet
While Noverre was a pivotal figure, Gaetano Apolline Balthazare Vestris is identified as undoubtedly the greatest ballet-master apart from him. Vestris, who referred to himself as *le dieu de la danse*, is described as the finest male dancer ever produced in Europe. Vestris collaborated with Gluck on the composition of *Iphigenie en Aulide*. 

Other notable figures of the era include Madame Camargo, who introduced short skirts to the ballet. In 1750, Camargo and Vestris, then the two greatest dancers of the period, performed together in Paris in a ballet-opera titled *Leandre et Hero*.

## Other Historical Works on Ballet
The archive lists several other significant texts regarding the history and analysis of the ballet. Claude Francois Menestrier, a Jesuit who invented a ballet for Louis XIV in 1658, wrote *Des ballets anciens et modernes* (1682). This work is considered one of the most complete books on the subject and contains analyses of approximately fifty early French and Italian ballets.

Additionally, the works of Castel-Blaze provide further historical documentation on the subject, specifically *La Danse et les ballets* (1832) and *Les Origines de l'opera* (1869).
