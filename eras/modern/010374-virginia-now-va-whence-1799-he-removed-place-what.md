# Virginia

Virginia is a state in the United States that historically held extensive territorial claims and played a significant role in the early development of the American frontier. By its charter of 1609, Virginia claimed the Kentucky country as part of its territory, although the region was then inhabited by a small tribe of Shawnees in the north and a few Chickasaws near the Mississippi river, while the Cherokees and Six Nations (Iroquois) also claimed the area as hunting grounds or conquests.

## Frontier Expansion and Kentucky
Virginia's involvement in the west was marked by exploration and conflict. In 1774, Virginia troops defeated the Ohio Indians at the battle of Point Pleasant, located in what is now West Virginia. Following this victory, a treaty was signed in October 1774 in which the Ohio Indians surrendered their claims south of the Ohio river. 

During this era, Daniel Boone, an American pioneer, represented settlers in the Virginia legislature at a time when Kentucky was still a part of Virginia. Boone also served as sheriff, county lieutenant of Fayette county, and deputy surveyor. Kentucky was divided into three counties during this period.

## Separation of Kentucky
Following the War of Independence, settlers in Kentucky expressed dissatisfaction with Virginia, claiming the "mother state" failed to provide an adequate system of local government or sufficient protection against enemies. Between 1784 and 1790, nine conventions were held at Danville to demand separation from Virginia. The Virginia authorities agreed to the separation on the condition that Congress admit the new district into the Union as a state.

Kentucky was formally admitted to the Union on June 1, 1792. The boundary between the two states was defined by the ridge of the Cumberland Mountains, as established in the Act of 1776 for dividing Fincastle county, Virginia. In 1799, a joint commission was appointed by both states to run the boundary line along this ridge.

## Political and Social History
Virginia was central to early American political disputes regarding federal power. In response to the alien and sedition laws, the legislatures of Virginia and Kentucky passed resolutions protesting the administration in Washington. While the Kentucky Resolutions of 1798 took a strong states'-rights view, Virginia was the only state to provide a favorable reply to these resolutions among several states that responded.

The state was also a starting point for the Underground Railroad, a system used to move slaves to freedom in New England, Canada, and across Ohio and Pennsylvania.

## Geography and Notable Locations
Virginia contains various cities and institutions of historical and educational note:

*   **Ashland:** A village in Hanover county located 17 miles northwest of Richmond. It was settled in 1845 and incorporated in 1856. It is the site of Randolph-Macon College, which moved to Ashland in 1868. The birthplaces of Patrick Henry (15 miles distant) and Henry Clay (7 miles distant) are located near the village.
*   **Bristol:** A city divided by the Tennessee-Virginia boundary line, which runs through its principal street. The Virginia portion was known as Goodson before being chartered as a city in 1890. Bristol is a railway centre and a distributing point for coke, coal, and iron, situated near the great mineral deposits of Virginia, West Virginia, Kentucky, North Carolina, and Tennessee. It is home to the Virginia Institute for Women (established 1884) and Sullins College (established 1870).
*   **Educational Institutions:** The University of Virginia employed Thomas Hewitt Key as a professor of mathematics from 1825 to 1827. Additionally, the "Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies" includes the Randolph-Macon Academy at Front Royal and Bedford City, the Randolph-Macon Woman's College at Lynchburg, and the Randolph-Macon Institute in Danville.
