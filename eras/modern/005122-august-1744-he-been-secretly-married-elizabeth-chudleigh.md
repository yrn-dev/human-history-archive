# The Marriage of Augustus John Hervey and Elizabeth Chudleigh

## The Secret Union of 1744
On August 4, 1744, Augustus John Hervey and Elizabeth Chudleigh were privately married at Lainston, located near Winchester. At the time of the wedding, Elizabeth was the daughter of Colonel Thomas Chudleigh (who died in 1726) and had been appointed as a maid of honour to Augusta, princess of Wales, in 1743. This appointment was likely facilitated by her friend, William Pulteney, earl of Bath. 

The marriage was kept secret primarily due to the financial status of the couple, as both husband and wife were poor. Maintaining the secrecy of the union allowed Elizabeth to retain her official post at court. Following the marriage, Hervey, who was a naval officer, returned to his ship, eventually returning to England toward the end of 1746.

## Augustus John Hervey
Augustus John Hervey (1724–1779) was born on May 19, 1724. He pursued a career in the navy, where he experienced rapid promotion and distinguished himself in several encounters with the French. In 1759, he provided significant assistance to Admiral Hawke, though he had returned to England before the battle of Quiberon Bay took place in November of that year. Hervey also served with distinction under Rodney in the West Indies. His active naval career concluded with the peace of Paris in February 1763, although he remained the nominal commander-in-chief in the Mediterranean that year and was later made vice-admiral of the blue in January 1778.

In addition to his naval career, Hervey was active in politics. He served as a member of parliament for Bury from 1757 to 1763 and again from 1768 until 1775. He also briefly represented Saltash. Between 1771 and 1775, he served as a lord of the admiralty. He was known as a contributor to periodical literature, a participant in parliamentary debates, a defender of Admiral Keppel, and an opponent of the Rockingham ministry. He succeeded his brother as the 3rd earl of Bristol in 1775 and died in London on December 23, 1779, without legitimate issue.

## Marital Dissolution and Legal Disputes
The marriage between Hervey and Chudleigh was very unhappy, leading the couple to cease living together. However, when it became probable that Hervey would succeed his brother as earl of Bristol, Elizabeth sought proof of their marriage. Despite this, she became the mistress of Evelyn Pierrepont, the 2nd duke of Kingston. Elizabeth remained a prominent figure in London society and, while in Berlin in 1765, received the attentions of Frederick the Great.

By this period, Hervey desired a divorce. While Elizabeth also wished to be free, she wanted to avoid the publicity associated with a divorce. Consequently, she initiated a suit of jactitation against Hervey. This case was likely collusive; after Elizabeth swore she was unmarried, the court pronounced her a spinster in February 1769. This legal declaration dissolved the union between Hervey and Chudleigh in 1769.

## Subsequent Marriage and Bigamy Trial
Within a month of being declared a spinster, Elizabeth married Evelyn Pierrepont, the 2nd duke of Kingston. Pierrepont, a general who had raised "Kingston's light horse" during the rebellion of 1745, died four years later. He left all his property to Elizabeth on the condition that she remain a widow.

Following the duke's death, Elizabeth visited Rome, where she was received with honour by Clement XIV. Upon her return to England, she faced a charge of bigamy brought by the duke's nephew, Evelyn Meadows. In 1776, the House of Lords found her guilty. Although she retained her fortune, she hurriedly left England to avoid further proceedings initiated by the Meadows family. Elizabeth Chudleigh, later known as the duchess of Kingston, died in 1788.
