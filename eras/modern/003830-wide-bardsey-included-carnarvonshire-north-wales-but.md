# Bardsey Island

## Geography and Location
Bardsey, known in Welsh as *Ynys Enlli* ("isle of the current") and translated as "Bards' Island," is an island situated at the northern extremity of Cardigan Bay. It is located approximately 4 miles from Aberdaron point, which is the width of the "sound" separating the island from the mainland. The island measures roughly 2-1/2 miles in length by 3/4 of a mile in breadth, covering an area of some 370 acres. Approximately one-third of this land is hilly, and the northwestern side of the island is characterized by high cliffs. On the southeastern side, there is a fairly deep harbour.

## Administration and Land Ownership
While traditionally associated with South Wales, Bardsey is included in Carnarvonshire, North Wales. The island was granted to John Wynn of Bodvel, Carnarvonshire, following the Civil War and the partial sack of Norwich by Puritans. Ownership later passed through Mary Bodvel to her husband, the earl of Radnor, who subsequently sold the island to Dr. Wilson of York. Dr. Wilson then sold it to Sir John Wynn of Bodfean Hall and Glynllifon, Carnarvonshire.

The farm known as "Cwrt" (Court), which served as the location where the abbot held his court, remains associated with Bardsey. However, the rectory of Aberdaron—located on the mainland opposite the island—along with Llangwnadl (Llangwynhoedl) and Penmachno in Lleyn, South Carnarvonshire, belong to St John's College, Cambridge.

## Religious History and Landmarks
Bardsey was made a famous sanctuary by St Dubricius, who died on the island in 612. It served as a burial site for monks whose friends could afford to transport their bodies there, with all the great abbeys of England sending their quota. A portion of the island consists of a large cemetery covering about 3 to 4 acres, featuring rough, rude graves placed as close together as possible and topped with slabs.

On the northeastern side of the island lie the ruins of the tower of St Mary's abbey, dating from the 13th century. Following the great massacre of Bangor-is-coed in 613 by Ethelfride of Northumbria, the remaining priests fled to Bardsey via the road of the Rivals (*Yn Eifl*) hill in South Carnarvonshire. Roads leading to the island from the south, east, and north are marked by monks' wells found at intervals of 7 to 9 miles.

In more recent times, there is no Anglican church on the island, as the inhabitants are Dissenters.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The inhabitants of Bardsey are primarily fishermen and farmers, growing oats and barley. The island is home to a locally celebrated lighthouse, which stands 140 feet high and features a fixed light visible for 17 miles.
