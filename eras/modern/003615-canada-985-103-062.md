# Canada

## Political History and Leadership
Sir John Alexander Macdonald (1815–1891) served as the first premier of the dominion of Canada. Born in Glasgow on January 11, 1815, to Hugh Macdonald, a native of Sutherlandshire, he emigrated to Canada in 1820. His family first settled in Kingston, Ontario. Macdonald entered a law office at age fifteen and was called to the bar in 1836, establishing a successful practice in Kingston.

Macdonald's political career began during a period of significant instability and racial and political antagonism. In 1837, the country experienced a rebellion; during this time, Macdonald volunteered for active service, though his military experience was limited to marching and drilling. The subsequent years were marked by several key political developments that influenced the constitution of the British Empire, including the union of the two Canadas, the publication of Lord Durham's report and mission, and the administrations of Sir Charles Metcalfe, Sir Charles Bagot, and Lord Sydenham.

## Trade and Exports
Canada is a primary exporter of colonial cheese to the United Kingdom. In the market for imported colonial cheese, Canada "virtually has the field to itself." While New Zealand also exports colonial cheese to the United Kingdom, its contributions are comparatively insignificant; for example, in 1900, New Zealand provided only 4,000 tons out of a total import of 128,600 tons. Australia also sent small quantities of cheese in several seasons following 1891, though these amounts are not considered worth quoting.
