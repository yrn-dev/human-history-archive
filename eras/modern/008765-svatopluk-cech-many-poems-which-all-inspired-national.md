# Svatopluk Cech

Svatopluk Cech is recognized as one of the best poets among the later writers of Bohemia. His literary contributions emerged during a period of significant development for Bohemian literature in the latter part of the 19th century, a growth that was particularly evident following the establishment of the national university in 1882.

## Literary Themes and Works

The poetry of Svatopluk Cech is characterized by its inspiration, as his many poems are all driven by national enthusiasm. Among his extensive body of work, three pieces are identified as the most notable:

*   *Vaclav z Michalovic*
*   *Lesetinsky Kovar* (translated as the smith of Lesetin)
*   *Basne otroka* (translated as the songs of a slave)

## Context within Bohemian Literature

Cech is situated among a group of distinguished later Bohemian poets that includes Jaroslav Vrchlicky (born 1853) and Adolf Heyduk. While Cech's work is defined by national enthusiasm, his contemporary Vrchlicky—who also possessed strong patriotic feelings—was more catholic in the subjects he chose for his prose and poetry.

The broader literary landscape of Bohemia during this era included a variety of influential figures. Earlier writers included Benes Trebizky, known for historical novels, and Bozena Nemceva, whose work *Babicka* achieved international reach through translations into many languages. Other significant contributors to the era's literature were John Neruda (1834–1891), who wrote the philosophical epic *Kosmicke basne* (cosmic poems), and Julius Zeyer (1841–1901), author of the novel *Jan Maria Plojhar* and the epic poem *Vysehrad*, which celebrates the ancient glory of the Prague acropolis.

The period also saw a rise in the number of Bohemian novelists and dramatists, such as Alois Jirasek, Jacob Arbes (author of *Romanetta*), and Vaclav Hladik, whose work *Evzen Voldan* provides a striking representation of modern life in Prague.
