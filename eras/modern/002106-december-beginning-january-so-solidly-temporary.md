# December and January

The months of December and January serve as critical markers for various natural phenomena, financial systems, religious traditions, and historical events across different regions and eras.

## Natural Phenomena and Climate
In certain geographical basins, the transition from December to January is marked by extreme freezing. One such lake typically freezes at the beginning of January or the end of December. The ice forms so solidly that a temporary post-horse station can be erected in the middle of the lake. This frozen state persists until the second half of May. The subsequent thawing of this ice in early summer produces a notable cooling effect on the surrounding area.

In Bangkok, the climate has recently shifted to become hotter and less humid, a change attributed to the removal of vegetation and extensive drainage near the town. Despite this trend, minimum temperatures below 60deg F. continue to be recorded during the months of December and January.

## Trade and Commerce
The arrival of colonial butter in British markets is heavily dependent on the season. Butter from Australasia primarily reaches these markets during December, January, and February. Conversely, butter from Canada begins its arrival in July and virtually ceases by the following January.

## Financial and Administrative Calendars
The commencement date of the financial year varies by country. In France, Belgium, and Austria, the financial year begins on the 1st of January. Historically, the English financial year also ran from the 1st of January to the 31st of December, though this practice ended prior to 1832.

The Bank of France has also seen significant extensions of its note circulation limits on specific dates within these months. For example, limits were extended on December 2, 1849; December 29, 1871; January 30, 1884; January 25, 1893; and December 17, 1897.

## Religious Traditions and Dates
Historical records indicate a period of confusion regarding the date of Christ's birth. A letter from a bishop of Jerusalem to the bishop of Rome describes a temporary union of feasts on January 6 in the holy places. The faithful would gather at Bethlehem to celebrate the Birth from the Virgin before hurrying to Jordan to celebrate the Baptism. Because this association made it impossible to keep either commemoration fully and reverently, the pope consulted the archives of the Jews brought to Rome after the destruction of Jerusalem. Through the works of Josephus, the pope determined the date of the birth to be December 25. This date was later communicated to the East and confirmed by Chrysostom in a homily preached around 392.

## Calendar Calculations
The use of epacts allows for the determination of the moon's age and the days of new moons. In the calendar, these are placed in retrograde order; for instance, the asterisk is placed beside January 1, with 29 beside January 2, and 28 beside January 3. If a lunation ends on December 2, the new moon falls on December 3, making the epact for the following year twenty-nine. Similarly, if a new moon falls on December 4, the epact for the following year is twenty-eight, corresponding to January 3.
