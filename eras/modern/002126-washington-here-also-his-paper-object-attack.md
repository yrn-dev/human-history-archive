# Washington, D.C.

Washington, D.C., serves as the national capital of the United States and has been a central location for political, artistic, and journalistic activity.

## Role in the American Civil War
During the American Civil War, Washington was one of the two chief objectives of the conflict, alongside Richmond. The war was characterized by a struggle between Federal forces—which eventually exceeded one million men—and the Confederates. Throughout the conflict, the city's security fluctuated; at certain points, Washington appeared to be within the grasp of the Confederate forces, while at other times, Federal armies drove toward Richmond.

## Journalism and Abolitionism
In 1847, the journalist Gamaliel Bailey assumed control of the *National Era*, an abolitional organ based in Washington, D.C. The publication achieved considerable circulation and was the first to publish Mrs. H. B. Stowe's *Uncle Tom's Cabin* between 1851 and 1852. Because of its stance, the paper was targeted by pro-slavery mobs. In 1848, these attacks escalated to the point where the printers and the editor were besieged in their office for a period of three days.

## Public Art and Monuments
The American government commissioned the sculptor Horatio Greenough to create a colossal statue of Washington for the national capital. This work was unveiled in 1843; while it was considered a fine piece of work for its time, it has been sharply criticized in modern times as incongruous and unworthy. Greenough also received a second government commission for a colossal group titled "Rescue," which was intended to depict the conflict between the Indian and Anglo-Saxon races. Greenough returned to Washington in 1851 to oversee the erection of this piece.

## Religious and Administrative Activity
Washington is the site of publication for *The Luther League Review*, a monthly paper produced by the Luther League. The league is a religious association for young people in the United States based on the Augsburg Confession, which seeks to foster loyalty to the church and encourage Christian activity among Lutheran congregations.

Additionally, the city serves as a hub for various government reports and bureaucratic publications, including the *U.S.A. Consular Reports* and the *Bulletins* of the Bureau of American Republics. The Bureau of American Republics also produced maps, including one published in 1903.
