# Badajoz

## Geography and Administration
Badajoz is the capital of the Spanish province of the same name, which was formed in 1833 from districts previously belonging to the province of Estremadura. It is the largest province in the kingdom, covering an area of 8,451 square miles with a population of 520,246 as of 1900. The province is bounded by Portugal to the west, Caceres to the north, Cordova and Ciudad Real to the east, and Seville and Huelva to the south.

The city of Badajoz is situated on the left bank of the river Guadiana, close to the Portuguese frontier. It serves as the official residence of the captain-general of Estremadura and is the see of a bishop. The city is located on the Madrid-Lisbon railway.

## City Features and History
The city of Badajoz occupies a slight eminence overlooking the Guadiana, crowned by the ruins of a Moorish castle. Its appearance is one of great strength, characterized by a broad moat, outworks, bastions, and a strong wall, with additional forts situated on the surrounding heights. A magnificent granite bridge crosses the river between the castle-hill and the fort of San Cristobal; this bridge was originally constructed in 1460, repaired in 1597, and rebuilt in 1833. The city's cathedral, dating from 1258, also resembles a fortress due to its massive embattled walls.

Badajoz first rose to importance under Moorish rule, becoming the capital of a small Moorish kingdom in 1031. Although the Portuguese held the city temporarily in 1168, it remained independent until 1229, when it was captured by Alphonso IX of Leon. As a frontier fortress, it was the site of numerous sieges, including those by the Portuguese in 1660 and the Allies in 1705 during the War of the Spanish Succession. During the Peninsular War, the French unsuccessfully attacked the city in 1808 and 1809. However, on March 10, 1811, the Spanish commander Jose Imaz was bribed into surrendering the city to the French forces under Marshal Soult. A British army under Marshal Beresford attempted to retake the city, defeating a relieving force at Albuera on May 16, though the siege was abandoned in June.

The city is the birthplace of the painter Luis de Morales (1509-1586) and the statesman Manuel de Godoy, duke of Alcudia (1767-1851). Two paintings by Morales are preserved in the cathedral, though they have been retouched in modern times.

## Provincial Economy and Industry
The province of Badajoz is characterized by a desolate and monotonous plain that is flat or slightly undulating. The Guadiana is its primary river, though it is often fordable without difficulty except during certain seasons. The climate is extreme, with fierce north and north-west winds in winter and prevalent intermittent fevers in the Guadiana valley during hot months.

Agriculture is in a backward condition due to scanty rainfall and a lack of irrigation. However, the province possesses more livestock than any other in Spain. It is particularly celebrated for its acorn-fed swine, which produce hams and bacon, as well as herds of goats and sheep. Mineral resources include lead, with small amounts of copper, though exploitation is hindered by poor communication and a lack of water; in 1903, only 26 of nearly 600 registered mines were active.

Local industries include the manufacture of leather, cork, soaps, oils, and coarse cotton and woollen stuffs. Commercial interests are primarily driven by transit trade with Portugal, facilitated by seven custom-houses.

## Diplomatic and Political Events
In the early 19th century, Badajoz served as the site of significant diplomatic activity involving France and Spain. In 1801, France and Spain were preparing to partition Portugal, and Spanish forces had begun an invasion. However, the court of Lisbon succeeded in inducing Lucien Bonaparte—who had been appointed ambassador to the court of Madrid in November 1800—and the Spanish minister Godoy to sign preliminaries of peace. This agreement was signed at Badajoz on June 6, 1801.

This event frustrated the plans of the First Consul, Napoleon, to seize Lisbon. Napoleon remonstrated with his brother Lucien over the agreement, leading Lucien to resign his post and return to Paris, where he joined the opposition in the Tribunate.

## Notable Locations in the Province
The province includes the town of Don Benito, located in the province of Badajoz near the left bank of the Guadiana on the Madrid-Badajoz-Lisbon railway. Founded in the 15th century by refugees from Don Llorente who were fleeing floods, Don Benito had a population of 16,565 in 1900. The town is a thriving center for the trade of fruit (especially melons), wine, and wheat, and manufactures flour, oil, soap, linen, cloth, and brandy.
