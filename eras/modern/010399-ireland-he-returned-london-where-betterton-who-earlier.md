# Ireland

The archive's records regarding Ireland encompass various aspects of its administrative, economic, and cultural history, including the lives of notable figures born or active within the region, as well as data on its financial systems and agricultural trade.

## Notable Figures and Public Service

Several individuals of prominence are recorded as having held significant roles or spent formative years in Ireland. George Macartney, Earl Macartney (1737-1806), was born on May 14, 1737, at Lissanoure, Antrim, to a Scottish family that had settled there in 1649. After graduating from Trinity College, Dublin, in 1759, he pursued a career in diplomacy and governance. He served as the chief secretary for Ireland, for which he was returned for Antrim in the Irish parliament in 1769. He was later created an Irish baron in 1776 and an Earl in the Irish peerage in 1792.

In the field of genealogy, Sir John Bernard Burke (1814-1892) held several high-ranking positions in Ireland. In 1853, he was appointed Ulster king-at-arms, and by 1855, he became the keeper of the state papers in Ireland. He eventually died in Dublin on December 12, 1892.

## Literature and the Arts

Ireland produced influential literary figures, such as the novelist John Banim (1798-1842), often referred to as the "Scott of Ireland." Born in Kilkenny on April 3, 1798, Banim studied drawing and painting at Kilkenny College and the Royal Society schools in Dublin. In 1820, he settled in Dublin to pursue literature. Alongside his brother Michael Banim (1796-1874), he planned a series of tales intended to illustrate Irish life, similar to the Waverley Novels of Scotland. Their collaborative efforts included the *Tales of the O'Hara Family*, first published in April 1825, and *The Nowlans* in 1826. Other works associated with the "O'Hara family" include *The Croppy, a Tale of 1798* (1828), *The Denounced*, *The Mayor of Windgap*, *The Ghost Hunter*, and *The Smuggler*.

The theatrical arts also saw contributions from English actors who spent time in Ireland. Barton Booth (1681-1733), educated at Westminster school and Trinity College, Cambridge, obtained employment with a theatrical company in Dublin in 1698. He made his first appearance there as Oroonoko and spent two seasons in Ireland before returning to London.

## Economy and Agriculture

Financial records from the early 20th century provide data on the circulation of currency in Ireland. In 1906, the average circulation in Ireland was L6,452,000. By May 1907, the authorized and actual issues for banks in the region were recorded as follows: 12 private banks had an authorized issue of L482,744 and an actual issue of L122,536, while 17 joint-stock banks had an authorized issue of L1,084,836 and an actual issue of L437,693. The Bank of Ireland is also noted with a figure of 3,597,850. The archive notes that the actual circulation of these banks was often limited to the immediate neighborhood of their issuance, despite being payable in London.

Regarding agriculture, Ireland served as a destination for produce from other regions. In Hampshire, the strawberry industry—centered in the Southampton district and led by parishes such as Botley and Sarisbury—exported a significant portion of its fruit. While the first week's gathering (between June 1 and June 15) was sent primarily to London, the greater part of the fruit was subsequently sent to Scotland, the Midlands, and Ireland.
