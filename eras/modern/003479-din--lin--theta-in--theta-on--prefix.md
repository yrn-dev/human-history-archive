# The 10th Prefix in Bantu Speech

In the study of Bantu languages, the 10th prefix exhibits significant variability across different dialects and historical stages of the speech. Originally, this prefix took the forms *Ti-*, *Tin-*, *Zi-*, or *Zin-*. Over time, these original forms evolved into various iterations, including *Jin-*, *Rin-*, *Din-*, *Lin-*, *[theta]in-*, and *[theta]on-*.

## Composition and the Role of the Singular Prefix
The presence of the letter *n* within the 10th prefix is a result of the influence of the 9th singular prefix. In some instances of Bantu speech, this singular prefix is retained within the plural form, while in other instances, it is omitted.

## Persistence of Concord
A defining characteristic of the 10th prefix is the persistence of its concord, or corresponding pronoun, even after the prefix itself has ceased to function as a definite article. This phenomenon is observed in almost every known Bantu language, suggesting that the 10th prefix was once in universal use.

An example of this persistence is found in the Swahili of Zanzibar. In this dialect, the 10th prefix is absent as a plural prefix for nouns; however, it reappears in the concord. For instance, in the phrase *Nombe hizi zangu* ("Cows these mine" or "These cows are mine"), the noun *Nombe* is no longer *zinombe* in the plural, yet the *Zi-* particle remains present in the words *hizi* and *zangu*.

## Comparison with Other Bantu Prefixes
The 10th prefix exists within a broader system of prefixes and concords characterized by frequent degeneration and variation:

*   **The 2nd Prefix:** Originally *Ba-* or *Aba-*, it appears as *Baba-* in its definite form in the most archaic Bantu speech (such as the languages of Mt. Elgon). While the concord remains *-ba-* in less corrupt tongues, the plural prefix can degenerate into *Va-*, *Wa-*, *Ma-*, or *A-*.
*   **The 4th Prefix:** The prefix *Mi-* utilizes concords such as *gi-*, *-i-*, *-ji-*, and occasionally *-mi-*.
*   **The 5th Prefix:** The most common modern form is *Li-*, though *Di-* is considered the older and more correct form. This prefix may be dropped entirely (leaving the concord), or it may shift to *Ri-*, *I-*, *Ji-*, or *Ni-*.
*   **The 7th Prefix:** The prefix *Ki-* follows a parallel course in various unrelated dialects, moving through *Ci-* into *Si-* (or *Shi*).
*   **The 8th Prefix:** This prefix is highly variable. Its oldest forms are *Ibi-* or *Mbibi-*, and it serves as the invariable plural of the 7th prefix. Depending on the Bantu speech, it may appear as *Vi-*, *Pi-*, *Fi-*, *Fy-*, *P[vs]i-*, *[vS]i-*, *I-*, *By-*, *Bzi-*, *Psi-*, *Zwi-*, *Zi-*, or *Ri-*.
*   **The 11th Prefix:** The prefix *-Lu-*, likely descended from the older *Ndu-*, most commonly appears as *Lu-*. In some cases, it loses the *L* to become *U-*, while in more archaic dialects, it is typically pronounced as *Du-* or *Ru-*.
