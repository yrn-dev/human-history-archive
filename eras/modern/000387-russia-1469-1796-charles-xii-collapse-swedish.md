# Russia from 1469 to 1796 and the Collapse of the Swedish Empire

## Political History of Russia and Slavonic Europe
The political history of Russia and Slavonic Europe from 1469 to 1796 is a period characterized by significant territorial shifts and the rise of the Romanov dynasty. Between 1613 and 1725, the First Romanovs held power. During the later part of the 18th century, Russia continued to expand its influence; for example, the principality of Lithuania was annexed to Russia in 1795. This territory, which included the government of Kovno (formerly known as Samogitia), had previously been part of Lithuania and had suffered during various conflicts involving Russia, Poland, and Sweden.

## Charles XII and the Great Northern War
The Swedish Empire, under the leadership of King Charles XII, engaged in a protracted conflict known as the Great Northern War. Charles XII was a dominant figure who, along with his predecessor Charles XI, treated the Swedish nation with a dictatorial approach. During the earlier stages of the war, Charles XII relied on foremost generals such as Count Arvid Bernhard Horn. Horn, a Swedish statesman born in Finland, served in various diplomatic and military capacities, including a mission to deposit Augustus II of Poland and elect Stanislaus I.

The conflict involved extensive military movements across the Baltic region. In 1701, Charles XII invaded the territory of Samogitia. The struggle between the Swedish King and the tsar of Russia became a subject of popular culture, most notably in the spirited ballad *Gotha kampavisa om Konungen och Herr Peder* (The Goth's Battle Song, concerning the King and Master Peter), written by the poet Gunno Dahlstjerna in 1701.

## The Collapse of the Swedish Empire
The decline of the Swedish Empire was accelerated by military failure and internal political shifts. A pivotal moment occurred at the Battle of Poltava; the disastrous news of this defeat was so profound that it is said to have hastened the death of the poet and land-surveyor Gunno Dahlstjerna on September 7, 1709. Following the defeat at Poltava, Charles XII collected his forces at Bender, a town in the government of Bessarabia on the right bank of the Dniester. There, he established a camp known as "New Stockholm," where he remained until 1713.

Following the death of Charles XII in 1718, Sweden entered a period of political transition. Count Arvid Bernhard Horn persuaded Princess Ulrica Leonora to be elected queen rather than claiming the throne by hereditary right. After Ulrica Leonora resigned, Frederick of Hesse was elected king. From 1720 to 1738, Horn exercised absolute control over Sweden's domestic and foreign affairs, a timeframe referred to as the "Horn period."

## Russian Territorial Expansion and Bender
As the Swedish Empire collapsed, Russia expanded its reach. The town of Bender serves as an example of this shifting control. While Charles XII used the town as a refuge after Poltava, the Russians took Bender in 1770 and 1789. However, Russia did not hold the town permanently until 1812.
