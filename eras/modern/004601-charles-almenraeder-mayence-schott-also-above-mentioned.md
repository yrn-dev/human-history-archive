# Charles Almenraeder

Charles Almenraeder was an inventor and author who introduced significant mechanical and theoretical improvements to the construction of the bassoon. His work focused on refining the acoustic properties of the instrument to achieve greater tonal clarity and accuracy.

## Improvements to the Bassoon
Almenraeder addressed specific acoustic phenomena related to wind instruments, particularly the issue where a lateral opening or bell smaller than the bore can lower the pitch of a note and damp the tone. This effect is especially prominent in the A of the bassoon, where the hole is typically too small in diameter and positioned too high.

To resolve this, Almenraeder readjusted the position of the A hole, which is stopped by the third finger of the right hand. Instead of a single large hole, he bored two holes of medium diameter lower down the tube. These holes are covered by an open key operated by the same finger from its usual position. One of these holes communicates with the wider bore, while the other communicates with the narrower bore in the butt joint. This modification results in a tone that is full, accurate, and perfectly clear.

## Influence and Adoption
Almenraeder's alterations were based on the same principles used for the A hole, resulting in an instrument that was theoretically and mechanically more perfect than the version created by Savary, although it lacked some traditional characteristics of the bassoon. 

His improvements were generally adopted in Germany. Most makers in the region follow his model, which features 16 keys; a notable example of a maker following this model is Heckel of Biebrich.

## Published Works
Almenraeder authored the *Traite sur le perfectionnement du basson*, which includes two tables and was published in Mayence by Schott. While Almenraeder was the inventor, some sources suggest that the explanations provided by Gottfried Weber in *Cacilia* regarding these improvements are clearer than those written by Almenraeder himself.
