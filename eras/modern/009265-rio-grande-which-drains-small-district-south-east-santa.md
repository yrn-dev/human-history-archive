# Rio Grande

The name Rio Grande is applied to several different watercourses and geographical features across the Americas, as well as appearing in the name of a railway system in the United States.

## Rio Grande (Tarija)
In the south-east drainage basin of Bolivia, the Rio Grande—also known as the Tarija—serves as a large tributary to the Bermejo, which is an Argentine river. The Rio Grande drains a small district located south-east of the Santa Victoria sierra. This drainage basin is smaller and of less economic importance than that of the Madeira, discharging into the Paraguay and extending from the Cordillera Oriental in the east to the Paraguay, and from the Sierras de Chiquitos in the north to the Argentine frontier.

## Rio Grande (Guapay)
Within the Amazon river-system of Bolivia, the Guapay is also referred to as the Grande. It is considered the main stream of its system, being longer and larger than the Mamore above their confluence. The Guapay rises in the Cordillera Oriental, east of Lake Pampa Aullaguas, and flows east toward the north extremity of the Sierra de Misiones. Upon emerging onto the Bolivian lowlands, it follows a magnificent curve to the north, passing the south-east extremity of the Sierra de Cochabamba and skirting the Llanos de Chiquitos. It eventually turns north-west to unite with the Mamore at Junta de los Rios (15 deg. 20' S. lat. and 64 deg. 40' W. long.). The river has a tortuous, non-navigable course exceeding 700 m. Its primary tributaries include the Piray (or Sara), the Mizque, and the Yapacani.

## Rio Grande de Lipez
In the third drainage basin of Bolivia, which encompasses the great central plateau or *alta-planicie*, is the Rio Grande de Lipez. This region is one of the world's most elevated lacustrine basins and currently possesses no surface outlet. In the extreme south of this district, the Rio Grande de Lipez is absorbed by evaporation and the dry soil of the desert.

## Rio Grande Rivers of the Pacific Coast
On the Pacific coast of Central America, specifically in the region where the narrowness of level ground between the mountains and the sea makes navigable rivers nearly impossible, there are three rivers that bear the prefix *Rio Grande* ("great river"). All of these are characterized as not being large and are subject to sudden floods. These include:
*   **Rio Grande de Tarcoles**: This river enters the Gulf of Nicoya. It rises at the base of Irazu, close to the sources of the Reventazon and the Ochomogo Pass. The headwaters of this river and the Reventazon mark the depression in the central plateau that separates the southern mountains from the northern mountains.
*   **Rio Grande de Pirris**: This river flows into Coronada Bay.
*   **Rio Grande de Terrabis (or Diquis)**: This river also flows into Coronada Bay.
