# The National Convention and the Legislative Assembly

The transition from the Legislative Assembly to the National Convention marked a pivotal era in the French Revolution, characterized by shifting power dynamics between political factions and the direction of France's foreign policy.

## The Legislative Assembly and Foreign Policy
During the period of the Legislative Assembly, Brissot emerged as a prominent figure. A capable and well-informed man who edited the *Patriote français* from 1789 to 1793, Brissot was a member of the municipality of Paris before being elected to the Legislative Assembly. 

Due to his knowledge of foreign affairs, Brissot served as a member of the diplomatic committee, where he practically directed the foreign policy of France. His influence was largely responsible for the declaration of war against the emperor on April 20, 1792, and the declaration of war against England on July 1, 1793. Brissot further shaped these conflicts by giving them the character of revolutionary propaganda. 

Other notable figures in the Legislative Assembly included Armand Guy Simon de Coetnempren, comte de Kersaint. A former naval officer and author of the pamphlet *Le Bon Sens*, Kersaint entered the Assembly as a *député suppléant*. He supported the advanced party's policies, denounced Louis XVI, and voted for the king's deposition on August 10, 1792. Additionally, Ange Elisabeth Louis Antoine Bonnier, a diplomatist, served as a member of the Legislative Assembly.

## Transition to the National Convention
The Legislative Assembly surrendered its authority in September, at which time elections for the National Convention took place. The Convention subsequently ruled France until October 1795. 

Several figures transitioned from the previous assembly to the Convention. Brissot was elected as a member, as was Bonnier, who typically voted with the majority. Kersaint was sent as a deputy to the Convention by the department of Seine-et-Oise in September. Another member, Dupuis—who had been a professor and a commissioner of public instruction—was elected by the department of Seine-et-Oise and was noted for his moderation. In the third year of the republic, Dupuis served as secretary to the Assembly.

## Political Struggles and the Mountain
The National Convention was defined by an intense struggle for power. For the first nine months, the Girondins (also known as Brissotins, with Brissot as their leading spirit) were engaged in an incessant struggle for their lives against the Mountain. 

While the Mountain had been small in the Constituent Assembly and lacked chiefs with seats in the Legislative Assembly, they achieved absolute power in June 1793. However, the members of the Mountain, having been accustomed to the methods of opposition, struggled to develop the instincts of government. Consequently, actual power shifted to two bodies chosen from the Convention: the committee of general security and the committee of public safety.

The subsequent nine months were marked by a struggle for power between these committees, the Convention itself, and the insurrectionary commune of Paris.

## Fall of the Girondists and Danton
The Girondists eventually fell before the Mountain. On June 2, 1793, a sentence of arrest was passed against the leading members of the party. Brissot attempted to escape using a disguise but was arrested at Moulins. Following a trial where he maintained a quiet and dignified demeanour, Brissot died bravely on October 31, 1793, along with several other Girondists.

Georges Danton was also a member of the Convention. After resigning from the ministry of justice, he played a foremost role in the proceedings and deliberations of the body until his execution in April 1794.
