# Boise, Idaho

Boise is the capital of the state of Idaho and serves as the county-seat of Ada county. The city is situated in the southwestern part of the state on the north side of the Boise river, at an altitude of approximately 2700 ft. Between 1890 and 1910, the population of the city grew from 2,311 to 17,358.

## Infrastructure and Transportation
The city is served by electric lines that connect it with Nampa and Caldwell. Additionally, it is served by the Oregon Short Line railway, acting as the terminus for a branch that connects to the main line at Nampa, located about 20 m. to the west.

## Principal Buildings and Public Facilities
Boise contains several significant structures, including the state capitol, the United States assay office, a Carnegie library, and a natatorium. The city also features a Federal building, which houses the United States circuit and district court rooms, the post office, and a U.S. land office.

The city is the seat of the state school for the deaf and blind, established in 1906. Just outside the city limits are the state penitentiary and the state soldiers' home. Federal barracks are located approximately 2 m. from the city.

The city's natatorium, as well as many public buildings and residences, utilize hot water (175 deg. F.) sourced from artesian wells located near the city.

## Economy and Industry
Boise serves as an important wool market and the trade centre for the surrounding mining, agricultural, and fruit-growing country. The Boise valley is considered an excellent region for the cultivation of prunes, apples, and other fruits. The city's manufactured products are primarily those required by a mining country, specifically machine-shop products, flour, and lumber.

## History and Development
The present city was chartered in 1864 and dates from 1863, though the oldest settlement in the area was established before 1860 by the Hudson's Bay Fur Company on the west side of the Boise river. After 1900, Boise experienced rapid growth, largely due to great irrigation schemes in southern Idaho. The immense Boise-Payette irrigation system draws its water from the Boise river, 8 m. above the city.
