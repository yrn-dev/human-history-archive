# The Game of 30 and the Rubber

In certain variations of bridge, the "game" is defined as 30 points. The structure of the competition is organized into a "rubber," which is won by the first player or side to win two games.

## Three-Handed Bridge
In three-handed bridge, the game is 30. The rubber is won by the player who is the first to win two games. Scoring for this format is as follows:
*   **Game Points:** 50 points are scored for each game won.
*   **Rubber Points:** An additional 50 points are scored for winning the rubber.

In some instances, three games may be played without reference to a rubber, in which case 50 points are scored for a game won. Regarding the accumulation of points, only tricks won in a player's own deal score toward the game. Tricks won in other deals, along with honours, slam, and chicane, are scored "above the line." At the conclusion of the rubber, all totals are added and points are adjusted between the players.

## Scoring and Game Requirements
In other bridge declarations, the game also consists of 30 points, which must be made by tricks alone. The rubber ends when one side has won two games, and the winners are then entitled to add 100 points to their score.

The value of tricks toward the 30-point game depends on the declaration:
*   **Spade declaration:** Each trick above 6 counts 2 points.
*   **Club declaration:** Each trick above 6 counts 4 points.
*   **Diamond declaration:** Each trick above 6 counts 6 points.
*   **Heart declaration:** Each trick above 6 counts 8 points.
*   **No-trump declaration:** Each trick above 6 counts 12 points.

## Additional Scoring Elements
While the game itself is won via tricks, other points are recorded on the same footing as honours. These include:
*   **Slam:** A "grand slam" (winning all tricks) scores 40 points; a "little slam" (winning 12 tricks) scores 20 points.
*   **Chicane:** Scored if a player holds no trump, in an amount equal to simple honours.
*   **Honours:** In suit declarations, honours consist of the ace, king, queen, knave, and ten. Conjointly holding three honours scores twice the value of a trick, four honours scores four times, and five honours scores five times. Individual holdings of four or five honours in one hand can score 4 or 10 by honours, respectively. In no-trump hands, partners conjointly holding three aces score 30 for honours, four aces score 40, and four aces in a single hand count 100.

At the end of the rubber, the total scores—including those from tricks, honours, chicane, slam, and rubber points—are summed to determine the number of points won based on the difference between the totals.

## Strategic Considerations
When playing toward the score, the value of points varies. In the first and second games of a rubber, a point scored for honours is generally worth about half of a point scored for tricks. However, in the deciding game of the rubber, the importance of winning the game causes the value of honour points to sink to one-third of a point scored for tricks.
