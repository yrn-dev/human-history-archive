## Early Scottish Monarchy
In the context of early Scottish history, the date 860 (or 862) is cited as the year of death for Kenneth I, MacAlpin. Often described as the first king of Scotland (kingdom of Scone), Kenneth I was the son of Alpin, who had been slain by the Picts in either 832 or 834. 

Kenneth I succeeded his father in the kingdom of the Scots. While the exact region of his rule is a matter of conjecture, Galloway is suggested as the most probable location, from which he may have led a piratic host against the Picts. His claims to the Pictish throne, shared with his father, were likely through female descent, with primary support found in Fife.

During his reign, Kenneth I took advantage of a Danish invasion of the Pictish kingdom in 839 or 841 to attack the Picts, eventually subduing them in 844 or 846. His achievements included:
*   The construction of a church at Scone in 846 or 848, to which he transported the relics of St Columba.
*   Six invasions of Northumbria, during which he took Melrose and burnt Dunbar.
*   The reduction of land as far as the Tweed, driving the Britons and Angles over the river; according to the *Scalacronica* of Sir Thomas Gray, this is when he first called his kingdom Scotland.

Kenneth I's reign lasted twenty-eight years. While the chronicle of Henry of Huntingdon states his reign began in 832, the Pictish Chronicle specifies the start date as Tuesday, February 13, which aligns with a reign beginning in 834 and ending in 862. He died at Forteviot and was buried at Iona.
