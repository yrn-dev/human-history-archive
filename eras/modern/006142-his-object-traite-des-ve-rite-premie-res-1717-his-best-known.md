# Traité des vérités premières

The *Traité des vérités premières*, published in 1717, is the best-known work of the French philosopher, historian, and educationalist Claude Buffier (1661–1737). Buffier was born in Poland to French parents before settling in Rouen, where he was educated at a Jesuit college and later joined the order at age nineteen. Following a dispute with the archbishop, he spent the remainder of his life at the Jesuit college in Paris, where he was regarded as an admirable teacher known for his power of lucid exposition.

## Objective and Ultimate Principle of Knowledge
The primary object of the *Traité des vérités premières* is the discovery of the ultimate principle of knowledge. Buffier identifies this principle in the internal sentiment of the self—specifically, the sense of one's own existence and the feelings experienced within oneself. In establishing this foundation, Buffier takes substantially the same ground as Descartes, although he explicitly rejected the use of the *a priori* method.

## The Concept of Common Sense
To understand and identify what exists distinct from the self, Buffier posits the necessity of "common sense." He defines common sense as a disposition placed in all or most men by nature. This disposition enables individuals, upon reaching the age and use of reason, to form a common and uniform judgment regarding objects that are different from the internal sentiment of their own perception. Buffier specifies that this judgment is not the result of any anterior judgment.

## Nature of Eternal Verities
Buffier describes the truths that this natural disposition obliges humans to accept as "eternal verities." These truths possess specific characteristics:
* **Proof:** They can be neither proved nor disproved.
* **Application:** They are followed in practice even by those who reject them from a speculative standpoint.
* **Certainty:** Unlike the absolute certainty found in the knowledge of one's own existence or in logical deductions derived from thought, the truths of common sense possess only the highest probability.

Buffier asserts that while a person who rejects these truths is not guilty of a contradiction, such an individual is to be considered a fool.

## Critical Reception
The writings of Buffier, including the *Traité des vérités premières*, have been characterized by an aversion to scholastic refinements. This approach has led some to view his work as having an appearance of shallowness or a lack of metaphysical insight. Specifically, it is noted that he failed to indicate the nature of the necessity and universality he ascribed to his eternal verities. Despite these criticisms, he is recognized as one of the earliest thinkers to recognize the psychological aspects of knowledge.
