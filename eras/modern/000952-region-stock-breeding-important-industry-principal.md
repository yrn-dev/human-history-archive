# Stock-Breeding and Livestock Industries

Stock-breeding and the raising of livestock serve as vital economic activities across various global regions, ranging from staple agricultural interests to specialized industries focused on wool, meat, and breeding.

## European Livestock Industries
In several European regions, stock-raising is integrated with diverse agricultural practices. In the Marne region, the raising of other stock and sheep of a mixed merino breed, alongside bee-farming, are considered profitable. In other areas, such as certain counties where stock-raising and agriculture are the staple interests, livestock raising is pursued with great enterprise. In these regions, hilly land is particularly well-suited for the industry, though lowland farms are also used for pasturing cattle. These cattle are primarily a native breed that has been improved through crossing. Additionally, a high number of sheep are kept, and while most horses are used for agricultural work, a significant proportion are maintained specifically for breeding.

In other districts, sheep breeding has reached remarkable dimensions. In higher hilly country, Cheviots are the primary breed, while richer low-lying pastures are reserved for half-bred lambs—a cross between Leicesters (or other long-woolled rams) and Cheviots. Horse-breeding is also pursued on a considerable scale in these areas. Historically, some regions raised heavy cattle for English markets, with shorthorns and Ayrshire dairy cattle eventually replacing the previously popular Galloways.

In Ireland, the breeding of horses is a point of significant attention, producing some of the country's best racers. Sheep breeding in these areas includes a small, hardy native race confined to the mountains, while other breeds have been improved via judicious crosses. Pig rearing is also prominent, driven largely by the demands of the Belfast market and subsequent exportation.

## Livestock in Spain and Central America
In the province of Avila in central Spain, stock-breeding is an important industry specifically within the southern division. This region is characterized by rugged granitic sierras and sheltered, well-watered valleys.

In Costa Rica, stock-farming is a relatively undeveloped industry that is currently trending toward greater importance. The state encourages this growth by importing swine, sheep, cattle, and horses from the United States and Europe to improve native breeds. However, the industry faces environmental challenges; in the south-east, farmers must occasionally move their herds and flocks to escape thousands of migratory vampires that can bleed a strong animal to death in a single night.

## South American Stock-Raising
In Bolivia, stock-raising was one of the earliest industries established after mining. The industry's persistence despite unfavorable conditions suggests that the climate and soil are well-suited for it. Cattle are raised in several areas:
* **The Mojos and Chiquitos llanos:** Originally stocked by Jesuit founders of the Mojos missions, these areas provide a steady supply of beef for inland markets.
* **The South:** Large herds of cattle are sustained on the well-watered plains of the Chaco and the more elevated districts of Tarija and Chuquisaca.
* **Other Departments:** Small districts in Cochabamba, Potosi, and La Paz also raise cattle.

Beef is prepared for home markets using *saladeros*, which typically involve sun-drying and salting the meat, while hides are exported. In temperate regions, sheep are raised for wool and goats are kept in both warm and temperate zones. On the colder, higher plateaus, breeding focuses on alpacas and llamas. Additionally, the higher plateaus support the breeding of the chinchilla (*C. laniger*), a native fur-bearing animal. The Bolivian government encourages the production of chinchillas and has prohibited the exportation of live animals. Horses are currently raised in other departments of the temperate region and in La Paz, though they previously failed to flourish in the north due to the introduction of a *peste* from Brazil.

In Maranhão, the southern highlands were once devoted to stock-raising as an important industry. However, this industry has declined due to the failure to introduce new blood into degenerated herds, as well as the presence of vampire bats and troublesome insects.
