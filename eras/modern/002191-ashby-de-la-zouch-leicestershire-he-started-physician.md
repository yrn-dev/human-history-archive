# Ashby-de-la-Zouch

Ashby-de-la-Zouch is a market town located in the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire, England. It is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London and is accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 4,726.

## History and Governance
During the Domesday survey, Ashby-de-la-Zouch was part of the estates belonging to Hugh de Grentmaisnel. Ownership later passed to Robert Beaumeis and subsequently, through female descent, to the family of la Zouch. The town, which had previously been known as Essebi or Ashby, derived the adjunct to its name from this family.

Market rights were formally recorded in 1219 when Roger la Zouch was granted a two-day fair at the feast of St Helen and a weekly market in exchange for a fine of one palfrey. By the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, earl of Ormond. Following Butler's attainder, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings in 1461. In 1474, Hastings received a royal licence to build and fortify a castle and to empark 3,000 acres.

## Ashby Castle
The remains of Ashby Castle are located to the south of the town. The castle served as a place of detention for Mary, queen of Scots, in 1569, while she was under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. During the English Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings and was visited by Charles in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament. The site is also featured in Sir Walter Scott's novel *Ivanhoe*.

## Economy and Infrastructure
In the 18th century, Ashby was recognized as one of England's premier markets for horses. The town also hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings. The neighboring district contains extensive coal mines, such as those at Moira. The Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal runs south from Moira to connect with the Coventry canal.

## Landmarks and Geography
The town's church, St Helen, is a Perpendicular style building that was enlarged and restored in 1880; it houses monuments to the Huntingdon family and an old finger-pillory used for punishing misbehaviour within the church. Other notable landmarks include a Queen Eleanor cross, created by Sir Gilbert Scott to commemorate the countess of Loudoun.

Three miles west of the town at Moira are springs providing saline waters containing bromine. These waters are utilized at the Ivanhoe baths, which were erected in 1826 and are frequented for the treatment of rheumatic and scrofulous complaints.

## Notable Residents
John Bainbridge (1582–1643), an English astronomer, was born in Ashby-de-la-Zouch. Bainbridge began his career as a physician and practiced for several years while also studying astronomy and keeping a school. After moving to London, he became a licentiate of the college of physicians on November 6, 1618. His publication on the comet of 1618 led Sir Henry Savile to appoint him in 1619 to the newly founded Savilian chair of astronomy at Oxford. Bainbridge was incorporated into Merton College and served as senior and junior reader of Linacre's lectures in 1635 and 1631, respectively. His written works include *Canicularia* (1648) and *An Astronomical Description of the late Comet* (1619), and he translated Ptolemy's *De Planetarum Hypothesibus* and Proclus' *De Sphaera* (1620). He died at Oxford on November 3, 1643.

Additionally, the town was home to the Ashby Grammar School. The Rev. Thomas Sheldon Green served as the master of this school and was the father of Alexander Henry Green (1832–1896), an English geologist who received part of his education at his father's school in Ashby-de-la-Zouch.
