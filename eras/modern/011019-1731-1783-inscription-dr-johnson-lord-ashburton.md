# John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton

John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was a prominent English lawyer and politician. Born on October 18, 1731, in Ashburton, Devonshire, he was the second son of an attorney, also named John Dunning. He received his early education at the free grammar school in his native town.

## Legal Career and Rise to Prominence
Dunning began his professional life articled to his father. At the age of nineteen, he was admitted to the Middle Temple and was subsequently called to the bar in 1756. His early entry into practice was slow; for several years while on the western circuit, he did not receive a single brief.

His reputation changed in 1762 when he was employed to draft a defense for the British East India Company against the Dutch East India Company, which had submitted a memorial to the crown regarding various grievances. The "masterly style" of this document brought him immediate emolument and reputation. In 1763, Dunning further distinguished himself as counsel for Wilkes. His reputation was firmly established following his powerful argument against the validity of general warrants in the case of *Leach v. Money* on June 18, 1763. As his practice grew, his income increased significantly, reaching nearly £10,000 per annum by 1776.

## Political Career
Dunning entered politics and was first chosen as the member for Calne in 1768, a borough he represented until his promotion to the peerage. In 1766, he served as the recorder of Bristol, and in December 1767, he was appointed solicitor-general. He held this position until May 1770, when he retired alongside his friend Lord Shelburne. In 1771, he was granted the freedom of the city of London.

During his time in parliament, Dunning was regarded as a regular member of the opposition and was known for delivering many able speeches. In 1780, he carried a motion by a majority of eighteen stating that the influence of the crown had increased, was increasing, and ought to be diminished. While he strongly opposed the system of pensions and sinecure officers, he later accepted a rich sinecure himself in 1782 when the marquis of Rockingham became prime minister, being appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster. Around this time, he was advanced to the peerage as the 1st Baron Ashburton.

## Literary Contributions and Legacy
Beyond his legal and political work, Lord Ashburton is believed to have assisted in writing a pamphlet concerning the law of libel. He is also the author of the 1764 work, *A Letter to the Proprietors of East India Stock, on the subject of Lord Clive's Jaghire, occasioned by his Lordship's Letter on that Subject*. At one point, there was suspicion that he was the author of the *Letters of Junius*.

Lord Ashburton died in 1783. He is remembered in the church of St. Andrew in Ashburton, a 15th-century cruciform building with a lofty tower. The church contains a tablet in his memory, the inscription of which was written by Dr. Johnson.

## The Town of Ashburton
The town of Ashburton, located in Devonshire, England, is a market-town and an ancient stannary town situated in a valley surrounded by hills near the river Dart. Historically governed by a bailiff and portreeve elected at the court leet, it was held in chief by Osbern, bishop of Exeter, according to Domesday. In 1552, the manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough were sold by the bishop and later became crown property before being acquired by the Clinton family.

The town's economy has historically involved the manufacture of serge, as well as breweries, saw-mills, and paint factories. The area is known for a large deposit of umber, and formerly possessed valuable tin and copper mines and slate quarries. The town's grammar school, where John Dunning was educated, was founded as a chantry in 1314.
