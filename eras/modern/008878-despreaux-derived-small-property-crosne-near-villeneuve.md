# Despreaux and the Property at Crosne

## Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux
Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux (1636–1711) was a French critic and poet. He was born in Paris on November 1, 1636, in the rue de Jerusalem. The name Despreaux was derived from a small property located at Crosne, which is near Villeneuve Saint-Georges.

Boileau-Despreaux was the fifteenth child of Gilles Boileau, who served as a clerk in the parlement. Among his siblings, two brothers achieved distinction: Jacques Boileau, who contributed to church history and became a canon of the Sainte-Chapelle, and Gilles Boileau (1631–1669), who authored a translation of Epictetus.

## Early Life and Education
The early years of Nicolas Boileau were marked by difficulty; his mother died when he was two years old. Due to a delicate constitution, it appears he suffered from a lack of care during this period. Sainte-Beuve suggests that the uninspiring circumstances of these early years, combined with the general character of the era, contributed to his unsympathetic and hard outlook. Boileau-Despreaux is described as having grown up without illusions, driven by a single passion: "the hatred of stupid books."

His formal education began at the College de Beauvais, after which he was sent to the Sorbonne to study theology. He eventually abandoned theology in favor of law and was called to the bar on December 4, 1656. However, he soon recoiled from the legal profession in disgust, citing the amount of chicanery present in what was termed law and justice.

## Literary Career
Following the death of his father in 1657, Boileau-Despreaux received a small fortune, which allowed him to devote himself entirely to letters. His life and work were later documented by Pierre Desmaiseaux (1673–1745), a French writer and fellow of the Royal Society, who wrote *Vie de Boileau-Despréaux* in 1712.
