Additionally, the movement began to take hold in Finland and Russia during the 1850s and 1860s.

This development differed from the growth of Baptist churches in Roman Catholic countries, such as France, Italy, and Spain, where churches were established through the efforts of missionaries from England or America.

## Core Baptist Principles and Governance
While the archive's records on the specific administration of Danish churches are limited, the general Baptist movement is distinguished by the belief that baptism should be administered only to believers. Modern Baptists typically utilize immersion as the mode of administration, though some groups, such as the Mennonites, practice pouring or sprinkling.

Regarding church government, Baptists generally align with Congregationalists in the belief that every separate church is complete in itself. This autonomy grants each church the power to make its own regulations and choose its own ministers to advance the kingdom of Christ. While many churches join voluntary associations based on geographical limits, these organizations do not exert authoritative action over the individual churches.

## Global Integration
The Baptist churches formed across Europe, including those in Denmark, are part of a global network. In 1905, the Baptist World Alliance was formed during the first Baptist World Congress in London. The alliance was established to manifest the "essential oneness in the Lord Jesus Christ" among churches of the Baptist order and faith worldwide and to promote cooperation, service, and fellowship while continuing to recognize the independence of each particular church.
