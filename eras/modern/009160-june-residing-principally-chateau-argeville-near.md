# Bolingbroke and the Chateau of Argeville

## Retirement to France
Bolingbroke retired to France in June, having been left baffled and disappointed by the fray. During this period of residence, he stayed principally at the chateau of Argeville, which is located near Fontainebleau. While residing there, he authored the *True Use of Retirement* as well as his *Letters on the Study of History*. The latter work was published in 1752, though it had been printed privately prior to his death.

## Political Activities and English Visits
Despite his retirement, Bolingbroke returned to England on several occasions. In 1738, he became a leading adviser and friend to Frederick, the prince of Wales, who was the head of the opposition. For this purpose, Bolingbroke wrote *The Spirit of Patriotism*, *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I.*, and *The Patriot King*. These essays were entrusted to Pope and remained unpublished at the time. 

After failing to secure a share in politics, Bolingbroke returned to France in 1739 and later sold Dawley. He visited England again in 1742 and 1743, during which time he quarreled with Warburton. In 1744, he settled permanently at Battersea with Hugh Hume, the 3rd earl of Marchmont. During this final period, he was active in assisting negotiations for the establishment of the new "broad bottom" administration and recommended the tutor for Prince George (the future George III). He also expressed no sympathy for the Jacobite expedition of 1745.

## Literary Disputes and Final Years
In May 1744, Bolingbroke was present at the death of Pope. He later discovered that the poet had secretly printed 1,500 copies of *The Patriot King*, prompting Bolingbroke to publish a correct version of the work in 1749. This discovery led to further altercations with Warburton, who defended Pope against Bolingbroke's bitter aspersions. Bolingbroke's conduct during this dispute was generally reprehended, and he published a response titled *Familiar Epistle to the most Impudent Man Living*. Around 1749, he also wrote an unfinished pamphlet called the *Present State of the Nation*.

Bolingbroke died on December 12, 1751; his wife had died previously in 1750. He and his wife were buried in the parish church at Battersea. A monument was erected to their memory featuring inscriptions and medallions composed by Bolingbroke himself. Lord Chesterfield recorded his final words as: "God who placed me here will do what He pleases with me hereafter and He knows best what to do."

## Definition of the Chateau
In the context of French residences, a chateau (from the Latin *castellum*, meaning a fort) refers to any country seat. This usage is a relic of the feudal age. While the term can refer to a small self-contained fortress or a citadel, it is often applied to the principal mansion of a nobleman or prince.
