# BALFOUR, ARTHUR JAMES (1848- )

Arthur James Balfour was a British statesman born on July 25, 1848. He was the eldest son of Lady Blanche Gascoyne Cecil and James Maitland Balfour of Whittingehame, Haddingtonshire. Lady Blanche was the sister of the third marquess of Salisbury. Balfour received his education at Trinity College, Cambridge, and Eton.

## Early Political Career
Balfour entered Parliament in 1874 as a Member of Parliament (M.P.) for Hertford, representing the Conservative interest. He served this constituency until 1885. In the spring of 1878, following the resignation of the fifteenth Lord Derby, Lord Salisbury became foreign minister. At this time, Balfour served as his uncle's private secretary. In this role, he attended the Berlin congress and gained his initial experience in international politics regarding the settlement of the Russo-Turkish conflict.

Following the general election of 1880, Balfour was released from his duties as private secretary and began participating more actively in parliamentary affairs.

## Intellectual Pursuits and the "Fourth Party"
Balfour was known for dividing his time between the study and the political arena, a balance reflecting a class tradition where government responsibilities were viewed as a duty. He gained recognition in the literary world through the publication of *Defence of Philosophic Doubt* in 1879. The literary capacity and intellectual subtlety of this work led some to suggest he might establish a reputation as a speculative thinker.

During his political ascent, Balfour was associated with a group known as the "Fourth Party," which included Sir John (then Mr) Gorst, Sir Henry Drummond Wolff, and Lord Randolph Churchill. The group became notorious due to the aggressive criticisms directed by its leader, Lord Randolph Churchill, against prominent members of the "old gang," such as Lord Cross and Sir Stafford Northcote. Balfour, however, did not take a direct share in these attacks and was perceived as merely amusing himself with politics.

## Public Perception and Associations
In his early career, there were doubts regarding Balfour's suitability for a political career. Observers noted the languor of his manner and the delicacy of his physique, leading to impressions that he lacked the energy of character and bodily strength required for such a path. It was further questioned whether his health could withstand the severity of English winters, despite his great intellectual powers. Consequently, he was viewed as the "odd man" of the Fourth Party.

Balfour was also involved in intellectual circles outside of politics. He was a member of a group that held meetings to read and discuss papers on various subjects, including the moral sciences and the ultimate grounds of belief, as well as the immortality of the soul.

## Family
Arthur James Balfour had a younger brother, Francis Maitland Balfour (1851–1882), who was a British biologist. Francis was born in Edinburgh and educated at Harrow and Trinity College, Cambridge. He became a fellow of Trinity in 1874 and authored a two-volume treatise titled *Comparative Embryology*, published in 1880 and 1881.
