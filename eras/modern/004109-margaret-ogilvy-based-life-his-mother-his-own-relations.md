# Margaret Ogilvy

The title *Margaret Ogilvy* refers to a quasi-autobiographical work written by the British novelist and dramatist James Matthew Barrie. Through this work and his other writings, Barrie provides insight into his early life, his creative development, and the influence of his mother.

## Literary Origins and Early Work
James Matthew Barrie was born on May 9, 1860, in the small village of Kirriemuir, located in Forfarshire. His education took place at the Dumfries academy and Edinburgh University. Barrie's literary inclinations manifested early; he wrote tales in the garret before he began attending school. While studying at Edinburgh, he composed the majority of a three-volume novel. A publisher, assuming the work was written by a "clever lady," offered to publish it for £100, though Barrie did not accept the offer.

Barrie eventually entered the field of literature through journalism. In February 1883, he became a leader-writer for the *Nottingham Journal*, where he also contributed notes and special articles. By November 17, 1884, he began publishing articles in the *St James's Gazette*, starting with a piece titled "An Auld Licht Community." Under the encouragement of editor Frederick Greenwood, Barrie wrote a series of "Auld Licht Idylls." This work eventually led to his move to London in 1885.

## Professional Development and Pseudonyms
During his career in journalism, Barrie contributed to several publications, including *Home Chimes* (edited by F. W. Robinson) and the *Edinburgh Evening Dispatch* (under the enlistment of Alexander Riach). His work for the *Edinburgh Evening Dispatch* led to contributions for Dr Robertson Nicoll's *British Weekly*, where he wrote under the signature "Gavin Ogilvy." He also contributed to the *Speaker* upon its foundation in 1890 and the *Scots* (later the *National*) *Observer*, which was edited by W. E. Henley.

In 1887, Barrie published his first book, *Better Dead*, which was a *jeu d'esprit* based on his humorous journalism from the *St James's Gazette*. This was followed in 1888 by *Auld Licht Idylls*, a collection of sketches centered on a Scots village.

## Influence of the Mother
The sketches in *Auld Licht Idylls* portrayed the humours and life of Barrie's native village, which he idealized in his writing as "Thrums." These works were the result of his early observations and the stories told to him by his mother. Barrie noted that his mother "told me everything," and as a result, his own memories of their "little red town" were colored by the memories of his mother.
