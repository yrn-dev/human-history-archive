# The Vernacular Writings of George Buchanan

## Overview and Composition
The *Vernacular Writings* of George Buchanan comprise a specific collection of works written in the native tongue rather than in Latin. This collection includes the *Chamaeleon*, a tract focused on the Reformation of St Andrews University, and a piece titled *Ane Admonitioun to the Trew Lordis*. Additionally, the collection contains two letters. These specific writings were edited for the Scottish Text Society by P. Hume Brown.

## Editions and Publications
The works of George Buchanan have been preserved in two primary editions. The first, titled *Georgii Buchanani Scoti, Poetarum sui seculi facile principis, Opera Omnia*, was edited by Ruddiman and published in Edinburgh by Freebairn in 1715 as two folio volumes. A second edition was edited by Burman and published in 1725 in quarto format.

## Biographical and Critical Studies
Several principal biographies have been written to document the life and work of Buchanan. David Irving published *Memoirs of the Life and Writings of George Buchanan* in 1807 and 1817 in Edinburgh. P. Hume Brown authored two significant works: *George Buchanan, Humanist and Reformer* (1890) and *George Buchanan and his Times* (1906), both published in Edinburgh. Additionally, the Rev. D. Macmillan published *George Buchanan, a Biography* in Edinburgh in 1906.

In 1906, Buchanan's quatercentenary was celebrated across various centers in Scotland. This event prompted several studies and encomia, most notably *George Buchanan: Glasgow Quatercentenary Studies* (1906) and *George Buchanan, a Memoir*, which was edited by D.A. Millar and published in St Andrews in 1907.

## Related Works and Legal Records
The *Glasgow Quatercentenary Studies* contains discussions regarding the authorship of a verse translation of the *Baptistes* titled *Tyrannicall-Government Anatomized* (1642), which some have attributed to Milton. 

Furthermore, the records of Buchanan's trial were discovered by the Portuguese historian G.J.C. Henriques. These were published in 1906 in Lisbon under the title *George Buchanan in the Lisbon Inquisition. The Records of his Trial, with a Translation thereof into English, Facsimiles of some of the Papers, and an Introduction*.
