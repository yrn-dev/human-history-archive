# Louis XIV

Louis XIV was the monarch of France whose reign lasted until 1715. His rule was characterized by an absolutist system of government, which he later introduced to Belgium.

## Diplomatic Relations and Treaties
Louis XIV engaged in extensive diplomacy and territorial negotiations throughout his reign. In 1662, Hugues de Lionne negotiated the treaty of Montmartre with Charles, the duke of Lorraine, through which Louis XIV purchased the succession to the duchy for a life-rent; however, the Lorrainers refused to ratify this agreement.

In 1670, Louis XIV concluded a secret treaty with Charles II of England. This agreement, which took shape in the treaties of Dover, was negotiated by Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour, who had been sent to France by Charles II for this purpose. Under the terms of this secret treaty, Charles II undertook to subordinate English foreign policy entirely to that of France in exchange for an annual pension of £100,000.

Louis XIV also maintained complex relations with the electorate of Cologne. In 1672, intrigues by the Furstenberg brothers led to a treaty of offensive alliance between the French monarchy and the electorate. As a reward for his services to the French cause, Louis XIV later appointed William Egon as bishop of Strassburg in 1682, obtained a cardinal's hat for him from Pope Innocent XI in 1686, and sought his election as coadjutor-archbishop of Cologne and successor to the elector Maximilian Henry in 1688.

## Domestic Policy and Social Order
Known as "le Grand Monarque," Louis XIV took a firm stance against the practice of dueling. During his reign, eleven edicts against duelling were issued. A 1643 edict established a supreme court of honour consisting of the marshals of France, and a more famous edict in 1679 confirmed the previous enactments of Louis XII and Henry IV. This was accompanied by a solemn agreement among the principal nobility to never engage in duels. The king's refusal to pardon offenders is credited with helping to restrain the practice.

In the arts, Louis XIV supported playwrights such as Corneille. Despite the failure of some later works, Corneille published verses in 1676 thanking the king for ordering the revival of his plays.

## Personal Life and Family
Louis XIV married Marie Thérèse, the daughter of Philip IV of Spain and Elizabeth of France, in June 1660. The marriage had been stipulated in the treaty of the Pyrenees (1659), in which Marie Thérèse renounced any claim to the Spanish succession. While Marie Thérèse was described as pious and humble, she was often neglected by Louis XIV in favor of mistresses, including Louise de la Vallière and Mme. de Montespan. Later in life, the influence of Madame de Maintenon led Louis XIV to bestow more attention on his wife. Marie Thérèse served as regent in 1672 during Louis XIV's campaign in Holland. Of their six children, only the dauphin Louis survived her, though he died in 1711.

## The Spanish Succession and Belgium
The death of Charles II, the last heir of the Hapsburg dynasty in Spain, in 1700, led to a succession crisis. Charles II's will named Philip of Bourbon, the grandson of Louis XIV, as his successor and the sovereign of the Netherlands. In practice, Louis XIV governed in Philip's name, introducing the French absolutist system to Belgium, which was organized by French generals and the Count of Bergeyck.

This period of French influence ended with the War of the Spanish Succession, which began in 1702 and saw England and the United Provinces ally against Louis XIV. The conflict was terminated by three treaties between 1713 and 1714: the treaties of Utrecht, Rastadt, and Bade. These agreements determined that Belgium (the Netherlands) would be transferred to the Austrian branch of the Hapsburgs to serve as a barrier protecting the United Provinces from French threats. This was finalized by the Treaty of Antwerp (the "Treaty of the Barriers") in 1715 and the Hague Convention of 1718.
