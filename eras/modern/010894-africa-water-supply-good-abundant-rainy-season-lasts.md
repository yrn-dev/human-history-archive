# Africa

## Regional Geography and Water Systems
The African continent contains diverse landscapes with varying water availability. In the Ashanti region, the water supply is described as good and abundant. This area features Lake Busumchwi, a sacred, nearly circular lake with a maximum diameter of 6 miles, surrounded by forest-clad hills reaching heights of 800 feet. The northern frontier is formed by the Black Volta and the Volta, with various tributaries traversing the eastern portion of the country. Other rivers in the region include the Ofin, the Prah, the Tano, and the Bia; with the exception of the Volta, these rivers are navigable only by canoes.

In contrast, Kordofan consists largely of riverless, barren, and monotonous undulating plains. While it lacks perennial rivers, it contains watercourses known as *khors* or *wadis* that flow during the rainy season, such as the Khor Abu Habl in the south-central region. In the district of Dar Homr, the Khor Shalango and Wadi el Ghalla drain toward the Homr affluent of the Bahr el Ghazal. Due to porous soil and rapid evaporation, the surface of Kordofan dries quickly. However, water that filters through granitic sand settles on mica schist, allowing a water supply to be obtained by sinking wells to the solid rock. The Dar Nuba district in the southeast is well-watered and provides a contrast to the rest of the country.

In Darfur, a state of the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, the landscape is characterized by a plateau and the volcanic Jebel Marra mountains. The region is scored by numerous *khors* that once contained large rivers. While no streams in Darfur are perennial, those in the west, south, and southwest contain turbulent torrents during the rainy season, occasionally reaching the Bahr-el-Homr.

## Climate and Seasonal Patterns
Rainfall patterns vary significantly across different African regions:

*   **Ashanti:** The rainy season lasts from the end of May until October, characterized by frequent and violent storms. The mean annual rainfall is 40 inches, and the mean temperature at Kumasi is 76 deg. F.
*   **Kordofan:** The steppes are green during the *kharif* (rainy season) but appear dull brown and burnt-up at other times.
*   **Darfur:** The rainy season lasts for three months, from the middle of June to the middle of September. In the south, rains are heavy and the soil consists of damp clay.

In other parts of the continent, such as German East Africa, the rainy season has historically impacted military movements. In 1917, the onset of the rainy season stopped the advance of forces across the Rufiji River and the valley of the Ulanga and Ruhuje rivers until June of the following year.

## Flora, Fauna, and Agriculture
Vegetation is often tied to the presence of water and seasonal rains. In Darfur, the most fertile land is found on mountain slopes, where grains such as wheat, durra, and *dukhn* (a type of millet and the staple food) are grown. Other products include onions, cucumbers, water-melons, sesame, and cotton. The chief trees in the neighborhood of the *khors* are the baobab and acacias, the latter of which provide gum.

The continent's wildlife includes specialized species adapted to these fluctuating water levels. The African lung fish lives in tropical regions with distinct rainy and dry seasons. During the rainy season, it breathes via gills. When rivers evaporate into ditches of baked mud during the dry season, the fish buries itself in the mud and survives by swallowing air into its swimming-bladder.

## Topography and Geology
The elevation of the Ashanti region is generally below 2000 feet, rising toward the north. Kordofan features an average altitude of 1500 feet, with isolated peaks like Jebel Kordofan and Jebel Abu Senum rising 150 to 600 feet above the plain. The mountain groups of Kaja and Katul reach 2000 to 3000 feet, while some Nuba hills exceed 3000 feet. Geologically, Kordofan is composed of a granitic sand upper stratum with mica and feldspar, resting upon an unbroken surface of mica schist.

Darfur's plateau sits between 2000 and 3000 feet above sea-level. The Jebel Marra range reaches heights of 5000 to 6000 feet. Other notable features include Jebel Medob (3500 feet) and Bir-el-Melh, an extinct volcano with a 150-foot deep crater.
