# Hornby Vellard and the City of Bombay

## Geography of the Bombay Peninsula
The Bombay Peninsula, also referred to as Bombay Island, is characterized by a coast of lofty hills and a harbour featuring precipices and rocky islands with peaks of great height. The peninsula consists of a plain approximately 3 miles broad and 11 miles long, which is flanked by two parallel lines of low hills. 

The harbour is located on the eastern side, enclosed by a neck of land stretching toward the south-west that protects the water—which ranges from 5 to 7 miles in width—from the open sea. To the south-west of the island is Back Bay, a shallow basin roughly 2 miles wide that extends inland for about 3 miles between the two ranges of hills.

## The Hornby Vellard and Land Reclamation
The nucleus of the city of Bombay is situated on a slightly raised strip of land located between the harbour and the head of Back Bay. From this point, the land slopes westward toward a central plain. This central plain is a low-lying tract of land that, prior to the construction of the embankment known as the Hornby Vellard, was subject to the tides and used to be submerged by the sea at high tide.

## Urban Characteristics of the Town
The town of Bombay is composed of spacious streets dedicated to European commerce and native bazaars that are described as unusually handsome and well-built. Within the native bazaars, houses typically rise three or four storeys and feature front work and pillars that are elaborately carved.

European commercial buildings and hotels in the city are built on an American scale, with no rivals in any other city in India. A notable example is the Taj Mahal hotel, the most modern and palatial hotel in India, which was constructed by the Tata family in 1904.

## Residential Areas and Suburbs
The private residences of European residents are situated apart from the mercantile and native quarters. These homes are generally built within large compounds or gardens. While the architecture of these residences is less imposing than the stately homes found in Calcutta, they are considered beautiful, comfortable, and well-suited to the local climate. 

The most popular suburb is Malabar hill. This is a high ridge that runs out into the sea and is terraced at the top with handsome houses that provide one of the finest views of its kind.
