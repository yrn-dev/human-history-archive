# Lonsdale and Laura Ragg

Lonsdale and Laura Ragg are the editors and translators of a specific version of the Gospel of Barnabas. In 1907, they produced an edition of the text that included an English translation.

## Analysis of the Gospel of Barnabas
The Gospel of Barnabas edited by the Raggs is a longish gospel found in a single Italian manuscript. The text is written from a Mahommedan standpoint, though it likely incorporates materials that are Gnostic in origin and character. The work is described as being highly imaginative and frequently grotesque, while simultaneously being pervaded by an unusually high ethical enthusiasm.

The Raggs hold the view that the text was the work of a Christian renegade to Mahommedanism. They date the creation of the work to approximately the 13th through 16th century.

## History of the Manuscript
The Italian manuscript was discovered in a private collection in Amsterdam by the Deist John Toland, who discussed it in his 1718 work, *Nazarenus*. Following Toland, the manuscript came into the possession of Prince Eugene of Savoy. It was eventually acquired by the imperial library at Vienna as part of Eugene's library.

The Raggs' findings contradict an older view held by Toland and others, who believed the Italian text was a translation from an Arabic original. This older theory is considered demonstrably wrong. The manuscript contains Arabic marginal notes, which appear to be a combination of notes intended to assist students of the Arabic language and pious ejaculations.
