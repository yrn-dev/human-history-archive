# Prince Eugene and the Austrian Campaigns

Prince Eugene of Savoy was a prominent military commander whose career spanned several major European conflicts, including the War of the Spanish Succession and various campaigns against the Ottoman Empire. His leadership was instrumental in securing Austrian interests in the Balkans and the Netherlands.

## Campaigns Against the Ottoman Empire

The struggle between Austria and the Ottoman Empire was of significant importance to the monarchy, determining the Austrian relationship with Turkey and the Slav peoples of the Balkans for a century. Following a period of instability in Hungary—marked by the suppression of the constitution in 1658 and the persecution of Protestants—the Turks burst into Hungary in 1683. They overran the country and reached the walls of Vienna, where they were defeated on September 12 by John Sobieski.

This victory initiated a series of successes that eventually drove Ottoman power from Hungary. A key achievement of Prince Eugene in this theater occurred in 1717, when he conquered Belgrade for Austria. Belgrade, located at the confluence of the Save and the Danube, had been a strategic "key of Hungary" and was known to the Turks as *Darol-i-Jehad* ("the home of wars for faith"). Under Austrian control from 1717 until 1739, the city's fortifications were improved and its commercial development was greatly stimulated.

Prince Eugene's influence extended to the training and recommendation of other officers. He recommended Ludwig Andreas Khevenhüller to the sovereign as the best general in the service in 1737. Khevenhüller had previously served under Prince Eugene in the War of the Spanish Succession and commanded Eugene's own regiment of dragoons by 1716.

## The War of the Spanish Succession and the Netherlands

Prince Eugene also played a major role in the War of the Spanish Succession. During this conflict, the allies faced the forces of Louis XIV, who had taken possession of the Netherlands. In 1712, near the town of Denain in northern France, Prince Eugene commanded the allies in a battle where they suffered a decisive victory at the hands of Marshal Villars.

Following the general peace concluded at Utrecht on April 11, 1713, the Belgic provinces were placed under the sovereignty of Emperor Charles VI, becoming known as the Austrian Netherlands for the next century. Prince Eugene's influence continued in this region through his deputies; the marquis de Prie served as the imperial governor from 1719 to 1726 as a deputy for Prince Eugene. During this tenure, the marquis de Prie attempted to rule the Netherlands as an Austrian dependency, facing vigorous resistance from local authorities and town gilds, which led to the beheading of the Brussels popular leader Francis Anneesens.

## Military Legacy and Associates

Prince Eugene's military operations attracted various international figures. For example, Louis Auguste de Bourbon (1700-1755), the prince of Dombes, served in Prince Eugene's army against the Turks in 1717. 

The broader context of the era's warfare included the long reign of Leopold I (who succeeded Ferdinand in 1657 and ruled for 48 years). Leopold's tenure was defined by the struggle with France and the campaigns against the Turks (1662-64 and 1683-99). While earlier victories, such as Montecuculi's victory at St Gothard on August 1, 1664, were limited by the peace of Vasvar, the later campaigns led by figures like Prince Eugene were more definitive in shifting the balance of power in Europe and the Balkans.
