# The Distributing Reservoir at Calles Córdoba and Viamonte

## Overview and Capacity
The distributing reservoir located at Calles Córdoba and Viamonte is a primary component of the water-supply infrastructure in Buenos Aires. The facility covers an area of four acres and possesses a total capacity of 13,500,000 gallons.

## Water Supply System
The reservoir serves as the destination for water drawn from the estuary at Belgrano. Before reaching the Córdoba and Viamonte reservoir, the water is conducted 3½ miles to the Recoleta. At the Recoleta, the system utilizes six acres of covered filters and three large settling basins, which have an aggregate capacity of 12,000,000 gallons. From these basins, the water is pumped into the distributing reservoir.

## Historical Context and Development
The construction of these water works began in 1873. Prior to this date, the city lacked public sewers, resulting in a sanitary state described as indescribably bad. These poor conditions intensified several major health crises, including:
*   The cholera epidemic of 1867-1868, which resulted in 15,000 victims.
*   The yellow fever epidemic of 1871, which resulted in 26,000 victims.

The water and drainage projects were initiated by English contractors and engineers. The construction of the sewer system took approximately 19 years. By 1892, the government took over the drainage and water works, and they are currently administered at a profit and at public expense.

## System Specifications and Impact
The water and drainage systems involved a total cost exceeding six million sterling. A key feature of the infrastructure is the main sewer, which extends 16 miles in length, reaching southward beyond Quilmes.

The implementation of these works significantly improved the sanitary condition of Buenos Aires, allowing it to compare favorably with other great cities. This improvement is reflected in the annual death rate, which was approximately 18 per thousand, a decrease from 27 per thousand in 1887.
