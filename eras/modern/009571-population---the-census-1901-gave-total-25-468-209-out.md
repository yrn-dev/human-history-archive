# Global and Regional Population Studies (c. 1901-1906)

## Global and Imperial Populations
The total population of the world is calculated to be over 1,500,000,000, a figure that includes a liberal estimate of 650,000,000 people who have not been included in any census. Of this global total, the British Empire accounts for more than one-fourth. The empire's population is estimated at approximately 400,000,000, consisting of a white population of over 53,000,000 (roughly one-eighth of the total) and a native population of 335,672,317.

The white population of the empire, which is mainly of Anglo-Saxon race but includes Spanish, Dutch, and French peoples, is distributed as follows:
*   United Kingdom and home dependencies: 41,608,791
*   British North America: 5,500,000
*   Australasia: 4,662,000
*   Africa (British and Dutch): 1,000,000
*   West Indies and Bermuda: 100,000
*   India: 169,677

The native populations of the empire include various brown, yellow, and black races. In India, the native population—described as mainly high-type brown—was recorded in the 1901 census as 294,191,379. Other native populations include East Africa (7,425,000), West Africa (18,863,000), South Africa (5,211,329), British Central Africa (2,000,000), Ceylon and Eastern Colonies (5,144,954), the West Indies (1,912,655), Australasia and Islands (824,000), and Canada (100,000 Red Indians).

## Population of China
The population of China has not been exactly ascertained. A 1902 census reportedly yielded a total of 410,000,000, though some estimates suggest 300,000,000 may be more accurate. Earlier estimates from 1842 placed the figure at over 400,000,000. Historically, a census from A.D. 156 recorded the population at slightly over 50,000,000. The Chinese census process involves issuing house-tickets to heads of households; however, because there is no fixed return date, the results are considered approximate.

## Regional Studies: India and Burma
In the Bombay Presidency, the 1901 census recorded a total population of 25,468,209. Literacy rates in this region were low; of the approximately 25.5 million people, nearly 24 million were illiterate.

In Calcutta, the population grew from an estimated 12,000 in 1710 to 117,000 in 1752. By the 1831 census, it reached 187,000, and by 1839, 229,000. The 1901 census recorded 949,144 inhabitants. In that year, the population of the town and suburbs included 615,000 Hindus, 286,000 Mahommedans, and 38,000 Christians.

In Burma, the 1901 census indicated that only 9.4% of the population was urban, with a significant portion of those being natives of India. Nearly two-thirds of the total population are engaged in agriculture or related occupations.

## Population of Bulgaria
The population of united Bulgaria was 3,154,375 in 1888, 3,310,713 in January 1893, and 3,744,283 in January 1901. A census on January 12, 1906, recorded the population of united Bulgaria at 4,028,239, with 2,853,704 in northern Bulgaria and 1,174,535 in Eastern Rumelia.

The 1901 census for Bulgaria provided the following demographic details:
*   **Sex:** 1,909,567 males and 1,834,716 females.
*   **Age:** 2,719 persons were aged 100 years or older.
*   **Occupation:** 74.85% (2,802,603 persons) were engaged in agriculture; 360,834 in productive industries; 148,899 in commerce; and 118,824 in liberal professions or government service.
*   **Language/Nationality:** Bulgars (2,888,219), Turks (531,240), Gipsies (89,549), Rumans (71,063), Greeks (66,635), Spanish-speaking Jews (33,661), Tatars (18,884), Armenians (14,581), and other nationalities (30,451).

Bulgarian inhabitants outside the principality are estimated at 1,500,000 to 1,600,000, bringing the possible grand total of the race to 5,500,000.

## Central American Population
In a region where the first census in 1791 recorded only 95,500 people, an official 1905 estimate placed the aggregate population at 500,136. The population density in 1905 was fewer than 11 inhabitants per sq. m. The Spanish population is concentrated on the Pacific slope, while the Atlantic declivity is sparsely occupied by Indian tribes of unknown numbers.

The capital, Tegucigalpa, had a population of about 35,000 in 1905. Other towns with over 10,000 inhabitants include Jutigalpa (18,000), Nacaome, La Esperanza, Choluteca, and Santa Rosa. Comayagua had 8,000, while the seaports of Amapala and Trujillo had 4,000 each, and Puerto Cortes had 2,500.
