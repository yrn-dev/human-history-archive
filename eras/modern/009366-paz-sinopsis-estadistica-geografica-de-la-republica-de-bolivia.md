# Sinopsis estadística y geográfica de la República de Bolivia

The Republic of Bolivia is a landlocked nation characterized by diverse climatic zones, varied agricultural resources, and a complex history of territorial and diplomatic relations with its neighbors. According to Gotha computations, adjusted for territory lost to Brazil in 1903, the total area of the republic is 515,156 square miles.

## Geography and Climate
Bolivia exhibits a wide range of temperatures across different elevations. The native population classifies the environment into five distinct climatic zones:

*   **Yungas:** Comprising lowlands and mountain valleys up to 5,000 feet, this tropical region is exceedingly humid with no known winter. The official mean temperature is 70°F. Malaria and fevers are common here due to decaying vegetation on the ground.
*   **Valle:** This sub-tropical zone consists of deep valleys between 5,000 and 9,500 feet. It is characterized by moderate temperature variations, no cold weather, and is sometimes called a region of perpetual summer.
*   **Cabezera de valle:** Located at the heads of deep valleys between 9,500 and 11,000 feet, this temperate region has regular seasons favorable for vegetables and cereals.
*   **Puna:** This zone includes the great central plateau between 11,000 and 12,500 feet. The air is cold and dry, with only two seasons (winter and a cold summer or autumn). The mean temperature is officially estimated at 54°F, and the short growing season only permits the production of barley and potatoes.
*   **Puna brava:** Extending from 12,500 feet to the snow limit at approximately 17,500 feet, this bleak territory is inhabited only by miners and shepherds. Above this lies the Arctic zone of eternal snow.

## Hydrography
The Beni River is a major tributary of the Madeira, originating near La Paz as the Rio de La Paz. It flows east and north-east, passing through mountainous districts of central Bolivia as a raging torrent before entering the forest-covered hills and open plains of the north. The lower river is navigable for 217 miles from Reyes to the Esperanza rapids. 

The Madre de Dios (or Mayu-tata) is a more important river than the Beni in terms of size and tributaries. It rises east of Cuzco and flows through northern Bolivia to join the Beni 120 miles above its mouth. During the wet season, the Madre de Dios is navigable to the foot of the Andes.

## Agriculture and Resources
Agricultural development in Bolivia has been slow. In the warm and temperate Andean valleys, cereals, fruits, and vegetables are cultivated. Sugar-cane is grown primarily in eastern Bolivia, though it is heavily taxed and used mainly for rum production. Other eastern products include rice and fair-quality tobacco. 

Cacao of a superior grade is grown in the department of Beni and the Andean valleys of Cochabamba and La Paz. Coffee is produced in the warmer districts of the eastern Andes. Two indigenous products are of particular importance: quinoa, a staple food for natives, and coca, which is masticated by natives and exported for medicinal use; coca is grown extensively in La Paz and Cochabamba, specifically in the Yungas province.

## Urban Centers and Administration
The republic's population is concentrated in provincial capitals. Four cities have served as the national capital: Sucre (Chuquisaca), La Paz, Cochabamba, and Oruro. While Sucre was the chosen capital, La Paz became the capital after the revolution of 1898, serving as the commercial metropolis.

Other notable towns include:
*   **Pulacayo:** A silver mining center in the department of Potosi with a population of 6,512.
*   **Uyuni:** A junction for highways and railways in Potosi (pop. 1,587).
*   **Tupiza:** A mining and commercial center near the Argentine frontier (pop. 1,644).
*   **Viacha:** A railway station 14 miles from Alto de La Paz (pop. 1,670).
*   **Tarata and Totora:** Trading centers in Cochabamba.
*   **Ascension:** A mission station in the Chiquitos hills of Santa Cruz (pop. 4,784).

## Communications and Infrastructure
Bolivia has historically been an isolated region. Until 1903, railway infrastructure was limited to the Antofagasta and Oruro line (350 miles within Bolivia), a 26-mile branch to Pulacayo, and a 54-mile line from Guaqui to Alto de La Paz. The latter is connected to La Paz via a 5-mile electric line.

To improve access, treaties were signed with Brazil (1903) and Chile (1904). Brazil agreed to build a 180-mile railway around the Madeira falls and provided £2,000,000 for internal Bolivian rail construction. Chile agreed to build a 295-mile railway from Arica to La Paz and provide financial assistance for interior lines. Argentina also undertook to extend its northern railway from Jujuy to Tupiza.

## Territorial and Political History
Bolivia's maritime access was lost following the war with Chile (1878–1881). A peace treaty signed on December 11, 1883, saw Bolivia cede its entire sea-coast, including the port of Cobija. Subsequent disputes over the provinces of Tacna and Arica remained unresolved for years. 

Under Presidents Pando and Ismail Montes (who took office in 1904), Bolivia reached a final settlement with Chile ratified in 1905. Bolivia ceded the maritime provinces occupied since 1881, remaining without a seaport. In exchange, Chile agreed to build the Arica-to-La Paz railway at its own cost, provide free transit to certain coastal towns, and pay a cash indemnity.
