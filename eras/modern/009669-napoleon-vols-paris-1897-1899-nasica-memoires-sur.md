# Napoleon and the Bonaparte Family

The Bonaparte family is the subject of extensive historical documentation, with various scholarly works detailing their origins, the lives of its leading members, and the reign of Napoleon I.

## The Bonaparte Family and Early Life
The origins and genealogy of the Bonaparte family, including Carlo and Letizia, have been explored in several texts, such as the *Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte* (1846) and *Le antichita dei Bonaparte* (1857). The family's history from its beginnings until 1860 is detailed by L. Ambrosini and A. Huard, while C. Leynadier provides a history spanning from the year 1050 to 1848 (continued to 1866 by de la Brugere).

Specific studies on the immediate family of Napoleon I include A. Kleinschmidt's work on his parents and siblings (1876) and D.A. Bingham's two-volume study on the marriages of the Bonapartes (1881). The youth of Napoleon is documented in A. Chuquet's three-volume *La Jeunesse de Napoleon* (1897-1899) and T. Nasica's *Memoires sur l'enfance et la jeunesse de Napoleon jusqu'a la age vingt-trois ans*, which includes a historical notice regarding Napoleon's father. Additionally, Baron H. Larrey wrote a two-volume work titled *Madame Mere* (1892), and Clara Tschudi authored *Napoleons Mutter* (1901).

## Siblings of Napoleon I
Napoleon I had several brothers and sisters. Among them were:

*   **Joseph Bonaparte:** His political and military correspondence and memoirs are documented in a ten-volume set by Baron A. du Casse (1854). Other sources include J.S.C. Abbott's *History of Joseph Bonaparte* (1869) and G. Bertin's work on Joseph's time in America.
*   **Lucien Bonaparte:** Lucien lived in Italy, primarily in Rome, where he exhibited marked hostility toward the emperor. In December 1807, the emperor proposed that Lucien become a French prince if he annulled his marriage, a condition Lucien refused. After living at his estate of Canino—where he took the title of prince of Canino—he departed for America. He was captured by a British ship and taken to Malta and then England, where he remained under surveillance until the peace of 1814. During the Hundred Days in 1815, he returned to Rome and offered Napoleon his help, appearing by his side at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris and defending his prerogatives during the second abdication. Lucien died in Rome on June 29, 1840, leaving behind four sons and six daughters. His writings include the epic *Charlemagne, ou l'Eglise delivree* (1814), as well as unfinished memoirs and *La Verite sur les Cent Jours*.
*   **Louis Bonaparte:** The life and reign of Louis are covered in works such as the *Documents historiques et reflexions sur le gouvernement de Hollande* by le comte de Saint-Leu (1820) and studies by T. Jorissen regarding the period between 1806 and 1813.
*   **Jerome Bonaparte:** Documentation on Jerome includes the *Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine Catherine* (1861-1866) and A. Martinet's *Jerome Napoleon, roi de Westfalie* (1902).
*   **Elisa and Pauline:** These sisters are listed among the siblings of Napoleon I.
*   **Maria Annunciata Caroline (1782-1839):** Born in Ajaccio on March 25, 1782, Caroline married Joachim Murat in early 1800. Through her ambitious nature, she advanced Murat's career; he served as governor of Paris, marshal of France (1804), grand duke of Berg and Cleves (1806), and lieutenant of the emperor in Spain (1808), eventually becoming king of Naples in the summer of 1808. Caroline's relations with Napoleon became strained due to her displeasure with the distance between Naples and Paris. She associated herself with her husband's equivocal movements in 1814-1815. Following Murat's death at Pizzo on October 13, 1815, she retired to Austrian territory under restraint and later lived in Trieste with her sister Elisa. She died on May 18, 1839.

## Later Years and Captivity
The final phase of Napoleon's life and his captivity at St Helena are documented in several works, including W. Forsyth's three-volume history (1853), the *Journal inédite de Sainte-Hélène* by Gourgaud (1815-1818), and R. C. Seaton's study on the relationship between Napoleon and Sir Hudson Lowe (1903).
