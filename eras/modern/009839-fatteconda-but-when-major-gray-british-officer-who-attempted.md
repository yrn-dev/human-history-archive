# Bondu

## Geography and Environment
Bondu is a French protectorate located in West Africa, dependent on the colony of Senegal. It is situated between the upper course of the Gambia and the Faleme river, specifically between 13 and 15 degrees North and 12 and 13 degrees West. The region consists of an elevated plateau. While the southern and central parts of the country are hilly and generally unproductive—covered in stunted wood—the lower regions are fertile. These lower areas are characterized by the presence of various valuable fruit-trees, as well as the tamarind and the baobab. The landscape is traversed by torrents known in West Africa as *marigots*, which flow rapidly during the rainy season but remain empty during the dry season.

## Population and Culture
The inhabitants of Bondu are primarily Fula, although Mandingos largely control the trade. The laws and religion of the country are Mahommedan, though the precepts of the faith are not observed with great rigor.

## Political History and European Contact
Mungo Park was the first European traveller to visit Bondu, passing through the region in 1795. During his visit, he was subjected to many exactions by the reigning prince. At that time, the royal residence was located at Fatteconda. By 1818, when Major W. Gray—a British officer who attempted to solve the Niger problem—visited the country, the royal residence had been moved to Bulibani. Bulibani is described as a small town with a population of approximately 3,000, enclosed by a strong wall made of clay.

In August 1845, the king of Bondu signed a treaty that recognized French sovereignty over the country. Although the natives disregarded this treaty, Bondu eventually came under definitive French control in 1858. Following this transition, the country experienced considerable prosperity.
