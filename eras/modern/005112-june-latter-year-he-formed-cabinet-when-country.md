# Eugène Henri Brisson

Eugène Henri Brisson (1835– ) was a French statesman born in Bourges on July 31, 1835. He began his professional life as an advocate, following the profession of his father. During the final days of the empire, Brisson became conspicuous for his role in the opposition. Following the overthrow of the empire, he was appointed as the deputy-mayor of Paris.

## Political Career and Early Legislative Work
Brisson was elected to the Assembly on February 8, 1871, representing the extreme Left. Although he did not approve of the Commune, he was the first person to propose amnesty for those condemned, though this proposal was voted down on September 13, 1871. Throughout his career, Brisson was a firm anti-clerical and a strong supporter of obligatory primary education.

He served as the president of the chamber from 1881, replacing Gambetta, until March 1885. At that time, he became prime minister following the resignation of Jules Ferry. However, Brisson resigned from the premiership after the general elections of 1885, as he was only able to just obtain a majority for the vote of credit regarding the Tongking expedition.

## Later Public Service and the Presidency
Brisson remained a prominent public figure, playing a significant role in the exposure of the Panama scandals. In 1894, following the murder of President Carnot, he was a powerful candidate for the presidency. He subsequently served again as the president of the chamber from December 1894 until 1898.

In June 1898, Brisson formed a cabinet during a period when the country was violently excited over the Dreyfus affair. His honesty and firmness during this tenure increased the respect held for him by good citizens. However, his ministry was overthrown in October of that year due to a chance vote occurring during a moment of especial excitement.

## Radical Leadership and Later Years
As one of the leaders of the radicals, Brisson provided active support to the ministries of Combes and Waldeck-Rousseau. His support was particularly focused on the separation of church and state and the laws concerning religious orders. 

Brisson was a candidate for the presidency in 1899. In May 1906, he was elected president of the chamber of deputies, receiving 500 out of 581 votes.
