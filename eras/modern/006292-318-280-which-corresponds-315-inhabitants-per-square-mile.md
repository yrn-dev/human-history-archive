# Bohemia

## Population and Demographics
In 1900, Bohemia had a population of 6,318,280 people. This population size placed it in the second position among the Austrian provinces, trailing only Galicia. In terms of population density, Bohemia ranked third, behind Lower Austria (which includes Vienna) and Silesia; its density was recorded at 315 inhabitants per square mile. This represented a significant increase from 1800, when the population was slightly over 3,000,000.

The population is divided by nationality into Czechs and Germans. Czechs make up approximately 65% of the population and are primarily located in the south, south-east, and the middle of the country. Germans comprise about 35% of the population and are concentrated in the large towns and near the borders, particularly in the west and north. There are also German-speaking enclaves located within purely Czech districts, while Czechs have tended to move into manufacturing and mining districts that are purely German.

## Religion and Administration
The vast majority of the population, 96%, belongs to the Roman Catholic Church. Administratively, the region is divided into one archbishopric, located in Prague, and three bishoprics: Leitmeritz, Koniggratz, and Budweis.

## Economy and Migration
Bohemia is characterized by great industrial development and rich natural resources. Despite these economic factors, the region experiences a steady flow of emigrants. These individuals settle in various locations, including Russia, Germany, and other provinces of the monarchy, or they travel across the Atlantic to settle in America.
