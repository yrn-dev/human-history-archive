# Johann Christian Bach

Johann Christian Bach (1735–1782) was a composer and musician, born in Leipzig as the eleventh son of Johann Sebastian Bach. His career was marked by significant periods of study and professional service in Germany, Italy, and England.

## Early Education and Italian Period
Following the death of his father in 1750, Johann Christian Bach became a pupil of his brother, Emanuel, in Berlin. In 1754, he traveled to Italy to further his musical education under the tutelage of Padre Martini. His time in Italy led to a professional appointment from 1760 to 1762, during which he served as the organist at the Milan cathedral. During this tenure, he composed several works, including a *Te Deum*, a *Requiem*, and two Masses.

## Career in London
Bach gained a reputation as a composer of opera, which resulted in an invitation to London in 1762. He remained in London for the rest of his life, becoming the most popular musician in England over the course of twenty years. His dramatic works were produced at the King's theatre, where they were received with great cordiality. 

His professional achievements in London included an appointment as music-master to the queen. Additionally, he entered into a partnership with Abel to provide concerts at the Hanover Square rooms; these events quickly became the most fashionable of public entertainments.

## Musical Style and Historical Significance
Johann Christian Bach is noted for his historical role as the first composer to prefer the pianoforte over older keyed instruments. While his works are described as pleasing and elegant, they are characterized as ephemeral and have been deservedly forgotten.

## Bibliographic References
A full account of the career of J. C. Bach is contained in the fourth volume of Burney's *History of Music*. Furthermore, a catalogue of his compositions was published by Max Schwarz in the *Sammelbaende* of the *Internationale Musik-Gesellschaft* (Jhrg. ii. p. 401). A list of his voluminous compositions is also available in Eitner's *Quellen Lexikon*, and a critical account of these works is provided in Bitter's *C. P. E. und W. F. Bach und deren Bruder* (published in Berlin, 1868, in two volumes).
