# Budapest

## Government and Administration
Budapest serves as the seat of the Hungarian government, the parliament, and all highest official authorities, including those that are financial, judicial, military, and civil. The city is administered by an elected municipal council consisting of 400 members. 

Historically, the political, social, and intellectual life of Hungary has been centered in Budapest, particularly since 1848, when it became the seat of the legislature. The development of the city as a brilliant and attractive Magyar capital was strongly encouraged by Count Stephan Széchényi, a great reformer of the pre-Revolutionary period, who sought to unite the intellectual flower and nobles of the country in service of the Fatherland.

## Social and Cultural Status
Budapest is considered the intellectual and artistic center of Hungary due to its cultural institutions. It is often compared to Paris in its relationship to France, as Budapest is seen as a reflection of Hungary itself. The city's composite population mirrors the heterogeneous elements found within the dominions of the Habsburgs. 

The city has also been a focal point for the movement to Magyarize all institutions, a process that included the removal of German names from streets and buildings. The progress of the city is attributed to the restoration of the constitution and the revival of the Hungarian national spirit during the first half of the 19th century.

## Economic and Political Position
Trade and industry in Hungary are centralized in Budapest to a degree rarely seen in other European capitals. In 1867, the city became a favored rival to Vienna. By 1892, Budapest was elected to an equality with Vienna as a royal residence. Additionally, Budapest serves as a meeting place for Austro-Hungarian delegations, alternating this role with Vienna.

## Religious Status
Budapest is the see of a Roman Catholic archbishop.
