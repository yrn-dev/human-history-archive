# Legislative Assembly and the Accusation of the Directory

The Legislative Assembly was a governing body in France that replaced the National Assembly under a new constitution. Its formation was marked by political maneuvering between the royalists and the Jacobins. Seeking to weaken the moderates, the Jacobins proposed that members of the National Assembly be ineligible for election to the Legislative Assembly. The royalists supported this proposal, believing that a Legislative Assembly stripped of experience would be politically incompetent, thereby allowing France to fall back into the hands of its legitimate masters. However, the royalists failed to account for the influence of the strongly Jacobin Commune of Paris, located at the Hôtel de Ville.

## Charles Jean Marie Barbaroux and the Department of Bouches-du-Rhone

Charles Jean Marie Barbaroux was a French revolutionist and successful advocate who had been educated by the Oratorians of Marseilles and served as the secretary (*greffier*) to the commune of Marseilles. In 1792, Barbaroux was commissioned to travel to the Legislative Assembly for a specific legal purpose: to demand the accusation of the directory of the department of Bouches-du-Rhone. This demand was based on the allegation that the directory had acted as an accomplice in a royalist movement located in Arles.

While in Paris, Barbaroux established relations with the Rolands and J. P. Brissot, and was received into the Jacobin club. He is credited with instigating Marseilles to send a battalion of volunteers to Paris, a force that contributed to the insurrection against the king on August 10, 1792.

## Other Figures of the Legislative Assembly and Convention

Several other statesmen served within the Legislative Assembly and the subsequent Convention. Ange Elisabeth Louis Antoine Bonnier was a member of both the Legislative Assembly and the Convention. Following his time in these bodies, he served as a diplomat during the Directory, taking missions to Lille and the congress of Rastadt. In April 1799, Bonnier was killed by Hungarian hussars at the gates of Rastadt.

Francois Antoine de Boissy d'Anglas, a former advocate to the parlement and deputy to the states-general, also served during the period of the Legislative Assembly. During his tenure in the Assembly, he held the position of *procureur-syndic* for the directory of the department of Ardeche. Later, in the Convention, he sat with the center group known as "*le Marais*."

## Transition to the Directory and Later Republics

The political landscape shifted rapidly following the era of the Legislative Assembly and the Convention. By 1794, the Jacobin republic ended with the execution of Robespierre, leading to the rule of the Convention. In 1795, the Directory was established. 

Under the Directory, various figures held different roles. For example, the individual known as Louvet was elected as a member and secretary of the Council of Five Hundred, as well as a member of the Institute. However, he faced harassment from the *jeunesse dorée* in Paris, which eventually forced him to leave the city. The Directory appointed him to a consulship at Palermo, though he died in August 1797 before he could take the post.

The Directory period also saw the rise of Bonaparte, who suppressed a revolt in 1795 and served as commander-in-chief in Italy. By 1799, Bonaparte returned to France and became First Consul with enormous powers, eventually becoming Emperor in 1804.
