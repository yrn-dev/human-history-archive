# Valparaiso

Valparaiso is a city in Chile that has served as a significant maritime center and a point of diplomatic and military activity in South American history.

## Naval and Military History
The city has been a focal point for various naval operations and the careers of military figures. Patricio Lynch (1825–1886), a prominent Chilean naval hero, was born in Valparaiso on December 18, 1825. Lynch entered the navy in 1837 and later served as the maritime prefect of Valparaiso.

In the mid-19th century, the city served as a place of retirement for political figures. General Ballivian, who held the presidency of Bolivia until 1848, retired to Valparaiso in the end of that year.

## World War I Naval Activity
During the early 20th century, Valparaiso was a site of strategic importance during naval conflicts involving British and German forces. In 1914, the British Admiralty received critical intelligence regarding the location of German ships via the Consul-General in Valparaiso. On November 3, 1914, a communication from Maclean in Valparaiso informed the Admiralty that a Chilean Admiral had reported a naval engagement. According to this report, German ships—including the *Gneisenau*, *Scharnhorst*, and *Nürnberg*—had met the *Good Hope*, *Glasgow*, *Monmouth*, and *Otranto* in "thick and wicked weather" on a Sunday at sunset. The report indicated that the *Monmouth* sank after approximately one hour of fighting, and the *Good Hope* was believed to have sunk following an explosion.

Admiral von Spee, leading the German squadron, spent a few days at Valparaiso before departing. During his stay, he was greeted with fervent acclamations from the local German colony. Despite these honors, von Spee remained mindful of his danger, remarking of the flowers presented to him, "They will do for my funeral."

Later, Admiral Sturdee and his squadron utilized Valparaiso as a coaling station. While Sturdee was coaling, German ships were sighted on the horizon. The *Canopus* fired the first shots of the engagement from a stationary position on the inner harbour's mudbanks. Following this, the German squadron, including the *Gneisenau* and a light cruiser, retreated at full speed in a westerly direction. Admiral Sturdee subsequently exited the harbour in the *Invincible*, followed by the *Inflexible*, *Cornwall*, *Kent*, *Carnarvon*, and *Glasgow*, while the light cruiser *Bristol* pursued the enemy.

## Local Publications and Figures
Valparaiso has been a site for the publication of historical and biographical works. In 1886, the work *Rasgos biograficos de hombres notables de Chile* by J.B. Suarez, which provides biographical sketches of prominent Chilean characters, was published in the city.
