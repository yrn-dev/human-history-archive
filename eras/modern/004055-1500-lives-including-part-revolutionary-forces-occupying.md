# The 1812 Destruction of Barquisimeto

## Geography and Foundation
Barquisimeto is a city located in western Venezuela and serves as the capital of the state of Lara. It is situated on the Barquisimeto river within a small, fertile valley of the Merida Cordilleras, at an elevation of 1,985 feet above sea-level. The city is located 101 miles by rail southwest of Tucanas, which is its port on the Caribbean coast. 

The city was founded in 1522 by Juan de Villegas, who was exploring the area in search of gold. Upon its founding, Villegas named the city Nueva Segovia, after his own native city. Barquisimeto is characterized by a healthy, temperate climate with a mean annual temperature of 78 degrees Fahrenheit. It is surrounded by a highly productive region that exports rum, cacao, sugar, and coffee, and the city itself functions as an important distributing center for the neighboring districts.

## Urban Development and Institutions
The city is regularly laid out, well built, and serves as the seat of a bishopric. It is well provided with charitable and educational institutions. By 1807, the population of Barquisimeto had grown to 15,000 people, a growth driven principally by the city's commercial importance. By 1899, the estimated population had risen to 40,000.

## The Earthquake of 1812
On March 26, 1812, Barquisimeto was totally destroyed by an earthquake. This disaster resulted in the loss of 1,500 lives. Among the deceased were a part of the revolutionary forces that were occupying the town at the time of the event.

## Recovery and Aftermath
Following the total destruction of the city, Barquisimeto was soon rebuilt. It is noted as being one of the few cities in Venezuela to have recovered from the subsequent disorders and the ravages of the war of independence.
