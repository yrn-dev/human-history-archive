# General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions

The General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions, commonly referred to as the "Triennial Convention," was a national organization established to coordinate Baptist missionary efforts in the United States.

## Origins and Establishment
The foundation of the Triennial Convention was preceded by a deep interest in missionary work among Baptist communities in Philadelphia, Charleston, Boston, and other areas during the early 19th century. These groups provided liberal financial support to the English missionary William Carey and his coadjutors working in India.

A pivotal moment in American Baptist history occurred in 1812 with the conversion of Luther Rice and Adoniram Judson to Baptist views. Both men had been sent to India by the newly-formed American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions. Following this conversion, Judson called upon his American brethren to support missionary work among the heathen, while Rice returned to the United States to organize missionary societies and generate interest in Judson's mission. This led to the formation of "The Baptist Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in India and other Foreign Parts" in Boston in January 1813, with similar societies quickly appearing across the Southern, Middle, and Eastern states.

## Organization and Governance
Recognizing the need for a national organization, thirty-three delegates representing eleven states convened in Philadelphia in May 1814. At this meeting, they organized the General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions. Because the organization scheduled its meetings to occur every three years, it became known as the Triennial Convention.

As part of its administrative structure, a Board of Commissioners was appointed. This board established its headquarters in Philadelphia, although these headquarters were later transferred to Boston in 1826.

## Educational and Home Mission Initiatives
The Triennial Convention identified a profound need for a larger supply of educated ministers to serve both in home and mission work. This necessity resulted in the 1821 establishment of Columbian College in Washington (now known as George Washington University), which included a theological department and was intended to serve as a national Baptist institution.

In addition to foreign missions, the Convention expanded its scope to address destitution on the frontiers. Starting in 1817, the Triennial Convention engaged extensively in home mission work. This effort eventually led to the constitution of the American Baptist Home Mission Society in 1832 to further promote these activities. To support these educational and missionary enterprises, Luther Rice worked to establish an organ for the dissemination of information and the quickening of interest.
