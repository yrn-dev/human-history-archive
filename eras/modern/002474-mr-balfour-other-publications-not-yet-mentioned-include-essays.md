# Arthur Balfour

Arthur Balfour was a prominent Conservative politician who served as the Prime Minister of England and held various high-ranking governmental and academic positions during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Political Career and Premiership
Mr. Balfour's early political career involved serving as president of the Local Government Board during Lord Salisbury's first administration (June 1885–January 1886) and as secretary for Scotland with a cabinet seat during the second administration (July 1886). He later served as chief secretary for Ireland, where he implemented a policy of "coercion" through the fearless administration of the Crimes Act, combined with remedial legislation. During this tenure, he is noted for breaking down the Plan of Campaign in Ireland and opposing the Home Rule party in parliament.

On July 11, 1902, Mr. Balfour succeeded Lord Salisbury as prime minister with the approval of all sections of the Unionist party. In his initial cabinet reconstitution, he assumed the roles of lord privy seal and first lord of the treasury. His premiership lasted three and a half years, a period marked by significant foreign and domestic challenges. He eventually resigned on December 4, 1905, following a period of internal party strife and a speech in Newcastle on November 14 appealing for party unity. Following his resignation, he was defeated by a large majority in Manchester during the subsequent general election.

## Fiscal Policy and the Unionist Struggle
A central conflict of Mr. Balfour's premiership was the struggle within the Unionist party regarding fiscal policy. Mr. Chamberlain advocated for a budget reorganization that included taxes on imports, including food, to stimulate employment and improve the position of the working classes. This was opposed by free-trade Unionists, including the duke of Devonshire, who formed the Free Food league.

Mr. Balfour initially maintained a position of "dialectical dexterity," avoiding a definitive expression of his fiscal opinions. However, on September 16, he published a pamphlet titled "Insular Free Trade." In this manifesto, he reviewed economic history since the time of Cobden, argued that England remained the only free-importing country, and advocated for the "freedom to negotiate" to increase freedom of exchange. 

The tension peaked on October 1, when Mr. Balfour spoke at Sheffield, declaring his intention to lead and his readiness to reverse traditional fiscal policy by removing the axiom that import duties should only be levied for revenue purposes. This stance led to the resignation of the duke of Devonshire.

## Publications and Academic Honors
Beyond his political pamphlets, Mr. Balfour authored several books, including *Essays and Addresses* (1893) and *The Foundations of Belief, being Notes introductory to the Study of Theology* (1895).

He received numerous honorary degrees and academic appointments:
* **Doctorates:** LL.D. from Edinburgh University (1881), St Andrews University (1885), Cambridge University (1888), and Dublin and Glasgow Universities (1891); D.C.L. from Oxford University (1891).
* **University Leadership:** Lord rector of St Andrews University (1886) and Glasgow University (1890); chancellor of Edinburgh University (1891); and member of the senate of London University (1888).

## Personal Interests and Affiliations
Mr. Balfour was a fellow of the Royal Society, appointed in 1888, and served as president of the British Association in 1904. From an early age, he was recognized as a cultured musician. He was also an enthusiastic golf player and served as the captain of the Royal and Antient Golf Club of St Andrews from 1894 to 1895.
