# The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College

## Overview
*The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College* is an endowed historical series published by Randolph-Macon College. The college is a Methodist Episcopal, South institution and is recognized as one of the oldest Methodist Episcopal colleges in the United States.

## Institutional History and Location
Randolph-Macon College received its charter in 1830 and opened its doors in 1832. Its original location was near Boydton in Mecklenburg County, Virginia. In 1868, the institution was moved to Ashland, a village in Hanover County, Virginia, situated 17 miles northwest of Richmond. 

By the 1907-1908 academic year, the college maintained a faculty of 16 and an enrollment of 150 students.

## The Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies
Randolph-Macon College is a constituent part of the "Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies." This system is managed by a single board of trustees to ensure that the work of the preparatory schools is correlated with that of the colleges. The system includes the following institutions:

*   **Randolph-Macon College:** Located in Ashland, Virginia.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Bedford City):** Established in 1890 for boys in Bedford City, Virginia.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Front Royal):** Established in 1892 for boys in Front Royal, Virginia.
*   **Randolph-Macon Woman's College:** Opened in 1893 in Lynchburg, Virginia. This college is conducted by a self-perpetuating board under the auspices of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South. In 1907-1908, it had an enrollment of 390 students.
*   **Randolph-Macon Institute:** A school for girls located in Danville, Virginia, which was admitted into the system in 1897.
