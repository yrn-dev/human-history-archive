# BARNAVE, ANTOINE PIERRE JOSEPH MARIE (1761-1793)

Antoine Pierre Joseph Marie Barnave was a prominent orator during the first French Revolution. Born on October 22, 1761, in Grenoble, Dauphine, he was born into a Protestant family. His father served as an advocate at the parlement of Grenoble, and his mother was described as a woman of noble character, superior ability, and high birth. Due to his Protestant faith, Barnave was unable to attend school and was instead educated by his mother. This upbringing produced a man who was studious, thoughtful, social, passionate, graceful in manners, and handsome in person.

## Early Career and Political Rise
Barnave pursued a career in law. At twenty-two years of age, he gained recognition through a discourse he delivered before the local parlement concerning the division of political powers. As the excitement of the coming revolution began to be felt in Dauphine, one of the first provinces to experience it, Barnave became a leading voice for the general sentiment. He expressed these views in a pamphlet titled *Esprit des edits enregistres militairement le 20 mai 1788*.

Following the publication of his pamphlet, Barnave and his father were elected as deputies to the states of Dauphine, where Barnave played a prominent role in the debates. His political influence expanded further when the states-general were convoked at Versailles for May 5, 1789. Barnave was chosen as a deputy of the *tiers etat* representing his native province.

## Role in the National Assembly
Upon entering the Assembly, Barnave quickly made an impression and established friendships with most of the leaders of the popular party. He formed a group known as "the triumvirate" during the Constituent Assembly, alongside Alexandre Lameth and Adrien Duport. 

Barnave's contributions to the early revolutionary government were significant:
* He participated in the conference regarding the claims of the three orders.
* He was responsible for drawing up the first address to the king.
* He supported the proposal made by Sieyes for the Assembly to declare itself National.

Additionally, until 1791, Barnave was a principal member of the club that later became known as the Jacobins. He was responsible for drafting the first rules and the manifesto of the club.

## Political Philosophy and Conflict
Barnave was a passionate lover of liberty, yet he maintained the hope that he could simultaneously secure the monarchy and the freedom of France. Despite these goals, he was carried away by the prevailing currents of the era. Consequently, he participated in attacks directed at the clergy, the monarchy, the provincial parlements, and church property.
