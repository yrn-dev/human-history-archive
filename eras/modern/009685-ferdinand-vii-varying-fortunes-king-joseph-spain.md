# Ferdinand VII

Ferdinand VII was the *de facto* king of Spain during the early 19th century, whose treatment by Napoleon Bonaparte served as a catalyst for the failure of reconciliation between the Spanish people and the French.

## Conflict with Joseph Bonaparte
In May 1808, Napoleon called Joseph Bonaparte to take the crown of Spain. Although Joseph issued a proclamation on June 23, 1808, announcing benevolent intentions toward the Spaniards, reconciliation proved impossible due to Napoleon's treatment of Ferdinand VII. 

Joseph Bonaparte's subsequent sovereignty was largely titular. Following the Spanish success at Baylen, Joseph was forced to leave Madrid hastily in August 1808, though Napoleon reinstated him by the end of that year. Joseph remained in a subordinate position, offering to abdicate on four separate occasions, though Napoleon ignored these requests and ordered him to govern with more energy. By 1810, Joseph's authority was further diminished when the emperor placed the north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals as military districts. 

Joseph's position became that of a "mere instrument of a military power," and he barely retained authority over a small portion of the French army as a general. Facing an exhausted treasury, Joseph traveled to Paris in April 1811 to seek better terms or offer his abdication. He returned to Spain with a monthly subsidy of 500,000 francs and control over the army of the centre. However, following Wellington's victory at Salamanca on July 22, 1812, Joseph was compelled to leave his capital.

## Restoration and Royal Orders
Ferdinand VII was restored to power in 1814. Upon his restoration, he took several actions regarding the Spanish orders of knighthood and merit:

*   **Order of Knights of Alcantara:** During his reign as king in 1808, Joseph Bonaparte had deprived the knights of this order of their revenues. These revenues were only partially recovered upon the restoration of Ferdinand VII in 1814.
*   **Royal and Illustrious Order of Charles III:** This order, founded in 1771, had been abolished by Joseph Bonaparte in 1809 (along with all Spanish orders except the Golden Fleece). Ferdinand VII revived the order in 1814.
*   **Order of St Ermenegild:** This order was established in 1814.

## Later Years and Marriage
Ferdinand VII was the groom in a marriage to Maria Christina. In 1829, a physician to the Royal Commission sent by the king of Naples to attend this marriage introduced homoeopathy to Spain.
