# Germans of All Parties

## Political Representation in Austria
In the context of Austrian political allotments, "Germans of all parties" were assigned 233 seats, an increase from a previous allotment of 205. Despite this number, the German constituencies were left in a permanent minority when compared to the 259 seats held by Slavs. Even when combined with "Latin" seats (representing Italians and Rumanians), the resulting "German-Latin block" totaled only 257 seats. In practice, this block ceased to exist as Italians began to cooperate with the Slavs rather than the Germans.

The allotment of these seats was intended to formally overthrow the fiction that Austria was predominantly a German country, establishing instead that it was a country predominantly Slav with a German facade and dynasty. While the German constituencies were allotted a proportion that was considered unduly favorable, the German parties stipulated that a two-thirds majority would be required for any future alterations to the law, anticipating that any revision would be to their disadvantage.

## Evolution of the German Opposition
Beginning around 1870, the character of the German opposition in Austria shifted toward an avowedly racial focus, prioritizing the defense of German nationality. This movement originated at the University of Vienna with the formation of a *Leseverein* (reading club) for German students, which was eventually suppressed. 

This political movement was characterized by a disregard for constitutionalism and "legal mummies," focusing instead on the preservation and extension of their nationality. Key features of this opposition included:
* **External Influence:** The movement was strongly influenced by figures outside of Austria. Bismarck was regarded as their national hero, and the anniversary of Sedan served as their political festival.
* **Political Goals:** Some representatives, such as Herr von Schoenerer, declared that Germans looked forward to union with the German empire, valuing approximation to Germany over the maintenance of Austria.
* **Language and Statehood:** In 1881, the various German parties in opposition formed a club known as the United Left. Their program demanded that German be expressly recognized as the official language and asserted that the defense of the German position was a necessary condition for the existence of the state.

## Internal Divisions and Conflict
The German political landscape was marked by bitterness and national animosity, which prevented the formation of a strong, homogeneous party. Tensions existed between the older constitutional leaders and younger, more ardent spirits who believed the party leadership lacked decisiveness in measures of self-defense.

A notable point of contention occurred in 1885 when the government forbade festivities in Graz honoring the eightieth birthday of Bismarck. This caused significant indignation among the Germans of Styria, who felt the party did not respond with sufficient energy. Following the elections of 1885, the United Left broke up into two separate entities.

## Historical Military and External Conflicts
Beyond the domestic politics of Austria, German forces were involved in various historical conflicts in Bohemia and the West. In 1421, following the deposition of Sigismund, a provisional government was established in Bohemia. Subsequent years saw German invasions of the region; Bohemians achieved a decisive victory over the Germans at Aussig in 1426. In 1427, several German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites, accompanied by 1,000 English archers under Henry Beaufort, bishop of Winchester. These crusaders fled into Bavaria following panics at Tachau and Mies.

In later military contexts, German armies operated in the West, with records noting the presence of Germans in locations such as Rheims and Romagne. Military figures included Field-Marshal von Hindenburg, known for his victories, and Crown Prince Rupprecht of Bavaria.
