# Province of Buenos Aires

The province of Buenos Aires is a maritime region of Argentina in South America. It is the largest and most populous province in the republic, with an area of 117,812 square miles and an official population estimate of 1,251,000 as of 1903 (excluding the federal district). It is bounded to the north by the provinces of Entre Rios and Santa Fé, to the east by the Atlantic and the La Plata estuary, to the south by the Atlantic, and to the west by the provinces of Santa Fé and Córdoba, as well as the territories of Las Pampas and Rio Negro.

## Early Colonial Foundation
The establishment of a colony at the site of Buenos Aires required three separate attempts. The first occurred in 1535, led by Don Pedro de Mendoza. This expedition from Spain failed completely due to mismanagement and hostility from the Indians; the colonists eventually abandoned the settlement, known as "Santa Maria de Buenos Ayres," and moved to Asunción, which Mendoza's expedition had founded further up the river. A second attempt in 1542, led by a portion of a Spanish expedition under Cabeza de Vaca, met with similarly little success.

The third and successful attempt was made in 1580 by Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay. Garay had previously established a post at Santa Fé in 1573. His 1580 expedition down the Paraná was driven by the need for a port near the sea to provision ships and receive supplies from Spain. Garay constructed a fort and laid out a town in the Spanish style, naming it "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad," though he kept Mendoza's descriptive name for the port because of the invigorating and agreeable atmosphere.

## Colonial and Early National History
Until 1620, Buenos Aires remained a dependency of Asunción. In that year, the Spanish settlements of the La Plata region were divided into three provinces: Tucuman, Paraguay, and Buenos Aires. The city of Buenos Aires became the capital of its province and the seat of a new bishopric. While trade and population grew, Spain initially showed little interest in the station, as the gold and silver of Peru remained the primary focus of the king and merchants. This neglect, combined with an unprotected coast and proximity to Portuguese settlements in Brazil, facilitated smuggling.

Following the War of Independence, during which patriots from Buenos Aires worked to liberate the interior provinces of the Rio de la Plata from Spanish dominion, the province entered a period of internal political struggle. For two generations, the early history of the province was characterized by a struggle for supremacy over the other provinces.

## Political Development and Administration
Buenos Aires became a province of the Confederation in 1820. In 1854, it adopted a constitution establishing a government consisting of a legislature of two chambers and a governor, both of whom are chosen by popular vote. 

In 1880, an unsuccessful revolt against the national government resulted in the federalization of the city of Buenos Aires. As a consequence, La Plata was selected as the provincial capital. To provide indemnification, the republic assumed the public indebtedness of the provinces. However, before the new capital was completed, the province incurred additional liabilities totaling ten million sterling, which has since hindered its development.

## Territorial Expansion and Geography
The province secured its large territory through successive conquests of adjoining Indian territories to the west and south, with the final additions occurring as late as 1879. 

While the province has over 900 miles of frontage on the Atlantic and the La Plata, it possesses few natural ports. The best natural port is Bahia Blanca, where the government has built a naval port (the Puerto Militar). Other significant ports include Ensenada (La Plata), which features artificial basins for ocean-going steamers, and a costly artificial port at Buenos Aires. San Nicolas also serves as a fairly good river port in the extreme north.

## Urban Centers and Economy
The city of Buenos Aires is the principal manufacturing center of the republic, with thousands of industrial establishments. Its residents, known as Porteños, refer to the city as the "Paris of America." The city features the Casa Rosada (government-house), which partially occupies the site of Garay's 1580 fort, and a congress hall finished in 1906.

Other significant towns and cities in the province include:
* **La Plata**: The provincial capital.
* **Bahia Blanca**: A major port for the export of wool and wheat from the southern districts and the national territories of Neuquen and La Pampa.
* **San Nicolas**: A river port on the Paraná.
* **Campana**: The site of Argentina's first mutton-freezing factory for export (1883).
* **Chivilcoy**: An important interior town.
* **Pergamino**: A northern inland railway center.
* **Mar del Plata**: A popular seaside resort.
* **Barracas al Sud**: Formerly the center of the jerked beef industries.

The province's economy is heavily supported by its fertile soil and an extensive network of trunk and branch railways that converge at the ports of Ensenada and Buenos Aires to facilitate transatlantic trade.
