# Theodore Lotz of Pressburg

Theodore Lotz of Pressburg was an instrumentalist and maker active during the late 18th century. He is primarily recognized for his contributions to the development of the basset horn and his status as a celebrated performer of the instrument.

## Contributions to the Basset Horn
The basset horn was developed from the same archetypes as the clarinet, emerging from a desire in the 16th and 17th centuries to create complete families of instruments for concert performance. The instrument's invention is attributed to a clarinet maker from Passau named Horn in 1770. 

In 1782, Theodore Lotz of Pressburg introduced modifications to the instrument. These changes were followed by further improvements made by two Viennese instrumentalists, Johann and Anton Stadler. The evolution of the instrument continued until 1812, when the famous clarinettist Iwan Mueller used the basset horn as a basis to invent the alto clarinet in Eb. Mueller achieved this by applying a construction and fingering modeled after the clarinet in Bb, rather than the clarinet in C.

## Performance and Influence
During the end of the 18th century, the basset horn flourished predominantly in Germany. It became the preferred solo instrument for several noted instrumentalists of the era, including Springer, Czerny, and Lotz.

The instrument was highly appreciated by great masters of music. Mozart, in particular, utilized the basset horn with significant effect across various compositions:
* **Operas:** In *La Clemenza di Tito*, Mozart wrote a fine obbligato for the instrument in the aria "Non piu di Flori," and he also employed it in *Zauberfloete*.
* **Chamber Music:** Mozart composed a short adagio for bassoon and two basset horns, as well as another piece for three basset horns and two clarinets.
* **Sacred Works:** In his *Requiem*, the reed family is represented by two bassoons and two basset horns, which are given independent parts.

Other composers who utilized the instrument include Beethoven, who used it in his *Prometheus* overture, and Mendelssohn, who employed it in military music and two concerted pieces for basset horn and clarinet with pianoforte accompaniment (opp. 113 and 114).
