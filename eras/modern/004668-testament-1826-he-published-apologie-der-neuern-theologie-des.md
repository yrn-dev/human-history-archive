# Testament

The study and exegesis of the New Testament in the 19th century involved significant scholarly debate regarding authorship, authenticity, and the intersection of rationalism and supernaturalism.

## Contributions of Bretschneider
A notable figure in the science of exegesis was Bretschneider, who produced the *Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum in libros Novi Testamenti* (first published in 1824, with a third edition in 1840). This lexicon was considered valuable due to the author's use of several sources to illustrate New Testament language, including the apostolic fathers, Josephus, the Old and New Testament Apocrypha, and the Greek of the Septuagint.

Bretschneider also engaged in debates concerning the Gospel of St John. In 1820, he published *Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine*, a treatise that discussed arguments against Johannine authorship with marked moderation. The work drew significant attention and prompted various replies. However, in the preface to the 1822 second edition of his *Dogmatik*, Bretschneider stated that he had never actually doubted the authenticity of the gospel. He claimed the *Probabilia* was published specifically to draw attention to the subject and encourage a more complete defense of its genuineness. Despite this clarification, the work had professional consequences; Bretschneider noted in his autobiography that it prevented him from succeeding Karl C. Tittmann in Dresden, as the minister Detlev von Einsiedel (1773-1861) denounced him as the "slanderer of John" (*Johannisschänder*).

## Theological Positions and Rationalism
In 1826, Bretschneider published *Apologie der neuern Theologie des evangelischen Deutschlands*. This work served as a response to Hugh James Rose, who had published a volume of sermons in England in 1825 titled *The State of the Protestant Religion in Germany*. Rose had categorized Bretschneider as a rationalist. In his *Apologie*, Bretschneider contended that he was not a rationalist in the ordinary sense, but rather a "rational supernaturalist." 

Bretschneider's dogmatic position was viewed as intermediate. On one side were the extreme school of naturalists, including Julius Wegscheider, J.F. Röhr, and Heinrich Paulus. On the other side were F.C. Baur and D.F. Strauss. While Bretschneider recognized a supernatural element in the Bible, he allowed for a full application of rational inquiry.

## Other New Testament Scholarship
Other theologians contributed to the study of the New Testament and related ecclesiastical history during this era. Christoph Ernst Luthardt (1823-1902), a strictly orthodox German Lutheran theologian, served as a professor of New Testament exegesis and systematic theology at Leipzig starting in 1856. He is recognized as the author of *Apologie des Christentums*, a series of apologetic lectures published in four volumes between 1864 and 1896.

In the realm of biblical theology, Christian Friedrich Schmid (1794-1852) authored the *Biblische Theologie des Neuen Testamentes*. Schmid was known as one of the most vigorous opponents of F.C. Baur. His influence extended to Isaac August Dorner (1809-1884), whom Schmid encouraged to write *Entwicklungsgeschichte der Lehre von der Person Christi* (first part published in 1835, completed in 1839). This work served as an indirect reply to the *Life of Jesus* published by Dorner's colleague, Strauss.

Additionally, Gottfried Christian Friedrich Lücke (1791-1855) contributed to the field with his 1816 publication, *Grundriss der Neutestamentlichen Hermeneutik*. Lücke later became a professor at Göttingen and helped found the *Theologische Studien und Kritiken*, which was the primary organ for "mediation" theology (*Vermittelungstheologie*).
