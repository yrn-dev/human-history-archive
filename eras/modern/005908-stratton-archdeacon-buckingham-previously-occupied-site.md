# Buckingham

Buckingham is a municipal borough, market town, and the county town of Buckinghamshire, England. Located 61 miles northwest of London, the town is situated in an open valley on the upper portion of the river Ouse, which encircles the main part of the town on three sides. As of 1901, the population of the town was 3,152.

## Town Infrastructure and Governance
The borough is governed by a mayor, 12 councillors, and 4 aldermen, covering an area of 5,006 acres. The town is accessible via a branch of the London & North-Western railway and is served by a branch of the Grand Junction Canal. 

Economically, Buckingham supports an agricultural trade and several industries, including flour-mills, maltings, and the manufacture of artificial manure and condensed milk. A modified version of an old industry, the manufacture of pillow-lace, also survives in the town.

## Historic Buildings and Sites
The town contains several structures of historical significance:
*   **The Church of St Peter and St Paul:** An 18th-century church that stands on the site of the old castle. It underwent extensive restoration by Sir Gilbert Scott, who was a native of the area.
*   **The Grammar School:** Founded by Edward VI, the school partially occupies older buildings featuring a Norman door and windows in the Decorated and Perpendicular styles. The site was previously occupied by a chantry founded in 1268 by Matthew Stratton, the archdeacon of Buckingham. Additionally, the Norman elements may be remnants of a chapel belonging to a gild of the Holy Trinity.
*   **The Town Hall:** This structure dates from the end of the 18th century.
*   **The Manor House:** This building dates from the early 17th century, and other old houses remain in the town.
*   **Stowe:** An adjacent mansion that served as the seat of the dukes of Buckingham until the title became extinct in 1889. It is surrounded by beautifully laid out gardens and is approached from the town via a magnificent avenue of elms.

## Titles of Buckingham
The history of the titles associated with Buckingham is complex, involving several different lines:

**The Early Earldoms**
The origin of the earldom of Buckingham is obscure, though some charter evidence suggests it existed under William Rufus. Walter Giffard, lord of Longueville in Normandy, is regarded as the first earl, described as "Comes Bucchingehamensis" in 1097 and 1102. Following the death of the 2nd earl, Walter Giffard, in 1164, the title was assumed by Richard de Clare, earl of Pembroke, through his wife Rohais. The title died with Richard in 1176.

**The House of Woodstock and Stafford**
In 1377, Thomas of "Woodstock" (duke of Gloucester) was created earl of Buckingham. After his death, his son Humphrey held the title of earl. The title then passed to Humphrey's sister, Anne, who married Edmund Stafford, earl of Stafford. Their son, Humphrey Stafford, became the earl of Stafford and was created duke of Buckingham in 1444. This dukedom remained in the Stafford family until 1521, when Edward, the 3rd duke, was executed following an attainder.

**The Villiers and Sheffield Lines**
In 1617, George Villiers was created earl of Buckingham, later becoming marquess in 1618 and duke in 1623. The dukedom and marquessate became extinct with the 2nd duke in 1687. A collateral line claiming descent from John Villiers, 1st Viscount Purbeck, claimed the earldom, but this claim ended with the death of a clergyman named George Villiers in 1774.

Separately, in 1703, John Sheffield, marquess of Normanby, was created duke of Normanby and the county of Buckingham. He was succeeded by his son Edmund, and the titles became extinct upon Edmund's death in October 1735. John Sheffield (1648–1721) was a poet and statesman who served as lord steward and lord president of the council. He died at his house in St James's Park, which is the site of the current Buckingham Palace.

**The Grenville Family**
The title of marquess and duke of Buckingham associated with the Grenville family was derived from the town of Buckingham rather than the county. This began in 1784 when the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham." This was later elevated to the dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos for his son in 1822.
