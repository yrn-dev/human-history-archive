# Ecclesiastical Authority in Brest-Litovsk

## Overview of Brest-Litovsk
Brest-Litovsk (also known as Brzesc-Litevski, Berestie, or Berestov) is a strongly fortified town located in the government of Grodno. It is situated at the intersection of railways from Moscow, Kiev, Warsaw, and East Prussia, and at the junction of the Bug and the navigable river Mukhovets. The town serves as a commercial hub, engaging in an extensive trade of leather, tar, wood, hemp, flax, and grain. 

Historically, the town has faced significant destruction. It was laid waste by the Mongols in 1241 and not rebuilt until 1275. In 1379, the Teutonic Knights burned its suburbs, and by the end of the 15th century, the town was again devastated by the khan of the Crimea. Later history includes captures by the Swedes in 1657 and 1706, and a victory by Suvarov over the Polish general Sierakowski in 1794. The town was added to the Russian empire in 1795. During the reign of King Sigismund III of Poland, the town hosted diets, and it served as the meeting place for two councils of bishops of western Russia in 1596 and 1594.

## Religious Administration and Demographics
The population of Brest-Litovsk was 22,493 in 1867 and grew to 42,812 by 1901. More than half of the 1901 population were Jews, and the town contains a Jewish synagogue that was considered the first in Europe during the 16th century.

The town is a center of ecclesiastical authority, serving as the seat for both a Greek Catholic bishop and an Armenian bishop. The Armenian bishop holds a broad jurisdiction, as he possesses authority over the Armenians throughout the entire country.

## Infrastructure and Geography
The town's geography is defined by its waterways and transport links. The King's canal (also called the Brest-Litovsk canal), which is 50 miles long, utilizes the Bug and Mukhovets rivers to create a link in the waterways connecting the Vistula with the Dnieper.
