# Sarikol Mountains

The Sarikol mountains are a significant meridional mountain range located in Asia. They serve as a major natural division and political boundary, specifically defining the western edge of the Kashgar plains and acting as the political boundary between Russia and China.

## Geographical Structure and Characteristics
The Sarikol range is characterized by its composition of two parallel chains. The western chain serves as the water-divide of the Pamirs, while the eastern chain—historically referred to as the Kandar or Kashgar range—is periodically split by lateral gorges. These gorges facilitate the passage of the main drainage originating from the eastern slopes of the Pamir.

In a broader continental context, the Sarikol range forms the eastern face of the great elevation of the Pamir. To the west of Kashgar, the range, along with the Pamir, limits a central depression that extends westwards from Mongolia through Chinese Turkestan. This depression, which may have once been a vast inland sea, generally does not exceed 2,000 feet above sea level and may even fall below sea level in central portions such as Lop Nor.

## Historical and Political Significance
The Sarikol range has been known by various names throughout history; it was referred to as Taurus by the ancients and as Bolor during medieval times. 

Politically, the rugged line of the Sarikol range intervenes between Chinese Turkestan and the Russian Pamirs, although the actual dividing line has been described as indefinite. In the context of Afghan boundaries, the Sarikol mountains mark the eastern extent of Afghanistan's northern limits. These limits were established by the Boundary Commissions of 1884-1886 and 1895, stretching from the Hari Rud in the west to the Sarikol mountains in the east.

Additionally, the Sarikol range, in junction with the Muztagh range, serves as a geographical barrier that shuts off India and Russia from China.
