# Bibliography of Historical and Academic Works (Late 19th and Early 20th Centuries)

The academic landscape of the late 19th and early 20th centuries is characterized by a vast array of specialized research across diverse fields, including the study of religion, national histories, physics, and classical antiquity. The following records detail significant publications and authorities within these disciplines.

## Studies in Religion and Asceticism
Research into the nature of religion and spiritual practices during this period involved extensive cross-cultural analysis. Key authorities include E.B. Tylor, who published *Primitive Culture* in 1903, and Robertson Smith's *Religion of the Semites* (1901). Other foundational texts include J.E. Harrison's *Prolegomena to the Study of Greek Religion*, F. Max Muller's *The Sacred Books of the East*, and J.G. Frazer's works *The Golden Bough* (1900) and *Adonis, Attis, Osiris* (1906).

Specific explorations of mysticism and asceticism are found in the works of Fr. Cumont, notably *Mysteries of Mithra* (Chicago, 1903) and *Les Religions orientales dans le paganisme romain* (1907). Zockler contributed to this field with *Gesch. der Ascese* (1863). Additionally, Goldziher examined early Islamic asceticism in "De l'ascetisme aux premiers temps de l'Islam" (1898). Other relevant texts on ethics and abstinence include James Martineau's *Types of Ethical Theory* (1885), T.H. Green's *Prolegomena to Ethics* (1883), and writings by Porphyrius (*De Abstinentia*) and Plutarchus (*De Carnium Esu*).

## Austrian and European Political History
The history of the House of Austria has been documented through various lenses. Archdeacon William Coxe's *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792* (1817), continued by W. Kelly (1853, 1873), served as a primary English-language general history, though it was later superseded by more recent research. Other significant German-language histories include J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates* (1834-1850) and A. von Arneth's ten-volume *Geschichte Maria Theresias* (1863-1879).

Political upheavals, particularly the revolution of 1848, were analyzed by Count F. von Hartig in *Genesis der Revolution in Oesterreich im Jahre 1848* (1851) and by W. H. Stiles in *Austria in 1848-1849* (1852). Diplomatic history and the "Austrian question" were further explored by A. Beer, A. Fournier, and Rene Henry, the latter of whom published *Questions d'Autriche-Hongrie et question d'orient* in 1903.

## Military History and the Schleswig Wars
The military campaigns of Frederick the Great and the wars of the Austrian Succession are documented in several official and academic accounts. These include the Prussian official *Die I. und II. schlesischen Kriege* (1890-1895) and the Austrian official *Kriege der Kaiserin Maria Theresia; Gesch. des oesterr. Erbfolgekrieges* (from 1895). Additional perspectives are provided by F. H. Skrine in *Fontenoy and the War of the Austrian Succession* (1906) and Francis Parkman in *A Half-Century of Conflict* (1892).

## Classical and Hellenistic Studies
The study of Macedonian kingdoms and the spread of Hellenic culture is supported by the works of Droysen (*Histoire de l'Hellénisme*), A. Holm (*History of Greece*), and B. Niese (*Geschichte der griechischen und makedonischen Staaten*, 1893-1903). Beloch provided a comprehensive analysis of the political, social, and economic character of Hellenistic kingdoms in *Griech. Gesch.* iii. Further research into the successor monarchies of Alexander the Great was conducted by E. Breccia in *Il Diritto dinastico helle monarchie dei successori d'Alessandro Magno* (1903).

## Science, Physics, and Medicine
In the realm of physics and medicine, several key texts were published in the late 19th century. These include *Kompendium der Physik für Mediziner* (Vienna, 1863) and *Einleitung in die Heimholtz'sehe Musiktheorie* (1866). Other notable works include *Die Mechanik in ihrer Entwickelung* (1883, with a revised edition in 1908) and *Populärwissenschaftliche Vorlesungen* (1903).

## Regional and Literary Histories
Regional histories include the study of Lüneburg, with contributions from W. F. Volger (*Urkundenbuch der Stadt Lüneburg*, 1872-1877) and W. Reinecke (*Lüneburgs ältestes Stadtbuch und Verfasstungsregister*, 1903). 

Scandinavian and Danish literary history is documented by R. Nyerup, N. M. Petersen, and P. Hansen (*Illustreret Dansk Literaturhistorie*, 1901-1902). F. W. Horn's *History of the Scandinavian North* was translated into English and published in Chicago in 1884.

## American Financial History
In the United States, the administration of the Second Bank of the United States was analyzed by Ralph C.H. Catterall in a work published in Chicago in 1903.
