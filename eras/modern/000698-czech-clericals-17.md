# Czech Clericals

The Czech Clericals were a political group within the parliamentary system of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, representing a clerical interest among the Czech population. They operated as one of several distinct Czech political factions, including the Young Czechs, Old Czechs, Czech Agrarians, Czech National Socialists, and Realists.

## Parliamentary Representation
The strength of the Czech Clericals in parliament varied over different periods. In one recorded instance of party distribution, the Czech Clericals held 17 seats. This placed them behind the Czech Agrarians (28 seats) and the Young Czechs (18 seats), but ahead of the Old Czechs (7 seats) and the Czech National Socialists (9 seats). 

In other parliamentary tables comparing the years 1897 and 1901, the "Clerical Czechs" are listed with a smaller presence, holding 1 seat in 1897 and 2 seats in 1901. During this same period, they were part of a broader Czech contingent that included the Young Czechs, Radical Young Czechs, and Agrarian Czechs.

## Political Alliances and Conflicts
The Czech Clericals were often aligned with other conservative and religious forces. In Bohemia, they provided support to the Czechs in their efforts to gain a large majority in the diet. They were part of a coalition that included the Feudals and other Czech interests.

Within the broader political landscape of the Reichsrath, the Clericals were often grouped on the Right. Under the leadership of Hohenwart, Clericals and representatives of other Slav races formed an opposition to the Constitutionalists. This alignment eventually contributed to the overthrow of the rule of the German Liberals. Following this shift, the government under Taaffe turned to the Right for support; consequently, German members of the government were replaced by Czechs, Poles, and Clericals.

## Internal Czech Dynamics
The role of the clergy in Czech politics was a point of contention between different nationalist factions. The Young Czechs, who emerged as a radical party, specifically expressed a dislike for the alliance with the clergy and the aristocracy. The Young Czechs sought to distance themselves from these traditional ties, instead recalling Hussite traditions and advocating for universal suffrage. This stood in contrast to the Old Czechs, led by Rieger, who maintained an alliance with the Feudalists.

## Electoral Context
The political activity of the Czech Clericals took place within a system where constituencies were divided by race. In mixed districts, separate registers were established so that Czech electors could only vote for candidates of their own race. This system ensured that while there could be internal victories—such as those of Czech Radicals over Young Czechs—there could be no victory of a Czech candidate over a German candidate.
