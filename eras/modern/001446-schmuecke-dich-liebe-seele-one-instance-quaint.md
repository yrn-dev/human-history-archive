# Schmuecke dich, o liebe Seele

## Overview
*Schmuecke dich, o liebe Seele* is a church cantata composed by the German musical composer Johann Sebastian Bach (1685–1750). The work is noted in historical archives for containing the aria "Ermuntre dich," which is described as hilarious. This specific piece is cited as an instance of Bach's playfulness, a quality that occasionally appeared in his work and was sometimes viewed as inconvenient by those with "little minds."

## Artistic Style and Humour
The art of Johann Sebastian Bach is characterized by a literal quality where serious subjects are treated with familiar directness and jests are treated as jests, with all elements being primarily beautiful. Bach's capacity for humour and wit is evident across various compositions. For example, in *Der Streit zwischen Phoebus und Pan*, Bach addresses critics who accused him of provincial ignorance and pedantry regarding the grand Italian operatic style. He achieves this by utilizing that very style in Pan's prize-aria ("Zum Tanze, zum Sprunge, so wack-ack-ack-ackelt das Herz"), representing his own style in Phoebus's aria, and then caricaturing his own style in the second part of Pan's aria ("Wenn der Ton zu muehsam klingt").

Further wit is found in the same work when the character Midas votes for Pan, stating, "denn nach meinen beiden Ohren singt er unvergleichlich schoen." At the mention of "Ohren" (ears), the violins perform a pianissimo "hee-haw," a musical aptness compared to Mendelssohn's clown-theme in the Overture to the *Midsummer Night's Dream*.

## Other Notable Works
Beyond *Schmuecke dich, o liebe Seele*, Bach produced other significant works displaying subtle inspiration and technical skill:

*   **The Magnificat**: Described as one of Bach's most profound and terse works, it contains a "quaint representation" of the words "dimisit inanes." It also features a soprano solo accompanied by an *oboe d'amore* for the words "quia respexit humilitatem ancillae suae," followed by a tone of quiet joy for the sentence "ecce enim beatam me dicent."
*   **Gott ist mein Koenig**: A "very great work" written when Bach was nineteen; it was one of the few church cantatas published during his lifetime.
*   **Instrumental Collections**: These constituted the majority of Bach's works published during his life, many of which he engraved himself.

## The Bach Family Legacy
Johann Sebastian Bach belonged to a family of musical importance spanning nearly two hundred years. The family genealogy, which Sebastian began and his son Philipp Emanuel completed, identifies Veit Bach (born about 1555), a miller and baker, as the founder. Veit's son, Hans Bach ("der Spielmann"), was the family's first professional musician.

Sebastian's grandfather was Christoph, the second son of Hans. Another son of Hans, Heinrich of Arnstadt, had two sons: Johann Michael and Johann Christoph. Johann Christoph is currently believed—though this remains disputed—to be the author of the motet *Ich lasse dich nicht* ("I wrestle and pray"), a work previously attributed to Sebastian Bach. Sebastian also held great admiration for another descendant of Veit, Johann Ludwig, whose church cantatas Sebastian copied and occasionally augmented with his own work.
