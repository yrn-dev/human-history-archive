# Clara Barton

## Early Life and Education
Clara Barton was an American philanthropist born in 1821 in Oxford, Massachusetts. She received her education at the Clinton Liberal Institute, which was located in Clinton, New York. At the age of sixteen, Barton entered the teaching profession; however, she was eventually compelled to give up this career due to ill-health. From 1854 to 1857, she worked as a clerk in the Patent Office located in Washington.

## Civil War and Post-War Relief
During the American Civil War, Barton was active in the distribution of large quantities of supplies intended for the relief of wounded soldiers. Following the conclusion of the war, she established a bureau of records in Washington to assist in the search for missing men. This work continued for approximately four years. As part of these efforts, Barton identified and marked more than twelve thousand soldiers' graves in the National Cemetery at Andersonville, Georgia.

## International Work and the Red Cross
In 1869, Barton traveled to Switzerland for health reasons. While in Geneva, she was visited by members of the International Committee of the Red Cross, who requested her cooperation in their society's work. At that time, the United States had declined to become a party to the treaty of Geneva, the foundation of the Red Cross Society. 

Despite this, Barton traveled to the seat of hostilities during the outbreak of the Franco-Prussian War alongside members of the society, where she assisted in the organization of military hospitals. Her subsequent relief work included superintending the distribution of aid to the poor in Paris in 1872 and in Strassburg in 1871. For these services, the German emperor decorated her with the Iron Cross.

## Establishment of the American Red Cross
Barton returned to the United States in 1873 and began working to bring the country into the treaty of Geneva and to organize a United States branch of the Red Cross. These efforts were successful between 1881 and 1882. Barton served as the first president of the American Red Cross, a position she held until 1904.

In her capacity as a representative of the United States, she attended several International conferences in Geneva (1884), Karlsruhe (1887), Rome (1892), Vienna (1897), and St Petersburg (1903). Additionally, she authored the American amendment to the Red Cross constitution, which expanded the society's mandate to distribute relief during times other than war.
