# Darchendo (Tachienlu)

Darchendo, also known as Tachienlu, is a town located on the borders of China. It is situated on the high road that connects Lhasa and Peking and is positioned on the extreme east of the high highland plateau.

## Geographical Significance and Longitude
In the context of modern map construction and geographical research of the vast central region encompassing Mongolia and Tibet, Darchendo is regarded as an obligatory point for determining coordinates. Because of this, various scientific explorers and organizations have recorded different longitude values for the town.

The Royal Geographical Society has adopted a longitude value of 102 deg. 12" for Darchendo. Other recorded values include:
*   **Krishna**: 102 deg. 15"
*   **Baber**: 102 deg. 18"
*   **Kreitner**: 102 deg. 5"

## Exploration of the Region
The knowledge of the great plateau, which stretches from the frontier of Kashmir eastwards to China and northwards from the Himalaya to Chinese Turkestan, has been expanded through the work of native surveyors attached to the Indian survey. Among these were Pundits Nain Singh and Krishna.

Between 1879 and 1882, Krishna undertook a remarkable journey that extended from Lhasa northwards through Tsaidam to Sachu (or Saitu) in Mongolia. Following this, he traveled through eastern Tibet to reach the town of Darchendo. Although he attempted to reach India via Upper Assam, he was unsuccessful and instead returned to the vicinity of Lhasa, eventually crossing the Himalayas using a more westerly route.

## Context of Asiatic Inquiry
The determination of the position of Darchendo is part of a broader period of intense scientific enterprise directed toward Asiatic inquiry. This research has sought to modify previous ideas regarding the orography of Tibet and Mongolia. Similar to Darchendo, the position of Sachu in Mongolia serves as another obligatory point for map construction, with a revised longitude value of 94 deg. 54' E. of Greenwich provided by Prjevalsky.
