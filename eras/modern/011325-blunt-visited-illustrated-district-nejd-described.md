# The District of Nejd and the Observations of Wilfrid S. Blunt

## Overview of Nejd
Nejd is a district located in the interior of Arabia. It is bordered to the west by the district of Asir, which consists of a highland plateau that slopes gradually toward the Nejd steppes. The region is characterized by wide spaces of interior desert. Geographically, the district is linked to Asir via the Wadi Dawasir, which receives waters from the Wadi Taraba and the Wadi Besha.

## Exploration and Documentation
Knowledge of the interior deserts of Arabia has been provided to the world through the works of several foreign and English travellers. Among the English, these include Sir Richard F. Burton, Wm. Gifford Palgrave, and Sir Lewis Pelly. Other foreign contributors include Karsten Niebuhr, John Lewis Burckhardt, and Joseph Halevy.

Wilfrid S. Blunt and Charles Doughty are specifically noted for having visited and illustrated the district of Nejd. Their work provided descriptions of the region and documented the waning glories of the Wahabi empire. In the archive's bibliography, Lady Anne Blunt is credited with the work *Pilgrimage to Neid* (1881), and W.S. Blunt is credited with *Bedouin Tribes of the Euphrates* (1879).

## Religious and Social Dynamics
The Bedouins of Nejd and adjoining districts are nominally Mahommedans. However, many Bedouins generally pay slight attention to the ceremonial precepts of the Koran, with the annual fast of Ramadan and the five daily prayers not being widely favored. In many areas, Mahommedanism is practically limited to the profession of the Divine Unity. Among more remote tribes, practices such as tree-worship, sun-worship, or a complete absence of worship are not uncommon.

The militant Wahhabi have periodically enforced a degree of Islamitic observance among the Bedouins within Nejd and its neighboring districts. In the nearby district of Asir, the inhabitants have been bigoted Wahhabis since the beginning of the 19th century, though they were previously viewed by neighbors as being very lax Mahommedans.

## Political History and Conflict
The region has been a site of significant conflict involving the Wahhabi and external powers. During the period when Mehemet Ali occupied Nejd, the inhabitants of the neighboring Asir district conducted constant raids on Egyptian communications. This forced Mehemet Ali to launch several punitive expeditions into the district, though these efforts met with little success.
