# Devonshire

Devonshire is a region in England containing various geographical landmarks, historical figures, and notable districts.

## Geography and Landmarks
A prominent feature of south-west Devonshire is Dartmoor, a high plateau. It measures approximately 23 miles from north to south and 20 miles in extreme breadth, with a mean altitude of about 1500 feet. The area exceeding 1000 feet in elevation covers about 200 square miles. Dartmoor is the easternmost and highest part of a broken chain of granitic elevations that extends through Cornwall to the Scilly Isles. 

The landscape of the moor is characterized by open, bleak, and wild higher parts, which contrast with the well-wooded lowlands. The main tableland has sloping heights crested with broken masses of granite known locally as "tors," some of which have fantastic outlines. The highest of these are High Willhays (2039 ft) and Yes Tor (2028 ft), both located in the north-west. The center of the moor is largely covered with morasses, and the head-waters of all the principal streams of Devonshire are found here.

Transportation across the moor is primarily handled by two main roads—one between Plymouth and Exeter, and another between Tavistock and Ashburton—which intersect at Two Bridges. These roads avoid the higher elevations, leaving the rest of the moor to be traversed by a few rough tracks.

## Historical Land Use and Administration
The central part of Dartmoor served as a royal forest from a date prior to the Conquest. While its woods were once more extensive, only small tracts of characteristic dwarf oaks remain in the lower areas. In 1337, Henry III granted the forest to Richard, earl of Cornwall, and it has since belonged to the duchy of Cornwall. 

The areas surrounding the moor are known as the Fenfield or Venville districts. Those who hold land by Venville tenure under the duchy possess rights to fishing and pasture within the forest. Their primary obligation is to "drive" the moor at specific times to prevent trespassing and determine which cattle are being pastured.

## Notable Figures from Devonshire
Several historically significant individuals were born in Devonshire:

*   **Saint Boniface (680-754):** Born Wynfrith to a good Saxon family at Kirton or Crediton, he became a monk and studied theology and grammar at Nutcell (near Winchester) and Exeter. He served as a missionary to Frisia and was later commissioned by Pope Gregory II in 719 to evangelize Germany. He was consecrated bishop on November 30, 722, and worked to convert heathens and suppress heresy.
*   **Benjamin Kennicott (1718-1783):** An English Hebrew scholar and divine born on April 4, 1718, in Totnes. After serving as a master of a charity school, he attended Wadham College, Oxford, in 1744. He published dissertations on the Oblations of Cain and Abel and the Tree of Life in Paradise. His career included roles as a fellow of the Royal Society (1764), keeper of the Radcliffe Library (1767), rector of Culham, and canon of Christ Church.
