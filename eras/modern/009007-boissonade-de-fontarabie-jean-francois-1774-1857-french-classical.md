# BOISSONADE DE FONTARABIE, JEAN FRANCOIS (1774-1857)

Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie was a French classical scholar who lived from 1774 to 1857. He was born in Paris on August 12, 1774.

## Public Service and Early Career
Boissonade entered public service in 1792 during the administration of General Dumouriez. Although he was driven from this position in 1795, he was later restored to public employment by Lucien Bonaparte. During Bonaparte's time of office, Boissonade served as the secretary to the prefecture of the Upper Marne. Eventually, he resigned from public employment entirely to devote himself to the study of the Greek language.

## Academic Appointments
Boissonade's academic career began in 1809 when he was appointed as the deputy professor of Greek at the faculty of letters in Paris. Following the death of P.H. Larcher in 1813, he became a titular professor. In 1828, he succeeded J.B. Gail in the chair of Greek at the College de France. In addition to these teaching roles, he held the positions of perpetual secretary of the Academie des Inscriptions and librarian of the Bibliotheque du Roi.

## Scholarly Works and Contributions
Boissonade focused his scholarly attention primarily on later Greek literature. His extensive bibliography includes the following works:

*   **Philostratus**: *Heroica* (1806) and *Epistolae* (1842)
*   **Marinus**: *Vita procli* (1814)
*   **Tiberius Rhetor**: *De Figuris* (1815)
*   **Nicetas Eugenianus**: *Drosilla et Charicles* (1819)
*   **Herodian**: *Partitiones* (1819)
*   **Aristaenetus**: *Epistolae* (1822)
*   **Eunapius**: *Vitae Sophistarum* (1822)
*   **Babrius**: *Fables* (1844)
*   **Tzetzes**: *Allegoriae Iliados* (1851)

He also produced a *Collection of Greek Poets* spanning 24 volumes. Furthermore, his works *Anecdota Graeca* (1829-1833) and *Anecdota Nova* (1844) are noted for their importance to the study of Greek grammarians and Byzantine history.

## Death
Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie died on September 8, 1857.
