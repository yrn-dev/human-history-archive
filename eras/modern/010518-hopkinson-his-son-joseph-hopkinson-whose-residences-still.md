# Francis and Joseph Hopkinson

## Francis Hopkinson
Francis Hopkinson (1737–1791) was an American statesman and author born in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, on October 2, 1737. He was the son of Thomas Hopkinson (1709–1751), a prominent Philadelphia lawyer who served as the first president of the American Philosophical Society and was among the first trustees of the College of Philadelphia (now the University of Pennsylvania).

Hopkinson was the first student to enter the College of Philadelphia, earning his bachelor's degree in 1757 and his master's degree in 1760. He studied law under Benjamin Chew in Philadelphia and was admitted to the bar in 1761. After 1768, he moved to Bordentown, New Jersey, where he became a member of the colony's council in 1774.

During the War of Independence, Hopkinson aligned himself with the whig or patriot element. He served as a delegate to the Continental Congress in 1776 and 1777. His contributions to the early American government included serving on the committee that framed the Articles of Confederation and acting as the treasurer of the Continental loan office. Additionally, alongside John Wharton and John Nixon (1733–1808), he managed the "business of the navy" under the direction of the marine committee.

Hopkinson's legal career included serving as the judge of the court of admiralty in Pennsylvania from 1779 to 1789, and as the United States district judge for that state from 1790 until his death.

## Literary and Scientific Contributions
Hopkinson was noted for his versatility. Beyond his roles as a jurist and political leader, he was a physicist, chemist, mathematician, mechanician, and inventor. He was also a composer, musician, and artist skilled with brush and pencil.

As a writer, he is remembered as one of the three leading satirists on the patriot side during the War of Independence. He was a humorist and a writer of "airy and dainty songs," and his ballad *The Battle of the Kegs* (1778) achieved long-term popularity. This work referenced the American tactic of floating gunpowder-charged kegs down the Delaware river to alarm British forces in Philadelphia.

## Joseph Hopkinson
Joseph Hopkinson (1770–1842) was the son of Francis Hopkinson. He graduated from the University of Pennsylvania in 1786 and pursued the study of law. 

His political and judicial career included serving as a Federalist member of the national House of Representatives from 1815 to 1819. He was a member of the state constitutional convention of 1837 and served as the Federal judge of the Eastern District of Pennsylvania from 1828 until his death in 1842. Despite these professional achievements, he is most recognized as the author of the patriotic anthem "Hail Columbia," written in 1798.

## Connection to Bordentown
Both Francis and Joseph Hopkinson resided for some years in Bordentown, New Jersey. Bordentown is a city in Burlington county situated on the east bank of the Delaware river. The residences of both Francis and Joseph Hopkinson remain standing in the city.
