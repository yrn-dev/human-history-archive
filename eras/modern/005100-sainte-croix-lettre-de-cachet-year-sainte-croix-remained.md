# Godin de Sainte-Croix

Godin de Sainte-Croix was a cavalry officer known for his extravagant tastes and a bad reputation. He is primarily noted in historical records for his relationship with Marie Madeleine Marguerite d'Aubray, the marquise de Brinvilliers, and their subsequent involvement in a series of poisonings in 17th-century France.

## Arrest and Imprisonment
Sainte-Croix was introduced to the marquise de Brinvilliers in 1659 by her husband, the marquis de Brinvilliers. The two became lovers, a relationship that resulted in a public scandal. Because the marquis de Brinvilliers had left France to avoid his creditors and made no effort to end the affair, the marquise's father, M. Dreux d'Aubray, secured the arrest of Sainte-Croix via a *lettre de cachet*. 

Sainte-Croix remained a prisoner in the Bastille for one year. It is popularly believed that during this period of incarceration, he acquired knowledge of poisons from a fellow prisoner, an Italian poisoner named Exili.

## Conspiracy and Poisonings
Following his release from the Bastille, Sainte-Croix plotted revenge against M. Dreux d'Aubray. He collaborated with the marquise de Brinvilliers, who acted as his willing mistress. Sainte-Croix prepared poisons, possibly with the assistance of a chemist named Christopher Glaser. To test the efficiency of these preparations, the marquise experimented on the sick in hospitals and poor individuals who sought her charity.

As Sainte-Croix became completely ruined financially, he expanded the scope of his plot. He determined that the marquise and himself should come into possession of the large family fortune by poisoning not only M. Dreux d'Aubray but also his daughter and two sons. 

In February 1666, the marquise poisoned her father. In 1670, with the assistance of their valet, La Chaussée, she poisoned her two brothers. While a post-mortem examination indicated the actual cause of death, the murderers were not initially suspected.

## Death and Legal Aftermath
Sainte-Croix died suddenly before any attempt could be made on the life of Mlle Théresè d'Aubray. Because he died without leaving any heirs, the police were called in. The contents of Sainte-Croix's casket subsequently led to the trial and condemnation of the marquise de Brinvilliers.
