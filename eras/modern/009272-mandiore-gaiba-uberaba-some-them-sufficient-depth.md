# Mandiore, Gaiba and Uberaba

## Geographic Characteristics
Mandiore, Gaiba (also referred to as Gahiba), and Uberaba are lakes located in the south-east region of Bolivia. These bodies of water are characterized as large and shallow, with sizes and characters that fluctuate according to the season. They occupy slight depressions in the landscape and are formed due to defective drainage. Specifically, these lakes are located near the Paraguay river and are partly the result of obstructed outlets. While shallow, some of these lakes possess sufficient depth to allow for navigation by small craft.

## Regional Hydrology and Environment
The lakes are situated in a region that, similar to the north, experiences periodical inundations during the summer months, which span from November to March and occasionally extend into May. During these periods, extensive areas of level country are flooded, rendering traffic possible only through the use of boats. 

Located above Lake Uberaba are the great Xarayes swamps, which are sometimes described as a lake. This area is part of a broader hydrographic network in the region that includes other features such as the great lacustrine basin between the Beni and the Mamore, which contains Lake Rogagua and the Roguaguado lagoon and marshes.

## Political Boundaries
Lakes Mandiore, Gaiba, and Uberaba serve as geographic markers for the boundary line between Bolivia and Brazil. The origin of this boundary is based on the limits of Spanish and Portuguese colonies established by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750), with subsequent modifications occurring in 1867 and 1903.

The boundary line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.). From there, the line ascends the river to a point on the west bank 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra, then moves inland 4 kilometres to a point at lat. 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and long. 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W. From this point, the boundary follows an irregular course moving North and East of North until it reaches Lakes Mandiore, Gaiba, and Uberaba. After passing these lakes, the line continues up the San Matias river and North along the Sierra Ricardo Franco to the headwaters of the Rio Verde, a tributary of the Guapore.

## Boundary Modifications
The boundary in this region was adjusted to ensure that settlements on the west bank, including Corumba and Fort Coimbra, remained under Brazilian jurisdiction. Furthermore, the boundary was modified in 1903, resulting in a recession of approximately 1158 sq. m. to Bolivia to improve commercial facilities on the Paraguay river.
