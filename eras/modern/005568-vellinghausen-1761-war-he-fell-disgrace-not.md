# Vellinghausen (1761)

The engagement at Vellinghausen in 1761 was a military action occurring during the Seven Years' War. It is noted in the archive's records primarily for its outcome regarding the French command.

## Military Outcome and Command
The action at Vellinghausen resulted in a defeat for the French forces. The command during this engagement was held by Victor François, Duc de Broglie. De Broglie had previously achieved success in the conflict, including a victory over Prince Ferdinand at Bergen in 1759 and an action at Corbach in 1760. However, the defeat at Vellinghausen in 1761 marked a turning point in his professional standing.

## Aftermath and Disgrace of the Duc de Broglie
Following the conclusion of the war, the Duc de Broglie fell into disgrace. As a consequence of this loss of favor, he was not recalled to active employment for several years. His period of professional exile lasted until 1778, at which time he was given command of the troops intended to operate against England.

## Career of Victor François, Duc de Broglie
The Duc de Broglie's military career extended well beyond the events of 1761. Born in 1718, he began his service at Parma and Guastalla alongside his father, obtaining a colonelcy in 1734. His early career included:
* **The German War:** Participation in the 1742 storming of Prague, which earned him the rank of brigadier.
* **Rhine Service:** Further service on the Rhine in 1744 and 1745.
* **Promotion:** He was made *maréchal de camp* in 1756.
* **Low Countries:** Service under Marshal Saxe, where he was present at Maastricht, Val, and Roucoux.

By the end of the war preceding the Seven Years' War, he had attained the rank of lieutenant-general. During the Seven Years' War, he served under Contades, Soubise, and d'Estrées, participating in every battle from Hastenbeck onwards. His victory at Bergen in 1759 was highly distinguished, earning him the rank of prince of the empire from Emperor Francis I and the rank of marshal of France from his own sovereign.

In his later years, de Broglie played a prominent role in the Revolution, which he opposed with determination. After emigrating, he briefly commanded the "army of the princes" in 1792. He died in 1804 at Münster.
