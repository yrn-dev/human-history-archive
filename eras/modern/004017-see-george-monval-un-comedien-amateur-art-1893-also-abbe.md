## Notable Figures in French Theatre
The archive provides extensive records on Benoit Constant Coquelin (1841–1909), a French actor known as Coquelin *aine*. Born in Boulogne on January 23, 1841, Coquelin was initially expected to enter his father's profession as a baker—a background that led a hostile critic to refer to him as *un boulanger manque*. However, his passion for acting led him to the Conservatoire, where he joined Regnier's class in 1859 and won the first prize for comedy within one year.

Coquelin made his debut at the Comedie Francaise on December 7, 1860, performing as the comic valet Gros-Rene in Moliere's *Depit amoureux*. He achieved his first major success the following year in the role of Figaro. By 1864, he was appointed *societaire*. Over the subsequent twenty-two years at the Francais, he created leading roles in forty-four new plays. Notable productions included:
* *Gringoire* (1867) by Theodore de Banville
* *Tabarin* (1871) by Paul Ferrier
* *Paul Forestier* (1871) by Emile Augier
* *L'Etrangere* (1876) by the younger Dumas
* *Jean Dacier* (1877) by Charles Lomon
* *Le Monde ou l'on s'ennuie* (1881) by Edward Pailleron
* *Les Rantzau* (1884) by Erckmann and Chatrian

Coquelin resigned from the Comedie Francaise in 1886 following a dispute with authorities regarding his right to conduct provincial tours within France. After touring the United States and Europe, he returned to the Comedie Francaise as a *pensionnaire* in 1890. During this tenure, he played Labussiere in Sardou's *Thermidor*, a production the government interdicted after three performances. 

In 1892, Coquelin permanently broke with the Comedie Francaise to tour European capitals with his own company. He joined the Renaissance theatre in Paris in 1895 and later became the director of the Porte Saint Martin in 1897. In this capacity, he found success in several plays, including *Cyrano de Bergerac* (1897) by Edmond Rostand, *Plus que reine* (1899) by Emile Bergerat, *Scarron* (1905) by Catulle Mendes, and *L'Attentat* (1906) by Lucien Descaves and Alfred Capus. In 1900, he toured America with Sarah Bernhardt, with whom he later appeared in *L'Aiglon* at the Theatre Sarah Bernhardt.

## American Dramatic Records
The archive also notes the preservation of prompt-books belonging to Edwin Booth, which were edited by William Winter in 1878. Further biographical material on Booth is found in the series *Actors and Actresses of Great Britain and America*, edited by Brander Matthews and Lawrence Hutton. This series includes recollections of Booth's father contributed by Edwin Booth himself, as well as an article on Edwin Booth written by Lawrence Barrett. Other relevant sources include Lawrence Hutton's *Edwin Booth* (1893), William Winter's *Life and Art of Edwin Booth* (1893), and A.B. Clarke's *The Elder and the Younger Booth* (1882).
