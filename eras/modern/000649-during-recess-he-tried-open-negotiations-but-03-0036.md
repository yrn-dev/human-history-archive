# Negotiations and Diplomatic Efforts in History

Throughout various historical periods and contexts, negotiations have been employed as a primary tool for resolving conflict, securing political influence, or establishing legal agreements. These efforts range from high-level state diplomacy and parliamentary maneuvers to legal plea bargaining.

## Political Negotiations in the Austro-Hungarian Empire
In the late 19th century, the government of Badeni sought support from various nationalities following an election. Badeni attempted to reach agreements with the Poles and the Young Czechs. While the Poles were consistently ready to support the government, the moderate members of the Young Czechs were prepared to negotiate to gain influence over the administration. Their primary requirement was further concessions regarding the language used in Bohemia.

In May 1897, Badeni issued ordinances stating that all documents and correspondence brought before government officials should be conducted in the language in which they were first introduced, effectively introducing Czech into government offices throughout the kingdom. Additionally, it was mandated that after 1903, any person appointed to a government post in Bohemia must pass an examination in Czech. 

These ordinances led to obstruction by German Radicals and Nationalists, who demanded the dismissal of Badeni and the repeal of the ordinances. This resulted in the prorogation of parliament in June. During the subsequent recess, Badeni attempted to open negotiations, but the Germans refused to enter any discussion until the ordinances were withdrawn.

## Diplomatic Efforts in Ancient Greece
In the 4th century B.C., Philip sought to detach Athens from the war through indirect overtures. During the summer of 348 B.C., Eubœan envoys intimated that Philip wished to conclude peace with Athens. Despite Philip's success in conquering much of Chalkidikê, he viewed Athens as a threat because of its ability to organize an extensive Grecian confederacy. Simultaneously, the Eubœans had attempted to negotiate an accommodation with Athens during a period of ongoing contest in Eubœa.

In other conflicts, negotiations were used as a means of surrender or alliance. Meidias, who held Skêpsis, sought to open negotiations with the Lacedæmonian commander Derkyllidas due to a mistrust of his own citizens and the impending arrival of Pharnabazus. Meidias solicited a conference and demanded hostages for his safety. When he inquired about the terms of alliance, Derkyllidas stipulated that the citizens must be left autonomous.

## Territorial and Maritime Disputes in Belgium
In the Austrian Netherlands, Empress Maria Theresa attempted to avoid the "Barrier" obligation—the maintenance of foreign garrisons—by withholding payments. Her son, Emperor Joseph II, later ordered the demolition of Dutch-occupied fortresses on Belgian soil. 

Joseph II also attempted to secure the opening of the Scheldt, which the Dutch had closed. Following the failure of diplomatic negotiations begun in 1784—which were opposed by the United Provinces and supported by France—the Emperor attempted a practical stratagem. He ordered vessels to travel from Antwerp to the sea and from Ostend to Antwerp, hoping the Dutch would not fire. However, the Dutch did fire upon the vessels, forcing their withdrawal. Joseph II did not pursue further action to avoid war, and the Scheldt remained closed.
