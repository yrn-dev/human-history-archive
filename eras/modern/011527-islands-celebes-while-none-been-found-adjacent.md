# Celebes and the Adjacent Islands

## Geographical Context and Wallace's Line
Celebes is an island in the Dutch East Indies, separated from Borneo by the Macassar Strait. Modern science identifies a physical separation along this channel, approximately 120 deg. E., known as Wallace's line. This line, named after the naturalist A.R. Wallace, serves as a boundary where flora and fauna to the west are essentially Asiatic, while to the south and east, the Australian element becomes distinctly marked and eventually predominant. The line is drawn from the south of the Philippines between Borneo and Celebes, and continues south between Bali and Lombok.

## Zoological Characteristics
The distribution of wildlife across these islands indicates the margin of the Australian region. Mammals of the Marsupial order are found in Celebes and the Molucca Islands, but are absent from the adjacent islands of Borneo and Java, which lie west of Wallace's line. Similarly, various other mammals—including Ruminants, Insectivora, Carnivora, and Quadrumana—abound in the western Archipelago but are absent from Celebes and the Moluccas.

Celebes possesses unique animals that appear to have no relation to Asiatic fauna, appearing instead to be allied with African animals; these include the singular ruminant *Anoa* and a peculiar form of baboon, *Cynopithecus*. Birds in Celebes also exhibit similar peculiarities, with some species akin to those of Africa.

In terms of ichthyology, the boundary between the Cyprinoid and Acyprinoid divisions of the equatorial zone follows Wallace's line. While Borneo abounds in Cyprinid fishes, and some are known from Bali and the Philippines, none have been found in Lombok or Celebes, or any islands further east.

## Regional Classifications
According to the zoological primary divisions of Dr. Sclater, Celebes, Papua, and other islands east of Java beyond Wallace's line fall within the Australian region. Specifically, Celebes is part of the Papuan Subregion, which also includes the Moluccas, the Timor group of islands, and New Guinea. This subregion is characterized by a distinctive avifauna, most notably the birds of paradise, as well as specialized parrots, pigeons, kingfishers, and cassowaries.

## Human Geography and Culture
The island of Celebes is home to the Bugis (or Bughis), a robust and enterprising people of Malayan stock. Originally occupying the kingdom of Boni in the south-western peninsula, the Bugis spread across the entire island and founded settlements throughout the Malay Archipelago. They are known for being industrious and ingenious, practicing agriculture and manufacturing cotton-cloth for export. The Bugis have maintained a near-monopoly on trade around Celebes, dealing in pearls, gold-dust, camphor, nutmegs, and tortoise-shell.

Politically, the Bugis of Boni are organized into a confederation of eight petty states. Their king is generally elected for life by the chiefs of these states and cannot make public decisions without their consent. In some states, the office of chief is hereditary, and women may sometimes govern. The Bugis have been Mahommedans since the 17th century, though they retain rites connected to the worship of Siva from earlier Hindu influences and nature-worship.

## Major Settlements and Administration
Macassar (also spelled Makassar or Mangkasar) serves as the chief town of the Dutch government of Celebes and the capital of its district. Located on the west coast of the southern peninsula near the Macassar Strait, the town consists of a Malay town and a Dutch town and port called Vlaardingen. Macassar is a significant trade center with an annual trade of approximately £1,250,000, primarily involving gums, spices, copra, coffee, valuable timber, and trepang.

Boni (or Bone) is a vassal state of the Celebes government located on the Gulf of Boni in the south-west peninsula. Covering an area of 2,600 sq. m., it produces sugar-cane, cotton, coffee, tobacco, and rice, and is noted for highly esteemed breeds of horses and buffaloes.

## Related Islands: Bachian
Bachian (or Batjan) is one of the Molucca Islands in the residency of Ternate. It is an irregular island of 914 sq. m. consisting of two mountainous parts joined by a low isthmus. The island features volcanic formations and hot springs, with its highest point, the mountain of Labua, reaching 6,950 ft. Bachian is the easternmost point on the globe inhabited by any of the *Quadrumana*, specifically a black ape also found in Celebes. The island's population of approximately 13,000 consists of Malays, immigrants from Celebes, Arabs, Chinese, Galela men, and the Sirani (Christian descendants of the Portuguese).
