# MATHILDE LETITIA WILHELMINE (1820-1904)

## Early Life and Family
Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine was born on May 20, 1820, in Trieste. She was the daughter of Jerome Bonaparte and the sister of Prince Napoleon. Her father, Jerome, had a complex personal history, including an early marriage to Elizabeth Patterson in Baltimore in 1803—a union disliked by Napoleon—and a subsequent marriage to Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg after an imperial decree annulled the first marriage. Through the latter union, Jerome was raised to the throne of the kingdom of Westphalia.

## Marriages and Personal Life
In 1840, Mathilde married Prince Anatole Demidov, though she had previously been almost betrothed to her cousin, Louis Napoleon. The marriage to Prince Anatole was short-lived, resulting in a separation within five years due to his conduct. Following this separation, Tsar Nicholas compelled Prince Anatole to provide Princess Mathilde with a handsome allowance.

## Life in Paris
Following the election of Louis Napoleon to the presidency of the republic, Mathilde moved to Paris. During this period, she resided at the Elysee, where she performed the honors of the residence until the marriage of Louis Napoleon.

Mathilde continued to live in Paris for the remainder of her life. She became a figure of significant influence within the city, establishing herself as a patron and friend to men of letters and art.

## Death
Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine died on January 2, 1904.
