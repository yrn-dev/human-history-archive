# John Payne Collier

John Payne Collier was a scholar and editor associated with several prominent literary and historical societies during the 19th century. He is noted for his contributions to the study of early English drama and poetry, as well as his involvement in scholarly disputes regarding the works of William Shakespeare.

## Literary and Editorial Contributions
Collier's work in dramatic editing includes the production of an edition of *Kynge Johan* (c. 1548), published for the Camden Society in 1838. The play *Kynge Johan* is identified as a polemic against the Roman Catholic Church, depicting King John as a champion of English rites in opposition to the Roman see. This work is considered a significant piece of dramatic history as it represents the transition from the old morality play to English historical drama, though it did not appear to directly influence the creators of the chronicle histories or the authors of *The Troublesome Raigne of King John* (1591).

Additionally, Collier is credited with the discovery of a noble passage in the play *The Massacre at Paris* (acted in 1593). This passage had been excised from the piratical edition of the play, which is the only extant version of the work.

## Academic Societies and Collaborations
Collier was a co-founder of the Percy Society, an organization dedicated to the publication of old English poetry. He established this society alongside other scholars, including Thomas Wright, Halliwell-Phillips, and Alexander Dyce.

## The Collier Controversy
Collier's professional relationship with the English dramatic editor and literary historian Alexander Dyce (1798–1869) was marked by a long friendship that eventually ended due to a scholarly conflict. Dyce contributed to the "Collier controversy" by publishing critical works targeting Collier's editions of Shakespeare. These publications included *Remarks on Collier's and Knight's Editions of Shakespeare* (1844) and *Strictures on Collier's new Edition of Shakespeare* (1859).
