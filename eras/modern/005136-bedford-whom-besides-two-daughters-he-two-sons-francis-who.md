# Bristol, 3rd Earl

The 3rd Earl of Bristol was a prominent figure of his era, recognized for his brilliant abilities and skill as an orator. He was noted for his capacity to distinguish himself effortlessly in any field he entered. However, these natural gifts were undermined by an instability of character and a restless ambition that hindered his achievement of real greatness.

## Character and Reputation
Contemporary accounts highlight the contradictory nature of his life. Clarendon described him as a man of incomparable parts who failed to gain wisdom from his misfortunes or experiences. He was further noted for his extraordinary ability to simultaneously make friends and enemies. 

Horace Walpole characterized him as a "singular person whose life was one contradiction," noting several paradoxes in his conduct:
* He embraced popery despite having written against it.
* He became a sacrifice for the court after being a zealous opposer of it.
* He acted as an unconscientious persecutor of Lord Clarendon after having been conscientiously converted during his prosecution of Lord Strafford.
* He spoke in favor of the Test Act despite being a Roman Catholic.
* He practiced astrology on the birthday of true philosophy.

Despite his intellectual capabilities, he frequently caused harm to himself and his associates, and his romantic bravery did not translate into success as a commander.

## Literary and Political Works
The Earl was a prolific writer and speaker. His published works include:
* An *Apologie* (1643), in which he justified his support for the king's cause.
* A comedy titled *Elvira* (1667).
* *Worse and Worse*, an adaptation of a Spanish work that was performed but not printed.
* *The Adventures of Five Hours* (1663), which he is credited with authoring alongside Sir Samuel Tuke.

His pointed and eloquent speeches were printed and are preserved in the *Biog. Brit.* and the *Thomason Tracts*. A catalogue of his personal library was published in 1680.

## Family and Succession
He married Lady Anne Russell, who was the daughter of Francis, the 4th Earl of Bedford. The marriage produced two daughters and two sons. His eldest son, Francis, died unmarried before his father. His other son, John, succeeded him as the 3rd Earl of Bristol.
