# Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne

The *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne* is a genealogical work published in 1709 by Baluze. The text is characterized by its inclusion of "Proofs," which the archive's records indicate consist of deeds that had been previously pronounced spurious.

## Origins and Context of Fabrication
The production of the *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne* was part of a broader effort to support certain pretensions through the use of fabricated documents. This practice of creating spurious genealogies had been initiated earlier in a work titled *Histoire de la maison d'Auvergne*, published by Christophe Justel in 1645. Further fabrications appeared in 1672, when the historian of Dauphiny, Chorier, included a forged deed in the second volume of his history to connect the La Tours of Auvergne with the La Tours of Dauphiny.

Following these early instances, a "regular manufactory" of forged documents was established by Jean de Bar, who was an intimate companion of the cardinal de Bouillon. These fabrications were executed with enough skill to deceive prominent scholars. On July 23, 1695, experts including Baluze, Dom Thierry Ruinart, and Dom Jean Mabillon (the founder of Diplomatics) issued a unanimously favorable report on the documents.

## Legal Inquiry and Baluze's Persistence
The cardinal de Bouillon's enemies initiated a war of pamphlets in response to these claims. In March 1698, Baluze wrote a *Letter* in reply, though it proved nothing. By 1700, Jean de Bar and his accomplices were arrested. Following a searching and lengthy inquiry, they were declared guilty in 1704.

Despite these legal findings, Baluze remained obstinate in his belief that the incriminated documents were genuine. He proposed to redo the work previously started by Justel. With the financial encouragement and support of cardinal de Bouillon, Baluze first released a *Table genealogique* in 1705. This was followed in 1709 by the publication of the *Histoire généalogique de la maison d'Auvergne*.

## Aftermath and Later Life
In the year following the publication of the *Histoire généalogique*, Baluze suffered a sudden fall from grace due to the disgrace of his patron, the cardinal de Bouillon. Consequently, Baluze was exiled from Paris and stripped of his pensions, benefices, and appointments.

While in exile, Baluze continued his literary pursuits, publishing *Historiae Tutelensis libri tres*, a history of his native town, in 1717. He eventually returned to Paris, where he died on July 28, 1718, remaining unconvinced of his errors. Historical analysis of his correspondence with the cardinal suggests that Baluze may have been the victim of clever cheats rather than an accomplice to the forgeries.
