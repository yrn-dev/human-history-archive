## Precedence of Life Peers and Baronets
In 1897, a fresh agitation was sparked by an order that granted the sons of life peers precedence over baronets. In response to this order, some baronets formed "the Honourable Society of the Baronetage" in 1898 to maintain the privileges of their order.

On the 15th of August 1898, a royal warrant was issued. This warrant confirmed the precedence that had been the subject of complaint, as the baronets viewed this arrangement as an infringement of their rights. Despite this ruling, the organization continued to exist, eventually becoming known as the "Standing Council of the Baronetage." This body later succeeded in securing invitations for several representatives of the order to attend the coronation of King Edward VII.

## The Baronetage of Nova Scotia
Efforts have been made to obtain other distinctions or badges for baronets. Additionally, there have been attempts to purge the order of wrongful assumptions. The baronetage of Nova Scotia is noted as being peculiarly exposed to this issue because the dignity is descendible to the grantee's collateral heirs male as well as to those of his body. To address these wrongful assumptions and determine the best methods for stopping them, a departmental committee was appointed at the home office in 1906.
