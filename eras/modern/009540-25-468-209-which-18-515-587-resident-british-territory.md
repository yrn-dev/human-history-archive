# Bombay Presidency

## Overview and Geography
The Bombay Presidency was a province and presidency of British India. It was administered by a governor and consisted of a combination of British districts and native states. Geographically, the territory extended from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. latitude and from 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. longitude. 

The presidency was bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, and Indore, Hyderabad, and the Central Provinces to the east. To the north, it was bordered by Rajputana, the Punjab, and Baluchistan. While the settlement of Aden was included politically, the total area of the presidency (including Sind but excluding Aden) was 188,745 sq. m. Of this total area, 122,984 sq. m. were under British rule, while 65,761 sq. m. were under native rule. The Portuguese settlements of Goa, Damaun, and Diu, as well as the native state of Baroda (which maintained direct relations with the government of India), were located within these limits.

## Population and Administrative Divisions
As of 1901, the total population of the Bombay Presidency was 25,468,209. This population was distributed between British territory, where 18,515,587 residents lived, and native states, which housed 6,908,648 residents.

Administratively, the province was organized into twenty-six districts divided among four commissionerships:
*   **Sind**
*   **Northern (Gujarat)**
*   **Central (Deccan)**
*   **Southern (Carnatic)**

The twenty-six districts included Bombay City, Ahmedabad, Broach, Kaira, Panch Mahals, Surat, Thana, Ahmednagar, Nasik, Poona, Satara, Sholapur, Belgaum, Bijapur, Dharwar, Kanara, Kolaba, Ratnagiri, Karachi, Hyderabad, Shikarpur, Thar and Parkar, and the Upper Sind Frontier. Additionally, Khandesh was partitioned into two districts in 1906.

## Native States
The presidency included 353 separate native state units. These were administered by collectors of the districts in which smaller states were located or by political agents. These states were organized into several chief groups:

*   **North Gujarat:** Including Cambay, Palanpur agency, Mahi Kantha agency, Rewa Kantha agency, Cutch, and Kathiawar agency.
*   **South Gujarat:** Including Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.
*   **North Konkan, Nasik, and Khandesh:** Including Surgana, Jawhar, and the Khandesh political agency.
*   **South Konkan and Dharwar:** Including Savanur, Sawantwari, and Janjira.
*   **Deccan Satara Jagirs:** Including Daphlapur, Jath, Phaltan, Aundh, Bhor, and Akalkot.
*   **Southern Mahratta states:** Including Kolhapur and other states.
*   **Sind:** Including Khairpur.
