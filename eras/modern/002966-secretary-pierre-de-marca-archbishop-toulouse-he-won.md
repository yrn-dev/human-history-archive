# Étienne Baluze

Étienne Baluze (1630–1718) was a French scholar who served as a secretary, librarian, and professor. Born in Tulle on November 24, 1630, he received his education in his native town and took minor orders.

## Relationship with Pierre de Marca
Baluze served as the secretary to Pierre de Marca, the archbishop of Toulouse. Baluze began this role in 1656 and earned the high appreciation of the learned prelate. Upon Marca's death, the archbishop left all of his papers to Baluze. 

Using these materials, Baluze produced the first complete edition of Marca's treatise *De libertatibus Ecclesiae Gallicanae* in 1663. He also wrote a biography of Marca in Latin, titled *Epistola ad Samuelem Sorbierium, de vita, gestis et scriptis Petri de Marca*, published in 1663. Later, Baluze assisted Marca with a geographical and historical description of Catalonia; after Marca's death, Baluze finished the work, added clever appendices, and published it in 1688 as *Marca hispanica*.

Baluze's devotion to the memory of the prelate led to a conflict with Paul de Faget, Marca's cousin. Faget published a collection of Marca's theological pamphlets in 1668, which included four treatises on the sacrifice of the Mass, the sacrament of the Eucharist (one in French), and the erection of the patriarchate of Constantinople. Because these writings were thought to contain heretical propositions and caused scandal, Baluze incited a dispute with Faget, and the two men exchanged abuses.

## Service to Colbert and the Crown
Around 1667, Baluze entered the service of Colbert. He remained in this position until 1700, during which time he was responsible for the library belonging to Colbert and his son, the marquis de Seignelai. Baluze is credited with enriching this invaluable library prodigiously. In recognition of his service, Colbert secured various benefices for Baluze and obtained the post of king's almoner for him in 1679.

## Academic Career
In the latter part of his career, Baluze moved into higher education. On December 31, 1689, he was appointed as a professor of Canon law at the College de France. He eventually rose to direct the institution, serving as its director from 1707 to 1710.
