# Bahia and the Sao Francisco River

## Geography of the State of Bahia
Bahia is an Atlantic state of Brazil with a total area of 164,650 square miles. It is bounded to the north by the states of Sergipe, Pernambuco, and Piauhy; to the east by the Atlantic and Sergipe; to the south by Minas Geraes and Espirito Santo; and to the west by Goyaz and Minas Geraes. 

A significant portion of the state consists of an arid, barren plateau known as a *chapada*. This plateau has a general elevation of 1,000 to 1,700 feet above the Sao Francisco river, or 2,300 to 3,000 feet above sea level. On the western boundary, the *chapada* reaches a breadth of 60 miles and an elevation of 2,300 feet, serving as the water-parting between the Tocantins and the Sao Francisco.

The regions east of the Sao Francisco are divided into three distinct zones:
*   A rough limestone plateau that rises toward the culminating ridges of the Serra da Chapada.
*   A gneissose plateau characterized by extensive exposures of bare rock that dip slightly toward the coast.
*   A narrower plateau with compact sandy soil that descends to the coastal plain.

The first two zones are inhospitable, arid, and barren, except at the dividing ridges where sea clouds provide some moisture. The third zone becomes less arid and more vegetated as it nears the coast. The coastal plain itself varies from low, sandy, or swampy areas filled with canals and lagoons to more elevated, fertile, and rolling terrain.

## The Sao Francisco River and Interior Economy
The drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river traverses the state from the south to the north and northeast. The interior climate is generally hot and dry. Historically, cattle-raising was the primary industry of the interior; however, this has been nearly extinguished due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts, which were caused by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve grass.

In the agricultural regions, the state produces coffee, cacao, tobacco, cotton, sugar, mandioca, and tropical fruits. Other exports from the region include rum, diamonds, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, cabinet woods, and hides. As of 1890, the population of the state was 1,919,802.

## The City of Bahia (Sao Salvador)
The capital of the state, Bahia (also known as Sao Salvador), is a maritime city located at 13deg S. lat. and 38deg 30' W. long. It is situated on the western side of the peninsula that separates the Atlantic from the Bay of All Saints (*Bahia de Todos os Santos*). The city's population was 174,412 in 1890 and was estimated to be 200,000 by 1900.

The city is divided into two primary sections:
*   **The Commercial Section:** Located on a long, narrow beach between the bluffs and the water-line, this area houses the post-office, custom-house, exchange, market, railway station, arsenal, and principal business houses. This section is characterized by narrow streets that are poorly drained and paved.
*   **The Upper Town:** Built on the western slope of a low ridge (the peninsula's backbone), this area rises 200 to 260 feet above sea level. It contains comfortable residences, attractive gardens, schools, hospitals, theatres, churches, asylums, a museum, a public library, academies of medicine and law, and the governor's palace. A public garden on the edge of the bluff overlooks the bay.

Communication between the lower and upper towns is provided by steep zigzag roads, a circular tramway, and two elevators. The city is also served by four street-car lines connecting the suburbs to both town levels; contracts were made in 1906 to reconstruct some of these for electric traction.

## Transport and Infrastructure
The port of Bahia is one of the most accessible and best harbours on the east coast of South America. It maintains a large foreign and coastwise trade and serves as a port of call for most steamship lines trading between Europe and the continent.

Railways radiate from the city to various inland points:
*   **Bahia & Alagoinhas:** This line is under construction to Joazeiro, which is located on the Sao Francisco river.
*   **Santo Amaro Line:** A short railway line connecting the city to Santo Amaro.
*   **Inland Lines:** Two lines—the Nazareth tramway and the Bahia Central—extend inland from points on the opposite side of the bay.

The city was founded in 1549 by Thome de Souza, who served as the first Portuguese governor-general of Brazil.
