# Napoleonic Tradition in its Democratic Aspect

The Napoleonic tradition in its democratic aspect is most prominently associated with Prince Napoleon (Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul), who lived from 1822 to 1891. The second son of Jerome Bonaparte and Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg, Prince Napoleon—often referred to by the sobriquet "Plon-Plon"—became known for expressing advanced democratic ideas on all possible occasions.

## Political Career and Democratic Affiliations
Following the French revolution of 1848, Prince Napoleon was elected to the National Assembly as a representative of Corsica. During this period, he assumed the name of Jerome after the death of his elder brother, Jerome Napoleon Charles, in 1847. 

Privately, the Prince considered himself the representative of the Napoleonic tradition in its democratic aspect. This orientation led him to associate primarily with men who held advanced political opinions. Within the environment of the court, he acted as a representative of the Liberal party in opposition to the empress Eugenie.

## Relationship with the Empire
Despite his ostensible opposition to the *coup d'etat* of 1851, Prince Napoleon was designated as the successor to the throne should Napoleon III die without children. While he received a liberal dotation, he was not permitted to participate in public affairs.

His public life included various administrative and military roles. In 1854, he served as a general of division during the Crimean campaign; although his conduct at the battle of the Alma led to some imputations regarding his personal courage, these claims appear to have been groundless. He later demonstrated significant capacity while directing the National Exhibition of 1855. In 1858, he was appointed minister for the Colonies and Algeria, an administration that initially aroused great hopes.

## Later Life and Diplomacy
The Prince's political activity shifted following his marriage in January 1859 to Princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy. During the subsequent war for the liberation of Italy, he commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany. Although it was expected that he would become the ruler of the principality, Prince Napoleon refused to exert pressure on the inhabitants, who preferred to unite with the Italian kingdom. In the years that followed, he became known as a great orator through a series of remarkable speeches.

## Broader Napoleonic and Democratic Contexts
The intersection of the Napoleonic name and democratic or revolutionary ideals appeared in other members of the family and associated figures:

*   **Joseph Bonaparte:** Born in Corsica in 1768, Joseph, along with his brothers Napoleon and Lucien, embraced the democratic or French side. He later served as a member for Corsica in the Council of Five Hundred.
*   **Napoleon III:** A nephew of the first Napoleon, he posed as a revolutionary and published *Des Idées Napoléoniennes*, a work that combined the Napoleonic legend with pacificism, socialistic reform, and socialism. Upon being elected President of the republic of 1848, he took an oath to be faithful to the democratic republic and to treat those who attempted to change the form of government as enemies, before becoming Emperor of the French in December 1852.
*   **Daru:** Serving as chief commissary under Napoleon Bonaparte, Daru entered the tribunate, where he was noted for ably maintaining the principles of democratic liberty.
