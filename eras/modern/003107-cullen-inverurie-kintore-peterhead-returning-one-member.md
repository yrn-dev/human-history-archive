# The Elgin Group of Parliamentary Burghs

The Elgin group of parliamentary burghs is a collective of Scottish burghs that unite to return one member to parliament. This group includes the burghs of Banff, Cullen, Elgin, Inverurie, Kintore, Peterhead, and Macduff.

## Member Burghs and Characteristics

### Banff and Macduff
Banff is the capital of Banffshire and serves as a royal, municipal, and police burgh. Situated on high ground on the left bank of the mouth of the Deveron, it is a seaport located 50 miles northwest of Aberdeen via the Great North of Scotland railway. The burgh is a place of great antiquity; its first charter was granted by Malcolm IV in 1163, with additional privileges conferred by Robert Bruce in 1324 and Robert II in 1372. Its economy is supported by the fishing trade and various manufactures, including leather, woollens, rope, sails, breweries, distilleries, iron foundries, timber-yards, and brick-yards.

Macduff is a police burgh and seaport in Banffshire located on the right bank of the mouth of the Deveron, one mile east of Banff. Originally a fishing village called Doune, the site was purchased by the 1st earl of Fife around 1732. The 2nd earl renamed the town Macduff and obtained a royal charter constituting it a burgh in 1783. The burgh's harbour was constructed by the duke of Fife and transferred to the burgh in 1808. The local population is primarily employed in the herring fishery, though the town also supports saw-mills, oilcake mills, manure works, boat-building, and the making of sails and rope.

### Kintore and Inverurie
Kintore is a royal and police burgh of Aberdeenshire situated on the Don, 13¼ miles northwest of Aberdeen. It was established as a royal burgh during the reign of William the Lion (who died in 1214). The town is located on the Great North of Scotland railway. Nearby landmarks include the ruins of Hallforest Castle—formerly a residence of the Keiths, earls marischal, and a hunting-seat of Robert Bruce—and the site of Bruce's camp, which some attribute to the Roman period. Kintore provides the title of baron in the British peerage and earl in the Scottish peerage to the head of the Keith-Falconer family.

Inverurie is also a member of the Elgin group and is located along the banks of the river Don in south Aberdeenshire.

### Cullen and Peterhead
Cullen is a royal burgh in Banffshire and a member of the Elgin group. It is a center for fishing and miscellaneous trade, with the herring season running from June to September. Geologically, the coast between Cullen and Portsoy is characterized by crystalline schists, including phyllites and quartzite. The "Binn of Cullen" is a prominent ridge formed by quartzite.

Peterhead is a burgh in Aberdeenshire and a member of the parliamentary group. It is the birthplace of Peter Buchan (1790–1854), a printer and editor known for collecting and editing Scottish ballads, including *Ancient Ballads and Songs of the North of Scotland*.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure
The burghs are situated across the shires of Banffshire and Aberdeenshire. The river Don, which flows through Inverurie and Kintore, rises in peat-moss east of Glen Avon on the Banffshire border at an elevation of nearly 2000 feet, eventually falling into the North Sea near Old Aberdeen.

Transportation between these regions is facilitated by the Great North of Scotland and Highland railways. These systems connect the chief towns of the area with Aberdeen, as well as with Inverness, Nairn, and Elgin.
