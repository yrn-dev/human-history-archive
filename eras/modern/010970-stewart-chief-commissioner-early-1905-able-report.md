# Sir Donald Stewart and the Administration of Ashanti

## Annexation and Governance of Ashanti
Ashanti was formally annexed to the British dominions on September 26, 1901, via an order in council. This order served as the first constitution granted to Ashanti by the British, establishing a separate administration under the control of the governor of the Gold Coast. The administrative structure included a chief commissioner who represented the governor during his absence, supported by a staff consisting of four assistant commissioners and four commissioners. To maintain order, a battalion of the Gold Coast regiment was stationed within the country, with its headquarters located at Kumasi.

The constitution mandated that the governor, when issuing ordinances regarding the raising of revenue, the administration of justice, or other matters, must respect native laws regulating the civil relations of populations, tribes, or chiefs. The only exceptions to this rule were instances where such laws were clearly injurious to the welfare of the natives or incompatible with British sovereignty.

## Tenure of Sir Donald Stewart
Sir Donald Stewart served as the chief commissioner of Ashanti during the early years following annexation. He was succeeded in early 1905 by Mr. F.C. Fuller. Following Stewart's tenure in Ashanti, he was appointed to succeed Sir Charles in another capacity. In this subsequent role, Sir Donald arranged a settlement of land claims and induced the Masai to move to another district because their grazing rights were threatened. 

During this period, an offer of 6,000 square miles of land for colonization by the Zionist Association was declined by that body in August 1905, following a report from a commissioner who had been sent to examine the land. Sir Donald Stewart died on October 1, 1905, and was succeeded by Colonel Hayes Sadler, who had previously been the commissioner of Uganda.

## Relations Between the Governing and Governed
Following the 1901 annexation, relations between the governed and the governing power showed steady improvement. By the year following the arrival of Mr. F.C. Fuller (who took over from Sir Donald Stewart), reports indicated that suspicion regarding the ulterior motives of the "white man" was quickly losing ground among the Ashanti.

Two primary factors contributed to this increase in confidence and respect:
* **Judicial Faith:** The natives showed a marked preference for using the criminal and civil courts established by the British, which demonstrated their belief in the impartial treatment provided by these courts.
* **Tribal Support:** The British provided support to lawful chiefs and maintained the tribal system. This was particularly effective in winning over a people who were naturally suspicious and mindful of their exiled king.
