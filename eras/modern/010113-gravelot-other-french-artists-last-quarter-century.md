# Gravelot and French Artists in the 18th Century

## French Influence on Book Illustration
During the 18th century, there was a notable improvement in presswork, type, and paper in England and France. This period saw a revival of book illustration, specifically through the French *livres-a-vignettes*. The influence of French artists extended to English publications, with Gravelot and other French artists providing illustrations for English books.

## Hubert Francois Bourguignon, dit Gravelot
Among the artists of this era, Hubert Francois Bourguignon, known as Gravelot, is recognized for his contributions to decorative arts. His designs and engravings are noted as being of high interest and value, particularly as specimens of bygone decorative fashion or as personal relics. Because of this, his work is considered worth collecting by ex-librists and bibliophiles in the form of book-plates.

## Developments in the Last Quarter of the Century
In the final quarter of the 18th century, the field of illustration underwent a significant shift. The work of Bewick heralded a great revival in woodcut illustrations. Due to the specific use of the graver in this process, these works came to be known as wood engravings.

## Related 18th-Century Book Production
The broader context of book production during this time included several technical and economic trends:

*   **Binding and Materials:** While the average technique of binding improved, the best binders of the 18th century remained inferior to those of the 17th century until the appearance of Roger Payne. Trade bindings shifted away from the common use of calf and sheepskin, with books mostly being cased in paper boards.
*   **Publishing Trends:** Some authors, such as Pope and Prior, followed the lucrative practice of publishing poetry by subscription at very high prices. However, Pope also sold single poems for 1s. and 1s. 6d.
*   **Pricing of Novels:** Novels were typically published in several volumes. While prices remained steady for many years, they rose toward the close of the century. For example, Miss Burney's *Evelina* (3 vols., 1778) sold for 7s. 5d., *Cecilia* (5 vols., 1882) for 12s. 6d., and *Camilla* (5 vols., 1796) for L1:1s.
*   **Reference Works:** Large-scale works also commanded high prices, such as Johnson's *Dictionary* (2 vols. folio, 1755), which cost L4:4s. in sheets and L4:15s. in boards.
