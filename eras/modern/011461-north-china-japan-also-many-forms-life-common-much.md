# Biological and Geographical Connections Between North China and Japan

## Botanical and Zoological Affinities
North China and Japan share many common forms of life. The flora of north China is described as being akin to that of Japan. This botanical group shows a manifest relation to the neighboring American continent, with many temperate forms extending from there to the Himalaya, reaching nearly as far as Kashmir. Additionally, the genus *Acer*, which includes maple and sycamore trees, is native to Asia—specifically Japan, China, and the Himalayas—as well as North America and Europe. Many species of maples have been introduced from Japan for ornamental purposes.

In terms of insect life, Japan is categorized within the Europeo-Asiatic group. This differs from the insects of southern Asia, including China, Siam, the Malayan Islands, and India south of the Himalaya, which belong to a single group where even the species are often the same across the Bay of Bengal.

## Geological and Physical Connections
Geological evidence indicates that the islands of Japan were once part of the Asiatic continent. This is supported by the discovery of Tertiary mammals in gravel, including *elephas primigenius*, *elephas Namadicus*, *stegodon Clifti*, and unnamed varieties of horse, rhinoceros, bison, deer, ox, bear, and whale.

Japan possesses six primary routes of communication with the Asian continent:
*   **North:** Two routes exist, the principal being across the narrow strait of Soya from the northwest of Yezo to Saghalien and the Amur region of Manchuria. A secondary route runs from the northeast of Yezo through the Kuriles to Kamchatka.
*   **Southwest:** One route leads from the northwest of Kyushu via Tsushima and Iki to southeast Korea; another runs from the south of the Izumo promontory.
*   **South:** Two routes are present, one from the southwest of Kyushu via the Goto Islands to southeastern China, and another from the south of Kyushu through the Philippines, Formosa, and the Ryukyu Islands to Polynesia and Malaysia.

These avenues likely allowed for immigrants from China, Siberia, Polynesia, and Malaysia.

## Human and Cultural Exchanges
The Japanese people are of the Mongolian race and physically resemble Amerindians. While the original inhabitants may have been the Hairy Ainu—a northern people with remote Nordic affinities—the Japanese may have also absorbed Negrito and Malay elements from the south.

Cultural and religious exchanges between China and Japan were significant. In the realm of Buddhism, the teacher Kukai (Kobo Daishi) traveled to China to gain comprehension of a Sutra he had studied in Japan; upon his return in 806, he presented his knowledge to the emperor and bonzes. Additionally, Buddhists recognized Shinto gods as incarnations of Bodhisattvas and Buddhas brought from China and India, leading to an amalgamation of the two faiths.

Trade also facilitated the movement of technology. Between the seventh and tenth centuries, flourishing trade between Persia and China brought certain forms to the Orient. A specific type of item found in the Kwanto provinces and further north was known in Persia and western Asia. While this type is not known to exist in China, it is noted that China was generally more conservative than Japan regarding foreign innovations.

## Regional Context of East Asia
The broader region of East Asia exhibits diverse biological zones. The area comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and central Asia (between the southern border of Siberia and the temperate alpine zone of the Himalaya and north China) is an almost rainless region with extreme temperatures. Its plants and animals are more closely allied to the northern Siberian tract than to other bordering regions.

In contrast, southern China is botanically hardly distinct from the great Indian region. Analysis of Hong Kong's flora shows that approximately three-fifths of its species are common to the Indian region, while the remainder are local or Chinese forms. The number of species common to northern Asia, Japan, and southern China is small.
