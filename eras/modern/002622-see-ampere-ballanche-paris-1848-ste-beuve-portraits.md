# Sainte-Beuve and Contemporary Portraits

The works of Sainte-Beuve, particularly his series *Portraits Contemporains*, serve as a significant source of biographical and critical material for various figures in French intellectual, political, and artistic history. His writings provide characterizations and analyses of a wide array of individuals, ranging from philosophers and poets to political figures.

## Literary and Philosophical Subjects

Sainte-Beuve's *Portraits Contemporains* includes evaluations of several prominent thinkers and writers. In volume II of this work, he provides portraits of the philosopher P. S. Ballanche and the poet and dramatist Henri Auguste Barbier. 

Henri Auguste Barbier (1805–1882) was a Paris-born poet inspired by the July revolution. He is best known for a series of vigorous poems titled *Iambes* (1831), which were modeled on the verse of Andre Chenier and denounced the evils of the era. These poems included works such as *La Curee*, *L'Idole*, *Paris*, *Dante*, *Varsovie*, *La Popularite*, and *Quatre-vingt-treize*. Despite his later admission to the Academy in 1869, Barbier's reputation had faded to the point that Montalembert remarked, "Barbier? mais il est mort!"

Sainte-Beuve also addressed the life of the historian Daunou in volume III of *Portraits Contemporains*. The archive notes that this particular account of Daunou is considered somewhat unfair and unfavourable.

## Historical and Social Figures

Beyond the *Portraits Contemporains*, Sainte-Beuve's other works, such as *Causeries du lundi* (volume IV), offer insights into figures of the late 18th century. This includes the life of Marmontel, who served as the historiographer of France starting in 1771, a professor of history in the Lycée (1786), and secretary to the Academy (1783). Marmontel's own *Mémoires d'un père* (1804) provided a literary history of two reigns and a gallery of portraits extending from Mirabeau to Massillon.

Sainte-Beuve also commented on Madame de Krüdener, observing that she possessed an "immense besoin que le monde s'occupât d'elle" and was driven by "l'amour propre, toujours l'amour propre."

## Related Biographical Works

The archive contains references to other portraitists and biographers who documented the same era. Elme Marie Caro (1826–1887), a French philosopher and member of the French Academy, authored *Melanges et portraits* (1888) and a work on George Sand (1887). Caro's philosophical work was primarily focused on defending Christianity against modern Positivism.

Other biographical sources mentioned in the archive include:
* **Ballanche:** Works by Ampere (1848), Damiron, Eugene Blum (1887), Gaston Frainnet (1903), and C. Huit (1904). An analysis of the *Palingenesie* was also provided by Barchou in the *Revue des deux mondes* (1831).
* **Daunou:** Historical notices by Mignet (1843) and bibliographic documents by Taillandier (1847).
* **Désaugiers:** A writer of comedies and vaudevilles who managed the Vaudeville from 1815 to 1820; his life is further detailed in volume V of Sainte-Beuve's *Portraits contemporains*.
