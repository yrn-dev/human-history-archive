# Indian Triangulation and Minor Extensions

The Indian system of geodetic triangulation serves as the foundation for exact surveys extending as far east as the eastern boundaries of Burma, at approximately 100 degrees east longitude. By the close of the 19th century, the final links of the great geodetic triangulation of India were forged regarding the peninsula. While a direct geodetic connection with European systems remains to be accomplished, the Indian system has been expanded through various minor extensions.

## Scope of Minor Extensions
Minor extensions of the triangulation, often utilizing systems involving varying degrees of irregularity, have been pushed outwards in all directions. These extensions reach through Baluchistan and Afghanistan to the eastern districts of Persia, and continue along the coast of Makran to that of Arabia. To the north and east, the system has long included the farther mountain peaks of Nepal and has branched outwards into Siam and towards western China.

These extensions provide the necessary basis for a significant volume of exploratory surveys of a strictly geographical nature. This work has contributed to raising the standard of accuracy in Asiatic geographical surveys to levels considered unattainable fifty years prior.

## Regional Developments and Explorations
The extension of the Indian triangulation has been instrumental in determining the geographical positions of specific landmarks and regions:

*   **Tibet and Lhasa:** The 1904 mission of Sir Francis Younghusband to Lhasa resulted in an extension of the Indian triangulation system that finally determined the geographical position of the city. This period also saw valuable reconnaissance of the Indus and Upper Brahmaputra valleys conducted by Captains C.G. Rawling and C.H.D. Ryder.
*   **Western Tibet and Turkestan:** Since the beginning of the century, the work of Deasy in western Tibet was extended by Captain C.G. Rawling and Dr. M.A. Stein, increasing knowledge of ancient commerce and industry in Tibet and Turkestan.
*   **Central Asia:** Explorations by Sven Hedin between 1899 and 1902, and again from 1905 to 1908, revolutionized knowledge of the region north of the upper Tsanpo. Additionally, Ellsworth Huntington's 1903 explorations provided new information on the Alai range and the Tian-shan plateau.
*   **Frontiers:** In the north, explorations of Chinese Turkestan have been brought into survey connection with India and Kashmir. This has clarified that the Kuen-lun mountains—which extend from the frontiers of Kashmir, north of Leh, east to the Chinese province of Kansu—are the northern limit of the Chang Tang (the elevated highland steppes of Tibet) rather than the southern limit of the Turkestan depression or Gobi.

## Connection with Russian Surveys
While Asia has not yet been connected to Europe via the direct process of earth measurement (principal triangulation), a topographical connection has been established between Indian and Russian surveys. This connection demonstrates that the deductive methods used by both nations to determine the co-ordinate values of fixed points agree sufficiently that no adjustment difficulties are expected for future Asiatic cartography.

In Russia, exact triangulation-based surveys extend as far east as Chinese Turkestan (approximately 75 degrees east of Greenwich). However, Russian surveys in Asia are separated from European surveys by the Caspian Sea. Due to the arid nature of trans-Caspian deserts, rigorous geodetic methods were impossible, leaving Russian geography in central Asia dependent on means other than direct measurement. Consequently, the trans-Caspian triangulation is ranked as second-class, starting from the astronomical observatory at Tashkent and extending to the Pamir frontier beyond Osh.

## Longitude Determination and Accuracy
To determine longitudes differentially with Greenwich, the telegraphic method has been applied rigorously since 1890. This resulted in a slight correction of about 2" of arc to previous determinations made through Suez. The determination was effected through four arcs:
1. Greenwich to Potsdam
2. Potsdam to Teheran
3. Teheran to Bushire
4. Bushire to Karachi

During these measurements, the only element of uncertainty was the retardation of the current between Teheran and Potsdam (3000 m.), which took 0.20 seconds to travel. It is probable that the final value is correct within 0.05 seconds. The final result of this determination placed the Madras observatory 2' 27" to the west of its previously adopted position based on absolute astronomical determinations.

## Current State of Asiatic Geography
By the end of the 19th century, western Asia (excluding Arabia) was largely freed from geographical perplexity. However, vast fields remain open for survey in Asia. Enormous areas of eastern Siberia, Mongolia, and China still possess geographical knowledge that is in a primitive stage of nebulous uncertainty.
