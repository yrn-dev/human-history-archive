# Heidelberg

Heidelberg is a town located in the district of Mannheim within the region of Baden. As of 1905, the population of Heidelberg was 49,439.

## Academic and Cultural Significance
Heidelberg is described as an ancient cradle of German culture and is home to a university of significant fame. The university's prestige was advanced through the efforts of Johann von Dalberg (1445–1503), a humanist and privy councillor to the elector palatine Philip. Dalberg was instrumental in establishing the university library, a college for students of civil law, and the first chair of Greek, which was held by Rudolph Agricola.

The university has been a site of study for various international figures, including Edward Livermore Burlingame, who was educated there in addition to his studies at Harvard. It has also been the professional home to several notable scholars:
* **Johann Christian Felix Baehr (1798–1872):** A German philologist who studied at the university and was appointed professor of classical philology in 1823. He later served as the chief librarian in 1832 and director of the philological seminary.
* **Heinrich Georg Bronn (1800–1862):** A German geologist born in nearby Ziegelhausen. He earned his medical doctor's degree from the university in 1821 and became a professor of natural history in 1822.

## Scientific and Paleontological Findings
The city is associated with the "Heidelberg Man," a prehistoric creature. This specimen is characterized by a jaw-bone that serves as a glimpse into a past wilderness inhabited by woolly rhinoceroses and sabre-toothed tigers. Scientific analysis distinguishes the Heidelberg creature from other early hominids, such as *Eoanthropus* (the Dawn Man). While these findings are significant to the study of human precursors, scientific consensus indicates that the Heidelberg Man is a related form rather than a direct ancestor of modern humans.

## Notable Events and Publications
Heidelberg has served as a location for the publication of academic works, such as Savigny's six-volume *Geschichte des Romischen Rechts im Mittelalter* (1815) and Morneweg's *Johann von Dalberg, ein deutscher Humanist und Bischof* (1887).

In a more contemporary incident, the Swedish explorer Sven Hedin was detained by the Heidelberg police for several days. The arrest occurred after Hedin delivered a public lecture; the police viewed his intense enthusiasm for the greatness of Germany as suspicious, leading to suspicions of espionage. He was released after the German Official Press Bureau, his employer, established that he was not a spy.
