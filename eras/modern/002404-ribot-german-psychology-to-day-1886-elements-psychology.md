# Ribot's German Psychology of To-day and Elements of Psychology

The works *German Psychology of To-day* (1886) and *Elements of Psychology* (1893) are associated with the academic contributions of James Mark Baldwin.

## James Mark Baldwin
James Mark Baldwin (born 1861) was an American philosopher who received his education at Princeton and various universities in Germany. Throughout his career, he held several prestigious academic positions, serving as a professor of philosophy at the University of Toronto in 1889, a professor of psychology at Princeton in 1893, and later a professor of both philosophy and psychology at Johns Hopkins University starting in 1903.

Baldwin was a prominent figure among experimental psychologists and was one of the founders of the *Psychological Review*. His leadership roles in the field included serving as the president of the American Psychological Association from 1897 to 1898 and as the vice-president of the International Congress of Psychology held in London in 1892. Additionally, he served as the honorary president of the International Congress of Criminal Anthropology in Geneva in 1896.

## Literary Contributions
Baldwin authored and edited numerous works across the fields of psychology and philosophy. His bibliography includes:
* *Handbook of Psychology* (1890)
* *Elements of Psychology* (1893)
* *Mental Development in the Child and the Race* (1896)
* *Social and Ethical Interpretations in Mental Development* (1898)
* *Story of the Mind* (1898)
* *Thought and Things* (vol. i., 1906)

In addition to his original writings, Baldwin provided the translation of Ribot's *German Psychology of To-day*, which was published in 1886. He also served as the editor-in-chief of the *Dictionary of Philosophy and Psychology*, published between 1901 and 1905, to which he contributed extensively.

## Recognition and Honors
For his contributions to the field, Baldwin was awarded a gold medal from the Royal Academy of Arts and Sciences of Denmark in 1897. He was also granted an honorary D.Sc. from Oxford University.
