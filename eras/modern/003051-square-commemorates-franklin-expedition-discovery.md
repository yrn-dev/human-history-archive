# The Franklin Expedition and the North-West Passage

The Franklin expedition was a voyage dedicated to the discovery of the North-West Passage. This historic effort and its participants are commemorated in various locations and through the geographical naming of regions in British North America.

## Commemoration in Banbridge
In the town of Banbridge, located in County Down, Ireland, a memorial exists in Church Square to commemorate the Franklin expedition to the discovery of the North-West Passage. The memorial specifically honors Captain Francis Crozier, who was born in Banbridge in 1796 and served on the expedition.

## Search Expeditions and Key Figures
Following the Franklin expedition, numerous search efforts were launched to locate Sir John Franklin's ships. These expeditions involved several notable naval officers and explorers:

*   **Sir Francis Leopold M'Clintock:** A British naval officer born in Dundalk, Ireland. M'Clintock participated in three search expeditions: he served as second lieutenant of the *Enterprise* in 1848 under Sir James Ross, as first lieutenant of the *Assistance* in 1850, and commanded the *Intrepid* in 1854. In 1857, he accepted command of the *Fox*, an expedition fitted out by Lady Franklin, which succeeded in its object in 1859.
*   **Sir Robert John Le Mesurier M'Clure:** An English Arctic explorer born in Wexford, Ireland. M'Clure joined the Franklin search expedition of 1848–1849 as first lieutenant of the *Enterprise*. He later commanded the *Investigator* during a search expedition from 1850 to 1854, which operated via the Bering Strait. During this voyage, M'Clure achieved the distinction of completing the work connected with the discovery of a North-West Passage.
*   **Joseph Rene Bellot:** A French Arctic explorer who joined the search for Sir John Franklin in 1851 under the command of Captain Kennedy. He also accompanied the Franklin search expedition under Captain Inglefield. Bellot disappeared in August 1853 while on a perilous journey to communicate with Sir Edward Belcher.
*   **Frederick William Beechey:** An English naval officer who served under Lieutenant (later Sir) John Franklin during Buchan's Arctic expedition. Beechey later published a narrative of this service.

## Geographical Connections
The Franklin expedition and subsequent searches are closely linked to the geography of British North America, particularly the Boothia Peninsula (Boothia Felix). This peninsula, which has an area of 13,100 square miles, is located within the Franklin district. 

The region is of scientific and historical importance; the north magnetic pole was first distinctly localized there by Captain (later Sir James) Ross. Additionally, Joseph Rene Bellot discovered the strait that bears his name, located between Somerset Land and Boothia Felix, during his search for Franklin.
