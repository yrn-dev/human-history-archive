# Taj Mahal Hotel

The Taj Mahal hotel is one of the most notable buildings in the city of Bombay. Built by the Tata family in 1904, it is described as the most modern and palatial hotel in India. Within the context of the city's commercial architecture, some of the European hotels and commercial buildings are constructed on an American scale, possessing no rival in any other Indian city.

## Location and Urban Context

The hotel is situated in Bombay, a city located on the Bombay Peninsula (also referred to as Bombay Island). The peninsula is characterized by a harbor studded with precipices and rocky islands, and is flanked by two parallel lines of low hills. The city's layout includes a central plain and a nucleus formed by a fort located on a raised strip of land between the harbor and Back Bay. 

The city is noted for its impressive waterfront, featuring a line of public offices along the esplanade facing Back Bay. These offices are designed in a Gothic style mixed with Saracenic. Other significant landmarks in the city include the Victoria terminus of the Great Indian Peninsula railway.

## Regional Geography and Demographics

The Taj Mahal hotel is located in a city that serves as a great meeting-place and port for the Eastern world, positioned centrally between the East and the West. Due to this location and the diversity of races in India, Bombay exhibits a vast variety of population types. While the Mahratta race is the dominant element alongside European rulers, the city is home to many other groups, including:

*   Arab traders from the Gulf
*   Afghans and Sikhs from northern India
*   Bengalis, Rajputs, Chinese, Japanese, Malays, negroes, Tibetans, Sinhalese, and Siamese

Among the various religious sections of the population—which include Hindus, Mahommedans, Jains, Christians, and Parsees—the Parsees are one of the smallest groups, numbering approximately 46,000. Despite their small size, they are among the most influential; Parsee millionaires own most of the great business houses and have founded most of the large charities.

## Surrounding Areas

A favorite suburb of the city is Malabar hill, a high ridge that runs out into the sea. This area is terraced to the top with handsome houses that offer some of the finest views of their kind. Towards the northern end of Malabar hill are the Parsee Towers of Silence. In these towers, the Parsees expose their dead until vultures devour the flesh, after which the bones are cast into a well to crumble into dust.
