# Johann Franz Buddeus

Johann Franz Buddeus (1667–1729) was a German Lutheran divine and academic who held various professorships in theology, philosophy, and languages during the late 17th and early 18th centuries.

## Early Life and Education
Buddeus was born in Anklam, a town in Pomerania, where his father served as a pastor. He pursued his higher education at Wittenberg and Greifswald, where he studied with great distinction. His academic focus was specialized in the fields of history, theology, and languages.

## Academic Career
Buddeus's professional career began in 1692 when he was appointed as a professor of Latin and Greek at Coburg. A year later, in 1693, he moved to the university of Halle to serve as a professor of moral philosophy. In 1705, he transitioned to Jena, where he was appointed professor of theology. Buddeus was held in high esteem at Jena, eventually becoming a member of the Consistory and the Primarius of his faculty in 1715.

## Principal Works
Buddeus authored several significant works across multiple disciplines:

*   **Theology and Church History:** He wrote the four-volume *Historia, Ecclesiastica Veteris Testamenti* (Halle, 1709) and the two-volume *Isagoge Historico-Theologica ad Theologiam Universam, singulasque ejus partes* (1727). He also produced the three-volume *Miscellanea Sacra* (Jena, 1727).
*   **Philosophy and Law:** His contributions to philosophy include the three-volume *Elementa Philosophiae Practicae, Instrumentalis, et Theoreticae* (1697) and the legal work *Selecta Juris Naturae et Gentium* (Halle, 1704).
*   **Lexicography:** He is credited with the *Leipzig, allgemeines historisches Lexikon* (Leipzig, 1709 ff.).
