# Cumberland's De Legibus Naturae and Pufendorf's De Officio Hominis et Civis

The treatises *De Legibus Naturae* by Cumberland and *De Officio Hominis et Civis* by Pufendorf are works of jurisprudence and philosophy that were brought to a wider audience through the scholarly efforts of the French jurist Jean Barbeyrac.

## Jean Barbeyrac and the Translations
Jean Barbeyrac (1674–1744) was a French jurist born in Beziers in Lower Languedoc. Following the revocation of the edict of Nantes, Barbeyrac and his family moved to Switzerland, where he pursued the study of jurisprudence. His academic career included positions as a professor of belles-lettres in the French school of Berlin, a professor of history and civil law at Lausanne starting in 1711, and finally a professor of public law at Groningen.

Barbeyrac is noted for translating several significant legal and philosophical texts, including Cumberland's *De Legibus Naturae* and Pufendorf's smaller treatise, *De Officio Hominis et Civis*. He also translated Grotius's *De Jure Belli et Pacis* and Pufendorf's *De Jure Naturae et Gentium*.

## Intellectual Influence and Theory
In his own work and his translations, Barbeyrac's fundamental principles were almost entirely aligned with those of Pufendorf and Locke. He applied great skill to the theory of moral obligation, which he attributed to the will or command of God. 

Barbeyrac's contributions to the field included:
* **Moral and Legal Distinction**: He indicated a distinction between the moral and legal qualities of an action, a concept that was later developed more extensively by Kant and Thomasius.
* **International Law**: He reduced the principles of international law to the principles of the law of nature, a move that led him to combat various positions held by Grotius.
* **Sovereignty and Contract**: Barbeyrac rejected the idea that sovereignty resembled property in any way. Additionally, he characterized marriage as a matter of civil contract.

## Other Works of Barbeyrac
Beyond his translations of Cumberland and Pufendorf, Barbeyrac authored several original works. These include *De la morale des peres*, a history of ancient treaties found in the *Supplement au grand corps diplomatique*, and a 1709 work titled *Traite du jeu*, in which he argued for the morality of games of chance.
