# The River Frome and Eastville Park

## Geography and Course
The Frome is a little river that flows through the region of Bristol. Its course leads it to a junction with the Avon, where it joins the larger river immediately above the Bristol Bridge.

## Eastville Park
One of the extensive open spaces located near the city is Eastville Park. This park is situated by the Frome, positioned to the north-east of the city beyond the Stapleton Road station.

## Context of the City
The nucleus of Bristol is located to the north of the river. The city's business center is found in a district where Corn Street, Wine Street, High Street, and Broad Street radiate from a central point located close to the Floating Harbour. To the south of this center, an island is formed between the New Course of the Avon and the Floating Harbour, connected to the center via the Bristol Bridge. This island area contains public buildings, the general hospital, Temple Meads station (located above Victoria Street), and two notable churches: St Mary Redcliffe and the Temple.

The city is characterized by irregular streets due to the nature of its site. In the inner sections of the city, these streets are generally narrow and are sometimes considered extremely picturesque because of their ancient gabled houses. The city is surrounded by principal suburbs to the east, north, and west.

## Other Regional Open Spaces
In addition to Eastville Park, there are several other significant open spaces in and near Bristol:
* **Durdham and Clifton Downs:** Located on the Gloucestershire side, these are among the most extensive open spaces and border the river in the neighborhood of the gorge.
* **Victoria Park:** Situated south of the river, near the Bedminster station.
* **St Andrew's Park:** Located to the north, near Montpelier station.
* **Brandon Hill:** An abrupt eminence located west of the cathedral that provides a fine view over the city. It is crowned with a modern tower built to commemorate the fourth centenary of the discovery of America by John Cabot and his sons Sanctus, Sebastian, and Lewis.
