## Jerez de la Frontera
Jerez de la Frontera (also referred to as Xeres) is a principal inland town with a population of 63,473 as of 1900. The city is renowned for the manufacture and export of sherry. It is situated on a railway line from Seville that continues to San Fernando and Cadiz. In the realm of cultural traditions, the town is associated with the *Jaleo de Jerez*, a whirling dance performed by gipsies.

Separately, the archive mentions Jerez de los Caballeros, located in Estremadura. This town is noted as the birthplace of the Spanish explorer Vasco Nunez de Balboa, who was born there around 1475.

## Alcantara and Valencia de Alcántara
The archive contains extensive records regarding the Order of Knights of Alcantara, a military religious order. Instituted around 1156 by Don Gomez de Barrientos and Don Suarez for protection against the Moors, the order was confirmed as a religious order of knighthood under Benedictine rule by Pope Alexander III in 1177. Originally known as the Knights of San Julian del Pereyro, the group adopted the name Alcantara after the city of Alcantara was wrested from the Moors by Alphonso IX of Castile and entrusted to their defense.

The order grew in wealth and influence, eventually becoming nearly as powerful as the sovereign. In 1494-1495, the grand-mastership was transferred to King Ferdinand, a move ratified by Pope Alexander VI and made permanent by Pope Adrian VI in 1523. In 1540, Pope Paul III permitted the knights to marry, though second marriages remained forbidden. The order's spiritual existence ended in 1835, though it continues to exist as an order of military merit, characterized by a red *cross fleury* badge and ribbon.

In terms of infrastructure, the more northerly of the two Madrid-Lisbon railways reaches the Portuguese frontier at Valencia de Alcántara.

## Regional Geography: Badajoz and Cáceres
The areas surrounding these locations are part of the frontier provinces of western Spain. 

**Badajoz** was formed in 1833 from districts of Estremadura. It is the largest province in the kingdom, bounded by Portugal to the west, Seville and Huelva to the south, Cordova and Ciudad Real to the east, and Caceres to the north. The province is characterized by a desolate, monotonous plain and the Guadiana river. It is the leading Spanish province for livestock, particularly sheep, goats, and acorn-fed swine celebrated for their bacon and hams.

**Cáceres** is also a province of western Spain formed in 1833 from Estremadura, bounded by Portugal to the west, Badajoz to the south, Toledo to the east, and Salamanca and Ávila to the north. It is the second largest province after Badajoz. Its economy is based on stock-breeding (ranking second to Badajoz), the raising of olives and grain, and the export of wool, hams, and red sausages known as *embutidos*.
