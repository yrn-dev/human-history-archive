# Phenyl-azo-carboxylic Acid

Phenyl-azo-carboxylic acid, represented by the chemical formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\cdot\text{N}_2\cdot\text{COOH}$, is a chemical compound categorized as a carboxylic acid and may also be considered a mixed azo derivative.

## Production and Properties
Phenyl-azo-carboxylic acid is obtained in the form of its potassium salt through the oxidation of phenylsemicarbazide using potassium permanganate in an alkaline solution. This process was documented by J. Thiele in 1895.

The substance is characterized by its physical form, crystallizing as orange-red needles. It is noted for its instability in the presence of water, as it is decomposed by it.

## Related Compounds
The oxidation of phenylsemicarbazide also results in the production of a corresponding amide known as phenyl-azo-carbonamide ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2\cdot\text{CONH}_2$). This amide forms reddish-yellow needles with a melting point of $114^\circ\text{C}$. When phenyl-azo-carbonamide is heated with benzaldehyde to a temperature of $120^\circ\text{C}$, it yields diphenyloxytriazole, which has the formula $(\text{C}_6\text{H}_5)_2\text{CN}_3\text{C}(\text{OH})$.

Additionally, the diazo cyanides ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N}_2\cdot\text{CN}$), specifically diazobenzenecyanide, are also considered mixed azo derivatives. Diazobenzenecyanide is an unstable oil produced when potassium cyanide is added to a solution of a diazonium salt.
