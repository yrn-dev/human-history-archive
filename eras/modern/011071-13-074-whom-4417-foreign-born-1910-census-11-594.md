# Ashland, Wisconsin

## Overview and Geography
Ashland is a city located in the northern part of Wisconsin, U.S.A., where it serves as the county-seat of Ashland county. The city is situated at the head of Chequamegon Bay, which is an arm of Lake Superior. Geographically, it is positioned approximately 70 miles east of Duluth and Superior, and about 315 miles northwest of Milwaukee. The city is described as being attractively situated with a dry and healthful climate, making it a destination as a summer resort.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Ashland has fluctuated over the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1890, the population was 9,956. By 1900, the population grew to 13,074, a figure that included 4,417 foreign-born residents. According to the 1910 census, the population was 11,594.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
Ashland is served by several railway lines, including the Wisconsin Central, the Northern Pacific, the Chicago & North-Western, and the Chicago, St Paul, Minneapolis & Omaha railways. Additionally, the city is connected to the Great Lakes via several steamboat lines. 

The city's infrastructure includes a fine Federal building and one of the best high-school buildings in the state of Wisconsin. Educational and health institutions include the Northland College and Academy (Congregational), a Roman Catholic hospital, the Rinehart hospital, and the Vaughn public library, which was established in 1895.

## Economy and Industry
Ashland possesses an excellent harbour and serves as the principal port for shipping iron ore from the rich Gogebic Range. Annual ore shipments are approximately 3,500,000 tons, with a value of $12,000,000. The city also maintains large coal docks and an extensive export trade in lumber and brownstone quarried in the vicinity. The total annual lake trade exceeds $35,000,000.

The city's industrial base is diverse, featuring:
* Iron and steel rolling mills, foundries, and machine shops.
* Large saw-mills.
* Railway repair shops specifically for the Chicago & North-Western railway.
* Knitting works.
* Manufactories producing wood-alcohol, charcoal, sulphite fibre, and dynamite.

In 1905, the total value of the city's factory product was $4,210,265.

## History
Ashland was settled around 1854. It was later incorporated as a village in 1863 and was granted a city charter in 1887.
