# Upper Austria

Upper Austria (German: *Oberoesterreich* or *Oesterreich ob der Enns*, meaning "Austria above the river Enns") is an archduchy and crown-land of Austria. It is bounded to the north by Bohemia, to the west by Bavaria, to the south by Styria and Salzburg, and to the east by Lower Austria. The province has a total area of 4,631 square miles.

## Geography and Climate
The Danube river divides Upper Austria into two unequal sections. The smaller northern portion is a prolongation of the southern angle of the Bohemian forest, featuring the Sternstein (3,690 ft.) and the Ploecklstein (4,510 ft.) as its highest points. The southern portion is part of the Eastern Alps region, containing the Upper Austrian Alps and the Salzkammergut. Between these mountains and the Danube lies the Alpine foothill region, characterized by swelling undulations and terraces. A significant feature of this region is the Hausruckwald, a wooded mountain chain rich in brown coal that reaches its peak at the Goeblberg (2,950 ft.).

The province is part of the Danube watershed. The Danube flows from west to east, receiving several tributaries: the Enns (with the Steyr), the Traun, and the Inn (with the Salzach) on its right bank, and the Little and Great Muehl rivers on its left. A direct navigable route between the Danube and the Elbe is provided by the Schwarzenberg canal, located between the Moldau and the Great Muehl.

The climate is generally moderate, though it varies by altitude. It is mild in the Salzkammergut and somewhat more severe in the northern regions.

## Population and Administration
As of 1900, the population of the duchy was 809,918, resulting in a population density of 174.8 inhabitants per square mile, the highest of any Alpine province. The inhabitants are Roman Catholics and almost exclusively of German stock.

Administratively, Upper Austria is divided into 12 districts and two autonomous municipalities: the capital, Linz (population 58,778), and Steyr (population 17,592). Other principal towns include Gmunden (7,126), Ischl (9,646), and Wels (12,187). The province is represented by 22 members in the Reichsrat at Vienna. The local diet consists of 50 members, including the bishop of Linz, who is a member *ex officio*.

## History and Notable Locations
The original archduchy, which included Upper Austria, serves as the nucleus of the Austrian empire and is the oldest possession of the house of Habsburg within its current dominions. Historically, the river Enns served as a boundary for a considerable period.

The town of Enns, located 11 miles southeast of Linz by rail, is one of the oldest towns in Austria and is situated near the site of the Roman *Laureacum*. The town's core was formed around the Anasiburg (or Anesburg) castle, built by the Bavarians in 900 to defend against Hungarian incursions. Enns became a free town by a charter in 1212 and passed to Rudolph of Habsburg in 1275. The town features a town hall built in 1565, a Gothic church, and a 15th-century castle. On November 5, 1805, Austrian and French troops engaged in a battle here.

Near Enns, three miles to the southwest, is the Augustinian monastery of St Florian. Founded in the 7th century, it was held by Benedictines until the mid-11th century before passing to the Augustinians in 1071. The current buildings, constructed between 1686 and 1745, are among the most magnificent in Austria. The monastery houses a collection of coins and a library containing over 70,000 volumes, including valuable manuscripts.

## Political Context
Upper Austria is part of the purely German district that forms the kernel of the empire, along with Lower Austria, Vorarlberg, Salzburg, most of Styria, and parts of Carinthia and Tirol. In the political struggles between Centralists and Federalists, the region was characterized by strong loyalty to the Austrian state and the ruling house. While the Liberal party was dominant in Styria and Lower Austria, a Conservative and Clerical party supported local institutions to protect against the influence of a centralized bureaucracy and parliament.
