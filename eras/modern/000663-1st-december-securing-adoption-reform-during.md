## Political Reform in Austria
In the early 20th century, the Austro-Hungarian Empire underwent a period of political transition. Following the fall of Baron Gautsch in April over differences with the Poles, Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst took over the reform bills but resigned six weeks later. His resignation served as a protest against the crown's consent to a customs tariff in Hungary that was distinct from the joint Austro-Hungarian tariff established in the Szell-Koerber compact and enacted by the Reichsrath.

On June 2, a new cabinet was formed under Baron von Beck, a former instructor in administrative and constitutional law to Archduke Francis Ferdinand. Through skillful negotiation with various races and parties, and with the steady support of the emperor—who informed recalcitrant party leaders at the Hofburg that the reform "must be accomplished"—Baron Beck reached a final agreement in October 1906. This process culminated on the 1st of December, when he succeeded in securing the adoption of the reform. As part of these negotiations, the number of constituencies was increased to 516, divided according to provinces.

## Calendar Reforms and Chronology
The 1st of December is situated within various systems of chronological and seasonal measurement. In the reformation of the calendar in 46 B.C., Julius Cæsar intercalated two months totaling 67 days between November and December. Because an intercalary month of 23 days had already been inserted in February that year according to old customs, the total intercalation for 46 B.C. reached 90 days, making it a year of 445 days. Cæsar's reformed year consisted of 365 days, with one day intercalated every fourth year to align the civil year, which began on the 1st of January, with the solar year.

Later, in 1582, Gregory XIII, with the sanction of the Council of Trent, dropped ten days between October 4th and 15th. In Great Britain, the Calendar (New Style) Act 1750 was eventually passed to adopt the new style for legal and public transactions. This act removed an eleven-day difference by designating the day after September 2, 1752, as September 14. This legislation also shifted the commencement of the legal year from March 25 to the 1st of January.

## Financial and Administrative Cycles
The 1st of December serves as a marker in financial and budgetary history. In England, prior to 1832, the financial year ran from the 1st of January to the 31st of December. In other nations, the start of the financial year varies: France, Belgium, and Austria begin theirs on the 1st of January, while the United Kingdom, Germany, and Denmark begin on the 1st of April.

This period is characterized by a lack of atmospheric moisture, causing vegetation to droop, skin to peel, and woodwork to warp and crack. This season follows the period of "little rains," which concludes on the 1st of December.
