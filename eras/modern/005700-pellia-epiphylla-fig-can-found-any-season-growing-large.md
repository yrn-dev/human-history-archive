# Pellia epiphylla

*Pellia epiphylla* is a thalloid liverwort that can be found growing in large patches during any season. Its preferred habitats include banks and the damp soil of woods.

## Physical Characteristics
The plant features a green, broad, and flat thallus that may reach a length of a couple of inches. The thallus is sparingly branched, with branching that appears to be dichotomous. At the anterior end of each branch, the growing point is located within a depression. 

The thallus consists of a midrib with wing-like lateral portions that gradually thin out from the center. While the thallus bears no leaf-like appendages, short glandular hairs are present behind the apex. On the projecting lower surface of the midrib, numerous rhizoids spring forth. These rhizoids are elongated superficial cells that function to obtain salts and water from the soil and to fix the thallus in place.

## Cellular Structure and Growth
The growth of the thallus is driven by the regular segmentation of a single apical cell. The plant is composed of very similar living cells throughout, though they differ by location:
* **Superficial cells:** Contain numerous chlorophyll grains.
* **Internal cells of the midrib:** Used for the storage of starch.

Additionally, the cells contain oil-bodies, although their specific function is imperfectly understood.

## Reproductive Organs and Fertilization
The sexual organs of *Pellia epiphylla* are located on the upper surface of the plant, with both archegonia and antheridia occurring on the same branch. 

The antheridia are scattered across the middle region of the thallus, and each is surrounded by a tubular upgrowth from the surface. The archegonia develop in a group behind the apex. Because the apex continues to grow after the archegonia are formed, they eventually become seated in a depression on the upper surface. These organs are further protected by a scale-like involucre, which is formed by the growth of the hinder margin of the depression.

Fertilization typically takes place around June.

## Development of the Sporogonium
The sporogonium is fully developed by the winter. It develops from a fertilized ovum, starting as an embryo consisting of several tiers of cells:
* **Terminal tier:** This tier gives rise to the capsule. The first divisions in the four cells of this tier distinguish the wall of the capsule from the cells that will produce the spores (archesporial cells).
* **Lower tiers:** These tiers give rise to the foot and the seta.

A mature sporogonium consists of a capsule, calyptra, seta, and foot, with an involucre also present.
