# Political Shifts in Vienna and Lower Austria

## The Decline of the Liberals
In the political landscape of Vienna and Lower Austria, a significant shift occurred regarding the representation of the Liberals. In 1879, the Liberals held a strong position, returning 33 members out of a total of 37 from Lower Austria. However, subsequent election results saw the disappearance of the Liberals in Vienna. To a large extent, these Liberal members were replaced by the Socialists.

## Impact on Government Stability
The loss of the Liberal presence had implications for the stability of the government. The government relied on a strong party of moderate constitutionalists; however, maintaining such a party was deemed impossible without a large nucleus of support originating from Lower Austria.

## The Influence of Lueger
During this period, the influence of Lueger became a source of embarrassment. Lueger achieved a two-thirds majority in the town council and was elected burgomaster. His appointment faced initial resistance from the emperor, who refused to confirm the election. Even after Lueger was re-elected, the emperor personally interviewed him and appealed for his withdrawal, to which Lueger consented.

Despite this, the election of 1897 provided Lueger with a significant number of followers in the Reichsrath. Following this development, Badeni advised that Lueger's election as burgomaster should be confirmed.

## Conflict in the Reichsrath
The political environment was further complicated by a violent antipathy between the German Nationalists and the Christian Socialists. The orderly conduct of debate in the Reichsrath was detrimentally affected as the quarrels between these two groups were transferred from the Viennese Council Chamber into the Reichsrath.
