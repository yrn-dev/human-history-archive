# The MSS. at Sherborne Castle

The manuscripts (MSS.) at Sherborne Castle constitute a collection of historical documents, a selection of which has been transcribed and deposited within the Public Record Office. These materials have been subject to official cataloging by the Historical Manuscripts Commission, with records of these efforts appearing in the commission's 8th Report (appendix i, page 213) and 10th Report (appendix i, page 520).

## Historical Context and Content
The manuscripts at Sherborne Castle contain numerous references to Bristol. These records are part of a broader body of documentation regarding Bristol that can also be found in various collections calendared in the same reports of the Historical Manuscripts Commission, as well as in the Calendar of State Papers, Domestic Series. Other related materials concerning Bristol are located in the British Museum, specifically within the Harleian MSS. (1580, articles 31-48) and the indexes and calendars of the Additional MSS.

## The Sherborne Missal
Separate from the archival manuscripts of the castle, the Sherborne Missal is a significant historical volume associated with the location. It is a large volume completed in the early 15th century for the Benedictine abbey of Sherborne in Dorsetshire. The missal is currently the property of the duke of Northumberland.

The Sherborne Missal is noted as a surviving instance of high-quality English illumination from the early 15th century. It was produced by a Dominican illuminator named John Siferwas, who also executed a fragmentary Lectionary for John, Lord Lovel of Tichmersh. The work is characteristic of a brilliant but short-lived epoch in English art, featuring careful modelling of human features, gold worked in stippled patterns, and delicate but brilliant colouring.

## Related Sites at Sherborne
The manuscripts and the missal are linked to a site of significant architectural and religious history. Sherborne Castle itself exists today as picturesque ruins, having been destroyed following the Civil War of the 17th century. Additionally, the abbey church of Sherborne is recognized as one of the three finest churches in the county of Dorsetshire.
