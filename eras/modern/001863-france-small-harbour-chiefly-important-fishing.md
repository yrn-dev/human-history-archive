# France: Maritime Ports and Fisheries

The maritime landscape of France includes a variety of ports and harbors that serve diverse economic functions, ranging from large-scale international commerce and industrial shipbuilding to specialized regional fishing operations.

## Bordeaux
Bordeaux serves as the fourth port in France, with trade conducted primarily by sea. The port is formed by the basin of the Garonne and is divided into two sections by the Pont de Bordeaux. The southern portion is reserved for small craft, while the northern portion is accessible to vessels drawing between 21 and 26 feet, depending on the tide. The harbor can accommodate between 1,000 and 1,200 vessels and is equipped with sloping wharves and quays served by railway lines.

At the northern extremity on the left bank, a floating basin covering 25 acres can receive the largest vessels. This facility includes over 1,900 yards of quays, a repairing dock, and machinery for loading and unloading goods. New docks were begun behind this basin in 1907.

The city maintains commercial relations with nearly all countries, most notably the United States, Portugal, Argentina, Spain, and Great Britain. The Messageries Maritimes' South American service is the most important line of steamers using the port. The annual value of imports and exports averages between 25 and 26 million sterling, with exports accounting for 13.5 million. Approximately one quarter of these exports are sales of wine, as the city is the center of the trade in "Bordeaux" wines. Other primary exports include sugar, brandy, fruit, chemicals, oil-cake, pitwood, salt-fish, hides and skins, potatoes, vegetables, and cotton and woollen goods. Chief imports include rubber, coal, wine, timber, fish, rice, wool, oil-grains, chemicals, and agricultural and other machinery.

Bordeaux is also a center for ship-building and refitting, constructing merchant vessels, torpedo-boats, and ironclads, as well as railway carriages. Subsidiary industries to the wine trade include cooperage, wine-mixing, and the production of bottles, wooden cases, corks, capsules, and straw envelopes. Additionally, a large fleet is sent annually to Iceland and Newfoundland for cod-fisheries.

## Douarnenez
Located in the department of Finistere in western France, Douarnenez is a fishing-port situated on the southern shore of the Bay of Douarnenez, 15 miles northwest of Quimper by rail. As of 1906, the population was 13,472.

The town's economy is heavily centered on its sardine fishery, which operates from the beginning of December to the end of June. This industry employs approximately 800 boats and between 3,000 and 4,000 men. The preserving of these fish is a significant industry in the area. Other local occupations include boat-building, mackerel fishing, and the making of nets and rope. A lighthouse is located off Douarnenez on the small island of Tristan.

## Other Regional Notes
In other parts of France, such as the ponds of Pont-Chartrain, there have been reports of carp living for 200 years, though these statements do not rest on satisfactory evidence.
