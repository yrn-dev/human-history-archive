## Conflicts in the Ashanti Region

The Prah river serves as a significant geographical marker in the history of the Ashanti kingdom in British West Africa. The region's geography is characterized by dense forests and rivers that are navigable only by canoes, with the exception of the Volta. The Prah's headstreams include the small river Dah, located near the capital, Kumasi.

Military hostilities involving the Prah occurred on multiple occasions. In January 1824, Sir Charles M'Carthy, the first governor appointed by the crown, was defeated by the Ashanti at Essamako, a location situated beyond the Prah. The Ashanti force of 10,000 men overwhelmed M'Carthy's 500 troops, resulting in the death of the governor and eight other Europeans.

Later, in 1873, the Prah again became a focal point of conflict. On January 22, 1873, an Ashanti force crossed the Prah to invade the British protectorate. This invasion followed tensions regarding the transference of Elmina fort from the Dutch to the British on April 2, 1872, and the captivity of Europeans—including Mr. Fritz A. Ramseyer, his wife, Mr. J. Kuhne, and a French trader named Monsieur M.J. Bonnat—who had been taken to Kumasi. The Ashanti force defeated the Fanti and encamped at Mampon near Cape Coast. In response, the British parliament voted £800,000 for an expedition led by Sir Garnet Wolseley. Upon landing at Cape Coast on October 2, Wolseley aimed to march to Kumasi, supported by Captain John Glover and native levies operating from the east.

## Allied Operations in Europe

Separate archival records detail Allied troop movements through difficult terrain involving crests, depressions, and fortified villages. In a series of successive operations, French forces captured the ridge of Monnes on July 19, the ravine of Neuilly-St-Front that same evening, and the hill of Latilly and its wood on July 20. On July 21, La Croix and Grisolles were taken.

By July 23, Allied troops had taken Rocourt and the wood of Le Chatelet. On July 24, they captured the deep ravine of Brécy. This was followed on July 25 by a joint attack by French and American forces on the hill of the forest of Fère, which stands 228 metres high. By July 27, the position was fully taken, leaving the Allies at the edge of the valley of the Ourcq.

## Invasions in Asia

The archive also records the "March to Seoul" in 1592. On May 24, the first army corps, consisting of 18,700 men under Konishi Yukinaga, crossed to the peninsula. The Korean forces were largely unaware of the invasion until the commandant of Fusan sighted the flotilla while hunting on Deer Island. Following the landing, Konishi's troops stormed the castle of Fusan on May 25 and took another fortress three miles inland on May 27. A second corps of 20,800 men under Kato Kiyomasa arrived four days after the first landing and proceeded via the east-coast road.

Additionally, records indicate conflicts between British and Burmese territories. Following Burmese incursions into Assam and Manipur and the attack on a British guard at the island of Shahpura on September 23, 1823, war was declared on March 5, 1824. These operations concluded with the treaty of Yandaboo on February 24, 1826.
