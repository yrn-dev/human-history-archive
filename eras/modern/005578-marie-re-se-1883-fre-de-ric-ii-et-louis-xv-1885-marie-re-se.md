## Marie Thérèse Charlotte de France
Marie Thérèse Charlotte (1778–1851), known as Madame Royale, was the daughter of Louis XVI. Following the loss of her elder brother in 1789, she and her brother Louis Charles (Louis XVII) remained the children of the king. During the French Revolution, she was imprisoned for three years, earning her the designation "the orphan of the Temple." Throughout this captivity, she was unaware of the fate of her parents.

In 1799, she married her cousin, Louis, the duke of Angoulême and son of Charles X. She died on October 19, 1851. Her experiences during her captivity were documented in her own account, which was first printed in 1817. A definitive edition of her autograph text was published by G. Lenôtre in 1907 under the title *La Fille de Louis XVI., Marie Thérèse Charlotte de France, duchesse d'Angoulême, le Temple, l'échange, l'exil*. Other compilations regarding her life include *Marie Thérèse de France* (1852), edited by the marquis de Pastoret, and *Mémoires de Marie Thérèse duchesse d'Angoulême*, which features extracts from the narratives of C. F. Beaulieu, L. G. Michaud, Charles Goret, and Mme de Tourzel.
