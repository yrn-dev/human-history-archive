# Henri Auguste Barbier

## Life and Career
Henri Auguste Barbier (1805–1882) was a French poet and dramatist. He was born in Paris on April 29, 1805. His early poetic work was heavily inspired by the revolution of July, leading him to produce a series of vigorous and eager poems that crudely denounced the evils of his era. These works are referred to collectively as the *Iambes* (1831), although this designation does not strictly apply to every poem in the group. In terms of style, these poems were modeled after the verse of Andre Chenier.

Barbier's professional contributions extended into opera; he collaborated with Leon de Wailly to write the libretto for the opera *Benvenuto Cellini* by Berlioz. Additionally, he authored two series of poems focusing on the social and political troubles of England and Italy, which were included in later editions of *Iambes et poemes*.

## Notable Works
Among the poems included in the *Iambes* are:
* *La Curee*
* *La Popularite*
* *L'Idole*
* *Paris*
* *Dante*
* *Quatre-vingt-treize*
* *Varsovie*

While these specific works are noted, the archive indicates that the remainder of Barbier's poems have been forgotten.

## Later Life and Recognition
Barbier received the honor of admission to the Academy in 1869, though this recognition was considered long delayed. His admission was met with a sentiment expressed by Montalembert, who questioned, "Barbier? mais il est mort!" Furthermore, there were unfounded assertions that Barbier was not the actual author of the *Iambes*.

Henri Auguste Barbier died on February 13, 1882, in Nice.
