# Miscellanies in Literature and Classification

The term "miscellanies" appears across various historical and academic contexts, referring to collections of diverse writings, the grouping of varied linguistic families, or the classification of human races.

## Literary Collections of Miscellanies

Several authors and editors have produced works titled as miscellanies to compile varied subjects, prose, and verse.

### Daines Barrington
Daines Barrington (1727–1800) was an English naturalist, antiquary, and lawyer who served as a second justice of Chester and a Welsh judge. In 1781, he published a quarto volume titled *Miscellanies on various Subjects*. This collection included an account of the visit of an eight-year-old Mozart to London, which Barrington had previously contributed to the *Philosophical Transactions* in 1780. Within this same volume, Barrington included accounts of four other prodigies: Samuel Wesley, Charles Wesley, Crotch, and Garrett Wellesley, Lord Mornington.

### Other Notable Collections
Other writers have utilized the miscellany format to organize their output:
*   **William King:** Published a volume titled *Miscellanies in Prose and Verse* in 1705.
*   **Charles Kingsley:** Included a work titled *Miscellanies* in his bibliography, published in 1859.
*   **The Fuller Worthies Library:** In 1871, Dr. A. B. Grosart reprinted two poems by Markham—*The Teares of the Beloved* (1600) and *Marie Magdalene's Teares* (1601)—within the *Miscellanies of the Fuller Worthies Library*.
*   **Thomas Carlyle:** Provided what is described as the best account of the character, adventures, and life of Giuseppe Balsamo within his *Miscellanies*.

## Linguistic and Ethnological Miscellanies

In the fields of philology and ethnology, the term is used to describe groups that lack a singular, tight family structure or are composed of diverse elements.

### Language Families
Philologists distinguish several great language families. While groups such as the Bantu in Africa, the Dravidian in South India, and the Malay-Polynesian (which includes Indian tongues and stretches over Polynesia) are recognized, American-Indian languages are treated differently. Because these languages vary widely among themselves and are separable from any Old World group, they are categorized as a miscellany rather than a family.

### Human Race Classification
Historically, students of mankind—influenced by the story of the Ark and the three sons of Noah (Japhet, Ham, and Shem)—tended to classify humans into three or four separate races descended from separate ancestors. This approach ignored local variations and the possibility of blended races. Modern views regard the varieties of men as a tangle of differentiations. While there are four main groups, each is considered a miscellany, and there exist smaller groups that do not fit into any of the four primary divisions.
