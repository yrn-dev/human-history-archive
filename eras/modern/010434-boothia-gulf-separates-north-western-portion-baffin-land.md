# Boothia Gulf

Boothia Gulf is a body of water located in the Arctic region of British North America. It serves as a geographical separator between the Boothia Peninsula to the west and the Melville Peninsula and the north-western portion of Baffin Land to the east.

## Connectivity and Geography
The gulf is integrated into a complex network of Arctic waterways. It is connected to Lancaster Sound and Barrow Strait via Prince Regent Inlet. Additionally, it connects to the Franklin Strait through the Bellot Strait and to the Fox Channel by way of the Fury and Hecla Strait. Within the gulf, the primary bays are Lord Mayor in the western region, and Pelly and Committee in the southern region.

## The Boothia Peninsula
The Boothia Peninsula (also known as *Boothia Felix*) forms the western boundary of the Boothia Gulf. It is situated between 69 deg. 30' and 71 deg. 50' N. latitude and 91 deg. 30' and 97 deg. W. longitude, covering an area of 13,100 sq. m. The peninsula is part of the Franklin district.

The peninsula is nearly separated from the main mass of the continent by various inlets and lakes. To its east, the Bellot Strait creates a narrow channel between the peninsula and North Somerset Island. The northernmost point of the American mainland is located here at Murchison Point.

## Discovery and Scientific Importance
The Boothia Peninsula was discovered during an expedition from 1829 to 1833 by Captain Ross (later Sir James Ross). He named the land after Sir Felix Booth, who was primarily responsible for fitting out the expedition.

The region holds significant scientific value because Ross first distinctly localized the north magnetic pole on the western side of the peninsula at 70 deg. 5' N., 96 deg. 47' W. Furthermore, the peninsula is noted for its connection to the Franklin search and the Franklin expedition.

## Surrounding Regions
The Boothia Gulf is situated near Baffin Land, a barren insular tract in the Franklin district of Canada. Baffin Land covers an approximate area of 236,000 sq. m. and is located between 61deg and 90deg W. and 62deg and 74deg N. The north-western part of Baffin Land is referred to as Cockburn Land.

Baffin Land is separated from other territories by several bodies of water:
* **Greenland:** Separated by Davis Strait and Baffin Bay.
* **Ungava:** Separated by Hudson Strait.
* **Keewatin and Melville Peninsula:** Separated by Fury-and-Hecla Strait and Fox Channel.
* **Boothia Peninsula and North Somerset:** Separated by Prince Regent Inlet and the Gulf of Boothia.
* **North Devon:** Separated by Lancaster Sound.

Baffin Bay, which separates Baffin Land from Greenland, was explored extensively in 1616 by the English navigator William Baffin. This sea is typically frozen for most of the year, though navigable channels along the coasts usually exist from the beginning of June until the end of September.
