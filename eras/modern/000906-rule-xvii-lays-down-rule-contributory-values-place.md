# Rule XVII of the York-Antwerp Rules

The York-Antwerp Rules were established to introduce uniformity and certainty into the principles used to adjust General Average (G.A.) losses. Prior to these rules, there was a significant lack of definition and a great variety of practices. Specifically, Rule XVII was introduced to replace the widely varying rules of different countries regarding the amounts upon which freight and ship shall contribute.

## Definition of Contributory Values
Under Rule XVII, the contribution to a General Average is calculated based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure. To this actual value, the amount made good as G.A. for any property that was sacrificed must be added.

## Deductions and Exclusions
The rule specifies several necessary deductions to determine the final contributory value:

*   **Shipowner's Freight and Passage-Money:** Deductions are made from freight and passage-money at risk for crew's wages and port charges. These deductions apply only to costs that would not have been incurred if the cargo and ship had been totally lost on the date of the sacrifice or G.A. act, provided they have not already been allowed as G.A.
*   **General Property Charges:** All charges incurred in respect of the property subsequent to the G.A. act are deducted from the value of the property, unless those specific charges are allowed in G.A.
*   **Excluded Items:** Passengers' personal effects and luggage are not required to contribute to G.A., provided they were not shipped under a bill of lading.

## Context within the York-Antwerp Rules
Rule XVII exists alongside other specific regulations designed to bring clearness to maritime adjustments. For example, Rule XIII provides a definite scale for "new for old" deductions regarding repair costs to replace uncertain customary rules. Other related rules include:

*   **Rule XIV:** Specifies that no "new for old" deductions are made for the cost of temporary repairs for damage allowable as G.A.
*   **Rule XV:** Dictates that loss of freight resulting from the loss of or damage to cargo is made good as G.A.
*   **Rule XVI:** Establishes that the amount made good for sacrificed goods is based on the loss sustained by the owner, using market values at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival.
