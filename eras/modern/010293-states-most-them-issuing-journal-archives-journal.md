# Journals and Archives of Specialized Societies and Institutions

The publication of journals and archives serves as a primary method for the dissemination of specialized knowledge, the recording of professional standards, and the documentation of institutional history across various fields, including collecting, commerce, academia, and industry.

## Collector Societies and their Publications
The systematic study and collection of book-plates—private tokens of book-ownership—became a recognized interest around 1875, gaining significant momentum with the 1880 publication of Lord de Tabley's *Guide to the Study of Book-Plates*. This work established a classification of styles, including early armorial, Jacobean (which encompasses the heavy decorative manner of the early Georgian, Queen Anne, and Restoration periods), Chippendale (rococo), and wreath and ribbon.

As the literature on book-plates grew, societies of collectors were established in England, France, Germany, and the United States. Most of these organizations issued their own archives or journals to support their members. Notable examples include:
*   *The Journal of the Ex-libris Society* (London), a monthly publication.
*   *Archives de la societe francaise de collectionneurs d'ex-libris* (Paris), also a monthly.
*   *Ex-libris Zeitschrift* (Berlin), which is published quarterly.

## Commercial and Statutory Journals
In the context of business and book-keeping, the "journal" refers to a book of first-entry used to record commercial transactions. The requirement to maintain such journals is often dictated by the legislative requirements of different states:
*   **France:** The Code of Commerce requires traders to maintain a journal, a letter book, and an inventory.
*   **Belgium:** Law requires every merchant to keep a journal of day-to-day transactions, which must be initialled by a prescribed officer.
*   **Spain and Portugal:** The Commercial Code of Spain requires a journal (alongside an inventory, ledger, letter book, and invoice book), while Portugal prescribes a journal, balance book, ledger, and copy-letter book.

In Great Britain and North America, the practice differs from the continental European method of using a single intermediate journal. Instead, these regions typically employ several specialized journals for different classes of transactions, such as a sales journal (or day book), a bought journal (or purchases book), a bills receivable book, a bills payable book, and books for returns inwards and outwards.

## Academic and Professional Journals
Universities and professional bodies utilize journals to publish research and student work. For example, Cornell University publishes several specialized titles, including the *Journal of Physical Chemistry*, the *Physical Review*, and *Studies in History and Political Science*. Student-led publications at the university include *The Sibley Journal of Engineering* (established 1882), *The Cornell Daily Sun* (1880), and *The Cornell Era* (1868).

In the field of education, Henry Barnard served as the editor of the *American Journal of Education* from 1855 to 1881, producing thirty-one volumes. He also edited the *Journal of the Rhode Island Institute of Instruction* (1846–1849) and the *Connecticut Common School Journal* (1838–1842 and 1851–1854).

Other professional and technical journals include:
*   **Brewing:** *The Journal of the Institute of Brewing* (London), *The Brewers' Journal* (published in both London and New York), and the *Zeitschrift für das gesammte Brauwesen* (Munich).
*   **Mathematics:** The *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* and its successor, the *Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal*.
*   **Canine Studies:** In England, *The Field* is considered the leading canine journal, while others include *Our Dogs*, *The Kennel Magazine*, and *The Illustrated Kennel News*.

## Geographical and Scientific Records
Journals also serve as repositories for ethnographic and geographical exploration. The *R.G.S. Journal* and *R.G.S. Proceedings* contain reports on the boundaries and history of regions such as Makran and Baluchistan. Similarly, the *Bulletin de l'Academie de St-Petersbourg* and *Crelle's Journal* have been used to publish mathematical and scientific memoirs.
