# Publications and Figures of 1650 and 1729

The years 1650 and 1729 mark significant points in the publication of collected works and the lives of various historical figures across Europe, spanning fields from theology and philosophy to literature and mineralogy.

## Literary and Philosophical Publications

Several major collected editions of works were published during these periods. In 1650, a collected edition of the works of Balde was published in Cologne, consisting of four volumes. A more complete edition of Balde's works, comprising eight volumes, was later published in Munich in 1729.

In the realm of philosophy, the *Opera philosophica* of Descartes saw six different editions published at Amsterdam between 1650 and 1678. Other significant editions of Descartes' collected works appeared later, including an eight-volume Latin edition in Amsterdam (1670-1683) and a thirteen-volume French edition in Paris between 1724 and 1729.

In Venice, 1729 saw the publication of a collected edition of the works of Bembo (*Opere de P. Bembo*). These works include poems, dialogues, essays, and a *History of Venice* (1551) covering the years 1487 to 1513. Notable among these is the dialogue *Gli Asolani*, which discusses and recommends Platonic affection.

## Theological Works and Figures

The year 1650 was also associated with the publication of theological materials. J. Fuligatti published the *Epistolae Familiares* in Rome in 1650, which served as a very incomplete collection of letters regarding Cardinal Bellarmine. Additionally, a two-volume edition of the collected works of Episcopius was published between 1650 and 1665, featuring a life of the author prefixed by his successor, Etienne de Courcelles.

Regarding individual figures, William King was an Anglican divine born in May 1650. The son of James King, an Aberdeen man who moved to Antrim, King was educated at Trinity College, Dublin. His ecclesiastical career included serving as the dean of St Patrick's in 1689, bishop of Derry in 1691, and archbishop of Dublin in 1702. He is recognized for his work *De Origine Mali* (1702) and for founding a divinity lectureship at Trinity College, Dublin, in 1718. William King died in May 1729.

## Scientific and Historical Contributions

In the field of mineralogy, Baron Wolfgang Xaver Franz Kobell (1803-1882), a German mineralogist born in Munich, authored a work titled *Geschichte der Mineralogie von 1650-1860*, published in 1864. Kobell, who became a professor of mineralogy at the university of Munich in 1826, is also credited with the 1835 invention of the stauroscope for studying the optical properties of crystals.

Other historical records from the archive mention the *Gesta episcoporum Cambracensium*, a chronicle of the bishops of Cambrai written in the 11th century. Although once attributed to Balderic, archbishop of Noyon, it is now believed to have been written by an anonymous canon of Cambrai. This work, which is important for the history of northern France in the 11th century, was first published in 1615.
