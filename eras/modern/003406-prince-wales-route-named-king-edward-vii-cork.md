# "Prince of Wales" Route

The "Prince of Wales" route is a noted coaching route in Ireland that runs from Cork to Killarney and Glengarriff. The route is named after King Edward VII.

## Bantry and the Route
The town of Bantry serves as a coaching station on the "Prince of Wales" route. Bantry is a seaside resort, market-town, and seaport located in County Cork, Ireland, within the west parliamentary division. It is situated 58 miles southwest of Cork, accessible via the Cork, Bandon & South Coast railway, and sits on Bantry Bay.

As of 1901, Bantry had a population of 3,109. The town is recognized as a significant center for both sport with the rod and sea fisheries. Bantry also serves as the terminus for the railway.

## Geography of Bantry Bay
Bantry Bay is described as a picturesque inlet, measuring approximately 22 miles in length and between 3 to 6 miles in breadth. The waters of the bay range from 12 to 32 fathoms in depth and provide excellent anchorage. 

The bay contains several islands, with Whiddy and Bear Island being the principal ones. On the northern shore of the bay, behind Bear Island and near the mouth of the bay, is the harbour at Castletown Bearhaven. This harbour is utilized by the Channel Squadron, for which Bantry is one of the headquarter stations.

## Local History
Bantry has been the site of several military events:
* In 1689 and 1796, the French made attempts to invade Ireland at this location.
* In 1697, troops belonging to William of Orange were landed there.

The town's history also includes the failure of the pilchard fishery during the 18th century, evidenced by the ruins of structures known as "fish palaces."
