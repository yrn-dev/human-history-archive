# The Russian Revolution and the Rise of Communism

## Overview
The Russian Revolution of 1917 was not a single event, but a series of upheavals that dismantled the Tsarist autocracy and led to the creation of the Soviet Union, the world's first socialist state. The revolution emerged from a volatile mix of systemic inequality, an archaic political structure under Tsar Nicholas II, and the catastrophic pressures of World War I.

The process unfolded in two primary stages. The **February Revolution** (March in the Gregorian calendar) saw spontaneous mass protests in Petrograd, leading to the Tsar's abdication and the establishment of a fragile Provisional Government. However, this government failed to address the populace's demands for "Peace, Land, and Bread." This vacuum allowed Vladimir Lenin and the Bolsheviks to seize power during the **October Revolution** (November), implementing a Marxist-Leninist framework that aimed to abolish private property and establish a "dictatorship of the proletariat."

Historians continue to debate the nature of the October Revolution. Some, following the traditionalist view, see it as a popular uprising of the masses. Others, such as Richard Pipes, argue it was a disciplined *coup d'état* carried out by a small minority of Bolsheviks who manipulated the chaos of the era.

## Key Facts
*   **The Role of WWI:** The war acted as a catalyst, draining Russia's economy and causing millions of casualties, which completely eroded the legitimacy of the Tsar.
*   **Leninism:** Vladimir Lenin adapted Marxism to the Russian context, arguing that a vanguard party of professional revolutionaries was necessary to lead the working class toward revolution.
*   **War Communism to NEP:** After the brutal economic policies of "War Communism" during the Civil War, Lenin introduced the New Economic Policy (NEP) in 1921, allowing limited capitalism to stabilize the economy.
*   **The Red Terror:** To consolidate power, the Bolsheviks utilized the Cheka (secret police) to eliminate perceived "class enemies" and political dissidents.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Calendar Gap:** The revolutions are called "February" and "October" because Russia used the Julian calendar at the time; by the modern Gregorian calendar, they occurred in March and November.
2. **The Romanov Fate:** In July 1918, Tsar Nicholas II and his entire immediate family were executed in a basement in Yekaterinburg to prevent them from becoming a rallying point for the White Army.
3. **The Bread Riots:** The February Revolution was sparked largely by women textile workers in Petrograd protesting food shortages on International Women's Day.
