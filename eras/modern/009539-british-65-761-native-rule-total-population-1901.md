# Bombay Presidency

The Bombay Presidency was a province of British India administered by a governor. Its territory was composed of a combination of British districts and native states. Geographically, the presidency extended from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. and from 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. It was bordered by the Arabian Sea to the west, Madras and Mysore to the south, and Indore, Hyderabad, and the Central Provinces to the east. To the north, it was bounded by Rajputana, the Punjab, and Baluchistan. While the native state of Baroda maintained direct relations with the government of India and the Portuguese settlements of Goa, Damaun, and Diu lay within these limits, the settlement of Aden was included politically.

## Area and Population
The total area of the Bombay Presidency, including Sind but excluding Aden, was 188,745 sq. m. This land was divided between different administrative rules: 122,984 sq. m. were under British rule, while 65,761 sq. m. were under native rule. 

According to 1901 data, the total population of the presidency was 25,468,209. Of this total, 18,515,587 residents lived in British territory, and 6,908,648 resided within the native states.

## Administrative Divisions
The province was organized into twenty-six districts divided among four commissionerships:
*   **Sind**
*   **Northern (Gujarat)**
*   **Central (Deccan)**
*   **Southern (Carnatic)**

The twenty-six districts included Bombay City, Ahmedabad, Broach, Kaira, Panch Mahals, Surat, Thana, Ahmednagar, Nasik, Poona, Satara, Sholapur, Belgaum, Bijapur, Dharwar, Kanara, Kolaba, Ratnagiri, Karachi, Hyderabad, Shikarpur, Thar and Parkar, and the Upper Sind Frontier. Additionally, Khandesh was partitioned into two districts in 1906.

## Native States
The native states of the presidency consisted of 353 separate units. These were administered either by collectors of the districts where smaller states were located or by political agents. These states were organized into several chief groups:

*   **North Gujarat:** This group included Cambay, Palanpur agency, Mahi Kantha agency, Rewa Kantha agency, Cutch, and the Kathiawar agency.
*   **South Gujarat:** This group comprised Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.
*   **North Konkan, Nasik, and Khandesh:** This group included Surgana, Jawhar, and the Khandesh political agency.
*   **South Konkan and Dharwar:** This group comprised Savanur, Sawantwari, and Janjira.
*   **Deccan Satara Jagirs:** This group included Daphlapur, Jath, Phaltan, Aundh, Bhor, and Akalkot.
*   **Other states:** These included Khairpur in Sind and the southern Mahratta states, which included Kolhapur.

One notable native state within the Gujarat province of Bombay was Baroda. Baroda maintained direct relations with the governor-general and covered a total area of 8,099 sq. m. It was composed of four isolated divisions: Nausari, Baroda, and Kadi in Gujarat proper, and Okhamandal with Amreli in the Kathiawar peninsula. In 1901, the population of Baroda was 1,952,692. This figure represented a 19% decrease over the previous decade, a decline attributed to the plague, epidemics of fever and cholera, and the famines of 1900-1901 and 1896-1897.
