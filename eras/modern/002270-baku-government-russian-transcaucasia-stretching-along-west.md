# Baku

Baku is a government of Russian Transcaucasia located along the west coast of the Caspian Sea. It extends from 41° 50' to 38° 30' N. lat. and covers an area of 15,172 sq. m. The government is bounded to the south by Persia and to the west by the province of Daghestan and the government of Elisavetpol.

## Geography and Land Use
The territory of the Baku government is characterized by diverse landscapes. It includes the Kuba plain, situated on the north-east slope of the Caucasus, and the eastern extremity of the Caucasus range, which stretches from the Bazardyuz (14,727 ft.) and the Shad-dagh (13,960 ft.) to the Caspian Sea, where it ends at the Apsheron peninsula. To the south of the Caucasus are the steppes of the Aras and lower Kura, as well as a narrow coast-belt between the Caspian and the Anti-Caucasus, which partially surrounds the south-opening Kizil-agach Bay.

The region's agriculture is varied. Both slopes of the Caucasus are well irrigated and very fertile, featuring fields of cereals, rice, flourishing gardens, and fine forests. While the Kura steppes are also fertile, they require artificial irrigation, particularly for the cultivation of cotton. In addition to cattle-breeding and general agriculture, mulberry and vine are grown extensively. In contrast, the Apsheron peninsula is bare of vegetation and dry.

## The Petroleum Industry
The Apsheron peninsula is the site of the famous petroleum wells of Baku. These wells reach depths between 700 and 1,700 ft. and produce crude naphtha. This naphtha is distilled to create kerosene or petroleum, while the heavier residue, known as *mazut*, is used as fuel—such as for the locomotives of the Transcaspian railway—and as lubricating oil.

The industry has seen massive growth; output rose from 5,500 tons of crude naphtha in 1863 to 9,833,600 tons by 1904, although a serious fire in 1905 caused significant injury to business. The oil-fields are concentrated around the town of Baku:
* **Balakhany-Sabunchi-Romany**: The largest field (6 sq. m.), located 8-1/2 m. north of the town.
* **Bibi-Eybat**: Located 3-1/2 m. south.
* **The "black town" (Nobel's)**: Located 2 m. south-east.
* **The "white town" (Rothschild's)**: Located beyond the black town.

Lighter oils are transported via pipes to Batum on the Black Sea for export. Heavier oils are transported in tank railway-cars to the ports of Poti, Novorossiysk, and Batum.

## The Town of Baku
Baku serves as the chief town and capital of the government. It is situated on the south side of the Apsheron peninsula at 40° 21' N. and 49° 50' E. The town is divided into a new town, which climbs the slopes behind a long stone quay by the harbour, and an old town to the west. The old town is characterized by an oriental appearance with narrow, winding, steep streets.

Historical landmarks in the old town include:
* Ruins of a 16th-century palace of the native khans.
* Mosques built by Persian shahs in 1078, which now serve as an arsenal.
* The "maidens' tower," which has been converted into a lighthouse.
* Remains of ancient walls with Arabic architecture from the 9th and 10th centuries.

The town's economy is supported by its excellent harbour, making it a primary depot for Russian manufactured goods and merchandise from Transcaspia and Persia, including silk, dried fruit, timber, wine, rice, raw cotton, and fish. Industrial facilities include petroleum refineries, tobacco factories, sulphuric acid works, flour-mills, and oil-works for fuel. The Russian Caspian fleet also maintains workshops, stores, barracks, and dry docks beside the harbour.

The town's population grew from 13,381 in 1860 to 112,253 in 1897, reaching 179,133 by 1900. The climate is extreme, with an annual rainfall of 9.4 in. and a mean yearly temperature of 58° F. (ranging from 38° F. in January to 80° F. in July). Violent winds occasionally blow from the N.N.W. during winter.

## History and Administration
The town of Baku was mentioned in the 10th century by the Arab geographer Masudi. It was possessed by the Persians from 1509 until 1723, when it was captured by the Russians. Although restored to the Persians in 1735, it was incorporated into the Russian empire in 1806. In 1904-1905, political anarchy led to serious conflicts between Armenians and Tatars, resulting in the burning of two-thirds of the Bibi-Eybat and Balakhany oil-works.

Administratively, the government is divided into six districts. The chief towns are:
* Baku (the capital)
* Shemakha (pop. 20,008)
* Kuba (pop. 15,346)
* Salyany (pop. 10,168, in the district of Jevat)
* Lenkoran (pop. 8,768)
* Geok-chai (pop. 2,247 in 1897)

The total population of the government in 1897 was 828,511. The majority were Tatars, with other populations including the Iranian tribes of the Talysh (34,994) and Tates (89,519), Armenians (52,233), Russians, and Caucasian mountaineers known as Kurins.

## Regional Connectivity
Baku is connected by rail to the south Russian railway system at Beslan. From there, it connects to Vladikavkaz (400 m.) via Petrovsk and Derbent, and to Poti (536 m.) and Batum (560 m.) via Tiflis. Other notable locations in the region include Astara, a port on the river of the same name which forms the frontier between Russia and Persia. Astara serves as a point where Russian merchandise is landed and forwarded to Tabriz and Azerbaijan via Ardebil. Additionally, the disused temple of the Parsee fire-worshippers is located at Surakhani, 13 m. east of Baku.
