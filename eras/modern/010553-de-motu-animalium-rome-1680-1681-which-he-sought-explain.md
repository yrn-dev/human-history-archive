# De motu animalium

*De motu animalium* is a scientific work published in Rome between 1680 and 1681 by the Italian physicist and physiologist Giovanni Alfonso Borelli. The text is recognized as the foundational work of the iatrophysical school.

## Purpose and Theoretical Framework
In *De motu animalium*, Borelli sought to explain the movements of the animal body by applying mechanical principles. His approach focused on the mechanism of bodily motion, treating biological movement as a subject of physics and mechanics.

## Contributions to Zoology and Anatomy
The work contains specific anatomical and physiological investigations into various animal species:

### Ichthyology
Borelli utilized the text to explain the function of the air-bladder and the mechanical process of swimming. His records of these anatomical researches contributed to the progress of ichthyology during a period when naturalists were moving away from a strict dependence on preceding authorities.

### Avian Physiology
Borelli provided an explanation for how birds are able to grasp the twigs upon which they rest during sleep without requiring active muscular exertion. He posited that this is achieved because the weight of the bird's body bends the ankle-joints and knee-joints. The tendons of a compound muscle—specifically the *ambiens* muscle, which is long and spindle-shaped and extends from the knee to the pectineal process or ilio-pubic spine—pass over these joints, facilitating the grip.

## Author Biography
Giovanni Alfonso Borelli was born in Naples on January 28, 1608. He held professorships in mathematics at Pisa (appointed 1656) and Messina (appointed 1649). After returning to Messina in 1667, he was forced to retire to Rome in 1674. During his final years, he lived under the protection of Christina, the queen of Sweden, until his death on December 31, 1679.

Beyond *De motu animalium*, Borelli produced a wide range of scientific literature:
* **Astronomy:** He wrote *Theorica mediceorum planetarum ex causis physicis deducta* (1666), examining the attraction of Jupiter's satellites, and a 1665 letter under the pseudonym Pier Maria Mutoli, *Del movimento della cometa apparsa il mese di decembre 1664*, in which he first suggested the concept of a parabolic path.
* **Physics and Mathematics:** His works include *De vi percussionis* (1667), *De motionibus naturalibus a gravitate pendentibus* (1670), *Euclides Restitutus* (1658), and *Apollonii Pergaei Conicorum libri v., vi. et vii.* (1661).
* **Other Sciences:** He authored *Meteorologia Aetnea* (1669), *De Renum usu Judicium* (1664), and *Della Causa delle Febbri maligni* (1658).
