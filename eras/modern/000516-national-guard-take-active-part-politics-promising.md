# National Guard and Political Involvement in 19th Century Austria

## The Crisis of 1848
During 1848, the Austrian monarchy faced a critical situation characterized by a lack of central government for several months following the fall of Metternich. In Vienna, the emperor authorized the establishment of a National Guard on March 14. During this period, the city was governed by a committee of citizens and students who imposed their will on the ministry and claimed a voice in imperial affairs.

## Political Concessions and Insurrections
The government attempted to address public opinion by proposing the summons of a central committee of local diets on March 15. Subsequently, on April 25, a constitution was proclaimed for the monarchy, excluding Lombardo-Venetia and Hungary. This constitution caused anger in radical Vienna due to its provisions for the indirect election of deputies and a partly nominated senate, and it was met with vigorous protests from Poles and Czechs.

In response, National Guards and committees of students were formed, leading to the establishment of a Central Committee on May 13. A new insurrection broke out on May 15, forcing the government to yield. As a result of this uprising, the government recognized the Central Committee and admitted the right of the National Guard to take an active part in politics. Additionally, the government promised the convocation of a National Convention, which was to be based on a single chamber elected by universal suffrage.

## Imperial Reaction and the Committee of Public Safety
On May 17, the emperor departed Vienna for Innsbruck, citing his health. From there, he issued a proclamation on May 20 that confirmed the concessions made in March but ignored the concessions granted on May 15. This proclamation, combined with the government's attempt to close the university to disperse students, triggered another outbreak on May 26. The ministry again conceded to the demands of the insurgents, eventually handing over the responsibility for keeping order and the public treasury to a newly formed Committee of Public Safety.

## National Guard Developments in Hungary
Parallel to the events in Vienna, a National Guard was established in May within the Hungarian context. Soldiers of the line were encouraged to join this Guard with the promise of higher pay. By June 1, the garrison of Pest had taken an oath to the Constitution. This occurred during a period of tension where the Hungarian government sought to alter the relations between the army and the monarchy.
