# Sarcina

## Overview and Classification
*Sarcina* is a genus of schizomycetes, which are minute vegetable organisms. These organisms are characterized by a lack of chlorophyll and an absence of both an ordinary nucleus and ordinary sexual reproduction. They multiply through a process of repeated bipartitions. Within the biological classification of the order Haplobacterinae, *Sarcina* is placed in the family Coccaceae and the sub-family Homococcaceae. 

The vegetative cells of *Sarcina* are spheroidal and non-motile. The genus is distinguished by having regular and definite division planes.

## Cellular Structure and Growth
The growth and division of *Sarcina* occur in three successive planes at right angles. This specific pattern of division results in the formation of packet-like groups of cells. Because these successive divisions occur in two or three planes rather than exclusively across the long axis, *Sarcina* presents aggregates in the form of plates, or colonies that are solid, hollow, or irregularly branched.

## Biological Analogues and Relationships
In the study of schizomycetes, there is a generally received view that most typical bacteria are derived from Cyanophyceae (blue-green algae). While bacteria and Schizophyceae (which include Cyanophyceae) are often united in the class Schizophyta due to their manner of division and lack of a nucleus, certain specific forms show different relationships.

*Sarcina* is noted as one of these forms; it has algal analogues found in the Palmellaceae among the green algae.
