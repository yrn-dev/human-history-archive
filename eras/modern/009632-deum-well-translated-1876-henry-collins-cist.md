# John Bona

## Biography
John Bona (1609–1674) was an Italian author and cardinal born on October 10, 1609, in Mondovi, Piedmont. In 1624, he entered the Congregation of Feuillants. Within his order, he held several leadership positions, serving as the abbot of Mondovi, the prior of Asti, and eventually as general of the order. 

In 1669, Clement IX created Bona a cardinal. Following the death of Pope Clement IX, Bona was considered a potential candidate for the papacy during the subsequent conclave. He died on October 27, 1674.

## Literary Works and Themes
The writings of John Bona focus primarily on devotional and liturgical subjects. His works have seen numerous editions, with the most notable published in Paris (1677), Antwerp (1777), and Turin (1747).

### Liturgical and Mystical Writings
Bona authored *De Libris Liturgicis* and *Divina Psalmodia*, both of which contain prayers, verses, and significant rubrical information, although the value of these texts has been somewhat diminished by modern advances in liturgical studies. Additionally, he wrote *De Discretione Spirituum*, a work that examines higher phases of mysticism.

### Devotional Treatises and Translations
Bona's *De Sacrificio Missae* is regarded as the classical work in the field of devotional treatises; a new edition of this text was published by Ildephonsus Cummins in 1903. 

Other significant works include:
*   ***Via Compendii ad Deum***: This work was translated into English in 1876 by Henry Collins, O. Cist., under the title *An Easy Way to God*.
*   ***Manuductio ad Coelum***: This text was translated by Sir Roger L'Estrange as *The Guide to Heaven* (1680) and was later reprinted in 1898.
*   ***Principia Vitae Christianae***: A new edition of this work, edited by D. O'Connor, was released in 1906.
