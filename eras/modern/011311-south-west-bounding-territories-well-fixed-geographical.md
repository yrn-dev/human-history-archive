# Geographical Bounding Territories

The determination of territorial boundaries is often established through a combination of natural geographical markers, survey determinations, and political agreements. Across various regions of the world, these limits are defined by mountain ranges, rivers, oceans, and specific longitudinal or latitudinal coordinates.

## Central and East Asian Boundaries
In the regions surrounding Tibet and the Gobi, bounding territories to the south and west are well fixed in geographical position via Indian survey determinations of Himalayan peak values. To the north, explorations in Chinese Turkestan have been brought into survey connection with India and Kashmir. 

The Kuen-lun mountains, extending from the frontiers of Kashmir (north of Leh) almost due east to the Chinese province of Kansu, are no longer viewed as the southern limit of the Turkestan or Gobi depression. Instead, this longitudinal chain serves as the northern limit of the Chang Tang, the elevated highland steppes of Tibet. A minor system branches north-east from approximately 83 deg. E. longitude, culminating in the Altyn Tagh and extending eastwards as a continuous water-divide to the Nan Shan mountains, north of the Koko Nor basin. This creates a system of elevated valleys known as Tsaidam, situated 8,000 to 9,000 ft. above sea-level, which acts as an intermediate steppe between the lowest and highest regions east of Lop Nor.

## African Territorial Limits
In East Africa, various linguistic and political boundaries are defined by natural landmarks. The Kaguru-Sagala-Kami group occupies inland territories of German East Africa, bounded on the south by the Ruaha river, on the north by the non-Bantu languages of the Taturu, Mbugu, and Masai, on the east by Swahili coast dialects, and on the west by the domain of the Nyamwezi. The Nyamwezi group itself is bounded on the south by the Upper Ruaha river and on the west by the eastern borderlands of Tanganyika, extending north as far as the Victoria Nyanza.

Other protectorates in the region define their southern frontier as coterminous with the northern frontier of German East Africa, running north-west from the mouth of the Umba river (4° 40' S.) to Victoria Nyanza (1° S.), though the boundary deviates to ensure Mount Kilimanjaro remains entirely within German territory. This territory is bounded on the west by Uganda, on the north by Italian Somaliland and Abyssinia, and on the east by the Indian Ocean.

In South Africa, Griqualand West is bounded to the south by the Orange river, to the north and west by Bechuanaland, and to the east by the Orange Free State Province and the Transvaal.

## South Asian Provinces
The boundaries of British Indian provinces are defined by both coordinates and neighboring states. The Bombay Presidency extends from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. and 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. It is bounded on the west by the Arabian Sea, on the south by Mysore and Madras, on the east by Hyderabad, the Central Provinces, and Indore, and on the north by Rajputana, the Punjab, and Baluchistan.

Burma, also a province of British India, is situated east of the Bay of Bengal. Its range extends from the Pakchan river (9° 55' north latitude) to the Chingpaw or Naga (Kachin) hills (between the 27th and 28th degrees of north latitude). Its longitudinal limits run from the Bay of Bengal in the west to the Mekong river in the east. It is bounded to the north by the Mishmi hills, portions of Chinese territory, and the dependent state of Manipur. Its eastern boundaries include the Chinese Shan States, the French province of Indo-China, portions of the province of Yunnan, and the Siamese Lao or Shan States and Siam. To the south, it is bounded by the Bay of Bengal and the Siamese Malay States.

## European and Mediterranean Frontiers
In Asia Minor, the peninsula is bounded on the north by the Black Sea, on the west by the Aegean, and on the south by the Mediterranean. To the north-west, it is separated from Europe only by the Dardanelles and Bosporus straits. While no natural boundary exists to the east, a descriptive line is often drawn from the southern extremity of the Giaour Dagh, along the eastern Taurus to the Euphrates near Malatia, and north to the Black Sea via the Churuk Su, making the Euphrates the primary eastern limit.

In the Balkans, the Illyrians were bounded on the west by the Adriatic and on the east by the mountain-range of Skardus. Their southern limits were marked by the Amantini tribe, while the Bulini were considered the northernmost tribe. Some accounts extend Illyrian tribes nearly as far north as the Julian Alps.

In Northern Europe, the frontier between Denmark and Germany was subject to determination by population self-determination. An initial evacuation line ran from the mouth of the Schlei, south of Friedrichstadt and Kappel (Schleswig), along the Eider to the North Sea south of Tonning. A new frontier was to be drawn by an international commission based on plebiscites and geographical and economic conditions.
