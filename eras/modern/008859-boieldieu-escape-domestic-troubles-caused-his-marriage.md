# François Adrien Boieldieu

## Early Life and Education
François Adrien Boieldieu was a French composer of comic opera born in Rouen on December 15, 1775. His initial musical training was provided by M. Broche, the cathedral organist, who is noted to have treated Boieldieu very harshly. Despite this, Boieldieu began composing chamber music and songs at a very early age. His first two operas, *La Fille coupable*—which featured a libretto written by his father—and *Rosalie et Myrza*, were produced in Rouen in 1795.

## Career in Paris
Seeking greater success beyond his local achievements, Boieldieu moved to Paris in 1795. He submitted his scores to composers such as Mehul and Cherubini, but these were met with little approbation, as grand opera was the prevailing style of the time. To support himself, Boieldieu relied on his skills as a pianoforte player.

His reputation began to grow when P.J. Garat, a fashionable singer, admired Boieldieu's piano technique and hired him as an accompanist. Through the drawing-rooms of the Directoire, where Garat performed the ballads and songs supplied by the young composer, Boieldieu's fame expanded. This led to the production of *Les Deux lettres* in 1796 and *La Famille suisse* in 1797, the latter of which was well received on the Paris stage. While several other operas followed, *Le Calife de Bagdad* (1800) is the only one from this period to have escaped oblivion. Following the immense success of this work, Boieldieu sought more thorough musical training and took lessons from Cherubini, whose influence is evident in the higher artistic finish of Boieldieu's later works.

## Exile to Russia
In 1802, Boieldieu fled to Russia to escape domestic troubles resulting from his marriage to Clotilde Aug. Mafleuroy, a celebrated ballet-dancer of the Paris opera. Upon his arrival, he was received with open arms by Emperor Alexander. During a prolonged stay in St Petersburg, Boieldieu composed various operas. He also ventured into the tragic style of dramatic writing by setting the choruses of Racine's *Athalie* to music.

## Return to France
Boieldieu returned to France in 1811. The following year, 1812, saw the production of *Jean de Paris*, which is considered one of his finest works. Boieldieu passed away in 1834.

## Biographical Records
Several biographies of Boieldieu have been written, including works by J.A. Refeuvaille (1836), Hequet (1864), Pougin (1875), and Emile Duval (1883). Additionally, Adolphe Charles Adam provided accounts of the composer in *Derniers souvenirs d'un musicien*.
