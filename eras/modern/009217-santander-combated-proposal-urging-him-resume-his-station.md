# Political Transitions of Simón Bolívar

## Resignation and the Role of Santander

In February 1827, Simón Bolívar formally resigned from the presidency of the republic. In doing so, he expressed a desire to retire into private life on his patrimonial estate, stating his determination to refute accusations of ambition that had been frequently directed toward him.

This proposal was combated by Santander, who urged Bolívar to resume his station as constitutional president. Santander declared his conviction that the personal influence and authority of the liberator were the only means by which the agitations and troubles of the country could be appeased. Although the resolution of congress confirming this view was not unanimous, Bolívar decided to resume his functions and traveled to Bogota to take the necessary oaths.

## Administrative Measures and Regional Stability

Prior to his arrival in Bogota, Bolívar issued three simultaneous decrees intended to stabilize the region:
* The establishment of constitutional order throughout Colombia.
* The convocation of a national convention at Ocana.
* The granting of a general amnesty.

These actions followed a period of significant unrest. In September 1826, Bolívar had departed Lima, leaving the government to a council headed by Santa Cruz. Upon arriving in Bogota on November 14, Bolívar assumed extraordinary powers authorized by the constitution for cases of rebellion. He subsequently moved to Venezuela to stop the effusion of blood, reaching Puerto Cabello on December 31, where he issued a decree of general amnesty. He later held a friendly meeting with Paez and established headquarters in Caracas to monitor the northern departments, which had served as the primary theater of disturbances.

## Military Campaigns and the Rise to Power

Bolívar's path to leadership involved several critical military expeditions. In May 1816, after receiving a small force from President Petion in Haiti, he landed on the mainland, though this initial attempt failed. He later obtained reinforcements at Aux Cayes and landed in Barcelona and Margarita in December. 

On February 16, 1817, Bolívar's forces engaged in a three-day conflict that resulted in the defeat of royalists led by Morillo. Morillo's retreat was further compounded by an overthrow at the hands of J.A. Paez and his *llaneros*. Following these victories, Bolívar established headquarters at Angostura on the Orinoco.

At the opening of the congress in Angostura on February 15, 1819, Bolívar submitted his views on government and surrendered his authority to the congress. However, he was required to retain power until the country's independence was fully established. He then reorganized his troops and crossed the Cordilleras to join General Santander, who commanded the republican force in New Granada. This union of forces led to the capture of Tunja in July 1819 and a victory at Boyaca on August 7, which granted Bolívar possession of all New Granada and Bogota.

## Later Challenges

Bolívar's return to the presidency was accelerated by events in the southern departments and Peru, which threatened the foundation of his power. This occurred shortly after his previous departure from Lima and the introduction of the Bolivian code.
