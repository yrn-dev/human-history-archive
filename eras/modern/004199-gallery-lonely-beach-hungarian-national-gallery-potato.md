# National Galleries and Art Collections

The term "national gallery" refers to various state-sponsored or public art institutions across different countries, housing diverse collections of paintings, sculptures, and historic works. These institutions serve as repositories for both classical and modern art, often incorporating collections originally formed by private individuals or acquired through government purchase.

## The National Gallery, London
The National Gallery in London houses a wide array of international works. Its collection includes examples of the Florentine painter Bronzino (Angelo Allori), who was a pupil of J. da Pontormo and known for portraits of Boccaccio, Petrarch, and Dante. The gallery also holds works by Correggio, including "Cupid, Mercury and Venus" and the "Ecce Homo," the latter of which was likely painted around 1520, though its authenticity is not unquestioned. Additionally, the gallery contains three works by De Hooch. To accommodate its growth, the architect Edward Middleton Barry designed new galleries for the institution.

## The Hungarian National Gallery
The national gallery in Hungary was originally formed by Prince Esterházy and was purchased by the government in 1865 for £130,000. Previously housed in the academy, it was installed in 1905 within the museum of fine arts (constructed between 1900 and 1905). The museum of fine arts also contains a collection of modern pictures from the national museum. One of the notable works held in the Hungarian National Gallery is "Lonely Beach," painted by the German artist Hans von Bartels.

## The Berlin National Gallery
The Berlin National Gallery is known for acquiring works that have sparked public or critical debate. An example is the "Fantasies upon the Finding of a Glove" series by Max Klinger. Klinger, a German painter, etcher, and sculptor, created this pen-and-ink series in 1878; while the work's originality led some to vote the artist insane, the Berlin National Gallery purchased the "Glove" series.

## Other Notable Galleries and Collections
Various other galleries house significant historical and artistic works:
*   **Dresden Gallery:** This collection includes Correggio's "Night" ("La Notte"), a nocturnal scene of the Nativity painted in 1522 for Alberto Pratoneris, as well as "St George" (painted around 1532), "St Sebastian," and Max Klinger's "Pietà."
*   **Parma Gallery:** This gallery holds Correggio's "Day" ("Il Giorno"), which depicts St Jerome with the Madonna and Child, the Magdalene, and two Angels. It also contains the frescoes "della Scala" and "della Scodella."
*   **Berlin Gallery:** This institution houses "Sturmflut" by Hans von Bartels and the "Leda" by Correggio, which depicts girls bathing.
*   **Modern Gallery in Vienna:** This gallery holds Max Klinger's "Christ in Olympus."
*   **Other Locations:** De Hooch's works are distributed among several institutions, including the Amsterdam Museum (six works), the Louvre, and galleries in St Petersburg, Munich, and Darmstadt, as well as Buckingham Palace.

## Architectural and Functional Galleries
Beyond art museums, the term "gallery" is used in architectural contexts to describe elevated platforms or walkways:
*   **Ecclesiastical Galleries:** In Eastern basilican churches, such as the 5th-century church of St John Studios in Constantinople, galleries were a characteristic feature. These were intended for women to provide more privacy than was typically studied in Western churches.
*   **Political Galleries:** In the early French Revolution, the Assembly featured a large gallery for strangers. This space often swarmed with people eager for "scenes," leading to interruptions and shouting that forced speakers to adopt sensational or sentimental lines to appeal to the gallery.
