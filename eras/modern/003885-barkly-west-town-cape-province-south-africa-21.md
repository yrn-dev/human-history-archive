# BARKLY WEST

Barkly West is a town located in the Cape province of South Africa. It serves as the capital of both an electoral division and a district of the same name within Griqualand West. The town is situated on the right bank of the Vaal River, where the waterway is spanned by a bridge, and is located 21 miles northwest of Kimberley.

## History and Development
Originally known as Klipdrift, Barkly West was the first town established by diggers following the discovery of diamonds in 1867 along the valley of the Vaal. In its early years, the town was characterized by a large floating population. However, after the discovery of "dry diggings" at Kimberley, the majority of the diggers relocated to that area. The town was named after Sir Henry Barkly, who served as the governor of the Cape Colony from 1870 to 1877.

## Economy and Industry
Despite the migration of diggers to Kimberley, Barkly West remains the center of the alluvial diamonds industry. The diamonds found in this district are specifically noted for their lustre and purity. These diamonds are generally associated with other crystals, including chalcedonies, quartz, agates, and garnets.

Barkly West is one of several locations on the banks of the Vaal—alongside Warrenton and Windsorton—where river diggings are present. Diamonds in this region are found along the river from the Harts confluence to Fourteen Streams.

## Demographics and Geography
According to 1904 data, the population of Barkly West was 1,037. The town is part of the larger Griqualand West region, a great tableland bounded by the Transvaal and Orange Free State Province to the east, Bechuanaland to the north and west, and the Orange river to the south. The broader region of Griqualand West has a general elevation between 3,000 and 4,000 feet above the sea.
