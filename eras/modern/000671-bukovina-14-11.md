# Bukovina

Bukovina is a duchy and crownland of Austria with a total area of 4,035 square miles. It is bordered to the north by Galicia, to the east by Russia and Rumania, to the south by Rumania, and to the west by Hungary and Transylvania.

## History
Originally, Bukovina was a part of the principality of Moldavia, and the ancient capital of that principality, Suczawa, was located within this province. The territory was occupied by the Russians in 1769 and subsequently by the Austrians in 1774. In 1777, the Porte, which held suzerainty over Moldavia, ceded the province to Austria. 

Administratively, Bukovina was incorporated into a single province with Galicia in 1786. However, it was separated from Galicia in 1849 and established as a separate crownland.

## Geography and Climate
The name of the country is derived from the abundance of beech trees. The landscape is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathian Mountains, particularly in the southern regions, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. The Stiol Pass is located here, and other principal passes include the Borgo Pass and the Radna Pass.

The region's hydrology is dominated by the Danube watershed, with the exception of the Dniester river, which skirts the northern border. Major rivers include the Pruth and the Sereth, the latter of which is joined by the Bistritza, Moldava, and Suczawa affluents. The climate is described as healthy but severe, particularly during the winter, though it is generally milder than the climate of Galicia. The mean annual temperature in Czernowitz is 46.9° F.

## Land Use and Economy
Woodland occupies 43.17% of the total area. The remaining land is divided into arable land (27.59%), meadows (12.68%), pastures (10.09%), and gardens (0.78%). The soil is fertile, supporting the production of rye, wheat, barley, oats, maize, hemp, flax, and potatoes. Cattle-rearing is another significant source of revenue.

The primary mineral resource is salt, extracted from a government-owned mine at Kaczyka. The chief industrial activities are milling, distilling, and brewing. Commerce is primarily handled by Armenians and Jews and is focused on raw products, including wood, cattle, wool, and agricultural produce.

## Population and Society
As of 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is ethnically diverse:
* Ruthenians: over 40%
* Rumanians: 35%
* Jews: 13%
* Others: Germans, Russians, Poles, Armenians, and Hungarians.

The official language for instruction at the university, the law-courts, and the administration is German. However, because few residents in remote districts like Bukovina understood German, administrative regulations allowed for the "external service" to use the local language of the district when communicating with private individuals or taking evidence in court.

Religious affiliation is predominantly Greek Orthodox, comprising nearly 70% of the population under the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Other religious groups include Roman Catholics (11%), the Greek United Church (3.25%), and Protestants (2.5%).

## Infrastructure
Bukovina is connected via railway to other regions; specifically, a main railway line runs from Czernovitz in Bukovina to Galatz.
