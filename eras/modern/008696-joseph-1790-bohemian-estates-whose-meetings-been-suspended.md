# Joseph II and the Bohemian Estates

## Administrative Centralization in Bohemia
During the reign of Maria Theresa and, more significantly, her son Joseph II, the internal administration of the Habsburg realm underwent numerous changes designed to limit the autonomy of Bohemia. This process of centralization included a 1749 decree that abolished the separate law-courts existing within Bohemia. Subsequently, an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was appointed to oversee the administration of Bohemia as well as the German domains of the house of Habsburg.

## The Suspension of the Estates
The governance of the Habsburg territories was marked by an emphatically absolutist instinct shared by Maria Theresa and Joseph II. In her effort to promote unity across an administration composed of various states and races with differing constitutions, Maria Theresa suspended the meetings of the estates in most parts of her dominions. This action was facilitated by the fact that the mass of her subjects perceived her rule as lighter than that of the privileged classes who comprised these bodies.

While Maria Theresa was cautious in her methods—resisting the suppression of torture in legal procedures to avoid weakening the authority of the law—Joseph II was characterized by a "boundless zeal for reform." This approach eventually brought his empire to the verge of a general rebellion.

## The Final Years of Joseph II
By the late 18th century, Joseph II's foreign policy faced significant failures. In 1784, he attempted to acquire Bavaria by negotiating an exchange of the Netherlands with the elector Charles Theodore. This plan was thwarted by the duke of Zweibruecken and Frederick the Great, who formed a confederation of German princes (the *Fuerstenbund*) on July 23, 1785, to oppose Austrian preponderance.

Joseph II subsequently entered a war with Turkey in alliance with Russia in 1788. The first campaign, which he led personally, was a dismal failure due to an army disorganized by disease. Although Marshal Loudon retrieved some of the initial disasters, these gains were offset by an alliance formed between Prussia and Turkey on January 31, 1790. Joseph II died on February 20, 1790, described as being broken-hearted.
