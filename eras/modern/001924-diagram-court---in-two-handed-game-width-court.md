# Badminton

Badminton is a fast-paced game played with rackets and shuttlecocks. The name of the sport is derived from the seat of the duke of Beaufort in Gloucestershire. While the game appears to have first been played in England around 1873, it was played in India prior to that time and remains very popular there. 

The laws of the game were framed from a code of rules established in 1887 for the Bath Badminton Club, which were based on the original Poona rules of 1876. In England, the Badminton Association was founded in 1895, and the sport is almost always played within a covered court.

## Gameplay and Variations
Badminton consists entirely of volleying and is noted for its speed; a single match is considered to require more staying power than a single match of lawn tennis. The game allows for significant skill and judgment, specifically through techniques such as "smashing" (hitting the shuttle with a hard downward stroke) and "dropping" (hitting the shuttle gently just over the net).

The game can be played under artificial light or daylight in two formats:
* **Two-handed (single game):** One player on each side.
* **Four-handed (double game):** Two players on each side.

## Court Specifications and Equipment
A Badminton hall must have a height of at least 18 ft. The court features a net that is 30 in. deep and edged at the top with white tape 3 in. wide. The length of the net ranges from 17 to 24 ft., depending on the position of the posts. The top of the net stands 5 ft. from the ground at the center and 5 ft. 1 in. at the posts.

The placement of net posts varies by game type:
* **Four-handed game:** Posts are placed on the side boundary lines or up to 2 ft. outside them, resulting in a distance between posts of 20 to 24 ft.
* **Two-handed game:** The court width is reduced to 17 ft. Long service lines are dispensed with, and the back boundary lines serve as the long service lines. The lines dividing the half courts are extended to meet the back boundary lines. The distance between posts in this format is 17 to 21 ft.

The equipment consists of a racket and a shuttlecock. The racket, which has no specified weight, shape, or size, is typically around 6 oz. and is strung with strong fine gut. The shuttlecock (or shuttle) weighs between 73 and 85 grains and possesses 16 feathers, each measuring 2-1/2 to 2-3/4 in. in length.

## Rules of the Four-Handed Game
In the four-handed game, the first server stands in his right-hand half court and serves to the opponent in the opposite right-hand half court. Once the shuttle is hit, all players may move anywhere on their side of the net.

The shuttle must be returned by the "in" (serving) side and then by the "out" (non-serving) side until a "fault" occurs or the shuttle is no longer "in play." 
* **Scoring:** Only the "in" side can score an ace. If the "out" side makes a fault, the "in" side scores an ace and the players on the "in" side switch half courts. The server then serves from the left half court to the opposite left half court.
* **Faults:** If the "in" side makes a fault, the server loses his "hand" (serve), and the player who was served to becomes the server, though no score is recorded.
* **Service Rotation:** In the first innings of a game, the starting side has only one "hand." In all subsequent innings, each player on each side has a "hand," and partners serve consecutively. Service alternates between half courts diagonally opposite whenever an ace is scored.

## Service and Play Regulations
A stroke is considered good if the shuttle falls on a line. In general play, a stroke is good even if the shuttle strikes the net but continues over. However, if this occurs during service and the service is otherwise good, it is declared a "let," and the server must serve again. A "let" is also called if the server misses the shuttle entirely during a service attempt.

## Competitive History
The All England championships were instituted in 1899 for mixed doubles, ladies' doubles, and gentlemen's doubles. In 1900, championships for ladies' singles and gentlemen's singles were added. The first championship match between Ireland and England took place in 1904.
