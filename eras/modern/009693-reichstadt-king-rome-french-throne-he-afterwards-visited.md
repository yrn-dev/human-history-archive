# Joseph Bonaparte and the Claims of the Duke of Reichstadt

## Role in the Napoleonic Campaigns
Joseph Bonaparte served as the lieutenant-general of France during the campaign of 1814 in eastern France. During this period, Napoleon expressed dissatisfaction with Joseph's conduct. On March 30, Joseph granted Marmont the authority to establish a truce with the forces attacking Paris if those forces possessed overpowering strength. Following the surrender of the capital, Joseph retired.

During the Hundred Days of 1815, Joseph's role was considered insignificant. Despite this, four days after the Battle of Waterloo, Napoleon urged Joseph to inspire the Chamber of Deputies to pursue national resistance. In practice, Joseph's primary efforts were directed toward assisting the emperor's plans to escape to America.

## Exile in the United States
Following the surrender of Napoleon to the captain of H.M.S. "Bellerophon" at Rochefort, Joseph traveled to the United States. He settled in Bordentown, New Jersey, where he took the title of comte de Survilliers. While residing in America, he attempted to promote plans to rescue his brother from St Helena.

## Political Advocacy and Later Years
In 1830, Joseph pleaded for the recognition of the claims of the duke of Reichstadt, also known as the king of Rome, to the French throne, though these efforts were unsuccessful. Following this period, Joseph visited England and resided for a time in Florence and Genoa. He died in Florence on July 28, 1844, a city described as the cradle of his race.

## Character and Relationship with Napoleon
In physical appearance, Joseph somewhat resembled Napoleon, but he was noted for lacking his brother's energy and strength. He was described as being too luxurious, supine, and mild for the tasks Napoleon assigned to him, though he was considered well-suited for a judgeship or an embassy. Despite these differences and the pressures of his roles, Joseph's memoirs and correspondence indicate that he maintained warm feelings of affection for Napoleon.
