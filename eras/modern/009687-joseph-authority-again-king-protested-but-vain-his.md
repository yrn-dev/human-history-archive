# Joseph's Authority

The historical archives contain records of several individuals named Joseph who held positions of authority, ranging from ancient administrative power in Egypt to the titular and contested sovereignties of the Napoleonic era.

## Joseph in Ancient Egypt
According to historical accounts, Joseph, the youngest child of Jacob, was brought to Egypt as a slave by Ishmaelitish merchants and sold to Potiphar. Through a series of events, he rose to the chief employment of the kingdom, attaining supreme authority. 

Joseph was endowed with a knowledge of futurity and the ability to interpret dreams. Utilizing uncommon prudence, he preserved Egypt from a threatened famine, an achievement for which he was highly esteemed by the king. This period of service ensured that when Jacob and his entire family later entered Egypt, they were treated with kindness by the Egyptians. The archive notes that this era of influence ended with the rise of a new king who had no knowledge of Joseph.

## Joseph Bonaparte in Naples
In 1806, Napoleon proclaimed Joseph king of Naples after Joseph led a French force to expel the Bourbon dynasty from southern Italy. While Joseph maintained his claims to the throne of France, his administration in Naples was marked by significant challenges, including a degraded and fickle populace, Bourbon plots, and an almost bankrupt treasury. His authority was further shaken by General Stuart's victory at Maida on July 3.

Despite these difficulties, Joseph implemented various reforms during his brief reign. He reorganized the financial, judicial, and educational systems, reformed monastic orders, abolished remnants of feudalism, and initiated public works. His stated goal was to make the people happy, provided the "scourge of war" permitted it.

## Joseph Bonaparte in Spain
In May 1808, Napoleon called Joseph to take the crown of Spain. Although Joseph issued a proclamation on June 23, 1808, expressing benevolent intentions, reconciliation with the Spaniards proved impossible due to Napoleon's treatment of Ferdinand VII, the *de facto* king.

Joseph's sovereignty in Spain was largely titular. Following a Spanish success at Baylen, he was forced to flee Madrid in August 1808, though Napoleon reinstated him by the end of the year. Joseph remained in a subordinate position, leading him to offer his abdication on four separate occasions; however, Napoleon ignored these offers and commanded him to govern with more energy.

His authority continued to erode between February and May 1810, when Napoleon placed the north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals as military districts. These districts were virtually independent of Joseph's authority. Despite the king's protests, the arrangement remained. His adviser, Miot de Melito, observed that Joseph had attempted to be a constitutional king but had instead become a "mere instrument of a military power." By this stage, Joseph barely retained a semblance of authority as a general over a small portion of the French army.

In April 1811, facing an exhausted treasury, Joseph traveled to Paris to seek better terms or offer his abdication. He returned with a monthly subsidy of 500,000 francs and control over the army of the centre, the smallest of the five French armies. His tenure ended when Wellington's victory at Salamanca on July 22, 1812, compelled him to leave the capital.

## Other References to Joseph
The archives also mention Joseph I, who in 1705 restored imperial freedom to the town of Donauworth, though this freedom was subsequently lost in 1714 when the town was reincorporated with Bavaria.
