# Caries

In medical terminology, the term *caries* (meaning rottenness or decay) is applied to two distinct pathological conditions: the tuberculous disease of bone and the decay of the teeth.

## Tuberculous Caries of the Bone
Caries is the name given to tuberculous disease of the bone specifically when tubercles run together and break down the cancellous tissue. This condition is generally referred to as tuberculous ostitis, although the term has also been applied to syphilitic ulceration of bone.

Chronic inflammation of tuberculous origin typically affects the spongy ends of long bones, the bones of the hands and feet, and the vertebrae. The process begins when bacilli in the spongy tissue cause colorless corpuscles from the blood to collect around them, forming small, greyish-white heaps known as tubercles. When these tubercles appear in large numbers at the expense of living tissue, a "rarefying ostitis" is produced.

As the disease progresses, these tubercles break down to form tuberculous abscesses. These abscesses typically find a way to escape to the surface slowly and almost without pain. Medical guidance suggests that these should not be allowed to open spontaneously, as the wounds may become infected with pus-producing germs, which accelerates the destruction of the tissue.

Treatment for these tuberculous foci includes:
* Placing the affected part or limb on a splint for absolute rest.
* Providing the patient with plenty of fresh air.
* Prescribing iron and cod-liver oil.
* Considering the injection of tuberculin.
* Surgical intervention if the abscess advances toward the surface, involving cutting down upon the part, scraping out the foci, and disinfecting with a strong antiseptic lotion.

## Caries of the Teeth
Caries of the teeth is described as a disease that affects the human race more extensively than any other. Research conducted by Professor W. D. Miller of Berlin in 1884 demonstrated that this form of caries is of bacterial origin and that acids play a critical role in the process.

The disease is caused by a group of bacteria that develop in the mouth. These bacteria grow on the debris of carbohydrate or starchy food, producing fermentation that results in lactic acid. This lactic acid then dissolves the calcium phosphate, which is the mineral constituent of the tooth structure, thereby leaving the organic matrix of the tooth exposed.

Historically, the treatment of dental caries evolved alongside the professionalization of dentistry. In the 19th century, "stopping" caries in an imperfect way was often performed by barbers or dental artisans who possessed manipulative skill but lacked medical education. This existed in contrast to practitioners who viewed dentistry as a specialty of medicine.

## Related Pathological Conditions
While caries refers specifically to the decay of bone or teeth, the archive notes other forms of tuberculous infection and decay in different systems:

* **Tuberculous Lymphadenitis (Scrofula):** An infection of the lymph glands (most commonly in the neck) by Koch's tubercle bacillus. It involves the enlargement of glands, progressing to caseation and potentially suppuration.
* **Tabes Mesenterica:** Tuberculosis of the mesenteric glands, usually occurring in children, where glands of the retroperitonaeum and mesentery become enlarged and may suppurate or caseate.
* **Tuberculous Peritonitis:** A condition that frequently supervenes on tabes mesenterica and can be a cause of ascites in young people.
* **Tuberculous Disease of the Kidney:** This may be primary or secondary, reaching the kidney via blood vessels (descending) or through the lymphatics and ureter (ascending).
