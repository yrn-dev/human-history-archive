# Argentine Republic

The Argentine Republic is a nation in South America characterized by its diverse geography, agricultural exports, and strategic regional infrastructure. The republic is composed of various provinces, including Buenos Aires, the largest and most populous province, and Corrientes, located in the north.

## Geography and Provinces

The province of Buenos Aires is a maritime region bounded by the Atlantic Ocean and the La Plata estuary to the east and south, the province of Santa Fé and Entre Rios to the north, and the provinces of Córdoba and Santa Fé, as well as the territories of Las Pampas and Rio Negro, to the west. It covers an area of 117,812 square miles and had an official population estimate of 1,251,000 in 1903. While it possesses over 900 miles of coastline, natural ports are few; consequently, the government has developed artificial basins at Ensenada (La Plata) and a costly artificial port at Buenos Aires, alongside a naval port at Bahia Blanca.

In the north, the province of Corrientes is part of a region known as the Argentine Mesopotamia. It is bounded by Paraguay to the north, Brazil to the east, Misiones territory to the northeast, Entre Rios to the south, and Santa Fe and the Chaco to the west. The province covers 32,580 square miles, with nearly one-third of the land consisting of lagoons and swamps, such as the Maloya and the Ibera lagoon. The Ibera lagoon covers a large portion of the north-eastern and central departments. While the northern swamps are tropical, the higher plains are sub-tropical, making Corrientes the hottest province in Argentina. The southern districts are high and rolling, which is well-suited for agriculture and grazing.

## Economy and Trade

During the latter quarter of the nineteenth century, the Argentine Republic, along with other South American republics, experienced increased access to European markets. This was driven by a growing European population requiring staple foods and a rise in scientific industrialism that created a demand for raw materials, including fats, greases, and rubber. Argentina specifically felt this impact through its coffee growing and cattle trade.

The province of Corrientes is of significant commercial importance due to its location near the mouth of the Bermejo and the confluence of the Paraguay and Upper Parana rivers. The city of Corrientes serves as a river port where freight for the Chaco and Upper Parana rivers is transhipped. Principal exports from this region include:
* Timber and firewood (including quebracho)
* Cereals, sugar, and tobacco
* Mate
* Hides, cattle, horses, and jerked beef
* Fruit

## Infrastructure and International Relations

Argentina has engaged in regional infrastructure projects to improve communication and trade. The republic undertook the extension of its northern railway from Jujuy to the Bolivian frontier town of Tupiza. Within the republic, the Argentine North-Eastern railway connects the province of Corrientes to Monte Caseros, where it links with the East Argentine line running north to San Tome and south to Concordia.

In the context of international diplomacy, the Argentine Republic was identified as a neutral power during a global conflict and was subsequently invited to join the original league of nations proposed in a projected Covenant.
