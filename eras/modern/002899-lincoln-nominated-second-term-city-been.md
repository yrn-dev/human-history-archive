# Abraham Lincoln and the Election of 1864

## Political Ascent and the 1860 Nomination
Abraham Lincoln's path to the presidency was influenced by the political activities of figures such as Greeley, a founder of the Republican party. During the National Republican Convention of 1860, Greeley acted as a delegate for Oregon. Greeley's active hostility toward William Seward, a rival candidate, played a significant role in preventing Seward's success and instead facilitating the nomination of Abraham Lincoln.

## The Civil War and Political Opposition
During the American Civil War, Lincoln's administration faced internal and external challenges. In 1864, President Lincoln sent Greeley to confer with representatives of the Southern Confederacy in Canada to discuss negotiations for peace, though these representatives were found to lack sufficient authority.

Opposition to Lincoln's general administration policy led political elements to select George B. McClellan as their presidential candidate in 1864. McClellan, who had previously commanded the Army of the Potomac and had been superseded by Burnside on November 7 after the battle of Antietam, was chosen on a platform that proposed negotiating peace with the South and denounced the war as a failure. Although McClellan accepted the candidacy, he repudiated the platform.

## The Election of 1864
The presidential election took place on November 8, 1864. Abraham Lincoln was triumphantly re-elected to a second term as president. Following the election, McClellan, who had resigned his army commission, traveled to Europe, remaining there until 1868.
