# Aschersleben

Aschersleben is a town located in Germany within the Prussian province of Saxony. It is situated at the junction of railway lines to Nienhagen and Cothen, and is 36 miles northwest of Halle by rail. As of 1900, the population was 27,245, increasing to 27,876 by 1905.

## Civic and Religious Buildings
The town's architecture includes a fine town-hall that dates back to the 16th century. Its religious institutions consist of one Roman Catholic church, four Protestant churches, and a synagogue. Additionally, the town contains several schools.

## Economy and Industry
The discovery of coal in the surrounding area stimulated and altered the industries of Aschersleben. While the town has long been recognized for the manufacture of woollen wares, it now features extensive production of potash, paraffin, vinegar, and especially beetroot-sugar. This industrial shift is reflected in the surrounding district, which was previously largely devoted to market-gardening but has been turned almost entirely into beetroot fields. Other industrial activities include chemical manufactures, iron and zinc manufactures, and the cultivation of agricultural seeds. The neighborhood also features a spa known as Wilhelmsbad and brine springs.

## History
Aschersleben was likely founded in the 11th century by Count Esico of Ballenstedt, who was the ancestor of the house of Anhalt. The town's history is linked to Esico's grandson, Otto, who took the title of count of Aschersleben and Ascania, the latter being derived from his castle located near the town. 

Following the death of Otto III in 1315, Aschersleben passed into the possession of the bishop of Halberstadt. Later, at the peace of 1648, the town was united to Brandenburg along with the bishopric.
