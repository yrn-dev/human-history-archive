# Thrums and the Works of J. M. Barrie

## Overview of Thrums
Thrums is the idealized name given to the village of Kirriemuir by the British novelist and dramatist James Matthew Barrie. Through his writings, Barrie made the town famous under this pseudonym. Kirriemuir is a police burgh located in Forfarshire, Scotland, situated on a height above the glen of the Gairie. As of 1901, the population of the town was 4,096. The community is characterized by its staple industry of linen-weaving, and it is noted that the hand-loom persisted there longer than in any other Scottish location, remaining not yet wholly extinct at the time of the archive's records.

## J. M. Barrie's Connection to Kirriemuir
James Matthew Barrie was born in Kirriemuir on May 9, 1860. His early observations of his native village, combined with the tales told to him by his mother, served as the primary inspiration for his depictions of Thrums. Barrie noted that his mother "told me everything," and as a result, his memories of the "little red town" were influenced and colored by her own memories. Despite the fame brought to the town through his work, the residents of Kirriemuir themselves were not wholly satisfied with the portrayal.

## Literary Development and "Auld Licht Idylls"
Barrie's path to literature began through journalism. After being educated at Edinburgh University and the Dumfries academy, he served as a leader-writer for the *Nottingham Journal* starting in February 1883. He later moved to London in 1885, contributing to various publications including *Home Chimes* and the *St James's Gazette*.

On November 17, 1884, the *St James's Gazette* published his article "An Auld Licht Community." This was followed by a series of "Auld Licht Idylls," which were sketches of a Scots village. In 1888, these sketches were collected and published as the book *Auld Licht Idylls*. These works portrayed the humours and life of his native village, idealized as Thrums. This followed his first published book, *Better Dead* (1887), which was a specimen of humorous journalism derived from his work with the *St James's Gazette*.

## Local Context of Kirriemuir
The village of Kirriemuir, the basis for Thrums, features several notable landmarks and historical associations. It is the terminus of a branch line of the Caledonian railway and contains a public hall, libraries, and a park. The town's religious history includes the original Secession church—the kirk of the Auld Lichts—which was established in 1806 and underwent rebuilding in 1893. 

Beyond Barrie, the town is the birthplace of the Rev. Dr. Alexander Whyte (born 1837). The surrounding area includes Cortachy castle, a Scottish Baronial style mansion and seat of the earl of Airlie, located approximately 4 miles north, and Kinnordy, the birthplace of the geologist Sir Charles Lyell, located 1.5 miles northwest.
