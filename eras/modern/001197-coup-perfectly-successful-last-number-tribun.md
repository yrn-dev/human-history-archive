# The Coup and the Tribun

## The Coup and the Trial of Babeuf
A coup described as "perfectly successful" resulted in the cessation of the publication of the *Tribun*, with its final issue appearing on April 24th. Following this, Lebois utilized the *Ami du peuple* in an attempt to incite soldiers to revolt, leading to temporary rumors of a military rising. 

The government subsequently arranged for the trial of the socialist Babeuf and his accomplices. Although more important individuals were implicated, the government designated Babeuf as the leader of the conspiracy, a role his own vanity helped facilitate. The trial took place at Vendome, beginning on February 20, 1797, and lasting two months. During the removal of prisoners from Paris on Fructidor 10 and 11 (August 27th and 28th), tentative riots occurred in attempts to rescue the prisoners, but these were easily suppressed. A further attempt by five or six hundred Jacobins to rouse soldiers at Grenelle on September 7th was also unsuccessful.

On Prairial 7 (April 26, 1797), Babeuf and Darthe were condemned to death and were executed the following day, Prairial 8. Other prisoners, including Buonarroti, were exiled, while others, such as Vadier and fellow-conventionals, were acquitted. According to Barras, Drouet managed to escape with the connivance of the Directory.

## French Revolutionary Coups and Political Figures
The era was marked by several significant *coups d'etat*. The *coup d'etat* of 9 Thermidor (July 27, 1794) saw the overthrow of the Robespierre faction, which elevated Barras to a front-rank position. Later, on 13 Vendemiaire (October 5, 1795), Barras commanded troops to disperse royalists and malcontents near the Tuileries, utilizing Bonaparte as a subaltern. Barras subsequently became one of the five Directors of the French republic.

In the summer of 1797, as royalist and constitutional opposition grew, Bonaparte dispatched General Augereau, a headstrong Jacobin, to repress the movement via the *coup d'etat* of 18 Fructidor (September 4). This event led to the exile of the politician Adrien Duport, who had previously served as president of the criminal tribunal of Paris. Eventually, Bonaparte overthrew the Directory through the *coup d'etat* of 18-19 Brumaire (November 9th-10th), replacing the five Directors with three Consuls and ending the political career of Barras.

## The Evolution of the Tribune Newspaper
In a separate historical context, the *Tribune* refers to a daily newspaper founded by Greeley. After editing the *Daily Whig* and *The Jeffersonian* in Albany, Greeley published a weekly campaign paper called *The Log Cabin* starting May 2, 1840. While it achieved a circulation of 80,000 and was a political success, it was not profitable and merged into the *Weekly Tribune* in September 1841.

On April 3, 1841, Greeley announced the launch of a daily newspaper, *The Tribune*, which began publication on April 10th. Founded with a $1,000 loan from James Coggeshall, the paper started with 500 subscribers. Its circulation grew rapidly, reaching 6,000 by the fourth week and 11,000 by the seventh week, which was the limit of its press capacity.
