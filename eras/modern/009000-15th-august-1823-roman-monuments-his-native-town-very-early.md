## Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier
Born in Nimes on 15th August 1823, Gaston Boissier developed an early interest in the study of ancient history, sparked by the Roman monuments located in his native town. He specialized in epigraphy and began his professional career at the age of twenty-three as a professor of rhetoric in Angouleme, a position he held for ten years.

Following a lecture heard by a travelling university inspector, Boissier was appointed as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne in Paris. His literary contributions began with a thesis on the poet Attius in 1857, followed by a study of the life and work of M. Terentius Varro in 1861. That same year, he was appointed professor of Latin oratory at the College de France and became an active contributor to the *Revue des deux mondes*.

Boissier is noted for his ability to reproduce the characteristics and society of ancient Rome with exquisite vivacity. His major published works include:
*   *Ciceron et ses amis* (1865), a work of erudition that achieved significant success.
*   *La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins* (1874), a two-volume analysis of the religious movement in antiquity preceding the acceptance of Christianity.
*   *L'Opposition sous les Cesars* (1875), which depicted the political decadence of Rome under the early successors of Augustus.

In 1876, following the death of H.J.G. Patin, Boissier was elected to the French Academy, later becoming its perpetual secretary in 1895.
