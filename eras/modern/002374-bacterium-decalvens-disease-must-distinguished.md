# Bacterium decalvens

*Bacterium decalvens* is the name given by Dr. George Thin to the organisms associated with alopecia areata. These organisms are invariably present in cases of the disease; in some instances, they are few in number, while in others they are very abundant and form a continuous sheath around the hair.

## Associated Condition: Alopecia Areata
Alopecia areata is a condition characterized by the development of round patches that are more or less completely denuded of hair. While these patches are most commonly observed on the scalp, they may occur on any part of the body where hair naturally grows. 

Physically, the patches are smooth, rounded, and somewhat depressed, a result of the loss of a large proportion of the follicles. Short broken hairs are usually visible at the margins of these patches. Clinical evidence suggests that this disease may be transmitted.

## Differential Diagnosis
It is necessary to distinguish alopecia areata from ringworm, particularly the bald variety. While this distinction can sometimes be difficult to make clinically, the use of a microscope removes any doubt.

## Prognosis and Treatment
The prognosis for recovery depends significantly on the age of the patient. For those under forty years of age, time alone will generally bring about the desired end, although treatment can hasten the recovery process. For patients over forty, the prognosis becomes less favorable with every additional year of age.

Management of the condition requires careful attention to the sufferer's general hygiene and mode of life, and any existing weakness must be suitably treated. A recommended daily application for the affected parts is a lotion consisting of:
* Acidi lactici [dr.] i-[oz.] i
* Ol. ricini [dr.] ii
* Spt. vini ad [oz.] iv

This lotion should be applied cautiously at first, and later more vigorously and in a stronger solution.
