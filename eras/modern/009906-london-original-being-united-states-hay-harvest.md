# The Horse Fair and Hay Harvests

## The Horse Fair and its Locations
The artwork titled "The Horse Fair," created in 1853 by the French painter Rosa Bonheur, exists in multiple versions. One of the two replicas of this work is located in the National Gallery in London. The original version of "The Horse Fair" is located in the United States.

## Rosa Bonheur and Her Artistic Style
Rosa Bonheur (1822–1899) was a French painter born in Bordeaux on March 22, 1822. She was the eldest of four children, all of whom were artists: Auguste (1824–1884), who painted landscapes and animals; Juliette (1830–1891); and Isidore (born 1827), a sculptor of animals. 

Taught to draw by her father, who died in 1849, Bonheur was permitted to abandon dressmaking to focus entirely on art. She exhibited at the salon from 1840 to 1845, receiving five prizes, and was awarded a medal in 1848. Her fame grew significantly following the exhibition of 1855, after which her works became highly sought after by public galleries and collectors in England.

Bonheur is noted for representing animals as they actually appear in the country, characterized by faultlessly true anatomy and a firm, "manly" treatment. However, some critics noted a dryness of style and a failure to provide a perfect sense of atmosphere. In addition to "The Horse Fair," her notable works include "Ploughing in the Nivernais" (1848), found in the Luxembourg gallery, and "Hay Harvest in Auvergne" (1835). For her achievements, she was decorated as an officer of the Legion of Honour by Empress Eugenie.

## Agricultural Hay and Forage Production
In the United States, hay and forage have historically been highly valuable agricultural crops. In 1899, these products were the most valuable of all crops, yielding 47.4% of the total crop value at $4,238,993. This represented an increase of more than 200% over the value recorded in 1889. By 1907, the United States Department of Agriculture's *Year-book* valued the hay and forage crop at $8,585,000.

## Hay and Forage in Animal Husbandry
The harvesting and use of hay vary by region and animal needs:

*   **Equine Feeding:** For horses performing quick work, Timothy hay harvested in dry weather is recommended. In England, farm horses are liberally fed red-clover hay, or alternatively, lucerne or crimson-clover hay, typically alongside approximately 10 lb. of oats per day.
*   **Honey Harvesting:** In the United States, honey is harvested in hundreds of tons yearly. Large apiaries, some containing between 2,000 and 3,500 colonies, have been established. In California, a single bee-farmer with 2,000 stocks of bees harvested 150,000 lb. of honey in one year, with his best 300 colonies producing 60,000 lb. of comb-honey in a single season. Many large bee-farmers in Canada and the United States harvest between 50,000 and 60,000 lb. of honey per season.
