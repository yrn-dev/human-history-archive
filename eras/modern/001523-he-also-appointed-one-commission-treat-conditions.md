# Commissions and Administrative Appointments

Throughout history, commissions and appointed boards have been utilized across various nations to manage legal disputes, oversee territorial governance, regulate economic conditions, and execute judicial functions. These bodies range from temporary investigative committees to permanent international governing authorities.

## International Commissions and Territorial Governance

In the aftermath of global conflicts, international commissions have been established to manage strategic waterways and territories. To ensure the Kiel Canal remains open to merchant and war ships of all nations at peace with Germany, any state may demand the appointment of an international commission in the event of a disagreement or violation of the canal's provisions. Similarly, the Elbe and Oder rivers were placed under international commissions. The Elbe commission consisted of four representatives from Germany, two from Czecho-Slovakia, and one each from Belgium, France, Italy, and Great Britain. The Oder commission included representatives from Russia, Poland, Czecho-Slovakia, France, Great Britain, Sweden, and Denmark.

In the case of the Sarre, a commission appointed by the League of Nations was tasked with governing the territory to guarantee the welfare of the population and French freedom in working the mines. This five-member body included one Frenchman, one native inhabitant of the Sarre, and three representatives from countries other than Germany and France. This commission held the powers of government previously held by Prussia, the German Empire, and Bavaria, including the administration of public services and railroads. While existing German legislation remained the basis of law, the commission could make modifications after consulting a local representative assembly.

## Financial and Reparation Commissions

Commissions have also been used to resolve financial disputes and recover assets. The Reparation Commission was empowered to appoint arbitrators to make final decisions regarding financial adjustments necessitated by the dismemberment of the former monarchy, specifically concerning land banks, savings banks, insurance and banking companies, and the resettlement of currency and public debts. Additionally, the Reparation Commission appointed a committee of three jurists to examine the conditions under which special objects were removed from Poland, Belgium, Italy, and Czecho-Slovakia by other dynasties and the House of Hapsburg, ordering their restoration if the removal was found to be illegal.

## Domestic Administrative and Economic Boards

In the United States, specifically within Illinois, economic conditions led to the creation of various administrative boards. A State Civil Service Commission was established by an act of the General Assembly in 1905. Other specialized bodies include the Live Stock Commission (1885), the State Food Commission (1899), and a Mining Board (1883). The Bureau of Labor Statistics, established in 1879, employs members styled as Commissioners of Labor who publish biennial reports on financial and economic problems. Furthermore, a Board of Arbitration was created in 1895 to investigate facts regarding lock-outs and strikes and to adjust these disputes through conciliation or mediation.

In Norway, following the revolution of 1660, a land commission was appointed in January 1661 to investigate the economical and financial conditions of the kingdoms.

## Judicial and Military Appointments

The appointment of officials to execute specific legal or military duties has varied by era and region. In the English parliament, the practice of appointing a steward to execute judgment upon a peer was maintained until 1477. Under the Stuarts, this practice returned when proceedings against a peer were based on indictment. During the impeachment of Strafford, the lords appointed Arundel as high steward. In the case of Danby, a commission under the great seal was initially issued; although a joint committee later suggested a steward was unnecessary, the lords petitioned for one, leading to a fresh commission in an amended form.

In Japan, commissions were used to grant official titles and authority. Akamatsu Norimura sought a commission from Kyoto to be appointed high constable of Harima to secure his support for the Imperialists. However, after receiving the patent, he rejected it, claiming he already held a patent from the shogun, Takauji. In another instance, following the death of Mochiuji in 1439, Noritada was appointed as manager (*shitsuji*) to the five-year-old Shigeuji. Later, partisans of the murdered Noritada received a commission from Muromachi, allowing them to march against Kamakura with a contingent of troops.
