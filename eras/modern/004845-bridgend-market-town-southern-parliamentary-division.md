# BRIDGEND

## Geography and Location
Bridgend is a market town located in the southern parliamentary division of Glamorganshire, Wales. The town is situated on both sides of the river Ogwr, which provides the basis for its Welsh name, Penybont-ar-Ogwr. The town serves as a natural outlet for the mining valleys of the two Ogwr rivers, the Garw, and the Llynvi. These rivers converge approximately 3 miles north of the town.

## Population and Infrastructure
As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 6,062. The town is well-connected by rail, featuring a station on the South Wales trunk line of the Great Western railway, located 165 miles from London. Additionally, Bridgend serves as the junction for the Barry Company's railway, which leads to Barry via Llantwit Major. The town is further linked to the nearby mining valleys through branch lines of the Great Western railway.

## Economy and Industry
Bridgend maintains a strong agricultural trade with a good market for agricultural produce. While the town does not possess large manufacturing industries, it supports several smaller industrial operations, including:
* A brewery
* A tannery
* A brass and iron foundry
* Joinery works

The surrounding area is also characterized by the presence of stone quarries and brick-works, and there is significant lime burning conducted in the neighbourhood.

## Local Institutions
Located just outside the town limits at Parc Gwyllt and Angelton are the Glamorgan county lunatic asylums.
