# Felton Hervey

## Biography and Political Career
Felton Hervey (1712–1773) was a member of the British parliament. He served as a representative for the family borough of Bury St Edmunds. 

## Family Lineage
Felton Hervey was a son of John Hervey, the 1st Earl of Bristol (1665–1751), and his second wife, Elizabeth. Elizabeth was the daughter and co-heiress of Sir Thomas Felton, Bart., of Playford, Suffolk. 

Through his father, Felton was part of a family with significant political ties to Bury St Edmunds. His father, the 1st Earl of Bristol, had been a member of parliament for the borough starting in March 1694. Felton's half-brother, Carr, Lord Hervey (1691–1723), also served as a member for Bury St Edmunds from 1713 to 1722. Additionally, another of Felton's brothers, Thomas (1699–1775), served as one of the members for Bury from 1733 to 1747.

## Descendants
Felton Hervey's grandson was Felton Elwell Hervey-Bathurst (1782–1819), who had assumed the additional name of Bathurst. In 1818, Felton Elwell Hervey-Bathurst was created a baronet. Following his death in 1819, the title passed to his brother, Frederick Anne (1783–1824), who is the direct ancestor of the present baronet.

## The Borough of Bury St Edmunds
The borough represented by Felton Hervey and several members of his family was originally one of the royal towns of the Saxons. It was founded as a monastery around 633 by Sigebert, king of the East Angles, and later became the burial place of King Edmund around 903. 

The town's status as a borough developed through various grants from abbots. It was not represented in parliament until 1608, when James I granted the privilege of sending two members. The borough's representation was later reduced to one member by the Redistribution Act of 1885.
