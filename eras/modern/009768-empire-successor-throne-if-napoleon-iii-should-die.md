# Empire: Succession to the Throne of Napoleon III

## Designation of the Successor
Upon the establishment of the Empire under Napoleon III, the individual designated as the successor to the throne in the event that Napoleon III should die childless was Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul. Commonly referred to as Prince Napoleon or by the sobriquet "Plon-Plon," he was born on September 9, 1822, in Trieste. He was the second son of Catherine, princess of Wurttemberg, and Jerome Bonaparte, who served as the king of Westphalia.

## Political Profile and Early Career
Prince Napoleon was known for expressing advanced democratic ideas on all possible occasions, which helped him gain popularity. Following the French revolution of 1848, he was elected as a representative of Corsica to the National Assembly. He assumed the name of Jerome after the death of his elder brother, Jerome Napoleon Charles, in 1847.

Despite his designation as the successor to the throne, Prince Napoleon was allowed no share in public affairs. He received a liberal dotation but maintained a position of ostensible opposition to the *coup d'etat* of 1851. Privately, he associated with men of advanced political opinions and viewed himself as the representative of the democratic aspect of the Napoleonic tradition. Within the court, he acted as a representative of the Liberal party in opposition to the empress Eugenie.

## Military and Administrative Service
Prince Napoleon's career included both military and civil contributions to the Empire:

*   **Military Service:** In 1854, he served as a general of division during the Crimean campaign. Although his conduct at the battle of the Alma led to some imputations regarding his personal courage, these claims appear to have been groundless. Later, during the war for the liberation of Italy, he commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany. While it was expected that he would become the ruler of that principality, he refused to pressure the inhabitants, who preferred union with the Italian kingdom.
*   **Civil Administration:** In 1855, he demonstrated great capacity while undertaking the chief direction of the National Exhibition. In 1858, he was appointed as the minister for the Colonies and Algeria. His administration initially aroused great hopes, though his focus was diverted in January 1859 by his marriage to the princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy, the daughter of Victor Emmanuel.

In the years following, Prince Napoleon became known as a great orator through a series of remarkable speeches.
