# Barbier

The name Barbier is associated with several distinct figures in French history, including a bibliographer, a poet, and a high-ranking church official.

## Antoine Alexandre Barbier (1765–1825)
Antoine Alexandre Barbier was a French bibliographer and librarian born on January 11, 1765, in Coulommiers (Seine-et-Marne). He initially took priest's orders but was released from them by the pope in 1801. During the French Revolution, specifically in 1794, he served on a temporary commission of the arts. In this capacity, he was responsible for distributing books confiscated during the Revolution to various libraries in Paris. This work led to his discovery of the manuscripts of the works of Fenelon and the letters of the bishop of Avranches, Huet.

Barbier held several prestigious positions as a librarian, serving the Directory, the Conseil d'Etat, and eventually Napoleon in 1807, for whom he executed various commissions. He was involved in the establishment of the libraries at Saint-Cloud, Compiegne, Fontainebleau, and the Louvre. Under the reign of Louis XVIII, he served as the administrator of the king's private libraries, though he was stripped of all his offices in 1822.

His primary scholarly contribution was the *Dictionnaire des ouvrages anonymes et pseudonymes*, published in four volumes between 1806 and 1809, with a third edition appearing between 1872 and 1879. A notice by his son, Louis Barbier, and a list of his works are prefixed to this third edition. He also wrote *Examen critique des dictionnaires historiques* (1820), though only the first part of this work was published. He died in Paris on December 5, 1825.

## Henri Auguste Barbier (1805–1882)
Henri Auguste Barbier was a poet and dramatist born in Paris on April 29, 1805. His early work was heavily influenced by the revolution of July, resulting in a series of vigorous poems that criticized the evils of the era. These works are known collectively as the *Iambes* (1831), although this title does not strictly apply to every poem in the set. Modelled on the verse of Andre Chenier, the *Iambes* include works such as *L'Idole*, *Paris*, *La Popularite*, *La Curee*, *Varsovie*, *Dante*, and *Quatre-vingt-treize*.

Barbier also wrote two series of poems focusing on social and political unrest in England and Italy, which were included in later editions of *Iambes et poemes*. In the realm of opera, he collaborated with Leon de Wailly on the libretto for Berlioz's *Benvenuto Cellini*.

Despite his early output, much of his poetry was forgotten over time. When he was admitted to the Academy in 1869, Montalembert remarked, "*Barbier? mais il est mort!*" reflecting a general sentiment regarding his relevance. There were also unfounded claims that he was not the actual author of the *Iambes*. He died in Nice on February 13, 1882.

## Louis Barbier (1593–1670)
Louis Barbier, also known as the Abbe de la Riviere, was a French bishop born to humble parents in Vaudelaincourt, near Compiegne. He rose through the church via his cleverness and wit, eventually becoming the adviser and friend, as well as the tutor, of Gaston d'Orleans (the brother of Louis XIII). 

Through this connection, Barbier entered the royal court and served as the grand almoner to the queen, receiving the revenues of several wealthy abbeys. In March 1655, he was appointed bishop of Langres. However, he continued to spend the majority of his time at court, where he was valued for his wit and accumulated significant wealth through gambling. He died in a state of great wealth.
