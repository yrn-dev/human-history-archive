# The Work of H. H. P. Deasy in Western Tibet

## Overview of Explorations
Captain H. H. P. Deasy, an officer of the 16th Lancers, was a prominent British traveler and explorer active in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Between 1896 and 1898, Deasy was one of two British cavalry officers—the other being Captain M. S. Wellby of the 18th Hussars—who took a leading position in Tibetan exploration. During this period, Deasy is credited with striking out a new line of travel and providing valuable service to the field of geography.

## Geographical and Cartographic Contributions
Deasy's work was significant for its contribution to the mapping and triangulation of Central Asia. He continued the Pamir triangulation, a project that had previously been carried across the Hindu Kush by Colonels R. A. Wahab and Sir T. H. Holdich during the Pamir Boundary Commission of 1895. Deasy extended this scientific work into the plains of Kashgar and reached the sources of the Zarafshan.

His findings were documented in his publication, *In Tibet and Chinese Turkestan* (London, 1901), which is noted for including a high-quality map. Since the beginning of the 20th century, Deasy's efforts in western Tibet were further extended by Captain C. G. Rawling and Dr. M. A. Stein, whose combined work increased the known data regarding ancient commerce and fields of industry in Tibet and Turkestan.

## Natural History Observations
Beyond geography, Deasy's writings provided detailed observations on the fauna of the Tibetan plateau.

### The Yak
Deasy recorded observations of Yaks found in herds of ten to thirty, and occasionally more, on lofty ground in Tibet. He noted that while brown specimens were very rare, most of the animals were black. He described these roving herds as possessing great agility when moving over stony and steep ground, observing that the animals appeared to enjoy the wind, frost, and snow. Additionally, Deasy noted that Yaks are capable of offering formidable resistance to sportsmen.

### The Ovis Poli
Deasy's work also contains information regarding the *Ovis Poli*, known as the Great Sheep of Pamir. His writings on the subject are cited as a reference for the species.
