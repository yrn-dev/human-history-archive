# The Acquisition and Administration of Bombay

## Early English Presence and Acquisition
The English established their first settlement in the Bombay presidency in 1618. This took the form of a factory at Surat, which operated under the protection of a charter granted by Emperor Jahangir. For several decades, the English sought to expand their holdings in the region. In 1626, an attempt was made by the English and the Dutch to gain possession of the island of Bombay, though this effort was unsuccessful. By 1653, proposals were put forward to purchase the island from the Portuguese.

The acquisition of Bombay eventually occurred in 1661, when the island was ceded to the English crown. This transfer was part of the dower provided for the infanta Catherine of Portugal upon her marriage to Charles II.

## Transfer to the East India Company
Despite the acquisition, the island was not highly valued in England. Due to this low esteem and the unsuccessful administration provided by crown officers, Bombay was transferred from the crown to the East India Company in 1668. This transfer was conducted in exchange for an annual payment of £10.

Along with the transfer of the island, the East India Company was granted powers for the administration of justice and for the defense of the territory. To secure the settlement, a European regiment was enrolled. The resulting fortifications proved effective in 1673, when they deterred a planned attack by the Dutch.

## Administrative Shifts and Regional Power
The status of Bombay within the Company's Indian holdings shifted over the following century. In 1687, Bombay was designated as the head of all the East India Company's possessions in India. However, this arrangement changed in 1753, when the government of Bombay became subordinate to the government of Calcutta.

## Conflict with the Mahratta Power
The English encountered the Mahratta power in a series of collisions. The first conflict began in 1774 and concluded in 1782 with the treaty of Salbai. Under the terms of this treaty, Broach was handed over to Sindhia, while Salsette was ceded to the British.

Further territorial gains were made following the second Mahratta war, which ended in 1803. Prior to the conclusion of this war, Surat had already been annexed in 1800. Following the war's end, the East India Company received additional districts, including Kaira and Broach.
