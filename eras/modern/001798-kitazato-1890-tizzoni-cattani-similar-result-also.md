# Shibasaburo Kitazato

## Biography and Education
Shibasaburo Kitazato (1856– ) was a Japanese doctor of medicine. Born in Kumamoto in 1856, he pursued advanced medical studies in Germany. Between 1885 and 1891, he studied under the guidance of Koch.

## Contributions to Bacteriology
Kitazato rose to become one of the world's foremost bacteriologists. He is credited with the discovery of the bacilli responsible for several major diseases, specifically tetanus, diphtheria, and plague. 

His work on the plague bacillus was conducted in conjunction with Dr. Aoyama. The two doctors traveled to Hong Kong in 1894 during an epidemic occurring at that location to carry out their research.

## Context of Bacteriological Research
Kitazato's career coincided with a period of rapid advancement in the understanding of infective conditions. This progress was stimulated by Pasteur's demonstration that bacteria could produce definite diseases. A critical factor in this era of discovery was the introduction of the "plate-method" and solid culture media by Koch in 1881, which simplified the separation and cultivation of bacteria.

During the decade of 1880–1890, researchers identified the organisms associated with various diseases, including cholera, typhoid fever, glanders, tubercle, and suppuration. Following this period, in the final decade of the 19th century, further discoveries were made, including the bacilli of influenza (1892), plague (1894), and dysentery (1898).
