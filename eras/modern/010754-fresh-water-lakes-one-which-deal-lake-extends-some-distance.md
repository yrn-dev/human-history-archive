# Fresh-Water Lakes

Fresh-water lakes are found globally, varying significantly in their geological origins, sizes, and characteristics. They range from small highland lochs and basins to vast inland seas and artificial reservoirs.

## Regional Examples and Characteristics

### North America
In the United States, the city of Asbury Park in Monmouth County, New Jersey, is bounded to the north and south by fresh-water lakes. One of these, Deal Lake, extends for some distance into the wooded country. In Canada's North-West Territories, a vast system of waters includes Great Slave Lake, Lake Athabasca, and Great Bear Lake. These lakes and their associated rivers, such as the Mackenzie and the Peace, are noted for being abundantly stocked with fish, particularly white fish.

### Asia
The lakes of Asia are described as innumerable. Some are vestiges of ancient glaciation and typically show a gradual decrease in size, while others are relics of earlier geological periods where land upheaved from the sea. Examples of the latter include Lop Nor and the Helmund *hamuns*, which change in extent and form seasonally and are often impregnated with saline deposits.

In Asiatic Russia, Lake Balkash (also known as *Ak-denghiz*, *Ala-denghiz*, or *Si-hai*) is the fourth largest lake in Eurasia, covering 7115 sq. m. It is shaped like a broad crescent and, despite having no outlet, its waters remain relatively fresh. In the Kum-kol valley, the upper lake, Chon-kum-kol, contains fresh water and covers an area of 8 sq. m. with a maximum depth of 13 ft.

### Africa
In British East Africa, a rift-valley contains a series of basins with lakes that lack outlets. Lake Naivasha is a small fresh-water lake located 6135 ft. above sea level, measuring approximately 13 m. in each direction. In British Central Africa, Lake Bangweulu is a shallow lake formed by the head streams of the Congo, situated about 3700 ft. above sea level. Much of its basin is a morass of reeds and papyrus, with the Luapula river serving as its outflow.

## Specialized Lake Types and Formations

### Artificial and Modified Lakes
The lake of Moiris in Egypt is described as an artificial body of water formed by digging. Its circuit measures 3,600 furlongs. Water is brought to the lake from the Nile via a canal; for six months the water flows into the lake, and for six months it flows back into the Nile. This cycle supports a fishing industry that contributes silver to the royal treasury.

### Brackish and Variable Salinity
Some lakes exhibit varying levels of salinity. Lake Maracaibo in western Venezuela is quadrangular and opens into the Gulf of Venezuela. While the water in the upper half of the lake is sweet, it becomes brackish in the lower half where tidal influence is stronger. Similarly, in the Kum-kol valley, while the upper lake is fresh, the lower lake, Ayak-kum-kol, is salt.

## Geological and Theoretical Perspectives

### Mediterranean Basin Theory
Theoretical suggestions regarding the Mediterranean basin propose that it once contained two lakes, one of which was a fresh-water lake located in the eastern depression. This lake reportedly drained into another in the western depression. It is suggested that as ocean levels rose due to the dissipation of ice-sheets, water poured into the Mediterranean area. Evidence for this is cited in the form of an enormous submarine valley or gorge running through the Straits of Gibraltar, which may have been created by inflowing ocean waters at the end of a period of interior drainage.

### Glacial and Tectonic Origins
Many high-elevation lakes, such as Lake Victoria (at 13,400 ft.), are considered vestiges of ancient glaciation. Other lakes are formed by tectonic activity, such as the basins in the rift-valley of East Africa, which are divided by transverse ridges and extinct volcanoes like Suswa and Longonot.
