# Bressuire

Bressuire is a town located in western France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement within the department of Deux-Sèvres and is situated 48 miles north of Niort by rail. As of 1906, the population of the town was 4,561.

## Geography and Economy
The town is positioned on an eminence that overlooks the Dolo, which is a tributary of the Argenton. Bressuire acts as the center for an agricultural and cattle-rearing region and maintains important markets. The local economy includes the manufacture of woollen goods and wooden type.

## Architecture and Landmarks
Bressuire contains two primary buildings of interest:

*   **The Church of Notre-Dame:** This structure dates primarily from the 15th and 12th centuries and features an imposing tower from the Renaissance period.
*   **The Castle:** Built by the lords of Beaumont, who were vassals of the viscount of Thouars, the castle is now in ruins. While a modern château occupies a portion of the site, the outer and inner lines of fortifications remain visible. This site is considered the finest assemblage of feudal ruins in Poitou.

## Administration and History
Administratively, Bressuire is the seat of a sub-prefect and possesses a tribunal of first instance. 

The town has suffered various disasters over time. The most memorable of these occurred in 1370, when the town was captured from the English and subsequently pillaged by French troops under the command of du Guesclin.
