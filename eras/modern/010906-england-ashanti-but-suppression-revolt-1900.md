# The Suppression of the Ashanti Revolt of 1900

The revolt of 1900 was a serious rebellion that broke out in Ashanti against British rule. The conflict was centered in Kumasi and involved the Kokofu, Adansi, and Kumasi tribes, while other tribes within the Ashanti confederation remained loyal to the British.

## Origins of the Rebellion
Following the deportation of Prempeh, the British did not appoint a successor to the Ashanti throne. Instead, the affairs of Kumasi were administered by chiefs under the guidance of a British resident, Captain Donald W. Stewart. While a fort was established in Kumasi and garrisoned by the Gold Coast constabulary, the Kumasi chiefs remained unreconciled to British authority. Their resentment was further fueled by a desire to erase the dishonor of having allowed Prempeh to be deported without a fight.

Two immediate triggers precipitated the outbreak of violence. On March 28, 1900, the governor of the Gold Coast, Sir F. Hodgson, held a public palaver in Kumasi where he announced that the Ashanti chiefs were required to pay the British government 4,000 oz. of gold annually. During this meeting, Hodgson also reproached the chiefs for hiding the golden stool, which had been concealed by the Kumasi since 1896. Three days later, Kumasi warriors attacked a party of Hausa who had been dispatched specifically to discover the location of the golden stool. This followed a failed secret attempt to seize the stool in January of that year.

## The Siege of Kumasi
After the attack on the Hausa party, the Kumasi warriors threatened the fort at Kumasi. A group of besieged residents took refuge within the fort, including Sir F. and Lady Hodgson, as well as Mr. Ramseyer and other Basel missionaries. 

The British response involved a series of reinforcements:
* **April 18:** 100 members of the Gold Coast constabulary arrived.
* **April 29:** The Kumasi attacked in force but were repulsed. On this same day, 250 Lagos constabulary reached the city, though they arrived with little ammunition after fighting their way up.
* **May 15:** Major A. Morris arrived from British territory north of Ashanti with an additional 250 men.

By mid-May, the garrison numbered 700, which included 29 Europeans and four women. The rebel forces were reported to number 40,000.

## Aftermath and Economic Recovery
The revolt caused further injury to the town of Kumasi, which had already suffered a decline following the destruction of the city in 1874 and the burning of houses in the Bantama suburb in 1896. However, the suppression of the 1900 revolt led to a period of strategic economic improvement. Measures were implemented to enhance trade between Cape Coast and Kumasi.

A significant development in the region's infrastructure was the construction of a railway from Sekondi to Kumasi, which was completed in September 1903. This railway passed through the auriferous regions of the Obuassi and Tarkwa gold-fields. The arrival of the railway prompted many merchants from the Gold Coast ports to open branches in Kumasi, sparking a marked revival in trade and the rapid expansion of the town. By 1906, Kumasi had supplanted the coast towns to become the distributing centre for the hinterland and the whole of Ashanti.

## Regional Economy and Administration
Following the suppression of the revolt, Ashanti's revenue and expenditure were integrated into those of the Gold Coast. Revenue was primarily generated through liquor licences, rents from government land, contributions from gold-mining companies, and caravan taxes. 

The region's commercial output was diverse:
* **Mining:** Gold was found in the western districts. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti was 68,259 oz., valued at £254,790, with much of the gold from the Gold Coast in 1902 and subsequent years originating from the Obuassi mines.
* **Agriculture:** The rubber tree (_Funtumia elastica_) was the most important commercial vegetable product. Other exports included gum copal, tobacco from the northern regions, and kola nuts. Cocoa plantations were established in the eastern districts starting in 1905.

While trade flowed southward through British territory, Ashanti also maintained considerable trade with its neighbors in Germany and France, as well as with the Niger countries to the north.
