# Islas Baleares

The *Islas Baleares* is an illustrated volume authored by P. Pifferrer. Published in Barcelona in 1888, the work consists of 1,423 pages and is part of a series titled "Espana." It is cited as one of the most valuable books for those seeking a general account of the Balearic Islands.

## Context of the Subject
The *Islas Baleares* provides a detailed account of the Balearic Islands (Baleares), which are an archipelago located in the Mediterranean Sea off the east coast of Spain. The archipelago forms a province of Spain and covers an area of 1,935 square miles. As of 1900, the population of the region was 311,649.

The archipelago is situated between 1° and 5° E. and between 38° 40' and 40° 5' N. It is divided into two distinct groups:

*   **The Eastern Group:** This group corresponds to the ancient *Insulae Baleares*. It includes the two principal islands, Majorca (Spanish: *Mallorca*) and Minorca (Spanish: *Menorca*), along with seven islets: El Rey, Pinto, Dragonera, Cabrera, Botafoch, Aucanada, and Aire.
*   **The Western Group:** This group corresponds to the ancient *Pityusae* or Pine Islands. It consists of two relatively large islands, Formentera and Iviza (Spanish: *Ibiza* or formerly *Ivica*), and the islets of Espalmador, Pou, Conejera, and Ahorcados.

## Regional Administration and Geography
The administration of the islands follows the same structure as other Spanish provinces on the mainland. The region is divided into five judicial districts, known as *partidos judiciales*, which are named after their primary towns: Port Mahon, Palma, Manacor, Iviza, and Inca.

While all eleven islets in the archipelago were inhabited as of 1900, their total population was only 171. Most of these islets are considered unimportant, with the exception of Cabrera. Cabrera is characterized by its numerous caverns and was previously utilized as a place of banishment. In 1808, Spanish captors landed a large group of Frenchmen on Cabrera, where they were left to almost perish from starvation.

## Related Bibliographic Works
Beyond Pifferrer's *Islas Baleares*, several other significant works document the history and nature of the islands:

*   **General Accounts:** Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria wrote *Die Balearen geschildert in Wort und Bild* (1896), and G. Vuillier wrote *Les Iles oubliees* (1904), which was translated into English as *The Forgotten Isles* (1896). George Sand also wrote a notable article on the subject in the *Revue des deux mondes* in 1841.
*   **Specialized Monographs:** Sir C. R. Markham authored *The Story of Majorca and Minorca* (1908). M. Willkomm produced *Illustrationes florae insularum Balearium* (1881-1892), and E. Cartailhac wrote *Monuments primitifs des iles baleares* (1892).
*   **Historical and Scientific Studies:** J. Dameto and V. Mut wrote *La Historia general del regno balearico* (1632-1650). For the era of Moorish rule, A. Campaner y Fuertes authored *Bosquejo historico de la dominacion islamita en las islas Baleares* (1888). Additionally, A. Lecoy de la Marche wrote an elaborate treatise titled *Les Relations de la France avec le royaume de Majorque* (1892).
*   **Commerce:** Information regarding the movement of commerce in the region is available in the *British Foreign Office Reports for the Consular District of Barcelona*.
