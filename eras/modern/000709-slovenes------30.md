# Slovenes

The Slovenes are a South Slav people who, according to archive records, formed a large majority of the population in Carniola and a considerable minority in the south of Styria and the adjoining territory of Carinthia. In the province of Carniola, as of 1900, the population was 508,348, with Slovenes comprising nearly 95% of the inhabitants and Germans making up 5%. The population of Carniola was 99% Roman Catholic.

## Political Aspirations and National Movement
The Slovenes sought the use of their language for the purposes of education and government. Their political ideal was the establishment of an "Illyrian" kingdom, which would include Croatia and all southern Slavs in the coast district. In pursuit of this, a movement was initiated to create a so-called "Illyrian" language to be accepted by both Slovenes and Croats, though this movement was not very successful.

In later years, attempts were made to shift the Slovenian national movement toward the Orthodox non-Austrian Slavs, attempting to attract Slovenes toward the direction of the Serbs. The Serbs, while sharing the same race and language as the Croats, were separated by religion as members of the Orthodox Church and were attracted by Russia and the hope of creating a great Servian kingdom.

## Administrative and Linguistic Struggles
Until 1879, the Slovenes lacked government support. However, by 1882, they won a majority in the diet of Carniola, which subsequently became the chief support for Slovene agitation, contrasting with the diet of Styria, which served as the center for Germans. In the same year, Slovenes won the majority in the town council of Laibach, the capital of Carniola. This victory allowed them to introduce Illyrian as the official language and to write street names in Illyrian. Similar efforts to use Illyrian for street names in some towns of Istria resulted in serious riots.

Following a resolution of the diet of Carniola and a motion by Slovene members of the Reichsrath, the government recognized Slovenian as a recognized language for the district of Cilli in Styria, the whole of Carniola, and the mixed and Slovene districts in southern Carinthia. In Laibach, the government determined that a Slovene gymnasium should be maintained alongside the German one.

Conflict regarding language persisted in other regions. In the town of Klagenfurt in Carinthia, the government declared Slovenian a customary language in 1880, requiring public offices and law courts to handle business in Slovenian, despite the town having a population of 16,491 German-speakers and only 568 Slovenian-speakers (including 180 inhabitants of the hospital or gaol). This was justified by the fact that the rural population in the surrounding villages was chiefly Slovene.

## Parliamentary Representation
Slovenes have been represented in the Reichsrath at Vienna. In one recorded parliamentary distribution, Slovenes held 30 seats, with 17 being Clericals. Another record lists "Clerical Slovenes" with 11 seats and "Radical" Slovenes with 5 seats. Additionally, Slovene Liberals were associated with the Southern Slav Club, which also included Serbs and Croats. In a different tally of Southern Slavs (including Slovenes, Croats, and Serbs), the group held 37 seats.

## Political Conflict and Government Collapse
The request by Slovenes to form Slovenian-language classes parallel to German classes in the gymnasium at Cilli became a point of significant political instability. While the request was supported by the ministry and the Hohenwart Club (of which the Slovenes were members), it caused great excitement in neighboring districts and protests from the Styrian diet. The German Left, pressured by popular indignation in German districts, opposed the measure. When the vote passed in favor of the Slovenes on June 12, 1895, the German Left made it a question of confidence and withdrew their support, leading to the resignation of the government.

## International Status
In the projected Covenant for a league of nations, the "Serb-Croat-Slovene State" was listed as one of the original members of the league.
