# Sisters of Mercy and St Joseph's Academy for Girls

## Location and Institutional Context
The Sisters of Mercy and St Joseph's Academy for Girls are located in Bordentown, a city in Burlington County, New Jersey, U.S.A. Bordentown is situated on the east bank of the Delaware River, approximately 6 miles south of Trenton and 28 miles northeast of Philadelphia. The city is positioned on a broad, level plain 65 feet above the river.

Within the city, the Sisters of Mercy maintain their mother-house and St Joseph's convent. Additionally, the city is home to St Joseph's Academy for Girls. These institutions exist alongside other local entities, including the Bordentown Military Institute (which houses the Woodward memorial library) and the state manual training and industrial school for coloured youth.

## City Infrastructure and Economy
Bordentown is served by several transportation links, including the Pennsylvania railway, the Camden & Trenton railway (an electric line connecting Philadelphia and New York), and various passenger and freight steamboat lines operating on the Delaware River.

The city's economy is supported by a variety of industrial enterprises, including:
* Iron foundries and forges
* Machine shops
* Ship-yards
* Shirt factories
* Canning factories
* A woollen mill
* A pottery specializing in the manufacture of sanitary earthenware

## Historical Background of Bordentown
The site of the city was first settled in the 18th century by several Quaker families. The city was laid out by Joseph Borden, for whom it was named. Its administrative history includes incorporation as a borough in 1825, re-incorporation in 1849, and its eventual charter as a city in 1867.

The city has been the residence of several notable figures. Francis Hopkinson and his son Joseph Hopkinson lived there, and their residences remain standing. Between 1817 and 1832, and again from 1837 to 1839, Joseph Bonaparte, the ex-king of Spain, resided on a magnificent estate called "Bonaparte's Park," where he hosted distinguished guests such as Lafayette. Due to a special law passed by the New Jersey legislature allowing Bonaparte to own real property as an alien, the state is said to have received the nickname "Spain." Other former residents include Prince Napoleon Lucien Charles Murat and Rear-Admiral Charles Stewart, who lived at the estate known as "Ironsides."
