# Mineral Resources and Asbestos Documentation

## Asbestos Research and Publications
Research regarding asbestos, specifically its occurrence, exploitation, and various uses, was documented by Fritz Cirkel in a 1905 publication based in Ottawa. Additional data on mineral resources can be found in the Annual Reports on Mineral Resources produced by the U.S. Geological Survey, featuring contributions from J.S. Diller and J.H. Pratt. Other relevant literature on the subject includes R.H. Jones's 1897 work, *Asbestos and Asbestic*, published in London, and G.P. Merrill's 1904 text, *The Non-metallic Minerals*, published in New York.

## Mineral Influence on Manufacturing
Mineral resources have served as a significant influence on the development of manufactures. By the year 1900, approximately 23.6% (nearly one-fourth) of manufactured products depended on minerals for raw materials. In the iron and steel industry, iron ore is provided by mines located in the Lake Superior region, while limestone and bituminous coal are supplied by deposits in Illinois.

## Coal Deposits in North America
The great central coal field of North America extends into Illinois from Indiana. Its boundaries reach as far north as a line from Rock Island to the northern boundary of Grundy county, west from Rock Island to Henderson county, and southwest to the southern part of Jackson county, before running south into Kentucky. This field covers more than three-fourths of the state's land surface, totaling 42,900 square miles.

Historical records indicate that Hennepin reported coal deposits near Ottawa on the Illinois in 1679. Mining activity was noted in 1810 on the Big Muddy river in Jackson county, and by 1833, 6,000 tons had been mined. By 1907, coal production occurred across 52 counties, with the highest yields coming from Madison, Macoupin, St Clair, Sangamon, and Williamson. In 1907, the total tonnage produced was 51,317,146, with a total product value of $54,687,882. By 1908, the value of coal produced in the state was exceeded only by Pennsylvania, which produced nearly six times as much. Additionally, nearly 30% of the coal mined in the state in 1907 was extracted using machinery.

## Petroleum Production in Illinois
Petroleum output in Illinois remained unimportant for a long period, with the first serious attempts to locate gas and oil occurring in the 1850s. In 1889, the yield was 1,460 barrels, but by 1902, it had dropped to 200 barrels. These 1902 yields came almost entirely from Washington in Tazewell county and Litchfield in Montgomery county, where commercial quantities of oil had been discovered in 1886; at that time, the oil was used for local lubrication. 

Drilling took place in Clark county in 1865, and the Westfield field was worked again in 1904. The state's total output saw a significant increase in the following years:
* 1905: 181,084 barrels
* 1906: 4,397,050 barrels (valued at $3,274,818)
* 1907: 24,281,973 barrels
