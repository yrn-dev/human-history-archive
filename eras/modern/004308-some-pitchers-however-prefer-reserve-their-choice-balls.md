# Pitching and the Battery in Base-Ball

In the game of base-ball, the pitcher is considered the most important member of the side. Together with the catcher, the pitcher forms a unit known as the battery. The pitcher's primary role is to deliver the ball to the batsman, a task performed by throwing either underhand or overhand.

## Pitching Mechanics and Regulations
To legally deliver the ball, the pitcher must maintain contact with one foot on the pitcher's plate. This white plate measures 24 inches in length and 6 inches in width and is positioned 60.5 feet from the back of the home-base. 

The history of pitching delivery has evolved over time. Prior to 1875, pitchers were required to use a full toss only. Around that period, a disguised underhand throw was introduced to increase pace and was subsequently legalized, followed by the adoption of the overhand throw.

## Ball Control and Strategy
Pitching strategy involves a combination of pace, delivery variation, and the exploitation of a batsman's idiosyncrasies or weaknesses. A critical component of this strategy is the use of curves, which are achieved through a movement of the wrist as the ball leaves the hand. This twist causes the ball to swerve due to air pressure. Common deliveries include:
* **The Out-curve:** The first curve invented; the ball travels toward the batsman and suddenly swerves toward the right (for a right-handed batsman).
* **The Incurve:** A delivery that shoots sharply to the left.
* **The Drop:** A downward swerving delivery.

Control is essential for the pitcher, as the umpire will call "balls" for deliveries that are not over the base, or are over the base but not positioned between the batsman's knee and shoulder. If four such balls are called, the batsman is entitled to become a base-runner and take first base.

Pitchers are subject to specific rules regarding their motions. For example, while in position, a pitcher may not make a motion to deliver the ball to the batsman without actually doing so, nor may they motion to first-base while a runner occupies that base without completing the throw.

## The Role of the Catcher
The catcher stands approximately 1 yard behind the home plate and must remain within 10 feet of the plate during the delivery. The catcher's duties include catching the ball before it hits the ground and preventing opposing players from stealing the next base. Because of these requirements, the catcher must be an accurate, strong, and quick thrower.

Due to the dangers of the position, the catcher wears a breast-pad, a mask, and a large glove.

## Coordination and Signalling
Because the catcher faces the entire field, they are in a position to warn the pitcher when to throw to a base to catch a runner "napping." The catcher typically directs the type of ball to be pitched—whether high, low, left, or right—through secret finger signals, allowing the catcher to be in the correct position to receive the ball. However, some pitchers prefer to reserve their choice of balls and perform the signalling themselves.
