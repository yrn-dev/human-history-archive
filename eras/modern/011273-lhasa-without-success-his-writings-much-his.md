# Lhasa

Lhasa is a city located on the great elevated table-land of Tibet. It is characterized by its "sacred walls" and "jealously-guarded portals," which for many years presented a significant challenge to foreign explorers attempting to enter the city.

## Exploration and Access
During the late 19th century, numerous European and American travelers attempted to reach Lhasa, often without success. Between 1886 and 1887, Pierre G. Bonvalot and Prince Henri d'Orleans crossed the Tibetan plateau from north to south but failed to enter the city. Similarly, the American traveler W.W. Rockhill attempted to reach Lhasa during his journeys from 1889 to 1891, though he was unsuccessful in entering. In 1891, Hamilton Bower traveled from Leh to Peking; while he failed to penetrate the portals of Lhasa, he was able to provide a significant addition to Tibetan mapping with the help of a native surveyor.

Between 1894 and 1895, St George Littledale made a second journey that brought him almost within sight of the city's sacred walls, yet he also failed to pass inside. It was not until 1904 that the mission of Sir Francis Younghusband to Lhasa resulted in the determination of the city's geographical position through an extension of the Indian system of triangulation.

In contrast to these failed attempts, native surveyors attached to the Indian survey were successful in visiting the city. Pundit Nain Singh followed a route from the eastern frontiers of Kashmir to the Tengri Nor lake and then to Lhasa in 1874-1875, where he remained for several months. Pundit Krishna also visited Lhasa during a remarkable journey between 1879 and 1882, which extended northwards through Tsaidam to Sachu in Mongolia. Krishna later passed through eastern Tibet to Darchendo on the high road between Lhasa and Peking before returning to the neighborhood of Lhasa and crossing the Himalayas via a westerly route.

## Landmarks and Culture
Lhasa is home to the Cho-khang, described as the grand Temple of Buddha. The city also serves as a terminus for the Tibetan express courier system. According to descriptions by "the Pandit," couriers travel from Gartokh to Lhasa—a distance of 800 miles—riding day and night. These messengers are subject to a strict protocol where their coats are sealed upon them; the seal is only officially broken upon their arrival at the terminus in Lhasa. The physical toll of this journey is severe, leaving messengers with bloodshot, sunken eyes, cracked faces, and bodies raw with vermin.

## Geography and Mineralogy
Lhasa is situated south of the great lakes of Tengri Nor. The region is known for its mineral deposits, specifically borates. Purer material known as *chu tsale* (water borax) is found at the lakes of Rudok and at the Tengri Nor lakes north of Lhasa. Historically, crude Tibetan mineral material was exported to Europe under the name *tincal* for the production of boron salts and pure borax.

## Related Varieties
The name Lhasa is also associated with the Lhasa terrier, which is categorized as a foreign dog breed.
