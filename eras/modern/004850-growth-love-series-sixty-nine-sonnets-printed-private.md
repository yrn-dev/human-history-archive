# The Growth of Love

## Overview
*The Growth of Love* is a literary work consisting of a series of sixty-nine sonnets. It was authored by the English poet Robert Bridges. The work was printed for private circulation in 1876 and 1889.

## Author Biography
Robert Bridges was born on October 23, 1844. His education took place at Eton and Corpus Christi College, Oxford. In addition to his literary pursuits, Bridges studied medicine in London at St Bartholomew's hospital. His medical career included serving as a physician at the Great Northern hospital and as an assistant physician at the Children's hospital, Great Ormond Street. He retired from medical practice in 1882 and married Mary, the daughter of R.A. Alfred Waterhouse, two years later.

## Literary Style and Theory
Bridges is noted for a poetic style characterized by precision, purity, restraint, and delicacy, while maintaining strength of expression. His work is recognized for embodying a distinct theory of prosody. Bridges argued that English prosody is dependent upon the number of "stresses" within a line rather than the number of syllables, asserting that poetry ought to follow the rules of natural speech.

Because his poetry was initially printed privately, it was slow to gain recognition beyond a small circle of admirers, though it exerted significant influence within that select group.

## Other Works by Robert Bridges
In addition to *The Growth of Love*, Bridges produced a wide variety of poetic, critical, and dramatic works:

*   **Critical Works:** He wrote *John Keats, a Critical Essay* (1895) and *Milton's Prosody* (1893), the latter of which combined two earlier essays from 1887 and 1889.
*   **Poetry:** His best work is found in *Shorter Poems* (1890). He also wrote *Prometheus* (privately printed in Oxford, 1883), a "mask in the Greek Manner," and *Eros and Psyche* (1885), which is a version of Apuleius. A complete six-volume edition of his *Poetical Works* was published between 1898 and 1905.
*   **Drama:** His dramatic output includes the historical tragedy *Nero* (1885), with a second part appearing in 1894; *Achilles in Scyros* (1890); *Palicio* (1890), a romantic drama written in the Elizabethan manner; and *The Return of Ulysses* (1890), a drama in five acts. He also wrote *The Christian Captives* (1890), a tragedy based on the same subject as Calderon's *El Principe Constante*, and *The Humours of the Court* (1893), a comedy based on Lope de Vega's *El Perro del hortelano* and Calderon's *El secreto á voces*.
*   **Other:** He wrote *The Feast of Bacchus* (1889), which was partly translated from Terence's *Heauton-Timoroumenos*, and *Hymns from the Yattendon Hymnal* (Oxford, 1899).
