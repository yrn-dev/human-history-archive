# Napoleon Eugene Louis Jean Joseph

## Early Life and Education
Napoleon Eugene Louis Jean Joseph, known as the Prince Imperial, was born in Paris on March 16, 1856. He was the only son of Empress Eugenie and Emperor Napoleon III. Described as a delicate boy during his youth, his life was significantly impacted by the war of 1870. During this conflict, his mother sent him to the army to help him win popularity, a move that led government journals to praise his bravery. Following the initial defeats of the war, the Prince was forced to flee France alongside the Empress. They settled in England at Chislehurst, where he completed his military education at Woolwich.

## Accession as Pretender
The political status of the Prince Imperial changed upon the death of his father on January 9, 1873. Following this event, the Imperialists proclaimed him Napoleon IV, and he became the official Pretender. In his personal nature, he was inactive; however, he was subject to the influence of two primary sources: his mother and the Bonapartist leaders in France.

## Military Ambitions and Death
The Bonapartist leaders and the Empress believed that the Prince should secure his crown through the acquisition of military prestige. Under this persuasion, he attached himself as a volunteer to the English expedition to Zululand in February 1879. This decision is characterized as a blunder that resulted in tragedy. On June 1, 1879, while conducting a reconnaissance with a small group of troopers, the Prince Imperial and his party were surprised by Zulus. The Prince was killed during the encounter. His body was subsequently returned to England and buried at Chislehurst.
