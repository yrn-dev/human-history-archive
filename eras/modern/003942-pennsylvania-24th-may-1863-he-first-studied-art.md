# Pennsylvania

Pennsylvania is a state in the United States that has served as the birthplace of various influential figures in the arts, architecture, and academia, and has hosted significant financial and industrial developments.

## Notable Figures in Art and Architecture
The state has produced several prominent artists and architects. George Grey Barnard, an American sculptor, was born in Bellefonte, Pennsylvania, on May 24, 1863. Barnard's education began at the Art Institute in Chicago before he spent twelve years in Paris, where he worked in the atelier of P. T. Cavelier from 1883 to 1887. He achieved success at the Salon of 1894 and created numerous works, including "The Boy" (1885), "Cain" (1886), "Brotherly Love" (also known as "Two Friends," 1887), and the allegorical "Two Natures" (1894), which is located in New York City's Metropolitan Museum. His other works include "The Hewer" (1902) in Cairo, Illinois, "Great God Pan" in New York City's Central Park, "Maidenhood," and the "Rose Maiden." Additionally, Barnard provided sculptural decorations for the state's new Capitol building in Harrisburg.

Charles Follen McKim (1847–1909) was an American architect born in Chester County, Pennsylvania, on August 24, 1847. The son of James Miller McKim—a prominent abolitionist and founder of the New York *Nation*—McKim studied at Harvard and the École des Beaux-Arts in Paris. He established a practice in New York City in 1872 and later formed the firm McKim, Mead & White in 1879 with William Rutherford Mead and Stanford White. McKim's architectural legacy includes the Columbia University buildings, the University Club in New York, additions to the White House in 1906, and the Boston Public Library. He was a founder of the American Academy in Rome and a National Academician.

## Academic and Intellectual Contributions
Pennsylvania has been a center for scholarship and higher education. Daniel Garrison Brinton (1837–1899), born in Thornbury, Pennsylvania, was a distinguished archaeologist and ethnologist. After serving as a Union army surgeon during the Civil War and practicing medicine in Westchester, Pennsylvania, he became a professor of ethnology and archaeology at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia in 1884. He later served as a professor of American linguistics and archaeology at the University of Pennsylvania from 1886 until his death. Brinton authored numerous works, including *The Myths of the New World* (1868) and *Religions of Primitive People* (1897), and edited the eight-volume *Library of American Aboriginal Literature*.

Other intellectuals associated with the state include Hiram Corson, a scholar born in Philadelphia who lectured on English literature there and later became a professor at Cornell University. In the field of philology, Francis Andrew March served as a professor at Lafayette College, where he established the first scientific study of English in an American college and chaired the Commission of the State of Pennsylvania on Amended Orthography.

## Financial Institutions and History
The state played a critical role in early American finance through the Bank of Pennsylvania and the Bank of North America. Authorized by congress on May 26, 1781, the Bank of North America was created by financier Robert Morris with a capital of $400,000. To facilitate this, Morris arranged for the Bank of Pennsylvania to take over foreign bills and pay its claims against the Federation. Due to uncertainties regarding the continental congress's power to charter a bank, the institution obtained a charter from the state of Pennsylvania. The bank began business on January 7, 1782, and helped resolve the chaos of Federation finances by loaning $1,249,975 to Morris. It operated under its state charter until 1863, when it was absorbed into the national banking system; it is regarded as the oldest organized banking institution in the United States.

## Industrial and Municipal Development
The borough of Dunmore, located in Lackawanna County and adjoining Scranton, exemplifies the state's industrial growth. First settled in 1783 and incorporated in 1862, Dunmore's expansion was accelerated in 1863 by the establishment of railway shops for the line from Pittston to Hawley, which had been built by the Pennsylvania Coal Company between 1849 and 1850. The borough's primary industry is the mining of anthracite coal, though it also produces silk, malt liquors, and stoves and furnaces. As of 1905, railway repair shops employed 48.9% of the borough's manufacturing wage-earners. Dunmore also serves as the seat of the state oral school for the deaf.
