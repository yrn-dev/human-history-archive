# Bass and Bast

The terms "bass" and "bast" refer to a diverse range of subjects across music, botany, zoology, industry, geology, and ancient geography.

## Music and Sound
In music, "bass" (a word identical to "base" and pronounced similarly, though influenced in spelling by the Italian *basso*) denotes something deep or low. It specifically refers to the lowest male voice, the lower part of a composition's harmony, or the lowest-pitched instrument within a specific class.

### Wind and Brass Instruments
The bass clarinet originated in Germany, with the first specimen created in 1793 by Heinrich Grenser of Dresden. Grenser was likely inspired by the tenor clarinet (basset horn) and his own expertise in fagottos. Early examples, such as one held in the Grossherzogliches Museum at Darmstadt, featured nine square-flapped brass keys. Other early specimens belonged to M. de Coussemaker and Adolphe Sax. In Italy, Nicolas Papalini of Pavia attempted to resolve mechanical issues by creating a serpentine bore pierced through pear-wood slabs.

In military bands, the bass and contrabass of the brass wind are known as the bombardon or bass tuba. The term bombardon is derived from the Italian *bombardone* (contrabass pommer), which served as the bass in medieval orchestras before the fagotto. The bombardon was the first bass wind instrument to utilize valves and was initially called the *clavicor*, *corno basso*, or *bass horn*. In Prussian Jager military bands, the keyed bass horn was introduced in 1829, followed by the bombardon (valve-ophicleide) in 1831. By 1835, the Guards replaced these with the bass tuba, invented by J.G. Moritz and W. Wieprecht.

### String Instruments
The double bass evolved from the bass viol, sharing a remote origin with the violin. Notable early constructors included Maggini and Gasparo da Salo; the latter created a three-stringed instrument for St Mark's in Venice. The double bass was the preferred concert instrument of Dragonetti, who used a straight bow held overhand. Giovanni Bottesini (1822-1889) is recognized as the world's greatest virtuoso of the instrument. Additionally, Praetorius documented a *gross contra-bass geige* (or sub-bass *viol da gamba*) that displaced other large contra-bass viols.

## Botany and Zoology
"Bast" (pronounced *b[)a]s*), a word of doubtful origin, refers to the fibrous bark of the lime tree. This material is used to create soft plaited baskets, mats, and for tying up plants in gardening. The American lime-tree (*Tilia Americana*) is known as basswood, while *T. heterophylla* is called white basswood. Separately, the name "bass" is applied to a type of fish that closely resembles the perch.

## Industry and Commerce
Bass is the name of a family of English brewers from Burton-on-Trent. The firm was founded by William Bass (born 1720), a former carrier. By 1777, Bass began brewing to meet the demand for Burton beer, initially securing a significant share of the Russian market. After the Russian government imposed a prohibitory duty on Burton ales in 1822, Bass expanded into the home market and London. 

The firm developed "Bass's pale ale," a lighter beer designed to withstand the Indian climate, which made the company the largest in Burton. The business was later managed by William's son, M. T. Bass, and grandson, Michael Thomas Bass (1799-1884). The firm expanded its trade to Ireland and north-west England following a shipwreck in the Irish Channel in 1827. Bass beer gained world-wide reputation after being supplied on draught at the 1851 Exhibition. In 1880, the business became a limited liability company.

## Geology and Geography
In geology, "bastite" (also known as schiller-spar) is an altered form of bronzite or enstatite. It is produced through hydration and has a composition similar to serpentine, appearing brown or green with a metallic sheen. A typical locality for this stone is Baste in the Radauthal, Harz, where it is embedded in darker serpentine.

In ancient geography, "Bast" (B;s-t) was an Egyptian city. The goddess Ubasti derived her name from this city, meaning "she of [the city] Bast." The city was later known as P-ubasti ("place of Ubasti"). The Greeks confused the names of the goddess and the city, calling both "Bubastis." Bubastis served as the capital of the 19th nome of Lower Egypt. Today, the site is represented by Tell Basta near Zagazig, which contains ruins of a large temple and monuments spanning from the thirtieth dynasty back to Khufu (Cheops).
