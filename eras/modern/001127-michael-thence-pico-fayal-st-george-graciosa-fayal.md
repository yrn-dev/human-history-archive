# The Azores

The Azores (or *Acores*), also known as the Western Islands, are a Portuguese archipelago located in the Atlantic Ocean. The islands are situated between 36° 55' and 39° 55' N. latitude and 25° and 31° 16' W. longitude, extending in an oblique line from the northwest to the southeast. With a total area of 922 square miles and a population of 256,291 as of 1900, the archipelago is the farthest island group in the Atlantic from any continent. The nearest continental land is Cape da Roca on the Portuguese coast, located 830 miles east of St Michael's. Despite this distance, the islands are generally regarded as part of Europe due to their European flora and climate.

## Geography and Physical Characteristics
The archipelago is divided into three widely severed groups that rise from depths exceeding 2.5 miles:
*   **South-eastern group:** Consists of St Michael's (Sao Miguel), St Mary (Santa Maria), and Formigas.
*   **Central group:** Consists of Fayal (Faial), Pico, St George (Sao Jorge), Terceira, and Graciosa.
*   **North-western group:** Consists of Flores and Corvo.

The islands generally feature an undulating, elevated outline with peaks and little to no tableland. The highest point in the archipelago is on Pico, reaching 7,612 feet above sea-level, while the lowest peak is on Corvo at 350 feet. The coastlines are typically high and precipitous, with harbors formed by open bays or inlets.

The islands are volcanic in nature, a fact confirmed by numerous eruptions and earthquakes. Basalt and scoria are the primary erupted materials. While Flores, Corvo, and Graciosa have been exempt from eruptions, and St Mary has escaped them entirely, other islands have seen significant activity. St Michael's has been the center of activity, experiencing a great eruption in 1444-1445 and a violent convulsion in 1522 that buried the then-capital, Villa Franca, and its 6,000 inhabitants. Other recorded eruptions occurred in Pico (1572), St George (1580), and Terceira (1614). Fayal suffered one eruption in 1672. Subterranean eruptions have also occurred, sometimes creating temporary islands, such as "Sabrina," which appeared in June 1811 near St Michael's.

## Administration and Infrastructure
The Azores are subdivided into three administrative districts named after their chief towns:
*   **Ponta Delgada:** The capital of St Michael's, this district includes St Michael's and St Mary.
*   **Angra (or Angra do Heroismo):** The capital of Terceira, this district includes Terceira, St George, and Graciosa.
*   **Horta:** The capital of Fayal, this district includes Pico, Fayal, Flores, and Corvo.

For military purposes, the islands are split into two commands headquartered at Ponta Delgada and Angra. In terms of political representation, Ponta Delgada returns four members to the parliament in Lisbon, while the other two districts return two members each. 

Communication is maintained via regular mail services and a cable system running from Lisbon to Villa Franca do Campo in St Michael's, then to Pico, Fayal, St George, and Graciosa. Fayal is further connected to Waterville, Ireland, by a cable laid in 1901. Meteorological stations are located at Ponta Delgada and Angra.

## Economy and Trade
The principal seaports are Ponta Delgada (population 17,620 in 1900), Angra (population 10,788 in 1900), and Horta (population 6,574 in 1900). Horta, located on the south-east coast of Fayal, serves as a hub for fisheries—specifically tunny, bonito, mullet, and whale—and exports sperm-oil, grain, wine, and fruit.

Trade, once a Portuguese monopoly, is now shared with Germany and the United Kingdom, with British vessels carrying much of the cargo. Key imports include coal from Great Britain, textiles from Portugal, and sugar from the United States, Madeira, and Germany. Exports consist of provisions, natural mineral waters, wine, and fruit. The pineapple trade is particularly significant; in 1902 and 1903, 940,000 pineapples grown under glass were exported to Hamburg and London.

Regional production varies by district:
*   **Ponta Delgada:** Cotton fabrics, pottery, tea, spirits, straw hats.
*   **Angra:** Soap, bricks, tiles, butter, cheese, woollen and linen goods.
*   **Horta:** Ornamental articles made from osier, straw, and the pith of dried fig-wood, as well as mats and baskets.

## Population and Culture
The population is primarily of Portuguese origin, with Flemish and Moorish strains. There is a high birth rate and low infant mortality. While illiteracy is high among the poorer classes and elderly, education is spreading, often through the custom of sending children to state schools in the United States. Significant immigrant populations of English, Scottish, Irish, mulattoes, and Negroes reside on the islands, particularly in St Michael's and Fayal. As of 1900, there were 1,490 resident foreigners. The majority of the population follows Roman Catholicism, and Angra serves as an episcopal see.

## History
Knowledge of the islands among ancient Greeks and Romans is not evident, though Carthaginian coins found on Corvo suggest they may have been visited. Arabian geographers in the 12th and 14th centuries, including Edrisi and Ibn-al-Wardi, described nine islands in the Western Ocean that likely correspond to the Azores. These writers noted the islands were once populous but reduced by internal warfare.

The islands appeared on a 1351 map, where the southern group was called the Goat Islands (*Cabreras*), the middle group the Wind or Dove Islands (*De Ventura sive de Columbis*), and the western group the Brazil Island (*De Brazi*). By 1375, a Catalan map identified Corvo as *Corvi Marini*, Flores as *Li Conigi*, and St George as *San Zorze*.

Portuguese discovery is associated with the navigator Gonzalo Velho Cabral, sent by the court of Lisbon after van der Berg was driven to the islands in 1432. Another account suggests Prince Henry the Navigator sent Cabral based on an existing map. In the late 15th century, the geographer Martin Behaim resided at Fayal, where his father-in-law, Jobst van Huerter, governed a Flemish colony.
