# Racine and the Journey to Italy

The archive's records contain disparate references to the name Racine, including a celebrated dramatist and a specific theatrical work.

## The Play "Racine"
In 1827, a one-act verse comedy titled *Racine* was produced at the Théâtre Français. This work was a collaboration between Philippe Busoni and Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux. Brizeux, a French poet born in Lorient on September 12, 1803, was educated in law and came from a family of Irish origin settled in Brittany.

## Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux and Italy
Following the production of the comedy *Racine*, Julien Auguste Pélage Brizeux undertook a journey to Italy in the company of Auguste Barbier. This experience made a great impression on Brizeux. He later made a second visit to Italy in 1834, which eventually led to the 1841 publication of a complete translation of the *Divina Commedia* written in *terza rima*. 

The influence of Italy is further evident in Brizeux's other works; he included poems written under Italian influence in *Les Ternaires* (1841) and *Primel el Nola* (1852). In contrast to these Italian-influenced works, Brizeux also focused on his native province of Brittany, writing the rustic idyl *Marie* (1836) regarding country life, *Les Bretons* (1845) based on legends and folklore, and *Telen-Aroor* (1844), which utilized the Breton dialect.

## The Dramatist Racine
Separate from the 19th-century play, the archive records a "rising star" dramatist named Racine. He was a constant friend to Moliere and participated in literary discussions at the *Pomme du Pin* and the *Moulon Blanc* alongside Moliere, Boileau, Chapelle, and Antoine Furetiere. 

Racine's professional relationship with other writers of his era was marked by mutual respect and rivalry. He provided a "noble and most just tribute of eulogy" to Pierre Corneille during the Academical reception of Thomas Corneille. Boileau, while acknowledging Racine as an "extremely clever fellow," claimed to have taught him the art of elaborate rhyming (*rimer difficilement*). While Racine's reputation grew during his time, it was the 18th century that eventually exalted him above Corneille.
