# Agenor Bardoux

Agenor Bardoux (1820–1897) was a French statesman and author born in Bourges. He began his professional life as an advocate in Clermont, where he openly proclaimed his republican sympathies.

## Political Career and the National Assembly
Bardoux entered national politics in 1871 when he was elected as a deputy of the National Assembly. He maintained his seat through subsequent elections, being re-elected in both 1876 and 1877. During his time in the chamber, he served as the president of the group of the left centre. In this capacity, he was a strong proponent of the republic, though he remained opposed to anti-clericalism.

Following the *coup d'etat* of May 16, Bardoux emerged as one of the leaders of the "363." In the republican chamber that was elected after the events of May 16, he was appointed minister of public instruction in December 1877. During his tenure as minister, he proposed several republican laws, with a particular focus on compulsory primary education.

## Later Public Life and Senate
Bardoux resigned from his ministerial position in 1879. Although he was not re-elected in 1881, he returned to public service in December 1882, when he was named a senator for life.

## Literary Works
In addition to his political activities, Bardoux authored several essays and publications regarding French society and politics. His written works include:
* *Les Legistes et leur influence sur la societe francaise* (1878)
* *Le Comte de Montlosier et le Gallicanisme* (1881)
* *Dix Annees de vie politique* (1882)
