# Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie

The *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie* is a philosophical publication associated with the academic and professional activities of the German philosopher Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius.

## Editorial History and Association
Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius (1843–1896) served as the first editor of the *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie*. Avenarius was a German philosopher born in Paris on November 19, 1843. He pursued his education in Berlin and Zuerich before completing his studies at the university of Leipzig, where he graduated in 1876. During his time at Leipzig, Avenarius was also one of the founders of the *Akademisch-philosophische Verein*.

## Related Philosophical Contributions
The publication of the *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie* exists within the context of Avenarius's broader philosophical career. In 1868, Avenarius published an essay concerning the Pantheism of Spinoza (specifically titled *Uber die beiden ersten Phasen des spinozischen Pantheismus*). 

His primary works include *Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten Kraftmasses*, published in 1876, and the *Kritik der reinen Erfahrung*, published between 1888 and 1890. In these texts, Avenarius attempted to coordinate action and thought. Drawing on the principle of economy of thinking, similar to the approach of Mach, he used the *Kritik* to explain the relationship between environment, knowledge, and pure experience. He concluded that pure experience is constituted by statements that are dependent upon environment. This specific philosophical framework is known as Empirio-criticism, which is characterized as an idealistic dualism rather than materialism or realism.

## Academic Context
Following his graduation from Leipzig, Avenarius moved to Zuerich, where he became a professor of philosophy in 1877. He remained in this position until his death on August 18, 1896. The scholarly discourse surrounding his work and the philosophy of Empirio-criticism has been documented in various sources, including the *Zeitsch. f. wiss. Philos.* (specifically volumes xx and xxii), as well as in J. Petzoldt's *Einfuehrung in d. Philos. d. reinen Erfahrung* (1900) and Wundt's *Philos. Stud.* xiii (1897).
