# Ayrshire: History and Political Sympathies (1660–1688)

The history of Ayrshire is marked by a progression of occupations and conflicts, ranging from early Roman presence to the political and religious upheavals of the 17th century.

## Early History and Medieval Period
Traces of Roman occupation exist within Ayrshire. During the campaigns of Agricola, the region was held by the Damnonii. Their town, Vandogara, has been identified as a site near Darvel at Loudoun Hill, where a serious encounter with the Scots occurred. Following the Roman withdrawal, Ayrshire became part of the kingdom of Strathclyde and eventually came under the control of the Northumbrian kings.

The historical record for the region is largely silent until 1263, with the exception of occasional intertribal conflicts, such as the 9th-century slaying of the Scottish king Alpin at Dalmellington. In 1263, the Scots under Alexander III crushed the pretensions of Haakon of Norway regarding the sovereignty of the Isles at the battle of Largs. A generation later, William Wallace conducted a vigorous campaign in the shire, which included burning the barns of Ayr where the forces of Edward I were lodged and surprising the English garrison at Ardrossan. Robert Bruce, who is alleged to have been born at Turnberry Castle, defeated the English at Loudoun Hill in 1307.

## Later Conflicts and the Covenanters
In later years, the county was visited by Cromwell, who demolished Ardrossan castle and is said to have used its stones to build a fort at Ayr. 

Between 1660 and 1688, the sympathies of the county were almost wholly aligned with the Covenanters. This period was characterized by significant hardship; the county was dragooned and ravaged by the Highland host. The Covenanters suffered one of their heaviest reverses at Airds Moss, a morass located between the Lugar and the Ayr. During this encounter on July 20, 1680, their leader, Richard Cameron, was killed. Following this era, the Hanoverian succession evoked no enthusiasm, though it did not excite active hostility.

## Antiquities and Landmarks
Ayrshire contains various antiquarian remains and architectural sites:
*   **Roman and Early Works:** A road attributed to the Romans extended from Ayr through Dalmellington and Dalrymple toward the Solway.
*   **Camps and Cairns:** There are cairns in Sorn and Galston, and camps on the hills of Dundonald and Knockgeorgan attributed to the Danes or Norwegians.
*   **Castles:** Notable castles include Dunure, Ardrossan, Portencross, Dundonald, Turnberry, and Loch Doon.
*   **Religious Ruins:** The county features ruins of celebrated abbeys at Crossraguel and Kilwinning, as well as the haunted church of Alloway.
