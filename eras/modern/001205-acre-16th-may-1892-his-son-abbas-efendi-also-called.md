# The Babi Movement and Associated Figures

## History of the Babis
The Babi movement is characterized by a history full of incident, though it covered a relatively short period. The movement's founder, the Bab, spent the majority of his career between May 1844 and July 1850 in captivity. He was held first at Shiraz, followed by Maku, and finally at Chihriq. Despite his imprisonment, his disciples maintained an active propaganda effort. This activity led to several serious revolts against the government, particularly following the death of Muhammad Shah in September 1848.

One of the first risings occurred from December 1848 to July 1849 in Mazandaran at the ruined shrine of Shaykh Tabarsi, near Barfurush. Led by Mulla Husayn of Bushrawayh—identified as the first who believed—and Mulla Muhammad 'Ali of Barfurush, the Babis resisted the shah's troops for seven months before being subdued and killed. Another revolt took place in the north-west of Persia at Zanjan, headed by Mulla Muhammad 'Ali Zanjani, which lasted from May to December 1850. Simultaneously, Aga Sayyid Yahya of Niriz led a less protracted struggle against the government at Niriz in Fars.

While these revolts were ongoing, the Bab was moved from Chihriq to Tabriz, where he was publicly shot in front of the citadel (arg). After his body was exposed for several days, it was recovered by the Babis and taken to a shrine near Tehran. The body was eventually moved to Acre in Syria, where it is currently buried.

## Persecutions and Later Events
Following a two-year period of relative quiet, a new wave of persecution began after August 15, 1852, when three Babis independently attempted to assassinate Nasiru'd-Din Shah as he returned to his palace at Niyavaran. Although the attempt failed, it resulted in the execution of approximately thirty Babis in Tehran on August 31, 1852, conducted with atrocious cruelty. Among the victims were the talented poetess Qurratu'l-'Ayn and Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan.

## Literature and Bibliography
The literature of the sect is voluminous, though it exists mostly in manuscript form. Rare manuscripts are preserved in European public collections in Paris (Bibliotheque Nationale), London (British Museum), and St Petersburg. The Paris collection includes manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, such as the history written by the Bab's contemporary, Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan.

Key works and authors associated with the movement include:
*   **Baha**: His most important work is the *Kitab-i-Aqdas*, which was edited with a Russian translation and introduction by Captain A. G. Toumansky in 1899.
*   **'Abbas Efendi**: The son of Baha, who composed the Persian work *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*. This work was edited, translated, and annotated by E. G. Browne and published in 1891.
*   **Mirza Muhammad Mahdi Khan (Za'imu'd-Duwla)**: A Persian who wrote a well-informed, though unsympathetic, Arabic history of the Babis, printed in Cairo in A.D. 1903-1904.

Other scholarly contributions include works by E. G. Browne, such as *The New History of the Bab* (1893), and Andreas's *Die Babi's in Persien* (1896). Baron Victor Rosen and Captain A. G. Toumansky also produced significant research and catalogues of Babi manuscripts.

## English-Language Works for American Converts
Several works were composed in English specifically for converts in America:
*   *Baha'u'llah (The Glory of God)*: Written by Ibrahim Khayru'llah with assistance from Howard MacNutt (1900).
*   *The Three Questions* and *Facts for Bahaists* (1901): Also by Ibrahim Khayru'llah.
*   *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi*: Written by Myron H. Phelps with a preface by E. G. Browne (1903).
*   *The Revelations of Baha'u'llah, in a Sequence of Four Lessons*: Written by Isabella Brittingham (1902).
*   *Some Answered Questions Collected*: Written by Laura Clifford Burney.
