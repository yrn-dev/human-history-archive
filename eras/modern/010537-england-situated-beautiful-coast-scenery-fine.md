# England

England is a country characterized by a diverse physical landscape, ranging from the high moorlands of the north and west to the precipitous coastlines of the south. Its geography includes significant river systems, varied hill scenery, and numerous market towns and resorts.

## Regional Geography and Scenery

### Derbyshire
Located in the north midlands, Derbyshire is bounded by Yorkshire to the north and northeast, Nottinghamshire to the east, Leicestershire to the southeast and south, Staffordshire to the south and southwest, and Cheshire to the west and northwest. The county covers an area of 1029.5 sq. m. Its physical aspect is highly diversified; while the extreme south is mostly level and lacks picturesqueness, the Peak District in the north offers some of the finest hill scenery in England. This beauty is derived from richly wooded glens and valleys, including Millersdale, Glossop, Castleton, and Dovedale. The landscape varies from the grey rocks of the Peak and wild moorland on the Cheshire borders to the woods and park lands of the Chatsworth district. Notably, the woods at Kedleston, located 3 m. from Derby, contain some of the largest and oldest oaks in the kingdom.

### Cornwall
Cornwall is a great promontory extending 75 m. from N.N.E. to S.S.W., with a maximum breadth of 45 m. at its junction with Devonshire, where the river Tamar forms much of the boundary. The scenery is marked by contrast. The highest point in the county is Brown Willy, reaching 1375 ft. west of Launceston. This area is picturesque and broken, featuring boulders and rough hills known as *tors*, such as the Cheese-wring, a rock pile resembling an inverted pyramid north of Liskeard. Much of this region consists of heather and furze. Further east, between Launceston and Mount's Bay, the land is described as the "dreariest strip of earth traversed by any English high road." The promontory ends in the Lizard, an area known as the Meneage or "rocky country." The interior is broken by long, well-wooded combes and valleys that descend to the coast.

## Hydrography and Coastal Features

### Rivers and Estuaries
In Derbyshire, the principal river is the Trent, which rises in the Staffordshire moorlands and forms part of the boundary with Leicestershire. Other significant rivers include the Derwent, which flows through a beautiful valley from the High Peak, and the Wye, which traverses Monsal Dale and Millersdale. The Dove serves as the boundary stream between Derbyshire and Staffordshire for nearly its entire course.

Cornwall's coast is defined by several fine estuaries. On the south coast, these include the Tamar, the Looe and Fowey rivers, the Helford river, and Falmouth Harbour, which is the most important purely Cornish inlet and can accommodate the largest vessels. On the north coast, the Hayle and Camel estuaries open into St Ives Bay and Padstow Bay, respectively.

### Coastal Towns
Lyme Regis, located in the western parliamentary division of Dorsetshire, is a municipal borough and watering-place situated at the mouth of a narrow valley. It features a fine precipitous coastline and a sandy shore. The town is known for its curved pier of ancient foundation called the Cobb.

## Notable Towns and Settlements

### Ashbourne
Ashbourne is a market town in Derbyshire, situated 13 m. W.N.W. of Derby on rising ground between two small valleys. It is located near the scenery of Dovedale. The town is known for its manufacture of corsets and a large agricultural trade. Its church, St Oswald, is an Early English cruciform building with a lofty octagonal spire. Ashbourne Hall is an ancient mansion associated with "Prince Charlie," who stayed there before and after his 1745 advance on Derby. The town also has connections to Dr Johnson, who visited his friend Dr Taylor there.

### Ilkley
Located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, 16 m. N.W. from Leeds, Ilkley is an urban district situated in the upper Wharfe valley. It is a favorite health resort due to its bracing high moorland air and fine scenery. The area is home to several hydropathic establishments, including those at Ben Rhydding. Local landmarks include the remains of Bolton Abbey and the church of All Saints. Historically, Ilkley is identified as the *Olicana* of Ptolemy, a town of the Brigantes tribe, and once housed a Roman fort.
