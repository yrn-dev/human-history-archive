# Young Czechs

The Young Czechs were a political party and movement within the Czech community of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, characterized by their impatience for national recognition and their opposition to the more conservative elements of Czech politics.

## Origins and Ideology
The Young Czechs emerged as a new party that broke away from the established leadership of the Czechs. They were generally known as Radicals and were defined by their dislike of alliances with the clergy and the aristocracy. In contrast to the Old Czechs, who maintained a policy of passive opposition and an alliance with the Feudalists, the Young Czechs recalled Hussite traditions and advocated for universal suffrage. Their political strategy involved taking their seats in the diet and collaborating with Germans to achieve political reform.

The early conflict between the two factions was marked by intense internal struggle. Led by figures such as Gregr and Stadkowsky, twenty-seven members of the diet resigned their seats after being outvoted in the Czech Club. Although they were initially defeated in subsequent elections, the Young Czechs and Old Czechs spent the following four years largely occupied with opposing one another.

## Political Struggle and the Bohemian Kingdom
The primary objective of the Czechs was the recognition of the Bohemian kingdom. While older party leaders may have been satisfied with their existing influence, the Young Czechs were more impatient for this recognition. 

In 1890, the government under Taaffe attempted to reconcile opposing parties through conferences in Vienna involving the German party, the Feudal party, and the Czechs. An agreement was reached regarding a revision of the electoral law and a separation between German and Czech districts. However, the Young Czechs were not invited to participate in these conferences. Consequently, they did not consider themselves bound by the decisions and utilized obstruction to oppose the resulting bills, arguing that the measures threatened the unity of the Bohemian country. This period of friction culminated in the elections of 1891, in which the Young Czechs were completely victorious over the Old Czechs.

## Parliamentary Influence and Language Ordinances
The Young Czechs became a significant force in the parliamentary landscape. In 1897, they held 60 seats, while the Radical Young Czechs held one. By 1901, the Young Czechs held 53 seats, and the Radical Young Czechs increased to four.

During the administration of Badeni, the government sought support from the nationalities, specifically the Poles and the Young Czechs. While some members of the Young Czechs remained "wilder spirits," the moderate wing was prepared to negotiate to gain influence over the administration. Their primary demand was further concessions regarding language in Bohemia. 

This led to the language ordinances of 1897, which mandated that:
1. All correspondence and documents brought before government officials in Bohemia be conducted in the language in which they were first introduced, effectively introducing Czech into government offices throughout the kingdom.
2. After 1903, any person appointed to a government post in Bohemia must pass an examination in Czech.

These ordinances caused severe backlash from German Nationalists and Radicals, who resorted to obstruction and motions of impeachment, eventually leading to the proroguing of parliament in June.

## Later Parliamentary Activity
In the early 20th century, the Czechs continued to clash with German parties over the administration of Bohemia. Under Dr. von Koerber, attempts were made to divide Bohemia into ten districts (five Czech, three German, and two mixed). The Czechs rejected this, demanding that the Czech and German languages be placed on an equal footing throughout Bohemia for all official purposes. 

When these demands were refused by the Germans, the Czechs employed aggressive tactics to block business, including a "pile of urgency motions" and noisy obstruction; one parliamentary sitting lasted 57 hours without interruption. This aggressiveness prompted various German parties—including the Christian Socialists, German Populists, German Progressists, and Constitutional Landed Proprietors—to form a joint executive committee to protect German racial interests.

## Electoral Structure
The Young Czechs operated within a racialized electoral system where constituencies and registers were divided by race. In mixed districts, electors could only vote for candidates of their own race. This system ensured that while there could be victories of Young Czechs over Czech Radicals, there could be no victory of a Czech candidate over a German candidate.
