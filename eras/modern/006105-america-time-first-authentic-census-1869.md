# America

The historical record of America encompasses a diverse array of developments across North and South America, ranging from the establishment of colonial settlements and the evolution of electoral systems to the growth of major urban centers and the activities of maritime adventurers.

## Urban Development and Population
In South America, the city of Buenos Aires experienced rapid growth following the establishment of settled conditions and its subsequent federalization. By the time of its first authentic census in 1869, the city had a population of 177,767. This population grew to an estimated 404,000 by 1887, before the annexation of the suburbs of Flores and Belgrano, which added another 28,000 people. Subsequent censuses recorded a population of 663,854 in 1895 and 950,891 in 1904. By the end of 1905, the national statistical office estimated the population at 1,025,653. At that time, the city contained approximately one-fifth of the total population of the republic.

## Colonial Settlements and Regional History
In North America, the city of Bangor's authentic history began in 1769 with the arrival of its first settlers. Initially called Conduskeag and briefly known locally as Sunbury, the town was incorporated in 1791 and renamed Bangor after a hymn-tune favored by the first pastor, Rev. Seth Noble. Bangor was chartered as a city in 1834 and became a site of early industrial milestones, including the state's first railway (completed in 1836 to Old Town) and the first electric street-railway in Maine in 1889. Additionally, one of the first iron steamships built in America, named "Bangor," operated in this port.

In Central America, British Honduras (formerly known as Balize or Belize) existed as a British crown colony. Bounded by Guatemala to the south and west, the Mexican province of Yucatan to the north and northwest, and the Caribbean Sea's Bay of Honduras to the east and northeast, the colony had an area of 7,562 square miles and a population of 40,372 in 1905. Its frontiers were defined by conventions in 1859 and 1893, running from the mouth of the river Sarstoon or Sarstun, through the rapids of Gracias à Dios, to Garbutt's Rapids on the Belize river, and then north to the Mexican frontier along the river Hondo.

## Electoral Systems and Voting
The evolution of voting in America saw a transition from *viva voce* (oral) voting to the use of ballots. By 1775, ballots were utilized in Pennsylvania, Delaware, North Carolina, South Carolina, and the New England states. New Jersey adopted them in 1776, and New York in 1778. At the time the United States constitution was adopted, *viva voce* voting remained the standard only in Georgia, Virginia, and Maryland.

Among states that entered the Union later, Arkansas, Missouri, Kentucky, and Illinois initially lacked ballot systems. Maryland, Georgia, and Arkansas (1846) adopted the ballot in the first half of the 19th century, followed by Illinois in 1848. Missouri generally adopted the system in 1863, and Virginia did not provide for voting by ballot until 1869. Early ballots were either written by voters or provided by candidates. Later, political committees prepared ballot-tickets for party-lists, which made voting mixed tickets difficult and increased the likelihood of bribery and intimidation due to a lack of secrecy.

## Maritime and Cultural History
The history of the West Indies is marked by the presence of buccaneers. Various accounts of these figures exist, including the 1678 work *De Americaensche Zee Roovers* by Exquemelin (translated as *The Bucaniers of America*), and works by Dampier, Raynal, and Lionel Wafer. Captain James Burney's *History of the Buccaneers of America* (1816) is cited as a really authentic narrative.

Culturally, America was a destination for international figures and religious movements. The Italian opera-singer Giulia Grisi toured America in 1854 with Mario. In the realm of religion, Baptist churches were formed in Roman Catholic countries through missionaries from America and England, with work beginning in France in 1832. Additionally, the English writer Benjamin Brierley visited America in 1880 and 1884, later publishing popular sketches titled *Ab-o'-th'-Yate* regarding his experiences.
