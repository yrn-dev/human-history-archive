# Père Charlevoix

Père Charlevoix was a writer and author of several historical and descriptive works, most notably focusing on the regions of North America and the history of New France. His writings have served as sources for later historians and researchers, though their reliability has been a subject of academic debate.

## Works on North America and New France
Charlevoix authored the *Histoire et description générale de la Nouvelle France* (also referred to as *History and General Description of New France*). This work is described as a famous old text, though some modern assessments characterize it as negligible. A six-volume edition of this work was edited by J. G. Shea and published in New York in 1866. Additionally, Charlevoix is the author of a *Journal* and a work titled *Histoire du Canada*.

His observations on the indigenous peoples of North America included notes on their social behaviors. According to Dr. Ferguson, Charlevoix observed that the nations he traveled among in North America did not associate acts of kindness or generosity with a notion of duty. Instead, these individuals acted based on appetite or affection without considering the consequences. Once a kindness was performed, the desire was gratified, and the event was removed from memory. This behavior was compared to observations made by Tacitus regarding the ancient Germans, where gifts were given and received without creating a sense of obligation, except when used to seal a treaty or a bargain.

## Influence and Academic Reception
The writings of Père Charlevoix have had a significant impact on subsequent historical narratives, sometimes through unauthorized paraphrasing. Professor E. G. Bourne, writing in the *American Historical Review* in 1906, demonstrated that a large portion of the alleged narrative in the *Travels* of Carver was actually a close paraphrase of Charlevoix's *Journal*, along with works by James Adair and La Hontan. Bourne theorized that the work was likely produced by Dr. John Coakley Lettsom, who had an intimate relationship with Carver.

Regarding the historical accuracy of his accounts, some sources suggest that statements found in Charlevoix's works, along with those of other authors such as Dampier, Raynal, and Exquemelin, should be received with caution.

## Commentary on Religion and Japan
Beyond his work in the Americas, Charlevoix provided commentary on religious history in Japan. He referred to a specific "unfortunate statement" regarding a Spanish galleon incident, noting that this statement caused a wound to religion that continued to bleed a century and a half later. This incident contributed to the anger of Hideyoshi, who viewed foreign doctors and others as "serpents" cherished in his bosom and traitors who sought to make enemies of him among his own family and subjects.
