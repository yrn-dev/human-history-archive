# Turenne and the Great Commanders of the Age of Louis XIV

The military history of the reign of Louis XIV was characterized by the emergence of a new method of military organization and training, serving as the first real test of the standing army created by Louvois. This era was defined by the leadership of several prominent commanders, most notably Turenne and Condé, whose careers spanned numerous conflicts across Europe.

## The Career of Turenne

Turenne is described as possessing a profound military genius, and his leadership was central to the direction of the French army during several major campaigns. In the war between France and Spain (1654–59), known as the Spanish Fronde, Turenne led the French and English forces to a notable victory at the battle of the Dunes on June 14, 1658. This victory brought Dunkirk into French hands. Turenne also served as the chief French commander during the War of Devolution (1667–68), which consisted primarily of sieges in the Low Countries.

During the first of the three great wars of Louis XIV (1672–78), Turenne's role was pivotal. In 1672, he was dispatched to Westphalia, where his skill checked the advance of the Brandenburgers under the Great Elector and the Imperialists under Montecucculi. Turenne conducted a severe winter campaign, defeating the elector in combat and manoeuvre and forcing him back to the Weser, which led to a separate peace on June 6, 1673.

In 1674, Turenne began a Rhine campaign with an invasion of Germany to prevent interference with Louis XIV's operations in Franche-Comte. On June 16, 1674, he achieved a complete victory over the inferior forces of Count Caprara at Sinsheim. Later that year, following an indecisive battle at Enzheim on October 4, Turenne retired to the camp of Dettweiler to prepare for a winter campaign in Alsace, regarded as the most brilliant of his career.

Turenne's final campaign took place in 1675 on the Rhine against Montecucculi. The two commanders manoeuvred between Philipsburg and Strassburg, each attempting to live off the enemy's land while protecting their own. Turenne eventually gained the advantage on the Sasbach, but while opening the action on July 27, 1675, he was killed by a cannon-shot.

## Condé and Other Military Leaders

Alongside Turenne, Condé was a primary figure in the French military establishment. He commanded the Spaniards during the battle of the Dunes in 1658 and later led the rapid conquest of Franche-Comte in 1668. In 1674, Condé operated in the Spanish Low Countries, where he opposed a combined army of Austrians, Dutch, and Spaniards under William. This period was marked by the battle of Seneffe on August 11, 1674, a desperate and indecisive engagement that served as Condé's last battle and William's first.

Other significant figures of the age included:
*   **Vauban:** A unique officer of his time who provided the genius behind the reduction of Besancon in nine days and directed the capture of Maastricht in June 1673. He retired as a marshal twenty-five years after the peace of Nijmwegen.
*   **Luxemburg and Crequi:** Described as worthy successors to Turenne and Condé, both marshals increased their reputations during the "Reunion War" of 1680–84. Crequi died in 1684 at age sixty-one.
*   **Marshal Schomberg:** He conducted a successful campaign against the Spaniards in Roussillon and invaded Catalonia during the 1675 campaign.
*   **Victor Maurice, Comte de Broglie:** Serving under Condé, Turenne, and other great commanders of the era, he rose through the ranks to become *maréchal de camp* in 1676, lieutenant-general in 1688, and marshal of France in 1724.

## Impact and Legacy

The effectiveness of the French army during this period was heavily dependent on the personality of its commanders; the death of Turenne illustrated this dependency. By the conclusion of the war of 1672–78, the French army had firmly established its reputation. The peace of Nijmwegen subsequently granted Louis XIV many of the frontier fortresses of the Netherlands.

The era also saw the rise of other military figures, such as Churchill. In June 1672, Churchill served as a captain under the duke of York and attracted the attention of Turenne. Turenne predicted Churchill's ultimate rise to distinction after his success at the siege of Nimeguen.
