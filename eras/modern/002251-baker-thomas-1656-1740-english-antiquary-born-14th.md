# BAKER, THOMAS (1656-1740)

Thomas Baker was an English antiquary born on September 14, 1656, in Lanchester, Durham. He was the grandson of Colonel Baker of Crook, Durham, who achieved fame during the civil war for his defense of Newcastle against the Scots.

## Education and Early Career
Baker received his early education at the free school in Durham. In 1672, he proceeded to St John's College, Cambridge, where he eventually obtained a fellowship. His professional career in the church began in 1687 when Lord Crew, the bishop of Durham, collated him to the rectory of Long-Newton within his diocese. While the bishop had intended to grant him the rectory of Sedgefield along with a prebend, Baker incurred the bishop's displeasure by refusing to read the Declaration of Indulgence issued by James II. Consequently, the bishop disgraced him for this refusal.

## Political and Academic Struggles
Baker's relationship with the monarchy remained complex. Although he had opposed James II, he refused to take the oaths to King William. This led to his resignation from the rectory of Long-Newton on August 1, 1690. Following his resignation, he retired to St John's College, where he remained protected until January 20, 1716-1717, at which time he and twenty-one other individuals were deprived of their fellowships.

Later in life, Baker faced further financial and legal difficulties. Following the passing of the Registering Act in 1723, he refused to comply with the act's requirements to register his annuity of L40. This annuity, provided by his father, combined with L20 per annum from the collieries of his elder brother, constituted his entire subsistence.

## Later Life and Legacy
Baker maintained a persistent sense of the injuries he had suffered throughout his career. He expressed this by inscribing himself as *rector ejectus* or *socius ejectus* in his own books and in the volumes he donated to the college library. He continued to live at the college as a commoner-master until his sudden death from apoplexy on July 2, 1740. Upon his death, he bequeathed his manuscripts and valuable books to the university.

## Published Works
Baker published only two works, both of which were released without his name:
* A preface to Bishop Fisher's *Funeral Sermon for Margaret, Countess of Richmond and Derby* (1708).
* *Reflections on Learning, showing the Insufficiency thereof in its several particulars, in order to evince the usefulness and necessity of Revelation* (1709-1710).
