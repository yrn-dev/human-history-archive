# Trinidad and Tobago

## Demographics and Population
Trinidad and Tobago is located within the West Indies, a region that includes seventeen islands or groups of islands as well as the continental colonies of Honduras and British Guiana. The total coloured population of the West Indies is approximately 1,912,655. Within this group, Trinidad and Tobago is noted as one of the colonies with the largest coloured populations, consisting of 250,000 people identified as black and brown.

## Regional Classification
In the study of the Neotropical region, different suggestions have been made regarding its subdivision. According to the suggestion by Newton, the region is divided into the Patagonian, Columbian, and Antillean subregions. While the Antillean subregion generally encompasses the islands of the area, Trinidad and Tobago is listed as an exception to this classification, along with islands situated on the northern coast of South America.

## Political and Legal History
The islands have a history of shifting colonial possession. Tobago was acquired via cession in 1763, though it later came under French possession before being reconquered in 1803. Trinidad was acquired through capitulation in 1797.

In terms of governance and jurisprudence, the law in Trinidad and Tobago is modelled on the English pattern. This legal alignment is shared with other territories such as Jamaica, British Guiana, Grenada, Gambia, Hong Kong, Mauritius, and the Straits Settlements.
