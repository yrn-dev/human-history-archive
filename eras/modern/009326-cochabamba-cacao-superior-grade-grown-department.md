# Cochabamba

Cochabamba is a department of the Bolivian republic. According to the census of 1900, the department encompasses an area of 23,328 square miles and has a population of 328,163. Its capital is the city of Cochabamba, which has a population of 21,886.

## Administrative and Political Status
The republic is organized into eight departments and one territory. Cochabamba is one of these eight departments. The city of Cochabamba is one of four provincial capitals—alongside Oruro, La Paz, and Sucre (Chuquisaca)—that have served as the national capital of the republic.

## Agriculture and Natural Resources
The department is located in a region where the agricultural resources are of great value, though their development has been described as slow and hesitating. The warm Andean valleys of Cochabamba are specifically noted for the production of cacao of a superior grade. Additionally, the warm regions of the east, which include the *yungas* valleys of Cochabamba, produce tobacco of a fair quality.

Cochabamba is also a site of extensive cultivation for coca, a plant highly esteemed by the natives who masticate the leaf. Coca is produced in the department for both local use and as an article of export for medicinal purposes.

## Urban Centers and Commerce
Beyond the capital city, the department contains other important trading centres, specifically the towns of Tarata, which has a population of 4,681, and Totora, with a population of 3,501.

## Education and Religion
Cochabamba is home to a university that provides instruction in law and medicine. In the broader context of the republic, public instruction is directed by a cabinet minister, but educational matters are practically under the supervision of the Church.

In terms of religious administration, Cochabamba serves as the seat of one of the three bishoprics in Bolivia. The state recognizes and supports the Roman Apostolic Catholic religion, and the domesticated Indians in the region profess this faith, although it is influenced by the superstitions of their ancestors.
