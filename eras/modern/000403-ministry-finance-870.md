# Ministry of Finance

The term "Ministry of Finance" refers to various governmental bodies across different nations and eras responsible for the administration of state funds, budgets, and national debt.

## The Dual Monarchy (Austria-Hungary)

In the Dual Monarchy, the administration of common affairs was divided among three joint ministries: the ministry of war, the ministry of foreign affairs and of the imperial and royal house, and the ministry of finance. The authority of these joint ministers was strictly limited to common affairs; they were prohibited from exercising influence over government matters that affected only one half of the monarchy.

The joint minister of finance was responsible for the finances of common affairs, the administration of the joint state debt, and the preparation of the joint budget. Until 1909, the joint minister of finance also administered the provinces of Bosnia and Herzegovina, with the exception of matters that fell under the jurisdiction of the minister of war. To ensure oversight, a joint supreme court of accounts was appointed to audit the accounts of the joint ministries.

### Budgetary Structure and Revenue
The common budget covered expenditures for the ministry of finance, the ministry of war (including the army and navy), the ministry of foreign affairs, and the Board of Control. Revenues for this joint budget were derived from the revenues of the joint ministries, the net proceeds of customs, and "the quota"—proportional contributions from Austria and Hungary.

The quota was fixed for several years, typically aligning with the duration of the commercial and customs treaty. The distribution of these contributions shifted over time:
* **Until 1897:** Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30% of the remaining joint expenditure.
* **1897 to July 1907:** Austria's quota was 66-46/49, while Hungary's was 33-2/49.
* **1907:** Hungary's contribution increased to 36.4%.

Additionally, 2% of total charges were debited to Hungary due to the incorporation of the former military frontier into that state. In 1905, the total joint budget revenue was L20,762,410, with proportional contributions accounting for L15,650,448 and customs providing L4,780,000. The Ministry of Finance's specific revenue in 1905 was L4,870, while its expenditure was L177,000.

### General Debt and Terminology
Austria and Hungary shared a general debt in addition to their individual state debts. This general debt decreased steadily between 1875 (232.41 million sterling) and 1905 (224.31 million sterling).

Due to the principle of Dualism, the monarchy maintained three separate ministries of finance:
1. The *Kaiserlich und Koeniglich* (Imperial and Royal) for joint affairs.
2. The *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich* (K.K.) for Austrian affairs.
3. The *Kiralye* for Hungarian affairs.

## France

The French Ministry of Finance has been led by various statesmen throughout its history. Nicolas, Sieur de Maillebois, Desmarets, served as a director of finances starting in 1703 and succeeded Chamillart as the minister of finance in 1708. Desmarets faced a grave financial situation in 1708, where expenses reached 200,251,447 livres against ordinary revenues of 81,977,007 livres. He was regarded as one of the greatest finance ministers of Louis XIV, second only to Colbert.

In the later era of the Bourbon restoration and the government of Louis Philippe, Baron Joseph Dominique Louis served as minister of finance during several periods: 1814–1815, 1815 (resuming the portfolio), 1818 (under the Decazes ministry), and 1831–1832. Baron Louis had previously studied the financial system of Pitt while in England.

In the late 19th century, Paul Doumer served as minister of finance in the Bourgeois cabinet from November 3, 1895, to April 21, 1896. During his tenure, he attempted to introduce an income tax, though he was unsuccessful.

## Denmark

In Denmark, the ministry of finance became a focal point of constitutional struggles starting in 1872 between the *Folketing* (Lower House) and the *Landsting* (Upper House). When J. B. Estrup became prime minister in 1875, he recognized that the political conflict would be primarily financial. Consequently, Estrup retained the ministry of finance in his own hands to strengthen national armaments and establish the political parity of the *Landsting* with the *Folketing*.
