# Biographical History of England to the Revolution

The *Biographical History of England to the Revolution* is a historical work authored by J. Granger and published in London in 1804. It serves as a reference source for biographical information regarding figures in English history.

## Use in Historical Research
The text is cited as a source for further details regarding the life and works of Sir Richard Baker. Baker was a writer who produced a significant amount of work during his period of imprisonment, most notably the *Chronicle of the Kings of England from the Time of the Romans' Government unto the Death of King James*, first published in 1643. While the *Chronicle* achieved extreme popularity for many years and was translated into Dutch in 1649, it is noted that its historical value is very slight due to numerous inaccuracies.

## Context of English Biography
The *Biographical History of England to the Revolution* exists within a broader tradition of biographical writing in England. Interest in the genre was influenced in part by the 1734 English translation of Pierre Bayle's *Dictionnaire historique et critique* (originally published in 1696). Bayle's work is recognized as one of the earliest biographical dictionaries of a modern form, utilizing genuine research despite the author's tendency to use biographies as a means to develop philosophical and controversial views.

Prior to the development of modern biographical dictionaries, biographical elements were often integrated into broader memoirs and histories, a practice common in medieval and renaissance France. The first deliberate contribution to the field of biography is generally considered to be the *Acta Sanctorum*, compiled by the Bollandists, with the first volume appearing in 1653. This work focused specifically on the lives of martyrs and saints. Later, in 1674, Louis Moreri's *Grand Dictionnaire* expanded the scope of the biographical dictionary to include a section of a general character.
