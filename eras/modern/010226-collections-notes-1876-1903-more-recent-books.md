# Collections and Notes (1876-1903)

The period between 1876 and 1903 saw significant activity in the curation of specialized libraries, the publication of historical and technical bibliographies, and the production of scholarly works across various disciplines, including art, finance, theology, and biography.

## Book Collecting and Bibliophilia
The motivation for book collectors often centers on works associated with famous events or persons, such as poets, soldiers, statesmen, or royalty. For example, books once owned by Mary, Queen of Scots, hold a particular appeal. Collectors also value the "pedigree" of a work, tracing its ownership through a series of book-loving predecessors. This interest led to the publication of works such as *Nouvel armorial du bibliophile* (1890), *Great Book-Collectors* (1893) by Charles and Mary Elton, and *English Book-Collectors* (1902) by W.Y. Fletcher.

While some writers are interested in books that caused the persecution of authors or were condemned to be burnt, collectors typically prioritize first editions. First editions are valued for their superiority in study and their associations, though some values are considered fanciful. For instance, first printed editions of Greek and Latin classics, prized during the days of George III, are now largely collected as specimens of printing rather than as useful texts. Conversely, the prestige of first editions of French and English literary masterpieces has risen; a first folio Shakespeare (1623) was sold in 1906.

## Art and Illumination
The study of illumination and medieval manuscripts produced a vast array of technical treatises and facsimile collections. Notable works from this era include:
*   **General Studies:** *The Illuminated Books of the Middle Ages* (1849) by Noel Humphreys and Jones, and *Illuminated MSS. in Classical and Medieval Times* (1892) by J. H. Middleton.
*   **Regional and Specific Collections:** *English Illuminated MSS.* (1895) by E. M. Thompson, *Histoire de l'ornement russe du X^e au XVI^e siècle* (1870) by V. de Boutovsky (including Byzantine MSS.), and *Facsimiles of Miniatures and Ornaments of Anglo-Saxon and Irish MSS.* (1868) by J. O. Westwood.
*   **Institutional Publications:** G. F. Warner published *Illuminated MSS. in the British Museum* in 1903, and Birch and Jenner produced *Early Drawings and Illuminations* (1879), which included a dictionary of subjects in the British Museum's MSS.
*   **Specialized Research:** *Paleografia artistica di Montecassino* (1876-1884), *Le Miniature nei codici Cassinesi* (1887), and works on the 10th and 11th-century Regensburg book painting by G. Schwarzenski (1901).

## Banking, Accounting, and Finance
Literature on banking and accounting expanded during the late 19th century. The library of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales is noted as perhaps the most complete collection of accounting works. Key publications include the *Encyclopaedia of Accounting* (1903) and *The Antiquity of Book-keeping* (1898) by J.W. Heaps.

In banking, significant works include John Dun's *British Banking Statistics* (1876), R. H. Inglis Palgrave's *Bank Rate and the Money Market in England, France, Germany, Holland and Belgium, 1844-1900* (1903), and J. George Kiddy's *The Country Banker's Handbook* (4th ed., 1903). Other influential texts include *Lombard Street* (1873, new ed. 1906) by Walter Bagehot and *Principles of Banking* (1867) by Thomson Hankey.

## Religious and Theological Scholarship
Scholarship regarding the New Testament and the Babi sect saw various contributions:
*   **New Testament:** Westcott's *General Survey of the History of the Canon of the New Testament* saw a 7th edition in 1896. Other contributions include Prof. Charteris's *Canonicity* (1880), the *Dictionary of the Bible* (1898-1904), and the *Encyclopaedia Biblica* (1899-1903). In 1903, Prof. Stanton published *The Gospels as Historical Documents* (part i.).
*   **Babi Literature:** Much of this literature remains in manuscript, with major collections in Paris, London, and St Petersburg. Key publications include E. G. Browne's *The New History of the Bab* (1893) and an edition of the *Kitab-i-Aqdas* by Captain A. G. Toumansky (1899). For American converts, works such as *Baha'u'llah* (1900) by Ibrahim Khayru'llah and *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi* (1903) by Myron H. Phelps were produced.

## Biography and History
The period featured various biographical and historical records:
*   **Queen Luise of Prussia:** Multiple works were published, including those by E. Engel (1876), A. Kluckhohn (1876), and Mommsen and Treitschke (1876), as well as A. Lonke's *Königin Luise von Preussen* (1903).
*   **Academic and Personal History:** James Bain, a professor at Aberdeen, founded the journal *Mind* in January 1876. His later works include the *Biography of James Mill* (1882) and a revised edition of *The Senses and the Intellect* (1894). Bain died on September 18, 1903.
*   **Venetian History:** H. Simonsfeld wrote a life of *Andrea Dandolo* in 1876, and H. Kretschmayr published *Geschichte von Venedig* in 1905.
