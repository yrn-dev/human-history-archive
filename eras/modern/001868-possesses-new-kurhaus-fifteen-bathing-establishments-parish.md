# Baden

## Overview and Location
Baden is a town and watering-place located in lower Austria. It is situated 17 miles south of Vienna by rail. The town is positioned on the banks of the Schwechat at the mouth of the romantic Helenenthal. Due to its location, it has become the primary summer resort for the inhabitants of the nearby capital city of Vienna. As of 1900, the population of Baden was 12,447.

## Town Facilities and Architecture
Baden possesses several key civic and religious structures, including a town-hall that houses interesting archives and a parish church built in the late Gothic style. For its visitors, the town provides a new *Kurhaus* and fifteen bathing-establishments. Additionally, the town features several parks.

## Thermal Springs and Bathing
The town derives its name from its warm baths, which number thirteen. These springs have temperatures ranging from 72°F to 97°F. The chief ingredient in the water is sulphate of lime. Most of these springs rise at the foot of the Calvarienberg, a peak reaching 1,070 feet and composed of dolomitic limestone; these waters are primarily utilized for bathing purposes. The town attracts approximately 20,000 visitors annually.

## Imperial Influence and Local Landmarks
Baden has served as a summer residence for several members of the Austrian imperial family, who have constructed beautiful villas in the area. The town is surrounded by various points of interest, most notably the picturesque valley of the Helenenthal, which is traversed by the Schwechat. 

Near the town, the Vienna waterworks' magnificent aqueduct crosses the valley. At the valley's entrance on the right bank of the river, there are ruins of a 12th-century castle called Rauheneck. At the foot of these ruins stands the Chateau Weilburg, which was built between 1820 and 1825 by Archduke Charles, the victor of Aspern. Opposite the Chateau on the left bank is the ruined castle of Rauhenstein, which also dates from the 12th century.

## Surrounding Region
Further into the countryside, approximately 4 miles up the valley, is Mayerling, a hunting-lodge. This site is where the crown prince Rudolph of Austria was found dead in 1889. Beyond this is Alland, which provides road access to the well-preserved abbey of Heiligenkreuz. This abbey features a Romanesque style church dating from the 11th century, complete with fine cloisters and the tombs of several members of the Babenberg family. The highest point in the vicinity of Baden is the peak of the Hoher Lindkogel, reaching 2,825 feet. This peak is popularly known as the Eiserne Thor and can be ascended in about three hours.
