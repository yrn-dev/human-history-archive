# York Rules

The York Rules were a set of maritime regulations established to create uniformity in the application of the principle of general average. This fundamental principle dictates that any loss incurred for the benefit of all coadventurers should be compensated by all parties in equitable proportion to their respective stakes in the adventure.

## Development and Evolution
The application of general average to the complex cases of maritime commerce originally led to diverse usages across different countries and time periods. Because the principle could not be applied consistently without technical or seemingly arbitrary rules, a small group of individuals sought to establish uniformity.

The process began in May 1860 at a congress in Glasgow, presided over by Lord Brougham and assisted by Lord Neaves. Subsequent congresses took place in London in 1862 and in York in 1864. It was at the York congress that the body of rules known as the "York Rules" was agreed upon.

The rules were later refined by the "Association for the Reform and Codification of the Law of Nations" during conferences held at the Hague (1875), Bremen (1876), and Antwerp (1877). Following these alterations, the body of rules was adopted at the Antwerp conference and renamed the "York and Antwerp Rules" (or "York-Antwerp Rules"). Due to certain points remaining unsatisfactory or insufficient, the Association held a further conference on the subject in Liverpool during the autumn of 1890.

## Application and Legal Impact
The York-Antwerp Rules were designed to be adopted by parties in their contracts of insurance and contracts of affreightment. While they did not significantly alter English common law regarding acts that constitute general average sacrifices as opposed to expenditures, they provided definite provisions where previous authority was doubtful or scanty.

One notable departure from common law is found in Rule I regarding the jettison of deck cargo. Under the York-Antwerp Rules, such jettison is not allowed as general average, even if the cargo was carried on deck according to an established custom of that specific trade.

## Specific Provisions and Rules
The York-Antwerp Rules introduced greater definiteness and clarity to maritime adjustments:

*   **Port of Refuge (Rule X):** A broad view is adopted regarding ports of refuge. If entering such a port was necessary for common safety, the expenses for entering and subsequently exiting (provided the ship sails again with some or all of its original cargo) are allowed as general average under Rule X (a).
*   **Repairs and Cargo Handling (Rule X and XII):** Under Rule X (b), the cost of discharging cargo to allow for repairs to the ship—whether those repairs were necessitated by accident during the voyage or by sacrifice—is allowed as general average, provided the repairs were necessary for the safe prosecution of the voyage. This applies to repairs conducted at a port of call as well as a port of refuge. Rule X (c) allows for the expenses of reloading, stowing, and storing cargo on shore as general average, while Rule XII covers any loss or damage incidental to these operations.
*   **Deductions and Values (Rule XIII and XVII):** Rule XIII established a definite scale for "new for old" deductions from repair costs, replacing uncertain customary rules that varied by location and adapting the scale to modern shipbuilding. Rule XVII established a rule for contributory values, replacing the widely varying national rules regarding the amounts on which freight and ship shall contribute.
