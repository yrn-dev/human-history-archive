# Mistake

The concept of a mistake is addressed across various disciplines and contexts within the archive, ranging from legal and professional definitions to religious guidance and historical accounts of human error.

## Legal and Professional Definitions
In the context of professional conduct for attorneys, a distinction is made between a mistake, poor judgment, and professional misconduct. A mistake is defined as an error resulting from excusable human error, occurring despite the attorney having exercised reasonable care under the given circumstances.

This differs from poor judgment, which occurs when an attorney chooses a course of action that stands in marked contrast to what the Department would reasonably expect from an attorney exercising good judgment. While professional misconduct involves the violation or reckless disregard of a clear obligation or standard, an attorney may exhibit poor judgment even if the standard in question is not sufficiently clear or unambiguous to support a finding of professional misconduct.

## Religious Teachings on Mistakes
The Qur'an provides guidance regarding mistakes in the context of asserting parentage. It teaches that individuals should call others by the names of their fathers, as this is more equitable and juster in the sight of Allah. In cases where the father's name is unknown, individuals are to be called "brothers in faith," "friends," or "maulas."

The text specifies that there is no sin or blame upon those who make a mistake in this matter unintentionally. The focus is placed on the intention of the heart; blame rests only upon that which the hearts do purposely. The text characterizes Allah as Forgiving, Merciful, and Oft-Returning.

## Historical and Narrative Accounts
Historical records and ancient narratives document various instances of mistakes, both in the actions of individuals and the writings of historians.

### Narrative Errors
In the account of Isaac, a mistake occurred regarding the delivery of a blessing. Isaac prayed to God believing his prayers were for Esau, but he later perceived his mistake. Because his prayers had already been spent upon Jacob, Isaac refused Esau's request for a similar blessing. This mistake caused Esau to lament and led Jacob's mother to fear that Esau might punish Jacob. Consequently, she persuaded Isaac to find a wife for Jacob from her own kindred in Mesopotamia.

### Historiographical Errors
The archive notes several instances where historians mistook facts or locations:
*   **Geographical Mistakes:** Zosimus and some of his followers mistook the Danube for the Tanais, which led them to place a field of battle in the plains of Scythia.
*   **Biographical Mistakes:** Plutarch is noted to have made a mistake regarding the events surrounding the death of Cæsar. While Plutarch's account varied, other sources such as Dion Cassius, Appianus, and Cicero indicate it was Caius Trebonius, not another individual, who kept Antonius engaged in talk.
