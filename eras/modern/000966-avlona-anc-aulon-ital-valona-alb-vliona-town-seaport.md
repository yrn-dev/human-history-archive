# AVLONA

## Overview and Geography
Avlona (known anciently as *Aulon*, in Italian as *Valona*, and in Albanian as *Vliona*) is a town and seaport located in Albania, Turkey, within the vilayet of Iannina. As of 1900, the town had a population of approximately 6,000. The town is situated on an eminence about 1-1/2 miles from the sea, near the Gulf of Avlona, which is an inlet of the Adriatic. The geography of the area is characterized by mountains that almost entirely surround the location.

## Port and Maritime Connectivity
The port of Avlona is regarded as the best on the Albanian coast and is the closest port to Italy. Its maritime security is provided by Cape Glossa—the northernmost headland of the Acroceraunian mountains—and the island of Saseno (anciently known as Saso). 

The port is a regular destination for small Greek and Turkish coasters, as well as steamers arriving from Fiume, Trieste, Brindisi, and various other Italian and Austro-Hungarian ports. Additionally, Avlona serves as an important station for the telegraph and cable line that runs from Constantinople to Otranto, Italy.

## Town Appearance and Local Industry
The town is described as having a pleasant appearance, featuring a palace surrounded by olive-groves and gardens, as well as minarets. 

The town is the namesake for Valonia, a material used extensively by tanners. Valonia is the pericarp of an acorn sourced from the oak-woods in the neighboring area.

## Economy and Trade
The district surrounding Avlona is primarily pastoral and agricultural. It produces a variety of commodities, including:
*   **Agricultural products:** Maize, oats, cotton, and olive oil.
*   **Livestock and animal products:** Sheep, cattle, hides, skins, and butter.

These goods are exported in considerable quantities. The region also exports bitumen, which is extracted from a mine operated by a French company. The primary imports for the area consist of petroleum, metals, and piece-goods made of cotton and wool.
