# Marshal Beresford and the Siege of Badajoz

## Military Operations at Badajoz
During the Peninsular War, the city of Badajoz—the capital of a Spanish province located on the left bank of the river Guadiana near the Portuguese frontier—was a site of significant conflict. The city is characterized by its strong walls, bastions, a broad moat, and forts situated on surrounding heights, giving it an appearance of great strength. 

In 1808 and 1809, the French unsuccessfully attacked the city. However, on March 10, 1811, the French force under Marshal Soult gained control of the city after the Spanish commander, Jose Imaz, was bribed into surrendering. Following this, a British army under the command of Marshal Beresford attempted to retake Badajoz. On May 16, Beresford's forces defeated a relieving force at Albuera, though the siege of the fortress was ultimately abandoned in June.

## Geography and History of Badajoz
Badajoz is situated on the Madrid-Lisbon railway and is the official residence of the captain-general of Estremadura, as well as the see of a bishop. The city occupies a slight eminence topped by the ruins of a Moorish castle. A magnificent granite bridge crosses the Guadiana river between the castle-hill and the fort of San Cristobal; this bridge was originally built in 1460, repaired in 1597, and rebuilt in 1833.

The city's architecture reflects its stormy history, notably its cathedral, built in 1258, which features massive embattled walls resembling a fortress. The cathedral also preserves two pictures by the painter Luis de Morales (1509-1586), though these have been retouched in modern times. Badajoz was also the birthplace of the painter Morales and the statesman Manuel de Godoy, duke of Alcudia (1767-1851).

Historically, the city first rose to importance under Moorish rule and became the capital of a small Moorish kingdom in 1031. While the Portuguese held it temporarily in 1168, it remained independent until 1229, when Alphonso IX of Leon captured it. As a frontier fortress, it faced numerous sieges, including those by the Portuguese in 1660 and the Allies in 1705 during the War of the Spanish Succession.

## Economy and Demographics
As of 1900, the population of Badajoz was 30,899. Due to its location, the city benefits from a considerable transit trade with Portugal. Its local industries include the manufacture of pottery and goods made from leather, woollen, and linen.
