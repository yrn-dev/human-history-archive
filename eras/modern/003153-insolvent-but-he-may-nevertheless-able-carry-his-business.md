# Insolvency and Bankruptcy

In legal and commercial usage, the terms "insolvency" and "bankruptcy" possess distinct significations, although they are sometimes used indiscriminately. Insolvency occurs when a person's financial liabilities exceed their means of meeting them. Bankruptcy, conversely, is the status of a debtor who has been declared by a judicial process to be unable to pay his debts.

## The Nature of Insolvency
A person is considered insolvent when the aggregate of their property, at a fair valuation, is insufficient to pay their debts. However, this is not necessarily the case simply because their realized assets are insufficient to meet liabilities. 

A critical distinction exists between being insolvent and being bankrupt. A debtor may be insolvent without becoming bankrupt. For instance, an insolvent person may still be able to carry on their business affairs by utilizing credit, paying old debts by incurring new ones. If fortunate, such an individual may regain a position of solvency without their creditors ever becoming aware of their true financial condition. Furthermore, even if insolvency becomes public and a default occurs, a debtor may avert bankruptcy by effecting a voluntary arrangement with creditors. 

In contrast, a person cannot be a bankrupt without first being insolvent. Bankruptcy is the legal declaration of that insolvency and functions as a statutory system for the administration of the debtor's property, which is removed from their personal control.

## Legal Frameworks and Regional Variations

### Italy
Under the Commercial Code of 1883, bankruptcy in Italy is restricted to merchants, defined as those who engage in commercial business as an habitual profession, including merchant companies. Bankruptcy can be ordered by the court or initiated by the debtor or a creditor for a commercial debt. The specific amount of the debt is immaterial, provided that non-payment proves insolvency. Once adjudicated bankrupt, the debtor loses the right to administer their affairs. A trustee is appointed under the supervision of a judge and a commission of creditors to sell all movable and immovable property via auction for distribution in dividends. Alternatively, bankruptcy may be closed by an arrangement supported by creditors representing three-fourths of the indebtedness. Italian law does not recognize composition before bankruptcy.

### Scotland
The Bankruptcy (Scotland) Act of 1856 regulates the law in Scotland. "Notour" (legally declared) bankruptcy is constituted through sequestration or by insolvency concurring with a duly executed charge for payment, the sale of effects under a poinding or sequestration for rent, or an application for the benefit of *cessio bonorum*. Sequestration may be awarded for a living debtor upon their own petition (with qualified creditors' concurrence) or by a petition of qualified creditors, provided the debtor is a notour bankrupt and had a place of business or dwelling-house in Scotland within the previous year. It may also be petitioned for in the case of a deceased debtor by their mandatory or qualified creditors.

### United States
The National Bankruptcy Act of 1898, amended in 1903, governs the United States. Acts of bankruptcy include transferring property to hinder creditors, transferring property with intent to prefer a creditor while insolvent, allowing a creditor to obtain a preference, making a general assignment for creditors, or admitting in writing an inability to pay debts and a willingness to be adjudicated bankrupt. Notably, the mere non-payment of debts is not an act of bankruptcy; a debtor can only be adjudicated bankrupt on the grounds of indebtedness with their own written consent.

## Administration and Arrangements

### English Law and Deeds of Arrangement
In England and Wales, the Deeds of Arrangement Act 1887 addresses insolvency. While private arrangements are binding as voluntary contracts between a debtor and assenting creditors, they are not binding on dissenting creditors unless confirmed by bankruptcy courts after full investigation. Such arrangements are viewed as assignments of the debtor's property; they are deemed fraudulent if the benefit is limited to some creditors, or they become acts of bankruptcy if extended to all. If honestly entered into, they provide an expeditious method of liquidating affairs for debtors whose insolvency resulted from no gross misconduct.

### Other Jurisdictions
In Canada, the British North America Act 1867 gives the Dominion parliament exclusive power over bankruptcy and insolvency, though no Dominion act currently exists following the repeal of the 1875 act in 1880. Provincial legislatures handle matters such as fraudulent assignments and the administration of estates of deceased insolvents. In the Transvaal, the Insolvency Law No. 13 of 1895 provides for compulsory sequestration and voluntary surrender. India's insolvency is regulated by the Indian Insolvency Act 1848 and the Act XI of 1889.

## Judicial Administration of Estates
An insolvent estate may be administered in bankruptcy, where bankruptcy law dictates the order of asset division among creditors. Under the Judicature Act 1875, such estates may also be administered in the chancery division. In these instances, the rules of bankruptcy regarding the rights of secured and unsecured creditors, provable debts, and the valuation of contingent liabilities and annuities are observed.
