# The Christian Captives

*The Christian Captives* is a tragedy written in 1890 by the English poet and physician Robert Bridges. The work is based on the same subject as the play *El Principe Constante* by the dramatist Calderón.

## Author Biography
Robert Bridges was born on October 23, 1844. He received his education at Eton and Corpus Christi College, Oxford, and pursued medical studies in London at St Bartholomew's hospital. His professional medical career included roles as a physician at the Great Northern hospital and as an assistant physician at the Children's hospital on Great Ormond Street. He retired from medical practice in 1882 and married Mary, the daughter of Alfred Waterhouse, R.A., two years later.

As a poet, Bridges was known for a style characterized by precision, purity, restraint, and delicacy, while maintaining strength of expression. He developed a distinct theory of prosody, arguing that English poetry should follow the rules of natural speech and that the structure of a line depends on the number of "stresses" rather than the number of syllables. His work was initially printed privately and gained recognition within a select circle of admirers before reaching a wider audience.

## Literary Works
Bridges produced a variety of dramatic and poetic works. His poetic output includes *The Growth of Love* (a series of sixty-nine sonnets printed in 1876 and 1889) and *Shorter Poems* (1890). His critical contributions include *John Keats, a Critical Essay* (1895) and *Milton's Prosody* (1893).

In addition to *The Christian Captives*, Bridges wrote several other dramas and tragedies:
* *Prometheus* (1883), described as a "mask in the Greek Manner."
* *Nero* (1885), a historical tragedy with a second part published in 1894.
* *Eros and Psyche* (1885), which is a version of Apuleius.
* *Achilles in Scyros* (1890).
* *Palicio* (1890), a romantic drama written in the Elizabethan manner.
* *The Return of Ulysses* (1890), a drama in five acts.
* *The Humours of the Court* (1893), a comedy based on Lope de Vega's *El Perro del hortelano* and Calderón's *El secreto á voces*.
* *The Feast of Bacchus* (1889), which was partly translated from Terence's *Heauton-Timoroumenos*.

He also contributed to the *Hymns from the Yattendon Hymnal* (1899). A complete six-volume edition of his *Poetical Works* was published between 1898 and 1905.
