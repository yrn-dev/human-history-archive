# Austria and Sardinia (April 1744)

## The Outbreak of the Second Silesian War
The year 1744 marked the beginning of the Second Silesian War. This conflict was precipitated by Frederick, who had been disquieted by the universal success of the Austrian cause. In secret, Frederick concluded a new alliance with Louis XV of France. Prior to this, France had acted as an auxiliary, with its officers in Germany wearing the Bavarian cockade, and France had been officially at war only with England. However, in April 1744, France declared war directly upon Sardinia and Austria.

## French Strategic Planning and the Dunkirk Corps
Following the declaration of war, Louis XV developed a comprehensive military plan. A corps was assembled at Dunkirk for the purpose of supporting the cause of the Pretender in Great Britain. This military expedition was intended to cross the channel under the cover of a fleet. However, the plan failed due to several factors: the French fleet was ill-equipped, the treasury was bankrupt, and the commanding admiral, Jacques Aymar, comte de Roquefeuil, was nervous. In February 1744, Roquefeuil entered the Channel with twenty sail of the line but precipitately retreated upon learning that Sir John Norris was nearby with twenty-five sail of the line. Consequently, the expedition prepared at Dunkirk did not start.

Simultaneously, Louis XV prepared to invade the Austrian Netherlands with 90,000 men, successfully taking Ypres and Menin. His expected opponents were an allied army consisting of Austrians, Germans, Dutch, and English. Other French movements included Coigny, who was tasked with opposing Prince Charles on the Rhine, and the prince de Conti, who was sent to assist the Spaniards in Lombardy and Piedmont.

## The War in Italy and the Alps
The Italian theater of the war became serious in 1744. The French and Spanish objective was to unite the army in Dauphine with the army on the lower Po, a goal that required securing the adhesion of Genoa to obtain a road into central Italy. 

The conflict in this region was characterized by several key engagements:
* **The Spanish and Neapolitan Front:** Lobkowitz took the offensive and drove the Spanish army of Count de Gages toward the Neapolitan frontier. A combined army formed at Velletri defeated Lobkowitz on August 11.
* **The Alpine Campaign:** The prince de Conti stormed Montalban and Villefranche on April 20. A desperate fight occurred at Peyre-Longue on July 18.
* **The Defeat of Sardinia:** On September 30, the king of Sardinia was defeated in a great battle at Madonna del Olmo, located near Coni (Cuneo). 

Despite these actions, Conti failed to take the fortress of Coni and retired to Dauphine for winter quarters. Because of this, the Austro-Sardinians remained positioned between the two French and Spanish armies, preventing them from combining.

## Disruption of the French Plan
The broader French strategy was dislocated by the maneuvers of Prince Charles. Assisted by the veteran Traun, Charles skillfully moved his army over the Rhine near Philipsburg on July 1, captured the lines of Weissenburg, and cut off the French marshal from Alsace. While Coigny managed to cut through the enemy at Weissenburg to post himself near Strassburg, Louis XV abandoned his invasion of Flanders. Instead, the French army moved to take a decisive role in the war in Lorraine and Alsace. Meanwhile, in August, Frederick crossed the Austrian frontier.

## Historical Context of Austria and Sardinia
The relationship between these powers was shaped by long-term diplomatic shifts. Earlier, in 1718, Charles had joined the Triple (and later Quadruple) Alliance to address threats from Spain, which had occupied Sicily and Sardinia. This resulted in a peace where Charles obtained Sicily in exchange for Sardinia. 

By 1733, during the War of Polish Succession, both Spain and Sardinia had adhered to the Pragmatic Sanction of April 19, 1713, which settled the succession of the Habsburg monarchy on Maria Theresa in the absence of a male heir. However, by 1740, the accession of Maria Theresa saw the monarchy threatened by various powers ambitious for expansion, including Sardinia, Spain, France, Bavaria, and Saxony. While Sardinia later allied herself with Austria, the geopolitical situation remained complex; for a period, neither state was at war with France, leading to combats in the Isere valley between Spanish and Sardinian troops in which the French did not participate.
