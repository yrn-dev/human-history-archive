# Military Organization of the Dual Monarchy (1902)

The military and administrative structure of the Dual Monarchy was organized into 16 military territorial districts. Of these, 15 districts corresponded to the 15 army corps. Additionally, the system included 108 supplementary districts, with 105 designated for the army and 3 for the navy. There was also a separate military district located at Zara.

## Army Corps and Infantry Divisions
As of 1902, the formal organization of the army consisted of 31 infantry divisions and 5 cavalry divisions, which were formed into 15 army corps. These corps were located in the following cities:
* I. Cracow
* II. Vienna
* III. Graz
* IV. Budapest
* V. Pressburg
* VI. Kaschau
* VII. Temesvar
* VIII. Prague
* IX. Josefstadt
* X. Przemysl
* XI. Lemberg
* XII. Herrmannstadt
* XIII. Agram
* XIV. Innsbruck
* XV. Serajewo

## Corps Strength and Composition
The typical strength of an army corps included one cavalry brigade (18 squadrons) and one artillery brigade consisting of 16-18 batteries or 128-144 field-guns. The infantry component of a corps usually consisted of 2 infantry divisions, which further broke down into 4 brigades, 8 or 9 regiments, and 32 or 36 battalions. 

Technical and departmental units were also included, and in certain instances, the corps included fortress artillery regiments. The army's branches were organized as follows:
* **Infantry:** Divided into line, Jaeger, and Tirolese regiments.
* **Cavalry:** Divided into hussars, Uhlans, lancers, and dragoons.
* **Artillery:** Organized into regiments.

## Landwehr and Landsturm
The Dual Monarchy maintained additional forces known as the Landwehr. The Austrian Landwehr (retaining the old designation *K.K.*, which previously referred to the Austrian regular army) was organized into 8 divisions of varying strength. The "Royal Hungarian" Landwehr, or Honveds, was organized into 7 divisions. Both the Austrian and Hungarian Landwehr possessed their own artillery and cavalry, specifically hussars and Uhlans.

During wartime, it was probable that a Honveds or Landwehr division would be integrated into each army corps. An exception was the Vienna corps, which maintained 3 divisions during peacetime. Men of military age up to 42 who were not in these units formed the *Landsturm*. This group included many individuals who might have been classified as Landwehr in other systems.
