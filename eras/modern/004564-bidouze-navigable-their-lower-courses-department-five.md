# Basses-Pyrenees

Basses-Pyrenees is a department located in south-western France at the angle of the Bay of Biscay. Formed in 1790, the department was created from three districts of Gascony—Labourd, Soule, and Basse-Navarre—and two-thirds from Bearn. The districts of Labourd, Soule, and Basse-Navarre comprise the Basque region of France and are situated in the west of the department.

## Geography and Topography
The department is bounded to the west by the Atlantic Ocean, to the south by Spain, to the north by Gers and Landes, and to the east by Hautes-Pyrenees. The latter possesses two enclaves consisting of five communes within Basses-Pyrenees. The department covers an area of 2,977 square miles and had a population of 426,817 in 1906.

The southern portion of the department is occupied by the lower and western summits of the Pyrenees, with the height of the mountains increasing gradually from west to east. In the south of the valley of Mauleon, the peak of Orhy reaches 6,618 feet, while the peak of the Rhune, located south of St Jean de Luz, rises to 2,950 feet. Higher elevations are found on the meridian of Orthez, which marks the boundary of Bearn, including the peak of Anie (8,215 feet), the southern peak of Ossau (9,465 feet), and Mourrous (9,760 feet) on the border of Hautes-Pyrenees.

The remaining landscape consists of hills divided by fertile valleys to the west of the Gave de Pau, and a region of plateaus and heaths to the northeast of the same river.

## Hydrography
The rivers of the department generally flow toward the north-west. The Nive, the Bidouze, and the Gave de Pau are streams that meet in the Adour. In the north-east, the two Luys join the Adour in Landes. In the south-west, the Bidassoa and the Nivelle flow directly into the sea.

The Adour's lower course serves as the boundary between Basses-Pyrenees and Landes, entering the sea below Bayonne via a shifting bar. The Adour, the Bidouze, and the Nive are all navigable on their lower courses. Important passes into Spain run along the upper valleys of the Aspe and the Nive, which are guarded by forts.

## Administration and Urban Centers
Basses-Pyrenees is divided into five arrondissements: Mauleon, Orthez, Oloron, Bayonne, and Pau. These are further subdivided into 559 communes and 41 cantons. The department constitutes the diocese of Bayonne, belongs to the district of the XVIII. army corps, and falls within the educational circumscription (academie) of Bordeaux.

Pau serves as the capital and the seat of a court of appeal. Other principal towns include Orthez, Oloron, Bayonne, St Jean-de-Luz, Biarritz, and Eaux-Bonnes. Other sites of interest include:
* **Lescar**: Home to a church from the 16th and 12th centuries that once served as a cathedral.
* **Sauveterre**: A town on the Gave d'Oloron featuring a church in Gothic and Romanesque styles, an old bridge, and the remains of a feudal castle.
* **Montaner**: Site of a stronghold built in 1380 by Gaston Phoebus, viscount of Bearn and count of Foix.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The department's economy is supported by various natural resources and industries. It produces building-stone, salt, and other quarry products. Mineral springs are located at Salies, St Christau, Eaux-Chaudes, Eaux-Bonnes, and Cambo-les-Bains, the latter of which is visited by the Basques on St John's Eve.

Industrial activities include chemical works and large metallurgical works (the *Forges de l'Adour*) at Le Boucau, located 3 miles from Bayonne. Other manufactures include sashes and woollen caps, chocolate, paper, and cord slippers, alongside flour-mills, saw-mills, and tanneries. Table delicacies and "Bayonne hams" are prepared at Orthez.

Bayonne serves as the principal port, with a considerable fishing population also residing at St Jean-de-Luz. Principal exports include resinous products, salt, wine, minerals, mine-props, and timber. Leading imports consist of wool, grain, phosphates, minerals, and coal. Interior commerce, involving the exchange of products between the plain and mountain regions, is of significant importance to the inhabitants.

Transportation is facilitated by the Southern Company's railway lines, the primary route of which runs from Bayonne to Toulouse via Pau and Orthez.
