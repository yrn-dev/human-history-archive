# The Digital Revolution: From Mainframes to the Palm of the Hand

## Overview
The Digital Revolution, often termed the Third Industrial Revolution, marks the shift from mechanical and analog electronic technology to digital electronics. Beginning in the mid-20th century, this era fundamentally restructured human communication, commerce, and cognition. At its core, the revolution was driven by the transition from vacuum tubes to transistors, and eventually to integrated circuits, allowing for the exponential miniaturization of computing power.

While the early phase focused on centralized "mainframe" computing used by governments and large corporations, the late 20th century saw a democratization of technology through the Personal Computer (PC). This trajectory culminated in the early 21st century with the "mobile revolution," where the convergence of telephony, internet access, and computing into the smartphone transformed the device from a tool into an essential prosthetic for modern life.

## Key Facts
*   **Moore's Law:** Named after Gordon Moore, this observation noted that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every two years. While some contemporary historians and engineers debate whether Moore's Law has finally reached its physical limit due to atomic-scale constraints, it served as the primary roadmap for the industry for decades.
*   **The Shift to Mobility:** The transition from the PC to the smartphone represented a shift in "computing paradigms." Computing moved from a destination (sitting at a desk) to a constant state of connectivity.
*   **Socio-Economic Impact:** The revolution enabled the rise of "Big Data" and the platform economy. Historians note that while this increased efficiency, it also created the "Digital Divide," where socio-economic inequality is exacerbated by varying levels of access to technology.

## Did You Know?
*   **The First "Bug":** The term "computer bug" became popular after Grace Hopper's team found a literal moth trapped in a relay of the Harvard Mark II computer in 1947.
*   **The First Smartphone:** While the iPhone (2007) revolutionized the market, the IBM Simon, released in 1994, is widely considered the first true smartphone, featuring a touchscreen and email capabilities.
*   **Storage Evolution:** The first hard disk drive, the IBM 305 RAMAC (1956), weighed over a ton and stored only about 5 megabytes of data.
