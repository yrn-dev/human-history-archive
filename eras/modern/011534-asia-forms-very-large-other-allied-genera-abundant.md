# Flora and Fauna of Asia

The distribution of life forms across Asia is heavily dependent on climatic conditions, resulting in distinct territorial divisions of flora and fauna.

## Northern and Temperate Flora
The flora of northern Asia is essentially the same as that of northern Europe, with differences occurring primarily in species rather than genera. Notable absences east of the Ural include all heaths and the oak. The primary trees found on mountains are birch, larch, and pines, while poplars, alders, and willow are found on lower ground. In Siberia, the northern limit for the pine is approximately 70 deg. N.

A flora of essentially European character extends along the warm temperate zone from the Himalaya to the Mediterranean. While many European species reach the central Himalaya, few are known in the eastern parts. Genera common to both Europe and the Himalaya are more abundant and extend throughout the entire chain across all elevations. Simultaneously, Chinese and Japanese forms are diffused along this zone, appearing most numerous in the eastern Himalaya and less frequently in the west.

## Marine Life of Asia
The seas of Asia host a diverse array of fish, particularly among the spiny-rayed fishes (Acanthopterygii). The perches (_Percidae_) are largely represented; specifically, the genus _Serranus_ is very numerous in Asia and features very large forms, contrasting with Europe where it has only one species. Other allied genera are abundant, extending from eastern Africa to the Indian seas.

In the seas of southern Asia, especially near coral reefs, the scaly-finned fishes (Squamipennes) are principally found. Other notable marine groups include:
*   **Red mullets (_Mullidae_):** Largely represented by genera that differ from European ones.
*   **_Polynemidae_:** Ranging from the Pacific through the Indian Ocean to the Atlantic, these provide animals used to prepare isinglass. The mango fish, a great delicacy, inhabits the seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal.
*   **_Sciaenidae_:** These extend to China from the Bay of Bengal but are not known to the west.
*   **Pomfrets (_Stromateidae_):** Extending to the Pacific and China, these resemble the Mediterranean dory.
*   **Other groups:** Sword fishes (_Xiphidae_), scabbard fishes (_Trichuridae_), and lancet fishes (_Acanthuridae_) are distributed through south Asian seas. Mackerels, mullets, blennies, and gobies also abound.

## Mammals of the Indian Region
The mammal fauna of the Indian region of Asia is more highly developed than that of the Palaearctic. This region is the home of the Indian elephant, one of only two remaining representatives of the order Proboscidea.

The region's mammalian diversity includes:
*   **Carnivora:** This region is considered the true home of the tiger, though it has wandered north into the Palaearctic division. Other characteristic animals include the benturong (_Arctictis_), various ichneumons, and civets. Two species of bears are restricted to this region.
*   **Ungulates:** There are four species of rhinoceros, one species of tapir, and several peculiar forms of the swine family. The Bovidae (hollow-horned ruminants) include several genera of antelopes and species of true _Bos_ (such as _B. bubalus_, _B. frontalis_, and _B. sondaicus_). Deer are numerous, and the chevrotains (_Tragulus_) are characteristic of the region.
*   **Other Orders:** The Quadrumana are represented by _Simia_, _Hylobates_, and _Semnopithecus_, along with two peculiar Lemurine forms. The Insectivora include peculiar forms like _Tupaia_ and _Gymnura_. Rodents include very numerous squirrels and two genera of porcupines. The order Edentata is represented by several species of scaly ant-eater (_Manis_). Bats are well represented in both frugivorous and insectivorous divisions.

## Regional Botany and Avifauna
In south India, several orders are most largely developed, including Jasmineae, Ebenaceae, Balsaminaceae, Dipterocarpaceae, Cyrtandraceae, and Aurantiaceae, though few contain as many as 100 peculiar Indian species. The Balsaminaceae are very abundant in the peninsula but rare in the eastern region. The genus _Nepenthes_ is specially developed in the Malayan area, extending from Madagascar to New Caledonia; it is found in Ceylon and as far north as the Khasi hills, but is absent from the peninsula of India and the Himalaya.

A distinct connection exists between the flora of Ceylon and the peninsula and that of eastern tropical Africa, evidenced by the identity of genera and families and the similarity of mountain flora, including species believed to be identical to Abyssinian forms. Notably, oaks, cycads, and conifers are absent from both the peninsula and Ceylon, even as mountains rise to elevations where the latter two are abundant to the east and north.

Regarding birds, the Greenfinch (_Fringilla chloris_) reaches the river Ob in Asia. In the adjacent islands and north-east Asia, two allied species occur: _Fringilla kawarahiba_ of Temminck and _Fringilla sinica_ of Linnaeus.

## Freshwater Fish
The _Cyprinidae_ family includes about 1400 known species, nearly all confined to fresh water. In Asia, the _Homalopterinae_ (mountain forms allied to loaches with a rudimentary air-bladder) are found in south-eastern Asia and China. Additionally, the _Catostominae_ subfamily has a few species from eastern Siberia and China. The _Cobitinae_ (loaches) are also found in Asia.
