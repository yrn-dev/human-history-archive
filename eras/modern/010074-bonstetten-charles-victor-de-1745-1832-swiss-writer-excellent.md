# BONSTETTEN, CHARLES VICTOR DE (1745-1832)

Charles Victor de Bonstetten was a Swiss writer and a member of one of the great patrician families of Bern. Described as a liberal patrician who was more French than Swiss, he served as a representative of the Gallicized Bern of the 18th century.

## Education and Early Influences
Bonstetten received his education in his native town of Bern, as well as at Yverdon. Between 1763 and 1766, he studied at Geneva, where he imbibed liberal sentiments under the influence of Charles Bonnet and Rousseau. Following a period where he was recalled to Bern by his father, he was sent to Leiden. In 1769, he visited England, during which time he became a friend of the poet Gray.

## Political Career and Public Service
After the death of his father in 1770, Bonstetten traveled extensively in Italy. He entered political life upon his return to Bern in 1774, though his liberal ideas rendered him unfitted for this sphere. These convictions led him to encourage and patronize Johannes Muller, who would later become a Swiss historian.

Bonstetten held several administrative appointments:
* **Saanen (Gessenay):** In 1779, he was named the Bernese bailiff of this region.
* **Nyon:** He was transferred to a similar capacity in Nyon in 1787. However, he was forced to retire from this post after participating in a 1791 festival celebrating the destruction of the Bastille.
* **Italian-speaking districts:** From 1795 to 1797, he governed the districts of Locarno, Mendrisio, Val Maggia, and Lugano on behalf of the Swiss Confederation. During this tenure, he is said to have introduced the cultivation of the potato.

## Later Life and Exile
The French revolution of 1798 in Switzerland forced Bonstetten back into private life. He spent the period from 1798 to 1801 in Denmark with his friend Fredirika Brun. In 1803, he settled in Geneva, where he remained for the rest of his life. In Geneva, he associated with many distinguished individuals, including Madame de Stael between 1809 and 1817.

## Literary Works
Bonstetten authored several works across different subjects:
* **Regional Descriptions:** While serving as bailiff of Saanen or Gessenay, he wrote *Lettres pastorales sur une contree de la Suisse*, which was published in German in 1781. In 1797, he published a description of the Italian-speaking districts he had governed.
* **Philosophical and Psychological Studies:** His works include *Recherches sur la nature et les lois de l'imagination* (1807) and *Etudes de l'homme, ou Recherches sur les facultes de penser et de sentir* (1821).
* **Major Work:** His most celebrated publication, *L'Homme du midi et l'homme du nord* (1824), examined the influence of climate on different nations, specifically exalting the north over the south.
