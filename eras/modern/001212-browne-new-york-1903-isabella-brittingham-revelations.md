# Browne (New York, 1903) and Isabella Brittingham

## Overview of Babi and Baha'i Literature
The literature associated with the Babi sect is described as voluminous, though much of it exists primarily in manuscript form. Significant public collections of these manuscripts are located in Paris (Bibliotheque Nationale), London (British Museum), and St Petersburg. The collection in Paris includes rare manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, such as a history written by Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, a contemporary of the Bab.

## Works of E. G. Browne
E. G. Browne has contributed several significant works to the study of this subject. In 1891, he edited, annotated, and translated a Persian work titled *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*, which was composed by 'Abbas Efendi, the son of Baha. Browne later published *The New History of the Bab* in 1893. Additionally, in 1892, he authored a "Catalogue and Description of the 27 Babi Manuscripts" for the *Journal of R. Asiat. Soc.*, published in the July and October issues.

## English Works for American Converts
Several works were composed in English specifically for converts in America. Among these is *Baha'u'llah (The Glory of God)*, written by Ibrahim Khayru'llah with assistance from Howard MacNutt and published in Chicago in 1900. Khayru'llah also authored *Facts for Bahaists* (1901) and *The Three Questions* (date not provided).

Other notable English contributions include:
* **Isabella Brittingham**: She wrote *The Revelations of Baha'u'llah, in a Sequence of Four Lessons*, published in 1902.
* **Myron H. Phelps**: He authored *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi*, published in New York in 1903. This work features a preface written by E. G. Browne.
* **Laura Clifford Burney**: She is credited with *Some Answered Questions Collected*.

## Other Related Scholarship
Other scholars have produced works on the subject, including Andreas (*Die Babi's in Persien*, 1896) and Baron Victor Rosen, who contributed to the *Collections scientifiques de l'Institut des Langues orientales* across several volumes (1877, 1886, and 1891) and wrote articles titled "Manuscrits Babys." Captain A. G. Toumansky wrote articles in the *Zapiski vostochnava otdyeleniya Imperatorskava Russkava Archeologicheskava Obshchestva* between 1890 and 1900. Toumansky also produced an edition of the *Kitab-i-Aqdas*—identified as the most important of Baha's works—which included a Russian translation, introduction, and notes, published in St Petersburg in 1899.

Additionally, a Persian author named Mirza Muhammad Mahdi Khan (Za'imu'd-Duwla) wrote an Arabic history of the Babis. Although described as unsympathetic, the work is noted as being well-informed and was printed in Cairo in A.H. 1321 (A.D. 1903-1904).
