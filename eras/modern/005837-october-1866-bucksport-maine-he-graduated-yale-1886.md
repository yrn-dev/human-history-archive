# Carl Darling Buck

## Early Life and Education
Carl Darling Buck was an American philologist born on October 2, 1866, in Bucksport, Maine. He pursued his higher education at Yale, where he graduated in 1886. Following his graduation, he remained at Yale as a graduate student for a period of three years. Buck further expanded his academic training abroad, studying at the American School of Classical Studies in Athens from 1887 to 1889, and subsequently in Leipzig from 1889 to 1892.

## Academic Career
In 1892, Buck joined the faculty of the University of Chicago, where he served as a professor of Indo-European comparative philology and Sanskrit. While his professional appointment covered these broad fields, his most significant scholarly contributions were concentrated in the narrower specialization of Italic dialects.

## Scholarly Works and Contributions
Buck produced several important works regarding the Oscan and Umbrian languages. His publications include *Der Vocalismus der oskischen Sprache*, published in 1892, and *The Oscan-Umbrian Verb-System* in 1895. In 1904, he published a *Grammar of Oscan and Umbrian*. 

Beyond these primary texts, Buck authored a précis of the Italic languages for *Johnson's Universal Cyclopaedia*. He also collaborated with W.G. Hale to prepare *A Latin Grammar*, which was published in 1903. In the realm of phonological review, his discussion of "Brugmann's Law" is considered perhaps his most important contribution.
