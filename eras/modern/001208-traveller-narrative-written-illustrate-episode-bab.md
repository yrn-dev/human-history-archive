# A Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab

*A Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab* is a Persian work that serves as a significant source for the study of Babiism. The text was composed by 'Abbas Efendi, the son of Baha.

## Publication and Editorial History
The work was edited, translated, and annotated by Edward Granville Browne, who served as a Fellow of Pembroke College, Cambridge, a Fellow of the British Academy, and Sir Thomas Adams's Professor of Arabic. Browne's edition of the narrative was published in Cambridge in 1891.

## Content and Bibliographic Value
The narrative is utilized as a primary resource for the bibliography of the Babi sect. Specifically, volume II, pages 173-211 of the *Traveller's Narrative* provides a comprehensive bibliography of the sect's literature up to the year 1889. 

The literature of the Babi sect is described as being very voluminous, though it exists mostly in manuscript form. Valuable public collections of these manuscripts are held in Paris (Bibliotheque Nationale), London (British Museum), and St Petersburg. The Paris collection includes rare manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, such as a history written by Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, a contemporary of the Bab.

## Related Works by E. G. Browne
In addition to his work on the *Traveller's Narrative*, E. G. Browne authored *The New History of Mirza Ali Muhammed the Bab* (also referred to as *The New History of the Bab*), published in Cambridge in 1893. Browne also provided the preface for the 1903 New York publication *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi*, written by Myron H. Phelps.
