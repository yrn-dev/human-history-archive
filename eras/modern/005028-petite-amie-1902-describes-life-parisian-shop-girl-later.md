# Petite amie (1902)

*Petite amie* is a play written in 1902 by the French dramatist Eugène Brieux. The work describes the life of a shop-girl in Paris.

## Author and Dramatic Style
Eugène Brieux was born in Paris on January 19, 1858, to parents who were poor. His body of work is characterized as essentially didactic, with plays designed to target iniquities or weaknesses within the social system. 

Brieux's career began in 1879 with the production of a one-act play titled *Bernard Palissy*, which he wrote in collaboration with M. Gaston Salandri. Following this, he did not obtain another hearing for eleven years until 1890, when Antoine produced *Ménage d'artistes* at the Théâtre Libre.

## Social Themes in Brieux's Work
The play *Petite amie* fits into a broader pattern of Brieux's commitment to social commentary. Throughout his career, he utilized the stage to address various societal issues:

*   **Working Class and Gender:** In *Blanchette* (1892), he highlighted the evil results of the education provided to girls of the working classes. In *Les Trois Filles de M. Dupont* (1897), he provided a brutal and powerful study of the miseries the French system of dowry imposed on poor middle-class girls.
*   **Legal and Political Systems:** He directed *M. de Réboval* (1892) against pharisaism and *L'Engrenage* (1894) against political corruption. His 1900 play, *La Robe rouge*, was aimed at the injustices of the law.
*   **Social Practices and Beliefs:** Brieux satirized the indiscriminate belief in the doctrine of heredity in *L'Évasion* (1896) and criticized the frivolity of fashionable charity in *Les Bienssaiteurs* (1896). He also addressed the practice of putting children out to nurse in *Les Remplaçantes* (1901) and the evil results of betting among Parisian workmen in *Le Résultat des courses* (1898).

Additionally, Brieux wrote *Les Avariés* (1901), which was forbidden by the censor due to its medical details; the author read this work privately at the Théâtre Antoine.

## Later Works
Following the 1902 production of *Petite amie*, Brieux continued to write for the stage. His later plays include *La Couvée* (1903), which had been acted privately in Rouen in 1893, and *Maternité* (1904). In 1904, he collaborated with M. Jean Sigaux on *La Déserteuse*. He also wrote a three-act comedy titled *Les Hannetons* in 1906.
