# Badminton

Badminton is a fast-paced game played with rackets and shuttlecocks. The game's name is derived from the seat of the duke of Beaufort in Gloucestershire. While it appears to have first been played in England around 1873, it was played in India prior to that time, where it remains very popular.

## Organization and History
The Badminton Association in England was established in 1895. The association's laws were based on the original Poona rules of 1876 and a code of rules created in 1887 for the Bath Badminton Club. In England, the sport is almost exclusively played within covered courts. 

Competitive championships were instituted in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The All England championships for mixed doubles, ladies' doubles, and gentlemen's doubles began in 1899, followed by ladies' singles and gentlemen's singles in 1900. The first championship between Ireland and England took place in 1904.

## Gameplay and Mechanics
Badminton consists entirely of volleying and can be played under artificial light or daylight. It is available in two formats: the two-handed (single) game, with one player per side, and the four-handed (double) game, with two players per side. A single game of badminton is noted to require more staying power than a single at lawn tennis. Players utilize skill and judgment through techniques such as "smashing" (a hard downward stroke) and "dropping" (hitting the shuttle gently just over the net).

The shuttle is considered "in play" from the moment the server's racket strikes it until it touches the ground, touches the net without crossing over, or a "fault" occurs.

## Rules of the Four-Handed Game
In the double game, the first server stands in the right-hand half court and serves to the opponent in the opposite right-hand half court. Other players may stand anywhere on their side of the net, though once the server hits the shuttle, all players are free to move anywhere on their respective sides.

If the receiver returns the shuttle into any part of the opponents' court before it hits the ground, it must be returned by the "in" (serving) side, then by the "out" (non-serving) side, continuing until the shuttle is no longer in play or a fault is made.

### Scoring and Service
Only the "in" side can score an ace. If the "out" side commits a fault, the "in" side scores an ace and the players on the "in" side switch half courts. The server then serves from the left half court to the player in the opposite left half court who has not yet been served to. If the "in" side commits a fault, the server loses their "hand" (serve), and the player who was served to becomes the new server, though no score is accrued.

The first service of each innings starts from the right-hand half court. In the first innings, the starting side has only one "hand." In all subsequent innings, every player on each side has a "hand," and partners serve consecutively. While a side remains "in," service alternates between the half courts into the diagonally opposite half court, with players switching courts whenever an ace is scored.

### Lets and Good Strokes
A stroke is considered good if the shuttle falls on a line. In play, a stroke is also good if the striker's racket follows the shuttle over the net. If the shuttle hits the net during play but still crosses over, the stroke is good. 

A "let" occurs—meaning the stroke does not count and the server must serve again—if:
* The server misses the shuttle entirely while attempting to serve.
* The shuttle hits the net during service but is otherwise a good serve (even if the receiver strikes it).

## Faults
A "fault" is called under the following conditions:
* **Service Errors:** The service is overhand (the shuttle is struck higher than the server's waist), the shuttle fails to fall into the diagonally opposite half court, or both the server and receiver do not have both feet inside their respective half courts before the strike (a foot on the line is considered out).
* **Boundary and Net Errors:** The shuttle falls outside the court, passes under or through the net, hangs in the net, or touches the side walls, roof, or any player's person or dress.
* **Play Errors:** The shuttle is hit before it reaches the striker's side, or a player's person, dress, or racket touches the net or its supports.
* **Striking Errors:** The shuttle is struck twice by the same player, struck successively by partners, or is not hit distinctly (such as being "spooned" over the net after being caught on the racket).
* **Conduct:** A player willfully obstructs an opponent.
