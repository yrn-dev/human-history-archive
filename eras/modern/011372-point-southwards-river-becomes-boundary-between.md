# River Boundaries and Frontier Demarcations

The use of river systems as political and geographical boundaries is a recurring theme in the demarcation of territories across Asia, Europe, and South America. These natural features often serve as the dividing lines between sovereign states, colonial interests, or administrative provinces.

## Southeast Asian Boundaries
In the region of Burma, the Mekong river serves as a critical boundary. At approximately latitude 21 deg. 45', the boundary strikes the Mekong; from that point southwards, the river becomes the boundary between the Shan States and Tongking for some 200 miles. In this stretch, the channel of the river defines the limits of occupation between British and French subjects, although it does not entirely define their interests. Further west, the boundary of Burma is defined by the eastern watershed of the Nmaikha (an affluent of the Irrawaddy) at a point level in latitude with Mogaung. From there, it follows an irregular course southwards to a position south-east of Bhamo (lat. 24 deg.), defines the northern edge of the Shan States, and eventually reaches the Mekong. Approximately on the parallel of 20 deg. N. lat., the boundary leaves the Mekong to run westwards toward the Salween, where the eastern watershed of the Salween basin divides Siam from the Lower Burma provinces.

## Central Asian and Afghan Frontiers
The boundaries of Afghanistan and its neighboring territories are heavily defined by river systems and commissions. The Oxus river serves as a primary northern boundary, separating Afghanistan from Russia from the junction at Khamiab (about 150 miles above the Russian Trans-Caspian railway crossing at Charjui) to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir. 

In the province of Badakshan, which includes Wakhan, the north-eastern boundaries were decided by the Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873, limiting the territory to the southern or left bank of the Oxus. The western boundary of Badakshan begins at the junction of the Oxus and Kunduz rivers, crossing the Turkestan plains southwards.

To the north-west, the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886 fixed a boundary between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). This boundary leaves the Hari Rud at Zulfikar, located about 70 miles south of Sarakhs, and reaches its most southerly point at the crossing of the Kushk, approximately 60 miles north of Herat.

In the south, the boundary between Afghanistan and Baluchistan follows the Kundar stream for about 40 miles south-west from Domandi, which is the junction of the Gomal and Kundar rivers. The Gomal river also serves as the point where the boundary of Baluchistan touches the northern limits of Afghanistan.

## South American Demarcations
In South America, the boundary between Brazil and Bolivia has been shaped by various treaties, including those of Madrid (1750), San Ildefonso (1777), 1867, and 1903. The line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.) and ascends the river to a point 9 kilometers below Fort Coimbra. The boundary subsequently follows the Verde, Guapore, Mamore, and Madeira rivers down to the mouth of the Abuna (approximately lat. 9 deg. 44' S.). 

The 1903 treaty of Petropolis further refined these limits, specifying that the line should ascend the Abuna river to lat. 10 deg. 20' S., then run west along that parallel to the Rapirran river, following it to its principal source.

## European River Frontiers
The Danube river has a significant history as a frontier. For a long period, it formed the boundary of the Roman empire, with the ancient Abusina near Eining serving as a chief outpost against northern barbarians. However, since the Roman era, the central Danube has not functioned as a state boundary; instead, it has served as a route for migratory tribes such as the Turks, Magyars, Slavs, Avars, and Huns. 

While the Danube itself does not form a boundary from Orsova upwards, its tributaries have served as lines of separation. For example, the rivers Leitha and March separate Hungary from Austria, and the river Enns served as a western boundary for a considerable period.
