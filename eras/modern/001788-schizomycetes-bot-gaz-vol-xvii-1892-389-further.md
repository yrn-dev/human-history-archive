# Schizomycetes

The Schizomycetes are a group of organisms that have been the subject of extensive study regarding their structure, classification, and biological functions. Research into this group spans various specialized areas, including their presence in water, their fossil records, and their cellular anatomy.

## General Authorities and Research
General knowledge of bacteria and Schizomycetes is documented in several key works. These include Fischer's *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (published in Oxford in 1900, with a German edition in 1903), Migula's *System der Bakterien* (1897), and Mace's *Traite pratique de bakteriologie* (5th edition, 1904). Additional general information is found in Lafar's *Technical Mycology* (vol. i, 1898) and in Engler and Prantl's *Die natuerlichen Pflanzenfamilien* (I. Th. 1 Abt. a).

## Cellular Structure and Anatomy
Detailed investigations have been conducted into the specific anatomical components of these organisms:

*   **Cell Membrane:** Research on the construction of bacteria and Cyanophyceen has been provided by Butschli (1896) and Fischer (1897), as well as Rowland's "Observations upon the Structure of Bacteria" (1899).
*   **Cilia:** The study of cilia in bacteria is covered in the works of Migula and Fischer, specifically Fischer's "Unters. ueber Bakterien" in *Pringsh. Jahrb.* vol. xxvii.
*   **Nucleus:** The nucleus and its division have been examined by Wager, Migula, and Fischer. Further research includes Vejdovsky's work on the nucleus of bacteria (1904) and his cytological studies on bacteria found in the Prager water supply (1905), as well as Mencl's 1907 research on the structural conditions of *Bacterium gammari*.
*   **Spores:** The biology of *B. ramosus* has been detailed by Marshall Ward (1895). Other spore-related research includes Sturgis's work on a soil bacillus of the type of de Bary's *B. megatherium* and studies by L. Klein (1889).

## Environmental and Fossil Presence
The archive contains records of bacteria in various environments:

*   **Water:** Frankland and Marshall Ward published "Reports on the Bacteriology of Water" in the *Proceedings of the Royal Society* (vol. li, liii, and lvi). Additionally, papers regarding the bacteria found in the river Thames were published in *Ann. of Bot.* (vol. xii and xiii).
*   **Fossils:** Renault's 1896 work, "Recherches sur les Bacteriacees fossiles," provides data on fossil bacteria.

## Classification
The methods and marks used for the classification of Schizomycetes have been explored by Marshall Ward in the *Annals of Botany* (1892, vol. vi). Further classification data is found in the works of Lehmann and Neumann in their *Atlas and Essentials of Bac*.
