# BAMPTON, JOHN

## Life and Education
John Bampton (c. 1690–1751) was an English divine. He was a member of Trinity College, Oxford, where he earned his Master of Arts degree in 1712. During his career, he served as a canon of Salisbury for a period of time. He died on June 2, 1751, at the age of 61.

## The Bampton Lectures
Through the directions in his will, Bampton established a series of annual lectures to be delivered at the University Church in Oxford. These lectures are held on Sunday mornings during full term, specifically between the end of the third week in Act term and the commencement of the last month in Lent term.

The lectures are designed to address specific theological subjects, which include:
* The confirmation and establishment of the Christian faith, and the confutation of all schismatics and heretics.
* The divine authority of the Holy Scriptures.
* The authority of the writings of the primitive fathers regarding the practice and faith of the primitive Church.
* The divinity of the Holy Ghost.
* The divinity of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.
* The articles of the Christian faith as they are comprehended in the Nicene and Apostles' Creeds.

## Administration and Requirements
The endowment for the lectures provides £120 for each lecturer. To be eligible for the position, the lecturer must be at least a Master of Arts from either Cambridge or Oxford. Historically, the lecturer was chosen annually on the fourth Tuesday in Easter term by the heads of colleges. A restriction exists stating that no individual can be chosen to deliver the lectures a second time. Additionally, the lectures are required to be published within two months of their delivery.

The series of lectures began in 1780 and has continued since. However, beginning in 1895, elections are only conducted in alternate years due to a depreciation of the fund's revenue.

## Notable Lecturers and Works
Various scholars have delivered the Bampton Lectures over the centuries. Notable lecturers and their respective works include:
* Heber (1815): *The Personality and Office of the Christian Comforter*
* R. Whately (1822): *Party Feeling in Religion*
* R. D. Hampden (1832): *The Scholastic Philosophy in relation to Christian Theology*
