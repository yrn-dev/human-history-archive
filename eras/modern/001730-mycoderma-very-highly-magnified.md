# Mycoderma

## Overview and Classification
Mycoderma is a form of organism identified in the study of microscopic cells. It is specifically associated with the zoogloea stage of a *Micrococcus*, in which it forms a close membrane on infusion. 

Within the broader classification of such cells, *Micrococci* are categorized as *Cocci*, which are cells that are either spherical or spheroidal. These are distinguished from other forms based on their relative sizes, which include *Macrococci* and *Monas* forms.

## Cellular Forms and Structures
The archive describes various cellular structures that exist alongside or in relation to these organisms:

*   **Cocci**: Spherical or spheroidal cells.
*   **Rods or Rodlets**: Cells that are elongated and may be cylindrical, fusiform, or biscuit-shaped. Cylindrical forms are categorized as *Bacterium* if they are short (three or four times as long as they are broad) or *Bacillus* if they are longer. Biscuit-shaped cells are identified as *Bacteria* in the early stages of division.
*   **Clostridia**: These cells are spindle-shaped.
*   **Filaments**: These consist of elongated cylindrical cells that remain united end to end following division. Filaments can be free or attached at a basal end. They are further divided into simple filaments (such as *Leptothrix*) and those exhibiting false branching (such as *Cladothrix*).

## Physical Properties and Behavior
Certain forms of these organisms are fixed to a substratum, while others are free. Those that are free may be either motile or immotile.
