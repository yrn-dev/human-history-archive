# Dependencies and Imperial Cohesion

The concept of dependency, whether political, economic, or physical, has manifested throughout history in various forms, ranging from the administrative structures of global empires to the survival strategies of isolated populations.

## Imperial Cohesion and Communication
The British Empire is described as a mixture of accumulations and growths that differed from any previous empire. It was an overseas empire, similar to the Athenian Empire, utilizing sea ways and relying on the British Navy as its common link. The cohesion of the British Empire was physically dependent upon methods of communication. Specifically, the development of steamships, ship-building, and seamanship between the sixteenth and nineteenth centuries enabled the "Pax Britannica." However, it is noted that the emergence of swift land transport or air developments could potentially render this system inconvenient.

Despite official insufficiencies, negligence by the "home" public, and official tyrannies, the empire was sustained and endured by many men of the "subject" races because it guaranteed security and wide peace. The scale of the empire was such that no single brain or office had ever fully comprehended it as a whole.

## Political and Territorial Dependencies
Throughout history, various states have maintained complex relationships of dependency:

*   **Carthage:** This Tyrian colony achieved federal preëminence over other Phenician colonies on the African coast. At its maximum dominion, Carthage included towns such as Leptis, Adrumêtum, Hippo, and Utica. While these original Phenician foundations were dependents of Carthage, they likely enjoyed a qualified autonomy. Carthage also maintained foreign dependencies in Spain, Sardinia, the Balearic isles, and Sicily, as well as a dependent territory of three hundred smaller towns inhabited by Liby-Phenicians.
*   **Korea and China:** Chinese statesmen maintained a "romance of intermediate sovereignty and ultimate dependency" regarding Korea. While they refrained from officially declaring Korea a dependency of China, they sought to maintain traditional relations. Consequently, in 1876, Korea was permitted to sign a treaty with Japan describing itself as an independent state with the same rights as Japan, a move the Peking Government did not protest.
*   **Thebes and Athens:** In contrast to Athens, the Thebans possessed no maritime dependencies and no fleet. This lack of maritime presence made them less afraid of offending the Great King than Athens had been.
*   **Armenia:** In an effort to detach Great Armenia from the Sassanid realm and ally it with Graeco-Roman civilization, Gregory established a religious hierarchy that was officially dependent on the Cappadocian. This effectively turned the country into a province of the Greek see of Cappadocia, though this hierarchical tie was eventually snapped.

## Strategic and Physical Dependencies
Dependency also appears in the context of military logistics and survival:

*   **Military Logistics:** During the Great War, the German position depended entirely on the great railway running from Lille by Valenciennes to Mezieres. To protect this vital link, Ludendorff focused on strengthening the German centre from St. Quentin to Douai (the main Hindenburg Line) and his left wing on the Meuse.
*   **Economic Survival:** In certain kingdoms characterized by perilous passes and towns on lofty hills, the population is noted for being excellent archers. Due to the high cost of stuffs, most of the people are dependent on the skins of beasts for their clothing, although great ladies wear expensive cotton cloth drawers—sometimes using 60 to 100 ells of stuff—to appear larger in the hips, which is considered a beauty by local men.
