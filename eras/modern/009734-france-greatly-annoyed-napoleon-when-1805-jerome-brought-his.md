# Jerome Bonaparte

## Early Life and Military Career
Jerome Bonaparte was born on November 15, 1784, in Ajaccio. During the early years of the French Revolution, he shared the fortunes of his family. He received an education at Juilly and was called to join his brother, the First Consul of France, in 1800. While serving in the Consular Guard, Jerome was wounded during a duel with the younger brother of General Davout.

Following this, he was transferred to the navy and cruised in the West Indies. During this period, a British cruiser blockaded him, prompting Jerome to leave his ship and travel through the United States.

## Marriage to Elizabeth Patterson
While in Baltimore, Jerome fell in love with Miss Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a Baltimore merchant named William Patterson. Despite being a minor, Jerome married her on December 24, 1803. This union was valid under American law, and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void.

However, Napoleon greatly disliked the marriage and refused to recognize its legality, viewing Jerome's actions as a disregard for the laws of France and military discipline. When Jerome brought his wife to Europe in 1805, the emperor ordered her to be excluded from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but he was unsuccessful. Consequently, Jerome was forced by Napoleon to separate from his wife. Elizabeth Patterson (often called Madame Bonaparte) stayed in England, where she gave birth to a son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870), before returning to Baltimore. She died in 1879.

## Naval and Military Service
In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. By November of that year, he sailed in a squadron under Admiral Willaumez with the intent to ravage the West Indies, though a storm scattered the fleet. After damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic, Jerome returned to France in August 1806.

Upon his return, Napoleon named him a prince of France and assigned him command of a division of South Germans for the 1806 campaign. Following the battle of Jena, Jerome received the surrender of several Prussian towns.

## Kingdom of Westphalia
Napoleon eventually annulled the marriage to Elizabeth Patterson by imperial decree and united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the Treaty of Tilsit, signed on July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia.

While ruling Westphalia, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor. He was known for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for incurring deep debts. Despite these personal tendencies, his kingdom saw some benefits from the connection to France: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, education received an impulse in its higher departments, and repressive laws against the Jews were removed.

## The Bonapartes of Baltimore
The union between Jerome and Elizabeth Patterson established a branch of the family in America known as the Bonapartes of Baltimore. Their son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870), resided primarily in Baltimore and was noted for his resemblance to the emperor. He maintained a good relationship with his father, who provided him with a large allowance. Their grandson, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), served in the French army during campaigns in Italy and the Crimea.
