# Paul Wayland Bartlett

## Early Life and Education
Paul Wayland Bartlett was an American sculptor born in New Haven, Connecticut. He was the son of Truman H. Bartlett, who was both a sculptor and an art critic. At the age of fifteen, Bartlett began his artistic studies in Paris. During this period, he studied under Fremiet and focused his work on modelling from animals located in the Jardin des Plantes.

## Career and Recognition
Bartlett achieved professional recognition at the Paris Salon of 1887, where he won a medal. In addition to his larger sculptures, he is noted for his remarkable bronze patinas depicting fish, insects, and reptiles.

## Principal Works
Bartlett's body of work includes a variety of statues and compositions located in several major cities:

*   **New York City:** "The Bear Tamer," which is housed in the Metropolitan Museum of Art.
*   **Paris:** An equestrian statue of Lafayette located in the Place du Carrousel. This work was presented to the French Republic by the school children of America.
*   **Washington, D.C.:** Sculptures of Michelangelo and Columbus, described as virile and powerful, located in the Congressional Library.
*   **Philadelphia:** The "Ghost Dancer" in the Pennsylvania Academy and an equestrian statue of McClellan.
*   **Boston:** A statue of Joseph Warren.

## Influence and Context
Bartlett's focus on animal modelling in Paris aligns with a broader tradition of French animal sculpture. This class of art was created by Antoine Louis Barye (1796-1875), who is regarded as the greatest artist of animal life of the French school. Barye's own inspiration came from watching wild beasts in the Jardin des Plantes. The artistic path established by Barye attracted several followers, including Fremiet, under whom Bartlett studied.
