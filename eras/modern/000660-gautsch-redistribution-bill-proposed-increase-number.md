# The Gautsch Redistribution Bill

The Gautsch redistribution bill was a legislative proposal introduced in Austria in 1906 by the premier, Baron Gautsch. The bill was part of a broader series of franchise reform measures designed to transition the Austrian electoral system toward universal suffrage.

## Political Context and Origins
The impetus for the reform arose from significant social unrest and political pressure. On September 15, 1905, a large working-class and socialist demonstration in support of universal suffrage occurred before the parliament at Budapest. This event, combined with the influence of the revolutionary movement in Russia, encouraged the Austrian Socialist party to push for similar franchise reforms within Austria.

Initial attempts to organize demonstrations in Vienna in early November resulted in some bloodshed. Similar collisions with police were frequent in other towns, including Graz and Prague. Baron Gautsch had previously expressed a preference for restricted reform and had discountenanced universal suffrage. However, he eventually changed his position, permitting a massive, orderly Socialist demonstration on November 28 in the Vienna Ringstrasse. During this event, workmen marched ten abreast in silence past the houses of parliament, causing traffic to be suspended for five hours.

## Provisions of the Reform Measures
Following the impact of the November demonstration, Baron Gautsch promised to introduce franchise reform by February, specifically framing the measures to ensure that racial minorities would not be overwhelmed by majorities of other races at the polls. On February 23, 1906, he presented a series of measures based on two main principles:
1. The establishment of the franchise on the basis of universal suffrage.
2. The abolition of the *curia*, or the electoral class system.

To protect various ethnic groups, the measures proposed dividing Austria into racial compartments, ensuring each race was assured against molestation from others. The reform bill proper specified that every male citizen over the age of 24 who met a one-year residential qualification would be enfranchised.

## The Redistribution Bill
The specific redistribution component of the proposal sought to reorganize the electoral map. The Gautsch redistribution bill proposed increasing the total number of constituencies from 425 to 455. These constituencies were to be allotted to each province based on a fixed number. Within those provinces, the allocation of constituencies to each race was to be determined by two factors: the number of people in that race and their tax-paying capacity.

## Outcome and Subsequent Reforms
Baron Gautsch eventually fell from power in April due to a disagreement with the Poles. His successor, Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst, took over the reform bills but resigned six weeks later. This resignation served as a protest against the crown's consent to a customs tariff in Hungary that was distinct from the joint Austro-Hungarian tariff established in the Szell-Koerber compact and enacted by the Reichsrath.

A new cabinet was formed on June 2 by Baron von Beck, a former instructor in administrative and constitutional law to Archduke Francis Ferdinand. Through skillful negotiation with different races and parties, and with the firm support of the emperor—who informed recalcitrant party leaders that the reform "must be accomplished"—Baron Beck reached a final agreement in October 1906. The reform was officially adopted on December 1. During these final negotiations, the number of constituencies was further increased to 516, divided according to the provinces.
