# Departments of Bolivia

The republic of Bolivia is divided into eight departments and one territory, which are further subdivided into 54 provinces, 415 cantons, 232 vice-cantons, 18 missions, and one colony. According to the census of 1900, the total population of these divisions was 1,766,451, with a total area of 640,226 square miles (though Gotha computations, adjusted for territory lost to Brazil in 1903, place the total area at 515,156 square miles).

## Departmental Demographics and Administration
The departments vary significantly in size and population. La Paz is the most populous department with 445,616 inhabitants and an area of 53,777 square miles. Santa Cruz is the largest department by area at 141,368 square miles, with a population of 209,592. Other departments include:
*   **Potosi:** Population 325,615; area 48,801 square miles.
*   **Cochabamba:** Population 328,163; area 23,328 square miles.
*   **Chuquisaca:** Population 204,434; area 26,418 square miles.
*   **Tarija:** Population 102,887; area 33,036 square miles.
*   **Oruro:** Population 86,081; area 19,127 square miles.
*   **El Beni:** Population 32,180; area 102,111 square miles.

The National Territory covers 192,260 square miles with a population of 31,883. The population figures from 1900 include a 5% addition for omissions, corrections, and estimated wild Indians.

## Urban Centers and National Capitals
The provincial capitals are the only populous towns in the republic. Four of these capitals—La Paz, Cochabamba, Oruro, and Sucre (also known as Chuquisaca)—have served as the national capital. While Sucre was originally chosen, the capital was located at La Paz following the revolution of 1898, as La Paz is the commercial metropolis and more accessible.

In the department of Potosi, other prominent towns include the silver mining center of Pulacayo (pop. 6,512), the railway junction of Uyuni (pop. 1,587), and the commercial and mining center of Tupiza (pop. 1,644). Other notable locations include Viacha in La Paz, the trading centers of Tarata and Totora in Cochabamba, and the large mission station of Ascension in Santa Cruz.

## Education and Higher Learning
Bolivia maintains a free and compulsory school system, though progress has been limited. As of 1901, there were 733 primary schools with 41,587 pupils. Secondary and superior instruction are managed through universities located in the departments of La Paz, Cochabamba, Oruro, Potosi, Santa Cruz, Tarija, and Sucre (Chuquisaca). All these universities provide instruction in law, while the first four also teach theology, and the first three (Sucre, La Paz, and Cochabamba) provide instruction in medicine. The universities at La Paz and Sucre are noted as the only ones sufficiently equipped to merit the title.

Specialized institutions include a military academy in La Paz, an agricultural school at Umala (La Paz), a mining and civil engineering school in Oruro, and commercial schools in Trinidad and Sucre.

## Religion and Ecclesiastical Division
The state recognizes and supports the Roman Apostolic Catholic religion; the public exercise of other worship is prohibited except in colonies or for resident foreigners. In 1900, the Roman Catholic population was 1,609,365.

The republic is divided into one archbishopric and three bishoprics. The archbishopric of La Plata, with its seat at Sucre, encompasses the departments of Tarija, Potosi, Oruro, and Chuquisaca, as well as the Chilean province of Antofagasta. The three bishoprics are seated in Santa Cruz, Cochabamba, and La Paz. Mission work, particularly among the Indians, is managed by the *Propaganda Fide*.

## Regional Industries and Agriculture
Agriculture and stock-raising are primary industries. Cattle are raised in the Mojos and Chiquitos llanos, as well as in the elevated districts and Chaco plains of Tarija and Chuquisaca. Smaller cattle-raising districts exist in Cochabamba, Potosi, and La Paz. Sheep are raised for wool in temperate regions, while llamas, alpacas, and the fur-bearing chinchilla are bred on the higher, colder plateaus.

Crop production varies by region:
*   **Cochabamba and La Paz:** Extensive cultivation of coca (especially in the Yungas province) and tobacco.
*   **Beni:** Production of superior grade cacao and rubber.
*   **Eastern Bolivia:** Cultivation of rice and sugar-cane, the latter primarily for rum production.
*   **General:** Quinoa is grown in large quantities as a staple food for natives.
