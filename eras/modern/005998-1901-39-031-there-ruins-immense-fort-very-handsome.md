# BUDAUN

Budaun is a town and district located in the Rohilkhand division of the United Provinces in British India. The town is situated near the left bank of the river Sot. As of 1901, the population of the town was 39,031.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town contains the ruins of an immense fort. It is also home to a very handsome mosque of imposing size, which is crowned with a dome. This mosque was built in 1223, utilizing materials from an ancient Hindu temple for a large part of its construction.

## History and Governance
According to tradition, Budaun was founded around A.D. 905. An inscription likely dating from the 12th century provides a list of twelve Rathor kings who reigned at Budaun, which was referred to as Vodamayuta. 

The first authentic historical event associated with the town is its capture by Kutb-ud-din in 1196. Following this event, Budaun became a very important post on the northern frontier of the Delhi empire. During the 13th century, two of the town's governors rose to the imperial throne: Rukn-ud-din Firoz and his father, Shams-ud-din Altamsh, who was the builder of the aforementioned great mosque.

In 1571, the town was burnt. Approximately one hundred years later, during the reign of Shah Jahan, the seat of the governorship was transferred to Bareilly, leading to a decline in the importance of Budaun. The town eventually came under the power of the Rohillas.

## British Administration and the 1857 Uprising
Budaun was established as the headquarters of a British district in 1838. During the events of 1857, the people of Budaun sided with the mutineers and established a native government. This government remained in place until April 1858, when General Penny achieved victory at Kakrala, resulting in the restoration of British authority.

## Education
The town provides various educational facilities. There is a high school for boys, and the American Methodist mission maintains several schools for girls.
