# Sir Almroth Wright and the Opsonic Index

Sir Almroth Wright was a medical researcher whose work significantly advanced the understanding of vaccines and the body's immune response. His contributions are most notably characterized by the discovery of opsonins in the blood and the subsequent development of the opsonic index to regulate the treatment of bacterial infections.

## Application of Vaccines
Historically, it was believed that vaccines—defined in the widest sense as dead organisms or attenuated cultures—were only useful as preventive measures to produce immunity against a specific organism. However, the research of Sir Almroth Wright demonstrated that such vaccines could be of service even after an infection had already occurred. This is particularly effective when the resulting disease is localized. In these instances, the vaccine stimulates a general reaction that may assist in the destruction of the invading organisms.

The fundamental principle of this treatment is to stimulate the general production of anti-substances throughout the body. These substances are then carried to the sites of bacterial growth, where they aid the cells of the tissues in destroying the organisms.

## The Opsonic Index
To control the administration of vaccines during the treatment of bacterial infections, Sir A. Wright and his co-workers introduced the method of observing the opsonic index. In this treatment, patients receive injections of varying amounts of a dead culture of the bacterium causing the infection. The primary goal is to raise the opsonic index of the serum, which serves as an indication of increased immunity.

The process of administering these vaccines typically follows a specific pattern:
* **Initial Phase:** The injection of a small quantity of vaccine usually causes the opsonic index to increase within a few days.
* **Negative Phase:** If an additional quantity of vaccine is injected following the initial rise, the opsonic index falls.
* **Recovery Phase:** This fall is later followed by a rise to a level higher than the original baseline.

By carefully choosing the timing and the amounts of the vaccine injections, a series of steps can be produced to raise the opsonic index to a high level.

## Clinical Regulation and Value
A critical aspect of registering the opsonic power is the avoidance of administering additional vaccines during the "negative phase" (when the opsonic index is low). Doing so could result in a further diminution of the opsonic action.

While a large number of favorable results have been published regarding treatment controlled by the observation of the opsonic index, the available records suggest that it would be unwise to offer a decided opinion on the ultimate value of the method at this time.
