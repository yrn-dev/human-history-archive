# The Bahamas: Government and Trade

The Bahamas is a British colony characterized as a partially self-governing community. It is categorized alongside other territories such as Bermuda, Jamaica, and Malta as possessing an appointed executive and elected legislatures.

## Administrative Structure
The colony is governed by a British governor who represents the crown. The governor is supported by two distinct councils, each consisting of nine members:
*   **Executive Council:** Comprised of both official and unofficial members.
*   **Legislative Council:** Comprised of members nominated by the crown.

Additionally, there is a legislative assembly consisting of 29 members who represent 15 electoral districts. In colonies like the Bahamas that possess representative institutions but lack responsible government, the crown generally does not legislate by order in council. Instead, laws are created by the governor with the concurrence of the legislative bodies.

## Electoral Qualifications
The franchise for the legislative assembly is extended to coloured and white men who meet the following criteria:
*   **Age and Residency:** Must be at least 21 years of age and have resided in the colony for a minimum of twelve months.
*   **Property Requirements:** Must possess land valued at £5 or more, or be a householder for six months with a rental of at least £2:18s. in New Providence, or £1:4s. in other islands.

To qualify as a member of the assembly, an individual must possess personal or real estate valued at £200.

## Economy and Trade
The financial status of the colony is marked by an average annual revenue and expenditure of approximately £75,000, though expenditure slightly exceeds revenue. The colony maintains a public debt of about £105,000.

Trade statistics indicate an average annual value of imports exceeding £300,000, while exports are valued at £200,000. The colony's shipping activity is significant, with the average annual tonnage of ships entering and clearing exceeding 1,000,000.

## Infrastructure and Social Services
The Bahamas does not have railways. While a few roads exist in various locations, New Providence possesses good roads. Communication with the exterior is facilitated by a cable connecting Nassau with West Jupiter in Florida.

The government provides support for elementary free schools, which are overseen by a nominated board of education, while local supervision is handled by committees that are partly elected. Higher education is available through higher schools and the Queen's College located in Nassau. Additionally, Nassau serves as the seat of a bishopric of the Church of England, which was established in 1861.
