# Life and Correspondence by Ferdinand Brock Tupper

## Overview of the Work
*Life and Correspondence* is a biographical work written by Ferdinand Brock Tupper. The author was the nephew of the subject of the biography. A second edition of this text was published in London in 1847. According to archival records, this particular volume remains the best account of the subject's life, although subsequent biographies were produced later by Lady Edgar (published in Toronto and London in 1905) and D.R. Read (published in Toronto in 1894).

For instance, Thomas Brock (1847–) was an English sculptor and a chief pupil of Foley. Brock's career was marked by a transition toward the new romantic movement, resulting in ideal works such as "Eve," "The Genius of Poetry," and "The Moment of Peril." He was known for his portraiture, including busts of Queen Victoria and Lord Leighton, and statues of Dr. Philpott (bishop of Worcester) and Sir Richard Owen. His larger works include a colossal equestrian statue of Edward the Black Prince, erected in Leeds in 1901, and the Imperial Memorial to Queen Victoria located in front of Buckingham Palace. Thomas Brock was elected as a full member of the Royal Academy in 1891, having previously been an associate in 1883.
