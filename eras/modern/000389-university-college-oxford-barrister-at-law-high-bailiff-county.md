# University College, Oxford; Barrister-at-Law; High Bailiff of County

## University College, Oxford
University College, Oxford, is an academic institution associated with individuals such as William Price James. The university of Oxford, alongside Cambridge, has made efforts since 1850 to promote the study of law. The legal curriculum at these universities is designed to provide students with an elementary acquaintance with the rules of modern English law and a sound fundamental knowledge of general principles. 

Subjects taught within this curriculum include:
*   Jurisprudence, International law, Constitutional law, and Roman law.
*   Criminal law, Procedure and Evidence.
*   The law of Tort, Contract, and Real and Personal Property.

Despite these offerings, the law schools and the law tripos are noted for their exclusively academical character and their remoteness from the law courts.

## High Bailiff of County
The role of the high-bailiff is governed by the County Courts Act 1888. According to this act, one or more high-bailiffs are appointed by the judge and may be removed by the lord-chancellor. 

### Duties and Powers
The primary duty of the high-bailiff is to execute all writs, precepts, and warrants issued by the court, as well as to serve all orders and summonses. The high-bailiff is empowered to appoint "able and fit persons" to serve as bailiffs to assist in these duties; the high-bailiff maintains the authority to dismiss these assistants at his pleasure.

### Legal Responsibilities and Restrictions
The high-bailiff bears responsibility for all defaults and acts committed by himself and the bailiffs he appoints, similar to the responsibility a county sheriff holds for his officers. Under section 49 of the act, bailiffs are answerable for any neglect, omission, or connivance regarding the levy of execution. 

Legal actions cannot be brought against a bailiff acting under court order without six days' notice (s. 54). Furthermore, if a bailiff is given a warrant to give possession of a tenement, he is justified in entering the premises and giving possession, provided the entry occurs between 4 P.M. and 9 A.M. (s. 142). Additionally, the Law of Distress Amendment Act 1888 stipulates that no person may act as a bailiff to levy distress for rent unless they have been authorized by a county-court judge.

William Price James is noted as having served as the High Bailiff of County Courts in Cardiff.
