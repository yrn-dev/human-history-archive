# Austro-Hungarian Navy

## Overview and Administration
The Austro-Hungarian navy functioned primarily as a coast defence force. Its administration was handled by the naval department of the ministry of war. Within the broader political structure of the Dual Monarchy—established by the 1867 "Compromise" (*Ausgleich*)—the navy was categorized as one of the common affairs affecting both Austria and Hungary. Consequently, it fell under a common administration, though this excluded the annual voting of recruits.

## Fleet Composition and Infrastructure
As of 1905, the navy's fleet consisted of the following vessels:
* 9 modern battleships
* 3 armoured cruisers
* 5 cruisers
* 4 torpedo gunboats
* 20 destroyers
* 26 torpedo boats

Additionally, the navy maintained a flotilla of monitors specifically for the Danube. During the same period in 1905, a naval programme was underway to expand the fleet, with plans to build 12 armourclads, 6 third-class cruisers, 5 second-class cruisers, and a number of torpedo boats.

The fleet's headquarters were located at Pola, which served as the principal harbour and naval arsenal for Austria. Trieste served as another major naval station.

## Personnel and Recruitment
The navy drew significant manpower from the Dalmatian coast. In Dalmatia, a crownland of Austria, conscription was in force. This region provided the navy with many of its best recruits, a tradition that had existed since the time of the Venetian navy.

## Post-War Dissolution
Following the conflict, the Austro-Hungarian navy was subject to strict terms of surrender to the Allies. The requirements included:
* The surrender of the entire Austro-Hungarian navy.
* The disarming of twenty-one specified auxiliary cruisers, which were to be treated as merchantmen.
* The breaking up of all warships under construction, including submarines, with the materials restricted to industrial use.
* The surrender of all naval arms and material.
* A prohibition on the future use of submarines.
