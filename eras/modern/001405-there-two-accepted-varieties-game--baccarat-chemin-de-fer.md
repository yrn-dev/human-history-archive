# Baccarat Chemin de Fer

Baccarat is a gambling card-game of unknown origin that is believed to have been introduced into France from Italy during the reign of Charles VIII. There are two accepted varieties of the game: *baccarat banque* (also known as *a deux tableaux*) and *baccarat chemin de fer* (meaning railway).

## Setup and Participants
In the *baccarat chemin de fer* variety, six full packs of cards are utilized. These packs are shuffled first by a croupier, and subsequently by any players who wish to do so. The game accommodates between three and eleven players. To facilitate betting, counters are generally used; these are sold by the banker and redeemed by the banker later.

## Roles and Betting
The role of the banker is rotational. The croupier takes several cards from the top of the pack and passes them to the player on his right (though sometimes the left), who then becomes the banker. This player retains the position until he loses, at which point the deal passes to the next player in order. All other participants are referred to as "punters."

To begin a round, the banker establishes a stake. The punters then place their own stakes. A punter may choose to "go bank" by announcing "*Banco!*", which indicates an intention to play against the banker's entire stake.

## Gameplay and Card Values
The dealer distributes cards face downwards in a specific sequence: one to the right for the punters, one to the dealer himself, a third to the left for the punters, and a final card to the dealer. 

The value of the cards is determined as follows:
*   **Court cards and tens:** Count as nothing.
*   **All other cards:** Count as the number of their pips.

## Winning and Drawing Rules
Each punter examines their cards. If a punter holds a value of 8 or 9, they turn the card up and announce it, ending the hand. In this scenario, the player with the highest stake plays for both punters. If the turned card is superior to the banker's card, the banker pays each punter the amount of their stake. If the banker's card is better, the banker wins all stakes.

If no player holds an 8 or 9, no announcement is made, and the banker deals an additional card to the player on his right. The rules for accepting this second card are as follows:
*   **Value of 6 or 7:** The player will refuse the card to avoid overrunning.
*   **Value of 5:** The player may accept or refuse the card based on their own judgment. If refused, the card is offered to the second punter.
*   **Value of 0 (baccarat), 1, 2, 3, or 4:** The punter always accepts the second card.

The second card is always turned face upwards on the table.
