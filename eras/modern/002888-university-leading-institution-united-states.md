# Johns Hopkins University

## Overview and Academic Standing
Johns Hopkins University is recognized as a leading institution in the United States specifically for graduate study. Located in Baltimore, it is part of a city that ranks high as an educational centre. The university maintains one of the principal libraries in the city.

## Educational Context in Baltimore
The university exists alongside a diverse array of other educational and professional institutions in Baltimore. These include:

*   **Higher Education and Specialized Colleges:** Goucher College, established in 1888 as a Methodist institution for women, is noted as one of the best of its kind in the southern United States. Morgan College, a Methodist institution opened in 1876, provides college education to coloured young people. Loyola College was founded in 1852 for the training of Catholics.
*   **Professional and Technical Schools:** The city hosts the University of Maryland and Baltimore University, both of which provide courses in dentistry, medicine, and law. Other professional institutions include the Baltimore Medical College, the Woman's Medical College, the College of Physicians and Surgeons, the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, and the Baltimore Law School. The Maryland College of Pharmacy became part of the University of Maryland in 1904.
*   **Theological and Vocational Training:** St. Mary's Seminary, established by the Society of St Sulpice in 1791, is cited as the oldest Catholic theological seminary in the United States; St. Joseph's Seminary is also present. For vocational preparation, the McDonogh farm school—founded via a bequest from John McDonogh—utilizes a 835-acre farm and shops for carpentry, machines, and printing to prepare poor boys for entry into any college in the country.
*   **Other Institutions:** The city contains a Polytechnic Institute, state normal schools (the older of which opened in 1867), and high schools for both coloured and white pupils.

## Related Cultural Institutions
The educational landscape of the city is further supported by the Peabody Institute. Founded in 1859 by George Peabody, a former resident of Baltimore, the institute is an important factor in promoting the fine arts, literature, and science. Like Johns Hopkins University, the Peabody Institute maintains one of the city's principal libraries.
