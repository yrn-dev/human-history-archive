# Historical Documents and Bibliographic Collections

The preservation of human history relies upon the compilation of primary documents, the creation of comprehensive bibliographies, and the publication of scholarly historical accounts. Across various regions and eras, these collections serve to supersede earlier works and provide a structured understanding of political, economic, and cultural developments.

## European Historical Collections

In Bohemia, the collection of historical documents titled *Fontes Rerum Bohemicarum*, published in Prague during the latter part of the 19th century, has superseded previous works such as Marquard Freher's *Rerum Bohemicarum Antiqui Scriptores*. Similarly, the historical research of Pelzl, Pubitschka, and De Florgy was superseded by Frantisek Palacky's *Geschichte von Bohmen* (1844-1867), which covers history up to the year 1526. Subsequent history after 1526 has been addressed by Gindely and Rezek. Other notable works include Professor Adolf Bachmann's *Geschichte Bohmens* (published in volumes in 1899 and 1905) and the continuous accounts of Bohemian history from the 15th century provided by Professor Ernest Denis.

In the study of Byzantine history, a collective edition of chroniclers and historians known as the *Paris Corpus* was initiated under Louis XIV and continued between 1648 and 1819. This collection was later republished with additions between 1828 and 1878 as the *Corpus scriptorum historiae Byzantinae*. Additionally, J. B. Bury edited a collection starting in 1898 that includes oriental and Byzantine historical works.

## North American Records and Bibliographies

The history of Illinois is documented through various standard histories, including those by H. Davidson and B. Stuvé (1874) and J. Moses (1889). For the colonial period and French explorations, Edward G. Mason's *Chapters from Illinois History* (1901) is noted. The period leading up to 1818 is supported by the *Territorial Records of Illinois*, the *St Clair Papers* by W. H. Smith, and works by C. E. Carter and R. L. Schuyler. Contemporary sources for the years 1818-1846 include the writings of Governor John Reynolds and Governor Thomas Ford.

Administrative and legal documentation for Illinois includes the *Blue Book of the State of Illinois* (1903), H. B. Hurd's *Revised Statutes of Illinois* (1903), and the *Annotated Statutes of the State of Illinois* by Starr and Curtis (1896).

In Louisiana, the first historian was Le Page du Pratz. Other significant diplomatic and historical records include Barbé-Marbois's *Histoire de la Louisiane, et de la cession de colonie par la France aux États-Unis* (1829). The scholarly research of Charles E. A. Gayarré produced several editions of the history of Louisiana, covering French, Spanish, and American domination.

## African and Middle Eastern Archives

Documentation regarding the British East Africa Protectorate includes annual *Reports* by the governor issued since 1896 and the *Précis of Information* (1901) issued by the War Office. Sir Charles Eliot's *The East Africa Protectorate* (1905) provides a comprehensive account of economic resources. The work of the Imperial British East Africa Company is documented in P.L. McDermont's *British East Africa or Ibea* (1895).

In the Middle East, Miguel Casiri, a Maronite scholar, produced the *Bibliotheca Arabico-Hispana Escurialensis* (1760-1770). This work is a catalogue of over 1,800 Arabic manuscripts found in the library of the Escorial, including quotations from historical works and manuscripts containing information on the wars between Christians and Moors in Spain.

## Sacred and Canonical Texts

The archive identifies the Old Testament as a remarkable collection of ancient documents known to Christian peoples, providing lights on the development of civilization and the spirit of human affairs during the struggles of Assyria and Egypt. These documents concern the Israelites.

Regarding the New Testament, Westcott's *General Survey of the History of the Canon of the New Testament* (with a 7th edition in 1896) is considered a standard work that includes a valuable appendix of documents. Other reliable sources for texts include the *Kleine Texte* (edited by Lietzmann from 1902) and the *Dictionary of the Bible* (1898-1904).
