# Glass Manufacturing in Selected U.S. Cities (1900-1910)

During the early 20th century, several cities across the United States established themselves as significant centers for the production of glass, ranging from specialized bottle manufacturing to plate and opalescent glass.

## Bridgeton, New Jersey
Bridgeton, the county-seat of Cumberland county and a port of entry located on Cohansey creek, emerged as a major industrial hub for glass production. By 1905, the city's glass industry—which focused chiefly on the production of glass bottles—was valued at $1,252,795. This output represented 42.3% of the total value of all factory products within the city. Due to this production volume, Bridgeton ranked eighth among all cities in the United States in the glass industry.

## Indiana Glass Centers
The state of Indiana hosted multiple cities with significant glass manufacturing interests, specifically in the regions surrounding Indianapolis.

### Kokomo
Located on the Wildcat River, Kokomo serves as the county-seat of Howard county. The city's manufacturing sector includes the production of plate, opalescent, and flint glass. Between 1900 and 1905, the total value of Kokomo's factory products grew by 77.1%, rising from $2,062,156 to $3,651,105. In 1905, the glass product specifically was valued at $864,567, accounting for 23.7% of the city's total factory value.

### Marion
Marion, the county-seat of Grant county and situated on the Mississinewa River, is located within the natural gas region of the state. Glass is listed among its various manufactures, alongside steel, paper, pulp, and leather. In 1905, the total value of factory products in Marion was $4,290,166. Of this amount, the glass product alone was valued at $1,042,057, representing 24.3% of the total.

The city experienced a massive increase in total factory product value between 1900 ($1,403,026) and 1905 ($6,832,007), a growth rate of 386.9%, which was the highest for any city in the state with a population of 8,000 or more in 1900.
