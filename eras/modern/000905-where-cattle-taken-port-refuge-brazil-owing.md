# Cattle Transport and Ports of Refuge in Brazil

In the context of maritime law and the application of General Average (G.A.), the use of ports of refuge in Brazil has served as a precedent for determining the allowance of losses related to livestock transport. These legal determinations often center on whether damages or losses of value sustained by cargo are the result of necessary actions taken for the common safety of the ship and its contents.

## General Average and Ports of Refuge
Under maritime regulations, specifically Rule XII, damage sustained by cargo when discharged at a port of refuge to facilitate ship repairs is generally allowed as General Average. While Rule VIII allows for damage sustained during lightering to get a ship off a strand, Rule XII addresses the specific necessity of landing cargo for repairs. However, the law is less clear regarding losses sustained while goods are stored on land, such as losses caused by fire or theft. It is argued that if the landing was necessitated by a General Average act—such as the cutting away of masts—the resulting loss should be made good due to the special risks the goods were exposed to.

## The Case of Cattle in Brazil
A specific legal precedent regarding the transport of cattle was established in the case of *Anglo-Argentine &c. Agency v. Temperley Shipping Co.* (1899, 2 Q.B. 403). In this instance, cattle were taken into a port of refuge in Brazil because the ship had sustained accidental damage. Due to this detour and the circumstances of the voyage, the cattle could not legally be landed at their original destination of Deptford. 

Consequently, the cattle had to be transported to another port, Antwerp. Because the value of the cattle was significantly lower at Antwerp than it would have been at the intended destination, this loss of value was allowed as General Average.

## Limitations on Shipper Allowances
Despite the allowance for the loss of value of the livestock, there are strict limitations on what a shipper can claim under Rules X and XI. While Rule XI allows for the wages and maintenance of the master, officers, and crew during the detention of a ship for repairs, this is often viewed as a concession to the shipowner. 

Under common law, these allowances are rarely made when the detention is to repair accidental damage. Furthermore, a shipper of cattle is not entitled to recover certain additional expenses incurred during the stay at a repairing port. These non-allowable expenses include:
* The extra provisions for cattlemen on board.
* The extra wages of the cattlemen.
* The additional fodder consumed by the cattle during the period of detention.

## Brazilian Geography and Cattle Industry
Brazil possesses various regions and ports that support the movement of goods and livestock. For example, Corumba is a fortified military post and river port located on the western bank of the Paraguay river. It serves as the commercial entrepot for the state of Matto Grosso and is situated near the Bolivian frontier. Despite an extremely hot climate, the areas surrounding Corumba contain many large cattle farms.

In other regions, such as the Atlantic state of Bahia, cattle-raising was historically a principal industry in the interior. However, this industry has been nearly extinguished in that region due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts, which were exacerbated by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve the grass.
