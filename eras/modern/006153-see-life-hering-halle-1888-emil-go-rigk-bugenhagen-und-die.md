# Sophus Bugge and Related Philological Studies

## Sophus Bugge
Sophus Bugge (1833–1907) was a Norwegian philologist born on January 5, 1833, in Laurvik, Norway. His academic preparation took place in Berlin, Copenhagen, and Christiania. In 1866, he was appointed as a professor of Old Norse and comparative philology at Christiania University.

Throughout his career, Bugge produced a vast number of books focusing on folklore and philology. His scholarly interests were broad, leading him to make significant contributions to the study of several languages, including the Romance, Oscan, Umbrian, Etruscan, and Celtic languages. He also wrote extensively on Runic inscriptions and engaged in the collection of Norwegian traditions and folk-songs.

## Major Works and Theories
Bugge's principal scholarly achievement was a critical edition of the elder Edda, titled *Norroen Fornkvoedi*, which was published in Christiania in 1867. In this work and his broader research, Bugge maintained the theory that the earlier sagas and the songs of the *Edda* were largely based on Latin and Christian traditions that had been imported into Scandinavian literature via England.

His other notable publications include:
* *Gamle Norske Folkeviser* (1858), a collection of Old Norse folk-songs.
* *Bidrag til den aeldste skaldedigtnings historie*, published in Christiania in 1894.
* *Helge-digtene i den Aeldre Edda* (Copenhagen, 1896), which was later translated into English as *The Home of the Eddic Poems* in 1899.
* *Norsk Sagafortaelling op Sagaskrivning i Island*, published in Christiania in 1901.

Sophus Bugge died on July 8, 1907.
