## Conceptual Note on Burlesque
While *The Burlesque Napoleon* is a biographical work, the term "burlesque" refers to a specific form of comic art. Derived from the Italian *burla* (meaning a joke or playful trick), it involves the imitation of a work of art to excite laughter through distortion, exaggeration, or a ludicrous contrast between subject and style. This can include making common men speak like gods or vice versa. 

In literature and drama, burlesque differs from parody in that it relies on broader and coarser effects rather than a close following of style. Historical examples include the *Batrachomachia* (a travesty of the Homeric epic) and the works of P. Scarron in France. In the later 19th century, the term was also applied to musical dramatic compositions, such as those associated with the Gaiety theatre in London.
