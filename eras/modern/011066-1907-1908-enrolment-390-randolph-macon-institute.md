# Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies

The Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies is a group of five educational institutions under the control of a single board of trustees. This administrative structure allows the work of the system's preparatory schools to be correlated with the work of its colleges. The institutions are associated with the Methodist Episcopal Church, South.

## Randolph-Macon College
Located in Ashland, a village in Hanover County, Virginia, Randolph-Macon College is one of the oldest Methodist Episcopal colleges in the United States. The college received its charter in 1830 and opened in 1832 near Boydton in Mecklenburg County, Virginia. In 1868, the institution was moved to Ashland. During the 1907-1908 academic year, the college had a faculty of 16 and a student body of 150. The college is also responsible for publishing an endowed historical series known as *The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College*.

## Other System Institutions
The system includes several other colleges and academies distributed across Virginia:

*   **Randolph-Macon Woman's College:** Located in Lynchburg, Virginia, this college opened in 1893. It is conducted by a self-perpetuating board under the auspices of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South. In the 1907-1908 period, the college had an enrolment of 390 students.
*   **Randolph-Macon Institute:** Situated in Danville, Virginia, this institute is designated for girls. It was admitted into the "System" in 1897.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Bedford City):** Established in 1890, this academy is for boys.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy (Front Royal):** Established in 1892, this academy is also for boys.

## Regional Context
The institutions are located in various Virginia cities and villages. Ashland, where the main college is situated, was settled in 1845 and incorporated in 1856; it is located 17 miles northwest of Richmond and serves as a favorite resort from that city. Lynchburg, home to the Woman's College, is a principal manufacturing city on the James River, noted for its boot and shoe industry. Danville, home to the Randolph-Macon Institute, is a city on the Dan River known as a major wholesale market for loose-leaf tobacco and a center for cotton mills.
