# Proceedings S.P.R.

The *Proceedings S.P.R.* is a recorded body of work that includes documentation on automatic writing and its associated psychological and paranormal phenomena. Within these records, particular attention is given to the manifestations of telepathy and the potential for communication with entities beyond normal consciousness.

## Automatic Writing and Telepathy
The *Proceedings S.P.R.* contains records of automatic writing, which frequently shows indications of telepathy. Among the most remarkable series of these writings are those produced by Mrs. Piper, an American medium who executed them while in a state of trance. These specific writings are noted for exhibiting remarkable telepathic powers; consequently, some observers believe they indicate communication with the spirits of the dead.

## Characteristics of Automatic Writing
Automatic writing generally brings to the surface half-formed impressions and the debris of lapsed memories that have not reached the focus of consciousness, similar to the material of dreams. However, some cases indicate more complex occurrences. Spontaneous instances of automatic writing have produced:
* Nonsense verses, puns, and anagrams.
* Occasional obscenities or blasphemies.
* Characteristics that diverge markedly from the agent's normal consciousness.

In one well-known case recorded by Th. Flournoy in *Des Indes a la planete Mars*, an automatist produced writing in an unknown character that was claimed to be the Martian language.

## Physical and Technical Manifestations
While the writing typically resembles the agent's ordinary handwriting, there are instances of marked differences, and a single automatist may use two or three distinct handwritings. In some cases, imitations of the handwriting of other people, whether living or dead, are produced.

A specific phenomenon known as *Spiegelschrift* occurs when the writing is reversed, making it readable only through a looking-glass. This ability is often linked to a liability for spontaneous somnambulism. During the act of writing, the arm and hand are often insensible. Furthermore, records indicate some cases where the automatist appears to guide the hand via a special extension of the muscular sense rather than by sight, a point noted in the *Proceedings American S.P.R.* (p. 554) by W. James.

## Clinical Applications
Beyond the study of telepathy, the ability of automatic writing to communicate with subconscious strata of the personality has been utilized by Pierre Janet and others. This has been applied to cases of other forms of dissociation of consciousness and hystero-epilepsy. For example, a patient suffering from hysterical convulsions who cannot respond to oral appeals may sometimes be induced to answer questions addressed to the hand in writing. This method can be used to accept therapeutic suggestions or to reveal the secret of the malady.
