# Austrian-Ottoman Conflicts (1662–1699)

The history of the Austrian monarchy during the reign of Emperor Leopold I was significantly shaped by a series of military campaigns against the Ottoman Empire. Leopold I, who succeeded his father Ferdinand in 1657 and was crowned emperor in 1658, ruled for 48 years. His external policy was heavily influenced by the struggle to drive Ottoman power from Hungary and establish the Austrian position regarding the Slav peoples of the Balkans for the following century.

## The War of 1662–1664
The first major conflict of this period was triggered by Ottoman aggression in Transylvania. This war concluded on August 1, 1664, when Montecuculi achieved a victory over the grand vizier at St Gothard on the Raab. Despite this military success, the general political climate prevented Emperor Leopold from fully capitalizing on the victory. The resulting peace of Vasvar, signed on August 10, 1664, left the Turks in possession of the fortress of Ersekujvar (Neuhaeusel) and Nagyvarad (Grosswardein). Additionally, Transylvania was recognized as an independent principality.

## Internal Unrest and the 1683 Invasion
The subsequent war with the Turks was a direct result of Leopold's internal policies within Hungary. In 1658, the suppression of the constitution and the persecution of Protestants led to a widespread conspiracy. Although the palatinate and the constitution were eventually restored with some concessions to Protestants following a period of arbitrary government between 1672 and 1679, discontent persisted.

In 1683, spurred on by Louis XIV and invited by Hungarian malcontents, the Turks invaded Hungary. They overran the country and advanced to the walls of Vienna. The Austrian capital was saved on September 12, 1683, by a victory gained over the Turks by John Sobieski, King of Poland.

## Expansion and Aftermath
The victory at Vienna served as the first in a series of successes that eventually drove Ottoman power out of Hungary. These campaigns continued through 1699. The geopolitical shifts resulting from these wars determined the Austrian attitude toward Turkey and the Balkan Slavs for a hundred years.
