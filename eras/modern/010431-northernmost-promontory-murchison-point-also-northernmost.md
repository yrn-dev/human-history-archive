# Northernmost Geographic Points and Regions

The archive's records identify various northernmost points, promontories, and regions across different continents and historical contexts, ranging from the American mainland to the ancient settlements of Asia Minor and Africa.

## North American Geography
In British North America, the Boothia Peninsula (also known as *Boothia Felix*) is located within the Franklin district. It spans an area of 13,100 square miles, situated between 69 deg. 30' and 71 deg. 50' N. and 91 deg. 30' and 97 deg. W. The peninsula's northernmost promontory is Murchison Point, which is also the northernmost point of the American mainland. 

The peninsula was discovered by Captain (later Sir James) Ross during an expedition from 1829 to 1833 and named for Sir Felix Booth, who helped fit out the expedition. Boothia is almost separated from the main continent by inlets and lakes, and is separated from North Somerset Island by the Bellot Strait. Ross first distinctly localized the north magnetic pole on the western side of the peninsula at 70 deg. 5' N., 96 deg. 47' W.

In the United States, the Bronx is identified as the northernmost of the five boroughs of New York City, a status it has held since 1898. Previously, it was a district of several towns in Westchester county, New York, with English and Dutch settlements established between 1640 and 1650.

## Ancient Asia Minor and Greece
In the region of Asia Minor, Herodotus reckoned twelve ancient Æolic foundations. Among these, Pitana was the northernmost of the twelve, located between the promontory of Kanê and the mouth of the river Kaïkus. The promontory of Kanê closes the Elæitic gulf to the northward. Regarding the Ionic settlements in the same region, Phokæa is identified as the northernmost.

In the Peloponnesus, the region of Achaia consisted of a narrow strip of level land and projecting spurs. This land lay between the Corinthian gulf and the northernmost mountains of the peninsula, which include Erymanthus, Skollis, Aroania, Krathis, and the eminence of Kyllênê.

## Africa and the Mediterranean
Ancient Phenician colonies included Utica, which was situated nearly on the northernmost point of the African coast in the gulf now known as the gulf of Tunis.

In the context of ancient geography and the expeditions of Alexander, the Hyrkanian or Caspian Sea was known to geographers as the northernmost of four gulfs proceeding from the exterior ocean.

## Other Regional Northernmost Points
In the South Pacific Ocean, the Loyalty Islands (belonging to France) consist of several islands, including Lifu, Tiga, and Maré. Among these, Uea (or Uvea) is the northernmost.

In the Turkish vilayet of Bagdad, the city lies on the river Tigris at 33deg 20' N. and 44deg 24' E. At this location, the Tigris and Euphrates are at their closest point, separated by little more than 25 miles. They are connected by the Sakhlawieh canal (formerly Isa), which is the northernmost of a series of canals that formerly united the two waterways and irrigated the plain.

## Historical Populations
The Cimmerians are described as perhaps the northernmost portion of the great Thracian name. They were conterminous with the Getæ on the Danube and previously occupied much of the territory between the Palus Mæotis and the Ister before being expelled by the Scythians in the seventh century B.C.
