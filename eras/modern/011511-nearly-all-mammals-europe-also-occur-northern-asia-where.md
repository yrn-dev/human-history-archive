# Fauna and Flora of Northern Asia and Related Regions

## Zoological Classification and Regionality
The northern portion of Asia, extending as far south as the Himalaya, is not zoologically distinct from Europe. Together with the strip of Africa located north of the Atlas mountains, these areas comprise the Palaearctic region, a primary zoological division of the earth defined by Dr. Sclater. This region is distinct from the Indian region, which encompasses south-eastern Asia and the islands of Borneo, Java, Sumatra, and the Philippines. Additionally, the extreme south-west of the continent—including southern Persia, Palestine, and Arabia, reaching to Sind and Baluchistan—belongs to the Ethiopian region, which extends over Africa south of the Atlas.

## Mammals of Northern Asia
The mammal fauna of northern Asia is closely linked to that of Europe, with nearly all European mammals also occurring in northern Asia. However, the Palaearctic fauna in northern Asia is enriched by numerous additional species, primarily those restricted to temperate and cold regions.

### Carnivores and Insectivores
Carnivora are numerous, especially species that frequent cold climates, including foxes, wolves, weasels, and bears. The northern lynx (*L. lynx* or *L. borealis*) is found in northern Asia, Russia, and Scandinavia. In the order Insectivora, hedgehogs, shrews, and various forms of moles prevail.

### Rodents and Ungulates
Rodents are well represented by mice, squirrels, and hares. Specific characteristic forms in northern Asia include pikas (tailless hares, *Lagomys*) and marmots (*Arctomys*). The order Ungulata includes several peculiar forms of antelope, such as the yellow sheep (*Gazella gutturosa*) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*). In the mountain chains of Asia, as many as ten or twelve wild species of *Ovis* (sheep) are found. Deer are also characteristic, particularly those of the genus *Cervus*. The musk deer (*Moschus*) is one of the most peculiar types and is restricted to northern Asia.

### Other Mammals
The Quadrumana (monkeys) are nearly unrepresented in this region; one species is found in Japan, and one or two others occur in Tibet and northern China. Insectivorous bats are numerous, though the frugivorous division is represented only by a single species in Japan.

## Ornithology of Northern Asia
The birds of northern Asia are even more closely allied to those of Europe than the mammals are. Nearly three fourths of the well-known species of Europe extend through Siberia into the islands of the Japanese empire. Tropical forms are absent, while groups characteristic of temperate and cold regions are highly developed.

The genus *Phasianus* is particularly peculiar to this region, with all its wild species restricted to northern Asia. Additionally, eared pheasants (*Crossoptilon*) and gold pheasants (*Thaumalea*) are confined to specific districts within the mountains of north eastern Asia. Among the *Passeres*, species of grosbeaks, linnets, finches, stone chats, and larks are well developed.

## Comparative Flora and Regional Botany
The flora of northern Persia and Asia Minor differs very little from that of southern Europe. In areas with abundant rain, mountains are clothed in various Coniferae and forests of *Corylus*, *Carpinus*, *Acer*, *Ulmus*, *Fagus*, and *Quercus*. Of these, *Fagus* is the only genus not found on the Himalaya. The evergreen oak of southern Europe (*Quercus Ilex*) is found in forests as far east as the Sutlej.

In northern Afghanistan, the flora approximates that of the contiguous western Himalaya. Higher parts of Persia and Afghanistan are characterized by thistles and Boraginaceae, as well as gigantic Umbelliferae—including *Prangos*, *Peucedanum*, *Bubon*, *Dorema*, *Galbanum*, and *Ferula*—some of which extend into Tibet.

## Comparison with Greenland
While it was once believed that Greenland's flora and fauna were essentially European, evidence suggests otherwise. Greenland's land mammals are more American than European. The reindeer, the dog, the Eskimo, the white polar wolf, the banded lemming (*Cuniculus torquatus*), and the musk-ox all came from America. Other mammals, such as the Arctic hare, the polar fox, the polar bear, and the stoat (*Mustela erminea*), are circumpolar.

Regarding birds, of the sixty-one species breeding in Greenland, eight are European-Asiatic and four are American, with the remainder being North Pacific, North Atlantic, or circumpolar. The vascular plants (approximately 310 species) are nearly equivalent in American and European-Asiatic elements, with forty species being American and forty-four being European-Asiatic.
