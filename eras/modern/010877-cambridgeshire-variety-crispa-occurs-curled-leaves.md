# Cambridgeshire

Cambridgeshire is a region in the east of England characterized by significant agricultural production and specific botanical observations.

## Fruit Cultivation
Fruit is grown extensively throughout Cambridgeshire and the counties adjacent to it in the east of England. A primary center for this industry is Cottenham, where the presence of Lower Greensand provides one of the best soils for fruit-culture. In Cottenham, approximately one thousand acres are dedicated to fruit, and nearly an equal acreage is used for asparagus, although fruit is increasingly replacing asparagus in this area.

The most widely grown fruits in Cottenham include strawberries, gooseberries, and currants, followed by raspberries, plums, and apples. Among the plum varieties, the Victoria is the most favored, followed by Gisborne, Tsar, and Rivers's Early Prolific.

Other notable fruit-growing areas in the county include:
* **Willingham:** This center produces raspberries, gooseberries, and plums, and is specifically noted for the production of outdoor tomatoes.
* **Cambridge:** Greengages are grown largely in the vicinity of Cambridge.
* **Wisbech:** Situated partly in Cambridgeshire and partly in Norfolk, Wisbech is the center of an extensive fruit district. Raspberries, strawberries, and gooseberries are grown here in large quantities; on a single day, as many as 80 tons of gooseberries have been sent away from the Wisbech station.

## Markets and Distribution
The chief market for fruit from this region is London, which receives about half of the fruit sent away. A considerable amount is also sent to Manchester. Additionally, some fruit is sent to a jam factory located at Histon, where a moderate acreage of fruit is also grown.

## Botanical Observations
The region is associated with specific varieties of the ash tree. It is said that the weeping variety of ash (*pendula*), characterized by pendulous branches, was first observed at Gamlingay, in Cambridgeshire. Other varieties of ash include *crispa*, which occurs with curled leaves, and *verrucosa*, which is characterized by warty branches and stems.
