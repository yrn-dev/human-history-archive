# The Bonapartes of Baltimore

The Bonapartes of Baltimore constitute a branch of the Bonaparte family settled in the United States of America. This lineage is descended from Jerome Bonaparte and his wife, Elizabeth Patterson.

## Origins and Marriage
The branch was established through the union of Jerome Bonaparte and Elizabeth (born 1785), the daughter of William Patterson, a merchant based in Baltimore. It is probable that William Patterson was descended from Robert Paterson, who served as the original inspiration for the character "Old Mortality" in the works of Sir Walter Scott. 

The marriage between Jerome and Elizabeth took place in Baltimore on December 24, 1803. The union was met with strong disapproval from Napoleon, who refused to recognize the legality of the marriage. Despite this, the marriage was considered valid under American law, and Pope Pius VII refused to declare the union void.

## Family Separation and Residence
Despite the legal validity of the marriage, Jerome was forced by his brother to separate from Elizabeth. After bringing his wife to Europe and a subsequent stay in England, Elizabeth—who was commonly referred to as Madame Patterson or Madame Bonaparte—returned to Baltimore, where she resided until her death in 1879.

## Second Generation: Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte
The only child produced by the marriage of Jerome and Elizabeth was Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870). Born in England, he spent the majority of his life residing in Baltimore. It has been noted that he bore a marked resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte maintained a positive relationship with his father, and the two met occasionally; for a period of time, Jerome provided his son with a large allowance.

## Third Generation and Political Career
The lineage continued through the sons of the first Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte. His elder son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), served in the French army, seeing service in Italy and the Crimea.

The younger son, Charles Joseph Bonaparte, attained a prominent position in American politics. Born in Baltimore on June 9, 1851, and educated at Harvard University, he entered the legal profession in 1874. His public service included the following roles:
* President of the National Municipal League.
* Secretary of the Navy in the cabinet of President Roosevelt from July 1905 to December 1906.
* Attorney-general of the United States, a position he held until March 1909.

Charles Joseph Bonaparte is the grandson of Jerome, the king of Westphalia.
