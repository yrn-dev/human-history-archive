# Avignon

Avignon is a city located in south-eastern France and serves as the capital of the department of Vaucluse. It is situated 143 miles south of Lyons on the railway line that connects Lyons and Marseilles. As of 1906, the city had a population of 35,356.

## Geography and Layout
The city is positioned on the left bank of the Rhone river, a few miles above the point where the Rhone meets the Durance. Avignon occupies a large, oval-shaped area; however, this area is not fully populated, as much of it is covered by gardens and parks. 

At the northern end of the town, the Rocher des Doms is a precipitous rock that rises from the edge of the river. This rock forms a plateau that extends southwards, reaching nearly to the Place de l'Hotel de Ville. The summit of the Rocher des Doms contains a public garden.

## Fortifications and Urban Structure
Avignon is encircled by ramparts constructed by the popes during the 14th century. These walls are considered one of the finest examples of medieval fortification currently in existence. The strong walls feature machicolated battlements and are flanked by thirty-nine massive towers. Several gateways pierce the walls, three of which date back to the 14th century. A line of pleasant boulevards surrounds the entire fortification.

The primary activity of the town is concentrated in the Place de l'Hotel de Ville and the Cours de la Republique, the latter of which leads away from the square and extends to the ramparts. In other parts of the city, the streets are characterized as being narrow, quiet, and generally badly paved.

## Bridges and Landmarks
A suspension bridge crosses the river to Villeneuve-les-Avignon. Further up the stream is the Pont Saint-Benezet, a picturesque ruined bridge from the 12th century. Of the original eighteen piles of the bridge, only four remain. One of these piles supports the chapel of Saint-Benezet, which is a small Romanesque building.

## Religious and Civic Architecture
The summit of the Rocher des Doms is home to both the Palace of the Popes and the cathedral of Notre-Dame des Doms. 

The cathedral is a Romanesque structure dating primarily from the 12th century. Its most prominent feature is a gilded statue of the Virgin located on the western tower. The interior contains various works of art, the most beautiful of which is the 14th-century Gothic carving of the mausoleum of Pope John XXII.

The Palace of the Popes is a sombre collection of buildings that dwarfs the cathedral. It covers an area of more than 1 1/4 acres.
