# Base-Ball Scoring and Errors

In the sport of base-ball, a detailed score is maintained using vertical columns to track the performance of players. This record includes the number of times a player has been at bat (excluding sacrifice-hits and bases taken on balls), the runs scored, base-hits made, and sacrifice-hits made. Additionally, the score tracks defensive contributions, specifically how many opponents a player has put out and the number of "assists," which occur when a player helps put out an opponent, such as by throwing a ground ball to first-base.

## The Recording of Errors

A specific column in the base-ball score is dedicated to the number of errors a player has made. An error is defined as a misplay by a fielder. Examples of errors include:
*   A muffed fly ball.
*   A failure to stop and field a ground ball.
*   A muffed thrown ball.
*   A bad throw.

However, not every failure to stop a ball is recorded as an error. If a batsman hits the ball so hard that it cannot be stopped, a fielder's failure to stop and field it does not count as an error, even if the ball came straight at them. The final decision regarding what constitutes an error in the score is determined by the official scorer of the home club.

Furthermore, certain events are explicitly not counted as errors. These include "passed balls" (balls not held by the catcher), wild pitches, balks, and bases on balls. These occurrences are instead recorded under their own regular columns.

## Fielding Records and Averages

The archive notes that careful records are kept for both amateur and professional players regarding their pitching, batting, base-running, and fielding averages. 

To determine a player's fielding record, the number of accepted chances is divided by the total chances. For example, if a player puts out 1188 men and provides 64 assists while making 15 errors, the fielding average is calculated by dividing 1252 by 1267. In this system of measurement, a score of 1000 represents perfect fielding.

## Related Gameplay Elements

The recording of errors is linked to the batsman's objective of reaching first-base without being put out. A batsman may earn his base through a fielder's error, such as the aforementioned bad throws or muffed balls. 

Other ways a batsman may reach first-base include:
*   Making a "safe-hit" that is fair and cannot be caught or fielded in time.
*   Being entitled to the base if the pitcher delivers four "bad balls" (balls not over the base, or over the base but not between the knee and shoulder of the batsman) that the batsman does not strike.
*   Being unavoidably struck by a pitched ball.
*   Reaching the base after a third strike if the catcher fails to catch the ball and the batsman reaches the base before the catcher can throw the ball to the first-baseman.
