# Cutch

Cutch is a territory located within the Bombay Presidency of British India. Politically, it is classified as a native state under the administration of a governor, specifically grouped among the native states of North Gujarat. It is also listed as being under the Bombay government.

## Geography and Physical Features
Cutch is situated in the northern part of the presidency, bounded to the west by the Arabian Sea. Its coastline is defined by the Gulf of Cutch, which, along with the Gulf of Cambay, frames the peninsula of Kathiawar. 

A prominent and peculiar lacustrine feature of the region is the Runn (or Lake) of Cutch. This area covers an estimated 8,000 square miles and varies in nature depending on the season, manifesting as an inland lake, a salt marsh, or an arm of the sea. The Runn serves as the western boundary of the province of Gujarat. During the rainy season, flooding in the Runn unites the Gulfs of Cutch and Cambay, effectively converting the territory of Cutch into an island.

The landscape of Cutch includes isolated hills which, from a geological perspective, constitute the western extremity of the Aravalli range.

## Geology
The geological composition of Cutch is characterized by several distinct layers. Horizontal lava flows of the Deccan Trap series extend into Cutch. These flows are underlaid by Neocomian and Jurassic beds. The Jurassic beds are marine in nature and contain numerous Ammonites. The Neocomian beds consist of shales and sandstones containing plant remains, some of which are identical to forms found in the upper portion of the Gondwana system. Additionally, Cutch contains Tertiary shales, sandstones, and limestones that overlie the Deccan Trap.

## Language and Industry
The primary language spoken in the region is Cutchi. 

Industrial activity in the vicinity includes salt production. In the Ahmedabad district of Bombay, the village of Kharaghoda is situated on the Little Runn of Cutch. This location serves as the terminus for a branch railway and houses a government salt factory. This facility produces "Baragra salt," with an annual output of nearly 2,000,000 cwt, the majority of which is exported to Northern and Central India.
