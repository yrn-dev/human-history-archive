# Austria-Hungary

The Austro-Hungarian monarchy was a dual state governed by a constitutional framework that balanced the independence of two separate entities—Austria and Hungary—under a single sovereign. The unity of the monarchy was centered on the head of state, who held the titles of Emperor of Austria and Apostolic King of Hungary.

## Constitutional Framework and Government
The government of the monarchy was based on three primary foundations: the Pragmatic Sanction of Emperor Charles VI (promulgated April 19, 1713), which established the succession of the Habsburg-Lorraine dynasty through primogeniture and the indivisibility of the monarchy; the diploma of Emperor Francis Joseph I (October 20, 1860), which introduced the constitutional form of government; and the *Ausgleich*, or "Compromise," of February 8, 1867.

Under the *Ausgleich*, Austria and Hungary functioned as completely independent states, each possessing its own government and parliament. However, they maintained a common administration for three specific areas:
1. Foreign affairs, including consular and diplomatic representation.
2. The army and navy (though each state maintained its own special army and voted on its own recruits).
3. Finance, specifically regarding joint expenditures.

## Internal Relations and Economic Treaties
The relationship between the two states was occasionally strained by political and economic crises. In 1907, a "customs and commercial treaty" was reached after a severe economic crisis in Hungary highlighted that separation from Austria would lead to commercial ruin for Hungary and increase the cost of living in Cisleithania. 

Key features of this 1907 agreement included:
* The adoption of separate but identical customs tariffs.
* Mutual facilitation of railway communications: Hungary assisted the link between Vienna and Dalmatia (to be completed by 1911), and Austria assisted communication between Hungary and Prussia.
* The establishment of a court of arbitration consisting of four judges from each state, with the presidency decided by lot.
* The abolition of trade impediments regarding sugar.
* Provisions for Hungary to redeem its share of the old Austrian debt.

In Austria, political tensions rose in the early 20th century regarding the use of "paragraph 14" for government by ordinance, which was viewed with bitterness by the public and the Czechs. By September 1905, the crown sanctioned the introduction of universal suffrage into the official programme.

## Foreign Policy and Alliances
Austria-Hungary's European position was strengthened in 1879 through a formal alliance with Germany. Arranged by Bismarck and Andrassy, this treaty committed Germany to support Austria against Russian attacks, while Austria-Hungary pledged to assist Germany against a combined attack by France and Russia. This alliance was later strengthened in 1882 by the addition of Italy.

The alliance was particularly popular among German Austrians, some of whom viewed it as a "national alliance of the Teutonic races" and attempted to use it to influence internal struggles against the Slavs. However, the governments of Germany and Austria-Hungary did not support this theory to avoid provoking the Poles and Czechs.

## Historical Background and Territorial Shifts
The Habsburg dominions in eastern Germany originally included the county of Tirol and the duchies of Carinthia, Carniola, Styria, Upper Austria, and Lower Austria. Following the death of Emperor Maximilian in 1519, his grandson Charles V succeeded him but resigned his Austrian lands and claims to Hungary and Bohemia to his brother, Ferdinand.

The region faced significant conflict with the Turks. At the battle of Mohacs on August 28, 1526, Suleiman the Magnificent defeated and killed King Louis of Bohemia and Hungary. This victory allowed the Turks to retain most of Hungary until the peace of Karlowitz in 1699. During this period, Ferdinand fought to secure Hungary against rivals like John Zapolya and paid an annual tribute to Suleiman for a small portion of Hungary (approximately 12,228 sq. m.).

## Dissolution and Post-War Status
Following the war, the Republic of Austria was recognized as an independent republic. Under the terms of the peace treaties, Austria was required to recognize the complete independence of Hungary, Czecho-Slovakia, and Jugoslavia. 

The new boundaries were established as follows:
* The boundary between Austria and Czecho-Slovakia was formed by the boundaries of Moravia and Bohemia.
* The eastern boundary at Radkersburg and Marburg was ceded to Jugoslavia.
* Frontiers facing Switzerland and Bavaria remained unchanged.

Additionally, Germany was required to deliver to the allied powers all sums deposited in Germany by Austria-Hungary and Turkey for financial support during the war. Germany also had to expropriate the rights of its nationals in public utilities within Austria-Hungary and transfer them to the Reparations Commission.
