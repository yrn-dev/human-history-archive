# After the Re-establishment of the Society of Jesus in Belgium

## Resumption of the Bollandist Work
Following the re-establishment of the Society of Jesus in Belgium, the work of the Bollandists was resumed in 1837. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and at the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. For this renewed effort, the Bollandists were installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels.

The first volume of the new series, vol. vii. of October, was published in 1845. The series continued to expand over the following decades, reaching vol. xiii. of October by 1883.

## Collaborators and Special Editions
Several Jesuit fathers collaborated on the work during this period, including:
* Joseph van der Moere
* Joseph van Hecke
* Benjamin Bossue
* Victor de Buck
* Remi de Buck
* Ant. Tinnebroeck
* Edu. Carpentier
* Henr. Matagne

Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan was tasked with editing the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*. This specific work appeared at the beginning of vol. xi. of October in 1864.

## Historical Context of the Work
The resumption of the work in 1837 followed a long period of dispersion and instability. Prior to the re-establishment, the Bollandists had remained at Antwerp until 1778, after which they were transferred to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. During their time at Coudenberg, they published vol. iv. of October (1780) and vol. v. of October (1786). 

The work ceased entirely in 1788 after the suppression of the monastery of Coudenberg. Following this cessation, the Premonstratensians of Tongerloo acquired the remains of the Bollandist library. They attempted to continue the project in their own abbey, resulting in the publication of vol. vi. of October in 1794.
