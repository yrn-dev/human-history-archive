# Asiatic Societies and Related Academic Publications

The archive's records indicate the existence of several learned bodies and publications dedicated to the study of Asia, including the Royal Asiatic Society, the Asiatic Society of Bengal, and the Asiatic Society of Japan. These organizations have served as venues for monographs, journals, and transactions covering linguistics, geography, and history.

## Linguistic Research and the Royal Asiatic Society
The Royal Asiatic Society has been a source of significant linguistic study. In 1906, Dr. Grierson published a monograph titled "The Pisaca Languages of North-Western India" through the society. In this work, Grierson argued that various dialects found on the north-western frontier—specifically those of Gilgit, Chitral, Kafiristan, and Dardistan—constitute a separate group. According to Grierson, while these languages descended from Aryan, they remained independent of both Iranian and Sanskrit.

## The Asiatic Society of Bengal and Geographical Studies
The *Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal* has published extensive research on the geography and antiquities of Asia. Notable contributions by Lieut.-Col. H. Yule include:
*   An account of ancient Buddhist remains at Pagân on the Iráwadi (1857).
*   Notes on antiquities located near Jubbulpoor (1861).
*   Notes regarding a brief visit to Indian remains in Java (1862).

Additionally, the society's journal published a memorandum by the Very Rev. Thomine D’Mazure, communicated by Lieut.-Col. A. P. Phayre and featuring notes and comments by Lieut.-Col. H. Yule, concerning the countries situated between Burmah, Yunân, and Thibet. This memorandum included a map of the North East Frontier prepared in August 1861 by the Office of the Surveyor-Gen. of India in Calcutta.

## Historical and Regional Scholarship
Various journals and societies have contributed to the study of Central and East Asian history. The *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society* has published articles by authors such as Specht, Drouin, E.H. Parker, and Vincent Smith. General Houtum-Schindler also contributed articles to this journal, which served as a resource for the editorship of the third edition of the *Book of Ser Marco Polo*.

In Japan, the "Transactions of the Asiatic Society of Japan" includes work by Tarring regarding the organization of Japanese society. This research touches upon the "Code and the Penal Law" that operated from the Daiho era, as well as the "Rules and Regulations of the Three Generations" (Sandai-kyaku-shiki), which were redrafted during the Engi (901-923), Jokwan (859-877), and Konin (810-824) eras.

## International Academic Recognition
The study of Asia has been a collaborative effort across multiple international learned bodies. The Geographical and Asiatic Societies of Paris, along with the Geographical Societies of Berlin and Italy, have historically enrolled distinguished scholars as honorary members. Other related publications contributing to the field of Asian studies include the *Asiatic Quarterly*, *Revue numismatique*, and *Journal asiatique*.
