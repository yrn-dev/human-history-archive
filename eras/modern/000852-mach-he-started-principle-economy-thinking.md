# Economy of Thinking and Empirio-criticism

## Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius
Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius (1843-1896) was a German philosopher born in Paris on November 19, 1843. He pursued his education in Berlin and Zuerich, eventually graduating from the university of Leipzig in 1876. Following his graduation, he became a professor of philosophy in Zuerich in 1877, where he remained until his death on August 18, 1896.

During his time at Leipzig, Avenarius was a founder of the *Akademisch-philosophische Verein* and served as the first editor of the *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie*. His early academic contributions included an 1868 essay regarding the Pantheism of Spinoza.

## Philosophical Works and Principles
Avenarius's primary intellectual contributions are found in two major works: *Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten Kraftmasses* (1876) and *Kritik der reinen Erfahrung* (1888-1890). Through these texts, Avenarius attempted to coordinate action and thought.

A central tenet of his approach was the "principle of economy of thinking," a starting point he shared with Mach. In his work *Kritik*, Avenarius sought to explain the relationship between environment, knowledge, and pure experience. He concluded that pure experience is constituted by statements that are dependent upon the environment.

## Empirio-criticism
The philosophy developed by Avenarius is known as Empirio-criticism. This system is characterized as an idealistic dualism; the archive notes that it is neither a realistic philosophy nor can it be classified as materialism.
