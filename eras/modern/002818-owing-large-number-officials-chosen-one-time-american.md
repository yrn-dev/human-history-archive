# American Election Ballots

## Ballot Structure and Dimensions
In American elections, the form and appearance of the ballot differ significantly from those used in Great Britain. This difference is primarily due to the large number of officials that are chosen at one time. 

In New York state, for example, during the quadrennial presidential election, each elector votes for a wide array of officers. These include thirty-six presidential electors, one congressman, a state-governor, a lieutenant-governor, and five additional state officers. Furthermore, voters select a member for each house of the state legislature, several judges, a county-clerk, a sheriff, and other county officers.

Because of the extensive list of candidates for these various offices, the column for each party typically measures between 2 and 3 feet in length. Additionally, since there are frequently eight to ten party-tickets competing, the ballot-paper usually reaches a width of 18 to 20 inches.

## Voting Procedures and Systems
Upon entering a polling-place, each voter is provided with one of these "blanket" ballots. The voter then retires to a booth to mark their choices. Depending on the voter's preference, they may mark an entire party column or select individual candidates from different columns.

Different states employ varying systems for organizing these ballots:
* **Massachusetts:** Candidates are arranged by the offices they seek rather than by party-lists. In this system, every voter must mark the name of each individual candidate for whom they wish to vote.
* **Separate Party Ballots:** This system is utilized in New Jersey, Connecticut, Missouri, Georgia, North and South Carolina, and New Mexico.
