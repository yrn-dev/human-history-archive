# Borodino

The battle of Borodino took place on September 7, 1812, during the Russian campaign. The engagement involved the Russian army and the forces of Napoleon.

## Russian Command and Engagement
During the battle, the Russian forces included the main army under the command of Kutusov. Prince Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly (known to the Russians as Prince Bogdanovich), a descendant of a Scottish family and a Russian field marshal, was present at Borodino, although he left the army shortly after the engagement.

The left wing of the Russian forces at Borodino was led by Prince Peter Bagration. Bagration was a Russian general born in 1765, descended from the noble Georgian family of the Bagratides. Prior to the 1812 campaign, Bagration had a distinguished military career, including service in the Caucasus, the siege of Ochakov in 1788, and the Polish campaign of 1794, where he was present for the taking of Warsaw and Praga. He had also served under Suvarov in the Swiss and Italian campaign of 1799, notably capturing the town of Brescia.

In the wars of 1805, Bagration achieved further distinction. He successfully resisted repeated attacks from forces five times his own size at Hollabruenn with a small rearguard, securing the retreat of Kutusov's main army despite the loss of half his men. He also fought at Eylau, Friedland, and Austerlitz, where he engaged the French army's left wing under Lannes and Murat. His other achievements included the capture of the Aland Islands in 1808 via a daring march across the frozen Gulf of Finland, and commanding against the Turks at the battles of Tataritza and Rassowa in 1809.

## Casualties and Commemoration
In 1812, Prince Bagration commanded the 2nd army of the West. Although he suffered a defeat at Mogilev on July 23, he rejoined the main army under Barclay. While leading the left wing at Borodino on September 7, Bagration received a mortal wound. In his honor, Tsar Nicholas I erected a monument on the Borodino battlefield.
