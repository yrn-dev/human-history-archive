# Valley Settlements and Geographic Sites

Across various regions of the world, the geography of valleys, plateaus, and highlands has historically dictated the placement of villages, towns, and defensive fortifications. The availability of water, the quality of soil, and the strategic advantages of elevation are primary factors in the selection of these sites.

## Agricultural and Residential Valleys
In certain regions, the intersection of plateaus and valleys creates ideal conditions for human habitation. In the Buldana district of India, located in Berar, the landscape consists of small plateaus that decrease in elevation toward the south. Between these plateaus are small fertile valleys watered by streams for most of the year and containing numerous wells of pure water; consequently, these valleys are favourite village sites. The northern portion of this district occupies the rich valley of the Purna, which, in seasonable years, is covered by a nearly unbroken sheet of cultivation.

Similarly, in the Soissonnais region, the plateau is intersected by broad, deep valleys, most notably those of the Aisne and the Vesle. These valleys are characterized by moist, clayey soils that support grassland, grain, and beets, while the lower slopes are used for orchards, beans, and grape-vines. Most villages in this area are situated on the lower slopes at the level where water-courses reappear as springs. These villages are often surrounded by trees and embellished by parks and châteaux.

## Health Resorts and Pilgrimage Sites
The specific environmental conditions of a valley can also make a site a destination for health or religious purposes. Ilkley, an urban district in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England, is situated in the upper part of the Wharfe valley. The combination of the valley's fine scenery and the bracing air of the high moorlands above it has made Ilkley a favourite health resort, hosting several hydropathic establishments.

In the Garhwal district of the United Provinces in British India, the village of Badrinath is located in a valley approximately one mile wide and four miles long on the right bank of the Vishnuganga. Badrinath is a celebrated pilgrimage site featuring a temple dedicated to an incarnation of Vishnu. The temple is situated at an elevation of 10,294 feet, surrounded by mountains that reach 23,000 feet above sea level.

## Strategic and Defensive Sites
Geography has frequently been used for military and defensive purposes. In ancient Greece, primitive villages initially sought security by choosing sites that were lofty and difficult of access, located inland on rocks or elevations to protect against marauding surprises. As society became more stable, these were often replaced by more convenient sites on the plains or declivities, with the original high sites becoming the Acropolis of the new towns.

In France, the city of Lyons utilizes the surrounding terrain for its defense. Its protection includes a modern enceinte in the eastern plain and a line of detached forts along the high ground on the right bank of the Saône. To the north-west, fort Verdun is positioned on the high plateau of Mont d'Or, while a connecting battery built in 1895 at Chapoly commands the westward approaches via the valley of Charbonnieres.

## Industrial and Urban Topography
Urban centers often develop based on the undulating nature of the land. Birmingham is situated upon New Red Sandstone in the valleys of the Rea and other small feeders of the river Tame. The site rises steadily toward the north and west, varying from 200 to 600 feet above sea-level, creating the appearance of a vast semicircular amphitheatre when viewed from the high ground to the south-east.

## Highland Plateaus and Vales
In other regions, the plateau itself is the dominant feature. The northward extension of a specific county is occupied by the plateau of the Peak, with summits such as Bleaklow Hill (2060 ft.) and Kinder Scout (up to 2088 ft.). This mass is demarcated by the valleys of the Goyt, Etherow, Derwent, and Noe. The flanks of this plateau are scored by abrupt ravines known as "cloughs." Within these areas, villages such as Ashopton and Derwent Chapel are located in the dale, and the village of Castleton is situated in a branch vale.
