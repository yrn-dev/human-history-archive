# Hong Kong

Hong Kong (properly Hiang-Kiang, meaning the place of "sweet lagoons") is a British island possession located off the south-east coast of China. It is situated opposite the province of Kwang-tung on the east side of the Si-kiang estuary, 75 miles south-east of Canton and 38 miles east of Macao. The island is part of a small cluster the Portuguese called "Ladrones" (Thieves) due to the habits of the former inhabitants.

## Geography and Flora
The island is extremely irregular in outline, covering an area of 29 square miles. It measures 10.5 miles in extreme length from north-east to south-west, with a breadth varying between 2 and 5 miles. A military road approximately 22 miles long encircles the island. Hong Kong is separated from the mainland by a narrow channel; at the roads between Kowloon Point and the capital, Victoria, the channel is about 1 mile broad, narrowing to just over a quarter mile at the Ly-ee-mun Pass.

The southern coast is deeply indented, featuring two bold peninsulas that create two natural harbours: Tytam Bay and Deep Water Bay, the latter of which contains the village of Stanley. On the west coast, the in-shore island of Aberdeen (or Taplishan) protects Aberdeen harbour, which includes the Hope dock (opened in 1867) and a granite graving dock. Lamma, the largest of the surrounding islands, features Mount Stenhouse, which reaches 1,140 feet.

Botanical analysis of the Hong Kong flora indicates that approximately three-fifths of the species are common to the Indian region. Nearly all remaining species are local forms or Chinese. There is a small number of species common to northern Asia, Japan, and southern China.

## Administration and Government
Formerly an integral part of China, the island was first ceded to Great Britain in 1841. This cession was confirmed by the treaty of Nanking in 1842, with the charter dated April 5, 1843. The colony is administered by a governor, a legislative council, and an executive council. 

The executive council consists of nominated members and holders of certain offices; in 1890, it comprised nine members. The legislative council includes the same officials and six unofficial members. Of the unofficial members, one is elected from the justices of the peace, one from the chamber of commerce, and three are appointed by the governor (two of whom are currently members of the Chinese community).

## Economy and Industry
The colony's main revenue sources include land sales, the post-office, rent of government property, and licences. The legal tender consists of the British and Hong Kong dollar, the Mexican dollar, or other silver dollars authorized by the governor. Small copper and silver coins are legal tenders for amounts not exceeding one and two dollars, respectively. Paper currency is issued by the National Bank of China, Limited, the Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, and the Chartered Bank of India, Australia and China. Public debt for public works is approximately £340,000, managed via a sinking fund.

Agricultural industry is minimal beyond vegetable gardens. While only 400 acres are cultivated on Hong Kong island and 400 acres in Kowloon, the "new territory" has 90,000 acres under cultivation, including over 7,000 acres of sugar-cane as of 1900.

Chief industries include:
*   **Manufacturing:** Cement, paper, sugar-refining, and the production of bamboo, rattan, gold, silver, and sandal-wood ware.
*   **Crafts:** Furniture making, umbrella and jinricksha making, wood and ivory carving, and gold-beating.
*   **Heavy Industry:** Working in iron and copper, and the manufacture of cotton.
*   **Maritime:** Ship and boat building, as well as rope and sail making.
*   **Other:** Granite quarries and industries related to matches and kerosene oil.

## Infrastructure and Society
The population of Hong Kong is described as Chinese and brown, totaling 306,130. The colony maintains a strong military garrison and a police force composed of Chinese, Indian Sikhs, and Europeans.

Education is provided through a large number of grant-in-aid schools and a few government schools. Secondary education for boys is provided by the Queen's College. The foundation-stone for Hong Kong University was laid in March 1910, with buildings provided by colonial broker Sir Hormusjee Mody. Medical services are provided by several hospitals, including one government institution. The Hong Kong savings bank holds deposits of approximately $1,100,000.

## International and Military Relations
Hong Kong serves as a strategic point for the British Navy. During the Great War, the British China squadron concentrated at Hong Kong. It is located approximately 2,750 miles from Ponape in the Caroline Islands.

In terms of regional diplomacy, Great Britain's cooperation in opium revenue work at Hong Kong served as a model for Portugal's 1887 treaty with China regarding Macao. Similarly, the Chinese imperial customs collected duties on vessels trading with Macao using the same arrangements established for the British colony of Hong Kong.
