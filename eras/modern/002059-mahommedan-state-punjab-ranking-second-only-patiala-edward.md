# Bahawalpur

Bahawalpur (also referred to as Bhawalpur) is a native state of India located within the Punjab. It is recognized as the principal Mahommedan state in the Punjab, ranking second only to Patiala.

## Geography and Boundaries
The state stretches for more than 300 miles along the left bank of the Indus, the Punjnud, and the Sutlej. Its boundaries are defined by Sind and the Punjab to the north and east, and the Rajputana desert to the south. The total area of the state covers 15,918 square miles.

## Administration and Governance
Bahawalpur is one of the native states under the Punjab government, alongside others such as Patiala, Jind, Nabha, Kapurthala, Mandi, Sirmur (Nahan), Faridkot, and Chamba. Like other native states, it became relatively dependent on Great Britain through treaty or conquest following the annexation of neighboring provinces.

The state is governed by a chief who holds the title of nawab. The current dynasty belongs to the Daudputra family from Sind, and the nawab claims descent from Abbas, the uncle of the Prophet. This dynasty established its independence from the Afghans toward the end of the 18th century. In 1838, the state entered into a treaty with the British, to which it has remained loyal.

## Demographics and Economy
As of 1901, the population of Bahawalpur was 720,877, which represented an 11% increase over the previous decade. The estimated gross revenue of the state is £146,700, and there is no tribute.

The state's economy saw significant improvement in the 1870s with the introduction of canal irrigation, a development that resulted in the doubling of revenue. The territory is served by the Southern Punjab and North-Western railways.

## Infrastructure and Education
The town of Bahawalpur is situated near the left bank of the Sutlej and is located 65 miles from Mooltan, where it has a railway station. A notable landmark in the town is a magnificent palace built in 1882 by Nawab Sadik Mahommed Khan, which can be seen from far across the Bikanir desert. The population of the town was 18,546 in 1901.

Educational facilities within the state include Anglo-vernacular schools and an arts college.
