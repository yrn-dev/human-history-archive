# Geography of Bohemia

Bohemia is a kingdom and crownland of Austria, situated in the geographical centre of the European continent. It is positioned at approximately equal distance from all European seas and serves as a borderland between the Slavonic and German worlds. The region covers an area of 20,060 square miles, which is roughly two-thirds the size of Scotland.

## Territorial Boundaries and Shape
Bohemia has the form of an irregular rhomb. Its borders are defined by the following territories:
*   **North-East:** Prussian Silesia
*   **South-East:** Moravia and Lower Austria
*   **South:** Upper Austria
*   **South-West:** Bavaria
*   **North-West:** Saxony

The northernmost point of this rhomb is Buchberg, located just above Hainspach, which is also the farthest north point in the entire Austro-Hungarian monarchy.

## Orography and Mountain Ranges
From an orographic perspective, Bohemia is a separate massif among the Austrian provinces, enclosed by high mountains on three sides.

**The Bohmerwald (Bohemian Forest)**
Located on the south-west, the Bohmerwald runs generally from south-east to north-west. It is divided into two parts by the pass of Neumark (also known as the pass of Neugedein). The northern section, known in Czech as *Cesky Les*, reaches an altitude of 3,300 feet at the massif of Czerkov. The southern section, known as *Sumava*, is the most picturesque and highest part of the range; it includes the Plockenstein (4,513 feet) and the Osser (4,053 feet). The highest peak of the range is the Arber (4,872 feet), though it is located in Bavaria. The range is characterized by its blue lakes, crystalline torrents, and extensive forests of pine and oak.

**The Erzgebirge (Ore Mountains)**
Forming the north-west frontier, the Erzgebirge (Czech *Rudo Hori*) has an average altitude of 2,600 feet, peaking at the Keilberg (4,080 feet). Due to easy passes, good roads, and numerous cultivated areas and mining villages, these mountains often resemble a hilly undulating plain. The village of Gottesgab, situated at 3,345 feet, is the highest place in central Germany and Bohemia. To the west, the Erzgebirge connects to the Fichtelgebirge via the Elstergebirge and the plateau of Waldsassen. To the east, the Nollendorf pass separates the Erzgebirge from the Elbsandsteingebirge.

**The Sudetes**
The north-east frontier is formed by the Sudetes, which include several ranges:
*   **Riesengebirge (Czech *Kroknose*):** Among the highest mountains in central Europe after the Alps, reaching 5,264 feet at the Schneekoppe.
*   **Isergebirge:** Featuring the Tafelfichte (3,683 feet).
*   **Adlergebirge:** Reaching 3,664 feet.
*   **Jeschkengebirge:** Reaching 3,322 feet.
*   **Lausitzergebirge:** Reaching 2,500 feet.
*   **Heuscheuergebirge:** Reaching 2,532 feet.

**The Bohemian-Moravian Hills**
The fourth side of the rhomb is composed of the Bohemian-Moravian Hills, which consist of a broad series of low hills or a plateau made of primitive rocks, reaching altitudes of 2,500 feet in some areas.

## Hydrography and Access
Bohemia is opened to the German plain by the valley of the Elbe. The lowest point in Bohemia is where the Elbe escapes into Saxony, at an altitude of 367 feet. Despite being enclosed by mountains, the region is easily accessible from the Danubian plain through Moravia.

The pass of Neumark has historically served as the primary approach to Bohemia from Germany. It extends eastward above the town of Taus (Czech *Domazlice*), a location once called *Tuhost* (meaning "the Fortress").

## Historical Geography and Geology
The geography of the region has influenced its history, making it a junction for the migration of peoples. The pass of Neumark, specifically, was the site of several significant battles:
*   In the first half of the 7th century, Samo repulsed Avar hordes.
*   In 1040, Wratislas II defeated German emperor Henry III.
*   In 1431, the Hussites won a major victory against a German army of crusaders.
*   George of Podebrad also vanquished a German army at this location.

Additionally, the region's geology has provided specific mineral findings. In the Erzgebirge, a meteorite fell at Breitenbach containing crystals of enstatite, a magnesium metasilicate.
