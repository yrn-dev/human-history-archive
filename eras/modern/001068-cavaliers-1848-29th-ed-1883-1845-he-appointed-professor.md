# Lays of the Scottish Cavaliers

The *Lays of the Scottish Cavaliers* is a poetic work authored by William Edmonstoune Aytoun. First published in 1848, the work achieved significant longevity and popularity, as evidenced by its 29th edition published in 1883. It is the primary work upon which Aytoun's reputation as a poet rests.

## Author Biography
William Edmonstoune Aytoun (1813–1865) was a Scottish miscellaneous writer, humorist, and poet born in Edinburgh on June 21, 1813. He was the only son of Roger Aytoun, a writer to the signet. Aytoun's literary tastes, political sympathies, and specific admiration for the Stuarts—as well as his love for ballad poetry—were derived from his mother, who was noted for her culture and originality of character.

Aytoun was educated at the Edinburgh Academy starting at age eleven, and later attended university. His early professional life was centered on the law; he studied in London for a few months in 1833 and resumed legal pursuits in his father's chambers after a period of studying German in Aschaffenburg between September 1833 and April 1834. He was admitted as a writer to the signet in 1835 and was called to the Scottish bar five years later, though he personally confessed that he never could "overtake" the law despite following it.

## Literary Career and Other Works
Aytoun's first publication appeared in 1832, a volume titled *Poland, Homer, and other Poems*, which reflected his interest in the state of Poland. While residing in Germany, he produced a blank verse translation of the first part of *Faust*, though this was never published because other translations had forestalled it.

Beginning in 1836, Aytoun contributed translations from Uhland to *Blackwood's Magazine*, and he remained on the staff of the publication from 1839 until his death. Around 1841, he began a collaboration with Mr. (later Sir) Theodore Martin, writing a series of light humorous papers regarding the follies and tastes of the era. These papers included verses that were later published in 1855 as the *Ban Gaultier Ballads*.

## Academic and Political Life
In 1845, Aytoun was appointed as the professor of *belles lettres* and rhetoric at Edinburgh University. He was a successful lecturer, and the number of students attending his courses increased as a result of the attractiveness of his lectures.
