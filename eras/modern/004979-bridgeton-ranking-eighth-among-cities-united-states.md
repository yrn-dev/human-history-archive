# Bridgeton

## Overview and Geography
Bridgeton is a city located in the southern part of New Jersey, United States. It serves as the county-seat of Cumberland county and is designated as a port of entry. Geographically, the city is situated on Cohansey creek, which is navigable up to the city, and is located 38 miles south of Philadelphia.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Bridgeton has seen fluctuations and growth over several decades. In 1890, the population was 11,424. By 1900, this number increased to 13,913, a population that included 701 negroes and 653 foreign-born residents. The population was recorded at 13,624 in 1905 and rose to 14,209 by 1910.

## Infrastructure and Education
The city is connected to other regions via several transportation methods. It is served by the Central of New Jersey and West Jersey & Sea Shore railways, as well as electric railways that connect Bridgeton with adjacent towns. Additionally, Delaware river steamboats utilize Cohansey creek to reach the city.

Bridgeton is described as an attractive residential city. Its public amenities include a fine public library and a park spanning 650 acres. The city is also an educational center, serving as the seat for Ivy Hall, a school for girls, and the West Jersey academy.

## Economy and Industry
Bridgeton functions as a distributing centre and an important market town for a rich agricultural region. While it is a port of entry, its foreign commerce is considered relatively unimportant.

The city's manufacturing sector is diverse, producing machinery, clothing, and canned vegetables and fruits. It also maintains dyeing and finishing works. The glass industry is a primary economic driver for the city, specifically the production of glass bottles. In 1905, the value of these glass products was $1,252,795, which accounted for 42.3% of the total value of all factory products in the city. Due to this output, Bridgeton ranked eighth among the cities of the United States in the glass industry.

## History
The first settlement in the area that is now Bridgeton occurred toward the close of the 18th century. Early industrial development began in 1814 with the establishment of a pioneer iron-works. The city was officially chartered in 1864, formed through the union of the township of Cohansey (incorporated in 1848) and the township of Bridgeton (incorporated in 1845).
