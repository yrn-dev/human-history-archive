# The Austro-Prussian War and the Constitutional Shift in Bohemia

The Austro-Prussian War of 1866 served as a pivotal catalyst for a complete change in the constitutional position of Bohemia. This shift followed a period of instability and failed attempts by the house of Habsburg to transition the Austrian empire from absolutism to a constitutional system.

## Pre-War Constitutional Efforts
The impossibility of continuing absolutism in the Austrian empire became evident following the events of the Italian campaign of 1859. In an effort to maintain imperial unity while recognizing the ancient constitutional rights of various countries under Habsburg rule, a decree was published on October 20, 1860. This decree established diets with limited powers, the composition of which was partially modeled after the ancient diets of other parts of the empire, including Bohemia.

While initially received favorably in Bohemia, these hopes were undermined by a subsequent imperial decree on February 26, 1861. This new decree established a central parliament in Vienna with extensive powers and introduced an electoral system that was grossly partial to the Germans. Although Bohemian representatives initially joined the parliament in Vienna, they withdrew in 1863, asserting that the assembly had encroached upon the constitutional powers belonging to the diet of Prague. By 1865, the central parliament in Vienna was suspended.

## The Austro-Prussian War of 1866
The conflict between Austria and Prussia reached a decisive point on July 3, 1866, with the battle of Königgrätz (also known as Sadowa), located near the town of Königgrätz in Bohemia. The war concluded with the peace of Prague, where a treaty was signed on August 23, 1866.

A primary outcome of this treaty was the exclusion of all lands ruled by the house of Habsburg from Germany. This had an immediate impact on Bohemia, which the Congress of Vienna in 1815 had previously declared part of the Germanic Confederation—a decision made without consulting the country's estates, contrary to the custom followed even after the battle of the White Hill.

## Post-War Constitutional Changes
The treaty with Prussia led to a decline in German influence within the Austrian empire and specifically within Bohemia. In the aftermath, the constitutional landscape was reorganized:

*   **Hungary:** Obtained complete independence.
*   **The New Constitution of 1867:** This constitution applied only to the Slavic and German portions of the Habsburg empire. It attempted to maintain the waning German influence and preserved a system of centralization.

The Bohemians energetically opposed this new constitution and refused to send representatives.

## The Dual Monarchy and the Ausgleich
The broader constitutional framework of the resulting Austro-Hungarian monarchy was based on three pillars: the Pragmatic Sanction of Emperor Charles VI (1713), which settled succession in the dynasty of Habsburg-Lorraine; the diploma of Emperor Francis Joseph I (October 20, 1860), which introduced the constitutional form of government; and the *Ausgleich* (Compromise) of February 8, 1867.

Under the *Ausgleich*, Austria and Hungary became two separate, completely independent states, each possessing its own government and parliament. Unity was maintained through a common head of state—the Emperor of Austria and Apostolic King of Hungary—and the joint administration of three specific areas:
1.  Finance, regarding joint expenditure.
2.  The army and navy (excluding special state armies and the annual voting of recruits).
3.  Foreign affairs, including consular and diplomatic representation.

Additionally, a customs and commercial agreement concluded in 1867 (and renewed in 1899) established the monarchy as a single commercial territory, including the principality of Liechtenstein and Bosnia and Herzegovina.
