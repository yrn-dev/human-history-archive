# Sarikol Mountains

The Sarikol mountains are a meridional range located in Asia, serving as a significant geographical and political landmark. The range is situated to the west of Kashgar and constitutes the eastern face of the great elevation of the Pamir.

## Geography and Physical Characteristics
The Sarikol range is part of a larger system that limits a central depression in Asia. This depression, which extends westwards through Mongolia to Chinese Turkestan and begins on the east of the Gobi desert, was once a vast inland sea. The level of this depression between the Sarikol range and the mountains enclosing the sources of the Hwang-ho likely never exceeds 2,000 feet above sea level.

Structurally, the Sarikol range consists of two parallel chains. The western chain serves as the water-divide of the Pamirs. The eastern chain, which has been referred to as the Kandar or Kashgar range, is characterized by lateral gorges that occur at intervals, allowing the main drainage from the eastern slopes of the Pamir to pass through.

## Political Boundaries
The Sarikol mountains play a critical role in defining the political borders of several regions:

*   **Russia and China:** On the western edge of the Kashgar plains, the meridional range of Sarikol defines the political boundary between Russia and China. However, the actual dividing line between the Russian Pamirs and Chinese Turkestan remains indefinite.
*   **Afghanistan:** The northern limits of Afghanistan were established by Boundary Commissions in 1884-1886 and 1895. These limits extend from the Hari Rud in the west to the Sarikol mountains in the east.

## Exploration and Research
The glacial regions of the Sarikol mountains were the subject of extensive investigation by the Swedish professor and explorer Sven Hedin. His work in these regions took place from 1894 to 1896, contributing to what is described as a monumental record of Asiatic exploration.

## Historical Nomenclature
The range has been known by different names throughout history. In ancient times, it was known as Taurus, and during medieval times, it was referred to as Bolor.
