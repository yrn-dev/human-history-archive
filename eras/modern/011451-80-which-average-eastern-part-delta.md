# Rainfall and Geography of the Ganges Delta and Related Regions

## Precipitation in the Ganges Delta
In the eastern part of the delta of the Ganges, the average rainfall is approximately 75 to 80 inches. For comparison, the rainfall at Calcutta stands at about 64 inches. The region is flanked to the east by hills where precipitation is significantly higher; on the Khasi hills, which reach an elevation of about 4,500 feet, the ten-year average exceeds 550 inches. Extreme weather events have been recorded in this area, including 150 inches of rain in a single month and 610 inches in one year.

## Regional Rainfall Comparisons in Asia
Rainfall patterns vary widely across the Asian continent. In northern Asia, between the Lena and the Volga, rainfall is generally equal, ranging from 19 to 29 inches, with increases noted in Japan, Siam, and Korea. In Siberia, the south-western region receives 12 to 14 inches, which decreases eastward to 6 or 7 inches at Barnaul and 5 or 6 inches at Urga in northern Mongolia; eastern Siberia receives about 15 to 20 inches.

In China, the fall at Peking is approximately 23 inches, while Canton, located near the northern tropic and influenced by the south-west monsoon, receives 78 inches. Similarly, Batavia in Java records about 78 inches, and Singapore records nearly 100 inches. The coast of the Malay peninsula, where it is not sheltered from the south-west by Sumatra, sees a considerable increase in quantity. On the Burmese and Tenasserim coasts, registered falls exceed 200 inches, and the quantity never drops below 75 or 80 inches.

## Other Regional Precipitation and Climate
In the Indian peninsula, sea-level rainfall on the west coast varies between 75 and 100 inches, with mountain elevations commonly registering more than 250 inches. The east coast receives far less, never rising to 50 inches and reducing to 25 or 30 inches toward the southern apex of the peninsula. Ceylon records between 60 and 80 inches. As one moves away from the coast toward the head of the Gangetic plain, rainfall diminishes to about 25 or 30 inches. The tract along the Indus is almost rainless within 60 or 80 miles of the Himalaya.

In Burma, the climate of the delta is more temperate and cooler than in Upper Burma. This difference is reflected in the stouter physique and fairer complexion of the people in the lower province compared to those in the hotter, drier upper districts as far as Bhamo. Rainfall in the coast districts ranges from 90 inches in the Irrawaddy delta and Rangoon to about 200 inches in the Tenasserim and Arakan divisions. North of the delta apex, rainfall decreases toward Minbu, where a "rainless zone" begins and extends to Katha. In the dry zone, the annual average falls to between 20 and 30 inches.

## Other Deltaic and River Systems

*   **The Danube:** The discharge of the Danube is split between three branches. In 1905, the proportions were Kilia (67%), St George's (24%), and Sulina (9%). The combined average outflow is 236,432 cubic feet per second. The delta between the St George's and Kilia branches covers about 1,000 square miles and consists primarily of a large reed-covered marsh with isolated elevations of beech, willow, and oak. The Kilia branch deposits approximately 3,000 cubic feet of mud and sand per minute during ordinary floods, causing the coast-line to advance and the sea to become shallower.
*   **The Don:** This river of European Russia rises in Lake Ivan (580 feet above sea-level) and flows 1,325 miles to form a 130 square mile delta at the north-east extremity of the Sea of Azov. Its drainage area is 166,000 square miles, with an average fall of about 5-1/4 inches per mile.
*   **The Fraser Delta:** In British Columbia, the station at Agassiz represents the conditions of the Fraser delta.
