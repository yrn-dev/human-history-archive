## Political and National Events in the Transvaal
In the town of Krugersdorp, located on the Witwatersrand in the Transvaal, December is a month of historical commemoration. The Paardekraal monument was erected to honor a victory achieved by the Boers under Andries Pretorius in 1838 over the Zulu king Dingaan. Consequently, the 16th of December is observed as a public holiday, during which large numbers of Boers gather at the monument.

This date also holds significance for the independence of the Transvaal. In December 1880, a great meeting of Boers took place in Krugersdorp where they resolved to proclaim the independence of the Transvaal. The formal proclamation was subsequently made on Dingaan's Day. Furthermore, the 16th of December was used to commemorate the defeat of the British at Majuba Hill in 1881.

## Colonial Developments in South Africa
In the region of Natal, the port of Durban saw a British military force stationed there from December 1838 to December 1839. Following the recall of this force, the settlement was taken over by Dutch emigrants from the Cape. These emigrants had previously defeated the Zulu king Dingaan and had established an encampment called *Kangela* (meaning look-out) at the upper end of the bay. Upon taking possession of Durban, the Dutch hoisted the flag of the "Republic of Natalia" and claimed independence.

In the Cape province, the town of King William's Town—founded by Sir Benjamin D'Urban in May 1835—was abandoned in December 1836. It was later reoccupied in 1846.
