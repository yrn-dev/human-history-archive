# Series, MSS. of Duke of Buccleuch at Montagu House, of Mrs Frankland-Russell-Astley

The "Series, MSS. of Duke of Buccleuch at Montagu House, of Mrs Frankland-Russell-Astley" is a collection of historical manuscripts cited as a source for correspondence and biographical research. It is listed among the calendars of the Historical Manuscripts Commission (Hist. MSS. Comm. Series), alongside other collections such as those of the Marquess of Ormonde and various other collections.

## Context and Usage
These manuscripts serve as primary authorities for tracing the life and correspondence of historical figures. Specifically, they are cited in the bibliography regarding the life of George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham. They are grouped with other significant archival sources, including the Calendars of State Papers and the documents of the Scottish History Society, to provide a comprehensive record of political and personal communications.

## The House of Buccleuch
The manuscripts are associated with the dukes of Buccleuch, a ducal house of the Scotts of Buccleuch. The substantial origin of this house dates to the 15th century, when James II granted large tracts of land in Scotland to Sir Walter Scott of Kirkurd and Buccleuch, a border chief, following the fall of the 8th earl of Douglas in 1452. The family's lineage is traced further back to Sir Richard le Scott (1240-1285).

The family's ascent included several key figures:
* **Sir Walter Scott of Branxholm and Buccleuch (d. 1552):** Distinguished himself at the battle of Pinkie in 1547.
* **Sir Walter (1565-1611):** Created Lord Scott of Buccleuch in 1606, with an earldom following in 1619.
* **Anne (1651-1732):** Daughter of the second earl and a countess in her own right, she married the duke of Monmouth in 1663, who was then created the 1st duke of Buccleuch.

The lineage continued through Francis (2nd duke) and Henry (3rd duke). Henry (1746-1812) succeeded to the dukedom of Queensberry in 1810 upon the death of William Douglas, the 4th duke of Queensberry, according to a 1706 entail by the 2nd duke of Queensberry. Henry also married the daughter of the duke of Montagu. Subsequent successors included Charles William Henry (4th duke), Walter Francis Scott (5th duke), and William Henry Walter Montagu Douglas Scott (6th duke).

## Associated Estates and Legacy
The dukes of Buccleuch have been associated with several notable properties and public works. Dalkeith Palace, a seat of the duke of Buccleuch, was designed in 1700 by Sir John Vanbrugh for the widow of the duke of Monmouth. The site originally belonged to the Grahams and later the Douglases before being sold in 1642 by William, the seventh or eighth earl of Morton, to Francis, the second earl of Buccleuch, to help fund Charles I during the Civil War. The palace has hosted various sovereigns, including George IV (1822), Queen Victoria (1842), and Edward VII (1903).

Additionally, the 5th duke (Walter Francis Scott) served as president of the council in 1846 and lord privy seal from 1842 to 1846. He was also the president of the British Association, the Society of Antiquaries, and the Highland and Agricultural Society. He is noted for funding the construction of the harbour at Granton, near Edinburgh, at a cost exceeding £500,000.
