# New South Wales

## Geography
New South Wales is located in Australia. Its south-eastern extremity contains the Australian Alps, where Mount Kosciusco is situated. Standing at 7,328 feet, Kosciusco is the highest mountain in Australia. A neighboring peak to the south, Mueller's Peak, reaches a height of 7,268 feet and was long considered the continent's highest point. In 1897, a meteorological station was established on Mount Kosciusco.

## Early Colonial History and George Barrington
The colony of New South Wales received convicts transported from England. One such individual was George Barrington, an Irishman born on May 14, 1755, in Maynooth. Barrington had a history of criminal activity, including robbing his schoolmaster in Dublin in 1771 and engaging in pocket-picking in Limerick and London. His crimes included the theft of a snuff-box from the Russian prince Orlov at the Covent Garden theatre, valued at £30,000. After multiple sentences of hard labour and a conditional release from England, he was sentenced to seven years' transportation in 1790.

During the voyage to Botany Bay, Barrington disclosed a conspiracy by fellow convicts to seize the ship to the captain. Upon arriving in New South Wales, the captain reported Barrington favourably to the authorities. Consequently, in 1792, Barrington received the first warrant of emancipation ever issued. He later served as the high constable of Paramatta and the superintendent of convicts. 

Barrington also contributed to the cultural life of the colony; when a theatre opened in Sydney in 1796 with convict actors, he wrote the prologue for the first production. He authored several works, including *A Voyage to Botany Bay* (1801), *The History of New South Wales* (1802), and *The History of New Holland* (1808). He died at a ripe old age in Paramatta, though the specific date is not recorded.

## Legal and Financial Systems
The legal framework of New South Wales has been influenced by English law. In matters of bankruptcy, the system is based on the English act of 1869, which emphasizes creditors' management over officialism. New South Wales improved upon the English system by requiring bankrupts to apply for their discharge within a fixed period; failure to do so results in a charge of contempt of court.

Regarding taxation, an income tax first came into operation in New South Wales on January 1, 1896. This tax is complementary to a land tax based on the unimproved value of freehold lands, subject to certain deductions and exemptions. The income tax rate is 6d. in the pound. Incomes of £200 per annum or less are exempt, and other incomes (excluding those of companies) receive a reduction of £200 in their assessments. Certain entities are exempt from this tax, including:
* Municipal corporations and other local authorities.
* Mutual life insurance societies.
* Companies or societies not operating for profit or gain.
* Public charitable, ecclesiastical, and educational institutions.
