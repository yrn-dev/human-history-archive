# Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine

*Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine Catherine* is a historical work authored by Baron Albert du Casse. Published in Paris between 1861 and 1866, the work comprises seven volumes. It is cited as one of the chief works relating to Jerome Bonaparte.

## Authorship and Context
The author, Baron Pierre Emmanuel Albert du Casse (1813–1893), was a French historian born in Bourges on November 16, 1813. Du Casse maintained close professional ties with the Bonaparte family. In 1849, he served as the aide-de-camp to Prince Jerome Bonaparte, the former king of Westphalia and then-governor of the Invalides. It was under Jerome's commission that du Casse wrote *Memoires pour servir a l'histoire de la campagne de 1812 en Russie* (1852). 

Later in his career, du Casse served as an attaché to Jerome's son, Prince Napoleon, during the Crimean War. In this capacity, he authored a historical précis of military operations in the Orient from March 1854 to October 1855, which he later expanded upon with a volume on Crimea and Sebastopol (1853–1856) and a comprehensive list of French officers killed or wounded in that conflict (1892).

## Related Works by Baron du Casse
Baron du Casse produced an extensive body of work concerning the Napoleonic era and the Bonaparte family. In addition to the seven-volume set on King Jerome and Queen Catherine, he wrote *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883), a work that provides insight into Napoleon I and his entourage. He also published the *Journal de la reine Catherine de Westphalie* (1893).

His other significant historical contributions include:
* *Memoires du roi Joseph* (1853–1855), a ten-volume set focusing on the political and military memoirs of King Joseph.
* *Histoire des negociations diplomatiques relatives aux traites de Morfontaine, de Luneville et d'Amiens* (1855–1857), which served as a sequel to his work on King Joseph.
* *Memoires du prince Eugene* (1858–1860) and *Refutation des memoires du duc de Raguse* (1857), both compiled from papers held by the imperial family.
* *Corresp. inedite de la reine Catherine de Westphalie* (1888–1893), which supplemented the correspondence between King Jerome of Westphalia, Queen Catherine, and King Friedrich of Wurttemberg.

Beyond his formal histories, du Casse wrote *Souvenirs d'un officier du 2e Zouaves* and *Les Dessous du coup d'etat* (1891), works noted for containing piquant anecdotes. He also authored several slight novels.

## Critical Reception
The historical reliability of Baron du Casse's work is a subject of archival note. While his publications—specifically *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* and the *Journal de la reine Catherine de Westphalie*—are credited with shedding significant light on Napoleon I and those around him, they are also described as having been edited with little care. Consequently, these specific works are viewed as not entirely trustworthy. 

Furthermore, critics have observed that du Casse's experience writing novels introduced a certain "levity" into his writing style, a trait that persists even within his more serious historical publications. In some instances, his anecdotal writing is described as degenerating into "mere tittle-tattle."
