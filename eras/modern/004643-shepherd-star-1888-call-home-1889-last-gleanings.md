# Shepherd's Star, The Call Home, and The Last Gleanings

"The Shepherd's Star" (1888), "The Call Home" (1889), and "The Last Gleanings" (1895) are works by the French painter Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton.

## The Artist
Jules Adolphe Aimé Louis Breton was born on May 1, 1827, in Courrières, Pas de Calais, France. His early artistic talent led him to study under the historical painter de Vigne in Ghent in 1843, followed by studies under Baron Wappers in Antwerp in 1846. He eventually worked in Paris under Drolling.

While Breton's initial works focused on historical subjects—such as "Saint Piat preaching in Gaul" and "Misery and Despair" (the latter created under the influence of the 1848 revolution)—he eventually determined that he was not suited for historical painting. Consequently, he shifted his focus to the memories of nature and the countryside from his youth.

## Artistic Focus and Style
Breton became primarily a painter of rustic life, with a particular emphasis on the province of Artois. He rarely left this region, making only three short excursions: one to Provence in 1864, and two to Brittany in 1865 and 1873. His time in Brittany provided him with some of his most successful studies of religious scenes.

The subjects of Breton's extensive body of work are generally categorized into four classes:
* Labour
* Rest
* Rural festivals
* Religious festivals

## Notable Works
Throughout his career, Breton produced numerous significant pieces. His early success included "The Day after St Sebastian's Day" (1855), which earned a third-class medal, and "Blessing the Fields" (1857), which earned a second-class medal. Other recognized works include "Erecting a Calvary" (1859), currently located in the Lille gallery, and "The Return of the Gleaners" (1859), housed in the Luxembourg. In 1861, he received a first-class medal for "Evening" and "Women Weeding."

Later works include "The Fountain" (1872), for which he received a medal of honour, and "Women mending Nets" (1876), located in the Douai museum. His later period includes "The Song of the Lark" (1884), "The Last Sunbeam" (1885), "The Shepherd's Star" (1888), "The Call Home" (1889), "The Last Gleanings" (1895), "Gathering Poppies" (1897), "The Alarm Cry" (1899), and "Twilight Glory" (1900).

## Professional Recognition
In 1886, following the death of Baudry, Breton was elected to the Institut. In 1889, he was made a commander.
