# 1774 in Intellectual and Scientific History

The year 1774 marked a period of significant publication and professional development across various fields, including education, natural history, literature, and the birth of several influential figures in science and theology.

## Educational Reform and Publications
Johann Bernhard Basedow, a German educational reformer born in 1723, achieved a major milestone in 1774. Having previously served as a professor of moral philosophy and belles-lettres in Denmark and later in Altona, Basedow devoted himself to a general reform of education in Germany. In 1768, he published *Vorstellung an Menschenfreunde fuer Schulen, nebst dem Plan eines Elementarbuches der menschlichen Erkenntnisse*, a work influenced by Rousseau's *Emile*. Basedow sought to reform common instruction methods and establish an institute for teacher qualification. To fund his *Elementarwerk*, which aimed to explain his principles and include illustrative plates, he solicited subscriptions that totaled 15,000 Talers (L2250). This funding enabled him to publish the work in four volumes in 1774, providing a complete system of primary education.

## Natural History and Science
In the realm of natural history, the work of Buffon continued to expand. His *Histoire naturelle, générale et particulière* was a pioneering effort to present natural history facts in an intelligible form. In 1774, a second edition of this work was begun, which was eventually completed in 1804 across thirty-six quarto volumes. This edition was similar to the first, though it recast the supplement and suppressed anatomical descriptions. Additionally, the first edition's supplement, consisting of seven volumes, began appearing between 1774 and 1789.

The year 1774 also saw the birth of several notable scholars. Sir Charles Bell, a Scottish anatomist, was born in Edinburgh in November 1774. He would later become known for his work on the anatomy of the human body and his treatise on the *Anatomy of Expression*. In England, mathematician Olinthus Gilbert Gregory was born on January 29, 1774, in Yaxley, Huntingdonshire. In Germany, Johann Nepomuk von Fuchs, a chemist and mineralogist, was born on May 15, 1774, in Mattenzell.

## Literature and Theology
Various literary and theological works were issued in 1774. The poet Klopstock published *Die Gelehrtenrepublik*, a scheme intended for the regeneration of German letters. In the English-speaking world, a complete collection of the *Works* of Lord Lyttelton was published by his nephew, G. E. Ayscough. Additionally, the work *A Father's Legacy to his Daughters* was published in 1774.

The year also marked the birth of Gottfried Daniel Krummacher, the brother of theologian Friedrich Adolf Krummacher. Born in 1774, Gottfried Daniel Krummacher became a pastor and a leader of the "pietists" of Wupperthal, eventually publishing sermons including *Die Wanderungen Israels durch d. Wüste nach Kanaan* in 1834.
