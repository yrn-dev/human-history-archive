# Victor François, Duc de Broglie

Victor François, Duc de Broglie (1718–1804), was a prominent French military commander and nobleman who attained the rank of marshal of France. A member of a noble family of Piedmontese origin that had emigrated to France in 1643, he spent much of his career in active military service across Europe.

## Early Military Career
Victor François began his military service alongside his father, François Marie, Duc de Broglie. He served with him at Guastalla and Parma, obtaining a colonelcy in 1734. During the German War, he participated in the storming of Prague in 1742, an action that led to his appointment as a brigadier. He continued his service on the Rhine in 1744 and 1745, and by 1756, he had been promoted to the rank of *maréchal de camp*.

## Service in the Low Countries and the Seven Years' War
Following his promotion to *maréchal de camp*, Victor François served under Marshal Saxe in the low countries, where he was present at the battles of Maastricht, Val, and Roucoux. By the conclusion of this conflict, he had reached the rank of lieutenant-general.

During the Seven Years' War, he served under the command of Contades, Soubise, and d'Estrées. He was present at every battle from Hastenbeck onwards. In 1759, he achieved a victory over Prince Ferdinand at Bergen. This success earned him the rank of prince of the empire from Emperor Francis I and the rank of marshal of France from his own sovereign. While he won an action at Corbach in 1760, he suffered a defeat at Vellinghausen in 1761.

## Later Career and the French Revolution
Following the war, Victor François fell into disgrace and was removed from active employment. He was not recalled until 1778, at which time he was given command of the troops intended for operations against England.

During the French Revolution, he played a prominent role as a determined opponent of the uprising. After emigrating, he briefly commanded the "army of the princes" in 1792. He eventually died at Münster in 1804.

## Family Context
Victor François was the son of François Marie, Duc de Broglie (1671–1745), a marshal of France and former ambassador to England who had commanded the French army in Germany. He was the grandson of Victor Maurice, Comte de Broglie (1647–1727), and the great-grandson of François Marie (1611–1656), the first head of the family to take the title of comte de Broglie after the family's move to France.

Victor François had a son, Victor Claude, Prince de Broglie (1757–1794), who attained the rank of *maréchal de camp*. Unlike his father, Victor Claude adopted revolutionary opinions, served in America with Rochambeau and Lafayette, and was a member of the Jacobin Club and the Constituent Assembly. He served as chief of staff to the Republican army on the Rhine before being arrested and executed during the Terror on June 27, 1794.

Victor François was also the father of Achille Charles Léonce Victor, Duc de Broglie (1785–1870), a diplomat and statesman who served in the Chamber of Peers under Louis XVIII.
