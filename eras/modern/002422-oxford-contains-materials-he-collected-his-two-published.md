# Oxford

Oxford is a center of higher education and scholarly activity, home to various colleges and the Bodleian library. It has served as a site for academic study in the natural sciences, history, and theology, and is a significant location for the publication of critical scholarly editions.

## Academic Institutions and Faculty
The university comprises several colleges, including Queen's College and Magdalen College. At Magdalen College, Charles Giles Bridle Daubeny was elected to a demyship in 1810, where the lectures of Dr. Kidd inspired his interest in natural science. Daubeny later graduated with second-class honours in 1814 and obtained a prize for the Latin essay in 1815. He eventually became the professor of chemistry in November 1822, succeeding Dr. Kidd, and held that position until 1855. In 1834, he was also appointed to the chair of botany, a role to which the chair of rural economy was attached. While at the Oxford botanic garden, Daubeny performed experiments regarding the composition of the atmosphere, light, and changes in soil and their effects on vegetation.

## The Bodleian Library and Collections
The Bodleian library serves as a repository for significant historical papers and manuscripts. The papers of the English historian Thomas Carte (1686-1754), who was educated at Oxford, became the property of the university and were deposited there. Additionally, the library preserves the Selden Collection, which contains an autograph note-book by Bale. This note-book includes materials Bale collected for two published catalogues, arranged alphabetically and including the sources of his information. The Bodleian library also houses three tracts from the collection of Anthony a Wood, which have served as primary sources for modern collections of ancient carols.

## Scholarly Publications and Presses
The Clarendon Press is a notable publisher associated with Oxford. Its publications include a seven-volume edition of Bull's works published in 1827 under the superintendence of E. Burton, and the 1902 publication of *Index Britanniae Scriptorum quos ... collegit Ioannes Baleus* (as part ix. of *Anecdota Oxoniensia*), which was prepared with notes by Dr. R. Lane Poole and Miss Mary Bateson.

Oxford has been the site of numerous critical academic editions:
* **Biblical Studies:** John Mill of Queen's College, supported by John Fell, published a critical edition of the New Testament in 1707. Later, the *Vetus Testamentum hebraicum cum variis lectionibus* (2 vols. fol.) was published in Oxford between 1776 and 1780.
* **Classical Studies:** W. Dindorf published an edition in 1875 that distinguished two groups of the Venetian Scholia, and another edition in 1788 (edited again by Dindorf in 1877) of a collection of exegetical Scholia from MS. Ven. 453. E. Maass edited the *Codex Townleianus* in Oxford between 1887 and 1888.
* **Political and Historical Works:** L. A. Burd published *Il Principe, by Niccolò Machiavelli* in 1891, and Lord Morley delivered the Romanes lecture on Machiavelli in 1897. Thomas Carte's *Life of James, duke of Ormond* saw a new edition in six volumes published in Oxford in 1851.

## Cultural and Scientific Activity
Oxford has hosted significant intellectual gatherings and maintained specific traditions. In 1831, at the request of Charles Giles Bridle Daubeny, the British Association held a session at Oxford. In terms of cultural tradition, a slightly altered form of the "Boar's Head carol" (*Caput apri defero, Reddens laudes Domino*) is sung at Queen's College during the bringing in of the boar's head.
