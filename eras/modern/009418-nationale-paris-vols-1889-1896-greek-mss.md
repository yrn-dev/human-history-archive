# Greek and Latin Manuscripts in the Bibliothèque Nationale at Paris

The Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris serves as a major repository for a diverse array of historical manuscripts and artifacts, ranging from hagiographic texts and religious sect literature to ancient Egyptian relics. Its collections have been the subject of extensive analysis and cataloging by various scholars and organizations during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Hagiographic Cataloging by the Bollandists
Beginning in 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align more closely with historical methods. This effort included the analysis of hagiographic manuscripts located in principal libraries. As part of this initiative, the Bollandists produced several separate volumes of catalogs. Among these was a five-volume set published between 1889 and 1896 specifically devoted to the Latin and Greek manuscripts held at the Bibliothèque Nationale in Paris.

These cataloging efforts were part of a broader scientific approach to hagiography, which also included the publication of the *Analecta Bollandiana*—a quarterly review established by Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt. The *Analecta Bollandiana* provided a venue for inedited texts, dissertations, and studies intended to remould the *Acta Sanctorum*. In addition to the Paris volumes, the Bollandists cataloged Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (1886-1889), Greek manuscripts in the Vatican (1899), and Latin manuscripts in the libraries of Rome (starting in 1905).

## Babi Manuscripts
The Bibliothèque Nationale also preserves valuable public collections related to the Babi sect. While much of the sect's voluminous literature exists in manuscript form, the Paris collection includes two or three very rare manuscripts collected by Gobineau. These include a precious history written by Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, who was a contemporary of the Bab.

## Other Collections and Reproductions
The library's holdings extend to ancient Egyptian artifacts. In 1820, one of the zodiacs from a roof chamber of the temple of Dendera was removed and transferred to the Bibliothèque Nationale.

Furthermore, the Bibliothèque Nationale is noted for its work in providing high-quality photographic reproductions of manuscripts on a reduced scale, contributing to the accessibility of historical texts alongside similar efforts by the British Museum.
