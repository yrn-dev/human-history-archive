# Geschichte Maria Theresias

*Geschichte Maria Theresias* is a multi-volume historical work authored by A. von Arneth. Published in Vienna between 1863 and 1879, the series consists of 10 volumes.

## Context within Austrian Historiography
The work emerged during a period of significant development in the study of Austrian history. While earlier general histories existed—such as Archdeacon William Coxe's *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792* (1817) and J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates* (1834-1850)—the latter half of the 19th century saw a shift toward the systematic exploitation of private and public archives. *Geschichte Maria Theresias* is situated among other mid-to-late 19th-century scholarly efforts, including the 17-volume series *Oesterreichische Gesch. fuer das Volk* (1864) and H. Bidermann's *Gesch. der oesterreichischen Gesamtstaatsidee* (1867, 1887).

## Related Publications by A. von Arneth
In addition to the 10-volume *Geschichte Maria Theresias*, A. von Arneth produced other significant works focusing on the era of Maria Theresa and her descendants. These include:
* *Maria Teresias ersten Regierungsjahre* (1863), which focuses on the early years of her reign.
* *Maria Theresia und Marie Antoinette, ihr Briefwechsel* 1770-1780 (1865), a publication of correspondence between the two.
* *Marie Antoinette, Joseph II. und Leopold II. ihr Briefwechsel* (1866).
* *Correspondance secrète de Marie-Thérèse et du comte de Mercy-Argenteau* (1874), co-authored with A. Geffroy.
* *Correspondance secrète du comte de Mercy-Argenteau avec Joseph II. et le prince de Kaunitz* (1889-1891), co-authored with J. Flammermont.

## Impact on Historical Research
The publications of A. von Arneth, particularly those released from 1865 onwards, provided a critical corrective to previous biographical works. Many accounts of Marie Antoinette published prior to Arneth's work were based, in part, on forgeries such as those found in the collections of F. Feuillet des Conches and Paul Vogt d'Hunolstein. By publishing authentic letters from the archives of Vienna, Arneth provided material of the highest importance for the historical record.
