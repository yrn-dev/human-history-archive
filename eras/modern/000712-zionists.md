# Zionists

The term "Zionist" appears in the archive's records in relation to two distinct movements: a religious organization founded by John Alexander Dowie and a political or colonization movement associated with the Jewish people.

## The Movement of John Alexander Dowie
John Alexander Dowie (1848–1907) was the founder of a movement he termed "Zionism." Born in Edinburgh, Dowie moved to South Australia as a child before returning to Edinburgh University in 1868 to study for the Congregationalist ministry. He later served as a pastor near Sydney, Australia. Dowie became a powerful preacher and developed a belief in his own ability to heal diseases through prayer. This led him to establish "The Divine Healing Association of Australia and New Zealand" in Melbourne, where he built a tabernacle.

In 1888, Dowie traveled to America to preach and perform healings. Despite facing ridicule and opposition, he gained a following. In 1896, he established "The Christian Catholic Apostolic Church in Zion," appointing himself as the "First Apostle." By 1901, using funds contributed by his followers, Dowie founded Zion City on the west shore of Lake Michigan. This settlement covered approximately 10 square miles and featured a central temple for the Zionist church.

Dowie's leadership eventually collapsed. During visits to global branches of the Zionist movement in 1903 and 1904, he was mobbed upon appearing in London. In April 1906, a revolt occurred in Zion City. Dowie was deposed with the agreement of his son and wife after being charged with practicing polygamy and peculation. He attempted to recover the Zion City property, which was valued at two million sterling, through a United States district court suit, but he was unsuccessful as his defalcations were proven. Dowie died in Zion City in March 1907, suffering from paralysis and insanity.

## Jewish Zionist Organizations and Figures
Separate from the movement of Dowie, the archive records the existence of Zionists among the Jewish people. This movement included organized bodies such as the Zionist Congress and the Zionist Association.

### Political Representation and Leadership
Records of political distribution indicate the presence of Zionists in legislative or representative contexts. For example, in a listing of various national and political groups, four individuals are identified as Zionists among a group of five Jewish representatives, with one other identified as a Democrat.

Prominent figures associated with the movement include Moses Gaster, Ph.D. Gaster served as the Chief Rabbi of the Sephardic Communities of England and held the position of Vice-President of the Zionist Congress in 1898, 1899, and 1900. He was also an academic, serving as the Ilchester Lecturer at Oxford on Byzantine and Slavonic Literature.

### Colonization Efforts
The Zionist Association was involved in evaluating opportunities for the colonization of Jews. In August 1905, the association declined an offer of land for colonization. This decision followed the receipt of a report from a commissioner who had been sent to examine the offered land, which consisted of 6,000 square miles.
