# John Shute Barrington, 1st Viscount

John Shute Barrington, 1st Viscount Barrington (1678–1734), was an English theologian and lawyer. He was born in Theobalds, Hertfordshire, the son of a merchant named Benjamin Shute. His education was partially completed at the university of Utrecht before he returned to England in 1698 to study law at the Inner Temple.

## Political Career and Public Service
Barrington belonged to the class of Protestant dissenters and published several pamphlets in 1701 advocating for their civil rights. Following a recommendation from Lord Somers, Barrington was employed to persuade the Presbyterians in Scotland to support the union of the two kingdoms. For his efforts in this service, he was appointed to the office of commissioner of the customs in 1708.

He remained in this position until 1711, when he was removed due to a change in administration. During this period, his financial situation improved through the bequest of two significant estates. One estate was left to him by John Wildman of Becket, and the other by Francis Barrington of Tofts; the latter led Barrington to assume the name Barrington by an act of parliament.

Following the accession of George I, Barrington was returned to parliament representing Berwick-upon-Tweed. In 1720, the king granted him the title of Viscount Barrington of Ardglass, raising him to the Irish peerage.

## Expulsion from Parliament and Later Life
In 1723, Barrington was expelled from the House of Commons after engaging in the Harburg lottery, which was one of the bubble speculations of that era. This punishment was viewed by some as being too severe and was attributed to the personal malice of Walpole. Despite this setback, Barrington remained a leading figure among the dissenters.

## Theological Works
Barrington published two primary works in 1725. The first, titled *An Essay on the Several Dispensations of God to Mankind*, explored divine dispensations. The second, his principal work, was titled *Miscellanea Sacra or a New Method of considering so much of the History of the Apostles as is contained in Scripture*. Originally published in two 8vo volumes, this work was later reprinted in three 8vo volumes in 1770 by his son, Shute, with corrections and additions.

Viscount Barrington died on December 14, 1734.
