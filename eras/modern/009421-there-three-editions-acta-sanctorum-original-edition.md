# Acta Sanctorum

The *Acta Sanctorum* is a comprehensive collection of legends and biographies of the saints, organized according to the order of the calendar by days. The work is published by the Bollandists, who are Belgian Jesuits.

## Origins and Early Development
The original concept for the collection was conceived by a Jesuit father named Heribert Rosweyde. In 1607, Rosweyde issued a prospectus titled *Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis Bibliothecis manuscriptae*, in which he outlined his intention to compile the lives of the saints from manuscripts across eighteen volumes, accompanied by sober notes. Although Rosweyde collected a significant amount of material, he died in 1629 before the work could begin.

The project was subsequently taken over by another Jesuit father, John Bolland, who was sent to Antwerp. Bolland expanded the scope of the work and continued to gather material. The first two volumes, covering the month of January, were published in 1643. This was followed by three volumes for February in 1658, three for March in 1668, and three for April in 1675.

## Collaboration and Evolution
The production of the *Acta Sanctorum* involved several key collaborators. Godfried Henschen (Henschenius) was associated with Bolland from 1635 until 1681. Between 1659 and 1714, Daniel van Papenbroeck (Papebroch) collaborated on the project. The period of Papebroch's involvement is regarded as the most brilliant in the history of the work, although his use of free criticism earned him many enemies and forced him to defend his work against their attacks.

The collection continued in a similar spirit, despite some inequalities, until 1773, when the Society of Jesus was suppressed. The final volume published during this initial period was volume iii of October, which appeared in 1770.

## Editions and Volumes
There are three primary editions of the *Acta Sanctorum*:
*   **The Original Edition:** Published in Antwerp, Tongerloo, and Brussels, consisting of 63 volumes produced between 1643 and 1902.
*   **The Venice Edition:** This edition ceased after volume v of September (1734–1770).
*   **The Paris Edition:** This edition ceased after volume xiii of October, consisting of 61 volumes (1863–1883).

Additionally, a volume of tables was edited by the abbe Rigollot.

## Modern Developments and Scientific Method
In 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align the work with modern historical methods. Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt established a quarterly review called *Analecta Bollandiana*. By 1906, this review had reached its 25th volume, edited by P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt. The review serves as a venue for inedited texts, dissertations, and studies intended to remould and continue the *Acta Sanctorum*. Since 1892, it has also included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which provides criticisms of recent hagiographic works.

To provide a more scientific and solid basis for the collection, the Bollandists undertook the analysis of hagiographic manuscripts in major libraries. This resulted in the publication of several library catalogues (including those of Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres) and separate volumes dedicated to:
*   Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (2 vols., 1886–1889).
*   Greek and Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (5 vols., 1889–1896).
*   Greek manuscripts in the Vatican (1899).
*   Latin manuscripts in the libraries of Rome (1905 onwards).

The Bollandists also created inventories of published hagiographic texts, including the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*.

Following these preparatory works, publication of the principal collection resumed with volume i for November in 1887 and volume ii in 1894. The latter was preceded by the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* by the abbe Louis Duchesne and J.B. de Rossi. In 1902, the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* was published, which includes the *Synaxarium ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae*.

## Influence and Related Works
The *Acta Sanctorum* has served as a source for other hagiological works. For example, Alban Butler (1710–1773), an English Roman Catholic priest, produced *The Lives of the Saints* (4 vols., London, 1756–1759). Butler's work is described as a compendious and popular reproduction of the *Acta Sanctorum* and is considered the best work of its kind in English literature.
