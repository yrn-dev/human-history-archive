# Barriers and Customs Controls in Global History

Across various civilizations and geographical contexts, barriers have served diverse functions, ranging from natural geological formations and coral structures to artificial military checkpoints and fiscal customs-barriers.

## Administrative and Fiscal Barriers
In several historical contexts, barriers were established primarily for the regulation of trade, the collection of revenue, and the control of movement.

### India and the Portuguese Influence
During the foundation of the modern city of Bombay, the Portuguese occupied the island of Salsette. They utilized their position to establish a customs-barrier in the channel separating the shore from Bombay. This served to cut the city off from the mainland. The city's development later shifted under Gerald Aungier, who succeeded Sir George Oxenden as president of Surat in 1669. Aungier worked to organize the settlement, fortifying the shore and town despite a lack of skilled engineers from the court of directors. He replaced oppressive Portuguese policies—characterized by religious intolerance and cruel treatment of natives—with a system that protected all castes in their religious ceremonies and forbade the forced carrying of burdens.

### East Asian Systems
In China, customs and monopolies provided a significant portion of the state's revenue. The empire maintained an old native customs service consisting of stations and barriers throughout the land, alongside a foreign customs service located specifically at treaty ports for foreign vessel shipments.

In Japan, barriers were used for both revenue and strict social control. Temples and shrines, such as the Hachiman temple of Iwashimizu and Engaku-ji of Kamakura, derived income from barrier-tolls at locations like the Yamazaki and Hakone barriers. The proliferation of these barriers often obstructed travel and trade. Additionally, the Tokugawa shogunate used barriers to maintain power through the *sankin kotai* system. At the Hakone barrier, guards—specifically the Okubo family—strictly regulated passage. While men required a passport from their feudal chief, women required an order signed by a Bakufu official and were subjected to rigorous searches by female searchers, including the loosening of their coiffure, to ensure the wives of the daimyo remained in Yedo as hostages.

## Natural and Geological Barriers
Barriers also manifest as physical geographical features that isolate regions or protect populations.

### Mountain Ranges and Passes
The Khyber Pass serves as a strategic gateway from Afghanistan into India, consisting of a narrow defile between limestone and shale cliffs reaching 600 to 1000 feet. This mountain barrier separates the Afghan highlands from the Peshawar plains. Further south, a different mountain barrier exists between the Indus plains and the plateau, characterized by flexures folded parallel to the river. In the region of Peshawar, the Safed Koh range forms a rugged southern wall and water-divide for the Kabul River basin.

### Coral Formations
In marine geography, barrier reefs are large-scale coral platforms. The great Australian barrier reef extends from Lady Elliot island (24 deg. S. lat.) to Torres Strait (9.5 deg. S. lat.). These reefs are separated from the mainland by a channel that is deeper than those found in fringing reefs. For example, opposite Cape York, the seaward edge of the reef is nearly 90 miles from the coast, with a channel depth of nearly 20 fathoms. Over time, waves may pile dead coral and sand on the reef's edge to form islets, which may eventually coalesce into strips of dry land separated from the mainland by a wide channel.

## Barriers in Sacred Texts and Social Contexts
The concept of the barrier also appears in religious narratives and social dynamics.

### The Qur'an
The Qur'an describes an account involving Zul-qarnain (or Dhu'l-Qarneyn). Upon reaching a place between two mountains, he encountered a people who could hardly understand a word. These people informed him that Gog and Magog were spoiling the land and causing great mischief. They offered to pay Zul-qarnain tribute if he would erect a barrier between them and Gog and Magog. Zul-qarnain declined the tribute, stating that the power in which his Lord had established him was better, and instead requested strength and labor to build a fortified barrier (or bank) between the two groups.

### Social and Linguistic Barriers
In the history of Belgium, the church established dioceses that grouped Gallo-Romans and Franks together. By creating shared religious centers, the church diminished racial differences. Consequently, the linguistic frontier ceased to operate as a real barrier between the people it separated, allowing the inhabitants to act as intermediaries between Teutonic and Latin civilizations.
