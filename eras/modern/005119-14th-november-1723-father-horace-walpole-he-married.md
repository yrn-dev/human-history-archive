# Horace Walpole and Associated Figures

The historical records of the archive provide various references to Horace Walpole, primarily through his literary contributions, his correspondence with contemporaries, and his critical assessments of artists and authors.

## Literary and Critical Works
Horace Walpole was a writer and critic who produced several notable works. He authored *Royal and Noble Authors* and *Anecdotes of Painting*, the latter of which contains his evaluations of the artist William Kent. Walpole also wrote a biography of Baker for the quarto edition of Baker's works. Additionally, he produced a *Catalogue of Pictures of George Duke of Buckingham* in 1758.

His critical perspective was often candid. In his writings on William Kent, Walpole described Kent as being "below mediocrity in painting," though he praised Kent's genius for landscape gardening, noting that Kent was the first in England to "vindicate the natural against the artificial" by rejecting mathematical symmetry and clipped topiary.

## Correspondence and Social Circles
Walpole maintained a wide network of correspondence with intellectuals and nobility. He was a correspondent of David Steuart Erskine, the 11th Earl of Buchan. He also exchanged letters with Miss Hannah More; in a letter dated August 29, 1796, Walpole expressed a very low opinion of the novel *Camilla* by Madame D'Arblay, stating that the author had "reversed experience" and lacked insight despite her previous penetrations of character.

## Political and Familial Connections
The archive notes a suggested familial link between Horace Walpole and the Hervey family. It has been suggested that Carr, Lord Hervey (1691–1723), was the father of Horace Walpole. Carr was the son of John Hervey, 1st Earl of Bristol, and his first wife, Isabella. Carr was educated at Clare Hall, Cambridge, and served as a member of parliament for Bury St Edmunds from 1713 to 1722. He died unmarried on November 14, 1723.

In the political sphere, the archive mentions the "Walpoles" as targets of a series of letters published in the *Craftsman* starting in 1727. These letters were written by an "Occasional Writer" who sought to organize an opposition to the administration. This writer had previously offered his services to Walpole in 1723 after receiving a pardon from the king through the Duchess of Kendal, though Walpole received these offers coldly.
