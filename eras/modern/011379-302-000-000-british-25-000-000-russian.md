# British and Russian Presence in Asia

## Population and Territorial Area
In Asia, both continental and insular, the distribution of land and people among extra-Asiatic powers varies significantly. The Russian Empire holds the largest area of dependent territory in Asia, totaling 6,495,970 square miles. In comparison, British dependent territories in Asia cover 1,998,220 square miles.

The total population of Asia is roughly estimated at 823,000,000. Within this total, the population inhabiting British territories is 302,000,000, while the population inhabiting Russian territories is 25,000,000. For further context, Chinese territory is inhabited by 330,000,000 people. The total area of the Asian continent and its islands is somewhat over 16,819,000 square miles.

## Trade and Commercial Interests
British and Russian interests intersect in various commercial hubs. At Bander Abbasi, which serves as a trade center for imports and exports for Khorasan and south-eastern and central Persia, both Great Britain and Russia maintain representation through consuls. Between 1890 and 1905, the average annual value of imports and exports at Bander Abbasi was approximately £660,000. British shipping dominated this port; in 1905, 237,000 of the 255,000 tons of shipping that entered the port were British.

British financial interests are also extensive throughout its colonies and other regions. In the Dominion of Canada, 29 banks operate with deposits of about £140,000,000 and capitals of nearly £19,000,000. In Australia and New Zealand, 24 banks hold deposits of about £130,000,000. In India, approximately 15 banks—including three Presidency banks—conduct business, sometimes connecting to locations such as Hong Kong, Bangkok, and Zanzibar. These Indian banks have capitals exceeding £5,000,000 and deposits of fully £36,000,000. Additionally, there are at least 8 banks in South and West Africa. In total, more than 80 joint-stock British banks operate in the colonies, with deposits totaling £360,000,000 and capitals of £48,000,000.

Beyond its own territories, Britain maintains about 20 banks in foreign countries. This includes six large banks doing business principally in the East with capitals over £6,700,000 and deposits of £77,000,000, as well as seven other banks, including Barings.

## Cartography and Surveying
Russian and British efforts in surveying and mapping have been prominent in various regions. In the Balkan Peninsula, Russian officers conducted surveys between 1877 and 1879, resulting in a map of the eastern Balkan (scale 1:126,000) and a map of southern Rumelia and Bulgaria (scale 1:200,000), both published in 1883. Russian officers also contributed to the 1894 map of Montenegro.

The British war office's intelligence division began work in 1906 on a map of Turkey in Europe at a scale of 1:250,000. In Africa, the British general staff has published maps of the entire continent on scales of 1:1,000,000 and 1:250,000. Specific British surveys include the Gold Coast Colony (published since 2007), the Gambia Colony (1904-1905), and southern Rhodesia (1909). In the states of British South Africa, a reconnaissance survey based on triangulation superintended by Sir D. Gill has been in progress since 1903.

## Military and Logistics
During the Great War, the British armed forces saw massive expansion. In August 1914, land forces consisted of 250,000 Regulars, 200,000 trained Reserves, and 250,000 partly-trained Territorials. By May 1916, over 5,000,000 men had voluntarily enrolled in the navy and army, and by August 1918, the total enrolled in the armed forces of the Crown reached 8,500,000.

The British navy grew from 145,000 officers and men and 2,500,000 tons in August 1914 to 450,000 men and 8,000,000 tons four years later. The navy's logistical efforts during the war included the transport of 20,000,000 men, 2,000,000 mules and horses, 130,000,000 tons of food and materials, 51,000,000 tons of fuel and oil, and 25,000,000 tons of explosives.
