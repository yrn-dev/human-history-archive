# The Budget

The budget is a financial practice utilized by various nations, including constitutional countries such as France and the United States, as well as autocratic Powers. While the forms of the budget may be modified, the practice generally involves the establishment of a yearly period for financial accounting, although the specific commencement dates vary by country.

## National Financial Calendars
The start date of the financial year differs across various nations. The United Kingdom, Denmark, and Germany begin their financial years on April 1st. France, Austria, and Belgium begin on January 1st. In Canada, the United States, Italy, and Spain, the financial year commences on July 1st. Historically, the English financial year ran from January 1st to December 31st prior to 1832.

In the United Kingdom, the timing of budget introductions has fluctuated due to administrative changes and treaties. For example, a budget introduced by Disraeli in the autumn of 1852 resulted in his defeat. In 1860, the budget was introduced on February 10th following the ratification of a commercial treaty with France. A change in administration in 1859 delayed the budget until July 18th, and the year 1880 saw two separate budgets: one in March under Disraeli and another in June under Gladstone.

## Types and Procedures of National Budgets
National budgets are generally categorized into two types: those that undergo parliamentary scrutiny and debate annually, and those issued via executive authority.

In the United Kingdom, the executive presents the budget to the whole House after it has been examined only by the cabinet; the House then scrutinizes it while sitting as a committee. In the United States, the budget effectively emanates from Congress due to the lack of connection between the legislative and executive departments. The secretary of the treasury submits estimates from executive departments to the House of Representatives. These are handled by two committees: the committee on appropriations, which manages expenditure through various sub-committees and appropriation bills, and the committee on ways and means, which handles taxation.

Many other countries employ a middle ground, where a specially selected committee performs a preliminary examination of the budget and possesses the power to make drastic changes to executive proposals.

## Budgetary Governance in Specific Nations
### Japan
Under the Japanese system, the annual revenue and expenditure of the State must receive the consent of the Imperial Diet in the form of an annual budget. The budget is first presented to the House of Representatives. Any expenditure not provided for in the budget, or any amount exceeding the set appropriations, must be referred back to the Imperial Diet for approval. Similarly, the consent of the Imperial Diet is required for contracting liabilities or raising national loans not provided for in the budget. 

Certain exceptions exist: expenditure for the Imperial House is defrayed annually from the National Treasury at a fixed amount and does not require Diet consent unless an increase is necessary. Furthermore, the Imperial Diet cannot reject or reduce fixed expenditure based on the Emperor's supreme powers, legal obligations of the Government, or expenditure arising by effect of law, without the concurrence of the Government.

### Denmark
In Denmark, the budget has frequently been a point of contention between the *Folketing* (House of Commons) and the *Landsting* (Upper House). Between 1882 and 1894, the *Folketing* repeatedly refused to vote supplies, leading the Estrup ministry to utilize provisional financial decrees to maintain government functions. This conflict ended on April 1, 1894, when the *Folketing* agreed to a retrospective budget that sanctioned funds used for military expenditure.

Budgetary disputes continued into the late 19th century. In May 1897, the *Folketing* voted for the budget while the *Landsting* rejected it, leading to the resignation of the ministry in favor of the Hörring cabinet. In 1898, the *Folketing* rejected the military budget by a majority of 85 to 12, though the ministry survived due to a surge of patriotism following the expulsion of Danish agitators from North Schleswig by the German government.

## Local and Federal Budgets
Beyond national budgets, most larger countries maintain local and municipal expenditures and taxations. In federal systems—such as the Argentine republic, the German empire, and the United States—a full understanding of finances requires consulting both the federal budgets and the budgets of the individual states within the federation.

## Etymology and Terminology
The term "budget" was historically applied to a small coffer or leather-covered case. The term *bougette* is translated as a "little coffer or trunk... covered with leather." This evolved into a term for a despatch box used for official papers. Consequently, the phrase "open his budget" was used to describe the chancellor of the exchequer making his annual statement.
