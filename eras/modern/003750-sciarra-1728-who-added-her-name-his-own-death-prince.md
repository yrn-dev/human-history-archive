# The Barberini and Colonna di Sciarra Families

## Origins and Rise of the Barberini
The Barberini were a powerful Italian family of Tuscan extraction who first settled in Florence during the early part of the 11th century. Over time, the family acquired significant influence and wealth. A major peak in their power occurred in 1623 when Maffeo Barberini was elevated to the papal throne as Urban VIII. During his papacy, Urban VIII extended the family's reach by appointing his brother, Antonio, as a distinguished soldier and naming two of his nephews as cardinals. A third nephew, Taddeo, was granted the principality of Palestrina.

The rapid increase in the family's power caused jealousy among neighboring princes. This tension led Odoardo Farnese, the duke of Parma, to wage war against Taddeo, resulting in the defeat of the papal troops.

## Decline of the Male Line
Following the death of Urban VIII in 1644, the family faced hostility from his successor, Innocent X. This political climate forced Taddeo to flee to Paris, where he died in 1647. With Taddeo's death, the Barberini family became extinct in the male line.

## Union with the Colonna di Sciarra
The Barberini legacy continued through the female line. Taddeo's daughter, Cornelia, married Prince Giulio Cesare Colonna di Sciarra in 1728. Upon this marriage, Prince Giulio Cesare added the Barberini name to his own.

The lineage later passed to Prince Enrico Barberini-Colonna. Upon his death, the name was inherited by his daughter and heiress, Donna Maria. Donna Maria's husband, Marquis Luigi Sacchetti, subsequently received the title of prince of Palestrina and was granted permission to use the Barberini name.

## Legacy and Cultural Impact
The wealth and magnificence of the Barberini are evidenced by their library and palace in Rome. The family is noted for the ruthless manner in which they plundered ancient buildings to decorate their own palaces. This practice gave rise to the saying, "Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini."
