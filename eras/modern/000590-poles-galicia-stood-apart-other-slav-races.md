# The Poles of Galicia

The Poles of Galicia were a distinct group among the Slav races within the Austrian Empire. Their political and social position was characterized by a desire for provincial autonomy, a complex relationship with other ethnic groups in their region, and a long-term aspiration for the restoration of the Polish kingdom.

## Demographics and Social Structure
The population of Galicia was diverse, featuring a large minority of Ruthenes, who comprised approximately 43% of the population. The German-speaking population was very small, consisting primarily of railway servants, government officials, and Jews. 

To manage this diversity, the empire utilized a system of separate constituencies and registers for electors based on race. This ensured that candidates were elected by members of their own race; consequently, while internal political victories occurred between different factions (such as Clericals versus Liberals), it was impossible for Poles to achieve victory over Ruthenes.

## Political Aspirations and Relations with Austria
The Poles sought to maximize the autonomy of their own province. While they pursued their own independence, they showed no interest in opposing the centralization of other parts of the empire. Their primary goal regarding internal demographics was for Austria to surrender the Ruthenes to them.

Unlike some other Slav groups, the Poles were little influenced by pan-Slav agitation. They viewed a strong and united Austria as beneficial, as the monarchy provided them with power and freedom. However, their ultimate interests lay outside the monarchy, as they continued to look forward to the restoration of the Polish kingdom. This ambition created a strategic risk for the empire, as there was a danger that the Poles might entangle Austria in a war with Russia.

## Constitutional Struggles and the Reichsrath
During 1868, nationalist movements in Galicia and Bohemia challenged the existing constitution. In Galicia, an extreme party led by Smolka advocated for imitating the Czechs by refusing to attend the Reichsrath in Vienna. Although this extreme faction was outvoted, all Polish parties eventually agreed on a declaration of final demands. They requested a significant increase in the powers of the Galician diet and asked that Galician members cease attending the Reichsrath for matters that the Galician diet was qualified to handle. The Poles threatened to leave the Reichsrath entirely if these demands were not met.

Despite these efforts, the political settlement of dualism placed the Slav races—including the Poles, Czechs, Slovenes, Serbs, and Croatians—in a position of political inferiority. The underlying principle of this agreement was that the empire would be divided between the rule of the Germans and the Magyars.

## Language and Administration
In remote districts like Galicia, where few people understood German, the government utilized "external service" regulations. This allowed for the use of the local language in communications between private individuals and government offices, as well as in law courts. While not originally a law, this practice was established as a right under Article 19 of the 1867 constitution, which recognized the inviolable right of every race to preserve its own nationality and language.

The state recognized the equality of "customary" (*landesueblich*) languages in public life, offices, and schools. However, the government maintained power by determining which languages qualified as customary. This led to friction, as Germans demanded that German be recognized as a customary language in Galicia.

## Historical Conflict and Government Policy
The Austrian government occasionally employed policies that set different races against one another. In Galicia, Count Stadion implemented such a policy by winning over Ruthenian peasants through the abolition of feudal dues and by supporting a petition for the official recognition of the Ruthenian language alongside Polish. This occurred shortly before the bombardment of insurgent Cracow into submission on April 26. Earlier, on March 18, Count Stadion had proclaimed a new constitution for Galicia.
