# Bordeaux and Related Historical Records

## The City of Bordeaux
Bordeaux is a major city located in south-western France and serves as the capital of the department of Gironde. It is situated on the west (or left) bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea, within a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district. The city is positioned 159 miles north-west of Toulouse and 359 miles south-south-west of Paris. As of 1906, the population of Bordeaux was 237,707.

The geography of the city is defined by a semicircle described by the Garonne, which separates the main city from La Bastide, an important suburb on the right bank. These two areas are connected by a railway bridge and the Pont de Bordeaux, a stone structure from the early 19th century that measures 1,534 feet in length.

## Urban Layout and Landmarks
The city's architecture is characterized by low white houses, which differ from the high tenements found in other large French towns. The center of the city is marked by the Place des Quinconces, where narrow, winding streets concentrate urban life. This area is surrounded by a semicircle of boulevards and several suburbs, including Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat.

Key landmarks in Bordeaux include:
* **Place des Quinconces:** A central area planted with plane trees featuring two large statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne. It also contains two rostral columns that function as lighthouses and a monument dedicated to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed in 1793.
* **Place de la Comedie:** Located at the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces, this area houses the Grand Theatre, which dates to the 18th century.
* **Public Spaces:** The city's principal promenades include the allees de Tourny and a beautiful public garden.
