# Des paures amourous

The phrase "Des paures amourous" is associated with a specific motif found in the popular ballads and songs of various European regions, particularly within the folk traditions of Provence.

## Folk Ballad Motifs
In the study of comparative ballad plots, certain "story-roots" appear across different lands. One such common plot involves the "faithful leman," where a lord brings home a "braw new bride," and the original lover recovers the lord's affection at the eleventh hour. This narrative is found in the Scottish ballad of Lord Thomas and Fair Annie, the Danish *Skiaen Anna*, and twice in M. Fauriel's collection of Romaic songs.

Another recurring theme involves a girl who pretends to be dead so that she may be carried on a bier to meet her lover. This plot is present in Scotland, as well as in the popular songs of Metz (collected by Puymaigre) and Provence (collected by Damase Arbaud). In the versions from Metz and Provence, the story includes an incongruous sequel in which the lover attempts to murder his bride, but she proves too cunning and drowns him.

## The Symbolism of the Rose and Grave
A further familiar feature in these ballads is the imagery of the bush and briar, or two rose trees. In these stories, the trees meet and plait over the graves of unhappy lovers. This visual marker serves as a sign to all passers-by, who, in the Provencal tradition, remark: "Diou ague l'amo Des paures amourous."
