# William James Ashley

William James Ashley (1860–) was an English economist born in London on February 25, 1860. He received his education at Balliol College, Oxford, and St Olave's grammar school, later becoming a fellow of Lincoln College.

## Academic Career
Ashley held several prestigious professorships across North America and England. In 1888, he was appointed as the professor of constitutional history and political economy at Toronto University. He remained in this position until 1892, when he resigned to accept a post as professor of economic history at Harvard University. 

His academic career continued in England, where he was appointed professor of commerce and finance at Birmingham University in 1901. The following year, in 1902, he was named the dean of the faculty of commerce.

## Economic Contributions and Influence
Professor Ashley gained significant recognition for his research regarding the early history of English industry. Within the field of economics, he was a prominent figure among those English economists who provided support for the tariff reform movement led by Mr. Chamberlain.

## Published Works
Ashley authored several influential texts on economic history, theory, and labor. His most important works include:

*   *Early History of the English Woollen Industry* (1887)
*   *Introduction to English Economic History and Theory*, published in two parts between 1888 and 1893
*   *Surveys, Historic and Economic* (1900)
*   *Adjustment of Wages* (1903)
*   *The Tariff Problem* (2nd edition, 1904)
*   *Progress of the German Working Classes* (1904)
