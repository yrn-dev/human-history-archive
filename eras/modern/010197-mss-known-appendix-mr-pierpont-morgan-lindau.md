# J. Pierpont Morgan

J. Pierpont Morgan was an American figure involved in the organization of major industrial trusts and the collection of fine arts and antiquities.

## Industrial Activity
Mr. J. Pierpont Morgan was responsible for organizing the United States Steel Corporation trust in 1901. This incorporation included the various companies of Andrew Carnegie, who had developed extensive iron and steel industries at Pittsburg, including the Keystone Bridge works, the Edgar Thomson steel-rail mill, and the Homestead steel works. As a result of this trust organization, Carnegie retired from business after being bought out for a figure equivalent to a capital of approximately £100,000,000.

## Art and Antiquities Collections
Morgan assembled a significant collection of art and historical objects, spanning miniature paintings and Byzantine enamels.

### Miniature Paintings
The collection of Mr. J. Pierpont Morgan included many of the finest paintings by Francois Dumont (1751–1831), a prominent French miniature painter who painted portraits of Marie Antoinette, Louis XVI, Charles X, and Louis XVIII. A privately printed four-volume catalogue exists detailing the collection of miniatures owned by Morgan.

### Byzantine Enamels
The J. Pierpont Morgan collection at South Kensington includes a reliquary of great beauty, noted for the clearness and color of its enamel. This piece measures approximately 4½ by 3 inches and features a central crucifixion scene with St. John and St. Mary to the left and right, surrounded by busts of the apostles. The reliquary utilizes gold cloisons with a ground color of emerald green, while the remaining sections are primarily white and blue. In this depiction, Christ is vested in a tunic.
