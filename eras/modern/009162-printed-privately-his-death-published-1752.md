# 1752: Notable Publications and Events

The year 1752 marks a point of intersection for various intellectual, literary, and academic activities across Europe, characterized by the posthumous publication of historical treatises, the release of critical scholarly annotations, and the birth of influential authors.

## Literary and Historical Works
In the realm of historical writing, Bolingbroke, who had retired to France at the chateau of Argeville near Fontainebleau, produced his *Letters on the Study of History*. While this work was printed privately before his death, it was published in 1752. Bolingbroke's later years were marked by political efforts in England, including his role as an adviser to Frederick, prince of Wales, and his involvement in negotiations for the "broad bottom" administration in 1744. He died on December 12, 1751, shortly before the publication of the *Letters*.

## Scholarly and Academic Contributions
The year saw significant activity in classical scholarship and philology. Dr. Zachary Grey, who had previously published an edition of *Hudibras* in 1744, released an additional volume in 1752 titled *Critical and Historical and Explanatory Notes*. This work served as the foundation for all subsequent editions of the poem.

In the Netherlands, the legacy of the Dutch classical scholar Arnold Drakenborch (1684–1748) continued through the republication of his earlier works. His *Dissertatio de praefectis urbi*, originally published in 1704, was reprinted at Frankfort in 1752. This reprint included a biography of Drakenborch, who had spent his career as a professor of philology at Utrecht and was best known for his influential edition of Livy.

## Professional Appointments and Births
The year 1752 also witnessed transitions in academic leadership and the birth of future literary figures. Pieter Burmann the Younger, a Dutch philologist and professor of history and philology at the Athenaeum in Amsterdam, was appointed as general librarian in 1752. Burmann the Younger was noted for his extensive learning and talent for Latin poetry, eventually producing works such as the *Anthologia Veterum Latinorum Epigrammatum et Poematum*.

Additionally, October 16, 1752, marked the birth of the German author Adolf Franz Friedrich, Freiherr von Knigge, on the family estate of Bredenbeck near Hanover. Knigge would later become a member of the *Illuminati* under the name "Philo" and achieve literary fame for his work *Über den Umgang mit Menschen* (1788), which provided rules for leading a useful, happy, and peaceful life.

## Other Notable Figures of the Era
The broader period surrounding 1752 included the career of Louis de Cormontaingne, a French military engineer who died in 1752. Cormontaingne was a brigadier and *marechal de camp* known for his system of fortification at Metz. Although his *Architecture militaire* was written in 1714, it remained secret for years until an unauthorized edition appeared in 1741, leading him to prepare the *Premier memoire sur la fortification*. His works were not widely known to the general public until the early 19th century.
