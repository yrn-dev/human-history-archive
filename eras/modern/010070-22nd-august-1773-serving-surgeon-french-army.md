# 22nd of August 1773

The date of 22nd of August 1773 is associated with the birth of a prominent French botanist and the death of an English statesman.

## Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland
Born on the 22nd of August 1773 in La Rochelle, Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland (whose real name was Goujand) became a noted French traveller and botanist. His early professional life included service as a surgeon in the French army and the study of medicine under J.N. Corvisart in Paris.

Bonpland spent five years traveling through Colombia, Mexico, and the districts bordering the Amazon and Orinoco rivers in the company of A. von Humboldt. During these explorations, he classified and collected approximately 6,000 plants, the majority of which were previously unknown in Europe. These findings were later described in his work *Plantes equinoxiales*, published between 1808 and 1816.

Upon his return to Paris, Bonpland was granted a pension and appointed as the superintendent of the gardens at Malmaison. His literary contributions to botany include the *Description des plantes rares de Navarre* (1813) and *Monographie des Melastomees* (1806).

In 1816, Bonpland traveled to Buenos Aires, bringing various European plants with him. Although he was elected professor of natural history there, he soon left the position to explore central South America. While traveling toward Bolivia in 1821, he was arrested by order of the Paraguayan dictator Dr. Francia, who detained him until 1831. After regaining his freedom, Bonpland lived in the province of Corrientes at San Borga. He eventually moved to Santa Anna in 1853, where he died on 4th May 1858.

## George Lyttelton, 1st Baron
George Lyttelton, 1st Baron, was an English man of letters and statesman who died on the 22nd of August 1773. Born at Hagley, Worcestershire, he was the eldest son of Sir Thomas Lyttelton, 4th bart., and a descendant of the jurist Sir Thomas Littleton. 

Educated at Oxford and Eton, Lyttelton undertook a grand tour in 1728, during which he spent significant time in Rome and Paris. He entered public life in the same year as Pitt, serving in parliament for Okehampton, Devonshire. His political career included serving as a lord commissioner of the treasury from 1744 to 1754. In 1755, he became the chancellor of the exchequer, succeeding Legge. He left this office in 1756 when he was raised to the peerage as Baron Lyttelton of Frankley in the county of Worcester.

During the political crisis of 1765, prior to the formation of the Rockingham administration, Lyttelton was suggested for the leadership of the treasury, but he declined to participate in the scheme. He spent the final years of his life focusing primarily on literary pursuits.
