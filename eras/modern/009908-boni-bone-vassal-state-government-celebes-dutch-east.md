# BONI

Boni (also referred to as Bone) is a vassal state of the government of Celebes in the Dutch East Indies. It is situated on the Gulf of Boni within the south-west peninsula of Celebes. The state covers an area of 2,600 square miles. Its chief town, Boni, is located 2-1/2 miles from the peninsula's east coast and 80 miles northeast of Macassar.

## Geography and Economy
The region produces several agricultural products, including sugar-cane, cotton, coffee, tobacco, and rice, though none of these serve as important exports. The state is noted for its breeds of horses and buffaloes, which are highly esteemed. Additionally, the Bugis people carry on a significant trade in vegetable and mineral products from Boni, specifically camphor, nut-megs, pearls, tortoise-shell, and gold-dust.

## The Bugis People
The native race of Boni is the Bugis (or Bughis), a people of Malayan stock who originally occupied only the kingdom of Boni before spreading across the island of Celebes and establishing settlements throughout the Malay Archipelago. There are approximately 70,000 Bugis within the area of Boni.

Physically, the Bugis are robust and of middle size, with a complexion slightly lighter than the average Malay. They are described as brave, haughty, fierce, and industrious. They are noted for being more predisposed toward "running amuck" than any other Malayans. The Bugis are ingenious and practice agriculture more than neighboring tribes; they also manufacture cotton-cloth for both export and personal use. Due to their love of the sea, they have maintained almost a monopoly on trade around Celebes.

The Bugis speak a language allied to that of the Macassars, which is written using similar characters. This language was studied and its letters reproduced in type by Dr. B.F. Mathes of the Netherlands Bible Society.

## Governance and Society
Boni is composed of a confederation of eight petty states. The king is generally elected for life from among the Bugis by the chiefs of these eight states. The king is unable to decide on any public measure without the consent of these chiefs. Within the individual states, the office of chief is hereditary in some cases, while in others, any member of the privileged classes may aspire to the position. Consequently, women have occasionally governed these states. In Celebes, succession to the throne generally gives precedence to the female line over the male line.

The Bugis have been Mahommedans since the 17th century. However, they retain rites connected with the worship of Siva, as their original nature-worship was heavily influenced by Hindu traditions. The Bugis also maintain their own schools and well-built towns.

## Political History
Boni was once the most powerful state on Celebes, with all other princes regarded as vassals to its ruler, though detailed history from this period is not known. In 1666, Rajah Palakkah allied with the Dutch against Hassan, the tyrant of Sumatra, whose family had murdered Palakkah's father and grandfather. This alliance established undisputed Dutch influence in the state from 1666 until the start of the 19th century.

In 1814, the British took control of Boni, retaining it for two years. Following the downfall of Napoleon and the subsequent European treaties, the state reverted to the Dutch. Native resistance to Dutch influence persisted, leading to several military conflicts:
* In 1825, General van Geen led an expedition that was not fully successful in enforcing Dutch influence.
* In 1858 and 1859, two expeditions were required to oppose a princess regent who attempted to seek independence.
* In 1860, a new prince who owed allegiance to the Dutch was installed.
