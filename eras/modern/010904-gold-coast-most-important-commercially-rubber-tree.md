# Gold Coast

The Gold Coast is a region characterized by a diverse range of vegetable and mineral products, with a commercial economy heavily reliant on the export of raw materials and the extraction of precious metals.

## Agricultural and Vegetable Products
The vegetable products of the Gold Coast are varied, with the rubber tree (*Funtumia elastica*) being the most important from a commercial standpoint. Other significant botanical exports and crops include:
*   **Cocoa:** Since 1905, numerous cocoa plantations have been established, particularly within the eastern districts.
*   **Kola:** The nut of the kola tree is noted for being in great demand.
*   **Tobacco:** This crop is cultivated specifically in the northern regions.
*   **Gum Copal:** This substance is produced for export.

## Mining and Mineral Wealth
Gold is found in the western districts of the country. Between 1895 and 1901, several companies were formed to operate the mines in this area. By 1902 and the years following, the majority of the gold exported from the Gold Coast originated from the Obuassi mines. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti alone reached 68,259 oz., which held a value of L254,790.

## Trade and Infrastructure
The region's trade infrastructure has been significantly influenced by the development of the railway and political shifts. A railway line from Sekondi to Kumasi was completed in 1903; this route is notable for passing through the auriferous (gold-bearing) region, including the Obuassi and Tarkwa gold-fields.

The city of Kumasi serves as the distributing centre for the hinterland and the entirety of Ashanti. While trade from British territory moving southward is included in the Gold Coast's figures, Ashanti maintains considerable separate trade with its neighbors in Germany and France, as well as with the Niger countries to the north.

Historically, trade between Kumasi and Cape Coast was hindered by wars between England and the Ashanti, which diverted some of Ashanti's trade to the French port of Assini. However, following the suppression of the revolt of 1900, measures were implemented to improve trade between these two centers. The arrival of the railway in September 1903 prompted many merchants from Gold Coast ports to open branches in Kumasi, leading to a rapid expansion of the town and its eventual supplanting of the coast towns as the primary distributing center by 1906.

## Finance and Revenue
The revenue and expenditure of Ashanti are integrated into those of the Gold Coast. The primary sources of revenue include:
*   Contributions from gold-mining companies.
*   Rents derived from government land.
*   Liquor licences.
*   Caravan taxes.
