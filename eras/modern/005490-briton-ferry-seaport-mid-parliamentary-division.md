# BRITON-FERRY

## Geography and Location
Briton-Ferry is a seaport situated in Wales, specifically within the mid-parliamentary division of Glamorganshire. It is located on the eastern bank of the estuary of the Neath river in Swansea Bay. The town is positioned 174 miles from London by rail.

## Transport and Infrastructure
The town is served by stations on both the Rhondda & Swansea Bay and the Great Western railways. Additionally, a tram-line provides a connection to Neath, which is 2 miles distant. The Vale of Neath Canal, constructed in 1797, has its terminus at Briton-Ferry.

## Industrial Development and Economy
While the district was previously celebrated for its scenery, industrial development has considerably marred this. The primary impetus for this growth was the 1861 construction of a 13-acre dock owned by the Great Western Railway Company. Around the same period, the South Wales mineral railway opened up the mining districts of Maesteg and Glyncorrwg, connecting them to the dock. This railway supplies the dock with its primary export, which is coal.

Beyond the export of coal, the area maintains a large-scale manufacturing presence in tinplates and steel. The district also contains a foundry and iron-works.

## History and Nomenclature
The town has been known by two different names. Norman settlers of the 12th century named the ferry across the Neath estuary "La Brittone." This ferry is still in use today and was the crossing point used by Giraldus and Archbishop Baldwin in 1188. However, the Welsh name for the town, Llansawel, has been in use since at least the 16th century.

## Demographics
As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 6,973.
