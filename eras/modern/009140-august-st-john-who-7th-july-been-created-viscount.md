# St John, Viscount Bolingbroke

## Appointment and Diplomatic Missions
On July 7, St John was created Baron St John of Lydiard Tregoze and Viscount Bolingbroke. Following these appointments, he traveled to France in August to conduct negotiations. During this mission, he signed an armistice between France and England on the 19th of August, which was set for a duration of four months. These diplomatic efforts eventually led to the signing of the treaty of Utrecht on March 31, 1713, by all allies with the exception of the emperor.

## Political Controversies and the Treaty of Utrecht
The peacemaking process led by Bolingbroke was characterized by underhand conspiracies with the enemy rather than open conferences with allies. This approach, which involved the violation of solemn international assurances and the abandonment of the Catalans, is cited as having left a lasting stain on national credit and honor; Bolingbroke is held primarily responsible for these actions.

The peace was met with indignation from the Whigs, who used the first production of Addison's *Cato* as an occasion for demonstration. Bolingbroke further drew criticism by presenting the actor Booth with a purse of fifty guineas, stating it was for "defending the cause of liberty against a perpetual dictator," referring to Marlborough. Additionally, a commercial treaty with France intended to establish free trade was rejected in June.

## Relationship with Harley and Political Decline
The Tory administration was based on the friendship between Bolingbroke and Harley, a bond that gradually dissolved over time. In March 1711, an attempt on St John's life by Guiscard resulted in Harley receiving the wound intended for St John, which granted Harley significant credit. 

By July, St John experienced great disappointment when he received only his viscountcy rather than the earldom that had recently become extinct in his family. He was also passed over for the Garter. While Harley obtained the earldom of Oxford and the position of lord treasurer in May, St John's standing diminished. In September 1713, Swift arrived in London and attempted to reconcile the two men, but the effort was unsuccessful.

## Secret Communications and the Hanoverian Succession
As the health of the queen visibly declined, the Tory ministers faced the prospect of their own downfall upon the accession of the elector of Hanover. In response to this political instability, both Bolingbroke and Oxford maintained secret communications with James.
