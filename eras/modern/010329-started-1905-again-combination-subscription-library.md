# The Book Trade and Related Historical Records

## The Evolution of Publishing and Bookselling
In the history of the book trade, a distinction is made between booksellers and publishers. Booksellers are those whose trade is the selling of books through wholesale or retail means. Publishers, while originally booksellers themselves, evolved into a separate class of intermediaries. Their primary business involves the production of books from the manuscripts provided by authors. Because of this role, publishers have often exerted a significant influence on the history of literature.

While these roles are distinct, they sometimes overlap. A publisher may also operate as a wholesale bookseller. In some instances, the two functions have amalgamated. For example, in 1902, the London *Times* engaged in direct sales of supplementary volumes for the 9th edition of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, acting as both the publisher and the seller.

## Innovations in Book Distribution
Further combinations of business models appeared in the early 20th century. The *Times* Book Club, established in 1905, combined the business of bookselling with that of a subscription library. This model utilized the organizational structure and publicity capabilities of a newspaper to promote the sale of books, introducing a new factor into the bookselling industry.

## Historical Manuscripts and the Koran
European libraries house various manuscripts of the Koran, including fragments and codices that may date back to the 1st century of the Flight. However, for the purpose of restoring the text, the works of ancient scholars regarding writing modes and readings are considered more important than the manuscripts themselves, as the latter were produced by irresponsible copyists.

Some manuscripts claim to be originals. The library of the India Office possesses a manuscript with a subscription stating it was written by 'Othman the son of 'Affan, as well as one professing to be from the hand of 'Ali. These are identified as barefaced forgeries, despite their ancient date. In more recent times, the Koran has been produced via printing and lithography in both the West and the East, though lithography is the sole method employed in Mahommedan countries.

## Bibliography of Book-keeping
The study of book-keeping is supported by various specialized collections and texts. The library of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales is noted for having perhaps the most complete collection of modern and ancient works on accounting from both foreign and British sources. 

Key texts of general interest in this field include:
* *Book-keeping* by G. van de Linde (1898)
* *Book-keeping* (5th ed., 1906) and *Advanced Accounting* (2nd ed., 1905) by L.R. Dicksee
* *Encyclopaedia of Accounting*, edited by G. Lisle (1903)
* *Accountants' Library*, edited by the editor of *The Accountant* (1901)
* *The Antiquity of Book-keeping* by J.W. Heaps (1898)
* *History of Accounting and Accountants*, edited by R. Brown (1905)

## Ancient Public Subscriptions
The concept of the subscription also appears in ancient historical accounts. Alkibiades, a figure of noble birth and wealth, performed his first public act during a voluntary subscription for the State. Although he did not initially intend to participate, he subscribed after hearing a great noise and discovering that others were making voluntary subscriptions. This act was met with cheers and applause from the people.
