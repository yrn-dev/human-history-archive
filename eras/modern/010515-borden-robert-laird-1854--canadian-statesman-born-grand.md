# BORDEN, ROBERT LAIRD

## Early Life and Legal Career
Robert Laird Borden was a Canadian statesman born on June 26, 1854, at Grand Pre, Nova Scotia. He pursued a career in law and was called to the bar in 1878. Following his admission to the bar, he established himself as a leading lawyer within his native province of Nova Scotia.

## Parliamentary Career
Borden entered the Canadian parliament in 1896, representing the city of Halifax. Although he later lost his seat in Halifax, he subsequently secured election for Carlton. He returned to represent Halifax once again following the general election of 1908.

## Political Leadership
In February 1901, Borden ascended to the position of leader of the Conservative opposition. This appointment occurred following the resignation of Sir Charles Tupper.

## Naval Policy and International Relations
As a leader of the Canadian Government, Sir Robert Borden was involved in complex naval efforts and disputes regarding the construction of Dreadnoughts. The Canadian Government had stipulated that three Canadian Dreadnoughts should be additional to the 60 per cent standard. Based on formal declarations that these ships were indispensable, Borden committed himself to a "fierce party fight" within Canada to secure them.

However, due to the actions of the Canadian Senate, it became clear that these indispensable ships would not be laid down in the ensuing year. During a crisis involving the acceleration of other ships to cover this interim default, Borden provided a "masterly telegram" to colleagues in England. In this communication, he set forth the embarrassed position his Government would face regarding their naval effort if additional measures were not taken to address the default.
