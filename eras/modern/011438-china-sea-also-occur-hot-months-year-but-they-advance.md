# Cyclones of the China Sea

## Overview of Asian Revolving Storms
Among the notable phenomena occurring in the hotter seas of Asia are revolving storms, also known as cyclones. These storms occur frequently during the hot months of the year within the Indian Ocean and the China Sea. In the China Sea, these specific cyclones are known as typhoons.

## Characteristics of China Sea Typhoons
Cyclones in the China Sea occur during the hot months of the year. They typically originate near the island of Formosa and extend to approximately the 10th degree of North latitude. While they develop in the same months and nearly the same latitudes as the cyclones of the Indian Sea, their movement differs. Typhoons in the China Sea generally advance from the north-east to the south-west, although they occasionally move from east to west.

## Comparison with Indian Ocean Cyclones
The cyclones of the Bay of Bengal typically originate over the Nicobar and Andaman islands. Unlike those in the China Sea, these storms commonly propagate in a north-westward direction. They strike the east coast of the Indian peninsula at various points and often advance over the land with an easterly tendency, passing across the delta of the Ganges with extreme violence. These occur from June to October, and less frequently in November, appearing to be caused by south-west monsoon currents meeting adverse currents from the north.

Despite the difference in the direction of their progress, both the China Sea and Indian Sea storms appear to advance toward the area of greatest heat. In both types of storms, the wind invariably circulates from north by west, through south, to east.

## Regional Environmental Context
The China Sea is part of a broader region—including southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, and the western part of the Malay Archipelago—characterized by high temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains. This region shares significant similarities in its animals and plants. In contrast, North China and Japan share many forms of life in common.
