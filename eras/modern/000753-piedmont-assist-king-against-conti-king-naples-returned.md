# Piedmont, the King of Naples, and the Campaign of 1744

## The Italian War of 1744
In 1744, the Italian war reached a stage of serious intensity. A grandiose campaign plan was developed with the objective of uniting the army located in Dauphine with the forces stationed on the lower Po. To facilitate a road into central Italy, the adhesion of Genoa was secured. However, the execution of these plans was hampered by orders from the respective governments of France and Spain, which restricted their generals at the front.

## Military Engagements and the Role of Naples
The offensive began with Lobkowitz, who drove the Spanish army, commanded by Count de Gages, back toward the Neapolitan frontier. This movement compelled the king of Naples to provide assistance to the Spaniards regardless of the hazards. A combined army was subsequently formed at Velletri, where they succeeded in defeating Lobkowitz on August 11.

Once this crisis had passed, the strategic movements of the primary commanders shifted. Lobkowitz traveled to Piedmont to provide assistance to the king against Conti. Following this shift in the conflict, the king of Naples returned home, while Count de Gages continued to follow the Austrians with a weak force.

## Conflict in the Alps and Apennines
The war continued with keen contestation across the Apennines and the Alps. On April 20, Conti stormed Montalban and Villefranche. A desperate fight occurred at Peyre-Longue on July 18. On September 30, the king of Sardinia suffered a defeat in a great battle at Madonna del Olmo, located near Coni (Cuneo). Despite this victory, Conti was unable to capture the fortress of Coni and was forced to retire to Dauphine for his winter quarters. Ultimately, the attempt to combine the two armies failed, as the Austro-Sardinians remained positioned between them.

## Broader Strategic Context
The events of 1744 coincided with the start of the Second Silesian War. Frederick, concerned by the success of the Austrian cause, entered into a secret alliance with Louis XV. France, which had previously acted as an auxiliary with its officers in Germany wearing the Bavarian cockade and being officially at war only with England, declared direct war upon Sardinia and Austria in April 1744.

As part of this broader conflict, a fresh army under the prince de Conti was tasked with assisting the Spaniards in Lombardy and Piedmont. Simultaneously, Louis XV prepared to invade the Austrian Netherlands with 90,000 men, capturing Ypres and Menin, though he later abandoned this invasion to take a decisive part in the war in Lorraine and Alsace. On the Rhine, Coigny faced Prince Charles, who, aided by Traun, maneuvered his army near Philipsburg on July 1, capturing the lines of Weissenburg and cutting off the French marshal from Alsace.
