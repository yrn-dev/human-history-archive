# Communications

In the context of human history, communications encompass the physical infrastructure used for transport and the strategic movement of forces, as well as the diplomatic and interpersonal exchanges between states and individuals.

## Strategic and Military Communications
The control of specific territories has historically been sought to facilitate communication between allied powers. In ancient Greece, for example, the city of Epidaurus was targeted for possession by the Argeians and Athenians. Acquiring Epidaurus would have significantly facilitated communication between Athens and Argos. This was due to the city's location immediately opposite the Athenian-occupied island of Ægina and its ability to provide land access to Athens. This land route would have allowed the Athenians to avoid the labor of circumnavigating Cape Skyllæum, the southeastern point of the Epidaurian and Argeian peninsula, when sending forces to Argos.

## Infrastructure and Transport
The development of physical communications, such as railways, has often been linked to broader political and humanitarian goals. In Uganda, the construction of a railway from Mombasa to Victoria Nyanza was proposed as a means to open up the country and support common efforts for the suppression of the slave trade. While the company involved in the region sought imperial aid for this undertaking, the initial scheme was wrecked in parliament despite the support of Prime Minister Lord Salisbury. The railway was eventually constructed, though it was built entirely at government cost.

## Diplomatic and Political Communications
Communications between nations often involve the establishment of spheres of influence and the negotiation of treaties. In the late 19th century, Great Britain and Germany engaged in diplomatic correspondence regarding the "hinterland doctrine," which suggested that Uganda fell within the British sphere of influence. However, this was contested by German public opinion and maps.

Tensions were further complicated by the actions of Karl Peters, a German official who led a raiding expedition into Uganda in 1889. In response to representations from Uganda and reports of Peters's activities, F.J. Jackson—who had previously been instructed to avoid the area—determined to enter the country. Peters claimed "effective occupation" based on a treaty dated March 1, 1890, while Jackson concluded a separate treaty in April. These disputes were eventually resolved through the Anglo-German agreement of July 1, 1890, which assigned Uganda to the British sphere. To consolidate this position and counter hostile French missionaries and intrigues, Captain F.D. Lugard was sent to the capital, Mengo, in December 1890 to establish the company's authority.

## Interpersonal and Secret Communications
Communications have also played a role in political betrayals and the maintenance of state loyalty. In early Japanese history, during the reign of Emperor Yuryaku, a high official named Tasa was sent to be governor of Mimana. After discovering that the Emperor had debauched his wife, Tasa revolted and sought aid from the Shiragi people. Emperor Yuryaku ordered Tasa's son, Oto, to lead a force against his father. While Oto appeared to comply, he opened secret communication with Tasa. The two agreed that Tasa would hold Imna and break relations with Japan, while Oto would do the same with Paikche. This plot was ultimately frustrated when Oto's wife, Kusu, killed her husband to prevent the treason and informed the Court of Yamato.
