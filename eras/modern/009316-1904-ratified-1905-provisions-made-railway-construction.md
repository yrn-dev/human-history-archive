# Railway Construction Provisions of 1904-1905 in Bolivia

In 1904, provisions were made for the construction of railways in Bolivia through treaties with Chile and Brazil. These agreements were designed to bring the isolated region of Bolivia into more effective communication with the outside world and to facilitate the development of its resources.

## Treaty with Chile
A treaty was ratified in 1905 to settle a long-standing boundary dispute between Bolivia and Chile regarding the occupation of the former Bolivian provinces of Arica and Tacna. Under the terms of this settlement, Bolivia was compelled to cede the maritime provinces occupied by Chile since the war of 1881, resulting in the republic losing its seaport.

In exchange, the government of Chile undertook the following obligations:
* **Railway Construction:** Chile agreed to construct a railway from Arica to the Bolivian capital, La Paz, at its own cost. This line spans 295 miles; the section located within Bolivia is designated to become Bolivian property fifteen years after its completion.
* **Transit Rights:** Chile agreed to grant Bolivians free transit through Chilean territory to specific towns on the coast.
* **Financial Assistance:** Chile provided a cash indemnity and agreed to lend pecuniary assistance for the construction of other railways necessary to open up the country. Specifically, Chile agreed to pay the interest (capped at 5%) that Bolivia might guarantee on capital invested in certain interior railways, provided these were constructed within thirty years. These interest payments were limited to £100,000 per year and a total aggregate of £1,600,000.

## Treaty with Brazil
Under a treaty signed in 1903, Brazil also made provisions to improve Bolivian communications. Brazil agreed to construct a railway approximately 180 miles long around the falls of the Madeira to provide north-eastern Bolivia with access to the Amazon. Additionally, Brazil paid £2,000,000 in cash, which Bolivia was to utilize for railway construction within its own territory.

## Other Railway Developments and Infrastructure
Bolivia's railway network was limited prior to these agreements. Up to 1903, the only existing lines were the Antofagasta and Oruro line (574 miles total, with 350 miles in Bolivian territory), a 26-mile private branch to the Pulacayo mines, and a 54-mile line from Guaqui on Lake Titicaca to Alto de La Paz. The latter is connected to the city of La Paz—which sits 1,493 feet below—by a 5-mile electric line. This system provides La Paz with access to the Peruvian port of Mollendo (496 miles distant) and is expected to eventually provide communication with Cuzco.

Furthermore, Argentina had undertaken to extend its northern railway from Jujuy to the Bolivian frontier town of Tupiza. The Peruvian Corporation also constructed the 54-mile line from Guaqui to Alto de La Paz for the Bolivian government.

## Historical Context and Future Projections
The necessity for these treaties arose from Bolivia's weakened diplomatic position. Following the war with Chile in 1878-81, the railways of the Litoral department (282 miles long) had passed under Chilean control. By 1904, President Ismail Montes and his predecessor, President Pando, recognized that Bolivia could not compel concessions from Chile or rely on the support of Argentina due to a final boundary settlement between Argentina and Chile.

By 1907, several additional lines were projected to further integrate the country, including:
* From La Paz to Cochabamba.
* From La Paz to the navigable waters of the Beni.
* From Viacha to Oruro.
* From Uyuni to Potosi, Sucre, and Tupiza.
* From Arica to La Paz via Coro.
