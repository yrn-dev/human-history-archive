# Glengarriff and Killarney

Glengarriff and Killarney are locations in the southwest of Ireland, associated with the scenic landscapes of County Kerry and the transport routes connecting them to County Cork.

## Transport and Connectivity
Killarney and Glengarriff are destinations on the "Prince of Wales" coaching route, which originates in Cork. This route is linked to the railway system via Bantry, a seaport and market-town in County Cork. Bantry serves as the terminus of the railway and acts as a coaching station for travelers proceeding toward Glengarriff and Killarney.

## Killarney: Geography and Scenery
Killarney is an inland center renowned for its scenic attractions and is widely visited. The area is characterized by striking and picturesque mountain scenery.

### Lakes and Waterways
The lakes of Killarney are among the most important features of the region's landscape. The Upper Lake of Killarney is situated in a hollow of Old Red Sandstone. In contrast, Lough Leane rests on Carboniferous Limestone and is noted for its low shores. 

The Flesk river flows northward through the lower Lake of Killarney; after this point, it is renamed the Laune and continues north-westward toward Dingle Bay. Additionally, the northern Flesk, which rises in the mountains dividing Kerry from Cork, flows into the Maine river.

### Mountains and Topography
The hills near Killarney rise abruptly into the ragged range of Macgillicuddy's Reeks. This range contains the highest summit in the area, Carntual (Carrantuohill), reaching 3,414 feet. Other significant peaks include Caper, at 3,200 feet, and several others exceeding 2,500 feet. The Gap of Dunloe is located between the Reeks, the Purple Mountains, and the precipitous sides of the Tomies.

Near the summit of Mangerton Mountain, a deep hollow collects water to form the Devil's Punchbowl. The surplus water from this hollow flows into Muckross Lake at the foot of the mountain via a succession of cataracts.

## Geology and Natural Resources
The geology of the region varies by location. The mountains of the great promontories are formed of Old Red Sandstone, which includes the "Glengariff Grits." 

In the vicinity of Killarney, a copper-mine was formerly operated at Muckross, where cobalt ores were also discovered. The region also features chalybeate mineral springs.

## Fauna
The mountains surrounding Killarney are inhabited by red deer. Other wildlife in the broader county includes numerous foxes, as well as badgers and otters. The alpine hare is described as very abundant in the area.
