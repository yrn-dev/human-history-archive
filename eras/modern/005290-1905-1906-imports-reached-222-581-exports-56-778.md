# Trade and Economic Statistics (1890–1908)

This archive entry details various trade figures, import and export values, and industrial production statistics across different global regions and sectors during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Regional Trade and Port Activity

### African Protectorates and Provinces
In the protectorate, the total trade value for the year 1899-1900 was £255,384, representing a 75% increase over the 1898-1899 figures. During this period, exports were valued at £79,449 (a 109% increase) and imports at £176,035 (a 62% increase). By 1905-1906, imports reached £222,581 and exports were £56,778. In the Rhodesian provinces during the same 1905-1906 period, exports were £18,000 and imports were approximately £50,000, excluding railway material. Principal exports from this region include ivory, rubber, tobacco, cotton, coffee, and minerals. Approximately 200 Scotsmen and Englishmen are settled as coffee planters, primarily in the Shiré Highlands.

In Uganda, exports rose from £89,858 in 1900-1901 to £234,664 in 1904-1905. In that same year, imports exceeded £500,000 for the first time. By 1906-1907, the total volume of trade reached £1,194,352, with imports valued at £753,647 and exports at £440,705. The United States received 33% of these exports, followed by Great Britain at 15%. Uganda's exports consist chiefly of fibre, cotton, beeswax, chillies, ivory, rubber, potatoes, grains, skins and hides, and copra. Imports are largely manufactured goods, hardware, and textiles from Europe and India, with Great Britain and India together supplying over 50% of the total. Germany holds the leading share among other trading countries. Retail trade is largely managed by Indians.

### European and Persian Ports
The Italian seaport of Brindisi, located in the province of Lecce, saw 1,492 vessels totaling 1,486,269 tons clear the port in 1905. Imports in 1905 were valued at £629,892 and exports at £663,201, reflecting increases of £84,077 and £57,807 respectively over the previous year. In 1899, these figures were lower, at £298,400 for imports and £253,000 for exports. Main imports include metals, timber, sulphur, flour, and coal, while main exports are dried fruits, oil, and spirits and wine. The harbour railway station was completed in 1905. Passenger traffic has declined; while the average was 14,728 between 1893-1897, it fell to 7,608 in 1905.

In France, the port of Calais serves as the primary hub for continental passenger traffic to England. Between 1902 and 1906, the average number of passengers between Calais and Dover was 315,012. From 1901-1905, the average annual value of imports was £4,145,000 and exports were £8,388,000. In 1905, general carrying trade involved 848 entering vessels (312,477 tons) and 857 clearing vessels (305,284 tons). Principal exports include fruit, glass-ware, lace, woven goods, potatoes, straw, hay, spirits, and champagne. Imports include cork, cotton yarn, raw wool, timber, petroleum, iron and steel, coal, and silk and cotton goods.

Bander Abbasi serves as a trade center for south-eastern and central Persia, as well as Khorasan. Between 1890 and 1905, the average annual value of imports and exports was approximately £660,000, with imports averaging £340,000 British (£400,000) and exports averaging £155,000 British (£260,000). In 1905, 237,000 of the 255,000 tons of shipping that entered the port were British.

## Industrial Production and Commodities

### Brewing and Hops
In the United Kingdom, brewing production peaked in 1900 at 37,090,986 barrels. Despite a steady decline since then, the 1905-1906 production of 34,109,263 barrels remained higher than any year before 1897. In the year ending September 30, 1905, the exchequer received £13,156,053 in beer duty. There were 5,180 brewers for sale; Messrs Guinness owned the world's largest brewery, producing over two million barrels and paying roughly one million sterling to revenue. Three other firms produced one million barrels or more.

Consumption in 1905-1906 was distributed as follows: 28,590,563 barrels in England, 3,265,084 in Ireland, and 1,648,463 in Scotland. Beer imports were 57,194 barrels and exports were 520,826 barrels. In 1905-1906, the average specific gravity of the beer was 1053.24. Materials used included 62,360,817 lb of hops, 51,818,697 bushels of malt, 2,746,615 cwt. of sugar, 1,348,558 cwt. of flaked maize, rice, and similar materials, 125,671 bushels of unmalted corn, and 49,202 lb of hop substitutes.

Regarding hop cultivation in the United States, the average annual produce from 1900 to 1906 was 423,471 cwt. Eighty percent was grown on the Pacific coast, with yields in Washington and California (12-14 cwt. per acre) and Oregon (9-10 cwt. per acre) exceeding those of New York (5-6 cwt. per acre). Between 1899 and 1905, the average annual export (mostly to Great Britain) was 108,400 cwt., while average imports (mostly from Germany) were about 50,000 cwt.

### Other Commodities
British corn exports reached a maximum of 1,667,778 quarters in 1749-1750, but fell to 600,000 quarters by 1760. Between 1740 and 1751, the exchequer paid £1,515,000 in bounties, with £324,000 paid in 1749 alone.

## Financial and Banking Systems

In the United States, the gold standard law of March 14, 1900, allowed banks to issue circulation up to the full par-value of deposited bonds. The tax on circulation was reduced from 1% to 1/2 of 1% for circulation secured by 2% refunding bonds. Consequently, the volume of notes outstanding secured by bonds rose from $207,920,774 on October 31, 1899, to $298,829,064 in 1900, $328,198,613 in 1901, $335,783,189 in 1902, $380,650,821 in 1903, $424,530,581 in 1904, $490,037,806 in 1905, $536,933,169 in 1906, and $562,727,614 in 1907.
