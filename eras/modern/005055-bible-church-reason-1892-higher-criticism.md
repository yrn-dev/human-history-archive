# The Higher Criticism of the Bible

The study of the Bible involves various analytical methods, most notably the distinction between textual criticism and "higher criticism." While textual criticism focuses on the correction and elucidation of the text, higher criticism provides a general historical account based on the substance and matter of the writings. This method treats the Old Testament as an ancient literature, applying the same critical methods that have been used since the Renaissance for other ancient texts.

## Nature and Methodology of Higher Criticism
Higher criticism is a method of analysis rather than a single set of conclusions. Its results are often numerous and vary in their interdependence. Some critical conclusions are mutually independent, meaning the proof or disproof of one does not affect the others. For example, the conclusions that Moses is not the author of the whole Pentateuch, that Isaiah is not the author of Isaiah xl.-lxvi., and that the book of Daniel was written in the 2nd century B.C. are independent of one another.

Conversely, some conclusions are interrelated. If the conclusion that Moses is not the author of the Pentateuch were disproved, the conclusion that the Priestly Code is post-exilic would immediately fall through. Furthermore, the validity of the dating of many Psalms would be weakened, as the arguments for their date depend on whether the Priestly Code is considered early or late.

Because of this structure, higher criticism cannot be overthrown simply by disproving a few points; it would require proving that the application of criticism to the Old Testament is unlawful or proving every independent judgment false one by one. Some scholars, such as Professor Sayce, have challenged higher criticism while still acknowledging that the Pentateuch is a compilation of various older materials that likely reached its final shape under Ezra.

## Relation to Archaeology
Since the middle of the 19th century, archaeology—including the excavation of Palestine and the decipherment of Assyrian and Egyptian inscriptions—has provided new facts regarding the Old Testament. However, archaeology has not yielded direct testimony regarding the authorship or the specific date of composition of any biblical book. Consequently, it neither proves nor disproves the documentary analysis of the Pentateuch. It has, however, strengthened critical judgments that certain writings, such as the book of Daniel and the story of Joseph in Genesis, were not written contemporary to the events they describe.

## Theological Conflicts and the Case of Charles Augustus Briggs
The application of higher criticism has led to significant conflict within religious institutions. Charles Augustus Briggs (1841–), an American Hebrew scholar and professor at the Union Theological Seminary, was tried for heresy by the presbytery of New York in 1892. The charges stemmed from an inaugural address in which Briggs argued that:
* Reason and the Church are fountains of divine authority that can enlighten men independently of Holy Scripture.
* The original text of Holy Scripture may have contained errors.
* Moses did not author the Pentateuch, and Isaiah did not author half of the book bearing his name.
* Many Old Testament predictions were reversed by history, and the majority of Messianic predictions cannot be fulfilled.
* Sanctification is not complete at death, and redemption extends to the world to come.

Although acquitted by the presbytery, Briggs was suspended by the general assembly in 1893, a decision influenced in part by the "dogmatic and irritating" tone of his address. He later became a priest of the Protestant Episcopal Church in 1899.

## Conservative and Orthodox Responses
In contrast to the views of Briggs, other scholars maintained a "conservative Higher Criticism." William Henry Green (1825–1900), a professor at Princeton Theological Seminary and leader of the orthodox wing of the American Presbyterian church, utilized his knowledge of Semitic languages to defend traditional views. His works include *The Pentateuch Vindicated from the Aspersions of Bishop Colenso* (1863) and *The Unity of the Book of Genesis* (1895).

## Historical Context of Biblical Authority
The shift in how the Bible is viewed as an authority began significantly during the 16th-century Church reform. Reformers used the New Testament to contrast Apostolical Christianity with the papal system. This led the Reformed Churches to transfer the authority previously ascribed to the Church to the Bible, summarized by the watchword "The Bible, the Bible alone, is the religion of Protestants." Even early reformers like Luther applied critical judgment; in his German translation, Luther placed the books of Jude, James, Hebrews, and the Apocalypse in an appendix with explanatory prefaces.
