# Euplocamus, Pajo, Ceriornis, and the Phasianidae

## Overview of the Indian Region's Avifauna
The assemblage of birds found within the Indian region is noted as one of the most varied and richest in the world, surpassed only by the birds of tropical America. Nearly every order of birds is well represented in this region, with the sole exception of the Struthiones, or ostriches. 

## Peculiar Genera of the Region
The Indian region is characterized by the presence of many peculiar genera that are not found in other parts of the world. Among these distinct genera are *Euplocamus*, *Pajo*, and *Ceriornis*. Other peculiar genera found in this region include *Buceros*, *Harpactes*, and *Lophophorus*.

## The Phasianidae
The *Phasianidae* (excluding true *Phasianus*) are highly characteristic of the Indian region. These pheasants have attained such a significant level of development across the islands and various parts of the Asiatic continent that their home is regarded as being fixed there. Furthermore, the *Phasianidae* are among the forms of birds that are prevalent throughout the Indian Region but are entirely wanting in the Australian region.

## Related Regional Bird Families
In addition to the *Phasianidae* and the peculiar genera such as *Euplocamus*, *Pajo*, and *Ceriornis*, other bird groups are characteristic of the area. These include:
* Certain genera of crows, specifically *Cissa*, *Dendrocitta*, and *Urocissa*.
* Certain genera of parrots, such as *Palaeornis*.
* Certain genera of barbets, such as *Megalaema*.
* The family *Eurylaemidae*, which is entirely confined to this part of Asia.
* Bulbuls (*Ixidae*), which are characteristic of most parts of Asia and Africa, including the sub-group *Phyllornithinae*, which is peculiar to the Indian Region.
* The widely-spread families of vultures (*Vulturidae*) and barbets (*Megalaeminae*).

## Regional Contrasts
The bird life of the Indian region stands in contrast to the Ethiopian fauna, which occupies the desert districts of Syria and Arabia and intrudes into the south-western corner of Asia. While some Ethiopian species reach western India, Sind, and Persia, the Ethiopian bird type is characterized by the prevalence of stone chats and larks. The Ethiopian type is further distinguished by the complete absence of the many peculiar genera—such as *Euplocamus*, *Pajo*, and *Ceriornis*—that define the Indian region.
