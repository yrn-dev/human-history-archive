# The Volta River

The Volta is a river in West Africa that serves as a significant geographical marker and frontier for several colonial territories and regions.

## Geographical Role and Borders
The Volta river acts as a boundary separating the British possession of Ashanti from the German colony of Togoland to the east. Within the territory of Ashanti, the river and the Black Volta form the northern frontier. Additionally, various tributaries of the Volta traverse the eastern portion of Ashanti, generally flowing in a northerly direction.

In the broader context of West African geography, the Volta is also associated with the region of Dahomey. The Dahomeyans, who inhabit the central part of the French colony of Dahomey, are among eighteen closely-allied clans occupying the land between Porto Novo and the Volta.

## Regional Context: Ashanti and Togoland
The Volta defines the eastern limit of Ashanti, a British possession that also borders the French Ivory Coast colony to the west and the British Protectorate of the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast to the north. Ashanti covers an area of 23,000 square miles and had an estimated population of 500,000 in 1907. While the Volta and its tributaries flow through the region, other rivers in Ashanti—such as the Tano, Bia, and the upper courses of the Ofin and Prah—are navigable only by canoes.

To the east of the Volta lies Togoland, a German colony. During the outbreak of the First World War, Togoland was the target of an invasion by an enterprising Captain. It was one of six separate expeditions planned by the British Cabinet to seize German colonies globally to deny the German cruisers bases or refuges and to secure hostages for the eventual liberation of Belgium. At the time of the war's onset, the Governor of Togoland was in Germany on leave and subsequently volunteered as an intelligence officer for his old army corps.

## Surrounding Territories
The regions surrounding the Volta basin include various colonial administrations:

*   **Dahomey:** A French colony and former independent kingdom bounded to the west by the German colony of Togoland. It has a population of over 1,000,000 and an area of approximately 40,000 square miles. The inhabitants of the central region, the Dahomeyans (or Fon/Fawin), are Ewe-speaking tribes.
*   **Benin:** Historically, the name Benin was applied to the coast extending from the Rio del Rey to the Volta (located at 0 deg. 40' E.). This area included the Slave Coast and the Niger delta. Until 1894, "Benin" was also used to describe the French possessions on the coast that are now part of Dahomey.
