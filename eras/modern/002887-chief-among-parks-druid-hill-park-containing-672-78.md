# Public Parks and Open Spaces in Selected Cities

The development of public parks and open spaces varies across global municipalities, ranging from expansive municipal systems in the United States to designated landmarks and donated estates in Scotland and Hungary.

## Parks of Baltimore
As of 1908, the department of public parks and squares in Baltimore had jurisdiction over 33 squares and 13 parks that each measured 10 acres or more. The total acreage for squares was 86.53 acres, while the total acreage for parks reached 2188 acres. 

Druid Hill Park, located in the northwest, is the chief among these parks; it is famous for its natural beauty and contains 672.78 acres. Other significant parks include:
* **Clifton Park**: Spanning 311.26 acres and located 2 miles east of Druid Hill, this land was formerly part of the Johns Hopkins estate before the city acquired it in 1895.
* **Patterson Park**: Located in the extreme southeast, this 125.79-acre park is a favorite resort for those living in East Baltimore.

## Parks of Los Angeles and Buffalo
Los Angeles features 22 parks containing approximately 3800 acres within or on the borders of the city limits. The largest of these is Griffith Park, which covers 3015 acres. Other parks include Elysian (532 acres), Eastlake (57 acres), Echo (38 acres), and Westlake (35 acres).

Buffalo's municipal park system is described as one of unusual beauty, consisting of a chain of parks totaling about 1030 acres. These are connected by driveways and boulevards and encircle the city. The largest is Delaware Park, which covers about 365 acres and includes a 46½-acre lake. The northern portion of Delaware Park was enclosed within the grounds of the 1901 Pan-American Exposition. Other parks in Buffalo include Humboldt Park, Cazenovia Park, South Park on the Lake Shore, and "The Front," a bluff overlooking the source of the Niagara river that contains Fort Porter.

## Parks of Louisville and Boston
Louisville possesses an extensive park system, primarily acquired after 1889 and situated on the outskirts of the city. Iroquois Park, located on a wooded hill, covers 670 acres and is accessed via South Parkway, which extends 6 miles south from the city center and is 150 feet wide. Other parks include Cherokee Park (nearly 330 acres) at the east end of Broadway and Shawnee Park (about 170 acres) at the west end of Broadway, the latter of which features a long sandy river beach used by bathers. Central Park is located in the old fashionable residence districts and occupies the space of two city squares.

In Boston, the residential and manufacturing district of Dorchester contains Franklin Park, which is one of the largest parks in the Boston park system. Franklin Field is also located in this district.

## Parks of Budapest and Brisbane
In Budapest, the largest and most popular park is the Városliget, located on the northeast side of town. It covers 286 acres and houses the zoological garden, as well as the ethnographical and agricultural museums (the latter built 1902-1904) on an island in its large pond. The park was the site of the 1896 millennium exhibition. Margaret island, a long narrow island in the Danube and property of the archduke Joseph, is laid out as an English park with bath-houses, villas, and fine trees. Additionally, the royal castle of Gödöllö, located 23 miles north, possesses a beautiful park.

Brisbane, the capital of Queensland, features Queen's Park, which serves as a recreation ground. From this park, Albert Street leads through the heart of the city to Albert Park, where the observatory is located. The city also contains botanical gardens adjoining the grounds of Government House.

## Parks of Scotland
In Kilmarnock, the grounds of Kilmarnock House were laid out as a public park after being presented to the town in 1893. Kay Park, which was purchased from the duke of Portland for £9000, covers 48¾ acres. It contains a Reformers' monument unveiled in 1885 and the Burns Memorial, which includes a tower, a museum of the poet's manuscripts, and the McKie library of Burns's books.

In the city where Dundee Law rises (571 ft.), the surrounding park covers 18 acres. Other open spaces include:
* **Baxter Park**: A 37-acre park presented to the town by his sisters and Sir David Baxter (1793-1872). It was laid out by Sir Joseph Paxton and features a statue of Sir David by Sir John Steell.
* **Balgay**: A finely wooded hill acquired in 1869, of which 36 acres were converted into a park.
* **Lochee Park**: A 25-acre park given to the city in 1891 by Messrs Cox Brothers of Camperdown Works.
* **Fair Muir**: A 12-acre park secured in 1890.
* **Dudhope (or Barrack) Park**: Purchased in 1893.
* **Magdalen Green**: An old common of 17 acres.
