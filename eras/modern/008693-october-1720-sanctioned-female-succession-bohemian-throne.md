# The Sanctioning of Female Succession to the Bohemian Throne (October 1720)

At the beginning of the 18th century, the house of Habsburg faced the possibility of the extinction of its male line. To address this crisis of succession, the estates of Bohemia convened a meeting in Prague on October 16, 1720. During this assembly, the estates sanctioned the female succession to the Bohemian throne.

## The Pragmatic Sanction
In sanctioning female succession, the Bohemian estates recognized the "Pragmatic Sanction." This legal instrument proclaimed the indivisibility of the Habsburg realm. The broader Pragmatic Sanction, first promulgated by Emperor Charles VI on April 19, 1713, settled the succession to the throne within the dynasty of Habsburg-Lorraine. It established that the throne would descend by right of lineal succession and primogeniture to male heirs; however, in the event that the male line became extinct, the succession would pass to the female line.

## Maria Theresa and Subsequent Conflict
These enactments were made specifically in favor of Archduchess Maria Theresa. Despite the formal sanctioning of her succession by the Bohemian estates in 1720, Maria Theresa encountered significant opposition upon the death of her father, Emperor Charles VI.

Charles, the elector of Bavaria, challenged the succession by raising claims to the Bohemian throne. He launched an invasion of the country supported by a large army consisting of French, Saxon, and Bavarian troops. This campaign resulted in the occupation of Prague. By December 19, 1741, a large number of Bohemian knights and nobles had taken the oath of allegiance to Charles of Bavaria. However, the tide of war eventually shifted, and Maria Theresa succeeded in recovering Bohemia along with the other lands previously ruled by the house of Habsburg.

## Impact on Bohemian Autonomy
The period following the succession disputes saw a trend toward the limitation of Bohemian autonomy. During the reign of Maria Theresa, and more extensively under her son, Joseph II, several changes were made to the internal administration of the Habsburg realm. 

Two significant administrative shifts occurred:
1. In 1749, a decree was issued that abolished the separate law-courts that had continued to exist within Bohemia.
2. A few years later, an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was appointed. This official was granted control over the administration of Bohemia as well as the German domains of the house of Habsburg.

## Historical Context of Habsburg Rule in Bohemia
The accession of the Habsburgs to the Bohemian throne had become permanent on October 23, 1526, when twenty-four electors—chosen equally from the three estates—elected Ferdinand of Habsburg as king. While Austrian archdukes Albert and Rudolph had ruled Bohemia for short periods previously, Ferdinand's election marked the start of the dynasty's permanent hold. 

Ferdinand I had spent much of his reign attempting to establish the hereditary right of his dynasty. In 1541, following a great fire in Prague that destroyed state documents, Ferdinand secured the consent of the estates to replace the original charter (which stated he was king by election) with a new charter. This new document claimed he was recognized as king due to the hereditary rights of his wife, Anna. This substitution caused significant dissatisfaction and contributed to subsequent troubles in the region.
