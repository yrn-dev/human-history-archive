# Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine

*Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine* is a treatise published in 1820 by Bretschneider. The work focused on the Gospel of St. John and garnered significant attention upon its release.

## Content and Reception
In this treatise, Bretschneider provided a comprehensive collection and discussion of the arguments against the authorship of the Gospel of St. John. The archive notes that he conducted this discussion with "marked moderation." The publication of these arguments prompted a number of replies from other scholars.

## Author's Intent and Controversy
Despite the nature of the arguments presented in *Probabilia*, Bretschneider later claimed that he had never doubted the authenticity of the Gospel. In the preface to the second edition of his *Dogmatik*, published in 1822, he announced that the purpose of publishing *Probabilia* was to draw attention to the subject and to encourage a more thorough defense of the Gospel's genuineness.

However, the work had negative professional consequences for Bretschneider. In his autobiography, he remarks that the publication prevented him from being appointed as the successor to Karl C. Tittmann in Dresden. This was due to the actions of the minister Detlev von Einsiedel (1773-1861), who denounced Bretschneider as the "slanderer of John" (*Johannisschänder*).

## Related Academic Contributions
Bretschneider's broader academic work included contributions to the science of exegesis, most notably his *Lexicon Manuale Graeco-Latinum in libros Novi Testamenti* (1824, 3rd ed. 1840). This lexicon utilized various sources to illustrate New Testament language, including the apostolic fathers, Josephus, the Old and New Testament Apocrypha, and the Greek of the Septuagint.

In 1826, he published *Apologie der neuern Theologie des evangelischen Deutschlands*. This work served as a response to Hugh James Rose, who had published a volume of sermons in England in 1825 titled *The State of the Protestant Religion in Germany*. Rose had categorized Bretschneider as a rationalist; in response, Bretschneider argued that he was not a rationalist in the ordinary sense, but rather a "rational supernaturalist."

## Theological Position
Bretschneider's dogmatic position is described as intermediate. He sat between the extreme school of naturalists—which included Julius Wegscheider, J.F. Röhr, and Heinrich Paulus—and the positions held by F.C. Baur and D.F. Strauss. While he recognized a supernatural element within the Bible, he also allowed for other perspectives.
