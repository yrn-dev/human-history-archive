# Treaty of Petropolis

The Treaty of Petropolis was a diplomatic agreement concluded in 1903 to settle a boundary dispute between Brazil and Bolivia. The treaty was necessitated by Brazilian invasions of the upper Purus region after the value of its rubber forests became known.

## Boundary Modifications and the Abuna River
The Treaty of Petropolis established a specific course for the frontier line to resolve the territorial conflict. According to the terms of the 1903 agreement, the boundary line ascends the Abuna river to latitude 10 deg. 20' S. From that point, the line proceeds as follows:
* It runs west along the 10 deg. 20' S. parallel to the Rapirran river.
* It follows the Rapirran river to its principal source.
* It continues due west to the Ituxy river, which it follows west to its source.
* It then proceeds to the source of Bahia Creek and follows that creek to the Acre (or Aquiry) river.
* Finally, the line ascends the Acre or Aquiry river to its source.

## Historical Context of the Bolivia-Brazil Border
The boundary between Bolivia and Brazil originated from the limits of Spanish and Portuguese colonies, as determined by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750). These limits underwent several modifications over time:
* **Treaties of 1867 and 1903:** These subsequent agreements modified the earlier colonial boundaries.
* **The 1867 Treaty:** This agreement shifted the starting point of the frontier to the mouth of the Beni (lat. 10 deg. 20' S.) and designated a straight line to the source of the Javary. This modification resulted in Brazil acquiring a large area of territory.
* **The 1903 Modifications:** Beyond the Treaty of Petropolis, 1903 saw a recession of approximately 1158 sq. m. to Bolivia to improve commercial facilities on the Paraguay river.

## General Geography of the Frontier
The broader boundary line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.). It ascends the Paraguay river to a point on the west bank 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra, then moves 4 kilometres inland to lat. 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and long. 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W. 

From there, the line follows an irregular course North and East of North toward Lakes Uberaba, Gaiba (or Mandiore), and Mandiore. It then proceeds up the San Matias river and North along the Sierra Ricardo Franco to the headwaters of the Rio Verde, a tributary of the Guapore. This specific routing was designed to ensure that settlements on the west bank, including Corumba and Fort Coimbra, remained under Brazilian jurisdiction. The line further follows the Guapore, Verde, Mamore, and Madeira rivers down to the mouth of the Abuna (approximately lat. 9 deg. 44' S.), as determined by the 1903 treaty.
