# History of British Honduras

## Geography and Status
British Honduras, formerly known as Belize or Balize, is a British crown colony located in Central America. The colony covers an area of 7,562 square miles. It is bordered to the north and northwest by the Mexican province of Yucatan and to the south and west by Guatemala. To the east and northeast, it is bounded by the Bay of Honduras, which is an inlet of the Caribbean Sea.

## Territorial Boundaries
The frontiers of British Honduras were established through conventions between Great Britain and Guatemala in 1859 and 1893. The boundary begins at the mouth of the river Sarstun (or Sarstoon) in the Bay of Honduras and follows that river upward to the rapids of Gracias à Dios. From there, the line runs straight to the right until it reaches Garbutt's Rapids on the Belize river. The frontier then proceeds due north to the Mexican frontier, where it follows the river Hondo to its mouth in Chetumal Bay.

## Population
According to data from 1905, the population of British Honduras was 40,372.

## Historical and Statistical Documentation
The local history of the colony is recounted in the *History of British Honduras* by A.R. Gibbs, published in London in 1883. Other historical accounts include *Notes on Central America* by E.J. Squier (1855) and a paper titled *Belize or British Honduras* read before the Society of Arts by Chief Justice Temple in 1847.

For general descriptions of the colony, the *Handbook of British Honduras* by L.W. Bristowe and P.B. Wright (1892) is cited. Statistical information regarding the colony is maintained in annual reports sent to the British Colonial Office in London.

## Exploration and Research
The progress of exploration within the colony has been documented in several works. H. Fowler published *A Narrative of a Journey across the unexplored Portion of British Honduras* in Belize in 1879. Additionally, J. Bellamy authored "An Expedition to the Cockscomb Mountains," which appeared in volume xi of the *Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society* in 1889.
