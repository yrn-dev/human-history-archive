# Democratic Candidates and Political Transitions

The political landscape of the 19th and early 20th centuries was characterized by shifting party loyalties, the emergence of third-party movements, and various gubernatorial and presidential contests.

## Gubernatorial and Legislative Contests

In Massachusetts, political candidates often navigated multiple party tickets. One individual served as a Republican representative in Congress from 1867 to 1879 (with an exception from 1875 to 1877) and was a prominent Radical Republican regarding Reconstruction legislation. This individual was a defeated candidate for governor of Massachusetts in 1871. He ran again in 1879 on the Greenback and Democratic tickets but was defeated. He eventually secured election by the Democrats in 1882, though he was defeated upon renomination in 1883.

In other regions, Democratic candidates faced varying results. In 1844, Bancroft, who had been influenced by the Democratic party despite being born into a Whig family, ran as the Democratic candidate for governorship but was defeated. 

In New York City, George Brinton McClellan, associated with the Tammany Hall organization, was elected mayor on the Tammany ticket in 1903, defeating the "Fusion" candidate Seth Low. He was subsequently re-elected for a four-year term in 1905, defeating William R. Hearst of the Independence League and Republican William M. Ivins.

## Party Coalitions and Third-Party Movements

The late 19th century saw the rise of several specialized political organizations. In Illinois, the Republican Party was generally dominant from the end of the Civil War until the century's close. However, in 1872, prominent figures including Senator Lyman Trumbull, Governor John M. Palmer, and Gustavus Koerner joined the Liberal Republican Party. This group united with the Democratic Party both locally and nationally.

By 1874, economic depression led to the popularity of the Granger Movement, which produced the Independent Reform Party. This party advocated for a tariff for revenue only, the state regulation of railways, and the retrenchment of expenses. In that same year, a Democratic Liberal Party was organized, led in part by Governor Palmer. Because of these divisions, no single party held a majority in the legislature elected in 1874. By 1876, the Greenback Party emerged as the successor to the Independent Reform Party in Illinois. Although the Democrats endorsed the Greenback candidate for governor, the Republicans regained control of the state administration.

## National and International Political Appointments

Democratic figures often held high-ranking administrative and diplomatic roles. Bancroft, after his unsuccessful 1844 gubernatorial bid, served as secretary of the navy in Polk's cabinet from 1845 to 1846, and briefly as acting secretary of war. During this tenure, he established the naval academy at Annapolis and ordered the occupation of California. He later served as minister to London in 1846 and minister to Berlin in 1867, resigning the latter post in 1874.

In the early 20th century, Woodrow Wilson, a former President of Princeton University and professor of political sciences, was elected as the Democratic Governor of New Jersey in 1910. He became the Democratic presidential candidate in 1913. He ascended to the presidency following a split in the Republican party caused by a violent quarrel between President Taft and ex-President Roosevelt.

## Regional Political Shifts

In Louisiana, the period between 1868 and 1872 was marked by significant corruption and a massive increase in government expenses and debt. A conflict among Republicans in 1872 led to the installation of two separate legislatures and governors. One was supported by the Liberal Republicans and Democrats, while the other was supported by radical Republicans. In 1876, Francis Tillon Nicholls, a Democrat, was chosen as governor, though the Republican candidate, S. B. Packard, contested the election.

In Kentucky, the state became strongly Democratic following the Civil War due to resentment over the arming of negro troops, the arbitrary imprisonment of citizens, and the emancipation of slaves without compensation. This Democratic dominance remained almost uniform until the Republicans found success in the 1895 gubernatorial and 1896 presidential elections.
