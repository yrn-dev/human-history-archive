# Monument Trustees and Related Archive Records

## The Ayr Burns's Monument Trustees
The Ayr Burns's Monument trustees are the current owners of the "auld clay biggin," the cottage where the poet Robert Burns was born on January 25, 1759. The building has undergone complete repairs. Within the kitchen of the cottage, the archive records the presence of the box bed in which the poet was born, along with various pieces of furniture that belonged to his family. 

The site includes a museum of Burnsiana adjoining the cottage. Nearby is the "auld haunted kirk," which remains in a fair state of preservation despite being roofless and having its woodwork removed by relic-hunters. The churchyard contains the grave of the poet's father, William Burness. Additionally, a Grecian monument to Burns is situated on a conspicuous position near the banks of the Doon; the grounds of this monument feature a grotto containing figures of Souter Johnnie and Tam o' Shanter, created by Thorn.

## Legal and Estate Trusteeship
The archive contains various records regarding the role of trustees in legal and personal estates.

### Bankruptcy Trusteeship
In the law of bankruptcy, the ultimate distribution and realization of assets are the responsibility of a trustee appointed by creditors. The official receiver, who manages the interim preservation of the estate under the Board of Trade, becomes a trustee by operation of law upon an order of adjudication. This official vacates the office once a creditor-appointed trustee is certified by the Board of Trade, though the official receiver resumes the role if the creditors' trustee is removed, resigns, dies, or is released. Because the bankrupt's property vests in the trustee and passes between trustees by operation of law without formal conveyance, the continuity of the office is maintained.

The trustee's title includes property in the bankrupt's actual possession and relates back to the date of the first act of bankruptcy committed within three months before the bankruptcy petition. This allows the trustee to require the restoration of property or money if payments or assignments to creditors were made during that period with knowledge of the act of bankruptcy. Furthermore, payments made to give a creditor preference over others within three months of the petition are void against the trustee. Settlements of property made within two years of bankruptcy are generally void unless they were made in good faith for valuable consideration or in consideration of marriage. Settlements made within ten years are void unless the settlor was solvent at the date of settlement and the interest in the property passed to the trustees upon the execution of the deed.

### Personal Estate Trustees
Historical records from the Will of the Elder Marco, dated August 5, 1280, at Rialto, illustrate private trusteeship. The testator, formerly of Constantinople and dwelling in the confine of S. Severo, nominated his brothers, Maffeo and Nicolo, as his sole executors and trustees. In the event of their absence, he nominated his sister-in-law Fiordelisa of the confine of S. Severo and Jordano Trevisano. 

The duties of these trustees included:
* Managing the sale of clothes and furniture to defray funeral costs.
* Using the remaining balance from those sales to purchase masses for the testator's soul.
* Holding a silver-wrought girdle of vermilion silk, two silver spoons, a silver cup without cover, a desk, a velvet quilt, a counterpane, a feather-bed, and two pairs of sheets until the testator's son, Nicolo, returned to Venice.
* Distributing a residue of 2000 lire for the good of the testator's soul.

The testator also named his wife, Donata, as a trustee, bequeathing her household utensils, linen, and three garnished beds, along with an annual payment of 8 lire of Venetian grossi.
