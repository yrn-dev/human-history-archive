# Littledale

St George Littledale was a British explorer known for his geographical research and expeditions within the Tibetan plateau during the late 19th century. His work contributed to the broader field of Asiatic inquiry, a period characterized by significant scientific enterprise aimed at modifying previous understandings of the orography of Mongolia and Tibet.

## Tibetan Expeditions
Littledale undertook two major journeys into Tibet. His first great Tibetan journey, during which he was accompanied by his wife, took place between 1891 and 1893. This initial expedition concluded in Peking. 

His second expedition occurred in 1894–1895. During this journey, Littledale traveled to the vicinity of Lhasa, coming almost within sight of the city's sacred walls. However, he was unsuccessful in his attempt to pass inside the city.

## Geographical Contributions
Littledale's explorations provided specific data regarding the topography and coordinates of the central Asian region. In the lower or outer range of the parallel border-ranges of the Tibetan plateau—specifically between the transverse breach of the Kara-muren (near the longitude of Cherchen, approximately 85½° E.) and the breach of the Keriya-darya (approximately 81½° E.)—the Chokur Pass is identified as "Littledale's Chokur Pass," situated at an altitude of 9,530 feet.

Additionally, Littledale contributed to the determination of the longitude of Sachu (also known as Saitu) in Mongolia, a location considered an obligatory point for modern map construction. Littledale recorded the longitude value for Sachu as 94 deg. 49' E. of Greenwich. This value is part of a series of varying measurements provided by different researchers, including Krishna (94 deg. 23'), Carey and Dalgleish (94 deg. 48'), and Kreitner (94 deg. 58'), while the revised value given by Prjevalsky in the map of his fourth exploration is 94 deg. 54' E.
