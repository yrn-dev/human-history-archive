# M. Caullery and F. Mesnil

M. Caullery and F. Mesnil were researchers active at the end of the 19th and beginning of the 20th centuries whose work contributed significantly to the fields of zoology and parasitology. Their research focused primarily on the life histories of parasites, specifically Gregarines and members of the Enteropneusta.

## Research on Gregarines

Caullery and Mesnil are credited as being among the researchers who, in the closing years of the 19th century, helped fully work out the complete life-history of Gregarines. Their contributions specifically advanced the understanding of the relations between these parasites and the cells of their hosts during early development.

Their published works on Gregarines include studies on:
* **Intracellular Parasitism:** In 1901, they published "Le Parasitisme intracellulaire des Gregarines."
* **Nuclear Division:** They authored a paper in 1900 regarding a particular mode of nuclear division within Gregarines ("Sur une mode particuliere de division nucleaire chez les Gregarines").
* **Multiplication Phases:** In 1898, they documented a Gregarine presenting a phase of asporulee multiplication.
* **Host Relations:** They conducted research on internal parasites of Annelides, published in 1899.

Additionally, their work is cited in the study of sexual multiplication (schizogony). Specifically, they identified a Monocystid form, a species of *Gonospora*, which remains intracellular for a long period.

## Research on Enteropneusta

Beyond their work with Gregarines, Caullery and Mesnil conducted research on the Enteropneusta. In 1900, they published a study in *C. R. Soc. Biol.* regarding a new species of *Balanoglossus* found in the English Channel, identified as *Balanoglossus Kochleri*.
