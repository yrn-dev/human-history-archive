# Babi Literature and History

The Babi movement, though spanning a relatively short duration, is characterized by a voluminous body of literature and a history marked by significant conflict and persecution. Much of the sect's written record remains in manuscript form, with the most significant European collections housed in Paris (Bibliotheque Nationale), London (British Museum), and St Petersburg. The Paris collection includes rare manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, notably a history written by Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, a contemporary of the Bab.

## Historical Overview and Conflict
The career of the Bab lasted six years, from May 1844 until July 1850. During this time, he was held in captivity at Shiraz, Maku, and Chihriq. Despite his imprisonment, his disciples conducted an active propaganda campaign that led to several serious revolts against the government, particularly following the death of Muhammad Shah in September 1848.

One of the first major risings occurred between December 1848 and July 1849 in Mazandaran. At the ruined shrine of Shaykh Tabarsi near Barfurush, Babi forces led by Mulla Husayn of Bushrawayh (identified as the first believer) and Mulla Muhammad 'Ali of Barfurush resisted the shah's troops for seven months before being subdued and executed. 

Further struggles took place in the north-west of Persia at Zanjan, led by Mulla Muhammad 'Ali Zanjani from May to December 1850. Simultaneously, Aga Sayyid Yahya of Niriz led a serious, though shorter, struggle against the government at Niriz in Fars. While these revolts were ongoing, the Bab and a devoted disciple were transported from Chihriq to Tabriz, where the Bab was publicly shot in front of the citadel. After his body was exposed for several days, Babis recovered it and moved it to a shrine near Tehran, and eventually to Acre in Syria, where it is buried.

## Persecution and Later Events
Following a period of relative quiet, an attempt was made to assassinate Nasiru'd-Din Shah on August 15, 1852, by three Babis acting on their own initiative. The attempt failed but triggered a new wave of persecution. On August 31, 1852, approximately thirty Babis were executed in Tehran with "atrocious cruelty." Among the victims were the talented poetess Qurratu'l-'Ayn and Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, the author of the oldest history of the movement written from the Babi perspective.

## Bibliography and Scholarly Works
The study of the Babis has been documented through various academic and descriptive works:

*   **Persian and Arabic Works:** 'Abbas Efendi, the son of Baha, wrote a Persian work titled *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*, which was edited and translated by E. G. Browne (1891). Mirza Muhammad Mahdi Khan, known as Za'imu'd-Duwla, wrote a well-informed but unsympathetic Arabic history of the Babis, printed in Cairo in A.H. 1321 (A.D. 1903-1904).
*   **European Scholarship:** E. G. Browne published *The New History of the Bab* (1893) and a "Catalogue and Description of the 27 Babi Manuscripts" in the *Journal of R. Asiat. Soc.* (July and October 1892). Other notable works include Andreas's *Die Babi's in Persien* (1896) and several volumes by Baron Victor Rosen in the *Collections scientifiques de l'Institut des Langues orientales* (1877, 1886, 1891). Captain A. G. Toumansky contributed articles to the *Zapiski vostochnava otdyeleniya Imperatorskava Russkava Archeologicheskava Obshchestva* (1890-1900) and produced an edition of the *Kitab-i-Aqdas* (the most important work of Baha) with a Russian translation and introduction in 1899.
*   **English Works for American Converts:** Several texts were produced for converts in America, including *Baha'u'llah (The Glory of God)* by Ibrahim Khayru'llah with assistance from Howard MacNutt (1900), *The Three Questions* and *Facts for Bahaists* (1901) by Khayru'llah, and *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi* by Myron H. Phelps (1903). Other contributions include Isabella Brittingham's *The Revelations of Baha'u'llah, in a Sequence of Four Lessons* (1902) and Laura Clifford Burney's *Some Answered Questions Collected*.
