# Tiers of Cells in Embryonic and Institutional Structures

The term "tiers of cells" appears in the archive's records in two distinct contexts: the biological development of certain plant embryos and the architectural organization of specialized housing units within correctional facilities.

## Embryonic Development in Plants

In the study of botany, the formation of an embryo from a fertilized ovum often involves the initial creation of a number of tiers of cells. This structural pattern is observed in various plant groups, including liverworts and mosses.

### Liverworts (Pellia epiphylla)
In the liverwort *Pellia epiphylla*, the embryo developed from the fertilized ovum begins as a series of cell tiers. These tiers are responsible for the development of different parts of the mature sporogonium:
*   **Terminal Tier:** This uppermost tier gives rise to the capsule. The first divisions within the four cells of this tier distinguish the wall of the capsule from the archesporial cells, which are destined to produce spores.
*   **Lower Tiers:** The tiers located below the terminal tier develop into the foot and the seta.

### Mosses (Sphagnum)
A similar developmental process is found in the genus *Sphagnum* (bog-mosses). In the embryo of these plants, a number of tiers of cells are first formed. The lower tiers of these cells specifically form the foot of the plant.

## Institutional Cell Tiers (MCC New York)

In a non-biological context, "tiers" refer to the physical arrangement of cells within the Special Housing Unit (SHU) at MCC New York. In this setting, inmates were assigned to six separate tiers or groups of cells, organized by their accessibility from a common area on the ninth floor.

### Tier Organization and Access
The tiers were divided into two primary groups based on their location relative to the ninth floor:
*   **Upper Tiers:** These consisted of the G, J, and L Tiers, which were accessed via stairs leading up from the common area.
*   **Lower Tiers:** These consisted of the H, K, and M Tiers, which were accessed via stairs leading down from the common area.

Each tier contained eight individual cells, each capable of housing one or two inmates. Access to any specific tier was restricted by a single locked door at the top or bottom of the staircase.

### Security and Construction
The cells within these tiers were constructed from metal and cement. Each cell was secured by a single locked door made of solid metal, featuring a small window and small locked slots. These slots allowed correctional staff to provide toiletries and food or to handcuff inmates. 

Security protocols required that keys for the SHU tier doors and the individual SHU cell doors be carried by different Correctional Officers (COs) during each shift. Only a limited number of COs on duty possessed the keys necessary to unlock these doors.
