# THE CAPITAL AND THE PROVINCES

The organization of territories into provinces and the designation of administrative capitals vary across different regions and historical eras, serving as primary mechanisms for governance, resource management, and territorial control.

## East Africa Protectorate
The East Africa protectorate is organized into several provinces, each further divided into districts and sub-districts. These include:
*   **Ukamba**: Occupying the center of the protectorate with its capital at Nairobi.
*   **Seyyidie**: The south coast province, with Mombasa as its capital.
*   **Kenya**: The district of Mt. Kenya, with its capital at Fort Hall.
*   **Tanaland**: Located north of Seyyidie and Ukamba, with its capital at Lamu.
*   **Jubaland**: The northern region, with Kismayu as its capital.
*   **Naivasha and Kisumu**: Both have capitals of the same name. These two areas originally formed the eastern province of Uganda before being transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902.

Nairobi, located 327 miles from Mombasa and 257 miles from Port Florence, was selected as the administrative capital of the protectorate in 1907. Mombasa serves as the chief port, with a population of approximately 30,000 and a harbor known as Kilindini. Other notable coastal towns include Malindi, Patta, Kipini, and Kismayu.

## Roman Provincial Administration
In Roman Britain, provincial towns were categorized into two classes. Five municipalities were founded by the Roman government with special constitutions: Colchester, Lincoln, York, Gloucester, and St Albans. Other country towns served as cantonal capitals, likely evolving from Celtic market centers. Examples include:
*   **Isurium Brigantum**: Capital of the Brigantes.
*   **Ratae (Leicester)**: Capital of the Coritani.
*   **Viroconium (Wroxeter)**: Capital of the Cornovii.
*   **Corinium (Cirencester)**: Capital of the Dobuni.
*   **Durnovaria (Dorchester)**: Capital of the Durotriges.
*   **Isca Dumnoniorum (Exeter)**: The most westerly of these towns.

Londinium (London) functioned as a rich trading center and the seat of finance officials, while Aquae Sulis (Bath) served as a spa.

In Gaul, Caesar initially combined the territories of the Celtae, Belgae, and Aquitani into a single province called Gallia Comata. Augustus later divided this into three provinces, including Belgica. The capital and governor's residence for Belgica was Durocortorum Remorum (Reims). Under Diocletian, the region was split into Belgica Prima (capital: Augusta Trevirorum, Trier) and Belgica Secunda (capital: Reims).

## South American Provinces
In Chile, the province of Atacama was organized in 1843 after Chile explored the desert coast; prior to 1842, it was considered Bolivian territory. Its capital is Copiapo, located 37 miles from the coast. The province is known for its nitrate, borax, and silver and copper mines.

In Ecuador, the province of Azuay (formerly called Cuenca) is an elevated mountainous district. Its capital is Cuenca. The region produces silver, quicksilver, wheat, barley, cattle, wool, cinchona, and straw hats.

In Argentina, the province of Buenos Aires secured its territory through the conquest of Indian territories to the south and west, with the last additions occurring in 1879. It became a province of the Confederation in 1820 and adopted a constitution in 1854. Following an unsuccessful revolt in 1880, the city of Buenos Aires was federalized, and La Plata was selected as the provincial capital.

## Other Regional Administrations
In Persia, the province of Fars includes the sub-province of Kuhgilu-Behbahan, with its capital at Behbahan. Behbahan is a walled town with a population of about 10,000 and a citadel called Narinj Kalah. The ruins of Arrajan, the former provincial capital, are located nearby.

In Belgium, the province of Luxemburg is the largest and least populous of the nine provinces, with its capital at Arlon. It covers an area of 1,725 square miles and had a population of 225,963 in 1904.

In Japan, the capital was administratively divided into Eastern and Western sections, controlled by the Right and Left Metropolitan Offices, respectively. In Chikuzen province, the Dazai-fu (Great Administrative Office) served as the seat of the governor-generalship for the island of Kyushu and handled foreign relations. Due to its distance from the capital, the Dazai-fu was often viewed as a place of exile for officials who lost Imperial favor.
