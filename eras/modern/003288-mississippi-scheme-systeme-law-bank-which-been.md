# Mississippi Scheme and the Systeme

## Origins and the Banque Générale
The Mississippi Scheme and the *Systeme* were financial initiatives authored by John Law in France. In 1716, Law established the first bank of issue in the country, which was originally named the *Banque Générale*. This institution served as a foundational element of Law's financial plans.

## Transition to the Banque Royale
In 1718, the *Banque Générale* was converted into the *Banque Royale*. Under this new form, the bank's notes were guaranteed by King Louis XV. This period of financial operation came to an end in 1721. Although an attempt was made to reconstruct the bank in 1767, the resulting institution was suppressed in 1793.

## Subsequent French Banking Development
Following the end of Law's bank, other banks operated with limited success, though none achieved significant power. In 1796, various negotiations took place regarding the establishment of a new bank, but immediate results were prevented by the financial difficulties of the era. However, the advice provided by those involved in these 1796 plans assisted Napoleon I. 

In 1800, Napoleon I, with the aid of his minister Mollien, founded the Bank of France. This institution was created to supply loanable capital at a moderate charge and to support French industry and trade. The Bank of France became the most powerful financial institution in the country, eventually extending its operations through branches and attached towns across the nation.

## Consolidation of the Bank of France
The Bank of France did not initially extend its operations over the entirety of France. Under a law adopted in 1803, departmental banks with the privilege of issue were formed. By the end of 1847, nine of these banks existed in the larger towns. In 1848, these departmental banks were absorbed into the Bank of France. Consequently, the Bank of France possessed an exclusive privilege of issue, a status it maintained after taking over the Bank of Savoy in 1863 following the union of that province with France.
