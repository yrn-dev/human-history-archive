# Vatican Library Manuscripts and Catalogues

The Vatican Library serves as a primary repository for ancient and medieval manuscripts (MSS.), housing a vast collection of Greek, Latin, and Oriental texts. Its holdings include spoils from Italian Basilian houses—Greek-speaking monasteries that were established in Calabria and spread to Rome from the 9th century—as well as acquisitions from expeditions to the East.

## Classical and Early Medieval Manuscripts
The library preserves several critical links between the art of the Roman Empire and the Middle Ages. Among the most significant are the *Schedae Vaticanae*, a 4th-century collection of fifty illustrative pictures of Virgil characterized by a classical style and the use of gilt shading and lozenges for decoration. Another Virgil manuscript, the *Codex Romanus*, is noted for its rougher art, consisting of illustrations copied from earlier classical models by an unskilled hand. Additionally, the library holds a long roll of the book of Joshua, likely from the 10th century, which features outline drawings of considerable merit.

The library's holdings also include important textual records, such as the confession of Michael, written in "barbarous Latin" by Ogerius and transcribed by Wading in 1278.

## Oriental Collections and Acquisitions
The Vatican's Oriental collections were significantly expanded through the efforts of Joseph Simon, a Maronite of Mount Lebanon. After being transferred to the Vatican library from the Maronite college in Rome, Simon was sent to Syria and Egypt in 1717, returning with approximately 150 choice manuscripts. A second expedition in 1735 yielded a further valuable collection. 

As the librarian of the Vatican library and titular archbishop of Tyre, Simon sought to edit and publish these treasures. His major work, the *Bibliotheca Orientalis Clementino-Vaticana* (1719-1728), was intended to be divided into four parts:
1. Syrian and allied MSS. (Orthodox, Nestorian, and Jacobite).
2. Arabian MSS. (Christian and Mahommedan).
3. Coptic, Aethiopic, Persian, and Turkish MSS.
4. Non-theological Syrian and Arabian MSS.

While only the first part was completed, extensive preparations were made for the remaining sections. Simon was assisted in these efforts by his nephew, Stephen Evodius, the titular archbishop of Apamea. Together they began the *Bibliothecae Apostol. Vatic. codd. manusc. Catal.*, though only three volumes were published before a fire in the Vatican library in 1768 destroyed the manuscript collections intended for the work's continuation.

## Hagiographic Research and Modern Catalogues
In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the Bollandists—Jesuit fathers including C. de Smedt, G. van Hooff, and J. de Backer—shifted their focus toward historical methods and the analysis of hagiographic manuscripts in principal libraries. This effort included the publication of the quarterly review *Analecta Bollandiana* (established in 1882), which provided studies for the remoulding of the *Acta Sanctorum*.

As part of this scientific effort to catalogue hagiographic texts, the Bollandists produced several specialized volumes, including a catalogue of the Greek MSS. in the Vatican published in 1899 and a catalogue of the Latin MSS. in the libraries of Rome beginning in 1905.

## Other Scholarly Works and Facsimiles
The Vatican library has been a center for various scholarly pursuits. Angelo Maria Bandini (1726-1800) spent the entirety of his time in Rome within the Vatican library and the libraries of Cardinals Corsini and Passionei. In the realm of biblical studies, the library is associated with the "Vatican" *correctoria*—lists of correct readings used in the 13th century to fix the text of the Vulgate.

To preserve and share its treasures, the Vatican Library has issued facsimiles of ancient illuminated manuscripts, specifically the *Schedae Vaticanae* and the *Codex Romanus* of Virgil. Furthermore, the *Collezione paleografia Vaticana* began its issue in 1905.
