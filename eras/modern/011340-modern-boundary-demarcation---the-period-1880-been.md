# Modern Boundary Demarcation

The period beginning around 1880 marked an era of boundary-making in Asia. This process involved defining the politico-geographical limits of empire and determining the responsibilities of government. During this time, China, India, Baluchistan, Afghanistan, Persia, and Russia all revised their borders, which provided a more assured basis for the political relations between these nations.

## Afghan and Russian Boundaries
The demarcation of Russian boundaries in Turkestan occurred concurrently with the demarcation of northern Afghanistan. The northern limits of Afghanistan were established by Boundary Commissions in 1895 and between 1884 and 1886, stretching from the Sarikol mountains in the east to the Hari Rud in the west.

The eastern and southern boundaries of Afghanistan were further defined by minor commissions working under the Kabul agreement of 1893. This process lasted nearly four years and concluded with the Mohmand settlement following an expedition in 1897. In the north-east, the Pamir extension of Afghan territory reaches a point slightly short of 75 deg. E., following the water-divide to the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir and the water-parting of the Hindu Kush.

The boundary leaves the Hindu Kush near the Dorah Pass, passing south-west to divide Bajour and Chitral from Kafiristan. It further separates sections of the Mohmands based on whether they fall within British or Afghan sovereignty and crosses the Peshawar-Kabul route at Lundi-Khana. This arrangement creates a broad belt of independent territory—including Bajour, Swat, and part of the Mohmand territory north of the Kabul river—between Afghanistan and the boundaries of British India, which remained practically untouched.

This principle of maintaining neutral intervening territory is established throughout Afghanistan's eastern borders, reaching the Gomal river where it touches the northern limits of Baluchistan. From the Gomal, Baluchistan serves as the intervening state between Afghanistan and British India, with the dividing line between Baluchistan and Afghanistan laid down with precision.

## Other Asian Frontiers
In the east, British interests in Burma are limited by the Mekong river, which also limits French interests in Tongking. For approximately 200 m., the channel of the Mekong defines the limits of occupation between British and French subjects. Southward, around the parallel of 20 deg. N. lat., the Burmese boundary leaves the Mekong to run west toward the Salween; it then follows the eastern watershed of the Salween basin to divide Siam from the Lower Burma provinces.

The boundaries of Burma on the Chinese frontier are not yet fully demarcated. At a point level in latitude with Mogaung, the boundary is defined by the eastern watershed of the Nmaikha (an affluent of the Irrawaddy). It then runs southwards to a position south-east of Bhamo (lat. 24 deg.), defines the northern edge of the Shan States, and strikes the Mekong river at approximately lat. 21 deg. 45'.

The political boundary between China and Russia on the western edge of the Kashgar plains is defined by the meridional range of Sarikol. This range consists of two parallel chains: the eastern chain (the Kandar or Kashgar range) and the western chain, which serves as the water-divide of the Pamirs.

## Central American Demarcations
In Central America, Costa Rica faced various boundary disputes. The boundary between Nicaragua and Costa Rica was referred to the president of the United States, who issued an award in 1888 that confirmed a treaty from 1858. Subsequent difficulties regarding the work of demarcation were resolved by a treaty in 1896. Additionally, the boundary between Costa Rica and Panama (which was then a province of Colombia) was fixed on September 15, 1900, via an award from the French president.

## Geographical Mapping and Surveys
The progress of scientific geographical mapping has been particularly notable on the north-western and northern frontiers of India. This progress was aided by the occupation of Chitral and Gilgit, the Afghan war of 1878-80, the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1885, and the systematic survey of Baluchistan starting in 1880. These efforts produced maps extending from the Euphrates to the Oxus and from the Indus to the Euphrates.

In western Asia, the mountain system connecting the southern abutment of the Hindu Kush near Bamian with the water-divide north of Herat has been mapped, as has the connection between the Elburz of Persia and the barrier between Herat and Merv.

While some regions have benefited from government-directed surveys—such as Cyprus (1884), the Sinai Peninsula (1870), Palestine (1880), Arabia Petraea (1907), and the Aden territory (1905)—others remain dependent on compilations. For example, in Asiatic Turkey, there is no general government survey, leaving researchers dependent on works such as the Russian general map (1880-1885) or R. Kiepert's *Asia Minor* (1904-1908). Similarly, for Afghanistan and Persia, researchers rely on compilations like the Russian staff map (1886) or Colonel Sir T. H. Holdich's map of Persia (1897-1899).
