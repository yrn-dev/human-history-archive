# Budweis

Budweis is a city in Bohemia with a history spanning several centuries, serving as a royal city and a strategic location during various European military conflicts, most notably during the War of the Austrian Succession.

## History and Foundation
The old town of Budweis was established in the 13th century by Budivoj Vitkovec, who was the father of Závi[vs] of Falkenstein. In 1265, Ottokar II founded the new town, which was subsequently designated as a royal city. The town received numerous privileges from Wenceslaus and Charles IV. While the population was primarily Catholic, Budweis declared its support for King George Pod[ve]brad, leading to its capture by crusaders under Zdenko of Stenberg in 1468. Following this period, the town remained loyal to the royal cause. In 1547, Emperor Ferdinand granted Budweis the privilege of ranking next to Pilsen and Prague at the diet. These privileges were later confirmed following the outbreak of the Thirty Years' War.

## Military Significance in the 18th Century
Budweis played a role in the strategic maneuvers of the mid-18th century, particularly during the conflicts involving Frederick the Great of Prussia, the French, and the Austrian Empire under Maria Theresa.

### The Campaign of 1741
During the early stages of the conflict in Bohemia, the allies—consisting of the French, the elector of Bavaria's forces, and the Saxons—advanced on Prague in three separate corps. As part of this movement, the elector of Bavaria marched on Budweis. While the Austrians initially offered little resistance, a significant force eventually intervened at Tabor, situated between the Danube and the allies.

### The Campaign of 1742
By 1742, the war recommenced after Maria Theresa divulged the provisions of a truce to compromise Frederick with his allies. During this period, the French commander Broglie was positioned on the Moldau. However, Broglie's forces were weakened by the departure of the Saxons to join Frederick and the Bavarians, who had left to oppose Khevenhueller. Consequently, Broglie was unable to take the offensive, as large forces commanded by Prince Charles of Lorraine were positioned in his front, extending from Budweis to Iglau.

### Later Prussian Advances
In a subsequent phase of the conflict, the Prussians advanced through Lusatia, Saxony, and Silesia. After the Austrian garrison at Prague was compelled to surrender on September 8, the Prussian forces advanced toward Budweis. However, Maria Theresa responded by assembling a corps of regulars to protect Vienna and organizing a new "insurrection" in Hungary. This shift in momentum, combined with the diplomatic alignment of Saxony with Austria, left Frederick isolated. He was eventually outmanoeuvred by the combined forces of Marshal Traun and Prince Charles, forcing a retreat to Silesia with heavy losses.
