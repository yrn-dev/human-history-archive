# 'Abdu'l-Baha

## Overview and Identity
'Abdu'l-Baha, also referred to as 'Abbas Efendi, is identified as the son of Baha. He is recognized as "the servant of Baha."

## Literary Contributions and Works
'Abbas Efendi authored a Persian work titled *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*. This work served as a bibliography for the movement up to the year 1889. The text was later edited, translated, and annotated by E. G. Browne and published in 1891. Additionally, Myron H. Phelps wrote a book titled *Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi*, which included a preface by E. G. Browne and was published in New York in 1903.

## Context of the Babi Movement
The movement associated with 'Abdu'l-Baha originated with the Bab, whose career spanned from May 1844 until July 1850. During this time, the Bab was held in captivity at Shiraz, Maku, and Chihriq. His disciples engaged in active propaganda, leading to several revolts against the government following the death of Muhammad Shah in September 1848. Notable uprisings occurred in Mazandaran at the shrine of Shaykh Tabarsi (December 1848–July 1849), in Zanjan (May–December 1850), and at Niriz in Fars.

The Bab was eventually brought to Tabriz and publicly shot in front of the citadel. His body was first taken to a shrine near Tehran and later moved to Acre in Syria, where it is buried. Following the Bab's death, a failed assassination attempt on Nasiru'd-Din Shah on August 15, 1852, led to fresh persecutions. On August 31, 1852, approximately thirty Babis were executed in Tehran, including the poetess Qurratu'l-'Ayn and the historian Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan.

## Evolution of Doctrines
The early Babis, now represented by the Ezelis, viewed revelation as progressive, asserting that no revelation is final and that truth and ordinances evolve as the human race progresses. While they recognized Muhammad as a true prophet and the Qur'an as a revelation, they denied the finality of these. Their beliefs included the denial of the resurrection of the body, though some accepted a form of personal immortality. They placed significant mystical value on numbers, specifically 18, 19 ("the number of the unity"), and 361 ("the number of all things").

Under the leadership of Baha, the aims of the sect shifted. The metaphysical complexities and "wilder pantheistic tendencies" of the early Babis largely disappeared, and the movement's focus became more ethical and practical.

## Persecutions and Social Standing
The sect has faced various persecutions, with notable instances occurring in Yazd in May 1891 and again with greater ferocity in June 1903. Despite these challenges, the intelligence and morality of the Babis are described as high. While there have been claims regarding their efforts to improve the social position of women, these have been characterized as exaggerated. Furthermore, the sect was not involved in the assassination of Nasiru'd-Din Shah in May 1896.
