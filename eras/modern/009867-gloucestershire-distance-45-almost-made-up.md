# Gloucestershire

Gloucestershire is an English county characterized by its diverse geology, agricultural production, and historical sites. It shares borders with Somersetshire, with the river Avon serving as the boundary between the two.

## Geography and Waterways
The Lower or Bristol Avon rises on the eastern slope of the Cotteswold Hills in Gloucestershire. This river collects water from several streams located east of Malmesbury and south of Tetbury. From its source, the river flows in a wide curve toward the east and south through a broad upper valley. After passing through Melksham and Chippenham, it turns abruptly west toward Bradford-on-Avon.

The river eventually enters a narrow valley containing Bristol and Bath. Below Bristol, the river forms the Clifton Gorge, noted for its wooded cliffs. The Avon finally enters the estuary of the Severn at Avonmouth.

## Geology
The county contains significant geological deposits known as bone beds. Geologists use the term "bone bed" generally to describe any stratum containing bones, including stalagmitic and brecciated deposits on cave floors. In a more restricted sense, it refers to thin layers of bony fragments on specific geological horizons. 

One prominent example is the Ludlow Bone Bed, located at the base of the Downton Sandstone in the Upper Ludlow series. In Ludlow, two such beds exist, separated by approximately 14 feet of strata. This specific bone bed can be traced from Ludlow into Gloucestershire for a distance of 45 miles and consists primarily of scales, teeth, and spine fragments from ganoid fish.

## Agriculture and Industry
Gloucestershire is involved in commercial fruit-growing. While apples are the primary fruit grown in the south-western and western counties of England, plum orchards are specifically found in parts of Gloucestershire.

## Historical Sites and Notable Figures
The county contains remnants of Roman Britain, where the countryside was divided into estates often called "villas." These ranged from simple farms to luxurious country-houses. Notable examples of these Roman country-houses in Gloucestershire include Chedworth and Woodchester.

Several notable individuals were born in the county:
* **Charles Bannister (1738–1804):** An English singer and actor with a fine bass voice who performed at Ranelagh and Drury Lane.
* **Wills Hill, 1st Marquess of Downshire (1718–1793):** Born at Fairford, he served as a member of parliament, secretary of state for the colonies, and secretary of state for the northern department.

Additionally, the game of Badminton takes its name from the duke of Beaufort's seat located in Gloucestershire.

## Bristol and Surrounding Areas
The city of Bristol is a seaport and borough located chiefly in Gloucestershire, though it extends partly into Somersetshire. The city is situated in a basin-like hollow approximately 4 miles in diameter, with the nucleus of the city resting on a narrow valley-floor. 

The city is served by the Great Western railway, which connects it to London, the southern and south-western counties of England, and South Wales via the Severn tunnel. A trunk line of the Midland railway further connects Bristol to the north of England through Worcester, Birmingham, Derby, and Gloucester.

Open spaces near Bristol include the Clifton Downs and Durdham, which are located on the Gloucestershire side of the river near the gorge.
