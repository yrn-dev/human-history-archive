# Heimbach

## Contribution to Legal Scholarship
K. W. E. Heimbach, along with G. E. Heimbach, served as an editor for a significant restoration of the Basilica, a major body of law. Their work resulted in a newly-restored and far more complete text of the sixty books of the Basilica, which was published in Leipzig in six volumes between 1833 and 1870.

## The Basilica
The Basilica is described as an important body of law, though it did not survive in its integrity. No perfect manuscript of the text has been preserved. The existence of any part of the code was largely ignored by jurists in western Europe until 1533, when Viglius Zuichemus brought its importance to the study of Roman law to their attention in the preface to his edition of the Greek *Paraphrase of Theophilus*.

Following this, a century passed before another edition of the sixty books was published. This version, published in seven volumes by Charles Annibal Fabrot, was produced under the patronage of Louis XIII of France. To support this work, the King provided the royal printing-press and an annual stipend of two thousand livres to the editor. Despite being a work of considerable merit and a great undertaking, Fabrot's edition was a very imperfect representation of the original code.

## Preservation and Scarcity
The lack of a complete original text of the Basilica can be attributed to several factors. A preserved letter from Mark, the patriarch of Alexandria, to Theodoras Balsamon indicates that copies of the Basilica were very scarce by the 12th century; the patriarch himself was unable to obtain a copy. 

The difficulty in preserving the work was partly due to the great bulk of the code, which acted as an obstacle to the creation of multiple copies. Additionally, the need for full copies was reduced because eminent jurists periodically published *encheiridia* and synopses of the Basilica's contents. A detailed account of these summaries is found in the *Histoire au droit byzantin*, published in Paris in 1846 by the advocate Mortreuil.
