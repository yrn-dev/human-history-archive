# Mahe de la Bourdonnais and the Capture of Madras

## Naval Operations in the East Indies
During a period of conflict characterized by significant privateering activity on both the French and Spanish sides, Mahe de la Bourdonnais operated in the East Indies. Utilizing a small squadron, La Bourdonnais conducted vigorous operations against British naval forces, who offered no effectual resistance to his movements. 

## The Attack on Madras
Between July 24 and September 9, 1746, La Bourdonnais captured Madras. This specific military action was noted for partaking largely of the nature of a privateering venture. The capture of Madras served as a strategic set-off for the British capture of Louisburg (which occurred from April 30 to June 16 of the same year). Consequently, Madras was exchanged for Louisburg at the conclusion of the war.

## French Internal Conflict and Diplomacy
The French presence in India was marked by internal friction, specifically a bitter mutual jealousy between La Bourdonnais, the governor of the isle of Bourbon, and Joseph Francois Dupleix, the governor-general of the French establishment in India. This rivalry partially averted the danger posed to British power and settlements.

Following the capitulation of Madras to the French in 1746, a diplomatic dispute arose between the two French leaders. La Bourdonnais had signed a treaty for the restoration of the town to the British, but Dupleix opposed this restoration, thereby violating the terms of the treaty.

## Context of Global Privateering
The actions of La Bourdonnais occurred within a broader war where French and Spanish corsairs were highly active in all seas. The total number of captures made by these corsairs was likely greater than those made by the British. This disparity was attributed in part to the fact that there were many more British merchant ships available to be taken, as well as the initial failure of the British government to strictly enforce the use of convoys.
