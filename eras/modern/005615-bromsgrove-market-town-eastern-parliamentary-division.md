# BROMSGROVE

## Geography and Location
Bromsgrove is a market town situated in England within the Eastern parliamentary division of Worcestershire. It is located 12 miles north-northeast of Worcester. The town is positioned in a pleasant undulating district near the foot of the Lickey Hills. 

## Transport and Infrastructure
The town is served by the Bristol-Birmingham line of the Midland railway, with a station located one mile from the town. The railway line towards Birmingham is notable for ascending one of the steepest gradients in England over a distance of two miles to surmount the Lickey Hills. Additionally, the Midland railway maintains carriage works near the town.

## Demographics and Architecture
As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 8,418. The town retains several picturesque half-timbered houses that date from 1572 and later. A prominent architectural feature is the church of St John, described as a fine building of Perpendicular and earlier date. The church is situated on an elevation above the town and features a lofty spire and tower.

## Education and Institutions
Bromsgrove hosts several educational and cultural institutions, including:
* A well-known grammar school founded by Edward VI, which provides university scholarships.
* A college school.
* A school of art.
* A literary institute.

Additionally, the Birmingham Sanatorium is located within the parish.

## Economy and Industry
Historically, cloth served as a staple of trade in Bromsgrove. In more recent times, the pre-eminent manufactures have become nails and buttons. The river Salwarpe provides power for a number of mills in the surrounding neighbourhood.
