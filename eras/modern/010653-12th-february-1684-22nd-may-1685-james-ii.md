# Events of 1684 and 1685

The years 1684 and 1685 were marked by significant legal transitions, political shifts following the accession of James II, and various intellectual and cultural developments in Europe.

## Legal and Political Developments in 1684
In 1684, several high-profile legal actions took place in England. Heneage Finch, the 1st Earl of Aylesford, served as a prominent crown lawyer during this period. In the trial of Algernon Sidney, Finch argued that the defendant's unpublished treatise constituted an overt act, supporting the legal opinion of Jeffreys that *scribere est agere*. During the same year, Finch acted as counsel for James in a successful libel action against Titus Oates.

The year also saw the resolution of long-standing imprisonments related to the "popish plot." Following the execution of Lord Stafford in December 1680, proceedings against other accused peers were interrupted. On the 12th of February 1684, three surviving peers, who had been imprisoned in the Tower and found guilty of high treason by the Middlesex grand jury, were released on bail. Among these was Henry Arundell, 3rd Baron Arundell of Wardour. Arundell, a Roman Catholic and royalist, had previously been involved in secret negotiations with Louis XIV in 1669 and 1670 regarding the treaties of Dover.

## The Accession of James II and its Aftermath
The transition of power occurred in February 1685 following the death of Charles II. This accession brought immediate changes to the fortunes and legal statuses of various figures. On the 22nd of May 1685, the charges against the surviving peers of the popish plot, including Lord Arundell, were annulled.

The new monarch, James II, also rewarded his supporters. Colonel Churchill, who had been a Scotch peer since 1682, was advanced to the English peerage as Baron Churchill of Sandridge in Hertfordshire on the 14th of May 1685, as a reward for a special mission to Louis XIV. Churchill's rise continued rapidly; he was appointed second in command of the king's army during the duke of Monmouth's enterprise in the western counties and was raised to the rank of major-general on the 3rd of July 1685. His leadership contributed to the royal victory at the battle of Sedgemoor on July 6.

Other officials experienced volatility during this transition. Graham, who had been deposed from the office of privy councillor shortly after Charles II's death, was reinstated in May 1685. He was subsequently ordered to use his cavalry to guard the borders and search for rebels. During this mission, Graham's forces overtook two men, including John Brown (the "Christian carrier"), who was shot dead after refusing an oath to abjure the declaration of Renwick.

## Religious and Liturgical Changes
The coronation of James II in 1685 led to considerable alterations in the English coronation service. Archbishop Sancroft introduced changes that were described as needless, and because James II was a Roman Catholic, the Communion was omitted from the service. These revisions, along with further changes in 1689, were later viewed as having mutilated the service by confusing the order of sections and altering the meaning of the prayers.

In the realm of theology, George Bull, an English divine, published his greatest work, *Defensio Fidei Nicenae*, in 1685. This work sought to demonstrate that the doctrine of the Trinity was held by the ante-Nicene fathers of the church through an examination of early church literature. Bull, who had previously refused the oath of allegiance to the Commonwealth in 1649, preached strenuously against Roman Catholicism during the reign of James II.

## Cultural and Scientific Context
The mid-1680s also saw the continued influence of literature and the loss of scientific figures. The ninth edition of John Bunyan's *Pilgrim's Progress* was published in 1684, followed by a tenth edition in 1685. The work remained immensely popular in puritanical circles, Scotland, the colonies—particularly New England—and among the Huguenots of France.

In France, the scientific community lost Edme Mariotte, a physicist and member of the Academy of Sciences. Mariotte, who spent most of his life in Dijon as the prior of St Martin sous Beaune, died in Paris on the 12th of May 1684. He is remembered for his original papers on the nature of colour, the barometer, the motion of fluids, the recoil of guns, and the fall of bodies.
