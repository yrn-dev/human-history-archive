# Les Avariés

## Overview
*Les Avariés* is a play written in 1901 by the French dramatist Eugène Brieux. Due to the inclusion of medical details, the work was forbidden by the censor. Because it could not be produced publicly, the author read the play privately at the Théâtre Antoine.

## Author and Dramatic Style
The play was authored by Eugène Brieux (born January 19, 1858, in Paris), who was born to poor parents. Brieux's body of work is characterized as essentially didactic, with plays designed to target iniquities or weaknesses within the social system. 

His approach to social commentary is evident in several other works produced around the same period, including:
* *Blanchette* (1892), which addressed the evil results of the education of working-class girls.
* *M. de Réboval* (1892), directed against pharisaism.
* *L'Engrenage* (1894), which targeted political corruption.
* *Les Biensfaiteurs* (1896), aimed at the frivolity of fashionable charity.
* *L'Évasion* (1896), a satire of indiscriminate belief in the doctrine of heredity.
* *Les Trois Filles de M. Dupont* (1897), a study of the miseries the French system of dowry imposed on poor middle-class girls.
* *Le Résultat des courses* (1898), which examined the evil results of betting among Parisian workmen.
* *La Robe rouge* (1900), directed against injustices within the law.
* *Les Remplaçantes* (1901), which targeted the practice of putting children out to nurse.

## Context within Brieux's Career
Brieux's career in drama began in 1879 with the production of a one-act play titled *Bernard Palissy*, written in collaboration with M. Gaston Salandri. Following this, he experienced an eleven-year gap before obtaining another hearing with *Ménage d'artistes*, produced by Antoine at the Théâtre Libre in 1890. 

Following the 1901 production of *Les Avariés*, Brieux continued to write plays, including *Petite amie* (1902), which described the life of a Parisian shop-girl, and *La Couvée* (1903), which had been acted privately at Rouen in 1893. His later works include *Maternité* (1904), *La Déserteuse* (1904)—written in collaboration with M. Jean Sigaux—and the three-act comedy *Les Hannetons* (1906).
