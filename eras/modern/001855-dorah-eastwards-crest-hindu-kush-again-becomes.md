# Badakshan and the Hindu Kush Boundary

Badakshan is a province located on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan, adjoining Russian territory. Its geographical and political boundaries were significantly defined by the Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873, which recognized Badakshan and its dependent district, Wakhan, as belonging fully to the amir of Kabul.

## Geographical Boundaries
The northern boundary of the province follows the course of the Oxus river. In areas where the boundary follows the Oxus under the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, the province is separated from its own southern boundary by only the length of these slopes, which measure approximately 8 to 10 miles. This creates a "bottle-shaped" extension into the Pamirs, narrow at the neck (the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush) and swelling eastwards to include parts of the little and great Pamirs.

The western boundary begins at the junction of the Oxus and Kunduz rivers, crossing the Turkestan plains southwards to the eastern water-divide of the Tashkurghan river (known as the Koh-i-Chungar). From there, it runs south-east, crossing the Sarkhab affluent of the Khanabad (Kunduz) before striking the Hindu Kush.

The southern boundary is defined by the crest of the Hindu Kush up to the Khawak pass, which leads into the Panjshir valley. East of the Khawak pass, the crest is occupied by the Kafirs, though the extent of their territory north of the main watershed is not ascertainable. The boundary becomes definite again at the Dorah pass. From the Dorah eastwards, the crest of the Hindu Kush serves as the boundary until it joins the Sarikol and Muztagh ranges, which separate India and Russia from China. The boundary then skirts the head of the Tagdumbash Pamir and merges into the Pamir boundary, turning westwards along the Oxus.

## Topography and Hydrography
The Hindu Kush represents the southern edge of a great central plateau. To the north, this plateau slopes toward the Oxus, descending from an average altitude of 15,000 feet to 4,000 feet at Faizabad and further down to 1,100 feet at Kunduz, where it meets the flat plains of the Oxus. To the south, the range breaks into long spurs with deep, rugged, and snow-capped altitudes that isolate the valleys of Kafiristan.

The Kokcha river is a primary waterway that traverses Badakshan from the south-east to the north-west. Along with the Kunduz, it drains the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush west of the Dorah pass. The Kokcha's chief affluent is the Minjan. The course of the Kokcha provides a high road across the province, moving from the Oxus (1,000 ft.) to Faizabad (4,000 ft.) and Zebak (8,500 ft.).

## Regional Subdivisions and Trade
Badakshan is divided into several provincial subdivisions:
*   **West:** Rustak, Kataghan, Ghori, Narin, and Anderab.
*   **North:** Darwaz, Ragh, and Shiwa.
*   **East:** Charan, Ishkashim, Zebak, and Wakhan.
*   **Centre:** Faizabad, Farkhar, Minjan, and Kishm.

The district of Darwaz, located north of the Kokcha within the Oxus bend, possesses a physiography more similar to the Pamir type than the Hindu Kush.

Historically, a significant trade route connected Afghan Turkestan with China and Kashgar. This route passed from Ishkashim via the Panja through the Pamirs. Another route leads through the high Valley of Zebak and over the watershed of the Hindu Kush into Chitral via the Nuksán or Dorah pass.

## Natural Resources
The region is known for various mineral deposits. The Yamgán tract, located in the Upper Valley of the Kokcha (called Korán), contains mines of iron, lead, alum, copper, sulphur, ochre, and salammoniac. It is specifically celebrated for its *Lájwurd* (lazuli) mines, though the quality of produce is reported as inferior, with annual quantities ranging from 30 to 60 poods (each pood weighing 36 lbs). Additionally, ruby mines are found in the Gháran country along both banks of the Oxus. South of the Hindu Kush crest in the Valley of Panjshir, silver mines in Paryán were heavily worked during the early Middle Ages.

## Regional Context
The Hindu Kush is part of a great continental water-parting that stretches from the Black Sea through the Elburz to the Tian-shan mountains. The area is characterized by specific cultural markers, such as the nearly universal use of the *Postín* (sheepskin coat) on both sides of the range and goatskin half-boots.

In the broader historical context of the region, the Aryans are believed to have settled north of the Hindu Kush before migrating south-eastwards around 1500 B.C. The tableland extending to the Hindu Kush is described as the cradle of all warring races.
