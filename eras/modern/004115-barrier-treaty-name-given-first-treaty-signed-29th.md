# BARRIER TREATY

The Barrier Treaties were a series of diplomatic agreements signed in the early 18th century between Great Britain and the states-general of the United Netherlands (Holland). These treaties were designed to secure the Netherlands against aggression from France by establishing a "barrier" of fortified towns.

## The First Barrier Treaty (1709)
The first treaty bearing this name was signed on October 29, 1709. Under its terms, the states-general of the United Netherlands engaged to guarantee the Protestant succession in England in favor of the house of Hanover. In exchange, Great Britain undertook to procure an adequate barrier for the Dutch on the side of the Netherlands. This barrier consisted of the following towns:
*   Furnes, Nieuport, Ypres, Menin, Lille, Tournai, Conde, Valenciennes, Maubeuge, Charleroi, Namur, Halle, Damme, Dendermond, and the citadel of Ghent.

This agreement followed the principle of securing Holland against French aggression, a concept that had previously inspired the treaty of Ryswick in 1698, which had mandated that Dutch troops garrison the chief frontier fortresses of the Netherlands.

## The Second Barrier Treaty (1713)
A second Barrier Treaty was signed between Great Britain and Holland on January 29, 1713. This agreement reduced the number of strong places designated for the barrier to the following:
*   Furnes, the fort of Knocke, Ypres, Menin, Tournai, Mons, Charleroi, the citadel of Ghent, and certain fortresses located in the neighborhood of Bruges and the city of Ghent.

Great Britain committed to obtaining the right for the Dutch to garrison these locations from the future sovereign of the Spanish Netherlands. The terms of this second treaty were later included in the treaty of Rastatt, signed on March 7, 1714, between France and the emperor.

## The Third Barrier Treaty and Treaty of Antwerp (1715)
A third Barrier Treaty was signed in November 1715. This period of diplomacy was influenced by the end of the Hapsburg dynasty's rule in Spain following the death of Charles II in 1700. Although Charles II had named Philip of Bourbon (grandson of Louis XIV of France) as his successor and sovereign of the Netherlands, the War of the Spanish Succession broke out in 1702, involving an alliance between England and the United Provinces against Louis XIV.

The conflict was terminated by three treaties: Utrecht, Rastadt, and Bade (1713-14). These powers decided that the Netherlands (Belgium) should be transferred to the Austrian branch of the Hapsburgs. To protect the United Provinces from French menace, the Dutch were permitted to occupy Belgian territory provisionally until all questions were settled. A final agreement was reached via the Treaty of Antwerp in 1715, which is better known as the "Treaty of the Barriers." This agreement, along with the Hague Convention of 1718, placed the Hapsburgs of Austria in full possession of Belgium.
