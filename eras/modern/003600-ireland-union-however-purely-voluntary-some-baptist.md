# Ireland

## Political History and the Union
The political landscape of Ireland was significantly shaped by the Union of the Parliaments. This union resulted in the simultaneous enfranchisement of both the Irish and English populations, meaning that Catholic enfranchisement in England effectively ensured Catholic enfranchisement in Ireland. Due to the fact that Irish seats were initially easier for the governing class to manipulate than English seats, Ireland was over-represented within the Union Parliament.

This political structure provided a legislative instrument to a Catholic Ireland that had previously lacked such a means and had not sought one. Following the general election of 1874, a distinct Irish "democracy"—characterized by its own religion, traditions, and needs—emerged in the British Parliament. This group sought separation and utilized obstruction and disorder of English business as a means to pursue Irish freedom. This nationalist spirit was viewed by the common English as unfriendly and incomprehensible.

In response to these movements, the oligarchy ruling the British Empire allied with "loyal" Protestants in the north of Ireland. These Protestants remained loyal to the Imperial Government primarily due to their fear of Catholic predominance within Ireland. Additionally, the spirit of independence seen in the creation of the Dominions of Australia, South Africa, and Canada was reflected in the introduction of three Home Rule Bills for Ireland.

## Baptist Presence and Organization
Ireland is included in the organizational structure of the Baptist Union of Great Britain and Ireland. This Union was originally formed in 1813 by the Particular Baptists. Later, in 1891, under the influence of General Baptist leader Dr. John Clifford, the General and Particular denominations united into a single body. 

The Baptist Union is a purely voluntary organization and does not exert authoritative action over its separate churches. Some prosperous and powerful Baptist churches choose to remain aloof from the organization, and other Baptist bodies and isolated churches exist outside of the Union.

## Baptist Education in Ireland
The Baptist denomination has emphasized the necessity of an educated ministry. While many colleges were established in England—such as the Bristol Baptist College, the college at Regent's Park in London, and the Midland Baptist College in Nottingham—educational institutions were also established elsewhere. In addition to a theological college in Glasgow and two colleges in Wales, there is one Baptist college located in Ireland. These various institutions collectively educate approximately 210 students.
