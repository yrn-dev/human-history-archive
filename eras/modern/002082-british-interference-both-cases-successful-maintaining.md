# British Interference and Protection in Bahrein

## Geography and Resources of the Islands
The Bahrein islands are characterized by a landscape of great beauty and fertility, featuring scattered date groves and cultivation across approximately fifty villages. For protection, most of these villages are walled. The islanders rely on fish and sea-weed as their staple food. The water supply on Moharek is considered unique, as it is sourced from springs that burst through beds below sea-level with enough force to remain fresh despite the surrounding salt water.

## History of Possession and Rule
The islands have been an object of contention among various powers over several centuries. The Portuguese first obtained possession of the islands in 1507, but they were driven from their settlements by Shah Abbas in 1622. Following this, the islands were contested between Arabs and Persians. In 1784, the Arabian tribe of the Athubis established themselves as the masters of the islands.

The ruling race of the present Sheik of Bahrein, who primarily resides at Moharek, is the family of El Kalifa. This family previously held great possessions on the mainland but was driven from those territories by the Turks around 1850.

## British Interference and Protection
During the late 19th century, the integrity of Arab rule in Bahrein was threatened by external powers. In 1867, the Persians threatened the islands, and in 1875, the Turks laid their hands on them. In both of these instances, British interference was successful in maintaining the integrity of Arab rule. As a result of these events, the Bahrein islands are currently under British protection.
