# Saltimbanques (1853), Esquisses parisiennes (1859), and Contes

The works *Les Saltimbanques* (1853), *Esquisses parisiennes* (1859), and various *Contes* are prose writings authored by the French poet and miscellaneous writer Théodore Faullain de Banville.

## Authorship and Literary Context
Théodore Faullain de Banville (1823–1891) was born on March 14, 1823, in Moulins, located in the Bourbonnais. The son of a French navy captain, Banville spent his youth at a lyceuse in Paris, where he remained detached from the amusements of his peers. Following his schooling, he pursued a career in letters despite having limited financial support. 

While Banville is primarily remembered for his poetry—including *Les Cariatides* (1842), *Les Stalactites* (1846), and the *Odes funambulesques* (1857)—he was an industrious writer of prose. Beginning in 1853, he produced a significant volume of prose work that included sketches of well-known individuals, such as *Camees parisiennes*, and studies regarding the manners of Paris.

## Prose Works and Tales
Among Banville's prose contributions are the tales *Les Saltimbanques*, published in 1853, and *Esquisses parisiennes*, published in 1859. He also authored *Contes feeriques*. Additionally, Banville wrote a series of tales including *Contes bourgeois* and *Contes heroiques*. The majority of these stories were later republished within his collected works, which were released between 1875 and 1878.

## Critical Reception
The prose stories of Banville are characterized by a fertility of invention. However, critics have noted that they are generally unreal and conventional in nature. Much of his prose is described as being that of a journalist in substance, even if not in its manner of delivery.
