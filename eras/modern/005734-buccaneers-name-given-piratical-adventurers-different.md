# BUCCANEERS

The buccaneers were piratical adventurers of various nationalities who operated primarily in the Caribbean Sea during the 17th century. They were characterized by their shared opposition to Spain, which served as the primary catalyst for their unification.

## Composition and Principles
The buccaneer forces were composed of sailors of Dutch, French, and English nationalities, and were occasionally supplemented by bands of native Indians. Their community was bound together by two central principles: a desire for gain and adventure, and a profound hatred of the Spaniard. 

While the pursuit of booty was a driving force, it was often an insufficient bond of union because individual sailors could frequently obtain booty through private ventures under the colors of their own separate European powers. The more valid bond was their commitment to a common foe; the buccaneers generally did not war with one another, nor did they treat their fellow adventurers with the same savagery they displayed toward the subjects of Spain.

## Rise and Peak of Power
The rise of the buccaneers is associated with the massacre of the islanders of Santo Domingo. By 1685, the power of the buccaneers had reached its height. During this period, large numbers of buccaneers, including those under Townley and others who had crossed the isthmus, joined forces in the Bay of Panama. 

The impact of their raids was significant enough to prompt action from the viceroy of Peru. The viceroy sought to end the buccaneers' activities after observing that property was being harassed by continual raids, towns were being burned and raided, and the colony's supplies and trade had been cut off. Despite these efforts, the buccaneers remained a formidable force; in 1685, ten buccaneer vessels were encountered by a Spanish fleet of fourteen sail, though the two forces did not engage.

## Internal Discord and Decline
Despite their success, the buccaneer community possessed inherent flaws that led to its decline. The diversity of nationalities among the members became a source of growing discord. Furthermore, the ideal of equality was rarely sustained. While the immense spoil captured from wealthy cities was divided equally, the subsequent gambling and debauchery often left half of the conquerors in pressing want by the following day. This created a rift between those who wished to return home with their remaining shares and those who demanded renewed attacks to recoup their losses.

The beginning of the final epoch of their history was marked by the separation of the French and English buccaneers, who had previously presented a united front. This division was cemented in 1689 when the two nations entered a war, breaking the bond of unity that had lasted for three-quarters of a century.

## Final Extinction
In their waning years, the buccaneers remained along the coasts of Santo Domingo and Jamaica. They appeared as a significant force one last time during the siege of Carthagena, though they served merely as auxiliaries to French regular forces. Following the treachery of the French commander of that expedition, the community experienced a brief reawakening of energy, but this could not prevent their extinction.

Between 1697 and 1700, during a short period of peace, both France and England attempted to gain favor with the king of Spain. The career of the buccaneers was effectively closed following the resumption of hostilities in 1700 and the rise of Spain accompanying the accession of the French claimant to the throne.

## Historical Records and Sources
The history of the buccaneers is documented in several works, though some are noted to be received with caution. These include:
* *De Americaensche Zee Roovers* (1678), written by a buccaneer named Exquemelin and published in Amsterdam. This work was translated into English as *The Bucaniers of America* and into French as *Histoire des avanturiers qui se sont signalez dans les Indes*.
* *Voyages* by Dampier.
* *Monarchs of the Main, &c.* (1855) by Geo. W. Thornbury.
* *Voyage and Description of the Isthmus of America* (1699) by Lionel Wafer.
* *History of the Settlements and Trade of the Europeans in the East and West Indies* (English translation 1782) by Raynal.
* Works by Père Charlevoix, including *Histoire de l'isle Espagnole, &c.* and *Histoire et description générale de la Nouvelle France*.

A narrative considered really authentic is Captain James Burney's *History of the Buccaneers of America* (1816). Additionally, the *Calendar of State Papers*, Colonial Series, provides evidence regarding their history in the West Indies.
