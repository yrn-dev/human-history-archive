# Populus euphratica and Tamarindus indica

## Distribution and Classification
*Populus euphratica* and *Tamarindus indica* are identified as principal timber trees within the forests of the Bombay Presidency. The forests of this region are categorized into two primary classes: those located in the alluvial plains and those found in the hills. 

The alluvial forests, where these species are situated, are located in Sind, either on or in close proximity to the banks of the Indus. These alluvial forests cover a total area of 550 square miles. In contrast, the hill forests are distributed over a broader area, extending from 14 to 23 degrees North latitude, with the majority situated within the Western Ghats or the Sahyadri hills.

## Associated Timber and Flora
*Populus euphratica* and *Tamarindus indica* exist alongside a variety of other principal timber trees in the Bombay Presidency. These include:
* Teak
* Two varieties of blackwood (*Dalbergia Sisu* and *Dalbergia latifolia*)
* *Dalbergia ujainensis*
* *Pterocarpus Marsupium*
* *Terminalia glabra*
* *Acacia arabica* and *Acacia Catechu*
* *Nauclea cordifolia* and *Nauclea parvifolia*
* *Bidelia spinosa*
* *Hardwickia binata*
* *Juga xylocarpa*

Beyond timber-quality trees, the forests contain numerous species valued specifically for their berries, nuts, or fruits. Examples include the jack (*Artocarpus integrifolia*), the mango (*Mangifera indica*), *Zizypkus Jujuba*, *Aegle Marmelos*, *Terminalia Chebula*, *Calophyllum Inophyllum*, *Bassia latifolia*, and *Pongamia glabra*. Additionally, the presidency's palms include the areca catechu, date, palmyra, and cocoa-nut.

## Forest Economy and Resources
The forests provide various materials beyond timber. In Sind, the Forest Department generates a small amount of revenue from lac, and jungle tribes are known to collect gum from several different varieties of trees.
