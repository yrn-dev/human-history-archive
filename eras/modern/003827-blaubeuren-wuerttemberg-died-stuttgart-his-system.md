# Wuerttemberg

Wuerttemberg is a region in Germany that contains several towns and cities, including Stuttgart, the kingdom's center of rail and administration, and Backnang.

## Geography and Towns
Backnang is a German town located in the kingdom of Wuerttemberg, situated 19 miles northeast of Stuttgart by rail. As of 1900, its population was 7,650. The town is known for its leather factories, tanneries, and woollen and cloth mills, as well as an interesting church that dates back to the 12th century. Historically, Backnang was ceded to Wuerttemberg by Baden in 1325. The Wilhelmsheim sanatorium, which serves consumptives, is located in the vicinity of the town.

## Notable Figures
The region has been the birthplace and residence of various philosophers, scholars, and poets.

### Philosophers and Scholars
Christoph Gottfried Bardili (1761–1808) was a German philosopher born at Blaubeuren in Wuerttemberg who later died at Stuttgart. Bardili's philosophical system focused on "pure thought" as the ground or possibility of being, urging that philosophy should consider thought in itself rather than the Kantian distinction between the form and matter of thought. He identified the law of identity as the fundamental principle of thought, asserting that logical thinking is real thinking. He argued that the matter upon which thought operates is indefinite until rendered definite by the action of thought. However, Bardili's work was characterized as one-sided, as he believed thought lacked the power of development and ultimately reduced it to arithmetical computation. His works include *Grundriss der ersten Logik* (1800) and *Philos. Elementarlehre* (1802-1806). While his system had little influence in Germany—with Reinhold being the only one to expound it against attacks from Fichte and Schelling—his ideas helped open the way for the later speculations of Hegel and Schelling.

Georg Bernhard Bilfinger (1693–1750) was a mathematician, statesman, and philosopher born at Kanstatt in Wuerttemberg. Initially devoted to theology, he later studied mathematics and philosophy based on the works of Leibnitz and Wolff. After a period of lecturing in St Petersburg at the invitation of Peter the Great, Bilfinger returned to his native land in 1731. He served as a privy councillor under Duke Charles Alexander starting in 1735 and later became a member of the Regency Council. He is credited with the successful reorganization of the state, specifically in the areas of commerce, agriculture, state-religion, and education. He died at Stuttgart on February 18, 1750.

Johann Albrecht Bengel (1687–1752) was a Lutheran scholar and divine born at Winnenden in Wuerttemberg. Educated by a master in the gymnasium at Stuttgart, he later attended the university of Tubingen. Bengel's early intellectual pursuits included the works of Spinoza and Aristotle. He served as a parochial charge at Metzingen-unter-Urach and as a theological tutor in Tubingen. From 1713 to 1741, he served as the master (*Klosterpraceptor*) of the *Klosterschule* at Denkendorf, a seminary for candidates of the ministry.

### Literary Figures
Hermann Kurz (1813–1873) was a novelist and poet born at Reutlingen. He studied at the university of Tübingen and the theological seminary at Maulbronn, and served as an assistant pastor at Ehningen. He later became the university librarian at Tübingen. His historical novels include *Der Sonnenwirt* and *Schillers Heimatjahre*. His daughter, Isolde Kurz, was born at Stuttgart on December 21, 1853, and became a noted lyric poet and author of short stories, such as *Florentiner Novellen*.

## Religious and Academic Institutions
The region has a strong history of theological and biblical activity. The Wuerttemberg Bible Society was founded in Stuttgart in 1812. By 1905, the Wuerttemberg Bible Institute in Stuttgart had circulated an estimated 247,000 copies of the Scriptures.

Academic life is centered in part around the university of Tübingen, where figures such as Johann Albrecht Bengel and Hermann Kurz pursued their studies or careers.
