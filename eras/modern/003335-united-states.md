# UNITED STATES

## General Overview
The United States is described as a great community and a new phenomenon in history. While previous empires have possessed populations exceeding 100 million, those were associations of divergent peoples, whereas the United States is characterized as a single people on such a scale. In terms of scale and possibility, it is positioned halfway between a European state and a global union of all the world.

## Legal and Judicial Systems
The United States does not utilize assize courts. Unlike some systems, it is not the custom for the supreme court judges of the states to go on circuit. Instead, the highest court in the several states is divided into departments with presiding supreme court judges residing within those departments.

The United States Supreme Court judges periodically sit as members of the United States circuit courts across various states. These circuit courts are federal rather than state courts and operate under a special and limited jurisdiction. Regarding commercial law, the United States utilizes a general act relating to negotiable instruments, prepared by the conference of state commissioners on uniform legislation and adopted in leading states.

## Naval Operations and International Cooperation
The United States has maintained extensive naval operations in Europe, involving cooperation with Allied navies, specifically the British and the French.

### Cooperation with the British
United States naval activities in cooperation with the British included a naval headquarters in London and various operations in Ireland. These included:
*   Battleship Division Six and a submarine detachment based in Berehaven.
*   Destroyers and Subchaser Detachment Three based in Queenstown.
*   Naval air stations, seaplane stations, and a kite-balloon station in Ireland.
*   Battleship Division Nine and a Mine Force.
*   Subchaser Detachment One based in Plymouth.
*   Naval Air Stations in Great Britain, including a Seaplane Station in Killingholme and an Assembly and Repair Plant for the Northern Bombing Group in Eastleigh.
*   A Cross-channel Transport Service.

### Cooperation with the French
Activities with the French included a naval staff representative in Paris and a naval headquarters in Brest, where destroyers were based. The United States also operated naval air stations on the French coast, which featured dirigible stations, seaplane stations, kite-balloon stations, an Aviation Training School in Moutchie, and an assembly and repair plant in Pauillac.

### Naval Aviation and Global Presence
The establishment of United States naval aviation in Europe was a difficult and involved task. Captain H.I. Cone managed this effort from October 1, 1917, maintaining headquarters in Paris until August 1, 1918, before moving to London to serve as an aid for aviation on the staff of the commander of United States naval forces in Europe.

Additionally, the United States maintained naval forces based at Gibraltar under Rear Admiral Niblack, forces based on the Azores, and the U.S.S. *Olympia* in northern Russia.

## Military History and Armistice
In the context of wartime events, the United States Navy suffered losses during ship engagements. In one instance, 23 men were lost; seven of these were killed or drowned by the explosion of two torpedoes while working below decks in the forward end of the ship. These individuals included storekeeper second class H.A. Himelwright, yeoman second class F.W. Wilson, jr., coxswain B. Zanetti, seaman second class A.S. Egbert, seaman G.B. Hoffman, seaman second class J.A. Jenkins, and seaman second class F.A. Hedglin. Other losses included Dr. Whiteside, Paymaster Mowat, and Paymaster Johnston.

Following conflict, the United States and the Allies collaborated on the disarmament of German surface warships. This included the designation of six battle cruisers, ten battleships, eight light cruisers (including two mine layers), and fifty modern destroyers to be disarmed and interned in neutral or allied ports. The United States and the Allies also reserved the right to sweep up mine fields and obstructions laid by Germany outside of German territorial waters.

## Federal Law Enforcement and Criminal Proceedings
The United States government, through the United States Attorney's Office and the Federal Bureau of Investigation, conducts investigations into offenses committed against the United States. Legal proceedings have addressed violations of Title 18 of the United States Code, including:
