# Komm Jesu komm

## Overview and Composition
*Komm Jesu komm* is a motet composed by Bach. It is categorized among his vocal works written without an orchestra. While it is a motet *a capella*, it is noted as an exception among a group of five such motets; while the other four in that group are believed to have been intended for partial support by the organ, *Komm Jesu komm* was not.

## Context within Bach's Vocal Works
The motet exists within a broader body of ecclesiastical and secular compositions. Bach's other works without orchestra include a sixth motet featuring an obligato figured-bass accompaniment, as well as several early choruses that were largely utilized in later compositions and a large collection of plain chorales, some of which feature original melodies.

These works are distinct from his orchestral ecclesiastical pieces, which include the Mass in B minor, the Magnificat in D, four short masses (consisting only of the Gloria and Kyrie) compiled primarily from church cantatas, and the Passions according to St John and St Matthew. Additionally, Bach composed a funeral ode for the Duchess Eberhardine, which is now understood to be arranged from portions of a lost Passion according to St Mark.

## Related Musical Categories
The composition of *Komm Jesu komm* sits alongside Bach's diverse output in other genres:

*   **Secular Vocal Music:** This includes the *Dramma per Musica* works *Der zufrieden gestellte Aeolus* and *Der Streit zwischen Phoebus und Pan*, which have no essential connection to the stage. Bach also wrote seven choral and seven solo cantatas. The latter three choral cantatas were almost entirely absorbed into the B minor mass and the Christmas oratorio. His solo cantatas include two burlesques and two Italian works, one of which is his only developed work for clavier and voice.
*   **Instrumental Music:** Bach's orchestral output includes two concertos for three claviers, three concertos for two claviers (two of which were arranged from concertos for two violins), and seven clavier concertos arranged from other sources, including violin concertos.
