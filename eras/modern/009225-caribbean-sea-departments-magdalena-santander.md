# Caribbean Sea

The Caribbean Sea is a broad basin of water that serves as a boundary for several nations and regions in the Americas. It is bordered to the north by Honduras and to the east by Costa Rica. The area bounded by Honduras, Guatemala, and British Honduras is specifically referred to as the gulf or bay of Honduras.

## Regional Geography and Borders

The Caribbean Sea forms the northern and western boundaries of Bolivar, which was a department of Colombia until 1908. Bolivar covers an area of 27,028 square miles and consists largely of densely wooded, low alluvial plains. These lands are slightly cultivated and are considered unsuited for north European labour. The department is bounded to the east by the departments of Santander and Magdalena, to the south by Antioquia, and to the southwest by Cauca.

In Central America, the republic of Honduras is bounded to the north by the Caribbean Sea. Its north coast is characterized by many small estuaries and bays that serve as harbours, such as Trujillo, La Ceiba, Ulua, Omoa, and Puerto Cortes, though it is otherwise unbroken by remarkable inlets except for the Carataska Lagoon, a land-locked lake on the east. Similarly, the republic of Costa Rica is bounded to the east by the Caribbean Sea.

## Maritime History and Trade

During the 17th century, the Caribbean Sea was the primary base for buccaneers. These were piratical adventurers of various nationalities who were united in their opposition to Spain. The period between 1671 and 1685 marked the height of their power, prosperity, and daring. While they achieved many successes on land and in the Caribbean—including a second plunder of Porto Bello—they frequently looked toward the South Sea as a more lucrative field for their power, often contemplating expeditions across the isthmus.

In Colombia, the city of Cartagena, located on the Caribbean coast, previously served as the principal commercial entrepot of the country. In more recent history, the city of Barranquilla and its seaport, Puerto Colombia, became the principal commercial centre of the republic. This shift occurred because a dangerous bar at the mouth of the Magdalena river forced much of the trade from the river's tributary territory—which accounts for approximately 60% of the entire country's trade—to pass through Barranquilla.

## Major Ports and Urban Centres

Several key cities and ports are situated along or near the Caribbean coast and its connected waterways:

*   **Barranquilla:** Founded in 1629, it became a major commercial centre in the mid-19th century. It is located on the left bank of the Magdalena river, about 7 miles above its mouth. The city features a mix of fine public buildings and warehouses alongside mud-walled cabins made of bamboo framework and thatched rushes.
*   **Puerto Colombia:** The current seaport for Barranquilla, replacing Savanilla around 1890. It features a steel pier 4,000 feet long to assist in handling freight.
*   **Cartagena:** The capital of the department of Bolivar and a former primary commercial hub.
*   **Other Towns:** In the department of Bolivar, other important towns include Mompox (population 8,000) and Barranquilla on the Magdalena river, as well as Lorica (population 10,596 in 1902) and Corozal (population 9,000) near the western coast.
