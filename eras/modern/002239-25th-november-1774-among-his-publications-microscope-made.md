# Henry Baker (1698-1774)

Henry Baker was an English naturalist born in London on May 8, 1698. His early professional life began with an apprenticeship to a bookseller, after which he developed a system for instructing the deaf and dumb. This endeavor proved financially successful, allowing him to amass a considerable fortune.

## Professional Associations and Marriage
Baker's work with the deaf and dumb brought him to the attention of Daniel Defoe. In 1728, Baker associated with Defoe to start the *Universal Spectator and Weekly Journal*, publishing under the name Henry Stonecastle. In 1729, he married Defoe's youngest daughter, Sophia.

## Scientific Contributions and Honors
Baker was recognized for his contributions to science and antiquity, being elected a fellow of the Royal Society and the Society of Antiquaries in 1740. He provided numerous memoirs to the *Transactions* of the Royal Society. In 1744, he was awarded the Copley gold medal for his microscopical observations regarding the crystallization of saline particles.

Beyond his research, Baker was active in the organization of the arts; he was one of the founders of the Society of Arts in 1754 and served as its secretary for a period of time.

## Publications
Baker authored several works across different genres. His scientific publications focused on microscopy, including:
* *The Microscope made Easy* (1743)
* *Employment for the Microscope* (1753)

He also wrote and translated several volumes of verse. One notable work in this category was the 1727 poem *The Universe, a Poem intended to restrain the Pride of Man*.

## Death and Legacy
Henry Baker died in London on November 25, 1774. He left a sum of L100 in his will to establish the Bakerian lecture of the Royal Society, ensuring his name continued to be associated with the institution.
