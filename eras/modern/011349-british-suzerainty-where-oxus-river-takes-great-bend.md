# British Suzerainty and the Oxus River Boundary

The geopolitical landscape of Central Asia in the late 19th century was defined by the demarcation of boundaries between the Russian Empire and territories under British suzerainty. A critical point of this geographic and political intersection occurs where the Oxus river takes its great bend to the north.

## The Russo-Afghan Boundary
The advance of Russia toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts necessitated a formal boundary to separate Russian trans-Caspian conquests from the kingdom of Afghanistan. This boundary was established in the north-west by the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission between 1884 and 1886. The resulting line, which is almost entirely artificial, was fixed between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). 

Specific markers of this boundary include Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs where the boundary departs from the Hari Rud, and a southerly point crossing the Kushk roughly 60 miles north of Herat. At Khamiab, the boundary joins the Oxus river approximately 150 miles above the Russian Trans-Caspian railway crossing at Charjui. From this junction, the main channel of the Oxus serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia, and continues to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## The Pamir and Siberian Frontiers
Beyond Victoria Lake, the Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 demarcated a line extending to the glaciers and snowfields overlooking the Chinese border. The Sarikol range provides a rugged intervention between Chinese Turkestan and the Russian Pamirs, though the actual dividing line remains indefinite. 

Further north-east, the southern boundary of Siberia follows an irregular path defined partly by the Alatau and Tian-shan mountains. It reaches a northerly point at approximately 53 deg. N. lat., marked by the Sayan range west of Irkutsk. The boundary then deflects south-east to the Kerulen affluent of the Amur river (approximately 117 deg. 30' E. long and 49 deg. 20' N. lat.), following that affluent to the Amur river, then to the Usuri river. It follows the Usuri to its head and finally reaches the Pacific coast at the mouth of the Tumen river (about 42 deg. 30' N. lat.), 100 miles south of the Amur bay and the Russian port of Vladivostok.

## Geography of the Oxus Bend and Badakshan
The Oxus river is characterized by a significant northward bend, a feature determined by a remarkable meridional range extending 100 miles northwards from the Hindu Kush. This range narrows the river's valley into a trough that lasts until the next westward bend at Kala Wamar. 

This region is central to Badakshan, a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan that adjoins Russian territory. According to the Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873, Badakshan and its dependent district, Wakhan, were acknowledged as fully belonging to the amir of Kabul, with the boundary limited to the southern (left) bank of the Oxus. 

The province's geography is complex:
* **Northern Boundary:** Follows the Oxus stream under the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush.
* **Southern Boundary:** Follows the crest of the Hindu Kush to the Khawak pass, though it remains indefinite in some areas. It becomes definite again at the Dorah pass, which connects Ishkashim (at the Oxus bend) and Zebak to the Lutku valley leading to Chitral.
* **Eastern Extension:** Badakshan forms a "bottle-shaped" arm that is narrow at the neck (the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush) and swells eastwards to include parts of the little and great Pamirs.

## Regional Topography and Trade
The area within the Oxus bend includes the mountainous district of Darwaz. While Darvaz is described as a small vassal state of Bokhara situated on the Panj river, the physiography of the region is more similar to the Pamir type than the Hindu Kush. 

The Kokcha river traverses Badakshan from south-east to north-west, draining the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush. Some of its sources are located near Zebak, close to the great northward bend of the Oxus. The Kokcha effectively cuts off the mountainous area within that bend from the rest of Badakshan. 

Historically, the route from Zebak to Ishkashim, passing through the Dorah, and continuing from Ishkashim via the Panja through the Pamirs, served as a major continental high-road in Asia, connecting China and Kashgar with Afghan Turkestan.

## Natural History of Badakshan
The recesses of Badakshan are noted for rugged mountains and narrow but fertile and populous valleys. The region supports a variety of livestock, most notably the yak, as well as cows, goats, ponies, sheep, Bactrian camels, and fine dogs. The wild fauna includes leopards, bears, boars, deer, foxes, wolves, jackals, and a large wild sheep (_Ovis poli_). Birdlife includes pheasants, partridges, ravens, jays, sparrows, larks, and a famous breed of hawks.
