# Bolivia

Bolivia is an inland republic located in South America. Formerly known as Upper Peru or the province of Charcas, it was once part of the Spanish vice-royalty of Peru. It is the third largest political division on the continent, bounded by Brazil to the north and east, Paraguay and Argentina to the south, and Peru and Chile to the west. Official estimates of its area are 640,226 and 703,633 square miles, though other estimates place it at 515,156 square miles.

## Early History and Independence
The region was inhabited by the Aymara (ancient Colla), a tribe of South American Indians centered around the valleys of the Andes and Lake Titicaca. The Aymaras possessed a considerable culture before being conquered by the Incas in the 13th and 14th centuries, as evidenced by the megalithic ruins of Tiahuanaco. Under Inca rule, the region around Lake Titicaca was considered a "holy land." Following the arrival of the Spaniards, the Aymaras remained in a decadent state but kept certain privileges, including their language.

The struggle for independence from Spain involved nearly uninterrupted warfare from July 1809 to August 1825. Conflict occurred primarily between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta. Early patriot successes, such as General Balcarce's 1811 invasion, were followed by a period of alternate victories and defeats. In 1815, the patriots suffered a total rout between Oruro and Potosi. This was followed by an unsuccessful insurrection of Creoles and Indians from the southern provinces of Peru. Between 1816 and 1823, patriot forces maintained guerrilla warfare from the mountains of the province of Yungas.

In August 1825, an assembly of fifty-four deputies at the capital, Chuquisaca, declared that Upper Peru should be a distinct and independent nation. The assembly gave the country the name Bolivia and issued a formal declaration of independence.

## Political Development and Internal Conflict
Following independence, General Sucre held supreme command until a constitutional government was established. In 1826, Bolivar presented a project for a constitution that proposed a president for life with the power to nominate a successor. This proposal caused alarm among republicans in Chile and Buenos Aires, and led to accusations in Peru that Bolivar intended to create a confederacy of Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia under his own perpetual dictatorship.

The early republic faced repeated instability. In late 1827, Colombian troops under General Sucre were driven from La Paz. A new congress in April 1828 chose Marshal Santa Cruz as president. Although a revolution led by General Blanco caused temporary disorder in 1829, Santa Cruz restored order by 1831, promulgated a code of laws, and organized the country's financial affairs.

Between 1835 and 1839, Santa Cruz intervened in a power struggle in Peru, defeating General Gamarra and naming himself protector of Peru with the goal of confederating the two nations. This prompted intervention from Chile, which supported Gamarra. Following a defeat at the battle of Jungay in June 1839, Santa Cruz was exiled. Gamarra became president of Peru, while General Velasco became the provisional chief of Bolivia. Subsequent revolts installed General Ballivian in power. In August 1841, Gamarra attempted to annex the province of La Paz but was routed and killed by Ballivian. Ballivian remained president until 1848.

## Relations with Chile and Peru
In the late 19th century, Bolivia's relationship with Chile was defined by territorial and economic disputes. A 1866 treaty established the 24th parallel as the boundary and granted Chile a half share of customs and trading facilities between the 23rd and 24th parallels, as well as tax-free mining rights. In 1870, Bolivia granted an Anglo-Chilean company the right to work nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel for $10,000.

Discontent over Chilean economic influence led Bolivia to enter a secret agreement with Peru on February 6, 1873, for mutual defense and the preservation of territorial integrity against possible Chilean encroachment.

## The War of the Pacific and Aftermath
During the conflict with Chile, a double revolution occurred in Peru and Bolivia. In Bolivia, troops at Tacna, citing the incompetence or cowardice of their president, removed him from command and elected Colonel Camacho. Simultaneously, a revolution in La Paz proclaimed General Narciso Campero president, who was formally elected in June.

Throughout 1880, the war was primarily a naval struggle between Chile and Peru, with Bolivia playing little to no part. Following the Chilean capture of Lima and Callao in 1881, the war lingered in the interior. In September 1882, President Campero and Peruvian Admiral Montero decided to hold out for better terms. However, Peru signed a peace treaty with Chile on October 20, 1883.

Bolivia signed a peace treaty with Chile on December 11, 1883. Under this agreement, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, including the port of Cobija. To address the resulting lack of sea access, a treaty was signed at Santiago on May 18, 1895. Chile agreed that if it acquired permanent sovereignty over Tacna and Arica, it would transfer them to Bolivia for an indemnity of $5,000,000 silver. If this failed, Chile bound itself to cede $5,000,000 silver and the roadstead (caleta) of Vitor, or another analogous port that satisfied Bolivia's commercial requirements.
