# Egg-shaped mass of zoogloea of Beggiatoa roseo-persicina

The egg-shaped mass of zoogloea associated with *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina* is a specific morphological manifestation of the zoogloea stage in Schizomycetes. ## Nature of Zoogloea
Zoogloea is a remarkable phenomenon in the life-history of Schizomycetes, serving as a resting condition. It corresponds to the "palmella" condition found in lower Algae. This stage occurs when dense swarms of vegetative cells become fixed within a matrix composed of their own contiguous, swollen cell-walls. 

The formation of zoogloea begins with the active division of one or several mother-cells. The resulting progeny continue to secrete cell-wall substance, which absorbs water many times its own volume, creating a consistent matrix in which the cells come to rest. Depending on the species, this matrix may consist primarily of a proteid substance or cellulose, and its consistency can range from a thick solution of gum to a horny, resistant material.

## Morphology and Variation
Young zoogloea can assume various characteristic forms, including ovoid, spherical, lamellar, filamentous, reticular, and fruiticose. The egg-shaped mass of *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina* (previously referred to by Lankester as *Bacterium rubescens*) is one such ovoid variation. In addition to the egg-shaped mass, *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina* can also exhibit a reticulate zoogloea.

These forms can vary as the mass increases in size or comes into contact with other masses. In environments where small quantities of iron are present in the medium, older zoogloeae may precipitate oxide of iron within the matrix. If the zoogloea forms on a solid substratum, it may become firm and horny, though it softens upon immersion in water.

## Life-History and Pleomorphism
The study of *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina* contributed to historical scientific discussions regarding pleomorphism, or the mutability of bacterial shape. In 1873, Lankester noted that *Bacterium rubescens* (now *Beggiatoa roseo-persicina*) passes through various conditions that previous observers, following the doctrines of Cohn, might have classified as entirely separate genera or species. ## Classification of Beggiatoa
Within the order Trichobacterinae, *Beggiatoa* is classified under the family Trichobacteriaceae. It is distinguished from other members of this family, such as *Crenothrix* or *Cladothrix*, by its vegetative body consisting of cell-filaments that exhibit creeping and slow pendulous movements and lack a distinct sheath. Additionally, *Beggiatoa* is characterized by the presence of sulphur particles.
