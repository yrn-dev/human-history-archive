# Bonn and its Monuments

## Memorials and Tombs of Bonn
The town of Bonn contains several significant monuments and burial sites. In the Munster-platz, a statue was erected in 1845 to commemorate Beethoven, who was born in the town. A museum dedicated to Beethoven relics was later opened in 1889 in the composer's birth house.

The cemetery located outside of the Sterntor serves as the resting place for several notable figures. This includes B.G. Niebuhr, for whom Frederick William IV placed a monument. Also located here are the tombs of the diplomatist Christian Karl von Bunsen, A.W. von Schlegel, Robert Schumann, Karl Simrock, E.M. Arndt, and the wife of Schiller.

## Public Monuments and Architecture
Bonn is adorned with a marble monument that commemorates the war of 1870-71. Other town features include a handsome fountain and a statue of Reinkens, who was an Old Catholic bishop. 

The town's museum landscape includes a municipal museum, which has been arranged in a private house since 1882, and an academic art museum established in 1884 by F.G. Welcker, which houses some classic originals. Additionally, the provincial museum is situated near the railway station; this institution contains works of art and a collection of medieval stone monuments, as well as a small picture gallery.

## Landmarks and Fortifications
One of the most prominent features of Bonn when viewed from the river is the pilgrimage (monastic) church of Kreuzberg, dating to 1627. Located above and behind Poppelsdorf, the church features a flight of 28 steps that pilgrims traditionally ascended on their knees.

Regarding the town's old fortifications, "Der alte Zoll" is the only remaining bulwark. It provides a magnificent view of the Siebengebirge. The Sterntor was removed to facilitate better communication with the terminus of the light railway to Cologne and the rapidly increasing western suburbs.
