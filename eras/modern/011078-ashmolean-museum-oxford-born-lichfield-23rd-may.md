# Ashmolean Museum

The Ashmolean Museum at Oxford is a public museum of curiosities, recognized as the first of its kind in the kingdom. It was established through the efforts and gifts of Elias Ashmole.

## Foundation and Establishment
The museum was presented to the University of Oxford in 1677 by Elias Ashmole (1617–1692), an English antiquarian. Ashmole had inherited the larger part of the collection from a friend named John Tradescant. As a condition of the gift, Ashmole required that a suitable building be constructed to house the collection. Consequently, the materials were not finally installed in the museum until 1683. Following the establishment of the museum, Ashmole also provided a further gift of his library to the university.

## Elias Ashmole
Elias Ashmole was born in Lichfield on May 23, 1617, to a father who was a saddler. His professional life began in 1638 when he became a solicitor, and by 1644, he was appointed as a commissioner of excise. This role brought him to Oxford during the period when the Royalist Parliament was sitting there. While at Oxford, he formed a friendship with Captain George Wharton (later Sir George Wharton). Through Wharton's influence, Ashmole received a commission from the king to serve as comptroller of the ordnance and captain of horse.

In 1646, Ashmole became the first amateur or gentleman to be accepted as a Freemason. During this same year, he met William Lilly and other astrologers in London, which sparked an interest in astrology. However, in later years, this interest subsided as he turned his attention toward antiquarian research and heraldry.

Ashmole's financial situation improved significantly in 1649 when he married Lady Mainwaring, a relative of his first wife (who had died eight years prior) and a woman approximately twenty years his senior. This affluence allowed him to devote his full time to his studies. Although his wife petitioned for a separation in 1657, she failed to win her case and returned to live with him until her death in 1668.

During the period between his domestic crisis and his wife's death, Ashmole held several positions of favor at court, including:
* Windsor herald
* Commissioner, comptroller, and accountant-general of excise
* Comptroller of the White Office
* Commissioner for Surinam

In 1668, Ashmole married the daughter of Sir William Dugdale; he notably refused the office of Garter king-at-arms so that Dugdale could hold the position. Ashmole's dedication to antiquarian research is evidenced by his 1672 publication, *Institutions, Laws and Ceremonies of the Order of the Garter*, which is described as a practically exhaustive work.

## Academic and Institutional Use
The Ashmolean Museum has served as a site for academic instruction and the preservation of historical artifacts. Between 1801 and 1803, John Kidd, the first Aldrichian professor of chemistry at Oxford, delivered voluntary courses of lectures on geology and mineralogy. These lectures took place in the dark chambers located under the Ashmolean Museum. Students who received their first lessons in geology there included W. Buckland, C. G. B. Daubeny, and J. J. and W. D. Conybeare. These efforts eventually led to the establishment of the geological chair, which was first held by Buckland.

The museum also houses significant historical finds, such as the Alfred jewel. This item, which bears the name of King Alfred, was found in 1693 at Athelney, the site of the king's retreat in 878–879.

## Administration
The archive records David George Hogarth, M.A., as a Keeper of the Ashmolean Museum. Hogarth was a Fellow of the British Academy and Magdalen College, Oxford, and was an active excavator who worked at sites including Assiut, Ephesus, Naukratis, and Paphos.
