# Austria-Hungary

Austria-Hungary, also known as the Austro-Hungarian Monarchy (German: *Oesterreichisch-ungarische Monarchie* or *Oesterreichisch-ungarisches Reich*), was a country located in central Europe. Its borders were defined by Russia and Rumania to the east, Rumania, Servia, Turkey, and Montenegro to the south, the Adriatic Sea, Italy, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, and the German Empire to the west, and the German Empire and Russia to the north. As of 1905, the monarchy occupied approximately one-sixteenth of the total area of Europe, totaling 239,977 square miles.

## Political Structure and Composition
The monarchy was officially established under its actual name by a diploma issued by Emperor Francis Joseph I on November 14, 1868, which replaced the previous designation of the Austrian Empire. Often referred to unofficially as the Dual Monarchy, it consisted of two independent states:
*   **Cisleithania:** Unofficially called Austria, this state comprised the kingdoms and lands represented in the council of the empire, known as the *Reichsrat*.
*   **Transleithania:** Unofficially called Hungary, this state consisted of the "lands of St Stephen's Crown."

The nucleus of the empire was the original archduchy, which included Upper Austria and served as the oldest possession of the house of Habsburg within its present dominions.

## Population and Territorial Changes
In 1901, the population of the monarchy was 45,405,267 inhabitants, representing about one-eighth of the total population of Europe. 

The monarchy's territory expanded through the Berlin Treaty of 1878, which placed the principalities of Bosnia and Herzegovina under Austro-Hungarian administration. These principalities had an area of 19,702 square miles and a population of 1,591,036 as of 1895, while Turkey remained their suzerain. The annexation of these territories occurred in 1908 and was recognized by the Powers in 1909, formally incorporating them into the dominions of the monarchy.

## Constitutional and Diplomatic Historiography
The study of the monarchy's constitutional and diplomatic history is documented through various scholarly works. Constitutional history is addressed in texts such as E. Werunsky's *Oesterreichische Reichs- und Rechtsgeschichte* (1894), A. Bechmann's *Lehrbuch der oesterreichischen Reichsgesch* (1895-1896), and G. Kolmer's *Parlament und Verfassung in Oesterreich*, which covers the period from 1848 to 1885. The relations between Austria and Hungary are specifically detailed in works by J. Andrassy regarding the 1867 *Ausgleich* and L. Eisenmann's *Le Compromis austro-hongrois de 1867*.

Diplomatic history is explored through the analysis of Austrian politics between 1801 and 1810 by A. Beer, and the oriental politics of Austria since 1774. Other significant diplomatic studies include F. von Demelitsch's work on Metternich and H. Uebersberger's research on the relations between Austria and Russia from the end of the 15th century. Recent developments regarding the "Austrian question" at the threshold of the 20th century have been analyzed by authors such as Andre Cheradame and Rene Henry.

## General Historical Records
General histories of the House of Austria include the three-volume work by Archdeacon William Coxe (1218-1792), later continued by W. Kelly. Other comprehensive accounts include J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates* and Franz X. Krones' *Handbuch der Gesch. Oesterreichs*. Specific historical events, such as the revolution of 1848, are detailed in the works of Count F. von Hartig and W. H. Stiles.
