# David Gray and other Essays, chiefly on Poetry and Master Spirits

*David Gray and other Essays, chiefly on Poetry* (1868) and *Master Spirits* (1873) are significant works by the British author Robert Williams Buchanan. These publications, along with *A Poet's Sketch Book* (1883) and *A Look round Literature* (1887), represent Buchanan's primary contributions to periodical literature.

## Robert Williams Buchanan
Robert Williams Buchanan (1841–1901) was a versatile British dramatist, novelist, and poet. Born on August 18, 1841, in Caverswall, Staffordshire, he was the son of Robert Buchanan (1813–1866), a journalist and Owenite lecturer. After his father moved from Manchester to Glasgow, Buchanan received his education at the university and the high school in Glasgow. It was during his time as a student that he became close friends with the poet David Gray.

## David Gray and other Essays, chiefly on Poetry
Published in 1868, *David Gray and other Essays, chiefly on Poetry* contains an essay on David Gray that was originally contributed to the *Cornhill Magazine*. This essay documents the close friendship between Buchanan and Gray, specifically detailing their journey to London in 1860 as they sought fame. The essay on Gray was later reprinted in Buchanan's 1883 work, *A Poet's Sketch Book*.

## Master Spirits and Periodical Contributions
*Master Spirits* was published in 1873. Along with his other essays, Buchanan was a frequent contributor to periodical literature. He gained notoriety through an article published in the October 1871 issue of the *Contemporary Review* titled "The Fleshly School of Poetry," written under the pseudonym Thomas Maitland. This piece was later expanded into a pamphlet in 1872. 

The article sparked significant responses from other literary figures, including Mr. Swinburne in *Under the Microscope* (1872) and D.G. Rossetti, who wrote a letter to the *Athenaeum* on December 16, 1871, titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism." Buchanan eventually regretted the violence of his attack; he later dedicated his novel *God and the Man* to Rossetti, whom he referred to as the "old enemy."

## Other Literary Works
Beyond his essays, Buchanan produced a wide array of poetry, novels, and plays:

*   **Poetry:** His early works include *Undertones* (1863), *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). He explored mysticism in *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic* (1870). Other notable poems include "The Drama of Kings" (1871), "St Abe and his Seven Wives" (1872), "Balder the Beautiful" (1877), "The City of Dream" (1888), "The Outcast: a Rhyme for the Time" (1891), and "The Wandering Jew" (1893). His poems were collected into three volumes in 1874, one volume in 1884, and finally as *Complete Poetical Works* in two volumes in 1901.
*   **Novels:** His early novels, *The Shadow of the Sword* (1876) and *God and the Man* (1881), are noted for their simplicity and breadth of treatment. Later novels include *The Martyrdom of Madeline* (1882), *Foxglove Manor* (1885), *Effie Hetherington* (1896), and *Father Anthony* (1898).
*   **Plays:** His successful theatrical works include *Lady Clare* (1883), *Sophia* (1886)—which was an adaptation of *Tom Jones*—*A Man's Shadow* (1890), and *The Charlatan* (1894).
*   **Other Prose:** He wrote *The Land of Lorne* (1871), a two-volume vivid record of his yachting experiences on the west coast of Scotland.
