# Appointments to the Privy Council under King James

The Privy Council served as the council of state or government during the Stuart era. In the English political system of this period, the council was the primary body of government, though some kings employed "cabinet councils"—select bodies of favorites who held a larger share of the sovereign's confidence than the general Privy Council.

## Appointments under James I

Several notable figures were appointed to the Privy Council or held high office during the reign of James I. 

**Francis Bacon**
In the early part of 1616, while the lord chancellor Thomas Egerton (Baron Ellesmere) was dangerously ill, Bacon proposed himself for the office of lord chancellor. Although he received a distinct promise of the reversion of that office, Ellesmere recovered. Consequently, Bacon proposed that he be made a privy councillor to increase his influence as an adviser to the king. He took his seat at the council board and the associated oaths on June 9, 1616.

**John Erskine, Earl of Mar**
The 2nd (or 7th) Earl of Mar, a Scottish politician educated alongside King James VI, maintained a high level of royal favor after 1585. Mar served as the tutor to Prince Henry and as the governor of Edinburgh Castle. After serving as an envoy to London, where Queen Elizabeth assured him that James would be her successor, Mar joined the English privy council. He was later created Lord Cardross in 1610 and served as the lord high treasurer of Scotland from 1615 to 1630.

**The Earl of Bristol**
The Earl of Bristol served as a diplomat and was created earl of Bristol on September 15. However, he fell into conflict with the king's favorites, specifically the Duke of Buckingham, due to Buckingham's "implacable jealousy." This tension arose after Bristol wrote a letter to James regarding Buckingham's unpopularity among Spanish ministers. Following the death of James, Bristol was removed from the privy council by Charles I.

## Other James-Related Council Appointments

The archive records various other appointments involving individuals named James or serving under different monarchs named James:

*   **James V:** Henry Balnaves, a Scottish politician and reformer, was sworn into the privy council of James V before 1440. Balnaves was known for favoring an ecclesiastical reformation and an alliance with England.
*   **James VI:** George Buchanan, a scholar and tutor to the young King James VI, served as lord privy seal, a position that provided him with a seat in parliament. He held this office until at least 1579.
*   **King James (II):** John Sheffield, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Normanby, was dismissed from court in 1682. However, upon the accession of King James, he was appointed as lord chamberlain and received a seat in the privy council. He remained loyal to James during the king's flight from London and supported his most unpopular measures.

## The Nature of the Privy Council and Related Offices

The Privy Council's functions were extensive, with various committees handling duties that were later transferred to other departments. For instance, committees of the lords of the council once managed work now handled by the Board of Trade and the secretary of state for foreign affairs.

The Lord President of the Council was a member of the ministry and the House of Lords. While the office did not become permanent until 1679, it was previously managed by the lord keeper of the seal, the lord chancellor, or other court officials. Additionally, James I created the office of master of the ceremonies, a role characterized by a gold chain and a medal featuring the mottos "Beati pacifici" (peace) and "Dieu et mon droit" (war).
