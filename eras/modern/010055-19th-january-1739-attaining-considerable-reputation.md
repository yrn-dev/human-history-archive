# Giuseppi Bonomi

## Early Life and Education
Giuseppi Bonomi was an English architect born in Rome on the 19th of January 1739. He spent his early professional years in Italy, where he worked to attain a considerable reputation.

## Career in England
In 1767, Bonomi moved to England, where he eventually established his professional practice. He is credited with being largely responsible for the revival of classical architecture within England. His most renowned architectural achievement was the design of the Italian villa at Roseneath, located in Dumbartonshire, which was created for the duke of Argyll.

## Relationship with the Royal Academy
Bonomi's tenure in England was marked by a professional conflict involving the Royal Academy. Sir Joshua Reynolds, the president of the Academy, viewed Bonomi as a suitable candidate to fill the vacant chair of perspective and desired for him to be appointed as a full Academician. 

However, this suggestion was opposed by the majority of the Academicians. Consequently, Bonomi was elected only as an associate, a decision that was reached solely through the casting vote of the president. This disagreement served as the innocent cause for the eventual retirement of Sir Joshua Reynolds from the presidency of the Royal Academy.

## Later Years and Death
In 1804, Bonomi received an appointment as the honorary architect to St Peter's in Rome. He died in London on the 9th of March 1808.
