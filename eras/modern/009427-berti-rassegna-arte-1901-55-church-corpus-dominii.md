# Bologna

## Geography and Administration
Bologna is a city and archiepiscopal see located in Emilia, Italy. It serves as the headquarters of the VI. army corps and is the capital of the province of Bologna. The city is situated at the base of the Apennines, 180 feet above sea level, at the edge of the Emilia plain. In terms of distance, it is 50 miles direct, 63 miles by road, and 82 miles by rail from Florence (due north), and 134 miles from Milan by rail. As of 1901, the population of the town was 102,122, while the commune had a population of 153,501.

## Urban Development
The city is divided into an inner Roman city and an outer city. The Roman city is more or less rectangular and orientated on the points of the compass, featuring streets arranged at right angles. No buildings from the Roman city remain above ground. The outer city received its fortifications in 1206. The streets that lead to the gates of the outer city radiate from the outskirts of the Roman city rather than from its center.

## Religious Architecture
Several of the oldest churches in Bologna are located outside the limits of the Roman city, including SS. Vitale ed Agricola, S. Giovanni in Monte, and S. Stefano. The latter is a group comprising no fewer than seven different buildings from various dates. Among these is the former cathedral of SS. Pietro e Paolo, constructed around the middle of the 4th century, which utilized debris from Roman buildings. Additionally, S. Sepolcro is a circular church featuring brick ornamentation and an imitation of *opus reticulatum*, likely dating to the 6th or 7th centuries.

The current cathedral, S. Pietro, was erected in 910 and is now almost entirely in the baroque style. The largest church in the city is S. Petronio, dedicated to the patron saint of Bologna. Construction began in 1390, though only the aisles and nave up to the transepts were completed. This Gothic fragment measures 157 feet wide and 384 feet long. The original design for the cruciform basilica was far more ambitious, projecting a total length of over 700 feet, a breadth of 460 feet across the transepts, and a crossing dome 500 feet high.

Another significant site is the church of S. Domenico, which houses the body of the saint who died in the city in 1221. While the interior was remodelled during the 18th century, the exterior remains unfinished.

## Notable Figures
Bologna is the birthplace of several illustrious individuals, including Prospero Lambertini, who became Pope Benedict XIV, and Luigi Galvani (1737-1798), the discoverer of galvanism.
