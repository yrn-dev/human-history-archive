# Strasburger's Text-book of Botany

Strasburger's *Text-book of Botany* (also referred to as *Lehrbuch der Botanik*) is a botanical work utilized as a source for detailed anatomical and morphological illustrations of various plant species and structures.

## Liverworts and Jungermanniales
The archive contains specific data regarding the *Jungermanniales*, a large series of liverworts characterized by significant variety in the organization of their sexual generation. This series is divided into two primary groups based on the growth of the branch in relation to the formation of archegonia:

*   **Anacrogynous Jungermanniaceae**: This is the more primitive group. In these plants, the branch continues to grow after the archegonia are formed. Consequently, the archegonia and the subsequent sporogonia are positioned on the leafy plant or the dorsal surface of the thallus.
*   **Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae**: These plants are entirely foliose. The archegonia are located at the ends of the branches or the main shoot. The antheridia in this group are typically long-stalked and globular, and the capsule opens by splitting into four halves.

Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae are found in diverse environments, including on stones, rocks, the ground, tree trunks, and on leaves within the damp tropics. While they generally form green tufts of varying sizes, some forms exhibit a reddish tint.

## Plant Anatomy and Morphology
The work provides detailed observations on the internal and external structures of various plants:

*   **Marchantia polymorpha**: Illustrations from the text show the stoma in surface view, as well as a vertical section of the air-chamber, which contains the stoma and filaments of assimilating cells.
*   **Anthoceros laevis**: The text identifies the columella and the sporogonium of this species.
*   **Blasia pusilla**: The thallus of this plant features leaf-like lobes on its margin, and it possesses rhizoids and a sporogonium.
*   **Andreaea petrophila**: Documentation of this plant includes the calyptra, the pseudopodium, and the foot of the sporogonium, as well as the opened capsule.

## Fruit and Seed Structures
Strasburger's work is cited in the study of fruit forms and seed anatomy. This includes the vertical section of a grain of wheat, which reveals the embryo at the base of the endosperm, separated by the scutellum. Other identified structures in the wheat grain include the primary root, the sheath of the primary root, the sheath of the plumule, and the foliage leaf.

The text also provides data on various fruit types:
*   **Achenes**: Specifically the achene of *Ranunculus arvensis*, showing the embryo and endosperm in longitudinal section.
*   **Dry one-seeded fruits**: Such as that of the dock (*Rumex*), which consists of a seed, endosperm, and an embryo with cotyledons pointing downwards and a radicle pointing upwards, all enclosed by a pericarp formed from the ovary wall.
