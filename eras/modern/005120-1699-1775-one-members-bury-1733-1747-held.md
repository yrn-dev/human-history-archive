# Thomas Hervey (1699-1775)

Thomas Hervey was an English politician, writer, and court official who lived from 1699 to 1775. He was a son of John Hervey, the 1st Earl of Bristol (1665-1751), and his second wife, Elizabeth, who was the daughter and co-heiress of Sir Thomas Felton, Bart., of Playford, Suffolk.

## Political Career and Public Life
Thomas Hervey served as one of the members of parliament for Bury from 1733 to 1747. In addition to his parliamentary service, he held various offices at court. He belonged to a family with a strong presence in the borough of Bury St Edmunds; his father, the 1st Earl of Bristol, had been a member for Bury St Edmunds starting in March 1694, and his half-brother, Carr, Lord Hervey, had represented the area from 1713 to 1722. Another of his brothers, Felton Hervey (1712-1773), also served as a member for the family borough of Bury St Edmunds.

## Personal Life and Character
Hervey's personal life was marked by instability and controversy. He eloped with Elizabeth, the wife of Sir Thomas Hanmer. He suffered from very poor health, and his reckless lifestyle frequently resulted in other difficulties as well as pecuniary problems. 

Despite these struggles, he was recognized for his manner. Upon his death, Dr. Johnson remarked that while Tom Hervey was a "vicious man," he was among the "genteelest men who ever lived."

## Literary Contributions
Beyond his political and court activities, Thomas Hervey was a writer who produced numerous pamphlets.
