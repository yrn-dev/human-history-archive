# Tower

The term "Tower" appears in the archive's records in several distinct contexts, ranging from royal prisons and military targets to religious sites and ancient architectural ruins.

## The Tower of London
The Tower of London has served as a place of detention for various political and noble figures. In May 1562, the Earl of Desmond was imprisoned in the Tower for a short period following his "insolent conduct" before the privy council. Later, Desmond and his brother, Sir John of Desmond, were sent to England and experienced a short period of imprisonment in the Tower before surrendering their lands to the queen.

In a later era, the Tower of London became the target of a conspiracy led by Edward Marcus Despard, an Irish conspirator. Between 1798 and 1800, after being arrested on complaints and remaining in gaol, Despard engaged in a plot to seize both the Bank of England and the Tower of London, with the additional goal of assassinating George III. This plan was described as "patently preposterous." Despard was subsequently found guilty of high treason and executed on February 21, 1803.

## Religious and Funerary Towers
In Bombay, the archive notes the existence of the Parsee Towers of Silence. These structures are located toward the northern end of Malbar hill and are used by the Parsees for the exposure of their dead. The bodies remain there until vultures devour the flesh, after which the bones are cast into a well to crumble into dust.

## Ancient and Imperial Towers
Archaeological and travel records describe towers used as markers or fortifications along ancient routes in Asia. Along the road from Shachau to the north-west toward the Kara-nor, towers built with clay—approximately 30 feet high—are found every five *lis*. These structures were abandoned by the Chinese and were once connected by a wall, which is now destroyed. M. Bonin identified these remains as part of the great route from the Han Dynasty that ran through Kan Suh, the Desert of Gobi, Eastern Turkestan, Pamir, and Bactria to China. Additionally, Dr. Sven Hedin observed four or five towers eastward of the junction of the Konchehe-daria and the Tarim.
