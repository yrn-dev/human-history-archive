# Bologna

Bologna is a city and archiepiscopal see located in Emilia, Italy. Serving as the capital of the province of Bologna and the headquarters of the VI. army corps, it is situated at the base of the Apennines, 180 feet above sea level. The city is positioned 63 miles from Florence by road (82 miles by rail) and 134 miles southeast of Milan by rail. As of 1901, the town population was 102,122, with the commune population reaching 153,501.

## History and Governance
The city's origins include the ancient Bononia, which served as a meeting point for important roads. In the Roman era, the city was rectangular and oriented to the points of the compass with streets arranged at right angles. While no Roman buildings remain above ground, the Roman city is distinguishable from the outer city, which received fortifications in 1206.

Bologna was subject to the Lombards for a short period but generally remained under the rule of the exarchate of Ravenna. In 756, Pippin gave the exarchate to the papacy. Following a sack by the Hungarians in 902, the city eventually became a free and independent city, with its first commune "constitution" dating to approximately 1123. Between the 12th and 14th centuries, Bologna was frequently at war, supporting the Guelph cause against neighboring cities of Emilia and Romagna and against Frederick II. In 1249, the Bolognese captured Enzio, the son of the emperor, who remained in confinement for the rest of his life.

Internal struggles between Ghibellines and Guelphs weakened the commune, leading Taddeo de' Pepoli to become master of the town in 1337. In 1350, his son sold the city to Giovanni Visconti of Milan. Though given to the papacy ten years later, the city revolted to recover its liberty. Giovanni Bentivoglio became lord of Bologna in 1401 but was killed in a 1402 rebellion. The city returned to the Visconti before being secured again by the Bentivoglio in 1438. This rule lasted until 1506, when Pope Julius II drove them out. Bologna remained under papal sway—except during the revolutions of 1821 and 1831 and the Napoleonic period (1796–1815)—until it became part of the kingdom of Italy in 1860.

## Architecture and Religious Sites
Several of the city's oldest churches are located outside the limits of the Roman city, including SS. Vitale ed Agricola, S. Giovanni in Monte, and S. Stefano. The latter is a group of at least seven buildings; its earliest, the former cathedral of SS. Pietro e Paolo, was built around the middle of the 4th century using Roman debris. S. Sepolcro, a circular church featuring brick ornamentation, likely dates to the 6th or 7th centuries. The current cathedral, S. Pietro, was erected in 910 and is now primarily in the baroque style.

The largest church in Bologna is S. Petronio, dedicated to the city's patron saint. Begun in 1390 in the Gothic style, only the aisles and nave up to the transepts were completed. This fragment measures 157 feet wide and 384 feet long, though the original plan called for a cruciform basilica over 700 feet long with a 500-foot high dome. Additionally, the church of S. Domenico contains the body of the saint who died in the city in 1221; its interior was remodeled in the 18th century.

## Notable Natives and Residents
Bologna has been home to several illustrious figures:
* **Prospero Lorenzo Lambertini (Pope Benedict XIV):** Born in Bologna on March 31, 1675, he served as pope from 1740 to 1758. He was an efficient and popular archbishop of Bologna starting in 1731. His scholarly works include *Libri octo de synoda diocesana* (begun in Bologna), *De sacrificio missae*, and *De servorum Dei beatificatione et canonizatione*. As pope, he recognized the king of Prussia, issued the bull *Immensa pastorum principis* regarding the humane treatment of Indians in Paraguay and Brazil, and rebuked Jesuit missionary methods in Malabar and China.
* **Luigi Galvani (1737-1798):** The discoverer of galvanism.
* **Leopoldo Marco Antonio Caldani (1725-1813):** An anatomist and physician who taught practical medicine in Bologna.
* **Petronio Maria Caldani (1735-1808):** A professor of mathematics described as Italy's first algebraist and geometer.
* **Marianne Elisa (1777-1820):** Sister of Napoleon and former grand duchess of Tuscany, she retired to Bologna as the countess of Compignano after her brother's fall.

## Geography and Economy
Bologna is a major railway center. The main line from Milan divides here, with one branch following the ancient Via Aemilia toward Brindisi, Ancona, and Rimini, and another crossing the Apennines toward Rome and Florence. Other lines connect the city to Padua, Ferrara, Budrio, and Portomaggiore, while steam tramways serve Malalbergo, Pieve di Cento, and Vignola.

The city is known for specific local specialties, including liqueurs, *tortellini* (a type of macaroni), and *salami* or *mortadella* (Bologna sausage).
