# Conflict Between the Turks and the Imperial Forces (1738-1739)

The conflict between the Turks and the imperial troops during the late reign of Charles VI was the result of a treaty of alliance signed with Russia in 1726. This obligation compelled Charles VI to embark on a war with Turkey that lasted from 1738 to 1739.

## The Campaign and Battle of Krotzka
The military operations of this war were characterized by varying fortunes. However, the tide turned decisively on July 23, 1739, when the Turks defeated the imperial troops at Krotzka. Following this victory, the Turks laid siege to Belgrade.

## The Treaty of Belgrade
On September 1, 1739, a treaty was signed at Belgrade to conclude the hostilities. Under the terms of this agreement, Austria was forced to surrender nearly all the territories it had previously acquired through the treaty of Passarowitz, with the sole exception of the banat.

## Aftermath and the Reign of Charles VI
The disastrous outcome of the war with Turkey embittered the final years of Charles VI's life. When he died on October 20, 1740, he left his dominions in a state of weakness. This condition rendered his lands unable to resist attacks from other powers who, despite having adhered to the Pragmatic Sanction, sought to take advantage of the empire's vulnerability.

Despite these military failures, Charles VI is noted for his contributions to internal development. He maintained a moderate and tolerant religious attitude and worked to promote the enlightenment of his subjects. He was also a zealous promoter of industry and trade. His economic initiatives included the establishment of the East India Company at Ostend and the development of Fiume and Trieste as centers of trade with the Levant and as sea-ports.
