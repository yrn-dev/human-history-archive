# RULE XVII.--CONTRIBUTORY VALUES

Rule XVII is a specific provision within the York-Antwerp Rules designed to establish a standardized method for determining contributory values in cases of General Average (G.A.) loss. Before the introduction of these rules, the amounts upon which freight and ships were required to contribute varied widely across different countries. Rule XVII replaced these inconsistent national practices with a uniform principle, providing greater certainty and definiteness in the adjustment of G.A. losses.

## Calculation of Contributions
Under Rule XVII, the contribution to a General Average loss is calculated based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure. To arrive at the final contributory value, the amount made good as G.A. for any property that was sacrificed must be added to the actual value of the property.

## Deductions from Values
The rule specifies several necessary deductions to ensure the accuracy of the contributory values:

*   **Shipowner's Freight and Passage-Money:** Deductions are made from the freight and passage-money at risk. These deductions consist of crew's wages and port charges that would not have been incurred if the cargo and ship had been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice or G.A. act, provided these costs have not already been allowed as G.A.
*   **General Property Charges:** A deduction is made from the value of all property for any charges incurred regarding that property after the G.A. act has occurred, unless those specific charges are allowed in G.A.

## Exclusions from Contribution
Rule XVII explicitly states that certain items are exempt from contributing to General Average. Specifically, personal effects and passengers' luggage that have not been shipped under a bill of lading shall not contribute to G.A.

## Context within the York-Antwerp Rules
Rule XVII is part of a broader effort by the York-Antwerp Rules to introduce uniformity and clarity to marine adjustments. This is seen in other related rules, such as Rule XIII, which established a definite scale for "new for old" deductions in repair costs to replace uncertain customary rules. Other adjacent provisions include Rule XIV, which prohibits "new for old" deductions for temporary repairs allowable as G.A., and Rule XV, which stipulates that loss of freight arising from the loss of or damage to cargo shall be made good as G.A. when caused by a G.A. act or when the cargo loss is made good. Additionally, Rule XVI defines the amount to be made good for sacrificed goods as the loss sustained by the owner, based on market values at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival.
