# Global Trade and Export Commodities

The exchange of goods across different regions involves a diverse array of raw materials, agricultural products, and manufactured items. Trade patterns vary significantly by region, with some areas specializing in the export of natural resources and others focusing on industrial output or the transit of goods.

## European Trade and Industry

In Austria-Hungary, the German Empire serves as the primary partner for trade, accounting for approximately 60% of exports and 40% of imports. The region's principal exports include wood, sugar, cattle, glass and glassware, iron and ironware, cereals, eggs, leather goods, earthenware, pottery, millinery, and fancy goods.

In Baden, the industrial output now equals or surpasses the agricultural industry. Key manufactured goods include chemicals, jewellery, clocks, glass, china, leather, tobacco, paper, silk ribbons, and cotton and woollen goods. The region also produces beet sugar, and the inhabitants of the Black Forest are noted for creating musical boxes, organs, toys, and wooden ornaments. While the bulk of Baden's trade consists of the transit of goods, Mannheim serves as a major emporium for exports moving down the Rhine.

Denmark's commerce is primarily based on home production and consumption. The most significant exports are foods of animal origin, specifically dairy produce. Butter is the most important export, representing nearly 40% of the total export value. Other major exports include bacon and large quantities of eggs. Home-bred horned cattle are the most important export among living animals. Other exports of lesser value include wool, vegetables, bones, tallow, cement, and dairy machinery.

In the Azores, trade is shared between Germany and the United Kingdom, with British vessels primarily handling the transport. The exports consist mainly of provisions, wine, fruit, and natural mineral waters. The export of pineapples is particularly significant; in 1902 and 1903, 940,000 pineapples—raised under glass—were exported to Hamburg and London in nearly equal amounts.

## Trade in the Americas

In Cartagena, the aggregate value of exports in 1906 was $3,788,094 U.S. gold. The city exports sugar, cattle, tobacco, coffee, fibre, coco-nuts, rubber, hides and skins, vegetable ivory, dividivi, dye-woods, medicinal forest products, and precious metals including gold, silver, and platinum.

Honduras possesses great natural wealth in vegetable products, though economic development has been slowed by national bankruptcy, political disturbances, and a scarcity of capital and labour. The most valuable exported commodity, excluding minerals, is bananas, which are grown especially in the northern maritime province of Atlantida. Other exports include coco-nuts, sugar, coffee, tobacco, maize, beans, and cattle (exported to Cuba). The country also exports rubber, though the stock of wild rubber is almost exhausted. Valuable cabinet-woods, such as cedar and mahogany, and dye-woods are exported, though reckless destruction has reduced their supply. Mineral exports include gold and silver, with the total value of ores exported being L174,800 in 1904 and L239,426 in 1905.

In Belize, the principal seaport of British Honduras, exports include sugar, coco-nuts, fruit (especially bananas), sarsaparilla, tortoiseshell, turtles, deerskins, and various woods, including rosewood, mahogany, cedar, logwood, and other dye-woods and cabinet-woods.

## Trade in Africa and the Middle East

In Cairo, commerce consists mainly of the transit of goods. Important articles include ostrich feathers, ivory, and gum from the Sudan; sugar and cotton from Upper Egypt; tobacco and sheep from Asiatic Turkey; and shawls and indigo from Persia and India. The city also handles European manufactures such as cotton and woollen goods, hardware, cutlery, and glass. Local manufacturing includes leather, gunpowder, saltpetre, silk goods, and paper. A sugar-refinery founded in 1859 is located on the Island of Roda.

In the region where El-Fasher is the capital, the chief exports are ivory, gum, and ostrich feathers. While the slave trade has ceased, these three items remain the primary exports. There is also an active trade in cattle and camels.

## Regional Import Trends

Import patterns often mirror the needs of the local economy. Austria-Hungary imports metals, tobacco, coffee, silk and silk goods, wool and woollen goods, and cotton and cotton goods. Denmark imports timber due to a scarcity of native timber suitable for building. In Belize, imports consist of hardware, cotton fabrics, and breadstuffs. In the region of El-Fasher, the principal imports are tea, sugar, and cotton goods. Similarly, Cartagena imports machinery, cutlery, hardware, kerosene, earthenware, and glass, as well as woollen, linen, and cotton fabrics.
