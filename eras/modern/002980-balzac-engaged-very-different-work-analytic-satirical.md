# Balzac's Analytic-Satirical Work and Literary Career

Honore de Balzac (1799–1850) was a French novelist whose professional literary career is recognized as having started in 1829. This year marked the publication of two distinct types of work that illustrated the different facets of his creative approach: the romantic and the analytic-satirical.

## The Physiologie du mariage
While Balzac was working on his first serious attempt at novel-writing, he was simultaneously engaged in a very different project consisting of analytic-satirical sketches. These sketches compose the *Physiologie du mariage*. This work serves to illustrate Balzac's non-romantic side. Although these sketches were written with some crudity, they represented a vast advance over his earlier productions.

## Early Romantic Works and Influences
In the same year as the *Physiologie du mariage* (1829), Balzac published *Les Chouans* (originally titled *Le Dernier Chouan*). This novel was written under the direct influence of Scott, a lifelong admirer of Balzac. To gather material, scenery, and atmosphere for the book, Balzac traveled to Brittany and stayed at the chateau of M. and Mme. de Pommereul near Fougeres. 

*Les Chouans* has been judged variously by critics; those who emphasize Balzac's realism tend to think little of it, whereas those who view him as a romantic "with a difference" value it more highly. The work is noted for its brilliant colouring, vivid scenes, and a level of passion and "curtain" at its ending that exceeds most of his other books. While it contains a touch of melodrama, it is distinguished from the tedious and confused imitations of C. R. Maturin, M. G. Lewis, and Mrs Radcliffe found in his *Oeuvres de jeunesse*.

## The Comedie humaine and Creative Process
Balzac's total production is characterized by a unique relationship between the author and his work, as it is suggested that his entire output existed in his head together. This serves as the justification for his syllabus of the work as the "Comedy" (or *Comedie humaine*). By 1842, he had organized this huge structure into subdivisions, including *Etudes philosophiques* and *Scenes de la vie parisienne*.

His creative process was marked by furious energy. Balzac typically wrote from midnight until the following day, sometimes in stretches of sixteen hours lasting for weeks. He frequently altered, re-shaped, and re-connected different parts of his work, often correcting small printed originals into long novels during the proofing stage.

Some of his planned works remained unfinished or lost. *La Bataille*, a book intended to cover the battle of Essling, was mentioned in letters to Madame Hanska as being in progress but has never been found. Victor Hugo, in his funeral discourse, summarized Balzac's work as a blend of "observation and imagination."

## Major Works and Chronology
Following his start in 1829, Balzac produced a vast array of works:
*   **1830:** This "epoch year" saw the partial publication of *La Peau de chagrin* (focused on the Nemesis of accomplished desire) and *La Maison du chat qui pelote*. Other works from this year include *Gobseck*, *La Vendetta*, and *Une Passion dans le desert*.
*   **1831:** *La Peau de chagrin* was completed, alongside *Le Chef-d'oeuvre inconnu* and *Maitre Cornelius*.
*   **1832:** Notable publications included *Le Colonel Chabert*, *Le Cure de Tours*, and the philosophic and autobiographical *Louis Lambert*.
*   **1833:** This year produced the masterpiece *Eugenie Grandet*, the amusing *L'Illustre Gaudissart*, and the first part of *L'Histoire des treize*.

Beyond novels, Balzac wrote political pamphlets, miscellaneous articles, and several dramas, including *Mercadet le faiseur* and *Vautrin*. He also nearly wrote a periodical titled the *Revue parisienne*.

## Critical Reception and Bibliography
The study of Balzac's work was significantly aided by the *Edition definitive* (starting in 1869), which reunited his scattered originals and correspondence. Various critics have analyzed his work, including Sainte-Beuve (with whom Balzac had a falling out), Gautier, and Taine, whose 1858 Essay is considered the start of a series of important, unbiased studies. Other notable analyses include Paul Flat's work on character types and A. Cabanes' investigation into Balzac's interest in occult studies.

The bibliography of his work is complex. Early editions included a fifty-five volume set by Michel (Calmann) Levy. A comprehensive history of his works was later attached to the *Edition definitive* by M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul.
