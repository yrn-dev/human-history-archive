# Franz von Baader

Franz Xaver von Baader (1765–1841) was a German theologian and philosopher. Born on March 27, 1765, in Munich, he was the third son of F. P. Baader, who served as the court physician to the elector of Bavaria. Baader's brothers were also distinguished figures: the eldest, Clemens, was an author, and the second, Joseph (1763–1835), was an engineer.

## Education and Early Career
Baader initially pursued medical studies in Vienna and Ingolstadt. Following his studies, he spent a short period assisting his father in medical practice, though he found this professional life uncongenial. Consequently, he shifted his focus to mining engineering, studying under Abraham Gottlob Werner at Freiberg. This path led him to travel through various mining districts in north Germany and eventually to England, where he resided for four years between 1792 and 1796.

## Intellectual Influences
During his time in England, Baader encountered the ideas of Godwin, Hartley, and Hume, all of which he found extremely distasteful. Instead, he found harmony in mystical speculations, specifically those of Saint Martin, Meister Eckhart, and especially Jakob Boehme.

Upon returning from England in 1796, Baader settled in Hamburg and established a long-term friendship with F. H. Jacobi. During this period, he became acquainted with the work of Schelling. While the publications Baader produced during this time show a manifest influence from Schelling, the archive notes that Baader was not a disciple of Schelling and likely contributed more to the relationship than he received. The friendship between the two philosophers lasted until approximately 1822, ending when Baader wrote a letter to Emperor Alexander I. of Russia denouncing modern philosophy, an act that entirely alienated Schelling.

## Collected Works and Publications
Baader's works were compiled and published between 1851 and 1860 by a group of adherents, including Schlueter, von Osten-Sacken, Lutterbeck, E. v. Schaden, J. Hamberger, and F. Hoffman, in the 16-volume collection *Baader's saemmtliche Werke*. This collection includes valuable introductions by the editors, a full biography in volume xv, and an index and sketch of Baader's system by Lutterbeck in volume xvi.

Other significant publications related to his work include:
* **By F. Hoffmann:** *Vorhalle zur spekulativen Lehre Baader's* (1836), *Grundzuege der Societaets-Philosophie Franz Baader's* (1837), *Die Weltalter* (1868), *Philosophische Schriften* (3 vols., 1868-1872), and *Biographie und Briefwechsel* (1887).
* **By J. Hamberger:** *Cardinalpunkte der Baaderschen Philosophie* (1855) and *Fundamentalbegriffe von F. B.'s Ethik, Politik, u. Religions-Philosophie* (1858).
* **By J. A. B. Lutterbeck:** *Philosophische Standpunkte Baaders* (1854) and *Baaders Lehre vom Weltgebaeude* (1866).

## Critical Surveys and Biographies
Several scholars have provided surveys and analyses of Baader's life and thought. Notable among these are:
* **J. Claassen:** Author of *Franz von Baaders Leben und theosophische Werke* (Stuttgart, 1886-1887) and *Franz von Baaders Gedanken ueber Staat und Gesellschaft* (Guetersloh, 1890).
* **Erdmann:** Provided a survey in *Versuch einer Gesch. d. neuern Phil.* (iii. 2, pp. 583-636).
* **Other contributors:** Otto Pfleiderer in *Philosophy of Religion* (vol. ii, 1887), R. Falckenberg in *History of Philosophy* (pp. 472-475), Reichel in *Die Sozietaetsphilosophie Franz v. Baaders* (1901), and Kuno Fischer in *Zur hundertjaehrigen Geburtstagfeier Baaders* (1865).
