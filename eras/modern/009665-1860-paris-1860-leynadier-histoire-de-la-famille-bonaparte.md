# C. Leynadier and the History of the Bonaparte Family

## Overview of the Work
C. Leynadier is the author of the historical work titled *Histoire de la famille Bonaparte de l'an 1050 a l'an 1848*. This text provides a chronological account of the Bonaparte family, tracing their history from the year 1050 through to 1848. The work was later expanded and continued up until the year 1866 by an author identified as de la Brugere. The publication of this continued version took place in Paris in 1866.

## Context within Bonaparte Historiography
The work by Leynadier exists among a variety of historical and genealogical studies concerning the Bonaparte family. Other texts documenting the family's origins and members include:

*   **Genealogical and Early History:** *Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte*, written by a Samminiatese (D. Morali) and published in Florence in 1846, focuses on the family's origins and the extinction of the branch that existed in the city of S. Miniato. F. de Stefani also wrote *Le antichita dei Bonaparte*, published in Venice in 1857.
*   **Imperial Family Records:** L. Ambrosini and A. Huard published *La Famille imperiale. Hist. de la famille Bonaparte depuis son origine jusqu'en 1860* in Paris in 1860.
*   **Biographical Studies:** Various authors have focused on specific family members. A. Kleinschmidt wrote *Die Eltern und Geschwister Napoleons I.* (1876), while Baron H. Larrey authored *Madame Mere* (1892). Clara Tschudi wrote *Napoleons Mutter*, translated from Norwegian by H. von Lenk (1901).
*   **Comprehensive Family Histories:** F. Masson produced a four-volume set titled *Napoleon et sa famille*, published between 1889 and 1900 (with some volumes dated 1897-1900). A. Chuquet wrote a three-volume work on the youth of Napoleon, *La Jeunesse de Napoleon* (1897-1899).

## Family Members and Related Sources
The Bonaparte family included several prominent figures whose lives are documented in the archive's sources. Napoleon I had several brothers and sisters, listed in order of age, starting with Joseph Bonaparte.

### Joseph Bonaparte
Extensive records exist regarding Joseph Bonaparte, including the ten-volume *Memoires et correspondance politique et militaire du roi Joseph* (1854) and *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883), both by Baron A. du Casse. Other sources include J.S.C. Abbott's *History of Joseph Bonaparte* (1869) and G. Bertin's *Joseph Bonaparte in America*.

### Lucien Bonaparte
Lucien Bonaparte's life was marked by conflict with the emperor. After refusing to annul his marriage in December 1807 to become a French prince, he resided at his estate of Canino, taking the title of prince of Canino. He later traveled to America but was captured by a British ship and held in Malta and England until 1814. He supported Napoleon during the Hundred Days in 1815 and remained by his side at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris. Lucien died in Rome on June 29, 1840, leaving behind six daughters and four sons. His own writings include the epic *Charlemagne, ou l'Eglise delivree* (1814), as well as *La Verite sur les Cent Jours* and unfinished *Memoirs*.

### Other Siblings
The archive also notes the existence of other siblings, including Elisa, Pauline, and Jerome Bonaparte. Jerome's history is detailed in the seven-volume *Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine Catherine* (1861-1866) by Baron Albert du Casse. The fortunes of the family continued through the descendants of Jerome and Lucien, including a descendant named Charles.
