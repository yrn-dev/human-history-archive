# British Central Africa

British Central Africa is a general designation for the British protectorates located in South Central Africa north of the Zambezi river. Specifically, it refers to a large territory situated between 8° 25' S. on Lake Tanganyika and 17° 6' S. on the river Shiré (near its confluence with the Zambezi), and between 36° 10' E. in the district of Mlanje and 26° 30' E. on the river Luengwe-Kafukwe.

## Early Exploration and British Interests
European penetration into the region was initially limited to vague Portuguese wanderings during the 16th and 17th centuries. The first educated European explorer to enter the country was the Portuguese official Dr. F.J.M. de Lacerda e Almeida, who traveled from Tete on the Zambezi toward Lake Mweru.

The modern history of the region began with David Livingstone, who discovered Lake Nyasa in 1859 after penetrating up the Shiré river. Livingstone's subsequent explorations to Lake Mweru, Lake Bangweulu (where he died in 1873), and the south end of Tanganyika established British interests in South Central Africa. Following his death, missionary societies entered the region to begin evangelization. These missionaries, along with a small number of Scottish settlers, resisted Portuguese attempts to expand their influence from the Zambezi and Moçambique provinces.

## Conflict and the Establishment of the Protectorate
From the missionary societies, the African Lakes Trading Corporation was formed. This organization entered into a conflict with Arabs established at the north end of Lake Nyasa. A struggle for possession of the country between Britons and Arabs began around 1885 and lasted until 1896. During this unofficial war, the African Lakes Corporation utilized volunteers, including Mr. (later Sir) Alfred Sharpe and Captain (later Sir F.D.) Lugard, both of whom were wounded in operations that did not achieve complete success.

In 1889, Mr. (later Sir) H.H. Johnston was dispatched to the region to attempt an arrangement between the African Lakes Corporation and the Arabs, and to ensure that friendly native chiefs were protected from Portuguese aggression. These efforts and the resulting treaties led to the creation of a British sphere of influence and protectorate north of the Zambezi.

## Administrative Evolution and Geography
Sir H.H. Johnston originally applied the term "British Central Africa" to all territories under British influence north of the Zambezi that were intended for a single administration. However, because the Rhodesian territories and Barotseland could not be connected with the direct British administration north of the Zambezi, the name was officially confined in 1893 to the British protectorate around Lake Nyasa and the Shiré. In 1907, this protectorate was renamed the Nyasaland Protectorate.

The territories of the British South Africa Company north of the Zambezi were divided into North Western Rhodesia (Barotseland) and North Eastern Rhodesia. Despite these official distinctions, "British Central Africa" remains a convenient designation for the region, which includes the Nyasaland Protectorate, North Eastern Rhodesia, and part of North Western Rhodesia. The western boundary of this region is defined by a line from near the source of the Lualaba on the Belgian Congo boundary to the western source of the Luanga river, following the Luanga to its junction with the Luengwe-Kafukwe, and then following the Kafukwe down to the Zambezi.

## Settlements and Infrastructure
The government capital of the protectorate is Zomba, located at the base of the mountain of the same name. Blantyre, situated in the Shiré Highlands at an altitude of approximately 3,000 feet, is the principal European town; it was founded in 1876 by the Church of Scotland mission and named after the birthplace of David Livingstone.

Other European settlements and townships include:
* **On the Shiré and Lake Nyasa:** Port Herald (lower Shiré), Chiromo (junction of the Ruo and Shiré), Fort Anderson (Mount Mlanje), Fort Johnston (near the Shiré's outlet from Lake Nyasa), Kotakota and Bandawe (west coast of Lake Nyasa), Likoma (island off the east coast), and Karonga (north-west coast).
* **Other regions:** Fife (Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau), Abercorn (south end of Lake Tanganyika), Kalungwisi (east coast of Lake Mweru), and Fort Rosebery (near the Johnston Falls on the Luapula).
* **North Eastern Rhodesia:** Fort Jameson, which serves as the capital of N.E. Rhodesia and is located near the river Luangwa.
