# Bakewell

Bakewell is a market-town located in the western parliamentary division of Derbyshire, England. It is situated on the river Wye, 25 miles north-north-west of Derby, and is served by the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of its urban district was 2,850.

## Geography and Scenery
The town is located within a region of Derbyshire characterized by diversified physical aspects. While the extreme south of the county is largely level, the north contains the Peak District, which offers some of England's finest hill scenery, featuring grey rocks and richly wooded glens and valleys. Bakewell sits on the river Wye, which rises near Buxton and traverses Monsal Dale and Millersdale. The scenery in the vicinity of Bakewell, encompassing both the Wye and Derwent valleys, is described as very beautiful. Nearby landmarks include the magnificent domain of Chatsworth to the east and Haddon Hall, located two miles from the town amidst meadows and gardens.

## History and Architecture
The town's church, All Saints, is mentioned in the Domesday Survey. Tradition attributes the construction of the nave to King John, though the western side of the tower is considered older. The church contains several monuments to the Manners and Vernon families, as well as admirable specimens of encaustic tiles; a runic rood-stone is located in the churchyard.

In the vicinity of the town, Castle Hill contains the remains of an earthwork believed to have been raised in 924 by Edward the Elder. The parish also includes the mansions of Castle Hill and Burton Closes. Other notable local buildings include the town hall, the museum, and the Bakewell and High Peak Institute.

## Industry and Commerce
Bakewell has a history of diverse industrial activity. Sir Richard Arkwright established cotton manufacture in the town. Additionally, marble and zinc are worked in the surrounding neighborhood. The town is also known for its chalybeate spring, which is used for cases of chronic rheumatism and features attached baths. Bakewell has further gained fame for a specific type of jam-cake known as a "Bakewell pudding."

## Education and Institutions
The town's institutional history includes the founding of almshouses in 1602, known as St John's hospital. In 1637, Lady Grace Manners endowed a free grammar school.
