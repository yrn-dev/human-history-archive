# Baron Gautsch and the Austrian Franchise Reform

Baron Gautsch served as the premier of Austria during a period of significant social unrest and political transition regarding electoral rights. His tenure was marked by a shift in policy concerning universal suffrage and the introduction of complex legislative measures to reform the franchise.

## Social Unrest and the Shift Toward Suffrage
In September 1905, a large demonstration of the working class and socialists took place in Budapest to advocate for universal suffrage. This event, combined with the influence of the revolutionary movement in Russia, encouraged the Austrian Socialist party to seek similar franchise reforms within Austria. This led to a series of demonstrations in various towns, including Graz and Prague, as well as in Vienna at the beginning of November, where collisions with police resulted in some bloodshed.

Initially, Baron Gautsch had discountenanced the idea of universal suffrage, though he acknowledged that a restricted reform was desirable. However, he eventually changed his position. On November 28, Gautsch permitted a massive, orderly Socialist demonstration in support of universal suffrage to occur in the Vienna Ringstrasse. During this event, traffic was suspended for five hours as workmen marched ten abreast past the houses of parliament. This demonstration had a profound effect on public opinion. On the day of the march, the premier promised to introduce a large measure of franchise reform by February, specifically designed to ensure that racial minorities would not be overwhelmed by majorities of other races at the polls.

## The Gautsch Reform Measures
On February 23, 1906, Baron Gautsch introduced a series of franchise reform measures. The primary objectives of these measures were the establishment of the franchise based on universal suffrage and the abolition of the electoral class system, known as the *curia*. 

To protect various ethnic groups, the plan proposed dividing Austria into racial compartments, which would ensure each race was protected from molestation by others. The redistribution bill proposed by Gautsch included the following specifics:
*   An increase in the number of constituencies from 425 to 455.
*   The allotment of a fixed number of constituencies to each province.
*   The further division of constituencies within each province to each race based on their numbers and tax-paying capacity.
*   The enfranchisement of every male citizen over the age of 24, provided they met a residential qualification of one year.

## Political Transition and Succession
Baron Gautsch fell from power in April over a difference with the Poles. He was succeeded by Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst, who took over the reform bills. However, Prince Konrad also resigned six weeks later. His resignation served as a protest against the crown's consent to enact a customs tariff in Hungary that was distinct from, though identical to, the joint Austro-Hungarian tariff established by the Reichsrath in the Szell-Koerber compact.

## Completion of the Reform under Baron von Beck
On June 2, a new cabinet was formed under Baron von Beck. Baron von Beck was a permanent under secretary of state in the ministry for agriculture and had previously served as an instructor in administrative and constitutional law to the heir apparent, Archduke Francis Ferdinand.

Through skillful negotiations with various races and parties, and with the steady support of the emperor—who once summoned recalcitrant party leaders to the Hofburg *ad audiendum verbum* to insist that the reform "must be accomplished"—Baron von Beck reached a final agreement in October 1906. The reform was officially adopted on December 1. As part of the final negotiations, the number of constituencies was increased to 516, divided according to the provinces.
