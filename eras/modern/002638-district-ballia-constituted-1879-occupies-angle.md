# Ballia

## Geography and Administration
Ballia is a town and district located in British India within the Benares division of the United Provinces. Constituted in 1879, the district occupies an angle at the junction of the Ganges and the Gogra rivers. It is bordered by two districts of Behar and encompasses an area of 1,245 square miles. 

The town of Ballia itself is situated on the left bank of the Ganges, positioned below the confluence of the lesser Sarju. Rather than a concentrated urban center, the town is characterized as an aggregation of rural villages.

## Demographics and Land Value
As of 1901, the population of the Ballia district was 987,768, which represented a decrease of 5% over the preceding decade. The town of Ballia had a population of 15,278 in 1901.

Agricultural land in Ballia possesses a market value higher than in almost any other district. This high valuation is attributed to several factors: the great pressure on the soil resulting from a dense population, the general productiveness of the land, a very light assessment of government revenue, and a characteristic reluctance among small proprietors to part with their land. For purely agricultural purposes, land commonly brings in Rs. 200 per bigha (or £20 per acre), and in some instances, this figure is doubled.

## Economy and Trade
The economy of the district is primarily agricultural. The principal crops grown in the region include:
* Rice
* Barley
* Pulse
* Sugar-cane
* Opium
* Other food-grains

Manufacturing is virtually non-existent in the district, with the exception of sugar production. Trade is conducted largely via the two bordering rivers.

## Regional Context
Ballia is one of the five districts that comprise the Division of Benares, alongside the districts of Benares, Mirzapur, Jaunpur, and Ghazipur. The total area of the Benares division is 10,431 square miles, and its total population in 1901 was 5,069,020, reflecting a 6% decrease over the decade.
