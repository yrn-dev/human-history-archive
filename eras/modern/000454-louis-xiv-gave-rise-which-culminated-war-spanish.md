# Louis XIV

Louis XIV (1638–1715) was the King of France, born on September 5, 1638, at Saint-Germain-en-Laye. He was the son of Louis XIII and Anne of Austria, the daughter of Philip III of Spain. His birth was viewed as a significant triumph because the previous childlessness of the king had threatened the policies of the minister Richelieu, as the heir apparent, Gaston of Orleans, opposed those policies.

## Early Life and Rise to Power
Louis XIV became king on May 14, 1643, following the death of his father. However, he did not begin to rule personally for sixteen years. During this period, power was held by the queen-mother and Cardinal Mazarin. Mazarin struggled to maintain the integrity of French territory and the power of the throne during the last stages of the Thirty Year's War and the domestic upheavals of the Fronde. 

The royal authority was weakened by the king's childhood and the hatred directed toward Mazarin as a foreigner. On two occasions, the court was forced to flee Paris; in one instance, the populace was admitted to see the king in his bed following a rumor of his intended flight. These humiliations contributed to the development of Louis XIV's later autocratic ideas. By 1653, the Fronde had ended, and the diplomacy and arms of France were successful, marked by the peace of Westphalia (1648) and the peace of the Pyrenees (1659).

## Territorial Ambitions and Early Conflicts
At age twenty-one, Louis XIV sought to rule actively. The peace of the Pyrenees stipulated his marriage to his cousin, the Infanta Maria Theresa, to cement a friendship with Spain. This marriage later provided the basis for Louis XIV's ambition to possess the Netherlands.

His reign was characterized by a series of aggressive wars. Between 1654 and 1659, France fought Spain in a conflict known as the Spanish Fronde, featuring the battle of the Dunes on June 14, 1658. Other early conflicts included the War of Devolution (1667–68), a war of sieges in the Low Countries, and the rapid conquest of Franche-Comte in 1668, though the latter was relinquished at the peace.

The first of three "great wars" of Louis XIV took place from 1672 to 1678. During this time, the French monarchy "ate its way" across the Spanish Netherlands. While Louis XIV was content to withdraw his ships from the ocean and North Sea—knowing his fleet could not meet the Dutch single-handed—he conducted the war mainly on land. In 1674, he took advantage of a Sicilian revolt against Spanish rulers to seize the island.

## European Coalitions and Internal Policies
The growth of aggressive French power frightened other European nations, leading to a coalition in support of Holland. This struggle lasted until 1678. Later, from 1688 to 1697, Louis XIV fought a war against the Grand Alliance, led by King William III. This conflict ended with the treaty of Ryswick, which forced France to cede certain frontier territories.

Internally, Louis XIV's reign was marked by the introduction of an absolutist system. He also oversaw the persecution of Protestant Huguenots through the *dragonnades*, which caused a great emigration of these people to England and Holland.

## The Spanish Succession
The death of the last Hapsburg heir in Spain, Charles II, on November 1, 1700, created a succession crisis. Through astute diplomacy, Louis XIV secured the inheritance for his grandson, Philip of Bourbon (Philip V). Although Louis XIV had formally renounced such claims under the treaty of the Pyrenees, he took immediate steps to support his grandson.

Louis XIV effectively governed in the name of Philip, and the French absolutist system was introduced into Belgium by the Count of Bergeyck and French generals. This sparked the War of the Spanish Succession (1702–1713), as England and the United Provinces allied against Louis XIV. 

The conflict was concluded through the treaties of Utrecht, Rastadt, and Baden (1713–14). While the French prince Philip retained the Spanish crown, France was forced to make territorial concessions. The Netherlands (Belgium) were transferred to the Austrian branch of the Hapsburgs to serve as a barrier protecting the United Provinces from French menace.

## Legacy and Impact
The ambitions of Louis XIV gave rise to a long struggle with France that impacted the history of Europe and Germany. His aggressions led to the ravaging of the margraviate of Baden by French troops, where he was resisted by Louis William, the margrave of Baden-Baden. In Hungary, Louis XIV spurred on the Turks, who burst into the country in 1683 and appeared before the walls of Vienna.

The eventual settlement of the War of the Spanish Succession ended the era of French invasions of Germany and established Austrian supremacy in Italy and the Netherlands.
