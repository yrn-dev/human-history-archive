# Captain H.B. Vaughan and Major Percy M. Sykes in Persia

## Geographical Mapping and Scientific Exploration
During the late 19th century, the northern and north-western frontiers of India underwent a significant extension of scientific geographical mapping. This progress was characterized by a shift from general exploration to professional surveying. Indian surveyors were assisted in these labors by several scientific geographers, including Captain H.B. Vaughan and Major Percy M. Sykes, who operated in Persia. Their work contributed to a series of geographical maps spanning from the Oxus to the Indus, and from the Indus to the Euphrates. This broader effort included the systematic survey of Baluchistan starting in 1880, as well as the extension of boundaries between Persia and Baluchistan.

## Major Percy M. Sykes' Geographical Research
Major P. Molesworth Sykes conducted extensive research and travel within Persia, producing works including a book titled *Persia* and articles in the *Geographical Journal*. His research often focused on the reconciliation of modern geography with the historical accounts of Marco Polo.

### The Persian Deserts
Sykes challenged existing geographical classifications regarding the great desert of Persia. He argued that geographers had divided the region into two areas—the Dasht-i-Kavir to the north and the Dasht-i-Lut to the south—without sufficient grounds. Sykes proposed that "Lut" is the single name for the entire desert, suggesting that "Dash-i-Lut" is nearly a redundancy. He further noted that the term *Kavir* (derived from the Arabic *Kafr*) is applied generally to any saline swamp. According to Sykes, this great desert extends from a few miles outside of Tehrán to the British frontier, covering a distance of approximately 700 miles.

### Exploration of Historical Sites
Sykes utilized his travels to identify locations mentioned in the writings of Marco Polo. While traveling to Darband, Sykes identified ruins he believed to be "Cobinan," noting that the modern Kúhbenán did not match Polo's descriptions and observing that caravan routes in the East seldom change. 

Additionally, Sykes explored the site of "Camadi" after arriving in Rudbar and leaving the Farman Farma. He discovered a large area covered in yellow bricks measuring eight inches square, though no standing walls remained of what was once a great city known as Sher-i-Jiruft. In his writings, Sykes noted that Camadi was first sacked following the death of Toghrul Shah of Kermán, when the province fell into anarchy under his four sons.

## Observations on Natural History and Culture
Beyond geography, Major Sykes contributed observations on the fauna and peoples of Persia.

### Ornithology
Sykes provided a list of seven hawks from a province, provided to him by a Khán. In this list, all the hawks were black and white except for the *Sháhin*, which is characterized by yellow eyes and is the third largest in size. Sykes praised Marco Polo's description of the *Sháhin*, stating it could not be improved upon.

### The Karwán Expedition
In a chapter of his work dedicated to "The Karwán Expedition," Sykes discussed the Karwánis. He observed that they are distinct from the surrounding Baluchis and do not pay tribute, leading him to question if the Karwánis are the same people as the "Caraonas" described by Marco Polo.

## Historical Analysis of Marco Polo's Route
Major Sykes offered a specific theory regarding the Venetian traveler's path through the region. He expressed disbelief that Marco Polo visited Baghdád. Instead, Sykes proposed that the Venetians entered Persia near Tabriz and traveled through Sultania, Kashán, and Yezd before proceeding to Kerman and Hormuz.
