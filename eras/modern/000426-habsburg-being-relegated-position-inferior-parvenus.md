# The Habsburg Monarchy and the Prevention of Inferior Status

The house of Habsburg, which developed a vast realm through the gradual accumulation of territories starting from a small mark on the south bank of the Danube, faced significant political challenges regarding its status and prestige during the early 19th century. A primary concern for the monarchy was the preservation of its standing relative to emerging political powers.

## The Proclamation of the Empire of Austria
In 1804, Francis II, the last of the Holy Roman emperors, proclaimed himself Francis I, emperor of Austria. This official designation of the territories ruled by the Habsburg monarchy was a strategic move intended to protect the house of Habsburg from being relegated to a position inferior to the Bonapartes, whom the text characterizes as *parvenus*. 

Francis II acted with this motive to guard against two specific possibilities: the final collapse of the Holy Roman Empire, or the potential election of Napoleon as his successor on the throne of Charlemagne. Consequently, the title of emperor of Austria replaced the title "Imperator Romanorum semper Augustus" when the Holy Roman Empire officially ended in 1806.

## Limitations and Perceptions of the Imperial Title
Despite its intent, the title of emperor of Austria was viewed by many as a mere title that did not accurately reflect the actual relationship between the Habsburg sovereigns and their various states. The title was met with resistance from different ethnic groups within the monarchy:
* **Magyars and Slavs:** These groups never willingly recognized a style that implied the superiority of the German elements of the monarchy and ignored their national rights.
* **Germans:** For the German population, the title was considered a poor substitute for the previous title that had represented the political unity of the German race under the Holy Empire.

While the "Kaiser" continued to hold a powerful influence over the imaginations of Germans outside Habsburg dominions for a time after the Vienna Congress of 1814-1815, this was largely due to the "ancient halo" surrounding the title. The essential change in status was not immediately recognized by the public.

## Decline of the Title's Significance
The prestige of the Austrian imperial title was eroded by subsequent political and military events. The long struggle with Prussia culminated in 1866, which "broke the spell" of the title. Following this, the proclamation of the German empire in 1871 stripped the title of emperor of Austria of everything except a purely territorial significance.

Furthermore, the compact with Hungary in 1867 meant the title no longer fully represented the emperor's relation to all his dominions. The title, originally devised to cover the entirety of the Habsburg monarchy, sank into the official style of the sovereign of only a half. Within the Austrian empire proper, the title remained a point of resentment for peoples such as the Bohemians, who sought the same recognition of national independence that had been granted to Hungary.

## Historical Context of Habsburg Power
The effort to maintain a superior position in the 19th century followed a long history of territorial expansion. The Habsburgs succeeded the house of Babenberg in ruling the land, extending their rule over neighboring territories with "remarkable perseverance and marvellous success." 

However, the monarchy's prestige had fluctuated in earlier eras. At the close of the middle ages, the rulers of Austria lacked the prestige associated with the electoral office. Although five had served as German king, the four preceding Maximilian had added little to the dignity or power of that position. The house later faced challenges to its indivisibility, such as when the estates of Bohemia had to sanction the female succession for Archduchess Maria Theresa via the Pragmatic Sanction in 1720 to prevent the extinction of the male line.
