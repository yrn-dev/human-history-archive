# Mineral Resources of Correze

## Geography and Administration
Correze is a department located in south-central France. It was formed from the southern part of the former province of Limousin. The department is bounded to the north by the departments of Creuse and Haute-Vienne, to the east by Puy-de-Dome, to the south by Lot, and to the west by Dordogne. To the southeast, it is bordered by Cantal. 

The department covers an area of 2,273 square miles and had a population of 317,430 as of 1906. Situated on the western edge of the central plateau of France, Correze consists of a hilly tableland. This region is elevated in the north and east and is intersected by various fertile river valleys that generally trend toward the south and south-west. The highest elevations, some exceeding 3,000 feet, are located in the north on the Plateau de Millevaches.

## Hydrography and Climate
The Plateau de Millevaches serves as a divide between the basins of the Garonne and the Loire. With the exception of a small district in the extreme north watered by the Vienne, the department belongs to the Garonne basin. The south-eastern region is watered by the Dordogne. The department's namesake river, the Correze, and the Vezere (of which the Correze is the primary tributary) both originate in the Plateau de Millevaches, flowing south-west before uniting west of Brive.

The climate of the department is generally variable, damp, and cold, although the south-west region is characterized as mild and agreeable.

## Economy and Agriculture
The majority of the population in Correze relies on agriculture. Arable land constitutes approximately one-third of the department, primarily located in the south-west. The most abundant cereals grown are rye, wheat, and buckwheat. Additionally, tobacco, flax, and hemp are cultivated.

The eastern and northern elevated regions are used for pasture, with sheep being particularly numerous on the Plateau de Millevaches. There is also significant rearing of goats and pigs, and the region practices cheese-making and poultry-farming. In the vicinity of Brive, vineyards produce wine of medium quality. The primary fruits grown are cider-apples, walnuts, and chestnuts, the latter of which are used extensively as food.

## Mineral Products and Industry
The mineral products of the department include building-stone, slate, and coal, though the latter is found in small quantities. Other stone is also produced, and clay is worked for use in tile-works and potteries.

Industrial activity in the department includes several types of mills: saw-mills, oil-works, breweries, and flour-mills. There are also dye-works and the production of coarse woollens and hats in Bort. The most significant industrial establishment in the department is the government manufactory of fire-arms located at Tulle.
