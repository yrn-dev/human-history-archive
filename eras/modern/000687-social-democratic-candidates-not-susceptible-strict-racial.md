# Social Democratic Candidates in Austria

## Electoral Impact and Racial Classification
In the context of the 1907 general election in Austria, the allotment of seats at the polls was influenced by the victory of several Social Democratic candidates. These specific candidates were noted for not being susceptible to strict racial classification. This electoral outcome contributed to a broader political shift that formally overturned the fiction that Austria was primarily a German country. Instead, the reality was presented as a country that was preponderatingly Slav, though it maintained a German dynasty and a German facade.

## Distribution of Parliamentary Seats
The redistribution of seats resulted in a permanent minority for the Germans, who held 233 seats compared to the 259 seats held by the Slavs, despite the German constituencies receiving a proportion that was unduly favourable. When combined with "Latin" seats—consisting of Italian and Rumanian representatives—the "German-Latin block" totaled 257 seats. However, this block ceased to exist in practice because the Italians began to cooperate with the Slavs rather than the Germans.

Among the various groups, the Ruthenes experienced the greatest gain from the redistribution, seeing their representation trebled, although this remained disproportionate to their actual numbers. Because German parties anticipated that any further revisions to the allotment would be to their disadvantage, they stipulated that a two-thirds majority would be required for any future alterations to the law.

## Perspectives on Social Democracy in the Military
Outside of the parliamentary structure, the perception of Social Democracy varied. In accounts from the West, an inquiry into the number of Social Democrats among a group of approximately 150 soldiers was met with laughter and derision.
