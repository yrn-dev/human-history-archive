# C. R. Maturin and the Oeuvres de Jeunesse

## Relation to Balzac's Early Works
C. R. Maturin is identified as one of the authors whose style Balzac imitated during his early writing period. The works produced during this time, which comprise the majority of the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*, are characterized as tedious and confused imitations of Maturin, as well as M. G. Lewis and Mrs Radcliffe. 

These early productions differ fundamentally from Balzac's later, more serious attempts at novel-writing. For example, his work *Les Chouans* (originally titled *Le Dernier Chouan*) is noted for its brilliant coloring and vivid scenes, standing in stark contrast to the imitations of Maturin and others found in the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*.

## The Oeuvres de Jeunesse
The *Oeuvres de jeunesse* consist of a series of singular productions that occupy an "outside place" in Balzac's overall body of work. These writings emerged from a period between 1820 and 1822, followed by another period of equal length. 

The earliest works, described as the *incunabula* of Balzac, include:
* *Les Deux Hector, ou Les Deux Families bretonnes*
* *Charles Pointel, ou Mon Cousin de la main gauche*

In 1822, six additional works were produced:
* *L'Heritiere de Birague*
* *Jean Louis, ou La Fille trouvee*
* *Clotilde de Lusignan, ou Le Beau Juif*
* *Le Centenaire, ou Les Deux Beringheld*
* *Le Vicaire des Ardennes*
* *Le Tartare, ou Le Retour de l'exile*

In 1823, three more followed:
* *La Derniere Fee, ou La Nouvelle Lampe merveilleuse*
* *Michel et Christine et la suite*
* *L'Anonyme, ou Ni pere ni mere*

Further additions included *Annette et le criminel* (1824), a continuation of *Le Vicaire*, and *Wann-Chlore* (1825), which was later renamed *Jane la pale*.

## Publication and Composition
Originally filling approximately two score volumes, these novels were published under various pseudonyms, such as "Horace de Saint Aubin" and "Lord R'hoone" (an anagram of "Honore"). Some of these works were created in actual collaboration with two or three other writers.

While these works are generally considered to lack overall quality—with very few parts deserving of praise—they are regarded as necessary for anyone wishing to apprehend the "true Balzac." The production of these works eventually ceased not because of their lack of quality or financial failure, but because Balzac became involved in business as a type-founder, printer, and publisher.

## Bibliographic History
The *Oeuvres de jeunesse* have a complex publication history. They were included in the fifty-five small 24mo volumes published by Michel (Calmann) Levy, which was the edition most well-known for many years. However, these early works were not reprinted in the *Edition definitive* (which began in 1869). Instead, the *Edition definitive* provided miscellaneous works in four volumes and a volume of correspondence.
