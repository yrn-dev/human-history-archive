# Spanish Exploration and Colonial Claims in the Americas

## Claims in Florida
During the period when French Huguenots attempted to establish a colony in Florida, the Spaniards claimed the territory as their own because they had already explored the country. The strategic importance of Florida was heightened by its location on the trade route used for returning from Mexico, making any possession of the area by a foreign power highly dangerous.

To mitigate this risk, Philip II entered into a contract, known as an *asiento*, with a seaman named Aviles on March 20, 1565. Under this agreement, Philip II advanced 15,000 ducats to Aviles, naming him the proprietor of any colony he could establish in Florida, provided the funds were repaid. Aviles departed on July 28, 1565, leading a force of 1,500 men with ten sloops and one 600-ton vessel. On August 28, he entered and named the Bay of St Augustine, where he began the construction of a fort.

## Conflict with the French
Spanish efforts in Florida led to violent confrontations with French forces. On September 20, 1565, Aviles captured the French post of Fort Caroline. In October of that year, he exterminated a group of Frenchmen led by the Huguenot Jean Ribault, who had arrived to provide relief to the colony. Aviles ordered nearly all prisoners slain and their bodies hung from trees with inscriptions stating they were hung "Not as Frenchmen but as Lutherans." Aviles viewed these Frenchmen as heretics and pirates.

In 1568, a French sea-captain named Dominique de Gourgues sought revenge for the massacre. He captured Fort San Mateo (the renamed Fort Caroline) and hung the Spanish garrison with inscriptions stating they were hung "Not as Spaniards but as murderers." Aviles remained active in his colony until 1567, later making one final voyage to Florida before his death on September 17, 1574.

## Conquest of Mexico and Peru
Spanish expansion in the Americas was characterized by the actions of adventurers and a period of violence and loot. In Peru, an adventurer named Pizarro sailed from the Isthmus of Panama in 1530 with 168 Spaniards. Pizarro utilized native dissensions and treachery to seize the Inca of Peru, attempting to rule in the Inca's name. This process, mirrored by Cortez in Mexico, eventually led to the reduction of the Peruvian state into a Spanish province.

The early phase of Spanish presence was marked by the ill-treatment of natives and internal quarrels among the Spaniards, as the law and order of Spain were distant. Over time, this phase of conquest transitioned into government and settlement. Despite the initial disorder, a steady flow of silver and gold was sent from the Americas to the Spanish people and government.

## Territorial Shifts and Losses
Over time, Spanish holdings in the Americas were reduced as other powers expanded. Britain eventually acquired the Bahamas, Jamaica, and British Honduras from the Spaniards.

Outside of the Americas, Spanish influence varied by region. In Belgium, after the death of Archduke Albert in 1621, Spaniards increasingly dominated the country. A Spanish army permanently occupied important cities and fortresses, and the Spanish government utilized *Juntas* (special committees of Spaniards) to handle important matters, effectively sidelining native nobles. In Japan, the Spaniards were expelled in 1624. This expulsion was part of a broader move by the Yedo Bakufu to terminate foreign intercourse, which included the expulsion of the kith and kin of Spaniards and a ban on building ocean-going ships. However, the 287 people indicated by the edict were permitted to take their fortunes with them to Macao.
