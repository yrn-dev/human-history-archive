# Bidwell, Annals of an East Anglian Bank (1900) and Richardson, Coutts & Co.

The history of banking houses in Great Britain and Scotland is documented through various specialized historical accounts. Among these are the works *Annals of an East Anglian Bank* (1900) by Bidwell and the history of Coutts & Co., Bankers, Edinburgh and London, authored by Richardson.

## Coutts & Co. and the Bank of Scotland
The origins of the name Coutts in banking are linked to the establishment of a public bank in Scotland. In 1695, shortly after the Bank of England was established, the Scottish parliament passed an act to create this public bank. Thomas Coutts is among the first names associated with this institution. 

The terms for the establishment of the Bank of Scotland were more favorable than those of the Bank of England; specifically, the Scottish bank obtained the exclusive privilege of banking for twenty-one years without providing any consideration. Additionally, the Bank of Scotland was the only Scottish bank established by an act of parliament. Due to the kingship of William III or general national caution, the bank was prohibited under a heavy penalty from lending money to the king under any circumstances. Early in its operations, the directors began granting cash credit accounts and receiving deposits with interest, with records of the mode of keeping cash credit accounts dating as far back as 1729. The name Coutts remains commemorated in one of London's most substantial banks.

## The Legacy of Thomas Coutts
Thomas Coutts left behind immense wealth, which approached two million sterling. Following his death in 1821, this wealth was left to his second wife, the actress Henrietta Mellon (the duchess of St Albans). Through the will of the duchess, the wealth was inherited by Thomas Coutts' granddaughter, Angela Georgina Burdett-Coutts, when she was twenty-three years old.

Upon inheriting the fortune, Miss Burdett added the name Coutts to her own. She became a subject of public curiosity and received many marriage offers, but she remained single for many years to devote her riches to philanthropic work. In May 1871, she was created Baroness Burdett-Coutts of Highgate and Brookfield, Middlesex. In July 1872, she became the first woman admitted to the fellowship of the freedom of the city of London. At the age of sixty-seven in 1881, she married William Lehman Ashmead-Bartlett, an American who subsequently took his wife's name and entered the House of Commons as the member for Westminster in 1885. Baroness Burdett-Coutts died of bronchitis on December 30, 1906, at the age of ninety-two and was buried in Westminster Abbey.

## Context of Banking Histories
The works by Bidwell and Richardson are part of a broader collection of histories regarding banking houses. Other related histories include:
* L. H. Grindon's *Manchester Banks and Bankers* (1877).
* J. B. Martin's *"The Grasshopper" in Lombard Street* (1892).
* M. Phillips' work on banks and banking in North Yorkshire, Durham, and Northumberland (1894).
* C. H. Cave's *History of Banking in Bristol* (1899).
* H. T. Easton's *History of a Banking House* regarding Smith, Payne & Smiths (1903).
* J. Hughes' *Liverpool Banks and Bankers, 1760-1837* (1906).

Furthermore, the study of Scottish banking is supported by other authors, including W. H. Logan, Robert Somers, W. Mitchell, A. W. Kerr, and Boase, the latter of whom wrote *A Century of Banking in Dundee* (1867).
