# Bombay Presidency

The Bombay Presidency was a province of British India administered by a governor. Its territory extended from 13 deg. 53' to 28 deg. 45' N. and from 66 deg. 40' to 76 deg. 30' E. The province was bounded to the north by Rajputana, the Punjab, and Baluchistan; to the east by Hyderabad, the Central Provinces, and Indore; to the south by Mysore and Madras; and to the west by the Arabian Sea. While the settlement of Aden was included politically, the total area of the province (including Sind but excluding Aden) was 188,745 sq. m.

## Administrative Divisions and Population

The province was divided into twenty-six districts and four commissionerships. These four divisions were Sind, the southern or Carnatic, the central or Deccan, and the northern or Gujarat. The twenty-six districts comprised:
*   Ahmedabad, Bombay City, Broach, Kaira, Surat, Thana, and Panch Mahals.
*   Ahmednagar, Nasik, Poona, Satara, Sholapur, Khandesh (which was partitioned into two districts in 1906), and Kolaba.
*   Belgaum, Bijapur, Dharwar, Kanara, Ratnagiri.
*   Karachi, Hyderabad, Shikarpur, Thar and Parkar, and the Upper Sind Frontier.

As of 1901, the total population of the presidency was 25,468,209. Of this total, 18,515,587 residents lived in British territory, while 6,908,648 resided in native states. Regarding land distribution, 122,984 sq. m. were under British rule, and 65,761 sq. m. were under native rule. Within the limits of the presidency also lay the native state of Baroda, which maintained direct relations with the government of India, as well as the Portuguese settlements of Goa, Damaun, and Diu.

## Native States

The native states within the Bombay Presidency consisted of 353 separate units. These were administered by either the collectors of the districts where smaller states were located or by political agents. The states were organized into several chief groups:

*   **North Gujarat:** This group included Cambay, the Palanpur agency, the Mahi Kantha agency, the Rewa Kantha agency, the Kathiawar agency, and Cutch.
*   **South Gujarat:** This group comprised Sachin, Bansda, and Dharampur.
*   **North Konkan, Nasik, and Khandesh:** This group included Jawhar, Surgana, and the Khandesh political agency.
*   **South Konkan and Dharwar:** This group comprised Savanur, Sawantwari, and Janjira.
*   **Deccan Satara Jagirs:** This group included Daphlapur, Jath, Phaltan, Aundh, Bhor, and Akalkot.
*   **Southern Mahratta States:** This group included Kolhapur and other states.
*   **Sind:** This group included Khairpur.

## Notable Native States

### Baroda
Located within the Gujarat province of Bombay, Baroda was a native state in direct relations with the governor-general. It consisted of four isolated divisions: Nausari, Baroda, and Kadi (all in Gujarat proper), and Amreli with Okhamandal (located in the Kathiawar peninsula). The state covered a total area of 8,099 sq. m. In 1901, its population was 1,952,692. This figure represented a 19% decrease over the previous decade, attributed to the plague, epidemics of fever and cholera, and the famines of 1900-1901 and 1896-1897.

### Kuruntwad
Also known as Kurandvad, this native state was located in the Deccan division of Bombay and was part of the Southern Mahratta jagirs. It was created in 1772 via a grant from the peshwa. The state was divided in 1811 into two parts; one part, Shedbal, lapsed to the British government in 1857. In 1855, Kuruntwad was further divided into a junior and senior branch, with territories widely scattered among British districts and other native states. 

The senior branch had an area of 185 sq. m., a population of 42,474 (1901), and revenue of £13,000. The junior branch had an area of 114 sq. m., a population of 34,003 (1901), and revenue of £9,000. The two branches paid a joint tribute of £640. The chiefs of Kuruntwad were of the Patwardhan family and were Brahmans by caste. The town of Kuruntwad, where both branches resided, was situated on the right bank of the Panchganga river near its junction with the Kistna, with a population of 10,451 in 1901.

## Imperial Context

The Bombay Presidency was one of nine great provinces of the Indian empire. While the empire was overseen by the viceroy in council, the Bombay and Madras provinces were presided over by governors. For legislative purposes, the governor-general's council had the power, under certain restrictions, to make laws for native Indian subjects of the crown, British subjects in native states, and British India. In England, the administration of the empire was managed by a secretary of state for India and a council of at least ten members, who controlled the expenditure of revenues.
