# Longitudinal Section of the Sporogonium

In botanical studies of bryophytes, the longitudinal section of a sporogonium provides a detailed view of the reproductive structures as they develop and mature. The sporogonium is the spore-bearing organ that develops from a fertilized ovum, and its structure varies across different species of liverworts and mosses.

## Sporogonium in Pellia epiphylla
In the liverwort *Pellia epiphylla*, the sporogonium is fully developed by winter. A longitudinal section of an almost mature sporogonium reveals that it is attached to the thallus. The primary components visible in this section include:
*   **The Capsule (caps):** The upper portion of the sporogonium.
*   **The Seta (s):** The stalk supporting the capsule.
*   **The Foot (f):** The basal part of the sporogonium.
*   **The Calyptra (cal):** A protective covering.
*   **The Involucre (in):** A scale-like structure that protects the archegonia.

The development of the *Pellia* sporogonium begins with an embryo consisting of tiers of cells. The terminal tier gives rise to the capsule, where the first divisions separate the capsule wall from the archesporial cells destined to produce spores. The tiers below the terminal one give rise to the foot and the seta.

## Sporogonium in Mosses (Musci)
The structure of the sporogonium in mosses varies by genus, though they generally follow a similar life-history pattern.

### Funaria hygrometrica
In *Funaria hygrometrica*, the sporogonium initially grows with uniform thickness. Over time, the upper region increases in diameter to form the capsule, while the lower portion develops into a long seta and a foot, the latter of which is embedded in the end of the stem. A median longitudinal section of the capsule reveals several specialized structures:
*   **The Apophysis:** The region where the seta gradually widens at the base of the capsule.
*   **The Columella (c):** A central structure within the capsule.
*   **The Spore-sac (s):** The area containing the archesporium.
*   **The Operculum (d):** The lid of the capsule.
*   **The Peristome (p) and Annulus (a):** Structures associated with the capsule opening.
*   **Air-space (h):** A gap located between the wall of the capsule and the spore-sac.

During growth, the archegonial wall is broken through; the basal portion remains as a tubular sheath around the lower seta, while the upper part, including the neck, is carried up on the capsule as the calyptra.

### Sphagnum
In the genus *Sphagnum* (bog-mosses), the mature sporogonium differs significantly from *Funaria*. It consists of a wide foot separated from a globular capsule by a constriction. Unlike other mosses, there is no distinct seta. Instead, the capsule is raised on a leafless outgrowth of the branch known as a pseudopodium. A longitudinal section of the *Sphagnum* capsule shows a short, wide columella over which a dome-shaped spore-sac extends. Notably, there is no air-space between the spore-sac and the capsule wall.

## Comparative Development and Variations
The development of the sporogonium involves various protective and supportive tissues across different species:

*   **Calyptra and Archegonial Walls:** In many mosses, the sporogonium is initially enclosed in the archegonial wall. As it develops, the calyptra is formed from this wall. In some stages, the sporogonium remains enclosed in the calyptra even as it nears maturity, as seen in *Cephalozia bicuspidata*.
*   **Attachment and Support:** While *Funaria* uses a seta and foot embedded in the stem, *Sphagnum* utilizes a pseudopodium. In other instances, the sporogonium is borne on a pseudopodium with a foot and capsule, sometimes accompanied by a ruptured calyptra and an operculum, as seen in *S. squarrosum*.
*   **Liverwort Diversity:** Among liverworts, the *Riccia* genus possesses the simplest sporogonia, consisting only of a single layer of cells forming a wall around the spores. In contrast, the Anthocerotales possess a sterile columella and are capable of continued growth and spore production.
