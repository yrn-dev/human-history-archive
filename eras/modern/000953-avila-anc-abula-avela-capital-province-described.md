# AVILA

Avila (anciently *Abula* or *Avela*) is the capital of the province of Avila, located in central Spain. The city is situated on the right bank of the river Adaja, 54 miles west by north of Madrid.

## Geography and Urban Layout
The city is constructed upon the flat summit of a rocky hill that rises abruptly from a brown, arid, and treeless table-land. This surrounding wilderness is characterized by immense grey boulders and is enclosed by lofty mountains. 

Avila is enclosed by ancient walls made of brown granite and surmounted by a breastwork. These fortifications feature nine gateways and eighty-six towers and remain in excellent repair, although a significant portion of the city now extends beyond the circuit of these walls.

## Religious and Historic Architecture
Avila serves as the seat of a bishop and contains several ecclesiastical buildings of high interest. The Gothic cathedral, which tradition dates to 1107—though it is likely the work of the 13th or 14th century—resembles a fortress with its two solid towers and embattled walls. Notable items within the cathedral include various paintings and sculptures, as well as a silver pyx created by Juan de Arphe in 1571.

The city features several Romanesque churches from the 15th century, including Santo Tomas, San Segundo, San Pedro, and San Vicente; the latter two may contain sections dating back to the 12th century. In the church of Santo Tomas, there is a marble monument carved by the 15th-century Florentine sculptor Domenico Fancelli over the tomb of Prince John (d. 1497), the only son of Isabella and Ferdinand. Additionally, the church and convent of Santa Teresa mark the presumed birthplace of the saint (c. 1515-1582).

Other notable structures include an old Moorish castle (*alcazar*) that has been used as barracks.

## Education and Public Institutions
The city houses a military academy, an infirmary, a foundling hospital, and training schools for teachers of both sexes. Between 1482 and 1807, Avila was also the seat of a university.

## Economy and Trade
Avila maintains a considerable trade in various goods, including pottery, leather, hats, linen, cotton goods, and agricultural products.

## The Province of Avila
The province of Avila is a modern division of the kingdom of Old Castile, formed in 1833. It is bounded by Madrid and Segovia to the east, Valladolid to the north, Salamanca to the west, and Caceres and Toledo to the south. As of 1900, the province had an area of 2,570 square miles and a population of 200,457.

The province is divided into two distinct sections:
*   **Northern Portion:** Generally level with indifferent soil (strong and marly in some areas, rocky in the valleys of the Sierra de Avila). The climate fluctuates between extreme summer heat and severe winter cold, and the population is primarily agricultural.
*   **Southern Division:** A mass of rugged granitic sierras with well-watered, sheltered valleys and rich vegetation. Stock-breeding is a primary industry here.

The province's geography is defined by major mountain chains, including the Guadarrama (separating Avila from Madrid), the Paramera and Sierra de Avila, and the Sierra de Gredos along the southern frontier, where peaks reach 6,000 to 8,000 feet. The Paramera ridges are covered in forests of firs, oaks, and beeches. The primary rivers are the Tormes, Adaja, Trabancos, Tietar, and Alberche.

The province's mineral resources include coal, lead, iron, copper, and silver, though mining was practically abandoned by the beginning of the 20th century. Jasper and fine marble quarries are located in the district of Arenas.

The provincial economy is heavily reliant on the wool of merino sheep, which previously generated immense revenue. Other agricultural productions include grapes, chestnuts, olives, and the keeping of silk-worms. Local manufactures include soap, lime, paper, cloth, and copper utensils.

## Infrastructure and Society
Avila is connected by rail to Madrid, Valladolid, and Salamanca. While the main roads are considered fairly good, communication in many other parts of the province is defective. 

The province experienced a decline in population and wealth during the 19th and 18th centuries. This was attributed to strict laws of mortmain and entail, as well as oppressive feudal and manorial rights. Despite this, elementary education is comparatively good, with more than two-thirds of the population being able to read and write, and the ratio of crime is proportionately low.
