# Charles Badham

## Early Life and Education
Charles Badham was an English scholar born on July 18, 1813, in Ludlow, Shropshire. He was the son of Charles Badham, a classical scholar and translator of Juvenal who served as the regius professor of physic at Glasgow. His mother was a cousin of the poet Thomas Campbell. 

Badham's education began abroad; at approximately seven years of age, he was sent to Switzerland, where he became a pupil of Pestalozzi. He later returned to England to attend Eton. In 1830, he was elected to a scholarship at Wadham College, Oxford. He graduated in 1837, though he obtained only a third class in classics in 1836. This result may have been the consequence of his dislike for the study methods fashionable at Oxford during a period when classical scholarship was in an unsatisfactory state.

## Academic Travels and Qualifications
Following his degree, Badham traveled to Italy to study ancient manuscripts, focusing specifically on those held in the Vatican library. During this period, he established a lifelong friendship with G. C. Cobet. He also spent time in Germany. Upon returning to England, he was incorporated as an M.A. at Peterhouse, Cambridge, in 1847. He later earned a D.D. from Cambridge in 1852. In 1860, the university of Leiden awarded him an honorary degree of doctor of letters.

## Professional Career
Badham entered holy orders and pursued a career in education. He served as the headmaster of Louth grammar school in Lincolnshire from 1851 to 1854, and subsequently became the headmaster of the Edgbaston proprietary school near Birmingham. 

In 1866, Badham left England to move to Australia, where he took the professorship of classics and logic at Sydney University. He held this academic position until his death on February 26, 1884.

## Reputation and Scholarship
Despite his professional trajectory, Badham was not a professional success. This is attributed to his hasty temper and his dislike of anything that approached disingenuousness, which may have hindered his advancement. Nevertheless, his eminence as a textual critic and scholar was recognized across Europe. His classical attainments were acknowledged by several famous critics, including Tischendorf, A. Ritschl, J. A. F. Meineke, F. W. Schneidewin, W. Dindorf, Ludwig Preller, and G. C. Cobet. Despite this international reputation, he made a comparatively small mark in his native country.

## Personal Life
Charles Badham was married twice.
