# Army Bills of 1902

## Introduction and Legislative Intent
In the autumn of 1902, the governments of Austria and Hungary introduced bills to their respective parliaments to increase the annual contingents of recruits by 21,000 men. These measures were initiated at the instance of the crown and developed in agreement with the Austrian and Hungarian ministers for national defence, as well as the joint minister for war. 

The proposed increase was divided by necessity: 16,700 men were intended for the joint army, while the remaining recruits were designated for the national defence troops, specifically the *honved* in Hungary and the *Landwehr* in Austria. Under this plan, Austria would have contributed approximately 14,500 men, and Hungary would have contributed some 6,500 men.

## The Supernumerary Controversy
The passage of these Army bills was complicated by a decision made by military authorities to detain several thousand supernumerary recruits in barracks. These individuals were recruits liable for military service but exceeded the legal annual enrollment limit of 103,000. 

The military authorities took this high-handed step to avoid the delays and expenses associated with summoning these supernumeraries back to the colours once the parliaments had sanctioned the bills. However, this action was resented by the Hungarian Chamber, which maintained a lively sense of its prerogatives.

## Political Obstruction in Hungary
The grievance over the supernumeraries was utilized by the Opposition, which was led by Francis Kossuth (the eldest son of Louis Kossuth) and consisted primarily of the independence party. The Opposition decided to obstruct ministerial measures as a means of forcing the discharge of the supernumeraries.

As a result of this obstruction, the estimates could not be sanctioned. Although Kossuth granted the Szell cabinet a vote on account for the first four months of 1903, the Government remained at the mercy of the Opposition. By the end of 1902, the supernumeraries were discharged, but this action failed to calm the Opposition.

## Nationalist Demands and Outcome
Following the discharge of the recruits, the Opposition shifted its demands. They insisted that the Army bills be withdrawn entirely. Alternatively, they argued that if the bills were adopted, they must be counterbalanced by concessions to Magyar nationalist feeling. These requested concessions were intended to:
* Promote the use of the Magyar language within the Hungarian portion of the army.
* Increase the Magyar character of Hungarian regiments, as few of these were purely Magyar.

The premier, Szell, advised the military authorities and the crown to make timely concessions to resolve the deadlock, but his advice was not followed. Consequently, Szell was obliged to reject the demands of the Opposition.

## Context of Austro-Hungarian Military Relations
The struggle over the 1902 bills followed a pattern of increasing nationalist tension. Earlier Army bills in 1889, which sought to increase the peace footing of the joint Austro-Hungarian army, had also been passed with difficulty despite the efforts of Count Julius Andrassy the Elder and Koloman Tisza. 

During that earlier period, demands for the "Magyarization" of the joint army had gained significant traction in public opinion. This had led Count Andrassy to solemnly warn the country regarding the obligations of the Compact of 1867 and the dangers of nationalist Chauvinism.
