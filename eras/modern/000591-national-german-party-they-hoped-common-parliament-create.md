# National German Party

The National German party was a political entity in Austria that evolved from the Liberal German party. It served as a primary vehicle for the party of centralization, advocating for the maintenance of German dominance within the monarchy.

## Political Objectives and Ideology
The National German party sought to utilize a common parliament to foster a sense of common Austrian nationality. A central pillar of their strategy was the promotion of the German language through the establishment of German schools. The party viewed the German schoolmaster as an "apostle of German culture" and aimed for a future where a shared Austrian national identity would obscure the national feelings of the Slavs. In their vision, Slavonic idioms would survive only as local dialects of the peasantry, and the various territories would function merely as provinces of a centralized, united state.

The party maintained that German ascendancy was the only method of preserving the unity of the monarchy. They asserted that German was the sole language capable of allowing different races to communicate and insisted it remain the language of the parliament, the civil service, and the army. Furthermore, they emphasized Austria's historic mission to bring German culture to the "half-civilized races of the east," demanding that universities and higher schools remain German and that elementary schools be Germanized wherever possible.

## Defense of German Hegemony
The party strongly opposed any grants of self-government to the territories, arguing that such measures would restrict the use of the German language and diminish German influence. They were particularly concerned with regions like Bohemia, where they believed full self-government would result in Germans becoming the "subject race." 

This position was driven by a refusal to resign the ruling position Germans had held for the previous three hundred years, especially at a time when the national power of non-Austrian Germans was increasing and Germans were becoming the leading race in Europe. Despite their goals, the total German population constituted less than one-third of the whole population, meaning the maintenance of their rule required significant political ability. This effort was further complicated by internal divisions, as the Feudal party and the clergy opposed them.

## Evolution and Racial Shift
Beginning around 1870, the character of the German opposition shifted to become more avowedly racial, placing the defense of German nationality at the forefront of its program. This movement originated at the University of Vienna with the formation of a *Leseverein* (reading club) for German students, which was eventually suppressed. 

A key representative of this movement in parliament was Herr von Schoenerer, who stated that Germans looked forward to union with the German empire. This faction was heavily influenced by figures outside Austria; they viewed Bismarck as their national hero and celebrated the anniversary of Sedan as a political festival. For these individuals, approximation to Germany was more desirable than the maintenance of Austria.

## Parliamentary Activity and Alliances
The German political landscape was fragmented into several groups, including the German Nationalists, German Populists, German Radicals, and German Progressives. In 1881, the opposition parties joined in a club called the United Left, demanding that German be expressly recognized as the official language. However, younger, more ardent members often clashed with older constitutional leaders, whom they viewed as insufficiently decisive in self-defense.

In response to "Czech aggressiveness," various German parties—including the Christian Socialists, German Populists, German Progressives, and Constitutional Landed Proprietors—formed a supreme committee of four members and a joint executive committee to protect German racial interests.

The party also engaged in significant parliamentary conflict regarding the constitution. They specifically targeted Paragraph 14 of the Constitutional law of 1867, which allowed the emperor to proclaim orders in cases of pressing necessity without the assent of the Reichsrath. The Germans contended that the application of this clause to the *Ausgleich* was invalid and demanded its repeal.
