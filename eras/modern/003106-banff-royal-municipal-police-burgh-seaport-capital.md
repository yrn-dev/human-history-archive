# BANFF

Banff is a seaport and the capital of Banffshire, Scotland. It is designated as a royal, municipal, and police burgh. As of 1901, the population of the town was 7,161.

## Geography and Transport
The town is situated on high ground on the left bank of the mouth of the Deveron. It is located 50 miles northwest of Aberdeen via the Great North of Scotland railway. Transport links include a railway station at Bridge of Banff, which communicates with Aberdeen via Inveramsay, and a second station located at the harbour, which provides communication with Keith and Portsoy.

## History and Governance
Banff is a place of great antiquity. Its first charter was granted in 1163 by Malcolm IV. The town received further privileges in 1324 from Robert Bruce and in 1372 from Robert II. 

The burgh is governed by a council and a provost. In parliamentary matters, Banff unites with several other burghs—including Macduff, Cullen, Elgin, Inverurie, Kintore, and Peterhead—to return one member to parliament. This group is known as the Elgin burghs.

## Public Buildings and Landmarks
The town's architecture includes several notable edifices:
* **Banff Castle:** The modern castle, belonging to the earl of Seafield, was built on the site of an old castle on the hill by the sea. The older structure was the birthplace of Archbishop Sharp, though scarcely a trace of it remains.
* **Town Hall:** A public building surmounted by a spire reaching 100 feet in height.
* **The Academy:** A modern structure built in the Grecian style. It features an extensive museum that contains examples of the early mechanical genius of the astronomer James Ferguson. This museum was originally part of the defunct Banff Institution before being taken over by the town council; it was curated for a few years by Thomas Edward, known as the "working naturalist."
* **Other Edifices:** The town contains the county buildings, a masonic hall of tasteful design, and Chalmers hospital, which was founded by Alexander Chalmers of Clunie, a shipowner and merchant from the town.

## Economy and Trade
The economy of Banff is supported by both the fishing trade and various manufactures. Principal manufacturing industries include the production of leather, woollens, rope, and sails. The town also hosts timber-yards, brick-yards, iron foundries, distilleries, and breweries, as well as some ship-building.

Trade is conducted through the seaport, with imports consisting primarily of timber and coal. Exports mainly include potatoes, dairy produce, fish, cattle, and grain.

## Local Benefactions
The town benefits from the "Cassie Gift," which originated from a bequest of £20,000 left to the poor of Banff by Alexander Cassie of London, a native of the town. The interest from this bequest is divided twice a year.
