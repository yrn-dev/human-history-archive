# The Annexation of Walcheren and the Fall of Louis Bonaparte

## Appointment and Early Reign of Louis
Following the victory of Austerlitz on December 2, 1805, Napoleon began planning a ring of allied states to surround the French empire. As part of this strategy, Napoleon designated his brother, Louis, for the throne of Holland, proclaiming him king on June 6, 1806. Louis had previously been supervised in his education by Napoleon and had served as his aide-de-camp in Egypt from 1798 to 1799.

From the beginning of Louis's reign, the emperor expressed dissatisfaction with him, reproaching Louis for courting popularity too much and being too lenient with his subjects. The relationship between the two brothers was further strained by a violent jealousy Louis felt toward his wife, Hortense.

## Escalation of Conflict
The rupture between Napoleon and Louis became open due to the increasing rigour of the continental system. In 1808, Napoleon offered Louis the vacant throne of Spain, but Louis refused the offer, and the position was given to Joseph instead.

By the latter part of 1809, Napoleon had virtually resolved to annex Holland. This decision was driven by a desire to stop the secret trade that the Dutch were conducting with England. In an attempt to resolve these tensions and procure a divorce from Hortense, Louis traveled to Paris at the close of the year, but he failed to achieve either goal.

## The Annexation of Walcheren
In January 1810, Napoleon annexed the island of Walcheren. The emperor justified this action by alleging that Louis had failed to do his share in defending French interests during the British Walcheren expedition of 1809. Concurrent with this annexation, French troops occupied Bergen-op-Zoom and Breda. While Louis acquiesced to these points in dispute, this submission only served to postpone the eventual crisis.

## Final Annexation of Holland
Following the collapse of negotiations with Great Britain in the spring of 1810, Napoleon continued to pressure Louis. The emperor eventually sent French troops against the Dutch capital. Despairing of any ability to offer resistance, Louis fled his kingdom and eventually settled at Toplitz in Bohemia. On July 9, 1810, Napoleon formally annexed Holland to the French empire.

## Later Life of Louis
Louis spent the remainder of his life separated from his wife. In 1815, he gained custody of his elder son. He resided primarily in Rome, where he focused on philosophic and literary studies and the fortunes of his sons. He found pleasure in his sons' devotion to the democratic and national cause in Italy between 1830 and 1831, though this was overshadowed by the death of his elder son, Napoleon Louis, during the spring campaign. Louis died in 1846.
