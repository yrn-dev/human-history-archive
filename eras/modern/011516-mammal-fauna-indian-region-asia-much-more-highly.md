# Mammal Fauna of the Indian Region of Asia

The Indian region of Asia is characterized by a mammal fauna that is much more highly developed than that of the Palaearctic region. Geographically, this region encompasses Asia south of the Himalayas and the Yang-tse-Kiang, including the islands of Sumatra, Java, Borneo, and the Philippines. Its boundaries are defined by the Himalayas to the north and Wallace's line to the east, beyond which the Australian region begins.

## Primates and Small Mammals
The order Quadrumana is represented in the Indian region by several peculiar genera, including *Simia*, *Hylobates*, and *Semnopithecus*. Additionally, two peculiar forms belonging to the Lemurine group are found here. 

Among the Insectivora, the region contains very peculiar forms such as *Tupaia* and *Gymnura*. The bats of the region are well represented in both their frugivorous and insectivorous divisions. The order of Rodents is also prominent, featuring numerous squirrels and porcupines of two different genera. Furthermore, the region hosts representatives of the order Edentata in the form of several species of the scaly ant-eater, *Manis*.

## Carnivora and Proboscidea
The Carnivora are numerous in the Indian region. It is considered the true home of the tiger, although the animal has migrated far north into the Palaearctic division of Asia. Other characteristic members of the Carnivora include various ichneumons, civets, and the benturong (*Arctictis*). There are also two species of bears that are restricted specifically to the Indian region.

The Indian region is the home of the Indian elephant, which is one of only two remaining representatives of the order Proboscidea.

## Ungulates and Ruminants
The Ungulates in this region include one species of tapir and four species of rhinoceros, as well as several peculiar forms of the swine family. The Bovidae, or hollow-horned ruminants, are represented by several genera of antelopes and species of true *Bos*, specifically *B. bubalus*, *B. frontalis*, and *B. sondaicus*. 

Deer are numerous in the region, and the group of chevrotains (*Tragulus*) is noted as being characteristic of the Indian region.

## Marine Mammals
The marine orders of Cetacea and Sirenia are represented in the region. The Dugong (*Halicore*) is found exclusively in the Indian Ocean. Additionally, a dolphin known as *Platanista* is peculiar to the Ganges river, where it ascends to a great distance from the sea.

## Regional Distinctions and Boundaries
The zoological divisions of Asia align closely with botanical provinces. While the south-eastern portion forms the Indian region, the northern portion of Asia (extending south to the Himalaya) is not zoologically distinct from Europe; together with northern Africa, these form the Palaearctic region. 

In contrast to the Indian region, the extreme south-west of the continent—comprising southern Persia, Palestine, and Arabia, and reaching to Sind and Baluchistan—belongs to the Ethiopian region. This Ethiopian fauna is subordinate in Asia, intruding only into the south-western corner and desert districts. Ethiopian types that extend into this area include the hunting leopard and the lion, as well as other desert-loving forms and various species of jerboa.

The eastern boundary is marked by Wallace's line. Marsupials are found in Celebes and the Molucca Islands but are entirely absent from the Indian region and the islands of Java and Borneo. Similarly, Ruminants, Insectivora, Carnivora, and Quadrumana abound in the western Archipelago but are absent from Celebes and the Moluccas.
