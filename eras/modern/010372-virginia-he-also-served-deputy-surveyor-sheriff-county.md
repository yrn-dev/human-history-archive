# Virginia and Its Historical Territories

## Territorial Claims and Early Settlement
Virginia claimed the Kentucky country as part of the territory granted to it by its 1609 charter. During the mid-18th century, this region was also claimed by the Six Nations (Iroquois) as western conquests and by the Cherokees as hunting grounds. At the time, the area was primarily inhabited by a small tribe of Shawnees in the north and a few Chickasaws near the Mississippi river.

Early exploration of the region included a 1750 mission by Dr. Thomas Walker, an agent and surveyor for the Loyal Land Company, who entered via the Cumberland Gap but remained in the mountain region. In 1751, Christopher Gist explored westward from the mouth of the Scioto river for the Ohio Company. In 1752, an Indian trader named John Finley reached the site of Louisville by canoe.

## The Role of Daniel Boone
Daniel Boone, an American pioneer of English descent born in Pennsylvania in 1734, was attracted to the Kentucky region by the descriptions provided by John Finley. Boone first visited the area in 1767 and later spent two years (1769–1771) roaming the region, collecting deer, beaver, and bear skins while encountering roving bands of Indians.

In 1775, Boone led a party of settlers to found Boonesborough. His experiences in the region included being captured by Shawnees on February 7, 1778, and being adopted into their tribe. After being taken to Detroit, he escaped and traveled 160 miles in four days to warn Boonesborough of an impending attack, which took place from September 8 to 17. Boone also participated in the "Battle of Blue Licks" in 1782.

## Governance and Administration
During the period when Kentucky was part of Virginia, Daniel Boone represented the settlers in the Virginia legislature. He also held several administrative roles in Fayette county—one of the three counties Kentucky was divided into at the time—serving as sheriff, deputy surveyor, and county lieutenant.

In other parts of Virginia, such as Hanover county, the village of Ashland was settled in 1845 and incorporated in 1856. Ashland is the site of Randolph-Macon College, which opened in 1831 near Boydton in Mecklenburg county before moving to Ashland in 1868. The college is part of a system that includes the Randolph-Macon Institute in Danville, Randolph-Macon Woman's College in Lynchburg, and Randolph-Macon Academies in Front Royal and Bedford City.

## Regional Geography and Urban Centers
The city of Bristol is situated across the boundary of Washington county, Virginia, and Sullivan county, Tennessee. The Tennessee-Virginia boundary line runs through the city's principal street. The Virginia portion of the city was known as Goodson before it was chartered as a city in 1890. Bristol serves as a railway center and a distributing point for coke, coal, and iron, benefiting from nearby mineral deposits in North Carolina, Kentucky, West Virginia, Tennessee, and Virginia.

## Military Conflict and Treaties
Virginia troops were involved in the battle of Point Pleasant (located in present-day West Virginia). Following the defeat of the Ohio Indians in October 1774, a treaty was signed in which the Indians surrendered their claims to the lands south of the Ohio river.
