# Glogau

Glogau is a city located in Silesia. It is noted in historical records as the birthplace of the German philologist and philosopher Georg Gustav Fulleborn, who was born there on March 2, 1769.

## Military History: The Silesian Campaign of 1741

During the Silesian Campaign of 1741, Glogau was a site of significant military action. In February of that year, the Austrians had assembled a field army under the command of Count Neipperg (1684–1774) with the objective of reconquering Silesia. While Austrian forces continued to hold out in Brieg and Neisse, Glogau was targeted by Prussian forces.

On the night of March 9, Glogau was stormed by the Prussians. The operation was led by Prince Leopold (the younger) of Anhalt-Dessau. The task was executed within one hour and was characterized by a mathematical precision that earned universal admiration.

## Aftermath of the Storming of Glogau

Following the fall of Glogau, the strategic situation shifted as the Austrian army in Moravia entered the field. At this time, the cantonments of Frederick were dispersed throughout Upper Silesia. Collecting the army proved to be a task of great difficulty due to the ground being deep in snow.

Before the Prussian army could be fully collected, Neisse was relieved. Count Neipperg marched from Neisse toward Brieg, effectively cutting the Prussians off from their own country. This period of slow maneuvering between Brieg and Neisse eventually culminated in the battle of Mollwitz on April 10, 1741. This engagement marked the first pitched battle fought by Frederick and his army.

The battle of Mollwitz began with the speedy routing of the Prussian right wing of cavalry. However, the Prussian infantry's tenacity and discipline retrieved the day. Despite the efforts of Neipperg and his officers, the Austrian infantry gradually melted away before Prussian volleys, and the Austrian cavalry was shattered during repeated attempts to ride the infantry down. 

Frederick had participated in the cavalry melee but was persuaded by Field Marshal Schwerin to ride away when the battle appeared lost. Schwerin remained behind to secure the victory, while Frederick narrowly avoided capture by wandering Austrian hussars. The immediate result of this victory was that the king secured Brieg, forcing Neipperg to fall back to Neisse.

## Geopolitical Impact

The events surrounding the campaign and the battle of Mollwitz signaled to Europe the emergence of a new military power. Consequently, France dispatched Marshal Belleisle to Frederick's camp to negotiate an alliance. This transition shifted the nature of the conflict, as the "Silesian adventure" evolved into the broader War of the Austrian Succession.

## Notable Figures

Glogau is associated with Georg Gustav Fulleborn (1769–1803), a miscellaneous writer, philologist, and philosopher. Educated at the University of Halle, Fulleborn earned a doctorate of philosophy for his thesis *De Xenophane, Zenone et Gorgia*. He served as a professor of classics at Breslau after taking diaconal orders in 1791. 

Fulleborn's scholarly contributions include:
* **Philosophy:** Annotations to Garve's translation of Aristotle's *Politics* and collaborations on the *Beitrage zur Geschichte der Philosophie*.
* **Philology:** The *Encyclopaedia philologica sive primae lineae Isagoges in antiquorum studia*, *Kurze Theorie des lateinischen Stils*, and *Leitfaden der Rhetorik*, as well as an annotated edition of the *Satires* of Persius.
* **Popular Literature:** Under the pseudonym "Edelwald Justus," he published collections of popular tales, including *Bunte Blatter*, *Kleine Schriften zur Unterhaltung*, and *Nebenstunden*.
