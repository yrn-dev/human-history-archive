# Amino-azo-benzene

Amino-azo-benzene is a chemical compound with the formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{.N}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_4\text{NH}_2$. It is characterized by its physical form, crystallizing as yellow needles or plates, and possesses a melting point of 126°C.

## Chemical Constitution and Preparation
The constitution of amino-azo-benzene is determined by its reaction to specific reduction processes. It can be prepared by the reduction of nitro-azo-benzene using ammonium sulphide. Furthermore, when it undergoes reduction with stannous chloride, it yields meta-phenylene diamine and aniline.

## Amino-azo Compounds
Amino-azo-benzene belongs to a broader class of amino-azo compounds. These substances are typically red or yellowish brown in color. The specific shade of the compound is influenced by its chemical structure: the introduction of alkylated amino groups results in redder shades, while the presence of additional amino groups leads to browner shades. These compounds generally crystallize well and are readily reduced. When amino-azo compounds are heated with aniline hydrochloride and aniline, they produce indulines.

Several other related amino-azo-benzene derivatives exist:
*   **Diamino-azo-benzene (chrysoidine):** With the formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{.N}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NH}_2)_2$, it was first prepared by O. Witt in 1877. It is obtained by coupling meta-phenylene diamine with phenyl diazonium chloride, crystallizing in red octahedra, and is used to dye wool and silk yellow.
*   **Triamino-azo-benzene:** Also known as vesuvine, phenylene brown, Manchester brown, or Bismarck brown ($\text{NH}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_4\text{.N}_2\text{.C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NH}_2)_2$), it is prepared via the action of nitrous acid on meta-phenylene diamine. It forms brown crystals that are soluble in hot water and can dye mordanted cotton a dark brown.

Alkylated amino-azo-benzenes are also known; these are formed by coupling diazonium salts with alkylated amines, provided there is a free para position relative to the amino group. Research by A. Merz and H. Goldschmidt indicates that the velocity of the formation of these compounds depends on the nature of the reagents rather than the concentration. In the coupling of diazobenzene sulphonic acid with the hydrochloride of a tertiary amine, the reaction occurs most rapidly with the weakest acid.

## Related Azo and Diazo Compounds
Amino-azo-benzene is part of a wider family of nitrogen-based aromatic compounds:
*   **Azo-benzene:** Discovered by E. Mitscherlich in 1834 ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:NC}_6\text{H}_5$), it crystallizes in orange red plates and melts at 68°C.
*   **Diazo-amines:** These crystalline solids, usually yellow, are obtained through the action of primary amines on diazonium salts or the action of nitrosamines on primary amines. They are readily converted into amino-azo compounds. Diazoamino benzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N:N.NHC}_6\text{H}_5$) crystallizes in yellow laminae and melts at 96°C.
*   **Azoxy Compounds:** These are typically red or yellow crystalline solids. Azoxybenzene ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{N})_2\text{O}$ crystallizes in yellow needles and melts at 36°C.
*   **Mixed Azo Compounds:** These unite an azo group with one aromatic radical and one aliphatic radical. Examples include benzene-azo-methane and benzene-azo-ethane, both of which are yellow oils.

## Theory of Color and Dyestuffs
The coloring properties of substances like amino-azo-benzene are linked to their chemical constitution. According to O.N. Witt, colorless hydrocarbons such as benzene become colored through the introduction of "chromophorous groups" (color-bearing groups), such as $-\text{N:N}-$ or $\text{NO}_2$. Compounds containing these groups are called chromogens. While chromogens are not dyestuffs themselves, they can become dyestuffs through the addition of "auxochromous groups," such as $\text{NH}_2$ or $\text{OH}$. Generally, coloring matters with the simplest constitutions are yellow, and as the molecular weight increases, the color shifts toward orange, red, violet, and blue.
