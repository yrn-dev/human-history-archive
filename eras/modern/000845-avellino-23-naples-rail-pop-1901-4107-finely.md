# Avellino

Avellino is a city and episcopal see located in the Campania region of Italy. It serves as the capital of the province of Avellino and is situated at the foot of Monte Vergine, at an elevation of 1,150 feet above sea level. Geographically, it is positioned 28 miles direct and 59 miles by rail east-north-east of Naples. As of 1901, the population of the city was 23,760.

## History and Architecture
The city's name is derived from the ancient Abellinum. Ruins of this ancient site are located 2-1/2 miles to the north-east, situated close to the village of Atripalda. These ruins include an amphitheatre constructed in *opus reticulatum* and remains of city walls, dating to the early imperial period. During this era, Abellinum is believed to have been the chief place of a tribe that included the *Abellinates cognominati Marsi* among the Apulians and the *Abellinates cognomine Protropi* among the Hirpini.

In the city of Avellino itself, there are ruins of a castle built during the 9th or 10th century. This site is historically significant as the location where the antipope Anacletus II crowned Count Roger II as the king of Apulia and Sicily.

## Geography and Transport
Avellino is a key railway junction, providing lines to Rocchetta S. Antonio and Benevento. In the broader regional context, the province of Avellino borders the province of Potenza (also known as Basilicata). Transport between these areas is facilitated by a railway line from Avellino that runs through the province of Potenza as far as Palazzo S. Gervasio before reaching the junction at Rocchetta S. Antonio.

## Related Localities
Within the province of Avellino lies the city of Avella (ancient *Abella*), located 23 miles north-east of Naples by rail, with a population of 4,107 in 1901. Avella is situated in fertile territory and was renowned in Roman times for its fruit and nuts (*nuces Abellanae*). 

Approximately 2 miles north-east of Avella is Avella Vecchia, the site of the ancient Abella, which the ancients regarded as a Chalcidian colony. This site contains an amphitheatre and remains of citadel walls. It was located on the road from Abellinum to Nola, and may have been joined by a branch from Suessula. Historical records from the 2nd century B.C. include an important Oscan inscription regarding a treaty with Nola concerning a joint temple of Hercules. By the time of the early empire, Avella had become a colony, a status it may have held since the time of Sulla.
