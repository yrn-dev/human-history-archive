# Bombay

## Geography and Maritime Importance
Bombay is located within the Bombay Presidency, a region consisting of a long strip of land along the Indian Ocean extending from the north of Mysore to the south of the Punjab. The coastline of this region is generally regular and rock-bound, making access difficult. While several bays serve as fairweather ports for coasting trade, Bombay is one of only four harbours—alongside Karwar, Marmagoa, and Karachi-in-Sind—that are sufficiently land-locked to protect shipping during the prevalence of the south-west monsoon.

## Global Connectivity and Trade Routes
As a primary hub within the British Empire, Bombay serves as a critical point in the geographical distribution of the empire's five principal divisions. The distance from a British port to Bombay via the Mediterranean is 6,272 nautical miles. In the broader network of oceanic travel, the distance from the Cape of Good Hope to Bombay is 4,610 nautical miles, and the distance from Bombay to Melbourne is 5,630 nautical miles.

## Regional Trade and Persian Gulf Relations
Bombay maintains significant commercial links with the ports of the Persian Gulf. The British Indian Steam Navigation Company operates weekly vessel services between Bombay and Basra, with Bushire serving as the chief station in the Gulf for this route.

The city is a destination for various goods transiting through Persian ports. For example, at Bander Lingah, the annual value of imports and exports between 1890 and 1905 averaged approximately £800,000; nearly half of this value consisted of pearls passing in transit from fisheries on the Arab coast to Bombay.
