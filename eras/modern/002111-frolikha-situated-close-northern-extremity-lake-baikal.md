# Lake Frolikha

Lake Frolikha is a small body of water located near the northern extremity of Lake Baikal in East Siberia. It is connected to Lake Baikal by a river that shares its name.

## Biological Characteristics
Lake Frolikha is noted for its unique fauna, specifically containing a peculiar species of trout known as *Salmo erythreas*. This particular species of trout is not known to exist anywhere else.

## Regional Context: Lake Baikal
Lake Frolikha exists within the broader ecosystem of Lake Baikal, which is the largest fresh-water basin in Eurasia and the sixth largest lake in the world. Known to Turkish tribes as *Bai-kul* and to the Mongols as *Dalai-nor*, Lake Baikal separates the government of Transbaikalia from that of Irkutsk. The lake spans from 51deg 29' to 55deg 50' N. latitude and 103deg 40' to 110deg E. longitude, covering an area of 13,200 sq. m.

The fauna of the Baikal region, explored by Korotnev (1900-2) as well as Godlewski and Dybowski, is characterized by a great wealth of species within specific zoological groups, despite a relative poverty of groups overall. For example, there are as many as 300 species of gammarids; those found at depths of 330 to 380 fathoms exhibit abyssal characters similar to deep-sea ocean fauna. 

The waters of Lake Baikal are abundant with plankton and fish, including *salmonidae* and sturgeons. The chief product of the fisheries is the herring (*Salmo omul*), although catches have notably decreased over the last forty or fifty years. The lake also hosts a seal (*Phoca vitulina* or *Phoca baikalensis*), which is closely related to the seals found in Spitsbergen.

## Geography and Hydrology of the Baikal Basin
Lake Baikal is surrounded by lofty mountains, with the exception of the Selenga delta. Its borders include:
* **South:** The Khamar-daban border-ridge, featuring a summit 5,300 ft. above the lake.
* **North-west:** The Baikal and Onot ridges (also Primorskiy).
* **North-east:** An Alpine complex of mountains that remain unexplored.
* **East:** The Barguzin range.
* **South-east:** The Ulanburgasu mountains.

The lake receives more than 300 streams, primarily short mountain torrents. Major inflows include the Selenga on the south-east, the Barguzin on the east, and the Upper Angara at the north-east extremity. The only outflow is the lower Angara, which exits through a rocky cleft on the western shore. While the Irkut river once reached Lake Baikal, it now joins the Angara at Irkutsk after passing through a narrow mountain gap.

## Historical and Geological Notes
In the beginning of the thirteenth century, Jengis Khan began consolidating power among nomads in the region between Lake Baikal and Lake Balkash. 

Regarding the lake's origins, hypotheses suggesting a direct communication between Lake Baikal and the Arctic Ocean during the Tertiary or Post-Tertiary ages have not been proven. However, the presence of marine sponges, polychaetes, a marine mollusc (*ancilodoris*), and certain marine gammarids is noted.
