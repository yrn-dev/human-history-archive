# Linguistic and Philological Works of the Late 19th and Early 20th Centuries

The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw a proliferation of philological studies and the production of comprehensive grammars and dictionaries across a diverse array of global languages. These works ranged from the classification of Bantu speech in Africa to the study of Basque and Modern Greek, as well as the historical analysis of the French language.

## Bantu Languages and African Linguistics
Research into the languages of Africa during this period focused heavily on the Bantu group. Various scholars contributed to the classification and documentation of these speeches. In the Congo, the groups of Bantu speech, including the Cameroon and Fernandian, were carefully classified in *George Grenfell and the Congo* (1908). Other significant contributions include the *Dictionary of the Congo Language* (1891) by the Rev. Holman Bentley and the *Grammar of Kimbundu* and *The Folklore of Angola* by Dr. Heli Chatelain.

In East Africa, Dr. L. Krapf’s *Swahili-English Dictionary* (1882) provided a mass of invaluable, though not well-sorted, information on the Swahili language as spoken on the coast, preserving many words that were becoming obsolete. Bishop Steere also published numerous vocabularies and handbooks on the languages of the East African coast-lands, which are noted for documenting prefixes that were passing out of use.

Further inland, the languages of British Central Africa were documented by the Rev. D. C. Scott in *An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mananja (Mang'anja) Language* (1891). Other linguistic works in this region were produced by Father Torrend, who wrote the *Comparative Grammar of the South African Bantu Languages* (1894) based on the languages of the Central Zambezi, and Monsieur E. Jacottet.

Among the Bantu languages, *Ci-subiya* is identified as the dominant language of South-West Zambezia, located between the Chobe-Linyante river and the Zambezi river south of Barotseland. It is characterized as one of the most archaic of the Bantu languages, more so than *Tonga*.

## Basque and European Philology
The study of the Basque language was supported by several exhaustive works. Julien Vinson’s *Essai d'une bibliographie de la langue basque* (1891), along with its 1898 additions, serves as a primary mine of information. Other key texts include the *Grammaire basque* by L'Abbe Ithurry (1895-1906), the *Dictionnaire basque-francais* (1873) by W. J. Van Eys, and the *Diccionario vascongado espanol-francais* (1906) by R. M. de Azkue. The first Basque vocabulary is found in *Le Codex de Saint-Jacques de Compostella*, lib. iv. (1882), authored by J. Vinson and R. P. F. Fita.

In the realm of French philology, Arsene Darmesteter (1846-1888), a professor at the Sorbonne, contributed significantly to the elucidation of Old French. He achieved this by studying glosses found in the medieval writings of other French Jews and Rashi. His works include the *Dictionnaire general de la langue francaise* (1895-1900), co-authored with Adolphe Hatzfeld, and the *Cours de grammaire historique de la langue francaise*, edited by L. Sudre and E. Muret.

## Other Regional Linguistic Studies
Philological efforts extended to the Mediterranean and Asia. Modern Greek studies were advanced by authors such as J. Psichari, who wrote *Essais de grammaire historique neo-grecque* (1886 and 1889) and *Etudes de philologie neo-grecque* (1892). Other authorities in this field included Constantine Sathas, D. Bikelas, and R. Nicolai.

In Asia, Klaproth’s *Asia polyglotta* (1823 and 1831) served as a résumé of knowledge regarding Eastern languages, particularly those of the Russian Empire, and introduced a new classification system. Klaproth also produced a *Vocabulaire et grammaire de la langue géorgienne* (1827).

Additionally, the languages of the Marquesas Islands were documented by Dordillon in *Grammaire et dictionnaire de la langue des Îles Marquises* (1904).
