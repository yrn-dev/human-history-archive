# Vegetation and Geography of High-Altitude Regions

The distribution of flora and the physical characteristics of mountainous regions vary significantly across different global geographies, with elevation serving as a primary determinant for the types of vegetation and forests that can persist.

## Himalayan Vegetation and Zonal Distribution
In the Himalaya, mountains rise abruptly from the plains. The slopes are clothed below with tropical forms, transitioning into warm temperate vegetation that is chiefly evergreen. This vegetation includes *Pinus longifolia*, oak, rhododendron, *Ilex*, *Symplocos*, Magnoliaceae, Ternstroemiaccae, and subtropical Rosaceae, alongside tropical orders such as *Musa*, *Vitis*, *Pandanus*, and palms.

The abundance and variety of vegetation are most pronounced in the east, where luxuriant forests extend to an elevation of 12,000 ft. Above this height, the sub-alpine region begins, characterized by rhododendron scrub that often covers the ground up to 14,000 or 13,000 ft. In the east, epiphytal orchids are very frequent between 4,000 and 8,000 ft., sometimes reaching 10,000 ft. On the drier slopes of Tibet, vegetation ascends to over 18,000 ft.

The western region, characterized by a drier climate, features less dense and luxuriant forests. Warm mountain slopes are covered with rhododendron, oaks, or *Pinus longifolia*. Below 8,000 ft., forests are not commonly dense, except in some low-elevation secluded valleys. Between 8,000 and 12,000 ft., a thick forest of deciduous trees is almost universal, with vegetation continuing up to 18,000 ft. or more in the sub-alpine region. Regarding pine distribution, only one pine is found below 8,000 ft., while several other Coniferae occur above this elevation.

## High Plateaus and Mountain Ranges of Asia
Various high-altitude tablelands and ranges across Asia exhibit distinct botanical and geological features:

*   **The Suliman Hills (Northern Baluchistan):** The massif of the Takht-i-Suliman is a high tableland approximately 8,000 ft. above sea level, composed of a coral limestone cap estimated at 4,000 to 5,000 ft. in thickness. This plateau is covered with a sprinkling of juniper on higher slopes and a fairly thick growth of the "edible" pine, known as chilghosa.
*   **Wakhán:** The lowest part of this district is about 8,000 ft. above the sea, with the highest village (*Kishlak*) reaching approximately 11,500 ft. Due to bitter blasts in the valley, only a few poplars and willows can survive.
*   **Khasi Hills:** In this district, indigenous pine predominates over all other vegetation at an elevation of 3,000 ft., forming almost pure pine forests. Higher ridges contain sacred groves of chestnuts, magnolias, and oaks, with rhododendrons, rare orchids, and wild cinnamon growing in the shade.
*   **General Plateau:** A great plateau extending from the Indus to the Mediterranean generally lies at altitudes between 2,000 and 8,000 ft.

## North American Forestry and Topography
In the United States, significant pine belts exist. The eastern pine belt, covering about 3,900 sq. m., consists of long-leaf pine interspersed with some loblolly. The south-western pine belt covers about 4,200 sq. m. and contains the world's heaviest growth of long-leaf pine timber, occasionally interspersed with short-leaf pine. Short-leaf growth is particularly heavy in the north-western portion of the state. Other specific varieties include curly pine, which is fairly abundant, and pond pine, found only near the Pearl river.

In the Rocky Mountains, the average height along the United States boundary is 8,000 ft. The range culminates in Canada, with Mount Fairweather reaching 15,287 ft. and Mount Robson reaching 13,700 ft. Various passes exist at different elevations, including the Pine River Pass at 2,850 ft. and the South Kootenay or Boundary Pass at 7,100 ft.

## African Orographical Features
Much of the described region in Africa consists of high plateau with an average altitude of 3,500 ft. Notable elevations include:
*   **Mount Mlanje:** The highest peak reaches 9,683 ft.
*   **Mount Rungwe:** Located in German territory, its altitude exceeds 10,000 ft.
*   **Other Peaks:** Mounts Chongone and Dedza reach 7,000 ft., while Mount Zomba is 6,900 ft. and Mount Chiradzulu is 5,500 ft.
*   **Plateaus:** The Nyasa-Tanganyika Plateau has a highest point of 7,000-8,000 ft., and points in the Konde Mountains and on the Nyika Plateau probably exceed 8,000 ft.
