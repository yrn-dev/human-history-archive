# 1740

The year 1740 was marked by significant political transitions in Europe, the outbreak of a major continental conflict, and various notable biographical events.

## Political Transitions and the War of the Austrian Succession
In 1740, Maria-Theresa began her reign. Because she was a woman, she could not be empress; her husband, Francis I, held that title until 1765. Simultaneously, Frederick the Great ascended to the throne as King of Prussia.

The accession of Frederick II of Prussia led directly to the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748), which began with the Prussian invasion of Silesia. At the time, Prussia was a small, thoroughly organized power with an army of 100,000 men. Although regarded as a minor European army, the Prussian forces were drilled to a level of perfection that allowed the infantry to fire five shots for every three fired by the Austrians. While the Prussian cavalry and artillery were less efficient, the army benefited from the "standing army theory," allowing regiments to take the field immediately without waiting for drafts.

On December 16, 1740, without a declaration of war, Frederick's army crossed the frontier into Silesia after concentrating quietly on the Oder. The Austrian generals were only able to garrison a few fortresses before falling back to the mountain frontier of Moravia and Bohemia. By the time the Prussian army entered winter quarters, they held all of Silesia and had invested the strong places of Neisse, Brieg, and Glogau.

This conflict eventually involved nearly all European powers after 1741. The primary struggle was between Austria and Prussia over Silesia. France, Spain, and Bavaria acted as constant allies to Prussia, while England and Holland—traditional enemies of France—supported Austria. Sardinia and Saxony also served as important allies to Austria at various intervals.

## Notable Figures
Several individuals of note reached milestones or passed away in 1740:

**Johann Philipp Baratier**
A German scholar born in 1721 near Nuremberg, Baratier was recognized as a precocious genius. By age five, he could read Greek fluently and speak Dutch, Latin, and French. He later studied Hebrew, translating the Hebrew Bible into French or Latin within three years and translating Tudela's *Itinerarium* around the age of eleven. He was admitted to the Royal Academy at Berlin and became a master of arts at Halle in his fourteenth year. He spent his final years studying antiquities and history, collecting materials for an *Inquiry concerning Egyptian Antiquities* and histories of Antitrinitarianism and the Thirty Years' War. He died on October 5, 1740, having published eleven separate works.

**William Wildman Shute Barrington**
The 2nd Viscount Barrington, born in 1717, entered parliament in March 1740 as the member for Berwick-upon-Tweed. He also married Mary, the daughter of Mr. Henry Lovell, in 1740.
