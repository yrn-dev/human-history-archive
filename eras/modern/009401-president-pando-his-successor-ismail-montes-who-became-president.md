# President Pando and Ismail Montes

## Rise to Power of Colonel Pando
The ascent of Colonel Pando to the presidency was precipitated by a political crisis regarding the capital of the republic. In December, an attempt was made to establish Sucre as the perpetual capital, ending a system where the role had rotated between Sucre, Oruro, Cochabamba, and La Paz. This move triggered an open revolt in La Paz. On January 17th of the following year, government forces fought a battle approximately 40 miles from La Paz, resulting in a defeat for the government and the loss of forty-three men and a colonel.

Colonel Pando, leading the insurgents, gained a significant following and marched on Oruro, entering the town on April 11, 1899, after a complete victory over government troops. Following this, Dr. Severo Alonso sought refuge in Chilean territory, and Colonel Pando established a provisional government. Pando was subsequently elected to the presidency without opposition, officially entering office on October 26.

## Domestic and Regional Administration
As chief magistrate, Pando was regarded as a capable and strong leader. His administration focused on improving national communications to revive other industries and the mining sector. He also managed two complex boundary settlements involving Brazil and Chile.

The dispute with Brazil centered on the rubber-producing territory of Acre. Tensions rose because the majority of those working in the rubber industry were Brazilians who resisted the authority of Bolivian officials. This friction led to the state of Acre declaring independence, prompting Pando to dispatch Bolivian troops in 1900 to restore order. Seeking to avoid full-scale hostilities with Brazil, Pando pursued an amicable diplomatic settlement. This resulted in a treaty signed in November 1903, which established a new boundary line and ceded a portion of the Acre province to Brazil in exchange for a cash indemnity of $10,000,000.

## Boundary Disputes with Chile
Bolivia faced a long-standing dispute with Chile over the occupation of the former Bolivian provinces of Arica and Tacna under the Parto de Tregna of April 4, 1884. By 1895, there was a possibility that Chile would grant Bolivia an outlet to the sea if Bolivia recognized Chilean ownership of Arica and Tacna. However, Chile abandoned this conciliatory stance after discovering secret negotiations between Bolivia and Argentina.

Bolivia found itself unable to compel concessions from Chile or venture into hostilities. Furthermore, the resolution of the boundary dispute between Chile and Argentina removed any hope that Bolivia could rely on Argentine support.

## Succession of Ismail Montes and the 1905 Treaty
Ismail Montes succeeded Pando, becoming president in 1904. Both Pando and Montes recognized the necessity of yielding to Chile to secure the best possible terms. This led to the ratification of a treaty in 1905.

Under the terms of this treaty, Bolivia was compelled to do without a seaport and cede the maritime provinces that Chile had occupied since the war of 1881. In exchange, the treaty provided several advantages for Bolivia:
* The Chilean government agreed to fund and construct a railway from Arica to the Bolivian capital, La Paz.
* Chile granted Bolivians free transit through Chilean territory to specific coastal towns.
* Chile agreed to provide a cash indemnity to Bolivia.
* Chile provided pecuniary assistance for the construction of other railways necessary to open up the country.
