# Brigade

A brigade is a unit of military organization composed of two or more regiments of artillery, cavalry, or infantry. Depending on the specific military structure, a brigade is commanded by a colonel, brigadier-general, or major-general.

## Organization and Composition
In Great Britain, the composition of a brigade varies by its branch:
*   **Infantry Brigade:** Typically consists of four battalions, totaling approximately 4,000 bayonets. These units have attached medical, transport, and supply units.
*   **Cavalry Brigade:** Comprised of two or three cavalry regiments.
*   **Artillery Brigade:** A smaller unit under the command of a lieutenant-colonel, consisting of two or three batteries. These may be categorized as heavy, horse, or field artillery.

The staff of a cavalry or infantry brigade generally includes a brigade-major, an aide-de-camp, and the commanding brigadier. The brigade-major serves as a staff officer with duties falling between those of a general staff officer and an adjutant.

## Historical Applications in Combat

### The Crimean War
During the action of Balaklava on October 25, 1854, the British cavalry division was divided into two brigades. The Light Brigade, commanded by Lord Cardigan, consisted of the 17th Lancers, the 8th and 11th Hussars, and the 4th and 13th Light Dragoons (later Hussars). The Heavy Brigade, under Brigadier-General J. Y. Scarlett, consisted of the 1st, 2nd, and 6th Dragoons and the 4th and 5th Dragoon Guards. While the Light Brigade was positioned in the valley north of the Vorontsov ridge, the Heavy Brigade was in the Balaklava plain. The Heavy Brigade conducted a decisive charge against a mass of Russian cavalry, breaking the enemy in less than five minutes.

### The Great War
During the First World War, brigades were utilized in various strategic roles by Allied and Central powers:

**Canadian Forces:** In battles involving the Canadian front, the 1st Brigade was held in reserve while the 2nd Brigade (under General Currie) and 3rd Brigade (under General Turner) held the line. The 3rd Brigade suffered from gas attacks and was forced to draw back its left wing under pressure from four German divisions, eventually bending back to a wood east of the hamlet of St. Julien. By the 24th, the 3rd Brigade was gassed a second time and could no longer maintain its position. In the 2nd Brigade, the 8th Battalion (90th Winnipeg Rifles) held the point of the Salient against significant odds.

**British and French Forces:** In actions near Compiègne on September 1, the 4th Guards Brigade and the 1st British Cavalry Brigade engaged German forces. The Guards Brigade bore the brunt of the fighting, losing approximately 300 men killed and wounded while capturing ten German guns. In other operations, the 5th Brigade of the 2nd Division captured a footing on Mont St. Quentin, while the 14th Brigade of the 5th Division marched seven miles to assault Peronne. During this operation, the 6th Brigade of the 2nd Division was positioned on the left of the 14th Brigade to complete the capture of Mont St. Quentin.

**Belgian and Other Contingents:** During the defense of Antwerp, the German siege army included one brigade of *Landsturm*, while the Military Government of Brussels utilized a weak *Landwehr* brigade. Efforts to relieve Antwerp involved various units, including the Marine Fusilier Brigade and the Fusiliers Marins brigade under Rear-Admiral Ronarc’h. The latter was composed of a regiment of Zouaves (2,000 men), two regiments of Fusiliers Marins (6,000 men), and 86 mitrailleuses manned by 260 seamen.

## Early Military Use
The term "brigade" has roots in French, German, Italian, and Spanish, with English usage beginning in the early 17th century. In earlier warfare, such as the conflicts involving Gustavus Adolphus, infantry brigades were sometimes distinguished by color. During a battle involving the Imperialists and Wallenstein, the "Blue" brigade overran a battery of heavy guns, while the "Yellow" and "Swedish" brigades successfully engaged the left face of the Imperialist lozenge.
