# Principal Buildings of Buffalo

The city of Buffalo is recognized for the beauty of its residential sections, where houses are generally detached, surrounded by attractive lawns, and set well back from the street. The city's architectural landscape includes a variety of significant government, commercial, cultural, and religious structures.

## Government and Public Buildings
Among the city's most prominent public structures is the Federal building, which was erected at a cost of $2,000,000. The city and county hall cost $1,500,000 and features a clock tower reaching a height of 245 feet. Other notable public and civic buildings include the city convention hall, the chamber of commerce, and the builders' exchange.

## Commercial and Office Structures
Buffalo contains several significant commercial buildings, including the Masonic temple and the Prudential, Fidelity Trust, White, and Mutual Life buildings. The Ellicott Square building is noted as being one of the largest office structures in the world.

## Cultural and Educational Institutions
In Delaware Park, the city hosts the Albright art gallery and the Buffalo Historical Society building. The latter was originally constructed as the New York state building for the Pan-American Exposition held in 1901. The Buffalo Historical Society (founded in 1862) maintains a library of 26,600 volumes within this building.

The Buffalo public library, founded in 1837, is housed in a fine building erected in 1887 with a valuation of $1,000,000; it contains approximately 300,000 pamphlets and books. Additionally, the Buffalo Society of Natural Sciences maintains a museum located within the public library building.

## Religious and Social Buildings
The city features many handsome churches. These include the St. Paul's (Protestant Episcopal) and St. Joseph's (Roman Catholic) cathedrals, as well as the First Presbyterian, Delaware Avenue Baptist, Westminster Presbyterian, and Trinity (Protestant Episcopal) churches.

The city's social infrastructure includes several hotels—specifically the Genesee, Niagara, Lafayette, and Iroquois—and various social clubs, including the Country, Saturn, Park, University, and Buffalo clubs.

## Entertainment and Specialized Facilities
Buffalo's entertainment venues include the Shea's Park, Star, and Teck theatres. In the realm of commerce and industry, the city is a major grain and flour market; in 1843, Joseph Dart erected the first grain elevator ever constructed in the city.
