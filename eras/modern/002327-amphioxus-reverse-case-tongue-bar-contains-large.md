# Amphioxus

Amphioxus is a biological organism characterized by specific anatomical features, most notably its respiratory system and developmental patterns, which provide a basis for comparison with other animals such as *Balanoglossus* and craniate vertebrates.

## Respiratory System and Gill-Slits
Amphioxus possesses gill-slits that are organized according to a specific structural plan. These slits are subdivided by branchial bars. In Amphioxus, there are two kinds of branchial bars: primary bars (which correspond to the septal bars found in *Balanoglossus*) and tongue-bars.

The tongue-bar in Amphioxus differs significantly from that found in *Balanoglossus* in several ways:
* **Bulk and Importance:** While the tongue-bar is the essential organ of the gill-slit in *Balanoglossus* and exceeds the septal bars in bulk, the reverse is the case in Amphioxus.
* **Composition:** The tongue-bar in Amphioxus is solid, whereas in *Balanoglossus* it contains a large coelomic space.

These differences suggest a gradation in evolution. It is proposed that the tongue-bar may have originally served as the essential organ of the cleft but underwent modification and reduction in Amphioxus, becoming a secondary bar that is subordinate to the primary bars in terms of development, vascularity, and size. This process of involution may have continued in craniate vertebrates, where it is suggested that the thymus-primordia represent these tongue-bars.

## Nervous System
The nervous system of Amphioxus includes a cerebral vesicle. In comparative anatomical studies, the collar nerve-tube of the Enteropneusta (such as *Balanoglossus*) is regarded as the equivalent of the primary fore-brain of vertebrates and the cerebral vesicle of the Ascidian tadpole and Amphioxus.

## Development
The developmental process of Amphioxus provides a point of comparison for the direct development observed in the family *Balanoglossidae*. In the direct development of *Balanoglossus*, the three divisions of the coelom arise as pouches that are constricted off from the archenteron, or primitive gut. This process resembles the development of the mesoblastic somites of Amphioxus.
