# Clerks and Clerical Roles in History

The term "clerk" has historically described a wide variety of professional roles across different sectors, ranging from legal and administrative functions to specialized oversight in construction and public health.

## Construction and Architectural Oversight
In the execution of large or important construction works, it is customary to employ a clerk of works. This individual serves as a delegate from the architect and is tasked with remaining on-site while works are in progress. The primary duties of the clerk of works are to ensure the use of the best construction methods and materials through constant attention, and to report any difficulties that arise to the architect for further instruction. 

The role requires a thoroughly practical man, distinguishing the clerk of works from an architectural draughtsman. Regarding compensation, the client pays the salary of the clerk of works; this payment is separate from and not included in the remuneration provided to the architect.

## Legal and Professional Guilds
In France, the Basoche (also referred to as Bazoche, Basoque, Basogue, or Bazouges) was an ancient guild of clerks. The name is derived from the Latin *basilica*, referring to law courts. This guild predated the guild of the *procureurs* (legal representatives, advocates, or procurators), with whom the Basoche was often at variance.

The Basoche originated during a period when the profession of *procureur* was "free," meaning those providing legal services were not yet ministerial or public officers. Groups of clerks skilled in law established themselves near important juridical centers to act as advocates or representatives. Over time, this organization disintegrated into specialized bodies: one for advocates and another for legal representatives (the latter of which became a saleable charge and was regularized in 1344). The remaining clerks formed the new Basoche, consisting of those who worked for the *procureurs*. Some of these clerks aspired to become *procureurs* themselves.

The Basoche maintained picturesque traditions, referring to its organization as the "kingdom of the Basoche." Its chief, who was elected annually by a general assembly, held the title of "king" until the end of the 16th century, when the title was abolished by order.

## Public Health and Mortality Records
In England, the company of parish clerks supervised the issuance of the "Bill of Mortality," a weekly return detailing the number of deaths within a parish. These records were particularly vital during the Tudor period to provide early warning of the plague, allowing for the implementation of isolation and quarantine measures.

The process relied on "searchers"—typically women—who were appointed to report the cause of each death to the clerk. The clerk then forwarded this information to the parish clerks' hall. The resulting weekly returns listed deaths from the plague and other causes, while also identifying parishes that remained free from the plague.

While parish registers were established by Cromwell (Lord Essex) in 1538, extant bills date back to August 1535, and possibly earlier. Regular compilation in a recognized manner began in December 1603 and continued until 1842, when they were superseded by the registrar-general's returns under the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1836. The bills gained significant statistical value in 1728 with the introduction of the ages of the deceased, providing the data upon which the science of life insurance was founded.

## Administrative and Commercial Roles
Clerical roles also appeared in various commercial and military contexts:

*   **Commercial:** In Calcutta, the term "banyan" (derived from the Sanskrit *vanij*, meaning merchant) was once used to describe a head clerk or native broker in a private house or business, a role now commonly known as a *sircar*. Additionally, individuals such as the American writer Henry Cuyler Bunner began their professional lives as clerks in importing houses before transitioning to other fields.
*   **Military:** In the approved form of the Fleet Flagship Staff, the organizational structure includes a section for "Secretary and clerks" reporting to the Commander-in-Chief.
