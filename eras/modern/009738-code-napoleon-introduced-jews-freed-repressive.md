# Code Napoleon

The *Code Napoleon* was a monument of civil law promulgated in France in 1804. It served as a comprehensive codification that gathered, revised, and clarified a vast and disorderly accumulation of both old and new laws, substituting plain statements for what had previously been inaccessible legal mysteries.

## Development and Nature
The creation of the code was largely the work of the Council of State. Napoleon Bonaparte's role in its deliberations was characterized by a tendency to hinder rather than help; he frequently interrupted sessions without notice to deliver lengthy, egotistical, and often irrelevant monologues. Despite this, the Council maintained a posture of profound respect, and Napoleon often kept his councillors working until unearthly hours.

In his later years, Napoleon expressed a particular satisfaction with the code, remarking that his glory consisted not in having won forty battles, but in having created the *Code Napoleon*. The code was designed for immediate efficiency, defining relations and things clearly so that they could be acted upon without further discussion. However, critics note that it lacked intellectual power, as it took existing conditions for granted and frequently defined them wrongly. By fixing the status of peasants, labourers, and women into "hard definitions," the code created a restrictive framework that continued to affect France into the nineteenth century.

## Legal Provisions and Social Impact
The *Code Napoleon* maintained restrictive views regarding women. Specifically, a wife was granted no control over her own property, which remained entirely in the hands of her husband.

Beyond the borders of France, the introduction of the code brought significant social and legal changes to territories under French influence. In the kingdom of Westphalia, which was established for Jerome Bonaparte under the treaty of Tilsit (July 7, 1807), the introduction of the *Code Napoleon* coincided with the abolition of feudalism and the freeing of the Jews from repressive laws.

## International Influence and Legacy
The spirit of organization embodied in the *Code Napoleon* left a lasting impression on various regions:

*   **Belgium:** The code served as a monument of civil law that continued to form the basis of Belgian jurisprudence long after the end of French domination in 1814.
*   **Louisiana:** Although the code itself was not available in Louisiana at the time, the project of the *Code Napoleon* was used by compilers in the arrangement and substance of their work. This influenced the organic developments of the legal system in the territory acquired from France via the Louisiana Purchase of 1803, integrating French traditions into the existing Spanish and American legal framework.
*   **Italy:** In the duchies of Parma, Piacenza, and Guastalla, some Napoleonic laws and institutions were preserved. In 1817, the ruler of Parma established the equality of women in heritage and ordered the compilation of a civil code.
