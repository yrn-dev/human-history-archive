# RULE III.--EXTINGUISHING FIRE ON SHIPBOARD

Rule III is a regulation concerning the damage incurred during the process of extinguishing a fire on board a ship. Under this rule, damage caused to a ship, its cargo, or both—whether resulting from the use of water or other means—is to be made good as General Average (G.A.). This includes damage resulting from the scuttling or beaching of a burning vessel. However, the rule specifies an exception: no compensation is provided for damage to separate packages of cargo, bulk cargo, or portions of the ship that were actually on fire.

## Legal Interpretations and Jurisdictions

While modern legal decisions have largely resolved previous doubts regarding whether damage to ship or cargo should be allowed in G.A. under Rule III, specific circumstances regarding the authority in charge of the extinguishing efforts have led to differing judicial outcomes.

### United States Jurisprudence
In the United States, questions have arisen regarding whether G.A. allowances apply when a fire occurs in port and is extinguished by a public authority acting in the public interest rather than by the ship's master. In the 1894 case of *Ralli v. Troup* (157 U.S. 386), the Supreme Court of the United States ruled against such an allowance. In this instance, the port authority scuttled the ship to put out a fire based on their own judgment, although the master assented. The Court held that the damage should not be made good by G.A. contributions because the sacrifice was a compulsory act of a public authority, rather than an act performed by someone specifically charged with the control and safety of the adventure.

### English Jurisprudence
Conversely, English law has seen different rulings. In the case of *Papayanni v. Grampian S.S. Co.* (I. Com. Ca. 448), Mathew, J. determined that the scuttling of a ship at a port of refuge in Algeria was a G.A. act. This decision was reached because the scuttling was ordered by the captain of the port in the interest of the cargo and the ship, and there was no evidence of any other motive.
