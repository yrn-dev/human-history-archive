# United Kingdom: Economic and Demographic Overview

## Banking and Currency
The banking system of the United Kingdom has evolved from a heavy reliance on note circulation in the late 18th and early 19th centuries to a system where deposits became far more important. Trustworthy statistics for the United Kingdom's banking position are generally unavailable for periods extending back more than forty or fifty years. For instance, Scottish banks did not generally publish accounts until 1865, and many other banks showed a long-continued reluctance to publish balance sheets.

Early estimates of total deposits (including the Bank of England) for England, Wales, Scotland, and Ireland in 1851 ranged from £250,000,000 to £360,000,000. By 1872, an estimate excluding bank capitals and deposits in discount houses placed this figure between £430,000,000 and £450,000,000. By the close of 1906, total deposits, including acceptances, reached approximately £997,000,000. Total resources for 1906, which included reserves, capitals, and a note circulation of roughly £177,500,000, were also recorded.

Regarding note circulation in 1906, the average figures were:
*   Bank of England: £28,890,000
*   Joint-stock banks: £429,000
*   Private banks: £124,000

This resulted in a total for England of £29,443,000. When combined with Scotland (£7,477,000) and Ireland (£6,452,000), the total note circulation in the United Kingdom was £43,372,000.

In Ireland, the authorized issue of notes for 17 joint-stock banks was £1,084,836, while the actual issue was £437,693. For 12 private banks, the authorized issue was £482,744 against an actual issue of £122,536. The disparity between authorized and actual circulation is attributed to the fact that the circulation of these banks extends over very limited areas; notes from country banks are largely unknown outside their immediate neighborhood and can be difficult to cash, even if payable in London.

## Brewing and Liquor Trade
The brewing industry in the United Kingdom has reached vast proportions. Production peaked in 1900 at 37,090,986 barrels. Although production declined steadily after 1900, the 1905-1906 figure of 34,109,263 barrels remained higher than any year prior to 1897. This growth is notable compared to 1875, when the industry produced roughly 25,000,000 barrels.

In the year ending September 30, 1905, the exchequer received £13,156,053 in beer duty. There were 5,180 brewers for sale. The largest brewery in the world, owned by Messrs Guinness, brewed over two million barrels and paid approximately one million sterling to the revenue. Three other firms each brewed close to or more than one million barrels.

Production materials for the 1905-1906 period included:
*   Malt: 51,818,697 bushels
*   Hops: 62,360,817 lb
*   Sugar: 2,746,615 cwt.
*   Rice, flaked maize, and similar materials: 1,348,558 cwt.
*   Unmalted corn: 125,671 bushels
*   Hop substitutes: 49,202 lb

The average specific gravity of beer produced in 1905-1906 was 1053.24. Imports and exports remained nearly stationary over thirty years, with 520,826 barrels exported and 57,194 barrels imported. Consumption was highest in England (28,590,563 barrels), followed by Ireland (3,265,084 barrels) and Scotland (1,648,463 barrels) for 1905-1906.

The capital invested in the liquor trade was estimated at £117,000,000 in 1871, a figure that may have safely doubled by 1908. Specifically, a 1906 *Brewers' Almanack* entry placed the capital invested in limited liability breweries alone at £185,000,000.

## Dairy Produce and Imports
The United Kingdom is unable to produce sufficient dairy products to supply its own population. Butter imports rose from 102,500 tons in the year ending June 30, 1891, to 170,700 tons by June 30, 1900, an average annual increase of 6,800 tons. Between 1891 and 1900, the Australasian colonies increased their butter quota by 13,400 tons and Canada by 11,100 tons. Among foreign sources, Denmark showed the most development, increasing its supply by 28,678 tons.

Cheese imports also show a heavy reliance on external sources. Over 80% of imported cheese comes from North America, with Canada supplying nearly 60% of the total. In 1900, the value of cheese exported from Canada to the United Kingdom was nearly £3,800,000. Other participants in the cheese trade include France, Holland, and Australasia. French shipments are dominated by soft or "fancy" cheese, which commanded an average price of 61s. per cwt. in 1900, compared to 50s. per cwt. for cheese from all other sources.

Between 1891 and 1900, total cheese imports fluctuated, with a ten-year average of 255,603 tons. This total included home production (average 141,396 tons), colonial imports (average 61,908 tons), and foreign imports (average 52,299 tons). In 1900, Canada provided 128,600 tons of the total colonial cheese imports, while New Zealand provided 4,000 tons.

## Population and Empire
The population of the British Empire is calculated to be more than one-fourth of the world's population (which is estimated at over 1,500,000,000). The empire's population includes a white population of 53,040,468 and native populations totaling 335,672,317. These native populations include 294,191,379 in India, 18,863,000 in West Africa, 7,425,000 in East Africa, 5,211,329 in South Africa, 5,144,954 in Ceylon and Eastern Colonies, 2,000,000 in British Central Africa, 1,912,655 in the West Indies, 824,000 in Australasia and Islands, and 100,000 Red Indians in Canada.

Within the white races of the empire, and notably in the United Kingdom, there is a considerable sprinkling of Jewish blood.
