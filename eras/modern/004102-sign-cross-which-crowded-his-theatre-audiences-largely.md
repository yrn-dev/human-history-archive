# The Sign of the Cross

*The Sign of the Cross* is a drama of religious emotion written and produced by the English actor, manager, and playwright Wilson Barrett. First appearing in 1895, the play achieved a significant level of success, crowding Barrett's theatre with audiences. Notably, these audiences were largely composed of individuals who existed outside the ordinary circle of playgoers.

## Author and Production
The play was written by Wilson Barrett (1846–1904), who was born in Essex to a farmer. Barrett's career in the theatre began in 1864 with an appearance in Halifax, followed by provincial performances in *East Lynne* alongside his wife, Caroline Heath. He gained managerial experience in Leeds and at the old Court theatre, where he introduced Madame Modjeska to London in various plays, including *Adrienne Lecouvreur*, *La Dame aux camelias*, and an adaptation of Schiller's *Maria Stuart*.

Barrett became well known to the public in 1881 at the Princess's theatre through the emotional drama *The Lights o' London* by G. R. Sims. He further established himself as a favorite in 1882 with the production of *The Silver King* by Henry Arthur Jones, which is considered perhaps the most successful melodrama ever staged. In this production, Barrett played Wilfred Denver, while his brother George and E. S. Willard (who played the "Spider") were also in the cast. Barrett performed the role of Wilfred Denver for three hundred consecutive nights.

## Career Context and Later Works
Before the success of *The Sign of the Cross*, Barrett had explored other genres, appearing in *Hamlet* in 1884 and starring in W. G. Wills's *Claudian*. While he had occasional seasons in London, he primarily acted in the provinces. He also toured internationally, visiting America for the first time in 1886 and visiting Australia in 1898.

By the time *The Sign of the Cross* was produced in 1895, the vogue for melodrama on the London stage had waned. While the drama of religious emotion struck a new vein of success for Barrett, his subsequent attempts to repeat this achievement with other plays of a religious type were not as effective. Several of his later plays resulted in failures.

## Artistic Profile
Wilson Barrett was described as a sterling actor of a robust type with a striking physique. While he was not noted for intellectual finesse, he was an excellent performer in the emotional and melodramatic styles that defined much of his career. He died on July 22, 1904.
