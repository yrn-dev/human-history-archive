# Carlo Buonaparte and the Bonaparte Family

## Life and Career of Carlo Buonaparte
Carlo Buonaparte, also known as Charles Marie de Bonaparte (1746–1785), was a lawyer who earned his degree from the university of Pisa. Following the French conquest of Corsica, he served as an assessor to the royal court of Ajaccio and the neighboring districts. 

Characterized by a restless and dissatisfied nature, Carlo frequently intrigued for different posts and engaged in risky business enterprises. These ventures had a long-term negative impact on his family, compromising their fortunes for many years. By conviction, he was a Voltairian and a schemer by nature.

## Marriage and Family
In 1764, Carlo married Letizia Ramolino, who was fourteen years old at the time. Letizia was described as a beautiful and high-spirited girl from a well-connected family that had been domiciled in Corsica since the middle of the 15th century. 

The couple's first two children, born in 1765 and 1767, died during infancy. Their first surviving son, Joseph, was born in 1768, followed by Napoleon in 1769. Napoleon's birth occurred during the unrest following the French conquest; during this period, Letizia had accompanied her husband on several escapes and journeys.

## Influence on Children
The parents provided contrasting influences on their children. Letizia was devout in her thought and manner of life, simple and frugal in her tastes, and possessed a firm and courageous disposition. Her influence helped bind her children to the life of Corsica. In contrast, Carlo pointed the way toward careers in France. This direction ultimately molded the destinies of Europe and the fortunes of the family.

## Death
Carlo Buonaparte died in 1785 at Montpellier. The cause of death was cancer in the stomach.
