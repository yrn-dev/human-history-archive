# Chequamegon Bay

Chequamegon Bay is an arm of Lake Superior located in the northern part of the state of Wisconsin, U.S.A. It serves as the site for the city of Ashland, which is situated at the head of the bay.

## Ashland
Ashland is the county-seat of Ashland county and is located approximately 315 miles northwest of Milwaukee and about 70 miles east of Duluth and Superior. The city was settled around 1854, incorporated as a village in 1863, and received its city charter in 1887.

The population of Ashland has fluctuated over the early 20th century. In 1890, the population was 9,956; by 1900, it had grown to 13,074, including 4,417 foreign-born residents. According to the 1910 census, the population was 11,594.

## Infrastructure and Education
The city is served by several railway lines, including the Northern Pacific, the Wisconsin Central, the Chicago & North-Western, and the Chicago, St Paul, Minneapolis & Omaha. Additionally, it is connected by several steamboat lines operating on the Great Lakes.

Ashland is characterized by a dry, healthful climate and serves as a summer resort. Its public and educational facilities include:
* The Vaughn public library, established in 1895.
* A fine Federal building.
* One of the best high-school buildings in Wisconsin.
* The Northland College and Academy (Congregational).
* The Rinehart hospital and a Roman Catholic hospital.

## Economy and Industry
Ashland possesses an excellent harbour and is a major center for trade and manufacturing. The city's lake trade is valued at more than $35,000,000 annually. It serves as the principal port for shipping iron ore from the rich Gogebic Range, with annual shipments approximating 3,500,000 tons, valued at $12,000,000. The city also maintains large coal docks and an extensive export trade in lumber and brownstone quarried in the vicinity.

The industrial base of Ashland includes:
* Iron and steel rolling mills, foundries, and machine shops.
* Large saw-mills.
* Railway repair shops for the Chicago & North-Western railway.
* Manufactories producing charcoal, wood-alcohol, sulphite fibre, and dynamite.
* Knitting works.

In 1905, the total value of the city's factory product was $4,210,265.
