# BARRY, ELIZABETH (1658-1713)

Elizabeth Barry was an English actress whose career spanned the late 17th and early 18th centuries. While the details regarding her early life are meagre, she became one of the most prominent figures of the English stage during her era.

## Career Development and Training
Barry's initial attempts at acting were unsuccessful, and she was dismissed from the stage on more than one occasion. Her professional trajectory changed when she was coached by her lover, the Earl of Rochester. Rochester had entered into a wager that he could transform her into a first-rate actress within a short period, a judgment that was eventually confirmed by her subsequent success.

## Notable Performances and Royal Patronage
Mrs. Barry achieved significant acclaim for her role as Isabella, the queen of Hungary, in the play *Mustapha* by the Earl of Orrery. This performance was so delightful to Charles II and the Duke and Duchess of York that the Duchess took English lessons from Barry. Later, when the Duchess became queen, she provided Mrs. Barry with her own coronation robes to wear for the role of Elizabeth in Banks's *Earl of Essex*.

Throughout her career, Mrs. Barry is said to have created over 100 parts. She found particular success in the works of Thomas Otway. Her talent was highly regarded by her contemporaries; Dryden described her as "always excellent," and Betterton noted that her acting brought success to plays that would otherwise "disgust the most patient reader."

## Professional Contributions and Personal Life
According to Cibber, Mrs. Barry was the catalyst for a significant change in the financial structure of the acting profession. By command of James II, "benefits"—which had previously been reserved exclusively for authors—were first established for actors on her behalf.

In her private life, Mrs. Barry had two children. One was fathered by Lord Rochester and a second by Sir George Etheredge; both fathers provided for their children.

## Retirement and Death
Mrs. Barry retired from the stage in 1709. She died on November 7, 1713.
