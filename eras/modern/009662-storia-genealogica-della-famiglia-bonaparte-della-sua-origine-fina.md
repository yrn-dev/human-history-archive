# Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte, della sua origine fina

The *Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte, della sua origine fina all' estinzione del ramo gia esisente nella citta di S. Miniato* is a genealogical work written by a "Samminiatese" identified as D. Morali. Published in Florence in 1846, the text focuses on the Bonaparte family, specifically detailing the history of the branch that existed in the city of San Miniato until its extinction. The work provides genealogical information regarding the family in general, with specific focus on Carlo and Letizia Bonaparte.

## Related Genealogical and Historical Literature
The study of the Bonaparte family's origins and lineage has been addressed by various other authors and publications. These include:

*   **Early and General Histories:** F. de Stefani authored *Le antichita dei Bonaparte* (Venice, 1857), which includes an introduction by L. Beretta. L. Ambrosini and A. Huard published *La Famille imperiale. Hist. de la famille Bonaparte depuis son origine jusqu'en 1860* in Paris in 1860. Additionally, C. Leynadier wrote *Histoire de la famille Bonaparte de l'an 1050 a l'an 1848*, a work that was later continued until 1866 by de Brugere.
*   **Family Members and Biographies:** A. Kleinschmidt's *Die Eltern und Geschwister Napoleons I.* (Berlin, 1876) examines the parents and siblings of Napoleon I. The marriages of the family are documented in the two-volume set *The Marriages of the Bonapartes* by D.A. Bingham (London, 1881). F. Masson produced a four-volume work titled *Napoleon et sa famille* (Paris, 1897-1900).
*   **Youth and Parentage:** The early life of Napoleon is covered in A. Chuquet's three-volume *La Jeunesse de Napoleon* (Paris, 1897-1899) and in the *Memoires sur l'enfance et la jeunesse de Napoleon jusqu'a la age vingt-trois ans* by T. Nasica, which includes a historical notice regarding Napoleon's father.
*   **Letizia Bonaparte:** The life of Napoleon's mother is detailed in the two-volume *Madame Mere* by Baron H. Larrey (Paris, 1892) and in Clara Tschudi's *Napoleons Mutter*, which was translated from Norwegian by H. von Lenk (Leipzig, 1901).
