# Histoire du Directoire de la Republique francaise (1855); Etudes

## Overview of the Work
*Histoire du Directoire de la Republique francaise*, published in 1855, is a historical work authored by the French statesman and historian Baron Amable Guillaume Prosper Brugiere de Barante. This publication is part of a broader body of historical writing by de Barante, which also includes the six-volume *Histoire de la Convention Nationale* published between 1851 and 1853.

## About the Author
Amable Guillaume Prosper Brugiere, Baron de Barante (1782–1866), was born on June 16, 1782, in Riom to the son of an advocate. His academic background included attending the Ecole Polytechnique in Paris at age sixteen. By age twenty, he began his career in the civil service, where his abilities led to rapid promotion. In 1806, he was appointed as an auditor to the council of state.

De Barante's political career was extensive and spanned several regimes:
* **Napoleonic Era:** He served in political missions in Spain, Poland, and Germany. He held the prefecture of Vendee and later the prefecture of Nantes, a post he resigned immediately upon the return of Napoleon I.
* **Bourbon Restoration:** He served as secretary-general of the ministry of the interior and a councillor of state. He also spent several years as the director-general of indirect taxes. In 1819, he was created a peer of France and became a prominent figure among the Liberals.
* **July Monarchy:** Following the revolution of July 1830, he served as ambassador to St Petersburg and Turin. He remained a supporter of the government throughout the reign of Louis Philippe.
* **Later Life:** After the fall of the monarchy in February 1848, de Barante retired to his country seat in Auvergne and withdrew from political affairs. He was awarded the grand cross of the Legion of Honour shortly before his retirement.

## Literary and Academic Contributions
De Barante was admitted to the French Academy following the publication of *Histoire des ducs de Bourgogne de la maison de Valois* (1824–1828). While the romantic school praised the work for its purity of style and narrative qualities, it was noted for a lack of scientific scholarship and critical sense. 

In addition to his work on the Directoire and the Convention Nationale, his literary output included:
* *Des communes et de l'aristocratie* (1821)
* *Tableau de la litterature francaise au dixhuitieme siecle* (published in several editions)
* *Questions constitutionnelles* (1850)
* A French translation of the dramatic works of Schiller
