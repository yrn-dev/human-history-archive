# Memorials in Bristol Cathedral

## Overview of Bristol Cathedral
Bristol Cathedral is situated on the south side of College Green, positioned above the area known as Canons' Marsh, which borders the Floating Harbour. The cathedral's architectural layout includes a nave, two western towers, a central tower, a choir with aisles, short transepts, an eastern Lady chapel, and various other chapels. Additionally, the site features a cloister court and a chapter-house on the south side. 

The nave is a modern construction completed in 1877 by Street, designed to imitate the 14th-century choir, which is noted for its curious skeleton-vaulting in the aisles. The cathedral's origins are linked to the abbey of St Augustine, founded in 1142 by Fitz-Harding. Several original structures from the abbey remain largely undamaged, including the abbot's gateway, the vestibule to the chapter-house, and the chapter-house itself. The latter is recognized as one of the finest Norman chambers in Europe, characterized by its Byzantine exuberance of decoration. The stately entrance gateway also retains its sculptured mouldings.

## Interments and Memorials
The cathedral serves as the burial place for several notable figures. Among the memorials are those dedicated to Bishop Butler, Lady Hesketh—who was a friend of Cowper—and Sterne's Eliza, identified as Elizabeth Draper. 

The cathedral also contains the canopied tombs of the Berkeleys, which feature effigies in chain mail, as well as fine tombs belonging to crosiered abbots. Another significant memorial is the fine epitaph written by William Mason for his wife, who died in 1767; the inscription begins with the words, "Take, holy earth, all that my soul holds dear."

## Related Ecclesiastical Sites
In the vicinity of the cathedral, on the north side of College Green, is the Mayor's chapel. Originally known as St Mark's, this small but ornate chapel is devoted to the services of the corporation and the mayor. Its architectural style is primarily Perpendicular and Decorated.
