# Treasury Notes and Public Debt in Bolivia (1903–1905)

## Public Debt and Foreign Indemnities
The acknowledged public debt of Bolivia has been characterized as comparatively small. Following the conclusion of the war with Chile, the country held an indemnity debt owed to the citizens of the Chilean republic totaling 6,550,830 bolivianos. By 1904, this debt—which constituted Bolivia's only foreign debt—was nearly liquidated after Chile assumed the unpaid balance.

## Internal Debt and Treasury Issues
By 1905, Bolivia's internal debt reached a total of 6,243,270 bolivianos (L546,286). This figure included 1,998,500 bolivianos in the form of treasury bills. 

In 1903, the government authorized the issuance of treasury notes specifically for the National Territory and the department of Beni. These notes amounted to one million bolivianos (L87,500). To ensure the redemption of these notes, the government set aside 10% of the customs receipts generated from these two districts.

## Currency and Circulation
The paper currency used within the republic is not considered part of the public debt, as it consists of bank-notes issued by four private banks. As of June 30, 1903, the official estimate of paper currency in circulation was 9,144,254 bolivianos (L800,122), issued on a par with silver.

## Coinage and Minting
Bolivia utilizes a coinage system consisting of copper, nickel, and silver. The denominations are as follows:
*   **Silver:** 1 boliviano (equal to 100 centavos), 50, 20, 10, and 5 centavos.
*   **Nickel:** 10 and 5 centavos.
*   **Copper:** 2 and 1 centavos.

The Potosi mint produces approximately 1,500,000 bolivianos in silver coins annually. By law, silver mining companies are required to deliver 20% of their product to the mint. Despite this production, silver bolivianos are rarely seen in general circulation because the paper currency is cheaper. Additionally, the government has slightly debased the fractional denominations of the coins to discourage the exportation of silver coin.
