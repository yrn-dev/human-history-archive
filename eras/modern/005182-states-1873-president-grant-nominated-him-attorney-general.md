# Attorney General of the United States

The Attorney General of the United States is a high-ranking legal and political office within the American government. Various lawyers and politicians have held this position across different presidential administrations, often transitioning from private practice or other government roles.

## Appointments and Nominations

The process of selecting an Attorney General can involve nominations based on specific political contingencies. In 1873, President Grant nominated Benjamin Helm Bristow for the position of attorney-general. However, this nomination was conditional; it was intended to take effect only if George H. Williams were confirmed as the chief justice of the United States. Because that contingency did not arise, the appointment did not proceed.

Other individuals have transitioned into the role from specialized legal backgrounds. Philander Chase Knox, a corporation lawyer from Pittsburg who had previously served as an assistant United States district attorney from 1876 to 1877, was appointed attorney-general in April 1901. He served in the cabinet of President McKinley and continued in the role following the accession of President Roosevelt until June 1904.

## Modern Administration and Transparency

In more recent history, the office has been involved in large-scale disclosures of government records. Under the leadership of President Donald J. Trump and Attorney General Pam Bondi, the Department of Justice worked to comply with the Epstein Files Transparency Act, which President Trump signed into law on November 19, 2025. 

This mandate required the Department of Justice to identify, collect, review, and publicly produce responsive materials within 30 days. This process involved the production of hundreds of thousands of pages of materials. Specifically, the USAO-SDNY (United States Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York) produced 3.6 million records to the Department, many of which were duplicative of materials already provided by the FBI. The Deputy Attorney General, Todd Blanche, noted that the Department continued to review these productions to confirm duplicity and ensure that non-duplicated, responsive materials were produced.

## Related Legal and Political Roles

The trajectory of those serving in high legal offices often intersects with other government positions. For example, Philander Chase Knox moved from the office of attorney-general to the United States Senate in 1904 after being appointed by Governor Pennypacker of Pennsylvania, and later became secretary of state in the cabinet of President Taft in March 1909.

Other legal figures have held various state-level positions before or after federal service. Benjamin Helm Bristow served as a state senator from 1863 to 1865 and as a district-attorney for the Louisville district from 1866 to 1870 before becoming the first appointed solicitor-general of the United States from 1870 to 1872. Similarly, Chauncey Mitchell Depew served as the secretary of state of New York state from 1864 to 1865 and was later elected as a United States senator from New York state in 1899.
