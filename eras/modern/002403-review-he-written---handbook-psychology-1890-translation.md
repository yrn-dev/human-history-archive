# Handbook of Psychology (1890)

The *Handbook of Psychology*, published in 1890, is a written work by the American philosopher James Mark Baldwin.

## Author Biography
James Mark Baldwin (1861– ) was born in Columbia, South Carolina. He received his education at Princeton and at several universities in Germany. Throughout his career, Baldwin held several academic positions, serving as a professor of philosophy at the University of Toronto in 1889, a professor of psychology at Princeton in 1893, and later as a professor of both philosophy and psychology at Johns Hopkins University starting in 1903.

Baldwin was a prominent figure among experimental psychologists and was one of the founders of the *Psychological Review*. His professional leadership included serving as the president of the American Psychological Association from 1897 to 1898 and as the vice-president of the International Congress of Psychology in London in 1892. In 1896, he served as the honorary president of the International Congress of Criminal Anthropology in Geneva. His contributions to the field were recognized with an honorary D.Sc. from Oxford University and a gold medal from the Royal Academy of Arts and Sciences of Denmark in 1897.

## Related Works and Contributions
In addition to the *Handbook of Psychology* (1890), Baldwin authored several other significant texts:
* *Elements of Psychology* (1893)
* *Mental Development in the Child and the Race* (1896)
* *Story of the Mind* (1898)
* *Social and Ethical Interpretations in Mental Development* (1898)
* *Thought and Things* (vol. i., 1906), published in New York and London.

Baldwin also translated Ribot's *German Psychology of To-day* in 1886 and served as the editor-in-chief of the *Dictionary of Philosophy and Psychology*, to which he contributed extensively between 1901 and 1905.
