# Victoria County History--Sussex

The *Victoria County History* is a series of historical records and studies concerning the counties of England. Within the archive's records, the Sussex volumes serve as a primary reference for the history, antiquities, and geography of the county of Sussex.

## Historical Context of Sussex
Sussex is identified as one of the ancient kingdoms of southern England. According to tradition, the first king of Sussex was Ella (also referred to as Ælle). The region was part of a landscape occupied by various separate kingdoms, each with its own royal family. To the west of Sussex lay Wessex, which occupied the south of England between Sussex and "West Wales" (an area eventually reduced to Cornwall).

## Geography and Land Use
The geography of Sussex is characterized by the South Downs, which are part of a larger system of chalk hills in south-east England. These hills, along with the North Downs (found primarily in Kent and Surrey), enclose the rich district known as the Weald.

In more recent historical data, Sussex is noted as one of the primary hop-growing counties of England. Between 1895 and 1907, the county maintained significant acreage for this crop, though the figures showed a general decline over that period:
*   **1895:** 7,489 acres
*   **1900:** 4,823 acres
*   **1907:** 4,243 acres

During the decade from 1897 to 1906, Sussex recorded an average yield per acre of 9.41 cwt., the highest among the six main hop-growing counties, which also included Kent, Hereford, Worcester, Hants, and Surrey.
