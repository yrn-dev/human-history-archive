# Hanover

## Political Transitions and the Elector of Hanover
In the early 18th century, the political landscape of England was heavily influenced by the declining health of the queen. The Tory ministers of the period viewed the impending accession of the elector of Hanover as the catalyst for their own downfall. During this time, both Bolingbroke and the earl of Oxford maintained secret communications with James.

As the transition of power approached, Bolingbroke's political maneuvers became increasingly complex. On the day preceding the queen's death, he ordered that overtures be made to the elector. However, these actions occurred alongside other contradictory efforts; on the very day the earl of Oxford was dismissed, Bolingbroke hosted a dinner for Whig leaders.

## The Fall of Bolingbroke
Following the sudden death of the queen on August 1, the political scene shifted instantly. Shrewsbury was appointed to the lord treasurership, an event that ruined Bolingbroke. In a letter to Swift dated August 3, Bolingbroke noted the rapid change in fortune, observing that the earl of Oxford had been removed on Tuesday and the queen had died on Sunday.

Prior to this collapse, Bolingbroke had appeared to be in a position of supremacy, with indications tending toward a Jacobite restoration. This faction had gained significant influence, including the appointment of the Jacobite Sir William Windham as chancellor of the exchequer and the placement of faction members in important military posts. While Bolingbroke later claimed in *The Patriot King* that there were no designs to place the Pretender on the crown, this is described as a palpable falsehood. His primary objective was likely the acquisition and maintenance of supreme power through any means or betrayal required by the circumstances.

## Colonial History under the House of Hanover
The historian John Andrew Doyle (1844–1907), a fellow of All Souls' and winner of the Arnold prize at Balliol College, Oxford, documented the history of English colonies in America from 1607 to 1759. His comprehensive five-volume work, *The English Colonies in America*, includes a specific volume titled *The Colonies under the House of Hanover*, published in 1907.
