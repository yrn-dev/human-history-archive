# History of the Pacific States of North America

The *History of the Pacific States of North America* is a monumental historical work authored by Hubert Howe Bancroft. Spanning 21 volumes published between 1882 and 1890, the work serves as a comprehensive record of the Pacific region.

## Authorship and Methodology
Hubert Howe Bancroft was born on May 5, 1832, in Granville, Ohio. Before becoming a historical writer, he spent sixteen years, from 1852 to 1868, working as a bookseller in San Francisco. During his tenure as a bookseller, Bancroft accumulated an extensive library of historical materials. He eventually left his business to focus entirely on the publication of his historical research.

Because of the scale of his projects, Bancroft relied on a corps of assistants to collect the necessary data. Due to this collaborative method of data collection, the resulting publications are described as an encyclopaedia rather than a unified history. Bancroft provided a detailed account of his professional methods in his 1890 work, *Literary Industries*.

## Related Works and Legacy
Prior to the *History of the Pacific States of North America*, Bancroft published *Native Races of the Pacific States* in five volumes between 1874 and 1876. This earlier work utilized several principal authorities, including the *Noticias del Estado de Chihuahua* by Escudero (who visited ruins in 1819), an article from the first volume of the *Album Mexicano* (written by an author at Casas Grandes in 1842), and John Russell Bartlett's 1854 *Personal Narrative of Explorations and Incidents in Texas, New Mexico, California, Sonora and Chihuahua*, based on explorations conducted in 1851.

The vast collection of materials gathered by Bancroft was acquired by the University of California in 1905. His works are regarded as enduring and valuable storehouses of historical material.
