# Equal Rights to State Offices in Bohemia

## The Religious Demands of the Bohemian Estates
In the early 17th century, the estates of Bohemia sought to address religious controversies and grievances resulting from the misrule of King Rudolph. These tensions were further complicated by personal enmities, including Rudolph's resentment toward his brother and his secret negotiations with Christian of Anhalt, a leader of the German Protestants.

In March of the year preceding 1609, the estates drafted a document known as the "twenty-five Articles." This document formulated specific demands regarding religious matters. Among these demands was the request that the *Confessio Bohemica* be recognized as one of the fundamental laws of the kingdom and that complete religious liberty be granted to all classes of people.

## Equality in State Offices
A central tenet of the twenty-five Articles was the demand for professional and political equality between different religious groups. The estates requested that Roman Catholics and Protestants—a term used at the time to collectively describe the Bohemian Brethren, Lutherans, and Utraquists—should have an equal right to hold all offices of state. 

In addition to this demand for equal access to office, the estates sought to limit the power of the Jesuits to acquire land.

## Royal Response and Negotiations
King Rudolph viewed the twenty-five Articles as illegal and demanded that the document be delivered to him for destruction. However, the document remained in the possession of Budova, who refused the king's demand. 

To resolve the conflict, the estates appointed twelve negotiators, including Count Henry Matthias Thurn, to engage with the king and his councillors. Following protracted discussions, King Rudolph ultimately stated on March 31 that he could grant no concessions regarding matters of religion.
