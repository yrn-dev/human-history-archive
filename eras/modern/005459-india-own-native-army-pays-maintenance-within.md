# Land Defence and Military Maintenance in India

## Principles of Local Defence
In the administration of the British Empire, the approach to land defence varied by region. While the American colonies were initially expected to provide for their own land defence, this system eventually failed as the costs of the French wars exceeded local resources, leading the imperial exchequer of Great Britain to bear the expenses. In contrast, the principle of local self-defence was carried into practice by the East India Company in India from the beginning.

Following the American War, the imperial authorities assumed charge of land defences for most of the empire. However, India and the Hudson Bay territories remained exceptions to this rule, as the trading companies in those regions determined to pay their own expenses. This arrangement persisted until the end of the Napoleonic Wars.

## The Indian Army and Specialized Corps
The Indian army included various native units and specialized formations designed for specific strategic needs. One such unit was the 59th Bengal Native Infantry, which served during the first and second Sikh wars and was involved in the forcing of the Khyber Pass in 1842.

In 1847, the Corps of Guides was raised by Sir Harry Burnett Lumsden. This corps, consisting of both foot and horse soldiers, was stationed at Mardan on the Peshawar border. Its primary objectives were to collect intelligence both within and beyond the North-West frontier of India and to provide trustworthy guides for troops operating in the field. The Corps of Guides is noted for being one of the most famous regiments in the Indian army, and it was for this specific corps that Lumsden originated the khaki uniform.

## Regional Administration and Native States
The political geography of India included native states that maintained direct relations with the governor-general. An example of such a state is Baroda, located within the Gujarat province of Bombay. Baroda consists of four isolated divisions: Nausari, Kadi, Baroda (all in Gujarat proper), and Amreli with Okhamandal in the Kathiawar peninsula. These divisions are interlaced with other native states or British territory.

## International Agreements Regarding India
The maintenance of peace and security in India was also a subject of international diplomacy. Great Britain and Japan entered into agreements aimed at the consolidation and maintenance of general peace in the regions of India and Eastern Asia. These stipulations included the defence of the special interests of the high contracting parties and the maintenance of their territorial rights within these regions. Under these agreements, the two governments pledged to communicate fully and frankly if any of these rights or interests were in jeopardy to determine the necessary safeguards.
