# British Colonial Administration and Government Classes

The colonial empire consists of over fifty distinct governments, which are categorized into three primary classes of colonies and various dependencies. In some instances, these entities are associated into federated groups for administrative purposes.

## Classes of Colonial Government

The three classes of colonies are distinguished by the level of control the crown and the home government exercise over legislation, public officers, and finance.

### Crown Colonies
In crown colonies, the crown maintains entire control over legislation. Public officers within these colonies are under the control of the home government, and the imperial government directly controls the colony's finance. Legislation is typically conducted by the governor with the concurrence of a council nominated by the crown. However, in St Helena and Gibraltar, laws may be made by the governor alone. 

The nature of the crown council varies based on how the colony was acquired:
* **By conquest or cession:** The council's authority rests wholly on the crown, as seen in the crown council of Ceylon.
* **By settlement:** The council is created by the crown under imperial or local laws, as seen in the crown council of Jamaica.

### Representative Colonies
Colonies possessing representative institutions but not responsible government operate under a different structure. In these colonies, the crown generally cannot legislate by order in council and possesses only a veto on legislation. Laws are made by the governor with the concurrence of legislative bodies; where a second chamber exists with a preponderance of elected representatives, at least one such body must concur. 

While the home government does not directly control the finance of representative colonies, it usually influences important departures through its opinion. The home government retains control of the public officers. Examples of such legislative structures include:
* **The Bahamas, Bermuda, and Barbados:** These possess two legislative bodies, one nominated by the crown and one elected.
* **Malta and the Leeward Islands:** These have a single body that is partly nominated and partly elected.

### Responsible Colonies
In colonies with representative institutions and responsible government, legislation is carried out via parliamentary means similar to the system used at home. This includes a cabinet responsible to parliament. The crown retains a right of veto, which the governor exercises at his discretion for certain bills. 

In these colonies, finance is entirely under local control, and the imperial government is dissociated from material or moral responsibility for colonial debts. The home government has no control over public officers except for the governor. Executive councils are designated by parliamentary choice and appointed by the governor alone; other public officers are appointed nominally by the governor based on the advice of the executive council.

## Administrative Roles and Home Government

Colonial governors represent the crown, and their specific titles—including governors-general, governors, lieut.-governors, administrators, high commissioners, and commissioners—vary according to the status of the colony, dependency, or group they preside over.

In the home government, the crown receives advice on colonial administration from the secretaries of state for the colonies and for India. The colonial office was established on its current footing in 1854 with the appointment of a separate secretary of state for the colonies. Previously, until 1782, the "Council of Trade and Plantations" handled these affairs.

## India and Federated Groups

### Administration of India
Following the complete transference of East India Company territories to the crown in 1858, the board of control was abolished and replaced by the India Council, presided over by a secretary of state for India. Members of this council are prohibited from sitting in parliament. 

The Indian empire is divided into four minor commissionerships and nine great provinces. These provinces are presided over by various officials, including two governors (Madras and Bombay), five lieut.-governors (Burma, the Punjab, United Provinces [Agra and Oudh], Bengal, and Eastern Bengal and Assam), a chief commissioner (Central Provinces), and an agent to the governor-general (N.-W. Frontier Province). Supreme executive authority is vested in the viceroy in council. For legislation, the governor-general's council is augmented by fifteen crown-nominated members.

### Federated Groups
Federated groups allow separate governments to adjudge matters of common interest to a common government. 
* **Self-governing groups:** The Commonwealth of Australia and the Dominion of Canada are the two great self-governing federated groups. In South Africa, the self-governing colonies were united into one state, the Union of South Africa, in 1910.
* **Dependencies and Crown Colonies:** India serves as an example of a practical federation of dependencies. The federated Malay States and the Leeward Island group of the West Indies are examples of federated crown colonies and lesser dependencies.
