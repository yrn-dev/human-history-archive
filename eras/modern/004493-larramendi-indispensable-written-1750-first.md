# Larramendi, S.J.

Father Larramendi, S.J., was a writer and scholar whose work is noted for its contributions to the study of the Basque Provinces, specifically regarding the geography, customs, and linguistic history of the region.

## Corografia de Guipuzcoa
Larramendi authored a work titled *Corografia de Guipuzcoa*, written around 1750. This text is described as indispensable for those studying the manners and customs of the people of the region. Although written in the mid-18th century, it was not first printed until 1882 in Barcelona, with a subsequent edition published in San Sebastian in 1896.

Within this work, Larramendi provided a vehement defense of religious dances. These dances are part of a broader tradition of primitive race dances preserved by the Basques, which include ceremonial, war, animal, and agricultural dances, as well as those representing simple arts like weaving.

## Linguistic and Toponymic Studies
Larramendi is also recognized for his observations regarding the ancient toponymy of Spain. In 1760, he was among the scholars—including Andres de Poza (1587) and Juan B. Erro (1806)—who noted that certain ancient place-names transcribed by Greek and Latin writers bore a strong resemblance to actual place-names in the Basque language. 

These observations contributed to later academic discussions, such as those by W. von Humboldt in 1821, concerning the extension of a people speaking a language akin to Basque throughout Spain and potentially into Sardinia and Sicily.
