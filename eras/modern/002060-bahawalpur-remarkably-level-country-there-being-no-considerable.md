# Bahawalpur

## Geography and Topography
Bahawalpur is a native state of India located within the Punjab. The territory stretches for more than 300 miles along the left banks of the Indus, the Punjnud, and the Sutlej rivers. Its boundaries are defined by the Punjab and Sind to the north and east, and the Rajputana desert to the south.

The country is characterized as being remarkably level, with no considerable eminence within its limits. The only exceptions are occasional sand-hills, which seldom exceed 60 or 50 feet in height. The region's fertility is concentrated in a strip approximately 10 miles in breadth extending from the eastern or left river bank. Beyond this cultivable area lies a sandy desert where the wind creates ever-changing hills of sand; in this region, beasts and men sink as if in loose snow when they leave beaten paths.

In areas of the desert featuring hard level clay soil, the vegetation consists of tufts of grass, rue, various aromatic and bitter plants, and stunted acacias and mimosas. Much of the desert soil appears to be alluvial, evidenced by numerous traces of former streams. Where irrigation is practicable, the clayey tracts remain fertile. However, the region suffers from scanty rains and a scarcity of wells, which are generally 100 feet deep or more.

## Administration and Governance
Bahawalpur is the principal Mahommedan state in the Punjab, ranking second only to Patiala. Administratively, it is placed under the Punjab government. The state is governed by a chief holding the title of nawab. The current dynasty belongs to the Daudputra family from Sind and claims descent from Abbas, the uncle of the Prophet.

The dynasty established its independence from the Afghans toward the end of the 18th century. In 1838, the state entered into a treaty with the British, to which it has remained loyal.

## Demographics and Economy
As of 1901, the area of Bahawalpur covered 15,918 square miles with a population of 720,877, representing an 11% increase over the previous decade. The estimated gross revenue of the state was L146,700, and there is no tribute.

The economy benefited significantly from the introduction of canal irrigation in the 1870s, which resulted in the doubling of revenue. The state is well-connected by rail, as it is traversed throughout its length by the Southern Punjab and North-Western railways.

## The Town of Bahawalpur
The town of Bahawalpur is situated near the left bank of the Sutlej river and is located 65 miles from Mooltan. It possesses a railway station and educational facilities, including Anglo-vernacular schools and an arts college. 

A prominent feature of the town is a magnificent palace built in 1882 by Nawab Sadik Mahommed Khan, which can be seen from far across the Bikanir desert. The population of the town was 18,546 in 1901.
