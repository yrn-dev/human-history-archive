# University

The university serves as a center for higher education, research, and professional training, employing faculty members across a wide array of disciplines including economics, law, linguistics, and theology. These institutions are governed by various administrative structures and charters that define their academic and religious orientations.

## Governance and Administration
Some universities operate under specific charters to ensure a non-sectarian environment. For example, Cornell University's charter mandates that individuals of any religious denomination, or no denomination, are equally eligible for all appointments. To maintain this balance, the board of trustees is structured so that a majority cannot belong to a single religious sect or be of no religious sect. 

The administration of Cornell University is managed by a board of forty trustees, ten of whom are elected by alumni. Ex officio members of this board include the university president, the librarian of the Cornell Library in Ithaca, the state governor and lieutenant-governor, the speaker of the state assembly, the state commissioners of agriculture and education, and the president of the state agricultural society. Internal governance is divided between the university faculty—comprising the president, professors, and assistant professors—and special faculties that handle distinctively collegiate matters.

## Academic Appointments and Faculty
University professors often hold chairs in specialized fields and may transition between different institutions throughout their careers.

### Economics and Finance
Academic roles in commerce and political economy are prominent. William James Ashley, an English economist, served as a professor of constitutional history and political economy at Toronto University from 1888 to 1892, before moving to Harvard University as a professor of economic history. In 1901, he was appointed professor of commerce and finance at Birmingham University, and subsequently became the dean of the faculty of commerce in 1902. Similarly, Charles Francis Bastable served as the Professor of Political Economy and Regius Professor of Laws at the University of Dublin. In Italy, Luigi Luzzatti, who studied law at the university of Padua, later held a professorship of constitutional law at Padua and was transferred to the university of Rome.

### Humanities and Social Sciences
Universities also support extensive research in linguistics and philosophy. Edward Washburn Hopkins, after graduating from Columbia University and studying at Leipzig, became a professor of comparative philology and Sanskrit at Yale University in 1895. In the field of rhetoric and literature, Hiram Corson held various positions at Cornell University; he was a professor of rhetoric and oratory (1870-1871), professor of English literature and rhetoric (1886-1890), and professor of Anglo-Saxon and English literature (1872-1886) before becoming professor emeritus of English literature in 1903.

Other notable appointments include:
* **John Bascom**: Served as president of the University of Wisconsin and professor of moral and mental philosophy from 1874 to 1887.
* **Thomas William Rhys Davids**: Professor of Comparative Religion at the University of Manchester and previously a professor of Buddhist Literature and Pali at University College, London (1882-1904).
* **Vincent Henry Stanton**: Held the position of Ely Professor of Divinity at the University of Cambridge.

## Theological and Philosophical Studies
The study of theology and philosophy has historically been integrated into university systems. Johann Franz Buddeus, a German Lutheran divine, was appointed professor of moral philosophy at the university of Halle in 1693 and later became a professor of theology at Jena in 1705, where he eventually became the Primarius of his faculty. In the United States, Albert Henry Newman served as a professor at McMaster University in Toronto from 1881 to 1901 and later as a professor of Church History at Baylor University in Texas.
