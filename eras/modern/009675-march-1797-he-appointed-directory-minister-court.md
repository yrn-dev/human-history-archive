# Joseph Bonaparte (1768-1844)

## Early Life and Education
Joseph was born on January 7, 1768, at Corte in Corsica. His early education took place at a college in Autun, France. Following the death of his father, he returned to Corsica in 1784 to pursue legal studies at the university of Pisa. By June 1788, he had become a barrister at Bastia and was subsequently elected as a councillor for the municipality of Ajaccio.

## Political Alignment and Exile
Joseph, along with his brothers Lucien and Napoleon, adopted the democratic or French side of the political divide. This alignment led to his flight from Corsica with his family after the victory of the Paolist party. After a brief period in Paris, during which he became disgusted by the excesses of the Jacobins, he relocated to Marseilles. There, he married Mlle Julie Clary, the daughter of a local merchant. During this time, the Bonaparte family moved frequently to coordinate efforts aimed at the recovery of Corsica.

## Diplomatic and Military Activity
Joseph participated in the efforts to recover Corsica, including a mission to Genoa in 1795. In 1796, he accompanied Napoleon during the early stages of the Italian campaign. He was involved in negotiations with Sardinia that resulted in the armistice of Cherasco on April 28, and he was the individual tasked with delivering this news to the French government. Later, Joseph traveled to Leghorn and participated in the French expedition to recover Corsica, where he assisted Miot de Melito, the commissioner of the French Republic, in reorganizing the island.

## Ministerial Appointments and the Roman Republic
In March 1797, the Directory appointed Joseph as minister to the court of Parma. By early summer, he proceeded to Rome in the same capacity. During his tenure, tensions rose between the French Republic and the Vatican. It is indicated that the French Directory and Napoleon ordered Joseph to encourage revolutionary movements within Rome. 

On December 28, 1797, a disturbance occurred opposite the French embassy, resulting in the death of General Leonard Duphot. Following this event, Joseph left Rome, which shortly thereafter became a republic.

## Parliamentary Career
After leaving Rome, Joseph returned to Paris to enter parliamentary life. He served as one of the members representing Corsica in the Council of Five Hundred. He did not make a significant mark during his time in the chamber and retired from this position in 1799.
