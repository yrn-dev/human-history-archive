# BARRES, MAURICE

## Early Life and Education
Maurice Barres was born on September 22, 1862, at Charmes (Vosges). He received his education at the *lycee* of Nancy. In 1883, Barres moved to Paris to pursue further studies in law. During this period, he began his involvement in journalism, serving as a contributor to the monthly periodical *Jeune France* and launching his own periodical, *Les Taches d'encre*, though the latter only lasted for a few months.

## Literary Career and Individualism
Following four years of work in journalism, Barres traveled to Italy. This experience led to the publication of *Sous l'oeil des barbares* (1888), which served as the first volume of a *trilogie du moi*. This trilogy was later completed by *Un Homme libre* (1889) and *Le Jardin de Berenice* (1891). In these works, Barres presented a worldview divided between *moi* (the self) and the "barbarians," a category he used to describe anyone antipathetic to his own individuality.

He further explored these apologies for individualism in *L'Ennemi des lois* (1892). Additionally, he wrote *Du sang, de la volupte et de la mort* (1893), a volume of travel impressions. The style of his early writings is characterized as being elaborate and frequently very obscure.

## Political Activity
Barres applied his theories of individualism to the political sphere, becoming an ardent partisan of General Boulanger. While in Nancy, he directed a Boulangist newspaper. His political career led to his election as a deputy in 1889, a seat in the legislature he held until 1893. In 1894, his play *Une Journee parlementaire* was produced at the Comedie Francaise.

## National Energy and Later Works
In 1897, Barres began a new trilogy titled *Le Roman de l'energie nationale*, starting with the publication of *Les Deracines*. This series serves as a plea for the preservation of the distinctive qualities of old French provinces and for local patriotism. The first novel in the series follows seven young Lorrainers who travel to Paris to seek fortune. The second novel, *L'Appel au soldat* (1900), focuses on the history of Boulangism and features six of the original characters. The final sequel, *Leurs figures* (1902), addresses the Panama scandals.

Barres continued to write on themes of nationalism and patriotism in subsequent years. His later works include:
* *Scenes et doctrines du nationalisme* (1902)
* *Les Amities francaises* (1903), in which he argues that patriotism should be instilled through the early study of national history
* *Ce que j'ai vu a Rennes* (1904)
* *Au service de l'Allemagne* (1905), which details the experiences of an Alsatian conscript serving in a German regiment
* *Le Voyage de Sparte* (1906)
