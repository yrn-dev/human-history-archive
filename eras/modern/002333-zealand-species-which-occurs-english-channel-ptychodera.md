# New Zealand

New Zealand is a region characterized by a diverse range of biological species, specific legal frameworks, and traditional crafts. Its natural history includes a variety of marine life, insects, and prehistoric reptiles, while its administrative systems reflect its relationship with the British Empire.

## Fauna and Marine Life

The marine provinces of New Zealand are home to several distinct species. Among these are members of the genus *Balanoglossus*; specifically, a species of *Balanoglossus sensu stricto* found on the New Zealand coast has been noted as being hardly distinguishable from a species occurring off the coast of Japan. In the broader context of *Balanoglossus*, approximately thirty species are known across principal marine provinces ranging from Greenland to New Zealand.

The waters of New Zealand also contain mackerel. These extra-Atlantic species differ in many conspicuous points from those found in the Indian Ocean, as well as those found in the Japanese seas, which are more closely allied to European mackerel.

## Insects and Prehistoric Life

In terms of insect populations, New Zealand is described as being excessively poor regarding certain species. For example, while there are about 2200 known species of insects distributed globally (excluding polar regions), New Zealand numbers only 8 species, a stark contrast to the numerous species found in Australia.

The geological record of New Zealand also provides evidence of prehistoric marine reptiles. Remains of Ichthyosaurians from the Lower Cretaceous age have been discovered in New Zealand, as well as in South America, Australia, the East Indies, and Europe. These remains are noted for their very wide geographical distribution.

## Traditional Crafts and Materials

New Zealand utilizes local botanical resources for traditional manufacturing. Specifically, strips from the New Zealand flax plant, *Phormium tenax*, are used to create baskets.

## Legal Systems

Within the British Empire, New Zealand's law of bankruptcy has been modelled upon the English system. New Zealand, along with Victoria, South Australia, and Western Australia, follows the lines of existing English acts. However, New Zealand has implemented a specific improvement over the English system: while a bankrupt in the United Kingdom is under no obligation to apply for discharge, a bankrupt in New Zealand is bound to apply for their discharge within a fixed period. Failure to do so results in the individual being guilty of a contempt of court.

## Comparative Geography
