# Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege

The *Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege* is a series of publications concerning the state treaties of Austria. Issued by the Imperial Commission for the Modern History of Austria, the first volume of this series was published in 1907.

## Purpose and Methodology
The *Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege* was developed as a resource for the study of Austrian diplomatic history and is described as being of the utmost value. The series is compiled according to a specific organizational principle: it devotes separate volumes to the treaties entered into with various different states. This methodology is similar to that used in the *Recueil des traites conclus par la Russie* authored by T. T. de Martens.

## Context within Austrian Historical Sources
The publication of the *Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege* followed a period of increased systematic exploitation of public and private archives that began in the latter half of the 19th century. It exists alongside other significant collections of Austrian historical sources, such as:

*   **Fontes rerum Austriacarum**: Published by the Historical Commission of the Imperial Academy of Sciences at Vienna, this series began in 1855 and is divided into *Scriptores* (with a 9th volume in 1904) and *Diplomataria et Acta* (with a 58th volume in 1906).
*   **Quellen und Forschungen zur Geschichte, Literatur und Sprache Oesterreichs und seiner Kronlaender**: Edited by J. E. Wackernagel and J. Hirn, with a 10th volume appearing in 1906.
*   **Scriptores rerum Austriacarum veteres et genuini**: An early collection of authorities published by Hieronymus Pez (1721-1725), later reprinted in 1745 and again in 1793-1794 as *Rerum Austriacarum scriptores* by A. Rauch.

## Relation to Other Treaty Collections
Prior to the issuance of the *Oesterreichische Staatsvertraege*, the most notable collections of treaties were those produced by L. Neumann and A. de Plason. Neumann's *Recueil des traites conclus par l'Autriche avec les puissances etrangeres depuis 1763* consisted of six volumes published in Leipzig in 1855. This work was subsequently continued by A. de Plason in a series of 18 volumes published in Vienna between 1877 and 1905.
