# AUTOMATISM

In philosophical and technical contexts, the term automatism refers to processes, actions, or mechanisms that operate without conscious volition, deliberate effort, or manual intervention. The term is applied across several distinct fields, including philosophy, psychology, psychical research, and mechanical engineering.

## Philosophical and Psychological Perspectives
In philosophical terminology, automatism is used in two primary senses. In the field of ethics, it refers to the view that human beings are not responsible for their actions, and consequently, those actions possess no moral value. In psychology, it refers to any actions that do not result from conscious endeavour or conation.

Descartes argued that all actions of lower animals are purely mechanical. This theory has been extended to humans, suggesting that while mechanical phenomena of action occur, they are entirely disconnected from the phenomena of consciousness. In this view, physical changes in the brain produce a specific action, while any accompanying volition or mental desire is not prior to, nor causally connected with, that physical change. This position has been supported by Shadworth Hodgson and T. Huxley. It is distinct from "double aspect theory" or psychophysical parallelism, which posits that both physical phenomena and mental states result from a single substance termed "mind stuff."

## Types of Automatic Acts
Psychologically, automatic acts are categorized into two main types:
* **Purely Automatic:** Actions that continue while the individual's attention is focused on entirely different subjects, such as cycling.
* **Relative Automatism:** Actions where the individual focuses attention on a particular part of the act or its end goal, while the other component parts are performed unconsciously.

Additionally, "motor automatism" describes non-reflex movements of voluntary muscles performed in a waking state but not controlled by ordinary waking consciousness. These range from vague muscle movements used in "bier-divination" or witch-seeking to more specialized actions, such as those involving a divining rod to trace criminals or locate water.

## Psychical Research and Sensory Phenomena
Students of psychical research use the term "sensory automatism" to describe centrally initiated hallucinations. These may be provoked by crystal-gazing, the use of a shell (shell-hearing), or other sensory triggers.

A significant area of study in this field is automatic writing, which is writing performed without the volition of the agent. The writer may be entirely unconscious of the words being produced, or they may be aware of the immediate words they are writing without perceiving the meaning of the whole. This phenomenon can occur during normal wakefulness, in states of hystero-epilepsy, other morbid states, or in spontaneous or induced trances. 

Automatic writing was prominent in American spiritualism between 1850 and 1860, with many reports of long texts produced under spirit guidance, some written in "unknown tongues." Notable published examples include *Messages from the Spirit Life* by J. Murray Spear, *The Healing of the Nations* by Charles Linton, and Andrew J. Davis's *Great Harmonia*. Similar processes occur in speech centres, resulting in utterances ranging from unintelligible babblings to "Glossolalia." In the case of Helene Smith, Th. Flournoy observed that such utterances could form a real language based on one already known to the speaker.

## Mechanical and Industrial Automatism
In engineering and industry, automatism refers to machinery designed to save labour by performing tasks automatically.

* **Waste Management:** A furnace patented in 1891 by Henry Whiley for the Manchester corporation features automatic feeding, stoking, and clinkering. Refuse is conducted via a hopper onto movable eccentric grate bars that automatically push the material into the furnace and eventually through a flap-door. The Manchester corporation utilized 28 such cells, burning 6 to 8 tons of refuse per cell every 24 hours.
* **Manufacturing:** The production of covered buttons utilizes elaborate automatic machinery. This process was advanced in Birmingham by B. Sanders around 1807, who created buttons from two metal disks locked together. In the United States, Samuel Williston began the mechanical manufacture of covered buttons in 1827.
* **Boiler Systems:** In certain water-tube boilers used on ships and some land boilers, the feeding of water is automatically regulated by a mechanism actuated by a float, though hand regulation is typically provided as a backup.
