# Pufendorf

Samuel Pufendorf was a writer whose works on law and ethics were influential to later scholars and translators. His contributions to the study of natural and civil law are preserved through his treatises and the subsequent academic analysis of his theories.

## Legal and Moral Treatises
Pufendorf authored several significant works concerning the nature of law and human duty. Among these is the treatise *De Jure Naturae et Gentium*, which focuses on the law of nature and nations. Additionally, he wrote a smaller treatise titled *De Officio Hominis et Civis*, which addresses the duties of the human being and the citizen.

## Influence on Jean Barbeyrac
The work of Pufendorf was central to the career of the French jurist Jean Barbeyrac (1674–1744). Barbeyrac, who served as a professor of public law at Groningen and previously held professorships in Berlin and Lausanne, gained much of his fame through his translation of *De Jure Naturae et Gentium*. 

Barbeyrac's translation is particularly noted for its preface and accompanying notes. In his own academic approach, Barbeyrac followed the fundamental principles of Pufendorf and Locke. Utilizing Pufendorf's framework, Barbeyrac skillfully developed a theory of moral obligation, which he linked to the will or command of God.

## Historical Collaborations
Beyond his theoretical writings, Pufendorf engaged in historical documentation. He was assisted in the production of his work *Histoire de Charles X Gustave* by Erik Dahlberg, who is also known for the collection of drawings titled *Suecia antiqua et hodierna*.
