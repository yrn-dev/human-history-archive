# Banking and Currency Circulation in England

## Note Circulation and Average Figures
In the early years of the 19th century and the latter years of the 18th, note circulation served as a very important component of business. However, following that period, deposits became far more significant and have remained so. 

Based on average circulation figures from 1906, the total circulation in England was L29,443,000. This total was comprised of the following:
* Bank of England: L28,890,000
* Joint-stock banks: L429,000
* Private banks: L124,000

For comparison, during the same period, Scotland had a circulation of L7,477,000 and Ireland had L6,452,000, bringing the total circulation for the United Kingdom to L43,372,000.

## Banking Reserves and Fluctuations
The reserve of the Bank of England provides the basis for the business requirements of the country, though it has been regarded as inadequate, even in years when it was larger than in previous periods. These figures do not show a uniform increase and fluctuate more severely than decennial averages suggest. 

Recorded figures for the reserve include:
* 1845: L8,500,000
* 1855: L8,400,000
* 1865: L8,000,000
* 1875: L11,600,000
* 1885: L15,100,000
* 1895: L29,900,000
* 1906: L23,500,000

The volatility of these reserves is evidenced by the fact that the L15,100,000 recorded in 1885 was followed by L12,700,000 in 1888. Similarly, the L29,900,000 in 1895 was followed by L34,600,000 in 1896 and then L21,200,000 in 1899.

Beyond the Bank of England's reserve, other banks maintain their own reserves. These are held partly as balances at the Bank of England and partly as bank notes and specie in their own tills. Because of this overlap, these figures are susceptible to being estimated twice.

## Deposits and Total Resources
Historically, obtaining trustworthy statistics for banking in the United Kingdom has been difficult for periods extending back more than fifty years. English banks, in particular, were reticent; Scottish banks generally did not publish accounts until 1865. While Irish joint-stock bank cash balances and deposits were published collectively starting in 1840, general statements remained estimates for a long time due to the reluctance of many banks to publish balance-sheets.

Trustworthy estimates began with a paper by W. Newmarch in 1851, which suggested that total deposits in Ireland, Scotland, England, and Wales ranged from L250,000,000 to L360,000,000. By 1872, an estimate in Palgrave's *Notes on Banking* placed this figure between L430,000,000 and L450,000,000 (excluding bank capitals and deposits in discount houses). By the close of 1906, the total deposits, including acceptances, reached approximately L997,000,000. Total resources for 1906, which included note circulation (approximately L177,500,000), reserves, and capitals, were also recorded.

## Regional Banking in Ireland
In Ireland, the Bank of Ireland held L3,597,850 and private and joint-stock banks held L2,456,261, totaling L37,276,254. By May 1907, these amounts were reduced. The actual issue for 17 joint-stock banks (L437,693) and 12 private banks (L122,536) remained far below their authorized issues (L1,084,836 and L482,744, respectively). 

This discrepancy exists because the circulation of these banks is limited to very small areas. Notes from country banks are largely unknown outside the immediate vicinity of their issuance. Although these notes may be payable in London, cashing them often presents considerable difficulty.
