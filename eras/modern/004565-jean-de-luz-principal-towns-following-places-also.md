# Basses-Pyrenees

Basses-Pyrenees is a department of south-western France located at the angle of the Bay of Biscay. Formed in 1790, the department was created from three districts of Gascony—Labourd, Soule, and Basse-Navarre—as well as two-thirds of Bearn. The districts of Labourd, Soule, and Basse-Navarre comprise the Basque region of France and are situated in the west of the department.

## Geography and Climate
The department is bounded to the north by Gers and Landes, to the east by Hautes-Pyrenees (which contains two enclaves consisting of five communes within Basses-Pyrenees), to the south by Spain, and to the west by the Atlantic Ocean. Its total area is 2,977 square miles, and as of 1906, its population was 426,817.

The southern portion of the department is occupied by the lower and western summits of the Pyrenees. The mountains increase in height from west to east. In the south of St Jean-de-Luz, the peak of the Rhune reaches 2,950 feet. The peak of Orhy, located in the south of the valley of Mauleon, reaches 6,618 feet. Higher elevations are found on the meridian of Orthez, which marks the boundary of Bearn, including the peak of Anie (8,215 feet), the southern peak of Ossau (9,465 feet), and Mourrous (9,760 feet) on the border of Hautes-Pyrenees. The main range's crest-line generally forms the frontier between France and Spain, where forts guard the Aspe and Nive upper valleys and their passes into Spain.

The rest of the department consists of hills divided by fertile valleys to the west of the Gave de Pau, and a region of plateaus and heaths to the northeast of the same river. The climate is mild with abundant rainfall, influenced by the west wind from the gulf of Gascony. While spring is rainy, summer and autumn are considered the best seasons, as the sea moderates summer heat.

## Hydrography and Infrastructure
The rivers of the department generally flow northwest. The Adour, the Bidouze, and the Nive are navigable on their lower courses. Most streams meet in the Adour via the Nive, the Bidouze, and the Gave de Pau. In the northeast, the two Luys flow directly into the Adour in Landes. In the southwest, the Bidassoa and the Nivelle flow directly into the sea. The Adour forms the boundary between Landes and Basses-Pyrenees, entering the sea below Bayonne via a shifting bar.

The department is divided into five arrondissements: Mauleon, Orthez, Oloron, Bayonne, and Pau. These are further divided into 559 communes and 41 cantons. Administratively, it belongs to the district of the XVIII. army corps and the educational circumscription (academie) of Bordeaux. It also constitutes the diocese of Bayonne. The Southern Company owns the railway lines, the primary route of which runs from Bayonne to Toulouse via Pau and Orthez.

## Economy and Industry
Basses-Pyrenees is mainly agricultural despite having extensive uncultivated land. The chief cereals are wheat and maize, while vegetables, flax, and potatoes are also produced. Livestock rearing is common for pigs, sheep, cattle, and horses due to abundant pasture. The vine is grown on lower slopes sheltered from the north wind; the most renowned wines are those of Jurancon, produced near Pau. Important fruits include pears, cider-apples, and chestnuts. Woods cover about one-thirteenth of the department, owned by private individuals, communes, and a small portion by the government.

The department's interior commerce involves the exchange of products between the plain and mountain regions. Industrial products include:
* **Quarry products:** Building-stone and salt.
* **Manufactures:** Sashes, woollen caps, paper, chocolate, and cord slippers.
* **Other industries:** Saw-mills, flour-mills, and tanneries.
* **Specialties:** "Bayonne hams" and other table delicacies are prepared at Orthez.

At Le Boucau, located 3 miles from Bayonne, there are chemical works and large metallurgical works known as the *Forges de l'Adour*. Bayonne serves as the principal port. Leading imports include wool, grain, phosphates, minerals, and coal, while exports consist mainly of resinous products, salt, wine, minerals, mine-props, and timber.

## Principal Towns and Sites of Interest
Pau serves as the capital and the seat of a court of appeal. Other principal towns include St Jean-de-Luz, Biarritz, Eaux-Bonnes, Orthez, Oloron, and Bayonne. Bayonne and St Jean-de-Luz have considerable fishing populations, and Biarritz and St Jean-de-Luz are frequently visited by winter tourists. The air in Pau is noted for agreeing with delicate constitutions and invalids.

Mineral springs are found at Salies, St Christau, Cambo-les-Bains (visited by Basques on St John's Eve), Eaux-Chaudes, and Eaux-Bonnes.

Other sites of interest include:
* **Lescar:** Features a church from the 16th and 12th centuries that was once a cathedral.
* **Montaner:** Contains a stronghold built in 1380 by Gaston Phoebus, viscount of Bearn and count of Foix.
* **Sauveterre:** A town on the Gave d'Oloron featuring a church in Gothic and Romanesque styles, an old bridge, and the remains of a feudal castle.
