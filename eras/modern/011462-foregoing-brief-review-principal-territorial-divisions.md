# The Foregoing Brief Review of the Principal Territorial Divisions

## Distribution of Life in Asia
A review of the principal territorial divisions in Asia demonstrates a close dependence between the distribution of forms of life and climatic conditions. This relationship is further evidenced by the specific characteristics of the region's flora and fauna.

## Northern Asian Flora
The flora found throughout northern Asia is essentially the same as that of northern Europe, with differences occurring primarily in species rather than in genera. Notable absences in the region east of the Ural include all heaths and the oak. The primary trees found on the mountains are birch, larch, and pines. On lower ground, poplars, alders, and willow are the principal trees. In Siberia, the northern limit for the pine is approximately 70 deg. N.

## Temperate Zones and Regional Diffusion
A flora that is essentially European in character extends along the warm temperate zone, stretching from the Himalaya to the Mediterranean. While many European species reach the central Himalaya, few are known in the eastern portions of the range. However, genera common to both Europe and the Himalaya are more abundant and are found throughout the chain across all elevations.

Simultaneously, there is a diffusion of Chinese and Japanese forms along this same zone. These forms are most numerous in the eastern Himalaya and become less frequent as one moves toward the west.
