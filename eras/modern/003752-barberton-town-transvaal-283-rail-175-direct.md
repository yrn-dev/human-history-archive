# BARBERTON

## Overview and Geography
Barberton is a town located in the Transvaal. It is situated 283 miles by rail (or 175 miles in a direct line) east of Pretoria and 136 miles west-north-west of Delagoa Bay. The town is built on the side of a valley known as De Kaap, positioned beneath a bold headland of the Drakensberg that towers over the settlement. Barberton sits at an elevation of 2,825 feet above sea level.

## History and Development
The town dates from 1886 and owes its existence to the discovery of gold within the Kaap valley. It serves as the chief town of the district of the same name. Architecturally, the town features several fine public buildings, which are primarily grouped around President Square.

During the war of 1899-1902, Barberton was the site of military action; on September 13, 1900, the Boers were driven out of the town by General (later Sir John) French.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
Barberton is connected to the Lourenco Marques-Pretoria trunk railway via a branch line. This branch line is 35 miles long and runs north-east through mountainous country before joining the main line at Kaapmuiden.

## Regional Geography
The town is located in a region characterized by the Drakensberg. Approximately 30 miles south of Barberton, the Komati River descends the Drakensberg through a pass. The Komati is a river of south-eastern Africa that rises in the Ermelo district of the Transvaal at an elevation of about 5,000 feet and eventually reaches the Indian Ocean at Delagoa Bay.
