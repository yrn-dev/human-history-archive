# Remarkable Phenomena of the Hotter Seas and Regions of Asia

The hotter seas and regions of Asia exhibit a variety of remarkable meteorological, biological, and geographical phenomena, ranging from violent revolving storms to specialized flora and fauna adapted to extreme elevations and climates.

## Meteorological Phenomena
Among the most notable phenomena of the hotter seas of Asia are the revolving storms, or cyclones. These occur frequently during the hot months in the China Sea and the Indian Ocean. In the China Sea, these storms are known as typhoons. They typically originate near the island of Formosa and advance from the north-east to the south-west, though they occasionally move from east to west, extending to approximately the 10th degree of North latitude.

In the Bay of Bengal, cyclones appear to originate over the Nicobar and Andaman islands. They generally propagate in a north-westward direction, striking the east coast of the Indian peninsula before often advancing easterly across the delta of the Ganges with extreme violence. These storms occur from June to October, and less frequently in November; they are believed to be caused by south-west monsoon currents meeting adverse currents from the north. In both the China Sea and the Indian Ocean, storms tend to advance toward areas of greatest heat, and the wind invariably circulates from north by west through south to east.

In other regions, such as the neighborhood of Kermán, dry fogs or dust storms occur. These can be so dense that large objects, such as trees and houses, cannot be distinguished at a distance of a hundred paces.

## Marine and Freshwater Biology
The seas of south Asia contain a diverse array of marine life. While the cod family is represented by only one or two species, soles and allied fishes are numerous along the coasts. Other common inhabitants include:
* **Physostomi:** Siluroids are abundant in muddy waters and estuaries; some males exhibit the remarkable habit of carrying ova in their mouths until hatching.
* **Clupeidae:** Herrings are most abundant, with anchovies and sardines appearing in shoals at irregular intervals.
* **Cartilaginous Fishes:** True sharks, hammer-headed sharks, saw-fishes, electrical rays, and ordinary rays and skates are found in considerable numbers. One species of dog-fish extends from the Indian seas to the Cape of Good Hope.
* **Other forms:** Flying fishes, gar-fish, sea-horses, pipe-fishes, and the gelatinous *Harpodon* (bumalo) are also present. Marine eels (*Muraenidae*) are more numerous in the Malay Archipelago than in the Indian seas.

Freshwaters in southern Asia are characterized by a deficiency of typical *Acanthopterygii* forms. They are primarily inhabited by siluroids, carp, spined or simple eels, and walking and climbing fishes. The latter are peculiar to Africa and south-eastern Asia and are organized to breathe out of water, allowing them to survive in regions of periodical rain and drought. The *Cyprinidae* (carp) are largely represented and can reach sizes unknown in Europe; for example, a *Barbus* in the Tigris has reached a weight of 300 lb. While *Salmonidae* are absent from southern Asian waters, trout are found in streams flowing into the Caspian beyond the Indus watershed.

## Regional Flora and Vegetation
The vegetation of Asia varies significantly by latitude and elevation:

**Northern and Temperate Zones:** The plants and animals of northern Asia resemble those of adjacent Europe. Siberia, north of the 50th parallel, has a climate similar to Europe, though with hotter summers and more severe winters. A temperate belt extending from the Mediterranean across to northern China—including Afghanistan, northern Persia, and Asia Minor—also shows a marked similarity to south Europe.

**Tropical and Mountainous Zones:** The tropical flora of eastern India is continuous with that of the Malayan peninsula and islands. In the Malayan Islands, the uniformity of humidity and temperature leads to a predominance of certain tropical forms, such as nutmeg and liquidambar. Due to the proximity of Australia, Australian types like the casuarina are found on the mountains of eastern Java, and Australian genera extend to southern China and the Philippines.

The Himalaya chain exhibits a transition in flora. Higher, cooler ranges show a closer approach to European forms, including alpine plants identical to those of the north Arctic regions. On the Tibetan plateau, increased dryness causes the flora to assume Siberian features. Remarkably, assemblages of marine plants are found growing at elevations of 14,000 to 15,000 ft. above the sea, particularly near salt lakes.

**Arabian Peninsula:** Cultivated plants in Arabia—such as wheat, barley, cotton, indigo, lemons, and dates—are similar to those of northern India. Coffee is restricted to the western hills. In the south-western mountainous region of Arabia Felix, where summits reach 6,000 to 7,000 ft., the rainfall supports a more luxuriant vegetation similar to that of Baluchistan, Afghanistan, and southern Persia.
