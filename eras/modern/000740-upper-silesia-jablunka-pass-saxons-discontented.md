# Upper Silesia and the Jablunka Pass

## Geography of the Jablunka Pass
The Jablunka pass is a significant mountain pass located in the western Carpathians at an elevation of 1,970 feet. It serves as the principal route connecting Hungary and Silesia and is crossed by the Breslau-Budapest railway. Geographically, the pass is situated just to the east of the Beskid proper (or western Beskid group), a mountain group that extends from the river Poprad to a point slightly west of the Jablunka pass. This region is characterized by high peaks, including Smrk (4,395 ft.), Lissa Hora (4,350 ft.), and Ossus (5,106 ft.).

## Upper Silesian Industry and Demographics
Upper Silesia contains a prominent coal and iron district. A central town in this industrial region is Königshütte, located 122 miles southeast of Breslau by rail and 3 miles south of Beuthen. Founded in 1797, Königshütte was originally government-owned before being transferred to a company. It is home to the largest iron-works in Silesia, which features rolling-mills, zinc-works, and puddling works. The town also supports trades in coal, wood, and the manufacture of glass and bricks. As of 1900, the population of Königshütte was 57,919, nearly half of whom were Poles.

## Military Campaigns of 1742
During the campaign of 1742, the region became a focal point of conflict between Frederick and Maria Theresa. While Frederick had conducted a foray southwards toward Vienna, Prince Charles of Lorraine moved to cut off the Prussian forces from Silesia. Simultaneously, Hungarian levies entered Upper Silesia via the Jablunka Pass. 

During these operations, the Saxons, who were demoralized and discontented, abandoned the campaign and marched back to their own country. This left Frederick and his Prussian forces to fall back through Leutomischl and Zwittau to Kuttenberg in Bohemia. From there, Frederick maintained contact with Broglie and Silesia, the latter of which was accessible following the surrender of Glatz.

## Regional Context and Related Territories
Silesia is situated near Bohemia, a kingdom and crownland of Austria that is bounded to the northeast by Prussian Silesia. To the northwest of this region lies Saxony and Upper Lusatia. The portion of Upper Lusatia retained by Saxony consists of approximately 960 square miles as of 1845. This area is generally picturesque and mountainous, with chief towns including Zittau and Bautzen, and industries focused on cloth making, coal mining, and linen weaving.

In the broader historical context of the mid-18th century, Silesia remained a primary objective of Frederick's warfare. In subsequent campaigns, such as those in 1745, Frederick operated in Silesia with forces concentrated at the camp of Frankenstein, located between Neisse and Glatz. During this period, the region saw a "small war" and major engagements, including the battle of Hohenfriedberg (or Striegau) on June 4, where Frederick defeated a combined force of Saxons and Austrians under Prince Charles.
