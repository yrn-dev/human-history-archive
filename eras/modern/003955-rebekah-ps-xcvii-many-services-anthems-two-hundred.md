# Sir Joseph Barnby

## Life and Career
Sir Joseph Barnby (1838–1896) was an English conductor and musical composer. Born in York on August 12, 1838, he was the son of Thomas Barnby, who served as an organist. Barnby's musical training began at a young age; he was a chorister at York Minster starting at age seven. He later received his education at the Royal Academy of Music, where he studied under Charles Lucas and Cipriani Potter.

In 1862, Barnby was appointed as the organist of St Andrew's, Wells Street, in London, a position where he is credited with raising the services to a high degree of excellence. He began conducting "Barnby's Choir" in 1864. In 1871, he succeeded Gounod as the conductor of the Albert Hall Choral Society, a role he maintained until his death. His professional appointments continued with his tenure as director of music and precentor at Eton in 1875. In 1892, he became the principal of the Guildhall School of Music and was knighted in July of that same year.

## Musical Works
Barnby's compositions spanned several genres of sacred and secular music. His notable works include the oratorio *Rebekah* and *Ps. xcvii.* He composed many anthems and services, as well as some pieces for the organ. He was a prolific writer of hymn-tunes, producing two hundred and forty-six of them, which were published in a single volume in 1897. Additionally, he wrote part-songs, including the popular piece "Sweet and Low."

## Conducting and Influence
As a conductor, Barnby was characterized by his precision and rectitude, qualities he instilled in his singers. However, he was noted for lacking ideality or the higher kind of imagination, traits described as typical of a north-countryman. While he displayed little practical sympathy for opera, he organized a notable concert performance of *Parsifal* at London's Albert Hall in 1884. 

Barnby played a significant role in promoting Gounod's sacred music, particularly among the less educated members of the London public. His conducting career also extended to the Cardiff Festivals, which he conducted in 1895 and 1892.

## Death
Sir Joseph Barnby died in London on January 28, 1896. Following a special service held at St Paul's Cathedral, he was buried in Norwood Cemetery.
