# Banka

Banka (also known as Banca or Bangka) is an island located in the Dutch East Indies. It is situated off the east coast of Sumatra, separated from the mainland by the Banka Strait, which reaches a minimum width of approximately 9 miles. To the east, the island is separated from Billiton by the broader, island-studded Gaspar Strait.

## Geography and Physical Features
Banka measures 138 miles in length and has an extreme breadth of 62 miles. Including several small adjacent islands, its total area is 4,460 square miles. The island's terrain is characterized by dry and stony soil, with the majority of the surface covered in forests where the logwood tree is particularly abundant.

The island features hills covered in vegetation up to their summits; the highest of these is Maras, located in the north, reaching an elevation of 2,760 feet. Geologically, Banka is similar to the Malay Peninsula. Its formations consist primarily of Devonian and Silurian slate, granite, and are frequently covered by alluvium, laterite (a red ironstone clay of low fertility), and sandstone. The granite formations extend from the west-north-west to the south-south-east, creating short and irregular hill-chains. Because these hills are generally positioned near the east coast, the rivers on the west coast are longer. There are no volcanoes on the island.

## Climate
The climate of Banka is influenced by monsoons. From May to August, during the south-east monsoon, the weather is hot and dry. The wet and cool season occurs from November to February, accompanying the north-west monsoon. The island receives a mean annual rainfall of 120 inches, with rain occurring on an average of 168 days per year. This heavy rainfall is of significant importance to the industry of tin-streaming.

## Tin Production
Banka is principally noted for the production of tin, a mineral discovered on the island in 1710. The production of tin is a government monopoly. The mineral is found in lodes and as stream-tin. The mining operations are carried out by large numbers of Chinese workers who reside in their own villages.

The island is organized into nine mining districts containing approximately 120 mines under government control. These mines employ 12,000 workmen and have achieved an annual production of as much as 12,000 tons of tin. The chief rivers—the Waringin, Kotta, and Jering—are used to transport the tin and are navigable for some 19 miles from their mouths.

## Population
As of 1905, the total population of Banka was 115,189. This population includes 40,000 Chinese and 70,000 natives. The native population is composed mainly of immigrant Malayan peoples, though aborigines are also represented.
