## Cartographic Projections and the Constant h

In the study of map projections, the constant $h$ is used to determine the scale and distortion of various representations of the earth.

### Perspective and Stereographic Projections
In certain perspective representations, the value of $h$ influences the projection's characteristics. When $h = 1$, the projection is stereographic. For a perspective representation of a hemisphere, the optimal values to minimize misrepresentation are determined to be $h = 1.47$ and $k = 2.034$.

### Conical and Orthomorphic Projections
In Lambert's orthomorphic projection (also known as Gauss's projection), $h$ is a constant used in the orthomorphic conical projection with two standard parallels. Sir John Herschel suggested a value of $h = 1/3$, which is considered admirably suited for a map of the world, resulting in a fan-shaped representation with minimal distortion. 

The behavior of the projection changes as $h$ varies:
*   **Small values of h:** The angles between meridian lines become very small.
*   **Limit of h = 0:** The meridians become parallel, the vertex of the cone is removed to infinity, and the projection becomes Mercator's projection.
*   **Equatorial scale:** If the equator is a parallel true to scale, the radius of the equator is expressed as $1/h$.

### Equal-Area Projections
In Lambert's conical equal-area projection with one standard parallel, $h$ is defined as $\cos^2 \frac{1}{2}z_1$, where $z_1$ is the co-latitude of the parallel to be correctly represented. If $z_1 = \theta$, then $h = 1$, and the result is a zenithal equal-area projection where the scale at the pole is correct and meridians are inclined at their true angles. In H. C. Albers' conical equal-area projection with two standard parallels, $h$ is one of two constants (along with $C$) derived from the co-latitudes of two selected parallels to ensure they are represented at their true lengths.
