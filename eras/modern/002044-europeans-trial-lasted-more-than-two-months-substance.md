During the period following the German religious war of 1546, the European mind was heavily concentrated on the struggle for European ascendancy. ## Conflict in China
In 1900, Europeans in China faced increasing threats from the Boxers. The situation escalated when the German ambassador was shot by a soldier of the imperial guard in the streets of Peking. In response, foreign ambassadors established a fortification within the more favorably situated embassies and endured a siege that lasted two months. The legations were eventually relieved by a combined allied force of 20,000 men led by a German general, causing the old Empress to flee northwestward. During these events, some European troops committed grave atrocities against the Chinese civil population.

## Civil Calendar
The civil calendars used across all European countries were borrowed from the Romans. The original division of the year is attributed to Romulus, who divided it into ten months totaling 304 days. The ancient Roman year began in March, which is reflected in the names of the final four months: September, October, November, and December. July and August were originally called Sextilis and Quintilis before being renamed for Augustus and Julius Caesar.

Under the reign of Numa, January and February were added to the beginning and end of the year, respectively. This remained until 452 B.C., when the Decemvirs placed February after January. To align with the synodic revolution of the moon, months alternated between twenty-nine and thirty days, totaling 354 days, with one day added for fortune to reach 355 days. To correct the difference from the solar year, Numa introduced an intercalary month called *Mercedinus* or *Mercedonius* every second year. This month, likely named after *merces* (wages) because it was the season for paying domestics and workmen, consisted of twenty-two and twenty-three days alternately. Further corrections were made to reduce the mean length of the year to 365¼ days by adjusting the number of intercalary months every third period of eight years.

## Legal Proceedings and Trials
The archive records several significant trials involving Europeans and those under European rule:

*   **Bahadur Shah II:** The last Mogul emperor of Hindustan (1837-1857) was a titular ruler, as real power had resided with the East India Company since 1764. Following the Mutiny of 1857, during which he was controlled by the "Twelve Heads" (Barah Topi), he was captured by Major Hodson at the Tomb of Humayun. In January 1858, he was tried for rebellion and complicity in the murder of Europeans. The trial lasted more than two months. He argued he was a mere instrument of the mutineers but was found guilty on March 29 and sentenced to life imprisonment. He died in Rangoon on November 7, 1862.
*   **Babeuf:** In 1797, the socialist Babeuf and his accomplices were tried at Vendome before a newly constituted high court of justice. The trial began on February 20 and lasted two months. On April 26, 1797, Babeuf and Darthe were condemned to death and executed the following day. Other prisoners, such as Buonarroti, were exiled, while others, including Vadier, were acquitted.
*   **Sir Nicholas Throckmorton:** In 1553, Throckmorton was tried for high treason and acquitted. However, the jury members were required to give recognizances for their verdict and were subsequently fined heavily and imprisoned for nearly eight months by a sentence of the Star-chamber.
