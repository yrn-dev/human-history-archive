# Le Jardin de Berenice

*Le Jardin de Berenice* (1891) is a literary work by the French novelist and politician Maurice Barres. It serves as the final volume of a trilogy titled *trilogie du moi*, which was preceded by *Sous l'oeil des barbares* (1888) and *Un Homme libre* (1889).

## Philosophical Concepts
In these works, Barres presented a specific division of the world, categorizing existence into *moi* (the self) and the barbarians. Within this framework, the "barbarians" are defined as all individuals who are antipathetic to the writer's own individuality. These writings are characterized as apologies for individualism.

## Author Biography and Style
Maurice Barres was born on September 22, 1862, in Charmes (Vosges). He received his education at the *lycee* of Nancy before moving to Paris in 1883 to pursue legal studies. His early career involved journalism, including contributions to the periodical *Jeune France* and the creation of his own short-lived periodical, *Les Taches d'encre*.

The early books written by Barres, including the *trilogie du moi*, are noted for an elaborate style that is frequently very obscure. Following the publication of *Le Jardin de Berenice*, Barres continued his exploration of individualism in *L'Ennemi des lois* (1892) and published a volume of travel impressions titled *Du sang, de la volupte et de la mort* (1893).

## Political Career and Later Works
Barres applied his theories of individualism to the political sphere as a fervent supporter of General Boulanger. He served as a deputy in the legislature from 1889 until 1893 and managed a Boulangist newspaper in Nancy.

His later literary output shifted toward themes of national identity and local patriotism. In 1897, he began a new trilogy, *Le Roman de l'energie nationale*, starting with *Les Deracines*. This series advocates for the preservation of the distinctive qualities of old French provinces. The trilogy continues with *L'Appel au soldat* (1900), which chronicles the history of Boulangism, and *Leurs figures* (1902), which addresses the Panama scandals.

Other subsequent works by Barres include:
* *Scenes et doctrines du nationalisme* (1902)
* *Les Amities francaises* (1903), which argues for the study of national history to instill patriotism.
* *Ce que j'ai vu a Rennes* (1904)
* *Au service de l'Allemagne* (1905), detailing the experiences of an Alsatian conscript in a German regiment.
* *Le Voyage de Sparte* (1906)

Additionally, Barres authored the play *Une Journee parlementaire*, which was produced at the Comedie Francaise in 1894.

## Other References to Berenice
Separate from the work of Maurice Barres, the name "Berenice" appears in the context of contemporary French drama. The dramatist Charles Maurice Donnay wrote a play titled *Amants*, which achieved significant success. The critic Jules Lemaitre referred to this play as the *Berenice* of contemporary French drama.
