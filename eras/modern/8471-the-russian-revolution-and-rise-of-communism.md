# The Russian Revolution and the Rise of Communism

## Overview
The Russian Revolution of 1917 was not a single event, but a series of upheavals that dismantled the Tsarist autocracy and led to the creation of the Soviet Union, the world's first socialist state. The revolution was precipitated by decades of social inequality, the incompetence of Tsar Nicholas II, and the catastrophic toll of World War I, which pushed the Russian economy and peasantry to a breaking point.

The process unfolded in two primary stages. The **February Revolution** saw spontaneous mass protests in Petrograd, leading to the Tsar's abdication and the establishment of a weak Provisional Government. However, this government failed to exit the war or implement land reform, creating a power vacuum. In October, the **Bolsheviks**, a radical faction of the Social Democratic Labour Party led by Vladimir Lenin, seized power in a disciplined coup.

The subsequent Russian Civil War (1917–1923) pitted the "Reds" (Bolsheviks) against the "Whites" (a loose coalition of monarchists, capitalists, and other socialists). The Bolshevik victory solidified the Communist Party's control, transitioning Russia from a monarchy to a totalitarian one-party state. Historians continue to debate whether the October Revolution was a genuine popular uprising of the proletariat or a strategic coup d'état by a small minority of professional revolutionaries.

## Key Facts
*   **Ideological Basis:** The revolution was based on a modified version of Marxism known as **Marxism-Leninism**, which argued that a "vanguard party" was necessary to lead the working class toward revolution.
*   **War Communism:** During the Civil War, the Bolsheviks implemented "War Communism," involving the forced requisitioning of grain from peasants to feed the Red Army and urban centers.
*   **The Red Terror:** To consolidate power, the Cheka (secret police) conducted a campaign of mass arrests and executions of perceived "class enemies."
*   **The NEP:** Following the failure of War Communism, Lenin introduced the **New Economic Policy (NEP)** in 1921, which allowed for limited capitalism to stabilize the economy.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Calendar Gap:** The "October Revolution" actually took place in November according to the Gregorian calendar; Russia was still using the Julian calendar at the time.
2. **The Romanov Fate:** Tsar Nicholas II and his entire immediate family were executed by Bolsheviks in a basement in Yekaterinburg in July 1918 to prevent them from becoming a rallying point for the White Army.
3. **International Ambition:** Lenin and Trotsky believed the Russian Revolution was merely the first spark of a "World Revolution" that would inevitably spread to industrialized nations like Germany.
