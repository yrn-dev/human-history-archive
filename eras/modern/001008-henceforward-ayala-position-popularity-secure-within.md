# Adelardo Lopez de Ayala y Herrera

## Early Life and Education
Adelardo Lopez de Ayala y Herrera (1828–1879) was a Spanish politician and writer born on May 1, 1828, in Guadalcanal. From a very young age, he began writing plays for the local theatre in his hometown. Because travelling companies did not visit Guadalcanal and women did not participate in theatrical representations, his early juvenile works—specifically *La Corona y el Punal*, *Me voy a Sevilla*, and *Salga por donde saliere*—were written exclusively for male performers. 

Ayala later introduced a change by persuading his sister to play the heroine in his comedy *La primera Dama*. While this innovation scandalized some residents of his town, it allowed him to develop his talent more freely. At the age of twenty, he matriculated at the university of Seville, though his time as a student there was undistinguished. During this period, he met Garcia Gutierrez, a playwright who is reported to have provided Ayala with the benefit of his own experience and encouraged his ambitions in drama.

## Professional Career in Madrid
In early 1850, Ayala left the university and moved to Madrid to pursue a career as a professional dramatist. Despite lacking influence or friends in the city, he quickly found success. On January 25, 1851, the managers of the Teatro Espanol produced his four-act verse play, *Un Hombre de Estado*, which was a remarkable success. This achievement secured Ayala's popularity and professional position.

Within a year, his recognition grew through the production of *Los dos Guzmanes*, a humorous work, and *Castigo y Perdon*. Following these successes, the *Moderado* government appointed him to a position within the home office. He held this post until 1854, when he lost it due to the Liberal party's accession to power.

## Later Works and Political Shift
In 1854, Ayala produced *Rioja*, a work considered perhaps the most admirable and admired of all his productions. Between 1854 and 1856, he was actively involved in political campaigning through the journal *El Padre Cobos*. Around this time, Ayala shifted his political allegiance from the Moderates to the Progressives.

In addition to his plays, Ayala collaborated with Emilio Arrieta on *zarzuelas*. This partnership produced *El Agente de Matrimonios* and *Guerta a muerte*, the latter of which dates to 1855.
