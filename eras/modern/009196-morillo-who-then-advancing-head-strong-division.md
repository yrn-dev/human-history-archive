# Morillo

Morillo was a military commander who led royalist forces during the War of Independence in South America. He is primarily noted for his campaigns against the republican forces led by Simón Bolívar.

## Campaigns in Venezuela
In December 1816, Simón Bolívar landed in Margarita and subsequently at Barcelona, where a provisional government was established and troops were gathered to resist Morillo, who was advancing with a strong division. The royalist and republican forces engaged in a desperate conflict beginning on February 16, 1817. This battle lasted for three days and resulted in the defeat of the royalists. Following this defeat, Morillo retired in disorder. During his retreat, he encountered J.A. Paez and his *llaneros*, which led to a more complete overthrow of his forces.

Following this victory, Bolívar was recognized as commander-in-chief and established his headquarters at Angostura on the Orinoco before the end of the year.

## Later Conflicts and Armistice
By 1820, Bolívar had organized the Republic of Colombia, uniting the republics of New Granada and Venezuela under his presidency. Leading a well-appointed republican army, Bolívar gained significant advantages over the Spanish forces commanded by Morillo. 

On November 25, 1820, an armistice of six months was concluded at Truxillo between Bolívar and Morillo. This agreement was likely pursued in the hope of avoiding further bloodshed and encouraging the Spaniards to reach terms. However, these hopes were not realized. Morillo was recalled from his position and replaced by General Torre. Once the armistice expired, the conflict resumed, eventually leading to the Battle of Carabobo, where Bolívar routed the Spanish army.

## Impact on Maracaibo
The city of Maracaibo did not play an active role in the War of Independence until January 28, 1821, when the province declared its independence and sought an alliance with Colombia. This political move brought an end to the armistice that had previously existed between Bolívar and Morillo. Consequently, the city experienced the shifting fortunes of the war until the revolutionists finally captured it in 1823.
