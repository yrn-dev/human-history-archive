# The Strategic Position of Great Britain in Baluchistan

The strategic position of Great Britain in Baluchistan is considered a very important factor in the effort to maintain good administration and order within the region. British interests in the area are focused on maintaining security, checking the movements of restless tribes, and preventing foreign advances toward India.

## Strategic Objectives and Security
The British government utilizes specific geographical positions to maintain regional stability. The occupation of the Zhob valley is used to hold in check the ever-restless Pathan tribes of the Suliman hills. Simultaneously, the central dominant position at Quetta serves multiple strategic purposes: it safeguards the security of Kalat and the wildest Baluch hills—which are occupied by the Bugtis and Marris—and it acts as a barrier to block any advance upon India via Kandahar.

## Administrative Structure and Control
Baluchistan is not regarded as a distinct entity among Asiatic nations in the same manner as Afghanistan. Instead, it exists in varying degrees of independence, all of which are subject to British control. The region is divided into two primary categories:

**British Baluchistan:** This is an official province and a distinctive block of upland territory leased from Kalat. It is brought under the ordinary forms of civil administration used in British India. Geographically, it includes the Toba plateau, all upper Lora tributaries, the basins of the Nari and Bolan as far as Sibi to the south-east, and the Thal Chotiali valley to the east. It reaches south through Nushki and rests on the Kwaja Amran (Kojak) Range to the west, including the Chaman railway terminus. Officially, this province includes the districts of Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali; these were transferred to British authority via the treaty of Gandamak in 1879, though they had nominally been handed to Kalat forty years prior.

**Independent Baluchistan:** This area lies to the north and south of British Baluchistan and remains under British political control. While some districts within the Baluchistan Agency are as independent as any in Afghanistan, the overall political status of the province resembles that of the native states of the Indian peninsula. Supreme control is practically exercised by the agent to the governor-general of India and a staff of political assistants. The level of independence enjoyed by various districts generally varies in direct proportion to their distance from Quetta.

## Regional Governance and Social Conditions
Nominally, all districts and provinces of Baluchistan—excluding the ceded territory of British Baluchistan—fall under the khan of Kalat, who is acknowledged as the suzerain by all chiefs. However, it is doubted whether this suzerainty was ever complete or could be maintained without the assistance of the British government. 

The archive describes the Baluch as essentially being raiders and robbers, a trait noted as common to all tribes. The history of the region is characterized as a story of bloodshed, lawless rapine, and successful robberies, where devastation and plunder were viewed as honourable returns.

## Geography and Climate
The climate of British Baluchistan is described as bracing, dry, and even exhilarating, which is generally favourable for European existence. However, the region experiences extremes of temperature that can lead to severe forms of fever.

Geologically, the mountain ranges consist mainly of Tertiary and Cretaceous beds arranged in folds running parallel to the ridges. These folds form a system stretching between Mount Ararat and Peshawar, with a subsidiary festoon forming the Bugti Hills and Suliman hills. The region also contains Jurassic rocks, Triassic beds along the south side of the upper Zhob, and volcanic tuffs and conglomerates in north-western Baluchistan. A line of recent volcanic cones extends from eastern Persia into western Baluchistan, though these appear extinct. The Makran coast is characterized by horizontal deposits and the presence of mud "volcanoes."
