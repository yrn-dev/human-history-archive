# Providence and Edward Teach

## Concepts of Providence
According to ancient traditions, there was a belief that Providence interposed visibly in all human affairs. This perspective included the supposition that great misery was a sign of great sin. To identify these great sinners, the practice of casting lots was employed. This tradition was observed not only among the Israelites (Bani Israel) but also among heathen mariners. The most ancient Book indicates that this was the state of mankind for approximately the first three thousand years of the world, continuing until the days of Moses and Job.

## New Providence
New Providence is an island that served as a settlement and a strategic location in the Atlantic. While a British settlement was formed in 1629 in a place also called New Providence, that location was actually Providence Island off the Mosquito Coast; the British held that site until the Spaniards expelled them in 1641. The actual island of New Providence was colonized in 1666 by settlers arriving from the Bermudas.

In 1670, Christopher, duke of Albemarle, and others received a grant of the islands from Charles II. The lords proprietors appointed governors to the region, and state papers contain extensive records of efforts to develop the islands' resources. However, these efforts faced heavy opposition from pirates and buccaneers who used the area as a retreat.

## Conflict and Destruction
The island suffered significant attacks from foreign powers. In July 1703, the French and Spaniards launched a descent on New Providence. During this attack, they spiked the guns, blew up the fort, and burnt the church. The governor and the principal inhabitants were captured and carried off to Havana. In October of the same year, the Spaniards conducted a second descent to complete the destruction. It is reported that a governor appointed by the lords proprietors arrived at New Providence unaware of the raid, only to find the island entirely without inhabitants.

## Edward Teach
Following the periods of destruction, New Providence once again became a resort for pirates. The island is associated with many of the worst ruffians of that era, chief among them being the notorious Edward Teach, known as Blackbeard. Teach's career ended in 1718, when he was killed in action during a conflict against two American ships.
