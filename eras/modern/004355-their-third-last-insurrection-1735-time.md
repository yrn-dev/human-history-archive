# The Bashkirs

The Bashkirs are a people historically situated in the upper regions of the river Ural. Early European accounts of the group were provided by William of Rubruquis (1220–1293) and Joannes de Plano Carpini (c. 1200–1260), who referred to them as the Pascatir and noted that they spoke a language identical to that of the Hungarians.

## Political History and Russian Relations
Until the mid-13th century, the Bashkirs remained a strong, independent people who proved troublesome to the Petchenegs and Bulgarians. However, by the time the Kazan kingdom fell, the Bashkirs had entered a state of weakness. In 1556, they voluntarily recognized the supremacy of Russia. Following this recognition, the city of Ufa was established to provide them protection from the Kirghiz, and the Bashkirs were subjected to a fur-tax.

The relationship between the Bashkirs and the Russian government was marked by several periods of unrest:
* **1676:** A rebellion occurred under a leader named Seit, which was reduced with difficulty.
* **1707:** A second rebellion took place under the leadership of Kusyom and Aldar, prompted by the ill-treatment they received from Russian officials.
* **1735:** A third and final insurrection began at the time of the foundation of Orenburg. This conflict lasted for six years.

Following these upheavals, the Bashkirs were freed from taxes in 1786. In 1798, an irregular army was formed from among their population. In more recent times, they have been divided into cantons and generally cause little trouble, although land questions have led to some differences with the government.

## Society and Mode of Life
The Bashkirs are divided into two primary lifestyles: settled and nomadic.

### Settled Bashkirs
Those who are settled engage in bee-keeping, cattle-rearing, and agriculture. This group is described as living without want.

### Nomadic Bashkirs
The nomadic population is further subdivided based on the districts where they wander, categorized as those of the steppes and those of the mountains. Their primary occupation is the rearing of cattle, though this is performed negligently. Because they fail to collect sufficient winter fodder for their herds, a portion of their cattle perish. Consequently, the nomadic Bashkirs are usually very poor. During the winter, their diet consists of a badly prepared cheese called *skurt* and a type of gruel known as *yuryu*.

## Physical and Behavioral Characteristics
The Bashkirs are described as being strong and muscular. Their physical features include swarthy skin, black hair, small foreheads, and narrow, flat eyes. Their ears are noted as always sticking out, and they possess large heads. Behaviorally, they are characterized as being hospitable but suspicious, lazy to the last degree, and apt to plunder.
