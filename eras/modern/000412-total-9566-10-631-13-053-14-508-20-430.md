# Joint Budget and General Debt of Austria and Hungary (1875–1905)

The financial administration of the Dual Monarchy involved a joint budget and a general debt borne jointly by Austria and Hungary. This system encompassed expenditures for foreign affairs, defense, finance, and accounting, as well as specific revenue streams and debt management.

## Joint Expenditure and Budgetary Trends
Between 1875 and 1905, the joint budget of the Dual Monarchy showed a significant upward trend in total expenditure. In 1875, the total expenditure was 9,566.1 thousand sterling. This figure rose to 10,631.5 thousand sterling by 1885, and further increased to 13,053.1 thousand sterling in 1895. By 1900, the total reached 14,508.4 thousand sterling, eventually peaking at 20,430.3 thousand sterling in 1905.

The primary driver of this expenditure was the Ministry of War, which covered both the Army and Navy. The Ministry of War's costs rose from 9,005.4 thousand sterling in 1875 to 18,087.7 thousand sterling in 1905. Other expenditures included:
*   **Ministry of Foreign Affairs:** Expenditure fluctuated over the period, starting at 396 thousand sterling in 1875, dipping to 333 thousand sterling in 1895, and rising to 493.8 thousand sterling by 1905.
*   **Ministry of Finance:** Costs remained relatively stable, moving from 154.2 thousand sterling in 1875 to 177.1 thousand sterling in 1905.
*   **Supreme Court of Accounts:** This department maintained the lowest expenditure, ranging from 10.5 thousand sterling in 1875 to 13.3 thousand sterling in 1905.

Detailed expenditure records also note specific allocations, such as L485,480 for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and L177,000 for the Ministry of Finance. Military spending was further divided into the Army (L12,679,160) and Navy (L2,306,100), with additional extraordinary military expenditures totaling L4,785,500, including L315,920 specifically for Bosnia.

## Revenue Sources
The revenue used to fund these departments was derived from three primary sources: proportional contributions, customs, and revenue for the departments themselves.

Proportional contributions constituted the largest portion of the revenue. In 1875, these contributions were 8,136.7 thousand sterling; by 1905, they had increased to 15,650.4 thousand sterling. Customs revenue showed more volatility, recorded at 997.4 thousand sterling in 1875, peaking at 5,202.3 thousand sterling in 1900, and ending at 4,799.7 thousand sterling in 1905. Revenue for the departments themselves started at 432 thousand sterling in 1875 and was 331.9 thousand sterling in 1905.

## General Debt
In addition to the individual debts of the states within the Dual Monarchy, there existed a general debt borne jointly by Austria and Hungary. This general debt saw a gradual decline between 1875 and 1905. In 1875, the debt stood at 232.41 million sterling. It decreased to 231.02 million sterling in 1885, 229.67 million sterling in 1895, and 226.81 million sterling in 1900, reaching 224.31 million sterling by 1905.
