# E. Noelting and E. Grandmougin

E. Noelting and E. Grandmougin were researchers who, as documented in *Berichte* (1891, 24, p. 2546), developed methods for obtaining azoimide (also known as hydrazoic acid). Their work focused on the chemical synthesis of this compound of nitrogen and hydrogen through various precursors and reactions.

## Synthesis from Dinitraniline
Noelting and Grandmougin successfully obtained azoimide starting from dinitraniline, $\text{C}_6\text{H}_3(\text{NO}_2)_2\text{NH}_2$. The process began with diazotization, which converted the dinitraniline into a diazo compound. This diazo compound was then converted into a perbromide, represented as $(\text{NO}_2)_2\text{C}_6\text{H}_3\text{N}_2\text{Br}_3$. 

Following this, the perbromide was decomposed using ammonia to form dinitrophenylhydrazoate. This intermediate underwent hydrolysis with alcoholic potash, resulting in the production of dinitrophenol and potassium hydrazoate (azide). To isolate the azoimide, the resulting solution was acidified and distilled, allowing the azoimide to pass over.

## Synthesis from Diazobenzene Imide
At a later time, Noelting and Grandmougin discovered that azoimide could also be prepared from diazobenzene imide, provided that a nitro group was present in either the para or ortho position relative to the diazo group.

For the para-nitro compound, the researchers employed a method where the compound was dropped slowly into a cold solution consisting of one part caustic potash to ten parts absolute alcohol. This mixture turned dark red and was subsequently warmed on a water bath for two days. Once the majority of the alcohol had distilled off, the solution was acidified with sulphuric acid, and the azoimide was then distilled over.

The efficiency of these reactions varied based on the position of the nitro group:
*   **Para-nitro compound:** The yield was approximately 40% of the theoretical requirement, a reduction attributed to the occurrence of secondary reactions.
*   **Ortho-nitro-diazobenzene imide:** This precursor yielded only 30%.
