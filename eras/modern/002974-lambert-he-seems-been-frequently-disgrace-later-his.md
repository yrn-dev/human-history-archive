# Lambert

The name Lambert is associated with several distinct historical figures across various fields, including religious leadership, cartography, military history, and literature.

## Lambert le Begue and the Beguines
Lambert le Begue was a priest of Liege who died around the year 1187. Around 1170, he used his personal fortune to establish the church and hospital of St Christopher, intended for the children and widows of crusaders. He subsequently conceived the idea of creating an association for women who wished to devote themselves to a religious life without taking monastic vows. 

His preaching had a significant impact, attracting many women, particularly those widowed by the crusades. By approximately 1180, Lambert organized some of these women into a semi-conventual community in a walled district of the city belonging to him, located near the church of St Christopher. Within this district, the women lived in separate small houses. They were not subject to a formal rule, except for the requirements of chastity while remaining in the community and the obligation to perform good works. 

The members of this community were ironically called "Beguines" by opponents of Lambert. The name "le Begue" itself is noted by the chronicler Aegidius, a monk of Orval, to mean "the stammerer" (*quia balbus erat*). Following Lambert's death, the movement spread into France—where it received encouragement from Louis IX—as well as Germany, Switzerland, and the Netherlands. These communities were modeled after the original Liege establishment, functioning as "little cities within the city" governed by a mistress (*magistra*). While women of all classes were admitted and some wealthy members contributed their riches, there was no rule of poverty; poorer members supported themselves through sick-nursing and manual labor.

## Lambert of St Omer
Lambert, a canon of St Omer in 1120, authored the encyclopaedic work *Liber floridus*. This work included maps of the world that reflected Lambert's apparent belief in the sphericity of the earth. In these maps, the southern land is described as being "unknown to the sons of Adam" and is stated to be inhabited by Antipodes, according to the philosophers.

## Other Historical Figures named Lambert
The archive records several other individuals bearing the name:

*   **General Lambert:** Mentioned as a former occupant of Holland House, a mansion in Kensington built in 1607.
*   **Lambert Simnel:** A pretender involved in an enterprise supported by the Yorkist party. He was accompanied to Ireland by John de la Pole, earl of Lincoln, and fought at the battle of Stoke on June 16, 1487.
*   **Paul Lambert:** An author who provided a detailed description of the city of Marrakesh in the 1868 publication *Notice sur la ville de Maroc*. His plan of the city was later reproduced with additions by Dr A. Leared.
*   **J. F. de Saint-Lambert (1716-1803):** A poet who wrote *Les Saisons* (1769), an imitation of Thomson. While the poem remained popular for fifty years and was praised by Voltaire, Madame du Deffand described Saint-Lambert as "*froid, fade et faux*."

## Literary References
In the context of literature, the author Balzac drew upon his own childhood experiences at the grammar school of Vendome—where he was a strict boarder from 1806 to 1813 and was frequently in disgrace—as the basis for his work *Louis Lambert*.
