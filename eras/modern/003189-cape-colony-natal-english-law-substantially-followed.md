# Cape Colony and Natal

Cape Colony and Natal were British colonial territories in South Africa that shared significant legal, political, and social developments during the 19th and early 20th centuries. Both regions were eventually integrated into a broader South African confederation.

## Legal and Social Frameworks
In both Cape Colony and Natal, English law was substantially followed regarding bankruptcy and insolvency. This contrasted with the Transvaal, where Roman-Dutch law prevailed via the Insolvency Law, No. 13 of 1895, and the Orange River Colony, which maintained a similar system.

Social legislation also evolved in these colonies. Both Cape Colony and Natal passed statutes legalizing marriage with a deceased wife's sister, following a trend seen in other self-governing colonies such as South Australia, Victoria, and Canada.

## History and Administration of Natal
The colony of Natal was established as a settlement in 1824. The town of Durban was founded that same year by Englishmen, including Lieutenant F.G. Farewell and ten companions who arrived from Cape Town on the brig *Salisbury*. In 1835, a township named D'Urban was laid out in honor of Sir Benjamin D'Urban, the governor of Cape Colony.

The region experienced periods of instability and shifting control. From December 1838 to December 1839, a small British military force was stationed at the port of Durban. Following their recall, Dutch emigrants from the Cape—who had defeated the Zulu king Dingaan—took possession of the settlement and hoisted the flag of the "Republic of Natalia," claiming independence. British sovereignty was restored in 1842 after a military force reoccupied Durban and a treaty was signed. 

Natal's administrative boundaries expanded over time; for instance, Zululand was annexed in 1887 and incorporated into Natal in 1897. The Natal Boers had previously submitted in 1843.

## History and Administration of Cape Colony
The Cape of Good Hope saw its first British occupation from 1795 to 1803, followed by a capitulation in 1806. The colony's present limits were not fully attained until 1895, a year in which the southern portion of Bechuanaland was annexed to the Cape Colony.

The Cape Colony also exerted influence over neighboring regions. The town of Kokstad, the capital of Griqualand East, came into the possession of Cape Colony in 1879 and was granted municipal government in 1893.

## Imperial Contributions and Infrastructure
As the value of the British fleet for protecting commerce became more recognized, the self-governing colonies began contributing to imperial naval defense. In 1897, the Cape Colony offered £30,000 annually for naval purposes, a contribution that increased to £50,000 by 1902. During the same period, Natal voted a yearly contribution of £35,000.

Infrastructure developments linked the colonies and their neighbors. In 1860, the first railway in South Africa was opened in Natal, running from the Point to the town of Durban. Later, in 1906, the linking of the town of Kroonstad (in the Orange River Colony) with the Natal system created the shortest railway connection between Durban and Cape Town.

## Integration into South Africa
The political landscape of the region was shaped by conflicts between the British and the Boer republics of the Transvaal and the Orange River district. After the Anglo-Boer War (1899–1902), which ended in the surrender of the two republics, a period of subjugation followed. 

In 1907, under a Liberal government, the former republics became associates with Natal and Cape Colony. Together, they formed a confederation of all the states of South Africa, organized as one self-governing republic under the British Crown.
