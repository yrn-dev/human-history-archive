# Pan-American Exposition of 1901

The Pan-American Exposition was an event held in 1901 in Buffalo. The exposition utilized a portion of the city's municipal park system, specifically the north part of Delaware Park, which encompasses approximately 365 acres and includes a lake of 46½ acres.

## Legacy and Architecture
Following the conclusion of the 1901 exposition, the New York state building, which had been erected for the event, was repurposed. This structure now serves as the Buffalo Historical Society building. The building is located in Delaware Park and houses the library of the Buffalo Historical Society, which was founded in 1862 and contains 26,600 volumes. Additionally, the John C. Lord library, which holds 10,620 volumes, is also housed within the Historical Society building.

## Buffalo Social and Civic Context
The city of Buffalo is characterized by its residential beauty, featuring detached houses set back from streets with attractive lawns. The city's infrastructure includes several significant buildings, such as the Federal building (costing $2,000,000), the city and county hall (costing $1,500,000 with a 245 ft. high clock tower), and the Ellicott Square building, which is noted as one of the largest office structures in the world. Other prominent structures include the city convention hall, the chamber of commerce, the builders' exchange, the Masonic temple, and two state armouries.

The city's social life is supported by various establishments. Among the social clubs specifically mentioned are the Buffalo, the University, the Park, the Saturn, and the Country clubs. For lodging, the city features the Iroquois, Lafayette, Niagara, and Genesee hotels.

## Geography and Urban Planning
Buffalo is situated on a broad, undulating tract of land covering 42 square miles, with an average altitude of slightly less than 600 ft. above sea-level. The site rises from the lake to elevations between 50 and 80 ft. The city was originally platted by Joseph Ellicott, whose plan somewhat resembled that of Washington, though the final layout was significantly altered. 

Modern Buffalo is characterized by spacious, tree-lined streets and a municipal park system totaling about 1,030 acres. This system consists of a chain of parks connected by driveways and boulevards. In addition to Delaware Park, the system includes Humboldt Park, Cazenovia Park, South Park on the Lake Shore, and "The Front," a bluff overlooking the source of the Niagara river. The latter contains Fort Porter, where a United States government garrison is maintained.
