# Bahia

## Geography and Territory
Bahia is an Atlantic state located in Brazil. It is bounded to the north by the states of Sergipe, Pernambuco, and Piauhy; to the east by the Atlantic Ocean and Sergipe; to the south by Minas Geraes and Espirito Santo; and to the west by Goyaz and Minas Geraes. The state encompasses a total area of 164,650 sq. m.

A significant portion of this area consists of an arid and barren *chapada* (plateau). This plateau is traversed from the north and northeast to the south by the drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river. The general elevation of the *chapada* ranges from 1,000 to 1,700 ft. above the river, or between 2,300 and 3,000 ft. above sea level. On the western side, the *chapada* forms the state's boundary and serves as the water-parting between the Tocantins and the Sao Francisco, maintaining a breadth of 60 m. and an elevation of 2,300 ft.

## Regional Divisions
East of the Sao Francisco river, the state is divided into three distinct regions:

*   **Limestone Plateau:** A rough plateau that rises gradually toward the culminating ridges of the Serra da Chapada.
*   **Gneissose Plateau:** An area characterized by extensive exposures of bare rock that dip slightly toward the coast.
*   **Sandy Plateau:** A narrower plateau covered with compact sandy soil that descends toward the coastal plain.

The limestone and gneissose plateaus each have a breadth of approximately 200 m. Both are described as inhospitable, barren, and arid, with the exception of the dividing ridges where sea clouds deposit some moisture. The third zone, the sandy plateau, loses its arid nature as it nears the coast and possesses better vegetation.

The coastal plain varies in character and width. In certain areas, it is swampy, low, and sandy, containing intersecting canals and lagoons. In other areas, it is rolling, more elevated, and very fertile.

## Climate and Industry
The climate of Bahia closely follows its physical geography. The interior is characterized as hot and dry, while the coast is hot and humid, and in some locations, unhealthy.

In the interior, cattle-raising was previously the primary industry. However, this has been nearly extinguished due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts. These conditions were caused by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve the grass.

The agricultural regions of the state produce a variety of crops, including coffee, cacao, tobacco, cotton, sugar, tropical fruits, and mandioca. Other exports from the state include rum, cabinet woods, diamonds, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, and hides.

## Population
As of 1890, the population of Bahia numbered 1,919,802. The population is described as being largely of an unprogressive and mixed character.
