# London Building Act and Related Regulations

The London Building Act, including its second schedule, serves as a primary regulatory framework for construction within London. Along with the London County Council Theatre and Factory Acts, it governs the standards for fire-resisting construction in the city.

## Fire Safety and Escape Requirements
The London Building Act and its second schedule are central to the implementation of fire-resisting construction. In 1907, a section was introduced under the London Building Act to address specific safety hazards in existing structures. This legislation required all existing projecting one-storey shops to be equipped with fire-resisting roofs. Additionally, any existing buildings exceeding 50 feet in height were required to provide means of escape to and from the roof in the event of a fire.

## Structural Standards and Wall Construction
The London Building Act 1894 contains specific stipulations regarding the thickness of walls, as detailed in its schedule I. These regulations influence the architectural choices made in the city; for instance, hollow walls are not frequently used in London. This is partly because surrounding buildings provide weather protection, reducing the need for such walls, and partly because the authorities ignore the outer shell of a cavity wall. Consequently, the main wall must meet the full thickness stipulated in schedule I, which greatly increases construction expenses. This contrasts with many English provincial authorities, who may take the outer portion of a cavity wall into consideration when determining thickness.

## London County Council Regulations
The London County Council has issued further regulations to ensure building quality and moisture protection. Under section 16 of the Metropolis Management and Buildings Acts Amendment Act 1878, by-laws dated 1891 were established with the following requirements:
* **Damp Courses:** Every wall of a building must have a damp course made of materials impervious to moisture. This course must be approved by the district surveyor and extend through the wall's entire thickness at a level at least 6 inches below the lowest floor.
* **External Wall Protection:** Any external or enclosing wall of cellars or habitable rooms (including their appurtenances) that abuts against the earth must be protected by moisture-impervious materials to the satisfaction of the district surveyor.
* **Wall Finishing:** The top of every parapet-wall and party-wall must be finished using a coping of waterproof and fire-resisting material that is properly secured, or by one course of hard, well-burnt bricks set on edge in cement.

## Architectural and Administrative Procedures
The London Building Act is described as being very extensive and is subject to numerous amendments. Architects must strictly observe these acts when preparing plans, elevations, and sections to ensure compliance regarding light, air, open spaces, frontage lines, the thickness of walls, and distances from surrounding properties.

During the development process, architects often consult with the London County Council, the district surveyor, or other local authorities. These consultations frequently result in necessary alterations to the plans. Once drawings are approved by the local authorities, the district surveyor, the client, and adjoining owners, a copy made on linen is typically deposited in London with either the London County Council or the district surveyor.

## Professional Responsibilities
While the architect is responsible for interpreting the enactments of the building acts and designing the structure to comply with them, the builder is tasked with the actual execution of these plans. Builders are expected to have a thorough knowledge of all materials and their qualifying brands. Furthermore, they must be familiar with various acts, specifically those relating to the pulling down of old buildings, as well as the erection of gantries, hoardings, shoring, and scaffoldings.
