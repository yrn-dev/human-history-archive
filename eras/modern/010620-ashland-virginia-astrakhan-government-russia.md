# ASHLAND (Virginia, U.S.A.) AND ASTRAKHAN (Government of Russia)

## Ashland, Virginia
Ashland is a village located in Hanover County, Virginia, United States, situated 17 miles northwest of Richmond. Settled in 1845 and incorporated in 1856, the village had a population of 1,147 in 1900, which grew to 1,324 by 1910. It is served by the Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac railway and is considered a favorite resort for those visiting from Richmond.

The village is the site of Randolph-Macon College, a Methodist Episcopal, South institution and one of the oldest of its kind in the United States. The college received its charter in 1830 and opened in 1832 near Boydton in Mecklenburg County, Virginia, before being moved to Ashland in 1868. During the 1907-1908 period, the college maintained a faculty of 16 and a student body of 150. It publishes an endowed historical series known as *The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College*. 

Randolph-Macon College is part of the "Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies," which is managed by a single board of trustees to correlate the work of preparatory schools with that of the colleges. This system includes:
* Randolph-Macon Academy at Front Royal, Virginia (established 1892) for boys.
* Randolph-Macon Academy at Bedford City, Virginia (established 1890) for boys.
* Randolph-Macon Woman's College at Lynchburg, Virginia (established 1893), which had an enrolment of 390 in 1907-1908.
* Randolph-Macon Institute for girls in Danville, Virginia, which joined the system in 1897.

Additionally, the area surrounding Ashland is notable for the birthplaces of Patrick Henry (approximately 15 miles distant) and Henry Clay (approximately 7 miles out of Ashland).

## Government of Astrakhan
The government of Astrakhan is located in Southeast Russia on the lower Volga. It is bounded to the north by the governments of Saratov and Samara, to the west by Saratov and the government of the Don Cossacks, to the south by Terek and Stavropol, and to the east by the government of the Urals and the Caspian Sea. The government covers an area of 91,327 square miles, with 6,730 square miles consisting of brackish lagoons and the Volga delta. A further 62,290 square miles are covered by the Kirghiz and Kalmuck Steppes.

The terrain is primarily a low-lying plain, though the Ergeni Hills in the west reach heights of 500-575 feet, serving as the water-parting between the Don and Volga basins. The climate is characterized as very hot and dry, with an average annual temperature of 50 degrees Fahrenheit (21 degrees in January and 78 degrees in July) and an annual rainfall of 7.3 inches; summer rain is often nonexistent.

As of 1897, the population was 1,005,460, with 132,383 residents living in urban areas. The population includes semi-nomadic Kirghiz (260,000) and Kalmucks (138,580 in 1897), as well as nearly 44,000 Tatars, 4,270 Armenians, and populations of Poles and Jews. The government is divided into five districts with chief towns at Astrakhan, Tsarev (pop. 8,900), Chernyi-yar (5,140), Krasnyi-yar (4,680), and Enotayevsk (2,810 in 1897). Local administrations exist for the Astrakhan Cossacks (25,600), the Kirghiz, and the Kalmucks.

The economy of the region relies heavily on:
* **Fishing:** 50,000 people are occupied by fishing off the mouth of the Volga, primarily for sturgeon and herrings. Caviare and fish are largely sold at Nizhniy-Novgorod.
* **Salt Extraction:** Over 300,000 tons of salt are extracted annually, mainly from the lakes of Elton and Baskunchak.
* **Agriculture:** Cattle-breeding is a major industry, and market-gardening for fruit, water-melons, and mustard is increasing, though pure agriculture is not highly developed.
