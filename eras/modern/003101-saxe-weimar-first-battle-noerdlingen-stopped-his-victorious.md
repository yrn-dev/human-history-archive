# Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar and the First Battle of Noerdlingen

## Military Career in the Thirty Years' War
Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar was a military commander described as "fiery" during the Thirty Years' War. He demonstrated significant tactical skill in managing reserves during combat. In one engagement, he avoided wasting his reserves, instead deploying only a few units from the second line to support disordered brigades of the first line. This maneuver allowed his forces to drive back the Imperialists and recapture their guns across the entire line.

Even when wounded, Bernhard continued to lead. In a specific instance, he forced the Swedish army to attack a final time using three infantry brigades of the second line that had not yet been engaged. By resolutely handling the last closed reserve, he secured a victory that caused Wallenstein's army to give way at all points.

## The First Battle of Noerdlingen
The first battle of Noerdlingen served as a critical turning point in the conflict. Prior to this engagement, the Swedish field-marshal Johan Baner had entered Bohemia with 16,000 men and combined his forces with the Saxon army to march on Prague. However, the victorious advance of these forces was stopped by the complete defeat of Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar at the first battle of Noerdlingen.

## Aftermath and Swedish Influence
The defeat at Noerdlingen led to the peace of Prague, which placed the Swedish army in a very precarious position. The paramount influence of Sweden in central Germany was only restored later through the victories of the united forces of Torstensson, Wrangel, and Baner at Wittstock (October 4, 1636) and Kyritz. Despite these later successes, the combined Swedish armies remained decidedly inferior in force to the enemies they defeated.

## The Grand-Duchy of Saxe-Weimar
Saxe-Weimar is also identified as a grand-duchy in Germany. It included the town of Ilmenau, located on the river Ilm at the north foot of the Thuringian Forest. Ilmenau, which passed to Saxe-Weimar after previously being part of the county of Henneberg and electoral Saxony, was a summer resort frequented by Goethe. Another town within the grand-duchy was Dornburg, situated on a hill above the Saale. Dornburg is noted for three grand-ducal castles: the Altes Schloss, the Neues Schloss (built 1728-1748), and the Stohmannsches Rittergut.

Additionally, the grand-duke of Saxe-Weimar provided patronage for the Burschenschaft, an association of students that originated at Jena. Founded to foster patriotism and Christian conduct, the association spread rapidly after 1818, though it later faced suppression under the Carlsbad Decrees following the 1819 murder of Kotzebue by Karl Sand.

## Honors and Orders
The state of Saxe-Weimar established the Order of Vigilance, also known as the Order of the White Falcon. This order was founded in 1732 and subsequently renewed in 1815.
