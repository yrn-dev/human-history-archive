# French Colonial Expansion in Bagirmi

## Early European Exploration
The region of Bagirmi was first introduced to Europe through the travels of several explorers during the 19th century. These included Dixon Denham in 1823, Heinrich Barth in 1852—who spent some time imprisoned by the Bagirmese—Gustav Nachtigal in 1872, and the pair P. Matteucci and A. M. Massari in 1881.

## Political Shifts and Conflict
During the late 19th century, Bagirmi experienced significant shifts in power. In 1871, the country was conquered by the sultan of Wadai. By approximately 1890, the region was over-run by Rabah Zobeir, who later moved further west to Bornu. 

Around this time, France developed an interest in the territories surrounding Lake Chad. The initial French expedition to the area through Bagirmi ended in disaster when its leader, Paul Crampel, was killed by order of Rabah.

## French Establishment of Control
Following the failure of the first expedition, subsequent missions achieved more success. In 1897, Emile Gentil, acting as the French commissioner for the district, entered into a treaty with the sultan of Bagirmi. This agreement placed the country under French protection. Although a resident was stationed at the capital, Massenia, the French presence was temporarily disrupted when Gentil withdrew; Rabah descended from Bornu and forced both the resident and the sultan to flee.

French authority was not firmly established until 1901, following the rout of Rabah's sons and Rabah's own death in battle. During this same period, France brought Kanem under its control. Kanem is located north of Bagirmi and had previously been subject to both Wadai and Bagirmi.

## International Legal Basis
The French claim to these regions was based on two international agreements: the Anglo-French declaration of March 21, 1899, and the Franco-German convention of March 15, 1894.
