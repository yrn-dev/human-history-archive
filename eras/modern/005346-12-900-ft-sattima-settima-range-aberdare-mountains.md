# Parallel Mountain Ranges and Cordilleras

The term "cordillera" is a Spanish word derived from *cordilla* (a diminutive of *cuerda*, meaning a cord or rope), used to describe a range or chain of mountains. While the term was first applied to the Andes of South America and extended to Mexico, it is also used in North America to describe the parallel ranges between and including the Sierra Nevada and the Rocky Mountains. Outside of the Americas, the word is not used as a generic term for parallel ranges, although it has been applied to the eastern mountain system of Australia.

## North American Systems
In British Columbia, the landscape is characterized by a series of parallel mountain ranges separated by long, narrow valleys. This region comprises the Cordilleran belt of North America, featuring two primary systems: the Rocky Mountains to the northeast and the Coast Range (also known as the Cascade Range) to the southwest. The Coast Range borders the Pacific coast for 900 meters, creating deep sounds, inlets, and fjords. Between these two primary systems lies the "Interior Plateau," an elevated tract of hilly country. Additionally, Vancouver Island and the Queen Charlotte Islands are remnants of another parallel mountain range that is now almost entirely submerged.

## Asian Mountain Systems
### The Kuen-lun System
The Kuen-lun system in Central Asia features several complex parallel structures. The western extremity consists of a double border-ridge—the Sarik-kol range and the Muztagh (or Kashgar) range—which separates the Pamir highlands from the East Turkestan lowlands. These are continued eastward by twin ranges: the northerly range (including the Kilian, Khotan, and Keriya Mountains) and the southerly range (including the Raskan, Sughet, and Ullugh-tagh Mountains). 

Further east lies the Arka-tagh (or Przhevalsky Range), described as the backbone of the Kuen-lun system. It consists of numerous parallel crests of nearly uniform altitude, with four predominant ranges. The Arka-tagh serves as the border-range of the Tibetan plateau. Its peaks are characterized as broad, rounded, flattened domes rather than jagged pinnacles.

In the Nan-shan mountains to the southwest, a succession of narrow ranges exists between a lower border terrace and a higher terrace (12,000-13,500 ft.). This area includes the Humboldt (Ama-surgu) range and other parallel ranges such as the Semenov, Suess, Alexander III., Mushketov, and Bain-sarlyk.

### The Indian Subcontinent
In the District of Kurnool, the landscape is defined by two long mountain ranges, the Nallamalais and the Yellamalais, which extend in parallel lines through the center of the district. The Nallamalai range features peaks such as Biranikonda (3,149 ft.), Durugapukonda (3,086 ft.), and Gundlabrahmeswaram (3,055 ft.). The Yellamalai is a lower, generally flat-topped range with a highest point of approximately 2,000 ft.

## African and European Ranges
### East Africa
In British East Africa, the geography rises in steps from the coast plain ("Temborari") to the "Nyika" uplands. Beyond the Nyika, there is an intermittent line of gneissose or schistose mountains running north-north-west, sometimes appearing in parallel chains. This zone contains high volcanic elevations, including Kenya (17,007 ft.), Sattima (13,214 ft.), and Nandarua (about 12,900 ft.). The Sattima range, also known as the Aberdare Mountains, maintains a general elevation of 10,000 ft.

### Egypt
According to historical accounts, Egypt is narrowed by two mountain ranges. One range belongs to Arabia and stretches from North toward the South Wind to the Erythraian Sea. On the side of Egypt toward Libya, another rocky range enveloped in sand extends in the same direction. The space between these two ranges consists of plain-land.

### The Carpathians
The eastern or wooded Carpathians are formed of small parallel ranges. This monotonous sandstone range extends from the river Poprad to the sources of the Golden Bistritza and the river Viso. The range has a mean altitude of 3,250 ft. in its northern two-thirds, rising to 5,000 ft. in the south. Its culminating peak is Cserna Gora at 6,505 ft.
