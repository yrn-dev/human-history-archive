# Aix-la-Chapelle

Aix-la-Chapelle, also known as Aachen, is a city located in the Rhine Province of Prussia. Its name is derived from the word *Aquæ*, a term used by the Romans for places noted for medicinal or warm springs, which is likely related to the Celtic word *Ac* or *Acq*.

## Imperial and Political History
Aix-la-Chapelle served as a favorite residence of Charles the Great (Charlemagne), who utilized the city as his winter quarters. During his time there, Charlemagne gathered learned men to engage in theological discussions and learning.

The city became a primary site for the conferral of the German crown, which eventually became the most important of the imperial crowns. While early emperors were crowned in Rome, the German coronation was most commonly performed at Aix-la-Chapelle. In these ceremonies, the archbishop of Cologne served as the chief officiant, and the emperor was presented to him by the archbishops of Trier and Mainz. This tradition continued after 1440, when Frederick III became the last emperor to be crowned in Rome, leaving the German coronation as the sole celebration. Additionally, Otto I (the Great) was elected as successor at Aix-la-Chapelle in 936 before later being crowned emperor in Rome in 962.

## Architecture and Art
The cathedral of Aix-la-Chapelle features west doors dating from the 9th century. These doors are wrought in bronze and were likely brought from Constantinople, similar in manufacture to the bronze plates of the 6th-century church of the Nativity at Bethlehem and the 8th and 9th-century doors of Sta Sophia in Constantinople.

## Diplomatic Significance
The city has served as a site for major international treaties. In 1748, the Peace of Aix-la-Chapelle ended the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). This conflict had begun with Frederick II of Prussia's invasion of Silesia and involved nearly all European powers, including France, Austria, England, Holland, Spain, and Bavaria. Despite French victories at Raucoux and Fontenoy, the peace treaty brought no gains to France, leading to diplomatic criticism of the king.

## Geography and Regional Context
Located in the Prussian Rhine province, Aix-la-Chapelle is the terminus for the main railway from Berlin. It is situated in the same region as Barmen, a manufacturing center that lies immediately east of Elberfeld.
