# Halberstadt

## Military History and Johan Baner
Halberstadt served as a point of retreat for the Swedish army during the Thirty Years' War. Johan Baner, a Swedish soldier and field-marshal born at Djursholm Castle in 1596, was a chief subordinate to Gustavus Adolphus. Baner had a distinguished career serving in wars with Poland and Russia, and later led the right wing of Swedish horse at the first battle of Breitenfeld. His service included the taking of Munich and Augsburg, as well as actions at Donauwoerth and the Lech.

In 1632, Baner was left in command in the west, opposing the imperial general Aldringer, after receiving a wound during an unsuccessful assault on Wallenstein's camp at the Alte Veste. By 1634, as field-marshal, he led 16,000 men into Bohemia and marched on Prague in combination with the Saxon army, though this advance was halted by the defeat of Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar at the first battle of Noerdlingen.

Following the peace of Prague, the Swedish army's position became precarious. However, Baner, along with Torstensson and Wrangel, won victories at Wittstock (October 4, 1636) and Kyritz, which restored Swedish influence in central Germany. Despite these wins, Baner was unable to make headway against the enemy in 1637 and was forced to retreat into Pomerania beyond the Oder after rescuing the garrison of Torgau.

In 1639, Baner overran northern Germany and invaded Bohemia after defeating the Saxons at Chemnitz. During the winter of 1640-1641, he executed a rare mid-winter coup-de-main on the Danube, uniting with the French forces under the comte de Guebriant to surprise Regensburg. The capture of the city, where the diet was sitting, was prevented only by the breaking up of the ice. Following this event, Baner retreated to Halberstadt, where he died on May 10, 1641.

## Academic Publications
Halberstadt is noted in the archive as a location of publication. In 1832, the work *Ueber das Zeitalter und Vaterland des Homer* was published there by Bernhard Thiersch.
