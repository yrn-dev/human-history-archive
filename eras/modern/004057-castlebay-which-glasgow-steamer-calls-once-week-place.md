# Castlebay

Castlebay is the chief village of Barra (also known as Barray), an island located in the outer Hebrides of Inverness-shire, Scotland. The village serves as a key maritime link for the island, as it is the point at which the Glasgow steamer calls once a week.

## Etymology and History
The name of the village is derived from the castle of Kishmul. This castle stands on a rock within the bay and formerly served as the stronghold for the McNeills of Barra, who are recognized as one of the oldest Highland clans.

## Geography of Barra
Barra is situated approximately 5 miles southwest of South Uist. The island measures 8 miles in length and varies in breadth from 2 to 4 miles. However, at a sandy isthmus located 2 miles below Scurrival Point, the island narrows to a width of only a few hundred yards. The geological composition of the island is gneiss. Its highest elevation is Heaval, which reaches 1,260 feet, and the landscape includes several small lochs.

The parish of Barra encompasses various smaller islets and islands. To the northeast are Flodda, Hellisay, Gighay, and Frida. To the southeast are Berneray, Mingalay (which had a population of 135 in 1901), Pabbay, and Vatersay. Barra Head, located on Berneray, is the most southerly point of the outer Hebrides chain and the highest point of that island; it features a lighthouse positioned 680 feet above high water.

## Land Use and Economy
The island contains 18,000 acres of hill pasture and meadow, along with 4,000 acres of arable land. The economy is significantly supported by fisheries, specifically those for ling, herring, and cod. Additionally, the coasts are rich in shell-fish, with the island being particularly famous for its cockles.

## Local Landmarks and Communications
Beyond the castle of Kishmul, the island contains Druidical circles, Danish duns, and the remains of ancient chapels. In addition to the weekly steamer service from Glasgow to Castlebay, there is ferry communication available between Barra and South Uist.
