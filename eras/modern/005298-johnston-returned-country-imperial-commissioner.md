# H.H. Johnston and the British Protectorate

## Mission to South Central Africa
In 1889, Mr. (afterwards Sir) H.H. Johnston was dispatched to South Central Africa. His mission was twofold: first, to attempt to reach a possible arrangement regarding a dispute between the African Lakes Corporation and a number of Arabs who had established themselves at the north end of Lake Nyasa; and second, to ensure that friendly native chiefs were protected from Portuguese aggression beyond a specific point.

## Background of the Region
The territory had a limited recent history prior to these events. While Portuguese wanderings occurred during the 16th and 17th centuries, the first educated European explorer to penetrate the country was the Portuguese official Dr. F.J.M. de Lacerda e Almeida, who traveled from Tete on the Zambezi toward Lake Mweru. 

The region's modern history is tied to David Livingstone, who discovered Lake Nyasa in 1859 after penetrating up the Shiré river. Livingstone's further explorations to Lake Mweru, Lake Bangweulu (where he died in 1873), and the south end of Tanganyika established British interests in South Central Africa. Following his death, Scottish settlers and various missionary societies entered the area to begin evangelization. These groups actively opposed Portuguese attempts to extend their influence from the Zambezi and the provinces of Moçambique.

## Conflict and the African Lakes Corporation
From the missionary societies, a trading entity known as the African Lakes Trading Corporation was formed. This corporation entered into a struggle for possession of the country against the Arabs, a conflict that began around 1885 and lasted until 1896. During this unofficial war, the African Lakes Corporation utilized volunteers, including Mr. (afterwards Sir) Alfred Sharpe and Captain (afterwards Sir F.D.) Lugard, both of whom were wounded during operations that did not achieve complete success.

## Establishment of the Protectorate
The efforts of H.H. Johnston and the treaties he negotiated resulted in the creation of a British sphere of influence and protectorate located north of the Zambezi.
