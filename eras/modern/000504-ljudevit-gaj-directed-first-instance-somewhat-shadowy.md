# Ljudevit Gaj

Ljudevit Gaj was a figure associated with the "Illyrian" movement among the southern Slavs. Starting in 1836, he voiced this movement through the publication of the *Illyrian National Gazette*.

## Political Objectives and Evolution
The movement led by Gaj was initially directed toward a "somewhat shadowy" Pan-Slav union. However, the objectives of the movement shifted following interference from the Austrian government in 1844. At that time, the goal was exchanged for a more definite objective: the revival of "the Triune Kingdom," consisting of Croatia, Slavonia, and Dalmatia, as an entity independent of the Hungarian crown.

## Historical Context
The activities of Ljudevit Gaj occurred during a period of significant political instability and rising national movements within the Austrian Empire. Following the death of Francis I on March 2, 1835, he was succeeded by his son, Ferdinand I. Because Ferdinand I was enfeebled by epilepsy and incapable of ruling, a veiled regency was established to manage the government. This period was marked by divided counsels at the center and weakness in administration, which allowed popular discontent to grow rapidly.

During this era, various populations grew restive under German-Magyar domination. In Hungary, the diet showed signs of vigorous life, and Slav separatist movements—particularly in the south of the kingdom—roused the spirit of Magyar ascendancy. Similarly, in Bohemia, the Czech literary movement evolved into an organized resistance to the established order, utilizing "Repeal" as a watchword for nationalists, mirroring the terminology used by Irish nationalists in their criticism of English administration in Ireland.

Beyond the Slav movements, national movements had also gained entrance in the German provinces despite the efforts of Metternich's police and censors. These conditions created an environment where advanced revolutionary views were making headway, as later evidenced by the revolution of 1848 in Vienna.
