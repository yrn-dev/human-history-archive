# Kaiserlich-Koeniglich

The term *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich*, frequently abbreviated as "K.K.," refers to a specific designation used within the administrative and royal structures of the Austrian-Hungarian Monarchy (also known as the Austrian-Hungarian Realm). The terminology reflects the complex political arrangement of Dualism and the separation of the districts over which the monarch ruled.

## Evolution of Royal Titles and Dualism
The application of these titles was driven by a need for logical consistency regarding the principle of Dualism. On November 14, 1868, the emperor issued a letter to Beust ordering a change in his official styling. Previously, he had been titled "Emperor of Austria, King of Hungary, King of Bohemia, &c." Following the order, his title became "Emperor of Austria, King of Bohemia, &c., and Apostolic King of Hungary." This shift was intended to signify the separation of the two districts under his rule. 

In shorter form, the monarch is styled as "His Majesty the Emperor and King" or "His Imperial and Apostolic Royal Majesty."

## Imperial and Royal Terminology
Following the establishment of this dual structure, the terminology "Imperial and Royal" (*Kaiserlich und Koeniglich*) was introduced. This designation was applied to branches of the public service that fell under the jurisdiction of the common ministries. The diplomatic service was the first to adopt this new terminology. 

The transition to "Imperial and Royal" occurred at different times across various institutions:
* **The Army:** The military retained the old style of *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich* for a period, only adopting the new "Imperial and Royal" terminology in 1889.
* **Ministry of the Imperial House:** This office, which was always held by the minister for foreign affairs, adopted the terminology in 1895.

## Administrative Divisions and Ministries
The distinction between the "Imperial and Royal" and "Kaiserlich-Koeniglich" designations serves to differentiate between joint affairs and those specific to the Austrian half of the monarchy. While "Imperial and Royal" is used for common ministries, the old style *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich* (K.K.) is retained for the Austrian half.

This division is exemplified by the structure of the finance ministries. There are three distinct ministries of finance:
1. The *Kaiserlich und Koeniglich* ministry, which handles joint affairs.
2. The *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich* ministry, which handles Austrian affairs.
3. The *Kiralye* ministry, which handles Hungarian affairs.

## The Ministry for Foreign Affairs
The leadership of foreign affairs also underwent a change in terminology to accommodate political sensitivities. The minister for foreign affairs was originally titled the *Reichskanzler*. However, in 1871, when Andrassy succeeded Beust, this title was abandoned. The change was made in deference to Hungarian feeling, as the title *Reichskanzler* could be interpreted as implying the existence of a single state of which the minister was the head.
