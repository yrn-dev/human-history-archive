# Augustus

Augustus was a Roman leader whose administrative maxims, fiscal policies, and urban planning left a lasting impact on the Roman Empire. His influence extended to the naming of the Roman calendar and the strategic development of Italian cities.

## Administrative and Fiscal Policies
Augustus implemented several impositions to manage the empire's finances, including a twentieth tax on legacies and inheritances. This specific tax was comprehensive and fruitful, and its revenue continued to increase as the Roman City gradually extended its reach. 

In the administration of the empire, Augustus adopted a policy of prudent liberality regarding the diffusion of the city's freedom. He guarded the dignity of the Roman name with strict care, a practice followed by other wise princes who adopted his maxims.

## Urban Development and Ravenna
Augustus recognized the strategic opportunity provided by the location of Ravenna on the Adriatic coast, situated approximately ten or twelve miles from the southernmost of the seven mouths of the Po. He established a capacious harbor there capable of receiving two hundred and fifty ships of war. This naval establishment served as a permanent station for the Roman fleet and included magazines, arsenals, barracks for troops, and houses for artificers.

To support the city, Augustus constructed a principal canal that directed a copious stream of water from the Po through the city to the harbor entrance. This system included a network of a thousand subordinate canals that distributed water throughout the city, dividing it into various small islands. These waters also fed the profound ditches that surrounded the city walls. Due to this geography, the houses of Ravenna were built on wooden piles, and communication within the city relied on bridges and boats.

## Diplomatic and Military Exploits
Augustus was involved in the recovery of Roman military honors. In 20 B.C., Phraates IV delivered to Augustus the Roman soldiers, standards, and eagles that had previously been captured by Crassus. Augustus recorded this event among his exploits in the *Monumentum Ancyranum*, and the occurrence was commemorated by extant medals.

## Legacy and Honors
The influence of Augustus extended to the Roman calendar. The sixth month of the Roman year, originally known as Sextilis (in a year beginning in March), was renamed Augustus in his honor. This followed the precedent of the fifth month, Quintilis, which had been renamed Julius to honor the Dictator Cæsar.
