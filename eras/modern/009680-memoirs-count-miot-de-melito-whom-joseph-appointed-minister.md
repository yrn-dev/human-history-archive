# The Memoirs of Count Miot de Melito

The *Memoirs of Count Miot de Melito* serve as a primary historical record documenting the political and military administrations of Joseph Bonaparte. The memoirs, which were edited by General Fleischmann and published in two volumes in 1881, provide detailed insights into Joseph Bonaparte's tenure as a monarch in both Naples and Spain, as well as his earlier roles within the French Republic.

## Early Association with Joseph Bonaparte
Before serving as a minister, Miot de Melito acted as a commissioner of the French Republic. In this capacity, he worked alongside Joseph Bonaparte to assist in the reorganization of Corsica during a French expedition for the island's recovery.

## Administration in Naples
Joseph Bonaparte was proclaimed king of Naples by Napoleon on March 30, 1806, following the expulsion of the Bourbon dynasty from southern Italy. During this period, Joseph appointed Count Miot de Melito as his minister of war. 

According to the memoirs, the new monarch faced severe challenges during his reign in Naples. Miot de Melito details the difficulties Joseph had to contend with, which included:
* A treasury that was almost bankrupt.
* A populace described as degraded and fickle.
* Plots and intrigues orchestrated by the Bourbons.
* Frequent attacks launched by the British from Sicily.

These instabilities were further exacerbated by General Stuart's victory at Maida on July 3, which shook the foundations of Joseph's throne. Despite these obstacles, the memoirs and accompanying records note that Joseph implemented various improvements, including the reorganization of educational, financial, and judicial systems, the reform of monastic orders, the abolition of feudal relics, and the initiation of public works.

## Tenure in Spain
In May 1808, Joseph Bonaparte was called by Napoleon to take the crown of Spain. During this sovereignty, Miot de Melito served as Joseph's trusted adviser. The memoirs reflect a period of significant frustration and diminished authority for the king.

Miot de Melito observed that while Joseph attempted to function as a constitutional king of Spain, the realities of the Peninsular War and the experience of 1808–1809 rendered such an ambition impossible. The memoirs state that Joseph could only succeed in the Peninsula by becoming "the mere instrument of a military power." 

The records indicate that Joseph's role became largely titular. He faced a treasury reduced to the last extremity and saw his authority stripped as Napoleon placed north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals as military districts between February and May 1810. Miot de Melito describes the king as bearing a title that was "only an oppressive burden," noting that Joseph had effectively ceased to exist as a monarch, retaining only a semblance of authority as a general over a small portion of the French army.
