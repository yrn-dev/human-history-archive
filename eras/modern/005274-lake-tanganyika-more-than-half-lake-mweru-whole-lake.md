# Lakes Tanganyika, Mweru, and Bangweulu

The lakes of Tanganyika, Mweru, and Bangweulu are significant bodies of water located in South Central Africa. These lakes, along with their surrounding basins and plateaus, form a critical part of the region's physical geography and have been central to European exploration and the establishment of colonial interests.

## Physical Characteristics

### Lake Tanganyika
Lake Tanganyika is situated at an elevation of approximately 2,600 feet above sea level. It has a length of about 400 miles and an average breadth of nearly 40 miles. The lake is characterized by a "halolimnic" fauna, which distinguishes it from other nearby bodies of water such as Lake Kivu. To the north of Tanganyika lies Lake Kivu, which discharges its waters into Tanganyika via the Rusizi River; the river descends 2,000 feet through steep gorges to reach the lacustrine plain at the head of Lake Tanganyika.

### Lake Mweru and Lake Bangweulu
Lake Mweru and Lake Bangweulu are located at higher altitudes than Tanganyika, sitting at 3,000 feet and 3,760 feet above sea level, respectively. The territory in this region includes more than half of Lake Mweru and the entirety of Lake Bangweulu. The Luapula (or upper Congo) river exits Lake Bangweulu and flows into the north end of Lake Mweru.

## Regional Geography and Drainage
The lakes are part of a broader territory of about 250,000 square miles. Much of this country consists of high plateau with an average altitude of 3,500 feet. Key geographical features include:

*   **The Nyasa-Tanganyika Plateau:** Located between the vicinity of Lake Tanganyika, Lake Mweru, and the basin of the river Luangwa, reaching heights of 7,000 to 8,000 feet.
*   **Drainage Basins:** While more than two-thirds of the region lies within the Zambezi basin, other portions drain into the basins of Lake Tanganyika and the river Congo.
*   **Other Water Bodies:** The Great Mweru Swamp is situated between Lake Tanganyika and Lake Mweru.

## Exploration and History
The discovery and exploration of these lakes were pivotal to European interests in Africa. In February 1858, Richard Francis Burton and John Hanning Speke discovered Lake Tanganyika. This expedition served as an incentive for later explorers, including David Livingstone.

Livingstone's journeys to Lake Mweru, Lake Bangweulu (where he died in 1873), and the south end of Lake Tanganyika opened the region to British interests. Following Livingstone, missionary societies and Scottish settlers entered the area, opposing Portuguese attempts to extend their influence from the Zambezi and Moçambique provinces. This period saw the rise of the African Lakes Trading Corporation, which engaged in a struggle for possession of the country against Arabs established at the north end of Lake Nyasa between 1885 and 1896. These events eventually led to the creation of a British protectorate north of the Zambezi.

## Human Geography and Linguistics
The regions surrounding these lakes are inhabited by various peoples speaking Bantu languages. 

*   **Tanganyika Region:** The south-west and south coasts of Tanganyika are occupied by those speaking Bemba (or Emba) dialects, including the locally-modified Ki-lungu and Ki-mambwe of South and South-East Tanganyika. At the north end of Lake Tanganyika, the language Ki-rundi is spoken in the Burundi country.
*   **Mweru and Other Regions:** The Bemba genus also extends to Lake Mweru and the Upper Chambezi river.
*   **Migration:** It is noted that approximately 3,000 years ago, Bantu-speaking peoples pushed from the heart of Africa into the southern half of the continent, assimilating or absorbing previous populations of Forest-negro, Nilotic-negro, Hottentot, and Bushman from Lake Nyasa and Lake Tanganyika south-westwards to the Cape of Good Hope.

## Settlements and Infrastructure
Several European settlements have been established in the vicinity of these lakes:
*   **Abercorn:** Located on the south end of Lake Tanganyika.
*   **Kalungwisi:** Situated on the east coast of Lake Mweru.
*   **Fort Rosebery:** Located near the Johnston Falls on the Luapula.

In terms of proposed infrastructure, British projects have considered a railway line from the south of Elizabethville to Bismarckburg at the south of Lake Tanganyika. From there, steamers would transport passengers to Ujiji, connecting to existing railways leading toward Lake Victoria Nyanza.
