# Railway Communication and Commercial Agreements between Austria and Hungary

The development of railway communication and commercial relations between Austria and Hungary was a central component of the Dual Monarchy's internal stability and economic integration. These relations were governed by a series of customs and commercial treaties and the efforts of key statesmen to unify the infrastructure of the two states.

## The Customs and Commercial Treaty of 1907
In October 1907, Austria and Hungary reached a new "customs and commercial treaty." This agreement was facilitated by a severe economic crisis in Hungary, which demonstrated that separation from Austria would likely result in commercial ruin or a period of distress. Simultaneously, Austria recognized that separation from Hungary would deprive Austrian manufacturers of their primary market and increase the cost of living in Cisleithania.

A primary feature of this 1907 agreement concerned the facilitation of railway communication. Specifically, the treaty mandated that Austria facilitate railway communication between Hungary and Prussia. In exchange, Hungary agreed to facilitate the establishment of direct railway communication between Vienna and Dalmatia. This connection was to be completed by the end of 1911, with each state responsible for building the sections of the line located within its own territory.

Other key provisions of the 1907 treaty included:
* The maintenance of separate but identical customs tariffs for each state.
* The practical abolition of impediments to free trade in sugar.
* The establishment of a court of arbitration to settle differences, consisting of four Hungarian judges selected by Austria and four Austrian judges selected by Hungary, with the presidency decided by lot.
* Requirements for Hungary to reform its Stock Exchange and produce laws to prevent agrarian speculation.
* Financial arrangements regarding the old Austrian debt, allowing Hungary to redeem its share at a rate of 4.325% within ten years.
* The recognition of government securities as investments for insurance companies and savings banks in both countries.

## Infrastructure and the Role of Gabor Baross
The unification and expansion of the railway system were significantly advanced by Gabor Baross (1848–1892), a Hungarian statesman who served as the minister of ways and communications starting in 1886. Baross is credited with the unification of the gigantic Austro-Hungarian railway network. 

One of Baross's most original contributions was the implementation of the zone system. This system cheapened and facilitated the circulation of produce and wares, effectively bringing the most remote districts into direct communication with the central hub at Budapest. Following the amalgamation of the ministry of commerce with the ministry of ways in 1889, Baross sought to increase the commercial productiveness of the kingdom and make Hungarian trade independent of foreign influences through a revision of tolls. This policy met with protests from Germany and Austria at the 1890 Vienna conference, leading Baross to modify the system.

## Broader Economic and Political Framework
The railway and commercial arrangements existed within a larger constitutional framework established by the *Ausgleich* (Compromise) of February 8, 1867. Under this system, Austria and Hungary were independent states with their own governments and parliaments, united by a common head of state—the Emperor of Austria and Apostolic King of Hungary.

Common administration was maintained for foreign affairs, the army and navy, and finance regarding joint expenditure. Additionally, a customs and commercial agreement, originally concluded in 1867 and renewed every ten years (including a renewal in 1899), ensured that both states followed the same principles regarding:
* Customs legislation and commercial affairs.
* Duties on mineral oils, sugar, brandy, and beer.
* The principles of the Austro-Hungarian Bank and the determination of coinage and legal tender.
* Ordinances for railways that affected the interests of both states.

This framework created a single identical customs and commercial territory that included the principality of Liechtenstein as well as Bosnia and Herzegovina.

## External Railway Ambitions
Beyond internal communication, the monarchy pursued strategic railway concessions in the Balkan Peninsula. In January 1908, Baron von Aerenthal obtained a concession from the sultan for a railway extending from the Bosnian frontier through the sanjak of Novibazar to the Turkish terminus at Mitrovitza. This move was pursued with the support of Germany but was opposed by Russia and caused resentment in Servia, which viewed the railway as another link in the chain of Habsburg influence.
