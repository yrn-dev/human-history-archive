# 1641

The year 1641 was a period of significant political and religious activity in Scotland and Ireland, characterized by the influence of the Covenanters and the efforts of King Charles I to manage opposition within his realms.

## Political Developments in Scotland
In August 1641, King Charles I personally opened parliament in Edinburgh. This action was part of a broader policy of conciliation directed toward the leaders of the Covenant. As a result of this policy, John Campbell, 1st Earl of Loudoun, was appointed lord chancellor of Scotland and first commissioner of the treasury; his title of earl was also allowed during this time.

The parliament that met in Edinburgh in August 1641 was presided over by John, the elder son of Balmerino (who died in 1649). Balmerino was a trusted counsellor to the marquess of Argyll and a chief among the Covenanters. He had previously faced severe opposition to Charles I, which led to his imprisonment in 1634 and a death sentence for "leasing-making," though he was eventually pardoned after intercession by others, including the poet Drummond of Hawthornden.

## Religious and Social Impact in Ireland
During the reign of Charles I, many Scottish Covenanters settled in the neighborhood of Carrickfergus to escape persecution. By 1641, the civil wars had begun, and Carrickfergus became one of the primary places of refuge for Protestants located in the county of Antrim. This period of religious transition culminated shortly after in June 1642, when the first Presbytery held in Ireland met at Carrickfergus.

## Other Events of 1641
In other personal histories recorded in the archive, 1641 marked the death of Eleanor, the daughter of Hugh Grove of Chisenbury, Wiltshire. Eleanor had married her husband around 1640. Their eldest child, John, was baptized by his father at Broadwindsor on June 6, 1641; he later became the rector of Great Wakering, Essex, and rector of Sidney Sussex College. Additionally, the archive notes the birth of Sir Patrick Hume (later the 1st Earl of Marchmont) in 1641.
