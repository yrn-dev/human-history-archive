# BONNEY, THOMAS GEORGE (1833- )

Thomas George Bonney was an English geologist born on July 27, 1833, in Rugeley. He was the eldest son of the Rev. Thomas Bonney, who served as the master of the grammar school in that town.

## Education and Early Career
Bonney received his education at Uppingham and St John's College, Cambridge. In 1856, he graduated as 12th wrangler and was ordained the following year. Between 1856 and 1861, he served as the mathematical master at Westminster school. During this period, he pursued geology primarily as a recreation, focusing his efforts mainly on Alpine regions.

## Academic and Professional Appointments
In 1868, Bonney was appointed as a lecturer in geology and a tutor at St John's College. He later expanded his professional standing in 1877 when he was chosen as the professor of geology at University College, London. His academic and professional contributions were recognized through several leadership roles; he served as the secretary and subsequently the president of the Geological Society from 1884 to 1886. Additionally, he was the secretary of the British Association from 1881 to 1885 and held the presidency of both the Alpine Club and the Mineralogical Society. In 1887, he was appointed as an honorary canon of Manchester.

## Scientific Research and Publications
Bonney's scientific attention was specifically directed toward the study of metamorphic and igneous rocks. His research spanned various locations, including the Alpine regions and several areas within England, such as Salcombe, the Lizard, Charnwood Forest, the Scottish Highlands, and Wales.

His purely scientific bibliography includes the following works:
* *Cambridgeshire Geology* (1875)
* *The Story of our Planet* (1893)
* *Charles Lyell and Modern Geology* (1895)
* *Ice Work, Past and Present* (1896)
* *Volcanoes* (1899)

Beyond these volumes, Bonney contributed numerous papers to the *Geological Magazine* and the *Quarterly Journal of the Geological Society*. He also authored several popular works concerning English and Welsh scenery and Alpine Regions, as well as writings on theological subjects.
