# The District of Banda and Regional Mutiny

## History of Banda
Banda is one of the districts located within the general region of Bundelkhand. Throughout its history, it has served as an arena of contention for various races struggling for sovereignty over India. The town of Kalinjar, which served as the capital, was the site of several significant sieges: it was unsuccessfully besieged by Mahmud of Ghazni in A.D. 1023, captured by Kutab-ud-din (the general of Muhammad Ghori) in 1196, and taken in 1545 by Shere Shah, who was mortally wounded during the assault.

Around the year 1735, the territory of the raja of Kalinjar, which included the district of Banda, was bequeathed to Baji Rao, the Mahratta peshwa. Following treaties conducted between 1802 and 1803, the territory passed from the Mahrattas to the Company.

## The Mutiny and Recovery
At the time of the Mutiny, the district of Banda was over-taxed and poverty-stricken, leading the district to join the rebels. The town of Banda was eventually recovered on April 20, 1858, by General Whitlock. Following these events, the fiscal system of the district was remodelled. While the district subsequently experienced a greater degree of prosperity, this progress was interrupted by famine.

## Other Regional Mutiny Activities
The Mutiny manifested across various other districts and regions:

*   **Azamgarh:** This district became a center of mutiny in 1857. On June 3, 1857, the 17th Regiment of Native Infantry mutinied, murdered several of their officers, and transported the government treasure to Fyzabad. The area became a site of conflict between rebels and Gurkhas, and was not cleared until October 1858 by Colonel Kelly.
*   **Trans-Gangetic Districts:** George Yule worked to suppress the Mutiny within his limits by organizing a force that included British sailors, a small detachment of the 5th Regiment, and mounted volunteers. Yule utilized dog-carts as limbers for three-pounders and collected elephants to assist the sailors and infantry. He routed Mutineers on several occasions and eventually drove them across the Nepaul frontier.
*   **Other Areas:** In the Bilaspur district, a hill chief caused trouble during the Sepoy mutiny but was captured and executed. In the Gonda district, the raja Sir Drigbijai Singh K.C.S.I. of the Balrampur estate remained conspicuously loyal during the Mutiny, for which he was granted hereditary privileges and accessions of territory. Additionally, Sir Alfred Comyn Lyall saw service during the Mutiny at Meerut, in the Bulandshahr district, and with the Khaki Risala of volunteers.

## Administrative Changes
Following the Mutiny, certain administrative shifts occurred. For example, the town of Bela, which had been founded as a cantonment in 1802, became a district headquarters after the Mutiny.
