# New Atlantis and Wisdom of the Ancients

The *New Atlantis* and *Wisdom of the Ancients* (*Sapientia Veterum*) are works attributed to Francis Bacon, Lord Verulam (1561–1626), who served as Lord Chancellor to James I of England. While Bacon has been referred to as the "Father of Experimental Philosophy," some perspectives suggest he was the "apostle" rather than the founder of the scientific method.

## The New Atlantis
The *New Atlantis* is described as a philosophic romance and a "fantastic book" in which Bacon outlines a vision of an ideal state. In this work, the principles of a "new philosophy" are implemented through state guidance and political machinery. 

A central feature of this ideal society is a "palace of invention," described as a great temple of science. In this institution, the pursuit of knowledge across all branches is organized according to principles of the highest efficiency. The work serves as a description of a state where many of the results Bacon contemplated are attained through imagination. Originally, the work was intended to include a second part detailing the laws of a model commonwealth, but this section was never written.

## Wisdom of the Ancients and Related Tracts
The *Wisdom of the Ancients* (*Sapientia Veterum*) is associated with Bacon's method of uncovering deep truths concealed within older narratives. This approach is mirrored in another tract, *De Principiis atque Originibus secundum Fabulas Cupidinis et Caeli*. In this work, Bacon uses two mythological stories as a disguise to explore the nature of primitive matter, the origin of things, and the limits of science. This specific tract also provides detailed notices of the modern philosopher Telesio and the ancient philosopher Democritus.

## Publication History and Editions
Various editions of Bacon's works have been produced across several languages. The *New Atlantis* and *Wisdom of the Ancients* have appeared together in "Cassell's National Library" in editions published in 1886 and 1903. Additionally, the *New Atlantis* was published alongside the *Advancement of Learning* in the "World's Classics" series in 1906, featuring an introduction by Professor T. Case. A separate edition of the *New Atlantis* was produced by G. C. M. Smith in 1900.

Other significant works by Bacon mentioned in the archive include:
*   ***Novum Organum***: Notable editions include those by T. Fowler (1878, 1889) and G. W. Kitchin (1855).
*   ***Essays***: Edited more than twenty times since 1870, with editions by figures such as Archbishop Whately, W. Aldis Wright, and E. A. Abbott. A facsimile of the first edition was published in New York in 1904.
*   ***Advancement of Learning***: Edited by W. Aldis Wright, F. G. Selby, and H. Morley.
*   ***Other Treatises***: These include *Cogitationes de Natura Rerum* (likely written around 1605) and *De Fluxu et Refluxu Maris*, a treatise on the theory of the tides written probably around 1616.
