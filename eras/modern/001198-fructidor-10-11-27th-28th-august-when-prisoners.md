# Fructidor 10 and 11

The dates of Fructidor 10 and 11, corresponding to the 27th and 28th of August, are associated in the historical record with the removal of prisoners from Paris during a period of political instability following a successful coup.

## Events of Fructidor 10 and 11
On Fructidor 10 and 11 (August 27th and 28th), prisoners were removed from Paris. During this transfer, tentative efforts were made to start a riot with the intention of rescuing the prisoners; however, these attempts were easily suppressed. 

These events occurred in a climate of political tension. The *Tribun* had ceased publication on April 24th, and Lebois, writing in the *Ami du peuple*, had attempted to incite soldiers to revolt, leading to temporary rumors of a military rising. Following the removal of the prisoners, further unrest occurred on September 7th, when five or six hundred Jacobins attempted to rouse soldiers at Grenelle, though this effort also failed.

## The Trial of Babeuf
The prisoners removed from Paris were subject to a trial before a newly constituted high court of justice located at Vendome. The trial began on February 20, 1797, and lasted for two months. 

The government designated the socialist Babeuf as the leader of the conspiracy, despite the implication of more important individuals. The archive notes that Babeuf's own vanity assisted the government in this characterization. The legal proceedings concluded on Prairial 7 (April 26, 1797), resulting in the following outcomes:
* **Death Sentences:** Babeuf and Darthe were condemned to death and subsequently executed at Vendome on Prairial 8 (1797).
* **Exile:** Several prisoners, including Buonarroti, were exiled.
* **Acquittals:** Other prisoners, including Vadier and his fellow-conventionals, were acquitted.

Additionally, it is noted that Drouet managed to escape, an act that Barras attributed to the connivance of the Directory.
