# Low-Lying Plains and Swamps of Southern Santa Cruz

## Geography and Characteristics
In the region of Bolivia, specifically within southern Santa Cruz and eastern Chuquisaca, there are extensive low-lying plains and swamps. These areas are characterized by their susceptibility to periodical inundations. Due to these frequent floods, these specific lands are considered to be of little value for pastoral and agricultural purposes.

The primary geographical challenge facing this region is defective drainage. The streams in the area possess a current that is too sluggish to effectively carry off water during the rainy season. Despite these drawbacks, there are considerable areas within this part of Bolivia that remain above the flood levels, providing rich grazing lands.

## Surrounding Regions and Topography
The low-lying plains of southern Santa Cruz are situated above the llanos of Manzo and the Bolivian Chaco, which are located in the extreme south. To the north, between the Andes and the Chiquitos sierras, lie the Llanos de Chiquitos, a region featuring a more diversified surface and a higher general elevation.

Further north of the Chiquitos elevation—which served as the southern shore of the ancient Mojos Lake—are the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos. This extensive region is traversed by several rivers and their tributaries, including the Beni, Mamore, Guapay, San Miguel, Guapore, Yacuma, and Madre de Dios. This area still contains large undrained sections, such as Lake Rojoagua (also known as Roguaguado), as it was once covered by the Mojos Lake.

## Land Use and Population
Unlike the inundated plains of southern Santa Cruz, the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos contain extensive open plains and rich agricultural districts. In these areas, cattle-raising has been successfully practiced since the period of the Jesuit missions.

To the north-west, the lower slopes of the Andes are covered with heavy forests, particularly where the Madre de Dios and Beni rivers traverse the land. This specific district is noted as one of the richest in Bolivia and possesses the capacity to sustain a large population.
