# Lucien Bonaparte

Lucien Bonaparte (1775–1840), also known as the prince of Canino, was a French political figure, diplomat, and writer. Born in Ajaccio on May 21, 1775, he was educated at the schools of Brienne and Autun. Although he initially desired a career in the French army, defective sight led him toward the church, and he attended the seminary at Aix in Provence in 1786. However, his volatile disposition clashed with the seminary's discipline.

## Early Political Career and the Revolution
Following the outbreak of the Revolution in 1789, Lucien embraced anti-clerical and democratic movements. Upon returning to Corsica, he became a leading speaker for the Jacobin club in Ajaccio. He urged his brothers to break ties with Paoli, the leader of the conservative party that sought an alliance with England. After the Paolists gained control, the Bonapartes left the island for Toulon. In southern France, Lucien continued his Jacobin activities in the town of St Maximin (renamed Marathon), where he used the pseudonym "Brutus."

On May 4, 1794, Lucien married Catherine Boyer without his family's consent and while still a minor, an act that resulted in near penury and disgrace. Following the coup d'etat of Thermidor on July 28, 1794, he fled St Maximin and took a minor post at St Chamans, where he was briefly imprisoned. He was released through the influence of Napoleon, who secured him a position as a commissioner in the army campaigning in Germany. Dissatisfied with this role, Lucien returned to Corsica and was eventually elected to the Council of Five Hundred in Paris in 1798.

## Diplomatic Service and Conflict with Napoleon
During the period leading up to the coup d'etat of Brumaire, Lucien assisted Napoleon in making overtures to Moreau and Sieyes. He later served as a member of the *Corps Legislatif* and the council of state. While he participated in negotiations for the Concordat, he personally viewed the measure as retrograde and ill-advised.

Lucien achieved significant diplomatic success in the following years:
*   **1800:** He concluded the Convention of Mortfontaine with the United States envoy at his country-house.
*   **1801:** He presided over negotiations leading to the treaty of Luneville with Austria.
*   **1802:** He and Maret represented France in discussions with Lord Cornwallis, resulting in the treaty of Amiens.

Despite these triumphs, relations between Lucien and Napoleon became strained during the consulate (1799–1804). Lucien had previously expressed suspicions that Napoleon's ambition surpassed his love for the general weal. After serving as minister of the interior, Lucien was removed due to personal and political differences and appointed ambassador to Madrid in November 1800. In June 1801, Lucien and the Spanish minister Godoy signed peace preliminaries at Badajoz, frustrating Napoleon's plans to seize Lisbon. This led to Lucien's resignation and his subsequent opposition to Napoleon's schemes in the Tribunate.

The final breach occurred in 1803. After the death of his first wife in 1800, Lucien became enamored with Mme Jouberthou. Despite Napoleon's express prohibition—and the Emperor's desire for Lucien to marry the widow of the king of Etruria for reasons of state—Lucien secretly married Mme Jouberthou on October 23, 1803. Consequently, Napoleon ordered him to leave French territory.

## Later Life and Exile
Lucien lived in Italy for several years, primarily in Rome, where he maintained a hostile stance toward the emperor. In December 1807, Napoleon offered to recognize Lucien as a French prince if he would annul his marriage, a condition Lucien refused. After residing at his estate of Canino, from which he took the title of prince of Canino, he departed for America. However, he was captured by a British ship and taken to Malta and then England, where he remained under surveillance until the peace of 1814.

During the Hundred Days in 1815, Lucien returned to Rome and offered his help to Napoleon. He stood by his brother at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris and was the final person to defend Napoleon's prerogatives during his second abdication. He spent his remaining years in Italy and died in Rome on June 29, 1840. He was a father to four sons and six daughters. His literary works include the epic *Charlemagne, ou l'Eglise delivree* (1814), as well as unfinished *Memoirs* and *La Verite sur les Cent Jours*.

## Descendants
Lucien's descendants included several notable figures:
*   **Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803–1857):** The prince of Canino and a scientist. He published *American Ornithology* and various catalogs on European fauna. He was active in the 1848 Italian political agitation, favoring Italian independence and the expulsion of Austrians, before fleeing to France in 1849.
*   **Louis Lucien (1813–1891):** Born in England, he served as a deputy for Corsica and the Seine in France starting in 1848. Though named a senator and prince by Napoleon III, he remained largely apolitical during the Second Empire. In his later years in England, he published works on the Basque language.
*   **Pierre Napoleon (1815–1881):** An adventurer who spent time in the Romagna, the United States, and Colombia. He was elected deputy for Corsica in 1848 as a republican. After reconciling with Napoleon III and accepting the title of prince, he withdrew from politics. He gained notoriety in 1870 for killing Victor Noir during a dispute, though he was acquitted by the High Court.
