# National Baptist Convention

The National Baptist Convention is a religious organization within the United States. Based on archival statistical records, the organization maintains a significant presence in terms of both its institutional reach and its membership.

## Membership and Scale
According to recorded data, the National Baptist Convention consists of 16,996 churches. The total membership of the organization is listed as 2,110,269 members. In a comparative context of religious bodies in the United States, these figures place the National Baptist Convention as having a larger membership than the Southern Baptist Convention, which is recorded as having 20,431 churches and 1,832,638 members.

## Context within Baptist Organizations
The National Baptist Convention exists alongside various other Baptist and Christian entities. Other notable groups in the United States include the "Disciples of Christ," with 11,157 churches and 1,235,798 members, and various bodies across thirty-five Northern States. 

The broader history of Baptist organization in the United States has been marked by efforts toward national coordination and subsequent sectional divisions. Early in the 19th century, interest in foreign missions led to the 1814 formation of the "General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions," which became known as the "Triennial Convention" due to its three-year meeting cycle. 

By 1845, tensions regarding slavery between Northern and Southern members led to the creation of the Southern Baptist Convention. While Northern and Southern Baptists maintained fellowship, they operated separate annual meetings and missionary boards. Later, in 1905, a General Baptist Convention for America was established to promote comity, fellowship, and denominational *esprit de corps*, though this specific body was designed not to undertake administrative work or interfere with existing sectional organizations.
