# Dismantlement of Fortifications and Structures (1648)

During the mid-17th century, specifically around 1648, several significant structures and fortifications were dismantled or removed across Europe, often as a consequence of political conflict or urban management.

## Military Dismantlements in England
In England, the conclusion of the Civil War led to the ordered destruction of several fortified positions. At Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a castle had been built and fortified in 1474 under a royal licence obtained by Lord Hastings. During the Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings and was visited by Charles in 1645. Following the close of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament.

Similarly, in the West Riding of Yorkshire, Knaresborough Castle—which had been founded probably in 1070 by Serlo de Burgh—met a similar fate. After the battle of Marston Moor, the castle was taken by Fairfax. In 1648, it was ordered to be dismantled, leaving behind remains from the 14th century, including a massive keep situated on a cliff above the river Nidd.

## Urban and Political Changes in Europe
Beyond military fortifications, other types of structures were removed during this era. In 1645, in the inner town of Leipzig, swine pens were pulled down. 

The year 1648 also marked a period of significant geopolitical restructuring through the Treaty of Westphalia. This treaty recognized Switzerland and Holland as free republics and increased the importance of Prussia. In the Belgian provinces, the Treaty of Munster (1648) between Spain and the United Provinces resulted in the Dutch gaining the right to control and close the Scheldt, which served as the source of wealth for Antwerp. This treaty established the final separation between Holland and Belgium.

## Other Events of 1648
The year 1648 was characterized by various intellectual and political upheavals:
* **France:** The country was experiencing the civil war of the Fronde. René Descartes visited Paris in 1648 after being summoned by a royal order for a permanent post and an additional pension, but he left without reproach upon finding the city "rent asunder" by the conflict. Around this time, Descartes also lost his friends Mydorge and Mersenne to death.
* **England:** In May 1648, William Legge was arrested after assisting Charles I in escaping from Hampton Court in 1647. Additionally, an ordinance dated May 2, 1648, prescribed death for the denial of the doctrine of the Trinity. Despite this, John Biddle issued two tracts in 1648 concerning the Holy Trinity and the one God, which were subsequently suppressed by the government.
