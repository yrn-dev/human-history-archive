# Barium Iodate

Barium iodate, represented by the chemical formula $\text{Ba}(\text{IO}_3)_2$, is a chemical compound of the element barium. It is obtained through two primary methods of preparation: by the action of an excess of iodic acid on a hot caustic baryta solution, or by adding sodium iodate to a solution of barium chloride.

## Physical Properties and Structure
Barium iodate crystallizes in the form of monoclinic prisms. Its composition is $\text{Ba}(\text{IO}_3)_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}$. In terms of solubility, the compound is only very sparingly soluble in cold water.

## Chemical Relations and Decomposition
Barium iodate serves as a precursor for the production of barium monoxide ($\text{BaO}$). The monoxide can be obtained in small quantities through the ignition of the iodate.

## Context of Barium Compounds
Barium iodate is one of several barium salts and oxides. Other related compounds include:
*   **Barium Halides:** Barium chloride ($\text{BaCl}_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which is a strong poison with a bitter taste; barium bromide ($\text{BaBr}_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$); and various other salts such as barium nitrate and barium carbonate.
*   **Barium Oxides:** Barium monoxide ($\text{BaO}$), barium dioxide ($\text{BaO}_2$), and a suboxide produced by heating $\text{BaO}$ with magnesium in a vacuum to $1100^\circ\text{C}$.
*   **Other Barium Salts:** Barium chlorate, barium bromate, barium sulphide, barium nitride, and barium sulphate. Barium sulphate ($\text{BaSO}_4$) is noted as the most abundant naturally occurring barium compound.

## Detection and Analysis
Barium and its salts, including the iodate, can be detected using several methods:
*   **Flame Test:** When moistened with hydrochloric acid and heated in a Bunsen flame, they produce a yellowish-green colour.
*   **Spectroscopy:** The observation of their spectra reveals two characteristic green lines.
*   **Precipitation:** In solution, barium salts can be identified by the immediate precipitate they form upon the addition of calcium sulphate, or by the yellow precipitate of barium chromate that forms when potassium chromate is added.
