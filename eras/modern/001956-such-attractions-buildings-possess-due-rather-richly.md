# Architectural and Decorative Features of Bagdad and Other Global Structures

## Architecture of Bagdad
In Bagdad, the general architectural style is described as neither imposing nor distinctive. The mosques of the city typically do not merit particular attention. The attractions that these buildings do possess are attributed to inscriptions—such as the Kufic inscription dated A.D. 944 found on the ruined *tekke* of the Bektash dervishes in western Bagdad—or to the use of richly coloured tiles for adornment.

Tomb mosques are considered more important than the mosques proper. The most imposing of these is the mosque of Kazemain, located in the northern suburb of the western city. This shrine serves as the burial place for Musa Ibn Ja'far el-Kazim and his grandson, Mahommed Ibn Ali el-Jawad, both of whom are recognized by Shi'as as the seventh and ninth successors of Ali. The mosque's current form dates from the 19th century. Its four lofty minarets, two great domes above the tombs, and part of the facade are overlaid with gold, making the glittering structures the first objects visible to travelers approaching Bagdad from any direction. It is one of four great Shi'ite Moslem shrines in the vilayet of Bagdad. Access to its precincts is forbidden to Christians, and the surrounding Kazemain quarter is inhabited by a population so fanatical that approaching the area is difficult and potentially dangerous.

## Notable Global Buildings and Structures
Various other regions exhibit distinct architectural characteristics and scales of magnificence:

*   **India:** In the Puri district of Orissa, the ruined temple of Konarak (or Kanarak) was dedicated to the sun-god in the middle of the 13th century. It has been described as the most richly ornamented building in the world, at least externally. The structure featured a porch 140 ft. high, sculptured with horses, elephants, and lions, and a tower that was likely over 180 ft. high.
*   **Budapest:** The city features imposing buildings along the broad quays of the Danube's left bank, extending 2½ m. from the custom-house to the Margaret bridge. The 1 m. long Franz Joseph Quai is a favorite promenade containing fashionable hotels and cafés. The Andrássy Ut, a 1½ m. long thoroughfare, is lined with magnificent new buildings in its first half and handsome villas with gardens in its second half.
*   **Other Magnificences:** Historical accounts from Marco Polo describe a system of more than 10,000 great buildings that were all richly furnished, existing on a scale of cost and magnificence that was difficult to describe.

## Functional and Military Construction
Architectural design is not always driven by ornamentation. In the British military system, the design of barrack buildings is governed by the *Barrack Synopsis* and "Standard Plans." These documents prioritize sound sanitary construction and convenience of plan over external effect. Architectural effect in these buildings depends on the balance and grouping of parts rather than expensive ornamentation.

In the realm of transport, carriage-building saw rapid progress during the reign of Louis XIV (which lasted until 1715), with contributions from England, France, Italy, and Germany. Early coaches were plain and rude, lacking complete doors or glass windows until approximately 1650. A significant advancement occurred around 1670 with the application of steel springs to wheel carriages, which reduced vibration and allowed for lighter construction.
