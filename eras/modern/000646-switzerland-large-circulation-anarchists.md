# Switzerland

## Political and Social Movements
Switzerland served as a significant hub for socialistic and anarchical activities during the 19th century. Mikhail Bakunin, a Russian anarchist born into an aristocratic family in 1814, resided in Switzerland for a period after traveling from Paris, during which time he took an active role in various socialistic movements. Following a period of imprisonment and exile in eastern Siberia and subsequent travel through Japan and the United States, Bakunin spent the remainder of his life in exile in western Europe, primarily in Switzerland. In 1873, he retired to Lugano and later died in Bern on June 13, 1876.

The country also played a role in the dissemination of radical literature to other regions. The paper *Freiheit*, published by Most, was introduced through Switzerland and achieved a large circulation in Austria, contributing to the introduction of anarchical doctrines that interrupted the growth of the socialist party there after 1879.

## Religious Activity
Switzerland has been active in the circulation of the Scriptures. In 1905, nine cantonal Bible societies within the country circulated a total of 71,000 copies of the Scriptures.

## Military History
Switzerland was the site of a military campaign led by Macdonald. This campaign is noted as having provided Macdonald with a rank superior to other generals of division, such as Dupont and Oudinot, due to his capacity for independent command. The exploits of this campaign were documented by Ségur and Mathieu Dumas, both of whom served on Macdonald's staff in Switzerland.

## Geology and Mineralogy
The region contains various distinct geological formations and minerals. In the Binnenthal near Brieg, beautiful water-clear crystals of dolomite are found within white crystalline dolomite-rock. These crystallized masses are also found embedded in talc-schist, serpentine, and other magnesian silicate rocks. Additionally, a variety of dolomite known as taraspite is found in Tarasp.
