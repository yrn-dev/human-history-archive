# James Matthew Barrie

## Early Life and Education
James Matthew Barrie was born on May 9, 1860, in Kirriemuir, a small village located in Forfarshire. His early education took place at the Dumfries academy and Edinburgh University. Barrie's inclination toward storytelling began before he entered school, as he wrote tales while in the garret. During his time at Edinburgh, he wrote the majority of a three-volume novel; a publisher, believing the work was written by a clever lady, offered to publish it for L100, though Barrie did not accept the offer.

## Career in Journalism
Barrie entered the field of literature through journalism. Following a brief waiting period in Edinburgh, he became a leader-writer for the *Nottingham Journal* in February 1883. In this role, he contributed notes and special articles, which served as training for his personal talent. 

He eventually began submitting work to editors in London. On November 17, 1884, Frederick Greenwood published Barrie's article "An Auld Licht Community" in the *St James's Gazette*. This led to a series of "Auld Licht Idylls," and by 1885, Barrie moved to London. While there, he continued writing for the *St James's Gazette* and *Home Chimes*, the latter of which was edited by F. W. Robinson.

Barrie's professional network expanded when he was enlisted by Alexander Riach for the *Edinburgh Evening Dispatch*. This position subsequently led to him writing for Dr Robertson Nicoll's *British Weekly* under the signature "Gavin Ogilvy." He later contributed to the *Speaker* upon its foundation in 1890, as well as the *Scots* (later known as the *National*) *Observer*, which was edited by W. E. Henley.

## Literary Works
In 1887, Barrie published his first book, *Better Dead*. This work was a *jeu d'esprit* and a specimen of his humorous journalism, developed from his contributions to the *St James's Gazette*. He followed this in 1888 with *Auld Licht Idylls*, a collection of sketches of a Scots village. 

These sketches portrayed the humours and life of his native village, which he idealized as "Thrums." The material for these stories was derived from his own early observations and tales told to him by his mother. Barrie noted that his mother "told me everything," and as a result, her memories colored his own memories of their "little red town."
