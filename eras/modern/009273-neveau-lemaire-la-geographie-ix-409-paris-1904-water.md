# Neveau-Lemaire and the Survey of Lake Titicaca

The work of Dr. M. Neveau-Lemaire, specifically his findings published in *La Geographie* (ix. p. 409, Paris, 1904), provides critical data regarding the physical characteristics and water measurements of Lake Titicaca.

## Lake Titicaca Characteristics
Lake Titicaca is situated near the northern end of the great Bolivian *alta-planicie*. It is recognized as one of the most elevated lakes in the world, positioned 12,644 feet above sea level. The lake's geography is characterized by numerous coves and bays. In terms of its overall dimensions, the lake reaches a maximum length of 138 miles and a maximum breadth of 69 miles.

## Water Surface and Depth
According to the survey conducted by Dr. M. Neveau-Lemaire, the water surface of Lake Titicaca is 1,969 square miles, a figure that excludes its peninsulas and islands. The survey further identifies the greatest depth of the lake's waters as 892 feet.

## Water Level Fluctuations and Drainage
The water level of Lake Titicaca is subject to seasonal changes. During the summer, the level of the lake rises by approximately 5 inches. The loss of water during the winter months is noted to be even greater than the summer rise. The lake's outlet is the Desaguadero river.

## Regional Context and Navigation
Lake Titicaca is shared between Peru and Bolivia. Due to its navigable waters, steamers operate between the ports of Bolivia and the Peruvian railway port of Puno. The lake contains several islands, the largest of which shares the lake's name.
