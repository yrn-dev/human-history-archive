# French Conquest of Corsica and the Bonaparte Family

## The Conquest of Corsica
The French conquest of Corsica was a period characterized by significant instability and "troubles." During this era of transition, the region experienced various upheavals that affected the local population and the administration of its districts.

## Carlo Buonaparte
Carlo Buonaparte (1746–1785), the father of Napoleon I, was a law graduate from the university of Pisa. Following the French conquest of Corsica, he secured a position as the assessor to the royal court of Ajaccio and the neighboring districts. 

Buonaparte is described as having a restless and dissatisfied nature. This disposition led him to engage in intriguing for different posts and embarking on risky business ventures. These enterprises had a long-term negative impact, compromising his family's fortune for many years. In his personal convictions, he was a Voltairian and a schemer by nature. He eventually died in Montpellier in 1785 from cancer of the stomach.

## Letizia Ramolino
In 1764, Carlo married Letizia Ramolino, who was fourteen years old at the time. She was a beautiful and high-spirited girl from a well-connected family that had been domiciled in Corsica since the middle of the 15th century. 

Letizia is characterized by a firm and courageous disposition, which became evident during the trying times following the French conquest. During this period, she accompanied her husband on several escapes and journeys. In contrast to her husband's ambitions in France, Letizia was devout in her thought and manner of life, and simple and frugal in her tastes. Her influence helped bind her children to the life of Corsica.

## The Bonaparte Children
The couple's first two children, born in 1765 and 1767, died during infancy. Their first surviving son, Joseph, was born in 1768. Napoleon was born in 1769, arriving in the midst of the troubles resulting from the French conquest. While Letizia fostered a connection to their Corsican roots, Carlo pointed the family toward careers in France. This shift in direction molded the fortunes of the Bonaparte family and the destinies of Europe.
