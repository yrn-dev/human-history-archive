# Siberian Species and Genera

The biological landscape of Siberia and its associated regions is characterized by a diverse array of flora and fauna, ranging from specialized crustaceans and rodents to high-latitude mammals and distinct botanical types. These species often intersect with other geographic regions, such as the Tibetan plateau and the North American continent.

## Botanical Distribution
The flora of the Tibetan plateau exhibits many features of the Siberian type due to increased dryness. This region contains many true Siberian species and a variety of Siberian genera. Some of these Siberian forms extend into the plains of upper India and the rainy portions of the mountains, having been brought into proximity with Indian flora. 

In the northernmost parts of Siberia, between the Arctic Ocean and the Seas of Okhotsk and Bering, the environment is a barren plateau reaching heights of 1,000 to 2,000 feet. Vegetation in this specific district is limited to mosses, lichens, and a few dwarf species of Siberian trees. Further south, in the middle province along the Sea of Okhotsk, the landscape changes to rugged mountains and deep valleys clothed in thick forests of larch.

## Mammalian Species
Siberia is home to several distinct mammalian species, many of which have historically been central to the Siberian fur trade.

### The White Fox
The white fox inhabits the extreme northern sections of Siberia, as well as Greenland, Labrador, Newfoundland, and Hudson Bay. Siberian white foxes are characterized as being rather whiter and more woolly than the silky, creamy-colored Canadian variety. Those found in central Asia or near Chinese territory are yellowish and poorer in quality. All varieties generally possess underwool with a bluish-grey tone, though rare skins with completely white underwool are more expensive.

### Ground-Squirrels
While ground-squirrels (chipmunks) are primarily confined to North America, there is one Siberian species known as *Tamias asiaticus*. These rodents are distinguished from squirrels by their cheek-pouches used for storing food.

### Marine and Land Fauna
The northern Siberian regions and surrounding seas have provided high-value furs, including the black sable and blue fox, though both have been nearly exterminated. Other marine species include the sea-bear (*Otaria ursina*), which multiplies rapidly and is nearly domesticated, and the sea-lion (*Otaria stelleri*), which is rapidly becoming extinct. The sea-cow (*Rhytina stelleri*) was completely extirpated over a period of forty years.

## Crustaceans and Other Genera
Within the study of fresh-water Entomostraca, specifically the family *Branchipodidae*, the genus *Artemiopsis* is identified as Siberian. This genus is distinguished by the absence of frontal appendages in the male, a characteristic it shares with the South African *Branchipodopsis*.

## Regional Commerce and Geography
Siberian productions have historically been traded through various hubs. Taganrock serves as a great deposit for these goods, receiving iron, furs, and tallow from Orenburg. 

The geography of the region is further defined by its infrastructure and urban centers. The town of Mariinsk, located in West Siberia within the government of Tomsk on the bank of the Kiya river, serves as an entrepôt for goldmines. It is situated on the Siberian railway, a critical transport link that has been used for the movement of Russian peasants to distant battlefields and as a route for travelers, including Czech soldiers who distributed the speeches of President Wilson to Siberian peasants.
