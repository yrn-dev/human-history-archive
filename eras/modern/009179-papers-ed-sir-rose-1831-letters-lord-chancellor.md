# Marchmont Papers and Letters to Lord Chancellor Hardwicke

The *Marchmont Papers* and the correspondence addressed to Lord Chancellor Hardwicke constitute specific archival and bibliographic resources used in the study of historical figures and political history.

## The Marchmont Papers
The *Marchmont Papers* are a collection of documents edited by Sir G.H. Rose and published in 1831. These papers serve as a bibliographic source for the study of Bolingbroke.

## Letters to Lord Chancellor Hardwicke
Additional correspondence relating to Bolingbroke is found in the form of letters addressed to Lord Chancellor Hardwicke. These documents are located in the *Add. MSS. Brit. Museum* (referenced in the Index, 1894-1899). The majority of these letters were transcribed by W. Sichel.

## Context of the Lord Chancellor
The Lord Chancellor is a high-ranking legal and political official in England. The appointment of a king's counsel—a barrister "learned in the law" appointed by letters patent—rests with the lord chancellor. Historically, the lord chancellor's role has been linked to the custody of the Great Seal of England. In some periods, the personal custody of this seal was entrusted to a lord keeper or vice-chancellor. For example, Sir Nicholas Bacon served as lord keeper, and an act of Elizabeth declared that the lord keeper was entitled to the same jurisdiction, pre-eminence, and execution of laws as the lord chancellor. Other notable figures who held the office of lord chancellor include Sir Thomas Bromley (appointed in 1579) and Lord Chancellor Macclesfield.

## Related Bibliographic Materials
The *Marchmont Papers* and the letters to Hardwicke are part of a broader array of sources used to document the life and works of Bolingbroke. Other relevant sources include:
*   **Published Works:** Bolingbroke's philosophical essays, such as *On the Rise and Progress of Monotheism* and *Concerning the Nature, Extent and Reality of Human Knowledge*, as well as *A Collection of Political Tracts* (1748).
*   **Correspondence:** Letters published by G. Parke (1798) and Grimoard (1808), as well as correspondence found in the works of Swift and Pope.
*   **Other State Papers:** The *Hardwicke State Papers* (vol. ii), the *Hist. MSS. Comm.* (MSS. of the Duke of Portland at Welbeck and Marquis of Bath), and a collection of letters in the British Museum concerning the treaty of Utrecht.
