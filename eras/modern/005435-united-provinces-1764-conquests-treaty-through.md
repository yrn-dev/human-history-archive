# United Provinces

The term "United Provinces" appears in the archive's records in two distinct historical contexts: as a territorial entity in India acquired by the British East India Company, and as the "United Provinces of Holland" during the seventeenth century.

## United Provinces of India

The United Provinces in India were acquired through a combination of conquests and treaties. The process began in 1764, with the conquest of Oudh starting with the Battle of Buxar. The acquisition of the region, including Agra and Oudh, occurred in successive stages, with the primary dates of these developments being 1801, 1803, 1814, and 1815.

The conquest of Oudh reached its conclusion when the territory was finally annexed in 1856. Additionally, the nominal sovereignty of Delhi, which had been retained by the Great Mogul, was resigned into the hands of the East India Company in 1832.

Under the administration of the United Provinces government, certain states remained under its control, specifically Garhwal and Rampur.

## United Provinces of Holland

In the seventeenth century, the United Provinces of Holland were involved in a prolonged struggle for independence and territorial expansion. Their independence was won by William the Silent and his sons. The northern provinces of the Netherlands had previously united under the Union of Utrecht in 1579 to carry on a revolt against Spanish rule, a move that followed the Treaty of Arras and the subsequent separation of the Walloons and Flemings.

During the latter eighty years of the seventeenth century, the United Provinces maintained a close alliance with France. Together, they repeatedly attacked the declining Spanish monarchy in an effort to seize the Belgian provinces.

## The Treaty of Munster and Belgian Territories

The Treaty of Munster, concluded in 1648 between Spain and the United Provinces, had significant territorial and economic consequences:

*   **Independence and Territory:** Spain officially renounced all claims and rights over the United Provinces. The Dutch retained possession of Northern Flanders and Northern Brabant and shared sovereignty over Maestricht with Belgium.
*   **Economic Impact:** The treaty allowed the Dutch to control and close the Scheldt. This was ruinous to the commercial interests of Belgium, as it cut off Antwerp's primary source of wealth and its only access to the ocean.
*   **Further Divisions:** Following the treaty, Limburg was divided between the two countries in 1661. The Dutch acquired a portion of Rolduc as well as the larger part of Daelhem and Fauquemont.
