# Czech Agrarians

The Czech Agrarians were a political group active within the parliamentary systems of the Austro-Hungarian territories, specifically operating among the Czech population. They functioned as one of several distinct Czech political factions during a period of significant ethnic and linguistic tension in regions such as Bohemia and Moravia.

## Parliamentary Representation
The Czech Agrarians held varying levels of representation in parliamentary bodies. In one recorded instance of party distribution, the Czech Agrarians were credited with 28 seats. This placed them as the largest group among the Czechs, who collectively held 177 seats. Other Czech factions during this period included the Young Czechs (18 seats), Czech Clericals (17 seats), Czech National Socialists (9 seats), Old Czechs (7 seats), and Realists (2 seats), with one unattached Czech member.

In a separate parliamentary table comparing the years 1897 and 1901, the "Agrarian Czechs" showed a notable increase in their presence. In 1897, the group held only 1 seat; by 1901, this number had risen to 6 seats. During this same period, the broader Czech parliamentary presence grew from 63 seats to 65 seats.

## Political and Social Context
The Czech Agrarians operated within a highly contested environment in Bohemia and Moravia, where the Czech and German populations were nearly equal in size. This era was marked by a constant encroachment by the Czechs upon German influence, facilitated in part by government ordinances. 

Key developments that shaped the environment in which the Czech Agrarians existed included:
* **Language Rights:** An 1880 ordinance mandated that government office and law court business be handled in the language in which it was introduced. In 1886, this was extended to the high courts of Prague and Bruenn to avoid frequent translations.
* **Education:** The period saw the founding of numerous Czech elementary and middle schools, the introduction of Czech classes in German schools, and the 1882 establishment of Czech classes at the university of Prague.
* **Electoral Systems:** To manage racial tensions, mixed districts utilized separate constituencies and registers. This system ensured that Czechs voted for Czech candidates and Germans for German candidates, preventing one race from achieving victory over the other in these specific contests.

## Conflict and Obstruction
The parliamentary atmosphere was often characterized by instability and ethnic friction. Between 1902 and 1904, attempts by Dr. von Koerber to find a compromise between Germans and Czechs failed. Koerber proposed dividing Bohemia into ten districts (five Czech, three German, and two mixed) and allocating 133 of 234 district tribunals to the Czechs. 

The Czechs rejected this proposal, demanding instead that the Czech and German languages be placed on an equal footing for all official purposes throughout Bohemia. Because this required recognizing Czech as a language of internal service, the Germans refused. Consequently, Czech political elements blocked government business through "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction, including one parliamentary sitting that lasted 57 hours without interruption.
