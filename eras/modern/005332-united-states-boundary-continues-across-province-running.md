# United States Boundary

The boundary of the United States is referenced in the archive's records primarily in relation to its proximity to Canadian provinces and its role in international arbitration.

## Boundary with Canada
In the province of British Columbia, the United States boundary serves as a geographical marker for regional infrastructure and districts. A branch of the Canadian Pacific railway leaves the main line at Medicine Hat and travels south-west, crossing the Rocky Mountains via the Crow's Nest Pass. This railway enters British Columbia a short distance north of the United States boundary and continues across the province, running approximately parallel to the boundary until it reaches Midway. This specific area is identified as the Boundary district. Additionally, the Great Northern railway operates several short lines in the southern portion of British Columbia that connect with its own system located within the United States.

## International Arbitration
The president of the United States has also acted as an arbitrator in boundary disputes between other nations. In 1888, the president of the United States provided an award regarding the boundary question between Nicaragua and Costa Rica, which confirmed a treaty established in 1858.

## Internal State Boundaries
Within the United States, boundary lines between states have been established through joint commissions and legal settlements. In the case of Kentucky, the ridge of the Cumberland Mountains was designated as part of the east boundary of the state in the Act of 1776 for dividing Fincastle county, Virginia. In 1799, a joint commission was appointed by Virginia and Kentucky to formally run the boundary line along this ridge. Furthermore, a dispute regarding the southern boundary of Kentucky with Tennessee was settled in a similar manner in 1820.
