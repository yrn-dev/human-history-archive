# General Gamarra and the Peru-Bolivia Conflicts

General Gamarra was a prominent military and political leader in Peru during the mid-19th century, characterized by his leadership of an opposing political faction and his efforts to expand Peruvian influence over neighboring Bolivia.

## Conflict with Santa Cruz
In 1835, the republic of Peru was divided by a struggle for chief power between two factions. During this contest, Marshal Santa Cruz of Bolivia was induced to intervene. Santa Cruz marched into Peru and defeated General Gamarra, who served as the leader of one of the opposing parties. By the spring of 1836, Santa Cruz completed the pacification of Peru and appointed himself its protector, with the objective of establishing a confederation between Peru and Bolivia.

## Chilean Intervention and Presidency
The government of Chile actively interfered in the situation by supporting Gamarra's cause and sending troops into Peru. This triggered three years of fighting. The conflict concluded in June 1839 at the battle of Jungay, where Santa Cruz was defeated and subsequently exiled. Following this victory, Gamarra became the president of Peru. In Bolivia, General Velasco was appointed as the provisional chief.

## Annexation Attempt and Death
Despite Gamarra's presidency in Peru, the party of Santa Cruz remained strong in Bolivia and successfully revolted against General Velasco, eventually installing General Ballivian in power. 

Taking advantage of the political instability within Bolivia, Gamarra attempted to annex the rich province of La Paz. He invaded the province in August 1841 and laid siege to its capital. However, Gamarra's army was totally routed in a battle against General Ballivian, and Gamarra was killed during the engagement. Following this event, General Ballivian attempted to invade Peru, though this was prevented by further interference from Chile.
