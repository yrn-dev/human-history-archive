# Supplement au grand corps diplomatique and Traite du jeu

The *Supplement au grand corps diplomatique* and the *Traite du jeu* are works authored by Jean Barbeyrac, a French jurist and professor who lived from 1674 to 1744.

## Author Biography
Jean Barbeyrac was born on March 15, 1674, in Beziers, located in Lower Languedoc. He was the nephew of Charles Barbeyrac, who was a distinguished physician in Montpellier. Following the revocation of the edict of Nantes, Barbeyrac and his family moved to Switzerland, where he pursued the study of jurisprudence. 

His academic career included time spent in Frankfort-on-Main and Geneva. He served as a professor of belles-lettres at the French school in Berlin before being appointed to a professorship of civil law and history at Lausanne in 1711. He eventually settled in Groningen as a professor of public law, where he remained until his death on March 3, 1744.

## Intellectual Contributions and Legal Theory
Barbeyrac is most noted for his notes and preface to his translation of Pufendorf's *De Jure Naturae et Gentium*. His fundamental principles were heavily influenced by Pufendorf and Locke. He utilized great skill in developing a theory of moral obligation, which he linked to the will or command of God. Furthermore, he identified a distinction between the moral and legal qualities of an action, a concept that was later expanded upon by Kant and Thomasius.

In the realm of international law, Barbeyrac reduced its principles to those of the law of nature, a position that led him to oppose various positions held by Grotius. He rejected the idea that sovereignty shared any resemblance to property and viewed marriage as a civil contract. In addition to his own original works, he translated several significant texts:
* Pufendorf's *De Officio Hominis et Civis* (a smaller treatise).
* Cumberland's *De Legibus Naturae*.
* Grotius's *De Jure Belli et Pacis*.

## Supplement au grand corps diplomatique
Among Barbeyrac's original productions is the *Supplement au grand corps diplomatique*. This work is a history of ancient treaties. 

The term "corps diplomatique" generally refers to the body of foreign diplomatic agents accredited to a government. In a broader historical and archival context, diplomatic collections often include records of peace, alliance, and commerce. For example, Dumont's *Corps universel diplomatique du droit des gens* provided a collection of such treaties in Europe from the time of Charlemagne onward.

## Traite du jeu
Barbeyrac also authored a work titled *Traite du jeu*, published in 1709. This text is described as curious because it contains a defense of the morality of games of chance. This stands in contrast to his other academic and legal writings, such as his treatise *De la morale des peres*.
