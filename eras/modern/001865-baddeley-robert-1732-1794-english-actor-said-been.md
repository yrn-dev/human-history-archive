# BADDELEY, ROBERT

Robert Baddeley (c. 1732–1794) was an English actor who achieved success in low comedy and servants' roles during the 18th century.

## Early Life and Career
Before his transition to the professional stage, Baddeley is said to have served as a valet and a cook to Samuel Foote, who was known as "the English Aristophanes." By 1761, Baddeley was associated with the Drury Lane theatre. During this time, he appeared at the theatre in Smock Alley in Dublin, performing the role of Gomez in Dryden's *Spanish Friar*.

Two years later, Baddeley became a regular member of the Drury Lane company in London. He remained affiliated with this theatre and the Haymarket for the remainder of his career. One of his notable contributions to the stage was originating the role of Moses in the *School for Scandal*.

## Personal Life
Baddeley was married to Sophia Baddeley (1745–1786), an actress and singer born in London to a sergeant-trumpeter named Snow. Sophia was noted for her beauty, though she was also characterized by notorious conduct and excessive vanity. At the age of eighteen, she ran away with Baddeley while he was acting at Drury Lane. She made her own stage debut at the same theatre on April 27, 1765, playing the role of Ophelia.

In addition to her acting, Sophia obtained singing engagements at Vauxhall and Ranelagh. Due to her misconduct, the couple separated, although Sophia continued to perform in the same company as her husband for several years. Her reputation was marked by extravagance and the squandering of her earnings, which eventually forced her to seek refuge from her creditors in Edinburgh. She made her final stage appearance there in 1784.

## Death and Legacy
Robert Baddeley died on November 20, 1794. In his will, he bequeathed property to establish a home for decayed actors. He also left a legacy of £3 per annum to provide cake and wine in the green-room of the Drury Lane theatre to be served on Twelfth Night. This tradition, known as the Baddeley cake, has persisted as a regular institution.
