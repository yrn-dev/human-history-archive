# BADAKSHAN

Badakshan, which includes the district of Wakhan, is a province located on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan. It adjoins Russian territory, and its north-eastern boundaries were established by the Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873. This agreement limited the province to the southern or left bank of the Oxus and expressly acknowledged that Badakshan and its dependent district, Wakhan, belonged fully to the amir of Kabul.

## Geography and Boundaries

The province is characterized by a bottle-shaped extension into the Pamirs. This "arm" is narrow at the neck, formed by the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, and swells eastward to encompass portions of the little and great Pamirs. 

The boundaries of Badakshan are defined as follows:
*   **North:** The boundary follows the course of the Oxus river to the junction of the Khanabad (Kunduz) and the Oxus. In some areas, the northern boundary is separated from the southern boundary by only 8 to 10 miles, consisting of the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush.
*   **West:** The boundary begins at the junction of the Kunduz and Oxus rivers, crossing the Turkestan plains southwards to the eastern water-divide of the Tashkurghan river (known as the Koh-i-Chungar). It then runs south-east, crossing the Sarkhab affluent of the Khanabad (Kunduz), until it reaches the Hindu Kush.
*   **South:** The boundary follows the crest of the Hindu Kush to the Khawak pass, which leads into the Panjshir valley. Beyond this point, the boundary is indefinite; it is known that Kafirs occupy the crest of the Hindu Kush east of the Khawak, though the extent of their territory north of the main watershed is not ascertainable. The boundary becomes definite again at the Dorah pass, which connects Ishkashim and Zebak at the bend of the Oxus with the Lutku valley leading to Chitral. From the Dorah eastwards, the crest of the Hindu Kush serves as the boundary until it joins the Sarikol and Muztagh ranges, which separate Russia and India from China. The boundary then skirts the head of the Tagdumbash Pamir before merging into the Pamir boundary.

## Topography and Climate

The province features a great central upheaval or plateau. To the north, this plateau slopes toward the Oxus, descending from an average altitude of 15,000 feet to 4,000 feet at Faizabad in the center of the province, and further down to 1,100 feet at Kunduz in Kataghan, where it meets the flat plains of the Oxus.

The southern districts of Badakshan, along with the northern valleys and hills of Kafiristan, consist of mountain districts analogous to the rest of the Hindu Kush to the west. The Hindu Kush breaks into long spurs to the south, creating deep, rugged, and snow-capped altitudes that isolate the valleys of Kafiristan from one another. The water-divide of the Hindu Kush through the center of Kafiristan, from the Khawak pass to the Dorah, has not been accurately traced.

## Hydrography and Transport

The Kokcha river is a primary waterway, traversing Badakshan from the south-east to the north-west. Along with the Kunduz river, it drains the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush west of the Dorah pass. The Kokcha's chief affluent is the Minjan, a considerable stream near the Dorah. Some sources of the Kokcha are located near Zebak, close to the great bend of the Oxus, effectively cutting off the mountainous area within that bend from the rest of the province.

The course of the Kokcha provides a high road across Badakshan, connecting the Oxus (1,000 ft.), Faizabad (4,000 ft.), and Zebak (8,500 ft.). Between Zebak and Ishkashim, at the bend of the Oxus, there is a pass of 9,500 feet. From Ishkashim, the route continues through the Pamirs via the Panja. This path is considered one of the great continental high-roads of Asia, as it once served as a heavily traversed trade route connecting China and Kashgar with Afghan Turkestan.

North of the Kokcha, within the bend of the Oxus, lies the mountainous district of Darwaz, which possesses a physiography more similar to the Pamir type than to the Hindu Kush.

## Provincial Subdivisions

Badakshan is divided into several chief provincial subdivisions:
*   **West:** Anderab, Ghori, Kataghan, Narin, and Rustak.
*   **North:** Darwaz, Ragh, and Shiwa.
*   **East:** Charan, Ishkashim, Wakhan, and Zebak.
*   **Centre:** Faizabad, Farkhar, Kishm, and Minjan.
