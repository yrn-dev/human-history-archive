# Barium Dioxide and Related Barium Compounds

## Barium Dioxide
Barium dioxide ($\text{BaO}_2$) is a chemical compound that can be produced in a hydrated crystalline form, specifically as $\text{BaO}_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$. This hydrated state is prepared by adding an excess of baryta-water to a solution of hydrogen peroxide. When these crystals are heated to $130^\circ\text{C}$, they lose their water of crystallization, leaving behind a residue of anhydrous peroxide.

In industrial applications, specifically the Brin process for the manufacture of oxygen, barium dioxide serves as an intermediate product. In this process, it is obtained by heating barium monoxide with air under pressure. The resulting substance is a grey coloured powder. Barium dioxide is readily decomposed by dilute acids, a reaction that produces hydrogen peroxide.

## Barium Hydroxide
Barium hydroxide ($\text{Ba(OH)}_2$) is a white powder. It can be produced by slaking barium monoxide with the necessary amount of water. On a large scale, it is typically manufactured by heating heavy spar with small coal to produce a crude barium sulphide. This sulphide is then heated in a current of moist carbon dioxide to form barium carbonate ($\text{BaS} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 = \text{BaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{S}$). Finally, the carbonate is decomposed using a current of superheated steam ($\text{BaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} = \text{Ba(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2$), which leaves a residue of the hydroxide.

Barium hydroxide is moderately soluble in cold water and readily soluble in hot water. Its solution, known as baryta-water, has an alkaline reaction and readily absorbs carbon dioxide. Because of these properties, baryta-water is used in practical chemistry for gas-analysis to determine the amount of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere. In organic chemistry, it is used as a hydrolysing agent for the decomposition of substituted aceto-acetic esters and complex ureides. Additionally, E. Fischer has utilized it as a condensing agent to prepare $\beta$- and $\alpha$-acrose from acrolein dibromide. When a saturated solution of barium hydroxide is cooled, it deposits a hydrated form, $\text{Ba(OH)}_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$, appearing as colourless quadratic prisms. These prisms lose seven molecules of water of crystallization upon exposure to air.

## Other Barium Salts
The archive contains data on several other barium salts with varying properties:

*   **Barium Chloride ($\text{BaCl}_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$):** This salt can be obtained by dissolving witherite in dilute hydrochloric acid or from heavy spar via ignition in a reverberatory furnace with limestone, coal, and calcium chloride. It forms colourless rhombic tables with a specific gravity of 3.0. It is a strong poison with a bitter taste. While readily soluble in water, it is almost insoluble in absolute alcohol and concentrated hydrochloric acid. Heating the salt gently to approximately $120^\circ\text{C}$ produces the anhydrous condition.
*   **Barium Bromide ($\text{BaBr}_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$):** Prepared by decomposing barium carbonate with hydrobromic acid or by saturating baryta-water, this salt is isomorphous with barium chloride.
*   **Barium Bromate ($\text{Ba(BrO}_3)_2$):** This compound is prepared by the action of excess bromine on baryta-water, or by combining boiling aqueous solutions of potassium bromate (100 parts) and crystallized barium chloride (74 parts). It separates from its aqueous solution as $\text{Ba(BrO}_3)_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}$ in the monoclinic system and decomposes when heated to $260\text{--}265^\circ\text{C}$.
*   **Barium Chlorate ($\text{Ba(ClO}_3)_2$):** This salt is obtained by adding barium chloride to a sodium chlorate solution or by passing chlorine through barium carbonate suspended in boiling water. It crystallizes as monoclinic prisms of $\text{Ba(ClO}_3)_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}$ and begins to decompose at $250^\circ\text{C}$.
*   **Barium Iodate ($\text{Ba(IO}_3)_2$):** This is produced by adding barium chloride solution to sodium iodate or by the action of excess iodic acid on hot caustic baryta solution. It crystallizes as monoclinic prisms of $\text{Ba(IO}_3)_2 \cdot \text{H}_2\text{O}$ and is only very sparingly soluble in cold water.
