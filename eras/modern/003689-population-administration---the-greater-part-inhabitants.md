## The Austro-Hungarian Monarchy and its Provinces
The Austro-Hungarian Monarchy, also known as the Dual Monarchy, was situated in central Europe. As of 1901, it had a population of 45,405,267, representing approximately one-eighth of Europe's total population. The monarchy consisted of two independent states: the "lands of St Stephen's Crown" (unofficially Hungary or Transleithania) and the kingdoms and lands represented in the council of the empire, the *Reichsrat* (unofficially Austria or Cisleithania). In 1905, the monarchy's area was 239,977 sq. m. Following the Berlin Treaty of 1878, the administration of Bosnia and Herzegovina (population 1,591,036 in 1895) was placed under Austria-Hungary, and their annexation in 1908 was recognized by the Powers in 1909.

Within the monarchy, the province of Bohemia had a population of 6,318,280 in 1900, with a density of 315 inhabitants per square mile. This population was 65% Czech and 35% German. The Czechs primarily occupied the middle, south, and south-east regions, while Germans were concentrated in the large towns and near the borders, particularly in the west and north. Regarding religion, 96% of the population belonged to the Roman Catholic Church, and the region was divided into the archbishopric of Prague and the bishoprics of Leitmeritz, Koniggratz, and Budweis.

The province of Carniola had a population of 508,348 in 1900 (132 inhabitants per sq. m.), of which 95% were Slovenes and 5% Germans. The population was 99% Roman Catholic. Administratively, Carniola was divided into one autonomous municipality, the capital Laibach (population 36,547), and eleven districts. Its local diet included the bishop of Laibach *ex officio* and consisted of thirty-seven members; the province sent eleven deputies to the *Reichsrat* in Vienna.

## Barbados
Barbados is characterized as one of the most densely populated areas in the world. In 1901, the population was 195,588 (1,178 per sq. m.), increasing to 196,287 by 1906. The majority of the population consists of negroes, who outnumber whites by 9 to 1.

Administratively, the island is governed by a governor, supported by an executive council, a house of assembly with 24 members elected via limited franchise, and a legislative council of 9 nominated members. While the colony has representative institutions, it does not have responsible government; the home government appoints public officials (except the treasurer), and the crown maintains a veto on legislation.

The Church of England is the largest denomination, exceeding the combined total of all others. The government pays the clergy of all creeds and the bishop of the see. Educational institutions include Codrington College, founded via a 1710 bequest by Colonel Christopher Codrington to the Society for the Propagation of the Gospel to train men for holy orders. Secondary education is provided by The Lodge and Harrison College for boys, and Queen's College for girls. Additionally, the island serves as the headquarters for the Imperial Agricultural Department of the West Indies.

## Other Regional Populations
### The Balkan Peninsula and Macedonia
In the Balkan Peninsula, the Slavonic population (including Bulgars and Serbo-Croats) is the most numerous, exceeding 10,000,000. The Turkish population, which declined during the 19th century, totals scarcely more than 1,800,000. In Macedonia, the greater part of the population is Slavonic and mainly Bulgarian in characteristic. Greeks inhabit the southern districts west of the Gulf of Salonica and the coast-line, while Albanians, Vlachs, and Turks exist in sporadic groups or settlements.

### Central America and the West Indies
In Costa Rica, the 1904 official population was 331,340. The majority of inhabitants claim descent from Spanish colonists, primarily Galicians. There is a significant population of *mestizos* or *ladinos* (half-castes) resulting from intermarriage with native Indians. Native Indians, estimated at 4,000, remain in a state of complete savagery in areas such as the Talamanca country and the Nicaraguan border. The black population is small, numbering only about 1,200.

In the West Indies, including Honduras and British Guiana, the total coloured population is approximately 1,912,655. The largest coloured populations are found in Jamaica (790,000, chiefly black), British Guiana (286,000, black and brown), and Trinidad and Tobago (250,000, black and brown).

### Eastern Dependencies
In the Eastern dependencies, the most significant populations are the 2,000,000 Sinhalese and 954,000 Tamil in Ceylon. Other populations include 570,000 brown, mixed, and Chinese in the Straits Settlements, 306,130 Chinese and brown in Hong-Kong, and 700,000 mixed brown and Sarawak in North Borneo.

## Local Administration in Portugal
In Portugal, the administrative district of Beja is the largest and most thinly populated district, with a 1900 population of 163,612 over an area of 3,958 sq. m. (41.3 inhabitants per sq. m.). Beja is an episcopal city and the capital of the district.
