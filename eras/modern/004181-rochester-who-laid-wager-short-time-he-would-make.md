# The Earl of Rochester and Elizabeth Barry

## Coaching of Elizabeth Barry
Elizabeth Barry (1658–1713) was an English actress whose early career was marked by significant struggle. In her initial attempts to find success on the stage, she was dismissed more than once. Her professional trajectory changed when she was coached by her lover, the Earl of Rochester. Rochester had laid a wager that he could transform her into a first-rate actress within a short period of time. The subsequent results of his coaching confirmed his judgment.

## Career Success and Recognition
Mrs. Barry became a highly regarded performer, creating over 100 parts during her career. She found particular success in the plays written by Thomas Otway. Her talent was noted by several contemporaries; Dryden described her as "always excellent," and Betterton remarked that her acting brought success to plays that would otherwise "disgust the most patient reader."

One of her most notable performances was as Isabella, the queen of Hungary, in the play *Mustapha* by the Earl of Orrery. This performance delighted Charles II as well as the duke and duchess of York. The duchess was so impressed that she took English lessons from Mrs. Barry. Later, upon becoming queen, she provided Mrs. Barry with her own coronation robes to wear for the role of Elizabeth in Banks's *Earl of Essex*.

## Contributions to the Acting Profession
Mrs. Barry is credited with a significant change in the financial structure of the theater. According to Cibber, it was on her behalf that "benefits"—which had previously been reserved exclusively for authors—were first established for actors. This change was implemented by the command of James II.

## Personal Life and Later Years
Mrs. Barry had two children. One was fathered by Lord Rochester and a second by Sir George Etheredge; both fathers provided for their children. Mrs. Barry retired from the stage in 1709 and died on November 7, 1713.

## Literary Associations
The Earl of Rochester is also associated with a collection of works titled *The Works of the Earls of Rochester, Roscommon and Dorset, the Dukes of Devonshire, Buckinghamshire, &c., with Memoirs of their Lives*, published in 1731. This volume was catalogued by H. G. Bohn in 1841.
