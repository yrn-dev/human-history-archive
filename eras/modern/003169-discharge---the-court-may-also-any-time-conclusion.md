# Discharge in Bankruptcy

In bankruptcy law, the discharge serves as a critical mechanism for the efficiency of the system, allowing the court to apply moral censorship to a debtor's conduct and maintain commercial integrity. A discharge releases a debtor from their obligations, though certain exceptions apply.

## Process and Requirements for Discharge
The court may grant a bankrupt a discharge, either absolutely or under specific conditions, at any time following the conclusion of the bankrupt's public examination. Before this can occur, the court must hear from the official receiver, the trustee, and any creditor, all of whom must be given previous notice of the application.

An order of discharge generally releases the debtor from all obligations. However, it does not release them from:
* Debts due to the crown.
* Obligations of a public character that require the consent of the Treasury for discharge.
* Debts incurred through fraud.
* Judgment debts.

## Qualifications and Restrictions
The court may refuse or restrict a discharge based on the conduct of the bankrupt. If the bankrupt has committed a criminal offence connected to the bankruptcy, the application must be refused unless the court determines there are special reasons to do otherwise.

Furthermore, the court shall either refuse the discharge, suspend it for at least two years (or until a dividend of at least ten shillings in the pound is paid), or qualify the order by requiring judgment for the payment of unpaid balances from future property or earnings if any of the following are proven:
* Assets are not equal in value to ten shillings in the pound of unsecured liabilities (unless the bankrupt is not responsible).
* Proper books were not kept.
* The bankrupt traded after knowing they were insolvent.
* Debts were contracted without a reasonable probability of payment.
* The bankrupt failed to account for their deficiency.
* The bankruptcy was caused by gambling, rash speculation, culpable neglect, or unjustifiable expenses.
* Legal proceedings were taken or defended on frivolous grounds.
* An undue preference was given within three months before the receiving order.
* Liabilities were increased to make assets equal to ten shillings in the pound.
* The individual had previously been bankrupt or made an arrangement with creditors.
* The individual was guilty of fraud or a fraudulent breach of trust.

If a discharge is qualified, the bankrupt may apply to the court to modify the conditions after two years if they are unable to comply.

## Disqualifications and Annulment
A bankrupt is disqualified from serving in several public roles during their bankruptcy or until five years after their discharge. These roles include acting as a member of the legislature, a justice of the peace, mayor, alderman, councillor, guardian or overseer of the poor, or members of various boards (sanitary, school, highway, or burial) and select vestries in the United Kingdom. This disqualification is waived if the bankruptcy is annulled or if the court provides a certificate stating the bankruptcy was caused by misfortune without misconduct.

An order of adjudication may be annulled if the court believes it should not have been made, if debts are paid in full, or if a court-approved composition or scheme of arrangement is reached after adjudication.

## Compositions and Schemes of Arrangement
A debtor may submit a proposal for the liquidation of affairs or the payment of a composition without adjudication. This proposal is lodged with the official receiver and must be entertained by a majority in number and three-fourths in value of creditors who have proved their debts. If approved by the court, it becomes binding on all creditors who would have been bound by a discharge.

Such a scheme does not release the debtor from criminal law liabilities or the requirement for a public examination. The court must be satisfied that the proposal is reasonable, benefits creditors, and that no bankruptcy-related criminal offences were committed. If facts are proven that would have prevented an absolute discharge had the debtor been adjudged bankrupt, the scheme cannot be approved.

## Comparative and International Recognition
Under the United States system, a judge investigates the application and grants a discharge unless the bankrupt has:
* Committed an offence punishable by imprisonment.
* Destroyed, concealed, or failed to keep records to hide their financial condition.
* Obtained credit through a materially false written statement.
* Transferred or concealed property within four months preceding the petition to defraud creditors.
* Been granted a discharge in voluntary proceedings within six years.
* Refused to obey a lawful court order or answer a material question.

Regarding jurisdiction, an English bankrupt may plead their discharge as a defence in a colonial court. This is because the English act vests all the bankrupt's property—whether in the colonies or the United Kingdom—in the trustee, effectively denuding the bankrupt of everything.
