# Christian Castes of the Konkan

## Overview and Origins
In the Konkan region, there are various Christian castes whose origins are attributed to Portuguese rule. These groups exist within a broader religious landscape in the presidency where, according to 1901 data, the Christian population numbered 216,118. This is in contrast to other predominant faiths in the area, including Hinduism (19,916,438), Mahommedanism (4,567,295), Jainism (535,950), and Zoroastrianism (78,552).

## Regional Context
The Konkan is situated in a region of diverse ethnic and religious compositions. The Deccan, for instance, is the home of the Mahrattas, who make up 30% of the population. As of 1901, the Mahrattas numbered 3,650,000, consisting of 1,400,000 not otherwise specified, 1,900,000 Kunbis, and 350,000 Konkanis. In other nearby areas such as the Carnatic, 45% of the population has embraced Lingayatism, a Hindu reformation movement from the 12th century.

## Portuguese Influence in Western India
The presence of the Portuguese in western India is linked to the history of the islands of Bombay. Originally occupied by the Kolis—a race of fishermen and husbandmen—the islands were part of the province or kingdom of Gujarat until 1534. At that time, Sultan Bahadur ceded the islands to the Portuguese.

## Comparison with Other Caste and Social Structures
While the Christian castes of the Konkan are specifically linked to Portuguese rule, other distinct social castes exist in different regions. In India, the traditional caste system includes the four original castes: the Brahman, the Vaisya, the Sudra, and the Kshattriya. The Kshattriya served as the warrior caste, tasked with protecting the people and abstaining from sensual pleasures.
