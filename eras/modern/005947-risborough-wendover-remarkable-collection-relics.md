# Prince's Risborough and Wendover

Prince's Risborough and Wendover are two lesser market towns located in the south midland county of Buckinghamshire, England. They are situated in a region characterized by the Chiltern system of hills, which runs in a general direction from the south-west to the north-east.

## Geography and Topography
The towns of Prince's Risborough and Wendover are positioned along the crest-line of the Chiltern Hills. This specific line, which crosses the county at its narrowest point, does not exceed 11 miles in length. This geographical feature serves as a dividing line for the county, separating it into two distinct physical areas. To the south of this line, the land is predominantly hilly, well-wooded, and diversified by narrow vales. The Chilterns reach an elevation of nearly 900 feet within the county.

## Population and Administration
As of the records provided, Prince's Risborough is classified as a lesser market town with a population of 2,189. Wendover is similarly categorized as a lesser market town with a population of 2,009. 

Administratively, the county of Buckinghamshire is divided into eight hundreds. Three of these—Desborough, Burnham, and Stoke—constitute the "Chiltern Hundreds." The county is further divided into thirteen petty sessional divisions and contains 230 civil parishes.

## Transport and Communications
Both towns are served by railway networks. The Great Western branch from Maidenhead to Oxford served both Prince's Risborough and High Wycombe prior to 1906. From Prince's Risborough, there are further railway branches extending to Aylesbury and Watlington. Additionally, a line jointly owned by the Great Western and Great Central companies passes through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield, connecting northward with the Great Central system.

## Local Architecture and Relics
Situated high among the Chiltern Hills between Wendover and Prince's Risborough is Chequers Court. Dating from the close of the 16th century, this country house is noted for its architectural interest and its beautiful location. Chequers Court is further distinguished by its possession of a remarkable collection of relics of Oliver Cromwell.
