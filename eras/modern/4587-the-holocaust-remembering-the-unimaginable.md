# The Holocaust: Remembering the Unimaginable

## Overview
The Holocaust, or the *Shoah* (Hebrew for "catastrophe"), was the state-sponsored, systematic persecution and genocide of European Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators between 1933 and 1945. Driven by a pseudoscientific ideology of racial purity and virulent antisemitism, the Nazi regime sought to eliminate the Jewish people from the face of the earth. 

While Jews were the primary targets, the regime also murdered millions of others, including Romani people, individuals with disabilities, Soviet prisoners of war, political dissidents, and LGBTQ+ individuals. The Holocaust represents a watershed moment in modern history, leading to the development of the legal concept of "genocide" and the establishment of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

## Key Facts
The scale of the Holocaust was unprecedented due to its industrial nature. The Nazis transitioned from haphazard mass shootings in Eastern Europe to the use of extermination camps—such as Auschwitz-Birkenau, Treblinka, and Belzec—where gas chambers were used to kill thousands of people daily.

Historians generally agree that approximately six million Jews were murdered, though some scholarly debates persist regarding the exact totals in specific regions of Eastern Europe due to the destruction of records by the SS as the Allies approached. Another point of historical discussion involves the "Intentionalist" vs. "Functionalist" debate: Intentionalists argue Hitler had a master plan for genocide from the start, while Functionalists suggest the policy evolved incrementally through bureaucratic competition and the pressures of the war.

## Did You Know?
* **The Righteous Among the Nations:** This is an official title awarded by Yad Vashem to non-Jews who risked their lives to save Jews during the Holocaust.
* **The Sonderkommando:** These were prisoners, mostly Jewish, forced by the Nazis to aid with the disposal of gas chamber victims; their secret diaries, buried in the soil of Auschwitz, provided crucial evidence after the war.
* **The Nuremberg Trials:** These trials established the precedent that "following orders" is not a valid defense for committing crimes against humanity.
