# Bettingen and Klein Hueningen

Bettingen and Klein Hueningen are rural districts located within the Swiss half canton of Basel Stadt (also known as Bale Ville). Both districts are situated to the north of the Rhine.

## Administrative Status and Integration
The rural districts of Basel Stadt consist of Riehen, Bettingen, and Klein Hueningen. While Bettingen remains a distinct rural district, Klein Hueningen has been united to the city of Basel. 

The governance of the half canton is established by a constitution dating from 1889. The political structure includes an executive composed of seven members and a legislature, known as the *Grossrat*, which consists of 130 members. Additionally, the half canton sends one member to the Federal *Staenderat* and six members to the Federal *Nationalrat*. All of these representatives are elected via direct popular vote for three-year terms. Since 1875, the system has allowed for a facultative referendum on all bills if claimed by 1,000 citizens, as well as the right of initiative regarding laws or revisions to the cantonal constitution.

## Geography and Demographics
The total area of the Basel Stadt half canton is 13.7 square miles. Of this area, 11 square miles are classified as "productive," while forests account for 1.5 square miles. 

As of 1900, the total population of the half canton was 112,227. The rural districts, which include Bettingen and Klein Hueningen, were inhabited by 3,066 of these residents. The population of the half canton was mainly German-speaking. The religious composition in 1900 consisted of 73,063 Protestants, 37,101 Romanists (a figure that includes Old Catholics), and 1,897 Jews.

## Relation to Basel
Bettingen and Klein Hueningen are part of the administrative region centered around Basel, the capital of the half canton. Basel is divided by the Rhine into two main sections: Gross Basel to the south and Klein Basel to the north, with Gross Basel being the larger of the two. The city is noted as the second most populous town in the Swiss Confederation, with 109,161 inhabitants, and is reputed to be the richest Swiss town due to a high number of resident millionaires. This wealth and the city's flourishing transit trade are attributed largely to the 1882 opening of the St Gotthard railway, which allows merchandise from central and north Europe to be stored in Basel before redistribution.
