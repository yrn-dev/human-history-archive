# Cincinnati, New York, Pittsburg, Philadelphia and St Louis

The cities of Cincinnati, New York, Pittsburg, Philadelphia, and St Louis are prominent urban centers in the United States, appearing in historical records as hubs of government, transportation, sport, and industry.

## New York
New York was originally known as Nieuw Amsterdam before becoming British by treaty in 1674, at which point it was renamed New York. The city served as the location for the first Federal Congress of the United States in 1788. 

In the mid-19th century, New York was a center for the development of base-ball. Matches between organized clubs first took place in the neighborhood of New York, including the founding of the Washington Baseball Club in 1843. In 1845, the Knickerbocker Baseball Club drew up the first regular code of rules, which were utilized in matches against the Empire and Gotham Eagle clubs of New York, as well as several Brooklyn clubs: the Atlantic, Putnam, Eckford, and Excelsior.

## Philadelphia
Philadelphia is noted for its role in the early political organization of the United States. In 1787, the Constitutional Convention of Philadelphia was held, which established the Federal Government of the United States. The city also has a history in early athletic organizations, such as the Olympic Town-Ball Club of Philadelphia, which was organized in 1833.

## Pittsburg
Pittsburg serves as a major railway and industrial hub. It is connected to various regions via several rail lines, including the Pittsburg, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis. The city is the regional center for the borough of Carnegie, located 6 miles southwest of Pittsburg in Allegheny county, Pennsylvania. Carnegie, formed in 1894 from the union of Mansfield and Chartiers, is situated in a coal and natural gas district and produces glass, steel, lead, enamel- and tin-ware, and ploughs.

## Cincinnati and St Louis
Cincinnati and St Louis are frequently linked through the transportation networks of the American interior. The Pittsburg, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis railway connects these cities and extends to other locations, including Kokomo, Indiana, and Coshocton, Ohio. Additionally, the Cleveland, Cincinnati, Chicago & St Louis railway provides further connectivity. 

Cincinnati is also recognized in the history of American sport as the home of the Red Stockings, one of the many famous base-ball clubs that emerged as the game's popularity spread. In terms of publishing, Cincinnati was the site of the 1881 publication of *King's Mountain and its Heroes* by L. C. Draper, the 1863 publication of *Life and Services of Lyman Beecher* by D.H. Alien, and the 1853 publication of *Asbury and His Co-Laborers* by W.C. Larrabee.

St Louis is connected to other cities via multiple rail lines, such as the Louisville, Henderson & St Louis and the Toledo St Louis & Western railways. Steamboat lines also facilitate travel between St Louis and other river cities.
