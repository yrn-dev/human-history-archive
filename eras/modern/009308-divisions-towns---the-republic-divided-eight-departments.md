# Divisions and Towns

The term "department" (derived from the French *département*, meaning to separate into parts) refers to a division. This term is applied to branches of administration within a municipality or state. In the United States, "department" refers to the eight chief branches of the executive, whereas subordinate divisions are called "bureaus." In Great Britain, the term is used for subordinate divisions of boards of state and great offices, such as the bankruptcy department of the Board of Trade.

## Administrative Divisions of the Bolivian Republic

The republic is divided into eight departments and one national territory. These are further subdivided into 54 provinces, 415 cantons, 232 vice-cantons, 18 missions, and one colony. According to the 1900 census (which includes an estimated number of wild Indians and a 5% addition for sundry corrections and omissions), the total population was 1,766,451 across a total area of 640,226 square miles. These figures require corrections due to the loss of territory to Brazil in 1903.

### Governance and Judiciary in Bolivia
The supreme administration of each department is managed by a prefect who is appointed by and responsible solely to the president. Because the prefect appoints subordinate officials—including sub-prefects in provinces, *corregidores* in cantons, and *alcaldes* in rural districts—the national executive's authority reaches every hamlet. There are no legislative assemblies in the departments; government is handled by the congress and national executive. Department capitals have municipal councils with jurisdiction over local affairs and the maintenance and construction of some highways. The national territory bordering Peru and Brazil is governed by two *delegados nacionales* appointed by the president.

The judiciary consists of a national supreme court, eight superior district courts, lower district courts, and *juzgados de instruccion* for case preparation and investigation. The supreme court features seven justices elected by the Chamber of Deputies from lists provided by the Senate. In cantons and rural districts, *alcaldes* and *corregidores* act as justices of the peace.

## Other Regional Administrative Systems

### Costa Rica
Under a constitution of 1870 (which came into force in 1882), Costa Rica is divided into two maritime districts (*comarcas*)—Limon and Puntarenas—and five provinces: Alajuela, Cartago, Guanacaste, Heredia, and San Jose. Except for Guanacaste, these divisions share names with their chief towns. Each is controlled by a prefect or governor appointed by the president. The executive is assisted by four ministers representing departments: finance and commerce; war and marine; foreign affairs, justice, religion and education; and police, public works, and the interior.

### Burma
Burma's forests are divided into three circles, each managed by a conservator and twenty-one deputy conservators. The administration includes various officials such as a chief collector of customs, a deputy postmaster-general, and an inspector-general of jails. There are 41 municipal towns, 14 of which are located in Upper Burma. The units of regular civil and revenue jurisdiction are called townships, which are grouped into sub-divisions of districts. Township officers are the ultimate government representatives in direct contact with the people. In towns, there are elders of blocks and headmen of wards; in Upper Burma, these headmen have historically served as revenue collectors.

### English Counties
In Buckinghamshire, the administrative county (479,358 acres) contains eight hundreds, including the "Chiltern Hundreds" (Stoke, Burnham, and Desborough). It is divided into three parliamentary divisions: Southern or Wycombe, Northern or Buckingham, and Mid or Aylesbury. The county includes municipal boroughs (Buckingham and Wycombe) and various urban districts such as Slough, Aylesbury, and Chesham.

In Durham, the county is divided into eight parliamentary divisions, including Jarrow and Bishop Auckland. It is further divided into 16 petty sessional divisions. The area contains 288 civil parishes and 243 ecclesiastical parishes.

## Urban Planning and Historical Divisions
In historical Japanese city planning, main streets divided the city into nine districts. Each district contained four divisions, each division contained four streets, and each street consisted of four rows. Every row contained eight "house-units" measuring 50 by 100 feet.

Historically, Europe was characterized by division and diversity. Italy and Germany were divided among princes and minor dukes, and Switzerland existed as a group of republican cantons. The Polish kingdom was divided among Russia, Prussia, and Austria.
