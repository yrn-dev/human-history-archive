# Mixed Zoogloea as a Vegetable Surface Pellicle

## Overview of Zoogloea
Zoogloea represents a specific stage in the life-history of Schizomycetes, functioning as a resting condition. This state corresponds to the "palmella" condition observed in lower algae. It manifests physically as a membrane on the surface of a medium, or as branched masses and irregular clumps submerged within the medium, which can sometimes reach several inches in width.

The structure consists of a more or less gelatinous matrix that encloses various elements of the Schizomycete, such as bacteria, cocci, or other components. This matrix is formed when the cell-walls of the organisms become enormously swollen and diffluent, causing the elements to be glued together as they become contiguous.

## Formation and Composition
The zoogloea is produced through the active division of several mother-cells or a single mother-cell. The resulting progeny continue to secrete cell-wall substance, which absorbs water many times its own volume. This process creates the consistent matrix in which the cells come to rest.

The composition of this matrix varies depending on the case:
* **Chemical Makeup:** In some instances, the matrix consists primarily of a proteid substance, while in others, it is mainly composed of cellulose.
* **Physical Texture:** The consistency can range from a thick solution of gum to a horny and resistant material. If the zoogloea forms on a solid substratum, it may become horny and firm, though immersion in water will soften it.

## Mixed Zoogloea and Pellicles
A specific manifestation of this phenomenon is the mixed zoogloea found as a pellicle on the surface of vegetable infusions. Because of the gelatinous nature of the matrix, foreign bodies can become entangled within it. Compound zoogloeae occur when several distinct forms are apposed, a phenomenon that is a common event in macerating troughs.

These mixed pellicles consist of various forms, including rodlets in series and cocci. Other characteristic forms assumed by young zoogloea of different species include:
* Spherical and ovoid
* Reticular and filamentous
* Lamellar and fruiticose

These forms may vary as the mass increases in size or comes into contact with other masses. Additionally, if the medium contains small quantities of iron, older zoogloeae may precipitate oxide of iron within the matrix.

## Life Cycle and Transition
The transition into the zoogloea state occurs when dense "swarms" of vegetative cells—which may have previously clouded a fluid or formed deposits—become fixed in the matrix of their own swollen contiguous cell-walls. 

This state is temporary. Under favorable conditions, the elements within the zoogloea regain activity. They move out of the gelatinous matrix and distribute themselves into the surrounding medium, where they resume growing and multiplying.
