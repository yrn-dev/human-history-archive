## Collected Works and Correspondence
Beyond the manuscript reports, Bolingbroke's life is documented through his political and philosophical writings. His collected works—which include essays such as *On the Rise and Progress of Monotheism*, *On Authority in Matters of Religion*, *On the Folly and Presumption of Philosophers*, and *Concerning the Nature, Extent and Reality of Human Knowledge*—were first published in a 1754 edition by Mallet. This edition was denounced by Johnson as "the blunderbuss charged against religion and morality." Subsequent editions were published in 1778, 1809, and 1841.

His correspondence is preserved across several venues:
* **Published Letters**: Published by G. Parke (1798) and Grimoard (1808).
* **External Correspondence**: Letters found in the correspondence of Pope and Swift, as well as W. Coxe's *Walpole* and Phillimore's *Life of Lyttelton*.
* **British Museum**: Letters to Lord Chancellor Hardwicke (mostly transcribed by W. Sichel) and a collection of letters regarding the treaty of Utrecht.
