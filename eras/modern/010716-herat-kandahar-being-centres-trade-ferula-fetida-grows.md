# Asafetida and the Trade Centres of Herat and Kandahar

## Asafetida: Properties and Production
Asafetida is a gum-resin obtained primarily from the root of the plant *Ferula fetida*, though it may also be derived from one or two other closely related species of umbelliferous plants. The name is derived from the Latin *asa* (a form of the Persian *aza*, meaning mastic) and *fetidus* (stinking), a designation used to distinguish it from *asa dulcis* (also known as *laser cyrenaicum*), which was a highly esteemed drug among the ancients.

The *Ferula fetida* plant grows to a height of between 5 and 6 feet. It reaches the stage where it is ready to yield asafetida when it is four years old. To extract the resin, the stems are cut down close to the root, allowing the juice to flow out. This sap initially appears milky but quickly hardens into a solid resinous mass. Producers make fresh incisions as long as the sap continues to flow, a duration that depends on the strength and size of the specific plant.

## Physical Characteristics and Commercial Forms
When first exposed, the surface of asafetida has a pearly-white, translucent appearance. However, it darkens rapidly upon exposure to air, turning first pink and eventually reddish-brown. The substance is characterized by an acrid and bitter taste and a strong alliaceous odour caused by the presence of organic sulphur compounds. This pungent smell has led to the German name *Teufelsdreck*, meaning "devil's dung."

In commercial trade, asafetida is available in two forms: "lump" or "tear," with the "tear" form being the purer of the two.

## Uses and Applications
Asafetida serves various culinary and medicinal purposes:

*   **Culinary:** It is used as a condiment in Persia and India and is also in demand for cookery in France. In the regions where the plant grows, the entire plant is consumed as a fresh vegetable.
*   **Medicinal:** It is administered in doses of 5 to 15 grains, acting as a stimulant to the nervous system as well as the respiratory and intestinal tracts. It is used in enemas to relieve flatus. Due to its "nasty properties," it is sometimes used to treat hysteria—defined as a lack of inhibitory power—because the drug induces enough inhibitory power to make further administration unnecessary. Additionally, it may be used in an effervescing draught for cases of malingering, as the drug "repeats" in the mouth, making the act of malingering undesirable.

## Regional Trade and Geography
Asafetida is produced in Afghanistan and eastern Persia. Within these regions, the cities of Kandahar and Herat serve as centres of the trade. 

Geographically, Herat is situated behind a mountain barrier that separates it from Merv and is connected to the Elburz of Persia. A mountain system connects the southern abutment of the Hindu Kush near Bamian with the water-divide north of Herat. In political and strategic terms, both Herat and the Pamirs have been assessed as bases of aggression.
