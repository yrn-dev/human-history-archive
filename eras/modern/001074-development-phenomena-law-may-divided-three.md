# The Law of Equilibrium in the Philosophy of Pierre Hyacinthe Azais

## Overview of Pierre Hyacinthe Azais
Pierre Hyacinthe Azais (1766-1845) was a French philosopher born in Soreze and deceased in Paris. His early professional life was spent serving as a village organist and a teacher. During the onset of the Revolution, Azais initially viewed the events with favor; however, he soon became disgusted by the violence employed by the revolutionists. After publishing a critical pamphlet that drew the hatred of the revolutionists, he was unable to settle in Paris until 1806.

Azais gained significant recognition in 1809 with the publication of his major work, *Des Compensations dans les destinees humaines* (5th edition 1846). This work was highly regarded by Napoleon, who subsequently appointed Azais as a professor at St Cyr. His later career included serving as the inspector of the public library at Nancy from 1812 to 1815, and previously at Avignon in 1811. Although the Restoration government initially viewed him with suspicion as a Bonapartist, he was eventually granted a pension, allowing him to dedicate his time to lecturing and the publication of philosophical texts.

## The Principle of Compensation
In *Des Compensations dans les destinees humaines*, Azais attempted to demonstrate that happiness and misery are, on the whole, equally balanced. Based on this premise, he argued that individuals should accept their given government rather than risk the horrors associated with revolution. Azais posited that the "principe de l'inegalite naturelle et essentielle dans les destinees humaines" (the principle of natural and essential inequality in human destinies) inevitably leads to either religious fanatism or revolutionary fanatism.

## The Law of Equilibrium and Universal Harmony
Azais extended the principles of equilibrium and compensation beyond human destiny to the physical universe. He identified God as the cause of the physical universe, which is the product of force and matter. According to Azais, force is naturally expansive and operates on the homogeneous atoms that make up elemental matter. This force is governed by the law of equilibrium, which he defined as the equivalence of reaction and action.

Under this law, the development of phenomena is divided into three distinct stages:
1. The physical stage.
2. The physiological stage.
3. The intellectual and moral stage.

In the context of human existence, Azais identified the immaterial aspect of man as the expansive force inherent within him. He viewed moral and political phenomena as the result of the opposing forces of preservation and progress. He believed that perfection is found in the fulfillment of universal harmony or the law of equilibrium.

## Timeline of Universal Equilibrium
Azais proposed a specific timeline for the achievement of this harmony. He suggested that the law of equilibrium may be achieved in seven thousand years, at which point man will vanish from the world. Following this, in an additional five thousand years, a similar equilibrium will be established in the physical sphere, which will then itself pass away.
