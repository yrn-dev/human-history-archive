# Hanserd Knollys and William Kiffin

Hanserd Knollys (ca. 1599–1691) and William Kiffin (1616–1701) were prominent figures associated with the early development of the Particular Baptist movement in England. Both men were members of the Jacob church in Southwark, a congregation that played a central role in the rise of the first Calvinistic, or Particular, Baptist Church.

## The Jacob Church and the Particular Baptists
The Jacob church in Southwark originally functioned as an Independent congregation before transitioning to become Baptist. It served as a foundational hub for the Particular Baptist movement, and from this single church, six other churches emerged, five of which were Baptist. 

The membership of the Jacob church included several notable individuals, such as Henry Jessey (1601–1663), Praise-God Barbon (ca. 1596–1679), and John Lothropp (also spelled Lathrop, d. 1653), alongside Knollys and Kiffin. Prior to the Jacob church's own transition to Baptist theology, it had dismissed a group of members because the congregation had grown beyond what were considered proper limits. This dismissed group went on to establish the first Particular Baptist Church in 1633.

## Hanserd Knollys
Hanserd Knollys (ca. 1599–1691) was a Baptist minister. In addition to his ecclesiastical involvement with the Jacob church, he is noted for his scholarly contributions. Specifically, he is the author of the work *Grammatica Latina, Graeca et Hebraica*. This text had previously been attributed by others, including the historian Anthony Wood, to Richard Knolles (c. 1545–1610), an English historian and former master of a school at Sandwich, Kent.

## William Kiffin
William Kiffin (1616–1701) was a member of the Jacob church in Southwark during its period as a Particular Baptist congregation. He is listed among the key members of the church during the era of the rise of Calvinistic Baptist theology in England.
