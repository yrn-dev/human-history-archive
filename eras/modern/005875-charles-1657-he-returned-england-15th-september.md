# Charles

The name Charles is associated with several prominent figures in European history, most notably within the royal houses of England and France.

## Charles I of England
Charles I served as king during a period of significant internal conflict. He was responsible for the upbringing of George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, and his brother Francis, raising them alongside his own children. In 1630, Charles I knighted Sir James Balfour, a Scottish antiquary and annalist, and appointed him as Lyon king-at-arms in the same year.

During his reign, Charles I faced severe political instability. He was influenced by George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, who is described as the "evil genius" of the king. Buckingham urged Charles I to impeach five members of Parliament and encouraged a failed attempt to arrest them on January 4, 1642. Following the outbreak of the Civil War, Charles I was supported in battle by Buckingham, who fought for the king and distinguished himself at Lichfield. However, the king's affairs were further complicated by Buckingham's imprudent negotiations regarding foreign alliances and help from Ireland.

Charles I also had a contentious relationship with the Earl of Bristol. After the death of James, Charles I removed Bristol from the privy council and ordered him to be absent from the first parliament. When Bristol requested to attend the coronation in January 1626, Charles angrily refused, accusing Bristol of attempting to pervert his religion while in Spain. Later, Charles sent Bristol a writ to the second parliament accompanied by a letter from Lord Keeper Coventry requesting that he not use it. Following the dissolution of parliament on June 15, Charles ordered a prosecution of Bristol in the Star Chamber, leading to Bristol's imprisonment in the Tower until March 17, 1628.

## Charles II of England
The son of Charles the "Martyr," Charles II returned to England in 1660 and was welcomed back with manifestations of personal loyalty. Upon his return, he assumed control of the state's foreign affairs. In 1670, he entered into a secret treaty with Louis XIV of France, agreeing to subordinate English foreign policy to that of France in exchange for an annual pension of £100,000. During this time, Dunkirk, which had been captured by Cromwell, was sold back to France.

Charles II is noted for his patronage of science and sport. In 1662, he granted a Royal Charter to the Royal Society of London, an institution that promoted co-operative work and the open publication of scientific findings. Personally, the king was a great sportsman with a particular love for watching horse races, leaving the racing centre at Newmarket as a characteristic monument to his interests.

Earlier in his life, while in exile, Charles II conferred the Garter upon George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, on September 19, 1649, and admitted him to the privy council on April 6, 1650. Charles II accompanied Buckingham to Scotland in June 1650 and was later present with him at the battle of Worcester on September 3, 1651.

## Charles of France and Other Figures
In the 14th century, King Charles V of France was served by his brother, Louis I, Duke of Anjou. Louis I provided "good service" to Charles V during the war against England that resumed in 1369. Following the death of Charles V in September 1380, Louis I became the regent of France. However, Louis I's ambitions in Italy led him to clash with a rival, Charles, Duke of Durazzo, who held Naples.

In the 15th century, Charles VII of France had several faithful ministers, including Antoine de Chabannes, Pierre de Brézé, and Étienne Chevalier. After Louis was anointed king at Reims on August 15, 1461, he initially imprisoned these ministers of Charles VII, though the more serviceable officers were eventually reinstated. Additionally, Philippe de Commines served as an intimate adviser to Louis after 1472, having previously been in the service of Charles the Bold of Burgundy.

Other historical figures include Charles, the eldest son of the Cossé family, for whom the countship of Brissac was made in 1560; he served as the governor of Picardy and Piedmont and as grandmaster of artillery. There was also a Charles II de Cossé, who served as the governor of Paris and opened the city gates to Henry IV, who subsequently created him marshal of France in 1594.
