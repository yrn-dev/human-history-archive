# BORDEAUX

Bordeaux is a city located in south-western France and serves as the capital of the department of Gironde. As of 1906, the city had a population of 237,707. It is described as one of the most extensive and finest cities in France.

## Geography and Layout
The city is situated on the west (or left) bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea. It lies within a plain that encompasses the Medoc wine-growing district. At this location, the Garonne forms a semicircle, which separates the city proper on the left bank from La Bastide, an important suburb located on the right bank.

The city is surrounded by a semicircle of boulevards. Beyond these boulevards are the suburbs of Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat. Within the city, the area containing most important buildings features narrow, winding streets where city life is concentrated. Outside of this quarter, the streets are quiet and narrow, characterized by low white houses rather than the high tenements found in other large French towns.

## Infrastructure and Landmarks
Bordeaux is connected to other cities via the railway; it is 159 miles north-west of Toulouse on the main line of the Southern railway and 359 miles south-south-west of Paris via the Orleans railway main line. A railway bridge connects the Southern company station on the left bank with the Orleans railway company station in La Bastide. Additionally, the river is crossed by the Pont de Bordeaux, a stone structure from the early 19th century that measures 1,534 feet in length.

Looking west from the Pont de Bordeaux, the landscape consists of a crescent of busy, wide quays backed by mansions, factories, and lofty warehouses, with steeples and towers rising behind them. Key landmarks include:

*   **Place des Quinconces:** Located near the center of the quays, this area is planted with plane trees and features two huge statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne. It terminates at the quays with two rostral columns that function as lighthouses. On its west side stands a monument to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed in 1793 under the convention.
*   **Place de la Comedie:** Located at the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces, this square contains the 18th-century Grand Theatre.
*   **Promenades:** The city's principal promenades are clustered near the center and include the Place des Quinconces, the allees de Tourny, and a beautiful public garden.

## History
Originally known as *Burdigala*, Bordeaux was the chief town of the Bituriges Vivisci. During the Roman Empire, it flourished as a commercial city and became the capital of Aquitania Secunda in the 4th century. The 4th-century writer Ausonius, a native of the city, described it as being surrounded by lofty towers and walls in a four-square shape, noting its status as one of the greatest educational centers in Gaul.

Following the disintegration of the Roman Empire, the city suffered and did not regain its prosperity until the start of the 10th century. For nearly three hundred years, from 1154 to 1453, Bordeaux and Guienne belonged to the English kings. During this period, the city served as the seat for the court of Edward the Black Prince, and his son Richard was born there. 

The English administration supported the city's trade and liberties. In 1235, Bordeaux gained the right to elect its own mayors, who were assisted by a municipal council known as a "jurade." The city's regional influence grew as it led a federation that included other important towns such as Libourne and St Emilion. Extensive commerce developed between Bordeaux merchants and English ports, specifically Chester, Bristol, Dartmouth, Exeter, Hull, and London.

The English era ended after the battle of Castillon in 1453. Following a three-month siege, Bordeaux submitted to Charles VII. While the city's privileges were initially curtailed, they were partially restored under Louis XI, who established the parlement of Guienne in the city. In 1548, the inhabitants rose up by force of arms against the imposition of the salt-tax; this rebellion was suppressed with merciless severity by the constable Anne de Montmorency.
