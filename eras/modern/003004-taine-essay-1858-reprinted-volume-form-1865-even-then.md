# Taine's Essay on Balzac

## Overview and Significance
Taine's Essay of 1858, which was later reprinted in volume form in 1865, represents a pivotal moment in the critical study of the novelist Balzac. According to the archive's records, it marks the beginning of a series of important studies of Balzac that were characterized by two primary factors: they were based on the entirety of the author's work and remained unbiased by either friendship or enmity.

## Context of Balzac Scholarship
Prior to Taine's contribution, various works on Balzac existed, though they often lacked the comprehensive or objective nature of Taine's approach. Some notable earlier or contemporary efforts included:
* **Sainte-Beuve:** Produced a postmortem examination in *Causeries du lundi*, vol. ii. While not considered unfair, the work was hardly cordial, reflecting a relationship in which the novelist and critic had fallen out, leading to a furious diatribe by Balzac against Sainte-Beuve.
* **Gautier:** A trusty and very intimate friend of Balzac, Gautier wrote an excellent study that was mainly personal in nature, later reprinted in his *Portraits contemporains*.
* **Lamartine:** Published a volume on Balzac in 1866, though it is noted as not being of much value.
* **Minor Contemporaries:** Figures such as Champfleury, Lemer, and Gozlan also provided contributions to the body of work on the novelist.

## Research Limitations in the 19th Century
Even at the time of Taine's 1865 volume reprint, scholars faced significant obstacles in accessing primary materials. The *Oeuvres diverses* could only be accessed through "immense labour" because the originals were scattered. Furthermore, the *Correspondance* was not accessible at all. 

The archive notes that a full study of Balzac's man and work only became possible after the completion of the *Edition definitive*, which reunited all these materials.

## Subsequent Critical Works
Following the foundation laid by Taine, several other critical analyses emerged:
* **Marcel Barriere:** Wrote *L'Oeuvre de Balzac* (1890) as a sort of official critical introduction to the *Edition definitive*. However, this work is largely focused on elaborate analyses of different books, and its purely critical sections are considered small and not of the first value.
* **Paul Flat:** Published *Essais sur Balzac* (2 vols., 1893-1894), which focused specifically on tracing types of character.
* **Edmond Bire:** Provided new and important biographical details, including the correct spelling of the novelist's name, in *Honore de Balzac* (1897).
* **A. Cabanes:** Wrote *Balzac ignore* (1899), which is primarily noted for investigating Balzac's interest in occult studies.
* **Hanotaux and Vicaire:** In the first part of *La Jeunesse de Balzac* (1903), titled *Balzac imprimeur*, they detailed Balzac's intimacy with Madame de Berny and his involvement in the printing business.
* **A. Le Breton:** Published *Balzac, l'homme et l'oeuvre* (1905), described as a very well-informed and critical examination, though somewhat severe.
* **Ferdinand Brunetiere:** Wrote *Honore de Balzac* (1906), characterized as a brilliant but rather one-sided panegyric.
