# Bardsey Island

Bardsey, also known as *Ynys Enlli* ("isle of the current"), is an island situated at the northern extremity of Cardigan Bay in North Wales. It is approximately 2-1/2 miles long and 3/4 miles broad, covering an area of some 370 acres. The island is characterized by high cliffs on its northwest side, while a third of its total area is hilly.

## Geography and Economy
The island is separated from Aberdaron point by a "sound" that is roughly 4 miles wide. Its economy is based on agriculture and fishing; the inhabitants are farmers and fishermen who grow oats and barley. A fairly deep harbour is located on the southeast side of the island.

## Religious History and Inhabitants
Bardsey is historically significant as a sanctuary made famous by St Dubricius, who died there in 612. It served as a burial place for monks whose friends could afford to transport their bodies there, with quotas sent by all the great abbeys of England. Roads leading to the island from the north, east, and south are marked by monks' wells found at intervals of 7 to 9 miles.

Following the great massacre of Bangor-is-coed in 613 by Ethelfride of Northumbria, the remaining priests fled to the island via the road of the Rivals (*Yn Eifl*) hill in South Carnarvonshire. A portion of the island, spanning about 3 to 4 acres, remains a great cemetery featuring rude, rough graves placed as close together as possible with slabs upon them.

While the ruins of the 13th-century tower of St Mary's abbey exist on the northeast side, there is no Anglican church on the island. The current inhabitants are Dissenters.

## Landmarks and Administration
The island is home to a locally celebrated lighthouse. This structure stands 140 feet high, utilizes a fixed light, and is visible for 17 miles.

Administratively, Bardsey is included in Carnarvonshire, though it is traditionally associated with South Wales. The farm known as "Cwrt" (Court), where the abbot once held court, is associated with Bardsey. This property was granted to John Wynn of Bodvel, Carnarvonshire, following the partial sack of Norwich and the battle involving the Puritans during the Civil War. It later passed through Mary Bodvel to her husband, the earl of Radnor, who sold it to Dr Wilson of York. Dr Wilson subsequently sold the property to Sir John Wynn of Bodvelan Hall and Glynllifon, Carnarvonshire.

Separately, the rectory of Aberdaron—located on the mainland opposite the island—along with Llangwnadl (Llangwynhoedl) and Penmachno in Lleyn, South Carnarvonshire, belong to St John's College, Cambridge.
