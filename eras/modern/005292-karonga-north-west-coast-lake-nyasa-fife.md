# Karonga and Fife

Karonga and Fife are sites of European settlement located within the region of British Central Africa, specifically associated with the geography of Lake Nyasa and the surrounding plateaus.

## Karonga
Karonga is a township and site of European settlement situated on the north-west coast of Lake Nyasa. The region surrounding the north and north-west coasts of the lake is characterized by the North Nyasa or Nkonde group of dialects, which includes languages such as *Iki-nyikiusa* and *Ici-wandia*. These dialects are considered to be of an archaic Bantu structure.

The broader area to the north-west of Lake Nyasa is marked by significant orographical features, specifically the Konde Mountains, where peaks are believed to exceed a height of 8,000 feet.

## Fife
Fife is a township and site of European settlement located on the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau. This plateau is a high ridge situated between the vicinity of Lake Mweru, the vicinity of Tanganyika, and the basin of the river Luangwa, reaching highest points of 7,000 to 8,000 feet.

The linguistic landscape of the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau includes the *Ishi-nyi[chi]a* dialect, which is part of the North Nyasa or Nkonde group.

## Regional Geography and Resources
The settlements of Karonga and Fife are positioned within a landscape dominated by high plateaus, with an average altitude of 3,500 feet above sea level. Lake Nyasa itself sits at an elevation of 1,700 feet, stretching approximately 350 miles in length.

The western region of Lake Nyasa and the surrounding highlands are rich in various mineral deposits. Specifically, the mountainous region west of Lake Nyasa contains gold, while the western basin of the lake contains graphite. Copper (both pure ore and pyrites) is found in the west Nyasa region. Additionally, coal deposits occur in the west and north Nyasa districts, particularly within the Karroo sandstones of the Rukuru valley. Iron ore and mica are described as being almost universally present in the area.

## Demographics and Linguistics
The indigenous population of the region is of the Negro type and belongs exclusively to the Bantu linguistic family. In the north end of Lake Nyasa, the physique of the native Awankonde is noted for recalling that of the Nilotic negro.

The linguistic distribution in the vicinity of these settlements is diverse:
* **North Nyasa Group:** Comprising the north-west and north coasts of Lake Nyasa and the Nyasa-Tanganyika plateau.
* **Tumbuka Section:** Occupying the area between the Upper Luangwa river and the western shores of Lake Nyasa, south of the North Nyasa group. This section includes the *A-tonga*, *Henga*, and *Tumbuka* peoples.
* **Nyanja Group:** Associated with the west and east shores of the southern half of Lake Nyasa.
