# Africa

The history of Africa, as recorded in the archive's sources, encompasses ancient military campaigns, 19th-century exploration, and 20th-century political unification.

## Ancient Military Expeditions
In ancient times, Africa served as a base for large-scale military operations directed toward Sicily. During one such campaign, a mercenary force was assembled over the winter, consisting of a "motley crowd of barbaric warriors." The size of this host is disputed among sources: Ephorus claims there were 300,000 men, while Xenophon and Timæus state there were 120,000. This force was composed of Libyans, Numidians, Iberians, Campanians, and Mediterranean islanders.

The expedition was led by Hannibal, who was induced to accept command alongside his relative, Imilkon. The army was gathered at Carthage and prepared for transport in a fleet consisting of 1,500 transports and 120 triremes. To secure the landing, 40 Carthaginian triremes were sent ahead to the Bay of Motyê. Although Syracusan forces initially destroyed 15 of these triremes and drove the remainder back to Africa, Hannibal subsequently countered with 50 fresh triremes, forcing the Syracusans to retire and allowing the grand armament to disembark near the western cape of Sicily.

## 19th Century Exploration and Settlement
In the mid-19th century, British expeditions focused on the Niger and Benue rivers. In 1854, William Balfour Baikie, a Scottish naturalist, philologist, and M.D., was appointed as surgeon and naturalist for a Niger expedition supported by the government and Macgregor Laird. Following the death of Consul Beecroft at Fernando Po, Baikie assumed command. He led the steamer *Pleiad* 250 miles up the Benue beyond previous exploration limits, returning to the mouth of the Niger after a 118-day voyage without losing any men. During this trip, Baikie attempted to find Heinrich Barth, who had crossed the upper Benue in 1851, but he could not find trustworthy information regarding him.

In March 1857, Baikie returned to the region as a British consul aboard the *Pleiad*. After two years of exploring the Niger, the vessel was wrecked in the river's rapids. While the rest of his party returned to England, Baikie remained. Landing at the confluence of the Benue and Niger with one or two native followers, he established a base of operations at Lokoja. This site had previously been the location of a model farm established by a British government expedition in 1841, which had been abandoned within a year due to the deaths of most white settlers. To secure the site, Baikie purchased the land and concluded a treaty with the Fula emir of Nupe.

## Political Unification in South Africa
In the early 20th century, the political structure of South Africa underwent a transition toward centralization. While other parts of the British empire utilized federation—such as the Commonwealth of Australia and the Dominion of Canada—South Africa opted for unification. In 1910, the self-governing colonies of the region were united into a single state known as the Union of South Africa.
