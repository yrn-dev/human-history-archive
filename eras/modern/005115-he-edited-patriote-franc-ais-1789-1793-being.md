# Brissot

Brissot was a prominent figure during the French Revolution, characterized as a man of wide knowledge who was quick, eager, and impetuous, though also vacillating. He dedicated himself heart and soul to the Revolution from its onset.

## Political Career and Journalism
From 1789 to 1793, Brissot edited the publication *Patriote français*. His capabilities and information led him to take a significant role in revolutionary affairs; notably, the keys to the Bastille were presented to him following its demolition. He gained fame for his speeches delivered at the Jacobin club.

His political ascent included elections to several governing bodies:
* The municipality of Paris
* The Legislative Assembly
* The National Convention

## Foreign Policy and the Girondists
While serving in the Legislative Assembly, Brissot utilized his knowledge of foreign affairs to practically direct the foreign policy of France as a member of the diplomatic committee. He was largely responsible for the declaration of war against the emperor on April 20, 1792, and the declaration of war against England on July 1, 1793. Brissot is credited with giving these conflicts the character of revolutionary propaganda.

He served as the leading spirit of the Girondists, a political group that was also referred to as the Brissotins. While it is noted that Vergniaud was superior to Brissot in the art of oratory, Brissot remained a central figure of the faction.

## Fall and Execution
The Girondists eventually fell before the Mountain. On June 2, 1793, a sentence of arrest was passed against the leading members of Brissot's party. In an attempt to avoid capture, Brissot tried to escape in disguise, but he was arrested at Moulins.

During his trial, Brissot maintained a dignified and quiet demeanour. He died bravely on October 31, 1793, alongside several other Girondists.
