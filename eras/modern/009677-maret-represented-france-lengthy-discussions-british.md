# Hugues-Bernard Maret

Hugues-Bernard Maret, the Duc de Bassano (1763–1839), was a French publicist and statesman who served in various high-ranking diplomatic and administrative capacities during the French Revolution and the reign of Napoleon. Born in Dijon, Maret was educated and entered the legal profession as an advocate at the King's Council in Paris.

## Early Career and Journalism
Maret's career was profoundly altered by the ideas of the French Revolution. He became involved in publishing the debates of the first National Assembly in the *Bulletin de l'Assemblée*, a project he undertook with Méjean. This work was later merged into a larger publication, the *Moniteur universel*, at the suggestion of the publisher Charles Joseph Panckoucke. The *Moniteur universel* became widely recognized for its impartiality and correctness.

Politically, Maret was a member of the Feuillants, a moderate club. Following the overthrow of the monarchy on August 10, 1792, he accepted a position within the ministry of foreign affairs, where he was noted for exercising a steadying influence.

## Diplomatic Missions to Great Britain
Maret was involved in several attempts to secure peace between France and Great Britain. In December 1792, following the withdrawal of the British legation from Paris, Maret traveled to London on a mission where he held a favorable interview with Pitt on December 2. However, these hopes for accommodation were ended by the execution of Louis XVI on January 21, 1793, and the subsequent declaration of war by the French Convention on February 1, 1793. These events made a second mission to London in January impossible.

Later, during the summer of 1797, Maret played a useful role in peace negotiations with Great Britain held at Lille. These efforts were frustrated by the *coup d'état* of Fructidor in September 1797, which ended Pitt's hopes for peace and resulted in another reverse for Maret.

## Service Under Napoleon
Maret maintained a close relationship with Napoleon, accompanying him through most of his campaigns, including the campaign of 1809. In 1810, he expressed support for Napoleon's marriage alliance with the archduchess Marie Louise of Austria.

In the spring of 1811, Maret was appointed minister of Foreign Affairs, replacing the duc de Cadore. In this role, he concluded treaties between France and Prussia and between France and Austria prior to the 1812 invasion of Russia. Although he remained with Napoleon for most of that campaign and helped prepare forces for the 1813 campaign, he was replaced as minister in November 1813 by the duc de Vicence, who was perceived as more devoted to peace and grateful to Emperor Alexander I of Russia. Despite this, Maret continued to serve as the emperor's private secretary through the campaigns of 1814 and 1815.

## The Treaty of Amiens and Other Diplomatic Work
Maret was a key figure in the diplomatic successes that consolidated Napoleon's power as First Consul for life. He and another official represented France in the lengthy discussions with the British envoy, Lord Cornwallis. These negotiations resulted in the signing of the treaty of Amiens on March 25, 1802.

Beyond his work with the British, Maret's diplomatic services included presiding over the negotiations for the treaty of Luneville with Austria, signed February 9, 1801. In 1800, at his country-house in Mortfontaine, he concluded a convention with the envoy of the United States.

## Later Life and Legacy
Following the second restoration of the Bourbons, Maret was exiled to Grätz, where he focused on literary work. He was permitted to return to France in 1820. After the Revolution of 1830, King Louis Philippe made him a peer of France, and he briefly held two high offices before his death in Paris in 1839.

Maret is regarded alongside Daru as one of the most devoted and hardest-working supporters in Napoleon's service. However, some critics suggest that his devotion reached the level of servility, which may have compromised the actual interests of France—a view contested by Baron Ernouf in the biography *Maret, duc de Bassano*.
