# Legislation primitive (1802) and Du divorce considere au XIXe siecle

## Overview
*Legislation primitive* (1802) and *Du divorce considere au XIXe siecle* (1801) are works authored by the vicomte de Bonald. These texts are part of a broader body of philosophical and social writing produced by Bonald during the late 18th and early 19th centuries.

## Author and Related Works
The vicomte de Bonald published several other notable works alongside these titles, including the *Essai analytique sur les lois naturelles de l'ordre social* in 1800. His later publications include *Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales*, published in two volumes in 1818, and *Melanges litteraires et politiques, demonstration philosophique du principe constitutif de la societe*, which appeared in 1819 and 1852.

## Publication History
The collected works of the vicomte de Bonald have been issued in multiple editions. The first collected edition was released in 12 volumes between 1817 and 1819. A later edition was produced by the Abbe Migne, consisting of 3 volumes published in 1859.

## Academic Reception and References
The works and life of the vicomte de Bonald have been addressed in various biographical and philosophical studies. His son, Victor, authored a *Notice sur M. le Vicomte de Bonald* (1841), which was edited in Avignon in 1853. Additionally, Bonald's contributions to philosophy are discussed in the works of Damiron, specifically in *Phil. en France au XIXe siecle*, and in the *History of Philosophy* by Windelband (translated by J.H. Tufts in 1893). E. Faguet also wrote regarding Bonald in the *Rev. des deux mondes* on April 15, 1889.
