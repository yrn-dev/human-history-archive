# Coke

The term "coke" refers to both a prominent legal figure in English history and a carbonaceous fuel product derived from coal.

## Sir Edward Coke and the English Legal System
Sir Edward Coke was a legal figure known for his constant tendency to limit the royal prerogative through the use of law and precedent. This stance made him an object of particular dislike to King James. Coke's efforts to restrict the jurisdiction of courts that served as direct exponents of the king's wishes led to open collisions with the king's rights on two primary points.

The first instance involved an action of *praemunire* against the court of chancery. Although instigated by Coke, the action was brought by parties whose adversaries had received redress in the chancellor's court after a trial in the court of king's bench. Despite his ingenuity and learning, Coke failed to force the jury to bring in a bill against the court of chancery; it is noted that he was likely wrong on the technical point of law involved. This action was driven in large part by a personal dislike for Ellesmere and a desire to restrict the court's jurisdiction.

The second and more significant case concerned *commendams*. The central question was whether the king's prerogative included the right to grant livings *in commendam* (to be enjoyed by someone other than the incumbent) at his pleasure. During these proceedings, the attorney-general, Bacon, delivered a speech. King James was informed that the arguments presented had moved beyond the special case to directly impugn the general prerogative right of granting livings. Consequently, James directed Bacon to tell the judges to delay their judgment until the matter had been discussed with the king. Bacon communicated this to Coke, who requested that the other judges receive similar notice.

In the broader context of English law during Coke's era, the legal system was developed more through court debates than open legislation. Coke viewed the era of Edward III as a golden age of good pleading. He also considered Sir Thomas Littleton's treatise on Tenures to be the most perfect work ever written.

## Industrial Production of Coke
Coke is a product of the coal industry, produced through the coking of coal. This process occurs in two primary methods:

**Beehive Ovens:** In old beehive ovens or similar apparatus, the gas that issues at the mouth of the ovens is lost, and attempts to utilize these gases have generally been unsuccessful.

**Destructive Distillation:** Coke is also manufactured via the destructive distillation of coal in closed apparatus known as retorts, which are heated from the outside. This method originated in France and is more prevalent in Germany, where over half of the coke is produced this way. In the United Kingdom and the United States, progress has been slower, though it is recognized as the only proper method. This process increases coke output by approximately 15% compared to beehive ovens because the heat is produced by burning part of the gas rather than burning part of the coal itself. The resulting coke is of equal quality for iron-making as beehive coke, though it differs in appearance. Additionally, the gases produced can yield benzene vapours, tar, and ammonia, the value of which can sometimes exceed the value of the coke itself.

## Chemical Examination and Classification
The determination of coke is a vital part of chemical examinations of coal. To determine coke, a finely powdered sample is placed in a covered porcelain crucible, surrounded by an outer crucible packed with small coke, and heated in a wind furnace for 1 to 1.5 hours.

Coke is classified into four types based on its appearance, which provides information about the nature of the coal:
*   **Pulverulent:** Indicates a non-caking bituminous coal. If the amount is below 60%, it is rich in oxygen; if significantly less, it generally indicates a lignite; if above 80%, it indicates an anthracite with little hydrogen or oxygen.
*   **Slightly fritted:** Indicates a slightly coking coal.
*   **Spongy and swelled:** Points to a highly coking coal that has been partly fused in the furnace.
*   **Compact:** Yielded by good coking coals and is usually large in amount.

## Regional Industrial Impact: Birmingham
In the United States, the production of coke was central to the industrial development of Jefferson County and the city of Birmingham. By 1900, more than 300 mining and manufacturing establishments in Jefferson County were engaged chiefly in the production of coke, coal, and iron. 

The region benefited from natural advantages, including the 25-mile-long Red Mountain (rich in hematite iron ore), limestone deposits, and three great coalfields: the Cahaba, the Coosa, and the Warrior. These resources allowed for the production of pig iron at an unusually low cost. By 1902, nine-tenths of Alabama's pig iron, coke, and coal were produced in this district.
