# Balthazare Vestris

## Overview and Artistic Standing
Gaetano Apolline Balthazare Vestris was a preeminent figure in the history of ballet, regarded as the finest male dancer ever produced in Europe. He was a contemporary of Jean Georges Noverre, who is credited as the father of modern ballet dancing and the inventor of the *ballet d'action*. While Noverre focused on the theory that ballet must be a direct expression of a particular attitude of mind rather than mere display, Vestris stood apart as one of the greatest ballet-masters of his era. He was known for his confidence in his own abilities, modestly referring to himself as *le dieu de la danse* (the god of dance).

## Notable Collaborations and Performances
Vestris engaged in high-profile collaborations with other leading artists of the 18th century. He worked in conjunction with the composer Gluck on the production of *Iphigenie en Aulide*. Additionally, in 1750, Vestris performed in Paris alongside Madame Camargo, who is noted for introducing short skirts to ballet. The two dancers, recognized as the greatest of their day, appeared together in a ballet-opera titled *Leandre et Hero*.

## Family Connections
The Vestris family maintained connections within the performing arts. Gaetano Vestris had a brother, Angelo, who married the actress Marie Rose Gourgaud (1743-1804). Gourgaud, who first performed in Stuttgart, later made her debut at the Comedie Francaise in 1768 under the protection of the dukes of Duras and Choiseul, where she performed important roles in various tragedies.
