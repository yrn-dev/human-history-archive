# Bibliography and Statistical Records of the Early 20th Century

The early 20th century saw a proliferation of specialized literature and detailed statistical recording across various fields, including accounting, medicine, theology, and industry. The following sections detail the authoritative works and data available during this period.

## Accounting and Book-keeping
For those researching the bibliography of book-keeping, the library of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales is noted as likely containing the most complete collection of both modern and ancient works on the subject, covering both foreign and British sources. Key texts of general interest from this era include G. van de Linde's *Book-keeping* (1898) and the works of L.R. Dicksee, specifically *Book-keeping* (5th ed., 1906) and *Advanced Accounting* (2nd ed., 1905). Other significant references include the *Encyclopaedia of Accounting* edited by G. Lisle (1903), the *Accountants' Library* edited by the editor of *The Accountant* (1901), and J.W. Heaps' *The Antiquity of Book-keeping* (1898). Additionally, R. Brown edited the *History of Accounting and Accountants* in 1905.

## Bacteriology and Medical Science
Bacteriological literature became so extensive by the early 1900s that references to original articles were often impossible to provide in full. However, the New Sydenham Society published *Microparasites in Disease: Selected Essays* in 1886, which translated classical researches from German and French. 

Important books published in recent years include:
* **General Bacteriology:** Abbott's *Principles of Bacteriology* (7th ed., 1905), Crookshank's *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* (4th ed., 1896), and Hewlett's *Manual of Bacteriology* (2nd ed., 1902).
* **Specialized and Foreign Works:** Duclaux's *Traite de microbiologie* (1899-1900), Fluegge's *Die Mikroorganismen* (3rd ed., 1896), and Guenther's *Einfuehrung in das Studium der Bakteriologie* (6th ed., 1906).
* **Pathogenic Bacteria:** Kolle and Wassermann's *Handbuch der pathogenen Mikroorganismen* (1904) is cited as the most important work on the subject, with supplements continuing to be published. Other works include McFarland's *Text-book upon the Pathogenic Bacteria* (5th ed., 1906) and Park's *Pathogenic Micro-organisms* (1906).
* **Immunity:** Key works include Metchnikoff's *Immunity in Infective Dis* and Ehrlich's *Studies in Immunity* (English translation, 1906).

## Biblical Studies and Theology
Literature regarding the New Testament is vast, with specific bibliographies existing for individual books. General and historical studies include the *Prefaces* of Jerome and Luther, and A. Schweitzer's *Von Reimarus zu Wrede* (1906). 

Studies on the Gospels are divided by focus:
* **Synoptic Gospels:** Works include W.G. Rushbrooke's *Synopticon* (1880) and Julius Wellhausen's four-book series including *Einleitung in die drei ersten Evangelien* (1905).
* **Fourth Gospel:** Notable texts include W. Sanday's *Criticism of the Fourth Gospel* (1905) and P.W. Schmiedel's *Das vierte Evangelium gegenueber den drei ersten* (1906).
* **Semitic Elements:** G. Dalman's *The Words of Jesus* (1905) and Johannes Weiss' *Die Predigt Jesu vom Reiche Gottes* (2nd ed., 1900) are primary sources.

Regarding the Epistles, commentaries on *Ephesians* were produced by C.J. Ellicott, H.A.W. Meyer, and T.K. Abbott. Investigations into the genuineness of these letters were conducted by A. Julicher (5th and 6th ed., 1906) and F.J.A. Hort.

## Banking and Industrial Statistics
### The Bank of France
The Bank of France operated primarily through note circulation, which averaged £186,300,000 in 1906. The circulation limits grew steadily from 350 million francs on March 15, 1848, to 5,800 million francs (equivalent to £232,000,000) in 1906. As of the reporting period, the bank held 66,569 total accounts, an increase from 59,182 at the end of 1903. These included 24,106 deposit accounts and 26,709 current accounts with advances.

### The United Kingdom Brewing Trade
The brewing industry in the UK reached a production peak of 37,090,986 barrels in 1900. For the period of 1905-1906, production was 34,109,263 barrels. In the year ending September 30, 1905, the exchequer received £13,156,053 in beer duty. 

Industry specifics for 1905-1906 include:
* **Production:** 5,180 brewers were in operation. Messrs Guinness owned the world's largest brewery, producing over two million barrels.
* **Materials:** The trade used 51,818,697 bushels of malt, 62,360,817 lb of hops, and 2,746,615 cwt. of sugar.
* **Consumption:** Of the beer retained for consumption, 28,590,563 barrels were used in England, 3,265,084 in Ireland, and 1,648,463 in Scotland.
* **Capital:** While capital in the liquor trade was estimated at £117,000,000 in 1871, this figure likely doubled by 1908. The *Brewers' Almanack* for 1906 estimated capital in limited liability breweries alone at £185,000,000.
