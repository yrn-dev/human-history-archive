# 133rd Ballot

The 133rd ballot refers to the conclusion of a protracted speakership contest in the United States House of Representatives during the opening of the Thirty-Fourth Congress. This event resulted in the election of Nathaniel Prentiss Banks as speaker.

## The Speakership Contest of 1855-1856
The contest for the speakership was one of the most bitter and protracted in the history of the congress. It began on December 3, 1855, and lasted until February 2, 1856. During this period, anti-Nebraska men gradually united in their support for Nathaniel Prentiss Banks. The deadlock was finally broken on the 133rd ballot, when Banks was chosen as the speaker. This specific outcome has been characterized as the first national victory of the Republican party.

## Nathaniel Prentiss Banks
Nathaniel Prentiss Banks (1816–1894) was an American soldier and politician born in Waltham, Massachusetts. His early life was marked by a common school education and employment as a bobbin-boy in a cotton factory managed by his father. Banks later edited a weekly newspaper in Waltham and studied law, eventually gaining distinction as a public speaker.

His political career included several roles:
* **Massachusetts House of Representatives:** He served as a Free Soiler from 1849 to 1853, acting as speaker in 1851 and 1852.
* **Constitutional Convention:** He served as president of the state Constitutional Convention in 1853.
* **National House of Representatives:** He was elected in 1853 as a coalition candidate of Free Soilers and Democrats. In 1854, he was re-elected as an American or "Know-Nothing," though he left that party shortly thereafter and presided over a Republican convention in Massachusetts in 1855.

Following his tenure as speaker of the House, Banks was re-elected as a Republican in 1856. He resigned his seat in December 1857 to become the governor of Massachusetts (1858–1861), a term noted for educational and administrative reforms. Later, he served as president of the Illinois Central railway.

## Military Service
Despite advocating for peace during his governorship, Banks offered his services to President Lincoln in 1861 and was appointed major-general of volunteers. He became a prominent volunteer officer. During George B. McClellan's Peninsular Campaign in 1862, Banks's corps was tasked with the important duty of defending Washington against the army of "Stonewall" Jackson. In the spring, Banks was ordered to move against Jackson in the Shenandoah Valley, but he was defeated at Winchester, Virginia, on the 25th by Jackson's superior forces.
