# Dresden

Dresden is a city in Saxony that has served as a center for the arts and a location of strategic military importance during various European conflicts.

## Arts and Culture
Dresden has a history of supporting musical and visual arts. In 1711, the court orchestra in Dresden expanded its instrumentation by adding two Bohemian horn-players, Franz Adam Samm and Johann Adalbert Fischer.

In the early 19th century, the city became a residence for the Norwegian landscape painter Johann Christian Dahl. Dahl, who was born in Bergen and spent time in Copenhagen, settled in Dresden in 1818. Despite being nearly forty years old upon his arrival, he was quickly admitted into the Academy and appointed as a professor. Dahl is credited with helping to improve German landscape painting, which was not greatly advanced at the time. While he traveled to Italy and Tirol, he remained a resident of Dresden. His works, including "Dresden by Moonlight," "Storm at Sea," and a large painting titled "Norway," are noted as being visible in the city.

## Military History and Conflict
Dresden and the surrounding region of Saxony have been involved in several major military campaigns. In 1745, Saxony was part of a Quadruple Alliance formed on January 8 with England, Austria, and Holland. During this campaign, Frederick of Prussia faced an army of 85,000 Saxons and Austrians led by Prince Charles. Following the battle of Hohenfriedberg (or Striegau) on June 4, which was one of Frederick's greatest victories, Prince Charles was defeated and withdrew through the mountains. The subsequent summer was characterized by maneuvers between the two leaders on the upper Elbe.

## Naval History: The SMS Dresden
The name *Dresden* is also associated with a German cruiser during the Great War. During a naval engagement, the *Dresden* attempted to run out of action at a speed of 27 knots. While Captain Luce used the *Leipzig* to tempt the German consorts to fall back, the *Dresden* refused to turn back and disappeared into the mist. The ship remained unseen until March 1915. After a five-minute action with the *Glasgow* and the *Kent*, who had caught the vessel at anchor, the *Dresden* surrendered and blew up. The hunting down of the *Dresden* marked the end of German cruiser warfare in the outer seas, leaving no German ships of war on the world's oceans, with the exception of the *Karlsruhe* (which had been sunk by an internal explosion on November 4).
