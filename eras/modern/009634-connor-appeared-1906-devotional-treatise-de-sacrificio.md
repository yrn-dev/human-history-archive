# De Sacrificio Missae and Related Works

The archive contains references to several works titled *De sacrificio missae*, as well as publications associated with D. O'Connor and V.D. Scott O'Connor.

## De Sacrificio Missae
The title *De sacrificio missae* is associated with two different authors in the archive's records:

**John Bona**
John Bona (1609–1674) was an Italian author and cardinal born in Mondovi, Piedmont. He served as the general of the Congregation of Feuillants and held the positions of abbot of Mondovi and prior of Asti. He was created cardinal by Clement IX in 1669. Bona's literary output focused primarily on devotional and liturgical subjects. His devotional treatise, *De Sacrificio Missae*, is described as the classical work in its field; a new edition of this text was published in 1903 by Ildephonsus Cummins.

**Pope Benedict XIV**
Prospero Lorenzo Lambertini, who served as Pope Benedict XIV from 1740 to 1758, also published a work titled *De sacrificio missae*. Born in Bologna in 1675, Benedict XIV was a scholar of canonistic and theological study. Before his papacy, he served as the archbishop of Theodosia *in partibus* and later as the archbishop of Ancona and Bologna. In addition to *De sacrificio missae*, his published works include *De servorum Dei beatificatione et canonizatione* and a treatise regarding the feasts of some saints honored in Bologna and those of the Virgin and Christ.

## D. O'Connor and the Principia Vitae Christianae
In 1906, a new edition of the *Principia Vitae Christianae* was published. This specific edition was edited by D. O'Connor. This work is part of the broader collection of writings by John Bona, whose other works include *De Libris Liturgicis*, *Divina Psalmodia*, *De Discretione Spirituum*, and *Manuductio ad Coelum* (translated as *The Guide to Heaven*).

## V.D. Scott O'Connor
Separate from the theological editors and treatises, the archive lists V.D. Scott O'Connor as the author of a work titled *The Silken East*, which was published in London in 1904. This text is cited as a non-official authority regarding Burma.
