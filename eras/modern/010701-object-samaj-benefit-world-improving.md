# The Arya Samaj

The Arya Samaj is a religious movement established by Dayanand, who founded the first *Samajes* in various parts of India, beginning with one in Bombay in 1875. Dayanand chose the term "Arya" for the organization, as he considered it more dignified than the term "Hindu," which he viewed as slightly contemptuous. Before his death at Ajmere in 1883, Dayanand conducted a series of tours where he engaged in public debates with Christian missionaries and orthodox pundits.

## Creed and Philosophy
The Arya Samaj is characterized by an intensity of conviction and a narrower scope than eclectic systems such as the Brahma Samaj, which seeks a common basis among all great religions. The movement appealed to educated natives whose faith in current Hinduism had been undermined but who remained predisposed against foreign religious influences. Supporters of the Samaj viewed it as a representation of the original faith of India that harmonized with the ethics of European civilization and the discoveries of modern science.

The creed of the Samaj is summarized in ten articles. Central to these is the belief that God is the source of all true knowledge. God is described as almighty, boundless, just, merciful, omniscient, and the cause of the universe; he is eternal, immortal, imperishable, and without a beginning. According to the creed, worship is due to God alone. The *Vedas* are recognized as the medium of true knowledge, and truth is to be accepted as the guiding principle.

## Objectives and Social Reform
The primary object of the Samaj is to benefit the world by improving its moral, intellectual, social, and physical conditions. The movement emphasizes that the good of others must be sought and that knowledge must be spread. In matters of general interest, members are expected to subordinate themselves to the good of others, while maintaining independence in their personal interests. Love and justice are regarded as the correct guides for conduct.

The Samaj's program for reform is wide-ranging and includes the following initiatives:
*   The promotion of female education.
*   The abolition of idolatry and the caste system.
*   Marriage reform.
*   Physical exercise and cleanliness.
*   Abstinence from animal food and spirituous liquors.

## Membership and Internal Divisions
Admission to the Arya Samaj is open to individuals who have reached the age of eighteen. Members are expected to contribute at least 1% of their income to the funds of the organization. By the year 1890, the membership of the Samaj had grown to 40,000, increasing to over 92,000 by 1900.

Despite this growth, the movement experienced internal divisions, specifically a cleavage between two sections: the *Mansi* and the *Ghasi*. The *Ghasi* section adheres to vegetarianism. The *Mansi* section consists of flesh-eating members, including certain Rajputs who, while generally sympathetic to the movement, refused to follow the tenet of the Samaj that forbade the consumption of animal food and the destruction of animal life.
