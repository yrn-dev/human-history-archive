# Henri Evrard, Marquis de Dreux-Brézé

Henri Evrard, marquis de Dreux-Brézé (1762–1829), was a French court official who served as the master of the ceremonies to Louis XVI. He succeeded his father in this role in 1781. The lordship of Brézé had previously passed to Claire Clémence de Maillé, princess of Condé, who sold it to Thomas Dreux; Thomas took the name Dreux Brézé when the estate was established as a marquisate.

## Role in the French Revolution
During the meeting of the states-general in 1789, Henri Evrard was tasked with regulating matters of precedence and etiquette among the three estates. As the immediate representative of the crown, his actions frequently wounded the susceptibilities of the deputies, partly because little effort was made to adjust traditional etiquette to the changing circumstances of the time.

Brézé is noted for his role in the events leading to the session in the tennis court. He did not formally notify President Bailly regarding the proclamation of the royal séance until June 20, at a time when carpenters were already preparing to enter the hall for the event. Following the royal séance, Brézé was dispatched to reiterate the orders of Louis XVI that the estates were to meet separately. This led to a confrontation with Mirabeau, who responded that the hall could not be cleared unless it was done by force.

## Emigration and Later Life
Following the fall of the Tuileries, Brézé emigrated from France for a short period. Although he eventually returned to France, he was spared during the period of the Terror. 

Upon the Restoration, Brézé resumed his previous functions as the guardian of an antiquated ceremonial and was appointed a peer of France. He held these positions until his death on January 27, 1829. He was succeeded in the peerage and at court by his son, Scipion (1793–1845).
