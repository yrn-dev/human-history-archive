# Portugal

## Colonial History and Territories
Portugal has maintained various colonial interests and territorial claims. In the case of Macao, although it was a de facto colonial possession of Portugal, the Chinese government initially refused to recognize Portuguese territorial rights, claiming the Portuguese were merely tenants at will or lessees. Consequently, until 1849, Portugal paid an annual rent of £71 to the Chinese. This dispute delayed a commercial treaty until 1887, when a formal agreement was signed. Under this treaty, China confirmed Portugal's government and perpetual occupation of Macao and its dependencies. In return, Portugal agreed to cooperate with opium revenue work in Macao, similar to the British operations in Hong-Kong, and pledged never to alienate the territory without Chinese consent. Macao was also briefly occupied by the English in 1802 and 1808 to prevent seizure by the French.

In the Americas, Portugal established an empire in Brazil. In 1807, after French armies under Napoleon occupied Portugal, the Portuguese monarchy fled to Brazil. During the period leading up to their eventual separation, Portugal became more of a dependency of Brazil than vice versa. In 1822, Brazil declared itself a separate Empire under Pedro I, who was a son of the Portuguese King.

## International Relations and Trade
Portugal has been involved in various international agreements and trade activities. In terms of agricultural exports, Portugal has provided tomatoes to England, though its contributions were significantly lower than those of other nations; in 1907, Portugal sent 11,700 cwt of tomatoes, trailing far behind the Canary Islands, the Channel Islands, France, and Spain.

In the aftermath of global conflict, Portugal was among the nations—alongside France and Rumania—that were relieved of all obligations toward Germany regarding The Hague Convention of 1903, which related to civil procedure.

## Military Conflicts
During the Napoleonic era, Portugal was conquered by Napoleon, who intended to unite the kingdoms of Portugal and Spain. However, this period of French control was challenged by the British. A British army led by Sir Arthur Wellesley (the Duke of Wellington) landed in Portugal and defeated the French at Vimiero, which forced the French forces to retire into Spain.
