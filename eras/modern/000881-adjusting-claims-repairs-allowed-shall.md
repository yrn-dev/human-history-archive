# Adjusting Claims for General Average Repairs

In the adjustment of claims for General Average (G.A.), the York-Antwerp Rules provide a standardized framework to ensure uniformity and certainty. These rules replaced previous customary rules that varied by the place of adjustment and were designed to adapt to modern shipbuilding conditions. A primary focus of these regulations is the determination of which repairs are allowable and how deductions are applied to those costs.

## Deductions for "New for Old"
Under Rule XIII, repairs allowed in G.A. are subject to specific deductions based on the principle of "new for old." These deductions are regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired.

### Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel vessels, deductions are calculated from the date of the original register to the date of the accident according to the following scale:
*   **Up to 1 year old:** All repairs are allowed in full, with the exception of the painting or coating of the bottom, from which one-third is deducted. Painting of the bottom is not allowed if it had not been painted within six months prior to the accident.
*   **Between 1 and 3 years:** One-third is deducted from repairs and renewals of painting, upholstery, crockery, metal and glassware, furniture, sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers (excluding wire and chain), covers, awnings, and woodwork of the hull, masts, and spars. A deduction of one-sixth is applied to chain cables, chains, wire ropes, wire rigging, wire hawsers, steam cranes and connexions, steam winches and connexions, and donkey engines. Other repairs are allowed in full.
*   **Between 3 and 6 years:** Deductions remain as listed above, except that a one-sixth deduction is now applied to machinery (including mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 6 and 10 years:** Deductions remain as listed above, except that a one-third deduction is applied to all hawsers, ropes, rigging, sheets, the renewal and repair of all machinery (including boilers and mountings), and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 10 and 15 years:** A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs, except for cementing, chain cables, and ironwork of the hull, from which one-sixth is deducted. Anchors are allowed in full.
*   **Over 15 years:** A one-third deduction is applied to all renewals and repairs, though anchors are allowed in full and chain cables are subject to a one-sixth deduction.

### Wooden or Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, no "new for old" deduction is made if the ship is under one year old at the time of the accident. After one year, a general deduction of one-third is applied, with specific exceptions:
*   **Anchors:** Allowed in full.
*   **Chain cables:** Subject to a deduction of one-sixth.
*   **Metal sheathing:** The cost of a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing is allowed in full, minus the proceeds of the old metal.
*   **Nails, felt, and labour metalling:** Subject to a one-third deduction.

## General Allowances and Exemptions
Certain expenses are allowed in full regardless of the ship's age. This includes the expense of replacing and taking out bent ironwork for straightening, as well as graving dock dues (including use of stages, shears, cartages, removals, and graving dock materials). 

Additionally, no deductions are made for provisions and stores that have not been in use, nor for old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material. Under Rule XIV, no "new for old" deductions are applied to the cost of temporary repairs for damage that is allowable as G.A.

## Related G.A. Costs and Contributions
Beyond physical repairs, other costs may be admitted as G.A. under specific rules:
*   **Wages and Maintenance:** Under Rule XI, the cost of maintenance and wages for the crew, officers, and master are admitted as G.A. during the extra period of detention in a port for the purposes of repairs or under other specified circumstances. However, these are not admitted if the ship is abandoned or condemned, or if the wages were incurred after the date of condemnation.
*   **Cargo Damage:** Damage or loss of cargo caused by discharging, reloading, storing, and stowing is made good as G.A. only when the costs of those specific measures are also admitted as G.A.
*   **Freight and Values:** Loss of freight resulting from cargo loss or damage is made good as G.A. if caused by a G.A. act. Contributory values are based on the actual value of property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed property. Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight at risk for crew wages and port charges that would not have occurred had the ship been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice. Notably, personal effects and passengers' luggage not shipped under bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.
