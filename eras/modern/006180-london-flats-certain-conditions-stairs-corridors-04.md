# London Flats: Construction Requirements for Stairs and Corridors

## Building Regulations in London
The construction and planning of buildings in London are subject to various building acts. These regulations govern a wide array of structural details, including the specific materials to be used and the methods of their employment. Other requirements mandated by these laws include the thickness of walls, the rates of inclination for roofs, drainage, the amount of space at the rear of the property, and the means of escape from fire. Notably, these laws specifically forbid the use of timber framed buildings.

## Fire-Resisting Materials in Residential Structures
Specific requirements regarding fire-resisting materials are applied to certain types of dwellings in London based on their use and size:

*   **Combined Premises:** For buildings used as both a shop and a dwelling-house, fire-resisting materials must be used for the stairs and a large portion of the building if the premises exceed 10 squares or 1,000 square feet in super area.
*   **London Flats:** Under certain conditions, the stairs and corridors of London flats must be constructed of fire-resisting materials.

These requirements contrast with building practices in other cities; for example, timber buildings are permitted in parts of New York.

## Comparative Planning and Oversight
The oversight of building construction varies by the type of edifice. While dwelling-houses follow the building acts, other structures are governed by different authorities:
*   **Public Schools:** Governed by the board of education in England.
*   **Churches:** Governed by various financing societies, with the Incorporated Church Building Society exercising the strongest control.
*   **Factories:** Planned and erected to meet separate acts in both France and England.
*   **Insurance Requirements:** Fire insurance companies establish their own requirements based on the building's size and the special trade for which it is intended, which in turn determines the rate of the premium.

In certain English districts where model by-laws are not in force, such as Letchworth, Herts, it is possible to construct buildings using sound materials without being restricted by these by-laws.

## General Definitions
Within the context of large buildings, a corridor is defined as a main passage on which various apartments open. While corridors in hospitals, workhouses, prisons, and public offices are typically characterized by severe simplicity, those in palaces and mansions may be adorned with works of art, leading to the term "picture gallery."
