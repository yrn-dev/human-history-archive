# BUCKINGHAM

Buckingham is a market town, municipal borough, and the county town of Buckinghamshire, England. It is situated 61 miles northwest of London and is accessible via a branch of the London & North-Western railway. The town is located in an open valley on the upper part of the river Ouse, which encircles the main portion of the town on three sides. As of 1901, the population of the town was 3,152.

## History and Governance
In pre-Conquest times, Buckingham served as an important stronghold. In 918, Edward the Elder encamped there with his army for four weeks and established two forts on either side of the water. During the Domesday survey, the town—along with the hamlet of Bourton—was assessed at one hide and contained twenty-six burgesses.

Although it appeared as a borough early on, Buckingham did not receive a charter until 1554. Queen Mary created it a free borough corporate, establishing a steward, twelve principal burgesses, and a bailiff. The boundaries were defined as extending from Chackmore bridge to Padbury Mill bridge in length, and from Dudley bridge to Thornborowe bridge in width. While Charles II issued a charter in 1684, it was quickly abandoned in favor of the original 1554 grant, which remained in force until the Municipal Corporations Act of 1835.

The borough is currently governed by a mayor, 12 councillors, and 4 aldermen. The total area of the borough is 5,006 acres.

## Parliamentary Representation
Buckingham is located within the Buckingham (Northern) parliamentary division. The town returned two members to parliament in 1529 and again from 1545 onwards. However, the Representation of the People Act of 1867 deprived the town of one member, and the Redistribution of Seats Act of 1885 removed the remaining member.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town contains several notable buildings:
*   **Church of St Peter and St Paul:** An 18th-century church that stands on the site of the old castle. It was extensively restored by Sir Gilbert Scott, who was a native of the neighborhood.
*   **Grammar School:** Founded by Edward VI, the school partially occupies older buildings featuring a Norman door and Decorated and Perpendicular windows. The site was previously occupied by a chantry founded in 1268 by Matthew Stratton, the archdeacon of Buckingham. The Norman elements may be remnants of a Holy Trinity gild chapel.
*   **Town Hall:** This structure dates from the end of the 18th century.
*   **Manor House:** A building dating from the early 17th century.

Adjacent to the town is the mansion of Stowe, which served as the seat of the dukes of Buckingham until the title became extinct in 1889. Stowe is reached from the town via a magnificent avenue of elms and is surrounded by beautifully laid out gardens.

## Economy and Trade
Buckingham is served by a branch of the Grand Junction Canal. Historically, the town was a significant agricultural center; Edward III established one of the staples for wool there. However, after these staples were moved to Calais, the trade declined to the point that Buckingham was listed as one of thirty-six impoverished towns in an act of 32 Henry VIII.

Early records mention fairs and markets. In 1522, Henry VIII granted the borough a Saturday market and two annual fairs to Sir Henry Marney, and subsequent sovereigns provided numerous additional grants of fairs. Current industries include agricultural trade, maltings, flour-mills, and the manufacture of artificial manure and condensed milk. A modified version of an old industry, the manufacture of pillow-lace, also survives.
