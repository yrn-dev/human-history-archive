# Gaiba

## Overview of Lake Gaiba and Puerto Quijarro
Lake Gaiba is the site of Puerto Quijarro, which is described as a projected port. Within its specific region, Puerto Quijarro is noted for being more accessible than any other port.

## Regional Connectivity and Bolivian Trade
Puerto Quijarro and Lake Gaiba are connected with the upper Paraguay. This network of connectivity includes other key locations such as Puerto Suarez, situated on Lake Caceres, and Puerto Pacheco on Bahia Negra. Puerto Suarez is located approximately 1,600 miles from Buenos Aires by river and currently handles the bulk of Bolivian trade moving in that direction.

## Bolivian Maritime and River Challenges
The importance of projected ports like Puerto Quijarro stems from Bolivia's difficult maritime situation. Following the loss of its maritime department, Bolivia has been left without ports other than those found on the Madeira and Paraguay rivers, their affluents, and Lake Titicaca (specifically Guaqui, or Huaqui, which conducts trade with Puno, a Peruvian port).

Because these existing outlets cannot be reached without transhipment in foreign territory, the cost of transport is increased. This situation allows neighboring nations to exclude Bolivia from direct commercial intercourse with other nations.

## Alternative Trade Routes and Obstacles
Bolivia's access to the Amazon is currently restricted to the Madeira river. This route is hindered by a series of falls before Bolivian territory is reached. The port of entry for this trade, Villa Bella, is located at the confluence of the Mamore and Beni rivers, above the Madeira falls. Reaching Villa Bella from the lower river requires a long and costly portage; furthermore, the port is shut off from navigable rivers further upstream by the falls of the Mamore and Beni.

To remedy these unfavorable conditions and promote the development of north-eastern Bolivia—an area featuring an admirable system of navigable rivers above the Beni and Mamore falls—Brazil intends to build a railway.

## Future Commercial Prospects
In addition to the projected developments at Lake Gaiba, there is the potential for further expansion in southern Bolivia. If the trade in this region becomes significant enough to justify the expense of opening a navigable channel in the Pilcomayo, it would enable direct river communication with Montevideo and Buenos Aires.
