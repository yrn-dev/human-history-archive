# Hamilton, Lord George, and Associated Figures

## Political Opposition to Tariff Reform
During the administration of Mr. Balfour, a significant political conflict emerged regarding the English free-trade system. While Mr. Balfour acknowledged the one-sided nature of the system and supported retaliation against "dumping" and unfair foreign competition, he remained hesitant to adopt a radical alteration of the established fiscal system. This hesitation was highlighted by the proposal of Mr. Chamberlain, who advocated for a general tariff and new taxes on food designed to provide preference to colonial products.

This programme faced strong opposition from "out-and-out Unionist free-traders." Within the cabinet, this opposition was led by Mr. Ritchie and Lord George Hamilton. Outside the cabinet, they were supported by Mr. Arthur Elliot, who served as secretary to the treasury, and Lord Hugh Cecil.

## Resignations and Fiscal Conflict
The tension over the fiscal question persisted through August, with the government awaiting a report from the Board of Trade regarding statistics on British trade. Mr. Balfour, anxious to avoid a rupture in his party and uncertain of public sentiment, professed to have "no settled convictions" on the matter.

The conflict reached a climax in September. On September 16, Mr. Balfour published a pamphlet titled "Insular Free Trade," in which he reviewed economic history since the time of Cobden and argued for the "freedom to negotiate" to increase freedom of exchange. Following this, on September 18, the "strait free-traders" resigned from the government. These individuals included Lord George Hamilton and Mr. Ritchie, followed shortly by Mr. Arthur Elliot and Lord Balfour of Burleigh. Simultaneously, Mr. Chamberlain also resigned.

The transition was marked by confusion and personal distrust. Lord George Hamilton stated that Mr. Balfour had circulated a second document among colleagues that was in fuller agreement with Mr. Chamberlain's views than the published pamphlet. Despite these resignations, the duke of Devonshire remained in the cabinet, which was viewed as a sign of typical orthodoxy.

## Other Individuals Named Hamilton
The archive contains records of several other individuals with the name Hamilton across different historical contexts:

*   **William Hamilton (1765–1814):** A descendant of John Hamilton (the paternal great-grandfather of the 2nd baron), he was declared by the House of Lords in 1799 to be entitled to the dignity of the 7th baron. He was succeeded by his son, Robert.
*   **Captain Alex. Hamilton:** Mentioned in accounts of the port of Bander Abbási, where he was present around 1721 during plundering raids by horsemen of the Mekranis and Biluchis.
*   **Captain A. Rowan Hamilton:** A resident of Killyleagh Castle, Down, and the father of Hariot, who married in 1862.
*   **Arthur Hamilton:** The subject of a work of fiction titled *Memoirs of Arthur Hamilton* (1886), written by Arthur Christopher Benson under the pseudonym "Christopher Carr."
*   **William (1635–1694):** Created earl of Selkirk in 1646, he became the 3rd duke of Hamilton following his 1656 marriage to Anne, duchess of Hamilton.
