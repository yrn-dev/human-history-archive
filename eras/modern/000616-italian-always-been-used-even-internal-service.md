# Italian Language and Administration in the Austrian Empire

## Administrative Use of Language
In the Austrian monarchy, the use of language in public offices, law courts, and schools became a political issue. Since the era of Joseph II, German served as the ordinary language of the government. This included the publication of all laws, the operations of the central public offices in Vienna, and the administration of the army and the courts. In most parts of the monarchy, including Galicia and Bohemia, German was the language of the "internal service," meaning all correspondence and books were maintained in German. Consequently, proficiency in speaking and writing German was a requirement for anyone seeking a share in the government.

An exception to this rule existed in the Italian districts. Italian was used for the internal service of government offices not only within Italy itself—specifically in Venetia and Lombardy—but also in Dalmatia, Istria, Trieste, and South Tirol. While officers were eventually required to know Serbo-Croatian and actual words of command were given in German, Italian remained the language of the Austrian navy.

## Military and Transport Logistics
The Italian army employed a different organizational system for the transport of guns compared to the British army. Rather than permanently assigning a motor or horse-haulage to every gun, the Italian army utilized a mechanical transport service that provided haulage for all units as required. This method allowed for considerable economies, as it avoided keeping large numbers of mechanics, drivers, and vehicles idle when units did not immediately need transport.

During military operations, such as those near the Isonzo, Italian artillery colonels supervised the employment of corps artillery. In urgent situations, preparations were made for tractors to tow multiple guns simultaneously to ensure the equipment could be moved or, if necessary, destroyed.
