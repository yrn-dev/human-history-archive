# The Campaign of 1743 and the Battle of Dettingen

The military operations of 1743 were characterized by significant instability for the emperor, beginning with a disastrous opening. The French and Bavarian forces suffered from poor cooperation, evidenced by quarrels between Seckendorf and Broglie. This lack of cohesion allowed for a converging march of opposing forces: Prince Lobkowitz moved from Bohemia toward the Naab, Khevenhueller advanced from Salzburg toward southern Bavaria, and Prince Charles's army marched along the Danube.

## The Advance of King George II
Following the withdrawal of Maillebois, an Anglo-allied army was formed on the lower Rhine. This force was commanded by King George II, who began advancing southward toward the Neckar and Main country. Simultaneously, the Bavarians experienced a severe reverse near Braunau on May 9, 1743. As Prince Charles captured the strong places of Bavaria one by one, the French and Bavarian forces were driven back almost to the Rhine.

## The Engagement at Dettingen
To counter the advance of the Anglo-allied army, a French force was assembled on the middle Rhine under the command of Marshal Noailles. King George II found himself completely outmanoeuvred by Noailles, his veteran antagonist. This placed the king in a position of extreme danger in the defile between the river Main and the Spessart Hills, specifically between Hanau and Aschaffenburg.

Marshal Noailles attempted to trap the allied forces by blocking the outlet and establishing posts around the perimeter. Despite this, the allied troops successfully forced their way through the blockade and inflicted heavy losses on the French. This engagement, the battle of Dettingen on June 27, is regarded as a notable victory for the British arms.

## Aftermath and Strategic Shifts
Following the battle, both Marshal Noailles and Broglie—the latter of whom was replaced by Marshal Coigny due to age and exhaustion—adopted a strict defensive posture behind the Rhine. This resulted in the complete removal of French soldiers from Germany. 

Prince Charles subsequently prepared to force a passage of the great river in the Breisgau. To assist this effort, King George II moved forward via Mainz to draw the attention of the two French marshals. While the Anglo-allied army succeeded in taking Worms, they were unable to cross the river after several attempts. Consequently, Prince Charles entered winter quarters, and the king followed suit, moving his troops northward to address the French army collecting on the frontier of Flanders.

## Related Figures in the Rhine Region
The Rhine has historically been a site of various strategic conflicts. In 1288, the battle of Worringen took place on the Rhine to decide supremacy between the Meuse and the Rhine. Duke John I of Brabant led an army of knights and communal infantry from cities including Brussels, Antwerp, Louvain, Nivelles, Jodoigne, and Tirlemont to a complete victory over a coalition that included the Count of Luxemburg and the Archbishop of Cologne.

In later centuries, the region remained a focal point of French military activity. Turenne conducted a Rhine campaign involving an invasion of Germany to prevent interference with Louis in Franche-Comte, crossing the Rhine at Philipsburg in June and achieving victory at Sinsheim. Additionally, Crequi drove the duke of Lorraine back to the Rhine and forced an imperial army under the duke of Saxe-Eisenach to make terms after they were shut up in an island of the Rhine.
