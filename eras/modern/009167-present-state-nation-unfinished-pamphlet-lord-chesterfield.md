# Present State of the Nation

The *Present State of the Nation* is an unfinished pamphlet written around 1749 by Bolingbroke.

## Authorship and Context
The pamphlet was composed by Bolingbroke during the later years of his life. Bolingbroke had a complex political career, having retired to France in June after becoming baffled and disappointed in the political fray. During his time in France, he resided primarily at the chateau of Argeville near Fontainebleau, where he authored works such as the *True Use of Retirement* and *Letters on the Study of History*.

Bolingbroke maintained a periodic connection to England, visiting in 1738, 1742, and 1743. In 1738, he served as an adviser and friend to Frederick, prince of Wales, who led the opposition at the time. For this purpose, Bolingbroke wrote *The Patriot King*, as well as *The Spirit of Patriotism* and *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I*. These works were initially entrusted to Pope and remained unpublished until 1749, when Bolingbroke published a correct version after discovering that Pope had secretly printed 1,500 copies.

By 1744, Bolingbroke had settled finally at Battersea with Hugh Hume, the 3rd earl of Marchmont. During this period, he was heavily involved in negotiations to establish the new "broad bottom" administration and recommended the tutor for Prince George (the future George III). It was in this general era, approximately 1749, that he wrote the unfinished *Present State of the Nation*.

## Connection to Lord Chesterfield
Lord Chesterfield is noted in the archive as the person who recorded the final words spoken by Bolingbroke: "God who placed me here will do what He pleases with me hereafter and He knows best what to do." 

Lord Chesterfield himself was a prominent figure of the mid-18th century, recognized as a writer of European knowledge and aristocratic experience. Along with Horace Walpole, Chesterfield was influenced by the best French styles of the period. He is characterized in polite English literature as the first fine gentleman and epicurean, known for his focus on social amenity, tact, and *savoir faire*. His own literary contributions include a series of *Letters* to his son and godson, which introduced Gallic irony, wit, and a Ciceronian polish to English prose.

## Death of the Author
Bolingbroke died on December 12, 1751. He was predeceased by his wife in 1750. Both were buried in the parish church at Battersea. A monument was erected to their memory featuring inscriptions and medallions composed by Bolingbroke himself.
