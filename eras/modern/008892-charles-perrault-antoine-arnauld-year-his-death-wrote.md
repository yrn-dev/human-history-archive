# Charles Perrault

Charles Perrault (1628–1703) was a figure associated with literary criticism and the application of Cartesianism to the study of modern literature.

## Literary Criticism and Cartesianism
Perrault is noted for applying the Cartesian method to the field of literary criticism. Along with Jean Terrasson and Fontenelle, he utilized this approach to develop a worthier estimate of modern literature. This application of Cartesianism occurred during a period when the method was also being applied to other disciplines; for instance, Pierre Nicole and Antoine Arnauld applied it to logic and grammar, while Henri François Daugesseau and Jean Domat applied it to jurisprudence.

## Conflict with Boileau
Perrault was involved in a literary dispute with the poet Boileau. Boileau, who was appointed historiographer to the king in 1677, wrote a series of satires that earned him many enemies. Specifically, Boileau's 10th satire, which focused on women (*Sur les femmes*), prompted Charles Perrault to write a response titled *Apologie des femmes*.

This conflict extended to other contemporaries. Antoine Arnauld wrote a letter in defense of Boileau in the year of his death. However, when Arnauld submitted this reply to the bishop Bossuet at the request of friends, Bossuet declared that the 10th satire was subversive of morality and that all satire was incompatible with the spirit of Christianity. Furthermore, friends of Arnauld argued that writing about a subject as trivial as poetry was inconsistent with the dignity of a churchman.
