# The War of the Fronde and the Reign of Louis XIV

The War of the Fronde was a period of domestic trouble and civil war in France that occurred during the early years of the reign of Louis XIV. It was characterized by a struggle between the French crown and oppositions formed by landlords and merchants who created leagues of resistance. This conflict took place while Louis XIV was young and while Charles I was fighting for his life in England.

## Political Context and Leadership
Louis XIV became king on May 14, 1643, following the death of his father, Louis XIII. However, he did not begin to rule personally for sixteen years. During this interval, power was held by the queen-mother, Anne of Austria, and her minister, Cardinal Mazarin. Mazarin sought to maintain the integrity of French territory and the power of the throne, but he faced significant challenges. He was hated by many as a foreigner, and the royal authority was further weakened by the king's childhood.

The Fronde represented an effort by the aristocracy and other interests to oppose the crown and Mazarin. Despite these disadvantages, Mazarin triumphed over both his foreign and domestic opponents. The conflict ended by 1653, resulting in a complete victory for the French crown.

## Military Actions and Royal Humiliations
The instability of the period forced the royal court to flee from Paris on two separate occasions. In one instance, the populace was admitted to see the king in his bed following a rumor of an intended flight. These humiliations contributed to the development of Louis XIV's later autocratic ideas.

Military operations to maintain royal authority extended to various regions of France. In Bordeaux, which served as a chief support for the Fronde for a time, troops were dispatched in 1653 to repress insurrections against royal measures. Additionally, the town of Louviers experienced troubles during the wars of the Fronde.

## Key Figures and Alliances
The conflict involved various members of the nobility and foreign entities. Gaston of Orleans, the brother of Louis XIV, survived the king's father and was involved during the wars of the Fronde, though he is noted as having given unexpectedly little trouble. Charles, the duke of Lorraine, also took part in the wars of the Fronde after being restored to his estates in 1644.

The resolution of the Fronde had a lasting impact on the French social structure. After 1652, the court entirely dominated the aristocracy. The great French nobles were effectively tamed, remaining prominent only as court officials and servants. This shift in power allowed the monarchy to flourish, though it resulted in the burden of taxation being shifted onto the common people, as the nobility and clergy remained exempt from many taxes.

## Aftermath and Transition to Rule
The end of the Fronde coincided with other major diplomatic and military successes for France. The peace of Westphalia in 1648 and the peace of the Pyrenees in 1659 marked the success of French diplomacy and arms. By the time the Fronde concluded, Louis XIV was twenty-one years of age and anxious to rule. 

The peace of the Pyrenees was particularly decisive for Louis XIV's personal history, as it stipulated his marriage to his cousin, the Infanta Maria Theresa, cementing a new friendship with Spain.
