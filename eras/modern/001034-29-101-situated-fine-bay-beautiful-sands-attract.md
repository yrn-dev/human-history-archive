# Ayr

Ayr is a seaport, county town of Ayrshire, Scotland, and a royal, municipal, and police burgh. It is located at the mouth of the river Ayr, situated 41-1/2 miles south-south-west of Glasgow. The town is accessible via the Glasgow & South-Western railway.

## Geography and Tourism
The town is situated on a fine bay, where the beautiful sands attract thousands of visitors during the summer. Ayr proper is located on the south bank of the river. The town has experienced significant expansion on the southern side of the stream, particularly in the direction of the racecourse, where numerous fine villas are now located.

## Infrastructure and Landmarks
The river Ayr is crossed by a railway viaduct and three bridges. These include the Victoria Bridge, which was erected in 1898, and the "Twa Brigs" of Burns. The Auld Brig is said to date from the reign of Alexander III (who died in 1286). The New Brig was built in 1788, largely due to the efforts of Provost Ballantyne. Following a prophecy put into the mouth of the older structure by Burns, the newer bridge yielded to floods in 1877 and was rebuilt in 1879. The older bridge was eventually closed in 1904 for public safety.

The town features several notable buildings:
* **County Buildings:** Designed after the temple of Isis in Rome, these buildings house various local authorities as well as provincial and circuit courts.
* **Town Buildings:** These include reading and assembly rooms and are topped by a fine spire reaching 226 feet in height.
* **Wallace Tower:** A Gothic structure on High Street, built on the site of an older building of the same name that was removed in 1835. The clock and bells of the Dungeon steeple were transferred to this tower. A statue of the Scottish hero, created by the self-taught sculptor James Thorn (1802-1850), is located in a niche in front.

## Education and Culture
The town's most notable school is the Academy, which was rebuilt in 1880. In 1764, the Academy superseded the burgh's grammar school, which had existed since the 13th century. For public reference, the Carnegie free library was established in 1893. 

Ayr is home to several statues, including those of General Smith Neill, the 13th earl of Eglinton, Sir William Wallace, and Burns. In terms of local media, the *Ayr Advertiser* began publication on August 5, 1803, making it the earliest newspaper published in Ayrshire.

## Public Institutions
The town provides various charitable and medical services, including:
* A district asylum and county hospital.
* St John's refuge.
* A home for the deaf and dumb.
* Industrial schools for girls and boys.
* The Kyle combination poor-house.

## Demographics
The population of Ayr was recorded as 24,944 in 1891 and grew to 29,101 by 1901.
