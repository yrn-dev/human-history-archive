# 1882 and 1890 in Academic and Literary History

The years 1882 and 1890 mark significant milestones in the publication of scholarly works, the advancement of academic careers, and the production of literary and artistic contributions across various disciplines, including ethnology, education, poetry, and history.

## Contributions to Ethnology and Archaeology
Daniel Garrison Brinton (1837–1899), an American ethnologist and archaeologist, was active during this period. In 1882, he published *American Hero Myths*. By 1890, he had released both *Essays of an Americanist* and *Races and Peoples*. Brinton's work in *Essays of an Americanist* includes scholarly views on the figure "Michabo"; he argued that the term should be translated as "the Great White One," representing the light of the dawn, rather than "Great Hare," despite the beliefs of the Algonkins and the theriomorphic nature of the myth.

Additionally, Brinton edited the *Library of American Aboriginal Literature*, an eight-volume set published between 1882 and 1890, which served as a contribution to American anthropology. Brinton held several prestigious positions during his career, including professor of ethnology and archaeology at the Academy of Natural Sciences in Philadelphia (starting in 1884) and professor of American linguistics and archaeology at the University of Pennsylvania from 1886 until his death in 1899.

## Developments in Education and History
In the field of education, Nicholas Murray Butler (born 1862) graduated from Columbia College in 1882. Following his graduation, he served as a graduate fellow in philosophy from 1882 to 1884. By 1890, Butler had progressed through several academic ranks at Columbia, serving as an adjunct professor of ethics, psychology, and philosophy before becoming a full professor and the dean of the faculty of philosophy in 1890.

In historical writing, Hubert Howe Bancroft (1832–) published his extensive *History of the Pacific States of North America* between 1882 and 1890, a work spanning 21 volumes. Bancroft, who had previously been a bookseller in San Francisco, utilized a corps of assistants to compile this material, which is described as an encyclopaedia of data. In 1890, he also published *Literary Industries*, which provides an account of his working methods.

## Literary and Poetic Works
The English poet Robert Bridges (born 1844) retired as a physician in 1882. His literary output was substantial around 1890, a year in which he published several dramas and poems, including *Achilles in Scyros*, *Palicio* (a romantic drama in the Elizabethan manner), *The Return of Ulysses* (a five-act drama), *The Christian Captives* (a tragedy), and his *Shorter Poems*. Bridges' work is noted for its precision, restraint, and a theory of prosody based on the number of stresses in a line rather than syllables.

*   **Religious and Biographical Works:** In 1882, a biography of George Ripley was published as part of the "American Men of Letters" series. In 1890, the work *Boston Unitarianism, 1820-1850* was published, which served as a biography of the author's father.
*   **Translations and Editions:** The year 1882 saw the publication of the Gitlbauer edition of Babrius. By 1890, translations of Babrius were available in French by Leveque, and editions were published by Feuillet and Desrousseaux.

## Art and Regional Studies
The year 1890 saw the release of several works related to art and regional history. In the realm of art, *Society Pictures from Punch* and *A Legend of Camelot* were published in 1890, alongside *Social Pictorial Satire*. Additionally, the Barbizon School of Painters was documented in 1890 through works by D. Croal Thomson and J. W. Mollett.

Regarding regional studies of Basel, the *Basler Chroniken* (original chronicles) were published in five volumes between 1722 and 1890. Other 1890 publications include L. A. Stocker's *Basler Stadtbilder* and the city's *Urkundenbuch*.
