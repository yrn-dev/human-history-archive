# Arctic Circle and Northern Geographic Extremities

The Arctic circle encompasses several significant landmasses and geographic features across the northern hemisphere, including large portions of Asia and the continental island of Greenland. The region is characterized by specific climatic conditions and biological distributions that vary between the surface and the deep ocean.

## Northern Asia and its Boundaries
The northern boundary of Asia is defined by the Arctic Ocean. The coastline of this region generally falls between 70 deg. and 75 deg. N., placing it within the Arctic circle. The extreme northern point of the Asian continent is located at Cape Chelyuskin (also known as Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi, meaning north-east), situated at 78 deg. N.

The broader outline of Asia can be visualized as a great isosceles spherical triangle. Its north-eastern apex is East Cape (Vostochnyi or Dezhnev) at the entrance of the Bering Strait (190 deg. E.). From this apex, two equal sides of approximately 6500 m. extend to the extremity of the Malay peninsula in the east and the southern point of Arabia in the west. A great circle drawn from East Cape to the southern point of Arabia follows the coastline of the Arctic Ocean and passes over the Ural Mountains and the western Caspian.

## Greenland
Greenland is a large continental island of which the greater portion lies within the Arctic circle. The island is arctic in character and is separated from the American continent's archipelago (specifically Ellesmere Land) by a narrow strait in the extreme north. 

The north coast of Greenland fronts the polar sea and reaches its highest point at approximately 83 deg. 40' N. In contrast, its most southerly point is Cape Farewell, located at 59 deg. 45' N. The island's extreme length is about 1650 m., with a maximum breadth of approximately 800 m. occurring at 77 deg. 30' N. Greenland is a Danish colony, with the Danish crown holding the west coast and the southern east coast.

## Flora and Fauna of the Arctic Regions
The biological life within and around the Arctic circle varies by species and habitat:

**Terrestrial Life**
In Siberia, the range of the aspen tree extends to the Arctic circle. Similarly, white hares are found in Siberia, Russia, and other regions within the Arctic circle. The fur of these hares is used in the trade of the British colonies, America, and Europe, particularly for making stoles and muffs.

**Aquatic Life**
For freshwater fishes, there is no separate arctic zone; life becomes extinct near the pole where water remains frozen for most of the year. However, some fish types from the temperate south extend into high latitudes, with specimens of char (*Salmo naresii* and *Salmo arcturus*) having been obtained as far north as 82° N. lat.

In the deep sea, conditions are nearly identical regardless of latitude. Deep-sea fishes, which are not influenced by surface temperature or light, can inhabit abyssal depths near the arctic circle as well as under the equator. Because of these identical tellurian conditions, the same species may be found in both regions, and no separate horizontal regions can be distinguished in abyssal fauna.
