# Adoniram Judson and Luther Rice

## Early Missionary Efforts and Conversion
At the beginning of the 19th century, Baptist communities in Philadelphia, Charleston, Boston, and other areas expressed a strong interest in the missionary work conducted in India by the English missionary William Carey and his coadjutors, providing liberal financial support for these efforts. In 1812, the newly-formed American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions sent several individuals, including Adoniram Judson and Luther Rice, to open missionary work in India.

A significant turning point in American Baptist history occurred when Judson and Rice converted to Baptist views. Following this conversion, Judson appealed to his American brethren to provide support for missionary work among the heathen. Luther Rice returned to the United States to organize missionary societies and generate interest in the mission led by Judson.

## Organization of Baptist Missionary Societies
The efforts of Rice led to the formation of "The Baptist Society for the Propagation of the Gospel in India and other Foreign Parts" in Boston in January 1813. This was quickly followed by the establishment of similar societies across the Southern, Middle, and Eastern states.

As the need for a national organization became apparent, thirty-three delegates from eleven states met in Philadelphia in May 1814. They organized the "General Missionary Convention of the Baptist Denomination in the United States of America for Foreign Missions." Because the convention's meetings were scheduled every three years, it became known as the "Triennial Convention." The organization appointed a Board of Commissioners, which was headquartered in Philadelphia until 1826, when it was transferred to Boston.

## Educational and Home Mission Expansion
The Triennial Convention recognized a profound need for a larger supply of educated ministers for both mission and home work. This led to the 1821 establishment of the theological department of Columbian College in Washington (now George Washington University), which was intended to serve as a national Baptist institution.

Beginning in 1817, the Triennial Convention engaged extensively in home mission work due to destitution on the frontiers. This effort resulted in the constitution of the American Baptist Home Mission Society in 1832. To further these educational and missionary enterprises, Rice worked to establish an organ for the dissemination of information and the quickening of interest.
