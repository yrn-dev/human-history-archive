# Baku

Baku is the chief town of the government of the same name, located in Russian Transcaucasia. It is situated at 40deg 21' N. and 49deg 50' E. on the south side of the Apsheron peninsula.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The town is divided into two primary areas. The old town, located to the west, is characterized by a decidedly oriental appearance with steep, narrow, and winding streets. The new town climbs the slopes behind a long stone quay that sits next to the harbour.

Baku is connected to the south Russian railway system at Beslan. From this junction, it connects to Vladikavkaz (400 m.), and via Tiflis, it reaches Poti (536 m.) and Batum (560 m.) on the Black Sea, as well as Derbent and Petrovsk. The harbour is excellent and serves as a chief depot for Russian manufactured goods and merchandise arriving from Transcaspia and Persia, including timber, dried fruit, fish, wine, rice, silk, and raw cotton. The harbour area also contains barracks, stores, workshops, dry docks, and engineering works belonging to the Russian Caspian fleet.

## Climate
The climate of Baku is described as extreme. The mean temperature for the year is 58deg F., with a mean of 38deg in January and 80deg in July. The annual rainfall is 9.4 in. During the winter, a wind of exceptional violence sometimes blows from the N.N.W.

## History and Architecture
The town was mentioned in the 10th century by the Arab geographer Masudi. It came into the possession of the Persians in 1509. The Russians captured the town from the Persians in 1723, though it was restored to them in 1735, before eventually being incorporated into the Russian empire in 1806.

The town's architecture reflects its diverse history:
* **Arabic Influence:** Remains of ancient walls projecting above the sea show traces of Arabic architecture from the 9th and 10th centuries.
* **Persian Influence:** The mosques of the Persian shahs, built in 1078, have been converted into an arsenal.
* **Native Influence:** The ruins of a palace built in the 16th century by native khans are located in the old town.
* **Other Landmarks:** The "maidens' tower" has been transformed into a lighthouse.

## Economy and Industry
Baku possesses several industrial facilities, including tobacco factories, sulphuric acid works, flour-mills, and oil-works for fuel, in addition to petroleum refineries.

## Population and Civil Unrest
The population of Baku grew significantly between the mid-19th and early 20th centuries, recorded at 13,381 in 1860, 112,253 in 1897, and 179,133 in 1900. Between 1904 and 1905, general political anarchy led to serious conflicts between the Armenians and the Tatars. These conflicts resulted in the burning of two-thirds of the Bibi-Eybat and Balakhani oil-works.
