# Protestant Episcopal Church

The Protestant Episcopal Church is a religious institution with various representatives in the ministry and educational spheres, and it maintains a presence in several American cities.

## Clergy and Notable Members

Individuals associated with the Protestant Episcopal Church have held diverse roles in academia and the ministry. Frederick Augustus Porter Barnard (1809–1889), an American scientist and educationalist, was ordained as a deacon in the church in 1854. In that same year, he became a professor of mathematics and natural philosophy at the University of Mississippi, where he later served as chancellor from 1856 until the start of the Civil War. Barnard subsequently became the tenth president of Columbia College in New York City, a position he held until the year before his death in 1889.

John James McCook (b. 1843) entered the Protestant Episcopal ministry in 1867. After graduating from Trinity College in Hartford, Connecticut, in 1863 and the Berkeley divinity school in 1866, he served as the rector of St John's in East Hartford, Connecticut, beginning in 1869. In 1883, McCook became a professor of modern languages at Trinity College. He also served as the president of the board of directors of the Connecticut reformatory from 1895 to 1897 and authored works on kindred topics and prison reform.

Charles Augustus Briggs (1841– ), an American theologian and Hebrew scholar, was ordained as a priest of the Protestant Episcopal Church in 1899. Prior to this, Briggs had been a pastor of the Presbyterian church of Roselle, New Jersey, and a professor at the Union Theological Seminary. His transition to the Episcopal Church followed a period of controversy in which he was tried for heresy by the presbytery of New York in 1892 and subsequently suspended by the general assembly in 1893.

## Institutional Presence and Recognition

The church maintains significant architectural presences in certain urban centers. In Buffalo, the city is noted for several handsome churches, which include Trinity and the St Paul's Protestant Episcopal cathedral.

The American Episcopal Church has also engaged in the formal recognition of ecclesiastical successions. Specifically, the convention of the American Episcopal Church formally recognized the integrity of the succession of the Swedish episcopate. This succession was preserved through Peter Magnusson, who was elected bishop of the see of Westeraes and consecrated around 1524. Magnusson later consecrated bishops to fill vacant sees in 1528 and assisted in the consecration of the Reformer Lawrence Peterson as archbishop of Upsala on September 22, 1531. The validity of this succession was accepted following investigations by scholars such as Routh and Grabe.
