# The Downfall of Prempeh

## Overview of the Work
*The Downfall of Prempeh* is a historical account written by General R.S.S. Baden-Powell. Published in London in 1896, the text provides a detailed record of the British military expedition conducted between 1895 and 1896. It serves as a primary narrative source for the events leading to the deposition of the Ashanti king and the subsequent establishment of a British protectorate.

## Political Context of Ashanti
The expedition described by Baden-Powell followed a period of significant political instability within Ashanti. Following the war of 1873-74, several states dependent on Ashanti sought British protection, though this was refused. This inaction by the colonial office allowed Ashanti power to reconsolidate. 

The Ashanti leadership underwent several transitions during the late 19th century. After the deposition of Kofi Karikari, his brother Mensa was placed on the golden stool, the symbol of sovereignty. Mensa's reign was characterized as tyrannous, marked by repeated human sacrifices and a failure to adhere to the Fomana treaty, including the failure to pay the stipulated indemnity of 50,000 oz. of gold (only 4,000 oz. were paid). Following a revolution in 1883, Mensa was succeeded by Kwaka Dua II, who died in June 1884.

## The Reign of Prince Prempeh
After a desolating civil war and the death of the ex-king Kofi Karikari, Prince Prempeh was chosen as king on March 26, 1888, taking the name Kwaka Dua III. His election occurred with the sanction and presence of an officer from the Gold Coast government, as the colonial government had been forced to intervene due to troubles in the Gold Coast. 

Under Prempeh's rule, Ashanti initially experienced a period of peace and prosperity. However, tensions resurfaced in 1893 between Ashanti and the tribes of the protectorate. In response, Prempeh ordered the closure of roads to traders.

## Conflict with the British Government
The British government intervened in Ashanti affairs, partly because the region had been included in the British sphere of influence by international agreement. A British mission was dispatched to Prempeh with several demands:
* The fulfillment of the terms of the 1874 treaty.
* The acceptance of a British protectorate.
* The acceptance of a British resident at Kumasi.

Prempeh refused to treat with the governor of the Gold Coast and instead sent informal agents to England; however, the secretary of state refused to receive these agents. Prempeh did not reply to the demands of the British mission within the three-week grace period, which expired on October 31, 1895.

## The 1895-96 Expedition
The failure of diplomatic negotiations led to the military expedition detailed in General Baden-Powell's account. The result of this campaign was the deposition of Prempeh and the formal establishment of a British protectorate over the region.
