# Global Low-Temperature Regions

The distribution of the lowest temperatures on the globe is characterized by extreme cold in northern Asia and the northern hemisphere's polar regions, with significant variations based on latitude, elevation, and geography.

## Northern Asia and the American Continent
Some of the places where temperatures fall lowest are located in northern Asia. On the north coast of eastern Siberia, the mean temperature is only a few degrees above 0° Fahrenheit. However, the lowest mean temperature observed anywhere is approximately 4° Fahr., recorded at Melville Island, north of the American continent. 

In northern Asia, the isothermals of mean annual temperature follow regular curves. In these regions, a reduction of 1 degree of latitude generally corresponds to a rise of 1/2 degree Fahr. in temperature, up until approximately 30 degrees N, where the mean temperature is about 75° Fahr.

## Greenland and the Northern Hemisphere
North Greenland is identified as a location where the lowest temperatures of the northern hemisphere may be found. The interior of Greenland contains a "pole of cold" during both winter and summer, which is situated at the opposite longitude of Siberia and is comparable in its extreme severity. 

The climate of the Greenland interior is continental, featuring large temperature ranges and an almost permanent anti-cyclonic region over the inland ice. These low temperatures are attributed to the radiation of heat from the snow-surface into the rarefied air of the interior. On Nansen's expedition, nighttime temperatures in early September were experienced at about -49° Fahr. It is estimated that the minimum temperature during winter in the interior of the inland ice may sink to -90° Fahr.

Other temperature data for Greenland include:
* **64th Parallel:** At an elevation of 6,560 ft., the mean annual temperature is supposed to be -13° Fahr. (reduced to sea-level, this is 5° Fahr.).
* **Further North Interior:** The mean annual temperature is supposed to be -10° Fahr. when reduced to sea-level.
* **Monthly Variations (64th Parallel):** Reduced to sea-level, the mean temperature of the warmest month (July) is 32° Fahr., while the coldest month (January) is about -22° Fahr. In North Greenland, the January mean is probably -40° reduced to sea-level.

## Regional Temperature Minimums
Various other regions exhibit significant low-temperature extremes:

**Idaho, United States**
The state of Idaho contains several climate zones. The coldest region is found in the Lost river regions and the Camas prairie in the Upper Snake basin, where the lowest temperature recorded in 1902 was -35° F. In contrast, the region north of the Clearwater river recorded a minimum of -16° F., and the valley of the Boisé, Payette, and Weiser rivers (along with the Snake river basin between Lewiston and Twin Falls) recorded a minimum of -13° F.

**British Columbia**
In the southern interior district of British Columbia, the station at Kamloops recorded an absolute lowest temperature of -27° F. Other stations in the region recorded lower absolute minimums: Agassiz at -13° F., Port Simpson at -10° F., and Victoria at -1° F.

**The British Shan States**
In the region of Keng Tung, temperatures during cold weather appear to reach a lowest experienced level of 40° F., or a few degrees more or less.

**Bolivia**
In the tropics, extreme cold is associated with elevation. The *puna brava* zone extends from 12,500 ft. up to the snow limit at approximately 17,500 ft. Above this limit lies the region of eternal snow, described as an Arctic zone within the tropics.
