# Austria-Hungary and Macedonian Reforms

## Diplomatic Agreements with Russia
At the beginning of the 20th century, Austria-Hungary sought to manage its interests in the Balkan Peninsula through diplomatic coordination with Russia. In 1903, the two powers reached a temporary agreement known as the "February Programme," which was later supplemented in October of that same year by the "Muerzsteg Programme." These terms were upheld by Count Goluchowski throughout his tenure in office, maintaining the agreement even after Russian prestige suffered during the war with Japan.

## The Policy of Baron von Aerenthal
The diplomatic direction of the Habsburg monarchy shifted following the retirement of Count Goluchowski in October 1906. His successor, Baron von Aerenthal, sought to utilize the evolving European political climate to reinstate the traditional policies of the monarchy within the Balkan Peninsula. Baron von Aerenthal gradually moved away from the foundations established by the Muerzsteg Programme.

By January 1908, Baron von Aerenthal took deliberate steps to undermine the existing Austro-Russian agreement. He secured a concession from the sultan for the construction of a railway that would run from the Bosnian frontier, through the sanjak of Novibazar, to the Turkish terminus at Mitrovitza. This action was taken despite the expressed wishes of Russia.

## Consequences of the Railway Concession
The railway project had significant geopolitical repercussions. It caused resentment in Servia, which viewed the scheme as another link in a chain forged by the Habsburg empire. This resentment was compounded by Servia's economic dependence on the Dual Monarchy, a situation emphasized by a war of tariffs that had begun in 1906.

Furthermore, the concession offended several of the great powers. These nations interpreted the railway agreement as a sign that Austria-Hungary was abandoning its interest in Macedonian reforms.

## Relations with Germany
Baron von Aerenthal's ability to pursue such a rash policy was supported by the close relationship between Austria-Hungary and Germany. The intimacy between the two powers had been demonstrated during the dispute between France and Germany regarding Morocco. During the Algeciras Conference on March 3, 1906, Austria-Hungary was the only power to side with Germany. Additionally, the final settlement between Germany and France was based on a proposal made by the Austro-Hungarian plenipotentiary. These cordial relations emboldened Baron von Aerenthal to pursue an even bolder policy by the autumn of 1908, coinciding with the revolution in Turkey.
