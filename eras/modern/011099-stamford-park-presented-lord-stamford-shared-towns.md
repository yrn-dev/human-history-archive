# Stamford Park

## Overview and Location
Stamford Park is a public space presented by Lord Stamford. It is shared by the towns of Stalybridge and Ashton. The park's geography is notable for extending across the river Tame into the county of Cheshire.

## Ashton-under-Lyne
Ashton-under-Lyne is a municipal and parliamentary borough and market-town located in Lancashire, England. It is situated on the river Tame, which is a tributary of the Mersey. The town is located 6-1/2 miles east of Manchester and 185 miles north-west by north from London. As of 1901, the population of the town was 43,890, having been 40,486 in 1891. The total area of the town is 1,346 acres.

The town is served by three railway lines: the Lancashire & Yorkshire (via Charlestown station), the London & North-Western, and the Great Central (via Park Parade station).

## Local Governance and Infrastructure
The parliamentary borough of Ashton-under-Lyne returns one member and extends into Cheshire. The local corporation is composed of a mayor, 24 councillors, and 8 aldermen. 

The town maintains several public institutions, including a free library, a school of art, a technical school, and various hospitals. Notable landmarks include the church of St Michael, which is Perpendicular in style though almost entirely rebuilt, and barracks located in the vicinity. The Old Hall, which served as the manor house for the Asshetons, still exists in an altered state and is joined by an ancient prison. The area known as Gallows Meadow serves as a reminder of the summary justice once carried out by the lords of the manor. While many modern residences have been built, a few picturesque old houses remain in the vicinity of the town.

## Economy and Industry
The primary industries in Ashton-under-Lyne include:
* Iron-founding and machinery works
* Hat-making
* Cotton-spinning

Additionally, there are large collieries located in the surrounding neighbourhood.
