# Inspirational Writing and Automatic Writing

Inspirational writing, particularly in the context of modern spiritualism, refers to texts produced through processes where the agent lacks deliberate volition. This phenomenon is closely associated with automatic writing, a term used by students of psychical research to describe writing performed without the conscious will of the person producing it.

## Automatic Writing and Spiritualism
Automatic writing may occur while the agent is in a state of normal wakefulness, or during induced or spontaneous trances, as well as in other morbid states such as hystero-epilepsy. While the agent may be aware of the specific words being written—sometimes perceiving two or three words on either side of the current word—there is rarely a clear perception of the overall meaning of the text.

This phenomenon played a significant role in the history of modern spiritualism, appearing on a large scale in America between 1850 and 1860. Many of these early reports involved lengthy writings purported to be produced under the guidance of spirits, some of which were written in "unknown tongues." Notable published works from this period include *The Healing of the Nations* by Charles Linton, *Messages from the Spirit Life* by J. Murray Spear, and *Great Harmonia* by Andrew J. Davis.

In England, early spiritualist newspapers were filled with "inspirational" writing, including the *Pages of Ike Paraclete*. One of the most notable English series of automatic writings is the *Spirit Teachings* of the Rev. W. Stainton Moses. To facilitate the writing process during the earlier years of the movement, a "planchette"—a small, heart-shaped board on wheels—was often employed.

## Psychological and Experimental Perspectives
While the theory that external inspiration causes automatic writing has been generally discredited in recent years, the practice has been used to experimentally investigate subconscious mental processes. This method has revealed knowledge that had lapsed from primary consciousness, such as foreign languages or forgotten fragments of poetry.

Edmund Gurney developed an experimental parallel to this reproduction of forgotten knowledge. Gurney demonstrated that information given to a subject during a hypnotic trance could later be reproduced through handwriting, even while the subject's attention was fully occupied by reading aloud or conversing. Similarly, arithmetical problems set during a trance could be solved through handwriting without the subject's apparent consciousness.

## Prophetic Inspiration and Revelation
In a broader historical and religious context, the concept of inspiration is linked to the transmission of divine law and the existence of a "God of nature." According to some accounts, a chain of inspiration extended from the fall of Adam to the promulgation of the Koran. In this tradition, prophetic light was imparted to 124,000 of the elect, based on their grace and virtue. 

Within this framework, 104 volumes were dictated by the Holy Spirit, and 313 apostles were commissioned to recall their countries from vice and idolatry. Six legislators of "transcendent brightness" announced six successive revelations of various rites, though they represented one immutable religion. These figures include Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Christ, and Mahomet, whose authority rises in gradation.

The remnants of these inspired writings were found in the New and Old Testaments. While the writings of the patriarchs existed only in apocryphal copies from the Syrians and Greeks, the Koran consecrated and embellished the miraculous story of Moses. Furthermore, the prophet taught that a high and mysterious reverence should be held for Christ Jesus, the son of Mary, who is described as the apostle of God.
