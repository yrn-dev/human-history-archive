# Allotment of Constituencies and Racial Representation

The allotment of constituencies based on racial or ethnic identity has been utilized in various historical contexts to manage political representation, protect minorities, or organize regional governance. These systems often involve the division of territory or voting rights into specific "compartments" to ensure that different groups maintain a level of autonomy or guaranteed presence within a governing body.

## Austro-Hungarian Franchise Reform (1905–1907)

In the early 20th century, Austria underwent a significant transition in its electoral system. Following a large socialist and working-class demonstration in Budapest on September 15, 1905, in favor of universal suffrage, the Austrian Socialist party pressed for similar reforms in Austria. This movement led to demonstrations in Vienna, Prague, and Graz, some of which resulted in collisions with police and bloodshed.

By November 28, 1905, Premier Baron Gautsch changed his previous stance against universal suffrage and permitted a massive, orderly Socialist procession along the Vienna Ringstrasse. Consequently, Gautsch promised a franchise reform by February 1906 designed to protect racial minorities from being overwhelmed by majorities of other races.

### The Gautsch and Beck Reforms
On February 23, 1906, Baron Gautsch introduced measures to abolish the *curia* (electoral class system) and establish universal suffrage for every male citizen over 24 years of age with a one-year residential qualification. The proposed "racial compartments" were intended to ensure each race was assured against molestation from others. The Gautsch bill suggested increasing constituencies from 425 to 455, allotting them to provinces and races based on population numbers and tax-paying capacity.

Following the fall of Baron Gautsch in April and the subsequent resignation of Prince Konrad zu Hohenlohe-Schillingsfuerst, Baron von Beck formed a new cabinet on June 2, 1906. With the support of the emperor, Baron Beck secured the adoption of the reform on December 1, 1906, raising the total number of constituencies to 516.

### Impact of Racial Allotment
Under this system, tax-paying capacity was a primary consideration in the allotment of constituencies. In mixed districts, separate registers and constituencies were established. Electors were required to vote on their own racial register for a candidate of their own race; for example, Germans voted for Germans and Czechs for Czechs. This prevented victories of one race over another (such as Czechs over Germans or Poles over Ruthenes), though political victories between factions of the same race (such as Clerical over Liberal Germans) remained possible.

The resulting distribution of seats formally ended the fiction that Austria was predominantly a German country. While Germans were allotted a proportion of seats that was unduly favorable, they remained a permanent minority with 233 seats, compared to 259 seats for the Slavs. The "German-Latin block" (including Romanian and Italian seats) totaled 257, though this block ceased to exist in practice as Italians began cooperating with Slavs. The Ruthenes were the greatest gainers, seeing their representation trebled, although it remained disproportionate to their actual numbers. To prevent further revisions that might disadvantage them, German parties stipulated that any future alterations to the law would require a two-thirds majority.

## The Amphiktyonic Constituency in Ancient Greece

In ancient Greece, the Amphiktyonic convocation utilized a constituency based on "races" (or sub-races) rather than cities. The twelve constituent members included the following:
*   Thessalians, Bœotians, Dorians, and Ionians
*   Perrhæbians, Magnêtes, Lokrians, and Œtæans
*   Achæans, Phokians, Dolopes, and Malians

In this system, each race counted equally regardless of the size of the cities within that race. Each of the twelve members was given two votes. According to the historian Æschines, powerful cities such as Athens, Sparta, and Thebes had no more influence in determining deputies or voting than the smallest cities, such as Priênê or Erythræ. 

While the original constitution established a footing of equal independence among the twelve members, in practice, the votes of the Ionic race became substantially the vote of Athens, and the Doric vote was largely managed by Sparta, unless obstructed by other powerful Doric cities like Corinth or Argos. Other Hellenic races not included in the Amphiktyons included the Ætolians, Akarnanians, Arcadians, Eleians, Pisatans, Triphylians, and the Dryopes.

## Other Historical Allotments

### Roman Provincial Division
Following the massacre of the Flavian race, the Roman provinces were divided among three brothers. Constantine, the eldest, took possession of the new capital. Constantius was allotted the countries of the East and Thrace, while Constans became the sovereign of Africa, Italy, and Western Illyricum.

### Athletic Allotments
In the context of the Stadium, "allotment" referred to the placement of prizes for victors. These prizes were set up in the middle of the Stadium to be viewed by the champions of the three types of races: the footrace, the horse race, and the chariot race.
