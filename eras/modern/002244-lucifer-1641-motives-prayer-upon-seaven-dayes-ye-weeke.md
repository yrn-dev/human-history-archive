# Lucifer and Motives for Prayer upon the seaven dayes of ye weeke

## The Term Lucifer
The term "Lucifer" is a Latinized form of the Greek word *phôsphoros*, meaning "light-bearer." In an astronomical context, it refers to the "morning star," which is the planet Venus when it appears above the eastern horizon before sunrise. It is sometimes used to describe the "evening star," the same planet appearing in the western sky after sundown, though that is more commonly called Hesperus.

In poetic usage, the prophet Isaiah used the term "day star" (rendered as Lucifer in the Authorized Version) to refer to the king of Babylon, asking, "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, son of the morning! how art thou cut down to the ground, which didst weaken the nations." Christian theology later associated this passage with the words of Christ in Luke x. 18, "I beheld Satan as lightning fall from heaven." Consequently, the Christian Fathers interpreted the passage in Isaiah as referring to Satan, and Lucifer became regarded as the name of Satan before his fall. This theological concept is expressed in the literary work *Paradise Lost* by Milton and is the basis for the common phrase "as proud as Lucifer."

## Lucifer of Cagliari
Separate from the theological entity, Lucifer (who died in 370 or 371) was the bishop of Cagliari, also known as *Caralitanus*. He was a fervent supporter of Athanasius. Following the synod of Arles in 353, Lucifer attempted to secure a new, impartial council, traveling with Hilarius the deacon and Pancratius the presbyter under the direction of Pope Liberius. However, he was unable to prevent the condemnation of Athanasius, which was renewed at Milan in 355.

Due to his persistent adherence to the orthodox creed, Lucifer was banished to Germanicia in Commagene, later residing in the upper Thebaid and Eleutheropolis in Palestine. He returned from exile in 362 following an edict by Julian. From 363 until his death, he lived in Cagliari in a state of voluntary separation from former associates, including Athanasius and Eusebius of Vercelli. This separation resulted from their mild decision at the 362 synod of Alexandria regarding those who had unwillingly Arianized during the persecutions of Constantius.

Lucifer founded a sect, though it is noted that he may have lacked the education to fully appreciate the issue at hand. The sect did not last long after his death and developed no doctrines of significant importance. While he is popularly regarded as a saint in Sardinia, he was never canonized.

## Writings of Lucifer of Cagliari
During his exile, Lucifer produced controversial writings characterized by bold and violent language. He referred to the reigning emperor as a second Ahab, Saul, and Jeroboam, as well as the enemy of God. His works include:
* *De non parcendis in Deum delinquentibus*
* *De regibus apostaticis*
* *Ad Constantium Augustum pro Athanasio libri ii.*
* *De non conveniendo cum haereticis*
* *Moriendum esse pro Filio Dei*

These texts are considered valuable to students of the Latin text prior to Jerome. They were first edited by Tilius in 1568, with a later edition by W. Hartel in 1886.

## Sir Richard Baker's Works
Sir Richard Baker, who spent much of his imprisonment writing, authored several religious and meditative texts in the early 17th century. In 1641, he wrote *Apologie for Laymen's Writing in Divinity, with a Short Meditation upon the Fall of Lucifer*. This was followed in 1642 by the work *Motives for Prayer upon the seaven dayes of ye weeke*. 

Baker's other writings include *Meditations on the Lord's Prayer* (1637), *Cato Variegatus* (1636), and various meditations on the psalms of David. He also produced translations, including *New Epistles by Moonsieur D'Balzac* (1638) and Malvezzi's *Discourses upon Cornelius Tacitus* (1642), as well as the *Chronicle of the Kings of England from the Time of the Romans' Government unto the Death of King James* (1643).
