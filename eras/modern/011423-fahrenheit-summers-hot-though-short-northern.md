# Temperature and Climate Patterns in Asia and Other Regions

The distribution of air temperatures across the globe varies significantly based on latitude, proximity to oceans, elevation, and the physical characteristics of the land surface. In Asia, these factors create a spectrum of extremes, ranging from some of the lowest temperatures recorded on Earth in the north to some of the highest in the south.

## Temperature Extremes in Asia
The northern portion of Asia is characterized by winters of extreme severity and long duration. In these latitudes, the minimum temperature frequently drops nearly to zero degrees Fahrenheit, even as far south as the 35th parallel North. Conversely, the summers in the northern latitudes are hot, though short; the maximum summer heat in these areas is only slightly less than the heat observed in tropical countries further south.

In southern Asia, the highest mean temperature attained is not much above 82 degrees Fahrenheit. For comparison, the mean temperature of the north coast of eastern Siberia is only a few degrees above zero Fahrenheit. Generally, as far as 30 degrees North, a reduction of one degree of latitude corresponds to an approximate rise of 1/2 degree Fahrenheit in mean temperature, where the mean temperature is about 75 degrees Fahrenheit.

## Factors Influencing Regional Climates
Several geographical factors exaggerate or moderate these temperatures:

*   **Oceanic Influence:** The proximity of the ocean provides a moderating effect along the eastern and southern parts of Asia, particularly where the land consists of peninsulas or islands.
*   **Elevation:** High elevations in central Asia, as well as the table-lands of Persia and Afghanistan, tend to increase the severity of winter cold.
*   **Surface Composition:** In central Asia, the sterility of the surface and low rainfall increase summer heat. Solar heat accumulates on dry soil because it cannot be released through evaporation (as it would be if the soil were moist or vegetated) nor conveyed downward as it is in the ocean. Similarly, long, clear nights lead to a prolonged loss of heat by radiation in winter, an effect intensified by air with little vapour or surfaces covered in snow.

## Regional Climate Profiles

### Siberia and Central Asia
In Siberia, near Yakutsk, the earth remains permanently frozen to a depth of more than 380 feet, where the temperature remains 5 or 6 degrees Fahrenheit below the freezing point of water. Summer heat only thaws the surface to a depth of approximately 3 feet. At a depth of 50 feet, the temperature is about 15 degrees Fahrenheit below the freezing point. Despite these conditions, the land produces grain and wheat crops from fifteen to forty fold.

The region comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and central Asia is almost rainless, experiencing intense summer heat and winters of extreme severity. Siberia north of the 50th parallel has a climate similar to adjacent parts of Europe, though its summers are hotter and winters more severe.

### China and the Indo-Chinese Region
China, located between the North Pacific and the eastern flank of the Tibetan plateau, has a climate analogous to southern Europe. It experiences hot summers and cold winters, which become decidedly severe in the north. The region encompassing southern China, the Indo-Chinese peninsula, and India is characterized by high temperatures and heavy periodical summer rains.

### Bokhara and Bulgaria
The climate of Bokhara is extreme. In its lowlands, very hot summers are followed by short, cold winters where frosts of -20 degrees Fahrenheit may occur, occasionally freezing the Oxus for a fortnight. In the highlands, the hot, dry summer is followed by a four-month winter.

In Bulgaria, the climate is influenced by the Balkan Peninsula's configuration and mountain ranges. The region experiences rapid temperature transitions and great local contrasts. In districts exposed to the north wind between the Balkans and the Danube, winter temperatures have fallen to 24 degrees below zero. In the high tableland of Sofia, the maximum temperature is 86 degrees and the minimum is 2 degrees. In the sheltered plain of Eastern Rumelia, the climate is comparatively warm, and the mean temperature for Bulgaria is 52 degrees.

## Other Global Observations
In certain coastal regions, such as those described in the archive's records of Africa, the average temperature near the sea is about 80 degrees Fahrenheit. During the "harmattan" or long dry season, the thermometer falls by about 10 degrees, and the lack of moisture causes vegetation to droop and woodwork to crack.

On a planetary scale, the precession of the equinoxes affects hemispheric climates. When the summer of one hemisphere occurs at perihelion and the winter at aphelion, that hemisphere experiences a short, hot summer and a very cold winter, while the opposite hemisphere experiences a long, cold summer and a briefer, warmish winter.
