# Mahommed Hussein and the Conflict in Baluchistan

## The Conflict of 1839
In 1839, the British army advanced through the Bolan Pass toward Afghanistan. During this campaign, the conduct of Mehrab Khan, the ruler of Baluchistan, was viewed by the British as dangerous and treacherous. This perception led to the determination that retribution must be exacted from the chieftain and that arrangements were necessary to ensure future security in the region.

In response, General Willshire was detached from the army of the Indus with a force of 1,050 men to assault Kalat. The British forces used field-pieces to knock in a gate, allowing them to storm the citadel and the town within a few minutes. The assault resulted in the deaths of over 400 Baluches, including Mehrab Khan, and the capture of 2,000 prisoners.

## The Role of Mahommed Hussein
Subsequent inquiries revealed that the treachery against the British had not been committed by Mehrab Khan. Instead, the responsibility lay with his vizier, Mahommed Hussein, and certain chiefs with whom Hussein was in league. At the instigation of Hussein and these chiefs, British convoys were plundered while passing through the Bolan Pass and Kach Gandava.

Mahommed Hussein manipulated the situation by leading "too credulous" British political officers to believe that Mehrab Khan was the party to blame. Hussein's objective in this deception was to bring his master to ruin so that he could obtain all the power in the state for himself, as he was aware that the successor to Mehrab Khan was only a child.

## Aftermath and Succession
The political stability of the region remained volatile following the death of Mehrab Khan. In the year following the assault on Kalat, the town changed hands when a feeble garrison and the governor established by the British were overpowered. By the end of that same year, the British reoccupied the area under the command of General Nott.

In 1841, the British recognized Nasir Khan II, the youthful son of the slain Mehrab Khan, as the ruler. Shortly after this recognition, the British evacuated the country.
