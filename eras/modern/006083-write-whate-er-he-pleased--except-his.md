# And write whate'er he pleased--except his will

The phrase "And write whate'er he pleased--except his will" appears in the *Epistle to Dr Arbuthnot* (lines 378-379) in reference to Budgell.

## Budgell
Budgell was a writer who produced a translation of Theophrastus's *Characters* in 1714. He became embroiled in a scandal involving the second volume of Tindal's *Christianity as Old as the Creation*, which Budgell is said to have sold to Bishop Gibson; the Bishop subsequently destroyed the work. 

This scandal resulted in Budgell's ruin. On May 4, 1737, he committed suicide by taking a boat at Somerset-stairs and throwing himself into the river while passing under the bridge, having filled his pockets with stones. A slip of paper was discovered on his desk stating, "What Cato did, and Addison approved, cannot be wrong." Budgell never married, though he left a natural daughter named Anne Eustace, who pursued a career as an actress at Drury Lane.

The archive contains various accounts where the concept of "pleasing" or "willing" appears in political, social, or personal contexts:

*   **Philippides and Lysimachus:** Philippides, a friend of Lysimachus, was valued for not veiling his thoughts in courtly periphrases. When Lysimachus offered to bestow any of his possessions upon him, Philippides replied that he would take whichever Lysimachus pleased, "except your secrets."
*   **Demetrius and the Athenians:** The people of Athens proposed that a citizen be chosen to ask Demetrius, their saviour, for an oracle regarding the consecration of shields at Delphi. The proposal stated that the people would perform whatever oracle it pleased Demetrius to give them. This flattery is noted to have unbalanced Demetrius's intellect, which was not very powerful.
*   **Croesus and the Oracles:** When consulting various oracles, Croesus found that at first none of the answers pleased him. However, he accepted the answer from Delphi and worshipped the god, believing it to be the only true oracle because it revealed a secret device he had used involving boiling a lamb and a tortoise in a bronze caldron.
