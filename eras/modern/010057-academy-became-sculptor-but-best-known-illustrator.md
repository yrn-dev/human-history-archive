# Sculptors and Artists of the 18th to 20th Centuries

The history of sculpture and art during this period is characterized by a diverse range of international figures, many of whom were trained in prestigious academies in London, Paris, and Stockholm. These artists contributed to the public sphere through colossal statues, portraiture, and specialized illustrations.

## British Sculptors and Academicians

Several prominent British sculptors achieved recognition through the Royal Academy. Thomas Brock, a chief pupil of Foley, was influenced by the romantic movement. His work includes ideal pieces such as "Eve" and "The Genius of Poetry," as well as portrait busts of Queen Victoria and Lord Leighton. Brock's significant contributions include a sepulchral monument to Lord Leighton in St Paul's cathedral and a colossal equestrian statue of "Edward the Black Prince" in Leeds (1901). He was elected an associate of the Royal Academy in 1883 and a full member in 1891. He was also commissioned to create the Imperial Memorial to Queen Victoria for Buckingham Palace.

Edward Hodges Baily (1788–1867) began his artistic journey producing wax models and busts of schoolfellows before becoming a pupil of J. Flaxman in 1807. After entering the academy schools in 1809, he won the academy gold medal in 1811 for "Hercules restoring Alcestis to Admetus." Baily was elected R.A. in 1821 and is noted for "Eve at the Fountain," the bas-reliefs on the south side of the Marble Arch in Hyde Park, and statues of Earl Grey, Lord Mansfield, and Nelson in Trafalgar Square.

Giuseppe Bonomi (1796–1878) studied at the Royal Academy in London. While he became a sculptor, he is most recognized as an illustrator for leading Egyptological publications. Bonomi spent significant time in Egypt (1824–1832 and 1842–1844), the latter period attached to a Prussian government exploration expedition under Lepsius. His work included drawings of monuments and the mosque of Omar (1833). He later assisted in arranging the Egyptian court at the Crystal Palace in 1853 and served as curator of the Soane Museum starting in 1861.

## Continental European Sculptors

In Sweden, Johan Niklas Byström (1783–1848) studied under Sergel in Stockholm and won the academy prize in 1809. He gained immediate fame with "The Reclining Bacchante." Although appointed professor of sculpture at the academy, he spent much of his career in Italy. His notable works include colossal statues of Swedish kings and representations of the female form, such as "Pandora," "Hebe," and "Juno suckling Hercules."

In Italy, Lorenzo Bartolini (1777–1850) studied painting under Desmarets and sculpture under F. F. Lemot in Paris. He gained the second prize of the Academy in 1803 for the bas-relief "Cleobis and Biton." A patron of Bartolini was Napoleon, who commissioned a colossal bust and sent him to Carrara to establish a school of sculpture. His style is exemplified in works such as "Faith in God," "Hercules and Lichas," and the group of Charity.

France produced the Dumont family, a lineage of prominent sculptors. Francois Dumont (1688–1726) is best known for figures in the church of Saint Sulpice in Paris. He was followed by his son Edme (1720–1775), grandson Jacques Edme (1761–1844), and great-grandson Augustin Alexander (1801–1884), all of whom were famous sculptors.

## American Artists and Sculptors

Cyrus Edwin Dallin (born 1861) is an American sculptor known for his plastic representations of the North American Indian, influenced by his childhood spent living among them in the Far West. His works include "The Medicine Man" in Philadelphia's Fairmount Park and "The Signal of Peace" in Chicago's Lincoln Park. Other works include "Don Quixote," "Sir Isaac Newton" for the Congressional Library, and the "Pioneer Monument" in Salt Lake City. Dallin studied at the Academie Julien and Ecole des Beaux Arts in Paris, as well as under Henri M. Chapu and Jean Dampt.

Will Hicok Low (born 1853) was an American artist and writer who studied at the École des Beaux Arts in Paris under J. L. Gérôme and Carolus-Duran. He became a member of the National Academy of Design in 1890 and the Society of American Artists in 1878. Low is known for illustrations of Keats and pictures of New England types. His decorative work includes stained-glass windows for churches and panels for the Waldorf-Astoria Hotel in New York and the Essex County Court House in Newark, New Jersey.

## Other Artistic Figures

Samuel Lover (1797–1868) was an Irish novelist and artist who was elected a member of the Royal Hibernian Academy in 1828, later serving as its secretary. He was a miniature painter who produced portraits of the local aristocracy and a portrait of Paganini that was exhibited at the Royal Academy.
