# Parliament, Custom-Houses, and Landed Structures

The archive contains records of various prominent public buildings, including houses of parliament, custom-houses, and land-related offices, situated across several global cities. These structures often serve as architectural landmarks and centers of administration.

## Houses of Parliament

The archive identifies different Houses of Parliament in various regions, varying significantly in style and scale. In Brisbane, the capital of Queensland, Australia, the Houses of Parliament are located on a tongue of land formed by a southward bend of the river Brisbane. They are situated at the extremity of this tongue, adjoining Queen's Park, the botanical gardens, and the grounds of Government House. These buildings are noted as being among the city's most prominent structures of architectural merit.

In another location, the Houses of Parliament front the left bank of the Danube. This structure is described as a huge limestone edifice built in the late Gothic style. Erected between 1883 and 1902, it covers an area of 3¾ acres.

## Custom-Houses

Custom-houses are frequently located on river-banks or beachfronts to facilitate trade and administration. In Brisbane, the custom-house is described as a great domed building situated on the river-bank and is cited as one of the city's most prominent architectural features.

Other custom-houses mentioned in the archive include:
* **Danube River-bank:** An extensive custom-house is located at the lower end of the quays.
* **Baltimore:** A custom-house is listed among the city's notable buildings.
* **Calabar:** In this West African seaport, the customs office is located on the beach.
* **Tigris River-bank:** A custom-house stands just below the pontoon bridge. This specific building is constructed from the materials of the medreseh (college) of Mostansir, which dates to A.D. 1233; only a small portion of the original outer walls and a minaret of the caliph Mostansir remain.

## Lands and Public Offices

Administrative offices related to land and government are often grouped with other civic structures. In Brisbane, the lands office is listed as one of the city's most prominent buildings, alongside the general post-office and the treasury buildings.

In other cities, similar administrative clusters exist. In Baltimore, the city hall, post-office, and court-house stand in a row along E. Fayette Street. The city hall, completed in 1875 in the Renaissance style, features an iron dome 260 feet high and is faced with white Maryland marble.

## Geographic and Urban Contexts

The placement of these buildings is often dictated by the geography of the city. Brisbane is built on hills rising from the river-banks, though low-lying flats like South Brisbane and Woollongabba have experienced disastrous floods. The city's main thoroughfare, Queen's Street, leads across the Victoria Bridge—a steel structure that replaced a bridge swept away by floods in February 1893.

In Bucharest, the capital of Rumania, the city is built mainly on the left bank of the river Dimbovitza. The urban landscape is characterized by a mixture of modern reforms—such as stone embankments and electric light—and older districts where land is least valuable, featuring antique one-storeyed houses surrounded by acacias and poplars.
