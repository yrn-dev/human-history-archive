# Kendals

The term "Kendals" refers primarily to the professional partnership of the English actors William Hunter Kendal and his wife, Margaret (Madge) Shafto Robertson.

## William Hunter Kendal
Born in London on December 16, 1843, William Hunter Kendal was the son of a painter and was born with the family name Grimston. He began his stage career in Glasgow in 1862, appearing as Louis XIV in *A Life's Revenge* under the name "Mr Kendall." Following further experience in Birmingham and other locations, he joined the Haymarket company in London in 1866, where his roles ranged from Romeo to burlesque.

## Margaret Kendal
Margaret Shafto Robertson was born in 1849 and was the sister of the dramatist T. W. Robertson. She made her first stage appearance in London in 1854 as a child character named Marie in *The Orphan of the Frozen Sea*. Her skills as a singer and actress led to numerous engagements; by 1865, she was performing the roles of Desdemona and Ophelia. She also appeared as Pauline and Mary Meredith in *Our American Cousin* alongside Sothern.

## Professional Partnership and Career
William and Margaret married in 1869, after which their professional careers became inseparable, and they performed as "Mr and Mrs Kendal." 

At the Haymarket, the couple achieved significant success in old English comedies and Shakespearian revivals. Their performances were described as a perfect combination, with Mr Kendal playing roles such as Jack Absolute, Charles Surface, Young Marlowe, and Orlando, while Mrs Kendal performed as Kate Hardcastle, Lydia Languish, Lady Teazle, and Rosalind. Additionally, Mrs Kendal created the role of Galatea in Gilbert's 1871 play *Pygmalion and Galatea*.

The Kendals also performed short seasons at the Prince of Wales's and the Court theatre. At the Prince of Wales's, they joined the Bancrofts in other plays and in *Diplomacy*. From 1879, they entered a long association as joint-managers of the St James's theatre with Mr (later Sir John) Hare. Their notable successes during this period included *A Scrap of Paper*, *The Ironmaster*, *Impulse*, and *The Squire*. This management régime ended in 1888, after which Mr and Mrs Kendal primarily toured America and the provinces, with occasional seasons in London.

## Association with the Haymarket Theatre
The Kendals were part of an "admirable company" assembled by John Baldwin Buckstone during his tenure as manager of the Haymarket. Under Buckstone's management, the theatre produced plays by Robertson, Tom Taylor, Planché, and Gilbert, as well as Buckstone's own works.

## Other Uses of the Name Kendal
The name Kendal also refers to several other entities:

**Geography and Administration**
Kendal (also known as Kirkby-Kendal or Kirkby-in-Kendal) is a municipal borough and market town in Westmorland, England. Located on the river Kent, it is the largest town in the county. It is known for its white-walled houses, blue-slated roofs, and the limestone eminence known as Scout Scar. The town's history includes a free grammar school founded in 1525 and a church of the Holy Trinity dating from approximately 1200. The town was incorporated in 1576 by Queen Elizabeth and later had a mayor appointed by Charles I in 1635. Historically, the town was noted for woollen manufactures since 1331, including a coarse cloth referred to by Shakespeare as "Kendal green."

**Titles of Nobility**
The English title of duke of Kendal was first bestowed in May 1667 upon Charles, the infant son of the duke of York (later James II). Additionally, several individuals have held the title of earl of Kendal, including John Beaufort, duke of Somerset; John, duke of Bedford (son of Henry IV); and George, prince of Denmark, the husband of Queen Anne.
