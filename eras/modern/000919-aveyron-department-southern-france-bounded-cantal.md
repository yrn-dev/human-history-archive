# AVEYRON

Aveyron is a department located in southern France. It is bounded to the north by Cantal, to the east by Lozère and Gard, to the southwest by Tarn, and to the west by Lot and Tarn-et-Garonne. The department covers an area of 3,386 square miles and had a population of 377,299 in 1906.

## History and Administration
The department corresponds nearly to the old district of Rouergue. Early in the 9th century, a countship was established there, which was later united with the countship of Toulouse toward the end of the 11th century. The earliest known inhabitants of the region were the Celtic Rutheni, who are credited with the creation of the department's numerous megalithic monuments.

Administratively, Aveyron is divided into five arrondissements, with the chief towns being Rodez, Espalion, Millau, St Affrique, and Villefranche. It contains 43 cantons and 304 communes. Rodez serves as the capital of the department and is the seat of a bishopric whose diocese encompasses the entire department. In terms of broader regional administration, Aveyron belongs to the 16th military region and the educational circumscription (academie) of Toulouse, while its court of appeal is located in Montpellier. The department is served by the Southern and Orleans railway lines.

## Geography and Climate
Aveyron is situated on the southern border of the central plateau of France. The landscape is divided into four zones by its three chief rivers—the Lot in the north, the Aveyron in the centre, and the Tarn in the south—all of which flow from east to west as tributaries of the Garonne.

In the northeast, the volcanic mountains of Aubrac form the department's limit and contain its highest summit, reaching 4,760 feet. This area includes the lonely pastoral plateau of the Viadene, located between the Lot and its tributary, the Truyère. The town of Entraygues is situated at the confluence of these two rivers.

Between the Lot and the Aveyron lies a belt of *causses*, which are monotonous limestone table-lands characterized by profound and beautiful gorges. This zone is watered by the Dourdou du Nord, a tributary of the Lot. Further south, between the Aveyron and the Tarn, is the plateau of the Segala. This region is traversed from east to west by the deep valley of the Viaur (a tributary of the Aveyron) and is bordered to the east by the Palanges and Levezou heights.

The region south of the Tarn is largely occupied by the huge plateau of Larzac, situated between the Causse St Affrique and the Causse Noir. These three areas form the south-western termination of the Cévennes. The Causse Noir features Montpellier-le-Vieux, a fantastic chaos of precipices and rocks that resembles the ruins of a large city.

The climate of the department varies significantly, ranging from mildness in sheltered valleys to extreme rigour in the mountains.

## Notable Towns and Sites
The more important towns in the department include Rodez, Millau, St Affrique, Decazeville, and Villefranche-de-Rouergue. Other sites of particular interest include:
* **Sauveterre:** Founded in 1281, it is a striking example of a *bastide*.
* **Conques:** Home to a remarkable 11th-century abbey-church with a rich treasury of reliquaries and a plan similar to St Sernin of Toulouse.
* **Espalion:** Contains a church from the Romanesque period and the remains of a feudal stronghold.
* **Najac:** Features the ruins of a magnificent 13th-century chateau.
* **Sylvanes:** Home to a 12th-century church that was once attached to a Cistercian abbey.

## Agriculture and Production
Aveyron is known for the production of Roquefort cheese. This small, rather soft, white cheese is abundantly veined with a greenish-blue mould and typically weighs between 4 and 5 lb. It is made from the milk of ewes, which are kept primarily as dairy animals within the department. The cheese is cured in natural mountain caves located at the village of Roquefort.
