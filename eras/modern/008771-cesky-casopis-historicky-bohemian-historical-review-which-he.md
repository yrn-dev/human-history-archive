# Cesky Casopis Historicky

The *Cesky Casopis Historicky*, known in English as the *Bohemian Historical Review*, is a scholarly publication dedicated to the study of Bohemian history. It was established in 1895 through the joint efforts of Professor Jaroslav Goll and Dr. Anton Rezek.

## Contributors and Academic Context
The review served as a venue for important historical studies, particularly those authored by Dr. Anton Rezek. Rezek's contributions to the field of history appeared both in the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* and in the *Journal of the Bohemian Museum*. 

The publication emerged during a period of significant intellectual activity in Bohemia. This era saw the contributions of various historians and scholars, including Jaroslav Goll, who wrote extensively on the community of the Bohemian Brethren. Other notable figures in Bohemian historical and legal scholarship during this broader period included Professor Joseph Kalousek, who produced a valuable study on the ancient constitution (*Statni pravo*) of Bohemia, and Wenceslas Tomek (1818-1905), whose most important work was the *Dejepis miesta Prahy*, a history of the town of Prague.

## Relation to Bohemian Historiography
The *Cesky Casopis Historicky* exists within a wider tradition of Bohemian historical writing that evolved from early chronicles to modern academic journals. Early historical records included the rhymed chronicle often called the chronicle of Dalimil, written likely during the reign of King John (1310-1346). By the 16th century, historians such as Wenceslas Hajek of Libocan, Bartos (the writer), and Sixt of Ottersdorf produced significant works, though their perspectives varied from the dynastic and Romanist tendencies of Hajek to the more personal accounts of rising against Ferdinand I provided by Sixt of Ottersdorf.

In the 19th century, the field was further advanced by Frantisek Palacky, whose *Geschichte von Bohmen* (1844-1867) superseded earlier works by authors such as Pelzl, Pubitschka, and De Florgy. Following the period covered by Palacky (which ended in 1526), historians including Gindely and Rezek focused on the history of Bohemia posterior to that year.

## Academic Environment
The foundation of the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* occurred alongside other developments in Bohemian national scholarship, such as the establishment of the national university in 1882. This period was characterized by a revival of the Bohemian language and a surge in learned studies, including archaeological research on the earliest periods of Bohemian history conducted by Professors Niederle and Pic.
