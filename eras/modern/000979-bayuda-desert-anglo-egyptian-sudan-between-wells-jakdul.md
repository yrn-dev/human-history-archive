# Bayuda Desert

The Bayuda desert is a region located within the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan. It is characterized geographically as a series of steppes that form the northern boundary of Kordofan, a province (*mudiria*) of the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan.

## Inhabitants and Tribal Groups

The Bayuda desert is home to two small nomad tribes, the Awadia and the Fadnia. These groups are described as being of pure Arab blood. While they are often incorrectly classified as Ja'alin, they maintain a distinct identity. 

The Awadia and Fadnia are pastoralists who own cattle and horses. Specifically, their horses are of the black Dongola breed, a breed noted for being strong and hardy.

## Military History

The tribes of the Bayuda desert participated in the conflict involving British forces in the late 19th century. During the battle of Abu Klea, which took place on January 17, 1885, the Awadia and Fadnia were conspicuous for their courage, specifically in their actions riding against the British square.

## Geography and Regional Context

The Bayuda desert is situated between the wells of Jakdul and Metemma. In the broader geography of the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan, it sits north of Kordofan. Kordofan itself is a province of approximately 130,000 square miles, bounded to the west by Darfur and to the east by the White Nile *mudiria*.

To the north and west of the general region lies the broader desert landscape of the Sudan, including the Nubian desert to the east of the Dongola province and the Libyan desert to the west. The region of Dongola, which is part of Nubia, extends along the Nile from 18 to 20 degrees North and is characterized by slight rainfall, with fertility largely confined to lands watered by the Nile.
