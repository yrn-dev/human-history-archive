# BARROW

The Barrow is a river located in south-eastern Ireland. It originates in the Slieve Bloom Mountains, a range of lofty and precipitous crags that form the boundary between King's County and Queen's County. The river's course begins by flowing in an easterly direction before turning almost due south.

## Course and Estuary
The Barrow continues its southward flow until it joins the Suir. Together, these rivers form the estuary of the south coast known as Waterford Harbour. The total length of the river's valley, including the 12 miles of the estuary, is slightly more than 100 miles, though this figure does not account for the river's lesser windings.

## Drainage and Geography
The total area of drainage to Waterford Harbour, which includes the basin of the Suir, covers 3500 square miles. This drainage area encompasses the entirety of county Kilkenny, as well as portions of the following counties:
* King's
* Queen's
* Carlow
* Tipperary
* Waterford
* Cork
* Limerick

The river serves as a geographic boundary in several locations. It forms the south-eastern boundary between King's County and Queen's County, and it also forms the boundary between Kildare and Queen's County. In county Kilkenny, the Barrow serves as the eastern boundary from near New Bridge.

## Navigation and Urban Centers
The Barrow is navigable beyond the limits of Kilkenny and into Kildare. At the town of Athy, the river becomes navigable and features a junction with the Grand Canal. Other chief towns situated on the banks of the Barrow include Carlow, Bagenalstown, and New Ross.

## Tributaries and Related Waters
The chief affluent of the Barrow is the Nore, which flows from the north-west and joins the Barrow a little above New Ross (approximately 9 miles above Ross). Both the Barrow and the Nore, along with the Suir, have their origins in the Slieve Bloom Mountains of Queen's County and county Tipperary.

In county Kildare, the Barrow receives the Lane and the Greese shortly after entering the county. Additionally, a separate stream known as the Lesser Barrow flows southward from the Bog of Allen to a point near Rathangan.

## Landscape
The scenery along the banks of the Barrow is described as being very beautiful in certain parts.
