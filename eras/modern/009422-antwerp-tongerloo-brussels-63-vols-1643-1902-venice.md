# Acta Sanctorum

The *Acta Sanctorum* is a monumental collection of hagiographic works produced by the Bollandists. The project has been released in three primary editions: an original edition consisting of 63 volumes published between 1643 and 1902 in Antwerp, Tongerloo, and Brussels; a Venice edition, which concluded at volume v. of September (1734-1770); and a Paris edition, which concluded at volume xiii. of October (61 volumes, 1863-1883). Additionally, the abbe Rigollot edited a volume of tables for the work.

## History of the Bollandists
The production of the *Acta Sanctorum* was managed by the Bollandists, whose work continued in Antwerp following the dispersion of the Jesuits. In 1778, they were transferred to the monastery of canons regular of Coudenberg in Brussels. During their tenure there, they published volume iv. of October in 1780 and volume v. of October in 1786. The work of the Bollandists ceased in 1788 after the suppression of the Coudenberg monastery.

The remains of the Bollandist library were subsequently acquired by the Premonstratensians of Tongerloo. This group attempted to continue the project, resulting in the publication of volume vi. of October in 1794 at their abbey.

The work was resumed in 1837 following the re-establishment of the Society of Jesus in Belgium. This revival occurred with the support of the Belgian government and the suggestion of the Academie Royale of Belgium. The Bollandists were installed at the college of St Michael in Brussels, and in 1845, they published volume vii. of October. This began a new series that eventually reached volume xiii. of October in 1883. Collaborators in this series included the Jesuit fathers Remi de Buck, Victor de Buck, Benjamin Bossue, Joseph van Hecke, Joseph van der Moere, Edu. Carpentier, and Ant. Tinnebroeck. Additionally, Father John Martinov of Theazan edited the *Annus Graeco-Slavicus*, which appeared in the beginning of volume xi. of October in 1864.

## Scientific Evolution and Analecta Bollandiana
In 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align more closely with historical methods. This led to the creation of a quarterly review titled *Analecta Bollandiana*, established by Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt. By 1906, the review had reached its 25th volume, edited by P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt.

The *Analecta Bollandiana* served several purposes:
* It published dissertations, inedited texts, and studies intended for the remoulding and continuation of the *Acta Sanctorum*.
* Since 1892, it included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which provided criticisms of recent hagiographic works.
* It published various library catalogues, including those from Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres.

## Auxiliary Research and Later Volumes
To provide a more scientific and solid basis for the principal collection, the Bollandists undertook the analysis of hagiographic manuscripts in major libraries. This resulted in several separate publications:
* Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (2 volumes, 1886-1889).
* Greek and Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (5 volumes, 1889-1896).
* Greek manuscripts in the Vatican (1899).
* Latin manuscripts in the libraries of Rome (1905 and following).

The Bollandists also created inventories of previously published hagiographic texts, producing the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*.

While these auxiliary works delayed the publication of the main collection, they provided a strictly scientific stamp to the project. Subsequent volumes of the *Acta Sanctorum* included volume i. for November (1887) and volume ii. (1894), the latter of which was preceded by the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* by the abbe Louis Duchesne and J.B. de Rossi. In 1902, the *Propylaeum ad Acta Sanctorum Novembris* was published, which included the *Synaxarium ecclesiae Constantinopolitanae*.
