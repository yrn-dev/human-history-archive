# The Bampton Lectures

## Overview and Establishment
The Bampton Lectures are a series of annual lectures delivered at the University Church in Oxford. The lectures were established through the will of John Bampton (c. 1690–1751), an English divine and member of Trinity College, Oxford, who graduated with an M.A. in 1712 and served as a canon of Salisbury. 

Bampton's will mandated that eight lectures be delivered every year on Sunday mornings during the full term. These lectures are scheduled between the commencement of the last month in Lent term and the conclusion of the third week in Act term.

## Subject Matter and Objectives
The lectures are designed to address specific theological themes. According to the directions in Bampton's will, the subjects must be chosen from the following:
* The confirmation and establishment of the Christian faith, including the confutation of all schismatics and heretics.
* The divine authority of the Holy Scriptures.
* The authority of the writings of the primitive fathers regarding the practice and faith of the primitive Church.
* The divinity of the Holy Ghost.
* The divinity of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ.
* The articles of the Christian faith as they are comprehended in the Nicene and Apostles' Creeds.

## Lecturer Qualifications and Selection
To be eligible to deliver the lectures, a candidate must hold at least a Master of Arts degree from either Cambridge or Oxford. Historically, the lecturer was chosen annually on the fourth Tuesday in Easter term by the heads of colleges. A strict rule prohibits any individual from being chosen to deliver the lectures a second time.

## Administration and Funding
The series of lectures began in 1780. The endowment provides a payment of £120 for each lecturer. A requirement of the appointment is that the lectures must be published within two months after they have been delivered. 

While the lectures were initially annual, a depreciation of the fund's revenue led to a change in 1895, after which elections for lecturers were only conducted in alternate years.

Since their inception, the Bampton Lectures have been delivered by various scholars and theologians. Notable examples include:
