# Czech National Socialists

The Czech National Socialists were a political group active within the parliamentary system of the Austrian Empire. They formed one part of the broader Czech political representation in the Reichsrath, alongside other groups such as the Czech Agrarians, Young Czechs, Old Czechs, and Czech Clericals.

## Parliamentary Representation
In the distribution of parliamentary seats, the Czech National Socialists held a minority position compared to other Czech factions. Archive records indicate that the group was represented by 9 members. For comparison, during the same period, the Czech Agrarians held 28 seats, the Young Czechs held 18, and the Czech Clericals held 17.

## Political Context in Bohemia
The Czech National Socialists operated within a volatile political environment characterized by ethnic tension between Czechs and Germans, particularly regarding the administration of Bohemia. A central point of contention was the use of language in government offices. 

Under the ordinances published by Badeni in May 1897, it was determined that all correspondence and documents brought before government officials should be conducted in the language in which they were first introduced, effectively introducing Czech into government offices throughout the kingdom. Additionally, it was mandated that after 1903, no one could be appointed to a government post in Bohemia without passing an examination in Czech. These measures were fiercely opposed by German Nationalists and Radicals, who utilized obstruction tactics in parliament to prevent business from being conducted.

Further attempts at compromise were made by Dr. von Koerber, who proposed dividing Bohemia into 10 districts (5 Czech, 3 German, and 2 mixed). However, Czech representatives demanded that both the Czech and German languages be placed on an equal footing throughout Bohemia for all official purposes. This demand was refused by German parties, leading to a period where the Czechs blocked legislative business through "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction, including one sitting that lasted 57 hours without interruption.

## Relation to Socialism and Patriotism
While the term "Socialist" appears in the name of the party, contemporary accounts of Czechs during the era of the World War suggest a distinction between theoretical political affiliation and practical action. Observations of Czech soldiers and officers—including those who may have been Socialists in theory—emphasized a primary identity as patriots. These individuals were described as being finely disciplined and possessing a strong loyalty to the cause of freedom, which distinguished them from the Russian proletariat of the time.

## National Leadership and Aspirations
Czech political and national identity during this period was closely tied to leaders such as Professor Masaryk. Members of the Czech National Assembly and officers sought to understand the speeches of U.S. President Woodrow Wilson, ranking him alongside Masaryk as a national leader. The Czechs of this era are characterized as being literate in several languages, university-educated or skilled artisans, and possessing a determined air and enthusiastic cheer.
