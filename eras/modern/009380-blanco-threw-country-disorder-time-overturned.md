# General Blanco and the Political Instability of Bolivia

## The Revolution of General Blanco
Following the achievement of independence, the country did not experience a peaceful future. After General Sucre and his Colombian troops were driven from La Paz in late 1827, a new congress was established at Chuquisaca in April 1828. This congress modified the constitution provided by Bolivar and appointed Marshal Santa Cruz as president. However, only one year later, a revolution led by General Blanco occurred. This uprising threw the country into disorder and, for a period of time, overturned the government.

## The Presidency of Santa Cruz
Order was restored in 1831, allowing Santa Cruz to promulgate a code of laws bearing his name. During this period, he brought the country's financial affairs into some order and established a treaty of commerce with Peru, leading to several years of peace for Bolivia.

In 1835, Santa Cruz became involved in a power struggle between two factions in the neighboring republic of Peru. He marched into Peru and defeated General Gamarra, the leader of one of the opposing parties. By the spring of 1836, Santa Cruz completed the pacification of Peru and named himself its protector, intending to create a confederation of the two countries.

## Conflict with Chile and Peru
The government of Chile actively interfered in these events by supporting Gamarra and sending troops into Peru. This resulted in three years of fighting. In June 1839, Santa Cruz was defeated in a battle at Jungay and subsequently exiled. Following this, Gamarra became the president of Peru, while General Velasco was appointed as the provisional chief in Bolivia.

## Subsequent Power Struggles
The party supporting Santa Cruz remained strong within Bolivia and successfully revolted against the new head of government, eventually installing General Ballivian in the chief power. Taking advantage of the instability in Bolivia, Gamarra attempted to annex the rich province of La Paz in August 1841 by invading and besieging the capital. However, Gamarra's army was totally routed in a battle with Ballivian, and Gamarra was killed.

General Ballivian then attempted to invade Peru, but Chile interfered once more to prevent the invasion. Ballivian remained president until 1848, when he retired to Valparaiso. At the end of that year, General Belzu led a successful military revolution.
