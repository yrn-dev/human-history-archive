# Lake Baikal

## Overview and Geography
Lake Baikal is a lake located in East Siberia and is recognized as the largest fresh-water basin in Eurasia. In terms of global scale, it is the sixth largest lake in the world. The lake is known as *Bai-kul* to Turkish tribes and *Dalai-nor* to the Mongols. Geographically, it serves as a separator between the government of Transbaikalia and the government of Irkutsk, stretching from the south-west to the north-east (between 51deg 29' to 55deg 50' N. lat. and 103deg 40' to 110deg E. long.).

The lake's southern extremity extends into the high plateau of Asia. Baikal is situated entirely within the Alpine zone that fringes the north-west of that plateau.

## Dimensions and Area
Lake Baikal has a length of 386 m. and a width that varies from 20 m. to 50 m. Its total area is 13,200 sq. m., a size described as being nearly as great as Switzerland. The lake's coastline differs in length between its two shores, measuring 525 m. along the western shore and 640 m. along the eastern shore.

## Altitude and Water Levels
The altitude of Lake Baikal has been subject to different estimates. Chersky estimated the altitude at 1587 ft., while Suess estimated it at 1679 ft. A more correct altitude is considered to be 1500 ft. based on *Izvestia East Sib. Branch* (xxviii. 1, 1897). These measurements place the lake 118 ft. above the level of the Angara at Irkutsk, according to *Zapiski Russ. Geog. Soc.* (xv., 1885).

The water level of the lake is subject to slight oscillations. A notable instance occurred in 1869 following a heavy rain that lasted five weeks, causing the lake to rise 7 ft. This event resulted in the submergence of an immense territory located at the mouth of the Selenga.
