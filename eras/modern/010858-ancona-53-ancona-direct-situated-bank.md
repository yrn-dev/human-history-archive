# Ancona and the Marches

The Marches (Italian: *Le Marche*) is a territorial division of Italy consisting of the provinces of Ancona, Macerata, Ascoli Piceno, and Pesaro and Urbino. Covering an area of 3,763 square miles, the region had a population of 1,088,763 in 1901. Geographically, it is bounded by the Adriatic to the east, the Emilia to the north, the Abruzzi to the south, and Tuscany and Umbria to the west.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The general elevation of the Marches is more than 500 feet above sea level, except for the river valleys and a frequently narrow coastal strip. The terrain consists of limestone in the Apennines and loose, unstable clayey earth in the lower hills. The primary rivers, which flow northeast into the Adriatic, are the Tronto (ancient *Truentus*) and the Metauro (ancient *Metaurus*). The Tronto serves as the southern boundary of the *compartimento* for a certain distance.

Transportation in the region is heavily reliant on the railway from Bologna to Brindisi, which follows the coastline. At Ancona, this line connects with the main line from Rome and Foligno. Other branches extend to Macerata, San Severino, and Fabriano (a junction for Urbino), as well as to Fermo and Ascoli Piceno. Due to the mountainous nature of the interior, north-south communication is limited to devious roads outside of the coastal railway.

## Economy and Industry
Agriculture and pastoral pursuits are the primary occupations, utilizing the *mezzadria* (or *métayer*) system, where land owners and cultivators divide products equally. Despite the naturally poor quality of the soil, it is highly cultivated. Other significant industries include:
*   **Manufacturing:** Silk industries, straw-plait and straw hat making, sugar-refining, tobacco, brickworks, ironworks, and terra-cotta manufacture.
*   **Specialized Production:** A major paper industry is located at Fabriano, and Chiaravalle hosts one of the largest tobacco factories of the Italian *régie*.
*   **Resources:** The region provides sulphur from mines and building stone from limestone quarries; petroleum and chalk are also present.
*   **Maritime Activity:** Ancona serves as the only truly good harbour in the province and is a site for important shipbuilding works. Other canal ports include Fano, Pesaro, and Senegallia (Sinigaglia). The coastal population is heavily engaged in fishing and provides many sailors for the Italian navy.

## Notable Cities and Sites
### Ancona
Ancona is a central hub of the region, serving as a major railway junction and the primary port. In the 6th century, the historian Procopius described Ancona as the harbour for Auximum.

### Auximum (Osimo)
Located on an isolated hill 8 miles from the Adriatic on the road from Ancona to Nuceria, Auximum (modern Osimo) was an ancient town in Picenum. The Romans established it as a fortress to protect northern Picenum settlements, strongly fortifying it in 174 B.C. with walls of large rectangular stone blocks that still largely exist. It became a colony by 157 B.C. and played a role in the civil wars due to its strong position; Pompey served as its patron and intended for it to be a point of resistance against Caesar in 49 B.C. By the 6th century, Procopius identified it as the chief town of Picenum.

### Ascoli Piceno
The capital of its province and an episcopal see, Ascoli Piceno (ancient *Ausculum*) is situated 53 miles direct from Ancona on the south bank of the Tronto at its confluence with the Castellano. The town features the Porta Romana, a double-arched Roman gate, and massive ancient walls. Notable structures include the church of S. Gregorio, built into a Roman tetrastyle Corinthian temple, and the church and convent of the Annunziata, which sit upon Roman concrete substructures. The town's picturesque market-place contains the original Palazzo del Comune and the Gothic church of S. Francesco. The cathedral, originally Romanesque, was restored in 1888 by Count Giuseppe Sacconi.

### Macerata
The chief town of the province of Macerata and a bishop's see, this city is located 44 miles by rail south of Ancona. Situated on a hill 919 feet above sea level, it features a university, an industrial institute, and an agricultural school. It is known for a communal library founded by Leo XII and a large amphitheatre called a *sferisterio* used for the popular ball game *pallone*.

## Historical Figures
The region has been associated with various notable figures:
*   **Annibale Caro (1507–1566):** An Italian poet born at Civita Nuova in Ancona. He is recognized for his translation of the *Aeneid* and his development of the *verso sciolto*. He served as a secretary to the Farnese family.
*   **Guillaume Durand (c. 1230–1296):** A French canonist and bishop of Mende who served as the governor of the March of Ancona and Romagna starting in 1283. He defended papal territories during the conflicts between the Guelfs and Ghibellines.
