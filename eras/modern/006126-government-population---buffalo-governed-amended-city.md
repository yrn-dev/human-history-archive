# Government and Population of Buffalo

## Municipal Government
Buffalo is governed by an amended city charter dating from 1896. Under this charter, governmental power is vested in a mayor and a bicameral city council. The mayor is elected to a four-year term and is responsible for appointing the heads of the principal executive departments, which include the police, fire, civil service, health, and parks departments. The city council is responsible for electing the city clerk.

## Population and Demographics
Buffalo is located at the upper end of the Niagara river and the eastern extremity of Lake Erie. It serves as the county-seat of Erie county, New York, and is a port of entry. In terms of population, it is the eighth largest city in the United States and the second largest in the state of New York.

## Public Infrastructure and Utilities
The municipality owns its own water-supply system, drawing water from Lake Erie, and maintains several public baths that are well-equipped. The city's lighting is provided by manufactured gas and electricity, the latter of which is generated by the water power of Niagara Falls. For heating and lighting, the city makes extensive use of natural gas delivered via pipe lines from the Canadian (Welland) and Ohio-Pennsylvania natural gas fields.

## Public Institutions and Health
Buffalo is noted for its numerous and well-administered correctional, charitable, and hospital institutions, many of which are richly endowed. The city's hospitals include:
* The Buffalo General, Children's, and Women's hospitals.
* The Erie county and state hospital for the insane.
* The German, German Deaconess, and Homeopathic hospitals.
* The Riverside hospital and the Buffalo hospital of the Sisters of Charity.
* The United States marine hospital, which is maintained by the Federal government.

Most of these hospitals are connected to nurses' training schools. Charitable institutions in the city include St. Joseph's, St. Vincent's, and the Buffalo orphan asylums, as well as St. John's orphan home, the Home for the Friendless, the Ingleside home for erring women, and St. Mary's asylum for foundlings and widows. 

A significant institution is the Charity Organisation Society, founded in 1877 with headquarters in Fitch Institute. As the first such society in the United States, it has served as a model for similar institutions elsewhere due to its activities in improving social conditions.

## Urban Geography and Environment
The city covers an area of 42 square miles on a broad, undulating tract of land. Its altitude averages slightly less than 600 feet above sea-level, rising from the lake to elevations between 50 and 80 feet. Buffalo is considered one of the healthiest cities in the United States due to its temperate climate, high land, and efficient water-supply and drainage systems; its death rate was 14.8 per thousand in 1900 and 15.58 in 1907.

The city features broad, tree-lined streets, squares, and a municipal park system totaling approximately 1,030 acres. This system consists of a chain of parks connected by driveways and boulevards. Key parks include:
* Delaware Park: The largest at about 365 acres, containing a 46½ acre lake. Its northern section was part of the 1901 Pan-American Exposition grounds.
* Cazenovia Park, Humboldt Park, and South Park on the Lake Shore.
* "The Front": A bluff overlooking the source of the Niagara river, which houses Fort Porter, a site where the United States government maintains a garrison.

The Forest Lawn cemetery is also located adjacent to Delaware Park, containing monuments to the Seneca chief Red Jacket (1751-1830) and President Millard Fillmore.

## Commerce and Communications
Buffalo is a major industrial and commercial center in the Union, positioned as a principal northern trade route between the East and West. It is situated almost equidistant from New York, Boston, and Chicago. 

The city's commerce is driven by several factors:
* **Rail:** More than 500 miles of track exist within city limits, including a belt line transfer company and terminals for fourteen trunk lines.
* **Lake Commerce:** Buffalo serves as the eastern terminus for nearly all great Great Lakes steamship lines, providing freight and passenger connections to Milwaukee, Detroit, Cleveland, Chicago, and the "Head of the Lakes" (Duluth-Superior). The Great Northern Steamship Company operates what are likely the finest and largest inland passenger steamships.
* **Port Facilities:** The port handles more than 5,000,000 tons annually. It has a waterfront of roughly 20 miles and 8 to 10 miles of wharfs. The Federal government has improved the harbor with a 4-mile outer breakwater of cement and stone (costing over $2,000,000) and various inner piers and breakwaters.
* **Canals and Bridges:** The Erie Canal, completed in 1825, has its western terminus here. Buffalo is connected to the Canadian shore via ferry and the International bridge from Squaw Island, which was completed in 1873 at a cost of $1,500,000.

## Media
The city's first newspaper, the *Gazette* (a weekly), was established in 1811 and became a daily called the *Commercial* in 1835. The first daily paper, the *Courier*, was established in 1831. By 1908, there were eleven daily papers, including two in Polish and three in German. Weekly publications included three in Polish, several in German, and one in Italian.
