# H. F. Brownson and the Curso de Filosofia

The works associated with H. F. Brownson and the *Curso de Filosofia* are linked through the philosophical writings of Jaime Luciano Balmes, a Spanish ecclesiastic and philosopher.

## Jaime Luciano Balmes
Jaime Luciano Balmes (1810–1848) was born on August 28, 1810, in Vich, Catalonia, where he also died on July 9, 1848. He was an eminent philosopher and political writer. In his political life, Balmes founded and edited a Catholic and Conservative weekly publication titled *El Pensamiento de la Nacion* following a period of exile resulting from his attacks on the regent Espartero.

Balmes is noted for his work *El Protestantismo comparado con el Catolicismo en sus relaciones con la Civilisacion Europea* (published in three volumes between 1842 and 1844, with a sixth edition in 1879 and an English translation in London in 1849). In this text, he provided a defense of Catholicism, arguing that it represents a spirit of order and obedience, whereas he characterized Protestantism as a spirit of anarchy or revolt. ## Philosophical Works and H. F. Brownson
The philosophical contributions of Balmes are regarded as clear expositions of the scholastic system of thought. Among his most significant philosophical works are:

*   ***Filosofia Fondamental***: Published in four volumes in 1846. This work was translated into English by H. F. Brownson and published in New York in 1856 as a two-volume set.
*   ***Curso de Filosofia Elemental***: Published in four volumes in 1847. Balmes translated this work into Latin to facilitate its use in seminaries.
