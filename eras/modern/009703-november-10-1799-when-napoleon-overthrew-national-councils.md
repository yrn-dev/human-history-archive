# The Accession of Napoleon Bonaparte (November 1799)

The events of November 1799 marked a pivotal transition in French governance, characterized by the overthrow of parliamentary institutions and the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte to power. This transition is specifically associated with the *coup d'état* of the 18th Brumaire, which occurred on November 9, 1799.

## The Coup of 18th Brumaire
The *coup d'état* of the 18th Brumaire resulted in the dismantling of existing parliamentary structures in France. While many accepted the new order, there was internal resistance within the government. Joseph Marie Dessaix, a French general and member of the Council of Five Hundred, was among the few members of that body who opposed the coup.

The event had a profound impact on the political trajectory of those involved. Lucien Bonaparte, who had previously been an eager exponent of the cause of democracy and a leading speaker in the Jacobin club at Ajaccio, played a role in helping his brother triumph over the parliamentary institutions. However, this action proved fatal to the democratic cause Lucien had championed.

## The Consulate (1799–1804)
Following the events of November 1799, Napoleon assumed the role of First Consul, establishing a period of governance known as the consulate that lasted until 1804. During this time, the administrative and military structure of France was reorganized.

Napoleon's leadership style during the consulate was marked by a tension between his ambition and the general weal. Lucien Bonaparte had previously noted in letters to his brother Joseph that Napoleon possessed an ambition that surpassed his love for the general weal and that, in the event of a counter-revolution, Napoleon would attempt to "ride on the crest of events." This suspicion grew into a dominant feeling for Lucien, leading to strained relations between the brothers during the consulate.

## Administrative and Military Appointments
The shift in power led to the appointment of key officials to manage the state and its military campaigns. One such figure was Daru, who was employed as the chief commissary to the Army of Reserve intended for North Italy following Napoleon's accession in November 1799. This army was nominally commanded by Berthier but was actually under the control of the First Consul. Daru later played a significant role in signing the armistice with the Austrians in June 1800 and eventually became the intendant-general of Napoleon's military household.

Other figures transitioned into the new regime's legislative or advisory roles. Gregoire, who had been elected to the Council of 500, became a member of the Corps Legislatif and later the Senate (1801) after the 18th Brumaire.

## Political Fallout and Internal Conflict
The consolidation of power under the First Consul created friction with former allies. Lucien Bonaparte initially accepted a position as minister of the interior but was soon removed due to personal and political differences with Napoleon. To mitigate this, Napoleon appointed him ambassador to the court of Madrid in November 1800.

The relationship between the First Consul and his brother further deteriorated due to diplomatic disagreements. In June 1801, Lucien signed the preliminaries of peace at Badajoz with the Spanish minister Godoy, which frustrated Napoleon's plans to seize Lisbon. This led to Lucien's resignation and his subsequent alignment with the opposition in the Tribunate.
