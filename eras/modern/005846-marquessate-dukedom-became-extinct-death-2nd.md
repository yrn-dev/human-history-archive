# The Extinction of the Marquessate and Dukedom of Buckingham (Villiers Line)

The marquessate and dukedom of Buckingham associated with the Villiers family were titles created by King James I in the early 17th century. These titles became extinct upon the death of the 2nd Duke of Buckingham in 1687.

## Creation and Early History
The progression of titles for George Villiers began in 1617, when King James I created him earl. This was followed by his elevation to marquess in 1618, and finally to duke of Buckingham in 1623. 

## Extinction and Subsequent Claims
The dukedom and the marquessate both became extinct following the death of the 2nd Duke (Villiers) in 1687. However, the earldom remained a subject of contention. A collateral line, which claimed descent from John Villiers, 1st Viscount Purbeck, asserted a claim to the earldom based on a special remainder in the 1617 patent. The legitimacy of this line was considered doubtful.

The title of earl was not actually borne after 1723, the year of the death of John Villiers, who had styled himself the earl of Buckingham. The claim to the title was finally extinguished in 1774 with the death of George Villiers, a clergyman.

## Other Buckingham Titles
Following the extinction of the Villiers dukedom, other distinct titles using the name Buckingham were created:

### The Normanby Line
In 1703, John Sheffield, the marquess of Normanby, was created the "duke of the county of Buckingham and of Normanby." He was succeeded by his son, Edmund, who died in October 1735, at which point these titles became extinct.

### The Grenville Line
A later title of marquess and duke of Buckingham was established within the Grenville family. Unlike the Normanby title, this version was derived from the town of Buckingham rather than the county. This line originated in 1784 when the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham." In 1822, this title was elevated into the dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos for his son.

The 2nd duke of Buckingham and Chandos (1797–1861), Richard Grenville, was educated at Eton and Oriel College, Oxford. He served as a member of parliament for Buckinghamshire from 1818 to 1839 and was known as the "Farmers' Friend" due to his opposition to the repeal of the corn laws. He also served as lord privy seal from September 1841 to January 1842. His tenure was marked by financial difficulty; his residences were seized by creditors in 1847, leading him to leave England. Upon his return, he focused on literature, publishing memoirs regarding the courts of George III, George IV, William IV, and Victoria.

The Grenville dukedom eventually became extinct upon the death of the 3rd duke, Richard Grenville (1823–1889), the only son of the 2nd duke. Because the 3rd duke left three daughters but no son, the dukedom ended. His other titles were distributed as follows:
* The Scottish barony of Kinloss passed to his eldest daughter, Mary.
* The earldom of Temple passed to his nephew, William Stephen Gore-Langton.
* The viscounty of Cobham passed to his kinsman, Charles George, 5th Baron Lyttelton.
