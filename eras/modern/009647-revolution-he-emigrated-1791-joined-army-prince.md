# Louis Gabriel Ambroise Bonald

Louis Gabriel Ambroise Bonald (1754–1840) was a French politician and philosopher known for his staunchly conservative views and his opposition to the principles of the French Revolution.

## Early Life and Emigration
Born on October 2, 1754, at Le Monna, located near Millau in Aveyron, Bonald spent his early years in France. Due to his dislike of the principles driving the Revolution, he emigrated from France in 1791. During this period of exile, he joined the army of the prince of Conde. Following his military service, he settled in Heidelberg.

## Philosophical Works and Return to France
While residing in Heidelberg, Bonald authored his first significant work, *Theorie du pouvoir politique et religieux*. Published in three volumes in 1796 (and later in a two-volume new edition in Paris in 1854), the work was highly conservative in nature and was condemned by the Directory. 

Upon his eventual return to France, Bonald was viewed as an object of suspicion, which forced him to live in retirement for a period.

## Political Career and Public Service
Bonald gradually re-entered public life and professional circles. In 1806, he collaborated with Fievee and Chateaubriand in the management of the *Mercure de France*. Two years later, he was appointed as a councillor of the Imperial University, an institution he had frequently attacked.

Following the restoration, Bonald's career in public instruction and governance expanded:
* He served as a member of the council of public instruction.
* From 1815 to 1822, he sat in the chamber as a deputy.
* In 1822, he was appointed minister of state.

Throughout his time in the chamber, his speeches were characterized by extreme conservatism; he went so far as to advocate for the implementation of literary censorship. In his capacity as minister of state, he presided over the censorship commission.

## Later Years and Death
In 1823, Bonald was granted the dignity of a peer. However, he lost this status in 1830 after refusing to take the required oath. He had also been a member of the Academy since 1816.

After 1830, Bonald withdrew entirely from public affairs. He retired to his seat at Le Monna, where he remained until his death on November 23, 1840.
