# Boundary Commissions

Boundary commissions are official bodies appointed to demarcate, investigate, or negotiate the territorial limits between different states, regions, or political entities. These commissions often employ geographical surveys, historical research, and diplomatic negotiations to establish permanent borders.

## Russo-Afghan Boundary Commissions
Between 1884 and 1895, several commissions were tasked with defining the northern limits of Afghanistan. The Boundary Commissions of 1884–1886 and 1895 established the northern boundaries from the Sarikol mountains in the east to the Hari Rud in the west. One such commission, operating in 1885, surveyed the district of Badghis (meaning "home of the winds"), located between the Hari Rud and Murghab rivers and extending to the edge of the Sarakhs desert. This survey included the Chul formations, through which the Russo-Afghan boundary runs.

Research conducted by L. Griesbach in connection with the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission provided insights into the Oxus basin. Griesbach reported the gradual formation of a ridge or anticlinal extending longitudinally through the great Balkh plain of Afghan Turkestan. This geological feature effectively shuts off the northern affluents of the basin from joining the river. This evidence of ongoing upheaval suggests physical and climatic changes in the region since the time when the plains of Balkh were green with cultivation and Balkh served as a great trade center of Asia known as the "mother of cities."

## Japanese and Russian Border Commissions
In the late 18th century, Japan dispatched commissions from Yedo in 1792 to investigate Russian possession of Kamchatka and the establishment of Russians on the island of Urup. During their journey northward, the Yedo commissioners removed several boundary posts that had been erected by the Russians in Urup.

Later, following a Treaty of Peace signed on August 23 (September 5), 1905, at Portsmouth, a Commission of Delimitation was established to mark the exact boundary between Russian and Japanese possessions on the Island of Saghalien. This commission was composed of an equal number of members from both parties. Their mandate was to follow the fiftieth parallel of north latitude as the boundary line, making correlative deflections if topographical considerations required deviations from that line. The commission was also responsible for preparing maps of the ceded territory and a description of adjacent islands included in the cession.

## European Post-War Commissions
Following the First World War, international and mixed commissions were utilized to settle boundaries in Europe. In certain territories, German troops were required to move out within fifteen days of peace, placing the land under an international commission of five members appointed by the principal allied and associated powers. This commission's duties included arranging for secret, fair, and free votes (plebiscites) and recommending a boundary to the powers.

Additionally, a mixed commission was tasked with finally fixing the boundaries of Austria, Jugoslavia, and Czecho-Slovakia. Regarding Austria specifically, the boundaries of Bohemia and Moravia were to form the border between Austria and Czecho-Slovakia, subject to minor rectifications.

## North American Boundary Commissions
In the United States, joint commissions were used to resolve disputes between states. In 1799, Virginia and Kentucky appointed a joint commission to run the boundary line along the ridge of the Cumberland Mountains, which had been named as part of the east boundary of Kentucky in the Act of 1776. Similarly, a dispute regarding the southern boundary between Kentucky and Tennessee was settled via a similar commission in 1820.
