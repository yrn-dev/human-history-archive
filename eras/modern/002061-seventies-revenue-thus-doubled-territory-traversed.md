# Revenue and Territorial Administration

Across various historical and geographical contexts, the administration of territory has been closely linked to the generation and management of revenue, whether through taxation, labor, or natural resource development.

## Revenue in South Asia and the Middle East
In the territory governed by the nawab—a Mahommedan of the Daudputra family from Sind who claims descent from Abbas, the uncle of the Prophet—the area covers 15,918 sq. m. with a population of 720,877 as of 1901. This region, which established independence from the Afghans toward the end of the 18th century and signed a treaty with the British in 1838, reported an estimated gross revenue of L146,700, with no tribute required. The introduction of canal irrigation in the 'seventies resulted in the doubling of this revenue. The region is served by the Southern Punjab and North-Western railways. The town of Bahawalpur, located near the left bank of the Sutlej, features a palace built in 1882 by Nawab Sadik Mahommed Khan.

In Baluchistan, a country within British India extending from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, the territory is divided into British Baluchistan and foreign territories. British Baluchistan, covering 9,403 sq. m., consists primarily of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the 1879 treaty of Gandamak and formally incorporated into British India in 1887. The foreign territories include native states (Kalat, including Kharan and Makran, and Las Bela) and tribal areas held by the Bugti and Marri tribes. Certain directly-administered districts, including the eastern portion of the Quetta tahsil, the Bolan Pass, and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, are areas where it was decided that revenue shall be taken.

## Ancient Greek and Roman Revenue Systems
In ancient Sicily, the army of Gelo acquired abundant plunder, including numerous prisoners. These prisoners were distributed among cities based on the number of troops provided, with the largest shares going to Agrigentum and Syracuse. These public slaves were used for religious solemnity, ornament, and defense undertakings, or were let out to private masters to provide revenue for the state. In Agrigentum, this system contributed to the city's signal grandeur during a flourishing period of seventy years. Some private individuals in this city employed as many as five hundred slaves.

Regarding the Roman Empire, historical records indicate varying revenue figures. One account suggests that Pompey's conquests added 85,000,000 drachms to the ordinary revenue. Another record via Appian notes that Antony made Asia pay 200,000 talents at one time; as this represented ten years of revenue, the annual revenue at that time was 3,875,000 L. sterling.

## East Asian Financial Administration
In Japan, cash payments were transported to the castles of Osaka or Yedo under the care of the finance administrator (*kane-bugyo*). The *kane-bugyo* also compiled the accounts, which were then audited by officials known as *katte-kata*, presided over by a member of the *waka-doshiyori* or *roju*. Statistics from 1836 indicate that the Tokugawa estates collected annual revenue consisting of 93,961 gold ryo and 807,068 koku of rice. Land-tax rates varied by province, ranging from a split of seventy per cent for the landlord and thirty per cent for the tenant, to thirty per cent for the landlord and seventy per cent for the tenant.

## European Territorial and Financial Obligations
In the aftermath of war, the distribution of German territory involved the assumption of pre-war debt. Powers receiving ceded territory assumed a portion of this debt based on the ratio between the revenue of the ceded territory and Germany's total revenues for the three years preceding the war. However, France did not assume any part of Germany's pre-war debt for Alsace-Lorraine due to the circumstances of 1871, and Poland did not share in debts incurred for the oppression of Poland. Germany was required to pay the total cost of the armies of occupation as a first charge on its resources, followed by the cost of reparation.

## Geographic and Infrastructure Notes
Various territories are defined by the infrastructure that traverses them. The Territory of Belfort, with its capital at Belfort, is traversed by the Paris-Lyon-Mediterranee and Eastern railways. The Swiss canton of Basel, which includes territory north of the Rhine, is traversed by the Jura chain and railways connecting Basel to Laufen (14-1/4 m.) and Olten (25 m.), as well as local lines to Waldenburg and Fluehen. In the scientific study of minerals, the term "doubly-refracting spar" refers to Iceland-spar, where polarized rays of light traverse the crystal, causing objects viewed through it to appear double.
