# Taxation of Solicitors' and Agents' Bills

In legal and financial administration, the "taxation" of a bill refers to the formal process of reviewing and auditing the charges and disbursements incurred by a solicitor or agent to ensure they are reasonable and compliant with established rules.

## Taxation in Bankruptcy Proceedings
Within the context of bankruptcy, a trustee who receives remuneration is prohibited from claiming costs for any other person regarding duties that the trustee should have performed personally. All bills submitted by agents or solicitors employed in these matters must be taxed before payment can be made. This process ensures that the charges are in accordance with prescribed scales of costs.

The taxing master is required to verify that the employment of the solicitor or agent was properly authorized before the work was performed. To ensure timely processing, all bills of costs must be delivered to the trustee within seven days of a request; failure to do so may result in the estate being distributed without regard to those specific costs.

## Solicitor and Client Arrangements
A retainer of a solicitor constitutes a contract for the client to pay proper charges and disbursements for the work performed. The nature of these charges varies by the type of business:
*   **Conveyancing:** Remuneration is largely regulated by *ad valorem* scales based on the value of the property, as per the Solicitors' Remuneration Order 1882.
*   **Non-litigious Business:** Clients and solicitors may enter written agreements to fix costs as a percentage of the amount involved.
*   **Litigious Business:** The courts and the profession object to "no cure no pay" arrangements, as they are seen as leading to speculative actions. Furthermore, stipulations regarding a share of the proceeds from a successful action are considered illegal and champertous.

A traditional English solicitor's bill is a voluminous, itemized narrative detailing every act performed, including charges for individual letters written. Before a solicitor can recover these charges, they must deliver a signed bill of costs and wait one month before initiating a lawsuit.

## The Process of Taxation
Taxation is the mechanism used to ascertain the reasonableness of charges. The High Court maintains a threefold jurisdiction over solicitors' costs: through its authority over solicitors as its officers, via statutory power under the Solicitors Act 1843 and other legislation, and through ordinary jurisdiction to determine the reasonableness of claims.

### Orders for Taxation
A client may obtain an order for taxation as a matter of course within one month of the delivery of a solicitor's bill. Either the solicitor or the client may obtain such an order within twelve months of delivery. Beyond this twelve-month window, the court may still order taxation if special circumstances warrant it, up to twelve months after the actual payment has been made.

### Methods of Taxation
In litigious costs, two primary methods of taxation are utilized:
1.  **Between Party and Party:** This is the default method unless a special order is made. Under practice dating from 1902, the taxing office allows all expenses necessary or proper for defending rights or attaining justice. However, it excludes costs resulting from negligence, over-caution, unusual expenses, or special fees paid to witnesses or counsel.
2.  **Between Solicitor and Client:** These costs are taxed in the same office as litigious costs. While "party and party" costs were historically an imperfect indemnity for the winner, the High Court can now use its equitable jurisdiction (derived from the court of chancery) to order the losing party to pay costs as between solicitor and client, though this is most common in the chancery division.

## Review and Appeals
Decisions made by the taxing officer are not final if objections are properly raised. Objections to these decisions can be taken for review by a judge of the High Court and may further be appealed to the Court of Appeal.
