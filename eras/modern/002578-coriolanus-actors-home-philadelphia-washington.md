# Coriolanus, in the Actors' Home, Philadelphia, and the Washington

## The Sculpture of Edwin Forrest as Coriolanus
The depiction of Edwin Forrest as "Coriolanus" is a work of sculpture created by Thomas Ball. This piece is located in the Actors' Home in Philadelphia. 

## About the Artist
Thomas Ball (1819–1897) was an American sculptor born on June 3, 1819, in Charlestown, Massachusetts. The son of a house-and-sign-painter, Ball began his career as a self-taught portrait painter. He transitioned to sculpture in 1851, producing a bust of Jenny Lind as his earliest work. 

Ball spent over thirty years in Florence for study, though he returned to Boston, Massachusetts, for a period of work between 1857 and 1865. During his time in Florence, he was part of an artistic colony that included Hiram Powers and the Brownings. He eventually returned to America in 1897, maintaining a studio in New York City and residing in Montclair, New Jersey. In 1891, he published an autobiographical work titled *My Three Score Years and Ten*.

## Other Notable Works by Thomas Ball
Thomas Ball's work is noted for having a significant influence on monumental art in the United States, particularly within New England. In addition to the sculpture of Edwin Forrest, his portfolio includes:
* **Equestrian Statue of Washington:** Located in the Boston public gardens, this is considered probably his best work.
* **Washington Monument:** Located in Methuen, Massachusetts.
* **Daniel Webster:** Located in Central Park, New York City.
* **Lincoln Emancipation Group:** Located at Washington.
* **Josiah Quincy:** Located in City Hall Square, Boston.
* **Charles Sumner:** Located in the public gardens of Boston.
* **Cabinet Busts:** Early works depicting musicians, a subject Ball was familiar with as an accomplished musician and the first person in America to sing "Elijah."
