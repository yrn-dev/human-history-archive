# Khawak Pass

The Khawak pass is a geographical landmark situated on the crest of the Hindu Kush. It serves as a critical point of demarcation for the boundaries of Badakshan, a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan.

## Boundary and Geography
The southern boundary of Badakshan is carried along the crest of the Hindu Kush as far as the Khawak pass. From this point, the pass leads from Badakshan into the Panjshir valley. Eastward of the Khawak pass, the crest of the Hindu Kush is occupied by the Kafirs, though the exact extent of their presence north of the main watershed cannot be ascertained.

The topographical conformation of the region is characterized by a water-divide of the Hindu Kush that extends from the Dorah pass to the Khawak pass, passing through the center of Kafiristan. While this divide has never been accurately traced, its conformation is an evident continuation of the topography observed in the districts of Badakshan located to the west of the Khawak. The Hindu Kush in this region represents the southern edge of a great central plateau, which breaks into long spurs southwards. These spurs create rugged, snow-capped altitudes that isolate the hidden valleys of Kafiristan from one another.

## Regional Connectivity and Trade
The Khawak pass is part of a broader system of lateral valleys that drain the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush. These valleys run more or less parallel to the crest and reach westwards toward the pass. 

The region is linked to other major landmarks via the Kokcha river, which traverses Badakshan from the south-east to the north-west. The course of the Kokcha provides a high road across Badakshan, connecting several key locations at varying altitudes:
*   **The Oxus:** 1,000 ft.
*   **Faizabad:** 4,000 ft.
*   **Zebak:** 8,500 ft.

Beyond Zebak, at the bend of the Oxus, there is an insignificant pass reaching 9,500 ft. between Zebak and Ishkashim. From Ishkashim, the route continues via the Panja and through the Pamirs. This network constitutes what is described as one of the great continental high-roads of Asia, historically serving as a much-traversed trade route connecting China and Kashgar with Afghan Turkestan.

## Administrative Context
The Khawak pass sits within the broader context of Badakshan's provincial subdivisions. The province is divided into several districts:
*   **Central:** Faizabad, Farkhar, Minjan, and Kishm.
*   **East:** Charan, Ishkashim, Zebak, and Wakhan.
*   **North:** Darwaz, Ragh, and Shiwa.
*   **West:** Rustak, Kataghan, Ghori, Narin, and Anderab.

The administration of these districts varies; while central districts like Faizabad are under the immediate administration of the Mír of Badakhshan, others—including Zebak, Ishkashim, and Wakhan—are dependencies held by hereditary rulers or relations of the Mír on a feudal tenure.
