# BONNAT, LEON JOSEPH FLORENTIN

## Early Life and Education
Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat was a French painter born on June 20, 1833, in Bayonne. He received his education in Spain, where he studied in Madrid under Madrazo. His artistic development was significantly influenced by the Spanish realists and the work of Velasquez, a connection evident in his extensive series of portraits.

## Career and Professional Achievements
Bonnat established himself as one of the leading artists of his era in Paris. In 1869, he was awarded a medal of honour in the city. His professional ascent continued into the late 19th century; in 1888, he was appointed as a professor of painting at the Ecole des Beaux Arts. He later reached a high administrative position in the arts when he succeeded Paul Dubois as director in May 1905.

## Artistic Style and Works
Bonnat is most characterized by his vivid portrait-painting. In addition to his portraiture, he is recognized for his subject pictures. One of his famous works in this category is the "Martyrdom of St Denis," which is located in the Pantheon.

## Influence and Associations
Bonnat's influence extended to other artists; for example, the American landscape painter John Joseph Enneking studied under Bonnat in Paris during the period of 1873-1876. Additionally, Bonnat was a subject of the sculptor and painter Paul Dubois, who produced a bust of him noted for its refinement, likeness, and vivacity.
