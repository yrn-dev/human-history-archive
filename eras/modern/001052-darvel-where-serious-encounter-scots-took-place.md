# Darvel and Loudoun Hill

## Roman Occupation and Early History
Traces of Roman occupation have been identified in Ayrshire. During the campaigns led by Agricola, the region was held by the Damnonii. A site at Loudoun Hill, located near Darvel, has been identified as the town of Vandogara. It was at this location that a serious encounter with the Scots took place.

Following the withdrawal of the Romans, the area of Ayrshire became part of the kingdom of Strathclyde. Eventually, the region passed under the control of the Northumbrian kings.

## Medieval Conflicts and Sovereignty
The historical annals of the region are largely silent regarding intertribal troubles, with a notable exception in the 9th century when the Scottish king Alpin was slain at Dalmellington. 

Later, in 1263, the battle of Largs occurred. In this engagement, the Scots, under the leadership of Alexander III, crushed the pretensions of Haakon of Norway regarding the sovereignty of the Isles. 

## Wars of Independence
A generation after the battle of Largs, William Wallace conducted a vigorous campaign within the shire. His actions included surprising the English garrison at Ardrossan and burning the barns of Ayr, which were being used to lodge the forces of Edward I. 

In 1307, Robert Bruce defeated the English at Loudoun Hill. Bruce is alleged to have been born at Turnberry Castle, situated approximately 12 miles southwest of Ayr.

## Later Military and Political Events
The county was later visited by Cromwell, who demolished the castle of Ardrossan. It is said that Cromwell used the stones from that castle to rear a fort at Ayr.

Between 1660 and 1688, the sympathies of the county were almost entirely aligned with the Covenanters. This group suffered a heavy reverse at Airds Moss, a morass located between the Ayr and Lugar. Their leader, Richard Cameron, was killed there on July 20, 1680. During this period, the county was dragooned and ravaged by the Highland host. Later, the Hanoverian succession evoked no enthusiasm, though it did not excite active hostility.

## Antiquarian Remains
The region contains various antiquarian remains, including:
* **Cairns:** Found in Sorn, Galston, and other localities.
* **Roman Works:** A road believed to be Roman, extending from Ayr through Dalmellington and Dalrymple toward the Solway.
* **Camps:** Located on the hills of Dundonald and Knockgeorgan, these are attributed to the Danes or Norwegians.
* **Castles:** The ruins of Dunure, Ardrossan, Portencross, Dundonald, Turnberry, and Loch Doon.
* **Religious Sites:** Ruins of celebrated abbeys at Crossraguel and Kilwinning, as well as the haunted church of Alloway.
