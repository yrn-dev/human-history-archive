# Rio Colorado

The Rio Colorado is a river that flows through the extreme southern extension of the province of Buenos Aires in Argentina. In this region, the river covers a distance of approximately 80 m. The lower course of the Rio Colorado is subject to occasional disastrous inundations, and its mouth is obstructed.

## Geographic Context of Buenos Aires

The Rio Colorado is situated within the province of Buenos Aires, which is the largest and most populous province of the Argentine Republic. As of an official estimate in 1903, the population of the province was 1,251,000, and its total area is 117,812 sq. m. 

The province is bounded to the north by the provinces of Santa Fé and Entre Rios, and to the east by the Atlantic and the La Plata estuary. To the south, it is bounded by the Atlantic, and to the west, it is bordered by the provinces of Santa Fé and Córdoba, as well as the territories (gobernaciones) of Las Pampas and Rio Negro.

The general landscape of the province is a great treeless plain that extends from the Andes in the west to the Atlantic and La Plata estuary in the east. The central and south-eastern portions are characterized as low and marshy, presenting long-standing problems regarding effective drainage. The sea-shore from Bahia Blanca northward to the delta region of the Paraná is low and sandy, featuring a zone of partially submerged lands and lagoons immediately behind the coast.

## Regional Topography and Waterways

The province of Buenos Aires contains two ranges of low mountains in its southern region. The first extends north-east from Mar del Plata on the coast and includes the Sierra Baya, the Sierra de Tandil (1,476 ft.), and the Sierra del Volcan (885 ft.). The second, shorter range is located closer to Bahia Blanca and includes the Sierra de la Ventana (3,543 ft.) and the Sierra Pillahuinco.

The region is well watered by small rivers and numerous lakes. While the Rio Colorado marks the extreme southern extension, the largest river in the province is the Rio Salado del Sud. This river rises near the north-western boundary and flows in a south-easterly direction across the province for a course of about 360 m.

## Maritime Infrastructure

Despite having a frontage of over 900 m. on the Atlantic and the La Plata, the province possesses few natural ports of high quality. The most significant ports include:
* **Bahia Blanca**: The best natural port, where the Argentine government has built a naval port.
* **Ensenada (La Plata)**: A site featuring extensive artificial basins designed for ocean-going steamers.
* **Buenos Aires**: A location where a costly artificial port has been constructed.
* **San Nicolas**: A fairly good river port located in the extreme north.
