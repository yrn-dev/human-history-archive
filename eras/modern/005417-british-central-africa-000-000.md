# British Central Africa

## Population
British Central Africa has an estimated native population of 2,000,000. This population is part of a broader distribution of native peoples across the British empire's African territories, which include South Africa (5,211,329), East Africa (7,425,000), and West Africa (18,863,000).

## Demographics and Racial Characteristics
The tribes of Central Africa are noted as being less known than those of other regions. However, many of these tribes exhibit racial characteristics that are allied to the tribes found in South Africa. In some instances, there is an admixture of Arab blood among the populations of Central Africa.

## Geography and Climate
British Central Africa is categorized as one of the notable exceptions within the British empire regarding suitability for white settlement. While a greater portion of the empire's land surface lies within temperate zones suitable for such settlement, Central Africa—along with West Africa, East Africa, the West Indian colonies, New Guinea, British Borneo, the northern portion of Australia, and the northern reaches of North America—is not among those temperate regions.

## Imperial Context
The territory of British Central Africa is part of a global empire that occupies nearly one-quarter of the earth's land surface, totaling approximately 12,000,000 square miles. The native population of British Central Africa contributes to the total native population of the empire, which is calculated at 335,672,317. When combined with the white population of 53,040,468, the total population of the empire amounts to more than one-fourth of the world's total population (which is estimated at over 1,500,000,000).
