## Central Asia
Bokhara is identified as the most important trading town in central Asia, as well as its principal book-market. For ages, it has served as a center of religious life and learning. Following the Mongol invasions of the 13th century, which devastated other Moslem cities such as Samarkand, Bokhara remained independent and continued to be a chief seat of Islamitic learning. While many rich *madrasa* libraries were lost, confiscated by emirs, or destroyed by fire, rare books remain in private libraries, attracting bibliophiles from Turkey, Armenia, Persia, and Afghanistan.

The population of Bokhara is estimated between 50,000 and 100,000, according to different sources, and includes a large and ancient colony of Jews. In the city's bazaars, carpets, metal wares (particularly copper), silk stuffs, and Kara-kul (astrakhan) lamb-skins are sold. Nearby is New Bokhara (or Kagan), a Russian town located 8 miles from the city on territory ceded by the emir, with a population of 2,000.
