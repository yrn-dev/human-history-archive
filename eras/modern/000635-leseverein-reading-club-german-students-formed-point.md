# Leseverein of German Students

The *Leseverein*, or reading club, was an organization formed by German students at the university of Vienna. It served as a point of cohesion for Germans during a period of significant transition in the character of the German opposition in Austria.

## Origins and Purpose
The movement associated with the *Leseverein* can be traced back to approximately 1870. During this time, a group of young Germans emerged who prioritized the preservation and extension of their own nationality above all else. This group expressed little interest in constitutionalism or what they termed "legal mummies," focusing instead on the defense of German nationality. The *Leseverein* was established at the university of Vienna to provide a center of unity for these students.

## Political Context and Evolution
The formation of the *Leseverein* coincided with a shift in the German opposition, which became increasingly racial in nature. This growing national animosity introduced bitterness into political life and hindered the development of a strong, homogeneous party capable of supporting a government.

The movement found its first representative in parliament in Herr von Schoenerer. Schoenerer openly stated that Germans anticipated a union with the German empire. The participants of this movement were heavily influenced by figures outside of Austria; they regarded Bismarck as their national hero and celebrated the anniversary of Sedan as their political festival. For these individuals, approximation to Germany was more important than the maintenance of Austria.

## Expansion of the Movement
Following 1878, racial feelings intensified among the Radicals. By 1881, the various German opposition parties merged into a club known as the United Left. The programme of the United Left emphasized the defense of the position of Germans as a necessary condition for the existence of the state and demanded the express recognition of German as the official language.

Tensions eventually arose between the younger, more ardent spirits of the movement and the older constitutional leaders. The younger members criticized the party leadership for lacking decisiveness in measures of self-defence. This friction became evident in 1885 when the government forbade festivities in Graz intended to honor the eightieth birthday of Bismarck. The Germans of Styria expressed indignation that the party did not respond to this prohibition with sufficient energy, leading the Left to break into two separate groups following the elections of 1885.

## Outcome
Despite its role as a point of cohesion for German students at the university of Vienna, the *Leseverein* was eventually suppressed.
