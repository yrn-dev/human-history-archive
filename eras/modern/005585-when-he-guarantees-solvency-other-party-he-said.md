# Del Credere Terms

In commercial brokerage, a broker is an individual employed to facilitate the buying or selling of goods. For performing these duties according to the terms of their employment, the broker is entitled to payment known as "brokerage." This remuneration typically takes the form of a percentage of the total price of the goods involved, though the specific rate varies based on the conditions and nature of the business.

## Del Credere Employment
When a broker goes beyond the standard scope of facilitating a transaction and guarantees the solvency of the other party involved in the deal, they are said to be employed upon *del credere* terms. Because the broker assumes the risk of the other party's ability to pay, they are entitled to a higher rate of remuneration than a standard broker.

## Rights and Obligations of the Broker
The duties and rights of a broker vary depending on the specific trade and the nature of the agreement:

*   **Payment and Accounting:** In certain trades, it is customary for the buyer or the buyer's broker to pay the selling broker. In such instances, the selling broker is required to account to their principal for the purchase money.
*   **Reimbursement:** If a broker incurs liability or spends money on behalf of their principal during the course of their employment, they are entitled to be indemnified against that liability and reimbursed for the expenditures.
*   **Lack of Lien:** Unlike a factor, a broker does not have possession of the goods. Consequently, a broker possesses no lien to enforce their rights against their principal.
*   **Failure of Duty:** If a broker fails to perform their duties, they forfeit their rights to indemnity, reimbursement, and remuneration. Furthermore, the principal may bring an action for damages against the broker for breach of the employment contract.

## Related Financial Concepts
The concept of *del credere* is closely linked to the financial status of the parties involved, specifically regarding solvency and bankruptcy.

### Solvency and Insolvency
A person is described as "insolvent" when their financial liabilities exceed their means of meeting them. Insolvency does not always result in an immediate cessation of business; an insolvent person may continue operations using credit by incurring new debts to pay old ones. In some cases, a person may regain solvency without their creditors ever becoming aware of the insolvency.

### Bankruptcy
While insolvency refers to a financial state, bankruptcy is a legal status. Bankruptcy occurs when a debtor is declared by a judicial process to be unable to pay their debts. It is a statutory system for the administration of a debtor's property, which is removed from the debtor's personal control. While a debtor may be insolvent without being bankrupt—potentially avoiding bankruptcy through voluntary arrangements with creditors—it is impossible to be bankrupt without first being insolvent.

## Other Brokerage Types
The archive distinguishes the *del credere* broker from other specialized roles:

*   **Chartering Brokers:** These brokers are typically paid by the ship-owner once a charter-party is effected. They often include a provision in the charter-party for a percentage of the estimated freight. When performing "ship's business" in port, the chartering broker acts as the "ship's husband," which includes clearing the vessel at customs.
*   **Bill-Brokers:** Originally, bill-brokers acted as agents who procured the discounting of bills in London for country bankers. Over time, the practice evolved where the broker guaranteed the financier. Eventually, bill-brokers ceased depositing received bills with London bankers and began buying bills on their own account at a discount. In this capacity, the bill-broker borrows money on their own security and profit is made from the difference between the interest and the discount. In this specific role, the bill-broker acts as a principal rather than an agent and is therefore not a broker.
