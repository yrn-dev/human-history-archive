# Boppard

## Overview and Geography
Boppard is a town located in Germany within the Prussian Rhine province. It is situated on the left bank of the Rhine, 12 miles south of Coblenz, and is positioned on the mainline to Cologne. As of 1900, the population of the town was 5,806. Because it is less pent in by hills than various other locations within the picturesque gorge of the Rhine, Boppard has developed into a residential town in modern times and serves as a favorite center for tourists.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town is described as an old town that remains partly surrounded by medieval walls. Several noteworthy buildings define its landscape:
* **Religious Sites:** The town features a Roman Catholic parish church dating from the 12th and 13th centuries, a Carmelite church built in 1318, and an Evangelical church constructed in 1851 and later enlarged in 1887.
* **The Marienberg:** Crowning a hill 100 feet above the Rhine is the former Benedictine monastery of the Marienberg. Founded in 1123, this site has functioned as a hydropathic establishment since 1839.
* **Administrative Sites:** The town contains a former castle which is currently utilized for administrative offices.

## History
Founded by the Romans under the name *Baudobriga*, Boppard later served as a royal residence during the Merovingian dynasty. During the middle ages, the town was a significant center for shipping and commerce. Under the rule of the Hohenstaufen emperors, it was elevated to the status of a free imperial city.

The political administration of the town shifted in 1312 when Emperor Henry VII pledged Boppard to his brother, Baldwin, the archbishop-elector of Trier. The electors maintained possession of the town until it was absorbed by France during the Revolutionary epoch. Following this, the congress of Vienna assigned the town to Prussia in 1815.

## Economy and Industry
The direct trade of Boppard is centered on fruit and wine. While the town possesses some industries, they are characterized as comparatively insignificant; these include tobacco manufacture and tanning.
