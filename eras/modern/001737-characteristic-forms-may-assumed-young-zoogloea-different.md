# Zoogloea

Zoogloea is a stage in the life-history of Schizomycetes that corresponds to the "palmella" condition found in lower Algae. Once considered a distinct genus, it is now understood to be a resting condition of the Schizomycetes. This state occurs when dense "swarms" of vegetative cells become fixed within a matrix composed of their own contiguous, enormously swollen, and diffluent cell-walls.

## Formation and Composition
The zoogloea is formed through the active division of several mother-cells or a single mother-cell. The resulting progeny continue to secrete cell-wall substance, which absorbs water many times its own volume. This process creates a consistent matrix in which the cells come to rest. 

The composition of this matrix varies by case:
* **Chemical makeup:** In some instances, the matrix consists primarily of a proteid substance; in others, it is mainly cellulose.
* **Physical properties:** The matrix may be horny and resistant, or it may resemble a thick solution of gum.

Because of the gelatinous nature of the matrix, foreign bodies can become entangled within it. Additionally, compound zoogloeae may form when several distinct forms are apposed, a phenomenon frequently observed in macerating troughs.

## Physical Forms and Appearance
Zoogloea can manifest in various physical structures. It may appear as a membrane on the surface of a medium or as branched masses and irregular clumps submerged within the medium, sometimes reaching several inches across. 

Young zoogloea of different species can assume several characteristic forms, including:
* Spherical
* Ovoid
* Reticular
* Filamentous
* Lamellar
* Fruiticose

These forms may vary significantly as the mass increases in size or comes into contact with other masses. In older zoogloeae, oxide of iron may precipitate in the matrix if the metal is present in small quantities in the medium. When formed on a solid substratum, the zoogloea may become horny and firm, though immersion in water will soften it.

## Examples and Species
Various types of zoogloea have been identified across different species:
* ***Beggiatoa roseo-persicina*** (also known as *Bacterium rubescens* of Lankester): This species can form egg-shaped masses where the swollen walls of crowded cocci fuse into a common gelatinous envelope, or it may form reticulate zoogloea.
* ***Cladothrix***: This species exhibits branched fruticose zoogloea.
* ***Bacterium merismopedioides***: The zoogloea of this species contains cocci arranged in tablets.
* ***Myconostoc***: Colonies of this species are enveloped in a diffluent matrix.
* ***Micrococcus***: A zoogloea stage of *Micrococcus* can form a close membrane on infusion, referred to as Pasteur's *Mycoderma*.
* ***Bacillus subtilis***: This species can form a zoogloea pellicle.

## Biological Role and Symbiosis
Under favorable conditions, the elements within the zoogloea can become active again, moving out of the matrix to distribute themselves in the surrounding medium to grow and multiply.

The zoogloea form also plays a role in symbiosis between bacteria and plants. For example, the motile rod-like bacteria *Bacterium radicicola* found in arable soil enter the root-hairs of leguminous plants. They penetrate the root tissues in the form of a long, slimy zoogloea thread. This process leads to the production of a nodule in the tissues, where the bacteria multiply and develop. In this symbiotic relationship, nitrogen from the air is absorbed by the nodules, built into the bacterial cell, and eventually handed on to the host-plant.
