# The South-East Drainage Basin of Bolivia

The south-east drainage basin is one of the three distinct river-system regions of Bolivia, alongside the Amazon and the Central Plateau. It is characterized as being smaller and economically less important than the drainage basin of the Madeira.

## Geographic Extent and General Characteristics
The basin discharges into the Paraguay river. Its geographic boundaries extend from the Cordillera Oriental in the west to the Paraguay in the east, and from the Sierras de Chiquitos in the north south to the Argentine frontier. 

## The Pilcomayo River System
The Pilcomayo is the only large river within Bolivia in this drainage basin. It originates on the east slopes of the Cordillera Oriental, opposite the south end of Lake Pampa Aullaguas. From its source, the river flows in an east and south-east direction through the sierra region until it reaches the Bolivian Chaco. 

The Pilcomayo flows through a nearly level country. Because its current is so sluggish, its channels are greatly obstructed. While the tributaries of the river within the Chaco are not definitely known, it possesses several small tributaries in the sierra region. The largest of these include the Mataca, the Cachimayo, and the Pilaya (also known as the Camblaya); the latter is formed by the San Juan and the Cotagaita.

## Other Regional Waterways
Several other streams characterize the south-east drainage region, though many are limited in size or disappear before reaching a major outlet:

*   **The Tarija (Rio Grande):** This is a large tributary from the Bolivian uplands that flows into the Bermejo, which is an Argentine river. The Tarija drains a small district located south-east of the Santa Victoria sierra.
*   **The Otuquis:** This is the most southern group of the Bolivian tributaries of the upper Paraguay, which are generally described as small and unimportant. The Otuquis is formed by the Tucabaca and San Rafael, both of which drain the slopes of the Cerro Cochii range. The river is lost in great marshes approximately 50 m. from the Paraguay.
*   **The Parapiti:** Another considerable stream in the region, the Parapiti rises on the eastern slopes of the Sierra de Misiones. It flows north-east through a low plain for about 150 m. before it is lost in the great marshy districts of the Bolivian plain.
