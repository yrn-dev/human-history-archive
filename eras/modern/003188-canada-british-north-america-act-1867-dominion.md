# The Dominion of Canada and the British North America Act 1867

The Dominion of Canada was established in 1867 under the British North America Act, a new constitution that created a Federal Dominion to relieve internal strains caused by serious revolts and dissensions between British and French inhabitants. This confederation was the result of a broad scheme of British-American union advocated by figures such as Macdonald and Alexander Galt in Upper Canada, as well as Joseph Howe and others in the maritime provinces. The movement received a decisive impulse from the international situation, including the dangers of the period, the Fenian raids across the American border, and the conclusion of the American Civil War.

## Formation and Legislation
The process of confederation began with a conference at Charlottetown in 1864, where Macdonald led a representative delegation from Quebec and Ontario. The outline agreed upon there was further detailed at a subsequent conference in Quebec. The final framing of the British North America Act took place between December 1866 and January 1867 at the Westminster Palace Hôtel in London. This work was conducted by provincial delegates in cooperation with the law officers of the Crown, presided over by Lord Carnarvon, the secretary of state for the colonies.

The Act defines the jurisdiction of the federal parliament as distinct from that of the provincial legislatures. While provinces make laws for their own governance within their specific jurisdiction, the governor-general in council may disallow a provincial act within one year of its passage. Furthermore, the legality of such acts can be challenged in the supreme court of Canada or appealed to the juridical committee of the privy council of the United Kingdom.

## Governance and Political Leadership
Sir John Macdonald took the leading role in the discussions surrounding the Act and became the first premier of the Dominion. He held the position of premier from 1867 until his death on June 6, 1891. In his final years, Macdonald focused heavily on the development and organization of the North-West. He remained a committed British subject throughout his political life, a sentiment expressed in his last manifesto to the people of the Dominion.

The federal parliament sits at Ottawa. British Columbia, as a province of the Dominion, contributes seven members to the lower house and three senators.

## Territorial Expansion and Development
Following the 1867 confederation, the Dominion expanded its territory. Prince Edward Island entered the confederation in 1873. By 1880, all British possessions in North America, with the exception of Newfoundland, were annexed to the Dominion. In 1870, Rupert's Land was transferred to Canada, leading to the cessation of the Hudson's Bay Company's district of Assiniboia (formed in 1835). A new district of Assiniboia was later established by a Dominion parliament act in 1875, extending from the western boundary of Manitoba to 111 deg. W. In 1905, the districts of Assiniboia and Saskatchewan were united to form the province of Saskatchewan. Similarly, Alberta was separated from the North-West Territories of Canada in 1905.

The growth of the Dominion was significantly altered by the railway, which allowed Canada to expand westward and market produce, such as corn, in Europe. Along with the telegraphic cable and the steamship, the railway helped Canada remain a single community in terms of interests, sympathy, and language despite its extensive growth.

## Provincial Jurisdictions and Legal Systems
The British North America Act grants the Dominion parliament exclusive legislative power regarding insolvency and bankruptcy. However, there has been no existing Dominion act on the subject for some time; an act passed in 1875 was repealed in 1880. This failure is attributed to the contrasting provincial systems, specifically the French code based on *cessio bonorum* in Quebec and the English law used in Ontario. Consequently, bankruptcy is handled fragmentarily by provincial legislatures through acts regulating preferences, fraudulent assignments, the administration of estates of deceased insolvents, priority of execution creditors, and the imprisonment of debtors.

In British Columbia, the supreme court—presided over by a chief justice and five puisne judges—maintains jurisdiction over divorce cases, a right the colony possessed prior to confederation.

## Geography of British Columbia
British Columbia serves as the western province of the Dominion. Its boundaries are defined by the Pacific Ocean to the west and the coast strip of Alaska for 400 miles. The northern boundary is 60° N., and the southern boundary is formed by 49° N. and the strait separating Vancouver Island from the state of Washington. To the east, it is bounded by the continental watershed in the Rocky Mountains until it intersects 120° W. The province includes the Queen Charlotte Islands, Vancouver Island, and various smaller islands off the western coast.
