# Cronica dei Matematici

The *Cronica dei Matematici* is a historical work published at Urbino in 1707. It serves as an abridgment of a more extensive project authored by the Italian mathematician and writer Bernardino Baldi.

## Author and Origins
Bernardino Baldi (1533–1617) was born on June 6, 1533, into a noble family in Urbino. He was a scholar of significant versatility, described as perhaps the most universal genius of his age. Baldi pursued his studies at Padua with great success and zeal, and it is noted that he acquired either twelve or sixteen languages during his life, depending on whether one follows the inscription on his tomb or other accounts.

Baldi's professional life included serving as a tutor to Ferrante (Ferdinand) Gonzaga, which led to his appointment as the abbot of the rich abbey of Guastalla, a position he held for twenty-five years. Later, in 1612, the duke employed him as an envoy to Venice. While there, Baldi delivered a congratulatory oration before the Venetian senate regarding the election of Andrea Memmo as the new doge.

## The Work and Its Scope
The *Cronica dei Matematici* is an abridged version of a larger work that Baldi spent twelve years composing. The original, more comprehensive version of the text was intended to provide the lives of more than two hundred mathematicians. 

Although the *Cronica dei Matematici* was published in 1707, Baldi himself died much earlier, on October 12, 1617, in his native city of Urbino. He is said to have written over one hundred different works, though the majority of these remained unpublished. His collective writings demonstrate his proficiency across multiple disciplines, including poetry, history, geography, theology, mathematics, and antiquarianism.

## Context of Urbino
The work was published in Urbino, a city located within the territorial division of Italy known as the Marches (Le Marche). This region encompasses several provinces, including Ancona, Macerata, Ascoli Piceno, and Pesaro and Urbino. The province of Pesaro and Urbino is situated within the boundaries of ancient Umbria.
