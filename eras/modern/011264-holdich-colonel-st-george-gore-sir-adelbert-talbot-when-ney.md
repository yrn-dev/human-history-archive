# Exploration and Mapping of Central Asia: Holdich, Gore, Talbot, and Elias

The late 19th century marked a period of significant geographical discovery in Central Asia, as European explorers and military officers sought to map the highlands of the Pamirs and the regions of the great central depression. Much of this progress was achieved through official boundary commissions and adventurous expeditions that expanded the known maps of the continent.

## The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission
A major advancement in the knowledge of Central Asiatic geography occurred during the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884–1886. During this period, the regions of the Oxus and Afghan Turkestan were mapped by a team including Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich. 

Colonel Sir Thomas Hungerford Holdich, a Colonel in the Royal Engineers, was a central figure in these surveys. In addition to his work with the Boundary Commission, he served as the Superintendent of Frontier Surveys in India from 1892 to 1898 and was the H.M. Commissioner for the Persa-Beluch Boundary in 1896. His contributions to cartography include a map of Persia published between 1897 and 1899. He was awarded the Gold Medal of the Royal Geographical Society in 1887 and authored works such as *The Gates of India* and *The Indian Borderland* (1901).

## The Expeditions of Ney Elias
Concurrent with the work of the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission, Ney Elias conducted a significant crossing from China. Traveling through Badakshan and the Pamirs, Elias eventually reached the camp of the commission. During this journey, he identified Rangkul, described as the great "Dragon Lake." His findings were later detailed in "Explorations in Central Asia," published in the *Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society* between 1886 and 1887.

## Related Geographical Surveys
The mapping of the region was furthered by other contemporary missions. Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart led a mission across the Hindu Kush into Wakhan, returning to India via the Bashgol valley of Kafiristan. This expedition allowed Colonel Woodthorpe to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush and verify previous explorations conducted by W.W. M'Nair.

In 1895, during the Pamir Boundary Commission, Colonel R.A. Wahab and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich carried the Pamir triangulation across the Hindu Kush. This work was later extended by Captain H. Deasy of the 16th Lancers, who continued the triangulation into the plains of Kashgar and to the sources of the Zarafshan.

Other notable contributions to the map of Asia during this era include:
* **Arthur Douglas Carey and Andrew Dalgleish (1885):** Who followed the tracks of Prjevalsky to provide new geographical data.
* **Captain (later Sir Francis) Younghusband (1886):** Who crossed the Muztagh, the mountain barrier between Kashmir and China, in a journey across the heart of the continent.
* **Sir Douglas Forsyth (1870):** Whose mission to Yarkand preceded these later commissions; his mission included Robert Barkley Shaw and Henry Trotter, the latter of whom produced the first maps of Chinese Turkestan.
