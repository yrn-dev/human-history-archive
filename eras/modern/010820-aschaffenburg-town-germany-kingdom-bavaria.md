# ASCHAFFENBURG

Aschaffenburg is a town in Germany, located within the kingdom of Bavaria. It is situated on the right bank of the river Main at the point where the Aschaff joins it, near the foot of the Spessart. By rail, the town is located 26 miles southeast of Frankfort-on-Main. The population of the town was recorded as 18,091 in 1900 and grew to 25,275 by 1905.

## History
Originally a Roman settlement, Aschaffenburg served as a station for the 23rd and 10th Roman legions. Following this period, the Frankish mayors of the palace constructed a castle upon the ruins of the Roman *castrum*. Early religious foundations include a Benedictine monastery and a chapel to St Martin, both established by Bonifacius. In 989, Archbishop Willigis constructed a stone bridge over the Main, and the town's importance was further increased by Adalbert around 1122.

In the Middle Ages, the town was known as Askenburg and Aschafaburg. It hosted a synod in 1292 and an imperial diet in 1474; the latter was a preliminary to the diet of Vienna and resulted in a concordat sometimes referred to as the Aschaffenburg Concordat. The town experienced significant suffering during the Thirty Years' War, as it was occupied by various belligerents. In 1866, the Prussians achieved a severe victory over the Austrians in the vicinity of the town.

Between 1842 and 1849, King Louis constructed a country house to the west of the town known as the *Pompeianum*, which was designed as an imitation of the house of Castor and Pollux at Pompeii.

## Administration and Territory
The principality of Aschaffenburg, which covered an area of 654 English square miles, took its name from the city. Originally part of the electorate of Mainz, it was transferred to the archchancellor, Archbishop Charles of Dalberg, in 1803. In 1806, the principality was annexed to the grand-duchy of Frankfort. Following a treaty between Austria and Bavaria concluded on June 19, 1814, it was transferred to Bavaria. It currently forms a district of the kingdom of Bavaria alongside lower Franconia.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town features several notable buildings:
* **Johannisburg:** Built between 1605 and 1614 by Archbishop Schweikard of Cronberg, this structure houses a collection of paintings, engravings, and a library containing *incunabula*.
* **Stiftskirche (Cathedral):** Founded in 980 by Otto of Bavaria, the majority of the current structure dates from the 13th and early 12th centuries. It contains a sarcophagus holding the relics of St Margaret (1540) and various monuments created by the Vischers.
* **Other Structures:** The town includes a Capuchin hospital, several mansions belonging to the German nobility, and a theatre that previously served as the house of the Teutonic order. An archaeological museum is located within the buildings of the old abbey.

## Education and Culture
Aschaffenburg has been recognized for its educational establishments since the 10th century. Current institutions include a lyceum, a gymnasium, a seminarium, and other schools. Notably, in 1823, Johann Joseph Ignaz von Dollinger served as a professor of canon law and ecclesiastical history at the lyceum.

The town's churchyard contains the graves of Christian Brentano (who died in 1851) and his brother Klemens Brentano. Wilhelm Heinse is also buried within the town.

## Economy and Industry
The town's primary manufactures include cellulose, tobacco, lime, liqueurs, ready-made clothing, and both white and coloured paper. Additionally, Aschaffenburg engages in a significant export trade via the Main, shipping wine, cattle, and wood.
