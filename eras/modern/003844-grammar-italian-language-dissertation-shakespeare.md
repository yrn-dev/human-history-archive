# Grammar of the Italian Language and the Works of Giuseppe Baretti

The study of the Italian language and its literary criticism in the 18th century is exemplified by the works of Giuseppe Marc' Antonio Baretti. A critic and author, Baretti produced a useful *Dictionary and Grammar of the Italian Language*, contributing to the formalization of the language's rules for students and scholars.

## Giuseppe Marc' Antonio Baretti
Giuseppe Marc' Antonio Baretti (1719–1789) was an Italian critic born in Turin. Although his father intended for him to enter the legal profession, Baretti fled Turin at the age of sixteen for Guastalla, where he worked in a mercantile house. During his leisure time, he developed expertise in criticism and literature. For many years, Baretti lived a wandering life, supporting himself through his writing.

Eventually, Baretti settled in London for a significant period. During his time there, he served as the secretary to the Royal Academy of Painting and became associated with figures such as Garrick and Samuel Johnson. He was also a frequent visitor to the Thrales and is mentioned repeatedly in Boswell's *Life*. In 1769, Baretti was tried for murder after wounding a man with a fruit knife during a street assault; however, he was acquitted after Samuel Johnson provided evidence in his favor. Baretti died in May 1789, and his collected works were published in Milan in 1838.

## Literary Contributions and Criticism
Baretti's first significant work was the *Italian Library*, published in London in 1757, which served as a catalogue of the lives and works of various Italian authors. Between 1761 and 1765, he traveled through France, Portugal, and Spain, documenting these journeys in *Lettere famigliari*. These letters were published in English in 1770 in four volumes and received high commendation from Samuel Johnson.

While traveling in Italy, Baretti authored a journal of literary criticism titled *Frusta letteraria* (the literary scourge). This work was published under difficult circumstances and was eventually discontinued. While some of his criticisms of contemporary writers were considered just, they were frequently marked by coarseness and undue vehemence.

## Dissertations on Shakespeare and Voltaire
In addition to his linguistic contributions through his *Dictionary and Grammar of the Italian Language*, Baretti authored a dissertation focusing on the works of Shakespeare and Voltaire. This work represents his engagement with the major literary figures of the European tradition.

## Italian Linguistic Influence in Other Texts
The presence of the Italian language is also noted in historical manuscripts. In the case of the travels of Marco Polo, the text is written in a form of French that is not the "Frenche of Paris." This version of the language is characterized by a "consummate confusion" of tenses, moods, numbers, subject, and object. In this specific manuscript, Italian words are frequently introduced, appearing either in a crude form or "rudely Gallicized." This linguistic blending is attributed to the fact that the original words were written down by Rusticiano, a Tuscan, from the dictation of Marco, a Venetian who had become "Orientalized," in a language (French) that was foreign to both men.
