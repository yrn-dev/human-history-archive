# Missions Evangeliques de Paris

The Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris was a missionary organization that established a presence in the Barotse kingdom. Its activities in the region began in 1884, following an earlier two-year period (1882–1884) spent in the country by an Englishman named F. S. Arnot. The mission established by the Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris is described as having been fruitful in its results.

## Francois Coillard
The first agent of the mission was Francois Coillard (1834–1904). Prior to his appointment in Barotseland, Coillard had been involved in mission work in Basutoland. After arriving in the Barotse kingdom, he devoted the remainder of his life to the region. 

Beyond his religious duties, Coillard contributed to the early recording of the region's environment; he began taking records of rainfall in 1884. These records indicate that since that year, Barotseland experienced no droughts, contrasting with the periodic droughts suffered by South Africa.

## Political Relations and Influence
Francois Coillard maintained a complex relationship with the political developments of the region. He was an admirer of British institutions and expressed a desire for the country to eventually fall under British jurisdiction. However, for the sake of his mission, he initially sought to delay the arrival of white men into the country.

This perspective put him at odds with Lewanika, the paramount chief of the Barotse. Contrary to Coillard's advice, Lewanika petitioned the "Great White Queen" to assume a protectorate over his dominions.

## Context of Western Presence
The missionaries, including those from the Societe des Missions Evangeliques de Paris, are identified as the pioneers of Western civilization in the region, preceding the agents of the British South Africa Company. 

The British South Africa Company later established rule in regions north of the Middle Zambezi (excluding Portuguese dominions) via a charter granted in October 1889. Following a treaty between Great Britain and Portugal on June 11, 1891, the Barotse kingdom was declared to be within the British sphere of influence. The company subsequently entered into friendly relations with Lewanika, appointing an administrator to reside in the country and raising a native police force commanded by a British officer. Despite these administrative changes, the company did not interfere in the internal affairs of the Barotse.
