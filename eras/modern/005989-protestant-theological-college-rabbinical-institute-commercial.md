# Educational and Professional Institutions

The establishment of specialized higher education, including theological colleges, rabbinical institutes, and commercial academies, varies significantly across different global urban centers. These institutions serve to train professional ministries, legal and medical practitioners, and specialists in trade and science.

## Theological and Religious Institutions

Theological education is provided through various denominational seminaries and colleges across Europe and the United States. 

In Budapest, the city's educational landscape includes a Protestant theological college, a Roman Catholic seminary, and a rabbinical institute. Similarly, in the United States, Baltimore hosts St. Joseph's Seminary and St. Mary's Seminary; the latter was established by the Society of St. Sulpice in 1791 and is regarded as the oldest Catholic theological seminary in the United States.

In Kentucky, the city of Louisville is home to the Southern Baptist Theological Seminary, founded in 1859. It also hosts the Presbyterian Theological Seminary of Kentucky, which was created in 1901 through the consolidation of the Louisville Presbyterian Theological Seminary (1893) and the Theological Seminary of the Presbyterian Church at Danville (1853).

Baptist education in England and other regions has historically relied on voluntary colleges. The Bristol Baptist College was erected around 1811, following the efforts of the Bristol Education Society (formed in 1770) and a bequest from Edward Terrill for the instruction of young men training for the ministry. Other Baptist institutions include a college in London (formed in 1810 and moved to Regent's Park in 1856), the Pastors' College connected to the Metropolitan Tabernacle (1856), and the Baptist College at Manchester (1866), which serves "Strict" Baptist views. Additionally, the Midland Baptist College in Nottingham has operated since 1797. Baptist theological colleges are also located in Glasgow, Ireland, and Wales, with a combined total of approximately 210 students.

## Commercial and Technical Education

Institutions dedicated to commercial and technical training are integrated into the educational systems of several major cities to provide scientific and vocational skills.

Budapest features a commercial academy, which was expanded in 1899 to include an academy for the study of oriental languages. In Russia, the city of Kharkov possesses a commercial school alongside other higher education facilities such as a technological institute and a railway engineering school.

In Bristol, the technical college of the Company of Merchant Venturers, established in 1885, provides commercial, technical, and scientific education, although its extensive buildings were destroyed by fire in 1906.

## General University and Professional Schools

Major cities often center their intellectual activity around a primary university and associated professional colleges.

Budapest's university, established in 1635 at Tyrnau before moving to Buda (1777) and then Pest (1783), serves as the intellectual capital of Hungary. By 1900, it had nearly 200 professors and lecturers and 4,983 students.

In the United States, the university of Louisville includes a medical department (1837), a law department (1847), and a College of Liberal Arts (1907). In Baltimore, the university of Maryland and Baltimore University both offer courses in dentistry, medicine, and law. Other specialized schools in Baltimore include the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery, the Woman's Medical College, and the Baltimore Medical College.

In India, Bombay University was constituted in 1857 as an examining body. The city's primary educational institutions include the government law school, the government Elphinstone College, the Grant medical college, the Victoria Jubilee technical institute, and two missionary colleges, St. Xavier and Wilson.

## Specialized and Charitable Education

Various cities maintain schools for specific demographics or vocational needs. Baltimore features the McDonogh farm school, founded via a bequest from John McDonogh, which uses a 835-acre farm and shops for carpentry, machines, and printing to prepare poor boys for college. Danville, Kentucky, hosts the Kentucky state institution for deaf mutes (1823) and Caldwell College for women (1860). In Bristol, Colston's girls' day school (1891) provides instruction in calisthenics and domestic economy.
