# British Columbia and Vancouver Island

## Geography and Boundaries
British Columbia is the western province of the Dominion of Canada. Its eastern boundary is defined by the continental watershed in the Rocky Mountains, continuing north-westerly until it intersects 120° W., which is then followed north to 60° N. This boundary includes a portion of the Peace river country located east of the mountains. The northern boundary is set at 60° N., while the western boundary is the Pacific Ocean, where the province fronts for approximately 600 m. Additionally, the province fronts the coast strip of Alaska for 400 m. The southern boundary is formed by 49° N. and the strait that separates Vancouver Island from the state of Washington. The province includes Vancouver Island, the Queen Charlotte Islands, and various smaller islands off the western coast of Canada.

## Early Exploration and Administration
The discovery of British Columbia was made by the Spaniard Perez in 1774. Geographical exploration of the coast began in 1778 with a visit from Cook. Between 1792 and 1794, Vancouver surveyed almost the entire coast of British Columbia, as well as significant portions to the north and south, on behalf of the British government. Around the same time, Mackenzie and traders from the N.W. Company entered the interior. In 1821, the N.W. Company amalgamated with the Hudson's Bay Company, which subsequently ruled the immense territory for twenty-eight years with beneficent despotism.

## Colonial Development and Confederation
Vancouver Island was proclaimed a British colony in 1849. In 1858, following the discovery of gold and a large influx of miners, the mainland territory was established as a colony named British Columbia. These two colonies were united under the name British Columbia in 1866.

In 1871, British Columbia entered the confederation to become part of the Dominion of Canada. As part of this arrangement, the province sends six (now seven) members to the House of Commons of the federal parliament and three senators. A primary condition of entering the dominion was the speedy construction of the Canadian Pacific railway. When this promise was not fulfilled by 1876, and the government in Ottawa appeared indifferent to the representations of British Columbia, relations became strained. These tensions were only ameliorated once the construction of a transcontinental road began.

## Urban Centers and Economic Development
The capital of the province is Victoria, located on Vancouver Island. Other chief cities include Vancouver on the mainland, Nanaimo on Vancouver Island, and New Westminster on the Fraser. Other places of importance include Grand Forks in the Boundary district, as well as Nelson and Rossland in West Kootenay, and Fernie in East Kootenay.

Key events in the province's subsequent history include the founding of the city of Vancouver by the C.P.R. and the discovery of the Boundary and Kootenay mining districts. The province also established the first Canadian steamship lines to Australia and to Japan and China. Additionally, the province was involved in disputes with the United States regarding pelagic sealing.
