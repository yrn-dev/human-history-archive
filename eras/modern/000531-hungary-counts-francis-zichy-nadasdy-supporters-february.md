# Hungary and the Mid-19th Century Political Transition

The political history of Hungary during the mid-19th century was defined by a struggle for the restoration of its independence and a conflict between conservative magnates and supporters of constitutional governance. This period was marked by shifting administrative appointments and a complex relationship with the Austrian empire, eventually leading to a reorganized dual monarchy.

## The February Constitution and Administrative Shifts
In the years leading up to the war of 1866, the Magyar nation and the Czechs refused to recognize the validity of the 1861 constitution, which had attempted to establish a common parliament for the entire empire. The Hungarians specifically demanded the restoration of the independence of the kingdom of Hungary.

The necessity of reaching terms with the Hungarians was recognized by Emperor Francis Joseph prior to the 1866 conflict. In June 1865, the emperor visited Pest to implement a change in leadership. He replaced the chancellors of Hungary and Transylvania, Counts Francis Zichy and Nadasdy, with Count Majlath. While Zichy and Nadasdy were supporters of the February constitution, Count Majlath was a leader of the old conservative magnates. This administrative shift was followed by the resignation of Schmerling, who was succeeded by Count Richard Belcredi.

## The War of 1866 and the Restoration of Rights
The war of 1866 initiated a new era for the Austrian empire. Following the treaty of Prague on August 23, 1866, the emperor surrendered his ancestral position in Germany, and territories such as Bohemia, Salzburg, Tirol, and Austria ceased to be German. Additionally, the surrender of Venetia ended Habsburg domination in Italy.

These military defeats provided the Hungarian diet with the leverage to dictate its own terms for reorganization. The diet rejected any union between Hungary and other parts of the monarchy unless it was based on the Pragmatic Sanction. All innovations and attempts made over the previous century to absorb Hungary into a "greater Austria" were revoked. Under the resulting agreement, the emperor was required to be crowned at Pest and take the ancient oath to the Golden Bull.

## The Ausgleich and the Dual Monarchy
The relations between Austria and Hungary were formally regulated by the *Ausgleich*, or "Compromise," concluded on February 8, 1867. This agreement, alongside the Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 and the diploma of Emperor Francis Joseph I from October 20, 1860, formed the basis of the monarchy's constitution.

Under this system, Austria and Hungary existed as two completely independent states, each maintaining its own government and parliament. The unity of the Dual Monarchy was expressed through a common head of state—who held the titles of Apostolic King of Hungary and Emperor of Austria—and a joint administration of specific affairs:
*   **Foreign Affairs:** Including consular and diplomatic representation.
*   **The Army:** Including the navy, though this excluded the special army of each state and the annual voting of recruits.
*   **Finance:** Specifically regarding joint expenditures.

## National Identity and Military Tension
Despite the Compromise, tensions persisted regarding Magyar nationalist feeling and the prerogatives of the Hungarian Chamber. In the autumn of 1902, the Austrian and Hungarian governments proposed bills to increase annual recruit contingents by 21,000 men (with Hungary contributing approximately 6,500 and Austria 14,500). 

Conflict arose when military authorities detained several thousand supernumerary recruits in barracks to avoid the delay of re-summoning them after parliamentary sanction. This move was resented by the Hungarian Chamber. The Opposition, led by Francis Kossuth (the eldest son of Louis Kossuth) and the independence party, obstructed ministerial measures until the recruits were discharged at the end of 1902. The Opposition further demanded that the Army bills be withdrawn or counterbalanced by concessions that would promote the use of the Magyar language within the army and increase the Magyar character of Hungarian regiments.
