# Funtumia elastica

## Overview and Commercial Importance
*Funtumia elastica* is a species of rubber tree. In certain regions, it is identified as the most important vegetable product from a commercial perspective. Its production is noted as being similar to the vegetable products found on the Gold Coast.

## Regional Distribution and Related Trade
The rubber tree is a significant commercial asset in Ashanti. The trade infrastructure of this region is centered around Kumasi, which serves as the distributing center for the hinterland and the whole of Ashanti. Trade from this area moves southward through British territory, with figures included in those of the Gold Coast, though Ashanti also maintains considerable trade with its German and French neighbors, as well as the Niger countries.

In other regions, such as Dahomey proper, plantations of rubber trees and vines have also been established. In Dahomey, rubber is one of the products exported along with copra, kola-nuts, and dried fish.

## Associated Botanical and Agricultural Products
In the regions where *Funtumia elastica* is commercially significant, other vegetable products are frequently cultivated:

*   **Kola Nut:** The nut of the kola tree is in great demand. In Ashanti, the town of Kintampo—located approximately 130 miles north-east of Kumasi—serves as a depot for government stores and a center for the kola-nut commerce. In Dahomey, kola-nuts are also produced and exported. Chemically, the kola nut contains small quantities of caffeine (also known as theine).
*   **Cocoa and Tobacco:** Since 1905, many cocoa plantations have been established, particularly in the eastern districts of Ashanti. Tobacco is cultivated in the northern regions of the same area.
*   **Other Exports:** Gum copal is exported from the region. In Dahomey, the primary agricultural prosperity depends on the export of palm-kernels and palm-oil, the latter of which is produced in large quantities, specifically in the district of Toffo.

## Infrastructure and Economic Context
The transport of commercial goods in the auriferous regions of Ashanti is supported by a railway from Sekondi to Kumasi, which was completed in 1903. The economic revenue of the region is derived from several sources, including contributions from gold-mining companies, rents from government land, liquor licences, and caravan taxes.
