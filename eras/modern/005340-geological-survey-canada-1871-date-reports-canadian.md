# Geological Survey of Canada

The Geological Survey of Canada is a scientific body whose reports have been produced from 1871 to the present date. These reports serve as a primary authority for the study of the region, contributing to the broader body of departmental reports issued by the Dominion and Provincial governments, as well as the Minister of Mines.

## Mapping and Territorial Responsibilities
The Geological Survey of Canada is responsible for providing maps for specific territories within the Dominion. These include:
* **Yukon**: Maps produced on a scale of 1:380,200.
* **Labrador**: Maps produced on a scale of 1:1,584,000.

These maps are issued through the surveyor-general, who is attached to the department of the interior in Ottawa.

## Relation to Other Surveying Activities
The work of the Geological Survey exists alongside various other cadastral and topographical surveying efforts in Canada. Cadastral surveys are managed by a Dominion land office and a commission of Crown-lands in the older provinces. Since 1906, the surveyor-general has published topographical maps at a scale of 1:63,366, which utilize spirit-levelling for contours (spaced 25 ft. apart) and theodolite traverses (spaced 15 m. apart). 

Other specialized mapping includes "sectional maps" (1:190,000 and 1:40,000) and "Standard" topographical maps for the thinly populated west (1:250,000 and 1:500,000). Additionally, the intelligence branch of the Canadian department of military defence has published topographical maps with contours since 1904 on scales of 1:63,366 and 1:126,730. In 1909, a geodetic survey department was established under the direction of the Dominion's chief astronomer, Dr. W. F. King.

## Context within Regional Research
The reports of the Geological Survey of Canada are cited as essential references for the history and geography of various regions, including British Columbia. In the study of British Columbia, these reports are used in conjunction with other sources such as the *Annual Reports of the British Columbia Board of Trade*, the *Catalogue of Provincial Museum*, and the *Reports of Canadian Pacific (Government) Surveys* (1872-1880).
