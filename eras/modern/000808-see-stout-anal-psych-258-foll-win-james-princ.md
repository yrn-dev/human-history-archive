# Automatic Acts and Sensory Automatism

## Definitions of Automatic Acts
Automatic acts are categorized into two primary types based on the focus of the individual's attention. The first type is described as purely automatic; this occurs when an action continues to be performed while the person's attention is focused entirely on subjects that are different from the action itself, such as during the act of cycling.

The second type is known as relative automatism. This occurs when the individual's attention is fixed specifically on a particular part of the action or on the end goal of the action, while the other component parts required to complete the action are performed unconsciously.

## Sensory Automatism
In the field of psychical research, the term "sensory automatism" is used to describe hallucinations that are initiated centrally. These hallucinations are frequently provoked by the practice of crystal-gazing. While crystal-gazing is a common cause, auditory hallucinations can also be triggered by the use of a shell, a phenomenon referred to as shell-hearing. Additionally, other senses are occasionally affected by this type of automatism.

## Academic References
The study of these phenomena is associated with the works of G. F. Stout, specifically in *Anal. Psych* (i. 258 foll.), and William James in *Princ. of Psych.* (i. chap. 5). Further information on these topics can be found in general articles regarding psychology and suggestion.
