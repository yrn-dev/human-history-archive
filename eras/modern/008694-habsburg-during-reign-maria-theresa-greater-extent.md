# The Habsburg Monarchy under Maria Theresa and Joseph II

The reign of Maria Theresa (1740–1780) and her son Joseph II (1765–1790) marked a transformative era for the Habsburg realm, characterized by a shift toward centralization, the struggle for territorial integrity, and a fundamental restructuring of internal administration.

## Accession and the Pragmatic Sanction
The stability of the Habsburg succession was first addressed by Emperor Charles VI, who, lacking a male heir, issued the Pragmatic Sanction on April 19, 1713. This instrument proclaimed the indivisibility of the monarchy and settled the succession on his daughter, Maria Theresa. To secure international adherence, Charles VI made various diplomatic concessions, including the cession of Parma, Piacenza, and Guastalla to Don Carlos in exchange for the agreement of Great Britain and Holland in 1731. Spain and Sardinia later adhered to the sanction.

Despite these efforts, Maria Theresa's accession in 1740 was met with immediate opposition. Charles VI had neglected to leave an effective army of 200,000 men to guarantee the sanction, leaving the Austrian forces disorganized following the Turkish wars. This weakness was exploited by Frederick the Great of Prussia, who invaded Silesia in 1740. The subsequent Prussian victory at Mollwitz in April 1741 encouraged other powers—including France, Bavaria, Spain, Saxony, and Sardinia—to seek expansion at Austria's expense.

## Territorial Conflicts and Diplomacy
The mid-18th century was defined by the Habsburgs' struggle to recover lost territories and manage European alliances. In Bohemia, Charles, elector of Bavaria, claimed the throne and occupied Prague in 1741, gaining the allegiance of many Bohemian nobles and knights. However, Maria Theresa eventually recovered Bohemia and other Habsburg lands.

A pivotal shift occurred in foreign policy when the primary threat to Austria shifted from France to Prussia. This led to a "diplomatic revolution" on May 1, 1756, when Austria and France concluded the first Treaty of Versailles, ending their long rivalry. During the Seven Years' War (1756–1763), Maria Theresa allied with France and Russia against Prussia and Britain in an attempt to recover Silesia. This effort failed; the Peace of Hubertsburg in 1763 left Germany divided between Austria and Prussia.

Later, under Joseph II, the monarchy sought "compensation" for the loss of Silesia by eyeing Bavaria. Following the death of elector Maximilian III in 1777, Joseph II claimed the inheritance. This led to the War of Bavarian Succession, though Maria Theresa, fearing another conflict with Prussia, overruled her son. The Treaty of Teschen settled the matter with the cession of the Quarter of the Inn (Innviertel) and other districts to Austria.

## Internal Administration and Centralization
Maria Theresa is regarded as the practical founder of the unified Austrian state. Her administration focused on centralization while carefully preserving the form of old institutions to avoid the errors of her successor. In Hungary, she left the ancient constitution untouched and summoned the diet only four times during her reign, implementing reforms via royal ordinance. She also attempted to establish German as the official language of the monarchy, a move that saw partial success in the army, although Latin remained the official tongue of the courts and county-assemblies.

In Bohemia, the drive toward centralization further limited autonomy. A 1749 decree abolished separate law-courts, and an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was later appointed to control the administration of Bohemia and the German domains of the house of Habsburg.

The broader Habsburg policy toward its institutions, including those in Belgium, tended toward absolutism. Influenced by the French philosophical school, the government sought to strengthen sovereign power, override national privileges, and establish the state as superior to the church. In Belgium, this involved depriving the nobility of political participation and recruiting public officers exclusively from among jurists. Despite this, the government worked to restore material welfare, fostering agricultural and manufacturing interests through ministers such as Antoniotto di Botta Adorno and Count Giovanni Giacomo di Belgiojoso.

## Personalities and Dynastic Ties
Maria Theresa is described as possessing a noble individuality and a "masculine resolution and judgment." She utilized her youth, beauty, and eloquence to secure the loyalty of the Hungarians, who rallied to her support during the crisis of her accession. She married her cousin, Francis of Lorraine, on February 12, 1736; Francis served as emperor until his death in 1765.

The dynasty also used marriage to solidify international alliances. Maria Theresa arranged the marriage of her ninth child, Marie Antoinette, to the dauphin of France on May 16, 1770, to confirm the Austro-French alliance. To safeguard this relationship, Maria Theresa instructed the Austrian ambassador, the comte de Mercy-Argenteau, to act as Marie Antoinette's mentor and maintain a secret correspondence with her until Maria Theresa's death in 1780.
