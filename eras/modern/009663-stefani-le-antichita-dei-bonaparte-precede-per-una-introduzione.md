# Le antichità dei Bonaparte; precede per una introduzione

## Overview
*Le antichità dei Bonaparte; precede per una introduzione* is a historical work authored by F. de Stefani. Published in Venice in 1857 by L. Beretta, the text is categorized among the bibliographic sources concerning the general history and origins of the Bonaparte family.

## Context within Bonaparte Historiography
The work by de Stefani exists as part of a broader body of literature dedicated to the genealogy and history of the Bonaparte family, including the lives of Carlo and Letizia Bonaparte. Other contemporary and subsequent works on the family include:

*   **Genealogical Studies:** D. Morali's *Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte*, published in Florence in 1846, which focuses on the family's origins and the extinction of the branch existing in the city of S. Miniato.
*   **Imperial and Family Histories:** L. Ambrosini and A. Huard's *La Famille imperiale* (1860), which covers the family from its origin to 1860, and C. Leynadier's *Histoire de la famille Bonaparte de l'an 1050 a l'an 1848* (1866), which was continued by de la Brugere until 1866.
*   **Biographical Works:** A. Kleinschmidt's study on the parents and siblings of Napoleon I (1876), D.A. Bingham's two-volume *The Marriages of the Bonapartes* (1881), and F. Masson's four-volume *Napoleon et sa famille* (1897-1900).
*   **Early Life and Parentage:** A. Chuquet's *La Jeunesse de Napoleon* (1897-1899), T. Nasica's *Memoires sur l'enfance et la jeunesse de Napoleon*, and Baron H. Larrey's *Madame Mere* (1892). Additionally, Clara Tschudi's *Napoleons Mutter* was published in Leipzig in 1901, translated from Norwegian by H. von Lenk.

## Related Family Figures
The historical record of the Bonaparte family, as referenced in the archive's general sources, includes various members such as Joseph Bonaparte, who is listed as the first of Napoleon I's brothers and sisters when taken in order of age. 

Other descendants include Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803-1857), the prince of Canino and son of Lucien Bonaparte. A scientist by inclination, Charles Lucien Jules Laurent published *American Ornithology* (1825-1833) and several other zoological catalogs regarding European birds, fish, and mammals. He was a member of the academies of Berlin and Upsala and a correspondent of the Institute of France. In 1847, he engaged in Italian political agitation, advocating for the expulsion of the Austrians and the independence of Italy, eventually serving as a deputy for Viterbo to the national assembly in 1848 before leaving Italy in 1849.
