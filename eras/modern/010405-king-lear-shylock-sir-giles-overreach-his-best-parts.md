# Edwin Booth

Edwin Booth (1833–1893) was a prominent American actor, the second son of the actor Junius Brutus Booth. Born on November 13, 1833, in Belair, Maryland, Booth established himself as a leading figure of the stage, eventually surpassing the reputation of his father in several roles.

## Early Career and Development
Booth's first regular stage appearance took place on September 10, 1849, at the Boston Museum, where he played Tressel in Colley Cibber's version of *Richard III*, supporting his father. His New York debut followed on September 27, 1850, at the National theatre in Chatham Street, playing the character of Wilford in *The Iron Chest*. 

Initially, Booth's entry into the profession was met with skepticism; members of the theatrical community and his own father expressed disappointment, believing that the genius of the elder Booth had not been passed to the son. However, after his father's death, Booth secured an unassailable position on the stage. Between 1852 and 1856, he performed in the Sandwich Islands, Australia, and California, where he captivated audiences with brilliant acting.

## Artistic Contributions and Management
Booth was noted for his commitment to textual accuracy, using the true text of Shakespeare in his productions, a reform that preceded similar movements in England by many years. In 1862, he became the manager of the Winter Garden theatre in New York, where from 1864 to 1867 he staged Shakespearian productions of unexampled magnificence, including *The Merchant of Venice*, *Othello*, and *Hamlet*.

In 1868–1869, he built his own venue, Booth's theatre, located at the corner of 6th Avenue and 23rd Street in New York. He organized a high-quality stock company that produced plays such as *Macbeth*, *Julius Caesar*, *The Winter's Tale*, *Romeo and Juliet*, and *Much Ado about Nothing*. While his ventures were generally successful, Booth's generous and confiding nature led to uneconomical management. In 1874, he lost both his theatre and his entire fortune.

## Notable Roles and Performance Style
Booth was celebrated for his extraordinary beauty, grace, and eloquent sensibility, qualities that made his portrayal of Hamlet his most famous part. It is probable that he played Hamlet more often than any other actor before or since. His final appearance in this role took place in Brooklyn in 1891.

His extensive repertoire included:
* **Shakespearean roles:** Lear, Macbeth, Othello, Iago, Shylock, Richard II, Richard III, Benedick, and Petruccio.
* **Other significant roles:** Richelieu, Wolsey, Sir Giles Overreach, Ruy Blas, Don Cesar de Bazan, Brutus (Payne's), and Bertuccio in Tom Taylor's *The Fool's Revenge*.

His success as Sir Giles Overreach in *A New Way to Pay Old Debts* was noted to have surpassed that of his father. His performances as Richelieu, Richard, and Hamlet were also considered superior to those of Edwin Forrest.

## Personal Life and Later Years
Booth was married twice: first to Mary Devlin in 1860 (who died in 1863) and later to Mary F. McVicker in 1869 (who died in 1881). From his first marriage, he had a daughter, Edwina Booth Grossman, who later published *Edwin Booth: Recollections* in 1894.

Booth's career was marked by a period of great distress in 1865 when his brother, John Wilkes Booth, assassinated President Lincoln. The three Booth brothers—Junius Brutus, Edwin, and John Wilkes—had previously performed together in *Julius Caesar* during the autumn of 1864.

In his later years, Booth used his regained wealth to support his profession. He converted his residence in Gramcy Park, New York, into The Players' club for the elect of the acting profession and other chosen members. He donated the house, his works of art, his books, and various stage mementos to the club, retaining only a single apartment for himself. He died in that apartment on June 7, 1893.

Throughout his career, Booth achieved international success, visiting London in 1851, 1880, and 1882, where he performed with brilliant success at the Haymarket theatre. He also visited Germany, where his acting was received with the highest enthusiasm.
