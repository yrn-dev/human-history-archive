# Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales

The *Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales* is a philosophical work written by the vicomte de Bonald. Published in two volumes in 1818, it is part of a broader body of writing by Bonald that includes works on social order, primitive legislation, and divorce.

## Context and Publication
The *Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales* was released in 1818. It exists alongside other significant publications by the vicomte de Bonald, such as the *Essai analytique sur les lois naturelles de l'ordre social* (1800), *Du divorce considere au XIXe siecle* (1801), and *Legislation primitive* (1802). Later in his career, Bonald published *Melanges litteraires et politiques, demonstration philosophique du principe constitutif de la societe* in 1819 and 1852.

The works of Bonald have been compiled in various editions over time. The first collected edition was published in 12 volumes between 1817 and 1819. A later edition was produced by the Abbe Migne in three volumes in 1859.

## Scholarly Reception
The vicomte de Bonald's contributions to philosophy and social theory have been noted by several historians and scholars. His life and work were documented by his son, Victor, in *Notice sur M. le Vicomte de Bonald* (1841, edited in 1853). Additionally, his influence is discussed in Damiron's *Phil. en France au XIXe siecle*, Windelband's *History of Philosophy* (translated by J.H. Tufts in 1893), and by E. Faguet in a publication of the *Rev. des deux mondes* dated April 15, 1889.
