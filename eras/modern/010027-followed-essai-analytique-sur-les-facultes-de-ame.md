# Essai analytique sur les facultes de l'ame

The *Essai analytique sur les facultes de l'ame* is a philosophical work published in Copenhagen in 1760. It was authored by Bonnet, who transitioned toward the study of philosophy after his eyesight began to fail, threatening to disappear altogether.

## Context and Development
The publication of the *Essai analytique sur les facultes de l'ame* followed an earlier work by Bonnet titled *Essai de psychologie*, which had been published anonymously in London in 1754. In the 1760 *Essai analytique*, the author develops his specific views regarding the physiological conditions associated with mental activity.

## Related Scientific Works
Before turning to philosophy, Bonnet was active in the physical sciences. His first published work, *Traite d'insectologie* (1745), detailed his discoveries regarding insects and included a preface concerning the scale of organized beings and the development of germs. He subsequently spent several years studying botany, focusing on plant leaves. This research culminated in the 1754 publication *Recherches sur l'usage des feuilles dans les plantes*, in which he presented considerations suggesting that plants possess powers of discernment and sensation.

Following his philosophical essays, Bonnet returned to the speculative side of physical science. In 1762, he published *Considerations sur les corps organises* in Amsterdam, a work intended to defend the doctrine of pre-existent germs and refute the theory of epigenesis.

## Later Philosophical Contributions
Bonnet continued to produce influential works after the *Essai analytique*. Between 1764 and 1765, he published *Contemplation de la nature* in Amsterdam, which was translated into Dutch, English, German, and Italian. In this work, he used eloquent language to propose the theory that all beings in nature exist on a gradual scale from highest to lowest without any break in continuity.

His final significant work, *Palingenesie philosophique*, was published in Geneva between 1769 and 1770. In this text, he examined the future and past of living beings, arguing for the perfecting of faculties in a future state and the survival of all animals.
