# Buerger Ministerium

The Buerger Ministerium was a parliamentary ministry appointed in Austria following the retirement of Beust from the post of minister-president in December 1867. The cabinet was composed entirely of members from the ranks of the Liberal majority. Among its most important members were Herbst and Giskra, who served as the leaders of the German party in Bohemia and Moravia.

## Political Vision and Ideology
The Buerger Ministerium operated under a vigorous Liberal centralizing policy. The members of the government were primarily representatives of financial speculation, commercial enterprise, trade, and industry. They viewed parliamentary government as a mechanism for the rule of the well-to-do and educated middle class. Their overarching goal was to transform Austria into a great industrial state, a vision they pursued through private energy and a strong focus on railway enterprise.

As convinced free-traders, the ministry sought to build the country's fortunes through economic liberalization. This included the extension of a commercial treaty with Great Britain, which furthered the advancement of free trade.

## Financial Policy
One of the first priorities of the new government was the settlement of national finances. The ministry accepted the significant debt burden placed upon the country by the actions of Hungary. While they rejected proposals to repudiate this debt, they did impose a tax of 16% on all interest on the debt, despite protests from foreign bondholders.

## Constitutional Role
The Buerger Ministerium presided over the beginning of Austria's life as a modern constitutional state. The "Constitutional party" adopted the maintenance of the revised constitution of 1867 as its primary watchword.
