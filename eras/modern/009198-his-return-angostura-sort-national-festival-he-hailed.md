# Simon Bolivar and the Republic of Colombia

## Military Campaigns and the Rise to Power
Following a failed expedition in May 1816, Simon Bolivar obtained reinforcements at Aux Cayes in Haiti. In December, he landed first in Margarita and then at Barcelona, where he assembled troops and formed a provisional government to resist Morillo. On February 16, 1817, a conflict began between these forces and the royalists, lasting three days and ending in a royalist defeat. Morillo retired in disorder and suffered a further overthrow at the hands of J.A. Paez and his *llaneros*. 

Recognized as commander-in-chief, Bolivar established his headquarters at Angostura on the Orinoco before the end of 1817. On February 15, 1819, he opened a congress in Angostura, where he presented his views on government and surrendered his authority to the congress. However, he was required to resume power until independence was fully established. Bolivar then reorganized his troops and crossed the Cordilleras to join General Santander, who led the republican forces in New Granada. This strategy was successful; Bolivar entered Tunja in July 1819 and won the victory of Boyaca on August 7, granting him possession of Bogota and all of New Granada.

## The Republic of Colombia
Bolivar's return to Angostura was celebrated as a national festival, during which he was hailed as the father of his country and its deliverer. Taking advantage of this moment, he secured the enactment of the fundamental law on December 17, 1819. This law united the republics of New Granada and Venezuela into a single state known as the Republic of Colombia, with Bolivar serving as president. The seat of government was provisionally moved to Rosario de Cucuta, located on the frontier of the two provinces.

## Final Victories in Venezuela
After the formation of the Republic, Bolivar returned to the field leading the best-appointed and most numerous army the republicans had assembled. He gained advantages over the Spaniards under Morillo, leading to a six-month armistice concluded at Truxillo on November 25, 1820, in hopes of sparing further bloodshed. However, Morillo was recalled and replaced by General Torre. When the armistice expired, Bolivar sought a decisive blow, which he achieved at Carabobo. He routed the Spaniards so completely that the remains of their army fled to Puerto Cabello, where they surrendered to Paez two years later.

The battle of Carabobo effectively ended the war in Venezuela. Bolivar entered Caracas on June 29, 1821, and by the end of that year, the Spaniards were driven from the province, except for Puerto Cabello. To secure independence through permanent political institutions, the constitution of Colombia was adopted on August 30, 1821, with Bolivar as president and Santander as vice-president.

## Geography of Bolivar State
The inland state of Bolivar in Venezuela is located south of the Apure and Orinoco, bounded by the Caroni river and the Yuruari territory to the east, and Brazil and the Amazonas territory to the south. The region consists of great forests, numerous rivers, and extensive plains known as *llanos*, which are covered in nutritious grass during the rainy season but dry completely in the dry season. 

The capital is Ciudad Bolivar, formerly called Angostura, situated on the right bank of the Orinoco approximately 240 miles above its mouth. The city serves as a trade hub for exports including hides, cocoa, cotton, sugar, jerked beef, and various forest products, as vessels of light draught can easily ascend the river to this point. As of 1894, the population was 135,232, though the state's area has since been largely reduced.
