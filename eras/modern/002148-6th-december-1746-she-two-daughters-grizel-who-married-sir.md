# Lady Grizel Baillie

Lady Grizel Baillie (1665–1746) was a Scottish song-writer. She was the eldest daughter of Sir Patrick Hume (also known as Home) of Polwarth, who later became the earl of Marchmont. She was born on December 25, 1665, at Redbraes Castle in Berwickshire.

## Early Life and Political Turmoil
During her youth, Lady Grizel was involved in the political struggles of her father. At the age of twelve, she acted as a messenger, carrying letters from Sir Patrick to Robert Baillie of Jerviswood, a Scottish patriot who was imprisoned at the time. Due to his friendship with Baillie, Sir Patrick became a suspected man, leading the king's troops to occupy Redbraes Castle. 

While Sir Patrick remained in hiding within a churchyard for a period, his daughter provided him with food. Following the execution of Robert Baillie in 1684, Sir Patrick fled to Holland, where his family joined him shortly thereafter. The family eventually returned to Scotland during the Revolution.

## Marriage and Family
In 1692, Lady Grizel married George Baillie, who was the son of the patriot Robert Baillie. The couple had two daughters:
* **Grizel**, who married Sir Alexander Murray of Stanhope.
* **Rachel**, Lady Binning.

## Literary Contributions
Lady Grizel is recognized for her work in prose and verse. A manuscript containing these writings was held by her daughter, Lady Murray. Some of her songs were included in Allan Ramsay's *Tea-Table Miscellany*. Her most famous song, "And werena my heart light I wad dee," first appeared in *Orpheus Caledonius* in 1725.

Lady Grizel Baillie died on December 6, 1746.
