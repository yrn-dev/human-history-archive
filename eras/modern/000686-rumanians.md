# Rumanians

The Rumanians are a people with a historical presence across Eastern Europe and the Balkans, characterized by a distinct language, a history of dynastic rule in the principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia, and a national movement that expanded into Macedonia.

## History and Dynasties
In the principalities of Wallachia and Moldavia, the Bassarab dynasty was prominent. Notable members included Mircea (1386–1418), who accepted Turkish suzerainty, and Michael, known as "the Brave" (1592–1601). Petru Cercel, a scholar who spoke twelve languages and corresponded with Italian poets, was drowned in Constantinople in 1590 due to the intrigues of Mihnea, his successor on the throne of Wallachia. Petru Cercel also owned the oldest manuscripts of the Rumanian Gospels, which contain his autograph signature and were later published by Dr. M. Caster with funding from the Rumanian government. 

The Bassarab dynasty ended with Constantine Sherban in 1658. Earlier, Neagoe founded the cathedral at Curtea de Argesh. In 1654, Mateiu Bassarab (1633–1654) established the first printing-press in Rumania, and the first code of laws was compiled and published in Bucharest during his influence.

## National Movement in Macedonia
The Rumanian national movement in Macedonia followed the Bulgarian movement in timing. The Vlachs in this region were initially more susceptible to Greek influence than other non-Hellenic populations. While propaganda efforts began as early as 1855, national sentiment only became apparent after the 1861 union of Moldavia and Wallachia. 

In 1886, a priest named Apostol Margaritis founded a gymnasium at Monastir. This movement received support from French Catholic missions, encouragement from Austria, and countenance from the Porte. By the early 20th century, there were approximately forty Rumanian schools in Macedonia, including two gymnasia, maintained by the ministry of education at Bucharest, which also provided teachers.

Because the orthodox Vlachs lacked a separate ecclesiastical organization and owed allegiance to the Greek patriarchate, the government in Bucharest sought the recognition of a Vlach *millet* and the establishment of a Rumanian bishopric at Monastir. The Porte recognized the Vlach *millet* by iradé on May 23, 1905.

## Language and Culture
The Rumanian language, like the Servian and Bulgarian languages, has adopted many words from Greek and borrowed largely from Turkish in its vocabulary, though it did not adopt Turkish structural forms. 

Bucharest serves as a major educational center. The university, founded in 1864, includes faculties of law, science, medicine, pharmacy, philosophy, literature, and theology. The city's architecture is represented by the university buildings—which house the Rumanian senate meeting-hall and a national library of Oriental documents—the central girls' school, and the Athenaeum. Other cultural institutions include the library of the Academy (which has a museum of antiquities and natural history) and the Charles University Foundation, which rewards scientific and literary merit.

## Political and Regional Presence
In the Hungarian county of Krassó-Szörény, the city of Lugos is divided by the river Temes into a German quarter on the left bank and a Rumanian quarter on the right bank. Lugos is the seat of a Greek-United (Rumanian) bishop.

In parliamentary contexts, Rumanians have maintained a small but consistent presence. In one recorded parliamentary table, the "Rumanians" and "Young Rumanians" held 6 seats in 1897 and 5 seats in 1901. In another record, the "Rumanian Club" held 5 seats. Total counts for Rumanians in different parliamentary records are listed as 5.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Rumania is a producer of petroleum; in 1898, the first consignment of Rumanian petroleum was conveyed in tank boats to Regensburg. The Rumanian State Navigation Company operates a flotilla of tugs and barges that run to Budapest, where they coordinate with the South Danube German Company for transport to Regensburg. 

Regarding the Danube, Rumania has been represented by a delegate on the European commission since the Congress of Berlin in 1878. However, a proposed *Commission Mixte* was never formed due to opposition from Rumania.
