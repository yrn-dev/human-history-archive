# Flora of Northern Afghanistan

## Regional Affinities
The flora of the northern part of Afghanistan approximates to that of the contiguous western Himalaya. This region is part of a broader temperate belt extending from the Mediterranean across to northern China and the southern slopes of the Himalaya. This zone, which includes Afghanistan, northern Persia, and Asia Minor, possesses a marked similarity of character to the flora of southern Europe, with which it is virtually continuous.

## Vegetation of High Altitudes
In the higher parts of Afghanistan and Persia, thistles and Boraginaceae are abundant. These districts are further characterized by gigantic Umbelliferae, including genera such as *Dorema, Bubon, Peucedanum, Prangos, Ferula,* and *Galbanum*, some of which extend into Tibet.

## Rainless Regions and Lowlands
Different botanical characteristics apply to the rainless regions of south-western Asia, a tract that includes Afghanistan, Baluchistan, south Persia, the shores of the Persian Gulf, and the peninsula of Arabia. The flora of this rainless region is continuous with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa, extending from the coast of Senegal to the meridian of 75 deg. E.

In the less elevated parts of Afghanistan and Baluchistan, the flora partakes of the characters of the hotter Mediterranean region. This is similar to the vegetation found in the valleys of the more mountainous regions of south-western Arabia (Arabia Felix). In these areas, aromatic shrubs are abundant, while trees are rare and almost restricted to *Dodonaea, Celtis, Pistacia*, poplars, and the date palm. The dry hills in these regions are covered by prickly forms of *Astragalus* and *Statice*.

## Climatic Influence on Vegetation
The distribution of plant life in this region is closely dependent on climatic conditions. In Afghanistan, Syria, Asia Minor, and Persia, the chief seasons of condensation are winter and spring. In the temperate belt that encompasses Afghanistan, the rainfall is sufficient to maintain a vigorous vegetation, despite hot summers and severe winters.
