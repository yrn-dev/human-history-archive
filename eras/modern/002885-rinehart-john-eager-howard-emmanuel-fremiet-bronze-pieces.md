# Rinehart, Emmanuel Fremiet, and Bronze Works

## Artistic Works in Washington Place
In the square surrounding the white marble monument to George Washington—located at the intersection of Mount Vernon Place and Washington Place (a continuation of N. Charles Street)—several statues and artistic pieces are displayed. Among these are a statue of Roger Brooke Taney created by W. H. Rinehart and a statue of John Eager Howard created by Emmanuel Fremiet. 

Additionally, the square contains bronze pieces that represent Order, Force, War, and Peace, as well as a figure of a lion sculpted by Antoine L. Barye.

## William H. Rinehart
William H. Rinehart was a native of Maryland. His works are represented in the Rinehart collection, which is housed at the Peabody Institute. The institute is situated at the south-east corner of the square and also contains a conservatory of music, a collection of rare books, casts, and American paintings.

## Emmanuel Fremiet
Emmanuel Fremiet is the sculptor of the statue of John Eager Howard located in the square. Fremiet also served as a teacher in Paris; the American sculptor Paul Wayland Bartlett studied under Fremiet when Bartlett was fifteen years old, specifically modelling from animals at the Jardin des Plantes.

## Bronze Works and Materials
Bronze has been utilized in various artistic and functional capacities across different historical contexts:

*   **Sculpture and Art:** In addition to the bronze figures of Peace, War, Force, and Order in Washington Place, Paul Wayland Bartlett is noted for his remarkable bronze patinas of fish, insects, and reptiles. The Henry Walters collection, housed in the Walters Art Gallery at the south end of Washington Place, includes bronzes from Japan and China.
*   **Ancient Utility:** According to historical accounts of the Massagetai, bronze was used extensively for battle-axes, arrow-heads, and spear-points. They also employed bronze to create breast-plates for their horses.

## Other References to Rinehart
In the context of Romance literary movements in regions such as Flanders, Brabant, and Hainaut, the *épopée* (epic) of *Rinehart the Fox* is cited as a particularly famous work of *poésie bourgeoise*, a genre in which animals frequently served as personages.
