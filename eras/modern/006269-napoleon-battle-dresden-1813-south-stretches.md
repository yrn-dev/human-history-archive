# Napoleon I. and the Campaigns of 1813

## The Political Climate of 1813
By 1813, the influence of Napoleon I was significantly diminished. Following the retreat from Moscow, the European political landscape shifted as various powers rose against him, viewing him as a usurper and a defaulting trustee of freedom. Prussia initiated the German "War of Liberation," and Sweden joined the opposing forces. Later, Holland also revolted. 

Austria, under the diplomatic guidance of Metternich, initially sought to recover lost possessions without necessarily ensuring the complete overthrow of the French emperor. Austria refused to join the March 17 alliance between Russia and Prussia, instead massing between 200,000 and 300,000 troops in Bohemia. Austria acted as a mediator until the battle of Vittoria and Napoleon's refusal to accept demands regarding his political system in Central Europe led Austria to side with the Allies. This shift was formalized in the treaty of Trachenberg on July 12, 1813, which completed the Grand Alliance and ensured Napoleon's eventual fall.

## Military Engagements in Germany
Napoleon took chief command in the spring of 1813 with fresh forces. He achieved a victory over the Russians and Prussians at Lützen (also known as Gross Görschen) on May 2, 1813. This was followed by the battle of Bautzen on May 20–21. During the battle of Bautzen, Napoleon's first aide-de-camp, General Geraud Christophe Michel Duroc, was mortally wounded and died on May 23 in a nearby farmhouse; Napoleon later purchased the farm to erect a monument to him.

Earlier in May, on the 12th, a battle occurred at Bischofswerda in the kingdom of Saxony between the Allies and the French. Another significant engagement was the battle of Dresden, where Napoleon won a great victory. However, following this success, Napoleon appeared to struggle intellectually and morally, becoming insanely irritable and failing to follow up on the victory.

## Movement through Bohemia and Saxony
Following the battle of Dresden in 1813, Napoleon I utilized the ancient military route to Saxony. This route traversed the Nollendorf pass, which separates the Elbsandsteingebirge from the Erzgebirge. To the south of this area lies the region known as the "Thermopylae of Bohemia," which served as the site for the battles of Arbesau and Kulm. Russian field marshal Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly, who had been reinstated as commander-in-chief of the Russian forces after the battle of Bautzen, served at Kulm, Dresden, and Leipzig.

## The Collapse of the Empire
The military situation deteriorated for Napoleon in September 1813 during the "Battle of the Nations" fought around Leipzig. This defeat led the Saxons, who had previously supported Napoleon, to switch their allegiance to the Allies. By the end of the year, French forces were beaten back into France. 

The subsequent victorious advance of the Allies into France culminated on April 11, 1814, with Napoleon's abdication. During this period of conflict, the intensity of national feeling against Napoleon was reflected in the patriotic lyrics of the poet Körner, whose songs inspired heroism in 1813.
