# Antiquities, Ethnography, &c., of Las Bela and Makran

The "Antiquities, Ethnography, &c., of Las Bela and Makran" is a Survey Report published in Calcutta in 1894. It serves as one of the authorities for historical and ethnographic data regarding the region of Baluchistan.

## Context and Authorship
The report is associated with the work of Sir Thomas Hungerford Holdich (T. H. H.), a Colonel in the Royal Engineers and a Gold Medallist of the R.G.S. (London, 1887). Sir Holdich served as the Superintendent of Frontier Surveys in India from 1892 to 1898 and acted as the H. M. Commissioner for the Persa-Beluch Boundary in 1896. His contributions to the archive include research on Baluchistan, Bela, and the Bahrein Islands, among other topics.

## Geography of Las Bela and Makran
The region of Las Bela is a small independent state located to the south of Kalat in Baluchistan. It is situated at 26 deg. 27' 30" N. lat. and 66 deg. 45' 0" E. long., at an elevation of 350 feet above sea level. The state is bordered by Sind to the east and Makran to the west. 

The area of Las Bela encompasses 6,357 square miles. Its landscape consists primarily of hot, malarial, and low-lying alluvial plains covering approximately 6,000 square miles on the north-east corner of the Arabian Sea. These plains are highly fertile and irrigated by the Kud and Purali rivers, which flow from the north. The Purali delta is currently expanding over areas previously covered by the bay of Sonmiani.

The topography is further defined by hill ranges:
* **To the East:** The Mor and Kirthar ranges separate the plains from Sind. Between these ranges lies the narrow Hab valley. These hills diminish in height as they reach the sea, creating a coastal route between Bela and Karachi.
* **To the West:** The terrain consists of numerous minor ridges in parallel formation, curving south and west to form the coast barrier of Makran.

## Historical and Economic Significance
From time immemorial, Las Bela has been a critical point on the great trading route between Persia and Sind. The route from India curves north and crosses the barren ridges of the west, eventually entering the Kolwah valley. From there, it becomes an open road with an easy gradient leading to Kej and Persia. This path was one of the most significant trade routes during the medieval era of Arab ascendancy in Sind.

The town of Bela, the capital of the state, is situated on a rocky site above the banks of the Purali river. While its modern dimensions are modest, its historical importance is derived from its position on this trade route.

## Governance and Demographics
Las Bela is ruled by the Jam (or Cham), who is a protected chief under the British Raj. As of 1901, the population of the state was 56,109, with 54,040 of those inhabitants being Mussulmans.
