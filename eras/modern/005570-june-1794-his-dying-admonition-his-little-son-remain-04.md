# June 1794

The month of June 1794 was marked by significant political upheaval in France, shifts in Mediterranean sovereignty, and various military and diplomatic developments across Europe and Asia.

## The French Terror and Executions
During the period of the Terror in France, the rate of executions increased sharply. In the thirteen months leading up to June 1794, there were 1,220 executions; in the seven weeks that followed, the number rose to 1,376. 

Among those affected was Victor Claude, Prince de Broglie (1757–1794). A former *maréchal de camp* in the army, Victor Claude had served with Rochambeau and Lafayette in America. He was a member of the Jacobin Club and a member of the Constituent Assembly, where he consistently voted on the Liberal side. He also served as the chief of the staff for the Republican army on the Rhine. Despite these affiliations, he was denounced, arrested, and executed in Paris on June 27, 1794. His final admonition to his young son was to remain faithful to the principles of the Revolution, regardless of how ungrateful or unjust those principles might be.

Other figures of the era also met their end in 1794, including Antoine Simon (1736–1794), a member of the section of the Cordeliers who had previously prevented an attempt by the baron de Batz. Simon was sent to the guillotine in 1794 alongside Robespierre.

## Corsica and British Occupation
In 1794, the island of Corsica experienced a revolt against France led by Paoli. While the *consulta* at Corte elected Paoli as president, opinion on the island was divided. Napoleon Bonaparte indignantly rejected the idea of breaking with France, leading the Bonapartes to be classified as enemies of Paoli.

Paoli sought assistance from the British government, which sent a considerable force. Following hard fighting, the island was reduced by the summer of 1794. In June, the Corsican assembly formally offered sovereignty to King George III. This began a British occupation that lasted two years under the administration of Sir Gilbert Elliot. During this time, Paoli was invited to return to England, where he remained until his death.

## Diplomatic and Military Events
Various naval and military movements occurred during this period:

*   **British Naval Promotions:** Sir William Cornwallis, a British admiral who had previously engaged French forces near Bermuda and served in the East Indies until 1794, was promoted to vice-admiral on July 4, 1794.
*   **India:** Following the death of Mahadji Sindhia in 1794, Benoit de Boigne, Count (1751–1830), remained loyal to Daulat Rao Sindhia, despite having the potential to become the master of Hindostan.
*   **Intelligence Reports:** A police report dated May 17–24, Paris, was enclosed by Francis Drake on June 17, 1794, and sent from Milan to Lord Grenville. The report detailed a rumor that Robespierre had removed the king (the dauphin) from the Temple to Meudon on the night of May 23–24, only to return him the following night as a test to ensure he could be easily seized.
