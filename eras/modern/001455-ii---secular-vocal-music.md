# SECULAR VOCAL MUSIC

Secular vocal music encompasses a wide range of compositions and performances intended for non-religious contexts, ranging from early historical practices and formal operatic roles to specific compositions by noted musicians.

## Historical and Technical Foundations
In ancient Egypt, music was regarded as an important science and was diligently studied by priests. While the sacred rites of their gods permitted the use of vocal music, flute, and harp, the archive notes that dancing was an art confined to professional persons. Among the lower orders, dancing occurred on festive occasions, often characterized by a tendency toward pantomime, license, and wanton buffoonery.

Technically, secular vocal music utilizes various structures. A "cadence" refers to a falling or sinking of rhythmical or musical sounds, such as the fall of the voice in speaking or the measure of song. In a musical composition, a "cadenza" (the Italian form of cadence) is a free flourish introduced at the end of a piece or immediately before the close of a movement. These are designed to prevent an abrupt contrast between movements or to display the technique of the performer; while some are written in full by the composer or a famous executant, they are usually left to the performer's improvisation.

Additionally, the "duet"—derived from the Italian *duetto*—is a composition for two performers. For a work to be properly termed a duet, both vocal or instrumental parts must be of equal importance; if one part is subordinated, it is considered an accompaniment rather than a duet.

## Works of J.S. Bach
The secular vocal output of J.S. Bach includes several distinct forms:

*   **Dramma per Musica:** Bach composed *Der Streit zwischen Phoebus und Pan* and *Der zufrieden gestellte Aeolus*. Although titled *Dramma per Musica*, these works possess no more essential connection to the stage than Handel's *Acis and Galatea*.
*   **Cantatas:** Bach wrote seven solo and seven choral cantatas. Three of the choral cantatas were almost entirely absorbed into the B minor mass and the Christmas oratorio. Among the solo cantatas, two are burlesque and two are Italian; one of the Italian works is Bach's only developed composition for voice and clavier.
*   **Songs:** He produced several tunes with clavier bass that almost foreshadow the modern song.

## Notable Composers and Performers
Various figures have contributed to the development and execution of secular vocal music across different eras and regions.

**Dudley Buck (1839-1909)**
An American composer born in Hartford, Connecticut, Buck studied in Paris, Dresden, and Leipzig between 1858 and 1862. In addition to his work as an organist in Boston, Chicago, and New York, he composed a variety of vocal works, including a grand opera titled *Serapis* and a comic opera titled *Deseret* (1880).

**Carl August Peter Cornelius (1824-1874)**
A German musician and poet, Cornelius initially pursued a career on the stage, appearing in *Kean* as John Cook, *Das war Ich* as the lover, and Moreto's *Donna Diana* as Perin. He eventually abandoned the stage to pursue music, with the specific goal of becoming a comic opera composer. His early independent works included two comic opera texts.

**Katharina Klafsky (1855-1896)**
A Hungarian operatic singer born in Szt János, Wieselburg, Klafsky began her career as a chorus singer in Vienna. She became well-known for Wagnerian roles at the Leipzig theatre and later achieved great success in London. She was particularly noted for her exceptional vocal and dramatic gifts in the roles of Isolde and Brünnhilde.
