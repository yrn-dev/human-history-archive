# Italy

The history of Italy has been characterized by numerous military campaigns, foreign occupations, and a movement toward national liberation involving various European powers, most notably France and Austria.

## Napoleonic Era Campaigns
During the rise of Napoleon Bonaparte, Italy was a primary theater of conflict. Bonaparte accompanied his forces to Italy, and Auguste Frédéric Louis Viesse de Marmont, a marshal of France and the Duke of Ragusa, served as his aide-de-camp during this period. Marmont later organized the artillery for an expedition to Italy, which he commanded with great effect at the battle of Marengo, leading to his promotion to general of division.

Other French commanders also played significant roles in the peninsula. Jacques Étienne Joseph Alexandre Macdonald, the Duke of Taranto, served in the army of Italy starting in 1797. Under the direction of Berthier, Macdonald occupied Rome and served as its governor. He and Championnet subsequently defeated General Mack and established the Parthenopaean Republic in the kingdom of Naples. Later, Macdonald faced the Russian forces under Suvarov in northern Italy; despite having only 30,000 men against Suvarov's 50,000, Macdonald held the Russians at bay for three days at the Trebbia before retiring to Genoa.

Additionally, Emmanuel, Marquis de Grouchy, administered the civil and military government of Piedmont during the period when the king of Sardinia abdicated. In 1809, Grouchy commanded the cavalry of the Army of Italy during Viceroy Eugene's advance toward Vienna.

## Austrian and Other Interventions
Austrian forces have historically contested control of the region. During the War of the Polish Succession, Ludwig Andreas Khevenhüller, an Austrian field-marshal, held the chief command of the army in Italy following the death of Count Mercy at the battle of Parma on June 29, 1734. Khevenhüller distinguished himself at the battle of Guastalla on September 19 and continued his military and diplomatic work in Italy until the conclusion of that war.

## The War for the Liberation of Italy
In the mid-19th century, Italy moved toward unification and liberation. A prelude to the war for the liberation of Italy was the January 1859 marriage of Prince Napoleon (Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul) to Princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy, the daughter of Victor Emmanuel. 

During this conflict, Prince Napoleon commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany. Although it was expected that he would become the ruler of the principality, Prince Napoleon refused to exert pressure on the inhabitants. The people of Tuscany expressed a preference for union with the Italian kingdom. This period of transition culminated in 1861, when Victor Emmanuel became the first King of Italy.
