## Topography of the Grand Duchy of Baden
The Grand Duchy of Baden is a sovereign German state located in the south-west corner of the empire. It is bounded by the kingdom of Bavaria and the grand-duchy of Hesse-Darmstadt to the north, Switzerland to the south, the kingdom of Wuerttemberg and part of Bavaria to the east, and the Rhine to the west. The Rhine separates Baden from the imperial province of Alsace-Lorraine and the Bavarian Palatinate.

The state covers an area of 5823 sq. m., with mountains comprising nearly 80% of the total area. The Black Forest (_Schwarzwald_) extends from the river Neckar in the north to the Lake of Constance in the south. This region is divided by the Kinzig valley into two districts of differing elevations:
* **Southern District:** The mean height is 3100 ft., with the highest summit, the Feldberg, reaching approximately 4898 ft.
* **Northern District:** The mean height is 2100 ft., with the Belchen serving as the culminating point at 4480 ft.

Further north of the Neckar lies the Odenwald Range, which has a mean height of 1440 ft. and reaches an extreme of 1980 ft. at the Katzenbuckel. Additionally, the Kaiserstuhl is an independent volcanic group between the Dreisam and the Rhine, measuring nearly 10 m. in length and 5 in breadth, with a highest point of 1760 ft.

## Highlands of East Africa and Scotland
In British East Africa—a term encompassing the protectorates of East Africa, Uganda, and Zanzibar—the continental area consists largely of the great plateau of East Africa. This region rarely falls below 2000 ft. in elevation, and some extensive sections reach heights between 6000 and 8000 ft. The landscape features a series of steps and plateaus leading to a broad upland marked by volcanic action and the eastern "rift-valley." To the west, the ground rises again to a second volcanic belt, culminating in the Ruwenzori range.

In Scotland, Loch Maree is a fresh-water lake in the county of Ross and Cromarty. The lake sits at a height of 32 ft. above sea-level. It is flanked for two-thirds of its length by mountains. The principal heights to the north-east include Ben Slioch (3217 ft.), Ben Lair (2817 ft.), and Ben Airidh-a-Char (2593 ft.). To the south-west, the peaks of Ben Eay include four that exceed 3000 ft.

## Ancient Urban Sites and Land Surveys
The ancient Italian city of Carsioli (modern Carsoli), located on the Via Valeria 42 m. east by north of Rome, was founded between 302 and 298 B.C. in the country of the Aequi. The site is situated in a plain surrounded by mountains known as Piano del Cavaliere, at an elevation of about 2100 ft. above sea-level.

Regarding early land management, cadastral surveys for taxation were utilized in Babylonia during the age of Sargon of Akkad in 3800 B.C. Clay tablets dating to 2100 or 2300 B.C. contain land surveys, including one that depicts lower Babylonia encircled by the "salt water river" Oceanus.

## Regional Settlement Patterns and Military Geography
In Japan, neolithic sites are six times more numerous in the northern half of the main island (north of a line from the coast of Ise through Orai) than in the southern half. These sites are typically positioned on alluvial plains or near the sea at heights ranging from 30 to 300 feet.

In military history, the geography of the slope of Epipolæ played a critical role in conflicts involving the Athenians and Syracusans. The Syracusans constructed a counter-wall that began at the city wall on the lowest ground and extended in a northwesterly and then westerly direction to a fort on upper ground near a cliff. The Syracusans defended the north side of this wall, while the Athenians were positioned on the south side.
