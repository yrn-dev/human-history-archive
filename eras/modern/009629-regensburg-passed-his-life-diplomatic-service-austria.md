# Regensburg

Regensburg is a city located along the course of the Danube river. Situated at a height of 949 feet, it marks a significant geographical point where the river changes its direction to the south-east before passing along the southern slopes of the Bavarian Forest and entering Austria at Passau.

## Geography and the Danube
The Danube flows through Bavaria, narrowing as it reaches Regensburg. The river serves as a central artery for several regions, including Bavaria, Austria, Hungary, and Wurttemberg. Historically, the area near Regensburg held strategic military importance; above the city at Eining was the ancient Roman outpost of Abusina, which served as a primary defense against northern barbarians for nearly five centuries.

## Commerce and Navigation
In modern times, Regensburg has become a vital hub for Central European commerce. The river is navigable for various types of vessels, and barges with a register of 600 tons can be towed from the lower Danube up to Regensburg. 

The city is a terminus for several major navigation services:
* **Societe Imperiale et Royale Autrichienne:** Based in Vienna and starting operations in 1830, this company transports passengers and goods between Regensburg and Galatz.
* **Rumanian State Navigation Company:** This company operates a flotilla of tugs and barges that run to Budapest, where they coordinate with the South Danube German Company to transport goods from Pest to Regensburg.

Additionally, Regensburg serves as a storage center for Rumanian petroleum, with dedicated petroleum tanks constructed for this purpose. The first consignment of this petroleum arrived in tank boats in 1898, following a six-week voyage from Giurgevo.

## Notable Figures
Regensburg is the birthplace of Louis Philippe, comte de Bombelles (1780-1843). The son of Marc Marie, Marquis de Bombelles—a French diplomatist and ecclesiastic who served as the bishop of Oberglogau and later the bishop of Amiens—Louis Philippe spent his professional life in the diplomatic service of Austria. His career included serving as the Austrian ambassador to Denmark in 1814 and holding a similar diplomatic position at Dresden in 1816.
