# Prague

Prague is the capital of Bohemia and serves as one of its two autonomous municipalities, with a recorded population of 204,478. It is a primary center for education, hosting two universities—one Czech and one German.

## Urban Development and Governance
The city underwent significant expansion under the rule of King Charles, who made Prague his favorite residence. Through the foundation of the *nove mesto* (new town), Charles enlarged the city to three times its former size, which subsequently trebled the population. 

Historically, the city's governance has faced various interventions. Following a period of conflict, Ferdinand I occupied Prague on July 20 with a force of Spanish and Walloon mercenaries. He dealt severely with the town, revising its ancient privileges—an action that resulted in a broad confiscation of municipal lands. Ferdinand also imposed state officials over municipal affairs, appointing town-judges and town-captains who held almost unlimited control.

## Academic and Religious History
Prague is the site of a famous university founded by King Charles. In the early 14th century, during the Hussite troubles, the university became a point of national contention. On January 18, 1410, Wenceslaus IV signed the decree of Kuttenberg, which granted the Bohemian nation three votes in the elections to the faculty of Prague University, compared to one vote for the three other "nations."

The city's religious landscape was marked by the Hussite movement; while the town of Kuttenberg was fanatically Catholic and German, Prague was characterized as Czech and utraquist. Later, the estates supported John of Rokycan as the archbishop of Prague, though the Church of Rome refused to recognize him.

## Military and Political Events
Prague has been a focal point for various military conflicts and political transitions. In 1421, the men of Prague captured the town of Kolin and expelled German inhabitants who would not accept "the four articles." In 1757, the siege of Prague was raised following the defeat of the Prussians by the Austrians at the battle of Kolin on June 18.

The city also served as a gathering place for the Bohemian estates. Following the imperialist victory at Muhlberg, the estates assembled at Prague to attempt to propitiate Ferdinand I.

## Cultural and Literary Contributions
Prague has inspired various literary works and has been the subject of historical study. Julius Zeyer wrote the epic poem *Vysehrad*, which celebrates the ancient glory of the Prague acropolis. Vaclav Hladik's novel *Evzen Voldan* is noted as a striking representation of modern life in the city.

The history of the town has been documented by several writers. Wenceslas Tomek authored *Dejepis miesta Prahy* (history of the town of Prague), which is considered his most important work. Additionally, Bartos (also known as Bartholomew), who served as the secretary for the city of Prague for many years, wrote the *Chronicle of Prague*. This work is regarded as having great historical value, specifically in its description of the troubles that affected Prague and Bohemia during the reign of King Louis.
