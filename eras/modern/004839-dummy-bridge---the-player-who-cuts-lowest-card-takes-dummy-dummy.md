# Dummy Bridge

Dummy Bridge is a variation of the card game bridge. The game begins with the players cutting cards, and the player who cuts the lowest card takes the role of dummy. The dummy is responsible for dealing the first hand of the game.

## Gameplay and Declarations
The player who takes dummy always examines their own hand first, regardless of whether they are dealing for themselves or for the dummy. This player has the option to either declare trumps or "leave it" to the dummy. If the declaration is left to the dummy, the dummy's declaration becomes compulsory, a rule similar to that found in three-handed bridge.

The rules regarding when players may view their cards depend on who is dealing:

*   **When the dealer deals for dummy:** The player to the dealer's left is prohibited from looking at their cards until the dealer has declared trumps. If the declaration was left to the dummy, the player on the left must wait until their partner has led a card. In this scenario, the partner can double, but the player on the left can only double without having seen their hand. The dealer is restricted to redoubling only on their own hand.
*   **When the player of dummy deals for themselves:** If the declaration is passed, the player on the dummy's right looks at the dummy's hand. In this case, the restrictions and positions of the player and their partner are reversed. If the player of dummy declares from their own hand, the game follows the procedures of ordinary bridge, with the exception that the dummy's hand remains unseen until permission to play is granted.
*   **When the player on dummy's right deals:** The partner of the dummy may look at the dummy's hand to determine if they will double. However, that partner cannot look at their own hand until the dummy has led a card.

## Alternative Form
There is another version of dummy bridge in which two hands are exposed whenever the adversaries of the dummy deal. This specific form is considered unsuited for many players because every other hand results in a game of double-dummy.

## Related Bridge Variations
The archive contains records of several other bridge adaptations:

**Three-handed Bridge**
In this version, three players cut, and the one with the lowest card deals and takes dummy for one deal, with the role rotating among players. The dealer declares trumps without seeing the dummy's face-down cards. If the dealer passes the declaration to the dummy (the right-hand adversary), that adversary examines the dummy's hand and must make a forced declaration. If the hand contains three or four aces, "sans atout" (no trumps) must be declared; otherwise, the longest suit is chosen. If suits are equal in length, the strongest suit (containing the most pips, with the ace counting as eleven and the king, queen, and knave as ten each) is selected. If they remain equal in strength, the suit in which the trick has the higher value is chosen.

**Misery Bridge**
Adapted for two players, the non-dealer takes the dummy. The dealer may strengthen their hand by discarding four or fewer cards and replacing them with an equal number from the fourth packet dealt. A unique rule in this version is that the three of clubs, known as "Cato," can be played to any trick to win it, regardless of suit. The dealer may also declare "misery" if they must make fewer than two tricks.

**Draw- or Two-handed Bridge**
In this two-player form, each player has a dummy placed opposite them using a special stand to prevent the opponent from seeing the cards. The dealer declares or passes to the dummy using the same rules as dummy or three-handed bridge.

**Ordinary and Auction Bridge**
Ordinary bridge is played by four players where the dealer's partner acts as dummy after the first lead. Auction bridge, introduced around 1907, allows any player to make a declaration through a process of bidding, doubling, and overcalling, rather than restricting the declaration to the dealer or their partner.
