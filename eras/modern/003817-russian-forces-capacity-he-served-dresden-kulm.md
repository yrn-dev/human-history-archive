# Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly

## Early Life and Early Military Career
Michael Andreas Barclay de Tolly (1761–1818), known to the Russians as Michael, Prince Bogdanovich, was a Russian field marshal born in Livonia. He was a descendant of a Scottish family that had settled in Russia during the 17th century. Entering the Russian army at an early age, he served in several conflicts during the late 18th century, including campaigns against the Turks (1788–1789), the Swedes (1790), and the Poles (1794). His rise through the ranks continued with his promotion to colonel in 1798 and major-general in 1799.

## Napoleonic Wars and Command
During the war of 1806 against Napoleon, Barclay de Tolly distinguished himself at the battle of Pultusk and was promoted to lieutenant-general following his conduct at Eylau, where he was wounded. In 1808, he commanded forces against the Swedes in Finland. In 1809, he executed a daring and rapid march across the frozen Gulf of Bothnia to seize and surprise Umeo.

Barclay de Tolly served as the minister of war from 1810 until 1813. In 1812, he was appointed to command one of the armies operating against Napoleon. This appointment faced keen opposition because he was a foreigner. Following a defeat at Smolensk, public outcry led him to resign his command, subsequently taking a subordinate position under the veteran Kutusov. While he was present at Borodino, he left the army shortly thereafter.

## Return to Command and Later Honors
Barclay de Tolly returned to field service in 1813 during the campaign in Germany. Following the battle of Bautzen, he was reinstated as the commander-in-chief of the Russian forces. In this capacity, he served at Leipzig, Dresden, and Kulm. He was made a count after the battle of Leipzig.

In 1814, he participated in the invasion of France and received the baton of a field marshal at Paris. He served again as commander-in-chief of the Russian army that invaded France in 1815, and he was granted the title of prince at the conclusion of the war. He died on May 14 (16), 1818, at Insterburg in Prussia.

## The Battle of Kulm
The battle of Kulm took place on August 29 and 30, 1813, in a village of Bohemia located at the foot of the Erzgebirge. This location is described as the "Thermopylae of Bohemia." In this engagement, French forces were defeated by a coalition of Russians, Prussians, and Austrians. During this action, Karl Wilhelm Georg von Grolmann, a Prussian soldier serving as a commissioner at the headquarters of General Barclay de Tolly, took part in the victory and sustained a severe wound.
