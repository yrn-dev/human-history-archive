# St Chamans

St Chamans is identified in the archive's records as the location where Lucien Bonaparte held a small post during the late 18th century.

## Lucien Bonaparte at St Chamans

Lucien Bonaparte (1775–1840), the prince of Canino, was born in Ajaccio on May 21, 1775. After attending schools at Brienne and Autun, he was destined for the church due to defective sight, which prevented him from entering the French army. He attended the seminary at Aix in Provence in 1786, though his volatile and excitable disposition was poorly suited to the discipline of the institution.

Following the outbreak of the French Revolution in 1789, Lucien embraced the anti-clerical and democratic movements. He became a prominent speaker for the Jacobin club in Ajaccio and urged his brothers to break ties with Paoli, the leader of the conservative party that sought an alliance with England against the regicide republic of France. After leading a deputation to France to denounce Paoli and seek aid for the democrats, Lucien eventually joined his brothers at Toulon.

In the south of France, Lucien continued his work for the Jacobin cause, adopting the name "Brutus" within the Jacobin club of St Maximin, a town that had been renamed Marathon. On May 4, 1794, while still a minor and without family consent, he married Mlle Catherine Boyer, an action that resulted in a state of penury and near-disgrace.

The *coup d'etat* of Thermidor on July 28, 1794, forced Lucien, a disciple of Robespierre, to flee St Maximin. He subsequently accepted a small post at St Chamans. While stationed there, he was arrested and imprisoned for a period of time. He was eventually released through the influence of Napoleon, who further secured him a position as a commissioner in the French army during its campaign in Germany.

## Subsequent Career of Lucien Bonaparte

Lucien developed a dislike for his military duties, as the role did not provide opportunities for his political intrigue or his skills in oratory. He returned to Corsica with the intention of being elected as a deputy for the island. Because of these ambitions, he refused an appointment offered by Napoleon in 1798 to join the army of Egypt. His political goals were achieved in 1798 when he entered the Council of Five Hundred in Paris.
