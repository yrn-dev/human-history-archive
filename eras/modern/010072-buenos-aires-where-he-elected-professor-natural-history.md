# Buenos Aires

Buenos Aires is a maritime province of Argentina in South America. It is the largest and most populous province of the republic, with an area of 117,812 square miles. According to an official estimate from 1903, its population was 1,251,000. The province is bounded to the north by Entre Rios and the province of Santa Fé, to the east by the Atlantic, the La Plata estuary, and Entre Rios, to the south by the Atlantic, and to the west by the provinces of Santa Fé and Córdoba, as well as the territories of Las Pampas and Rio Negro.

## Foundation and Early History
The establishment of the city of Buenos Aires involved three separate attempts. The first occurred in 1535, led by Don Pedro de Mendoza. This well-equipped Spanish expedition failed due to mismanagement and hostility from the Indians. While the settlement of "Santa Maria de Buenos Ayres" was abandoned, an expedition sent upriver by Mendoza founded Asunción, where the colonists eventually relocated. A second attempt in 1542 by a portion of an expedition under Cabeza de Vaca also met with little success.

The third and successful attempt was made in 1580 by Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay. Garay had previously established a half-way post at Santa Fé in 1573. The primary objective of his 1580 expedition down the Paraná was to create a port near the sea to provision ships and receive supplies from Spain, a need keenly felt by colonists in Asunción. Garay laid out a town in the Spanish style and built a fort, naming the settlement "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad," though he retained the descriptive name "Buenos Aires" for the port because of the invigorating and agreeable atmosphere.

## Provincial Development and Governance
Buenos Aires remained a dependency of Asunción until 1620, when the La Plata region was divided into three provinces: Tucuman, Paraguay, and Buenos Aires. At this time, the city became the seat of a new bishopric and the capital of its province. While Spain initially showed little interest in the trading station—preferring the gold and silver of Peru—the town grew through trade and smuggling, facilitated by the unprotected coast and proximity to Portuguese settlements in Brazil.

The province secured its large territory through the conquest of Indian territories to the west and south, with the final additions occurring as late as 1879. In 1820, Buenos Aires became a province of the Confederation. It adopted a constitution in 1854, establishing a government consisting of a legislature of two chambers and a governor, both chosen by popular vote. Following an unsuccessful revolt against the national government in 1880, the city of Buenos Aires was federalized, and La Plata was selected as the provincial capital.

## The City of Buenos Aires
The city has grown rapidly to become the largest city in South America. Its population was 177,767 at the first authentic census in 1869; by 1895, it reached 663,854, and by the close of 1905, the national statistical office estimated it at 1,025,653. The city contains approximately one-fifth of the republic's total population. The residents, known as Porteños, often refer to their city as the "Paris of America."

The city is the principal manufacturing center of the republic, with thousands of industrial establishments. Notable public buildings include the Casa Rosada (the government-house), which stands partly on the site of Garay's 1580 fort, the municipal hall, the mint, the exchange (bolsa), and a congress hall finished in 1906. Higher education is provided by the university of Buenos Aires, which had 3,562 students in 1901 across various faculties including medicine and law. Other cultural institutions include a national museum, a national library, an aquarium, and a zoological garden.

The city's government is managed by a *concejo deliberante* (legislative council) elected by the people, and an *intendente municipal* (mayor) appointed by the national executive with senate approval. Municipal voting and council service rights are extended to every ratepayer, regardless of whether they are native or foreign.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The province has a frontage of over 900 miles on the Atlantic and the La Plata, though natural ports are few. The best natural port is Bahia Blanca, where the government has built a naval port. Other significant ports include Ensenada (La Plata), which features artificial basins for ocean-going steamers, and San Nicolas in the north. A costly artificial port was also constructed at Buenos Aires.

Other important towns in the province include:
* **La Plata**: The provincial capital.
* **Bahia Blanca**: A city and port on the Naposta river, serving as a shipping port for wheat and wool.
* **San Nicolas**: A river port on the Paraná.
* **Campana**: The site of Argentina's first mutton-freezing factory (1883).
* **Mar del Plata**: A popular seaside resort.
* **Chivilcoy, Pergamino, Azul, Tandil, Chascomús, Mercedes, and Barracas al Sud**.

## Notable Figures
In 1816, the French botanist and traveller Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland (born Goujand) arrived in Buenos Aires bringing various European plants. He was elected professor of natural history in the city, though he soon left the office to explore central South America.
