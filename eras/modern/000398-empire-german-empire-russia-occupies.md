# The German Empire and Associated Powers

## The German Empire and European Hegemony
The establishment of the German Empire followed the downfall of the empire of Napoleon III. For thirty-six years, European politics were centered on the possibility of a Europe consolidated under German auspices. To secure its ascendancy and maintain its conquests, particularly Lorraine, the German Empire under Bismarck developed an elaborate system of alliances. In 1879, Germany formed an alliance with Austria, which expanded in 1883 to include Roumania via a secret alliance and in 1883 to become the Triple Alliance between Germany, Austria, and Italy.

Bismarck sought to prevent a counteralliance between France and Russia. He initially pursued a "league of the three Emperors"—Germany, Austria, and Russia—but this was ruptured in 1887 due to clashing Russian and Austrian interests in the Balkans. Consequently, Germany entered into a Reinsurance Treaty with Russia to protect itself from an aggressive combination of France and Russia.

Under Emperor William II (1888-1918), Germany engaged in premature overseas enterprises and the aggressive development of a great navy. These actions alienated Great Britain, Japan, and the United States.

## Austria-Hungary
The Austro-Hungarian Monarchy (also known as the Dual Monarchy) was a country in central Europe. Its official name was established by the diploma of Emperor Francis Joseph I on November 14, 1868, replacing the name of the Austrian Empire. The monarchy consisted of two independent states: the "lands of St Stephen's Crown" (unofficially Hungary or Transleithania) and the kingdoms and lands represented in the council of the empire, the *Reichsrat* (unofficially Austria or Cisleithania).

As of 1905, the monarchy occupied approximately one-sixteenth of Europe's total area, covering 239,977 square miles. Its borders were defined by Russia and the German Empire to the north; Russia and Rumania to the east; Rumania, Servia, Turkey, and Montenegro to the south; and the German Empire, Liechtenstein, Switzerland, Italy, and the Adriatic Sea to the west. In 1901, its population was 45,405,267.

Under the Berlin Treaty of 1878, Austria-Hungary was granted administration of Bosnia and Herzegovina (which had a population of 1,591,036 in 1895 and an area of 19,702 square miles), while Turkey remained the suzerain. These territories were annexed in 1908, a move recognized by the Powers in 1909.

## Conflict and Collapse
By the end of 1916, Austro-German troops occupied large areas of Russia, France, Belgium, Serbia, and Roumania. While Russia was tottering toward collapse, German imperialists sought "world empire" and "triumph" rather than a reasonable peace. Russia eventually collapsed early in 1917.

Following the conflict, Germany was required to destroy fortifications, military establishments, and harbors on the Islands of Dune and Heligoland at its own expense. Germany was forced to renounce all rights, titles, and privileges to its own or its allies' territories outside of Europe. Furthermore, Germany agreed to respect the permanent independency of all territories that were part of the former Russian Empire and accepted the abrogation of the Brest-Litovsk treaty and other treaties made with the Maximalist Government of Russia.

## Other Historical Empires and Russia
The archive records several other imperial entities that interacted with Russia and Germany:

*   **The Mongol Empire:** Under Jengis Khan and his successor Ogdai Khan, the Mongols expanded across Asia. Jengis Khan conquered South Russia as far as Kieff, creating an empire reaching from the Pacific to the Dnieper. By 1235, Mongol hosts swept across Asia to Russia; Kieff was destroyed in 1240, and nearly all of Russia became tributary to the Mongols. In 1241, a mixed army of Germans and Poles was annihilated by the Mongols at the battle of Liegnitz in Lower Silesia.
*   **The Vandal Sea Empire:** Around 425 A.D., the Vandals moved from east Germany to Pannonia and then to Spain, where they encountered Visigoths from South Russia. Under Genseric, the Vandals captured Carthage in 439 and established a sea empire including Sicily, Corsica, Sardinia, and other western Mediterranean islands. This empire was eventually reconquered by the empire of Constantinople under Justinian I.
*   **The Russian Empire:** Under Tsar Nicholas I, Russia attempted to push toward Constantinople, leading to the occupation of the Danubian principalities in 1853. This triggered the Crimean War, as Great Britain and France allied to bolster Turkey and protect the Mediterranean route to India.
