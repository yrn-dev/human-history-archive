# Nadir Shah and Associated Historical Figures

## Nadir Shah of Persia
Nadir Shah was an energetic and able usurper of the Persian throne who lived until 1743. He is characterized as one of the Persian tyrants who enriched the tomb of Ali using spoils taken from the people. His military campaigns were extensive, spanning from Persia into Hindustan. During his invasion of Hindustan, he sacked Delhi. While stationed at Kandahar, Nadir Shah established his authority in Baluchistan by dispatching several detachments into the province. 

In 1739, after Nasir Khan—the second son of Abdulla Khan—had killed his brother Haji Mahommed Khan to end a period of tyrannical rule and oppressive taxation in Kalat, Nasir Khan reported the event to Nadir Shah. The Shah received this news with satisfaction and issued a firman appointing Nasir Khan as the *beglar begi* (prince of princes) of all Baluchistan. Nadir Shah also maintained a naval presence, as evidenced by Mulla 'Ali Shah, who served as his admiral and later established himself as an independent chief on the island of Hormuz and part of Kishm around 1765.

## Ahmed Pasha and the Independent Pashalic
During the first half of the 18th century, the Turkish empire experienced a decline in power and general disintegration. During this period, a governor-general named Ahmed Pasha established an independent pashalic from a province that had previously been nominally part of the Turkish empire. 

Nadir Shah attempted to annex this province back to Persia and laid siege to the city. However, Ahmed Pasha defended the city with such courage that Nadir Shah was forced to raise the siege after suffering great losses. Turkish authority over the pashalic was eventually restored in the first part of the 19th century.

## Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval
Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval (1675–1747), was a French adventurer born in Limousin who also took the name Ahmed Pasha. After a military career in the Royal Marine Corps and the army, where he served in the Netherlands and Italian campaigns, he faced a court-martial in 1704 and was condemned to death. He fled to Germany and subsequently served in the Austrian army under Prince Eugene, fighting against France and Turkey. He was wounded at Peterwardein and fought with distinction at Belgrade.

Following a second court-martial and a sentence of imprisonment and banishment due to his ungovernable temper, Bonneval offered his services to the Turkish government. He professed the Mahommedan faith, took the name Ahmed, and was made a pasha. He was appointed to command and organize the artillery, providing valuable services to the sultan during wars with Russia and with Nadir Shah. Although he was rewarded with the governorship of Chios, he was later banished to the shores of the Black Sea due to suspicions from the Porte. He died while contemplating a return to Christianity and Europe.

## Regional Impacts and Successors
The death of Nadir Shah in 1747 led to shifts in regional allegiance. Nasir Khan of Baluchistan acknowledged the title of Ahmad Shah (Durani), the king of Kabul, following Nadir's death. Although Nasir Khan declared himself entirely independent in 1758, leading to a conflict and a subsequent siege of Kalat, a treaty of peace was eventually reached. Under this treaty, Nasir Khan paid no tribute but provided troops to the king's armies in exchange for cash allowances. Nasir Khan remained an active prince until his death in June 1795.

In India, the decline of the Mogul empire after 1707 was marked by the independence of governors and the rise of the Mahratta confederacy. This period of confusion included the sacking of Delhi by Nadir Shah and repeated invasions by Ahmed Shah. The Mogul empire continued as a name until the Mutiny of 1857.
