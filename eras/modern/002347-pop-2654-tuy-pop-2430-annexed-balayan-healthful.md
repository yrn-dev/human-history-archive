# Municipal Annexations and Regional Profiles

The historical records of the early 20th century document a series of municipal annexations and the economic characteristics of various towns across the Philippine Islands and other global regions.

## Annexations in the Philippine Islands

During 1903, several towns in the Philippine Islands underwent administrative expansions. In the province of Batangas on the island of Luzon, the town and port of entry of Balayan, which had a population of 8,493, annexed Tuy (population 2,430) and Calatagan (population 2,654) in October of that year. Similarly, in the province of Pangasinán on Luzon, the town of Calasiao (population 16,539) annexed the neighboring town of Santa Barbara, which had a population of 10,367, after the census had been conducted.

In the province of Bulacán on Luzon, the town of Bulacán (population 11,589) annexed the town of Guiguintó, which had a population of 3,948, following the census enumeration. In the province of Pampanga on Luzon, the town of Macabebe reached a population of 21,481 after the annexation of San Miguel.

Annexations also occurred in the Visayas region. In the province of Iloilo on the island of Panay, the town of Cabatúan (population 16,497) annexed the neighboring town of Maasin, which had a population of 8,401. In the same province, the town of Barotac Nuevo (population 9,904) annexed the neighboring town of Dumangas (population 12,428) in 1903 after the census. On the island of Cebu, the town of Dumanjug (population 22,203) annexed the adjacent town of Ronda (population 9,662) after the census. Finally, in the province of Negros Oriental on the island of Negros, the capital town of Dumaguete (population 14,894) annexed the town of Sibulan (population 8,413) in 1903 after the census.

## Economic and Geographic Profiles of Philippine Towns

Many of these municipalities are situated in fertile districts. Balayan, located at the head of the Gulf of Balayan and approximately 55 miles south by west of Manila, possesses a healthful climate and volcanic soil. Its agricultural output includes coffee, cacao, cane-sugar, rice, cotton, pepper, Indian corn, native dyes, and fruits such as mangoes, bananas, and oranges. The town also relies on important fisheries and the raising of cattle and horses for market. The native language spoken there is Tagalog.

Cabatúan, located 15 miles northwest of the capital of Iloilo on a branch of the Suague river, was founded in 1732. It has a healthful climate and produces large quantities of Indian corn, sugar, coffee, tobacco, rice, and various fruits. The language of Cabatúan is Visayan.

In the province of Bulacán, the town of Bulacán is located on an arm of the Pampanga delta, 22 miles north-northwest of Manila, and is served by the Manila-Dagupan railway. The district is fertile, producing tropical fruits, indigo, rice, and sugar. The town is known for its fish-ponds and excellent fish, and its manufactures include sugar, piña, jusi, and ilang ilang perfume. Much of the town, excluding a few stone buildings and the churches, was destroyed by fire in 1898.

Other regional economies include Calasiao, which produces woven fabrics and hats, and Macabebe, where the principal industries are the weaving of cotton and hemp fabrics, the distilling of nipa alcohol, and the cultivation of sugar cane and rice. In Macabebe, the language is Pampango, and many male inhabitants serve as scouts in the U.S. Army.

In the province of Cebu, Dumanjug is located at the mouth of the Dumanjug river on the west coast, approximately 40 miles southwest of Cebu town. It maintains an important coast trade and successfully grows sugar-cane and Indian corn. In Negros Oriental, Dumaguete is located on Tanon Strait; while the inhabitants are chiefly natives, Chinese merchants keep the shops. The town features substantial public buildings, including a belfry and a watch-tower. In Iloilo, Barotac Nuevo lies in a fertile plain and deals in pina, trepang, and rice.

## International Municipal and Territorial Developments

Outside of the Philippines, other regions experienced similar administrative changes. In the United States, the city of Danville, Illinois, the county-seat of Vermilion county, expanded through several annexations: Vermilion Heights in 1905, South Danville (population 898 in 1900) in 1906, and Roselawn and Germantown (population 1,782 in 1900) in 1907. Danville is a center for coal trade due to the Grape Creek district and manufactures glass, flour, hominy, bricks, carriages, foundry products, and railway cars.

In the British West Indies, the island of Barbuda, a dependency of Antigua, was annexed by Great Britain in 1628. It was later bestowed upon the Codrington family in 1680, who held it as a feudal fief for over 200 years. The island is flat and wooded, with fertile cultivated areas and an air noted for its purity. Its exports include phosphates of lime and salt.
