# Chorillos and Miraflores

The battles of Chorillos and Miraflores were military engagements fought in January 1881 during a conflict involving Chile, Peru, and Bolivia. These battles were characterized by heavy slaughter and savage excesses committed by the Chilean troops.

## Strategic Context and Political Instability
The battles occurred during a period of significant political upheaval within the allied nations of Peru and Bolivia. In Peru, a double revolution resulted in the deposition of General Prado and the proclamation of Colonel Pierola as dictator. Similarly, Bolivia experienced internal instability. Troops at Tacna, citing the cowardice or incompetence of their president, removed him from command and elected Colonel Camacho to lead them. Concurrently, a revolution in La Paz proclaimed General Narciso Campero as president, a position he was formally elected to in June.

Throughout 1880, the struggle was primarily maintained at sea between Chile and Peru, with Bolivia playing little to no part in the fighting.

## Aftermath and the Fall of Lima
Following the heavy slaughter at Chorillos and Miraflores in January 1881, the cities of Lima and Callao surrendered almost immediately. This sequence of events left the Chilean forces practically as the masters of Peru.

Despite the fall of these major centers, the war continued in the interior of the country. Peruvian admiral Montero established a provisional government to maintain the struggle. In September 1882, Montero and President Campero held a conference where they decided to continue resisting in hopes of securing better terms.

## Conclusion of the War
The Peruvian forces eventually grew wearied of the struggle, which they viewed as useless. On October 20, 1883, Peru concluded a treaty of peace with Chile. As part of this resolution, Admiral Montero surrendered the town of Arequipa. Montero subsequently fled to Bolivia for refuge, though he was received coldly there and eventually withdrew to Europe.

The Chilean army of occupation concentrated at Arequipa on November 9. Meanwhile, the remnants of the Bolivian army were located at Oruro. Following negotiations, a peace treaty was signed between Chile and Bolivia on December 11. Under the terms of this treaty, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, including the port of Cobija.
