# The Sisters of Napoleon

The sisters of Napoleon—Marianne Elisa, Marie Pauline, and Maria Annunciata Caroline—were born in Ajaccio and played various roles in the political and social landscape of the French Empire and its Italian territories.

## Marianne Elisa (1777–1820)

Born on January 3, 1777, Marianne Elisa entered the establishment of St Cyr near Paris as a "king's scholar" through the efforts of her brothers. Following the disruption of this establishment by revolutionists in 1792, Napoleon took charge of her and returned her to Ajaccio. On May 5, 1797, she married Felix Bacciochi, a well-connected Corsican.

Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted her the principality of Piombino and, shortly thereafter, Lucca. In 1808, through her own importunities, she acquired the grand duchy of Tuscany. Because her husband, Bacciochi, was considered almost a nullity, Elisa's ability and pride allowed her to exert significant influence over Italian affairs and administration.

Her relationship with Napoleon was frequently strained. Between 1813 and 1814, she abetted Murat in his enterprises. After the fall of her brother, she retired with the title of countess of Compignano, residing first in Bologna and later in Santo Andrea near Trieste, where she died on August 6, 1820.

## Marie Pauline (1780–1825)

Born on October 20, 1780, in Ajaccio, Marie Pauline is described as the most beautiful and gayest member of the family. At seventeen, she married General Leclerc, a staff officer of Napoleon. She accompanied him to St Domingo, where he died of yellow fever in 1802.

Upon returning to Paris, she married Prince Camillo Borghese on August 23, 1803, and moved to Rome. However, she soon grew tired of him and returned to Paris, where she engaged in whims that caused some scandal. In 1806, she was granted the title of duchess of Guastalla. In 1810, she was removed from court due to her offhand treatment of the new empress, Marie Louise.

In 1814, Pauline joined "Madame Mere" in traveling to Elba. It is said that she expressed a desire to share Napoleon's exile on St Helena. She died of cancer in 1825. She is the subject of a well-known statue by Canova, which depicts her as Venus reclining on a couch.

## Maria Annunciata Caroline (1782–1839)

Born on March 25, 1782, in Ajaccio, Maria Annunciata Caroline married Joachim Murat early in 1800. Described as having an intriguing and ambitious nature, she used her power to advance Murat's interests. Under her influence and through his career, Murat served as the governor of Paris, a marshal of France (1804), the grand duke of Berg and Cleves (1806), and the lieutenant of the emperor in Spain (1808). In the early summer of 1808, Murat became the king of Naples.

Caroline was displeased by the distance between the capital of Naples and Paris. This led to strained relations with Napoleon, and she associated herself with the equivocal movements of her husband between 1814 and 1815. Before Murat's death at Pizzo on October 13, 1815, Caroline retired to Austrian territory, where she was placed under some measure of restraint. She eventually lived in Trieste with her sister Elisa and died on May 18, 1839.
