# Educational Funding and Administration in Selected Counties

The administration of education in various counties and boroughs involves a combination of school-board jurisdiction, grants earned by individual institutions, and supplemental funding provided by local government councils.

## Funding and Technical Education in Ayrshire
In Ayrshire, the shire operates under school-board jurisdiction. The region maintains secondary schools in Beith, Kilmarnock, Irvine, and Ayr, as well as a considerable number of voluntary schools. A specialized institution, the Kilmarnock Dairy School, was established in 1899 as part of the West of Scotland Agricultural College.

Funding for secondary and technical education in Ayrshire is derived from several sources. In addition to grants earned by the schools themselves, the borough and county councils expend significant sums of money. Further contributions are provided by the Kilmarnock Dairy School and the Glasgow and West of Scotland Technical College. These funds support technical classes at various local centres, offering instruction in engineering, mining, plumbing, gardening, agriculture, and various subjects in art and science.

## Educational Grants in Dumfries and Kincardine
In the shire of Dumfries, which is under school-board jurisdiction, some public schools earn grants for higher education. Academies are located in Annan, Moffat, Dumfries, and other centres. The bulk of the "residue" grant is provided by most of the borough councils and the county council to the county committee on secondary education. This committee utilizes the funds to subsidize high schools, provide apparatus and bursaries, and assist with building schemes. Additionally, the committee carries on technical and science classes covering practical chemistry, dairying (at Kilmarnock Dairy school), and agriculture.

Similarly, Kincardine is under school-board jurisdiction. Higher education grants are earned by a few public schools and the academy at Stonehaven. The county council provides the "residue" grant to the county secondary education committee, which then applies these funds toward grants for technical education.

## Schooling and Subsidies in Banffshire
Most schools in Banffshire are under school-board jurisdiction, and several earn grants for higher education. The county council utilizes the "residue grant" to subsidize various classes, specifically those in cookery and laundry work, veterinary science, navigation, and agriculture.

Furthermore, schoolmasters in Banffshire, along with those in the shires of Elgin and Aberdeen, benefit from a bequest made by James Dick (1743-1828), a West India merchant. Dick left over £110,000 to promote higher learning for these teachers. This bequest generates an annual income of £4,000, which is distributed to "dominies" who qualify via examination.

In Barrow, educational institutions include a technical school, a girls' high school, and a school of science and art.
