# The Taurus Range and Associated Highlands

The Taurus range is a primary geographical feature of Asia Minor, extending across the entire length of the peninsula to the south. It originates in the Armeno-Kurdish highlands east of the Euphrates and continues into the Aegean Sea via islands and rocky promontories.

## Topography and Elevation
The Taurus range has an average elevation of approximately 7,000 ft. However, its height varies significantly across different regions. In the Bulgar Dagh of Cilicia, the range reaches altitudes exceeding 10,000 ft., while in Lycia, it attains a height of 10,500 ft. Within the Lycian region, specific peaks are noted, such as Massicytus, which reaches 10,000 ft., and Solyma, which stands at 7,800 ft.

The range is pierced by the tributaries of the Jihun and Sihun rivers east of the Bulgar Dagh, though these rivers do not break the continuity of the range. The southern slopes of the Taurus chain feature numerous grassy "alps" known as *yailas*, which provide abundant water for nomads and villagers who move their flocks there during summer. These slopes are also covered by extensive forests of fir and oak.

## The Anti-Taurus and Related Ranges
The term "Anti-Taurus" is subject to various interpretations among geographers:
* Some define it as a more or less continuous range that parallels the Taurus and buttresses the central plateau to the north.
* Others describe it as a line of mountain peaks and heights separating waters that flow toward the Mediterranean and the Persian Gulf from those flowing toward the Anatolian plateau and the Black Sea. This interpretation traces the range from the source of the Kizil Irmak southwest toward Mt. Argaeus, eventually joining the Taurus at the sharp-peaked ridge of Ala Dagh, which reaches 11,000 ft.
* A third view identifies the true Anti-Taurus as a range of high, forest-clad mountains connected to a line of bare hills south of Sivas. This range includes a subsidiary chain called the Binboa Dagh, located in the latitude of Kaisarieh, which separates the waters of the Jihun from the Sihun.

Additionally, the range of Amanus (Giaour Dagh) is separated from the Taurus by the deep gorge of the Jihun. It runs south-south-west to Ras el-Khanzir, marking the boundary between Syria and Cilicia. Its highest point is Kaya Duldul, rising abruptly from the Jihun's bed to 6,500 ft.

## Regional Passes and Communications
The Taurus and Anti-Taurus ranges are crossed by several principal passes, many of which served as routes for Byzantine or Roman roads:
* **The Cilician Gates (Gulek Boghaz):** A pass at 3,300 ft. leading from Tarsus to Iconium or Caesarea Mazaca.
* **The Syrian Gates (Beilan Pass):** A route for the great Roman highway from Tarsus to Syria.
* **The Amanides Pylae (Baghche Pass):** A road connecting the Cilician Plain to Apamea-Zeugma on the Euphrates.
* **The Climax ("Ladder"):** A steep, rugged pass providing the only direct communication between Pamphylia and Lycia, situated between the sea and Solyma.
* **Other routes:** Passes exist from Laranda to Anemourium or Kelenderis (via Coropissus and the southern Calycadnus), and from Laodicea to Adalia via the Istanoz Chai valley and Khonas pass. Routes also connect Caesarea to Flaviopolis and to Germanicia (Marash) via the Anti-Taurus and Kuru Chai.

## Broader Regional Context
The Taurus range forms part of a larger high region in Asia west of British India (excluding Syria and Arabia). This extensive plateau covers an area comparable to Tibet but at a lower altitude. This high region encompasses Armenia, Persia (or Iran), Afghanistan, Baluchistan, and the provinces of Asia Minor. 

The southern and southwestern face of this plateau is formed by a mountain scarp that flanks the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, rising in many points to 10,000 ft. This scarp extends through Armenia and Kurdistan before turning along the Taurus range toward the northeastern angle of the Mediterranean.
