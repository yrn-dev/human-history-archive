# Indian Settlers and Populations

The presence of Indian populations across various global regions varies significantly by geography, ranging from colonial settlers in Africa to indigenous populations in the Americas and administrative territories in Asia.

## Indian Settlers in East Africa
In certain protectorate regions of East Africa, Indian immigrants have established themselves in various capacities. In the coast-land, the population is mixed, consisting of Indian immigrants alongside Arabs and Swahili, as well as representatives from interior tribes. Further inland, Indian settlers are found in the same districts as the white population, specifically within the Kenya region, the rift-valley, and the Kikuyu uplands.

To address the needs of the region, a scheme was developed in 1907 to assist the immigration of British Indians to Victoria Nyanza and the regions adjacent to the coast. These specific districts were identified as being unsuitable for settlement by Europeans.

## Indigenous Indian Populations of the Americas
In the Americas, "Indian" refers to indigenous peoples with diverse histories of settlement, conflict, and displacement.

### Central America and the Caribbean
In British Honduras, native Indians are found primarily in forest villages in the north and west, away from the sea coast. The colony's population includes a hybrid race descended from white settlers, aboriginal Indians, and negro slaves. Additionally, there are populations of Spanish-Indian origin in the south (from Honduras and Guatemala) and in the north (having arrived from Yucatan in 1848). The region contains ruined cities and traces of a lost Indian civilization.

In Costa Rica, the native Indian population is estimated at 4,000. While exterminated in some districts and civilized in others, some remain in a state of complete savagery in the Talamanca country and along parts of the Nicaraguan border, where they are known as Guatusos or Prazos. These groups live in stockaded encampments and maintain their ancestral customs and language. Additionally, the Mosquito Indians visit the Atlantic coast every summer for turtle fishing.

### North America
In the United States, indigenous Indian populations faced significant conflict over land claims during the expansion west of the Alleghany Mountains. In the Kentucky region, the land was claimed by the Six Nations (Iroquois) as western conquests and by the Cherokees as hunting grounds, though it was inhabited by a small tribe of Shawnees in the north and a few Chickasaws near the Mississippi river.

In Illinois, Indians resisted the extinguishment of their land claims, leading them to support the British during the War of 1812. Following the Black Hawk War—led by Sauk leader Black Hawk in 1832—all Indians were removed from Illinois by 1833. In the Indian Territory (Oklahoma), Colorado, and New Mexico, the Kiowas—a fierce people and the sole representatives of the Kiowan linguistic stock—conducted raids on Texas settlers until 1868. After a subsequent breakout in 1874, they were subdued and settled in Oklahoma, numbering approximately 1,200.

## Administrative Territories in Asia
By 1815, the British Empire included approximately one-third of the Indian peninsula. This territory was under the rule of the East India Company, which was noted as the only entity among the European powers of the time showing a spirit of expansion.
