# La Vie de boheme (1849)

## Overview
*La Vie de boheme* is a play first produced in 1849. It was written by the French dramatist Theodore Barriere and was adapted from a book by Henri Murger. The adaptation of the work was carried out with the assistance of the novelist Murger himself.

## Authorship and Production
The play was one of the most successful works produced by Theodore Barriere (1823–1877). Barriere was born in Paris to a family of map engravers who had a long-standing connection with the war department. Before pursuing a career in the theater, Barriere spent nine years in the service of the war department.

Barriere's transition to drama occurred after the success of a vaudeville he performed at the Beaumarchais. This particular work was quickly adopted into the repertory of the Palais Royal, an event that revealed his true vocation to him. Following this success, Barriere spent the next thirty years writing over a hundred plays, either alone or in collaboration with others.

## Context within Barriere's Work
*La Vie de boheme* stands as a significant entry in a prolific career that included several other successful plays, such as *Manon Lescaut* (1851), *Les Filles de marbre* (1853), *L'Heritage de Monsieur Plumet* (1858), and *Les Faux Bonshommes* (1856), the latter of which was written with Ernest Capendu. His later works included *Le Gascon* (1878) and *Malheureux vaincus* (1865), though the latter was forbidden by the censor. Barriere continued his work in Paris until his death on October 16, 1877.
