# Bolivia by the Rio de la Plata Route

The Rio de la Plata route serves as one of the primary geographic and historical conduits connecting the Atlantic coast of South America to the interior regions of Bolivia. This route involves the navigation and traversal of the Rio de la Plata estuary and the surrounding provinces of Argentina, extending westward toward the Andes.

## Geographic Context of the Rio de la Plata Region
The route begins in the maritime province of Buenos Aires, Argentina. This province is the largest and most populous in the republic, with an official 1903 population estimate of 1,251,000. It is bounded to the north by Entre Rios and Santa Fé, to the east by the Atlantic and the La Plata estuary, and to the south by the Atlantic. 

The landscape of the Buenos Aires province is characterized largely as a treeless plain that extends from the Atlantic and La Plata estuary westward to the Andes. While the region is well-watered by numerous lakes and small rivers—the largest being the Rio Salado del Sud—the south-eastern and central areas are low and marshy. The province features a few natural ports, including Bahia Blanca, though the government has constructed artificial ports at Buenos Aires and Ensenada (La Plata) to accommodate ocean-going steamers.

## River Systems and Biodiversity
The river systems of Bolivia are categorized into three distinct regions: the Central Plateau, the Amazon, and the La Plata. The La Plata system is a critical component of the drainage and transport network of the region.

In the waters of the Rio de la Plata estuary, specific wildlife is found, such as the La Plata dolphin (*Stenodelphis blainvillei*). This species reaches approximately 5 feet in length and possesses a palish brown color that harmonizes with the brown-colored water of the estuary.

## Historical and Military Movements
The route between the Rio de la Plata and Bolivia (historically referred to as Upper Peru) was a primary theater of conflict during the War of Independence from July 1809 until August 1825. After the patriots of Buenos Aires liberated the interior provinces of the Rio de la Plata from Spanish dominion, they directed their military efforts toward Upper Peru.

The scene of action for this prolonged warfare lay primarily between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta. Key events along this route included:
* **1811:** General Balcarce led the army of Buenos Aires in a successful invasion of Upper Peru, celebrating the first anniversary of independence near Lake Titicaca in May. However, by June, the patriot army was defeated by the Spanish army under Goyeneche and driven back into Jujuy.
* **1815:** A total rout of the patriot forces occurred in a battle located between Oruro and Potosi.
* **1816:** The Spanish general Laserna attempted to invade the Argentine provinces with the intention of marching on Buenos Aires, but he was forced to retire due to the activity of irregular *gaucho* troops from Jujuy and Salta.

## Administrative and Religious Framework
The destination of the route, Bolivia, is organized into several administrative and religious districts. The state recognizes and supports the Roman Apostolic Catholic religion, as defined in Article 2 of its constitution. The republic is divided into three bishoprics (La Paz, Cochabamba, and Santa Cruz) and one archbishopric. The archbishopric, known as the archbishopric of La Plata, has its seat at Sucre and encompasses the departments of Potosi, Oruro, Tarija, Chuquisaca, and the Chilean province of Antofagasta.
