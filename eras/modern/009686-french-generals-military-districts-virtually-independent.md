# French Generals as Military Districts in Spain

During the Peninsular War, the administrative and military structure of Spain underwent significant changes under the influence of Napoleon. While Joseph was placed on the Spanish throne in May 1808, his actual power was severely limited. Despite proclamations of benevolent intentions issued at Bayonne on June 23, 1808, reconciliation between the French and the Spanish people proved impossible due to Napoleon's treatment of the de facto king, Ferdinand VII.

## The Erosion of Joseph's Authority

King Joseph's sovereignty was largely titular. Following a Spanish success at Baylen, Joseph was forced to leave Madrid hastily in August 1808. Although Napoleon reinstated him by the end of that year, Joseph remained in a subordinate position. This lack of genuine power led the king to offer his abdication on four separate occasions, though the emperor ignored these requests and instead commanded him to govern with more energy.

The reality of Joseph's position was described by his trusted adviser, Miot de Melito, who noted in his memoirs that while Joseph aspired to be a constitutional king, the events of 1808 and 1809 meant he could only succeed by acting as "the mere instrument of a military power." By this stage, the king had ceased to exist as a monarch in reality, bearing a title that served as an oppressive burden and retaining only a semblance of authority as a general over a small portion of the French army.

## Establishment of Independent Military Districts

Between February and May 1810, Napoleon further diminished Joseph's control by restructuring the administration of the territory. The emperor placed the north-eastern and northern provinces under the command of French generals. These areas were organized as military districts that functioned virtually independent of Joseph's authority. Despite the king's protests, these changes remained in place.

## Financial Strain and Final Terms

The depletion of the treasury reduced Joseph to the last extremity. In April 1811, he traveled to Paris to either seek better terms or offer his abdication. He eventually returned to Spain with a monthly subsidy of 500,000 francs. Additionally, he received a promise that the army of the centre—which was the smallest of the five French armies—would be under his direct control.

## Later Developments

In late 1811, Napoleon united Catalonia to France. Joseph's tenure in the capital ended abruptly following the victory of Wellington at Salamanca on July 22, 1812, which compelled the king to leave Madrid.
