# BAKEWELL

Bakewell is a market town located in England within the western parliamentary division of Derbyshire. It is situated on the river Wye, 25 miles north-north-west of Derby, and is served by the Midland railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 2,850.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town's church, All Saints, is mentioned in Domesday. While tradition credits King John with the construction of the nave, the western side of the tower is considered to be older. The interior of the church contains several monuments belonging to the Manners and Vernon families, as well as admirable examples of encaustic tiles. In the churchyard stands an ancient runic rood-stone.

Other notable buildings and institutions include:
* **St John's Hospital:** Almshouses founded in 1602.
* **Free Grammar School:** Endowed in 1637 by Lady Grace Manners.
* **Modern Facilities:** The town hall and museum, as well as the Bakewell and High Peak Institute.

In the vicinity, Castle Hill contains the remains of an earthwork believed to have been raised in 924 by Edward the Elder. The parish also includes the mansions of Castle Hill and Burton Closes.

## Industry and Local Features
Bakewell is known for the working of marble and zinc in the surrounding area. Sir Richard Arkwright established the cotton manufacture within the town. 

The town is also noted for a chalybeate spring used for cases of chronic rheumatism, which features attached baths. Additionally, the town has gained fame for a type of jam-cake known as a "Bakewell pudding."

## Geography and Surroundings
The scenery in the neighborhood is described as very beautiful, particularly within the Derwent and Wye valleys. Two miles from the town are the meadows and gardens of Haddon Hall, and to the east lies the domain of Chatsworth.

The nearby village of Eyam, located near the Derwent and noted as being especially picturesque, was almost depopulated by the plague of 1665. This outbreak was carried to the village from London. The rector of Eyam, William Mompesson, received wide notice for his brave efforts to combat the plague.
