# ATHENS (GEORGIA, U.S.A.)

## Overview and Geography
Athens is a city located in the northeastern part of Georgia, U.S.A., where it serves as the county-seat of Clarke county. Geographically, it is situated approximately 73 miles east by north of Atlanta. The city was chartered in 1872.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Athens has shown steady growth at the turn of the 20th century. In 1890, the population was 8,639. By 1900, this number increased to 10,245, a population that included 5,190 negroes and 114 foreign-born residents. According to the 1910 census, the population had risen further to 14,913.

## Education
Athens is recognized as an important educational centre. The city was founded in 1801 specifically to serve as the seat of the university of Georgia, an institution that had been chartered in 1785. The university's academic department, Franklin College, opened in 1801. 

Over time, several other branches of the university were established within Athens:
* The State College of Agriculture and Mechanic Arts (which became the School of Science in 1872).
* The State Normal School, which opened in 1891 as a co-educational institution.
* The School of Pharmacy and the University Summer School, both established in 1903.
* The School of Forestry and the Georgia State College of Agriculture, both established in 1906.

Additionally, the Lumpkin Law School, incorporated in 1859, became the university's law department in 1867. Beyond the university, the city hosts several secondary schools and the Lucy Cobb Institute for girls. The institute opened in 1858 and was named after the daughter of its founder, Gen. T.R.R. Cobb (1823-1862).

While Athens is the primary hub, other university branches are located elsewhere: the Georgia School of Technology in Atlanta (1885), the medical department in Augusta (founded as the Georgia Medical College in 1829; university department in 1873), the North Georgia Agricultural College in Dahlonega (established 1871; university part in 1872), and the Georgia Normal and Industrial College for Girls in Milledgeville (1889). The Georgia Industrial College for Colored Youth was established near Savannah in 1890.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The city is served by several railways, including the Southern, the Seaboard Air Line, the Georgia, the Central of Georgia, and the Gainesville Midland.

Athens maintains various manufacturing industries, with the most significant being the production of cotton goods, fertilizers, and cotton-seed oil and cake. The city's industrial growth was notable in the early 20th century; in 1905, the total value of factory products was $1,158,205, representing a 70.9% increase over the previous five years.
