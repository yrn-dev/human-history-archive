# The 2nd Earl of Buckinghamshire

## Identity and Nomenclature
John Hobart (1723–1793) served as the 2nd Earl of Buckinghamshire. Throughout his life, he consistently signed himself as "Buckingham." This practice extended to his contemporaries, who knew him by that name. This naming convention resulted in a certain amount of confusion, as it persisted until 1784, the year George Grenville, Earl Temple, was created the marquess of Buckingham.

## Early Career and Diplomatic Service
Before ascending to his title, John Hobart held several prominent positions. He served as the comptroller of the royal household and was a member of parliament representing Norwich. His career included significant diplomatic and administrative roles, most notably serving as the ambassador to Russia from 1762 to 1766.

## Lord-Lieutenancy of Ireland
From 1776 to 1780, the 2nd Earl of Buckinghamshire served as the lord-lieutenant of Ireland. Historical records indicate that he was hardly equal to the exceptional difficulties he encountered while holding this position.

## Family and Succession
The 2nd Earl was the eldest son of John Hobart (c. 1694–1756), the 1st Earl of Buckinghamshire. The 1st Earl had been a descendant of Sir Henry Hobart, who served as chief justice of the common pleas and attorney-general under James I, and the great-grandson of Sir James Hobart, attorney-general to Henry VII. The 1st Earl had been created Baron Hobart of Blickling in 1728 and later became earl of Buckinghamshire in 1746.

The 2nd Earl died at Blickling Hall in Norfolk on August 3, 1793. Because he died without sons, he was succeeded as 3rd earl by his half-brother, George (c. 1730–1804). 

## Estates and Legacy
Upon the death of the 2nd Earl, his Norfolk estates and Blickling Hall did not pass to his successor in the earldom. Instead, they passed to his daughter, Henrietta (1762–1805). Henrietta married William Kerr, who later became the 6th marquess of Lothian, thereby bringing the Hobart estates into the Kerr family.
