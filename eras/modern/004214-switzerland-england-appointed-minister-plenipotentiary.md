# Diplomatic Appointments and Plenipotentiaries

The role of the minister plenipotentiary and other diplomatic representatives has been central to the international relations of various nations, facilitating the negotiation of treaties, the establishment of peace, and the management of foreign commerce.

## French Diplomatic Service

Several French figures held significant diplomatic posts during the revolutionary and imperial eras. François, Marquis de Barthelemy (1747 or 1750–1830), was educated for a diplomatic career by his uncle, the abbé Jean Jacques Barthelemy. His early service included roles as secretary of legation in England, Switzerland, and Sweden. He was eventually appointed minister plenipotentiary in Switzerland, where he was responsible for negotiating the treaties of Basel with Spain and Prussia in 1795. Barthelemy's political career later included election to the Directory in May 1797, though he was subsequently arrested during the coup d'état of 18 Fructidor (September 17, 1797) and deported to French Guiana. After escaping to the United States and England, he returned to France following the 18 Brumaire, entering the senate in February 1800.

Louis Pierre Edouard, Baron Bignon (1771–1841), entered the diplomatic service in 1797 after General Huet brought his abilities to the attention of Talleyrand, the minister of foreign affairs. Bignon served in legations in the Cisalpine republic and Switzerland before being appointed attachè to the French legation at Berlin in 1799, where he became chargé d'affaires three years later. Between 1804 and 1806, he served as minister-plenipotentiary at Cassel, playing a prominent role in forming the confederation of the Rhine. He later served as an administrator of finances and public domains in Prussia (after the battle of Jena) and Austria (after the battle of Wagram), and became the French resident at Warsaw at the end of 1810.

## American and Japanese Diplomacy

The United States utilized plenipotentiaries to negotiate peace and manage international missions. In 1779, Francis Dana (1743–1811) was appointed secretary to John Adams, who had been selected as minister plenipotentiary to negotiate commerce and peace treaties with Great Britain. Dana later served as a diplomatic representative to the Russian government from 1781 to 1783.

Anson Burlingame (1820–1870), an American legislator and diplomat, was appointed minister to China in June 1861 after Austrian authorities objected to his appointment as minister to Austria. In November 1867, Burlingame was appointed envoy extraordinary and minister plenipotentiary to lead a Chinese diplomatic mission to the principal European nations and the United States. This embassy arrived in the U.S. in March 1868 and concluded a series of articles in Washington on July 28, 1868.

From Japan, Count Jutaro Komura (1855–) served as a minister in Washington, St Petersburg, Seoul, and Peking. In 1905, he was appointed by his sovereign to meet Chinese representatives in Peking and Russian plenipotentiaries at Portsmouth. These meetings resulted in the conclusion of the Peking treaty and the Portsmouth treaty in November and September of 1905, respectively. Komura also negotiated the second Anglo-Japanese alliance.

## Other International Missions

Portuguese diplomacy saw Jose Francisco Correa da Serra (1750–1823) appointed as secretary to the Portuguese embassy in 1797. After a period of residence in Paris, he was made the Portuguese minister-plenipotentiary at Washington in 1816.

In South American relations, Bolivia resumed diplomatic relations with Great Britain in 1896. In July 1897, Senor Aramayo was sent to London as minister plenipotentiary, a mission that resulted in an extradition treaty concluded in March 1898.

## Regional Contexts

Diplomatic and political movements often intersected with military and territorial conflicts. In 1803, Napoleon's occupation of Switzerland precipitated a crisis that led to renewed war with England. Meanwhile, individuals such as the Hungarian soldier Georg Klapka (1820–1892), after serving as minister of war and defending Komárom, lived in exile for many years, residing first in England and later primarily in Switzerland.
