# Interlaken

Interlaken is a location in Switzerland associated with the Bernese Oberland region. It serves as a connecting point for transportation and historical administration within the area.

## Transportation and Connectivity
Interlaken is linked to the valley of Grindelwald via a rack railway, which covers a distance of 13 miles. Grindelwald is noted as one of the primary resorts for tourists in Switzerland and is characterized by its green landscape, fruit trees, and excellent pastures.

## Historical Administration
Historically, the Austin canons of Interlaken held significant influence over the surrounding region. In the valley of Grindelwald, these canons gradually purchased all other land ownership. This arrangement continued until 1528, when the house of the Austin canons was suppressed by the town of Bern, an event that resulted in the inhabitants of the valley gaining their freedom.

## Geographic Context
Interlaken is situated near Bönigen, a village with 1,515 inhabitants. Bönigen is located on the western side of Lake Brienz. Lake Brienz is the first lake into which the river Aar expands and is situated in a deep hollow within the Swiss canton of Bern. 

Lake Brienz is described as having a gloomy and sad character when compared to its neighbor, the lake of Thun. The lake's surface is 1,857 feet above sea level, with a maximum depth of 856 feet. Its area is 11½ square miles, with a length of approximately 9 miles and a width of 1½ miles. The lake's chief affluent is the Lütschine, which flows from the valleys of Lauterbrunnen and Grindelwald. The lake is bounded by the village of Brienz on the east—a center for the Swiss wood-carving industry with 2,580 inhabitants—and Bönigen on the west. Other features of the lake include the hamlet of Iseltwald and the Giessbach Falls on the south shore, while the north shore contains several small villages. The first steamer was introduced to Lake Brienz in 1839.
