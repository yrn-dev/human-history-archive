# Captives of the Ashanti War: Kuhne and Bonnat

The capture of several Europeans by Ashanti forces in 1869 served as one of the minor causes leading to a subsequent war between the British and the Ashanti. These events took place against a backdrop of territorial disputes and diplomatic tensions regarding the Gold Coast.

## The Capture of the Europeans
In June 1869, an Ashanti force invaded Krepi, a territory located beyond the Volta. During this invasion, the Ashanti captured Mr. Fritz A. Ramseyer, his wife, and their infant son. Also captured during this event were Mr. J. Kuhne and Mr. Ramseyer; both were members of the Basel mission. In a separate incident at a different location, a French trader identified as Monsieur M.J. Bonnat was also captured.

Following their capture, these individuals were taken as prisoners to Kumasi. While negotiations for the release of the captives were initiated, the Europeans remained in captivity during the period when the Elmina fort was transferred from the Dutch to the British on April 2, 1872.

## Context of the Ashanti Conflict
The primary cause of the war was the British occupation of the Elmina fort. The Ashanti viewed the Elmina as their subjects and considered the fort to be Ashanti territory. The king of Ashanti held the "custom-note," which entitled him to an annual payment from the Dutch. While the Dutch viewed this payment as a present for trade purposes, it originated as a ground rent for the fort.

The Ashanti delayed the onset of war until their preparations were complete, while officials on the Gold Coast believed the risk of hostilities was remote. However, on January 22, 1873, an Ashanti force crossed the Prah and invaded the British protectorate. This force defeated the Fanti and encamped at Mampon near Cape Coast, causing significant alarm among the local inhabitants.

## British Military Response
In response to the invasion, the British parliament voted £800,000 to fund an expedition for the defense of the territory and the punishment of the Ashanti. Sir Garnet (later Viscount) Wolseley was dispatched as the British administrator. 

Upon landing at Cape Coast on October 2, Wolseley observed that the Ashanti forces had been decimated by fever and smallpox and were preparing to return home. Despite this, Wolseley determined to march to Kumasi. He was supported by Captain (later Sir) John Glover, R.N., the administrator of Lagos, who led a force of native levies to cooperate from the east and take the Ashanti in the rear.

*   **The Basel Mission:** In addition to the captured members, the archive notes that C.C. Reindorf, a native pastor of the Basel mission, authored the work *History of the Gold Coast and Asante ... from about 1500 to 1860*, published in Basel in 1895.
*   **Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat:** Distinct from the French trader captured by the Ashanti, Leon Joseph Florentin Bonnat (born June 20, 1833) was a prominent French painter born in Bayonne. He became a professor of painting at the Ecole des Beaux Arts in 1888 and succeeded Paul Dubois as its director in May 1905. A museum bearing his name, the Musee Bonnat, is located at Bayonne and houses collections of drawings by Durer.
