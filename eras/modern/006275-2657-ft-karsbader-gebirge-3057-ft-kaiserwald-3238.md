# The Mountain Ranges of North-West Bohemia

## Geography of the Bohemian Interior
The interior of Bohemia is characterized as an undulating plateau that generally exceeds 1,000 feet in height. This region is formed by a series of terraces that slope gradually from the south toward the north. While some have compared the interior to a deep basin, its lowest points are located in the north within the valley of the Elbe. 

The country is divided into two primary sections by a line that passes through Komotau, Prague, and Hohenmauth. The area situated to the south of this line is designated as highland, whereas the area to the north is classified as lowland.

## Interior Mountain Ranges
The interior of Bohemia contains several distinct mountain ranges. In the middle of the region lies the Brdywald, which reaches an elevation of 2,798 feet. The northern corner of the interior is occupied by the Mittelgebirge, a volcanic massif that extends on both sides of the Elbe and reaches 2,739 feet.

In the north-west part of Bohemia, there are three specific mountain ranges:
*   **The Tepler Gebirge**, with a height of 2,657 feet.
*   **The Karsbader Gebirge**, with a height of 3,057 feet.
*   **The Kaiserwald**, which is the highest of the three at 3,238 feet.

## Marienbad and the Kaiserwald
The town of Marienbad is located in Bohemia, Austria, situated on the outskirts of the Kaiserwald at an altitude of 2,093 feet. By rail, it is 115 miles west of Prague and 40 miles south-west of Carlsbad. 

The town is surrounded on all sides, with the exception of the south, by gently sloping hills covered in fragrant pine forests and intersected by walks. Marienbad is recognized as one of Europe's most frequented watering-places. Its principal infrastructure includes a theatre, the *Kurhaus* (built in 1901), an English church, several hospitals, and a Roman Catholic church completed in 1851.

## Mineral Springs and Therapeutic Use
The mineral springs of Marienbad, which belong to the adjoining abbey of Tepl, number eight. These springs are utilized for drinking and bathing, though the Marienquelle is reserved exclusively for bathing. The springs are categorized by their chemical compositions:
*   **Alkaline-saline waters**: Found in the Ferdinandsbrunnen and the Kreuzbrunnen, these resemble the waters of Carlsbad but are cold and contain nearly double the amount of purgative salts.
*   **Iron waters**: The Karolinenbrunnen and the Ambrosiusbrunnen are among the strongest iron waters in the world.
*   **Earthy-alkaline waters**: Provided by the Rudolfsbrunnen.

These waters are employed to treat obesity, diabetes, gout, and liver affections, requiring patients to follow a strictly regulated diet during their cure. In addition to mineral baths, the town offers mud-baths using peat that is the richest in iron in the world. The town's mineral water industry is significant, with approximately 1,000,000 bottles exported annually.
