# Prairial and the French Convention

The term Prairial appears in the archive's records primarily in relation to the French Revolutionary calendar and the political upheavals of the late 18th century, specifically regarding the proceedings of the Convention and subsequent legal actions.

## The Convention and François Antoine de Boissy d'Anglas

During the year III of the French Republic, the Convention faced significant instability. François Antoine de Boissy d'Anglas, a French statesman and member of the "centre" (known as *le Marais*), played a prominent role during this period. Boissy d'Anglas had previously been a deputy to the states-general in 1789 and had later served as *procureur-syndic* for the directory of the department of Ardeche during the Legislative Assembly.

Boissy d'Anglas demonstrated notable composure during the critical days of Germinal and Prairial of the year III. On the 12th of Germinal, while reading a report on food supplies in the tribune, he was interrupted by rioters who invaded the hall of the Convention; upon their withdrawal, he quietly resumed his report. On the 1st of Prairial, Boissy d'Anglas presided over the Convention. During this session, he remained unmoved despite facing menaces and insults from insurgents. This steadfastness continued even when the severed head of deputy Jean Feraud was presented to him.

## Legal and Political Events of Prairial

The archive records several other distinct events occurring during the month of Prairial across different years of the revolutionary and post-revolutionary era:

### Financial Decrees
In the year III, the legal status of precious metals was subject to rapid change. A law dated 6 floréal declared that silver and gold were merchandise; however, this law was subsequently repealed by a decree on 2 prairial.

### The Conspiracy of Babeuf
In 1797, the high court of justice at Vendome conducted a trial involving the socialist Babeuf and his accomplices. Following the trial, on Prairial 7 (April 26, 1797), Babeuf and a man named Darthe were condemned to death. Other prisoners, such as Buonarroti, were exiled, while others, including Vadier and fellow-conventionals, were acquitted. Babeuf and Darthe were executed on Prairial 8, 1797.

### The Coup of Year VIII
The political landscape continued to shift in the year VIII. On the 30th of Prairial, a parliamentary *coup d'etat* took place. Following this event, Pierre Roger Ducos—a politician from the Landes who had sat in the "Plain" of the Convention and voted for the death of Louis XVI—was named a member of the executive Directory. This appointment was facilitated by the influence of Barras, who intended to use Ducos as a passive instrument.
