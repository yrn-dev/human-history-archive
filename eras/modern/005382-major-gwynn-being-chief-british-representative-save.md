# Major C.W. Gwynn and the Anglo-Abyssinian Boundary

## The Anglo-Abyssinian Commission
Following the establishment of a protectorate, the northern region of the district remained largely unknown and lacked formal administration for several years. During this period, the native populations were subject to frequent raids by Abyssinians and Gallas. Due to the absence of a defined frontier, the Abyssinian government extended its posts southward to Lake Rudolf and established control over the Boran country.

To resolve these territorial ambiguities, negotiations were held, resulting in an agreement signed at Adis Ababa on December 6, 1907. This agreement established the boundary line between the river Juba and Lake Rudolf. In 1908-1909, an Anglo-Abyssinian commission was tasked with the delimitation of this frontier. Major C.W. Gwynn served as the chief British representative for this commission.

## Territorial Delimitations
The work of the commission and the resulting agreement defined the regional borders as follows:
*   **Lake Rudolf:** Assigned to the British, with the exception of its north-eastern extremity.
*   **Lake Stefanie:** Assigned to Abyssinia.
*   **The Daua River:** The frontier was established from approximately 4° 20' N. at the Daua to the point where it joins the Juba.

## Other Contributions of Dr. Gwynn
Beyond his diplomatic and military role in the Anglo-Abyssinian commission, a Dr. Gwynn contributed to the study of ancient texts. In 1892, he published the Apocalypse from a manuscript belonging to Lord Crawford. This work was part of the study of the Philoxenian Version, a translation made in A.D. 508 by Polycarpus, a chorepiscopus, for Philoxenus, the bishop of Hierapolis. 

The Philoxenian Version is primarily known through notes in manuscripts of the Harklean version. While most of the version has not survived, fragments exist in Jude, 2 Peter, 2 and 3 John, and the Apocalypse. The first four of these were used to supplement the older Peshito version, as those epistles were not originally included in it. Dr. Gwynn's scholarly work on this subject includes the 1897 publication *The Apocalypse of St John in a Syriac Version* (Dublin) and an article published in volume vii of *Hermathena* in 1890.
