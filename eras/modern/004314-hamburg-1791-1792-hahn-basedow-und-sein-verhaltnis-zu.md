# Johann Bernhard Basedow

## Life and Education
Johann Bernhard Basedow (1723–1790) was a German educational reformer. He was born in Hamburg on September 11, 1723, to a father who worked as a hairdresser. Basedow received his education at the Johanneum in Hamburg, where he was influenced by H. S. Reimarus (1694–1768), the author of the *Wolfenbuetteler Fragmente* (published by Lessing). 

In 1744, Basedow moved to Leipzig to study theology; however, he shifted his focus entirely to the study of philosophy. This period of study initially led him toward sceptical notions. He eventually returned to the Christian faith after a more thorough examination of sacred writings and related matters, though he formed his beliefs according to his own ideas in retirement, resulting in a faith that was not orthodox.

## Professional Career
Between 1749 and 1753, Basedow served as a private tutor for a nobleman's family located in Holstein. During this time, he demonstrated remarkable abilities as an educator of the young. His distinction in this field led to his appointment in 1753 as a professor of belles-lettres and moral philosophy at the academy of Soro in Denmark.

Basedow's tenure in Denmark ended in 1761 when he was removed from his post due to his theological opinions. He was subsequently transferred to Altona. In Altona, the orthodox clergy viewed him with great disfavour because of some of his published works. While he was forbidden from continuing to provide instruction, he was permitted to keep his salary.

## Educational Reform and Works
Toward the end of 1767, Basedow abandoned the study of theology to focus his efforts on education, aiming for a general reform of the system in Germany. In 1768, he published *Vorstellung an Menschenfreunde fuer Schulen, nebst dem Plan eines Elementarbuches der menschlichen Erkenntnisse*. This work was strongly influenced by Rousseau's *Emile*.

In this publication, Basedow proposed several reforms, including:
* The reform of schools.
* The reform of common methods of instruction.
* The establishment of an institute dedicated to qualifying teachers.

Basedow solicited subscriptions to print his *Elementarwerk*, a work intended to illustrate and explain his principles at length through the use of plates. These subscriptions reached a total of 15,000 Talers (£2250). In 1774, he published the *Elementarwerk* in four volumes, which presented a complete system of primary education designed to develop intelligence.
