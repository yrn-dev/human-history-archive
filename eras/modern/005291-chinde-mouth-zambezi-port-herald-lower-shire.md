# From the Chinde Mouth of the Zambezi to Port Herald on the Lower Shiré

The region extending from the Chinde mouth of the Zambezi to Port Herald on the lower Shiré is a territory characterized by specific transport networks, diverse linguistic groups, and a variety of geological and mineral resources. This area is part of a broader region often designated as British Central Africa.

## Transport and Communication
Communication between the Chinde mouth of the Zambezi and Port Herald on the lower Shiré is maintained via light-draught steamers. However, during the dry season, which occurs from April to November, these steamers are not always able to ascend as far as Port Herald; in such instances, barges are utilized to complete the voyage. 

From Port Herald, a railway connects to Blantyre, which serves as the commercial capital of the Shiré Highlands. Additionally, the "Cape to Cairo" railway expanded through the region in the early 20th century, crossing the Zambezi in 1905 and the Kafukwe in 1906. This line reached the Broken Hill mine in 1907 and was extended to the frontier of the Belgian Congo by 1909. Other regional communications include regular steamer services between ports on Lakes Tanganyika and Nyasa. The protectorate is also traversed by the African trans-continental telegraph line, founded by Cecil Rhodes, which includes a branch line running from Fort Jameson—the headquarters of the Chartered Company in North Eastern Rhodesia—to Lake Nyasa.

## Linguistics and Languages
The region is home to the Nyanja language group, one of the most extensive cognate groups in the Bantu field. This group is associated with the shores of the southern half of Lake Nyasa, the entire valley of the Shire (excluding parts of the Shire highlands) down to its junction with the Zambezi, and the lands on both banks of the Zambezi including its delta.

Specific dialects within this region include:
* **Ci-mazaro**: Spoken in the Lower Zambezi.
* **Ci-sena or Ci-nyungwe**: Spoken in Sena and Tete (Zambezi).
* **Ci-mobo and Ci-machinjiri**: Spoken in the Shire valley.
* **Ci-mananja**: Spoken in the Shire highlands.
* **Ci-podzo**: A very distinct language found in the delta of the Zambezi, though it belongs to the Nyanja genus.

## Geography and Settlements
The area is situated within the broader context of British Central Africa, a term that officially referred to the British protectorate on the Shiré and Lake Nyasa starting in 1893. In 1907, this protectorate was renamed the Nyasaland Protectorate.

Key settlements in and around this region include:
* **Port Herald**: Located on the lower Shiré.
* **Blantyre**: The principal European town, situated in the Shiré Highlands at an elevation of approximately 3,000 feet. It was founded in 1876 by the Church of Scotland mission and named after the birthplace of Livingstone.
* **Zomba**: The government capital of the protectorate, located at the base of the mountain of the same name.
* **Chiromo**: Situated at the junction of the Shiré and Ruo rivers.

## Geology and Mineral Resources
The geological formation of the region is primarily Archean and Primary, consisting largely of gneiss, schists, quartz, felspar, and granite. The basin of the river Shiré, the Lake Nyasa rift depression, and the northern watershed of the Zambezi contain Primary limestones and sandstones.

The region is rich in various minerals:
* **Gold**: Found in the Shiré Highlands, the mountainous region west of Lake Nyasa, and the hills along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting.
* **Silver and Lead**: Silver (as galena or silver-lead) and lead are found in the hills of the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting.
* **Other Minerals**: Limestone is present in the Shiré basin. Iron ore and mica are found almost universally throughout the area. There are also indications of petroleum in places along the Nyasa-Zambezi waterparting.
