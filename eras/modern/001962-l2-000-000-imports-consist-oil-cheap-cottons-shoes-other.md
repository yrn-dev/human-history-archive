# Trade and Industry in Bagdad

## Commercial Trade and Volume
Bagdad serves as a significant center of commerce with a substantial disparity between its import and export values. The annual export trade of the city amounts to approximately L750,000. In contrast, the import trade is considerably larger, totaling about L2,000,000 annually.

## Imports and Local Impact
The imports into Bagdad consist primarily of oil, shoes, and cheap cottons, along with other similar goods. These imported items are increasingly replacing picturesque native manufactures. The influence of these imports is evident in the local customs and daily life; for example, Bedouin Arabs now wear headdresses made of cheap European cotton stuff purchased in or near Bagdad. 

Furthermore, petroleum tins of five gallons have become the common water vessels throughout the country. These tins also serve a secondary industrial purpose, as they provide the metal used in native bazaars to manufacture various utensils.

## Exports
The staple articles exported from Bagdad include dates, wool, and hides.

## Communications and Diplomacy
Bagdad maintains connections to Europe through two postal services and two telegraph lines, one of which is Turkish and the other British. 

The city hosts several diplomatic representatives, including consuls from the United States, Austria, France, and Russia. The British consul-general also serves as the political agent to the Indian government. This official holds a status second only to that of the British ambassador at Constantinople. The consul-general's presence is supported by a guard of sepoys and a gunboat in the river, and the residency includes an Indian post-office.

## Postal History
Historically, the British government operated a camel-post across the desert to Damascus. This service was abandoned around 1880 following the establishment of a similar service by the Turks. Through the Turkish camel-post, letters were able to reach Damascus in nine days.
