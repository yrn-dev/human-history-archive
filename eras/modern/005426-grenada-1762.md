# Grenada

Grenada is the southernmost of the Windward Islands in the British West Indies. It is an oval-shaped island measuring 21 miles in length and 12 miles at its maximum breadth, covering a total area of 133 square miles. Geographically, it is situated 85 miles north by west of Trinidad and 140 miles southwest of Barbados, located between 11 deg. 58' and 12 deg. 15' N. and 61 deg. 35' and 61 deg. 50' W.

## Geography and Natural Features
The island is of volcanic origin. Its landscape is characterized by a well-wooded range of mountains that traverse the island from north to south, creating fertile and picturesque valleys. The highest point is Mount Catharine, which reaches an elevation of 2,750 feet. In the northwest and southeast, the terrain consists of undulating or low ground used for cattle raising and fruit growing.

The island's geology includes porphyry, basaltic rocks, argillaceous schist, hornblende, and red and grey sandstones. Raised limestone beaches are found in the extreme north. Natural resources worked on the island include fuller's earth and sulphur.

Grenada features several notable water bodies and springs. In the center, at 1,740 feet above sea level, is the Grand Etang, a 13-acre circular lake located in an ancient crater; a large sanatorium and health resort are situated nearby. Lake Antoine, also in a crater, is located in the northeast at nearly sea level. The island is served by numerous fresh water springs, as well as sulphurous and hot chalybeate springs, and several short rivers primarily in the south and east.

## Climate
The climate is described as good with an equable temperature, and epidemic diseases are rare. In the lowlands, the average yearly temperature is 82 deg. F., while the heights are cooler. Rainfall is very heavy, reaching 200 inches annually in some areas. The rainy season occurs from May to December, though refreshing showers happen throughout the year. Recorded average annual rainfall is 79.07 inches at St Georges and 164 inches at Grand Etang.

## History
Grenada was discovered by Columbus in 1498 and named Conception. Although granted to the British in 1627, neither the British nor the Spanish settled the island. In 1650, du Parquet, the governor of Martinique, purchased the island. The French were initially well received by the Caribs, but the French subsequently extirpated them with great cruelty.

Administrative control shifted in 1665 when the French West India Company took over, managing the island until the company's dissolution in 1674, at which point Grenada passed to the French Crown. In 1714, cotton, coffee, and cocoa were introduced to the island.

During the conflicts between France and Great Britain, Grenada capitulated to British forces in 1762 and was formally ceded via the Treaty of Paris the following year. The French re-captured the island in 1779 under Count d'Estaing, but the Treaty of Versailles in 1783 restored it to Great Britain. In 1795, a rebellion against British rule occurred, instigated and assisted by the French; this was quelled in 1796 by Sir Ralph Abercromby.

The emancipation of slaves occurred in 1837. By 1877, East Indian labour was introduced to the island. Cocoa became the staple crop, which prevented Grenada from experiencing the economic depression that affected other sugar-growing islands in the West Indies.

## The Grenadines
The Grenadines are a chain of approximately 600 rocks and islets stretching 60 miles in a northeast to southwest direction between Grenada and St Vincent. For administrative purposes, these are divided between the two larger islands. 

Carriacou belongs to Grenada and is the largest islet in the group, measuring 7 miles long and 2 miles wide with an area of 13 square miles. It features a central ridge of hills reaching 700 feet, where admirable building stone is found. The island has two good harbours on its west coast: Tyrell Bay to the south and Hillsborough Bay, where the chief town of Hillsborough is located. The population of Carriacou consists largely of a negro peasantry working on the *metayer* system on small lots. The chief exports of the island are cattle and cotton, and excellent oysters are found along the coast. As of 1901, the population of the Grenadines group was 6,497, mostly residing on Carriacou.
