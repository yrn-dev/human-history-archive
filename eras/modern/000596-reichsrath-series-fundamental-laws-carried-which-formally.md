# Reichsrath

The Reichsrath served as the parliamentary body for the Austrian empire, playing a central role in the establishment of government structures, the ratification of international compacts, and the ongoing political struggle between various ethnic and ideological groups.

## Establishment and Fundamental Laws
Following the appointment of Beust as minister-president on February 7, 1867, an edict was issued ordering the diets to elect a Reichsrath according to the constitution. Upon meeting, the government held a large majority. In the House, where all races except the Czechs were represented, the *Ausgleich* (the compact with Hungary) was ratified almost unanimously.

Following this ratification, the Reichsrath carried a series of fundamental laws that formally established a parliamentary government. These laws introduced the responsibility of ministers and provided the parliament with complete control over the budget. Additionally, the laws included clauses that guaranteed personal liberties and rights consistent with modern constitutions. The territories involved were officially designated as "the kingdoms and lands represented in the Reichsrath."

## Composition and Electoral Changes
The composition of the Reichsrath was a point of significant contention between German Liberals and Federalists. Initially, the influence of the Poles, supported by the Slovenes, Dalmatians, and Tirolese, prevented the introduction of direct elections. This ensured that the functions of the Reichsrath remained slightly less extensive than they had previously been.

A major shift occurred in 1873 with the passing of a new electoral law. This measure transferred the right to elect members of the Reichsrath from the diets to the direct vote of the people. This change was designed to weaken the Federalists; it removed their ability to use the diets to formally refuse the appointment of deputies. While the Poles withdrew from the Reichsrath in protest and a petition of 250,000 names was presented from Bohemia, the law passed with a vote of 120 to 2. Along with this change, the number of members was increased to 353.

## The Delegation and Budgetary Power
The Reichsrath's power was further complicated by the system of the Delegation. Unlike in Hungary, where the Delegation was elected by each House as a whole, the Austrian Delegation was chosen by representatives of separate territories. For example, Galicians chose 7 delegates, Bohemians chose 10, and Trieste chose 1. 

Because the Delegation was not representative of the majority of the chamber of deputies but included representatives of all groups, opposition to the government could persist even within the Delegation. This was evidenced in 1869 during a joint sitting to settle a budget point; the Poles and Tirolese voted in favor of a Hungarian proposal that Austria had rejected.

## Political Conflict and Obstruction
The Reichsrath was frequently the site of ethnic and political deadlock. In one period, the Polish club, Tirolese, and Slovenes left the House after Galician demands were rejected, leaving only 110 members—primarily Germans and German representatives from Bohemia and Moravia.

Later conflicts centered on Paragraph 14 of the Constitutional law of 1867. This paragraph allowed the emperor to proclaim orders requiring Reichsrath assent in cases of "pressing necessity" if the Reichsrath was not in session. These orders required the signature of the whole ministry and had to be laid before the Reichsrath within four months or receive approval from both Houses to remain valid. 

Germans contended that applying this clause to the *Ausgleich* was invalid. This dispute led to the retirement of Thun in September 1899. His successor, Count Clary, attempted to restore peace by withdrawing the offending ordinances, but he was forced to resign after less than three months because the Czechs used obstruction to prevent a necessary excise law. Subsequently, a ministry of officials used Paragraph 14 for state purposes before making way for a ministry under Herr v. Koerber.

## Early History and the Kremsier Assembly
In earlier periods, the Reichsrath's role fluctuated with the empire's stability. Following the revolution, a Reichsrath assembled on July 10, where Slav deputies held a majority. This body passed a law on August 31 abolishing feudal services. 

Later, a Reichsrath transferred to Kremsier discussed "fundamental rights" and the reconciliation of theoretical unity with the actual dualism of the empire. However, this assembly was dissolved following the battle of Kapolna in February 1849. The emperor subsequently published a constitution on March 4 for a monarchy that was "one and indivisible," though this proved distasteful to the Magyars, Croats, and Czechs.
