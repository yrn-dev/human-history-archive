# Budaun and its Waterways

## Geography and Hydrography
The district of Budaun is characterized by a low, level, and generally fertile landscape. It is watered by several key rivers, including the Ganges, the Ramganga, the Mahawa, and the Sot (also referred to as the Yarwafadar). The town of Budaun itself is situated near the left bank of the river Sot. The district covers an area of 1,987 square miles.

## History of Budaun
Tradition suggests that Budaun was founded around A.D. 905. An inscription likely dating from the 12th century lists twelve Rathor kings who reigned at Budaun, which was then called Vodamayuta. The first authentic historical event recorded is the town's capture by Kutb-ud-din in 1196, an event that established the town as a significant post on the northern frontier of the Delhi empire.

During the 13th century, two governors of Budaun ascended to the imperial throne: Rukn-ud-din Firoz and his father, Shams-ud-din Altamsh. Altamsh is credited with the construction of a large, handsome mosque featuring a dome, built in 1223 using materials from an ancient Hindu temple.

The town's prominence declined over subsequent centuries. It was burnt in 1571, and approximately one hundred years later, during the reign of Shah Jahan, the seat of the governorship was moved to Bareilly. Eventually, the area came under the power of the Rohillas.

## British Administration and Modern Era
The district of Budaun was ceded to the British government in 1801 by the nawab of Oudh. By 1838, Budaun had been designated as the headquarters of a British district. During the events of 1857, the local population sided with the mutineers and established a native government. This administration lasted until April 1858, when British authority was restored following General Penny's victory at Kakrala.

As of 1901, the population of the district was 1,025,753, while the town of Budaun had a population of 39,031. The district's economy includes several indigo factories, and Bilsi serves as the primary center of trade.

## Infrastructure and Education
Budaun is served by two lines of the Oudh & Rohilkhand railway, as well as a narrow-gauge railway line connecting it to Bareilly. 

The town contains the ruins of an immense fort. Educational facilities include a high school for boys and several girls' schools maintained by the American Methodist mission.
