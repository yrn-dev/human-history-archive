# King's College, Windsor, and Harvard University

The provided archival records contain various references to individuals associated with King's College (in multiple locations), Windsor, and Harvard University, spanning academic, political, and athletic contexts.

## Harvard University

Harvard University appears in the records as a center for higher education in law, mathematics, and classical studies, as well as a participant in early organized amateur athletics.

### Academic Appointments and Alumni
Several notable figures are recorded as having studied or taught at the university:
*   **Abbott Lawrence Lowell**: A graduate of Harvard College in 1877 (highest honours in mathematics) and Harvard Law School in 1880. He served as a lecturer in 1897 and professor of government in 1898, eventually succeeding Charles William Eliot as president of the university in 1909.
*   **James Bradstreet Greenough**: A graduate of 1856 who spent one year at Harvard Law School. He served as a tutor in Latin (1865), assistant professor (1873), and professor of Latin (1883). Greenough offered the first courses in comparative philology and Sanskrit at Harvard between 1872 and 1880 and assisted in the founding of Radcliffe College.
*   **William James Ashley**: An English economist who served as a professor of economic history at Harvard University after resigning a post at Toronto University in 1892.
*   **James Russell Lowell**: An author and diplomatist who graduated from Harvard College in 1838.

### Athletics
Harvard was a founding member of the first college league for organized amateur base-ball in 1879, alongside Brown, Amherst, Princeton, and Dartmouth (with Yale joining in 1880). In 1887, Harvard joined Princeton, Yale, and Columbia to form the Eastern College League. While the Eastern College League was later dissolved, the records note that amateur base-ball continued to be played by university clubs.

### King's College, Windsor and London
Sir Frederick William Borden, a Canadian statesman born in 1847, was educated at King's College, Windsor, and at Harvard University. 

In London, King's College is associated with:
*   **Alfred Barry**: Educated at King's College, London, he later served as the institution's principal from 1868 to 1883.
*   **Thomas Roscoe Rede Stebbing**: A Fellow of King's College, London.

### King's College, Aberdeen
In Scotland, King's College in Aberdeen is linked to the Gregory family of physicians. Dr. James Gregory served as a professor of medicine there. His son, John Gregory (1724–1773), was educated at the college and later elected as a professor of philosophy. John Gregory also received a doctor of medicine degree from King's College, Aberdeen, while studying in Leiden. Following the death of his brother, James, in 1755, John Gregory was appointed to the office of professor of medicine at the college.

### Windsor
Beyond the educational institution in Windsor, the records mention Alfred Barry, who was appointed as a canon of Windsor in 1891.
