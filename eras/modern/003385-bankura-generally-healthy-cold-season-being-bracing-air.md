# Bankura

Bankura is a town and district located within the Burdwan division of Bengal in British India. The town has a population of 20,737, while the district, which covers an area of 2,621 square miles, had a population of 1,116,411 in 1901, reflecting a 4% increase over the preceding decade.

## Geography and Boundaries
The district is bounded by the Manbhum district to the west, the Midnapur district to the south, and the Burdwan district to the north and east. Geographically, Bankura serves as a connecting link between the mountainous highlands of Chota Nagpur in the west and the Ganges delta in the east. 

The landscape varies significantly across the district. Along the eastern boundary adjoining Burdwan, the land is alluvial and flat, resembling the typical paddy lands found in Bengal. Moving toward the north and west, the elevation rises into long undulating tracts. In these areas, swamps and rice lands are replaced by forests of trees or low thorny jungle. As one moves toward the western boundary, hamlets become more scattered and eventually nearly disappear into wild forests.

## Economy and Industry
The district's economy is characterized by a mix of natural resource extraction and artisanal manufacturing. In the hilly tracts, tussur silk and lac are gathered in large quantities. The district also contains two coal-mines and indigo factories, though its minerals and stone quarries are little worked. 

Local industries include the weaving of silk and cotton, as well as the carving of plates and other items from soap-stone. Bishnupur, the old capital of the country, remains the primary center for these local industries.

## Climate and Environment
The climate of Bankura is generally considered healthy. The air is dry and wholesome, and fogs occur rarely. The cold season is described as bracing, while the hot season is relaxing and very oppressive in temperature. 

Despite the generally healthy climate, the district is susceptible to destructive floods and drought. It was affected by famines during the years 1866, 1896-1897, and 1874-1875.

## Infrastructure and History
Bankura is served by several railway lines. The East Indian railway skirts the north-east part of the district beyond the Damodar river. Additionally, there is a railway line connecting Howrah to Bankura, and the Midnapur-Jherria line of the Bengal-Nagpur railway passes through the district.

Historically, the Bishnupur raj was one of the largest estates in Bengal toward the end of the 18th century. However, shortly after the permanent settlement of 1793, the estate was sold due to arrears of revenue.
