# Johan Baner

## Early Life and Military Career
Johan Baner (also referred to as Banner or Banier) was a Swedish soldier who lived from 1596 to 1641. He was born on June 23, 1596, at Djursholm Castle. Baner entered the Swedish army and established a record of distinction while serving in wars against Poland and Russia. By 1630, he had attained a high rank in the military by the time Gustavus Adolphus landed in Germany.

## Service in the Thirty Years' War
As one of the chief subordinates to the king, Baner participated in the campaign in north Germany. During the first battle of Breitenfeld, he was tasked with leading the right wing of the Swedish horse. His service was further noted during the taking of Munich and Augsburg, as well as at Donauwoerth and the Lech. 

During an unsuccessful assault on the camp of Wallenstein at the Alte Veste, Baner was wounded. Following this, when Gustavus marched toward Luetzen, Baner was left in command in the west, where he faced the imperial general Aldringer.

## Command as Field-Marshal
Two years after the events at Luetzen, Baner entered Bohemia as a Swedish field-marshal leading 16,000 men. He marched on Prague in combination with the Saxon army. However, his advance was halted following the complete defeat of Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar during the first battle of Noerdlingen. 

Despite the precarious position the Swedish army faced due to the peace of Prague, Baner, along with Torstensson and Wrangel, won victories at Wittstock (October 4, 1636) and Kyritz. These successes restored the paramount influence of Sweden within central Germany, even though the combined Swedish forces were decidedly inferior in number to the armies they defeated.

## Later Campaigns and Death
By 1637, Baner was unable to make further headway against the enemy. After rescuing the beleaguered garrison of Torgau with great difficulty, he retreated into Pomerania, moving beyond the Oder. In 1639, Baner again overran northern Germany, where he defeated the Saxons at Chemnitz, and subsequently invaded Bohemia.

Baner spent the winter of 1640-1641 in the west. His final achievement was an audacious *coup-de-main* on the Danube. In a rare 17th-century occurrence, he broke camp in mid-winter to unite with the French forces under the comte de Guebriant. Together, they surprised Regensburg while the diet was sitting; however, the capture of the location was prevented by the break-up of the ice. Baner was forced to retreat to Halberstadt, where he died on the 10th.
