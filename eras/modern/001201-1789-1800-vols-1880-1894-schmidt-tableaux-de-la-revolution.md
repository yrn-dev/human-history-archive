# 1789-1800 (4 vols., 1880-1894) and Tableaux de la Revolution

The works titled *Paris pendant la Revolution d'apres les rapports de la police secrete, 1789-1800* (published in four volumes between 1880 and 1894) and *Tableaux de la Revolution francaise, &c.* (published in Leipzig, 1867-1870) are historical studies authored by Adolf Schmidt. The former is a French translation by P. Viollet of Schmidt's earlier work, *Pariser Zustaende wahrend der Revolutionszeit von 1789-1800*, published in Jena in 1874. These texts are based on a collection of reports from the secret police.

## Historical Context and Figures

The period covered by these works, 1789 to 1800, encompasses the chaos of the French revolutionary era. One significant figure mentioned in relation to this period is Gracchus Babeuf. Described as a type of the French revolutionists—warm-hearted, excitable, and half-educated—Babeuf is noted for having lost his moral and mental balance during the revolutionary chaos. 

Historically, Babeuf is recognized as the first person to propose socialism as a practical policy. He is considered the father of movements that later became prominent during the revolutions of 1871 and 1848. Documentation regarding Babeuf includes the *Debats du proces, &c.*, a full report of the trial at Vendome published in four volumes in Paris in 1797.

## Military and Political Developments

The revolutionary period also saw the influence of military writers and strategists. Philippe Henri, Comte de Grimoard (1753-1815), a French soldier, had entered the royal army at sixteen and published an *Essai theorique et practique sur les batailles* in 1775. By the start of the Revolution, Grimoard had become a highly valued counsellor to Louis XVI on military and political matters, despite holding the rank of colonel. He was intended to be the next Minister of War. In 1791, he was tasked with preparing the scheme of defence for France, which later provided significant assistance to the Committee of Public Safety in 1793. However, the events of 1792 ended his military career, leading him to spend the rest of his life writing military books.

Other military contributions to the era include the writings of Dubois-Crance, specifically his *Observations sur la constitution militaire, ou bases du travail propose au comite militaire*.

## Revolutionary Documentation and Literature

The history of the period is further documented through the study of revolutionary groups and individuals. The Cordeliers, for instance, produced various papers that are enumerated in M. Tourneux's *Bibliographie de l'histoire de Paris pendant la Revolution* (1894). This bibliography includes references to the trial of the Hebertists.

Additionally, the works of Jean Paul Marat, known as "l'ami du peuple," provide insight into the intellectual and political climate of the time. Marat's writings spanned various subjects, including:
*   **Scientific works:** *Recherches physiques sur l'électricité, &c.* (1782), *Recherches sur l'électricité médicale* (1783), and *Notions élémentaires d'optique* (1764).
*   **Observations on aviation:** Letters regarding the "fatale catastrophe" of the aeronauts Romain and Pilatre de Rozier, as well as aérostation (1785).
*   **Political and social critiques:** *Les Charlatans modernes, ou lettres sur le charlatanisme académique* (1791) and an *Éloge de Montesquieu* (1785).
