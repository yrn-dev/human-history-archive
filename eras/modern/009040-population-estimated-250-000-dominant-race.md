# Bokhara

## Geography and Climate
Bokhara is characterized by extreme climatic conditions that vary significantly by elevation. In the lowlands, the region experiences very hot summers followed by short, cold winters. During these winters, the Oxus may freeze for a period of a fortnight, and temperatures may drop to a frost of -20 deg. Fahr. 

In the highlands, the hot and dry summer is followed by a winter lasting four months. In regions situated above 8,000 ft., there is a significant development of glaciers and snowfields, which result in the burial of passes under snow and a rainy short summer. The lowlands are also subject to terrible sand-storms originating from the west, which are capable of killing cotton trees and exhausting men. Additionally, malaria is widely prevalent in the area, becoming malignant in some years following a wet spring.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Bokhara is estimated at 1,250,000. The dominant race is the Uzbegs, who are described as fanatical Moslem Sunnites. The Uzbegs maintain their old divisions into clans or tribes, despise their Iranian subjects, and scorn work.

The population includes several other distinct groups:
* **Nomads:** Approximately 20% of the population are nomads, and about 15% are semi-nomads. Among these are the nomad Kirghiz and the nomad Turkomans, both of whom are of Turkish origin.
* **Sarts:** These individuals constitute the bulk of the population found in the towns and are a mixture of Iranians and Turks.
* **Iranian Tajiks:** These people compose the great bulk of the population in the country and differ very little from the Sarts.

Other groups present in the region include Persians, Arabs, Afghans, Armenians, and Jews. Furthermore, a colony of Hindus from Shikarpur manages much of the trade.
