# The Expedition to Panama and the Career of Morgan

## The Attack on Panama
In 1671, a force of 2,000 buccaneers—who held the official title of "privateers"—launched a major attack on the Isthmus of Panama. This operation was authorized through commissions granted by Thomas Modyford, the governor of Jamaica (1620–1679), who maintained a close alliance with these privateers. The strategic objective of the mission was the route used to transport bullion from South American mines to Porto Bello for shipment to Spain.

The buccaneers began their campaign by seizing Chagres before marching toward Panama. The journey was difficult, requiring the men to travel by canoe and on foot. Upon reaching the shores of the South Sea and sighting the city, the force engaged the defenders on the morning of the tenth day. The engagement resulted in the rout of the defenders and the capture of the town, which was subsequently burnt, whether accidentally or intentionally. The sack of the city was characterized by great barbarities.

## Division of Booty and Aftermath
Despite the capture of the city, the Spaniards had managed to remove the treasure before the buccaneers took control. When the remaining booty was divided, Morgan was accused of defrauding his followers. The resulting share per man was small, and many of the buccaneers died of starvation during their attempt to return to Jamaica.

The fallout from the expedition was significant. Governor Modyford was recalled, and in 1672, Morgan was called home and imprisoned in the Tower. He eventually returned to Jamaica in 1674 as lieutenant-governor, accompanying Lord Vaughan. However, he remained highly unpopular due to the 1671 expedition; he was threatened in the streets by the relatives of those who had died during the campaign. In his later years, Morgan shifted his activities toward suppressing the buccaneers, who by that time held inconvenient claims against him.

## Historical Epochs of the Buccaneers
The history of the buccaneers is divided into three distinct periods. The first epoch lasted from their initial rise until Morgan's capture of Panama in 1671. During this time, they operated without government restriction and without government aid. The second epoch extended from 1671 to 1685, marking the height of their power. During this phase, their operations expanded beyond the Caribbean to encompass the Pacific range from Chile to California. The third and final period began after 1685, characterized by disintegration and disunion, as the "rude honour" and independence of the earlier eras devolved into brutality and unmitigated vice.
