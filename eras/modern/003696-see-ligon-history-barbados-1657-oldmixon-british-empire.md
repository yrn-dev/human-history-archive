# Barbados

Barbados is an island located in the British West Indies, situated 78 miles east of St Vincent at 13deg 4' N. and 59deg 37' W. The island covers an area of 166 square miles (106,470 acres), a size roughly equal to the Isle of Wight. It measures 21 miles in length and 14-1/2 miles at its broadest point.

## Geography and Physical Features
The island's coasts are encircled by coral reefs that extend up to 3 miles seaward in certain areas. While the configuration of the land is elevated, it is not mountainous. The highest point is Mount Hillaby, located near the center of the island at an elevation of 1,100 feet. From this apex, the land descends toward the sea in a series of terraces. These hills incline so gently that the ascent is scarcely noticeable when driving on the island's well-constructed roads.

The only natural harbor is Carlisle Bay, located on the south-western coast. This harbor is described as a shallow roadstead and is accessible only to vessels with a light draught.

## Etymology and Early History
There are differing opinions regarding the origin of the name "Barbados." It may be derived from a species of bearded fig-tree, or from a Spanish word referring to the hanging branches of a vine that strike root in the ground. In maps from the 16th century, the name appeared in various forms, including Barnodo, Barnodos, Barbudoso, Bernados, and St Bernardo.

The island contains more numerous traces of the Carib Indians than any other of the Antilles. It is believed that the Portuguese were the first to visit the island.

## British Colonization and Settlement
Barbados is characterized by a process of peaceful colonization; it was acquired without conquest and has remained in British possession since its acquisition. The British ship "Olive Blossom" touched the island in 1605, and its crew took possession in the name of James I after finding it uninhabited.

The first actual settlement occurred in 1625. This was directed by Sir William Courteen under a patent from Lord Leigh (later the earl of Marlborough), to whom the king had granted the island. In 1627, following a compromise with Lord Marlborough, the earl of Carlisle obtained a grant for the island, based on a 1624 royal grant of all the Caribbean islands. 

In 1628, Charles Wolferstone, a native of Bermuda, was appointed governor. That same year, the present capital was founded when sixty-four settlers arrived at Carlisle Bay.

## Political History and Governance
During the English Civil War, Barbados became a refuge for many Royalists. Under the leadership of Lord Willoughby, who had leased the island from the earl of Carlisle, these Royalists offered stout resistance to the forces of the Commonwealth. Lord Willoughby was eventually exiled following his defeat.

After the Restoration, the crown took over the government of the island to appease planters who were uncertain about the titles of the estates they had developed into valuable properties. To satisfy the claims of the patentees, the proprietary or patent interest was abolished, and a duty of 4-1/2% was imposed on all exports.

By 1684, a census conducted under the governorship of Sir Richard Dutton recorded a population of 20,000.
