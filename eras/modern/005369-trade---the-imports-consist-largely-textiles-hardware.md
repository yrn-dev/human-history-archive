# Trade

Trade patterns across various global regions are characterized by the exchange of raw materials, agricultural products, and manufactured goods, with significant influence from colonial ties and geographical positioning.

## African Trade and Commerce
In Uganda, external trade is dominated by exports, which include cotton, fibre, rubber, ivory, beeswax, chillies, potatoes, grains, hides, skins, and copra. Imports consist largely of manufactured goods, hardware, and textiles from Europe and India; these two regions together supply over 50% of total imports, with Germany holding the leading share among other countries. The retail trade is primarily managed by Indians. The value of exports grew from £89,858 in 1900-1901 to £234,664 in 1904-1905. By 1906-1907, the total volume of trade reached £1,194,352, with imports valued at £753,647 and exports at £440,705. The United States receives 33% of Uganda's exports, followed by Great Britain at 15%.

In Beira, the port serves as a transit point for Rhodesia. In 1907, goods valued at L647,000 passed through the port to Rhodesia. Local exports include gold (from Manica), chromite (from Rhodesia), beeswax, oil seeds, ground-nuts, sugar, and rubber. Imports for local use include cotton goods and rice from India, while machinery, hardware, manufactured goods, and foodstuffs are imported for Rhodesia. Between 1905 and 1907, the average annual value of local trade (excluding Rhodesian transit) was L200,000 for imports and L90,000 for exports.

## Asian and Middle Eastern Commerce
Burma's chief exports are timber and rice. Rice exports rose from 1,419,173 tons (valued at Rs.9,77,66,132) in 1805 to 2,187,764 tons (valued at Rs.15,67,28,288) in 1905. England is the primary recipient of this rice, though it is also consumed in Germany, Holland, Belgium, Italy, and France. Trade can be deflected by famines; for example, over one million tons of rice were shipped to India during a famine in 1900. Other exports include petroleum, cotton, caoutchouc (india-rubber), jade, and cutch dye. The primary imports are woollen, silk, and cotton piece-goods, along with liquors, tobacco, sugar, gunny bags, and hardware.

In Kuwet, total trade in 1904-1905 was valued at £160,000. Exports include wool, dates, pearls, and horses, while imports consist of sugar, coffee, rice, piece goods, and ammunition and arms.

## Americas and Caribbean Trade
Bolivia's foreign trade is limited by the poverty of its people and geographical constraints, which restrict exports to pastoral, forest, and mineral products. In 1903, principal exports included tin and its ores (L1,039,298), silver and its ores (L636,743), rubber (L260,559), copper ores (L157,609), coca (L28,907), other minerals (L20,948), bismuth (L16,354), and cinchona (L9,197). Imports in 1904 totaled L1,734,551 and included machinery, hardware, clothing, spirits, wines, provisions, live-stock, woollens, and cottons. Much of this trade is credited to Peru and Chile as it passes through Mollendo, Arica, and Antofagasta.

In British Honduras, imports rose from £252,500 in 1901 to £386,123 in 2005, while exports rose from £285,500 to £377,623. Exports include sponges, turtles, tortoiseshell, deerskins, rum, and vegetable products, fruit, and timber. Principal imports are specie, groceries, spirits, wine, beer, hardware, and cotton goods. Trade is mainly shared between the United States and Great Britain.

In another region, exports officially valued at L1,398,000 in 1904 included gold, tortoiseshell, mother-of-pearl, hides and skins, dye-woods, cabinet-woods, cocoa, bananas, and coffee. Coffee and bananas accounted for over L1,250,000 of this value. More than 20,000,000 lb. of coffee (nearly 85%) was sent to Great Britain. The banana trade grew rapidly after 1881; by 1904, over 6,000,000 bunches were exported. Imports in 1904 were valued at L1,229,000, with the United States providing 52%, Great Britain 19%, and Germany 13%.

## European and Russian Trade
The Azores' trade, once a Portuguese monopoly, is now shared with Germany and the United Kingdom. Exports consist of provisions, natural mineral waters, wine, and fruit, with pineapples being especially important (940,000 were exported in 1902 and 1903 to Hamburg and London). Imports include coal from Great Britain; sugar from the United States, Madeira, and Germany; textiles from Portugal; and oils, paints, chemicals, hardware, and stationery from Germany and the United Kingdom. Local production varies by district: Ponta Delgada produces tea, straw hats, spirits, cotton fabrics, and pottery; Angra produces soap, bricks, tiles, butter, cheese, woollen and linen goods; and Horta produces ornamental articles made from osier, straw, and the pith of dried fig-wood.

In Russia, the town of Korocha exports dried cherries and sends salt, grain, and cattle purchased in the south to Moscow. In the government of Saratov, Kuznetsk trades in wooden goods, salt, grain, and sheepskins exported to south-east Russia. In West Siberia, Kuznetsk is a coal-mining center with trade in tallow, honey, wax, nuts, cedarwood, furs, cattle, and grain.

In Austria, Korneuburg serves as an emporium for the corn and salt trade and manufactures pasteboard and coarse textiles.

In the United States, the city of Atchison, Kansas, is a supply center with large trade in live-stock and grains. Its principal manufactures include drugs, hardware, lumber, furniture, and flour. The value of its factory products rose from $2,093,469 in 1900 to $4,052,274 in 1905.
