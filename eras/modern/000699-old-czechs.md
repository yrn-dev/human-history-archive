# Old Czechs

The Old Czechs were a political party within the Bohemian representatives of the Austrian empire. They were characterized by their leadership's commitment to specific diplomatic strategies and alliances, which eventually led to a political struggle with a more impatient faction known as the Young Czechs.

## Political Strategy and Objectives
The primary objective of the Czechs was the recognition of the Bohemian kingdom. This goal was based on the principle of the indissoluble unity of the Bohemian kingdom, where both the Czech and German languages were to be recognized as equal and parallel. Because of this commitment to unity, the Old Czechs and their allies refused to consider proposals to divide the country into separate German and Czech districts—a measure demanded by Germans to protect themselves against language ordinances.

Under the leadership of Rieger, the Old Czechs maintained a policy of passive opposition. They also sought to maintain an alliance with the clergy and the Feudalists (the aristocracy). While the leaders of the party grew older and may have been satisfied with the influence they had already attained, they faced increasing pressure from the Young Czechs.

## Conflict with the Young Czechs
The Young Czechs, also known as Radicals, emerged as a new party that violently attacked Rieger's leadership. The Young Czechs disagreed with the Old Czechs on several key points:
* **Alliances:** They disliked the alliance with the clergy and the aristocracy.
* **Governance:** They desired universal suffrage and wished to take their seats in the diet to join Germans in pursuing political reform.
* **Tradition:** They recalled Hussite traditions.

This internal division weakened the Czechs. For a period of four years, the Old and Young Czechs were as occupied with opposing one another as they were with opposing the Germans.

## The 1890 Reconciliation Attempt
In January 1890, Taaffe chaired conferences in Vienna intended to reconcile opposing parties. These meetings included representatives from the German party, the Feudal party, and the Czechs. An agreement was reached based on a revision of the electoral law and a separation between Czech and German districts.

However, the Young Czechs had not been invited to these conferences and did not feel bound by the resulting decisions. They opposed the measures, arguing that they threatened the unity of the Bohemian country. This opposition led to a gradual migration of some Old Czechs toward the Young Czech party. By the elections of 1891, a great struggle occurred between the two factions, resulting in a complete victory for the Young Czechs.

## Parliamentary Representation
The Old Czechs represented a small fraction of the Czech parliamentary presence compared to other factions. In one recorded distribution of representatives, the Old Czechs held 7 seats, while the Young Czechs held 18, Czech Agrarians held 28, and Czech Clericals held 17.
