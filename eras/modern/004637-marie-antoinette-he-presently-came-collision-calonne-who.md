# Marie Antoinette

Marie Antoinette was the Queen of France and the sister of the Austrian emperor. The daughter of Empress Maria Theresa, she married Louis XVI in 1770. Her life and influence during the reign of her husband became subjects of significant historical study and controversy, particularly regarding her role in the events leading to the French Revolution.

## Early Married Life and Family
Following the death of Louis XV on May 10, 1774, Marie Antoinette's husband ascended the throne. For a period, the queen's conduct was a point of concern for her brother, Emperor Joseph II, who visited France and provided her with a memorandum explicitly detailing the dangers of her behavior. These remonstrances had a temporary effect; after the birth of her daughter, Marie Thérèse Charlotte, in December 1778, the queen adopted a more quiet lifestyle.

The death of Maria Theresa on November 29, 1780, removed a devoted and wise friend from the queen's life and increased the dislike of the Austrian alliance due to the unrestrained rashness of Joseph II. Marie Antoinette's position was further strengthened by the birth of a dauphin, Louis Joseph Xavier François, on October 22, 1781. While she was described as a wise and devoted mother to her children, she generally displayed a repugnance toward serious public matters, preferring to spend her time with friends and amusements at Trianon.

## Political Influence and the Ministry of Calonne
Marie Antoinette's involvement in public affairs was often driven by personal motives, specifically the desire to secure favors or positions for her favorites. The Polignac family exerted significant influence over the queen, obtaining a dukedom, large sums of money, and various nominations. It was Madame de Polignac who secured the appointment of Calonne as controller-general of the finances.

Following the dismissal of the minister Necker in 1781, Marie Antoinette obtained an ascendancy over Louis XVI. This influence was partly responsible for the extravagance of Calonne's ministry. Calonne was viewed by the queen as her ideal minister of finance; between 1783 and 1787, he raised funds through repeated loans. However, in 1787, Calonne collapsed and declared that the monarchy was bankrupt, necessitating a gathering of the kingdom's Notables.

During this period, the queen used her influence to encourage aristocratic extravagance and thwart the economy attempts of the king's ministers. She sought to restore the nobility and the church to the positions they held during the era of Louis XIV, including extending the church's power over private life and removing non-aristocratic officers from the army.

## Relations with Court Officials
The queen maintained a close friendship with Louis Charles Auguste Le Tonnelier, Baron de Breteuil. Breteuil, a diplomat who had served in St. Petersburg, Stockholm, Vienna, and Naples, became the minister of the king's household and introduced reforms to prison administration. Because of his close ties to Marie Antoinette, Breteuil came into collision with Calonne, who demanded Breteuil's dismissal in 1787. Despite this, Breteuil's influence with the queen remained unshaken. He eventually succeeded Necker as chief minister on July 11, 1789, though his ministry ended three days later with the fall of the Bastille.

## Trial and Execution
The French Revolution led to the capture of the royal family at Versailles by a Parisian mob on October 6, 1789, and their subsequent transfer to the Tuileries. Following the failed flight to Varennes—an effort in which Breteuil had a share—the royal family's situation deteriorated.

Marie Antoinette's trial began on October 14, 1793, with her defense handled by Tronson-Ducourdray and Chauveau-Lagarde. She faced accusations brought by Fouquier-Tinville. The jury was asked to determine if she had cooperated in maneuvers and communications with foreign powers to assist enemies of the republic, and if she had participated in a conspiracy to kindle civil war by arming citizens against one another.

The jury reached a unanimous affirmative decision. On October 16, 1793, Marie Antoinette was led to the guillotine. Before her execution, she left a "Testament," a touching letter addressed to Madame Elizabeth.
