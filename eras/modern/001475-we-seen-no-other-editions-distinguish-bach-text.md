# Editions and Scholarship of J. S. Bach

The publication and editing of the works of Johann Sebastian Bach have been characterized by varying degrees of scholarly rigor, with a historical tension between the preservation of Bach's original text and the tastes or interpretations of various editors.

## The Bach-Gesellschaft and the Neue Bachgesellschaft
The *Bach-Gesellschaft* produced the only complete modern edition of Bach's works, though its quality is described as unequal. The society's first editors are noted for an ignorance of Bach's musical language; their admiration for his larger choruses led them to dismiss or retain details regarding figured bass, ornamentation, and variants between parts and scores without considering how these affected style and tempo. This was particularly evident in the recitatives and arias, which the editors viewed as archaic.

A significant shift occurred in the 9th *Jahrgang* with the introduction of scholarly methods by Wilhelm Rust. Rust, described as the "Porson of Bach-scholarship," served as editor for fourteen years, displaying an unrivaled insight into Bach's style and restoring valuable harmonies from incomplete texts. His prefaces to the *Bach-Gesellschaft* volumes are considered among the most valuable contributions to 18th-century music criticism.

Following Rust's death, the standard of the *Bach-Gesellschaft* deteriorated. One successor focused on reprinting early versions of works rather than digesting Rust's materials, and in *Jahrgang* xxx., a volume contained a line where the violin and bass were a bar apart. However, the final ten volumes returned to a satisfactory standard. In *Jahrgang* xliv., the English and French suites were re-edited, and the B minor mass was partially reworked. Additionally, the text of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* was supplemented using an autograph from the British Museum.

The *Neue Bachgesellschaft* was established with the goal of making the results of the first *Bach-Gesellschaft* accessible through cheap, practical editions and frequent festivals. However, its publications are characterized as disappointing, failing to distinguish Bach's original text from the taste of the editor.

## Other Notable Editions and Scores
Several other editions are recognized for their specific merits:
*   **The Steingraeber Edition:** Edited by Dr. Hans Bischoff, this edition of clavier works is regarded as incomparably the best. It provides a straightforward single text while using footnotes to list all variants and clearly distinguishing the editor's phrasing signs and nuances from Bach's own rare indications.
*   **Edition Peters:** The older vocal scores of cantatas in this edition are considered far better than the complete series issued by Breitkopf and Haertel (which followed the *Bach-Gesellschaft*), despite the *Edition Peters* being only a selection. Kroll also made the text of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* accessible in *Edition Peters*, replacing a widely accepted but corrupt tradition based on Czerny's edition.
*   **Novello:** The English vocal scores from Novello are generally considered very good, though they cover limited ground. The Novello score of the Christmas oratorio is noted for containing an analytic preface by Sir George Macfarren.

## Historical Context of Publication
During his own lifetime, Bach engraved most of the instrumental collections he published. Regarding his church cantatas, only one—*Gott ist mein Koenig*, written when he was nineteen—was published during his life.

The broader rediscovery of Bach's genius is closely linked to Mendelssohn, as well as the efforts of Schumann and others. In England, Wesley and Crotch provided earlier performances and publications. In 1850, a century after Bach's death, a society was formed to ensure the correct publication of his remaining works. Early efforts by Robert Franz to arrange Bach's works for modern performance were eventually superseded by a "purer scholarship" that emphasized the beauty of strict adherence to Bach's own scoring.
