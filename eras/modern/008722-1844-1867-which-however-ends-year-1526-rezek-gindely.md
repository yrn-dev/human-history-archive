# Historical Events and Figures (1844-1867)

The period between 1844 and 1867 was marked by significant developments in governance, academic scholarship, scientific research, and literature across several nations.

## Political Transitions and Governance

In the United States, the state of Kentucky saw a succession of governors during this era. William Owsley served from 1844 to 1848, followed by John J. Crittenden from 1848 to 1850. Governor Crittenden resigned on July 31 to become the Attorney-General of the United States, and John L. Helm served the remainder of that term. Subsequent governors included Lazarus W. Powell (1851-1855), Charles S. Morehead (1855-1859), Beriah Magoffin (1859-1862), James F. Robinson (1862-1863), and Thomas E. Bramlette (1863-1867). John L. Helm returned to office in 1867, followed by John W. Stevenson later that same year.

In Japan, the year 1867 marked a pivotal shift in power. On October 14, 1867, Keiki handed in his resignation to the Emperor following the Tosa memorial. The Emperor's response on December 15 signaled the end of the shogunate. The throne was held by Emperor Mutsuhito, who had ascended on February 13, 1867, following the death of his father, Komei. Mutsuhito had been born on November 3, 1852, and was fifteen years old at the time of his accession.

## Academic and Scientific Contributions

Scholarship in the mid-19th century produced extensive works on history and classical studies. Frantisek Palacky published *Geschichte von Bohmen* between 1844 and 1867, though the work concludes with the year 1526. Other historians, including Gindely and Rezek, addressed Bohemian history after 1526. In France, Professor Ernest Denis provided a continuous account of Bohemian history from the start of the 15th century through works published between 1878 and 1903.

In Germany, Hermann Bonitz (1814-1888) contributed significantly to the study of Plato and Aristotle. His works from this period include *Observationes Criticae in Aristotelis quae feruntur Magna Moralia et Ethica Eudemia* (1844) and *Aristotelische Studien* (1862-1867). Bonitz held various academic positions, becoming a professor at the university of Vienna in 1849 and director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium in 1867.

In the field of palaeontology, William Hellier Baily (1819-1888) served as Assistant Curator in the Bristol Museum from 1837 to 1844 before joining the Geological Survey in London. Later, in 1857, he became the acting palaeontologist for the Irish branch of the Geological Survey. Between 1867 and 1875, he published *Figures of Characteristic British Fossils, with Descriptive Remarks*, featuring stone drawings he created himself.

## Literature and Writing

The mid-19th century saw a variety of literary outputs. Beecher published several works, including *Seven Lectures to Young Men* (1844), *Plymouth Collection of Hymns and Tunes* (1855), *Star Papers, Experiences of Art and Nature* (1855), *Life Thoughts* (1858), and *Prayers from Plymouth Pulpit* (1867).

Thomas D'Arcy McGee produced numerous historical and political texts, such as *Historical Sketches of O'Connell and his Friends* (1844), *Memoirs of the Life and Conquests of Art McMurrogh, King of Leinster* (1847), and *A History of the Irish Settlers in North America* (1851). A biography of McGee by Fennings Taylor was published in 1867.

In France, the satirical poet Auguste Marseille Barthelemy (1796-1867) was known for his collaborations with J. P. A. Mery. While much of their most famous political satire occurred earlier in the century, Barthelemy's life concluded in 1867.

## Economic Observations

In the United Kingdom, banking and currency circulation underwent changes starting in 1844. In that year, there were 976 banking offices in England and Wales. By 1906, this number had grown to more than 5,880. This expansion contributed to an apparent increase in the circulation of Bank of England notes of more than £6,000,000 since 1844, as more offices required till-money.
