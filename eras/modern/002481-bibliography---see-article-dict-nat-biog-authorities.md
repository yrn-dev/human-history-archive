# BIBLIOGRAPHY

In the context of the Human History Archive's records, bibliographies serve as the primary means of identifying the authorities, manuscripts, and published works used to document the lives of historical figures and the development of specific titles or estates. These records frequently rely on a combination of national biographical dictionaries, state papers, and personal memoirs.

## Primary Biographical Authorities
A recurring central authority across various historical records is the *Dict. of Nat. Biog.* (Dictionary of National Biography). This work is cited as a foundational source for a wide array of figures, including George Grote—whose entry was written by G. Croom Robertson—and Martin Butzer, whose entry was authored by A.W. Ward. Other significant biographical references include *Biographia Britannica* (Kippis), which provides data on the 1st Earl of Cork and other figures, and *Athenae Oxonienses* (Bliss), used in the study of individuals such as Sir G. Calvert.

## State Papers and Official Records
Official government documentation is essential for verifying titles, legal proceedings, and political activities. The *Cal. of State Papers* (Calendars of State Papers) is utilized extensively, specifically the *Register of Privy Council of Scotland* (volumes i-iii), the *Scottish Series* (Thorpe i and Bain ii-iv), and the *Foreign* series. These records are used to track events such as the attaintment of a title in 1716 due to the 5th baron's involvement in the Jacobite rising of 1715.

Other critical official sources include:
*   *State Trials* and the *Lords Journals*.
*   *Calendars of State Papers, Domestic and Irish*.
*   *Letters and Papers of Henry VIII* (specifically records from 1537 and 1542).
*   *Clarendon State Papers*.

## Personal Papers and Memoirs
Private archives provide intimate details regarding the lives of the nobility and scholars. For the 1st Earl of Cork, the *Lismore Papers* (edited by A. B. Grosart, 1886-1887) are paramount, containing a diary from 1611 until his death, autobiographical notes, and correspondence. Similarly, the *Stanley Papers* and the *Hamilton Papers* serve as primary sources for their respective families.

Other notable personal records include:
*   The *Memoirs* of Sir J. Melville (Bannatyne Club, 1827).
*   The *Diary* of Pepys.
*   The *True Remembrances* written by the 1st Earl of Cork and printed by Birch.
*   The *Memoirs* by De Lloyd (1668).

## Specialized Academic and Literary Sources
Depending on the subject, bibliographies incorporate specialized academic journals and linguistic studies. For literary figures like John Lydgate, sources include the *Early English Text Society* (specifically *The Temple of Glass*), Warton's *History of English Poetry*, and Ritson's *Bibliotheca Anglo-Poética*. 

For theological or reformist figures, records point to the *Corpus reformatorum*, *Herzog-Hauck's Realencyklopädie*, and works such as *Capito and Butzer* (J.W. Baum, 1860). In the case of George Grote, the bibliography includes his *History of Greece* (which reached a final 10-volume edition in 1888) and his *Plato*, edited by Alexander Bain.

## Family Lineages and Estate Transitions
Bibliographical records are also used to track the transmission of titles. For example, the records of the Earls of Derby document how titles and estates passed from James, the 10th earl (who died in February 1736), to Sir Edward Stanley (1689-1776), a descendant of the 1st earl. Similarly, the restoration of a title in 1869 to Alexander Hugh Bruce (born 1849) as the 6th baron is noted, documenting his subsequent role as secretary for Scotland from 1895 to 1903.
