# Servia

## Geography and Demographics
As of 1909, Servia was a political division of the Balkan Peninsula. It covered an area of 18,782 square miles and had a population of 2,493,770. The population density for the region was recorded at 132.2 persons per square mile.

## History and Political Evolution
In the middle ages, the Servian Empire included the vilayet, with northern districts that continue to be referred to by Serbs as Old Servia (*Stara Srbiya*). A significant event in the history and legend of the Balkans occurred in 1389 at the plain of Kossovo (Kossovopolje, meaning "Field of Blackbirds"). This long valley, located west of Prishtina and watered by the Sibnitza (a tributary of the Servian Ibar), was the site of the battle of Kossovo, an engagement in which the power of Servia was destroyed by the Turks.

Later, during the initial years of Servia's autonomy under Prince Milosh, the town of Kraguyevats served as the seat of government and the residence of the prince from 1818 to 1839.

## Kraguyevats
Kraguyevats (also spelled Kragujevac or Kraguievatz) is the capital of the Kraguyevats department of Servia. It is situated on the Lepenitsa—a small stream that flows north-east to join the Morava—in a valley of the Shumadia, known as "forest-land," approximately 59 miles south-southwest of Belgrade. 

The town serves as the main arsenal of Servia and contains a steam flour-mill and an iron-foundry. Its administrative and civic infrastructure includes a tribunal, the seat of the district prefecture, a large garrison, and a fine library. Architecturally, the town is noted for having the finest modern cathedral in Servia, built in the Byzantine style, as well as the finest college building. Across the river from the town lies the hamlet of Obilichevo, which houses a large powder factory.

Politically, the national assembly (*Narodna Skupshtina*) frequently met in Kraguyevats between 1868 and 1880. The town's prosperity increased following 1885, when a branch line (Kraguyevats-Lapovo) connected it to the principal railway running between Belgrade and Nish. As of 1900, the population of Kraguyevats was 14,160.
