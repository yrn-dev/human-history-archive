# Sigmund Schnitzer and the Early Fagotte

## Overview of Sigmund Schnitzer
Sigmund Schnitzer was a craftsman based in Nuremberg who gained significant recognition for his production of the *fagotte*. According to an 18th-century historical work, Schnitzer was renowned "almost everywhere" for creating *fagotte* characterized by extraordinary size, pure intonation, and skilful workmanship, with instruments that spoke easily. His work was highly appreciated across Italy, France, and Germany, resulting in a continuous demand from lovers of music.

## Historical Records and Professional Status
The records regarding Schnitzer's specific contributions vary by source. Johann Neudorfer, a contemporary chronicler of Nuremberg's craftsmen writing in 1549, identified Schnitzer simply as a *Stadtpfeifer* and *Pfeifenmacher*. 

Some historical retrospection has questioned whether Schnitzer was the inventor of the bassoon's specific form—specifically the process of doubling the tube back upon itself to reduce the great length of the bass pommer. However, evidence suggests that if Schnitzer had been the inventor of this new form, Neudorfer would not have failed to note it. Furthermore, the eulogistic descriptions of Schnitzer as a maker of *fagottos* of extraordinary size were written by Doppelmaier nearly two centuries after the supposed invention of the instrument.

## The Bassoon and its Development
The bassoon (known in German as *Fagott* and in Italian as *fagotto*) is a member of the oboe family and serves as its bass. It is a woodwind instrument featuring a double reed mouthpiece. The German and Italian names derive from a perceived resemblance to a bundle of sticks. 

The bassoon was the first instrument of its kind to be doubled back upon itself; its direct ancestor was the bass pommer, which was a straight instrument measuring 6 feet in length. In more recent developments, the wind-instrument-maker W. Heckel of Biebrich-am-Rhein, acting at the instigation of Wagner, produced bassoons with an additional key to extend the compass downwards to A.

## Use in Classical Music
The bassoon has been favored by many great masters, though it was an exception for Handel. Notable uses of the instrument include:
* **Beethoven**: Utilized the bassoon extensively in his symphonies, writing original and beautiful independent parts.
* **Bach**: Included parts for two bassoons in his mass in B min.
* **Mozart**: Composed a concerto for bassoon with orchestra in Bb (Kochel, No. 191).
* **Weber**: Wrote a concerto for bassoon in F (op. 75) scored for full orchestra.
