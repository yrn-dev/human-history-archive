# Reichsrath

The Reichsrath served as a central parliamentary body within the Austrian empire, playing a pivotal role in the ratification of constitutional settlements and the administration of the state. Its history was characterized by intense ethnic tensions, legislative struggles over the balance of power between central and regional authorities, and frequent disruptions caused by nationalist opposition.

## Establishment and Constitutional Role
The Reichsrath was central to the implementation of the *Ausgleich* (compact with Hungary). In 1867, the government sought a parliament capable of ratifying this settlement, which required a German majority because the system of dualism opposed the ambitions of Federalists and Slavs. After initial conflicts regarding whether the February constitution remained valid, a new edict ordered the diets to elect a Reichsrath according to the constitution.

Once the *Ausgleich* was ratified almost unanimously—with all races represented except the Czechs—the Reichsrath proceeded to special legislation for territories officially designated as "the kingdoms and lands represented in the Reichsrath." A series of fundamental laws established a parliamentary government featuring the responsibility of ministers and complete control over the budget, alongside guarantees for personal liberties and rights.

## Composition and Political Influence
The composition of the Reichsrath often reflected the volatile ethnic and political landscape of the empire. German Liberals sought to introduce direct elections, but were prevented from doing so by the influence of the Poles. The Poles, along with the Slovenes, Dalmatians, and Tirolese, held sufficient leverage to secure concessions for Federalists by threatening to leave the House.

In 1873, a new electoral law transferred the right to elect members from the diets to the direct vote of the people, increasing the total membership to 353. This measure was intended to weaken the Federalists, as it prevented territories from formally refusing to appoint deputies; if a member failed to take their seat, only a single constituency remained unrepresented.

The political makeup of the body shifted over time. For example, by 1897, the influence of Lueger grew, and the Socialists largely replaced the Liberals in Lower Austria. The transference of quarrels between German Nationalists and Christian Socialists from the Viennese Council Chamber to the Reichsrath was noted as being detrimental to the orderly conduct of debate.

## Legislative Functions and Limitations
The functions of the Reichsrath were slightly less extensive than they had previously been due to the concessions made to Federalists. One specific area of weakness was the selection of the Delegation. Unlike in Hungary, where the Delegation was elected by each House as a whole, the Austrian Delegation was chosen by representatives of separate territories (e.g., 10 from Bohemia, 7 from Galicia, and 1 from Trieste). This meant the Delegation did not necessarily represent the majority of the chamber of deputies and could include groups opposing the government. In 1869, this resulted in the Poles and Tirolese voting in favor of a Hungarian budget proposal that Austria had rejected.

## Conflict and Obstruction
The Reichsrath was frequently the site of political deadlock. In the late 19th century, Germans prevented business from being conducted due to grievances over Paragraph 14 of the Constitutional law of 1867. This paragraph allowed the emperor to proclaim orders in cases of "pressing necessity" if the Reichsrath was not in session, provided they were signed by the ministry and later approved by both Houses. The Germans demanded its repeal, leading to the retirement of Thun in September 1899 and the subsequent resignation of Count Clary.

Czech representatives also frequently used obstruction. In 1900, the Czechs demanded the restoration of their language in the internal service of Bohemia and used noise and disturbance to suspend the president of the assembly. Earlier, in 1868, the Czechs and Poles had threatened to leave the Reichsrath if their demands for increased regional powers were not met. In March 1870, the Polish club, followed by the Slovenes and Tirolese, did leave the House, leaving only 110 members (Germans and German representatives from Bohemia and Moravia), rendering the parliament impossible to govern.

## Historical Precedents and Dissolutions
The Reichsrath experienced various forms of dissolution and relocation. In 1849, an assembly transferred to Kremsier discussed "fundamental rights" and the reconciliation of theoretical unity with dualism before being dissolved following the battle of Kapolna. In later years, the Reichsrath and all diets were dissolved during a crisis involving the war between Germany and France.

In October 1848, during a period of conflict in Vienna, the members of the Right and the Slav majority left the capital to announce a meeting of the diet at Bruenn, leaving only a "rump of German radicals" in Vienna.
