# Dukes of Bolton and Dorset

## The Dukes of Bolton
The title of duke of Bolton was held by the family of Paulet or Powlett between 1689 and 1794. The first duke, Charles Powlett (c. 1625–1699), had served as a member of parliament for Winchester and Hampshire from 1660 to 1675. He became the 6th marquess of Winchester in 1675 upon the death of his father. After supporting the claim of Mary and William to the English throne in 1688, he was appointed as the lord-lieutenant of Hampshire and restored to the privy council, receiving the dukedom in April 1689. Described as an eccentric, he was known for his hostility toward Marlborough and Halifax; it is said that in 1687 he traveled with 100 horsemen and four coaches, entertaining at night and sleeping during the day.

He was succeeded in February 1699 by his elder son, Charles, the 2nd duke of Bolton (1661–1722). Like his father, he was a supporter of William of Orange and a member of parliament for Hampshire. His career included serving as a lord justice of the kingdom, lord chamberlain of the royal household, and governor of the Isle of Wight. He also served as lord-lieutenant of Dorset and Hampshire, and for two brief periods as lord-lieutenant of Ireland. He was additionally a commissioner for the union of Scotland and England. His third wife was Henrietta, a natural daughter of James, duke of Monmouth, who died in 1730. Swift referred to this duke as "a great booby."

The 3rd duke, Charles (1685–1754), was the eldest son of the 2nd duke. He served in parliament from 1705 to 1717 and was created Baron Pawlet of Basing in 1717. He attained high rank in the British army and held many of the public offices his father had occupied. In 1733, he was stripped of several offices after displeasing Sir Robert Walpole, though some were later restored. In 1745, he raised a regiment to serve against the Jacobites. He was known as a famous gallant and married Lavinia Fenton, a singer and former mistress, as his second wife.

Following his death in August 1754, he was succeeded by his brother, Harry (c. 1690–1759), the 4th duke. Harry had been a member of parliament for forty years and served as the lord-lieutenant of Hampshire.

## The Dukes of Dorset
Lionel Cranfield Sackville, the 1st duke of Dorset (1688–1765), was the only son of the 6th earl. Born on January 18, 1688, he became the 7th earl of Dorset in January 1706 and was created duke in 1720. He held several high offices, including lord president of the council from 1745 to 1751 and lord steward of the royal household from 1725 to 1730 and again from 1737 to 1745. He served as lord-lieutenant of Ireland twice: first from 1730 to 1737, and again starting in 1750, though he was dismissed in 1755 following a stormy viceroyalty. He also served as one of the lords justices of Great Britain. He died on October 10, 1765, leaving three sons: George, John Philip (who died in 1765), and Charles.

Charles Sackville, the 2nd duke of Dorset (1711–1769), was an associate of Frederick, prince of Wales. He served as a lord of the treasury under Henry Pelham and was a member of parliament for many years. Upon his death on January 5, 1769, he was succeeded by his nephew, John Frederick (1745–1799), the 3rd duke. John Frederick served as lord steward of the household from 1789 until his death on July 19, 1799, and was the ambassador in Paris from 1783 to 1789.

He was succeeded by his only son, George John Frederick (1793–1815), the 4th duke. Because the 4th duke died unmarried in February 1815, the titles passed to a kinsman, Charles Sackville Germain (1767–1843). The son and heir of the 1st Viscount Sackville, Charles Sackville Germain became the 5th duke of Dorset. The titles became extinct upon his death on July 29, 1843.
