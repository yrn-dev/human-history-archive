# The Bonaparte Family

The Bonaparte family rose to global prominence through Napoleon I, the emperor of the French. The family's original name was Buonaparte, a name held by several distinct families in Italy during the early middle ages. One such family settled in Florence before the year 1100 and later split into two branches, Sarzana and San Miniato, during the 13th century. In the middle of the 16th century, Francesco Buonaparte, a member of the Sarzana branch, emigrated to Corsica. His descendants in Corsica were primarily involved in the magistracy and the law.

## Carlo and Letizia Buonaparte

Carlo Buonaparte (Charles Marie de Bonaparte, 1746–1785) was the father of Napoleon I. He studied law at the university of Pisa. Following the French conquest of Corsica, he served as an assessor to the royal court of Ajaccio and neighboring districts. Prior to this, he had served as the secretary to Paoli, supporting him at the *consulta* during the Corsican resistance against the French. Carlo is described as a Voltairian by conviction and a schemer by nature; his restless disposition led him to intrigue for various posts and engage in risky business ventures that compromised the family's fortune for many years. He died of stomach cancer in Montpellier in 1785.

In 1764, Carlo married Letizia Ramolino, a high-spirited and beautiful fourteen-year-old from a well-connected family that had been domiciled in Corsica since the middle of the 15th century. Letizia is characterized as devout, simple, and frugal in her tastes, possessing a courageous and firm disposition. While Carlo looked toward careers in France, Letizia helped bind her children to their Corsican life.

The couple had two children in 1765 and 1767 who died in infancy. Their first surviving son, Joseph, was born in 1768. Napoleon was born in 1769 during the turmoil of the French conquest, a period during which Letizia had accompanied her husband on several escapes and journeys.

## Siblings of Napoleon I

The brothers and sisters of Napoleon I, listed by age, include Joseph Bonaparte and Marianne Elisa.

Marianne Elisa (1777–1820) was born in Ajaccio. Through the efforts of her brothers, she attended the establishment of St Cyr near Paris as a "king's scholar." After revolutionists disrupted the institution in 1792, Napoleon took charge of her and returned her to Ajaccio. In 1797, she married a well-connected Corsican named Felix Bacciochi. 

Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted Elisa the principality of Piombino and later Lucca. In 1808, she obtained the grand duchy of Tuscany. Because her husband was considered "almost a nullity," Elisa's ability and pride significantly influenced Italian affairs and administration. Her relationship with Napoleon was often strained, and she abetted Murat in his enterprises between 1813 and 1814. After the fall of her brother, she lived as the countess of Compignano in Bologna and later Santo Andrea near Trieste, where she died in 1820.

## The Bonapartes of Baltimore

A branch of the family settled in America, descended from Jerome Bonaparte and Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a Baltimore merchant. They married in Baltimore on December 24, 1803. Napoleon disliked the union and refused to recognize its legality, though it was valid under American law and was not declared void by Pope Pius VII. Jerome was eventually forced by Napoleon to separate from his wife. Elizabeth returned to Baltimore and died in 1879.

The only child of this marriage was Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870), born in England but primarily a resident of Baltimore. He was noted for his resemblance to the emperor. His own elder son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), served in the French army during campaigns in Italy and the Crimea.
