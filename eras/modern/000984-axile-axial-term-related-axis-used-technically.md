# AXILE (AXIAL)

The terms axile or axial are technical descriptors used across various scientific and artistic disciplines to indicate a relationship to an axis. An axis is defined as an imaginary line about which a system of bodies or a body rotates, or a line around which an action or body is symmetrically disposed. In geometry and geometrical crystallography, it serves as a line to aid the orientation of a figure.

## Botanical Applications
In botany, the term axis refers to the stem. The terms axile or axial are specifically used to describe the orientation of an embryo in relation to the seed. An embryo is classified as axile or axial when it follows the same direction as the axis of the seed. Conversely, if the embryo does not follow the direction of the axis, it is termed abaxial or abaxile.

The embryo itself consists of an axis that bears the cotyledons, which are the first leaves of the plant. This structure is further divided into several parts:
*   **Plumule (or gemmule):** The terminal growing bud of the axis, representing the ascending axis.
*   **Hypocotyl (also called the caulicle or tigellum):** The part of the axis located immediately beneath the cotyledons.
*   **Radicle:** The descending axis, which is the young root. The point where the radicle and hypocotyl unite is known as the neck or collar.

The orientation of these extremities is generally determined by the position of the chalaza (the base of the ovule) and the micropyle; the cotyledonary extremity points toward the chalaza, while the radicular extremity points toward the micropyle.

## Mathematical and Physical Systems
In the study of coordinate systems, an axis is a line passing through an origin that is perpendicular to a fixed fundamental plane. The axis and the plane are often viewed as a single concept, as one determines the other. Different origins result in different axial definitions:
*   **Vertical Line:** When a point on the earth's surface is the origin, the fundamental axis is the direction of gravity, which defines the vertical line and a horizontal plane of the horizon.
*   **Equatorial System:** When the center of the earth is the origin, the fundamental axis is the earth's rotation, which intersects the surface at the North and South Poles.
*   **Heliocentric System:** In a system where the origin is the center of the sun, the axis of the ecliptic is the line perpendicular to the plane in which the earth moves around the sun.

In engineering and physics, the term is used to describe the "neutral axis" of a bent beam. The deviation of this neutral axis from a reference axis (OX) can be used to calculate the deflection produced by temperature changes or stress on chains.

## Mechanical and Artistic Usage
In mechanical engineering, specifically regarding breech-blocks, the "axial line" refers to the central line of the block. For example, in certain feed-box designs, the platform for feed-strips lies below the axial line of the breech-block. This ensures that the breech-block can clear the clips holding cartridges, allowing the cartridge to be extracted in the line of the axis of the block.

In the context of art and mosaics, "axial symmetry" refers to a method of treating figures. This technique often involves drawing figures in full front to keep features together on the general map of the face, arranging masses with strict regard for general symmetry.

## Related Terminology
The word axis is derived from the Latin for "axle." An axle is a pin or spindle on which a wheel turns. In the context of carriages, the axle-tree is the bar supporting the wheels, while the axles are the thinner rounded prolongations of that bar.
