# Mombasa

Mombasa is a primary port and town located on the east coast of the East Africa protectorate. Situated at 4° 4' S., it is an island (also referred to as Mvita) separated from the mainland by a narrow stretch of water. The town has a population of approximately 30,000.

## Administration and Geography
Mombasa serves as the capital of the Seyyidie province, which is the south coast province of the protectorate. The surrounding geography is characterized by the "Temborari," a coast plain of insignificant width, generally varying between 2 and 10 miles. Within the Mombasa district, the interior rises to a wide level plain known as the "Nyika" (uplands), which is largely composed of quartz and includes large waterless areas such as the Taru desert.

## Port and Infrastructure
The chief harbour of the protectorate is located on the south-west side of Mombasa island and is known as Kilindini. This harbour serves as the terminus for the Uganda railway. The town is a hub for international maritime travel, visited by several steamship companies, including the German East Africa line, which operates a fortnightly service from Hamburg, as well as a regular service to and from India.

Communication is facilitated by a cable connecting Mombasa with Zanzibar, providing the protectorate with direct telegraphic communication to the rest of the world. Additionally, an inland telegraph system links the chief towns of the protectorate with one another and with Uganda.

## Local Settlements and Society
Opposite Mombasa town on the mainland is Freretown (known by natives as Kisaoni), a settlement for freed slaves named after Sir Bartle Frere. Freretown serves as the headquarters of the Church Missionary Society in East Africa and is the residence of the bishop of the diocese of Mombasa, featuring a mission house and a fine church.

The region is home to colonies of Swahili-speaking people. Swahili is an archaic Bantu dialect indigenous to the East African coast south of the Ruvu (Pangani) river; through intermixture with Arabic, it has become the *lingua franca* of eastern Africa between the Zambezi and the White Nile. Education in Mombasa includes the presence of Arab schools.

## History and Political Transition
From the 8th to the 11th century, Persians and Arabs established settlements along the coast, gaining political supremacy and forming the Zenj empire. Until the arrival of British rule, the history of the coast towns was closely identified with that of Zanzibar.

A significant political transition occurred on July 1, 1895, when the territory administered by a company (previously known as "Ibea") was formally transferred to the British crown at Mombasa, becoming the East Africa protectorate. This transfer coincided with a ten-month revolt involving the Mazrui, the most important Arab family on the coast, regarding a dispute over a chieftainship. The rebellion ended in April 1896 when rebel leaders fled to German territory. This event marked a shift in influence, as the suppression of the revolt substituted European influence for Arab influence.

## Regional Context
Mombasa is the southern anchor of several notable coastal towns. To its north-east lies Lamu, an ancient settlement and headquarters of the coast Arabs, located 150 miles away. Further north-east, 320 miles from Mombasa, is Kismayu, the northernmost port of the protectorate and the last sheltered anchorage on the east coast, serving as an invaluable harbour of refuge. Other notable coastal towns include Patta, Kipini, and Malindi.
