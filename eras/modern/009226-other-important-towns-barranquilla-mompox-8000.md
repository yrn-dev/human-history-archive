# Barranquilla and Mompox

## Overview of the Region
Barranquilla and Mompox are important towns located on the Magdalena river within Colombia. They are situated in a region that, until 1908, was the department of Bolivar. This area is bounded by the Caribbean Sea to the north and west, the departments of Santander and Magdalena to the east, Antioquia to the south, and Cauca to the southwest. The region covers an area of 27,028 square miles and consists largely of densely wooded, low alluvial plains. These plains are slightly cultivated and are considered unsuited for north European labour.

The population of the Bolivar department was estimated at 323,097 in 1899. This population is composed largely of mixed races, who in some localities are estimated to constitute four-fifths of the inhabitants. While Cartagena on the Caribbean coast once served as the principal commercial entrepot of Colombia, Barranquilla has emerged as a primary commercial center.

## Barranquilla: Commercial and Port Infrastructure
Barranquilla is a city and port located on the left bank of the Magdalena river, approximately 7 miles above its mouth. It serves as the capital of a province of the same name within the department of Atlantico. As of 1902, its estimated population was 31,000.

The city's importance as the principal commercial centre of the republic is due to a dangerous bar at the mouth of the Magdalena river. Because of this obstacle, a great portion of the trade from the extensive territory tributary to the river—which accounts for about 60% of the entire country's trade—must pass through Barranquilla and its seaport. 

The city's seaport facilities have evolved over time:
* **Savanilla:** This location served as the seaport until approximately 1890, when drifting sands created shoals that necessitated a move.
* **Puerto Colombia:** Located a short distance westward from Savanilla, this site became the new seaport. It features a steel pier 4,000 feet in length to facilitate the handling of freight. Puerto Colombia is 18-1/2 miles from Barranquilla by rail.

## Urban Development and Local Life
Founded originally in 1629, Barranquilla did not attract attention as a commercial center until the middle of the 19th century, when efforts were made to secure trade that previously passed through Cartagena. The city is regularly laid out on a low plain. Its architecture is varied, featuring many residences, public buildings, and fine warehouses; however, a greater part of the city consists of thatched rush roofs and mud-walled cabins supported by bamboo (*guadua*) frameworks.

The city is equipped with electric light, telephone, and tram services, and its water supply is drawn from the Magdalena river. The surrounding country is subject to periodical inundations, which results in very little cultivation. Consequently, the population—which is of the mixed type common to the Colombian lowlands—is largely engaged in no settled productive occupation.

## Navigation of the Magdalena River
The Magdalena river is navigated using light-draught steamboats. These vessels ascend the river to Yeguas, which is 14 miles below Honda. At this point, goods are transhipped by rail to Honda, and from there they are transported to Bogota via pack animals or sent further up the river using smaller boats.

* **Corozal:** Located near the western coast with a population of 9,000.
