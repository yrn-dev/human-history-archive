# Political Figures and Assemblies After the February 1848 Revolution

The revolution of February 1848 led to significant shifts in the French political landscape, resulting in the election of various deputies and the reorganization of legislative bodies.

## Elected Deputies and Representatives

Following the revolution, several individuals were elected to represent different regions and interests in the legislative assemblies:

*   **Louis Joseph Buffet:** Born in Mirecourt, Buffet was elected as a deputy for the department of the Vosges. In the Assembly, he sat on the right, where he advocated for Louis Napoleon Bonaparte and the repression of the June 1848 insurrection.
*   **Louis Lucien:** The son of Lucien Bonaparte, Louis Lucien entered France in 1848. On November 28, 1848, he was elected deputy for Corsica. Although this election was invalidated, he was returned as a deputy for the Seine in June 1849. He sat on the right of the Legislative Assembly.
*   **Jules Barthelemy Saint-Hilaire:** A philosopher and former political journalist, Saint-Hilaire was elected as a republican deputy after the revolution. However, he was forced to withdraw following the *coup d'etat* of Louis Napoleon.
*   **Cormenin:** An advocate for universal suffrage prior to February 1848, Cormenin was elected to the Constituent Assembly by four departments. He served as one of its vice-presidents and as the president of the constitutional commission, where he played a leading role in drafting the republican constitution before resigning the presidency due to internal disputes.

## Ministerial and Executive Roles

The period following the revolution saw various figures ascend to ministerial positions within the provisional and subsequent governments:

*   **Lazare Hippolyte Carnot:** Having pronounced in favor of the republic on February 24, 1848, Carnot was chosen by Lamartine as the minister of education in the provisional government. He focused on organizing primary school systems, proposing laws for free and obligatory primary instruction and secondary education for girls. He resigned on July 5, 1848, after alienating both the Right and the Extreme Left by opposing purely secular schools.
*   **Jules Armand Stanislas Dufaure:** A moderate republican who accepted the Republic during the 1848 revolution, Dufaure became the minister of the interior under G. Cavaignac on October 13.
*   **Jacques Charles Dupont de l'Eure:** As the oldest member of the provisional assembly during the revolution of 1848, Dupont de l'Eure was appointed its president.

## Legislative and Legal Transitions

The revolution also marked a transition in the nature of French legislative proceedings. Prior to the revolution of February 1848, the French Chamber of Deputies operated under *le réglement de la Chambre*, meaning its internal rules were not considered laws but simply orders of the House. By March 1849, the National Assembly retained the expression *réglement* for its business, although it had become a sovereign assembly whose sanctioned rules were, in fact, laws.

Additionally, the transition of power saw failed attempts to maintain the monarchy. Andre Marie Jean Jacques Dupin (Dupin the Elder), who had served as president of the chamber of deputies, attempted to propose the young count of Paris as king with the duchess of Orleans as regent upon the abdication of Louis Philippe in 1848. This attempt failed, and Dupin subsequently submitted to the new circumstances.
