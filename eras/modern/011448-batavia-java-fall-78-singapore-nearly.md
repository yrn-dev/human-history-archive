# Batavia

## Overview and History
Batavia is a city located on the island of Java in the Dutch East Indies. It served as the early headquarters for the Dutch East India Company, which was formed in 1602 and dissolved in 1798. In 1619, the company founded its capital at Batavia, establishing the city upon the ruins of a native town called Jacatra. 

During the 17th century, the Dutch East India Company used Batavia as a base to extend its operations across the Malay Archipelago, Ceylon, and the Persian Gulf, while managing diplomatic relations with Japan and China. The company's influence in the region was marked by a rigid monopoly on trade, which led to violent hostilities with the English. By 1632, the Dutch had almost entirely driven the English from the Malay Archipelago and the Spice Islands, leaving the English with only a precarious footing at Bantam in Java.

## Geography and Climate
The residency of Batavia is situated on Java, bounded to the west by the residency of Krawang. The northern portion of the region is characterized by flat and occasionally marshy terrain. To the south lies Buitenzorg, a hill station located approximately 860 feet above sea-level at the foot of the Salak volcano.

The climate in Batavia is characterized by low temperature variation. The range of temperature is less than 5 degrees, and the thermometer very rarely rises above 90 degrees or falls below 70 degrees. In terms of precipitation, the annual rainfall in Batavia is approximately 78 inches.

## Administration and Buitenzorg
Buitenzorg, which is known as Bogor by the native population, forms the southern part of the residency of Batavia and covers an area of 1,447 square miles. It is the usual residence of the governor-general of the Dutch East Indies and serves as the seat for the department of mines and the general secretary of the state railway. 

Buitenzorg is noted for its cool and healthy climate and its botanical gardens, which were established in 1817 as part of the governor-general's palace park. Under the direction of J.S. Teysmann starting in 1830, the collection expanded and was recognized as a government institution in 1868. By 1880, the site included a school of agriculture, a museum, and a culture garden; subsequently, a herbarium, a library, and pharmacological, chemical, and botanical laboratories were added. The palace of the governor-general was originally founded in 1744 by Governor-General van Imhoff, though it was rebuilt following an earthquake in 1834.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The region surrounding Batavia and Buitenzorg is agriculturally productive. In the Buitenzorg district, the fertile soil supports the cultivation of rice, sugar-cane, coffee, tea, cloves, nutmeg, pepper, and cinchona. 

Batavia is also a significant maritime and logistics hub. It is listed as one of the sea ports that maintains storage for the supply of liquid fuel used in marine boilers.

## Culture
Batavia has been a site for the production of Dutch drama. Abraham Alewijn (born 1664), a writer known for a coarse vein of dramatic humor and the author of *Jan Los* (1721), lived in Java and had his plays produced in Batavia.
