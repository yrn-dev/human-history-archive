# Johannine Authorship

The question of Johannine authorship concerns the origin of the Johannine writings, which consist of the Fourth Gospel and the three Epistles of John. These texts represent the perspective of Christianity and Christ as held by a Christian teacher who appears to have written and lived in Asia Minor toward the end of the 1st century A.D. While the Church has accepted the view of the personality of Jesus Christ presented in the Fourth Gospel, its value as a narrative of events remains a subject of dispute.

## Academic Debates and Bretschneider

In 1820, a treatise titled *Probabilia de Evangelii el Epistolarum Joannis Apostoli indole et origine* was published. This work attracted significant attention by collecting and discussing, with marked moderation, the arguments against Johannine authorship. The publication prompted numerous replies.

However, in 1822, in the preface to the second edition of his *Dogmatik*, the author, Bretschneider, announced to the astonishment of others that he had never actually doubted the authenticity of the gospel. He stated that the *Probabilia* had been published specifically to draw attention to the subject and to encourage a more comprehensive defense of the gospel's genuineness. 

Despite his stated intentions, the work had professional repercussions. In his autobiography, Bretschneider noted that the publication prevented him from being appointed as the successor to Karl C. Tittmann in Dresden. This was due to the minister Detlev von Einsiedel (1773-1861), who denounced Bretschneider as the "slanderer of John" (*Johannisschänder*).

## The Position of Lücke

Lücke, a prominent and influential figure in the "mediation" school of evangelical theologians (*Vermittelungstheologie*), is known for his four-volume *Kommentar über die Schriften d. Evangelisten Johannes*, published between 1820 and 1832. Lücke acted as an intelligent maintainer of the thesis of Johannine authorship regarding the Fourth Gospel. 

In connection with this position, Lücke was among the first scholars to argue that the Apocalypse was written at an early date and was not authored by an apostle. His work on this subject, *Einleitung in die Offenbarung Johannis*, was first published in 1832.

## Literary Criticism and the Synoptic Problem

Literary criticism of the Gospels provides a broader context for the study of the Fourth Gospel. Research into the "synoptic problem"—the reasons why the Gospels of Matthew, Mark, and Luke are simultaneously similar and different—has demonstrated the priority of Mark. It is concluded that Mark was known to Matthew and Luke in its current state and contents, and may be dated a few years after A.D. 70. 

Matthew and Luke appear to have been published between 80 and 100. These authors utilized the Gospel of Mark as well as another now-lost document containing narratives and sayings of Jesus. Some scholars identify this lost document as the "Logia" mentioned by Papias as the work of Matthew the Apostle, though this identification is not certain.
