# Bahia & Alagoinhas Railway

The Bahia & Alagoinhas is a railway line in Brazil that radiates from the city of Bahia (also known as São Salvador) to inland points. At the time of the archive's records, the line was under construction to reach Joazeiro, which is situated on the Sao Francisco river.

## Context of the City of Bahia
The railway originates in Bahia, the capital of the state of Bahia and a maritime city located on the western side of the peninsula separating the Bay of All Saints (*Bahia de Todos os Santos*) from the Atlantic. The city is divided into a commercial lower town and an upper town. The lower town, situated on a long, narrow beach, houses the city's railway station along with the custom-house, post-office, exchange, market, and arsenal. 

The upper town is built on the western slope of a low ridge, reaching altitudes of 200 to 260 feet above sea level. This area contains the governor's palace, public library, museum, schools, hospitals, and various academies. Communication between the lower and upper towns is facilitated by zigzag roads, a circular tramway, and two elevators.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure
The state of Bahia is an Atlantic state bounded by the states of Sergipe, Pernambuco, and Piauhy to the north, Sergipe and the Atlantic to the east, Espirito Santo and Minas Geraes to the south, and Goyaz and Minas Geraes to the west. The state covers an area of 164,650 square miles.

A significant portion of the state's interior is a barren, arid plateau known as a *chapada*, with elevations ranging from 2,300 to 3,000 feet above sea level. This region is traversed from the south to the north and northeast by the drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river. The Bahia & Alagoinhas railway serves as a critical link between the coastal capital and this interior region, specifically extending toward Joazeiro on the Sao Francisco.

## Other Rail and Transport Links
The Bahia & Alagoinhas is one of several rail lines serving the region. Other railways radiating from the city of Bahia include:
* A short line extending to Santo Amaro.
* The Bahia Central railway.
* The Nazareth tramway.

Both the Bahia Central and the Nazareth tramway extend inland from points located on the opposite side of the bay. Additionally, the inland town of Cachoeira, located on the Paraguassu river approximately 48 miles from São Salvador, serves as the starting point for the Bahia Central railway, which extends 28 miles north to Feira de Santa Anna and 161 miles south-west to Machado Portella.

## Economic Importance
The development of rail infrastructure supports the state's diverse economy. While cattle-raising was once the primary industry of the interior, it has been largely extinguished by increasing aridity and devastating droughts. Current agricultural production in the region includes coffee, cacao, tobacco, cotton, sugar, tropical fruits, and mandioca. The state also exports rum, diamonds, cabinet woods, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, and hides.

The port of Bahia complements the railway system, providing one of the most accessible harbors on the east coast of South America. It handles extensive foreign and coastwise trade and serves as a port of call for most steamship lines traveling between Europe and the continent.
