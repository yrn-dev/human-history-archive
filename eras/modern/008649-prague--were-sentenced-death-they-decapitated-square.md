# The "Bloody Diet" and Executions in Prague

## The Krvavy'sneem
In the history of Bohemia, a significant event occurred on August 22 during the reign of Ferdinand I. Following a revolt, Ferdinand implemented policies aimed at consolidating royal power. By this time, the power of the cities had been thoroughly undermined, and the peasantry had ceased to be a danger due to the establishment of serfdom. Ferdinand focused his efforts on the knights and nobles, utilizing the influence of his court and the Jesuits, whom he established in Bohemia during this period, to make the nobility amenable to his will.

Upon achieving success, Ferdinand sentenced four of the principal leaders of the revolt to death. These individuals consisted of two knights and two citizens of Prague. The executions were carried out by decapitation on the square located outside the Hradcany palace, the site where the estates had met that day. Because of these events, this specific diet became known as the "Krvavy'sneem," or the "bloody diet."

## Political Succession and the Bohemian Throne
Ferdinand I held multiple titles, serving as the king of the Romans, the king of Bohemia, and the king of Hungary, and eventually becoming emperor after the death of Charles V. In 1562, one of the final years of his life, he secured the coronation of his eldest son, Maximilian, as king of Bohemia to ensure the succession to the throne. Ferdinand died in 1564.

Later, the Bohemian crown became vacant in March following the death of Matthias. On July 31, the Bohemian estates formally deposed Ferdinand, and on August 26, they elected Frederick, the elector palatine, as their king. Frederick and his queen, Elizabeth of England, arrived in Bohemia in October and were crowned at St Vitus's cathedral in Prague.

## Military Conflicts in Prague
Prague was a central location for various military and religious conflicts. In 1420, the Hungarian king Sigismund, leading a large army of Bohemians and men from various other countries, occupied the castle of Prague. The city was viewed as a heretical community because its inhabitants accepted other evangelical truths and used the sacred chalice. However, the attempt to conquer the city failed after the Taborites, led by Zizka, repulsed an attack on the Vitkov (now Zizkov) hill. Sigismund also failed in an attempt to relieve the garrison of the Vysehrad fortress on November 1, 1420.

In 1620, during a period of reconquest led by Ferdinand (the successor to Matthias as emperor) and the duke Maximilian of Bavaria, imperial forces arrived at the outskirts of Prague on November 7. Following a struggle on the plateau of "Bila Hora" (White Hill) on November 8, the Bohemians were defeated. The imperialists occupied the port of Prague on the left bank of the Vltava (Moldau) that evening, and King Frederick fled the city the next morning.

Further hostilities occurred during the Thirty Years' War, which began in Prague. In 1648, the Swedish general Konigsmark took part of Prague, marking the final event of that war. In 1741, Charles, the elector of Bavaria, occupied Prague and received oaths of allegiance from many Bohemian knights and nobles, though Maria Theresa eventually recovered the city and other Habsburg lands.

## Administrative and Legal Changes
In the early 18th century, the estates of Bohemia met in Prague on October 16, 1720. During this meeting, they recognized the Pragmatic Sanction, which proclaimed the indivisibility of the Habsburg realm and sanctioned female succession to the Bohemian throne in favor of archduchess Maria Theresa.

Under the reigns of Maria Theresa and her son Joseph II, the autonomy of Bohemia was further limited. A decree in 1749 abolished separate law-courts that had existed within Bohemia, and an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was subsequently appointed to control the administration of Bohemia and the German domains of the house of Habsburg.
