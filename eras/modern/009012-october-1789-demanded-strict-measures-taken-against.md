# October 1789 in Revolutionary France

The month of October 1789 was a period of significant political instability and financial crisis in France, marked by the forced relocation of the monarchy and the implementation of emergency economic measures.

## The Relocation of the Royal Family
In October 1789, a mob marched from Paris to Versailles. This event resulted in the violent transport of the king and queen from Versailles to Paris. Following this movement, the royal family resided in the Tuileries. For the period between 1789 and 1791, France functioned as a limited monarchy where the National Assembly ruled a country at peace while the king maintained a diminished state at the Tuileries.

## Political Reactions and Figures
The events of October 1789 drew various responses from political figures. Francois Antoine de Boissy d'Anglas, a French statesman and deputy to the states-general elected by the third estate of the *senechaussee* of Annonay, expressed approval in several speeches regarding the taking of the royal family to Paris. Boissy d'Anglas further demanded that strict measures be taken against royalists who were intriguing in the south of France.

Conversely, other observers viewed the events with horror. The march of the mob and the violent transport of the monarchs were seen by some as evidence of lawlessness and violence. These observers expressed a disgust for the political conceptions of the French assembly of 1789, fearing that the methods and principles used in France might infect the people of Great Britain.

## Financial Crisis and the Assignats
By 1789, the French government faced an extreme financial strait. The country was threatened with imminent bankruptcy as taxes ceased to be productive, loans were not taken up, and coin became scarce. To address this emergency, the government issued *assignats* as a substitute for metallic currency.

These *assignats* functioned as mortgage bonds on national lands. The lands used for this purpose included:
* Crown lands, which the nation took over on October 7.
* Church property, which was confiscated on November 2, 1789, following a motion by Mirabeau.

The *assignats* were first paid to state creditors, who could then use them to purchase national land. Because *assignats* were given preference over other forms of money for land purchases, it was believed that creditors who did not want land could obtain face-value for them from others. The theory was that as national lands were distributed, the *assignats* returned to the state as purchase-money would be cancelled, eventually causing the entire issue to disappear.

## Other Revolutionary Activities
While the central government struggled in Paris, other agitators were active in the provinces. Francois Noel Babeuf, a political agitator and journalist, spent the period from July to October 1789 in Paris. During this time, he superintended the publication of his first work, *Cadastre perpetuel, dedie a l'assemblee nationale, l'an 1789 et le premier de la liberte francaise* (which had been written in 1787 and was issued in 1790). Upon his return to Roye in October, Babeuf founded the *Correspondant picard*, a publication whose violent character led to his arrest.
