## Book Collecting and First Editions
The value of books to collectors is often driven by associations with famous persons, events, or a traceable pedigree of previous owners. First editions are particularly prized, partly for their utility in study and partly for the imaginative associations they create.

While the first printed editions of Latin and Greek classics were highly valued during the era of George III, their current value is questioned as they lack links to the personalities of their authors and are of little use as texts. Conversely, the prestige of first editions of French and English literary masterpieces has grown. An example of this is the 1623 first folio of Shakespeare, which was sold in 1906.
