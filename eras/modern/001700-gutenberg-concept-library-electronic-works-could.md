# Project Gutenberg

## Origins and Concept
The concept of Project Gutenberg was originated by Professor Michael S. Hart. The project was envisioned as a library of electronic works designed to be shared freely with any person. For a period of forty years, Professor Hart was responsible for the production and distribution of Project Gutenberg eBooks, operating with the assistance of a loose network of volunteers.

## Production of Electronic Works
Project Gutenberg eBooks are frequently developed using several different printed editions. To ensure legal compliance, the project confirms that these editions are not protected by copyright within the United States, unless a copyright notice is explicitly included. Because eBooks may be synthesized from multiple sources, they are not necessarily kept in compliance with any one specific paper edition.

## Access and Search
The primary point of entry for most users is the project's website, located at www.gutenberg.org. This site houses the main search facility for the Project Gutenberg collection.

## Funding and Donations
Project Gutenberg accepts donations through various methods, including credit card donations, online payments, and checks. Information regarding current addresses and methods for donating is available on the project's web pages, specifically at www.gutenberg.org/donate.

The project accepts international donations, although it does not make statements regarding the tax treatment of funds received from outside the United States. Due to the burden of U.S. laws on their small staff, the project does not solicit contributions from states where they have not met specific solicitation requirements. However, there is no prohibition against accepting unsolicited donations from donors in those states who approach the project with offers to donate.
