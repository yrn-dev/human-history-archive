# Populists

The term "Populists" refers to various political groups and affiliations across different national contexts, appearing in the political landscapes of the Austrian Empire, the United States, and specifically within the state of Idaho.

## Populists in the Austrian Empire

In the parliament of the Austrian Empire, Populists were represented among several ethnic and national delegations. According to archival records, the German Liberals included a Populist contingent consisting of 29 members. Additionally, among the Polish representatives, there were 18 Populists. The Italian delegation also included 11 "Clerical Populists."

The German Populists held a distinct position within the German Liberal framework. While most German Liberals were antagonistic toward measures intended to extend popular rights, the German Populists felt that a party identifying as "Liberal" could not logically oppose such an extension. 

During a period of political instability characterized by the inability of parliament to vote on budgets for 1903, 1904, and 1905, the German Populists aligned with other German factions. In response to "Czech aggressiveness" and the blocking of business via "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction, the German Populists joined the German Progressists, the Christian Socialists, and the Constitutional Landed Proprietors to form a supreme committee of four members and a joint executive committee. The purpose of this coalition was to protect German racial interests.

## Populists in the United States

In the United States, the Populists existed as a radical third party. Between 1896 and 1900, the Democratic party underwent a process of attracting the "masses" and radical elements of society during a general realignment focused on industrial-social issues. During this period, the Democratic party absorbed a large portion of the members and many of the principles of the Populist party. Following this absorption, the Democratic party was subsequently challenged by the growing strength of Socialism, which pushed for a further radical widening of its program.

## Populists in Idaho

The political history of Idaho shows the presence of Populist affiliation at the state executive level. Frank Steunenberg, who served as the Governor of Idaho from 1897 to 1901, is identified as a Democrat Populist.
