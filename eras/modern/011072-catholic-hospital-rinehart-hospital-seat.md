# Ashland, Wisconsin

## City Overview and Geography
Ashland is a city and the county-seat of Ashland county, located in the northern part of Wisconsin, U.S.A. It is situated at the head of Chequamegon Bay, which is an arm of Lake Superior. Geographically, the city is positioned approximately 70 miles east of Duluth and Superior, and about 315 miles northwest of Milwaukee. The city is noted for its attractive location and a dry, healthful climate, which makes it a destination for summer resorts.

## Demographics and Administration
The city was settled around 1854 and was later incorporated as a village in 1863. It received its city charter in 1887. Population records indicate that in 1890, the population was 9,956. By 1900, this number grew to 13,074, of whom 4,417 were foreign-born. According to the 1910 census, the population was 11,594.

## Infrastructure and Institutions
Ashland is served by several steamboat lines on the Great Lakes and four railway lines: the Northern Pacific, the Wisconsin Central, the Chicago & North-Western, and the Chicago, St Paul, Minneapolis & Omaha. 

The city's institutional infrastructure includes:
*   **Education:** A high-school building described as one of the best in Wisconsin, and the Northland College and Academy (Congregational), of which the city is the seat.
*   **Public Facilities:** A fine Federal building and the Vaughn public library, which was established in 1895.
*   **Healthcare:** The city contains the Rinehart hospital and a Roman Catholic hospital.

## Economy and Industry
Ashland possesses an excellent harbour and serves as the primary port for the shipment of iron ore from the rich Gogebic Range. Annual shipments of ore approximate 3,500,000 tons, with a value of $12,000,000. The city also maintains large coal docks and an extensive trade in the export of lumber and brownstone quarried nearby. The total annual lake trade exceeds $35,000,000.

The industrial base of the city is diverse, featuring:
*   Foundries, machine shops, and iron and steel rolling mills.
*   Large saw-mills.
*   Railway repair shops specifically for the Chicago & North-Western railway.
*   Knitting works.
*   Manufactories producing wood-alcohol, charcoal, sulphite fibre, and dynamite.

In 1905, the total value of the city's factory product was $4,210,265.
