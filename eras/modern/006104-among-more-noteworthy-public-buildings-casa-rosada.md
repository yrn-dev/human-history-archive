# Casa Rosada

The Casa Rosada is a noteworthy public building located in Buenos Aires. It serves as the government-house and is situated facing the Plaza 25 de Mayo. The building occupies a portion of the site where a fort was originally constructed by Garay in 1580.

## Other Public and Civic Buildings of Buenos Aires

The Casa Rosada is part of a broader collection of significant public structures in the city. These include:

*   **The Congress Hall:** Located on Avenida de Mayo and Calle Callao, this building was completed in 1906 with a construction cost of approximately £1,300,000.
*   **The Municipal Hall:** A new hall located on Avenida de Mayo.
*   **Commercial and Utility Buildings:** The city features a mint, a distributing reservoir, and the *bolsa*, or exchange.

## Education and Culture in Buenos Aires

Higher education in the city is represented by the university of Buenos Aires, which includes several faculties, such as medicine and law. As of 1901, the university had 3,562 students. The educational landscape also includes three normal schools, four national colleges, and various technical schools.

For public culture and research, the city maintains a national museum, a national library, an aquarium, and a zoological garden.

## City Life and Economy

The residents of Buenos Aires, known as Porteños, frequently refer to their city as the "Paris of America." The population is noted for its fondness for amusements, the drama, and music, spending significant time and expense on diversions ranging from horse-racing and *pelota* to Italian opera. The city's social infrastructure includes a large number of athletic, sporting, and social clubs, as well as two or three large public baths.

Economically, Buenos Aires has established itself as the principal manufacturing centre of the republic. The city's industrial establishments number in the thousands, with capital totaling hundreds of millions of dollars.
