# John Sheffield, 1st Duke of Buckingham and Normanby

John Sheffield (1648–1721) was an English poet and statesman who held several high-ranking titles and offices during the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Born on April 7, 1648, he was the son of Edmund, the 2nd earl of Mulgrave. Upon his father's death in 1658, Sheffield succeeded to the title of earl of Mulgrave.

## Military and Early Career
At eighteen years of age, Sheffield entered the fleet to serve in the first Dutch war. He was present at the battle of Southwold Bay during the renewal of hostilities in 1672 and was given command of a ship the following year. In addition to his naval service, he served as a colonel of infantry for a period under Turenne. In 1680, he was tasked with leading an expedition to relieve the town of Tangier; this mission was abortive, and it was suggested—though not definitively proven—that he had been provided with a rotten ship as part of a plot to ensure he would not return.

## Political Life and Court Appointments
Sheffield's political trajectory was closely tied to the monarchy. At court, he aligned himself with the duke of York and assisted in the disgrace of Monmouth. Although he was dismissed from court in 1682, allegedly for attempting to be a suitor for princess Anne, he returned to favor upon the accession of King James. Under James, Sheffield was appointed lord chamberlain and given a seat in the privy council. He remained loyal to the King during his flight from London and supported his most unpopular measures, while also providing protection to the Spanish ambassador from mob violence.

Following the Revolution, Sheffield acquiesced to the new order and was created marquess of Normanby in 1694. However, his political standing shifted in 1696 when he and other Tory peers refused to sign an agreement supporting William as their "rightful and lawful king" in the face of Jacobite attempts. This refusal led to his dismissal from the privy council.

## Later Offices and Dukedom
Upon the accession of Queen Anne, Sheffield became a personal favorite of the monarch. He was appointed lord-lieutenant of the North Riding of Yorkshire and lord privy seal. In 1703, he was created the duke of the county of Buckingham and of Normanby.

His tenure as lord privy seal ended between 1705 and 1710 during a period of Whig predominance. He returned to high office in 1710 as lord steward, and later in 1711 as lord president of the council. Following the death of Queen Anne, he held no further state appointments.

## Death and Succession
John Sheffield died on February 24, 1721, at his residence in St James's Park, which was located on the site where Buckingham Palace now stands. He was succeeded by his son, Edmund. Upon Edmund's death in October 1735, the titles of duke and marquess became extinct.

## Distinction of Titles
The dukedom held by John Sheffield was specifically "of the county of Buckingham and of Normanby." This is distinct from the later title of marquess and duke of Buckingham held by the Grenville family, which originated in 1784 and was derived from the town of Buckingham rather than the county.
