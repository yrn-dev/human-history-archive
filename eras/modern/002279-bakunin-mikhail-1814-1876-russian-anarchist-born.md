# BAKUNIN, MIKHAIL (1814-1876)

## Early Life and Military Service
Mikhail Bakunin was born in 1814 into an aristocratic family in Torjok, located in the government of Tver. He began his professional life as an officer of the Imperial Guard, serving in Poland. However, Bakunin resigned his commission after witnessing the repressive methods used against the Poles, which created in him a disgust for despotism.

## Intellectual Development and European Travels
Following his resignation, Bakunin traveled to Germany, where he studied Hegel and established connections with the leaders of the young German movement in Berlin. He subsequently moved to Paris, where he met George Sand and Proudhon, and became acquainted with the primary Polish exiles. 

Bakunin later journeyed to Switzerland, where he resided for a period and participated actively in various socialistic movements. During his time in Switzerland, the Russian government ordered him to return to Russia; upon his refusal to comply, his property was confiscated.

## Revolutionary Activity and Imprisonment
In 1848, Bakunin returned to Paris and published a violent tirade against Russia, an act that led to his expulsion from France. The revolutionary movements of 1848 provided him with the opportunity to engage in a violent campaign of democratic agitation. His participation in the Dresden insurrection of 1849 resulted in his arrest and a sentence of death. 

This death sentence was later commuted to life imprisonment. Bakunin was eventually handed over to the Russian authorities, who imprisoned him and finally sent him to eastern Siberia in 1855.

## Escape and Later Exile
Bakunin was eventually granted permission to move to the Amur region, from which he successfully escaped. He traveled through the United States and Japan, arriving in England in 1861. He spent the remainder of his life in exile in western Europe, primarily residing in Switzerland.

## Political Organizations and Final Years
In 1869, Bakunin founded the Social Democratic Alliance, though the organization dissolved within the same year. He subsequently joined the International. In 1870, he attempted a rising at Lyons, utilizing principles that were later exemplified by the Paris Commune.

Bakunin's tenure with the International ended at the Hague congress in 1872, where he was outvoted and expelled by the Marx party. He retired to Lugano in 1873 and died in Bern on June 13, 1876.
