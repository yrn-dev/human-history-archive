# Funeral Ode for the Duchess Eberhardine

The Funeral Ode for the Duchess Eberhardine is a musical composition associated with the works of Bach. It is characterized by its relationship to other ecclesiastical works, specifically those involving the Passions.

## Composition and Origins
The Funeral Ode for the Duchess Eberhardine is now known to have been arranged from portions of the lost Passion according to St Mark. Based on the movements that have been preserved within the *Trauer-Ode* (Funeral Ode), it is judged that the original Passion according to St Mark must have been larger in scale than the Passion according to St John.

## Context within Bach's Works
The piece exists within a broader body of ecclesiastical vocal music. Bach's other major Passion works include the Passions according to St Matthew and St John. While J. N. Forkel stated that Bach wrote five Passions, the archive notes that there is an indisputable Bach autograph of a Passion according to St Luke, though critics have described it as worthless, suggesting it may have been a copy made for personal interest.

The Funeral Ode is categorized alongside other sacred compositions, which include:
*   Four short masses consisting only of the Kyrie and Gloria, primarily compiled from church cantatas.
*   The Mass in B minor and the Magnificat in D.
*   Five *a capella* motets, though it is believed that all except *Komm Jesu komm* were intended to be partly supported by the organ.
*   A sixth motet featuring an obligato figured-bass accompaniment.
*   A large collection of plain chorales, some of which feature original melodies.
