# Napoleon I

Napoleon I, also referred to as Bonaparte, was a French leader who rose to power during the period following the French Revolution, serving as First Consul with enormous powers before becoming Emperor in 1804. His reign was characterized by extensive military campaigns, the restructuring of European borders, and significant influence over French domestic and commercial life.

## Military Campaigns and European Expansion
Napoleon's military activities spanned several decades and multiple continents. In 1798, he went to Egypt, a campaign that included the Battle of the Nile. Upon his return to France in 1799, he consolidated power, eventually assuming the imperial title. 

His expansionist efforts led to the overthrow of Prussia at Jena in 1806. The geopolitical shifts caused by his rise had a profound impact on other European monarchs; for instance, Francis II took the title of Emperor of Austria in 1805 and subsequently dropped the title of Holy Roman Emperor in 1806, effectively ending the Holy Roman Empire. In 1808, Napoleon extended his family's influence by making his brother, Joseph, the King of Spain.

Napoleon's fortunes shifted with the invasion of Russia in 1812, a campaign that ended in a disastrous retreat from Moscow. Following this, he waged another equally disastrous campaign in 1813. During these years, he was supported by devoted aides such as Maret (the duc de Bassano), who served as his minister of Foreign Affairs and later as his private secretary. Maret helped conclude treaties with Prussia and Austria prior to the Russian invasion and remained with Napoleon through the campaigns of 1814 and 1815.

## Domestic Impact and Administration
Napoleon's rule had varying effects on French cities and citizens. In Bordeaux, for example, commerce was greatly reduced under his administration. His government also created new titles of nobility; Baron Louis, a financial expert and statesman, was made a baron of the empire in 1809.

Napoleon's personal life was also entwined with European diplomacy. In 1810, he entered into a marriage alliance with the archduchess Marie Louise of Austria.

## Fall and Abdication
The decline of Napoleon's empire culminated in 1814. In March of that year, the Allies entered Paris. Through negotiations led by Talleyrand, the restoration of the House of Bourbon was effected. Napoleon abdicated in 1814 and was sent to the island of Elba, where he was permitted to maintain a certain state and continue to be called Emperor, a treatment insisted upon by Alexander I. Following his removal, his empress returned to Vienna, and the two never saw each other again.

The transition of power in 1814 saw the city of Bordeaux declare for the house of Bourbon. This period marked the beginning of the reign of Louis XVIII, the brother of Louis XVI. The restoration was met with malice by the Bourbons toward the veterans of Napoleon's Grand Army.

## Legacy and Succession
The Napoleonic era left a vacuum of power and a legacy of conflict that continued into the mid-19th century. In 1852, Napoleon III became Emperor of the French. His reign ended after he declared war against Prussia in 1870, leading to the surrender of Paris in January 1871 and the subsequent Peace of Frankfort, which saw the King of Prussia become the "German Emperor."
