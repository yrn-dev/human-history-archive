# Bolivia

Bolivia is an inland republic located in South America. Historically, it was known as the province of Charcas, or Upper Peru, and was once a part of the Spanish vice-royalty of Peru. It ranks as the third largest political division on the continent.

## Geography and Boundaries
The republic extends approximately from 9 deg. 44' to 22 deg. 50' S. latitude and from 58 deg. to 70 deg. W. longitude. Its borders are defined by several neighboring states: it is bounded to the north and east by Brazil, to the south by Argentina and Paraguay, and to the west by Peru and Chile.

The exact area of Bolivia has been subject to wide variation and confusion due to repeated losses of territory resulting from boundary disputes with neighboring states. Because of these revisions, definitive figures are difficult to establish. Official estimates place the area at 703,633 or 640,226 sq. m., while Supan, in *Die Bevolkerung der Erde* (1904), estimates it at 515,156 sq. m.

## Border with Brazil
The boundary between Bolivia and Brazil originated from the limits of Portuguese and Spanish colonies established by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750), with later modifications in 1867 and 1903. 

The line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.), ascending the river to a point on the west bank 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra. From there, it moves 4 kilometres inland to lat. 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and long. 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W., following an irregular course north and east of north toward Lakes Uberaba, Gaiba (or Gahiba), and Mandiore. The boundary then proceeds up the San Matias river and north along the Sierra Ricardo Franco to the headwaters of the Rio Verde. This specific section was adjusted to place Fort Coimbra, Corumba, and other west-bank settlements under Brazilian jurisdiction; a 1903 modification ceded approximately 1158 sq. m. back to Bolivia to improve commercial facilities on the Paraguay.

The frontier continues along the Madeira, Mamore, Guapore, and Verde rivers down to the mouth of the Abuna (approximately lat. 9 deg. 44' S.), as determined by the 1903 treaty. While the original colonial frontier ran down the Madeira to a point between the Amazon and the Beni before heading west to the Javary, the 1867 treaty moved the starting point to the mouth of the Beni (lat. 10 deg. 20' S.), creating a straight line to the source of the Javary. This gave Brazil a large territory. Later, when Brazilians invaded the valuable rubber forests of the upper Purus, the boundary was settled via the 1903 treaty of Petropolis. This agreement specified the line should ascend the Abuna river to lat. 10 deg. 20' S., run west along that parallel to the Rapirran river, follow it to its principal source, move west to the Ituxy river and its source, and finally proceed to the source of Bahia Creek and up the Aquiry (or Acre) river to its source.

## Climate and Zonal Divisions
Bolivia experiences a wide range of temperatures between locations of the same latitude. The native population categorizes the climatic zones as follows:

*   **Yungas**: This zone consists of mountain valleys and lowlands up to 5000 ft. The atmosphere is exceedingly humid and the temperature is tropical, with no known winter. Official estimates place the mean temperature at 70 deg. F., though this is likely an average of the low country and higher elevations. Malaria and fevers are common here due to decaying vegetation on the ground.
*   **Valle**: Including deep valleys from 5000 to 9500 ft., this sub-tropical region is sometimes called a region of perpetual summer. It is characterized by a warm climate with moderate temperature variations and no cold weather.
*   **Cabezera de valle**: This zone covers the heads of deep valleys from 9500 to 11,000 ft. The climate is temperate with regular seasons, which is favorable for growing vegetables and cereals.
*   **Puna**: Located between 11,000 and 12,500 ft., this zone encompasses the great central plateau. It has only two seasons: winter and a cold autumn or summer. The air is cold and dry, and the short warm season only allows for the production of barley and potatoes. The official mean temperature is 54 deg. F.
*   **Puna brava**: Extending from 12,500 ft. to the snow limit (approximately 17,500 ft.), this is a bleak and inhospitable territory inhabited only by miners and shepherds.

Above the *puna brava* lies the region of eternal snow, described as an Arctic zone within the tropics. Generally, the temperate (*cabezera de valle*) and sub-tropical (*valle*) regions are considered agreeable and healthy, featuring moderate temperatures and plentiful rainfall. High mortality rates among natives are attributed to diet and unsanitary habits rather than the climate.
