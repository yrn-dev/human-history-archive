# Works on the Life and Reign of Louis

The historical record regarding the various figures named Louis in French history is preserved through a diverse array of memoirs, diplomatic correspondence, and scholarly biographies. These sources vary significantly in their nature, ranging from original journals and letters to later historical analyses based on national archives.

## Louis Bonaparte
The life and reign of Louis Bonaparte are documented in several key works. Among the chief sources is the three-volume *Documents historiques et reflexions sur le gouvernement de Hollande* (2nd ed., 1820) by le comte de Saint-Leu. Other significant contributions include:
* **F. Rocquain**: *Napoleon I^er et le Roi Louis, d'apres les documents conserves aux archives nationales* (1875).
* **Baron A. du Casse**: *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883).
* **A. Garnier**: *La Cour de Hollande sous le regne de Louis Bonaparte, par un auditeur* (1823).
* **T. Jorissen**: *Napoleon 1'er et le roi de Hollande (1806-1813) d'apres des documents authentiques et inedits* (1868).
* **V. Loosjes**: *Louis Bonaparte, Koning van Holland* (1888).
* **L. Wichers**: *De Regeering van Koning Lodewijk Napoleon (1806-1810)* (1892).
* **F. Masson**: *Napoleon et sa famille* (4 vols., 1897-1900).

## Louis XVIII
The bibliography for Louis XVIII (Louis-Stanislas-Xavier, 1755-1824) is extensive, though it is noted that no complete or trustworthy edition of his writings and correspondence exists. 

### Primary Writings and Correspondence
Works directly from the hand of Louis XVIII include the *Relation d'un voyage à Bruxelles et à Coblentz* (1791, published 1823) and the *Journal de Marie-Thérèse de France, duchesse d'Angoulême*, which he corrected and annotated (ed. Imbert de St Amand, 1896). His letters are found in various collections, including the *Lettres d'Artwell* (1830), letters to the comte de Saint-Priest (ed. Barante, 1845), and correspondence with Talleyrand during the Congress of Vienna (1814-1815, ed. Pallain). Additional insights are available through the correspondence of Metternich, Castlereagh, J. de Maistre, the Wellington Dispatches, and the diplomatic correspondence of Pozzo di Borgo with the comte de Nesselrode.

### Secondary Works and Memoirs
The *Mémoires de Louis XVIII* (12 vols., 1832-1833) were compiled by Lamothe-Langon, who is described as a compiler of more or less apocryphal memoirs. In contrast, the works of E. Daudet are considered of the greatest importance due to their basis in original documents. These include:
* *La Terreur Blanche* (1878).
* *Hist. de la restauration 1814-1830* (1882).
* *Louis XVIII. et le duc Decazes* (1899).
* *Hist. de l'émigration* (originally three studies published between 1886 and 1890, later expanded into a 3-volume work, 1904-1907).

Other notable works include *Louis XVIII. et les cent-jours à Gand* (1898) by E. Romberg and A. Malet, *Le Retour des Bourbons* (1908) by G. Stenger, and *Bonaparte et les Bourbons* (1899) by Cte. L. de Remâcle. For specific episodes, the work of Vicomte de Reiset, *La Comtesse de Balbi* (1908), provides a long bibliography of memoirs.

## Louis XIV
The reign of Louis XIV is characterized by a wealth of memoirs detailing court life. The primary sources include the memoirs of Madame de Motteville (covering the Fronde period), the letters of Madame de Sévigné, and the memoirs of Saint-Simon. The king's own ideas are documented in the *Mémoires de Louis XIV. pour l'instruction du dauphin* (edited by Dreyss). His private life is further revealed through the letters of the Duchesse d'Orléans and Madame de Maintenon.

Among historians, Michelet is noted as being the fullest on the king's private life. Other relevant works include Voltaire's *Siècle de Louis XIV.*, P. Clément's *Histoire de la vie et de l'administration de Colbert*, and Sainte-Beuve's *Causeries de lundi*. Comprehensive bibliographies are found in G. Monod's *Bibliographie de l'histoire de France*, the *Cambridge Modern History* (vol. v), and the *Histoire générale* of Lavisse and Rambaud (vol. vi).

## Louis XV
For the general life of Louis XV, the archive points to the memoirs of Barbier, Villars, d'Argenson, and Saint-Simon. Details regarding his private life are contained in:
* E. Boutaric: *Correspondance secrète de Louis XV.*
* P. Malassi: *Correspondance* of Madame de Pompadour.
* Dietric: *Les Maîtresses de Louis XV.*
* Fleury: *Louis XV. intimes et les petites maîtresses* (1909).
