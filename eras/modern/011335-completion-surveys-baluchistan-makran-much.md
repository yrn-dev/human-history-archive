# Baluchistan and Makran

Baluchistan is a sparsely populated region extending from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. It is a territory of significant geographical scale, covering a larger area than any Indian province with the exception of Bengal, Madras, and Burma.

## Political and Administrative Divisions
The region is divided into several distinct administrative entities. British Baluchistan, a portion of British India under a chief commissioner, covers 9,403 sq. m. This area consists primarily of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the 1879 treaty of Gandamak and was formally declared part of British India in 1887. 

Other territories are managed as foreign territories under the same chief commissioner acting as agent to the governor-general. These are further subdivided into:
*   **Directly Administered Districts:** Including the Bolan Pass, the eastern portion of the Quetta tahsil, and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, totaling 36,401 sq. m.
*   **Native States:** Including Las Bela and the state of Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran), covering 79,382 sq. m.
*   **Tribal Areas:** Specifically those in the possession of the Bugti and Marri tribes, covering 7,129 sq. m.

Additionally, there is Persian Baluchistan, a province of Persia comprising the western part of the wider region. It has an area of approximately 60,000 sq. m. and is bounded by Kerman to the west, Seistan and the central Persian desert to the north, and British and independent Baluchistan to the east. It is an administrative division of Kerman subdivided into twenty districts, including Makran—the country of the Ichthyophagi—which contains the subdistricts of Bahu, Dasht, Bint, Geh, Kucheh, and Kasrkand. Persian supremacy over some extreme eastern districts was not recognized until 1872, following the 1849 occupation of Bampur.

## Geography and Geology
The landscape is characterized by diverse terrains. North of the railway line that runs through Quetta to New Chaman, the region consists of rough highlands and the central ranges of the Suliman system. South of the railway lies a square block of territory roughly 300 m. by 300 m. This area includes the Jalawan highlands of the Kalat plateau and the Kharan desert.

Geologically, the mountain ranges consist mainly of Tertiary and Cretaceous beds arranged in folds. These folds form a system extending between Mount Ararat and Peshawar. Jurassic rocks are present in the hills of British Baluchistan, and Triassic beds and *Fusulina* limestone are found along the south side of the upper Zhob. Most deposits are marine, though north-western Baluchistan features volcanic conglomerates and tuffs. A line of recent volcanic cones extends from eastern Persia into western Baluchistan; while these appear extinct in Baluchistan, the Koh-i-Tafdan beyond the Persian frontier still emits vapours. The Makran coast is characterized by horizontal deposits and "mud volcanoes."

## Hydrography and Trade
Drainage patterns vary by region. In the north, water flows toward the Gomal or east toward the Indus. In the south, the Hab river forms the boundary west of Karachi, while the Dasht and Hingol (ancient *Tomerus*) drain Makran into the Arabian Sea. The Purali (ancient *Arabus*) drains the flats of Las Bela. The Mulla river serves as an important ancient highway from the Indus plains to Kandahar.

The fortress of Kalat, situated 6,800 ft. above sea-level, acts as a "hub" for the Lora, Mulla, and Bolan systems. The completion of surveys in Makran and Baluchistan, along with the settlement of Afghanistan's southern boundaries, has reopened at least one old trade route between India and Seistan and clarified ancient connections between the east and west.

## Ethnography and Economy
The population is a mixture of various races. The Rind tribes claim Koraish extraction and Arab origin. In the central desert, nomads known as Rekis (specifically the Mohamadani) are also likely of Arab origin. The Naushirwanis of the Mashkel and Kharan deserts are a Persian race who entered Baluchistan in historic times. An underlying Persian (Tajik) population exists throughout the region, often as agricultural bondsmen or slaves, such as the Nakibs, Dehkans, or Durzadas.

Economically, the khan of Kalat receives revenues from agricultural sources and subsidies, totaling about Rs.500,000 per annum. This includes government rent for the Quetta district (Rs.25,000 since 1882) and transit duties in the Boian (Rs.30,000, recently increased by Rs.9,000). In 1899, Kalat's imports were valued at Rs.700,000 and exports at Rs.505,000.

## Scientific Surveying
Since 1880, systematic surveys of Baluchistan have progressed, contributing to a series of maps extending from the Euphrates to the Oxus. These efforts were part of a wider Indian triangulation that pushed outwards through Baluchistan and Afghanistan to eastern Persia, and along the Makran coast toward Arabia. These surveys have significantly raised the accuracy of Asiatic geographical mapping, effectively freeing western Asia (excluding Arabia) from geographical perplexity by the close of the 19th century.
