# Automata

An automaton is a self-moving machine, or a device in which the principle of motion is contained within the mechanism itself. While this description technically includes watches, clocks, and similar machines, the term is generally used to describe contrivances designed to simulate the motions of animal life for a period of time. When an automaton represents human figures and actions, it has sometimes been specifically referred to as an *androides*.

## Early History and Medieval Examples
Records of the construction of automata exist from very early periods, including the moving figures of Daedalus and the tripods of Vulcan. In 400 B.C., Archytas of Tarentum is said to have created a wooden pigeon capable of flight.

During the Middle Ages, numerous instances of such constructions were recorded:
* **Regiomontanus:** Said to have created an iron fly that could flutter around a room and return to his hand, as well as an eagle that flew before Emperor Maximilian during his entry into Nuremberg.
* **Roger Bacon:** Said to have forged a brazen head that spoke.
* **Albertus Magnus:** Said to have possessed an *androides* that served as a doorkeeper; this figure was later broken to pieces by Aquinas.

Less is accurately known regarding some later examples, such as the automata exhibited by Dr. Camus or the figure constructed by Descartes.

## 18th Century Developments
In the 18th century, the mechanician Jacques de Vaucanson exhibited three notable figures: a tambourine-player, a flute-player, and a duck. The duck was capable of drinking, eating, and exactly imitating the natural voice of the fowl. Because the means used to produce these results were clearly seen, these exhibitions provided a great impulse for the construction of similar figures.

Following this period, other notable constructions included:
* **Knauss:** Exhibited an automaton in Vienna that could write.
* **Droz (father and son):** Constructed several ingenious mechanical figures capable of playing music and writing.
* **Frederick Kaufmann and Leonard Maelzel:** Created automatic trumpeters capable of playing several marches.

The Swiss have been celebrated for their mechanical ingenuity, producing many curious toys, such as singing and flying birds, which are frequently found in industrial exhibitions.
