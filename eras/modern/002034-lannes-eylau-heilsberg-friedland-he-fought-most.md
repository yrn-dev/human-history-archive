# Lannes

Lannes was a French military commander who served under Napoleon during the early 19th century. He participated in several major campaigns across Europe, including engagements in Italy and against the Austrian and Russian empires.

## Early Campaigns and Marengo
During the campaign in Italy, Lannes was involved in the battle of Marengo. He commanded a force of approximately 4,000 men on the French right, opposing the column of Lieutenant-Field-Marshal Ott, which numbered 7,500. Lannes was eventually forced to retire from his position with heavy losses and was driven out of Castel Ceriolo.

Following the arrival of Monnier's division and the subsequent arrival of Desaix's corps, Lannes' troops rallied. In the final stages of the battle, Lannes' remnants were positioned on the right and left of Desaix's infantry, with Monnier on Lannes' right facing Ott's column.

## Conflicts with Russia and Prussia
Lannes served as a commander during the wars against the Russian and Prussian empires. At the Battle of Austerlitz, he commanded the left wing of the French army, where he was engaged against Prince Peter Bagration. 

During the subsequent conflicts in 1807, Lannes fought in the battles of Eylau, Heilsberg, and Friedland. These engagements were part of a broader campaign against Russia; while Napoleon was "roughly handled" at Eylau, the Russians were eventually defeated at Friedland. These victories, along with the defeat of Prussia at Jena in 1806, left Prussia at the mercy of France.

## The Battle of Aspern-Essling and Death
Lannes played a critical role in the fighting at Aspern and Essling. During the initial stages of the battle, Lannes made a heroic and successful resistance in the villages with a single division. On the 22nd, he resisted desperately at Essling and, reinforced by the division of St Hilaire, succeeded in driving out Rosenberg.

As the battle progressed, Lannes was positioned on the right of the French centre during a great attack launched by Napoleon against the Austrian centre. Although the French initially broke through the Austrian line, Lannes was eventually checked by the archduke's last reserve, which caused the impetus of the attack to die out.

The situation became critical when the Danube bridges were cut by the Austrians, forcing a costly French retirement. Lannes is credited with providing the steadiness necessary to prevent the French army from being driven into the Danube during the archduke's final, furious effort to break their resistance. Lannes was killed during this engagement.
