# Bonn

## Overview and Early History
Bonn, also known as *Bonna* or *Castra Bonnensia*, was originally a town of the Ubii. In its early history, it served as the site of a Roman military settlement and is frequently mentioned by the writer Tacitus. A notable event occurred there in A.D. 70, when the Romans suffered a defeat in a battle against the Batavians, led by the valiant Claudius Civilis.

## Medieval Development and Fortification
The town experienced significant decline due to successive inroads by barbarians. It was restored around 359 by the emperor Julian, though it continued to suffer from barbarian attacks following the break-up of the Roman empire. In 889, the town was devastated by bands of Norse raiders who had traveled up the Rhine.

Fortifications were later established by Konrad von Hochstaden, the archbishop of Cologne, who held office from 1238 to 1261. In 1265, his successor, Engelbert von Falkenburg (who died in 1274), established himself in Bonn after being driven out of his cathedral city by the townspeople.

## Residence of the Electors and Military Conflict
From 1265 until 1794, Bonn served as the residence of the electors of Cologne. During the 16th, 17th, and 18th centuries, Germany was devastated by various wars. During this period, the town was frequently occupied and besieged by several belligerents. Despite these conflicts, the town remained the property of the electors until 1794.

## Transition of Power
The French took possession of Bonn in 1794, and this occupation was formally recognized at the peace of Luneville. The town's fortifications had previously been dismantled in 1717. In 1815, the congress of Vienna made the town over to Prussia.
