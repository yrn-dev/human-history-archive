# Climate

The Earth's climate is characterized by perpetual change and fluctuation. According to the record of the rocks, these variations occur over vast ages and are not regular in period. The interplay of different systems of influence can result in ages of world-wide warmth, ages where the atmosphere is temperate, or ages of extreme bitterness and cold that inflict hardship upon life. These climatic shifts have historically dictated the expansion and multiplication of life or the disappearance of various species, genera, and classes.

## Regional Variations in Europe and Asia

In Europe, climatic conditions vary significantly by geography and elevation. In Bulgaria, the climate is influenced by the configuration of the Balkan Peninsula and its mountain ranges. The region experiences extreme heat in summer and cold in winter, with remarkable local contrasts. In districts between the Balkans and the Danube, winter cold is intense, with temperatures known to fall to 24° below zero, frequently freezing the river. Conversely, the high tableland of Sofia is generally covered in snow during winter but maintains a more equable climate, with a maximum temperature of 86° and a minimum of 2°. The eastern districts are moderated by the sea, though the coast is exposed to violent north-east winds. The sheltered plain of Eastern Rumelia is comparatively warm, with spring beginning six weeks earlier than in other Bulgarian regions.

In Asia, the climate of Siberia north of the 50th parallel is similar to similarly situated portions of Europe, although it possesses hotter summers and more severe winters. Moderate rainfall in this region supports abundant vegetation and the great rivers flowing to the Arctic Sea. A temperate climate exists in a belt extending from the Mediterranean across to northern China and the Himalaya, encompassing Afghanistan, northern Persia, and Asia Minor. In this zone, summers are hot and winters severe, but not extreme, and rainfall is sufficient to maintain vigorous vegetation.

Other temperate regions include those approximating southern England, though some are hotter in summer and colder in winter; in the Ardennes, higher elevation leads to more severe winters. Additionally, peat is found in treeless areas of more temperate climates, such as the North German plain, the tableland of Bavaria, and the west of Scotland.

## Tropical and Island Climates

Tropical regions exhibit diverse climatic zones often determined by altitude. In Costa Rica, three zones are distinguishable: tropical, temperate, and cold. The tropical zone, covering the foothills and coast, has a mean annual temperature between 72°F and 82°F. The temperate zone, specifically the San Jose plateau at 3000-5000 ft., averages 68°F with a seasonal variation of only 5°. Above 7500 ft., snow rarely falls, but frosts are frequent. Costa Rica's wet season occurs from April to December during the south-west monsoon, though it is interrupted in June by a fortnight of dry weather called the *Veranillo de San Juan*. Normal rainfall is about 80 inches, but common cloud-bursts can increase this to 150 inches or more.

In Barbados, the climate is described as pleasant, with temperatures ranging from 70°F to 86°F and rarely falling below 65°F. The year is divided into a wet season and a dry season (which is also the cold season), extending from December to the end of May. The tropical heat is tempered for eight months by invigorating N.E. trade winds. The average annual rainfall is approximately 60 inches, with September being the wettest month.

Other island and coastal variations include:
* **Corsica:** The climate ranges from warmth in the lowlands to extreme rigour in the mountains, with the intermediate region being the most healthy and temperate. The mean annual temperature at Ajaccio is 63°F.
* **Burma:** The delta region is cooler and more temperate than Upper Burma. Rainfall varies from about 200 inches in the Tenasserim and Arakan divisions to an average of 90 inches in Rangoon. In the "rainless zone" extending to Katha, annual averages fall as low as 20 to 30 inches.

## Temperature Extremes and Atmospheric Conditions

Certain regions are characterized by temperate climates where humidity increases the extremes of sensible heat and cold. In one such area near sea level, the thermometer ranges from a lowest known extreme of 45°F (or an ordinary lowest of 48°F in January) to a highest known extreme of 86°F (or an ordinary 82°F in July). In this region, winds prevail from the north-west, west, and south in winter, while north, north-east, and east winds are most frequent in summer. Navigation of the coasts in this area is rendered dangerous by winds from the south-west and west.
