# On the Western Edge of the Kashgar Plains: The Political Boundary

The political boundary between Russia and China on the western edge of the Kashgar plains is defined by the meridional range of Sarikol. This mountain system, referred to as Taurus by the ancients and Bolor during medieval times, serves as one of the world's most significant natural mountain divisions.

## Geography of the Sarikol Range
The Sarikol range consists of two parallel chains. The western chain serves as the water-divide of the Pamirs. The eastern chain, which has been known as the Kandar or Kashgar range, is characterized by lateral gorges that occur at intervals, allowing for the passage of the main drainage originating from the eastern slopes of the Pamirs.

The region surrounding Kashgar and Yarkand is characterized by an elevation ranging from 4,000 to 6,000 feet. This area forms part of Chinese or eastern Turkestan, which constitutes the western portion of the Gobi—the great desert of central Asia. This desert is bounded to the north and north-west by the Yablonoi Mountains and their western extension in the Tian-shan, to the east by the Khingan range, and to the south by the northern face of the Tibetan plateau.

## Boundary Demarcations and Political Status
While the Sarikol range intervenes between Chinese Turkestan and the Russian Pamirs, the actual dividing line has remained indefinite. To the north-east of Kashgar, the southern boundary of Siberia follows an irregular course. This boundary is partly defined by the Alatau and Tian-shan mountains until it reaches a northerly point at approximately 53 degrees N. latitude, marked by the Sayan range to the west of Irkutsk.

Further west, the Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 demarcated a line extending to the glaciers and snowfields that overlook the Chinese border. This follows a separate boundary fixed between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat), which was determined by the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886. In that sector, the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia up to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## Historical and Regional Context
Kashgar has historically served as a convergence point for roads. It is possible to travel from China to Kashgar via the Tarim valley or along the northern foothills of the Kuen Lun, and from there cross the mountains to Bokhara, Samarkand, and Kokand. Because of this geography, the area is described as a natural historical meeting-place for Mongolian and Aryan peoples.

Between 1865 and 1877, Kashgar served as the capital of Ya’kúb Kúshbegi. A soldier of fortune and said to be a Tajik of Shighnan by descent, Ya’kúb Kúshbegi took the title of *Atalik Gházi* and established a throne in Eastern Turkestan after the Chinese yoke was thrown off, bringing the entire basin under his authority.

Regarding the era of the Great Kaan, there is historical discussion concerning the status of the region. While some sources suggest the kingdom of Cascar was subject to the Great Khan, others note that the boundary between the territory of Kaidu and the Kaan lay much further east, between Komul and Karashahr. Marco Polo acknowledged that Yarkand belonged to Kaidu.

## Etymology and Regional Features
According to a Chinese traveller, the name Kashgar is derived from the words *Kash*, meaning fine colour, and *gar*, meaning brick house. The region is situated within the Tarim Basin (roughly Eastern Turkestan), an area between the Kuen Lun and Thien Shan mountains characterized by rivers that end in intermittent lakes and swamps rather than reaching the sea.
