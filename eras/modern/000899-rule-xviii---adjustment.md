# RULE XVIII.--ADJUSTMENT

Rule XVIII is a component of the York-Antwerp Rules, a set of technical guidelines designed to provide uniformity and certainty in the adjustment of General Average (G.A.) losses. These rules were developed to resolve the diversities of usage that arose across different maritime countries and periods when applying the fundamental principle of general average: that a loss incurred for the advantage of all coadventurers should be made good by all of them in equitable proportion to their stakes in the adventure.

## History of the York-Antwerp Rules
The creation of these rules was the result of efforts by a small group of individuals seeking to replace varying national practices with a standardized system. The process began in May 1860 at a congress in Glasgow, presided over by Lord Brougham and assisted by Lord Neaves. Subsequent congresses were held in London in 1862 and York in 1864, leading to the agreement of the "York Rules."

These rules were later refined by the "Association for the Reform and Codification of the Law of Nations" through conferences held at the Hague (1875), Bremen (1876), and Antwerp (1877). The resulting body of rules was named the "York and Antwerp (or York-Antwerp) Rules." Further refinements were pursued during a conference in Liverpool in the autumn of 1890.

## Principles of Adjustment
The York-Antwerp Rules provide a framework for adjusting G.A. losses that differs from English common law and former English practice. Under the strict principle of English common law, a sacrifice or expenditure only qualifies as G.A. if it is made under the pressure of a common risk for the common safety of the interests involved. In that view, it is insufficient for an expenditure to be prudent, necessary for the completion of the adventure, or beneficial to both the shipowner and cargo owner; the safety of the interests must be at stake.

In contrast, the York-Antwerp Rules follow practices prevalent in foreign countries, recognizing the idea of a common commercial adventure rather than strictly limiting G.A. to common safety from the sea. According to Rule XVIII, unless otherwise provided in the preceding rules, an adjustment is drawn up based on the law and practice that would have governed the adjustment if the contract of affreightment had not included a clause to pay G.A. according to these rules.

## Specific Rules for G.A. Claims
The rules provide definite scales and definitions to replace uncertain customary rules that previously varied by the place of adjustment.

### Repairs and Cargo
Rule XIII establishes a definite scale for deductions from the cost of repairs regarding "new for old," adapted to modern shipbuilding. For iron or steel ships, repairs are allowed in full for vessels up to one year old, except for the painting or coating of the bottom, which incurs a one-third deduction. Rule XIV specifies that no "new for old" deductions are made from the cost of temporary repairs of damage allowable as G.A.

Regarding cargo, Rule XV states that loss of freight resulting from the loss of or damage to cargo shall be made good as G.A. when caused by a G.A. act or when the cargo loss is made good. Rule XVI dictates that the amount made good for sacrificed goods is the loss sustained by the owner, based on market values at the termination of the adventure or the arrival of the vessel. Additionally, Rule XII allows damage to cargo caused by discharging, storing, reloading, and stowing to be made good as G.A., provided the cost of those measures is admitted as G.A.

### Contributory Values and Expenses
Rule XVII defines contributory values based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure, plus any amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed property. Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight at risk, specifically port charges and crew's wages that would not have been incurred had the ship and cargo been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice or G.A. act. Charges incurred for property after the G.A. act are also deducted, unless those charges are allowed in G.A. Notably, personal effects and passengers' luggage not shipped under bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.

Furthermore, when a ship is detained in a port for repairs mentioned in Rule X, the maintenance and wages of the master, officers, and crew during the extra detention period are admitted as G.A. However, if the voyage is abandoned or the ship is condemned, wages and maintenance incurred after that date are not admitted.
