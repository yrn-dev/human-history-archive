# Legislative Assembly and Associated Political Figures

The Legislative Assembly and its surrounding political environment in France were characterized by various shifts in power, including several *coups d'état* and the transition between different republican and imperial regimes.

## Louis Lucien and the Legislative Assembly
Louis Lucien (1813–1891), the son of Lucien Bonaparte, was born in Worcestershire, England, and spent his youth there. He did not enter France until 1848. Following the revolution of that year, he was elected as a deputy for Corsica on November 28, 1848. Although this election was later invalidated, he was returned as a deputy for the Seine in June 1849.

While serving in the Legislative Assembly, Louis Lucien sat on the right. Despite his position, he had no direct involvement in the *coup d'état* carried out by his cousin, Napoleon III, on December 2, 1851. Following this event, Napoleon III appointed him as a prince and senator. However, Louis Lucien remained largely inactive in politics during the Second Empire. After the Third Republic was proclaimed in 1870, he returned to England, where he focused on philology and published works regarding the Basque language, including *Grammaire basque* (1876), *Observations sur le basque Fontarabie* (1878), and *Remarques sur plusieurs assertions concernant la langue basque* (1876). He died without children on November 3, 1891.

## Other Members of the Assembly and National Bodies
Several other figures held prominent roles in the various assemblies and legislative bodies of the era:

*   **Athanase Laurent Charles Coquerel:** A French Protestant divine and eloquent speaker, Coquerel was elected to the National Assembly as a moderate republican after the February 1848 revolution. He later became a member of the Legislative Assembly. In this capacity, he supported the first ministry of Louis Napoleon and voted in favor of the restoration of the pope's temporal power and the expedition to Rome. Following the *coup d'état* of December 2, 1851, Coquerel ceased his political activities and confined himself to his duties as a pastor.
*   **Andre Marie Jean Jacques Dupin (Dupin the Elder):** An advocate and former pupil of the *Academie de Legislation*, Dupin served as the president of both the Legislative Assembly and the chamber of deputies. He was a member of the Liberal Opposition in 1815, opposing the election of Napoleon's son as emperor. In 1830, he exhorted citizens to resistance and later served as president of the chamber for eight years starting at the end of 1832. In 1848, following the abdication of Louis Philippe, he unsuccessfully proposed the young count of Paris as king with the duchess of Orleans as regent.
*   **Louis Joseph Buffet:** Elected as a deputy for the department of the Vosges after the February 1848 revolution, Buffet sat on the right in the Assembly. He supported Louis Napoleon Bonaparte and voted for the repression of the June 1848 insurrection. He served as minister of agriculture (August–December 1849 and August–October 1851) and later as finance minister in the cabinet of Emile Ollivier. He also served as president of the National Assembly from 1872 to 1875 and supported the *coup d'état* of May 16, 1877.

## Revolutionary Legislative Bodies and Coups
The archive records several other figures who navigated the volatile legislative landscape of the French Revolution and the rise of Bonaparte:

*   **Pierre Roger Ducos:** An advocate who served as a deputy to the Convention for the department of the Landes, Ducos was part of the "Plain," a party that leaned toward the stronger side. He voted for the death of Louis XVI and later presided over the Council of the Five Hundred. Following the parliamentary *coup d'état* of 30 Prairial (year VIII), he was named a member of the executive Directory. He accepted Bonaparte's *coup d'état* of 18 Brumaire and served as one of the three provisional consuls and vice-president of the senate.
*   **Dupuis:** A member of the Academy of Inscriptions and a former professor, Dupuis was chosen as a member of the National Convention by the department of Seine-et-Oise. He served as secretary to the Assembly in the third year of the republic and as a member of the Council of Five Hundred in the fourth. After Bonaparte's *coup d'état* of 18 Brumaire, he was elected to the Legislative Body, eventually becoming its president before abandoning politics.
*   **Hugues-Bernard Maret:** A statesman who published the debates of the first National Assembly in the *Bulletin de l'Assemblée* (later merged into the *Moniteur universel*), Maret was a member of the moderate Feuillants club. He served in the ministry of foreign affairs and as the ambassador of the French Republic at Naples. His diplomatic efforts were impacted by the *coup d'état* of Fructidor in September 1797.
