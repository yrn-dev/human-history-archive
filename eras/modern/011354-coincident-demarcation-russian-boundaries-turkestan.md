# Demarcation of Russian Boundaries in Turkestan and Northern Afghanistan

The demarcation of Russian boundaries in Turkestan was coincident with the establishment of the northern boundaries of Afghanistan. This process was part of a broader era of boundary-making in Asia beginning around 1880, during which Russia, China, Persia, Afghanistan, India, and Baluchistan revised their borders to define the politico-geographical limits of empire and government responsibilities.

## The Russo-Afghan Northern Boundary
The advance of Russia toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts necessitated a definite boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and Russia's trans-Caspian conquests. This boundary was determined in stages:

*   **North-West Sector:** The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884–1886 fixed the boundary between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). This line is described as almost entirely artificial. It leaves the Hari Rud at Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs. The most southerly point of this boundary occurs where it crosses the Kushk, roughly 60 miles north of Herat.
*   **The Oxus Line:** From Khamiab—situated about 150 miles above the Russian Trans-Caspian railway crossing at Charjui—the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia. This boundary continues along the river to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.
*   **North-East Sector:** Beyond Victoria Lake, an Anglo-Russian Commission in 1895 demarcated a line extending to the glaciers and snowfields that overlook the border of China.

## Badakshan and the Pamirs
Badakshan, a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan that includes Wakhan, adjoins Russian territory. An Anglo-Russian agreement in 1873 expressly acknowledged that Badakshan and its dependent district, Wakhan, fully belonged to the amir of Kabul, limiting the province to the southern or left bank of the Oxus.

The province's boundaries are defined as follows:
*   **West:** A line crossing the Turkestan plains from the junction of the Oxus and Kunduz rivers southwards to the eastern water-divide of the Tashkurghan river (the Koh-i-Chungar), then running south-east across the Sarkhab affluent of the Khanabad (Kunduz) until it reaches the Hindu Kush.
*   **South:** The boundary follows the crest of the Hindu Kush to the Khawak pass. While the area east of Khawak is occupied by Kafirs, the exact extent of their territory north of the main watershed is unascertainable. The boundary becomes definite again at the Dorah pass, which connects Ishkashim and Zebak at the bend of the Oxus with the Lutku valley leading to Chitral. From the Dorah eastwards, the crest of the Hindu Kush remains the boundary until it meets the Sarikol and Muztagh ranges.
*   **North-East:** The boundary skirts the head of the Tagdumbash Pamir and merges into the Pamir boundary, turning west to follow the Oxus to its junction with the Khanabad.

Because the northern boundary follows the Oxus under the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, and the southern boundary follows the crest, the province is only separated by the length of these slopes (approximately 8 to 10 miles). This creates a "bottle-shaped" arm of Afghan territory that is narrow at the neck and swells eastwards to include parts of the little and great Pamirs.

## Other Russian Boundaries in Asia
Beyond the Afghan frontier, the Russian boundary in Asia extends toward China and the Pacific:
*   **Chinese Turkestan:** The Sarikol range intervenes between the Russian Pamirs and Chinese Turkestan, though the actual dividing line remains indefinite.
*   **Siberian Boundary:** Beyond Kashgar, the southern boundary of Siberia follows an irregular course to the north-east, defined partly by the Alatau and Tian-shan mountains. It reaches a northerly point at approximately 53 deg. N. lat., marked by the Sayan range west of Irkutsk.
*   **Eastern Terminus:** The boundary deflects south-east to the Kerulen affluent of the Amur river (roughly 117 deg. 30' E. long. and 49 deg. 20' N. lat.), follows the Amur to its junction with the Usuri, and follows the Usuri to its head. It finally reaches the Pacific coast at the mouth of the Tumen river (about 42 deg. 30' N. lat.), 100 miles south of the Amur bay and the port of Vladivostok.

## Strategic Points and Infrastructure
The river Kushk forms part of the boundary between Russian and Afghan territory. The Kushkinski Post is a fourth-class Russian fortress located on a branch railway from Merv, with a terminus 12 miles to the south at Chahil Dukteran. This terminus is 66 miles from Herat and is served by the Orenburg-Tashkent and Transcaspian railways.
