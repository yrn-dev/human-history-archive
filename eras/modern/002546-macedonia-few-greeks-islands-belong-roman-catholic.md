# Roman Catholicism in Macedonia and the Greek Islands

## Religious Distribution in the Balkan Peninsula
The religious landscape of the Balkan Peninsula is characterized by a significant presence of the Orthodox Church, which is nominally headed by the oecumenical patriarch at Constantinople. While the Greek, Montenegrin, Servian, and Bulgarian churches are effectively autocephalous, they share a common dogma. In contrast, the Roman Catholic Church maintains a smaller presence across various regions of the peninsula.

Roman Catholic adherents include the Serbo-Croats of Croatia-Slavonia and Dalmatia, as well as some of the Gheg tribes in Albania and approximately 21% of the population of Bosnia. Within the kingdom of Bulgaria and Macedonia, the number of Roman Catholics is described as smaller still. Additionally, a few Greeks residing in the islands belong to the Roman Catholic Church.

## Catholicism and Uniate Communities in Macedonia
In Macedonia, the population is estimated at 2,200,000, with approximately 1,300,000 Christians. The vast majority of these Christians are Eastern Orthodox, owing allegiance to either the Bulgarian exarchate or the Greek patriarchate. 

The Catholic minority in Macedonia is composed primarily of Uniate Bulgarians, numbering about 3,600. These individuals are concentrated in the districts of Doiran and Kukush. The Uniate church is characterized by its acceptance of the dogma and authority of Rome while preserving its own distinct traditions.

## Catholic Presence in the Greek Islands
The presence of Roman Catholicism in the Greek islands is evidenced in specific administrative and ecclesiastical structures. In the town of Corfu, located on a peninsula with a citadel, the city serves as the seat for both a Roman Catholic archbishop and a Greek archbishop. 

## Administrative Classification under Turkish Rule
In the Turkish dominions, religious and political matters are closely linked. The Turkish government recognizes the heads of religious communities as the sole representatives of the Christian population. These leaders supervise Christian schools and hold seats on local administrative councils. 

The population is classified into *millets*, or nations, based on religion rather than nationality. While the *Rum* (Roman/Greek) *millet* includes those acknowledging the oecumenical patriarch (such as Greeks, Vlachs, Servians, and some Bulgarians), Catholics are categorized under their own specific *millet* known as the *Katolik*.
