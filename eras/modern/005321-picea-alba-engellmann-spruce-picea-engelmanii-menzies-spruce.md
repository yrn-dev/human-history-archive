# Spruce (Picea)

The spruce is a genus of coniferous trees characterized by their seed-bearing spikes, known as strobili or cones. These cones are elongated structures covered with scales, where the seeds are naked due to the absence of an ovary. Because of this fruit structure, spruces are classified within the Coniferae, or cone-bearers.

## Species and Distribution
Various species of spruce are found across different global regions:

*   **North America:** In certain Canadian provinces, the forests are rich in growth and include white spruce (*Picea alba*), Engellmann's spruce (*Picea Engelmanii*), and Menzies's spruce (*Picea sitchensis*). The western white spruce is noted for being employed for various purposes. Additionally, spruce forests are found inland on Mackinac Island in Michigan.
*   **Asia:** In the north-eastern Nan-shan highlands, forests of conifers including *Picea obovata* are common, appearing alongside deciduous trees such as birch, mountain ash, and Przhevalsky's poplar.
*   **Europe:** In the Balkans, conifers such as *Picea excelsa* are rare, though they are more abundant in the higher regions of the southern mountain group.

## Botanical Characteristics
Spruces are gymnosperms that may exhibit polycotyledonous embryos. This means that during development, they can possess more than two cotyledons arranged in a whorl; observations have recorded six, nine, twelve, and even fifteen cotyledons. These are linear in form and resemble the mode of development seen in the fasciculated leaves of the larch.

## Commercial and Ecological Roles
In regions where spruce is prevalent, such as parts of Canada, there is a steady demand for lumber in South America, Africa, Australia, China, and other Canadian areas. The timber industry in these provinces is supported by numerous sawmills. For example, records from 1904 and 1905 show significant timber cuts on both Dominion and provincial lands, with provincial cuts reaching 450,385,554 feet in 1905.

Ecologically, spruce forests provide habitats for various wildlife. In Canada, the "spruce partridge" is an abundant bird species, appearing in a western form in the province.

## Biological Interactions
Spruces serve as hosts for certain parasitic fungi. Specifically, the species *Chrysomyxa Rhododendri* produces aecidiospores on *Picea*.
