# BOLIVAR

## Geography and Administration
Bolivar is an inland state of Venezuela located south of the Apure and Orinoco rivers. Its boundaries are defined by the Caroni river, with the Yuruari territory to the east and Brazil and the Amazonas territory to the south. The state is characterized by great forests, numerous rivers, and extensive plains known as *llanos*. These plains are covered with nutritious grass during the rainy season, though this vegetation disappears entirely during the dry season.

The state comprises large areas of uninhabited territory, and its size and outlines have undergone various modifications due to frequent political changes in Venezuela. As of 1894, the population was recorded as 135,232, although the state's area had been largely reduced by that time.

## Capital and Commerce
The capital of the state is Ciudad Bolivar, formerly known as Angostura. It is situated on the right bank of the Orinoco river, approximately 240 meters above the river's mouth. The city had a population of 11,686. Because vessels of light draught can easily ascend the Orinoco to this point, Ciudad Bolivar serves as a center for considerable trade. Primary exports from the region include cotton, sugar, cocoa, hides, jerked beef, and various products derived from the forests.

## Simon Bolivar
The state shares its name with Simon Bolivar (1783–1830), the hero of South American independence. Born in Caracas, Venezuela, on July 24, 1783, to noble parents Juan Vicente Bolivar y Ponte and Maria Concepcion Palacios y Sojo, Bolivar was educated in Europe and resided in Madrid for several years. During his travels, he visited Paris and witnessed some of the final scenes of the Revolution. 

In 1801, Bolivar married the daughter of Don N. Toro and returned to Venezuela to manage his large estate. However, the death of his wife from yellow fever led him back to Europe. He returned home in 1809 via the United States, where he observed the functioning of free institutions. Shortly after his arrival in Venezuela, he joined the cause of independence.

## Revolutionary Career and the Republic of Colombia
Bolivar was a promoter of the April 1810 insurrection in Caracas and was commissioned as a colonel by the revolutionary junta. Following Venezuela's declaration of independence on July 5, 1811, Bolivar was entrusted with the command of Puerto Cabello. After initial setbacks and a period of flight to Curacao, he operated against Spanish troops on the Magdalena river in New Granada. He later entered Venezuela with 500 men, issuing a decree of "war to the death" and achieving a crushing victory over General Monteverde at Lastoguanes, entering Caracas in triumph on August 4, 1813.

Following a period of royalist recovery and further exile, Bolivar organized an expedition from Haiti, landing in Margarita and Barcelona in December 1816. After defeating royalist forces in February 1817, he established headquarters at Angostura. In 1819, he led his troops across the Cordilleras to join General Santander, winning the victory of Boyaca on August 7, 1819, which secured Bogota and New Granada.

On December 17, 1819, Bolivar enacted a fundamental law uniting the republics of New Granada and Venezuela into a single state called the Republic of Colombia, serving as its president. He later achieved a decisive victory over the Spaniards under General Torre at the battle of Carabobo, effectively ending the war in Venezuela. On August 30, 1821, the constitution of Colombia was adopted, with Bolivar as president and Santander as vice-president.

## Later Political Struggles
Bolivar's presidency was marked by internal instability. In Venezuela, Paez entered a state of open rebellion against the government, encouraged by parties seeking separation from the republic. In December 1826, Bolivar arrived at Puerto Cabello to stop the effusion of blood in Venezuela, issuing a general amnesty. 

In February 1827, Bolivar formally resigned the presidency to retire to his patrimonial estate and refute claims of ambition. However, Santander and the congress urged him to resume his functions to appease the country's agitations. Bolivar eventually agreed to resume his role, issuing decrees to establish constitutional order and convoking a national convention at Ocana.

## Honors
In 1854, Venezuela established the Order of the Bust of Bolivar. This non-European order consists of five classes and features a ribbon of red, blue, and yellow.
