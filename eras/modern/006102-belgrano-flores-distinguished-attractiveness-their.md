# Belgrano and Flores

Belgrano and Flores are suburban districts of Buenos Aires. They are specifically distinguished for the attractiveness of their gardens and country-houses.

## Integration into Buenos Aires
Historically, Belgrano and Flores existed as suburbs of the city. In 1887, these two areas were annexed to Buenos Aires. At the time of this annexation, the aggregate population of Belgrano and Flores was 28,000. This increment was added to a city population that was estimated at 404,000 prior to the annexation.

## Urban Context and Architecture
The districts are part of a larger urban environment in Buenos Aires that has experienced rapid growth, eventually becoming the largest city in South America. The city's architecture has evolved from a prevailing Spanish colonial style—characterized by one-storey buildings grouped around one or two patios with flat roofs and barred windows—to a more modern style influenced by foreign culture and the arrival of wealthy foreigners. 

In modern Buenos Aires, the French Renaissance style, which is lavishly decorated, has become the prevailing architectural trend. While the Avenida Alvear is noted for elegant private residences and the Avenida de Mayo for its ornamented business and public edifices, Belgrano and Flores maintain their distinction through their residential country-houses and gardens.

## Regional Geography
Within the province, Belgrano and Flores are categorized as suburbs of Buenos Aires. Other principal cities and towns in the province include the capital, La Plata, as well as Bahia Blanca, Chivilcoy, and Mar del Plata. Other notable locations include San Nicolas, a river port on the Paraná; Campana, the site of Argentina's first mutton-freezing factory established in 1883; and Barracas al Sud, which served as a center for jerked beef industries.

## Related Landmarks
The city of Buenos Aires contains various public spaces, including twelve notable plazas. Among these is a plaza named Belgrano. These plazas, which range from 4 to 12 acres, are characterized by their shade trees, walks, shrubbery, and flowers. These areas, along with the 840-acre park at Palermo (officially called 3 de Febrero) and the alamedas of Recoleta and Paseo de Julio, comprise a total of 960 acres of parks and plazas.
