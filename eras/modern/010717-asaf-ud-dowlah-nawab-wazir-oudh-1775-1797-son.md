# ASAF-UD-DOWLAH

Asaf-ud-dowlah served as the nawab wazir of Oudh from 1775 to 1797. He was the son of Shuja-ud-Dowlah, who was the third nawab of Oudh. His mother and grandmother were the begums of Oudh.

## Reign and Public Works
During his reign, Asaf-ud-dowlah was responsible for several significant architectural projects in Lucknow. In 1784, he built the Imambara to provide relief to people suffering from famine; the structure is noted as one of the largest rooms in the world, measuring 162 feet by 54 feet with an arched roof that requires no supports. Additionally, he constructed a country palace known as Bibiapur, located outside the city of Lucknow.

## Financial Affairs and the Begums of Oudh
The financial dealings of Asaf-ud-dowlah and the begums of Oudh were central to the charges brought against Warren Hastings. Upon the death of Shuja-ud-Dowlah, two million pounds sterling were left buried in the vaults of the zenana. The deceased prince's mother and widow claimed this treasure based on a will that was never produced.

Asaf-ud-dowlah faced pressure from Warren Hastings to pay debts owed to the Company. To address this, the nawab obtained a loan of 26 lakhs of rupees from his mother, in exchange for which he granted her a *jagir* valued at four times the loan amount. He later obtained an additional 30 lakhs of rupees. In return, the Company recognized her *jagirs* without interference for her lifetime and provided a full acquittal. These *jagirs* were eventually confiscated due to documentary evidence showing the begum's complicity in the rising of Chai Singh. Records suggest that Warren Hastings attempted to assist the nawab due to his own incapacity and showed leniency toward the begums.

## Regional Context and Administration
The court of Oudh was moved to Lucknow in 1775, an event that led leading bankers and merchants to abandon the city of Fyzabad. Fyzabad had been founded around 1730 by Sa'adat Ali Khan, the first nawab wazir of Oudh, and later fortified and expanded by the third nawab, Shuja-ud-daula, who was also buried there. Following the move to Lucknow, Fyzabad became the residence of the Begums of Oudh.

During the broader period of the nawabs of Oudh, the region saw significant territorial shifts. In 1774, the nawab of Oudh entered a treaty of alliance with the government of Calcutta to enforce claims against the Rohillas. With the assistance of a British brigade, the Rohillas were driven beyond the Ganges, and Bijnor was incorporated into the nawab's territories. Other regions, such as the district of Budaun and the territory of Bareilly, were also part of Oudh before being ceded to the East India Company in 1801. Additionally, the Hindu kingdom of Benares, which had been seized by the nawab wazir Safdar Jang, was ceded to the East India Company by the grandson of Safdar Jang via a treaty in 1775.
