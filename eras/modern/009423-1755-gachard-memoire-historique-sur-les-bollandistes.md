# Historical Memoirs and Biographical Works in the Archive

The Human History Archive contains a diverse array of historical memoirs, biographical studies, and critical editions spanning several centuries. These works range from eye-witness accounts of early modern warfare to 19th-century legal histories and geological memoirs.

## Military and Political Memoirs

A significant portion of the archive's records concerns military campaigns and the lives of political figures. François de Boivin, Baron de Villars (d. 1618), served as secretary to Charles, Marshal Brissac, and accompanied him to Piedmont in 1550. Boivin was involved in negotiations for the treaty of Cateau-Cambresis in April 1559 and authored *Memoires sur les guerres demelees tant dans le Piemont qu'au Montferrat et duche de Milan par Charles de Cosse, comte de Brissac* (Paris, 1607), which serves as an eye-witness testimony of the war.

Regarding the Napoleonic era and its aftermath, the archive holds extensive records on Marshal Grouchy. Grouchy authored several works, including *Observations sur la relation de la campagne de 1815 par le general de Gourgaud* (1818), *Refutation de quelques articles des memoires de M. le Duc de Rovigo* (1829), and *Fragments historiques relatifs a la campagne et a la bataille de Waterloo* (1829-1830). Other related works include *Le General Grouchy en Irlande* (1866) and *Le Marechal Grouchy du 16 au 18 juin, 1815* (1864).

The archive also documents the life of L.N.M. Carnot through various authorities, such as the works of Baron de B... (1816), Serieys (1816), Mandar (1818), W. Korte (1820), P.F. Tissot (1824), Arago (1850), Hippolyte Carnot (1863), C. Remond (1880), A. Picaud (1885 and 1887), A. Burdeau (1888), L. Hennet (1888), G. Hubbard (1888), M. Dreyfous (1888), and M. Bonnal (1888).

## Religious and Hagiographic Collections

The *Acta Sanctorum* is a major collection within the archive, existing in three editions:
*   The original edition (Antwerp, Tongerloo, and Brussels, 63 vols., 1643-1902).
*   The Venice edition, which concludes at volume v. of September (1734-1770).
*   The Paris edition, which concludes at volume xiii. of October (61 vols., 1863-1883).

Additional materials include a volume of tables edited by the abbe Rigollot and the work *Acta Sanctorum apologelicis libris ... vindicata* (Antwerp, 1755). Further scholarly analysis is provided by L.P. Gachard in *Memoire historique sur les Bollandistes* (Brussels, 1835), van Hecke in "De ratione operis Bollandiani," and Cardinal J.B. Pitra in *Etudes sur la collection des Actes des Saints* (Paris, 1880).

The archive also contains the works of Gregoire, who wrote on religious sects (*Histoire des sectes religieuses*, 1810), the liberties of the Gallican church (1818), the influence of Christianity on the condition of women (1821), and the marriage of priests in France (1826). Biographical accounts of Gregoire include *Gregoireana* (1821) and the *Memoires ... de Gregoire* (1837), the latter featuring a notice by H. Carnot.

## Legal and Scientific Memoirs

The archive preserves the contributions of Rodolphe Madeleine Cleophas Dareste de la Chavanne (born 1824), a French jurist who studied at the Ecole des Chartes and the Ecole de Droit. His legal histories include *Les Anciennes Lois de l'Islande* (1881) and *Memoire sur les anciens monuments du droit de la Hongrie* (1885). He also wrote extensively on Greek law, including works on the law of Gortyne (1885) and the science of law regarding Plato, Aristotle, and Theophraste (1893).

In the field of science, the archive contains the works of Jules Pierre François Stanislas Desnoyers (1800-1887). A geologist and archaeologist, Desnoyers was a founder of the Société Géologique de France in 1830 and later the librarian of the Museum of Natural History in Paris. His memoirs focus on the Tertiary, Cretaceous, and Jurassic strata of Northern France and the Paris Basin, as well as the antiquity of man and extinct mammalia.

## Royal and Imperial Histories

The history of the dauphin, Louis XVII, is documented through several perspectives. Simien Despréaux provided an official version in *Louis XVII* (1817), while M. Eckard wrote *Mémoires historiques sur Louis XVII* (1817). A. de Beauchesne's *Louis XVII., sa vie, son agonie, sa mort* (1852) provides original documents regarding the royal family's captivity at the Temple.

Additionally, the archive includes the works of Du Cange, who wrote *Historia Byzantina duplici commentario illustrato* (1680) and edited works by Byzantine historians John Cinnamus and John Zonaras. Du Cange also authored a *Memoire sur le projet d'un nouveau recueil des historiens de France*, which was later included in J. Lelong's *Bibliotheque historique de la France* (1768-1778).
