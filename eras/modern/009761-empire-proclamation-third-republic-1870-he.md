# The Proclamation of the Third Republic and Related Events of 1870

The year 1870 marked a period of significant political and ecclesiastical transition across Europe, characterized by the collapse of imperial structures in France and shifts in the relationship between state governments and the papacy.

## Political Transitions in France
The proclamation of the Third Republic in 1870 led to the displacement of figures associated with the Second Empire. Louis Lucien (1813–1891), the son of Lucien Bonaparte, had been named a prince and senator by Napoleon III. Although he had been elected as a deputy for Corsica in 1848 and later for the Seine in 1849, he played little role in politics during the Second Empire and did not participate in the *coup d'etat* of December 2, 1851. Following the proclamation of the Third Republic in 1870, Louis Lucien withdrew to England. He spent his remaining years focusing on philology, specifically publishing works on the Basque language, including *Grammaire basque* (1876) and *Observations sur le basque Fontarabie* (1878), until his death in 1891.

Other imperial supporters faced more volatile transitions. Bernard Adolphe Granier de Cassagnac (1806–1880), a journalist and deputy who had openly declared himself an imperialist in 1851, actively supported absolutist policies and the restoration of religion. Following the proclamation of the republic on September 4, 1870, Cassagnac fled to Belgium. He eventually returned to France for the elections of 1876, where he was elected deputy and continued to oppose republican reforms until his death in 1880.

## Ecclesiastical Developments and the Papacy
The year 1870 was also defined by the proclamation of papal infallibility. This event caused significant friction between the papacy and various national governments and clergy. In Austria, the government used the proclamation as a justification to declare that the concordat had lapsed, citing a fundamental change in the character of the papacy. This move was mirrored by the sentiment of nearly all Austrian prelates, who had opposed the new doctrine; many voted against it during the council and only submitted with great reluctance.

Other religious figures were similarly affected by the Vatican council of 1869–1870. John Machale (1791–1881), the Archbishop of Tuam and an Irish divine, attended the council. While Machale did not favor the dogma of Papal Infallibility, he submitted to it once it had been defined.

## Other Republican and Imperial Contexts
While the Third Republic was established in France, other regions experienced different republican movements. In a separate context, the abdication of Amadeus led to the proclamation of a federal republic. This state, which existed from February 11, 1873, to January 3, 1874, was driven by a republican minority including Castelar, Pi y Margall, Figueras, and Salmeron, despite a senate and congress largely composed of monarchists.
