# Geography and Boundaries of Asia

Asia is a vast landmass that can be visualized as a great isosceles spherical triangle. Its north-eastern apex is located at East Cape (Vostochnyi) in the Bering Strait. From this point, two equal sides of approximately 6,500 miles extend westward to the southern point of Arabia and eastward to the extremity of the Malay peninsula. The base of this triangle, spanning about 60 degrees of a great circle or 4,500 miles, is deeply indented by the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea on either side of the Indian peninsula.

## Northern and Eastern Boundaries
The northern boundary of Asia is formed by the Arctic Ocean. The coastline generally falls between 70 and 75 degrees North, placing it within the Arctic circle. The extreme northern point is Cape Sivero-Vostochnyi (also known as Chelyuskin) at 78 degrees North. 

The most easterly point of the continent is East Cape (Vostochnyi or Dezhnev), located at 190 degrees East at the entrance of the Bering Strait. From this point, the boundary follows the China Sea and the Northern Pacific coast toward the Malay Peninsula. This line is characterized by the recession of the Yellow Sea, the Sea of Okhotsk, and the Gulfs of Siam and Tongking, as well as the projections of the Korean and Kamchatka peninsulas.

## Southern and Western Boundaries
The southern coastline is highly irregular. The China Sea, the Bay of Bengal, and the Arabian Sea reach approximately to the northern tropic at the mouths of the Canton, Ganges, and Indus rivers. The Malay peninsula extends to within one and a half degrees of the equator, while the peninsulas of Cambodia, Hindostan, and Arabia descend to about 10 degrees North.

The westernmost point of Asia is Cape Baba (26 degrees East) on the Mediterranean shore, near the Dardanelles. From there, the boundary follows two paths:
*   One direction passes through the Mediterranean and down the Red Sea to the strait of Bab-el-Mandeb (45 degrees East) at the southern point of Arabia.
*   The other direction passes through the Black Sea and follows the Caucasus range (approximately 40 degrees North) to the Caspian. From the Caspian, the boundary turns north along the Ural Mountains, near the 60th meridian, until it meets the Arctic Ocean opposite the island of Novaya Zemlya.

## Hydrographic Basins and Interior Regions
Asia is divided into three primary hydrographic divisions:

*   **Northern Division:** This area covers approximately 6,394,500 square miles. It includes the Aralo-Caspian depression (1,759,000 sq. m.), the Mediterranean (268,500 sq. m.), and the Arctic river basins (4,367,000 sq. m.). The Ob is the largest Asiatic river basin, exceeding 1,000,000 square miles.
*   **Southern Division:** This region is nearly equal in extent to the north, totaling 6,514,000 square miles, divided between the Indian Ocean (2,873,000 sq. m.) and Pacific drainage (3,641,000 sq. m.). Notable basins include the Amur (776,000 sq. m.), the Yang-tsze-kiang (685,000 sq. m.), the Ganges (409,500 sq. m.), and the Indus (370,000 sq. m.).
*   **Interior Basins:** Covering about 3,141,500 square miles, these inland basins include the Tibetan plateau, the Gobi depression, the Syro-Arabian inland basin, the uplands of Iran (Perso-Afghan), and the basin of Asia Minor, as well as lacustrine regions south of the Arctic watershed.

## Regional Features and Fauna
In northern Russia, the Kola peninsula lies between the White Sea and the Arctic Ocean. Its Arctic coast, the Murman coast, is 260 miles long and remains ice-free year-round due to the North Atlantic drift. The peninsula's interior consists of a plateau and low mountains reaching over 3,000 feet, with rivers such as the Ponoi, Voronya, Tuloma, and Yovkyok (Yokanka) flowing into the Arctic.

The distribution of fish varies by region. Salmonidae are absent from southern Asia but are found in the northern Pacific and rivers flowing into the Arctic Ocean. In southern Asia, the waters are inhabited by siluroids, carp (Cyprinidae), and climbing fishes. The latter are peculiar to Africa and south-eastern Asia, possessing the ability to breathe outside of water to survive periods of drought. In the Arctic region, shore-fishes are characterized by Gadids, Zoarcids, Cyclopterids, and Cottids. The highest latitude at which freshwater fish (specifically char) have been obtained is 82 degrees North.

## Historical and Cartographic Perspectives
Early maps reflect evolving understandings of Asia's north. A map from 1522 depicted Tartaria bounded on the east by the Bautisis and Ochardes rivers, which were shown flowing into the Arctic Sea. This map also placed the "Ten Tribes" (Judei Clausi) along the northern coast, impinging upon the River Polisacus at 75 degrees latitude.

In prehistoric times, the region was shaped by different geography. The Aryan group of languages likely developed in a region extending east of the Ural mountains and north of the Caspian, involving the Volga, Don, Dnieper, and Danube rivers. At that time, the Danube flowed into a great sea that spanned from south-eastern Russia into Turkestan, potentially sending arms to the Arctic Ocean and acting as a barrier between Aryan speakers and the people of north-eastern Asia.
