# German Nationalist Movements and the Influence of Bismarck

The development of German nationalism in the 19th century was characterized by a shift from democratic aspirations toward a racial and state-centered identity, heavily influenced by the actions and leadership of Otto von Bismarck. This evolution manifested both within the borders of the German Empire and among German populations in neighboring territories, specifically Austria.

## Nationalist Movements in Austria
Beginning around 1870, a movement emerged among young Germans in Austria who prioritized the preservation and extension of their nationality over constitutionalism, which they dismissed as "legal mummies." This movement originated at the University of Vienna with the formation of a *Leseverein* (reading club) for German students, which served as a point of cohesion before eventually being suppressed.

The movement's political presence in parliament was represented by Herr von Schoenerer, who openly expressed the desire for union with the German Empire. These nationalists viewed Bismarck as their national hero and celebrated the anniversary of Sedan as their political festival, valuing approximation to Germany more than the maintenance of Austria. 

By 1881, various German opposition parties joined to form a club called the United Left. Their program emphasized the defense of the German position as a prerequisite for the state's existence and demanded the express recognition of German as the official language. However, internal friction developed between older constitutional leaders and younger, more ardent spirits who felt the leadership lacked decisiveness in self-defense. This tension peaked in 1885 when the government forbade festivities in Graz honoring Bismarck's eightieth birthday; the resulting indignation among Germans in Styria led to the breakup of the United Left.

## Bismarck and the Unification of Germany
Otto von Bismarck, a minister of the "seventeenth-century type," utilized diplomatic and military opportunities to establish Prussian ascendancy in Germany. In the 1860s, he championed German nationality in the duchies of Schleswig-Holstein during a conflict with King Christian IX of Denmark, persuading Austria to join Prussia in a military intervention. Following the defeat of Denmark, Bismarck provoked a "fratricidal war" between Germans by picking a quarrel with Austria over the possession of those two states to ensure the glory of Prussia and the Hohenzollern dynasty.

While romantic writers portray Bismarck as a statesman planning German unity, other accounts suggest he was simply imposing the Hohenzollern monarchy upon the existing reality of German unity that had been present since 1848. By 1870, during the war against Napoleon III of France, the German states—including those outside the North German Confederation—sided with Prussia, effectively welding the empire together.

## Bismarck's Administration and Key Associates
Bismarck maintained a circle of highly confident secretaries and agents who assisted in the legal and promotional framework of the empire. Lothar Bucher, a former democratic leader and exile, became Bismarck's secretary and a trusted confidant. Bucher drew up the text of the constitution for the North German Confederation and assisted Bismarck with the treaty of Frankfort, the congress of Berlin, and the composition of Bismarck's memoirs. Bucher also aligned himself with Bismarck's later colonial and commercial policies, criticizing the principles of the Cobden Club.

Julius Hermann Moritz Busch served as one of Bismarck's press agents from 1870 onward, accompanying the chancellor during the campaign of 1870-71. Busch later published several works on Bismarck, including *Bismarck und seine Leute* and *Unser Reichskanzler*, as well as the chancellor's 1890 petition to Emperor William II requesting to be relieved of office.

Additionally, Bernhard Ernst von Bülow, a former Danish official, became Bismarck's "most faithful henchman" after being persuaded to enter Prussian service in 1873 as secretary of state for foreign affairs.

## Political Opposition and the Fall of Bismarck
Not all German politicians adhered to Bismarck's later policies. Ludwig Bamberger, a leading authority on finance and economics in the Reichstag, initially accepted the results of Bismarck's work. However, after 1878, Bamberger broke with Bismarck over the introduction of state socialism, protectionism, and colonial development, describing the introduction of these policies as a *dies nefastus* for Germany.

Bismarck's era ended in 1890 with his dismissal by the young Emperor William II. The Emperor, who held a contempt for democracy and placed his trust in the army rather than parliamentary majorities, intended to be his own chancellor. Following Bismarck's departure, the safeguards he had maintained were dispensed with. The Reinsurance Treaty with Russia was dropped, and a naval rivalry with Britain began. By 1892, the policy Bismarck had worked to prevent was realized with the signing of the Dual Alliance between Russia and France, shifting Europe from a period of German predominance to a balance of power between two vast military combinations.
