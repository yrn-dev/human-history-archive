# Pulacayo Branch and the Antofagasta and Oruro Railway

The Pulacayo branch and the Antofagasta and Oruro railway are key components of the early railway infrastructure in Bolivia, primarily serving the industrial and mining interests of the Potosi department.

## The Antofagasta and Oruro Railway
Up until 1903, the Antofagasta and Oruro line was one of the only railways operating in Bolivia. The line possesses a total length of 574 miles, with 350 miles of that distance located within Bolivian territory. In addition to its rail operations, the Antofagasta and Oruro railway owns telegraph lines, some of which are situated within Chilean territory.

## The Pulacayo Branch and Mining
The Pulacayo branch is a private line extending 26 miles from the main Antofagasta and Oruro railway to the Pulacayo mines. This branch serves the Huanchaca mining centre of Pulacayo, a prominent town with a population of 6,512. The town is centered around a great silver mining company that employs 3,200 men in its surface works and mines.

## Uyuni Junction
The town of Uyuni, with a population of 1,587, serves as the critical junction where the Pulacayo branch meets the Antofagasta and Oruro railway. Located in the department of Potosi, Uyuni also functions as a converging point for several projected railways and important highways. Future railway projections from 1907 included lines extending from Uyuni to both Sucre and Potosi, as well as a line from Uyuni to Tupiza.

## Regional Context and Communications
The railway systems in Bolivia were developed to bring the isolated region into more effective communication with the outside world. In the department of Potosi, other significant centers include Tupiza, a mining and commercial center near the Argentine frontier and the terminus for the Argentine railway extension. 

The broader railway network of Bolivia was limited in the early 20th century. By 1903, the total railway mileage consisted of the Antofagasta and Oruro line, the Pulacayo branch, and a 54-mile line from Guaqui to Alto de La Paz, totaling only 430 miles. This limited infrastructure was further impacted by the war with Chile from 1878 to 1881, which resulted in the 282 miles of railways in the Litoral department passing under Chilean control.
