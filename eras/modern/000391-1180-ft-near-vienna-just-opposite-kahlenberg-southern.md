# Lower Austria

Lower Austria (known in German as *Niederoesterreich* or *Oesterreich unter der Enns*, meaning "Austria below the river Enns") is an Austrian crownland and archduchy. It covers an area of 7,654 square miles and is bordered by Styria to the south, Bohemia and Upper Austria to the west, Bohemia and Moravia to the north, and Hungary to the east. The Danube river divides the province into two parts, entering at the most westerly point and exiting at the eastern extremity near Pressburg.

## Geography and Topography

The province is characterized by distinct geographical divisions. North of the Danube lies the *Waldviertel*, a low hilly region that serves as a continuation of the Moravian and Bohemian plateau. Within this area, the Weinsberger Wald reaches a peak altitude of 3,478 feet at the Peilstein. The terrain descends toward the Danube valley through the Manhartsgebirge (1,758 feet) and the Gfoehler Wald (2,368 feet). The Bisamberg, reaching 1,180 feet, represents the most south-easterly offshoot of this region and is located near Vienna, opposite the Kahlenberg.

The southern division of Lower Austria is primarily hilly and mountainous, dominated by the Lower Austrian Alps and their offshoots. The highest summit in the entire province is the Schneeberg at 6,806 feet. Other principal groups include the Raxalpe (6,589 feet), the Oetscher (6,205 feet), the Duerrenstein (6,156 feet), and the Voralpe (5,802 feet). East of the Semmering ridge are the Leithagebirge (1,674 feet) and the Wechsel (5,700 feet). The Wiener Wald forms an offshoot of the Alpine group, reaching 2,929 feet at the Schoepfl and ending northwest of Vienna at the Leopoldsberg (1,380 feet) and the Kahlenberg (1,404 feet).

## Notable Towns and Locations

### Klosterneuburg
Located 5-½ miles northwest of Vienna by rail, Klosterneuburg is a town situated on the right bank of the Danube at the foot of the Kahlenberg. As of 1900, its population was 11,595. A small stream divides the town into lower and upper sections. It serves as an important pioneer station and contains various stores and military buildings. Educational facilities in the town include an academy of fruit and wine cultivation.

### Baden
Baden is a watering-place and town located 17 miles south of Vienna by rail, with a population of 12,447 in 1900. It is situated on the banks of the Schwechat at the mouth of the Helenenthal. The town is a primary summer resort for Vienna's inhabitants and has been used as a summer residence by members of the Austrian imperial family. 

Baden is known for thirteen warm baths containing sulphate of lime, with temperatures ranging from 72°F to 97°F. These baths mostly rise at the foot of the Calvarienberg (1,070 feet), which consists of dolomitic limestone. The town's architecture includes a town-hall with archives, a late Gothic parish church, and a new *Kurhaus*. Nearby landmarks include the ruins of the 12th-century castles Rauheneck and Rauhenstein, and the Chateau Weilburg, built between 1820 and 1825 by Archduke Charles. Other nearby sites include the 11th-century Romanesque abbey of Heiligenkreuz and Mayerling, a hunting lodge where crown prince Rudolph of Austria was found dead in 1889. The highest local peak is the Hoher Lindkogel (2,825 feet), also known as the Eiserne Thor.

## Geology

The northern Alpine region and the Carpathians feature a series of clastic beds formed while foraminiferal limestones were forming over most of southern Europe. These include the Vienna sandstone and the Flysch. Some portions of the Alpine Eocene are sandy or argillaceous and contain enormous boulders of non-local rocks, leading to suggestions of a glacial origin, though evidence remains inconclusive. 

C.W. von Gumbel classified the Eocene of the northern Alps into the Lower (Burberg Beds and Greensands), Middle (Kressenberg Beds), and Upper (Flysch, Vienna sandstone, and the Haring group) periods. In the southern and south-eastern Alps, the Upper Eocene is recognized as the Vienna Sandstone, Tassello, or Macigno, consisting of shales, marls, and conglomerates.

## Political History

During a period of national movements, the government of Austria exhibited a yielding spirit toward various racial and national claims. While the Jacobinism of the Vienna democracy was not representative of widespread opinion in the German parts of Austria, its "loud-voiced Germanism" caused opposition among other races. In Bohemia, a separate constitution was proclaimed on April 8th following the failure of elections for the Frankfort parliament. In Croatia, the diet of Agram confirmed Baron Joseph Jellachich as ban on March 23rd as a concession to southern Slavs. Additionally, Count Stadion proclaimed a new constitution for Galicia on March 18th.
