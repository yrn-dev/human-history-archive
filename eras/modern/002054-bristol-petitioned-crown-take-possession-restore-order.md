# Petition for Crown Possession of the Bahamas

## Restoration of Order in New Providence
During a period when conditions had become intolerable, the merchants of Bristol and London submitted a petition to the crown requesting that it take possession of the region to restore order. In response, Captain Woodes Rogers was dispatched as the first crown governor, arriving at New Providence in 1718. Following his arrival, families of good character began to settle in the Bahamas. While some progress was achieved in developing the colony's resources, this growth was hindered by the tyrannical behavior of several governors who succeeded Captain Woodes Rogers.

## Agricultural Development and Challenges
Agricultural efforts in the colony included the introduction of the pine-apple for cultivation at Eleuthera. During the American war of independence, a significant number of colonists arrived, bringing wealth and slave labour. This led to attempts to cultivate cotton on a large scale. By 1783, at Long Island, nearly 4,000 acres of land were under cultivation with 800 slaves at work. However, these efforts were undermined by the red bug, which destroyed cotton crops in 1788 and again in 1794. Consequently, cotton cultivation was almost entirely abandoned by the year 1800.

## Military Conflicts and Shifts in Possession
The progress of the colony was further retarded by various military occupations. In 1776, Commodore Hopkins of the American navy captured the island of New Providence, though he later abandoned it after deeming it untenable. In 1781, the Spanish governor of Cuba retook the island. The Spaniards maintained nominal possession of the Bahamas until 1783. However, before the notification of peace, New Providence was recaptured in June 1783 by Lieutenant-Colonel Deveaux, a loyalist of the South Carolina militia.
