## Maritime Law and General Average
In the context of maritime regulations, the abbreviation "G.A." is used to refer to General Average. The archive outlines several rules regarding this:
* **Temporary Repairs:** Rule XIV states that no "new for old" deductions are made from the cost of temporary repairs for damage allowable as G.A.
* **Loss of Freight:** Rule XV specifies that loss of freight resulting from the loss of or damage to cargo is made good as G.A., whether caused by a G.A. act or when the cargo damage is made good.
* **Sacrificed Cargo:** Rule XVI dictates that the amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed goods is based on the loss sustained by the owner, using market values at the termination of the adventure or the vessel's arrival.
* **Contributory Values:** Rule XVII states that contributions to G.A. are based on actual property values at the termination of the adventure, adding amounts made good as G.A. for sacrificed property. Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight, such as crew's wages and port charges that would not have been incurred had the cargo and ship been totally lost. Personal effects and passengers' luggage not shipped under bill of lading do not contribute to G.A.
