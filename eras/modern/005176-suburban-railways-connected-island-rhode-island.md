# Bristol, Rhode Island

## Geography and Demographics
Bristol is a shire-township located in Bristol county, Rhode Island, U.S.A. It is situated on a peninsula between Mount Hope Bay to the east and Narragansett Bay to the west, approximately 15 miles south-south-east of Providence. The township covers an area of 12 square miles and includes a small island known as Hog Island. 

The topography of the township is generally gently undulating and well-suited for agriculture, with a particular emphasis on the growing of onions. On the eastern side, Mount Hope reaches an elevation of 216 feet and provides views of the river scenery, the bay, and the landscape.

Population records indicate a steady increase in the early 20th century:
* 1900: 6,901 residents (including 1,923 foreign-born)
* 1905 (state census): 7,512 residents
* 1910: 8,565 residents

## Transportation and Connectivity
Bristol is served by the Rhode Island Suburban railways as well as the New York, New Haven & Hartford. Additionally, the township is connected to the island of Rhode Island via ferry.

## Economy and Industry
The principal village of Bristol serves as a port of entry and possesses a deep and capacious harbour. The local economy includes manufactories producing woollen goods and rubber. The village is also recognized as a center for yacht-building; specifically, the Herreshoff yards have constructed several defenders of the America's Cup, including the "Reliance" and the "Columbia."

## History
The land that now comprises most of the township of Bristol, including Mount Hope Neck—the former seat of the vanquished sachem—was awarded to the Plymouth Colony at the conclusion of King Philip's War in 1676. In 1680, after Plymouth conveyed the "Neck" to a company of four, the village was laid out. The following year, the village and township were named Bristol, after the town in England, in anticipation of future commercial importance.

The administrative history of the township is as follows:
* 1685: Became the shire-township.
* 1692: Passed under the jurisdiction of Massachusetts.
* 1747: Annexed to Rhode Island.

During the War of Independence, the village experienced two British attacks. On October 7, 1775, it was bombarded by the British, though it suffered little damage. Later, on May 25, 1778, a British force visited the village and partially destroyed it.
