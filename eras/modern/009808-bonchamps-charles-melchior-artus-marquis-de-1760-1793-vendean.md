# BONCHAMPS, CHARLES MELCHIOR ARTUS, MARQUIS DE (c. 1760-1793)

Charles Melchior Artus, Marquis de Bonchamps, was a Vendean leader born in Jouverteil, Anjou, around 1760. He is remembered for his military leadership during the French Revolution and his final act of clemency.

## Early Life and Military Career
Bonchamps acquired his initial military experience during the American War of Independence. Following his return to France, he served in the French army, where he attained the rank of captain of grenadiers. 

## Role in the French Revolution
A staunch supporter of the monarchy, Bonchamps resigned his military command at the onset of the French Revolution. Following his resignation, he retired to his chateau located at St Florent. 

In the spring of 1793, the insurgents of the Vendee chose him as their leader. The success of the peasants' military efforts is attributed in great measure to his counsels. During his leadership, he was present at the capture of Fontenay, Thouars, and Bressuire. It was at Fontenay that he sustained a wound.

## Defeat and Death
Despite early successes, the insurgent forces were weakened by dissensions among their leaders. This internal conflict contributed to a severe defeat for the Vendeans at the bloody battle of Cholet in October 1793. During this engagement, Bonchamps was mortally wounded and died the following day.

According to accounts, his final act was to pardon five thousand republican prisoners. This act of mercy occurred after his own troops had sworn to kill the prisoners in revenge for his death.

## Legacy
A statue of the Marquis de Bonchamps, created by David d'Angers, is located in the church of St Florent.
