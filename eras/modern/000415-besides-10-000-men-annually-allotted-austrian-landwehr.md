# Military System of the Austro-Hungarian Monarchy

The military system of the Austro-Hungarian monarchy, established in its current form since 1868, is based on the principle of personal and universal obligation for citizens to bear arms. This system is shared by both states within the monarchy.

## Organization and Administration
The military force is divided into several distinct components:
*   **The Common Army (_K. und K._):** This force is under the administration of the joint minister of war.
*   **Special Armies:** These are separate national institutions consisting of the Austrian _Landwehr_ (retaining the old designation _K.K._) and the Hungarian _Honveds_. These forces are administered by their respective ministries of national defence.
*   **The _Landsturm_:** A levy-in-mass composed of men of military age up to 42. This group includes many individuals who might be classified as _Landwehr_ in other systems.

The monarchy is divided into 16 military territorial districts for administrative and military service. Fifteen of these correspond to the 15 army corps, with an additional military district located at Zara. There are also 108 supplementary districts, with 105 serving the army and 3 serving the navy.

## Recruitment and Service
The annual contingent of recruits is determined by the Austrian and Hungarian parliaments through military bills, generally based on population figures from the most recent census. In 1905, the total recruit contingent was 103,100 men, with Austria providing 59,211 and Hungary providing 43,889. Additionally, 10,000 men are allotted annually to the Austrian _Landwehr_ and 12,500 to the Hungarian _Honveds_.

The term of service varies by branch:
*   **Active service with the colours:** 2 years (3 years for the cavalry).
*   **Reserve:** 7 or 8 years.
*   **_Landwehr_:** 2 years.
Men not drafted into the active army spend an equivalent total period of service in various special reserves.

## Force Strength and Structure
As of 1905, the peace strength of the active army was approximately 350,000 officers and men. This figure includes the two _Landwehrs_, the Hungarian crown guards, the Austrian "K.K." guards, and the gendarmerie. 

The specific peace-footing strength in 1905 was recorded as 20,564 officers and 336,818 men, utilizing 64,887 horses and 1,048 guns. Of this total, the Common Army comprised 15,863 officers and 285,733 men; the Austrian _Landwehr_ had 2,053 officers and 25,766 men; and the Hungarian _Honveds_ consisted of 2,648 officers and 25,319 men. Troops stationed in Bosnia and Herzegovina (6,372 men and 376 officers) were included in the Common Army totals.

In terms of organization (as of 1902), the army consisted of 31 infantry divisions and 5 cavalry divisions formed into 15 army corps. The typical corps strength includes one cavalry brigade (18 squadrons), one artillery brigade (128-144 field-guns or 16-18 batteries), and two infantry divisions (comprising 8 or 9 regiments, 32 or 36 battalions, and 4 brigades). The infantry is further organized into Tirolese, Jaeger, and line regiments. Cavalry is divided into hussars, Uhlans, lancers, and dragoons.

The Austrian _Landwehr_ is organized into 8 divisions of varying strength, while the Hungarian _Honveds_ are organized into 7 divisions. Both national forces include artillery and cavalry (hussars and Uhlans). In wartime, it is probable that a _Honveds_ or _Landwehr_ division would join each army corps, with the exception of the Vienna corps, which maintains 3 divisions during peace.

Estimated war strength for the first line army includes 460,000 infantry, 78,000 artillery, 49,000 cavalry, and 21,000 engineers. The _Honved_ and _Landwehr_ would provide 18,000 cavalry and 219,000 infantry, while other reserves would provide 223,000 men. This brings the approximate total strength to 1,147,000, excluding the _Landsturm_.

## Fortifications
The monarchy maintains several principal fortifications:
*   **Galicia:** Przemysl and Cracow.
*   **Hungary:** Temesvar, O-Arad, Petervarad, and Komarom (the inland fortifications centre).
*   **Bosnia-Herzegovina:** Bilek, Mostar, and Serajewo.
*   **Alpine Frontiers:** Numerous works centered at Franzensfeste and Trent, particularly in Tirol. Defensive works are also located on military roads into Carinthia, such as Predil Pass and Malborgeth.
*   **Adriatic Coast:** The naval harbour of Pola is strongly fortified; other fortified locations include Trieste and Dalmatian sites such as Cattaro and Zara.
*   **Capitals:** Neither Budapest nor Vienna are fortified.

## Post-War Reductions
Following later terms (June 2), the Austrian army was reduced to a maximum of 30,000 men, including depot troops and officers. This force was transitioned to a purely voluntary basis, and universal military service was abolished.
