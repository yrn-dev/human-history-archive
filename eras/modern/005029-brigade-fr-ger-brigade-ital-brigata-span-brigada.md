# BRIGADE

A brigade is a unit of military organization composed of two or more regiments of artillery, cavalry, or infantry. The English language began using the term in the early 17th century, deriving it from the French and German *brigade*, the Italian *brigata*, and the Spanish *brigada*.

## Command and Staff Structure
Depending on the type of unit, a brigade is commanded by a colonel, a brigadier-general, or a major-general. The staff of a cavalry or infantry brigade typically includes the commanding brigadier, an aide-de-camp, and a brigade-major. The brigade-major serves as a staff officer with duties that fall between those of a general staff officer and an adjutant.

## British Army Organization
In Great Britain, the composition of a brigade varies by its branch:

*   **Infantry Brigades:** These generally consist of four battalions, totaling approximately 4,000 bayonets. They include attached medical, transport, and supply units.
*   **Cavalry Brigades:** These are composed of two or three cavalry regiments.
*   **Artillery Brigades:** Whether heavy, horse, or field, an artillery brigade in Great Britain is a smaller unit commanded by a lieutenant-colonel and consisting of two or three batteries.

## Specialized Naval and Marine Brigades
Proposals for the use of surplus naval reservists have included the creation of permanent cadres consisting of two naval brigades and one marine brigade.

The marine brigade is organized into four battalions. With the addition of reservists to the active service men (1,880), the total strength reaches 3,900. This is structured as four battalions, each containing four double companies of approximately 250 men. A colonel commands the marine brigade, while lieutenant-colonels command the battalions, and majors and captains command the companies.

The two naval brigades are designed to consist of four battalions each, with a target of 880 men per battalion. These are organized into sixteen double companies of 220 men. The composition of each battalion includes 375 R.N.V.R., 313 R.F.R. (picked and under 30 years of age), and 190 R.N.R. Total requirements for the two naval brigades are 3,000 R.N.V.R., 2,500 R.F.R., and 1,500 R.N.R. A Captain of the R.N. commands each naval brigade. Battalion command is split between R.N.V.R. Commanders and Commanders or naval officers promoted to that rank. Companies are commanded by R.N.V.R. or R.N. Lieutenant-Commanders, or by R.M. Majors if the former are unavailable.

## Historical Operations
Brigades have been utilized across various global conflicts and campaigns:

**The Crimean and Indian Wars**
During the Crimean War, a marine infantry brigade occupied the heights of Balaclava. During the Indian Mutiny, infantry and artillery marines served with the Naval Brigade under Peel.

**Colonial and Regional Campaigns**
Marine force brigades and batteries were prominent in the China wars and all campaigns in the Sudan and Egypt between 1881 and 1898. In the Boer War of 1899, marine infantry and artillery served with the Naval Brigade, suffering exceptionally severe losses at the battle of Enslin.

**The Great War**
Canadian brigades were heavily engaged in battles involving gas attacks and heavy artillery fire. The 3rd Brigade, under General Turner, and the 2nd Brigade, under General Currie, held the Canadian front. The 3rd Brigade suffered gas attacks on two separate occasions, eventually forcing a fallback to the south-west of St. Julien. During these engagements, the 8th Battalion (90th Winnipeg Rifles) of the 2nd Brigade held the point of the Salient against great odds.

Other operations involved the 2nd Division's 5th Brigade, which captured a footing on Mont St. Quentin after advancing with the bayonet, and the 6th Brigade, which completed that capture. Additionally, the 14th Brigade of the 5th Division conducted a ten-hour, seven-mile march to deploy for an assault on Peronne.
