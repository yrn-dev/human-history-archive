# Socialist Demonstrations and Franchise Reform in Austria (1905-1906)

## The Budapest Demonstration of 1905
On September 15, 1905, a large-scale demonstration involving the working class and socialists took place before the parliament at Budapest. The primary objective of this manifestation was to advocate for universal suffrage. This event served as a catalyst for the Austrian Socialist party, which was influenced by the revolutionary movement occurring in Russia and encouraged by the Budapest demonstration to seek similar franchise reforms within Austria.

## Civil Unrest and Political Shift
Following the events in Budapest, the movement for reform spread to other Austrian cities. At the beginning of November, an initial demonstration was organized in Vienna, which resulted in some bloodshed. Frequent collisions with police and further demonstrations were reported in Graz, Prague, and other towns.

Initially, the premier, Baron Gautsch, had discountenanced the idea of universal suffrage, though he had admitted that a restricted reform was desirable. However, the escalating unrest led to a change in his attitude. On November 28, Gautsch permitted a massive Socialist demonstration in support of universal suffrage to be held on the Vienna Ringstrasse. This orderly procession consisted of workmen marching silently, ten abreast, and lasted for five hours, during which time traffic was suspended. The event left a deep impression on public opinion.

## The Gautsch Reform Measures
On the day of the Ringstrasse demonstration, Premier Gautsch promised to introduce a significant measure of franchise reform by February. He specified that the reform would be framed to ensure that racial minorities were protected from being overwhelmed by majorities of other races at the polls.

On February 23, 1906, Gautsch introduced a series of franchise reform measures based on two main principles:
1. The establishment of the franchise on the basis of universal suffrage and the abolition of the electoral class system, known as the *curia*.
2. The electoral division of Austria into racial compartments to ensure each race was protected against molestation from other races.

The proposed redistribution bill sought to increase the number of constituencies from 425 to 455. These constituencies were to be allotted to each province, and within those provinces, distributed to each race based on their numbers and their tax-paying capacity. The reform bill specifically proposed to grant the franchise to every male citizen over the age of 24 who met a one-year residential qualification.

## Context of Austrian Socialism
The rise of socialism as a political force in Austria had previously been hindered by limited suffrage and national divisions, which prevented the creation of a centralized party. While mass demonstrations and petitions for universal suffrage had occurred as early as 1867, the movement's growth was interrupted after 1879 by the introduction of anarchical doctrines. 

During the 1880s, the anarchist movement, led by Peukert and influenced by Most's paper *Freiheit*, grew in size. This period was marked by serious strikes, collisions with police, and assassinations, with some Austrian anarchists combining robbery with murder. These actions led the government to implement severe repressive measures, resulting in the flight or expulsion of leading anarchists. 

The socialist party began to revive in 1887 under the leadership of Dr. Adler after the "party of violence" had died away. From that point, the party steadily increased its numbers, placing the demand for universal suffrage at the forefront of its political programme. This movement is further reflected in the general celebration of the 1st of May as the festival of Labour.
