# Mecklenburg

## Hydrography and Water Circulation
In the study of water circulation within the Baltic proper, the coast of Mecklenburg and Ruegen, along with the islands of Moen and Falster, form a plateau that serves as a dividing line from the channels connecting to the North Sea. The Baltic contains great basins and hollows extending from Ruegen to the Gulfs of Finland and Bothnia. In these areas, the upper layers of water—ranging from 30 to 70 metres (16 to 38 fathoms) in thickness—maintain nearly the same salinity throughout, with vertical circulation driven by convection currents.

Beneath these upper layers lie masses of salter water. These deeper strata are characterized as being practically stagnant, surcharged with carbonic acid, and deficient in oxygen. A thermal wave of small amplitude propagates slowly to the bottom of these strata through conduction. These salter waters originated from outside sources and passed over the plateau between Mecklenburg and Falster. However, the horizontal extension of these waters is checked by the ridges that separate the various deep hollows of the Baltic from one another. The inflow into these deep basins is intermittent and likely follows a long period of reflux and flux.

## Political History and Honors
Mecklenburg is associated with the grand duchies of Mecklenburg-Strelitz and Mecklenburg-Schwerin. These two entities jointly possess the Order of the Wendish Crown, which was founded in 1864 by Grand Duke Frederick William of Strelitz and Grand Duke Frederick Francis II. of Schwerin. The order consists of four classes, including two divisions of the grand cross—which may be granted to ladies—and an affiliated cross of merit. The badge of this order features a white cross with a blue centre depicting the Wendish crown. The motto associated with the badge differs by duchy: knights of Schwerin use *Per aspera ad astra*, while knights of Strelitz use *Avito viret honore*.

Additionally, the Order of the Griffin was founded in 1884 by Frederick Francis III. of Schwerin. This order became common to both duchies in 1904.

## Regional Geography and Infrastructure
The region's geography includes connections to other significant German locations. For example, the city of Lübeck, the capital of the free state and former head of the Hanseatic League, is located 40 miles by rail northeast of Hamburg. Lübeck is situated on a gentle ridge between the rivers Wakenitz and Trave. It is connected by rail lines to Büchen, Eutin, Travemünde, and Strassburg, the latter of which is located in Mecklenburg-Schwerin.
