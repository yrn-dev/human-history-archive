# Bibliotheca Hagiographica Orientalis and Related Works of 1899

The *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis* is one of several indispensable inventories of published hagiographic texts prepared by the Bollandists. These works were designed to provide a strictly scientific stamp and a more solid basis for the continuation and remoulding of the *Acta Sanctorum*.

## The Bollandists and the Hagiographic Inventories
Beginning in 1882, the Bollandists shifted their activities to align more closely with the progress of historical methods. This effort included the establishment of a quarterly review, *Analecta Bollandiana*, founded by the Jesuit fathers J. de Backer, G. van Hooff, and C. de Smedt. By 1906, this review had reached its 25th volume and was edited by P. Peeters, A. Porcelet, H. Delehaye, F. van Ontroy, and de Smedt. The review served as a venue for dissertations, inedited texts, and studies preparing for the *Acta Sanctorum*. Since 1892, it also included the *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques*, which provided criticisms of recent works regarding hagiographic questions.

Alongside the review, the Bollandists analyzed hagiographic manuscripts in major libraries. This resulted in the publication of various library catalogues in the *Analecta* (including those of Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres) and separate volumes dedicated to:
* Latin manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (1886–1889).
* Latin and Greek manuscripts in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (1889–1896).
* Greek manuscripts in the Vatican (1899).
* Latin manuscripts in the libraries of Rome (beginning in 1905).

As part of their effort to inventory published hagiographic texts, the Bollandists produced three primary bibliographies: the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*. While these works delayed the publication of the principal collection, they were considered essential for the scientific rigor of the project.

## The Bibliotheca Hagiographica Latina (1899)
Published in Brussels in 1899, the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* serves as a critical reference for identifying hagiographic texts. It is cited in various historical and biographical studies to locate specific entries and records. For example, it is used to reference the biographies of St. Kilian, the British missionary bishop and apostle of eastern Franconia, noting that his first biography dates to the 9th century (Nos. 4660–4663). It is also utilized in the study of St. Margaret (Sancta Margarita), a virgin and martyr from Antioch, specifically in entries n. 5303–5313.

## The Bibliotheca Orientalis of Joseph Simon Assemannus
Distinct from the hagiographic inventories of the Bollandists is the *Bibliotheca Orientalis Clementino-Vaticana*, a work by Joseph Simon Assemannus. Assemannus was a Maronite of Mount Lebanon born in 1687 who was educated at the Maronite college in Rome and later served as the librarian of the Vatican library and titular archbishop of Tyre.

In 1715, Pope Clement XI dispatched Assemannus to the monasteries of Syria and Egypt to search for manuscripts. He returned from this first expedition with approximately 150 choice manuscripts. A second expedition in 1735 yielded an even more valuable collection. 

The *Bibliotheca Orientalis Clementino-Vaticana* (published in Rome between 1719 and 1728) was intended to be a four-part project:
1. Syrian and allied manuscripts (including Jacobite, Nestorian, and orthodox).
2. Arabian manuscripts (both Mahommedan and Christian).
3. Turkish, Persian, Aethiopic, Coptic, and other manuscripts.
4. Syrian and Arabian manuscripts that were not distinctively theological.

Although extensive preparations were made for the later sections, only the first part was completed. The work consists of 9 folio volumes, though some sources refer to four folio volumes published between 1719 and 1728. This work is noted for its research on the Nestorians—including a folio volume of 950 pages—and contains information on figures such as Josua Stylites.
