# Railway Communications and Infrastructure

## The Great Western and Great Central Systems
In certain regions, railway infrastructure is managed through joint ownership and strategic branching. The Great Central and Great Western companies jointly own a line that passes through Prince's Risborough, High Wycombe, and Beaconsfield. This specific line is connected to the Great Central system in a northward direction. 

Prior to the opening of this joint line in 1906, the only railway line serving Prince's Risborough and High Wycombe was the Great Western branch running from Maidenhead to Oxford. From Prince's Risborough, there are further branches extending to Aylesbury and Watlington. Additionally, the main line of the Great Western company crosses the extreme south of the county via Taplow and Slough.

## The London & North-Western Railway and Associated Lines
The London & North-Western railway maintains a main line that crosses the north-east part of the county. A significant junction on this system is located at Bletchley, where several branches diverge:
*   **Eastward:** Branches lead to Cambridge, Bedford, and Fenny Stratford.
*   **Westward:** Branches lead to Banbury and Oxford, with the western branch providing service to Buckingham.

Other regional connections include a branch from Cheddington to Aylesbury. Furthermore, the North-Western Oxford branch joins the Metropolitan-Great Central joint line at Verney Junction. This joint line serves Aylesbury, Chesham (via a branch), and Amersham. The Great Central railway utilizes this line, while its own main line continues in a north-westward direction from Quainton Road. A light railway also connects Quainton Road to the large village of Brill, situated to the south-west.

## Other Regional Railway Networks
Various other railway systems provide essential communications across different territories:

**South-Western and Southern Systems:**
In some areas, the Great Western railway serves as the primary provider of communications, with a main line terminating at Penzance. Branch lines from this system serve Helston, Falmouth, Penryn, and Fowey in the south, as well as Newquay, St Ives, and Wadebridge and Bodmin in the north. A light railway connects Looe to Liskeard. In the north-eastern parts of the county, including Bude, Wadebridge, and Launceston, service is provided by the London & South-Western railway. In areas beyond railway reach, such as between Helston and the Lizard or near Penzance, the Great Western company operates motor-car services.

In other districts, the London & South Western main line serves Sherborne and Gillingham. Branches from this system reach Dorchester, Weymouth, Portland, Swanage, Poole, and Wimborne. The Great Western railway also serves Bridport and the towns of Weymouth and Portland. Additionally, the Somerset & Dorset line (a joint venture between the South Western and Midland railways) follows the Stour valley through Wimborne and Blandford, while a light railway from Axminster on the South Western line terminates at Lyme Regis.

**Irish and International Systems:**
In Ireland, the Great Southern & Western railway's main line enters the county from the north at Charleville and serves Queenstown and Cork. Branches from this system run eastward from Cork to Youghal, and from Mallow toward the south-western coast and Killarney. Other lines include the Cork, Bandon & South Coast line, which serves Kinsale, Clonakilty, Bantry, Baltimore, and Skibbereen, and the Cork & Macroom line. The Timoleague and Courtmacsherry line connects these villages to the Clonakilty branch.

In Brazil, the city of Bello-Horizonte (or Minas) is connected to the Central of Brazil railway via a branch line measuring 9 miles in length. In France, the town of Bagneres-de-Luchon is located on a Southern railway branch line from Montrejeau.

## Complementary Transport and Waterways
Beyond rail, communications are supported by canals and rivers. The Grand Junction Canal reaches the valley of the Ouse via the Ouzel valley from the south, with branches extending to Buckingham and Aylesbury. In other regions, the Newry Canal skirts the west of the county, and the Lagan Canal intersects the Lagan valley. The Trent & Mersey canal crosses the southern part of a county, connected to the Erewash canal via the Derby branch canal; a little-used branch known as the Cromford canal leads to Matlock. Regarding natural waterways, the Thames is the only river in its respective county that is continuously navigable.
