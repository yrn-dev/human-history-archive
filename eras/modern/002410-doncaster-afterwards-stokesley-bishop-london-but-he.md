# Doncaster

Doncaster is a municipal borough and market-town located in the West Riding of Yorkshire, England. Situated on a flat plain on the river Don, the town is positioned 156 miles north by west from London. As of 1901, its population was 28,932. The town is located on the outskirts of a populous district that extends up the Don valley, with nearby urban districts including Wheatley (population 3,579) to the north east, Balby-with-Hexthorpe (population 6,781) two miles south west, and Tickhill seven miles south, the latter of which contains the remains of a Norman castle.

## History and Governance
The town's history includes a Roman station, and numerous remains from the Roman period have been discovered. During the reign of Edward the Confessor, Doncaster served as a *berewic* of the manor of Hexthorp and belonged to Earl Tostig. Before 1086, it was granted to Robert, earl of Mortain; however, his successor, William, was attainted for treason during the time of Henry I, causing the overlordship to fall to the crown. The manor was subsequently held as underlords by the families of Salvin, Mauley, and Frossard.

By 1194, Doncaster was a borough held of the crown for a fee farm rent, which Richard I confirmed by granting the burgesses their soke and town in exchange for twenty-five marks yearly. The town was incorporated in 1467 by Edward IV, who established a gild merchant and a government consisting of a mayor and two serjeants-at-mace. In 1505, Henry VII confirmed this charter and granted the burgesses the town, soke, and all manors in the soke upon payment of a fee farm. In 1508, Henry VII further confirmed 1341 letters patent from Peter de Mauley, which renounced manorial claims and "pernicious customs" affecting fishers, butchers, brewers, bakers, and wind-fallen trees.

In 1623, the mayor and burgesses paid approximately £3,000 to Ralph Salvin to end his attempt to regain the manor of Doncaster. Governance continued to evolve through charters granted by Charles II in 1664 (which was ineffective as it was not enrolled) and James II in 1684, the latter of which remained in effect until the Municipal Corporation Act. The borough is governed by a mayor and six aldermen.

## Infrastructure and Economy
Doncaster is a significant hub for rail transport, serving as an important station on the Great Northern railway, which maintains its principal carriage and locomotive works there. The town is also served by the Midland, Great Central, Great Eastern, North Eastern, and Lancashire & Yorkshire railways. The river Don provides intercommunication with the Humber and Goole. Within the town, a system of electric tramways connects the center with its principal suburbs.

The local economy is supported by an extensive agricultural trade, as well as works producing brass, iron, and agricultural machinery. Notable civic buildings include the corn exchange, markets, a free library, an art school, the mansion house, and the town hall. The town's grammar school was founded in 1553 and underwent reorganization in 1862.

## Religious Buildings
The parish church of St George is a fine cruciform structure of Decorated character featuring a 170 ft high central tower and a fine organ. The current building was finished in 1858 under Sir G. G. Scott, replacing an older structure of the same name that was destroyed by fire in 1853. Additionally, the Great Northern railway company erected St James's church, designed by Lord Grimthorpe and Sir G. G. Scott.

## Horse Racing
Doncaster is widely famous for its race-meetings, which are held annually in the second week of September. The racecourse is located one mile south east of the town. The old course measures 1 mile, 7 furlongs, and 70 yards, while the Sandall course (1 mile) was added in 1892. A stand existed on the course prior to 1615, and a grand stand was erected in 1777.

The St Leger race was established in 1776 by Colonel St Leger of Parkhill, near Doncaster. The inaugural race took place on September 24 as a sweepstake of 25 guineas each for three-year-old fillies and colts, run over a 2-mile course. The first race was won by Lord Rockingham's filly, Allabaculia. The following year, the race was won by Mr Sotheron's Bourbon. At the suggestion of the marquis of Rockingham, the race was named the St Leger in 1778 to honor its founder. By 1832, the stakes were increased to 50 sovereigns each. Additionally, the Doncaster Cup, a weight for age race for three-year-olds and upwards, was established in 1801.

## Notable Events
In 1534, John Bale, an ardent partisan of the Reformers and author, was summoned before the archbishop of York following a sermon he preached at Doncaster against the invocation of saints.
