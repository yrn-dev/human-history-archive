# Alexander Bain

Alexander Bain (1818–1903) was a Scottish educationalist and philosopher. Born on June 11, 1818, in Aberdeen, he received his early schooling in that city. In his early life, he worked as a weaver, a fact that led to the punning description of him as *Weevir, rex philosophorum*.

## Academic Development and Early Career
Bain entered Marischal College in 1836. During his time there, he was influenced by William Knight (professor of natural philosophy), Thomas Clark (professor of chemistry), and John Cruickshank (professor of mathematics). He distinguished himself in physics, mathematics, and mental philosophy. 

Toward the end of his arts course, Bain began contributing to the *Westminster Review*, publishing his first article, "Electrotype and Daguerreotype," in September 1840. This period marked the start of a lifelong friendship with John Stuart Mill. Between 1841 and 1844, Bain served for three successive sessions as a substitute for Dr. Glennie, the professor of moral philosophy. During this time, he continued his writing for the *Westminster* and assisted Mill with the revision of the manuscript for *System of Logic* in 1842, later providing the first review of the book in the *London and Westminster* in 1843.

## Professional Appointments
In 1845, Bain was appointed as the professor of natural philosophy and mathematics at the Andersonian University of Glasgow, though he resigned a year later to pursue literary work. In 1848, he moved to London to take a position on the board of health under Edwin Chadwick. In London, he became part of a prominent circle that included John Stuart Mill and George Grote.

In 1860, the crown appointed Bain to the newly created chair of English and logic at the university of Aberdeen. This chair was established following the 1858 amalgamation of Marischal and King's colleges by the Scottish Universities Commission. Bain sought to address the lack of adequate attention previously given to English and logic in Aberdeen. He is credited with influencing the teaching of English composition and grammar in the north of Scotland and forming a school of philosophy.

## Philosophical and Educational Works
Bain's contributions to philosophy and education were extensive. His first large work, *The Senses and the Intellect*, was published in 1855, followed by *The Emotions and the Will* in 1859. In 1861, he added *The Study of Character*, which included an *Estimate of Phrenology*, to *The Senses and the Intellect*.

To provide students with more accessible material than his large treatises, Bain published the *Manual of Mental and Moral Science* in 1868, which condensed his earlier doctrines and added new illustrations. In 1870, he published *Logic*, a work based on the teachings of J. S. Mill, though it differed in several areas. Notably, Bain's *Logic* applied logical principles to various sciences and treated the doctrine of the conservation of energy in relation to causation.

His other publications include:
* **Educational texts:** *Higher English Grammar* (1863), *Manual of Rhetoric* (1866), *A First English Grammar* (1872), and *Companion to the Higher Grammar* (1874).
* **International Scientific Series:** *Mind and Body* (1872) and *Education as a Science* (1879).
* **Psychology:** *Senses and Intellect* (4th ed., 1894).

## Recognition and Public Service
Bain's academic and professional contributions were recognized through several appointments and honors. He served as an examiner in moral science for the Indian Civil Service examinations. Additionally, from 1857 to 1862 and again from 1864 to 1869, he was an examiner for the university of London in moral and logical philosophy. In 1871, the university of Edinburgh conferred upon him an honorary degree of doctor of laws in recognition of his services to Scottish education.
