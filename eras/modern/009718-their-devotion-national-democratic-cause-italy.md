# Devotion to the National and Democratic Cause in Italy

## The Risorgimento and Benedetto Cairoli
The movement for Italian unity, known as the Risorgimento, saw significant contributions from figures such as Benedetto Cairoli (1825–1889). Born in Pavia, Cairoli devoted his entire activity to this cause from 1848 until the completion of Italian unity in 1870. His involvement was multifaceted, serving as a political refugee, an anti-Austrian conspirator, a deputy to parliament, and a Garibaldian officer.

Cairoli's military service included commanding a volunteer company under Garibaldi in 1859 and 1860, during which he sustained a slight wound at Calatafimi and a severe wound at Palermo. He continued his military efforts in 1866 as a colonel assisting Garibaldi in Tirol and fought at Mentana in 1867.

## Political Leadership and Diplomacy
In 1870, Cairoli conducted negotiations with the German chancellor Bismarck. During these talks, it is alleged that Bismarck promised Italy its natural frontiers and possession of Rome, provided the Democratic party could prevent an alliance between Napoleon and Victor Emmanuel.

Cairoli's standing among the Italian people was bolstered by the heroic conduct of his mother and the sacrifice of his four brothers, who died during the wars of the Risorgimento. He further endeared himself to the public by refusing any distinction or compensation.

After the Left rose to power in 1876, Cairoli became the parliamentary leader of his party. He formed his first cabinet in March 1878, pursuing an Irredentist and Francophil policy. However, his administration faced challenges, including the "clean hands" policy adopted by Cairoli and Count Corti at the Berlin Congress. This policy resulted in Italy obtaining nothing, while Austria-Hungary received a European mandate to occupy Herzegovina and Bosnia. Cairoli's first term ended following an attempt by Passanante to assassinate King Humbert in Naples on December 12, 1878; notably, Cairoli was severely wounded while protecting the king. He returned to power on July 3, 1879, and later formed a coalition ministry with Depretis in November of that year, maintaining the foreign office and the premiership.

## Other Influences and Perspectives
The broader spirit of national and democratic devotion in Italy during the early 19th century is noted in the records of the Bonaparte family. Louis, the brother of Napoleon, lived much of his later life in Rome, focusing on philosophic and literary studies. He derived much pleasure from the devotion his sons showed toward the democratic and national cause in Italy between 1830 and 1831, though this pleasure was later clouded by the death of his elder son, Napoleon Louis, during a spring campaign.
