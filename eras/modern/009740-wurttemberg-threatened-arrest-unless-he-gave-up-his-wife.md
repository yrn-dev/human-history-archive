# Württemberg

The kingdom of Württemberg is a region in Germany associated with various historical figures, royal residences, and administrative developments. Its history includes the establishment of planned towns, the governance of its nobility, and the intersection of its territory with neighboring states.

## Ludwigsburg and Royal Residences
Ludwigsburg is a town located in the kingdom of Württemberg, situated approximately 1.5 miles from the river Neckar and 9 miles north of Stuttgart by rail. Founded at the start of the 18th century by Duke Eberhard Louis of Württemberg, the town was later improved and enlarged by Duke Charles Eugène. Because it was constructed as an adjunct to a palace, Ludwigsburg is characterized by spacious squares and straight streets. 

The town serves as the chief military depot in Württemberg and is home to the royal palace, which is considered one of the finest in Germany. This palace features a portrait gallery and the burial vault for the rulers of Württemberg. Nearby is the royal residence of Monrepos, which is linked to the Ludwigsburg park by an avenue of lime trees. Historically, from 1758 to 1824, the town was renowned for producing a specific type of porcelain.

## Economy and Industry
The industrial output of Ludwigsburg includes the production of chemicals, iron, wire goods, brewing, and brick-making. The town also manufactures cotton, woollen, and linen goods, as well as pianos and organs.

## Political and Territorial Relations
The territory of Württemberg has intersected with other regions, such as the mediatized principality in the district of the Upper Danube and the Black Forest. This principality, which includes the lordships of Moskirch (or Messkirch), Hausen, Trochtelfingen, and Jungnau, as well as the landgraviates of Baar and Stuhlingen and the countship of Heiligenberg, is discontinuous. Because this territory lies partly in Prussia, Baden, and Württemberg, the head of the family is an hereditary member of the chamber of peers in Württemberg, Prussia, and the first chamber of Baden. The relationship between this principality and Württemberg was defined by a royal declaration in 1839.

*   **Catherine Kepler:** A resident of the town of Leonberg in Württemberg, Catherine Kepler was arrested in 1620 on a formal charge of witchcraft.
