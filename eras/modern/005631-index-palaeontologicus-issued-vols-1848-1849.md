# Index Palaeontologicus

The *Index Palaeontologicus* is a significant record of fossils that has been regarded as being of inestimable value to all palaeontologists. It was produced as the third part of the work *Handbuch einer Geschichte der Natur*.

## Authorship and Publication
The *Index Palaeontologicus* was authored by Heinrich Georg Bronn, a German geologist born on March 3, 1800, in Ziegelhausen near Heidelberg. Bronn, who earned his doctor's degree in medicine in 1821 and became a professor of natural history in 1822, developed the index with the assistance of H. R. Göppert and H. von Meyer. The work was issued in three volumes between 1848 and 1849.

## Context within Handbuch einer Geschichte der Natur
The *Index Palaeontologicus* served as the third part of Bronn's broader project, *Handbuch einer Geschichte der Natur*. The first part of this general account of the physical history of the earth was issued in 1841. The second part focused on life-history, presenting a vision in which species were regarded as direct acts of creation.

## Related Works of Heinrich Georg Bronn
The creation of the *Index Palaeontologicus* was part of a career dedicated to palaeontological studies and fieldwork across France, Italy, and various regions of Germany. Bronn's other major contributions to the field include:

*   ***Lethaea Geognostica***: Published in two volumes between 1834 and 1838 (with a third edition of three volumes published between 1851 and 1856 in collaboration with F. Römer), this work is considered one of the foundations of German stratigraphical geology.
*   ***Die Klassen und Ordnungen des Thier-Reichs***: An important work on fossil and recent zoology. Bronn authored the volumes on Malacozoa, Actinozoa, and Amorphozoa, which were published from 1859 to 1862, before other naturalists continued the work.
*   ***Jahrbuch für Mineralogie***: From 1830 until 1862, Bronn assisted in editing this publication, which continued as *Neues Jahrbuch*.

For his contributions to the field, Bronn was awarded the Wollaston medal by the Geological Society of London in 1861. He died in Heidelberg on July 5, 1862.
