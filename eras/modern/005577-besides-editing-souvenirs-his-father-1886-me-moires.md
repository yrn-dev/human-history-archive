# Literary and Academic Publications (Late 19th and Early 20th Centuries)

The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw a prolific output of edited memoirs, historical correspondences, and academic treatises across various disciplines, including diplomacy, theology, and linguistics.

## Diplomatic and Historical Works
A significant body of work was produced regarding European diplomacy and royal correspondence. One author edited the *Souvenirs* of his father (1886), the *Mémoires* of Talleyrand (1891), and the *Letters* of the Duchess Albertine de Broglie (1896). This individual's published works include *Le Secret du roi, Correspondance secrète de Louis XV avec ses agents diplomatiques, 1752-1774* (1878), *Frédéric II et Marie Thérèse* (1883), *Frédéric II et Louis XV* (1885), and *Marie Thérèse Impératrice* (1888). Further publications include *Le Père Lacordaire* (1889), *Maurice de Saxe et le marquis d'Argenson* (1891), *La Paix d'Aix-la-Chapelle* (1892), *L'Alliance autrichienne* (1895), and *La Mission de M. de Gontaut-Biron à Berlin* (1896). In 1898, *Voltaire avant et pendant la Guerre de Sept Ans* was published, followed by a translation of *Saint Ambroise* by Margaret Maitland in 1899 as part of "The Saints" series.

Additionally, the duc de Broglie authored *Écrits et discours* in three volumes (1863), *Le Libre Échange et l'impôt* (1879), and *Vues sur le gouvernement de la France* (1861), though the latter was confiscated by the imperial government before it could be published. He also left *Souvenirs* in four volumes published between 1885 and 1888.

## Biographical and Memorial Literature
Biographical works from this era often focused on explorers and military figures. Sir Richard F. Burton was the subject of several works: Lady Burton's *Life of Sir Richard F. Burton* (1893), which was later condensed and edited by W.H. Wilkins in 1898; *A Sketch of the Career of R.F. Burton* (1886) by A.B. Richards, Andrew Wilson, and St Clair Baddeley; and *The True Life of Captain Sir Richard F. Burton* (1896) by G.M. Stisted. In 1906, Thomas Wright of Olney published a two-volume *Life of Sir Richard Burton* that questioned Burton's originality as an Arabic translator and noted his reliance on Payne's 1881 translation of the *Arabian Nights*.

In military history, the *Precis des evenements militaires* serves as a source for its period, and the *Souvenirs du lieut.-general Comte Mathieu Dumas* was published posthumously by his son in 1839.

## Academic and Theological Contributions
Theological and educational scholarship was marked by significant editorial efforts. Timothy Dwight, president of Yale from 1886 to 1899, edited and annotated several volumes of the English translation of H.A.W. Meyer's *Kritisch-exegetischer Kommentar zum Neuen Testament* and published a collection of sermons titled *Thoughts of and for the Inner Life* (1899).

In the field of Arabic studies, Hartwig Derenbourg produced several important editions, including *Le Livre de Sîbawaihi* (1881-1889), *Chrestomathie élémentaire de l'arabe littéral* (1885; 2nd ed. 1892), and *Ousâma ibn Mounkidh, un émir syrien* (1889). He also published *Ousâma ibn Mounkidh, préface du livre du bâton* (1887), *Al-Fákhrî* (1895), *Oumâra du Gémen* (1897), and a catalogue of Arabic MSS. in the Escorial (vol. i., 1884).

In the realm of education, Nicholas Murray Butler founded and edited the *Educational Review* in 1891 and edited book series such as "The Teachers' Professional Library" and "The Great Educators." He also published *The Meaning of Education* (1898), as well as address series titled *True and False Democracy* (1907) and *The American as he is* (1908).

## Literary Criticism and Fiction
The period saw a variety of literary outputs, from poetry to critical editions. Arthur Christopher Benson wrote the fiction *Memoirs of Arthur Hamilton* (1886) under the pseudonym "Christopher Carr," as well as *Poems* (1893) and *Lyrics* (1895). His later works include *Fasti Etonenses* (1899), a study of Archbishop Laud (1887), and monographs on Walter Pater (1906), Edward FitzGerald (1905), and D.G. Rossetti (1904). He also collaborated with Lord Esher to edit the *Correspondence of Queen Victoria* (1907). His brother, Edward Frederick Benson, wrote novels including *Dodo* (1893), *The Vintage* (1898), and *The Capsina* (1899).

Regarding the works of Greene, Alexander Dyce edited his poems and dramatic works in 1831 (reissued in 1858), while A. B. Grosart edited the complete works for the Huth Library (1881-1886), which included a translation of Nicholas Storojhenko's 1878 monograph. J. Churton Collins later edited Greene's plays and poems in two volumes in 1905.
