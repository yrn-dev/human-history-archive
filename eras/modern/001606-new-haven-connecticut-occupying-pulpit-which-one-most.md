# New Haven, Connecticut

New Haven is a city in Connecticut, United States of America, that served as the center of the New Haven Colony. The colony's influence extended to other regions; for example, the first settlers of Greenwich arrived from the New Haven Colony in 1640. Although the Dutch laid claim to Greenwich in 1614 and the settlers there consented to union with New Netherland in 1642 due to a lack of assistance from New Haven, a 1650 treaty fixed the boundary between the two. The inhabitants of Greenwich eventually submitted to the New Haven Colony in October 1656 before joining Connecticut six years later.

## Religious and Educational Influence

New Haven has been a significant center for theology and ecclesiastical leadership. The First Church (Congregational) in New Haven featured a pulpit described as one of the most conspicuous in New England, having been made famous by predecessors Nathaniel W. Taylor and Moses Stuart. Leonard Bacon (1802–1881), an American Congregational preacher and writer, served as the pastor of this church from 1825 until his death in 1881, though he stepped away from the active pastorate in 1866. Bacon was a broad-minded advocate of liberal orthodoxy and was regarded by some as the most prominent Congregationalist of his era, earning the popular nickname "The Congregational Pope of New England." He also served as a lecturer on American church history and church polity, and as an acting professor of didactic theology at Yale University from 1866 to 1871.

Yale University, located in New Haven, has been a primary site for theological study and academic achievement. Lyman Beecher (1775–1863), a descendant of one of the New Haven colony's founders, graduated from Yale in 1797 after studying theology under Timothy Dwight. Similarly, Leonard Bacon graduated from Yale in 1820, and Sereno Edwards Dwight (1786–1850) graduated in 1803 and later served as a tutor at the institution from 1806 to 1810.

In the legal profession, Sereno Edwards Dwight practiced law in New Haven from 1810 to 1816 before pursuing a career in preaching, which included serving as the chaplain of the United States Senate for one year.

## Regional Geography and Connectivity

New Haven serves as a central point of reference for several surrounding Connecticut cities and townships. It is located approximately 10 miles east of Derby, a city at the junction of the Naugatuck and Housatonic rivers. Bridgeport is situated about 18 miles southwest of New Haven. 

The region is well-connected by rail, specifically via the New York, New Haven & Hartford railway, which serves New Haven as well as nearby locations including Bridgeport, Derby, and the villages of Greenwich.

## Publications and Intellectual Life

New Haven has been a hub for religious and intellectual publishing. Leonard Bacon served as an editor of the *Christian Spectator* in New Haven from 1826 to 1838. He was also a founder of the *New Englander*, which later became the *Yale Review*. Additionally, the city has been a site for historical documentation, such as the 1886 publication of S. Orcutt's *History of the Township of Stratford and the City of Bridgeport*.
