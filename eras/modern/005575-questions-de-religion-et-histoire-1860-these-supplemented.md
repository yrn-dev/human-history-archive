# Questions de religion et d'histoire

*Questions de religion et d'histoire* is a literary work published in 1860 by Jacques Victor Albert, duc de Broglie.

## Authorship and Context
The work was authored by Jacques Victor Albert, duc de Broglie (1821–1901), who was born in Paris on June 13, 1821. Before dedicating himself to literature, he had a brief diplomatic career in Rome and Madrid. Following the revolution of 1848, he withdrew from public life to focus on his writing.

Prior to the publication of *Questions de religion et d'histoire*, the author had already published a translation of the religious system of Leibnitz in 1846. He established his reputation through contributions to the clerical and Orleanist publication *Le Correspondant* as well as the *Revue des deux Mondes*.

## Publication and Related Works
*Questions de religion et d'histoire* (1860) was a collection of the author's earlier contributions to the aforementioned journals. It was published alongside another collection titled *Études morales et littéraires* (1853). In 1869, these works were supplemented by a further volume titled *Nouvelles études de littérature et de morale*.

The author also wrote *L'Église et l'empire romain au IVe siècle*, published between 1856 and 1866. This specific work led to his election to the Academy in 1862, where he succeeded Lacordaire.

## Later Career of the Author
Following his literary pursuits, the prince de Broglie succeeded his father in the dukedom in 1870. He entered politics, being elected to the National Assembly for the department of the Eure. On February 19 of that year, he was appointed as the ambassador in London. He resigned from this post in March 1872 due to criticisms regarding his negotiations on commercial treaties between France and England.

Returning to the National Assembly, he became a leading figure in the monarchical campaign against Thiers. Upon the appointment of Marshal MacMahon, the duc de Broglie served as the minister for foreign affairs and president of the council starting in May 1873. On November 26, he transitioned to the ministry of the interior. His time in office was characterized by extreme conservatism, which led to conflict with Republicans, the Legitimist party, and the Bonapartists. His cabinet fell on May 16, 1874, though he was later entrusted with forming a new cabinet on May 16, 1877.
