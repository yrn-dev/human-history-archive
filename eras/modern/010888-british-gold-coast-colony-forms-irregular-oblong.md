# Gold Coast Colony

The Gold Coast colony is a British possession located in West Africa. Its administrative and geographical reach is closely linked with the neighboring territory of Ashanti and the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast, the latter of which is a British Protectorate situated to the north.

## Geography and Surveying
The Gold Coast colony shares its northern frontier with the southern frontier of Ashanti. In terms of cartography, a survey of the Gold Coast Colony has been published by Major F. G. Guggisberg since 1907, utilizing scales of 1:125,000 and 1:200,000.

## Relation to Ashanti
The Gold Coast colony is inextricably linked to the region of Ashanti, a British possession that forms an irregular oblong with a triangular projection extending southward into the country of the Adansi. Ashanti is bounded to the west by the French Ivory Coast colony and to the east by the river Volta, which serves as the boundary with the German colony of Togoland.

The relationship between the Gold Coast government and Ashanti has been marked by periods of conflict and intervention. Following a war from 1873 to 1874, several states dependent on Ashanti sought British protection, though this was initially refused. Later, the colonial government was forced to intervene in an Ashanti civil war, leading to the election of Prince Prempeh (Kwaka Dua III) as king on March 26, 1888, an event that occurred with the sanction of an officer from the Gold Coast government.

Tensions resurfaced in 1893 when Prempeh closed roads to traders. Because the region was included in the British sphere of influence by international agreement, a British mission was dispatched. The mission demanded that Prempeh fulfill the 1874 treaty, accept a British protectorate, and receive a resident at Kumasi. Prempeh declined to treat with the governor of the Gold Coast and failed to reply to the mission's demands by the deadline of October 31, 1895.

## Economy and Trade
The economic activities of the Gold Coast and Ashanti are closely integrated. The revenue and expenditure of Ashanti are included in those of the Gold Coast. Revenue is primarily generated through liquor licences, rents from government land, contributions from gold-mining companies, and caravan taxes.

Trade between the two regions was improved following the suppression of a revolt in 1900, specifically enhancing the route between Cape Coast and Kumasi, the latter serving as the distributing center for the hinterland and all of Ashanti. A railway connecting Sekondi to Kumasi was completed in 1903, passing through regions rich in gold.

Gold mining is a significant industry; in the period between 1895 and 1901, several companies were formed to work mines in the western districts of Ashanti. By 1902 and subsequent years, the majority of gold exported from the Gold Coast originated from the Obuassi mines. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti was valued at £254,790, totaling 68,259 oz.

Other commercial products found in the region include:
* **Rubber:** The rubber tree (*Funtumia elastica*) is the most important commercial vegetable product.
* **Cocoa:** Many plantations were established starting in 1905, particularly in the eastern districts.
* **Kola:** The nut of the kola tree is in great demand.
* **Tobacco:** Cultivated primarily in northern regions.
* **Gum copal:** Exported from the region.
