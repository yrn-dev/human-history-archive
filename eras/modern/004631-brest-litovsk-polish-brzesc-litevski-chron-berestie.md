# BREST-LITOVSK

Brest-Litovsk (known in Polish as *Brzesc-Litevski* and referred to in chronicles as *Berestie* and *Berestov*) is a strongly fortified town located in the government of Grodno in western Russia. It is situated at 52° 5' N. latitude and 23° 39' E. longitude, positioned at the intersection of railways from Moscow, Kiev, Warsaw, and East Prussia. Geographically, the town lies at the junction of the Bug and the navigable river Mukhovets.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The town is located 137 miles by rail south of the city of Grodno. The Bug river, which flows through Brest-Litovsk, is navigable from the town downwards; it separates the Russian governments of Grodno and Volhynia from the Polish provinces of Siedlce and Lublin. 

Water transport is further facilitated by the Brest-Litovsk (or King's) canal. Spanning 50 miles and utilizing the Bug and Mukhovets rivers, this canal serves as a link in the waterways connecting the Vistula with the Dnieper.

## Demographics and Economy
As of 1867, the population of Brest-Litovsk was 22,493, increasing to 42,812 by 1901. In the latter census, more than half of the inhabitants were Jews. The town is the seat of a Greek Catholic bishop and an Armenian bishop, the latter of whom holds authority over Armenians throughout the entire country. The town also contains a Jewish synagogue that was considered the first in Europe during the 16th century.

The local economy is characterized by an extensive trade in leather, tar, wood, hemp, flax, and grain.

## History
Brest-Litovsk was first mentioned at the beginning of the 11th century. Its history is marked by several periods of destruction and reconstruction:
* **1241:** The town was laid waste by the Mongols and remained unbuilt until 1275.
* **1379:** The Teutonic Knights burned the town's suburbs.
* **Late 15th Century:** The khan of the Crimea destroyed the entire town.

During the reign of the Polish king Sigismund III, the town hosted diets. It also served as the meeting place for two notable councils of western Russian bishops in 1594 and 1596. The town was captured by Swedish forces twice, first in 1657 and again in 1706. In 1794, the town was the site of a victory by Suvarov over the Polish general Sierakowski, and it was subsequently added to the Russian empire in 1795.

## The Peace of Brest-Litovsk
In the context of the Great War, the peace of Brest-Litovsk was signed on March 2, 1918. This treaty is described as a crushing and exorbitant peace, dictated by the German imperial government with the arrogance of confident victors, providing an indication of what a German victory would mean for the Western allies.

Following the war, the Brest-Litovsk treaty was annulled and abrogated. The Allies required Germany and Austria to renounce the treaty, along with other agreements entered into with the Maximalist Government of Russia. These terms included the requirement that Germany respect the permanent and inalienable independence of all territories that had been part of the former Russian Empire.
