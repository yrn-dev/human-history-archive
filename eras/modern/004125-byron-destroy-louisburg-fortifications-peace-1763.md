# The Destruction of the Louisburg Fortifications (1760)

The expedition to destroy the fortifications at Louisburg in 1760 was a British naval operation conducted during the Seven Years' War. The mission was led by the Hon. John Byron, a British vice-admiral who had previously gained experience in a voyage of discovery around the world with Anson.

## The Expedition and Command
In 1760, the Hon. John Byron was sent in command of a squadron specifically tasked with the destruction of the fortifications at Louisburg. He was accompanied on this mission by Samuel Barrington, a British admiral and the fourth son of the 1st Viscount Barrington. During the operation, Barrington sailed with Byron, commanding the "Achilles," a ship of 60 guns.

## Historical Context of Louisburg
Louisburg was a town and port of entry located in Cape Breton county, Nova Scotia, Canada. Under the French régime, it served as the center of the cod-fisheries and was second in importance only to Quebec. A fortress had been erected there at enormous expense. 

The site had been a point of contention in previous conflicts. During "King George's War," a British expedition—including a military portion provided by the colonial militia under Colonel William Pepperell—captured the French Canadian fortress between April 20 and June 16, 1745. At that time, Louisburg was viewed primarily as a nest of privateers and was given up at the peace. However, during the Seven Years' War, the location became a matter of grand strategy. The second capture of the fortress in 1758, carried out by a British land and sea force under Admiral Boscawen and General Jeffrey Amherst, served as the preliminary step toward the British conquest of Canada.

## Aftermath and Destruction
Following the events of the Seven Years' War, the fortress of Louisburg was subject to almost utter destruction. This was driven by the jealousy of the British settlement of Halifax. As a result of this destruction, only a few case-mates of the original fortress remain. In the period of English rule, a fishing village developed on the opposite side of the harbour, which eventually became the winter shipping port for the Dominion Coal Company.

## Related Figures at the Peace of 1763
By the time of the peace in 1763, Samuel Barrington had spent twenty-two years almost continuously afloat. Following the war, his career continued with an appointment in 1768 to the frigate "Venus," where he served as governor to the duke of Cumberland.

The Hon. John Byron continued his naval career after the Louisburg expedition. In 1764, he undertook a prolonged cruise in the South Seas aboard the "Dolphin." He later published a *Narrative* of his early adventures with Anson in 1768 and was appointed governor of Newfoundland in 1769. He attained flag rank in 1775 and became a vice-admiral in 1778.
