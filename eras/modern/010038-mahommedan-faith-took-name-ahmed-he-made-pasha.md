# Ahmed Pasha and Related Historical Figures

The name Ahmed Pasha is associated with several distinct figures across different eras and regions, ranging from French adventurers in the Ottoman Empire to regional governors and military commanders.

## Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval
Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval (1675–1747), was a French adventurer born on July 14, 1675, to an old family of Limousin. His early career began at age thirteen when he joined the Royal Marine Corps, followed by three years in the army where he eventually commanded a regiment. He demonstrated military ability and courage during campaigns in the Netherlands under Luxemburg and in Italy under Vendome, Villeroi, and Catinat.

In 1704, Bonneval faced a court-martial and was condemned to death due to his insolent behavior toward the minister of war. He escaped to Germany, where Prince Eugene helped him secure a general's command in the Austrian army. In this capacity, he fought against France and Turkey, participating in the battle of Malplaquet and sustaining severe wounds at Peterwardein. Although the French proceedings against him were eventually dropped, allowing him to visit Paris and marry a daughter of Marshal de Biron, he returned to the Austrian army to fight at Belgrade.

His career in Austria ended after he became disagreeable to Prince Eugene, who appointed him master of the ordnance in the Low Countries. Following a quarrel with the marquis de Prie, Eugene's deputy governor, Bonneval was confined and again condemned to death by court-martial. The emperor commuted this to banishment and one year of imprisonment. Upon his release, Bonneval offered his services to the Turkish government, professed the Mahommedan faith, and took the name Ahmed. He was appointed a pasha and tasked with commanding and organizing the artillery. He provided valuable service to the sultan during wars with Russia and Nadir Shah. Though rewarded with the governorship of Chios, he was later banished to the shores of the Black Sea due to suspicions from the Porte. He died while contemplating a return to Christianity and Europe.

## Military and Administrative Roles of Ahmed Pashas
Other figures bearing the title Ahmed Pasha appear in various regional conflicts and administrations:

*   **Calafat Campaign:** In 1854, a Turkish force under Ahmed Pasha defeated a Russian force at Calafat, a town in Rumania on the river Danube, by surprising the enemy's camp.
*   **Independent Pashalic:** During the first half of the 18th century, amidst the general disintegration and decline of Turkish power, a governor-general named Ahmed Pasha established an independent pashalic. This territory was later besieged by Nadir Shah, the usurper of the Persian throne, who attempted to annex the province back to Persia. Ahmed defended the city with enough courage to force the invader to raise the siege after suffering great loss. Turkish authority over this pashalic was restored in the early 19th century.

## Other Historical Figures named Ahmed
The name Ahmed appears in various other historical contexts across Asia and Africa:

*   **Darfur:** In the 14th century, Mahommedanism was introduced to Darfur by Tunjur Arabs. The first Tunjur king, Ahmed-el-Makur, married the daughter of the last Tago monarch and brought prosperity to the country by reducing unruly chiefs to submission. Later, between 1682 and 1722, Ahmed Bahr made Islam the religion of the state and encouraged immigration from Bagirmi and Bornu.
*   **India:** Following the decline of the Mogul empire after 1707, the region faced repeated invasions by Ahmed Shah.
*   **Persia and Asia:** Malik Fakhruddin Ahmed at-Thaibi served as an ambassador to Khanbalig in 1297, sent by Ghazan Khan; he died in 1305 on the Coromandel coast. Additionally, Ahmed Tigudar is noted as having two wives from the Mongol tribe of Ḳungurat.
*   **Sudan:** In 1882, Mahommed Ahmed, known as the mahdi, led a revolt in Kordofan. This uprising eventually led to the annihilation of Hicks Pasha and his army in November 1883.
