# Austrian Rule in Italy and the Unification of Italy

## The Political Landscape of Italy
Historically, Italy was characterized by division and diversity, similar to much of Germany. The region was split among various minor princes and dukes. Within this framework, the Pope functioned as a prince ruling over the papal states; however, the Pope remained fearful of losing the allegiance of the remaining Catholic princes, which prevented him from interfering between those princes and their subjects or promoting the commonweal of Christendom.

## The Scandal of Austrian Rule
By the mid-19th century, Austria's diplomatic position had deteriorated. Although Austria had secured a settlement regarding the Eastern Question without military conflict, it found itself without friends or allies. The "Holy Alliance" of three autocratic northern powers, which had been recemented in 1833 and provided Austria with the intervention of the tsar in 1849, was shattered due to Austria's conduct during the Crimean War.

This isolation became evident at the congress of Paris. During this assembly, Cavour, the plenipotentiary for Sardinia, exposed the "scandal" of Austrian rule in Italy to the assembled European powers. This sentiment was further emphasized during the military campaign of 1859.

## The Campaign of 1859 and Unification
In 1859, Sardinia formed an alliance with France to challenge Austrian control. This campaign laid the foundations for a united Italy. While France helped liberate North Italy from Austria, this assistance came at the price of Savoy. 

The conflict ended with the armistice of Villafranca. The provisions of this armistice were influenced by the threat of Prussian intervention; however, Prussia's motive was not a desire to support Austria, but rather a fear that France would achieve undue aggrandizement.

The results of these events for Austria were twofold:
*   **External:** Austria lost all of its Italian possessions, with the exception of Venice.
*   **Internal:** The failure of the Italian campaign forced the Austrian government to make constitutional concessions to conciliate public opinion.

Following these events, Italy gradually unified itself into a single kingdom.

## The Role of the House of Savoy
The unification process involved the Dukes of Savoy, the ancestors of the Italian kings. For centuries, the Dukes of Savoy had maintained a strategic European importance by ruling in both Italy and France, positioned astride the Alps. They had held royal titles since 1713, serving first as Kings of Sicily from 1713 to 1720, and subsequently as Kings of Sardinia after Sicily was exchanged for Sardinia in 1720.

## Later Italian Expansion
Following the unification of the peninsula, Italy began to acquire territories in Africa. The first such territory was Assab Bay, which was purchased from the sultan of Raheita in 1870, although it was not occupied until 1880.
