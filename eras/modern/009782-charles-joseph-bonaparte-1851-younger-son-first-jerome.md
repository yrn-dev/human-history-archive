# Charles Joseph Bonaparte

Charles Joseph Bonaparte was a prominent figure in American politics during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Born on June 9, 1851, in Baltimore, he was the younger son of Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870) and the grandson of Jerome, the king of Westphalia.

## Education and Early Career
Bonaparte received his education at Harvard University. Following his studies, he entered the legal profession, becoming a lawyer in 1874. In addition to his legal practice, he held various public positions and served as the president of the National Municipal League.

## Political Career
Bonaparte attained a distinguished position within the United States government, serving in high-ranking cabinet roles under President Roosevelt. From July 1905 to December 1906, he held the office of secretary of the navy. Following this tenure, he served as the attorney-general of the United States, a position he held until March 1909.

## Family Background
Charles Joseph Bonaparte belonged to the "Bonapartes of Baltimore," a branch of the Bonaparte family settled in America. This lineage began with his grandfather, Jerome Bonaparte, who married Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a Baltimore merchant named William Patterson, on December 24, 1803. Although the marriage was valid under American law and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void, Napoleon disliked the union and refused to recognize its legality, eventually forcing Jerome to separate from Elizabeth.

The only child of that marriage was Charles Joseph's father, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870). Born in England and primarily residing in Baltimore, the elder Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte was said to bear a marked resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. He had two sons: an elder son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), who served in the French army during campaigns in Italy and the Crimea, and the younger son, Charles Joseph.
