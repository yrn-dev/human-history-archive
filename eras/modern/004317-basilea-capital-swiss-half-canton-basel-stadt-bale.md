# Basel

Basel (French: *Bale*; Italian: *Basilea*) is the capital of the Swiss half canton of Basel Stadt (or Bale Ville). It is the second most populous town in the Swiss Confederation, with 109,161 inhabitants, ranking only after Zuerich. It is reputed to be the richest Swiss town, with a number of resident millionaires (in francs) exceeding those of any other town in Switzerland.

## Geography and Urban Layout
The city is divided by the Rhine river into two sections: Gross Basel to the south and Klein Basel to the north, with Gross Basel being significantly larger. Several bridges span the river; notably, a stone bridge replaced an old wooden bridge in 1905. 

The city's infrastructure includes two primary railway stations: the main station is located in Gross Basel, while the Baden station is situated in Klein Basel. The city's economy is characterized by an extremely large and flourishing transit trade, which is largely attributed to the 1882 opening of the St Gotthard railway. This line allows merchandise collected from central and north Europe to be stored in Basel before redistribution.

## History
The town was founded in A.D. 374 by the emperor Valentinian and named after his residence. In the 5th century, the bishop of Augusta Rauricorum (now Kaiser Augst), located 7-1/2 miles to the east, moved his see to Basel. For centuries, the city's history was defined by the spiritual and temporal power of the bishops, though this secular influence was gradually supplanted by the burghers during the 14th century.

A disastrous earthquake in 1356 nearly ruined the city. After a period of swaying between the Swiss Confederation and neighboring Rhine cities, Basel was admitted into the Confederation in 1501. The city later became a primary center for the Reformation movement in Switzerland, leading the bishop to retire to Porrentruy in 1525. The bishop remained there until 1792 and eventually settled at Soleure in 1828, following a reorganization of the bishopric after 1814.

By the 18th century, trade gilds had secured most political power, favoring the city over rural districts. This tension led the rural districts to proclaim their independence in 1832. Consequently, in 1833, the region was organized into two half cantons: Basel Stadt (the city) and Basel Landschaft (the countryside).

## Architecture and Culture
The most prominent building in Basel is the Muenster (cathedral), constructed of deep red sandstone on a high terrace above the Rhine. Consecrated in 1019, it was mainly rebuilt following the 1356 earthquake. The cathedral's choir hosted public meetings of the great oecumenical council from 1431 to 1449, while its chapter-house served as a meeting place for committees.

The cathedral is the burial site of Erasmus, who lived in the city from 1521 to 1529 and died there in 1536. Other celebrated men buried in the attached cloisters include Grynaeus (d. 1541), Oecolampadius (d. 1531), and Buxtorf (d. 1732). Other notable landmarks include the restored 16th-century Rathaus (town hall).

The city's museums house significant art. The museum contains a fine collection of works by Holbein, who resided in Basel between 1528 and 1531. The historical museum, located in the old Franciscan church, contains various treasures, including fragments of the *Dance of Death*, which have been wrongly attributed to Holbein.

The university of Basel, founded by Pius II in 1460, is the oldest university in Switzerland. It was the site of the professorship of Jakob Burckhardt (1818–1897), a renowned art historian and writer who taught there during several periods between 1845 and 1893.

## Administration of Basel Stadt
The half canton of Basel Stadt comprises the city of Basel and three rural districts north of the Rhine: Bettingen, Riehen, and Klein Hueningen (the latter is now united with the city). The total area of the half canton is 13.7 sq. m., with 11 sq. m. classified as "productive" and 1.5 sq. m. consisting of forests.

As of 1900, the total population was 112,227, of whom 3,066 lived in rural districts. The population is mainly German-speaking and consists of 73,063 Protestants, 37,101 Romanists (including Old Catholics), and 1,897 Jews.

The cantonal constitution dates from 1889. The government consists of:
* An executive of seven members.
* A legislature (*Grossrat*) of 130 members.
* One member sent to the Federal *Staenderat*.
* Six members sent to the Federal *Nationalrat*.

All these positions are elected by direct popular vote for three-year terms. Since 1875, the constitution has allowed for a facultative referendum on all bills and the right of initiative regarding laws or the revision of the cantonal constitution, provided 1,000 citizens claim these rights.
