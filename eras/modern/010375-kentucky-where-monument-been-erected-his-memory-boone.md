# Daniel Boone

## Early Life and Early Expeditions
Daniel Boone (1734–1820) was an American backwoodsman and pioneer of English descent. He was born on November 2, 1734, near Reading, Pennsylvania. Around 1751, Boone and his family, led by his father Squire Boone, settled on the frontier in the Yadkin Valley, located in what is now Davie County, North Carolina. During this period, Daniel spent much of his time trapping and hunting while working on the family farm.

Boone's early experiences included serving as a blacksmith and wagoner during Braddock's expedition against the Indians in 1755. In 1765, he visited Florida, and by 1767, he made his first visit to the Kentucky region. His interest in the area had been sparked by descriptions provided by John Finley, an Indian trader who had descended the Ohio river to the site of Louisville in 1752.

## Exploration and Settlement of Kentucky
Between 1769 and 1771, Boone spent two years roaming through the Kentucky region with several companions, including John Finley. During these expeditions, he collected deer, beaver, and bear skins and encountered roving bands of Indians. Boone later served in Lord Dunmore's War in 1774.

In 1775, Boone led a party of settlers to Kentucky to found Boonesborough, which became an important settlement. That same year, Boone marked the "Wilderness Road," a trail running from the Cumberland Gap to the Falls of the Ohio via Bardstown, Danville, and Crab Orchard, starting from the Watauga settlement in east Tennessee. While this route was the primary path for a large number of early settlers moving west of the Alleghany Mountains, it remained passable only for horses and men until the state converted it into a wagon road in 1795.

## Conflict and Captivity
On February 7, 1778, Boone and the party he was leading were captured by a band of Shawnees. Boone was adopted into the Shawnee tribe and taken to Detroit. During the return journey from Detroit, he escaped and traveled 160 miles in four days to reach Boonesborough. His timely arrival provided warning of a formidable attack by his captors; Boone played a conspicuous role in repelling this attack, which took place from September 8 to September 17. Additionally, Boone participated in the "Battle of Blue Licks" in 1782.

## Public Service and Later Years
During the period when Kentucky was part of Virginia, Boone served as a representative for the settlers in the Virginia legislature. He also held several local offices in Fayette County—one of the three counties Kentucky was divided into at the time—serving as county lieutenant, sheriff, and deputy surveyor.

Due to carelessness regarding land titles, Boone lost all his land and moved to Point Pleasant, Virginia (now West Virginia) in 1788. Around 1799, he relocated to territory owned by Spain in what is now Missouri, approximately 45 miles west of St. Louis. There, he was appointed as the district's syndic and received a land grant of 1,000 arpents, which is about 845 acres.
