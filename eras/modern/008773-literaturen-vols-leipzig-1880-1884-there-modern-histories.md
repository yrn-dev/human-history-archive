# Historical and Literary Scholarship

The study of history and literature has been documented through a vast array of specialized works, ranging from national histories and archaeological studies to comprehensive bibliographies of philosophical and religious figures.

## Bohemian Historical and Literary Studies
Bohemia has produced a significant number of historic writers. Wenceslas Tomek (1818-1905) authored many works, with his history of the town of Prague, *Dejepis miesta Prahy*, being the most important. Jaroslav Goll (born 1846) wrote extensively, particularly regarding the community of the Bohemian Brethren. In 1895, Goll jointly founded the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* (Bohemian Historical Review) with Dr. Anton Rezek, who also published important studies in the Journal of the Bohemian Museum.

Other notable contributors include Professor Joseph Kalousek, who wrote on the early history of Bohemia and the ancient constitution (*Statni pravo*), and Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, who published studies on the writings and life of John Huss. Archaeological research into the earliest periods of Bohemian history has been conducted by Professors Pic and Niederle.

Literature of the region has been analyzed by Count Lutzow in *A History of Bohemian Literature* (1899) and W.R. Morfill in *Slavonic Literature* (1883). A Russian work by A.N. Pypin and V.D. Spasovic, translated into German by Trangott Pech as *Gesch. der slav. Literaturen* (2 vols., 1880-1884), also covers the subject. Modern histories written in the national language have been produced by Mr. Jaroslav Vlaek, Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, and Dr. Karel Tieftrunk.

## Greek Literature and Philology
The history of Greek literature began with the monumental 14-volume work of J. A. Fabricius (1705-1728), later edited by G. C. Harless. This was followed by F. Scholl's *Hist. de la litterature grecque* (1813). Both texts cover the period from the earliest times through the Byzantine empire. 

More recent scholarship includes:
* **General Handbooks:** W. Christ's *Geschichte der griechischen Literatur bis auf die Zeit Justinians* is noted as the most serviceable handbook for students. Other works include those by J. P. Mahaffy, A. and M. Croiset, and G. G. Murray.
* **Specialized Studies:** T. Bergk's *Griechische Literaturgeschichte* (1872-1894) focuses on lyric, drama (up to Euripides), epos, and early prose. F. Susemihl provided valuable notes on the Alexandrinerzeit period (1891-1892).
* **Modern Greek Philology:** Authorities on modern Greek literature and language include Constantine Sathas, D. Bikelas, R. Nicolai, and A. R. Rhangabe. Linguistic studies have been conducted by J. Psichari on historical grammar and F. Blass on pronunciation.

## Ancient Athenian and Philosophical Research
Authorities on ancient Athens are categorized by period. Early Athens is covered by W. Warde Fowler, while the fifth and fourth centuries are addressed by authors such as W. Oncken, L. Whibley, and J. Beloch. The Hellenistic and Roman periods are discussed by J.P. Mahaffy and A. Holm. Constitutional history is explored through the Aristotelian "Constitution of Athens" and works by A.H.J. Greenidge and G. Gilbert.

Research into Epicurus relies on ancient accounts from Plutarch, Cicero, Lucretius, and the tenth book of Diogenes Laertius. The *Volumina Herculanensia* contain fragments of treatises from Epicurus and his school. Later systematization of his doctrine was performed by Gassendi. Modern critical analysis has been provided by W. Wallace, E. Zeller, and H. Sidgwick.

## European Political and Religious History
Historical records of Austria include the English-language *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792* by Archdeacon William Coxe. Other significant German-language works include those by J. Majlath, A. von Arneth (on Maria Theresia), and Franz X. Krones.

Regarding the German Reformation, the life of Martin Luther has been documented by Melanchthon (1545), Myconius (1718), and more recently by A. Hausrath and Th. Kolde. Primary sources include the *Analecta Lutherana* and the correspondence of Luther edited by Enders.

In French history, the figure of Gracchus Babeuf is noted for propounding socialism as a practical policy. His life and the "Babouvisme" movement are detailed in works by V. Advielle and P. M. Buonarroti, as well as reports from the secret police compiled by Adolf Schmidt.
