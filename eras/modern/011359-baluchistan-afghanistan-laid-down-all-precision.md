# Baluchistan and Afghanistan: Boundary Demarcation and Administration

The demarcation of the boundaries between Afghanistan and Baluchistan, and the subsequent administration of these regions, represents a significant achievement in scientific geographical mapping. Since 1880, systematic surveys have been conducted to define the politico-geographical limits of empire and establish the responsibilities of government in the region. These efforts, which include the work of the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1885 and the surveys of Baluchistan, have produced professional maps extending from the Oxus to the Indus and from the Indus to the Euphrates.

## The Afghan-Baluchistan Boundary

The dividing line between Baluchistan and Afghanistan is laid down with precision, following a specific geographical course. Starting from Domandi at the junction of the Kundar and Gomal rivers, the boundary follows the Kundar stream south-west for approximately 40 miles. It then diverges northwards for about 100 miles toward Lake Ab-i-Istada before striking the Kadanai river and turning the northern spurs of the Toba plateau.

In the region west of the Kojak, the boundary departs from the river to follow the western edge of the Toba plateau, which reaches a height of 8,000 feet. It descends to the plains near the railway station of New Chaman, returns to the hills 40 miles south of Chaman, and is defined by hill ranges southwards to Nushki. From Nushki, the boundary cuts across the Lora hamun, leaving the frontier post of Chagai to Baluchistan. Between this point and the Malik Siah Koh, the line is based partly on the central mountainous water-divide and partly on straight lines through the desert south of the Gaud-i-Zirreh salt swamps. This path places the boundary 50 miles south of the Helmund, effectively shutting off the valley and the approach to Seistan from Baluchistan, except for a connected line of desert route between Nushki and Seistan that remains open in ordinary seasons.

## The Eastern Afghan Frontier

The eastern borders of Afghanistan are defined by a principle of maintaining a width of neutral or independent territory between Afghanistan and British India. To the north-east, the Pamir extension reaches nearly 75 deg. E., following the water-divide to the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir and the water-parting of the Hindu Kush. The boundary leaves the Hindu Kush near the Dorah Pass, dividing Kafiristan from Bajour and Chitral, and crossing the Peshawar-Kabul route at Lundi-Khana.

This independent belt includes Bajour, Swat, and portions of Mohmand territory north of the Kabul river. This system of demarcation continues along the full length of the eastern border until it touches the northern limits of Baluchistan on the Gomal river. From the Gomal onward, Baluchistan itself serves as the intervening state between Afghanistan and British India.

## Administration of Baluchistan

Baluchistan is a country within the borders of British India, extending from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. Unlike Afghanistan, which is a distinct entity, Baluchistan's independence is qualified, as it is subject to British control.

The region is divided into two main divisions:
*   **British Baluchistan:** An official province of British India under a chief commissioner. It covers 9,403 sq. m. and consists primarily of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the treaty of Gandamak in 1879 (including the districts of Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali), which were formally declared part of British India in 1887.
*   **Foreign Territories:** These are under the superintendence of the agent to the governor-general of India. This area is further divided into:
    *   **Directly-administered districts:** Including the Bolan Pass and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, totaling 36,401 sq. m.
    *   **Native States:** Including Las Bela and the state of Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran), covering 79,382 sq. m.
    *   **Tribal Areas:** Possessed by the Bugti and Marri tribes, covering 7,129 sq. m.

The agent to the governor-general, supported by political assistants, exercises supreme control over the province. In the northern section of Baluchistan, the "red line" at the foot of the frontier hills defines the border of British India, creating a buffer system of independent tribes. While the Indian government minimizes interference in the internal jurisdiction of tribal chiefs in the Suliman Range, the occupation of a line of posts on the Zhob river ensures British command of the hills and communication with Afghanistan.

## Economy and Geography

The region is characterized by a rainless climate, with flora continuous with the desert flora of northern and eastern Africa. The economy of the native states is exemplified by the khan of Kalat, whose annual revenues of approximately Rs.500,000 derive from agricultural revenue and subsidies, including government rent for the Quetta district and transit duties in the Bolan. In 1899, Kalat's imports were valued at Rs.700,000 and exports at Rs.505,000.

The final settlement of the southern Afghan boundaries and the completion of surveys in Makran and Baluchistan have facilitated the reopening of old trade routes between India and Seistan.
