# Collected Editions of Literary and Academic Works

The publication of collected editions serves as a primary method for preserving the complete output of authors, philosophers, and historians. These editions vary significantly in scope, ranging from comprehensive multi-volume sets to specialized collections of specific genres, such as speeches or correspondence.

## Variations in Comprehensive Editions
Comprehensive editions often exist in multiple formats to suit different audiences. For certain authors, these may be divided by physical size, such as quarto and octavo editions. One such example includes a quarto edition in eight volumes begun in 1792 under Dr. F. Lawrence, with the final volumes completed in 1827 by Dr. Walter King. A corresponding octavo edition in sixteen volumes was also produced, beginning at the author's death; its first eight volumes appeared in 1803 and were reissued in 1808, while the remaining volumes were published in 1813 and 1827. Further iterations include a two-volume octavo published in 1834 and a nine-volume Boston edition from 1839, the latter of which includes a reprint of the *Account of the European Settlements in America* not found in the English version.

Other authors have seen their works collected in various international editions. The collected works of Descartes were published in Latin in Amsterdam (8 vols., 1670-1683), Frankfort (7 vols., 1697), and by Elzevir (9 vols., 1713). French editions include a 13-volume set from 1724-1729, an 11-volume republication by Victor Cousin (1824-1826), and a later version by C. Adam and P. Tannery. A complete French edition was also initiated in the Romance Library starting in 1907.

## Specialized and Posthumous Collections
In many instances, a writer's output is divided into specific categories rather than a single unified set. For example, the works of Edmund Burke include *The Correspondence of Edmund Burke* (4 vols., 1844) and *The Speeches of Edmund Burke, in the House of Commons and Westminster Hall* (4 vols., 1816). Other specialized collections of Burke's speeches include those on Irish Affairs arranged by Matthew Arnold (1881) and those on American Taxation and Conciliation edited by F.G. Selby (1895).

Posthumous collections are also common. Thomas De Quincey's works were published in several iterations, including *Writings Published and Unpublished* (14 vols., 1853-1860) and a standard English edition, *The Collected Writings of Thomas De Quincey* (14 vols., 1889-1890), edited by David Masson. Further posthumous materials include *The Uncollected Writings of Thomas De Quincey* (2 vols., 1890) and *The Posthumous Writings of Thomas De Quincey* (2 vols., 1891-1893), edited by A. H. Japp.

## Editorial Contributions and Revisions
The accuracy and scope of collected works often depend on the editor's research. John Bigelow produced editions of Benjamin Franklin's autobiography and complete works (10 octavo volumes, 1887-1889) based on personal investigations of manuscript sources in France and elsewhere. These supplanted an earlier edition by Jared Sparks (1836-1840), and were later supplanted themselves by an edition by A.H. Smythe (10 vols., 1905-1907).

Similarly, Macaulay's works were collected in 1866 by Lady Trevelyan in eight volumes, with the final volume containing speeches and the *Lays of Ancient Rome*. A later "Albany" edition was published in 12 volumes in 1898.

## Incomplete or Uncollected Works
Not all authors have a complete collected edition. In some cases, a collected edition has never been published, though specific subsets of work may exist. For one such author, the *Tomus Anglicanus* (Basel, 1577) contains only the writings produced while in England. Similarly, while the poems of Donne were edited by E. K. Chambers in 1896, his prose works have not been collected.

## Bibliographical Manuals
The study of these collections is supported by bibliographical literature. Early general manuals include Brunet's *Manuel du libraire* (first published 1810) and W.T. Lowndes' *Bibliographer's Manual of English Literature* (first published 1834). While these were once the primary resources for collectors, they have largely been replaced by more detailed handbooks on special books.
