## Accounting and Business Journals
In the field of business, the journal serves as a book of first-entry for transactions. Due to the growth of business, it became impractical to maintain a single book for all transactions. Consequently, two primary methods of recording emerged. In continental European countries, the journal is generally treated as an intermediate book where transactions are focused before being recorded in a ledger. 

In North America and Great Britain, the practice is to split the journal into numerous specialized books of first-entry, each dedicated to a specific class of transaction. These include:
* **Sales journal (sales book or day book):** For recording goods sold.
* **Bought journal (invoice book or purchases book):** For recording goods purchased.
* **Returns inwards book:** For recording goods sold that were subsequently returned by customers.
* **Returns outwards book:** For recording goods purchased that were subsequently returned.
* **Bills receivable book:** For recording bills of exchange received from debtors.
* **Bills payable book:** For recording bills of exchange given to creditors.
