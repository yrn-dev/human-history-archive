# Ministry of War

In the context of the Dual Monarchy, the Ministry of War serves as one of three joint ministries established for the administration of common affairs, alongside the ministries of finance and foreign affairs. The authority of this joint ministry is restricted to these common affairs; it is prohibited from exercising influence or directing government affairs that affect only one of the two halves of the monarchy.

## Administrative Role and Authority
The Minister of War acts as the head for the administration of all military affairs. However, this jurisdiction excludes the Hungarian *Honveds* and the Austrian *Landwehr*, as these specific forces are committed to the ministries for national defence of their respective states. 

While the Minister of War manages administration, the supreme command of the army is vested in the monarch. The sovereign possesses the power to take all measures regarding the entire army, ensuring that the total armed power of the Dual Monarchy remains a single entity under the monarch's supreme command.

## Budget and Expenditure
The Ministry of War is a primary component of the common budget, which covers expenditures necessary for the army, the conduct of foreign affairs, and the ministry of finance. This joint budget is funded through the revenues of the joint ministries, net proceeds from customs, and proportional contributions (quotas) from the two states.

The proportional contributions for joint expenditure were historically divided as follows:
*   **Until 1897:** Austria contributed 70% and Hungary contributed 30%.
*   **1897 to July 1907:** The quota was 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary.
*   **1907:** Hungary's contribution was increased to 36.4%.

Additionally, 2% of total charges are debited to Hungary due to the incorporation of the former military frontier into that state.
