# The Bolivia-Brazil Boundary

The boundary between Bolivia and Brazil is rooted in the limits established between Portuguese and Spanish colonies. These limits were originally determined by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750), and were subsequently modified by treaties in 1867 and 1903.

## The Paraguay River Sector
The boundary line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river, located at latitude 28 deg. 08' 35" S. From this point, the line ascends the Paraguay river to a point on the west bank situated 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra. At this location, the boundary turns inland for 4 kilometres to a point at latitude 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and longitude 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W.

This specific inland diversion from the Paraguay river was implemented to ensure that Corumba, Fort Coimbra, and other settlements located on the west bank remained within Brazilian jurisdiction. In 1903, this section was further modified when Brazil ceded approximately 1158 sq. m. to Bolivia to facilitate better commercial facilities on the Paraguay.

## Northern and Eastern Course
From the point inland of Fort Coimbra, the boundary follows an irregular course toward the North and East of North. It passes through Lakes Uberaba, Gaiba (or Mandiore), and Mandiore. The line then proceeds up the San Matias river and continues North along the Sierra Ricardo Franco until it reaches the headwaters of the Rio Verde, which is a tributary of the Guapore.

The boundary then follows the Guapore, Verde, Madeira, and Mamore rivers. It continues down to the mouth of the Abuna, at approximately latitude 9 deg. 44' S., as established by the treaty of 1903.

## Colonial Frontiers and the Treaty of Petropolis
The section of the boundary reaching the mouth of the Abuna is part of the original colonial frontier. This original line extended down the Madeira river to a point midway between the Amazon and the Beni, then ran due West to the Javary. 

A treaty in 1867 modified this starting point to the mouth of the Beni (latitude 10 deg. 20' S.) and established a straight line to the source of the Javary. This modification granted a large area of territory to Brazil. However, following the discovery of valuable rubber forests in the upper Purus, Brazilians invaded the area and requested another modification.

This dispute was resolved in 1903 via the treaty of Petropolis. The resulting boundary line ascends the Abuna river to latitude 10 deg. 20' S., then follows that parallel West to the Rapirran river. The line follows the Rapirran to its principal source, then runs due West to the Ituxy river. It follows the Ituxy West to its source, then proceeds to the source of Bahia Creek. From there, it follows Bahia Creek to the Aquiry (or Acre) river and ascends the Aquiry to its source.

## Regional Context of Bolivia
Bolivia is an inland republic in South America that was formerly known as Upper Peru or the province of Charcas, once part of the Spanish vice-royalty of Peru. It is the third largest political division on the continent, extending approximately from 22 deg. 50' to 9 deg. 44' S. latitude and from 58 deg. to 70 deg. W. longitude.

Bolivia is bounded by Chile and Peru to the West, Paraguay and Argentina to the South, and Brazil to the North and East. Due to repeated losses of territory in boundary disputes with neighboring states, area estimates vary. Official estimates list the area as 703,633 or 640,226 sq. m., while Supan (1904) estimates it at 515,156 sq. m.
