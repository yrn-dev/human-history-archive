# Nadir Shah of Persia

Nadir Shah was the Turcoman ruler of Persia who reigned from 1736 to 1747. He is characterized in historical records as one of the tyrants of Persia who utilized the spoils of the people to enrich the tomb of Ali, which features a copper dome with massy gilding.

## Invasion of Hindustan
In 1738, Nadir Shah launched an invasion of Hindustan, advancing from the north through the Khyber pass. During this campaign, he broke every army that opposed him, eventually capturing and sacking the city of Delhi. He carried away an enormous booty from the city, leaving northern India in a state of utter collapse. This instability paved the way for six further successful plundering raids into North India from Afghanistan over the following twenty years.

While Nadir Shah was encamped near Kandahar, he established his authority in Baluchistan by dispatching several detachments into the province. He initially maintained Abdulla Khan in the government of the country. However, following the death of Abdulla Khan in a battle against the nawabs of Sind, the government passed to Abdulla's eldest son, Haji Mahommed Khan. Haji Mahommed Khan was noted for a tyrannical and licentious lifestyle and the imposition of oppressive taxation, which alienated his subjects.

Nasir Khan, the second son of Abdulla Khan, had accompanied Nadir Shah to Delhi and gained the monarch's confidence. Upon returning to Kalat, Nasir Khan was welcomed as a deliverer by the population. After attempting to reason with his brother to no effect, Nasir Khan stabbed Haji Mahommed Khan to the heart. Nasir Khan then ascended the *musnud* and reported these events to Nadir Shah. In 1739, the Shah expressed satisfaction with the news and issued a *firman* appointing Nasir Khan as the *beglar begi* (prince of princes) of all Baluchistan.

## Domestic Activities and Infrastructure
Nadir Shah was involved in the reconstruction of Persian sites. In 1731, he rebuilt the Chehel Situn (forty pillars) palace in the neighborhood of Bagh i Shah, near the town of Ashref in the province of Mazandaran. This palace had previously been destroyed by the Afghans in 1723, though it was already in ruins by 1743.

## Assassination and Legacy
The end of Nadir Shah's reign came through a conspiracy involving his own relations and guards. On June 8, 1747 (11th of Jemady ul Sany, A.H. 1160), near the city of Khojoon—located three days' journey from Meshed—Mohammed Kuly Khan Ardemee and the *Kushukchee Bashee* (captain of the guard) entered into an oath with seventy members of the *Kukshek* (guard) to assassinate the Shah.

Following his death, Afghanistan became an independent state. In the aftermath of his reign, some of his former officials established their own power; for example, by approximately 1765, Mulla 'Ali Shah, who had served as an admiral under Nadir Shah, established himself as an independent chief on the island of Kishm and part of Hormuz.

Additionally, the period of Nadir Shah's influence is associated with the immigration of Kizilbashes (Shiitic Turkish immigrants from Persia) into Afghanistan around 1737. These "Red-Heads," so named for their red caps, became known as industrious people serving as scribes, physicians, and traders, and they formed the bulk of the amir's cavalry.
