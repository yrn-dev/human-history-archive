# Cockscomb Mountains

The Cockscomb Mountains are a mountain range located in British Honduras. They form part of a succession of ridges that rise parallel to the coast of the region.

## Geography and Topography
The Cockscomb Mountains are situated inland from the coast, positioned beyond the Manatee Hills, which reach heights of 800 to 1000 feet. The Cockscomb Mountains are significantly higher, reaching approximately 4000 feet. 

The region is characterized by a variety of terrains. The approach to the coast involves coral reefs and islets known as cays. Moving inland, the land begins as low, swampy ground covered in tropical jungle and mangroves. This is followed by a narrow belt of rich alluvial land, no more than a mile wide. Beyond this belt are "pine ridges," which are vast tracts of arid, sandy land covered with red pines. Further inland, the landscape transitions into "broken ridges" and then "cahoon ridges," the latter of which feature deep rich soil and numerous palm trees. Broad savannas, containing clumps of trees, precede the mountains.

## Hydrology and Exploration
The Cockscomb Mountains serve as the source for sixteen streams large enough to be classified as rivers, which descend from the mountains to the sea between the Sarstoon and the Hondo.

In 1879, Henry Fowler, the colonial secretary of British Honduras, explored the uninhabited country between the coast south of Deep river and Garbutt's Rapids. His findings indicated that the area consists of forests containing valuable timber in the east and open, undulating grasslands providing fine pasturage in the west. The elevation of this explored area varies between 1200 and 3300 feet.

## Geology and Mineralogy
The geology, flora, and fauna of the region are noted to be materially similar to those of neighboring regions in Central America. Exploration has revealed the presence of auriferous quartz and traces of other minerals; however, these have not been found in quantities sufficient to justify the cost of mining.

## Historical Documentation
Information regarding the Cockscomb Mountains and the exploration of British Honduras has been recorded in several publications. J. Bellamy authored a piece titled "An Expedition to the Cockscomb Mountains," published in volume xi of the *Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society* in 1889. Other relevant records include H. Fowler's 1879 work, *A Narrative of a Journey across the unexplored Portion of British Honduras*, and the *Handbook of British Honduras* (1892) by P.B. Wright and L.W. Bristowe.
