# Capillary Bronchitis

Capillary bronchitis, also referred to as broncho-pneumonia, is a severe form of bronchitis that occurs when inflammation primarily affects or spreads into the minute ramifications of the bronchial tubes. These tubes are in immediate relation to the air-cells of the lungs.

## Clinical Presentation and Symptoms
When bronchitis assumes the capillary form, the symptoms associated with the disease become greatly intensified. The patient's life is placed in imminent peril because the entrance of air into the lungs is interrupted, which prevents the due aeration of the blood. 

Physical manifestations include:
*   **Respiratory Distress:** Respiration becomes extremely rapid and laboured, with the nostrils dilating during each effort. The patient may experience suffocative paroxysms that make it impossible to remain in a recumbent posture.
*   **Physical Appearance:** The surface of the body appears dusky or pale, and the lips become livid.
*   **Systemic Effects:** There is an increase in restlessness and feverishness. The cough becomes incessant.
*   **Progression:** If the chest is not cleared through expectoration and coughing, the patient's strength fails, leading to delirium and somnolence.

This progression can occur within a few days, and in some cases—particularly among the very young—it can prove fatal within forty-eight hours.

## High-Risk Populations
Acute bronchitis is considered a severe ailment, but it is pre-eminently dangerous at the extremes of life. Mortality statistics indicate it is one of the most fatal diseases for infants and aged people. This high mortality is attributed to the feeble frames of these populations and the specific tendency for the disease to assume the capillary form in these age groups, which often proves quickly fatal. Consequently, early attention to any evidence of bronchitis in the aged or very young is of critical importance.

Other groups at risk for severe acute bronchitis include:
*   Persons addicted to intemperance.
*   Individuals with diseases affecting respiratory functions, such as heart disease or consumption, for whom the attack is an alarming complication that increases the embarrassment of breathing.
*   Children who have suffered from conditions associated with bronchial irritation, such as whooping-cough and measles.

## Treatment Objectives and Management
When treating the capillary form of bronchitis, the primary objectives are to maintain the strength of the patient and to secure the expulsion of morbid secretion from the fine bronchi.

Management strategies include:
*   **Stimulants:** These are called for from the beginning of treatment.
*   **Medical Intervention:** If the cough is ineffectual at relieving the bronchial tubes, an emetic dose of sulphate of zinc may be administered to produce a good effect.
*   **Nutritional Support:** Due attention must be paid to the patient's nourishment throughout the attack.
*   **Convalescence:** Because recovery can be slow, especially in elderly patients, stimulants and tonics may be prescribed during the convalescent period.

## General Context of Bronchitis
Bronchitis is defined as the inflammation of the mucous membrane of the bronchial tubes. It is categorized into specific bronchitis (occurring in infectious disorders such as pneumonia, influenza, measles, or diphtheria) and non-specific bronchitis (resulting from mechanical or chemical irritation, or extension from above). 

While capillary bronchitis is a severe manifestation of the acute form—which often arises from exposure to cold and damp or sudden atmospheric changes—there is also a chronic form. Chronic bronchitis may result from repeated acute attacks or exist independently, often manifesting as a "winter cough" that recurs during colder seasons.
