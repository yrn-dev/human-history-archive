# Jerome Napoleon, King of Westphalia

Jerome Bonaparte (1784–1860) was a member of the Bonaparte family and the King of Westphalia. Born in Ajaccio on November 15, 1784, he experienced the early years of the French Revolution before receiving an education at Juilly. In 1800, he joined his brother, the First Consul of France.

## Early Career and Personal Life
During his time in the Consular Guard, Jerome was known for an affectionate but impetuous nature, which included fighting a duel with the younger brother of General Davout, resulting in a wound. He later transitioned to the navy, where he cruised in the West Indies. While blockaded by a British cruiser, he left his vessel and traveled through the United States.

In Baltimore, Jerome married Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a merchant named William Patterson. Because Jerome was a minor at the time, this marriage was viewed by Napoleon as a disregard for the laws of France and military discipline. Napoleon refused to recognize the legality of the union and ordered Elizabeth to be excluded from his states when Jerome brought her to Europe in 1805. Although Pope Pius VII refused to declare the marriage void and it remained valid under American law, Jerome was forced by his brother to separate from his wife. Elizabeth returned to Baltimore, where she lived until her death in 1879. The only child of this marriage was Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870), born in England.

## Reign in Westphalia
Following his naval service—which included a 1805 command in the Mediterranean and a 1806 mission to the West Indies—Jerome was made a prince of France. He commanded a division of South Germans during the 1806 campaign and accepted the surrender of several Prussian towns after the Battle of Jena.

Following an imperial decree that annulled his marriage to Elizabeth Patterson, Jerome married Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the Treaty of Tilsit, signed July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia. 

His reign was characterized by a fondness for luxury, significant debt, and numerous affairs, leading to frequent rebukes from the emperor. However, the kingdom saw several administrative advancements due to its connection with France. These included the introduction of the *Code Napoleon*, the abolition of feudalism, the provision of impulses for higher education, and the freeing of the Jews from repressive laws.

## Later Life and Descendants
After his time as king, Jerome served as the governor of the Invalides. He died in 1860. 

His descendants include a branch of the family known as the Bonapartes of Baltimore, stemming from his union with Elizabeth Patterson. Their son, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), served in the French army in Italy and the Crimea. This line continued with Prince Napoleon Victor Jerome Frederic (born 1862), who became the recognized head of the French Bonapartist party, and Prince Louis Napoleon, an officer in the Russian army who served as governor of the Caucasus in 1906.

Jerome's daughter, Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine (1820–1904), was born in Trieste. After a brief near-betrothal to her cousin Louis Napoleon, she married Prince Anatole Demidov, though they separated within five years. She later resided in Paris, where she acted as a patron of letters and art and performed the honors of the Elysee before the marriage of Louis Napoleon.

## Historiography
The life and reign of Jerome Napoleon have been documented in several historical works. Baron Albert du Casse authored *Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine Catherine* (1861–1866) and *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883). Other notable texts include *Konig Jerome Napoleon* by M.M. Kaisenberg, *The Bonaparte-Patterson Marriage* by W.T.R. Saffell, and *Jerome Napoleon, roi de Westfalie* (1902) by A. Martinet. 

Additional primary source materials were published by Baron du Casse, including the *Corresp. inedite de la reine Catherine de Westphalie* (1888–1893) and the *Journal de la reine Catherine de Westphalie* (1893). While some historians note that Ducasse's editing was occasionally lacking in care, these documents provided significant insight into the entourage of Napoleon I.
