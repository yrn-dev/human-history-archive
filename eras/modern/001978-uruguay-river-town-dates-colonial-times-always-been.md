# Uruguay River

The Uruguay river serves as a significant geographic and political boundary in South America, particularly defining the eastern border of the Argentine Republic and influencing the regional geography of Brazil.

## Geographic Boundaries and Regional Impact
The Uruguay river forms the boundary line between the eastern Argentine Republic and Uruguay. In Argentina, it defines the eastern limit of the province of Entre Rios. This province, which is sometimes referred to as the "garden of Argentina," is bounded to the east by the Uruguay river and to the west and south by the Parana river. Together, these two rivers create a region sometimes described as the Argentine Mesopotamia.

In the province of Corrientes, located in the north-east of the Argentine Republic, several streams flow into the Uruguay river. These streams often have their sources in the province's extensive lagoons, such as the Ibera lagoon, which sends its waters in both directions toward the Parana and Uruguay rivers.

## Economic and Transport Importance
The Uruguay river provides exceptional facilities for the shipment of produce. In the province of Entre Rios, the river is utilized alongside the Parana river to transport agricultural and livestock products. The province's principal industry is stock-raising, with exports including wool, mutton, hides, live cattle, horses, and various meat products such as beef extract, salted meats, tinned meats, and jerked beef. Agricultural exports include fruits, barley, Indian corn, and wheat, as well as firewood, gypsum, and lime.

Transportation is further supported by rail infrastructure. In Entre Rios, a trunk line runs from Parana to Concepcion del Uruguay, and another railway line follows the Uruguay river northward from Concordia into Corrientes. In the province of Corrientes, the town of Monte Caseros is situated on the Uruguay river and serves as the eastern terminus of the Argentine North-Eastern rail, acting as a point from which cattle are shipped to Brazil.

## Border Towns and Military Significance
The proximity to the Uruguay river has historically conferred military importance upon nearby settlements. Bagé, a town and municipality in the Brazilian state of Rio Grande do Sul, is located only 25 miles from the Uruguay frontier. Because of this nearness, Bagé has always been considered a place of military importance. The town dates from colonial times and has been a site of conflict, including its capture by the Argentine general Lavalle in 1827. It has featured prominently in most of the civil wars of Argentina and is frequently visited by Uruguayan revolutionists.

Geographically, Bagé is situated in a hilly region 774 feet above sea level. This area serves as a watershed for southern Rio Grande do Sul; while some streams flow south-east and east toward the Atlantic coast, others flow south-west and north-west to the Uruguay river.
