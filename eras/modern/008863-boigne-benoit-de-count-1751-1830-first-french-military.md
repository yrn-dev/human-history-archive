# BOIGNE, BENOIT DE, COUNT (1751-1830)

Benoit de Boigne was a French military adventurer and the first of his kind to operate in India. Born on March 8, 1751, in Chambery, Savoy, he was the son of a merchant who dealt in furs.

## Early Military Career
Boigne's military experience began in 1768 when he joined the Irish Brigade in France. Following this period, he entered the service of Russia, during which time he was captured by the Turks. Upon learning of the wealth available in India, he traveled to the region. His initial tenure in India included a short period of service with the East India Company, from which he eventually resigned.

## Service Under Mahadji Sindhia
In 1784, Boigne joined Mahadji Sindhia. His primary objective was to train Sindhia's troops using European methods of warfare. The effectiveness of this training was demonstrated during the battles of Chaksana and Lalsot; while the rest of the Mahratta army suffered defeat at the hands of the Rajputs, Boigne and his two battalions were able to hold the field.

Boigne's military fortunes peaked during the battle of Agra in 1788, where he restored the fortunes of the Mahratta and established Mahadji Sindhia as the undisputed master of Hindostan. As a result of this success, Boigne was granted command of a brigade consisting of ten infantry battalions. He led this force to victories at Merta and Patan in 1790.

Following these victories, Boigne was appointed commander-in-chief of Sindhia's army and was permitted to raise two additional brigades of disciplined infantry. In 1793, he led these forces to victory against Holkar's army at the battle of Lakhairi.

## Later Years and Retirement
Following the death of Mahadji Sindhia in 1794, Boigne possessed the means to make himself the master of Hindostan; however, he chose to remain loyal to Daulat Rao Sindhia. By 1795, Boigne's health began to decline, leading him to resign his command.

He returned to Europe in 1796, bringing with him a fortune of L400,000. During the era of Napoleon, Boigne lived in retirement. In his later years, he was greatly honoured by Louis XVIII. He died on June 21, 1830.
