# Roman Catholic Higher Education and Specialized Schooling

The archive contains records of various Roman Catholic educational institutions across different global regions, ranging from specialized colleges for men and women to faculties integrated into university systems. These institutions often coexist with state-funded or denominational schooling systems.

## Institutions in North America

In the United States, Roman Catholic higher education is represented in several cities. In Buffalo, Canisius College is a Jesuit institution for men. Established in 1870 and chartered in 1883, it consists of an academic (or high school) department and a college department; as of 1907, its library contained approximately 26,000 volumes. Other notable Catholic-affiliated schools in Buffalo include the Academy of the Sacred Heart, Holy Angels and St Mary's academies, and St Joseph's Collegiate Institute.

In Louisiana, several Roman Catholic colleges are listed as part of the region's higher schools. These include:
*   The College of the Immaculate Conception in New Orleans (founded 1847).
*   Jefferson College at Convent (founded 1864).
*   St Joseph's College at Baton Rouge (founded 1849).
*   St Charles College at Grand Couteau (founded 1835).

## Institutions in Europe

In England, the city of Durham is home to the Roman Catholic College of St Cuthbert, Ushaw, located 4 miles west of the city. This institution serves as the representative of the old college at Douai and houses the natural history collections of Charles Waterton. In Scotland, Dumbartonshire contains St Peter's Roman Catholic College, located in New Kilpatrick, which exists alongside several voluntary schools within a shire under school-board jurisdiction.

In France, the city of Lyons features "Catholic faculties" known as *facultés libres*. These provide instruction in the fields of science, letters, theology, and law. These exist alongside the city's state university and various specialized schools, such as the school of fine arts and the veterinary school.

## Integration with General Education Systems

The relationship between Catholic schooling and state systems varies by region:

*   **British Columbia:** The province maintains a system of common, graded, and high schools that are free, undenominational, and government-maintained. While the population includes 33,639 Roman Catholics (as of 1901), the only college in the province is the McGill University College of British Columbia.
*   **British Honduras:** In this colony, almost all primary and secondary schools are denominational. While Roman Catholics are represented among the churches, no church receives assistance from public funds, though grants-in-aid are provided to elementary schools.
*   **Dundee:** In addition to board schools, the area contains Roman Catholic and Episcopalian schools, as well as a high school for girls.
*   **Belgium:** Higher instruction is provided by state universities (Ghent and Liege) and free universities (Brussels and Louvain). The university at Louvain is the only one featuring a faculty of theology. The Belgian government also provides state-aided schools for military instruction, the arts, and commercial or professional education.
