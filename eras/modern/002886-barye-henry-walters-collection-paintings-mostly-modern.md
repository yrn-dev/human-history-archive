# Antoine Louis Barye and the Henry Walters Collection

## Antoine Louis Barye
Antoine Louis Barye (1796–1875) was a French sculptor recognized as the creator of a new class of art and the preeminent artist of animal life within the French school. Born in Paris on September 24, 1796, Barye began his professional life as a goldsmith, a path similar to many Renaissance sculptors. His formal education included studies under the painter Gros and the sculptor Bosio, leading to his admission to the Ecole des Beaux Arts in 1818.

Barye's specific artistic direction emerged in 1823 while he was employed by the goldsmith Fauconnier. By observing wild beasts at the Jardin des Plantes, he began producing vigorous pencil drawings and subsequent sculptures of animals on both small and large scales. He exhibited "Tiger devouring a Crocodile" in 1831 and developed his own distinct style with the "Lion and Snake" in 1832.

Throughout his career, Barye faced a constant struggle with poverty, yet he continued to exhibit animal studies. His work is characterized by a feeling for antique beauty and a spirit of true romance, exemplified by pieces such as "Lapitha and Centaur" (1848) and "Theseus and the Minotaur" (1847). He was equally proficient in small-scale sculpture, capturing animals in familiar poses, as well as large-scale works. Notable large works include the Lion of the Column of July (plaster model cast in 1839), various tigers and lions located in the gardens of the Tuileries, and the 1854 groups representing Order, Strength, Peace, and War. In 1852, he produced a bronze piece titled "Jaguar devouring a Hare."

Recognition arrived late in Barye's life; he was appointed professor at the museum in 1854 and elected to the Academy of Fine Arts in 1868. He died on June 25, 1875. His influence extended to subsequent artists such as Gardet, Cain, Peter, and Fremiet.

## The Henry Walters Collection
The Henry Walters collection is housed in the Walters Art Gallery, located at the south end of Washington Place. The collection is diverse, consisting of:
* Paintings, the majority of which are by modern French artists.
* Japanese and Chinese paintings.
* Chinese and Japanese bronzes, enamels, and porcelain.
* Ivory carvings.

## Monument Square and Related Works
The Walters Art Gallery is situated near a white marble monument dedicated to George Washington, located at the intersection of Mount Vernon Place and Washington Place (a continuation of N. Charles Street). This monument, the oldest of its kind in the United States, had its cornerstone laid in 1815 and was completed in 1829. It features a 130 ft. high Doric column on a base 50 ft. square and 24 ft. high, topped by a 16 ft. high statue of Washington.

The square surrounding the Washington monument contains several other artistic works, including:
* A figure of a lion created by Antoine L. Barye.
* Bronze pieces representing Force, Order, War, and Peace.
* Statues of John Eager Howard (by Emmanuel Fremiet), Roger Brooke Taney (by W. H. Rinehart), and George Peabody (a replica by W. W. Story of a work in London).
