# Brest

## Geography and Layout
Brest is a fortified seaport located in western France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement within the department of Finistère and is situated 155 miles west-north-west of Rennes by rail. The town is positioned to the north of a magnificent landlocked bay and is built upon the slopes of two hills. These hills are divided by the river Penfeld. The area of the town located on the left bank of the river is considered Brest proper, while the section on the right bank is known as Recouvrance. Additionally, the town has extensive suburbs located to the east.

The topography of the hill-sides is characterized by extreme steepness in certain areas. Consequently, movement from the lower town to the upper town often requires the use of flights of steps. This geography results in a layout where the ground storey of one house may be level with the second or third storey of an adjacent building.

## Urban Features and Landmarks
The primary street in Brest is the rue de Siam, named in honor of the Siamese embassy sent to Louis XIV. This street ends at a remarkable swing-bridge that crosses the mouth of the Penfeld, which was constructed in 1861. Along the southern shore of the town lies the Cours d'Ajot, described as one of the finest promenades of its kind in France. Named after the engineer who built it, the promenade is planted with trees and features marble statues of Abundance and Neptune, created by Antoine Coysevox.

The town's only building of architectural interest is the castle. This structure commands the entrance to the river and features a donjon and seven towers dating from the 12th to the 16th centuries.

## Naval and Commercial Infrastructure
Brest is the capital of one of France's five naval arrondissements. Its naval port is largely excavated from rock and extends along both banks of the Penfeld. The port's facilities include:
* Shipbuilding yards and repairing docks.
* Magazines, workshops, and gun-foundries.
* Large naval barracks, naval schools of various types, and training ships.
* An important naval hospital.

The naval port employs approximately 7,000 workmen. Separated from the town by the Cours d'Ajot is the commercial port, which includes a tidal port with docks.

## Administration and Education
Brest serves as the seat of a sub-prefect. The town's legal and commercial administration is extensive, comprising:
* Tribunals of first instance and of commerce.
* Two naval tribunals and a tribunal of maritime commerce.
* A chamber of commerce and a board of trade-arbitrators.

Educational institutions in the town include lycées for both girls and boys, as well as a school of industry and commerce.

## Population
As of 1906, the population of the town of Brest was 71,163, while the population of the commune was 85,294.
