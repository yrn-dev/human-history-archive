# Honoré de Balzac: Travel and Personal Affairs

## Travel and Meetings with Madame Hanska
During a twenty-year period of strenuous literary production, Honoré de Balzac engaged in frequent travel throughout France. This included constant visits to the Carrauds at Frapesle in Berry and the Margonne family at Sache in Touraine. 

Balzac's travels also extended beyond France for personal meetings with Madame Hanska. In 1833, he traveled to Neuchatel for their first meeting. He later visited Geneva for a second meeting, and in 1835, he traveled to Vienna for a third. Correspondence with Madame Hanska, including a portrait of her, was later published in the second volume of *Oeuvres posthumes* in 1906.

## Financial Ventures and Property
Balzac's life was characterized by a recurring process of "raising the wind," which involved contracting new debts to settle old ones. In 1838, he undertook a journey to Sardinia with the intent of making his fortune. His plan was to melt silver out of the slag-heaps of Roman mines; while this project appeared feasible and was actually accomplished, Balzac was anticipated in the venture.

In the year preceding his trip to Sardinia, Balzac purchased ground at Ville d'Avray to escape Paris apartments. There, he constructed his villa, Les Jardies, at an expense that was great, though perhaps exaggerated. This villa has become a significant part of the Balzacian legend.

## Legal and Political Activities
Balzac faced frequent legal difficulties resulting from his rash and complicated literary engagements, as well as his tendency to disregard them based on whim. The most serious of these legal issues was a law-suit in 1836 with the *Revue de Paris*.

He also attempted to enter politics. In 1831 and again in 1834, he considered standing for election as Deputy. In 1834, he actually ran for the position at Angouleme and Cambrai, though it remains uncertain if he received any votes. Additionally, Balzac made several attempts to become a candidate for the Academy. He withdrew on several occasions before the voting took place. When he finally stood for election in 1849, he obtained only two votes.

## Bibliography and Works
The bibliography of Balzac is described as extraordinarily complicated. His works were issued in various forms and installments during his lifetime. The earliest *Comedie humaine* was published between 1842 and 1846 in sixteen volumes. For many years, the most well-known edition consisted of fifty-five small 24mos published by Michel (Calmann) Levy; this edition included the *Oeuvres de jeunesse* but did not follow the author's final disposition and excluded miscellaneous works.

The *Edition definitive*, published from 1869 onwards, includes the miscellaneous works in four volumes and an invaluable volume of correspondence. Attached to this edition is the *Histoire des oeuvres* (1875 and later) by M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul, who also wrote *Autour de Balzac* and *Une Page perdue de Balzac*. In 1893, the *Repertoire des oeuvres de Balzac* was added, which tracks the history of the personages of the *Comedie* under separate articles.
