# Thiothrix and Cladothrix

Within the classification of bacteria, the order Trichobacterinae consists of organisms whose vegetative bodies are composed of cell-filaments, either branched or unbranched. The segments of these filaments separate as swarm-cells, known as gonidia. Within this order, the family Trichobacteriaceae includes several genera characterized by rigid, non-motile, and sheathed filaments.

## Thiothrix
*Thiothrix* (Winogr.) is a genus of bacteria within the family Trichobacteriaceae. It is characterized by rigid, non-motile, sheathed filaments that contain sulphur particles. 

As a member of the sulphur bacteria, *Thiothrix* plays an important role in the circulation of sulphur in nature, appearing in environments such as estuaries, sulphur springs, and marshes. These bacteria thrive in conditions where cellulose bacteria release marsh gas, which reduces sulphates (such as gypsum) to liberate hydrogen sulphide ($\text{SH}_2$). *Thiothrix* and other sulphur bacteria oxidize this $\text{SH}_2$ and store the resulting sulphur within their own protoplasm. In instances where $\text{SH}_2$ becomes scarce, these bacteria oxidize the stored sulphur further into sulphuric acid. This acid can then combine with calcium carbonate to form sulphate once again.

In these biological processes, the bacteria utilize $\text{SH}_2$ as a respiratory substance. Rather than liberating energy as heat through the respiratory combustion of sugars, as higher plants do with carbohydrates, these bacteria generate energy by oxidizing hydrogen sulphide.

## Cladothrix
*Cladothrix* (Cohn) is another genus belonging to the family Trichobacteriaceae. Unlike *Thiothrix*, the filaments of *Cladothrix* are branched in a pseudo-dichotomous manner. These organisms are typically found as branched, filamentous, and sheathed forms.

The genus is associated with a branched fruticose zoogloea. Certain species, such as *Cladothrix dichotoma*, exhibit specific seasonal behaviors; at certain times, the organism breaks up into a number of separate cells. These cells develop a tuft of cilia, allowing them to escape from the sheath. This process is noted for its similarity to the production of zoospores commonly found in many filamentous algae.
