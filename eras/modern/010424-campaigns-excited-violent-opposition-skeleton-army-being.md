# The Salvation Army and the "Skeleton Army"

The Salvation Army is a quasi-military organization founded by William Booth (1829–), who served as its "general." Born in Nottingham on April 10, 1829, Booth experienced a strong religious turn at age fifteen under the influence of the Wesleyan Methodists, eventually becoming a local preacher. After moving to London in 1849, his passion for open-air preaching led to his severance from the Wesleyans. He later joined the Methodist New Connexion and was ordained as a minister, though he left that body in 1861 because he was not employed in the "travelling evangelization" he desired.

## Origins and Development
Booth and his wife, Catherine Mumford, whom he married in 1855, both engaged in preaching. Their work took them to Cornwall, Cardiff, and Walsall. In Walsall, they organized a "Hallelujah band" consisting of others and converted criminals who provided public testimony of their conversion. 

In 1864, Booth returned to London, where he continued his services in the open air and in tents. He founded a body that underwent several name changes—initially the East London Revival Society, then the East London Christian Mission, and the Christian Mission—before finally becoming the Salvation Army in 1878.

## Operational Methods
The Salvation Army utilizes a variety of unconventional methods to conduct its work, including:
* Holding processions and outdoor meetings.
* Visiting private houses, prisons, and public-houses.
* Conducting meetings in factories, theatres, and other unusual buildings.
* Utilizing the language of everyday life and popular song-tunes.
* Encouraging every convert to act as a daily witness for Christ in both private and public settings.

The organization's structure is quasi-military; Booth based the "Orders and Regulations" of the Army on those of the British army.

## Opposition and the "Skeleton Army"
The early "campaigns" of the Salvation Army were met with violent opposition. This resistance manifested in the organization of a "Skeleton Army," which was formed specifically to break up the Army's meetings. For many years, the followers of William Booth faced imprisonment and fines for being characterized as breakers of the peace. These disorders largely ceased after 1889.

## Global Expansion
Under the leadership of "General" Booth, who was an organizer, speaker, and indefatigable traveller, the operations of the Salvation Army expanded globally. The organization extended its reach to the United States in 1880 and to Australia in 1881. It further spread to India, Ceylon, the European continent, and other regions.
