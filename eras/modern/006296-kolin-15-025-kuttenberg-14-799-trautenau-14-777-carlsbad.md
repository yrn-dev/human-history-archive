# Towns of Bohemia: Kolin, Kuttenberg, Trautenau, and Carlsbad

The following towns are located within Bohemia, an Austrian province characterized by advanced education and a high rank in industrial production within the Austrian provinces.

## Kolin
Kolin, also known as Neu-Kolin or Nový Kolín, is a town situated on the Elbe, approximately 40 miles east of Prague by rail. As of 1900, its population was 15,025, consisting mostly of Czechs. The town was established as a royal city in the 13th century through colonization by German settlers. Its history is marked by conflict; in 1421, the men of Prague captured the town and expelled German inhabitants who refused to accept "the four articles." By 1427, Kolin declared against Prague and was besieged by Prokop the Great, eventually surrendering at the end of that year.

Kolin is most famous for the battle fought there on June 18, 1757, during the Seven Years' War. In this engagement, Austrian forces under Daun defeated the Prussians led by Frederick the Great, which resulted in the evacuation of Bohemia by the Prussians and the raising of the siege of Prague.

Architecturally, the town is noted for the early Gothic church of St Bartholomew, which was built during the second half of the 14th century. The local economy is supported by the production of syrup, spirits, potash, tin ware, starch, brewing, steam mills, and sugar-refining. Additionally, the surrounding area is recognized for the quality of its vegetables and fruit.

## Kuttenberg
Kuttenberg (Czech: Kutná Hora) is located 45 miles east-by-south of Prague. Its population in 1900 was 14,799, mostly Czech. The town originated in the first part of the 13th century due to the presence of silver mines. By the early 14th century, it had become the second most important city in Bohemia after Prague and served as a favorite residence for several Bohemian kings.

The town is home to several significant Gothic structures: the five-naved church of St Barbara (begun in 1368), the 14th-century church of St Jacob, and the Trinity church from the end of the 15th century. Other notable buildings include the Steinerne Haus, a Gothic structure serving as the town hall since 1849 and housing one of Bohemia's richest archives, and the Wälscher Hof, a royal residence and mint built at the end of the 13th century.

Kuttenberg's history is defined by religious and political strife. In 1410, Wenceslaus IV signed the decree of Kuttenberg, granting the Bohemian nation three votes in Prague University faculty elections against one for the other three nations. Later that year, the town's fanatically Catholic, mainly German mining population carried out atrocities against Hussites as reprisals for outrages in Prague; approximately 1,600 people, including clergy and magistrates from the town of Kaurim, were killed. In 1420, Emperor Sigismund used the city as a base to attack the Taborites, but the city was taken by Zizka. In 1422, imperial troops burned the city to prevent it from returning to the Taborites, though Zizka eventually retook it.

The town's prosperity declined after the richest mine flooded in 1541. Further ruin was caused by the loss of privileges during the insurrection against Ferdinand I, the Thirty Years' War, and repeated outbreaks of plague. A fire devastated the town in 1770. The silver mines, once famous in the middle ages, were abandoned by the end of the 18th century. A government attempt to reopen one mine in 1874 ended in 1903. Modern industry in Kuttenberg includes brewing, sugar-refining, leather goods, agricultural implements, and the manufacture of woollen and cotton stuffs.

## Trautenau
Trautenau is listed as one of the important towns of Bohemia with a population of 14,777. It serves as a center for the linen industry.

## Carlsbad
Carlsbad is an important town of Bohemia with a population of 14,640. It is globally celebrated for its mineral springs, which are among the most famous in the world. The town is also a major center for the porcelain industry, with production concentrated in and around the city. Additionally, porcelain-earth is extracted near Carlsbad, and the nearby Giesshubl also possesses springs of importance.
