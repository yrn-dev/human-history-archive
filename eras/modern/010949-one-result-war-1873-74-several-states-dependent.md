# The War of 1873-74

## Origins and Causes
The war of 1873-74 was the result of preparations made throughout Ashanti to attack the Fanti tribes. These preparations followed a period of political transition in Ashanti. After the death of the peace-loving monarch Kwaka Dua I in April 1867, who had encouraged trade, the state experienced an interval of civil war. He was succeeded by Kofi Karikari, who, upon being enstooled, declared, "My business shall be war."

The tensions leading to this conflict had roots in earlier disputes. In 1852, the Ashanti had attempted to reassert their authority over the Fanti within the Gold Coast protectorate. Later, in 1863, a war occurred when the British refused the king's demand to surrender a runaway slave-boy and a fugitive chief. During that conflict, the Ashanti were victorious in two battles and retired unmolested. While the governor, Mr. Richard Pine, suggested an advance on Kumasi, the British government did not allow it. This outcome increased the prestige of the Ashanti, leading Kwaka Dua I to remark that while "the white men" brought many cannon to the bush, the bush was stronger than the cannon.
