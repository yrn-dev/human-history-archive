# The Aggrandizement of the House of Austria

## The War of the Spanish Succession
The House of Austria experienced a period of significant expansion following the War of the Spanish Succession (1702–1713). This conflict was left as an "evil inheritance" to the sons of Leopold, who died in 1705: Joseph I (d. 1711) and Charles VI. While the war resulted in the further aggrandizement of the House of Austria, the outcome did not reach the extent that had been hoped for. Specifically, Austrian and British troops were unable to remove Philip V from his throne.

Furthermore, the broader European perspective held that it was not desirable for Charles VI to revive the empire of Charles V. Consequently, the Treaty of Utrecht stipulated that Spain would remain with the House of Bourbon. In exchange, the House of Austria received Naples, Sardinia, and the Spanish Netherlands.

## Strategic Shifts in Europe
The settlement of 1713–1714 and the Treaty of Karlowitz established a new starting point for Austrian history. In eastern Europe, Turkey's attempts to regain ascendancy at the expense of the Habsburgs failed. From that point forward, Turkish efforts were limited to resisting the steady development of Austria as it moved in the direction of Constantinople.

Simultaneously, the treaties of Baden, Rastadt, and Utrecht strengthened the Austrian monarchy's position in western Europe. This era saw an end to French invasions of Germany for a time. The House of Austria achieved supremacy in Italy and replaced Spanish domination in the Netherlands as a form of revenge for the attacks carried out by Louis XIV.

## Later Diplomatic Isolation and Decline
Despite these gains, Austria eventually faced a period of diplomatic isolation. Although the monarchy secured a settlement regarding the Eastern Question through diplomacy without military conflict, it emerged from the contest without friends or allies. The "Holy Alliance" of the three autocratic northern powers, which had been recemented at Muenchengraetz in 1833 and provided the decisive intervention of the tsar in 1849, was shattered by Austria's conduct during the Crimean War.

This shift led Russia to strengthen ties with Prussia, where Bismarck was developing plans that would mature in 1866. Additionally, the goodwill of Russia was assured for France in the subsequent struggle for Italy, as the alliance of Tilsit could not be revived due to Napoleon's stance on the Polish question.

## Loss of Italian Possessions
Austria's isolation became evident at the Congress of Paris, where Cavour, the Sardinian plenipotentiary, exposed the scandal of Austrian rule in Italy to Europe. This culminated in the campaign of 1859, during which Sardinia and France allied to lay the foundations for a united Italy. While Prussia threatened to intervene, this was motivated by a fear of the undue aggrandizement of France rather than a desire to support Austria.

The results of these events for Austria were two-fold:
1. **External:** Austria lost all of its Italian possessions with the exception of Venice.
2. **Internal:** The failure of the campaign necessitated constitutional concessions to conciliate public opinion.
