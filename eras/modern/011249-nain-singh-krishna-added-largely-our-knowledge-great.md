# Nain Singh and Krishna

In the modern period of scientific geographical exploration in Asia, specifically regarding regions beyond the limits of actual surveys, significant expeditions were directed toward the central depression to the north of the great elevated table-land of Tibet and the plateau itself. Among these, the trans-Himalayan explorations conducted by native surveyors attached to the Indian survey—most notably Pundits Nain Singh and Krishna—contributed extensively to the knowledge of the great plateau.

## Expeditions of Nain Singh
Nain Singh conducted explorations into the sources of the Upper Brahmaputra and the Indus between the years 1865 and 1867. Later, during 1874–1875, he followed a route that began at the eastern frontiers of Kashmir and led to the Tengri Nor lake. From there, he traveled to the city of Lhasa, where he remained for several months.

## Expeditions of Krishna
The surveyor Krishna undertook a remarkable journey between 1879 and 1882. His route extended northwards from Lhasa, passing through Tsaidam to Sachu (also known as Saitu) in Mongolia. Following this, he traveled through eastern Tibet to reach the town of Darchendo (or Tachienlu), which is situated on the borders of China along the high road between Peking and Lhasa. 

After failing in an attempt to reach India via Upper Assam, Krishna returned to the vicinity of Lhasa. He eventually crossed the Himalayas using a more westerly route. Like Nain Singh, Krishna visited the city of Lhasa.
