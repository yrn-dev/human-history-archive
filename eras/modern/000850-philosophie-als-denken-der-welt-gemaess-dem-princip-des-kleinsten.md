# Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten

## Overview
*Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten Kraftmasses* (1876) is a primary work by the German philosopher Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius. In this text and his subsequent writings, Avenarius attempted to establish a coordination between thought and action.

## Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius
Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius was born in Paris on November 19, 1843. He pursued his education in Berlin and Zuerich, eventually completing his studies at the university of Leipzig, where he graduated in 1876. Following his graduation, he became a professor of philosophy in Zuerich in 1877, a position he held until his death on August 18, 1896.

During his time at Leipzig, Avenarius was involved in the founding of the *Akademisch-philosophische Verein* and served as the first editor of the *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie*. His early academic contributions included an 1868 essay regarding the Pantheism of Spinoza, titled *Uber die beiden ersten Phasen des spinozischen Pantheismus*.

## Philosophical Principles and Empirio-criticism
In *Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten Kraftmasses*, Avenarius utilized the principle of economy of thinking, a starting point he shared with Mach. This approach was further developed in his later work, *Kritik der reinen Erfahrung* (1888-1890), in which he sought to explain the relationship between environment, knowledge, and pure experience.

Avenarius concluded that pure experience is constituted by statements that are dependent upon the environment. This philosophical system is identified as Empirio-criticism. The archive notes that Empirio-criticism is characterized as an idealistic dualism; it is neither a realistic philosophy nor can it be classified as materialism.
