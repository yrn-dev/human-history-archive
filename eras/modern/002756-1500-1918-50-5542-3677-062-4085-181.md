## The Second Battle of the Somme (1918)
The Second Battle of the Somme took place in 1918 within the Plains of Picardy, an area extending between Bapaume, Noyon, Amiens, and St. Quentin, and encompassing the valleys of the Oise, Avre, and Ancre rivers. The operations in 1918 consisted of a German Offensive in March and April, followed by a Franco-British Offensive in August.

During the August offensive, the Allies executed repeated, inter-related attacks over six weeks to flatten a salient produced by the German push, stretching from St. Quentin to beyond Albert and Montdidier. This pressure eventually led the enemy to sue for peace and an armistice to avoid an unprecedented military disaster.

By the evening of August 12, the 1st Army had captured 250 guns, 1,600 machine-guns, numerous minenwerfer, and 8,500 prisoners, including 181 officers.
