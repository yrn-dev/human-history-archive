# Alcazar of Avila

The alcazar of Avila is an old Moorish castle located in the city of Avila. The city itself is situated on the flat summit of a rocky hill, surrounded by a brown, arid, and treeless table-land characterized by immense grey boulders and lofty mountains. Avila is positioned on the right bank of the river Adaja, 54 miles west by north of Madrid.

## Institutional Uses of the Alcazar
The alcazar has served a wide variety of civic, military, and educational functions. It has been utilized as:
*   Military barracks
*   A military academy
*   An infirmary
*   A foundling hospital
*   Training schools for teachers of both sexes

Additionally, the alcazar served as the seat of a university for a period spanning from 1482 to 1807.

## The City of Avila
The alcazar is part of a city known for its well-preserved ancient walls. Constructed of brown granite and surmounted by a breastwork, these walls feature nine gateways and eighty-six towers. While a large portion of the city now extends beyond this circuit, the walls remain in excellent repair.

Avila is the seat of a bishop and is home to several significant ecclesiastical structures. The Gothic cathedral, which tradition suggests dates to 1107 (though it likely dates to the 13th or 14th century), possesses the appearance of a fortress, featuring two solid towers and embattled walls. Within the cathedral is a silver pyx created by Juan de Arphe in 1571.

Other notable religious sites include the churches of San Pedro, San Segundo, Santo Tomas, and San Vicente. These are primarily Romanesque structures from the 15th century, though San Pedro and San Vicente may have sections dating back to the 12th century. The church of Santo Tomas contains a marble monument carved by the 15th-century Florentine sculptor Domenico Fancelli for the tomb of Prince John, the only son of Isabella and Ferdinand, who died in 1497. Furthermore, the church and convent of Santa Teresa are located at the supposed birthplace of the saint (c. 1515-1582).

## Economy and Demographics
As of 1900, the population of Avila was 11,885. The city maintains a considerable trade in various goods, including pottery, leather, hats, linen, cotton goods, and agricultural products.
