# Up to 1 Year Old (A.)

In the context of thoroughbred horse breeding and racing, the first year of a horse's life is governed by specific dating conventions and developmental milestones.

## Age Dating and Early Development
The age of a thoroughbred horse is always dated from the 1st of January. Foals are typically born in the months of February, March, or April, although some high-quality horses are born in May. Under this system, a foal becomes a yearling on the 1st of January following its birth. Twelve months after becoming a yearling, the horse is considered a two-year-old.

## Transition to Racing
Many thoroughbreds begin racing in the March following their second birthday. Flat racing generally commences during the week containing the 25th of March, unless Easter occurs unusually early. 

There are differing international systems regarding the start of racing for young horses. In France, no two-year-olds are permitted to run until the 1st of August. In contrast, the English system allows for earlier starts. Some young horses "come to hand" quickly and are capable of winning races at the start of the season; however, these animals may deteriorate rapidly and fail to compete later in the year against more powerful horses of the same age who required more time to mature.

## Early Two-Year-Old Competitions
The Brocklesby Stakes, held at Lincoln during the first week of the season, is the first noteworthy race for two-year-olds. While some winners of this race, such as Donovan in 1888 and The Bard in 1885, proved to be truly good animals, winners of the Brocklesby are generally found to be inferior by autumn compared to winners of later, high-class two-year-old races.

First-class two-year-olds rarely appear before the Ascot Meeting in mid-June, though some may run in other contests or the Woodcote Stakes at Epsom. Notable established races for this age group include:
* **The July Stakes at Newmarket:** The oldest existing two-year-old race, first run in 1786.
* **The New Stakes at Ascot:** First run in 1843.
* **The Coventry Stakes:** Originated in 1890 and regarded as being of practically equal importance to the New Stakes.
* **The Chesterfield Stakes:** Ranked alongside the July Stakes.

The most distinguished two-year-olds are typically seen racing at Goodwood.

## American Racing Practices
In the United States, racing for two-year-olds is prominent, though some view the level of encouragement given to this age group as undue. In New York, under the rules of the Jockey Club, two-year-olds may run as early as March. At winter meetings in San Francisco and New Orleans, they are raced from the very beginning of the year. 

The Westchester Racing Association provides valuable stakes for two-year-olds in May. The richest event of the year is the Futurity Stakes, a race for two-year-olds run in the autumn at Sheepshead Bay; on one occasion, this event reached a value of $67,675.
