## Authorship and Composition
Bolingbroke wrote *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I.* as part of a collection of works that included *The Patriot King* and an essay titled *The Spirit of Patriotism*. These writings were composed during a period of Bolingbroke's life marked by political disappointment and exile. ## Custody and Publication
The essay *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I.*, along with *The Patriot King* and *The Spirit of Patriotism*, was entrusted to Pope. These works were not published at the time they were entrusted to him. 

A later conflict arose regarding the publication of these materials. Bolingbroke discovered that Pope had secretly printed 1,500 copies of *The Patriot King*. This discovery prompted Bolingbroke to publish a correct version of that specific work in 1749. This event led to a bitter altercation with Warburton, who defended Pope against Bolingbroke's aspersions. Bolingbroke responded to this dispute by publishing a piece titled *Familiar Epistle to the most Impudent Man Living*.
