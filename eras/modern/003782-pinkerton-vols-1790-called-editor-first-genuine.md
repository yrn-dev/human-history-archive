# Pinkerton, 3 vols. (1790)

The publication titled *Pinkerton, 3 vols. (1790)* is a modern edition of the *Brus* manuscripts and early editions. It is specifically identified by its editor as the "first genuine edition" of the work.

## Editorial Basis and Quality
The designation of this work as the first genuine edition is based on the fact that it was printed using the text from the Advocates' Library. However, the archive's records note that the edition was produced carelessly.

## Context within the Brus Editions
The 1790 edition by J. Pinkerton is part of a broader history of editions of the *Brus*. Following Pinkerton's work, other editions were produced, including one by Jamieson in 1820 and another by Cosmo Innes in 1856 for the Spalding Club. Later, W. W. Skeat produced an edition for the Early English Text Society between 1870 and 1889, which was subsequently revised and reprinted by the Scottish Text Society from 1893 to 1895. Discussions regarding the recension of the text of Barbour have been further explored by J. T. T. Brown in the 1900 publication *The Wallace and The Bruce restudied*.

## Other Works by Pinkerton
In addition to the 1790 edition of the *Brus*, Pinkerton is credited with an edition published in 1792 concerning the poetical works of Lyndsay.

## Related Collections
Separate from his editorial work on Scottish poetry and manuscripts, Pinkerton is associated with a *Collection of Voyages and Travels*. Volume v of this collection contains a dissertation by Dolomieu regarding the earthquakes of Calabria Ultra in 1783. This dissertation describes the extreme fertility of the region, specifically the Plain south-west of the Apennines below the gulf of St. Eufemia. Dolomieu notes that the area produces grain and olive-trees of a growth larger than those seen elsewhere, and that the vines are so productive they load the trees on which they grow. The primary article of exportation for the region is oil.
