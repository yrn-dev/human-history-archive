# Lincoln's Inn Fields (1700-1704)

Lincoln's Inn Fields served as a prominent center for English theatrical activity during the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Under the influence of Davenant, who opened a new theatre there called the Duke's, the venue introduced a level of polish and luxury previously unknown in English theatrical life. This management style shifted the English stage away from its ancient simplicity, replacing it with a "tinsel" of French style and appearance adopted by the great actors of the Restoration, including Betterton and his contemporaries.

## Theatrical Productions and Performers (1700-1704)

Between 1700 and 1704, Lincoln's Inn Fields was a site of immediate success for emerging talent. Barton Booth, an English actor educated at Westminster school and Trinity College, Cambridge, made his first appearance at the venue during this period. He performed the role of Maximus in the production *Valentinian*, achieving immediate success. Booth would later establish his reputation as a tragedian in the title part of Addison's *Cato*.

The theatre also hosted works by dramatists such as John Dennis. In 1704, Dennis produced a tragedy titled *Liberty Asserted*. The play, which served as a violent attack on the French, was produced with great success at Lincoln's Inn Fields, aligning with the popular prejudices of the time.

## Associated Actors and Management

The venue was closely linked to the actor Betterton, who provided essential assistance to Barton Booth. Other notable actors associated with the theatre included:

*   **Thomas Doggett:** A Dublin-born actor who followed Betterton to Lincoln's Inn Fields. Doggett created the part of Ben in Congreve's *Love for Love*, the production with which the theatre opened in 1695.
*   **Edward Kynaston:** A London-born actor known for being one of the best male actors of female parts. Kynaston joined Betterton at Lincoln's Inn Fields in 1695, though he received less important roles following this move and eventually retired in 1699.
*   **Christopher Bullock:** The son of actor William Bullock, Christopher displayed a "considerable versatility of talent" while performing at Lincoln's Inn Fields, as well as at Drury Lane and the Haymarket.

## Legal and Institutional Context

While the theatrical venue operated in the area, Lincoln's Inn itself is one of the four ancient societies known as the Inns of Court. These societies have functioned as schools for the education of barristers in common law since the 13th century. The rise of these institutions is attributed to a clause in the Magna Carta that fixed the Common Pleas at Westminster, leading law professors to congregate in London. 

The legal society of Lincoln's Inn has historically been associated with various figures, such as the divine Donne, who was appointed as a reader in divinity to the benchers of Lincoln's Inn in October 1616, and Hugh McCalmont Cairns, who was a member of Lincoln's Inn before migrating to the Middle Temple.
