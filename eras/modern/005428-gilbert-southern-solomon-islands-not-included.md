# Gilbert and Southern Solomon Islands

## Administrative History and Jurisdiction
In 1877, a High Commission was created by order in council to establish jurisdiction over several groups of islands in the Western Pacific. This jurisdiction specifically included the Gilbert and Southern Solomon islands, as well as the Union and Ellice groups. The commission's authority extended over these islands, provided they were not already included in other colonial governments or within the jurisdiction of other civilized powers. By the year 1900, protectorates had been established over all of these islands.

## Regional Geography and Related Islands
The Solomon Islands include various landmasses, including an island known as Ongtong Java, which is also referred to as Lord Howe. This is distinct from the primary Lord Howe island located in the southern Pacific Ocean (31° 36´ S., 159° 5´ E.), which is a volcanic dependency of New South Wales discovered in 1778 by Lieutenant Ball.

## Zoological Classification
The islands of the tropical Pacific, which include the Gilbert and Southern Solomon groups, are part of the Tropical Pacific region. This region encompasses all islands east of Wallace's line, as well as New Guinea and Australia (excluding its south-eastern portion), extending to the Sandwich group. In terms of fish fauna, the Tropical Pacific region contains approximately 60 species.
