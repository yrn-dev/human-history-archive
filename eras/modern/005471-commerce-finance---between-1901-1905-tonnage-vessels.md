# Commerce and Finance

## Maritime Trade and Tonnage
Global shipping and port activity at the turn of the 20th century showed significant growth and variation across different regions. In British Honduras, the tonnage of vessels accommodated at ports rose from 300,000 between 1901 and 496,465 in 1905. Similarly, the Danube region experienced a substantial increase in traffic; between 1861 and 1902, the total tonnage of ships frequenting the Danube increased five-fold, while the mean size of individual ships grew ten-fold. British shipping in the Danube saw a marked increase in scale, moving from an average of 203 ships (averaging 193 tons each) between 1847 and 1860, to 905 vessels totaling 1,287,762 tons in 1893.

In Belgium, the ports of Antwerp and Ostend handled 7,650 vessels with a tonnage of 10,330,127 in 1904. The inland port of Ghent recorded 1,127 ships with a tonnage of 786,362 in the same year. In Greece, the Peiraeus served as a primary maritime hub, with 5,020 ships entered and cleared in 1905, totaling 5,796,590 tons. Of these, British vessels accounted for 416 ships and 609,822 tons. In Italy, the port of Castellammare di Stabia was cleared by 420 vessels of 477,713 tonnage in 1905.

## Regional Commercial Hubs
Calcutta's commercial prosperity is attributed to its location near the mouths of the Brahmaputra and Ganges river systems, which provide a cheaper mode of conveyance than railways. The port is one of the world's busiest, with 3,027 vessels arriving and departing during 1904-1905, averaging 3,734 tons. While the city is a central commercial hub, many industries are located outside municipal limits in Howrah, the headquarters of other large factories and the jute industry. Inland trade in Calcutta, conducted via road, rail, inland steamer, and country boat, amounted to approximately four and three-quarter millions sterling in 1904-1905.

In France, Brest serves as a fortified seaport and the capital of one of five naval arrondissements. Its naval port, largely excavated in rock, includes shipbuilding yards, repairing docks, and workshops employing roughly 7,000 workmen. The city also maintains a school of commerce and industry, a chamber of commerce, and a tribunal of maritime commerce.

## Imports and Exports
Trade compositions varied by region:
*   **British Honduras:** Exports included rum, tortoiseshell, turtles, sponges, deerskins, fruit, timber, and other vegetable products. Principal imports consisted of specie, groceries, spirits, wine, beer, hardware, and cotton goods.
*   **Castellammare di Stabia:** Exports were primarily green fruit and macaroni, with a sponge trade also present (though the coral trade was depressed). Imports in 1904, valued at £1,239,048, consisted mainly of cheese, scrap-iron, wheat, and coal.
*   **The Peiraeus:** This port received approximately 53% of all merchandise brought into Greece. Principal imports included articles of luxury, manufactured articles, grain, and coal. Exports, which were inconsiderable due to home consumption of local industry produce, primarily consisted of marble from Pentelicus, cognac, and wine.
*   **The Danube:** Between 1901 and 1905, the average annual export of cereals, principally wheat, was 13,000,000 quarters.

## Finance and Currency
In British Honduras, the American gold dollar was adopted as the standard coin on October 14, 1894, replacing the Guatemalan dollar and ending the legal tender status of silver from Central, South, and North America. The currency includes bronze one-cent pieces and silver pieces of 5, 10, 25, and 50 cents, alongside government notes in denominations of 1, 2, 5, 10, 50, and 100 dollars. The British half sovereign and sovereign also remain legal tender. Financial institutions include the government savings bank, founded in Belize in 1846, and the British Bank of Honduras, established in Belize in 1903.

In contrast, the republic of Honduras faced severe financial distress. By July 1909, its foreign debt reached £22,470,510, with over £17,000,000 in interest arrears. Much of the principal was borrowed between 1867 and 1870 for railway construction, but no interest had been paid since 1872, leaving the republic practically bankrupt.

## National Development and Treaties
Japan pursued a deliberate policy of developing its mercantile marine through grants-in-aid and liberal subsidies. This resulted in a growth from forty-six ships (17,948 total tonnage) in 1871 to 6,436 ships (1,564,443 total tonnage) by 1910. This capacity allowed Japan to transport large armies in its own vessels during the wars with China (1894-1895) and Russia (1904-1905). Additionally, four shipbuilding yards were established, producing vessels totaling 87,495 tons. Following the war with Russia, the two governments agreed to a system of reciprocal treatment on the "most favoured nation" footing for commercial relations, covering customs formalities, tonnage dues, import and export duties, and the treatment of vessels and agents.
