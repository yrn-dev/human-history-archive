# Holothurians (Sea-Cucumbers)

Holothurians, commonly known as sea-cucumbers, are an order of Echinoderms. They are noted in biological history for their role in the classification of other organisms and as hosts for certain parasitic life forms.

## Taxonomic History and the Case of Balanoglossus
The classification of certain worm-like animals has historically been linked to the Holothurians. In 1825, J. F. Eschscholtz discovered *Balanoglossus* at the Marshall Islands and initially described it as a worm-like animal belonging to the Echinoderm order of Holothurians. 

This classification remained until 1865, when Kowalevsky discovered that the respiratory organs of the animal consisted of numerous pairs of gill-slits. These slits lead from the exterior through the thickness of the body-wall to the digestive canal. Based on this discovery, Gegenbaur subsequently moved the animal into a special class of Vermes known as the Enteropneusta. Later research conducted by Bateson between 1883 and 1886 indicated that Enteropneusta actually possess chordate (vertebrate) affinities regarding their nervous, skeletal, coelomic, and respiratory systems.

## Larval Similarities
Despite the reclassification of *Balanoglossus*, a connection to the Holothurians persists in the study of indirect development. In the families *Ptychoderidae* and *Spengelidae* of the Enteropneusta, development culminates in a metamorphosis from a pelagic, transparent larva called *Tornaria*. 

The *Tornaria* larva possesses a longitudinal ciliated band (a complicated ciliated seam often featuring lappets and convoluted bays) and a characteristic form that differs significantly from the adult. Because of its external form and the possession of a dorsal water-pore that communicates with the anterior body-cavity, the *Tornaria* larva was described as having a similarity to the larvae of Echinoderms, including sea-cucumbers, star-fishes, and sea-urchins. It is considered possible that this indicates a remote affinity between Enteropneusta and Echinoderms.

## Biological Interactions
Holothurians serve as hosts for specific biological organisms. The tribe *Acephalina* (which is practically equivalent to *Haplocyta* or *Aseptata*) consists of non-septate bodies that never possess an epimerite. Members of this tribe act as coelomic parasites of insects, "worms," and Holothurians.

## Distribution and Environment
Echinoderms, the broader group to which Holothurians belong, are found in various marine environments. In the Eocene period, they were represented by abundant sea-urchins such as *Conoclypeus*, *Linthia*, and *Echinolampas*. In the English Corallian rocks, characteristic echinoderms include *Cidaris florigemma* and *Echinobrissus scutatus*.

Furthermore, echinoderms are found on submarine elevations. Deep-sea soundings from the "Challenger" and "Tuscarosa" have revealed submarine banks rising from depths of 2000 fathoms or more. These banks provide a habitat for numerous molluscs, crustacea, deep-sea corals, and echinoderms. The accumulation of their skeletons, along with a constant rain of silicious and calcareous skeletons from minute organisms, helps gradually raise these banks to depths where reef-building corals can eventually flourish.
