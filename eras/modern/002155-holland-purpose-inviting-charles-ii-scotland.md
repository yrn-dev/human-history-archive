# Invitation of Charles II to Scotland

## Diplomatic Mission to Holland
In 1649, a diplomatic effort was undertaken to invite Charles II to Scotland. Robert Baillie, a Scottish divine and former professor of divinity at Glasgow, was one of five Scottish clergymen selected as commissioners for this mission. The commissioners were sent to Holland with the specific objectives of inviting Charles II to Scotland and establishing the terms under which he would be admitted to the government.

## Political Context and Restoration
The invitation occurred during a period of significant political upheaval following the execution of Charles I of England in 1649. Charles II eventually ascended to the throne in 1660. During his reign, various figures maintained complex relationships with the crown. George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, had previously escaped to Holland in 1648 after being surprised at St Neots. He later accompanied Charles II to Scotland in June 1650, where he allied himself with Argyll and attempted to dissuade the king from joining a royalist plot in October of that year.

## Military and International Relations
The relationship between the English crown and Holland remained volatile throughout the 17th century. By 1672, Charles II entered into a policy with his cousin, Louis XIV of France, which led to the Third Dutch War (1672–74). The stated goal of this conflict was the partition of Holland. However, a secret understanding existed wherein Charles II intended to use the assistance of the French king to establish Roman Catholicism and implement a despotic rule in England. This hidden agenda caused the war to become intensely unpopular with the English nation and Parliament, resulting in a lack of supplies for the English fleets.

During this period, Scottish military figures were involved in the conflict. General Hugh Mackay, who had served in the English army, took part in the invasion of Holland under Turenne in 1672. While in Holland, Mackay married Clara de Bie of Bommel in Gelderland. Following a conviction that he was fighting for an unjust cause, Mackay resigned his commission to serve as a captain in a Scottish regiment within the Dutch service.

## Later Developments in Scotland
Following the restoration of the monarchy, the government of Scotland under Charles II was the subject of historical analysis by Sir George Mackenzie, who wrote a "Vindication of the Government of Scotland during the reign of Charles II" in 1691. Other figures, such as Robert Baillie, continued to be active in church disputes until his death in 1662, which was likely hastened by his mortification over the firm establishment of episcopacy in Scotland. Additionally, the period saw the reconstruction of Kinneil House, a seat of the duke of Hamilton, during the time of Charles II.
