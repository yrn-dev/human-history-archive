## Legal Cases Involving Hayes
The archive records legal proceedings involving an individual named Hayes regarding the nature of plea bargaining and prosecutorial discretion. In the case of *Bordenkircher v. Hayes* (1978), the Supreme Court examined a prosecutor's threat to seek more serious charges if the defendant, Hayes, did not plead guilty to an original offense. Hayes chose not to plead guilty and was subsequently convicted and sentenced under a new indictment for more serious charges.

While the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit initially ruled that this conduct was impermissible vindictive prosecution, the Supreme Court reversed this decision. The Court held that the decision of what charges to file rests entirely within the prosecutor's discretion, provided there is probable cause. The Court further noted that selectivity in law enforcement is only improper if it is based on arbitrary classifications, such as religion or race.

## Political History of Louisiana and President Hayes
The name Hayes also appears in the context of the United States national election of 1876. In Louisiana, double returns were submitted: one Republican return showing 73,515 votes for Hayes and 70,508 for Tilden, and one Democratic return showing 83,723 for Tilden and 77,174 for Hayes. These returns, along with those from South Carolina, Florida, and Oregon, were adjudicated by the Electoral Commission in favor of the Republican electors voting for Hayes.

Following this, President Hayes sent a commission to Louisiana, which included Gen. J. R. and Wayne McVeagh, to address the threat of civil war within the state. This occurred during a period of significant instability in Louisiana, characterized by the rivalry between radical Republican and Democratic/Liberal Republican governments, and a historic clash in New Orleans on September 14, 1874, between the Republican police and the "White League" (also known as the "White Man's Party").
