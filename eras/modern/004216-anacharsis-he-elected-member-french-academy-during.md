# Jean Jacques Barthelemy

## Early Life and Education
Jean Jacques Barthelemy (1716–1795) was a French numismatist and writer born on January 20, 1716, in Cassis, Provence. His early education took place at the college of the Oratory in Marseilles, followed by studies at the Jesuit college in the same city. While he originally intended to enter the priesthood, Barthelemy focused his studies on oriental languages. Through his friend M. Gary of Marseilles, he was introduced to the study of classical antiquities, with a particular emphasis on the field of numismatics.

## Career in Paris and Archaeological Research
In 1744, Barthelemy moved to Paris, utilizing a letter of introduction to M. Gros de Boze. De Boze served as the keeper of the royal collection of medals and the perpetual secretary of the Academy of Inscriptions and Belles-lettres. Barthelemy worked as an assistant to de Boze and succeeded him as the keeper of the medals upon de Boze's death in 1753.

In 1755, Barthelemy accompanied M. de Stainville (later the duc de Choiseul), the French ambassador, to Italy. He spent three years in Italy conducting archaeological research. Following his return to France, Barthelemy resided in the house of the duc de Choiseul, who held him in high regard and provided him with valuable preferments.

## Literary Work and the French Academy
Barthelemy authored a work titled *Voyage du jeune Anacharsis*. Following the publication of this text in 1789, he was elected as a member of the French Academy. His collected works, along with those of Mery, were later published in four volumes in 1831, featuring a notice by L. Reybaud.

## Political Turmoil and Later Years
During the French Revolution, Barthelemy was arrested on the grounds of being an aristocrat. However, he was immediately released after the duchess of Choiseul informed the Committee of Public Safety of his arrest. 

In 1793, he was nominated to be the librarian of the Bibliotheque Nationale. Although he refused this specific appointment, he resumed his previous role as the keeper of medals. In this capacity, he contributed many valuable accessions to the national collection. Jean Jacques Barthelemy died on April 30, 1795.

## Family and Legacy
Jean Jacques Barthelemy served as the uncle and educator of Francois, Marquis de Barthelemy (1747 or 1750–1830). He prepared his nephew for a diplomatic career, which eventually led Francois to serve as a secretary of legation in England, Switzerland, and Sweden, and later as a minister plenipotentiary in Switzerland.
