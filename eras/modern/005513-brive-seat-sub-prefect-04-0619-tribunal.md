# Brive

Brive, also known as Brives-la-Gaillarde, is a town located in south-central France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement within the department of Corrèze. The town is situated 62 miles south-south-east of Limoges and is positioned on the main line of the Orléans railway that runs from Paris to Montauban.

## Geography and Urban Layout
The town is located on the left bank of the Corrèze river, situated within an ample and fertile plain. Due to its position, Brive serves as a meeting-place for important railways and roads. 

The urban landscape is characterized by a mix of old and new developments. While many ancient houses and narrow, winding streets remain, the *enceinte* that once surrounded the town has been replaced by shady boulevards and several wide thoroughfares. Modern quarters are located outside these boulevards. This area includes a fine promenade planted with plane trees that extends to the Corrèze; this promenade houses the theatre and the chief restaurants. Additionally, a statue of Marshal Guillaume Marie Anne Brune, a native of Brive, is located here. A fine bridge connects the town to suburbs located on the right bank of the river.

## Architecture and Public Buildings
The public buildings of Brive are described as being of little interest, with two notable exceptions:

*   **Church of St Martin:** Located in the heart of the old town, this 12th-century building is constructed in the Romanesque style of Limousin. It features three narrow naves of almost equal height.
*   **Ecclesiastical Seminary:** This institution is housed in a graceful 16th-century mansion. The building is noted for its Renaissance workmanship, specifically in its staircase, façade, and fireplaces.

## Administration and Education
Brive serves as the seat of a sub-prefect. Its administrative and legal infrastructure includes a tribunal of commerce and a tribunal of first instance. Educational and civic facilities in the town include a school of industry and a communal college.

## Economy and Industry
Brive's geographic position makes it a market of importance. The town maintains a very large trade in grain, live-stock, truffles, and the fruit and early vegetables produced in the valley of the Corrèze.

The town's manufacturing sector is diverse, producing the following goods:
*   Paper, wax, and earthenware.
*   Hats and wooden shoes.
*   Table-delicacies.

Additionally, Brive hosts dye-works as well as millstone and slate workings.
