# Methods for Providing a Fixing for Joinery

In the construction of walls, various techniques have been employed to provide a fixing for joinery. These methods involve the integration of specific materials into the brickwork to ensure that fixtures can be securely attached.

## Historical and Modern Materials
Formerly, "fixing bricks" were used for this purpose. These were made of wood and manufactured to the same size as ordinary bricks, allowing them to be built directly into the wall where joinery fixing was required. However, the use of wooden fixing bricks has been practically abandoned due to their tendency to decay and shrink. 

In modern practice, these have been replaced by bricks made of coke-breeze concrete. Unlike their wooden predecessors, coke-breeze concrete bricks do not rot or shrink, and they provide a secure hold for screws or nails driven into them.

## Alternative Fixing Techniques
Beyond the use of specialized bricks, other methods are frequently adopted to provide a fixing for joinery:

*   **Wood Slips:** This method involves building wood slips into the wall. These slips are 4½ inches wide and possess a thickness equivalent to a joint.
*   **Wood Plugs:** In instances where suitable provision for fixing was not made during the initial construction, wood plugs may be driven into the joints of the bricks. 

When utilizing wood plugs, particular care is required during installation, especially at the corners of walls or in the joints of reveals, to avoid causing damage.
