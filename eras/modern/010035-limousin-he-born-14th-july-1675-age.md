# Limousin

Limousin is a region of France associated with several historical figures and administrative districts. In the middle ages, the town of Brive served as the capital of lower Limousin.

## Geography and Related Districts
The region is bordered to the south by the province of Marche (also known as la Marche). Marche was originally a small district created partly from Poitou and partly from Limousin. During the 10th century, Marche functioned as a border district, or march, separating the domains of the Frankish kings in central France from the duchy of Aquitaine. By the 13th century, the area of Marche increased, though it remained unchanged until the Revolution.

## Brive
Located within Limousin, Brive was known as *Briva Curretiae* (bridge of the Corrèze) during the Roman period. The area is characterized by numerous rock caves used as dwellings in prehistoric times, including those of Lamouroux. The town is also noted for four grotto-chapels visited by pilgrims in honor of St Anthony of Padua, who established a Franciscan monastery in Brive in 1226.

## Notable Figures from Limousin

### Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval
Born on July 14, 1675, Bonneval was a descendant of an old Limousin family. He began his military career at age thirteen by joining the Royal Marine Corps, moving into the army three years later. He eventually rose to command a regiment and served in the Netherlands under Luxemburg and in Italian campaigns under Vendome, Villeroi, and Catinat.

Following a court-martial in 1704 due to his insolent behavior toward the minister of war, Bonneval was condemned to death and fled to Germany. With the help of Prince Eugene, he joined the Austrian army as a general, fighting against Turkey and France. He was present at Malplaquet and suffered severe wounds at Peterwardein. Although he later visited Paris and married a daughter of Marshal de Biron after French proceedings against him were dropped, he returned to the Austrian army to fight at Belgrade.

After a subsequent conflict with the marquis de Prie, Eugene's deputy governor in the Netherlands, Bonneval faced another court-martial and death sentence, which the emperor commuted to banishment and one year of imprisonment. Upon his release, he entered the service of the Turkish government, took the name Ahmed, and professed the Mahommedan faith. As a pasha, he commanded and organized the artillery and served the sultan in wars against Nadir Shah and Russia. He was granted the governorship of Chios but was later banished to the Black Sea shores by the Porte. He died while contemplating a return to Christianity and Europe.

### Guillaume Dubois
Born in Brive, Limousin, on September 6, 1656, Guillaume Dubois became a French statesman and cardinal. The son of a doctor of medicine who operated a small drug store, Dubois was educated at the school of the Brothers of the Christian Doctrine in Brive, receiving the tonsure at age thirteen. In 1672, Jean, the marquis de Pompadour and lieutenant-general of the Limousin, provided him with a scholarship to the college of St Michel in Paris.

Dubois was supported by the abbe Antoine Faure, who helped him secure pupils and eventually the position of tutor to the young duke of Chartres (the future regent duke of Orleans). Dubois gained the favor of Louis XIV by arranging the marriage of his pupil to the king's legitimated daughter, Mademoiselle de Blois, for which he was rewarded with the abbey of St Just in Picardy. He accompanied his pupil to the battle of Steinkirk and served in the French embassy in London. When the duke of Orleans became regent in 1715, Dubois was appointed councillor of state, eventually gaining chief power.
