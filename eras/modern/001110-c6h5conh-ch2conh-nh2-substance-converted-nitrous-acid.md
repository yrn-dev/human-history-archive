# Hippuryl Hydrazine and its Derivatives

Hippuryl hydrazine, represented by the chemical formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}\cdot\text{CH}_2\text{CONH}\cdot\text{NH}_2$, is a chemical compound produced through an improved method of preparation involving the reaction of hippuric acid with hydrazine hydrate.

## Conversion by Nitrous Acid
When hippuryl hydrazine is treated with nitrous acid, it is converted into diazo-hippuramide, which has the formula $\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CONH}\cdot\text{CH}_2\cdot\text{CO}\cdot\text{NH}\cdot\text{N}_2\cdot\text{OH}$. This resulting substance, diazo-hippuramide, serves as a precursor for the production of salts of hydrazoic acid through hydrolysis via the action of caustic alkalis.

## Production of Hydrazoic Acid
To obtain free hydrazoic acid from diazo-hippuramide, the substance is dissolved in dilute soda and warmed to ensure the formation of a sodium salt. The resulting liquid is then distilled with dilute sulphuric acid. The pure acid is characterized as a colourless liquid with a very unpleasant smell that boils at $30^\circ\text{C}$. It is extremely explosive and soluble in water. 

In aqueous solution, hydrazoic acid dissolves various metals, such as iron and zinc, which liberates hydrogen and forms salts known as hydrazoates, azides, or azoimides. These salts are also explosive and interact readily with alkyl iodides. Because it forms mercurous, silver, and lead salts that are difficultly soluble, the acid shows some analogy to halogen acids. It is considered a "weak" acid, as it is ionized only to a very slight extent in dilute aqueous solutions. The metallic salts of this acid crystallize in an anhydrous state and leave a residue of pure metal upon heating.

## Synthesis of Ammonium Azoimide
Diazo-hippuramide is also utilized in the preparation of ammonium azoimide ($\text{N}_3\cdot\text{NH}_4$). This is achieved by boiling diazo-hippuramide with alcoholic ammonia until the escape of ammonia ceases. The chemical reaction is as follows:
$\text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO}\cdot\text{NHCH}_2\text{CONH}\cdot\text{N}_2\cdot\text{OH} + 2\text{NH}_3 = \text{N}_3\cdot\text{NH}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{C}_6\text{H}_5\text{CO}\cdot\text{NH}\cdot\text{CH}_2\cdot\text{CO}\cdot\text{NH}_2$.

Following this reaction, the mixture is left to stand for twelve hours, and the clear alcoholic solution is decanted from the precipitated hippuramide. The addition of ether (four times the volume of the solution) precipitates the ammonium salt. This salt is feebly alkaline, readily soluble in water, and extremely explosive.
