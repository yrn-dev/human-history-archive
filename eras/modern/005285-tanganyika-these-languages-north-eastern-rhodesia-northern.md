# Languages and Geography of the Tanganyika and North Eastern Rhodesia Regions

The regions surrounding Lake Tanganyika and extending into North Eastern Rhodesia form part of a broader area often designated as British Central Africa. This territory is characterized by a complex intersection of Bantu linguistic groups and a high-plateau geography.

## Linguistic Classifications of the Tanganyika Region
The languages of the Tanganyika region are categorized into several distinct Bantu groups based on their geographical distribution and phonetic relationships.

### The Tanganyika Group
The Tanganyika languages, which include dialects such as *Ki-rega*, *Kabwari*, and *Kiguha*, are primarily spoken in regions to the north, east, and west-north-west of Lake Tanganyika. Their range extends from the Lukuga outlet of Tanganyika in the south to the vicinity of Lake Albert Edward in the north. To the west, these languages are bounded by the Manyema genus and the Congo Forest.

### Eastern Tanganyika and the Uganda-Unyoro Connection
On the east coast of Lake Tanganyika, languages such as *Ki-rundi* (spoken in the Burundi country at the north end of the lake) and *Kigeye* are present. While these are sometimes included in the Tanganyika group, they are closely allied to the Unyoro sub-section of the Uganda-Unyoro group. This larger group encompasses dialects between the Victoria Nile and the east coast of Tanganyika, including those of the Hima (*Uru-hima*, *Ru-hinda*), as well as *Ru-songora*, *Ru-toro*, and *Ru-tusi*.

### The Manyema and Other Neighboring Groups
The Manyema (*Baenya*) group consists of "corrupt" Bantu dialects located between the main stream of the Luapula-Congo and the western watershed of Tanganyika. This group includes languages such as *Soko*, *Lokele*, *Kusu*, *Yalulema*, and *Tu-rumbu*. To the south-west of Tanganyika, the *Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu* group (including *Kanyoka* and *Lulua*) occupies the south central basin of the Congo.

## Languages of North Eastern Rhodesia and Northern Nyasaland
The Bantu dialects spoken in the plateau country between the river Luapula, the south end of Lake Tanganyika, and the north end of Lake Nyasa are noted for being of an archaic type. Among these, *Kibemba* (or *Kiwemba*) is identified as being very close to the original form of the Bantu mother-language, similar to the *Subiya* of southern Barotseland.

These languages of North Eastern Rhodesia, northern Nyasaland, and the Kafukwe basin are connected to the Bantu languages of Uganda through dialects spoken to the north and west of Lake Tanganyika. Additionally, they show a slight resemblance to the Zulu-Kaffir group. It is suggested that the Zulu-Kaffir race originally migrated from the countries north-east of Tanganyika, crossing the Zambezi to reach their home. Later, internal conflicts led to a return migration of Zulus in a north-eastward direction.

## Physical Geography and Geology
The region is dominated by high plateaus, with an average altitude of 3,500 feet. The Nyasa-Tanganyika Plateau, situated between the vicinity of Lake Mweru, Lake Tanganyika, and the Luangwa river basin, reaches heights of 7,000 to 8,000 feet.

### Hydrography
Two major lakes define the region:
*   **Lake Tanganyika:** Located at an elevation of 2,600 feet, it is approximately 400 miles long with an average breadth of nearly 40 miles.
*   **Lake Nyasa:** Situated at 1,700 feet, it is about 350 miles long and varies in breadth from 15 to 40 miles.

Other significant water bodies include Lake Mweru (3,000 feet), Lake Bangweulu (3,760 feet), and Lake Chilwa (1,946 feet). The region is also drained by the Zambezi river and its tributaries, including the Luangwa (an independent affluent of the Zambezi) and the Luanga (a tributary of the Luengwe-Kafukwe).

### Geology and Volcanism
The geological formation is primarily Archean and Primary, consisting of gneiss, quartz, granite, felspar, and schists. Primary limestones and sandstones are found in the river Shiré and Lake Nyasa basins. 

Evidence of volcanic activity is present in several areas:
*   **Mount Mlanje:** An isolated Archean plateau in the south-eastern corner with a peak of 9,683 feet, featuring extinct craters and basaltic outflow.
*   **Tanganyika Plateau:** The eastern edge of this plateau contains many craters, lava, basalt, and hot springs.
*   **Lake Nyasa:** Volcanic activity is also noted at the north end of the lake.

## Infrastructure and Administration
The area described as British Central Africa originally included territories north of the Zambezi. By 1893, the name was officially confined to the protectorate on the Shiré and Lake Nyasa, which became the Nyasaland Protectorate in 1907. Other divisions include North Eastern and North Western Rhodesia.

Transport is facilitated by light-draught steamers from the Chinde mouth of the Zambezi to Port Herald, and by regular steamer services between the ports of Lakes Nyasa and Tanganyika. A railway connects Port Herald to Blantyre. The "Cape to Cairo" railway crossed the Zambezi in 1905 and the Kafukwe in 1906, eventually reaching the Belgian Congo frontier in 1909. Communication is further supported by the African trans-continental telegraph line, with a branch extending from Lake Nyasa to Fort Jameson.
