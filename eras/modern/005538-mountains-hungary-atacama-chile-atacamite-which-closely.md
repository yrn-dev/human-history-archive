# Atacama and Associated Minerals

## The Province of Atacama
Atacama is a province located in northern Chile. It is bounded to the north by the province of Antofagasta and to the south by the province of Coquimbo, extending eastward to the boundary line of Argentina. The province covers an area of 30,729 square miles. As of 1902, its population was 71,446, with the capital, Copiapo, having an estimated population of 8,991. Copiapo is situated on a small river of the same name, located 37 miles from the coast and 51 miles south-east by rail from Caldera, which serves as the principal port for the mining district.

Historically, Atacama was considered Bolivian territory before 1842. However, after Chile decided to explore the desert coast in 1842, the region extending north to the 26th parallel was organized into the province of Atacama in 1843. This shift in status occurred as guano began to attract attention as an exportable product.

## The Atacama Desert
The Atacama Desert is an arid, saline, and barren region of western South America. It covers the majority of the Chilean provinces of Antofagasta and Atacama, the Bolivian department of Potosi (south-western corner), and the Argentine territory of Los Andes. 

The region is characterized by diverse geography:
*   **Puna de Atacama:** The higher elevations, ranging from 7,000 to 13,500 feet, are known as the Puna de Atacama. This broken, mountainous area is volcanic in some places and saline in others. Its culminating ridges consist of an irregular line of extinct volcanoes and peaks extending north-east into southern Bolivia. The plateaus here are dry and cold in winter and swept by rainstorms in summer, often appearing barren and forbidding, broken by dry salt basins and great saline lagoons.
*   **Western Slope:** The land descends gradually toward the Pacific. Higher elevations are broken into mountainous ridges and great basins or terraces, which widen into sandy plains known for nitrate deposits. The coast is marked by sharply-sloping bluffs between 800 and 1,500 feet in elevation.

While the region was considered worthless for three and a half centuries, it is now a source of significant mineral wealth, including iron, cobalt, nickel, lead, silver, copper, borax, and nitrates.

## Atacamite
Atacamite is a mineral first named by D. de Gallizen in 1801, having been originally found in the Atacama desert. Chemically, it is a cupric oxychloride with the formula $\text{CuCl}_2 \cdot 3\text{Cu}(\text{OH})_2$. It crystallizes in the orthorhombic system and is typically dark green, though it can appear in various shades of green. It has a specific gravity of 3.7 and a hardness of approximately 3.

Atacamite is a relatively rare mineral. It can form through the action of sea-water on copper-ores or as a volcanic product on Vesuvian lavas. While found in Chile—where it was known as *arsenillo* and termed *Remolinite* at the locality of Los Remolinos—it also occurs in West Africa at Bembe (near Ambriz) alongside malachite. Some of the finest crystals have been found in South Australian copper-mines, particularly at Wallaroo. In a pulverulent state, the mineral was previously used as a pounce under the name "Peruvian green sand."

## Related Minerals and Localities
In the Atacama region of Chile, atacamite is found associated with brochantite, a mineral that closely resembles it in general appearance. Brochantite is a basic copper sulphate ($\text{Cu}_4(\text{OH})_6\text{SO}_4$) that is emerald-green to blackish-green in color, with a hardness of 3½-4 and a specific gravity of 3.907.

Other notable mineral localities mentioned in the archive include:
*   **Hungary:** Brochantite is found in Rézbánya, located in the Bihar Mountains.
*   **Other Regions:** Brochantite also occurs in the Urals (where it was first found associated with native copper and malachite), Mexico, Iceland, Cornwall, Utah, and Arizona. In Arizona's Clifton and Morenci district, brochantite is extremely common but is often masked by intergrown malachite.
