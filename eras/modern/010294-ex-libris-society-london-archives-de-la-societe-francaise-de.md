# Ex-libris Society (London) and Archives de la société française de collectionneurs d'ex-libris

The Ex-libris Society of London and the *Archives de la société française de collectionneurs d'ex-libris* are organizations dedicated to the study and collection of book-plates, which serve as private tokens of book-ownership.

## History of Book-Plate Collecting
The taste for collecting book-plates and the formal study of them is a comparatively modern development, hardly dating further back than 1875. The primary impetus for this interest was the 1880 publication of the *Guide to the Study of Book-Plates* by Lord de Tabley (who was then known as the Hon. Leicester Warren). 

This foundational work established a general classification of styles that remains accepted. These styles include:
* **Early Armorial**: Plates created previous to the Restoration, such as the Nicholas Bacon plate.
* **Jacobean**: A term that, while somewhat misleading, is used to describe the heavy decorative manner found during the early Georgian days, Queen Anne, and the Restoration (exemplified by the Lansanor plate).
* **Chippendale**: This style is identified as rococo and is represented by the French plate of Convers.
* **Wreath and Ribbon**: A style belonging to the period characterized by the urn.

## Professional Societies and Publications
Following the growth of literature on the subject, societies for collectors were established in several countries, including England, France, Germany, and the United States. Most of these organizations produce their own archives or journals to document their findings.

The Ex-libris Society in London publishes *The Journal of the Ex-libris Society*, which is a monthly publication. Similarly, the *Archives de la société française de collectionneurs d'ex-libris* in Paris is also issued as a monthly. In Berlin, the *Ex-libris Zeitschrift* serves as a quarterly publication for collectors.
