# Madame Hanska and Related Literary Works

## Madame Hanska and Balzac
Madame Hanska was the long-term partner and eventual wife of the author Balzac. Their relationship began with a series of meetings in the 1830s, including a first meeting in Neuchâtel in 1833, a second in Geneva later that same year, and a third in Vienna in 1835. During the Genevan meeting of 1833, Madame Hanska formally promised to marry Balzac should her husband die.

Following the death of her husband at the end of 1841, Madame Hanska did not allow Balzac to visit her until the following year had passed. Although the engagement was renewed after a fashion during a trip to St Petersburg, its fulfillment was postponed indefinitely for several years. During this interval, the couple met in various cities, including Paris, Rome, Brussels, Wiesbaden, and Baden.

In September 1847, Balzac was invited to Madame Hanska's estate of Wierzschovnia in the Ukraine as her future husband. This visit lasted more than two years, interrupted only by one excursion to Paris. The couple finally married on March 14, 1850. Shortly after the wedding, they moved to a house in Paris on the rue Fortunee, which Balzac had purchased for his wife and filled with his collections. Balzac died on August 17, 1850, at half-past eleven at night; he was attended by his mother, but not by Madame Hanska. A portrait of Madame Hanska was included in the 1906 second volume of *Oeuvres posthumes* (specifically the *Lettres a l'etrangere*).

## Related Literary Context
The works of Balzac, including those mentioned above, were issued during his lifetime in various forms. The earliest *Comedie humaine* appeared between 1842 and 1846 in sixteen volumes. Another well-known edition consisted of fifty-five small 24mos published by Michel (Calmann) Levy, which included the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*. Later, the *Edition definitive* (from 1869 onwards) provided the miscellaneous works in four volumes and a volume of correspondence. In 1875, M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul attached the *Histoire des oeuvres* to the *Edition definitive*. Additionally, a *Repertoire des oeuvres de Balzac* was published in 1893 by MM. Cerfbeer and Christophe to track the history of the personages within the *Comedie*.
