# Pforzheim

## Geography and Demographics
Pforzheim is a town located within the region of Baden. As of 1905, it served as one of the chief towns of the Karlsruhe district. During that year, the population of Pforzheim was recorded as 59,307.

The broader region of Baden, which includes Pforzheim, had a total population of 2,009,320 in 1905. The demographic composition of Baden at that time consisted of approximately 60% Roman Catholics and 37% Protestants, with 1-1/2% identified as Jews. The population density of the region was about 330 people per square mile, with roughly half of the inhabitants classified as rural, meaning they lived in communities of fewer than 2,000 people.

## Historical Development
The history of Pforzheim is tied to the territorial divisions of the house of Baden. In 1535, after the family of Baden-Sausenberg became extinct and the territories were united by Christopher I, his sons Bernard and Ernest divided the land. This division resulted in the foundation of two distinct lines: Baden-Baden and Baden-Pforzheim. 

The line of Baden-Pforzheim was later called Baden-Durlach after 1565. The period following these divisions was marked by rivalry between the two main branches of the family, which eventually escalated into open warfare. Between 1584 and 1622, Baden-Baden was held by one of the princes of Baden-Durlach.
