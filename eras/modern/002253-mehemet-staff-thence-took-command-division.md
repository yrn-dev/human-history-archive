# Valentine Baker

## Early Military Career
Valentine Baker (1827–1887) was a British soldier and the younger brother of Sir Samuel Baker. Educated in Ceylon and Gloucester, Baker began his military service in 1848 when he entered the Ceylon Rifles as an ensign. He was subsequently transferred to the 12th Lancers, with whom he saw active service during the Kaffir war from 1852 to 1853. 

During the Crimean War, Baker was present at the fall of Sevastopol and the action of Traktir (also known as Tchernaya). In 1859, he attained the rank of major in the 10th Hussars, and a year later, he assumed command of the regiment. Baker held this command for thirteen years, a period during which he achieved the highest efficiency among his men and contributed to his arm of service through his writings.

## Later British Service and Dismissal
Baker served as a spectator with German armies during the wars of 1870 and 1866. In 1873, he undertook a famous journey through Khorassan. Although he did not reach Khiva, the expedition provided significant military, geographical, and political information, particularly regarding Russian advances in central Asia.

Upon returning to England in 1874, Baker took a staff appointment at Aldershot. However, his career in the British army ended abruptly less than a year later. He was arrested and sentenced to a fine and one year's imprisonment on a charge of indecent assault against a young woman in a railway carriage. This resulted in his inevitable dismissal from the service. It is noted that some good authorities believed Baker's conduct admitted of considerable extenuation when judged by conventional standards, though Baker himself never offered a self-defence.

## Service in Turkey
Two years after leaving England, Baker entered the service of Turkey during its war with Russia. He initially held a high position within the gendarmerie before being transferred to Mehemet's staff. From that position, he took over the command of a division of infantry.

While leading this division, Baker conducted a brilliant rearguard action at Tashkessan against the troops of Gourko. For this achievement, he was promoted to the rank of *Ferik* (lieutenant-general). He continued to serve with distinction, commanding Suleiman's rearguard. Following the peace, Baker was appointed to an administrative post.
