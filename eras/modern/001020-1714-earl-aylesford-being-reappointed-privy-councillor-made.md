# Heneage Finch, 1st Earl of Aylesford

Heneage Finch, 1st Earl of Aylesford (c. 1640–1719), was a prominent English lawyer and politician. He was the second son of Heneage Finch, 1st Earl of Nottingham. Finch received his education at Westminster School and Christ Church, Oxford, where he matriculated on November 18, 1664.

## Legal Career and Crown Prosecution
Finch entered the legal profession as a barrister of the Inner Temple in 1673. By 1677, he had attained the positions of bencher and king's counsel. In 1679, while his father served as chancellor, Finch was appointed solicitor-general. 

During his tenure as a crown lawyer, Finch represented the crown in several high-profile cases. In 1682, he represented the crown in an attack upon the corporation of London. The following year, he prosecuted Lord Russell. Regarding these and other trials, Burnet noted that Finch exhibited a "vicious eloquence," utilizing subtlety to turn matters against prisoners rather than employing sincere or strict reasoning. However, it is noted that he did not appear to exceed the duties of a crown prosecutor as they were understood at the time.

In 1684, Finch served as counsel for James in a successful libel action against Titus Oates, and in 1685, he prosecuted Oates on behalf of the crown for perjury. That same year, during the trial of Algernon Sidney, Finch argued that the accused's unpublished treatise constituted an overt act, supporting the opinion of Jeffreys that *scribere est agere*.

## Political Career and Religious Convictions
Finch was a Tory and a staunch churchman. This commitment led to a conflict with King James in 1686 when Finch refused to defend the royal dispensing power, resulting in his summary dismissal. In June 1688, he served as leading counsel for the seven bishops. During this case, he boldly and "strangely exposed" the dispensing power, though his tactical errors nearly led to his clients losing the case.

Finch represented Oxford University in the convention parliament and continued to represent this constituency in all subsequent assemblies until his elevation to the peerage, with the exception of the assembly of 1698. He had previously been returned to parliament for Oxford University in 1679 and for Guildford in 1685.

## Later Years and Political Stance
Finch was not a supporter of the House of Orange. He advocated for a regency in the name of James and was among the few members of the House of Commons who opposed the vote declaring that James had vacated the throne by breaking the contract between the king and the people. Consequently, he held no office during the reign of William and was described by Macky as being "always a great opposer" of that administration.
