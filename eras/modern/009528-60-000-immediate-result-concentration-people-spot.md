# The Population Growth and Sanitary Impact in Bombay

## Foundation and Early Administration
The modern foundation of the city of Bombay was established through the efforts of Gerald Aungier, who succeeded Sir George Oxenden as the president of Surat in 1669. Aungier, the brother of Francis Aungier (the 1st earl of Longford in Ireland and 3rd Lord Aungier of Longford), served in this capacity until his death in 1677. During this period, Bombay faced significant external threats: the Mahrattas pressured the city from the inland, while the Dutch and Malabar pirates threatened from the sea. Additionally, the Portuguese occupied the island of Salsette and maintained a customs-barrier in the channel, effectively cutting Bombay off from the mainland.

Despite a "niggardly policy" from the court of directors, who refused to pay for skilled engineers, Aungier successfully fortified the shore and the town. He established a military force consisting of regulars and militia; the regulars were primarily Germans, who were viewed as more trustworthy than the "riffraff" the Company's crimps had collected in London. In 1672, Aungier moved his headquarters to Bombay. After deterring a Dutch fleet that had attempted to surprise the island in 1670, he began a comprehensive reorganization of the settlement.

## Social and Fiscal Reforms
Aungier’s reorganization was guided by a sincere belief in the traditions of English liberty and an enlightened mind. He sought to reverse the oppressive nature of previous Portuguese rule, which had been characterized by religious intolerance, cruel treatment of natives, and an oppressive fiscal policy. 

Working with the consent of a "general assembly of the chief representatives of the people," Aungier implemented several key reforms:
* **Taxation:** He replaced the burdensome land tax with a fixed money payment.
* **Religious Freedom:** He provided protection to all castes, allowing them to celebrate their religious ceremonies.
* **Labor Rights:** He forbade the forced compulsion of natives to carry burdens against their will.

## Population Expansion and Demographic Shifts
These reforms led to a rapid increase in the population of Bombay. The city became a destination for various groups seeking security for their trade and religion. Specifically, Parsees and Armenians flocked to the city. Additionally, a special quarter was designated for the banya, who were the capitalist class of Hindus.

The scale of this growth was significant; within a period of eight years, the population of the settlement grew from 10,000 to 60,000 people.

## Immediate Results of Concentration
The rapid concentration of 60,000 people in a single location had immediate negative consequences due to the nature of the environment. Because the spot was "so unwholesome," the primary result of this population density was the prevalence of disease. This health crisis was produced by appalling sanitary conditions.
