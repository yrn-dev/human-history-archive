# Horatius Bonar

## Life and Education
Horatius Bonar (1808–1889) was a Scottish Presbyterian divine. He was born in Edinburgh on December 19, 1808. His education took place in his native city, where he attended both the university and the high school.

## Ministerial Career
Bonar began his professional service with a term of mission work at Leith. In 1837, he was appointed as the parish minister of Kelso. Following the Disruption of 1843, he became a minister of the newly formed Free Church, a position he held until 1866. At that time, he moved to the Chalmers memorial church in Edinburgh. 

Throughout his career, Bonar achieved several ecclesiastical and academic honors. He received a D.D. degree from Aberdeen University in 1853 and served as the moderator of the general assembly of his church in 1883. He died on July 31, 1889.

## Literary and Editorial Work
Bonar was a prolific author of religious literature. His editorial work included the management of several journals, specifically the *Presbyterian Review*, the *Christian Treasury*, and the *Quarterly Journal of Prophecy*.

## Hymnology
While Bonar was active in various forms of religious writing, his best work was produced in the field of hymnology. Between 1866 and 1857, he published three series titled *Hymns of Faith and Hope*, which saw a new edition in 1886. 

His influence on modern hymnody is significant, as nearly every modern hymnal contains perhaps twenty of his hymns. Notable examples of his work include:
* "I heard the voice of Jesus say"
* "Go, labour on"
* "When the weary, seeking rest"
* "Here, O my Lord, I see Thee face to face"
