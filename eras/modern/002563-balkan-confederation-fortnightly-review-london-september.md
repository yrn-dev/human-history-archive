# Balkan Confederation

The concept of a Balkan Confederation refers to a proposed political union of the Christian states within the Balkan Peninsula. The vision for such a union centers on the cooperation of various races and nations to ensure regional stability and a significant role in European affairs.

## Composition and Objectives
A proposed Balkan Confederation would comprise the states of Bulgaria, Serbia, Roumania, Montenegro, and Greece. The primary objective of such a union is to create an entity strong enough to play an effective part in the destinies of Europe. Proponents of this union argue that by acting in unity and good faith, these states can secure advantages and a decisive role on the world stage.

Conversely, the alternative to such a confederation is viewed as a state of disunion, which would lead the Balkan States to "tear each other’s throats without profit or reward," ultimately rendering them futile in the context of global destinies.

## International Perspectives and Support
England has been characterized as a friend to every Christian State in the Balkans throughout their history of struggle and suffering. It is asserted that England has no personal interests to seek within the Balkan Peninsula; instead, it is positioned to use its power and wealth to aid any righteous effort to build a strong union of Christian peoples. This vision of union is likened to the cooperation that triumphed during the first Balkan War.

In the context of the First World War, there were efforts to press for a policy of uniting the Balkan States regardless of the situation in Turkey. By September 1914, communications between officials, such as Mr. Churchill and Sir Edward Grey, emphasized the need for Greece and Bulgaria to recognize the "brilliant but fleeting opportunity" to secure the strength and union of the Balkan peoples through English support.

## The Role of Bulgaria
The future of a Balkan settlement has been closely tied to the attitude of Bulgaria. Following its surrender, the possibility emerged for Bulgaria to align with the Allies. This shift was driven by a disillusionment with *Mitteleuropa*, as Bulgarians realized that under such a system, they would have been virtually serfs to a Germany operating under the concept of a master race.

The Allies' vision for a Balkan confederation involves a system where varied races work together in common interest and mutual respect. This is presented as a safer alternative for Bulgaria than attempting to dominate the region as a minority holding down a majority of "sullen and vengeful race enemies." While imperialistic dreams are to be banished, the Allies recognized the legitimacy of solicitude for race-brethren outside Bulgaria's frontiers, pledging to satisfy this either through annexation to the homeland or by ensuring full cultural liberty where annexation was impossible due to the claims of other races.

## Historical and Academic References
The subject of a Balkan Confederation has been addressed in various historical and academic publications. J. D. Bourchier wrote on the topic in the September 1891 issue of the *Fortnightly Review*. Other general descriptions of the region's political problems and inhabitants can be found in works such as Odysseus's *Turkey in Europe* (1900), L. Villari's *The Balkan Question* (1905), and W. Miller's *The Balkans* (1896), the latter of which sketches the history of Servia, Montenegro, Rumania, and Bulgaria.
