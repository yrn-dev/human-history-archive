# Population and Administration of Selected Counties

The following records detail the demographic and administrative structures of various counties across the United Kingdom and Ireland, based on data primarily from 1891 and 1901.

## Ayrshire
In 1891, the population of Ayrshire was 226,386, increasing to 254,468 by 1901, which equates to 223 persons per square mile. By 1901, only 17 persons in the county spoke Gaelic exclusively. The most populous towns in 1901 were Kilmarnock (35,091) and Ayr (29,101), followed by Irvine (9,618), Saltcoats (8,120), and Stevenston (6,554). Other towns include Ardrossan, Maybole, Dalry, Beith, Galston, Troon, Hurlford, Kilbirnie, Newmilns, Kilwinning, Largs, Cumnock, Darvel, Muirkirk, Auchinleck, and Stewarton.

Administratively, the county returns two members to parliament for North and South Ayrshire. Ayr and Irvine are royal burghs within the Ayr group of parliamentary burghs, while Kilmarnock is a parliamentary burgh of the Kilmarnock group. The county is a sheriffdom with resident sheriffs-substitute at Kilmarnock and Ayr, who also sit at Girvan, Cumnock, Beith, and Irvine. Local administration includes drainage, lighting and scavenging, and special water districts under the county council. Education is under school-board jurisdiction, featuring secondary schools in Kilmarnock, Irvine, Ayr, and Beith, as well as various voluntary schools. The Kilmarnock Dairy School, established in 1899, is part of the West of Scotland Agricultural College. Technical education is subsidized at local centres, covering subjects such as engineering, mining, plumbing, gardening, agriculture, and various arts and sciences.

## Durham
Durham is a County Palatine, alongside Cheshire and Lancashire. Its ancient county area is 649,352 acres, while the administrative county is 649,244 acres. The population grew from 1,016,454 in 1891 to 1,187,361 in 1901. The county is divided into four wards. Its municipal boroughs include Sunderland (county borough, pop. 146,077), Gateshead (county borough, pop. 109,888), South Shields (county borough, pop. 97,263), West Hartlepool (62,627), Stockton-on-Tees (51,478), Darlington (44,511), Jarrow (34,295), Hartlepool (22,723), Durham city (14,679), and others. Industrial districts in the Tyne area include Felling, Hebburn, Blaydon, Whickham, and Ryton. The north-western district includes Stanley, Annfield Plain, Consett, Tanfield, Leadgate, Benfieldside. Historically, the county included outlying portions known as Crayke, Bedlingtonshire, and North Durham (including Islandshire and Norhamshire), which were part of the "patrimony of St Cuthbert."

## Dorsetshire and Buckinghamshire
Dorsetshire has an ancient county area of 632,270 acres (administrative area 625,578 acres) and a population that rose from 194,517 in 1891 to 202,936 in 1901. It contains 35 hundreds and is divided into four parliamentary divisions (southern, northern, eastern, and western), each returning one member. Municipal boroughs include Weymouth and Melcombe Regis, Poole, Dorchester (the county town), Bridport, Blandford Forum, Shaftesbury, Wareham, and Lyme Regis. Other urban districts include Portland, Sherborne, Wimborne Minster, and Swanage. The county is in the western circuit, with assizes at Dorchester and one court of quarter sessions divided into nine petty sessional divisions. It contains 289 civil parishes and 256 ecclesiastical parishes or districts, mostly within the diocese of Salisbury.

Buckinghamshire's ancient county area is 475,682 acres (administrative area 479,358 acres), with a population of 185,284 in 1891 and 195,764 in 1901. It contains eight hundreds, including the "Chiltern Hundreds" (Desborough, Burnham, and Stoke). Municipal boroughs are Wycombe (15,542) and Buckingham (3,152). Other urban districts include Slough, Aylesbury, Eton, Fenny Stratford, Chesham, Marlow, Newport Pagnell, Linslade, and Beaconsfield. Notable market towns include Amersham, Olney, Prince's Risborough, Stony Stratford, Wendover, Winslow, and Ivinghoe. Wolverton is home to the London & North-Western railway carriage works. The county is in the midland circuit with assizes at Aylesbury and one court of quarter sessions. It has 230 civil parishes and 215 ecclesiastical parishes, primarily in the diocese of Oxford. Parliamentary divisions are Southern (Wycombe), Mid (Aylesbury), and Northern (Buckingham).

## Derbyshire
Derbyshire's ancient county area is 658,885 acres (administrative area 652,272 acres), with a population of 528,033 in 1891 and 620,322 in 1901. It contains six hundreds. Municipal boroughs include Derby (county borough and county town, pop. 114,848), Chesterfield (27,185), Ilkeston (25,384), and Glossop (21,526). Other urban districts include Swadlincote, Alfreton, Heanor, Long Eaton, Staveley, Ripley, Buxton, and others. The county is in the Midland circuit with assizes at Derby and one court of quarter sessions divided into fifteen petty sessional divisions. It contains 314 civil parishes and 255 ecclesiastical parishes, mainly in the diocese of Southwell. Parliamentary divisions include Western, Southern, Ilkeston, Mid, Chesterfield, North-Eastern, and High Peak, each returning one member, while the borough of Derby returns two.

## Irish Counties and Kinross
Several Irish counties show population declines. In Kildare, the population fell from 70,206 in 1891 to 63,566 in 1901, with 86% Roman Catholic. In Donegal, the population decreased from 185,635 to 173,722, with 78% Roman Catholic. In Kerry, the population fell from 179,136 to 165,726. Another county (unnamed in excerpt [2]) saw a decrease from 219,405 in 1891 to 205,889 in 1901; its population is 39% Presbyterian, 31% Roman Catholic, and 23% Protestant Episcopalian.

Kinross has a population of 6,673 in 1891 and 6,981 in 1901, with 55 persons speaking both English and Gaelic. Its towns are Milnathort and the county town, Kinross. It returns one member to parliament jointly with Clackmannanshire and forms a sheriffdom with Fifeshire.
