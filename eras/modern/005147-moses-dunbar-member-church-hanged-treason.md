# Moses Dunbar

## Life and Execution
Moses Dunbar was a member of the Protestant Episcopal Church in the township of Bristol, located in Hartford county, Connecticut, U.S.A. During the period of the War of Independence, the members of this church belonged to the Loyalist party. As a result of these political affiliations, Dunbar was hanged for treason by the Connecticut authorities.

## Historical Context of Bristol, Connecticut
The township of Bristol is situated in the central part of Connecticut, approximately 16 miles southwest of Hartford. Originally a part of the township of Farmington, the area was first settled around 1727. It became an independent corporation following the formation of the first church in 1742, which was known as the New Cambridge Society after 1744.

The Protestant Episcopal Church, to which Moses Dunbar belonged, was organized in 1748. The church's association with the Loyalist party during the War of Independence led to persecution by the Whigs; for instance, the rector of the church, Rev. James Nichols, was tarred and feathered. Local Loyalists frequently gathered at Chippen's Hill, located about 3 miles from the center of the township, where a cave known as "The Tories' Den" remains a well-known landmark.

## Development of the Township
In 1785, the ecclesiastical societies of New Cambridge and West Britain were incorporated to form the township of Bristol. This arrangement lasted until 1806, when they were divided into the current townships of Burlington and Bristol.

The borough of Bristol, incorporated in 1893, and the village of Forestville are both located on the Pequabuck river. By 1910, the population of the township had grown to 13,502, with 9,527 residing in the borough. The area became known for various manufactures, including hardware, brass ware, silverplate, iron castings, woollen goods, bells, and clocks. Bristol clocks, which began production shortly after the War of Independence, achieved wide recognition.
