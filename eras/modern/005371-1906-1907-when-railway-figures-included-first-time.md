# 1906-1907

The period of 1906-1907 was marked by significant developments in global infrastructure, industrial production, and administrative shifts across various territories, ranging from East Africa and Morocco to the United States.

## East African Administration and Infrastructure
In the protectorate featuring the Uganda railway, the years 1906-1907 saw a notable increase in financial figures. For the period of 1906-1907, revenue reached £461,362 and expenditure totaled £616,088; this specific reporting period was the first time that railway figures were included in the totals. Revenue in the region was derived from posts and telegraphs, railway earnings, excise, licences, and customs, while natives paid a hut tax. Deficiencies in the budget were covered by grants-in-aid from the imperial exchequer.

The Uganda railway, a 584-mile metre-gauge line built by the British government at a cost of £5,331,000, had a profound impact on the region. By eliminating the need for the carriage of goods by men, the railway dealt a final death-blow to the slave trade in that part of East Africa and facilitated the development and occupation of Uganda.

Administratively, 1907 saw the transfer of extra-territorial jurisdiction to Great Britain, which had previously been granted by the sultan of Zanzibar to various Powers. Additionally, a decree was issued in 1907 abolishing the legal status of slavery, following an 1890 decree that prohibited the enslavement of anyone born after August 1st of that year.

## North African Developments
In Morocco, the seaport of Casablanca saw the beginning of harbour works in May 1907 to provide sheltered accommodation for ships. However, in August 1907, the town was occupied and bombarded by the French following the murder of several Spanish and French workmen who were employed on the harbour works. Between 1897 and 1907, the foreign trade of the port was valued at approximately £750,000 per year.

## United States Industrial and Urban Growth
### Mining and Energy
In Idaho, the mineral product for 1906 was valued at $24,138,317. Gold production in the state was $1,149,100 in 1906 and rose to $1,373,031 in 1907. Silver production was $6,042,606 in 1906 and $5,546,554 in 1907. Shoshone county remained a primary producer; its silver product was valued at $5,322,706 in 1906 and $4,780,093 in 1907. Lead production in Shoshone county was valued at $14,365,265 in 1906 and $12,232,233 in 1907.

In Illinois, coal production in 1907 spanned 52 counties, with the largest yields coming from Madison, Macoupin, St Clair, Sangamon, and Williamson. The total tonnage for 1907 was 51,317,146, with a total product value of $54,687,882. Nearly 30% of this coal was mined using machinery. Petroleum output in Illinois saw a sharp increase, rising from 4,397,050 barrels in 1906 to 24,281,973 barrels in 1907.

### Urban Expansion in Oklahoma
Two cities in Oklahoma experienced growth during this time:
*   **Enid:** The population grew to 10,087 by 1907 (including 355 people of negro descent). In 1907, natural gas was discovered near the city, and the co-educational Oklahoma Christian University was established.
*   **McAlester:** The population reached 8,144 in 1907, including 1,05 Indians and 1,681 negroes. In 1906, the town of McAlester was annexed by the city of South McAlester, which then adopted the name McAlester.
