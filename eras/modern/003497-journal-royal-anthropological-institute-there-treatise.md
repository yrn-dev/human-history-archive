# Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute

The *Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute* (also referred to as *Jour. Anthropological Institute*) is a publication associated with the Royal Anthropological Institute. It serves as a venue for scholarly contributions across various scientific and anthropological disciplines.

## Institutional Leadership and Administration
The Royal Anthropological Institute has been led by figures from diverse scientific backgrounds. George Busk (1807–1886), a British surgeon, zoologist, and palaeontologist, served as the president of the institute from 1873 to 1874. Busk was a highly decorated scientist, having been elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1850 and receiving the Royal Society's Royal medal, as well as the Lyell and Wollaston medals from the Geological Society.

Administrative support for the institute has included Thomas Athol Joyce, M.A., who served as the Honorary Secretary of the Royal Anthropological Institute. Joyce also held a position as an assistant in the Department of Ethnography at the British Museum.

## Scholarly Contributions and Publications
The journal and the institute have published a variety of research papers and lectures. In 1880, J. Hack Tuke contributed to volume ix of the *Journal of the Royal Anthropological Institute*. Additionally, the institute has been responsible for the publication of specialized academic lectures, such as the 1906 Huxley Lecture titled *Migrations*, delivered by Flinders Petrie. Petrie's work in this publication discussed the change in skull shape of the Lombards from brachycephalic to dolichocephalic over a period of a few hundred years.

## Context within Anthropological Study
The work associated with the Royal Anthropological Institute exists alongside other global anthropological efforts and publications. In France, similar scientific pursuits were led by Paul Broca (1824–1880), who established the Anthropological Society of Paris in 1859 and founded *La Revue d'Anthropologie* in 1872. Other French anthropological contributions include the *Bulletins de la société anthropologique* (with issues noted in 1861, 1867, 1868, and 1871) and the works of Joseph Deniker, a French naturalist and anthropologist. Deniker published several ethnological works, including *Races et peuples de la terre* (1900), *Les Ghiliaks* (1883), and *Étude sur les Kalmouks* (1883), and contributed frequently to French zoological and anthropological journals.
