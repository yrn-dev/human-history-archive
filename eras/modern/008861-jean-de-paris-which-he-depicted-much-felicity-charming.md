# Jean de Paris

## Overview
*Jean de Paris* is a musical work composed by the French composer Francois Adrien Boieldieu. Produced in 1812, it is regarded as one of Boieldieu's finest compositions.

## The Composer
Francois Adrien Boieldieu was born in Rouen on December 15, 1775. His early musical education was provided by M. Broche, the cathedral organist, who is noted for having treated Boieldieu very harshly. Despite this, Boieldieu began composing chamber music and songs at a very young age. His first two operas, *Rosalie et Myrza* and *La Fille coupable* (which featured a libretto written by his father), were staged in Rouen in 1795.

Seeking greater success, Boieldieu moved to Paris in 1795. However, his early scores were not well-received by contemporaries such as Mehul and Cherubini, as the prevailing trend of the time was grand opera. To support himself, Boieldieu utilized his skills as a pianoforte player. He eventually gained recognition after being hired as an accompanist by P.J. Garat, a fashionable singer of the period. Through the drawing-rooms of the Directoire, Boieldieu's reputation grew as he supplied Garat with ballads and songs.

## Career Development and Works
Boieldieu saw a series of productions on the Paris stage, including *Les Deux lettres* in 1796 and *La Famille suisse* in 1797. His most enduring success from this period was *Le Calife de Bagdad*, produced in 1800. Following the massive success of this work, Boieldieu sought more rigorous musical training and studied under Cherubini, whose influence is evident in the higher artistic finish of Boieldieu's later works.

In 1802, Boieldieu fled to Russia to escape domestic difficulties resulting from his marriage to Clotilde Aug. Mafleuroy, a celebrated ballet-dancer with the Paris opera. He was welcomed by Emperor Alexander in St Petersburg, where he spent a prolonged period composing several operas. During his time in Russia, he also attempted the tragic style of dramatic writing by setting the choruses of Racine's *Athalie* to music.

## Return to France
Boieldieu returned to France in 1811. The following year, in 1812, he produced *Jean de Paris*.
