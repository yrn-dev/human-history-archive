# Alfred Barry

Alfred Barry (1826– ) was an English clergyman, academic administrator, and author. He was the second son of a father for whom he later wrote a biography.

## Education and Early Academic Career
Barry received his education at King's College, London, and Trinity College, Cambridge. In 1848, he achieved a first-class in the classical tripos and was ranked as the 4th wrangler. Following his studies, he held several leadership positions in educational institutions:
* Sub-warden of Trinity College, Glenalmond (1849–1854)
* Head-master of Leeds grammar school (1854–1862)
* Principal of Cheltenham College (1862–1868)
* Principal of King's College, London (1868–1883)

## Ecclesiastical Appointments
Barry's clerical career included various canonries and bishoprics across England and Australia. He served as a canon of Worcester from 1871 to 1881, and subsequently as a canon of Westminster from 1881 to 1884. 

From 1884 to 1889, he served as the primate of Australia and the bishop of Sydney. Upon returning to England, he held the position of assistant bishop in the diocese of Rochester between 1889 and 1891. In 1891, he was appointed canon of Windsor. Later in his career, he served as the rector of St James's, Piccadilly, from 1895 to 1900, and was appointed assistant bishop in West London in 1897.

## Publications
In addition to writing the life of his father, Alfred Barry published numerous theological works.
