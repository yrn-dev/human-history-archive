# War of Independence

The War of Independence (also referred to as the War of American Independence) was a period of significant military conflict and political transition that impacted various settlements and institutions across North America. The war's influence extended from the frontiers of the West to the shipping ports of the East Coast, affecting land ownership, city naming, and the funding of educational institutions.

## Military Operations and Frontier Conflict
Military activities during the War of Independence were widespread, often centering on strategic fortifications and frontier settlements. In the region surrounding the Niagara river, the neighborhood was a scene of military operations until the conflict concluded. This area had been previously fortified by the marquis de Denonville in 1687, whose fort served as the predecessor to the fortifications known as Fort Niagara.

Further west, in the territory near the Falls of the Ohio, the war involved efforts to organize loyalist forces. Dr. John Connolly, a native of Pennsylvania and former commander of Fort Pitt, was imprisoned from 1775 until nearly the end of the war. His imprisonment resulted from his attempts, acting under instructions from Lord Dunmore, to organize a group called the "Loyal Foresters" to be deployed against rebellious colonists in the West.

In New York, the township of German Flats—which had been settled by Palatinate Germans around 1725—was the site of several Indian raids during the War of Independence. It was in this location that General Herkimer began his activities.

## Impact on Urban Development and Naming
The aftermath and progression of the war played a direct role in the establishment and naming of several American cities. The settlement at the Falls of the Ohio, which had been organized as a town government in April 1779, was incorporated by the Virginia legislature on May 14, 1780. The legislature named the settlement Louisville in recognition of the assistance provided to the colonies during the War of Independence by Louis XVI of France. This act also declared that the previous title holder, Dr. John Connolly, had forfeited his land.

In other regions, the war coincided with periods of rapid growth. By the beginning of the War of Independence, the population of Baltimore had grown to 6,755. During the conflict, Baltimore served as an important shipping center, from which many privateers were dispatched.

## Effects on Institutions and Funding
The war also disrupted the financial and administrative stability of colonial institutions. Dartmouth College, which had been incorporated by a charter from George III in 1769 and had received significant funding from Great Britain (including £10,000 raised by the Indian preacher Samson Occom), saw most of its British support withdrawn during the War of Independence.
