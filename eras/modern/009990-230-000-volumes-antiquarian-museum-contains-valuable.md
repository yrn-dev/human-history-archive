# Libraries and Antiquarian Museums of the Archive

The Human History Archive contains records of various institutional collections across Europe, North America, and Africa, detailing the preservation of literature, scientific specimens, and historical relics. These collections range from university libraries and state museums to specialized private holdings.

## European Institutional Collections

In Germany, several cities maintain significant academic and cultural repositories. In one city, the university—established in its present form in 1818 by King Frederick William III of Prussia—occupies a building that was originally an electoral palace constructed around 1717. This university library contains more than 230,000 volumes. Associated with the university is an antiquarian museum that houses a valuable collection of Roman relics found in the nearby area. Additionally, the palace of Clemensruhe at Poppelsdorf hosts an academy of agriculture with an attached botanic garden and natural history museum.

The city of Cassel (or Kassel), the capital of the province of Hesse-Nassau, features the Museum Fridericianum. This institution, characterized by a facade of Roman-Ionic columns, contains a library of 230,000 volumes and valuable manuscripts. Its collections include mosaics, coins, casts, and a cabinet of curiosities featuring a complete collection of clocks and watches. A notable item in this collection is the "Egg of Nuremberg," a watch created around 1500 by Peter Henlein.

In Darmstadt, the capital of the grand-duchy of Hesse-Darmstadt, a new museum was erected in 1905 to house state scientific and art collections. This facility contains a library of 600,000 volumes and 4,000 manuscripts, as well as a museum of German and Egyptian antiquities, state archives, a natural history collection, and a picture gallery featuring Dutch and old German school masterpieces.

In Hungary, the town of Maros-Vásárhely in Transylvania contains the Teleki palace. This palace houses the Teleki collections, which include a library of 70,000 volumes, a manuscript of Tacitus, the Teleki Codex, and various valuable manuscripts and old Hungarian poems. The palace also maintains collections of minerals and antiquities, while the town separately hosts a Szekler industrial museum.

## North American Collections

In Buffalo, the public library was founded in 1837 and is housed in a building from 1887 valued at $1,000,000, containing approximately 300,000 pamphlets and books. The city's public library building also houses a museum for the Buffalo Society of Natural Sciences. Other notable libraries in the city include:
*   The Grosvenor (founded 1859): 75,000 volumes and 7,000 pamphlets.
*   The Buffalo Historical Society (founded 1862): 26,600 volumes.
*   Canisius College (a Roman Catholic Jesuit institution): approximately 26,000 volumes.
*   The Law library (8th judicial district): 17,000 volumes.
*   The Catholic Institute: 12,000 volumes.
*   The John C. Lord library: 10,620 volumes.

## Middle Eastern and British Collections

The Arab museum and khedivial library were opened in 1903 in a building costing £66,000. The museum preserves treasures of Saracenic art, including items moved from mosques for security. The khedivial library holds some 64,000 volumes, with over two-thirds consisting of manuscripts and books in Syriac, Amharic, Turkish, Persian, and Arabic. The Arabic section is particularly noted for a unique collection of 2,677 korans, while the Persian section contains rich illuminated manuscripts. The museum also possesses one of the world's richest numismatic collections regarding later dynasties and the caliphs.

In London, the British Museum has been the site of significant cataloging efforts and bequests. Samuel Ayscough, an English librarian and index-maker, served as assistant librarian and cataloged 16,000 ancient charters and rolls. The museum also received the valuable Egerton manuscripts, which deal with the literature of Italy and France, bequeathed by Francis Henry Egerton, the 8th Earl of Bridgewater, upon his death in 1829.
