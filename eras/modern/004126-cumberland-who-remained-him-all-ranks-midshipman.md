## Samuel Barrington and the Duke of Cumberland
Samuel Barrington (1720–1800) was a British admiral and the fourth son of the 1st Viscount Barrington. After entering the navy at a young age, he attained a post-captaincy by 1747. His career included service during the Seven Years' War, where he commanded the "Achilles" (60) in the Basque roads under Hawke. In 1759, the "Achilles" captured a powerful French privateer following two hours of combat. During the same year, Barrington's ship carried the flag of Rear-Admiral Rodney during the Havre-de-Grace expedition. In 1760, he sailed with John Byron to destroy the fortifications at Louisburg.

In 1768, Barrington was appointed to the frigate "Venus" to serve as governor to the Duke of Cumberland. The Duke remained with Barrington through all naval ranks, from midshipman to rear-admiral. By 1778, the Duke's flag-captain, Barrington, had reached the rank of rear-admiral and was deployed to the West Indies. In conjunction with the army, he captured the island of Santa Lucia from the French and successfully repulsed an attempt by the Comte d'Estaing to retake the island. Although later superseded by Byron, Barrington served as Byron's second-in-command and was present at St. Kitts and Grenada on July 6 and 22, 1779. Following his return home, he refused an offer to command the Channel fleet. His final active service occurred in October 1782 during the relief of Gibraltar. He flew his flag as admiral for a short period in 1790 but did not serve in the French revolutionary wars.

## Geography and Industry in Great Britain
In Great Britain, Cumberland is associated with significant mineral deposits. The lead mines of Alston in Cumberland are noted for yielding many fine specimens of beautifully crystallized calcite. Additionally, the Cleator Moor district in west Cumberland is recognized, alongside the Furness district in north Lancashire, as being unsurpassed for the variety of forms and beauty of crystals found in haematite mines.

Historically, the district of Cumberland was targeted during the raids of Olaf Trygvason between A.D. 985 and 988. After ravaging England, Scotland, the Hebrides, Man, and Ireland, Olaf steered to Bretland, where he laid waste to the land and the district of Cumberland with fire and sword.

## North American Geography and Infrastructure
In the United States, the name Cumberland is linked to several locations and transport routes:

**Maryland and the Cumberland Road:**
The city of Baltimore is connected to Fort Cumberland by an old turnpike. From Fort Cumberland, the old Cumberland Road begins, serving as a route for travellers heading toward the Middle West. Nearby, the town of Frostburg in Allegany County is located 11 miles west of Cumberland. Frostburg is served by the Cumberland & Westernport electric railway and the Cumberland & Pennsylvania railway.

**Kentucky and the Cumberland Gap:**
A significant number of early settlers in Kentucky reached the state via the Cumberland Gap and the "Wilderness Road." This route began at Inglis's Ferry on the New river in present-day West Virginia and proceeded west by south to the Cumberland Gap. The "Wilderness Road" was marked by Daniel Boone in 1775 as a trail from the Watauga settlement in east Tennessee to the Cumberland Gap, continuing through Bardstown, Danville, and Crab Orchard to the Falls of the Ohio. It remained passable only for horses and men until 1795, when it was converted into a wagon road.

## Military History in the United States
During the American Civil War, the lower Cumberland river was guarded by the Confederates at Fort Donelson, an entrenched camp in Dover, Tennessee. The fort featured water batteries commanding the river and two continuous lines of entrenchments on the land side. On February 16, 1862, the fort was taken by Federal forces. The operation involved the Union army under Brigadier-General U. S. Grant and a gunboat flotilla led by Commodore A. H. Foote, which ascended the Cumberland river to meet Grant's forces. The Confederate garrison at Donelson, placed there by Albert Sidney Johnston, was commanded by General Floyd.
