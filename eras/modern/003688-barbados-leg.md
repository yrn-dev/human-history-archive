# Barbados Leg

## Definition and Occurrence
"Barbados leg" is the common name given to elephantiasis on the island of Barbados. The condition is noted for being frequent among the negro population of the island.

## Environmental Context
The occurrence of "Barbados leg" exists within a specific climatic and geographical setting. Barbados is an island in the British West Indies, measuring 21 miles in length and 14-1/2 miles at its broadest point, with a total extent of 166 square miles. The island is elevated but not mountainous, featuring a central apex at Mount Hillaby, which reaches 1100 feet.

The climate is described as pleasant, with temperatures generally ranging between 70°F and 86°F, and rarely dropping below 65°F even during the cold season, which extends from December to the end of May. The wet season sees an average annual rainfall of approximately 60 inches, with September being the wettest month. For eight months of the year, the tropical heat is tempered by invigorating N.E. trade winds.

## Public Health and Sanitation
The island's physical characteristics contribute to its general health profile. The porous nature of the soil, the extent of cultivation, and the absence of swamps prevent the presence of miasma. Consequently, fever is unknown on the island. The climate is further noted for its beneficial effects on pulmonary diseases, particularly in their early stages, and its ability to arrest the decay of vital power associated with old age. While "Barbados leg" (elephantiasis) is frequent, leprosy also occurs among the negroes.

## Population and Demographics
The population of Barbados is one of the most densely populated in the world. In 1901, the population was 195,588 (approximately 1178 persons per square mile), increasing to 196,287 by 1906. The majority of the inhabitants are negroes, who outnumber the white population by a ratio of 9 to 1. This group is characterized as having a well-marked dialect and physiognomy of their own and is described as being more intelligent than other West Indian negroes.
