# Brisbane

## Overview and Geography
Brisbane is the capital of Queensland, Australia, located in Stanley county on the banks of the Brisbane river, approximately 25 miles from its mouth at Moreton Bay. The city is constructed upon a series of hills that rise from the river-banks. However, certain areas, including South Brisbane and Woollongabba, are situated on low-lying flats that have occasionally experienced disastrous floods.

The primary buildings and main streets of the city are positioned on a tongue of land created by a southward bend in the river. The extremity of this tongue is open and contains several key sites: the Houses of Parliament, the botanical gardens, Queen's Park (used as a recreation ground), and the grounds surrounding Government House, which serves as the official residence of the colony's governor.

## City Layout and Infrastructure
The heart of the city is traversed by Albert Street, which runs for about three-quarters of a mile from Queen's Park to Albert Park, where the observatory is located. Queen's Street serves as the main thoroughfare of Brisbane, crossing Albert Street midway between the two parks.

Queen's Street leads to the Victoria Bridge, a fine steel structure that connects Brisbane to the separate city of South Brisbane on the opposite side of the river. The current Victoria Bridge replaced a previous structure that was swept away by floods in February 1893.

## Architecture and Landmarks
Brisbane features numerous buildings of architectural merit, although the narrowness of some streets occasionally mars their effect. Prominent structures include:
* **Government and Civic Buildings:** The Houses of Parliament, the lands office, the general post-office, the town halls of both Brisbane and South Brisbane, and a great domed custom-house located on the river-bank.
* **Religious Buildings:** The Roman Catholic cathedral of St Stephen on Elizabeth Street is an imposing structure featuring a detached campanile that houses the largest bell in Australia. The Anglican cathedral is situated on an elevated site in Ann Street; its foundation-stone was laid in 1901 by the prince of Wales (then the duke of York). The city serves as the seat for an Anglican bishop and a Roman Catholic archbishop.
* **Commercial and Other Buildings:** The massive treasury buildings and the Queensland National Bank, which is a classic Italian structure, are noted as being worthy of notice. The city also contains an opera house.

## History and Administration
The city was founded in 1825 as a penal settlement and was named after Sir Thomas Brisbane, who was then the governor of Australia. Sir Thomas Brisbane was a Scottish astronomer and soldier who had been appointed governor of New South Wales in 1821. During his tenure, exploring parties sent out at his instigation discovered the river that now bears his name.

Brisbane became a free settlement in 1842. In 1859, it was incorporated and became the capital of Queensland, having previously belonged to New South Wales. South Brisbane was established as a separate city in 1903.

The municipal governments of both Brisbane and South Brisbane are administered by a mayor and ten aldermen. The surrounding suburbs are managed by divisional boards and shire councils. Major suburbs include Indooroopilly, Highgate, Woollongabba, Bulimba, Breakfast Creek, Toowong, Milon, Paddington, Red Hill, New Farm, Fortitude Valley, and Kangaroo Point.

## Demographics
As of 1901, the population of the city proper was 28,953, while South Brisbane had a population of 25,481. The total population of the metropolitan area in 1901 was 119,907.
