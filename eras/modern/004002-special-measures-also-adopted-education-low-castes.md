# Education and Caste in India

The relationship between education and the caste system in India has been characterized by historical exclusivity and subsequent efforts by various agencies to expand access to learning across different social strata.

## The Influence of the Educated Class
In the early stages of developing social structures, the educated class exercised immense power before the commonalty or the crown began to read and think independently. In India, the Brahmins served as this educated class and, due to their exclusiveness, have retained their influence to the present day. This contrasts with the Western world, where education escaped the limitations of traditions, priesthoods, and castes, becoming integrated into the general life of the community.

## Missionary Approaches to Education
Historically, Protestant missions in India found success primarily in reaching outcaste and low-caste peoples, with particular activity in south Travancore and Tinevelly. However, the Hindu and Mahommedan communities remained largely untouched by these efforts.

Alexander Duff, a Scottish missionary who arrived in Calcutta in May 1830, identified that educational methods must replace evangelizing methods to reach these untouched communities. Duff established an English school where secular knowledge was taught from the rudiments to a university standard, with the Bible serving as the center of the school's work. He utilized the English language as a key to Western knowledge and as an instrument for higher education. 

Duff's educational mission had three primary results:
1. It altered the education policy of the government of India.
2. It led Christian churches in home countries to recognize education as a missionary agency.
3. It allowed Christian ideas to enter the minds of high-caste Hindus.

Following these developments, a government minute was adopted on March 7, 1835, stating that the British government's objective in higher education should be the promotion of European literature and science among the natives of India, and that funds for education would be best used for English education alone.

## State-Led Educational Measures
In certain states, specific experimental and targeted educational programs have been implemented. Since 1893, compulsory education has been conducted experimentally in the Amreli division. The compulsory age for this program is 7 to 12 for boys and 7 to 10 for girls. Within these state frameworks, special measures have been adopted specifically for the education of aboriginal tribes and low castes.

Further educational infrastructure in such states includes:
* **Female Training College:** Operated under the supervision of a Christian lady superintendent.
* **Kala Bhavan:** A technical school featuring departments for agriculture, weaving, dyeing, carpentry, and drawing.
* **State Resources:** The provision of a state library and a state museum managed by a European director.
