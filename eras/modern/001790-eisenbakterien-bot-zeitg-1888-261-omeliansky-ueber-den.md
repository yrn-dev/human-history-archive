# Authorities on Bacteria and Fungi

The study of bacteria and fungi is supported by a wide array of general and specialized authorities, focusing on structure, function, classification, and biological distribution.

## General Authorities on Bacteria
General knowledge regarding the structure and functions of bacteria is documented in the works of Fischer, including the second edition of *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (Oxford, 1900) and a 1903 German publication. Other foundational texts include Migula's *System der Bakterien* (Jena, 1897), Lafar's *Technical Mycology* (London, 1898), and Mace's *Traite pratique de bakteriologie* (5th ed. 1904). Additionally, Engler and Prantl provide information within *Die natuerlichen Pflanzenfamilien* (I. Th. 1 Abt. a).

## Specialized Bacterial Research
Research into specific aspects of bacterial biology is divided into several categories:

*   **Fossil and Water Bacteria:** Renault conducted research on fossil bacteria (*Bacteriacees fossiles*) in 1896. The bacteriology of water has been explored by Frankland and Marshall Ward in reports for the *Proc. R. Soc.*, and specifically by Marshall Ward regarding the biology of *B. ramosus*. Further studies on the bacteria of the river Thames are recorded in the *Ann. of Bot.*
*   **Cellular Structure:** The cell membrane and the construction of bacteria and Cyanophyceen have been studied by Butschli (1896) and Fischer (1897). Rowland provided observations on bacterial structure in 1899.
*   **Cilia and Nucleus:** Fischer, Migula, and Wager have contributed to the study of cilia and the nucleus. Vejdovsky's work includes research on the nucleus of bacteria and its division (1904), as well as cytological studies on bacteria from the Prager water pipeline (1905). Mencl provided additional notes on the structural conditions of *Bacterium gammari* in 1907.
*   **Spores and Classification:** Marshall Ward and Sturgis have written on spores, with Sturgis focusing on a soil bacillus of the type of de Bary's *B. megatherium*. L. Klein also contributed to this field in 1889. Classification of Schizomycetes has been addressed by Marshall Ward (1892), and further classification work is found in the *Atlas and Essentials of Bac* by Lehmann and Neumann.

## Authorities on Fungi
The study of fungi involves a broad range of morphological and anatomical research. General authorities include Engler and Prantl's *Die naturlichen Pflanzenfamilien* (from 1892 onwards), Zopf's *Die Pilze* (1890), De Bary's *Comparative Morphology of Fungi* (1887), and von Tafel's *Vergleichende Morphologie der Pilze* (1892). Brefeld's *Unters. aus dem Gesamtgebiete der Mykologie* (1872-1905) and Lotsy's *Vortrage uber botanische Stammesgeschichte* (1907) also serve as primary sources.

## Fungal Anatomy and Distribution
Specific fungal research is categorized as follows:

*   **Anatomy and Membranes:** Bommer studied sclerotes and mycelium cords (1894), while Mangin observed the membrane of mucorinees (1899). Zimmermann researched the plant cell nucleus (1896), and Wisselingh performed microchemical investigations on fungal cell walls (1898). Istvanffvi provided studies on the physical anatomy of fungi in 1896.
*   **Distribution and Spores:** Cooke's *Introduction to the Study of Fungi* (1895) and works by Felix and Staub address distribution. Spore dispersal by insects was studied by Fulton (1889), and Falck researched spore distribution in Basidiomycetes (1904). Zopf, von Tafel, and Brefeld are cited regarding spores and sporophores.
*   **Classification and Oomycetes:** Classification is detailed by van Tieghem (1893), Brefeld, Engler and Prantl, von Tafel, Saccardo, and Lotsy. Research on Oomycetes includes fertilization studies on *Peronospora parasitica* by Wager (1900), *Albugo Bliti* by Stevens (1899, 1901), and *Pythium de Baryanum* by Miyake (1901). Trow studied fertilization in the Saprolegnieae (1904), and Thaxter identified new aquatic fungi (1895). Lagerheim and Woronin provided research on the Monoblepharideae.
