# Sir Edward Augustus Bond

Sir Edward Augustus Bond (1815–1898) was a prominent English librarian who spent a significant portion of his career at the British Museum, where he is credited with transforming the organization and efficiency of the manuscript department.

## Early Life and Career
Born on December 31, 1815, at Hanwell, Bond was the son of a schoolmaster. He received his education at Merchant Taylors' school. His professional career began in 1832 when he obtained a position in the public record office. In 1838, he transitioned to the British Museum, serving as an assistant in the manuscript department. During this period, he came under the guidance of Sir Frederick Madden, who was considered the most eminent palaeographer of that era.

## Advancement at the British Museum
Bond's career at the British Museum was marked by a steady ascent through the administrative ranks. He was appointed Egerton librarian in 1852 and later became the assistant keeper of MSS. in 1856. By 1867, he was promoted to the post of keeper. In 1878, he reached the position of principal librarian.

During his tenure, Bond implemented reorganizations of the manuscript department that provided lasting value. He is specifically credited with the creation of the classified catalogue of MSS. and for improving the punctuality and efficiency of the department's publications. Under his supervision, the "White Wing" buildings were erected to accommodate newspapers, manuscripts, drawings, and prints. Additionally, the purchase of the Stowe MSS. was concluded during his time in office.

## Contributions to Palaeography and Scholarship
Beyond his administrative duties, Bond made significant contributions to the study of historic documents. In collaboration with Sir E. Maunde Thompson, he founded the Palaeographical Society. He is noted for being the first to establish classical palaeography as an exact science.

As an editor, Bond produced several important works, including:
* Four volumes of facsimiles of Anglo-Saxon charters, covering the period from 679 to the Conquest.
* *The Speeches in the Trial of Warren Hastings* (1859–1861).
* Various other historic documents.

## Honors and Death
In recognition of his achievements, Bond was awarded an LL.D. of Cambridge in 1879. He was created a C.B. in 1885. He received the honor of K.C.B. on January 1, 1898, the day before his death on January 2, 1898.
