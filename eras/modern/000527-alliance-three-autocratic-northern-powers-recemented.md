# The Holy Alliance of the Three Autocratic Northern Powers

The "Holy Alliance" was a diplomatic arrangement between three autocratic northern powers that played a significant role in the geopolitical landscape of the 19th century. This alliance was recemented at Muenchengraetz in 1833 and served as a mechanism for mutual support among the participating autocratic regimes.

## Role and Impact of the Alliance
The alliance provided critical strategic support for Austria during the mid-19th century. Specifically, the Holy Alliance was the instrument that secured the decisive intervention of the tsar in 1849 on behalf of Austria. However, the stability of this partnership was eventually undermined by Austria's own diplomatic choices.

The alliance was "hopelessly shattered" due to Austria's attitude during the Crimean War. This breakdown left Austria in a state of diplomatic isolation, emerging from the conflict without friends or allies.

## Aftermath and Shift in Power
The collapse of the Holy Alliance led to a realignment of ties among the northern powers. Russia, feeling justly offended by Austria, strengthened its relationship with Prussia. During this period, Bismarck in Prussia was developing plans that would eventually mature in 1866.

While the alliance of Tilsit was not revived—due to the attitude of Napoleon regarding the Polish question—Russia maintained goodwill toward France. This goodwill proved beneficial for France during its subsequent struggle with Austria in Italy.

## The Isolation of Austria
The consequences of the shattered alliance were evident in several diplomatic and military events:

*   **The Congress of Paris:** Austria's isolation was conspicuous during this congress, where Cavour, the Sardinian plenipotentiary, highlighted the scandal of Austrian rule in Italy before the assembled European powers.
*   **The Campaign of 1859:** Sardinia, allied with France, established the foundations of a united Italy. Although Prussia threatened to intervene, this was motivated by a fear of the "undue aggrandizement of France" rather than a desire to support Austria.

The results for Austria were both external and internal. Externally, Austria lost all its Italian possessions with the exception of Venice. Internally, the failure of its position necessitated constitutional concessions to appease public opinion.
