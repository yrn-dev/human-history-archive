# Arya Samaj

The Arya Samaj is a movement characterized by an intensity of conviction and a narrower scope compared to eclectic systems such as the Brahma Samaj, which seeks a common basis among all great religions. The movement appealed to educated natives whose faith in current Hinduism had been undermined, but who remained predisposed against foreign religious influences. These individuals, driven by patriotic ardour, adopted a view of India's original faith that appeared to harmonize with the ethics of European civilization and the discoveries of modern science. This alignment led them to support their leader's polemic against European rationalist and agnostic literature.

## Growth and Membership
The movement experienced significant growth during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. By 1890, the number of members had reached 40,000, and by 1900, this figure increased to over 92,000. To be admitted to the Samaj, an individual must be eighteen years of age. Members are expected to contribute at least 1% of their income to the organization's funds. The creed of the Arya Samaj is summarized in ten articles.

## Internal Divisions
Despite its growth, the movement experienced internal divisions. A primary cleavage emerged between two sections: the *Ghasi* and the *Mansi*. The *Ghasi* section is characterized as vegetarian. The *Mansi* section consists of those who eat flesh. The *Mansi* group includes Rajputs who, while generally sympathetic to the movement, refused to follow the tenet of the Samaj that forbade the consumption of animal food and the destruction of animal life.
