# Carinthia

## Historical Overview
Carinthia is a region located within the territory known to the ancients as Noricum and Pannonia, an area bounded by the Inn, the Danube, and the Save. Along with Austria, Styria, Carniola, and Sclavonia, it formed part of the patrimony of a single family and served as a center of Austrian power. 

During the Napoleonic era, parts of Carinthia were included in the Illyrian Provinces, which were ceded by Austria to Italy at the peace of Schönbrunn on October 14, 1809. These territories were occupied by French troops and governed in the interest of Napoleon. Following the end of the French occupation around the end of 1813, the provinces reverted to Austria. In 1816, Carinthia became one of the crown-lands used to constitute the kingdom of Illyria, a status it maintained until 1849.

## Demographics and Language
Carinthia is characterized by a mixed population of Germans and Slovenes. While the Slovenes form a large majority in Carniola, they exist as a considerable minority in the south of Styria and the adjoining territory of Carinthia.

The distribution of these populations often varied between urban and rural settings. For example, the town of Klagenfurt was predominantly German, with a population of 16,491 German-speaking Austrians. In contrast, the Slovenian-speaking population of the town numbered 568, though this figure included 180 individuals who were residents of the hospital or the gaol. Despite the town's German majority, the population of the surrounding rural villages was chiefly Slovene.

## Administrative and Political Tensions
The linguistic diversity of the region led to administrative disputes. In 1880, the government declared Slovenian a customary language in Klagenfurt. This mandate required that public offices and law courts make provisions for conducting business in the Slovenian language, a move that led to complaints from Germans who felt the government had acted unfairly toward them.

On a broader scale, the Austrian monarchy managed its diverse populations through a system of racial constituencies. In mixed districts, separate registers and constituencies were established for electors of each race. This system ensured that electors could only vote for candidates of their own race; consequently, Germans were required to vote for Germans and Slovenes for Slovenes. This structure prevented victories of one race over another, such as Slovenes over Italians. The allotment of these constituencies was determined by taking the tax-paying capacity of the various races into consideration. In this system, Carinthia was assigned 10 constituencies.

## Regional Aspirations
The Slovenes in Carinthia and other regions demanded that their language be utilized for education and government purposes. Their political ambition was the establishment of an "Illyrian" kingdom, which would encompass Croatia and all southern Slavs in the coast district. As part of this movement, there was an attempt to create a unified "Illyrian" language to be accepted by both Slovenes and Croats, though this effort was not very successful.
