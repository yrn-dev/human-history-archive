# Motor Automatism

Motor automatism is defined as a non-reflex movement of a voluntary muscle. These movements are executed while the subject is in a waking state, yet they are not controlled by ordinary waking consciousness. This phenomenon is a significant element in primitive ceremonies of divination and provides substantial material for the field of Psychical Research.

## Classifications and Levels of Automatism

Automatic acts are categorized into two primary types. An act is considered purely automatic when the action continues while the subject's attention is focused on entirely different subjects, such as during the act of cycling. Conversely, automatism is termed relative if the subject's attention is fixed on a specific part or the end goal of an action, while the other component parts are performed unconsciously.

Motor automatism manifests at various levels of complexity:
*   **Low Level:** This involves vague movements of large muscle groups. Examples include "bier-divination," where the actions of bearers are used to infer the residence or identity of a murderer, and various forms of witch seeking.
*   **Specialized Action:** A typical example is the divining rod, which is used to indicate the presence of water or, among the uncivilized, to trace criminals.
*   **Higher Stage:** This level involves delicate movements, most notably automatic drawing and automatic writing.

## Speech Automatism and Glossolalia

A parallel to automatic writing is found in the action of speech centres. This can result in a range of utterances, from babblings that are unintelligible to trance speeches delivered in the speaker's ordinary language. A specific form of this is known as Glossolalia. In the case of Helene Smith, Th. Flournoy demonstrated that such utterances can reach a higher plane and form a real language, although this language is based on one already known to the speaker.

## Automatic Writing

Automatic writing is writing performed without the volition of the agent. The agent may be entirely unconscious of the words being written, or they may be aware of the specific word being written and a few words surrounding it, though they rarely perceive the meaning of the entire text. This state can occur during spontaneous or induced trances, in morbid states such as hystero-epilepsy, or in conditions indistinguishable from normal wakefulness.

### Historical and Spiritualist Context
Automatic writing was prominent in the early days of the American spiritualist movement (c. 1850-1860), where many lengthy writings purported to be produced under spirit guidance, some in "unknown tongues." Notable published works include *The Healing of the Nations* by Charles Linton, *Messages from the Spirit Life* by J. Murray Spear, and Andrew J. Davis's *Great Harmonia*. In England, early spiritualist newspapers featured "inspirational" writing, such as *Pages of Ike Paraclete*, and the *Spirit Teachings* of the Rev. W. Stainton Moses. During the early years of the movement, a "planchette"—a small heart-shaped board on wheels—was used to facilitate the writing process.

### Psychological and Experimental Applications
While theories of external inspiration have been generally discredited, automatic writing is now used to investigate subconscious mental processes. It can reveal knowledge that has lapsed from primary consciousness, such as foreign languages or forgotten poetry. 

In clinical settings, Pierre Janet and others have used automatic writing to communicate with subconscious strata of the personality in cases of dissociation of consciousness or hystero-epilepsy. For instance, a patient experiencing hysterical convulsions who cannot respond to oral appeals may be induced to answer questions in writing to reveal the secret of their malady or accept therapeutic suggestions.

Additionally, Edmund Gurney conducted experiments showing that information given to a subject in a hypnotic trance could be reproduced through handwriting while the subject was occupied with reading aloud or conversing. Similarly, arithmetical problems set during a trance could be solved via handwriting without the subject's apparent consciousness. Some recordings, such as those by the American medium Mrs. Piper in a state of trance, are thought by some to indicate communication with the dead or exhibit telepathic powers.

## Related Concepts

### Sensory Automatism
Distinct from motor automatism, sensory automatism is a term used by psychical research students to describe a centrally initiated hallucination. These are commonly provoked by crystal-gazing, though the use of a shell can cause auditory hallucinations (shell-hearing), and other senses may also be affected.

### Biological and Mechanical Automatism
The term "automatic" also appears in other contexts within the archive:
*   **Biological:** In the human organism, the rhythmic principle manifests as automatic pulses and flexions of the blood and tissues.
*   **Mechanical:** The term describes contrivances such as those used for opening and closing landaus or lifting the hoods of victorias. In weaponry, "automatic" machine-guns (such as the British Maxim, Hotchkiss, and Colt types) are those where loading, firing, extracting, and ejecting are performed by the gun itself via the recoil of the barrel or escaping explosion gases, rather than by hand-power applied to a lever.
