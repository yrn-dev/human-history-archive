# Rivers of the World

The archive contains records of various river systems across Ireland, England, Canada, and the Middle East, detailing their geographical courses, drainage areas, and navigational utility.

## Rivers of Ireland

The Bann is a primary river in northern Ireland, originating in the Mourne mountains in south Co. Down. It flows northwest into Lough Neagh, which it drains in a north-northwest direction toward an estuary at Coleraine, with Lough Beg forming immediately below the larger lough. The valley's length is approximately 90 m. when excluding lesser windings. Its total drainage area is about 2300 sq. m., encompassing parts of the counties of Antrim, Armagh, Down, Londonderry, Monaghan, and Tyrone, and extending west to the borders of Co. Fermanagh. The river is divided into the Upper Bann (above Lough Neagh) and the Lower Bann (below). While it possesses valuable salmon fisheries, it is not considered important for navigation.

The Barrow is located in south-eastern Ireland, rising in the Slieve Bloom mountains. It flows easterly and then almost due south to join the Suir, forming the Waterford Harbour estuary on the south coast. Excluding lesser windings, the valley length is over 100 m., which includes 12 m. of the estuary. Its drainage area to Waterford Harbour, including the Suir basin, is 3500 sq. m., covering the entirety of county Kilkenny and portions of Carlow, Cork, King's, Limerick, Queen's, Tipperary, and Waterford. The river's chief affluent is the Nore, which enters from the north-west above New Ross. Key towns along its banks include Bagenalstown, Carlow, Athy—where the river is navigable and connects with the Grand Canal—and New Ross.

## Rivers of England

The Avon is represented by two distinct rivers. The East or Hampshire Avon rises in Wiltshire south of Marlborough, watering the Vale of Pewsey and collecting feeders from high downs between Devizes and Marlborough. After breaching Salisbury Plain and passing Amesbury, it reaches Salisbury, where it is joined by the Wylye on the west bank and the Bourne on the east. It continues through a fertile, widening valley past Ringwood, Fordingbridge, and Downton, skirting the New Forest to the west. At Christchurch, it receives the Stour from the west and enters the English Channel via Christchurch harbour 2-1/2 m. further. Excluding lesser sinuosities, its length is about 60 m., with Salisbury situated 35 m. above the mouth. It has a drainage area of 1132 sq. m. and a total fall of over 500 ft. (140 ft. from Salisbury). It is not of commercial value for navigation but is rich in loach and valuable salmon fisheries.

The Lower or Bristol Avon rises in Gloucestershire on the eastern slope of the Cotteswold Hills, collecting streams east of Malmesbury and south of Tetbury. It flows in a wide curve through an upper valley past Melksham and Chippenham before turning west to Bradford-on-Avon. It receives the Frome from the south and enters a narrow valley containing Bristol and Bath. Below Bristol, the river forms the Clifton Gorge, known for its wooded cliffs and the Clifton suspension bridge. The river enters the Severn estuary at Avonmouth. From Bristol downward, it is a significant commercial waterway. The Kennet and Avon Canal follows the river from Bradford to Bath, entering the river via seven locks. Excluding minor sinuosities, the river is about 75 m. long; distances are 10 m. from Bradford to Bath, 12 m. to Bristol, and 8 m. to the mouth. Its drainage area is 891 sq. miles, and its total fall is between 500 and 600 ft.

## Rivers of Canada

The Mackenzie is a river in the North-West Territories that discharges the waters of Great Slave Lake into the Arctic Ocean. Discovered and first navigated by Sir Alexander Mackenzie in 1789, it has an average width of 1 m. and an approximate discharge of 500,000 cub. ft. per second at a medium stage. Including its great tributary, the Peace, the total length is 2,350 m. The Mackenzie enters the Arctic Ocean near 68° 50´ W. and 135° W. after flowing through a flat delta for 70 to 80 m. It is navigable from June 10th to October 20th.

The Peace river rises as the Finlay in the mountains of British Columbia, flowing through an intermontane valley bordering the Rocky Mountains to its confluence with the Parsnip. The combined waters, now the Peace, flow east through the Rocky Mountains and then northeast to join the river discharging Lake Athabasca, becoming the Slave river from there to Great Slave Lake. The Peace is navigable for about 220 m. from its junction with the Slave river to Vermilion Falls. Its principal tributaries include the Battle, Halfway, Loon, Nation, North Pine, Omineca, Parsnip, South Pine, and Smoky rivers.

The Athabasca (or Elk) river rises in the Rocky Mountains near the Yellowhead Pass and flows northeast to Athabasca Landing, then north into Lake Athabasca. It is 740 m. long with tributaries including the Clearwater, Lesser Slave (which drains Lesser Slave lake), McLeod, and Pembina. Lake Athabasca is 195 m. long, 20 to 32 m. wide, with an area of 3085 sq. m. and an elevation of 690 ft. above the sea. The system discharges northward via the Slave river and Mackenzie system. Navigation is possible via shallow draught steamers, though interrupted at Grand Rapids near the mouth of the Clearwater river.

## Rivers of the Middle East

In ancient times, the Tigris is believed to have followed a course above Bagdad that was less winding and more to the westward than its current path. In the region, there are two distinct rivers known as the Greater Zab and the Lesser Zab. While Herodotus noted they were distinct rivers that shared the same name, Xenophon's narratives make no mention of the Lesser Zab, leading some to suggest he may have mistaken the two synonymous rivers for one.
