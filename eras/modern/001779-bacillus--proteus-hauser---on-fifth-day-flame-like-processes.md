# Bacillus--Proteus (Hauser)

## Overview of Bacteria
Bacteria are organisms that are typically colorless, although a small number secrete coloring matters other than chlorophyll. Due to the absence of chlorophyll, nearly all bacteria exist as either parasitic or saprophytic forms. In terms of size, their cells are commonly approximately 0.001 mm (1 micron) in diameter and range from two to five times that length, though some larger and smaller variations exist.

The historical presence of bacteria is evidenced by fossils. Research conducted by van Tieghem and Renault indicates that large numbers of bacteria existed during the Devonian and Carboniferous periods, and likely earlier.

## Bacillus--Proteus (Hauser)
*Bacillus--Proteus* (Hauser), also referred to as *Bacillus (Proteus) vulgaris* (Hauser), exhibits specific growth patterns and colony formations. When observed in a hanging drop, the formation of a colony of *Bacillus (Proteus) vulgaris* follows a rapid developmental sequence:
* At 11 A.M., a single rodlet appears.
* By 4 P.M., the rodlet has grown, divided, and broken into eight rodlets.
* Further development continues through 8 P.M. and 9:30 P.M.

By the fifth day, a plate-culture colony of *Bacillus--Proteus* (Hauser) displays distinct characteristics. The colony moves as a whole, and its contours are continually changing. This movement is characterized by "flame-like processes" and outliers, which are composed of writhing filaments.

## General Characteristics of Bacilli
The term *bacillus* is derived from the Latin for "little rod or stick." Various species of bacilli exhibit different behaviors and structures:
* **Bacillus subtilis:** This species produces ripe spores that are highly refractive, colorless, and measure 0.0006 mm broad by 0.0012 mm long. These spores can be spherical, long-ovoid, or ovoid. Spore formation typically occurs in a bacillar segment, and in some instances, the spore-forming protoplasm reacts blue when treated with iodine solutions.
* **Bacillus megaterium:** This species can form chains of motile rodlets that continue to grow and divide.
* **Bacillus anthracis:** When cultured in a drop of aqueous humour, these rodlets grow into septate, long *leptothrix*-like filaments where spores develop within the segments.
* **Bacillus ramosus:** Spore development in this species begins with the formation of brilliant granules that ball together to form spores, which then acquire a membrane and ripen.

## Bacterial Growth and Interaction
Bacterial reproduction can be extremely rapid; Brefeld observed that a bacterium may divide every half-hour. This rate of division means a single bacterium could potentially produce millions of millions of segments within a twenty-four-hour period.

In medical and biological contexts, certain serums can cause "agglutination," where bacteria distributed in a fluid aggregate into clumps. This process arrests the movement of motile bacteria and leads to "sedimentation," where the clumps settle and leave the upper fluid clear. This phenomenon has been demonstrated in various infections, including cholera, plague, and typhoid fever.

Regarding toxicity, some toxins may involve enzymes. For example, in diphtheria, Sidney Martin identified toxic albumoses in the spleen, which he attributed to the digestive action of an enzyme formed by the bacillus in the membrane.
