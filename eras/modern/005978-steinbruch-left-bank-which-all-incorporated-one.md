# Budapest

Budapest is the largest town and the capital of the kingdom of Hungary. It serves as the second town of the Austro-Hungarian monarchy and is located 163 miles southeast of Vienna by rail. The city is situated nearly in the center of Hungary, occupying a strategic position that dominates the approach from the west to the great Hungarian plain.

## Geography and Topography
The city is situated on both banks of the Danube River at the point where the river takes a definite southward course. It is located where the Carpathians meet the Bakony Mountains, which are the outlying spurs of the outer ramifications of the Alps. The Danube at this location is between 300 and 650 yards broad.

The two banks of the city present a sharp contrast. The left bank consists of a flat sandy plain. In contrast, the right bank is characterized by a series of small, steep hills. These hills are generally devoid of forests, though they were formerly covered with vineyards that produced a good red wine; however, the phylloxera has almost completely destroyed these vineyards. The right bank is commanded by the Blocksberg, which reaches a height of 770 feet (390 feet above the Danube), and a fortress. Beyond these are mountain spurs rising in terraces.

## Municipal History and Structure
Budapest was formed by the incorporation of several towns into one municipality in 1872. These included Buda (German: *Ofen*) and Ó-Buda (German: *Alt-Ofen*) on the right bank, and Pest and Köbánya (German: *Steinbruch*) on the left bank.

The city covers an area of 78 square miles and is divided into ten municipal districts. The districts on the right bank, which belong to Buda, are:
* Vár (Festung)
* Viziváros (Wasserstadt)
* Ó-Buda (Alt-Ofen)

The districts on the left bank, which belong to Pest, are:
* Belváros (Inner City)
* Lipótváros (Leopoldstadt)
* Terézváros (Theresienstadt)
* Erzsébetváros (Elisabethstadt)
* Józsefváros (Josephstadt)
* Ferenczváros (Franzstadt)
* Köbánya (Steinbruch)

## City Functions and Infrastructure
The city is divided by function between its two banks. Buda serves as the official center, housing the royal palace, various ministries, and other government offices. Pest serves as the industrial and commercial part of the city, as well as the center for intellectual and nationalistic life. Pest is described as modern in aspect, regularly laid out, and featuring a long frontage of handsome buildings facing the river.

The two banks are connected by six bridges, including two suspension bridges:
* **The Ketten-Brücke:** Constructed between 1842 and 1849 by Adam Clark and the brothers Tiernay, it is one of Europe's largest. It measures 410 yards long, 39 feet broad, and 36 feet high above the mean water level. Its chains are supported by two 160-foot pillars, and the ends are decorated with four colossal stone lions. Adam Clark also constructed a 383-yard tunnel in 1854 that pierces the castle hill to connect the Danube with the Christinenstadt quarter.
* **The Schwurplatz Bridge:** Completed in 1903, this bridge is 56 feet broad with a span of 317 yards.

Other connections include the Franz Joseph bridge, the Margaret bridge (which includes a junction bridge to Margaret island), and two railway bridges.
