## Military Ranks and Officers
The archive contains records of several individuals who held the rank of general or related senior officer positions across different eras and countries.

**British and Prussian Generals**
Sir Benjamin D'Urban (1777–1849) was a British general and colonial administrator who entered the army in 1793. He served in Holland, Westphalia, and San Domingo, and later held staff offices in Dublin. During the Peninsular War, he was selected as quartermaster-general for the reorganization of the Portuguese army, participating in nine sieges and pitched battles, including Toulouse, Salamanca, and Badajoz. He was promoted to major-general in the Portuguese army and colonel in the British army in 1813, later becoming a major-general in 1819 and a lieutenant-general in 1837.

Paul Bronsart von Schellendorf (1832–1891) was a Prussian general who entered the Prussian Guards in 1849. He served on the general staff starting in 1861 and later lectured at the war academy. During the war of 1870, he was chief of a section on the Great General Staff and handled preliminary negotiations for the French surrender at Sedan. He served as war minister from 1883 to 1889, during which time he introduced the magazine rifle to the Prussian army. He also authored *Der Dienst des Generalstabes*, a treatise on the duties of the general staff.

**Russian and German Officers**
General Dragomirov was a Russian officer who served as adjutant-general to the tsar and chief of the 53rd Volhynia regiment. He spent eleven years as chief of the Nicholas Academy, where he introduced European military literature to improve the Russian officer-corps. From 1889 to 1903, he was the governor-general of Podolsk, Volhynia, and Kiev, and commander-in-chief of the Kiev military district. He was promoted to general of infantry in 1891.

In the German army, records mention several high-ranking officers seated with Crown Prince Rupprecht, including General Falkenhayn (Minister of War), General Krafft von Dellmensingen (chief of the Crown Prince’s staff), and Colonel-General von Plessen (Adjutant-General).

**Service of Supply**
In one organizational structure, administrative and supply services were transferred to the headquarters of the services of supplies at Tours under a commanding general. This commanding general oversaw several subordinate roles, including:
* The Provost Marshal General (responsible for the maintenance of order in general).
* The Director General of Transportation.

**Ancient Greece**
Historical accounts from Xenophon and Diodorus discuss Athenian generals. In one instance, two generals were condemned by the Athenians for being accomplices in a conspiracy by Mellon against Theban rulers. In another case, generals were accused by Theramenês and Thrasybulus of neglecting drowning men. The generals defended themselves by claiming they had delegated the rescue duty to Theramenês and Thrasybulus, who were trierarchs with forty-eight triremes.
