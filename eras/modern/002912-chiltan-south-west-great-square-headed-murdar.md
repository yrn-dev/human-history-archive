# Chiltan and the Geography of Quetta

## Topography and Peaks
The region surrounding Quetta in Baluchistan is characterized by some of the area's most imposing precipices and peaks. These heights range in elevation from 10,500 to 11,500 feet. Among the prominent peaks overlooking the cantonment are Takatu to the north, Khalifat to the east (which flanks the Harnai loop of the Sind-Pishin railway), and the great square-headed Murdar to the south. Chahiltan, also referred to as Chiltan, is situated to the south-west.

## The Quetta Plain
Quetta itself is located in the midst of these peaks. It sits on an open plain at an elevation of 5,500 feet above the sea. This plain was formed by the high-level tributaries of the Lora, which also raised the Pishin valley located to the north.

## Tribal Populations
The area is defined by specific ethnic and tribal distributions. The Suliman system ends in a mass of curved wrinkles and twisted flexures; this region is occupied by true Baluchis. Specifically, the Bugti and Marri sections of the great Rind confederation of tribes, who claim an Arabic origin, own this area. The Bozdars, another Rind clan, are located to the north of the Marri and Bugti. These Rind tribes represent an exception to the general pattern of Pathan occupation found in northern Baluchistan.

In contrast, Pathans are prominent in other divisions of the region. The most notable tribal divisions among the Pathans include the Dumars and Kakars of Pishin, as well as the Mando Khel of Zhob. There are no Pathans residing in the area occupied by the Marri and Bugti sections.
