# Exports and Industry of Baden

The economic structure of Baden is characterized by a balance between its traditional agricultural roots and a substantial industrial output. While the agricultural industry formerly provided the bulk of the country's wealth, it is now equalled or surpassed by industrial production, which has reached considerable dimensions.

## Industrial Production
The manufacturing sector in Baden is diverse, producing a wide array of chief articles. These include:
*   **Textiles and Apparel:** Woollen and cotton goods, as well as silk ribbons.
*   **Materials and Chemicals:** Paper, leather, glass, china, and chemicals.
*   **Precision Goods:** Clocks and jewellery.
*   **Other Manufactures:** Machinery and tobacco.

Additionally, beet sugar is manufactured on a large scale. The region's Black Forest is specifically noted for the dexterity of its inhabitants in producing organs, musical boxes, toys, and wooden ornaments.

## Export Trade and Commerce
The exports of Baden are of considerable importance and largely coincide with the industrial goods mentioned above. However, the bulk of the region's trade is comprised of the transit of goods rather than direct exports. 

The country's commerce is supported by an extensive network of roads and railways, the majority of which are state-owned. A primary railway line runs the length of the land, generally parallel to the Rhine, with various branches crossing from east to west. Mannheim serves as the chief manufacturing town of the duchy and the administrative seat for the northern portion of the country; it also functions as the great emporium for goods exported down the Rhine, benefiting from significant river traffic.

## Natural Resources and Forestry
Baden possesses a higher proportion of forest land than any other south German state. These forests consist predominantly of pine and fir, though chestnut trees are also well represented. Forestry is a significant contributor to the export economy, with at least one-third of the annual timber supply being exported.

The land use of the area is divided between forest (38%) and cultivated land (56.8%). In the valleys, the fertile soil produces a variety of crops, including:
*   **Grains and Staples:** Wheat, barley, spelt, maize, potatoes, and rye (the latter also grown in mountainous areas).
*   **Industrial and Other Crops:** Beetroot, tobacco, flax, hemp, and hops.
*   **Horticulture:** Bee-keeping is common, and orchards provide an abundance of fruits, particularly walnuts and almonds.

The region also produces wines characterized by a mildness of flavour, which are in good demand. Livestock rearing is widely practiced, including the raising of goats, pigs, sheep, and cattle.
