# Auld Licht Idylls

The *Auld Licht Idylls* is a collection of Scots village sketches written by the British novelist and dramatist James Matthew Barrie. Published in 1888, the work is a compilation of articles originally written for the *St James's Gazette*.

## Origins and Development
James Matthew Barrie (born May 9, 1860, in Kirriemuir, Forfarshire) entered the world of literature through journalism. After serving as a leader-writer for the *Nottingham Journal* starting in February 1883, he began submitting articles to editors in London. On November 17, 1884, Frederick Greenwood of the *St James's Gazette* printed Barrie's article titled "An Auld Licht Community." This publication led to a series of further "Idylls" regarding the Auld Licht community, which eventually prompted Barrie to move to London in 1885.

## Content and Inspiration
The *Auld Licht Idylls* portray the humours and life of Barrie's native village, which he idealized in his writing as "Thrums." The sketches were the result of his own early observations combined with the tales told to him by his mother. Barrie noted that his mother "told me everything," and as a result, his memories of their "little red town" were influenced and coloured by her own memories.

## Literary Context
The work represents a transition in Barrie's early career. Prior to the *Idylls*, he had published his first book in 1887, titled *Better Dead*, which was a *jeu d'esprit* and a specimen of his humorous journalism also derived from the *St James's Gazette*. The *Auld Licht Idylls* continued this exploration of humor and local character.
