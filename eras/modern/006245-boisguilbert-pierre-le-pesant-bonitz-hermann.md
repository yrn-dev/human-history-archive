## Pierre le Pesant Boisguilbert

Pierre le Pesant Boisguilbert, Sieur de (1676-1714), was a French economist born in Rouen to an ancient noble family of Normandy. His family was allied to that of Corneille. Boisguilbert received a classical education in Rouen and entered the magistracy, serving as a judge at Montivilliers, located near Havre. In 1690, he was appointed president of the *bailliage* of Rouen, a position he held until nearly the end of his life, eventually leaving the post to his son.

Through his judicial and administrative roles, Boisguilbert conducted a detailed study of local economic conditions. He personally supervised the cultivation of his own lands and maintained relationships with the primary merchants of Rouen. These experiences led him to observe the misery of the populace resulting from the burden of taxation.

### Economic Theories and Works
In 1695, Boisguilbert published his primary work, *Le Detail de la France; la cause de la diminution de ses biens, et la facilite du remede*. In this text, he described the general ruin affecting all classes of French citizens, which he attributed to a poor economic regime. Boisguilbert disagreed with the views of Colbert, arguing that a country's wealth is not found in the abundance of money it possesses, but rather in what it produces and exchanges.

To remedy the economic evils of his time, Boisguilbert proposed the equalization of imposts rather than their simple reduction. He believed this would enable the poor to consume more, thereby increasing production and general wealth. His specific demands included:
* The reform of the *taille*.
* The suppression of internal customs duties.
* Greater freedom of trade.

In 1705 or 1706, he published *Factum de la France*, which provided a more concise summary of these ideas. He proposed replacing all customs duties and aides with a single capitation tax consisting of a tenth of the revenue of all property. This proposal found little support and was opposed by the farmers of taxes.

### Reception and Legacy
Due to a style described as inelegant and diffuse, Boisguilbert's work passed almost unnoticed during his lifetime. He sought a hearing with the comte de Pontchartrain, telling the comte that he would first believe him mad, then become interested, and finally realize he was right; Pontchartrain responded by stating he did think him mad and turned his back on him. He met with similar lack of success with Michel de Chamillart, who had served as intendant of Rouen from 1689 to 1690.

In 1712, a work titled *Testament politique de M. de Vauban* appeared, which was actually Boisguilbert's *Detail de la France*. Additionally, the work *Dime royale* was formerly wrongly attributed to Vauban. Boisguilbert's writings were later collected by Daire in the first volume of the *Collection des grands economistes*, and his letters are included in the *Correspondance des controleurs generaux*, vol. i., published by M. de Boislisle.

## Hermann Bonitz

Hermann Bonitz (1814-1888) was a German scholar born on July 29, 1814, in Langensalza, Saxony. He pursued his studies at Berlin under Lachmann and Bockh, and at Leipzig under G. Hermann.

### Academic Career
Bonitz held a variety of teaching and administrative positions throughout his career:
* Teacher at the Blochmann institute in Dresden (1836).
* Oberlehrer at the Graues Kloster (1840) and the Friedrich-Wilhelms gymnasium (1838) in Berlin.
* Professor at the gymnasium at Stettin (1842).
* Professor at the university of Vienna (1849).
* Member of the imperial academy (1854).
* Member of the council of education (1864).
* Director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium (1867).

Bonitz retired in 1888 and died in Berlin on July 25 of that year. He was heavily involved in higher education and was primarily responsible for the system of examination and teaching used in Prussian high schools after 1882.

### Scholarly Contributions
Outside of Germany, Bonitz is best known as a commentator on Aristotle and Plato. His significant works on these philosophers include:
* *Disputationes Platonicae Duae* (1837) and *Platonische Studien* (3rd ed., 1886).
* *Observations Criticae in Aristotelis Libros Metaphysicos* (1842).
* *Observationes Criticae in Aristotelis quae feruntur Magna Moralia et Ethica Eudemia* (1844).
* *Alexandri Aphrodisiensis Commentarius in Libras Metaphysicos Aristotelis* (1847).
* *Aristotelis Metaphysica* (1848-1849).
* *Uber die Kategorien des A.* (1853).
* *Aristotelische Studien* (1862-1867).
* *Index Aristotelicus* (1870).

Bonitz also authored works on other classical subjects, including *Beitrage zur Erklarung des Thukydides* (1854), *des Sophokles* (1856-1857), and *Uber den Ursprung der homerischen Gedichte* (5th ed., 1881). He contributed extensively to the *Zeitschrift fur die osterreichischen Gymnasien* on educational and classical topics. His scholarly notes were also utilized in the analysis of Aristotle's descriptions of the gifts of Sokratês, specifically regarding the term *διαπορῆσαι* (to go through the various points of difficulty).
