## Cartographic Compilation and General Maps
General and chorographical maps (derived from the Greek *chôra*, meaning region or country) are created either by reducing existing topographical maps or by compiling miscellaneous available sources. When a cartographer reduces a topographical map, the primary task is to generalize information and select place names judiciously to avoid overcrowding the map with details.

The process is significantly more complex when no surveys are available. In such instances, compilers must rely on a variety of sources, including astronomical observations and route surveys provided by travelers, reconnaissance surveys of small districts, and information from native sources. To combine these materials effectively, the compiler must possess knowledge of surveying, astronomical observation methods, and the languages used in travelers' accounts and native tongues.

The archive notes that compilers should independently examine explorer accounts rather than relying solely on existing maps, as draughtsmen or explorers often omit available information. Furthermore, while latitudes from travelers are generally reliable, longitudes are often cautioned against. For example, Captain Speke placed the capital of Uganda at longitude 32° 44´ E., whereas more trustworthy observations determined it to be 32° 26´ E., an error of 18´. Similarly, a map illustrating Livingstone's "Last Journals" incorrectly showed the Luapula issuing from the Bangweulu in the north-west, despite native accounts indicating it leaves the lake on the south.

## Military Information and Strategic Mapping
During military operations, maps serve as critical tools for general information and strategical studies. General Pershing utilized a map displayed to the public to demonstrate the military results achieved by United States armies. This map utilized colored pins and tags to distinguish between allied and enemy armies.

Such maps provide comprehensive data, including:
* The location of all allied and enemy divisions on the western front.
* The correct battle line and boundaries.
* The location of headquarters and commanding generals.
* The status of divisions, such as whether they were tired or fresh.

At the time the armistice was signed, United States forces held 145 kilometers of front, with 134 kilometers being active. These maps were updated daily by the third section of General Pershing's staff.

## The Victim Notification System (VNS)
The Victim Notification System (VNS) is a free, computer-based cooperative effort between the United States Attorneys' Offices, the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), the Federal Bureau of Prisons (BOP), and the United States Postal Inspection Service (USPIS). Its purpose is to ensure victims of federal crime are treated fairly by providing information on case events.

Victims access the system via the internet or a Call Center using a VIN and PIN. Available information includes:
* **Upcoming and Historical Events:** Information regarding future and past court hearings.
* **Defendant Details:** Criminal charges filed, the outcome of those charges, and sentencing.
* **Custody Information:** If a defendant is in BOP custody, the system provides the location and projected release date.

In the course of a criminal case, the FBI collects victim contact information during the investigation stage and exports it to the VNS. The FBI Victim Specialist can generate seven types of notification letters: initial notification, notice that the case is under investigation, arrest of the defendant, declination of prosecution, advice of victim rights, investigation closed, and "other." Once the prosecution stage begins after a charging document is filed, the USAO's Victim Witness Specialist assumes responsibility for notifications, including notices for arraignments, proposed plea agreements, and sentencing hearings.

## Legal Interpretations and Administrative Procedures
Within the Department of Justice, the Office of Legal Counsel (OLC)—which provides legal advice to the President and executive branch agencies—provided informal guidance on April 1, 2005, regarding the definition of a "crime victim" under the CVRA. The OLC concluded that "crime victim" status reasonably commences upon the filing of a complaint and ends if a decision is made not to prosecute or indict the Federal offense that caused the harm.

In specific investigations, such as the Epstein investigation, the FBI Victim Specialist handled formal notifications. For example, on January 10, 2008, notification letters were mailed to 14 victims articulating eight CVRA rights. Earlier, on August 8, 2006, a "Victim Notification Form" was prepared naming 30 victims.

## Correctional Facility Information
Under BOP standards, employees must cooperate with official investigations by truthfully responding to questions and providing all pertinent information. Regarding the operation of facilities, Special Housing Units (SHUs) are used to separate inmates from the general population. Inmates in an SHU are classified as being on disciplinary segregation or administrative detention.

Administrative segregation is a non-punitive status used to protect the public, ensure the orderly operation of the facility, or ensure the safety and security of the inmate or others. Reasons for placement in the SHU for protection include:
* Being a victim of an assault.
* Refusing to enter the general population.
* Acting or being perceived as an informant.
* Staff concerns regarding the inmate's safety.
