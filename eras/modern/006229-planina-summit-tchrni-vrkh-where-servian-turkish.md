# Tchrni Vrkh and the Rilska Planina

The summit of Tchrni Vrkh serves as a critical geographical marker in south-eastern Europe, functioning as the point where the territories of Bulgaria, Servia, and Turkey meet. This summit is the culmination of the crest of the Rilska Planina.

## Geography and Frontiers
The Rilska Planina is a majestic range that extends in a westerly direction from the imposing summit of Musallá. Musallá, which reaches an elevation of 9,631 feet, is the highest peak in the Peninsula, surpassed only by Olympus. It is located within the Bulgarian frontier at the head of the Mesta valley.

The frontier of Bulgaria follows the crest of the Rilska Planina until it reaches the summit of Tchrni Vrkh. From this tri-border point, the Servian frontier continues northwards. This boundary line leaves the town of Trn to the east and Pirot to the west, eventually reaching the Timok near Kula and following that river to its junction with the Danube.

## Regional Topography and Flora
The Rilska Planina is part of a broader southern mountain group that is distinct from the Balkan chain (known locally as the *Stara Planina* or "Old Mountain"). While the Balkans are characterized by rounded summits and gentle slopes, the southern group—which includes the Rilska Planina—possesses sharper outlines and pine-clad steeps that resemble the scenery of the Alps.

The forests of the Rilska Planina are noted for their magnificence, particularly the beech forests that clothe its valleys. While conifers are rare in the Balkan range, they are abundant in the higher regions of the southern mountain group. This includes the *Pinus peuce*, a species otherwise peculiar to the Himalayas. However, archive records indicate that the beautiful forests of the Rila district have been rapidly disappearing due to exploitation.

## Political Context of the Region
The territories surrounding these highlands have been subject to various administrative changes. Bulgaria, situated in the north-east of the Balkan Peninsula on the Black Sea, was an autonomous and tributary principality under the suzerainty of the sultan of Turkey from 1878 until October 5, 1908. 

The area of the Bulgarian kingdom comprises the territories between the river Danube and the Balkan chain, the western highlands of Sofia, Samakov, Trn, and Kiustendil, and the province of Eastern Rumelia. The southern frontier of Bulgaria was shaped by the Berlin Treaty of 1878, though certain adjustments occurred; for instance, the cantons of Rupchus and Krjali were restored to Turkey in 1886. The present frontier passes north of these districts to reach the watershed of Rhodope before following the Rilska Planina to Tchrni Vrkh.
