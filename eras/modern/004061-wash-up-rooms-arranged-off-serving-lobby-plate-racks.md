# Modern British Barracks: Catering and Facilities

The organization of modern British barracks for an infantry battalion is designed to provide a specific standard of accommodation, with older facilities being gradually remodeled to meet these authorized standards. A central component of this organization is the catering and dining infrastructure, which is separated from the sleeping quarters to ensure that the air in the barrack-rooms remains fresher and purer.

## Cook-house and Dining Facilities
The cook-house is positioned next to the dining-rooms, where meals for a half battalion are prepared and served directly to the dining-rooms on either side. The facility is equipped with a pastry slab, dressers, and tables for the preparation and serving of meals, as well as a sink for washing kitchen utensils. To ensure proper environmental conditions, the cook-house is well-ventilated via a top lantern and is well lighted.

The cooking apparatus is designed for the economical use of coal fuel; when used carefully, it consumes slightly more than 1/2 lb of coal per man per day. The dining-rooms themselves are lofty, warmed by hot water, and well ventilated.

## Wash-up and Utility Rooms
Arranged off the serving-lobby are the wash-up rooms. These rooms are equipped with shelves and plate-racks used for the storage of crockery after it has been washed. 

Below the kitchen block is a basement that houses the necessary heating infrastructure. This includes a boiler for heating the dining-rooms and a separate boiler to provide hot water for sinks and baths. In some instances, a hot-air furnace is also located here to heat drying-rooms, which are used by men to dry their clothing after a field day or a route-march.

## Soldiers' Recreation and Canteen
The recreation establishment, or soldiers' club, is located near the barrack blocks to provide relaxation and amusement for the rank and file when they are off duty. The ground floor features a large, lofty room with a stage at one end for entertainments or lectures. At the opposite end is a supper bar that extends across the room, selling light refreshments and mineral waters, with tables provided for suppers.

The establishment also includes:
* **Grocery Shop:** A facility where families and men can purchase goods bought at wholesale prices under regimental arrangements. This shop is operated to be self-supporting, selling goods without more profit than is necessary for that purpose.
* **First Floor:** This level contains a library, a reading-room, a writing-room, and a games and billiard room.
* **Management:** The establishment is completed by kitchen premises and the manager's quarter.

A separate canteen is located near the recreation establishment, devoted exclusively to the sale of beer. To ensure it does not vie in attractiveness with the recreation establishment, it is kept distinct. The canteen provides a bar for soldiers, a separate room for corporals, and a jug department for families. It also contains an office for checking accounts and an attached manager's quarter.

## Officers' and Non-Commissioned Officers' Messes
The officers' mess is situated a short distance from the men's barracks, typically overlooking the cricket ground or parade. The building includes an entrance-hall with a band alcove for guest nights, a reading-room (anteroom), and a mess-room (dining-room). The kitchen and billiard-room are located at the back to allow for the arrangement of lantern lights. The mess is supported by a mess office and accessories for the mess-man's department and the mess waiters' department, including a cellarage, plate-closet, and pantry.

For senior non-commissioned officers, a sergeants' mess is provided. This facility contains a billiard-room, reading-room, and dining-room, along with a liquor store and kitchen premises. The liquor store includes a jug department for the families of sergeants. Single non-commissioned officers take all their meals here, while married members use the mess as a club.
