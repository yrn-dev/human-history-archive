# BACKWARDATION

Backwardation, frequently referred to as "back" for the sake of brevity, is a technical term utilized on the London Stock Exchange. It refers to a specific financial arrangement involving the loan of stock from one account to another.

## Definition and Application
In the context of the London Stock Exchange, backwardation is the amount charged for the loan of stock. This sum is paid by the seller to the purchaser on a bear account. The primary purpose of this payment is to allow the seller to defer the delivery of the stock.

## Mechanics of the Bear Account
The process typically begins when a seller sells stocks or shares for delivery on a specific date, despite the fact that they likely do not possess those shares at the time of the sale. The seller operates under the expectation that they will be able to purchase the shares at a lower price before the fixed delivery date, thereby earning a profit.

However, if the prices do not decrease as expected by the time the settling-day arrives, the seller must pay the purchaser an agreed amount of interest, which is the backwardation. This payment grants the seller the privilege of deferring the delivery of the stock. The seller may seek this deferment for two primary reasons:
1. To provide more time to procure the necessary stock.
2. To maintain the hope that a subsequent shrinkage in price will occur, allowing them to eventually realize a profit.
