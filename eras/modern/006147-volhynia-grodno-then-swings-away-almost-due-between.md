# Volhynia and Grodno

Volhynia and Grodno are regions located in western Russia. Grodno is one of the Lithuanian governments, while Volhynia is a government located to the south of Grodno.

## Geography and Hydrography of Grodno
The government of Grodno is situated between 51 deg. 40' and 52 deg. N. latitude and between 22 deg. 12' and 26 deg. E. longitude. It is bounded to the north by the government of Vilna, to the east by Minsk, to the south by Volhynia, and to the west by the Polish governments of Siedlce and Lomza. The region covers an area of 14,926 sq. m. and consists primarily of a uniform plain, though some hills in the north reach heights of up to 925 ft.

The region is drained by several navigable rivers, including the Bobr, Niemen, Narev, and the Bug. Additionally, the Oginsky and Augustowo canals serve as important waterways. The geological composition of the area includes Tertiary and Cretaceous deposits, with granites and gneisses appearing along the Bug.

The landscape is characterized by extensive peat bogs, some reaching depths of 4 to 7 ft., and forests—mainly *Coniferae*—which cover more than one-fourth of the total area. Notable forests include Byelovitsa (Bialowice), covering 376 sq. m., and those of Grodno, covering 410 sq. m. The Byelovitsa forest has been a preserve for the wild ox since 1803.

## Climate and Economy of Grodno
The climate in Grodno is cold and wet, with an annual mean temperature of 44.5 deg. F. (January mean 22.5 deg. and July mean 64.5 deg.) and annual rainfall of 21-1/2 in., with frequent hail.

Agriculture is the primary industry. Approximately 4,000,000 acres of land are owned by peasants (42-1/2% of the land), with over 2-1/4 million of those acres being arable. Common crops include wheat, rye, oats, potatoes, hemp, flax, and some tobacco. The region also breeds sheep, cattle, and horses in large numbers.

Manufacturing is also present, particularly in distilling, tobacco, and woollens. The woollen industry, centered in Byelostok, is the second largest in the empire after Moscow. Other factories produce leather, shoddy, and silk. The government is served by main railway lines connecting Warsaw to Moscow and Warsaw to St Petersburg.

## The Town of Grodno
The town of Grodno serves as the capital of the government. Located at 53 deg. 40' N. and 23 deg. 50' E. on the right bank of the Niemen, it is 98 m. southwest of Vilna and 160 m. northeast of Warsaw by rail. As of 1901, its population was 41,736, nearly two-thirds of whom were Jews.

Founded in the 12th century, the town suffered near-total destruction by the Teutonic knights (in 1391 and 1284) and the Mongols in 1241. It later served as the capital for Stephen Bathory, king of Poland, who died there in 1586. Grodno was a frequent meeting place for the Polish Estates after 1673; the second partition of Poland was signed there in 1793, and Stanislaus Poniatowski resigned the Polish crown there in 1795.

The town is an episcopal see of the Orthodox Greek church and the headquarters of the II. Army Corps. Its architecture includes two churches from the 16th and 17th centuries and two old castles. Local industry includes distilleries, tobacco factories, and the production of firearms, vehicles, candles, soap, and machinery.

## Volhynia and Regional Waterways
Volhynia is a government in southern Russia. One of its notable towns is Lutsk (Polish: *Luck*), located on the Styr. Lutsk is the seat of a Roman Catholic bishop and possesses a 16th-century castle. Believed to have been founded in the 7th century, it was known as Luchesk in the 11th century and served as the chief town of an independent principality. While it became a wealthy seat of a bishop in the 15th century, it lost importance following wars between Poland and Russia in the late 16th century, during which 40,000 inhabitants were exterminated. Russia took the town in 1791. Its population, including many Jews, relies heavily on shipping goods via the Styr.

The region is influenced by the Bug rivers. The Southern Bug rises in the south of the government of Volhynia, flowing southeast through Kherson and Podolia. It is 470 m. long, with rapids in its upper section and sandbanks and rock blocks in its lower section. The other river, known as the Western Don or the Bug, also measures 470 m. and rises in Austrian Galicia. It flows north-northwest to Brest-Litovsk, where it separates the Russian governments of Grodno and Volhynia from the Polish provinces of Siedlce and Lublin. From Brest-Litovsk, it is navigable and flows west between the provinces of Lomza and Warsaw before joining the Vistula.
