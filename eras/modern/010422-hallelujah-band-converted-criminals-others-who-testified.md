# Hallelujah Band

The "Hallelujah band" was a group organized in Walsall consisting of converted criminals and other individuals. The members of this band engaged in public testimony regarding their conversions.

## Origins and Leadership
The organization of the Hallelujah band took place in Walsall during the period when William Booth and his wife, Catherine Mumford, were occupied with preaching. Booth, born in Nottingham on April 10, 1829, had a strongly religious turn at age fifteen under the influence of the Wesleyan Methodists, later serving as a local preacher. After moving to London in 1849, his passion for open-air preaching led to his severance from the Wesleyans. He subsequently joined the Methodist New Connexion and was ordained as a minister, though he left that body in 1861 because he was not employed in "travelling evangelization" as he desired.

## Evolution into the Salvation Army
Following the activities in Walsall, Booth moved to London in 1864, where he continued to conduct services in the open air and in tents. He founded an organization that underwent several name changes, being known successively as the East London Revival Society, the East London Christian Mission, and the Christian Mission. In 1878, the organization became the Salvation Army.

The Salvation Army adopted a quasi-military structure, with Booth—who became known as the "general"—modeling the organization's "Orders and Regulations" on those of the British army. The Army's operational methods included:
* Conducting processions and outdoor meetings.
* Visiting private houses, prisons, and public-houses.
* Holding meetings in factories, theatres, and other unusual buildings.
* Utilizing the language of everyday life and popular song-tunes.
* Requiring every convert to serve as a daily witness for Christ in both private and public settings.

## Opposition and Expansion
The early campaigns of the Salvation Army faced violent opposition. A group known as the "Skeleton Army" was organized specifically to break up the Army's meetings. As a result, followers of Booth were subjected to imprisonment and fines for many years on charges of being breakers of the peace, though these disorders had largely subsided by 1889.

Under the leadership of the indefatigable organizer and speaker "General" Booth, the operations of the Army expanded globally. This expansion included the United States in 1880, Australia in 1881, and further spread into India, Ceylon, the European continent, and other regions.
