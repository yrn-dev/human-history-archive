# The Master

## Literary History and Retitling
In the mid-19th century, Charlotte Brontë authored a novel originally titled *The Master*. Before the work was sent to London, it was retitled *The Professor*. This novel was part of a collective effort by the Brontë sisters to find publishers for their stories, which included Emily Brontë's *Wuthering Heights* and Anne Brontë's *Agnes Gray*.

*The Professor* eventually reached the London firm of Smith, Elder & Co. The firm's reader, R. Smith Williams (1800-1875), and his employers were impressed by the work, though it was suggested that the story lacked "varied interest." In August 1847, Charlotte Brontë was informed that the novel was too short to meet the then-imperative three-volume form. In response to a request for a longer novel, Brontë submitted a different work in the same month. This resulted in the October 1847 publication of *Jane Eyre*, which received widespread acclaim, although it was later reviewed in December 1848 by Miss Rigby (Lady Eastlake) in the *Quarterly*.
