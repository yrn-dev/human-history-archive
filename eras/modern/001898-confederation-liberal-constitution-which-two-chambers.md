# Liberal Constitutions and Two-Chamber Systems

In various historical contexts across Europe and South America, the establishment of liberal constitutions often involved the creation of two-chamber legislatures to manage governance, taxation, and representation. These systems appeared in different forms, ranging from the Germanic states and Denmark to the province of Buenos Aires.

## The Grand-Duchy of Baden
In 1815, Baden became a member of the Germanic confederation, an entity established by the Act of the 8th of June and annexed to the Final Act of the congress of Vienna. During this period, a dispute arose regarding the succession to the grand-duchy. While a treaty from April 1816 guaranteed succession to Maximilian I, king of Bavaria, in the event that the line of Zaehringen became extinct, Grand-duke Charles issued a pragmatic sanction (_Hausgesetz_) in 1817. This declared the counts of Hochberg—the issue of a morganatic marriage between Grand-duke Charles Frederick and Luise Geyer von Geyersberg—capable of succeeding to the crown.

To secure popular support for the Hochberg heir, Charles granted the grand-duchy a liberal constitution in 1818 under article xiii. of the Act of Confederation. This constitution constituted two chambers and mandated that their assent was necessary for taxation and legislation. The experiment was closely watched throughout Germany. However, success was hindered by several factors: the administration was managed by inefficient and hide-bound bureaucrats, the succeeding Grand-duke Louis (who took power in 1818) was unpopular, and the people—described as the "Celtic fringe" of Germany—had been heavily influenced by French revolutionary ideas. Consequently, the new chambers tended to model their activities on the proceedings of the Convention from the early days of the French Revolution.

## The Province of Buenos Aires
In South America, the province of Buenos Aires became part of the Confederation in 1820. After a period of struggle for supremacy over other provinces, it adopted a constitution in 1854. This document provided for a government administered by a governor and a legislature consisting of two chambers, both of which were chosen by popular vote. 

The political landscape shifted in 1880 following an unsuccessful revolt against the national government. This event led to the federalization of the city of Buenos Aires and the selection of La Plata as the provincial capital. As indemnification, the republic assumed the public indebtedness of the provinces. Despite this, the province incurred further liabilities of ten million sterling before the new capital was completed, which significantly handicapped its subsequent development.

## Constitutional Developments in Denmark
Denmark sought to establish a parliament on modern lines during the mid-19th century. A constitutional rescript from January 28, 1848, was eventually withdrawn in favor of an electoral law for a national assembly. This assembly was designed with two distinct houses:
*   **The Upper House (_Landsting_):** Composed of 38 members nominated by the king.
*   **The Popular Chamber (_Folketing_):** Composed of the remaining members, who were elected by the people.

This structure caused a rift between the National Liberals and the _Bondevenlige_ (philo-peasant party), as the latter preferred a one-chamber system and objected to the king's right of nomination. Ultimately, the National Liberals won the general election, and the Radicals eventually acceded to the majority's wishes. The new constitution received royal sanction on June 5, 1849.

## Other Constitutional Models
Other regions adopted different legislative structures. In Bulgaria, the Constitution of Trnovo (1879) was elaborated by an assembly of notables consisting largely of half-educated peasants. This body manipulated a liberal constitution submitted by the Russian governor-general, Prince Dondukov-Korsakov, to reflect a highly democratic spirit. The resulting constitution featured a single chamber, manhood suffrage, the payment of deputies, the prohibition of all titles and distinctions, and the absence of property qualifications for candidates.

In Belgium, the constitution provided for a senate and a chamber. In May 1892, after long parliamentary discussions and strikes, these bodies declared that certain articles of the constitution required revision. Following the prescribed constitutional process, a dissolution occurred, and two new chambers were elected.
