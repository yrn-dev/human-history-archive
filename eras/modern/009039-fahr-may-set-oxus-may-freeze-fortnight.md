# Temperature and Freezing Phenomena

## Meteorological Frost and Freezing
In meteorology, frost is defined as the agent or act of the process of freezing. It occurs when the surface temperature drops below 32 deg. F., which is the freezing point of water. Frost is categorized by intensity: a "light frost" occurs when temperatures are between 28 deg. and 32 deg. F., while temperatures below 28 deg. F. result in a "heavy," "black," or "killing" frost. The term "black frost" is also applied when no hoar-frost is visible.

Hoar-frost and white-frost are visible frozen vapors that form on exposed surfaces. These are most heavily formed during clear, calm nights when the absence of clouds allows heat to radiate rapidly from the land. Interestingly, hoar-frost can form on the land surface even if the air a few feet above is 12 deg. to 16 deg. above the freezing point. The risk of frost is reduced when soil is very moist; for example, after 10-12 mm. of rain, light fog sheets develop that check radiation. In America, fields are sometimes flooded upon receiving frost warnings for this reason. Similarly, smoke layers can impede radiation, which is why smoky fires are sometimes lit to protect planted ground.

## Regional Climates of Asia
Asia exhibits some of the most extreme temperature variations on the globe. In northern Asia, winters are long and severely cold, with minimum temperatures near zero Fahrenheit even as far south as 35 deg. N. lat. In Siberia, the difference between the mean temperatures of the hottest and coldest months is rarely less than 60 deg. Fahr., and near the coast, it can exceed 100 deg. Fahr. Extreme temperatures in Siberia range from maxima of 80 deg. to 90 deg. Fahr. to minima between -40 deg. and -70 deg. Fahr.

In the vicinity of Yakutsk, Siberia, the earth is permanently frozen to a depth of more than 380 ft., where the temperature remains 5 deg. or 6 deg. Fahr. below the freezing point of water. Summer heat only thaws the surface to about 3 ft., and at a depth of 50 ft., the temperature is approximately 15 Fahr. below the freezing point. Despite this, the land produces grain crops from fifteen to forty fold.

In contrast, southern Asia experiences higher temperatures, with the highest mean temperature not much exceeding 82 deg. Fahr. Temperature variations are much lower near the equator, reduced to about 5 deg. at Singapore. In northern India, the greatest difference does not exceed 40 deg., falling to 10 deg. or 12 deg. at Bombay and Madras.

## The Climate of Bokhara
Bokhara is characterized by extreme climate. In the lowlands, very hot summers are followed by short, cold winters where frosts of -20 deg. Fahr. may occur, and the Oxus may freeze for a fortnight. The lowlands also experience terrible sand-storms from the west that kill cotton trees and exhaust humans. Malaria is prevalent here, becoming malignant in some years following a wet spring.

In the highlands, the hot, dry summer is followed by a four-month winter. In regions above 8000 ft., the short summer is rainy, and there is a significant development of glaciers and snowfields, which bury the passes.

The population of Bokhara is estimated at 1,250,000. The dominant race is the Uzbegs, who are fanatical Moslem Sunnites and maintain divisions into clans or tribes. Other groups include the nomad Turkomans and Kirghiz (both of Turkish origin), Sarts (a mixture of Turks and Iranians), and Iranian Tajiks, who make up the bulk of the rural population. The population also includes Persians, Arabs, Armenians, Afghans, and Jews, with a colony of Hindus from Shikarpur managing much of the trade. Approximately 20% of the population are nomads and 15% are semi-nomads.

## Temperature and Living Matter
The properties of living matter are intimately tied to temperature. Vital activities—including reproduction, movement, and nutritive growth—are only possible between limits ranging from slightly above the freezing point of water to slightly below the boiling point. Exposure to heat sufficient to coagulate protein matter destroys life by demolishing its molecular structure.

While some low organisms, such as bacterial spores, have thick, non-conducting walls that protect protoplasm from external temperatures above boiling or below freezing, prolonged exposure that penetrates the living matter is fatal. Warm-blooded animals, including mammals and birds, use protective mechanisms to regulate temperature, ensuring their living cells do not appreciably rise or fall. In these animals, a variation of only a few degrees in the blood can cause death.

## Technical Applications and Chemical Freezing
In construction, it is generally not advisable to work when temperatures are below the freezing point. However, in urgent cases, bricklaying can be done using unslaked lime mortar prepared in small quantities immediately before use to ensure binding occurs before it cools. To prevent rain from penetrating and being destroyed by frost, the top course of a wall should be covered at night.

In chemistry and metallurgy, the freezing point (F.P.) of alloys and solutions is studied via cooling curves. For pure metals, the temperature remains constant during freezing. However, adding an impurity, such as salt in water or another metal in an alloy, typically lowers the freezing point. In these heterogeneous mixtures, the solvent generally separates in a pure state, causing the remaining solution to increase in strength and the temperature to continue falling during the freezing process until a eutectic temperature is reached.
