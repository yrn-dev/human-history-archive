# BUCCLEUCH, DUKES OF

The ducal house of the Scotts of Buccleuch is a Scottish noble family whose substantial origins are linked to large grants of land in Scotland provided by James II to Sir Walter Scott of Kirkurd and Buccleuch, a border chief, following the fall of the 8th earl of Douglas in 1452. While these grants established the house's substantial foundation, the family traces its descent further back to Sir Richard le Scott (1240–1285). The family estate of Buccleuch is located in Selkirkshire.

## Early History and Peerage
The family's prominence continued through Sir Walter Scott of Branxholm and Buccleuch (d. 1552), who distinguished himself at the battle of Pinkie in 1547. His life provided the material for the famous poem *The Lay of the Last Minstrel* written by his later namesake. In 1606, his great-grandson, Sir Walter (1565–1611), was created Lord Scott of Buccleuch, and an earldom was subsequently granted in 1619.

The transition to a dukedom occurred through Anne (1651–1732), the daughter of the second earl. Having succeeded her father as a countess in her own right, she married the famous duke of Monmouth in 1663, who was then created the 1st duke of Buccleuch. Their grandson, Francis, became the 2nd duke.

## Expansion and the Queensberry Succession
The 3rd duke, Henry (1746–1812), was the son of Francis. In 1810, upon the death of William Douglas, 4th duke of Queensberry, Henry succeeded to that dukedom as well as its honours and estates. This succession was based on an entail executed in 1706 by Henry's own great-grandfather, the 2nd duke of Queensberry. Henry, who married the daughter of the duke of Montagu, was noted for his benefactions and generosity.

The titles were subsequently held by his descendants:
* Charles William Henry (d. 1819), 4th duke of Buccleuch and 6th duke of Queensberry.
* Walter Francis Scott (1806–1884), 5th duke of Buccleuch and 7th duke of Queensberry.
* William Henry Walter Montagu Douglas Scott (b. 1831), 6th duke of Buccleuch and 8th duke of Queensberry.

## Notable Dukes and Public Service
The 5th duke, Walter Francis Scott, held several high-profile positions, serving as president of the British Association, the Society of Antiquaries, and the Highland and Agricultural Society. From 1842 to 1846, he served as lord privy seal, and in 1846, he was president of the council. He is credited with the construction of the harbour at Granton, near Edinburgh, which cost over £500,000.

The 6th duke, William Henry Walter Montagu Douglas Scott, served as a Conservative M.P. for Midlothian in the House of Commons from 1853 to 1868 and again from 1874 to 1880. His wife, a daughter of the 1st duke of Abercorn, held the office of mistress of the robes.

## Dalkeith Palace
A significant seat of the dukes of Buccleuch is Dalkeith Palace. The palace was designed in 1700 by Sir John Vanbrugh for the widow of the duke of Monmouth, the countess of Buccleuch. The site was previously a castle owned by the Douglases and the Grahams; it was sold in 1642 by William, the seventh or eighth earl of Morton, to Francis, the 2nd earl of Buccleuch, to help raise funds for Charles I during the Civil War.

Dalkeith Palace has hosted several sovereigns during visits to Edinburgh, including George IV in 1822, Queen Victoria in 1842, and Edward VII in 1903. The estate features a picture gallery with Old Masters, renowned gardens, and a park of over 1,000 acres containing a remnant of the Caledonian Forest.

## Lineage and Associations
The dukes of Buccleuch and Queensberry are represented by the name Montagu-Douglas-Scott. They are part of the broader Scottish noble family of Douglas, which also includes the dukes of Hamilton, the earls of Home, the earls of Morton, and the earls of Wemyss.
