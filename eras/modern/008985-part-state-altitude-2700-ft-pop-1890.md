# Boise

## Geography and Location
Boise is the capital of the state of Idaho, U.S.A., and serves as the county-seat of Ada county. The city is situated on the north side of the Boise river in the southwestern part of the state. It is located at an altitude of approximately 2700 ft.

## Population and Growth
The city experienced significant population growth during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1890, the population was 2,311; by 1900, it had increased to 5,957, and by 1910, it reached 17,358. This rapid growth after 1900 was primarily due to large irrigation schemes in southern Idaho. The water for the immense Boise-Payette irrigation system is sourced from the Boise river, 8 m. above the city.

## Infrastructure and Transport
Boise is served by the Oregon Short Line railway, acting as the terminus of a branch that connects to the main line at Nampa, located about 20 m. to the west. Additionally, the city is connected to Nampa and Caldwell via electric lines.

## Public Buildings and Institutions
The city contains several principal buildings, including the Federal building—which houses the United States land office, the United States circuit and district court rooms, and the post office—as well as the state capitol, a Carnegie library, the United States assay office, and a natatorium. 

Boise is the seat of the state school for the deaf and blind, established in 1906. Just outside the city limits are the state penitentiary and the state soldiers' home. Federal barracks are located approximately 2 m. from the city. The city utilizes artesian wells near the city to provide hot water (175 deg. F.) for the natatorium and for heating public buildings and many residences.

## Economy and Industry
Boise serves as the trade center for the surrounding mining, agricultural, and fruit-growing country, and it functions as an important market for wool. The Boise valley is considered an excellent region for the cultivation of prunes, apples, and other fruits. The city's manufactured products are primarily those demanded by a mining region, specifically machine-shop products, flour, and lumber.

## History
The oldest settlement in the vicinity was established before 1860 by the Hudson's Bay Fur Company on the west side of the Boise river. The present city dates from 1863 and was chartered in 1864.
