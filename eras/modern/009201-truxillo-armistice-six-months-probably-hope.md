# Armistices in Global History

An armistice is a formal agreement between warring parties to stop fighting for a specified period. Throughout history, these agreements have been utilized for various strategic purposes, including the facilitation of diplomatic negotiations, the mobilization of forces, or as a precursor to total surrender.

## South American Independence
In the conflict between republican forces and the Spaniards, Simon Bolivar utilized an armistice to seek a peaceful resolution. On November 25, 1820, Bolivar concluded an armistice of six months at Truxillo. This agreement was entered into with the probable hope that the Spaniards would agree to terms and that further bloodshed could be avoided. However, these hopes were disappointed. During this period, General Morillo was recalled and replaced by General Torre. Once the armistice expired, the contest resumed, leading Bolivar to seek a decisive victory at Carabobo, where he routed the Spanish army.

## Ancient Greek and Persian Conflicts
Armistices were frequently employed during the struggles between the Lacedaemonians, Athenians, and the Persian Empire.

### Lacedaemonian and Athenian Relations
Following a ten-year war, a treaty and alliance were established between the Athenians and Lacedaemonians. Despite this, an "unstable armistice" persisted for six years and ten months. During this time, the parties abstained from invading each other's territories, though they continued to inflict "effectual injury" upon one another abroad. In another instance, the Athenian popular assembly agreed to a one-year armistice beginning on the fourteenth of the month of Elaphebolion. This period was intended to allow ambassadors and heralds to travel between the two countries to discuss the bases of a pacification.

### Conflicts with Persia
In Asia, armistices were used to allow communication between military commanders and their respective governments. Derkyllidas concluded an armistice with Pharnabazus and Tissaphernes for the purpose of mutual communication with the Persian court and Sparta. Later, when Agesilaus arrived in Asia, Tissaphernes proposed a continuation of this armistice to communicate with the Persian court, hoping to be empowered to grant Agesilaus's demand for the autonomy of the Asiatic Greeks. This resulted in a fresh armistice sworn to by both sides for a term of three months.

In a separate engagement, Tithraustes endeavored to open negotiations with Agesilaus. While Agesilaus sought consultation with his authorities at home, he was prevailed upon by Tithraustes to conclude a six-month armistice. As part of this arrangement, Agesilaus moved out of his satrapy into that of Pharnabazus and received thirty talents to help maintain his army.

## East Asian Campaigns
In the history of Japanese incursions into the Korean peninsula, armistices were used both as strategic delays and as means of termination.

During one campaign, the Chinese envoy Chen Weiching (known as Chin Ikei) was instructed to seek an armistice rather than peace. The goal was to induce the Japanese to stop their advance, thereby allowing Chinese authorities time to mobilize a sufficient force. Konishi Yukinaga agreed to an armistice of fifty days. Under these terms, the Japanese pledged not to advance more than three miles northward of Pyong-yang while Chen traveled to Peking to arrange peace terms.

Later, upon receiving intelligence regarding the death of Hideyoshi, Japanese commanders in Korea arranged an armistice. This led to the withdrawal of invading forces and the end of a six-year campaign that resulted in immense loss of life and treasure.

## The First World War
The armistice signed by Germany brought an end to operations in Europe and mandated specific terms for the cessation of hostilities:

*   **Cessation of Hostilities:** Operations by land and air were to cease six hours after the signature.
*   **Evacuation:** Germany was required to immediately evacuate invaded countries, including France, Belgium, Luxemburg, and Alsace-Lorraine, within fourteen days. Troops remaining after this period were to be considered prisoners of war.
*   **Repatriation:** All inhabitants of the evacuated countries, including convicted persons and hostages, were to be repatriated within fifteen days.
*   **Surrender of Material:** The German armies were required to surrender war material in good condition, including 5,000 guns (2,500 heavy and 2,500 field), 25,000 machine guns, 3,000 minenwerfer, and 1,700 airplanes (specifically all D 7's and night bombing machines).

In Belgium, the signing of the armistice in November compelled the Germans to evacuate the country, leading to the return of King Albert and his queen to Brussels.
