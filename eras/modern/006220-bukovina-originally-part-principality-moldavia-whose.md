# Bukovina

Bukovina is a duchy and crownland of Austria, covering an area of 4,035 square miles. It is bordered to the north by Galicia, to the east by Russia and Rumania, to the south by Rumania, and to the west by Hungary and Transylvania.

## History and Administration
Bukovina was originally a part of the principality of Moldavia, and its province once housed the ancient capital, Suczawa. The region was occupied by Russian forces in 1769 and by Austrian forces in 1774. In 1777, the Porte—the power under whose suzerainty Moldavia existed—ceded the province to Austria. 

Following this acquisition, Bukovina was incorporated into a single province with Galicia in 1786. However, it was separated from Galicia in 1849 and established as a separate crownland. In the administration of the region, German serves as the official language for instruction in the university, the law-courts, and the administration.

## Geography and Climate
The name of the country is derived from the abundance of beech trees. The terrain is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathians, particularly in the southern regions, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. The region's primary mountain passes include the Borgo Pass, the Radna Pass, and the Stiol Pass.

With the exception of the Dniester river, which forms the northern border, Bukovina is part of the Danube watershed. Its main rivers are the Sereth and the Pruth; the Sereth's affluents include the Moldava, the Bistritza, and the Suczawa. The climate is described as healthy but severe, particularly during the winter months, though it is generally milder than the climate of Galicia. The mean annual temperature in Czernowitz is 46.9° F.

## Land Use and Economy
The land of Bukovina is fertile, and agriculture has seen significant progress. The distribution of the land is as follows:
*   **Woodland:** 43.17%
*   **Arable land:** 27.59%
*   **Meadows:** 12.68%
*   **Pastures:** 10.09%
*   **Gardens:** 0.78%

Principal agricultural products include rye, wheat, barley, maize, oats, potatoes, hemp, and flax. Cattle-rearing is another significant source of revenue. The primary mineral resource is salt, which is extracted from a government-owned mine at Kaczyka. The chief industries are milling, distilling, and brewing. Commerce is primarily focused on raw products—such as wood, wool, cattle, and agricultural produce—and is mostly conducted by Armenians and Jews.

## Population and Demographics
As of 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a population density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is diverse in terms of nationality:
*   **Ruthenians:** Over 40%
*   **Rumanians:** 35%
*   **Jews:** 13%
*   **Others:** Germans, Russians, Poles, Armenians, and Hungarians.

Religious affiliation is predominantly Greek Orthodox, with nearly 70% of the population belonging to this church under the ecclesiastical jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Roman Catholics make up 11% of the population, the Greek United Church accounts for 3.25%, and 2.5% are Protestants.
