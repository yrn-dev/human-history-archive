# Temperature Variations in Siberia

## Geographic Overview
Siberia is the portion of Asia situated between the Arctic Ocean and the mountainous belt that bounds Turkestan, Mongolia, and Manchuria to the north. The region is characterized by an immense, broken high plateau that extends from the south-west to the north-east, decreasing in both altitude and width as it moves north-east. This plateau is bordered by high ridges that transition into high plains (ranging from 1,500 to 2,000 feet) before dropping to lowlands a few hundred feet above sea level. The landscape is traversed by some of the world's largest rivers, specifically the Lena, Yenisei, and Ob.

## Temperature Extremes and Variations
Siberia is noted for having very great extremes of heat and cold. The variations in temperature are particularly significant; near the coast, the difference between the mean temperatures of the hottest and coldest months exceeds 100 degrees Fahrenheit. The difference between the absolute extreme temperatures of those same months is even greater. Throughout Siberia, the difference between the means of the hottest and coldest months is rarely less than 60 degrees Fahrenheit.

The extreme temperatures in the region are generally considered to range from maxima of 80 to 90 degrees Fahrenheit to minima between -40 and -70 degrees Fahrenheit. In northern Asia, some of the lowest temperatures on the globe are recorded. On the north coast of eastern Siberia, the mean temperature is only a few degrees above 0 degrees Fahrenheit.

## Regional Climatic Data
Specific locations within and bordering the Siberian region exhibit diverse thermal patterns:

*   **Nikolayevsk:** This location experiences winter temperatures as low as -41.5 degrees Fahrenheit and summer highs of 94.6 degrees Fahrenheit, with an average yearly temperature below zero (-0.9 degrees Fahrenheit).
*   **Ayan (56° 27´ N):** The average annual temperature is 25.5 degrees Fahrenheit, with a winter average of -0.4 degrees and a summer average of 50.5 degrees.
*   **Okhotsk (59° 21´ N):** The average annual temperature is 23 degrees Fahrenheit, with winter averages of -6 degrees and summer averages of 52.5 degrees.
*   **Maritime Province:** The climate here is described as very harsh. In Vladivostok, the average yearly temperature is 39.5 degrees Fahrenheit, and the harbor remains frozen for nearly three months of the year. The Usuri and Amur rivers freeze in November.
*   **Caspian and Aral Seas:** Near these seas, the extreme of heat reaches nearly 100 degrees Fahrenheit, while the cold falls to -20 degrees Fahrenheit or lower. Specifically, the temperature difference between the hottest and coldest monthly means is 80 degrees Fahrenheit on the Sea of Aral and more than 50 degrees at Astrakhan on the Caspian.

## Influencing Factors and Environment
The harshness of the Siberian climate is influenced by several factors. The warm sea-current of the Kuro-Siwo does not reach Siberian coasts; instead, a cold current from the Sea of Okhotsk brings chilling fogs and icy water to the coasts of Sakhalin and the Pacific shore of eastern Korea. In the southern parts of the Maritime Province, wet summers and cold winters are produced by the monsoons of the Chinese Sea and high coastal mountains. These moist monsoons bring heavy rains toward the end of summer, which cause serious inundations of the Amur and destroy harvests.

Despite the temperature extremes, the rainfall is sufficient to support vegetation compatible with the climate. The surface of the land is frequently peaty or swampy. Pine forests commonly clothe the mountain-sides, while shrubs or grasses cover the plains.

## Comparative Climate
When compared to other regions, Siberia's temperature ranges are far more extreme than those in southern Asia. For example, under the equator, the variation between the hottest and coldest monthly means is reduced to about 5 degrees Fahrenheit. In northern India, the greatest difference does not exceed 40 degrees, falling to approximately 10 or 12 degrees at Madras and Bombay. Similarly, at Singapore, the range is less than 5 degrees.
