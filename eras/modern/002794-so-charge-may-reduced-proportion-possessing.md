# Reductions and Proportions in Technical and Social Systems

The concept of reduction, often applied in proportion to other variables, appears across various historical, technical, and legal contexts within the archive, ranging from the chemistry of explosives and metallurgy to legal sentencing and social hierarchies.

## Ballistics and Explosives
In the field of interior ballistics, the transition from black gunpowder—which was used in artillery for six hundred years—to modern explosives has significantly altered the requirements for gun charges. Modern explosives are capable of producing a much larger volume of gas at higher pressures and temperatures; specifically, this increase is more than threefold. Consequently, the charge may be reduced in proportion to these increases. These modern explosives also provide the military advantage of being nearly smokeless.

In the study of muzzle velocity, the work realized by expansion is 2826 foot-tons, which can increase velocity from 1020 to 2250 f/s. Because this is approximately 5% greater than the 2150 f/s listed in range tables, it is estimated that 10% of work is lost to friction in the bore, resulting in a "factor of effect" of f = 0.9.

## Chemical and Industrial Applications
In chemistry, the study of azo compounds (organic substances of the type R.N:N.R') involves the reduction of nitro compounds in alkaline solution. This process utilizes either zinc dust and alkali or a solution of an alkaline stannite as a reducing agent. Additionally, reducing agents can convert azo compounds into amines or hydrazo compounds.

In the context of iron-smelting blast furnaces, the maintenance of a "sensibly reducing atmosphere" results in waste gases that contain a notable proportion of carbonic oxide. These gases, which were previously allowed to burn to waste and create large flames in the Black Country landscape, are now conveyed via wrought iron tubes to air-heating apparatus and boilers, or exploded in gas engines.

## Legal and Administrative Reductions
Historical and legal records indicate various mechanisms for the reduction of prices and sentences. The first English Copyright Act of 1709 allowed individuals to complain to the archbishop of Canterbury and other named persons if the published price of a book was unreasonably high; if the complaint was well founded, the price would be reduced. Booksellers who charged more than the fixed price faced a fine of L5 per copy sold, though this enactment apparently remained a dead letter.

In more recent legal memoranda, records show a reduction in a required term of imprisonment for an individual named Epstein, moving from 24 months to 18 months. ## Social and Historical Proportions
Historical analyses of the Roman Empire present conflicting views on the proportion of slaves to free citizens. Robertson suggested there were twice as many slaves as free citizens, while Mr. Blair estimated a ratio of three slaves to one freeman between 146 B.C. and A.D. 222–235. Conversely, Zumpt argued that reckoning the number of slaves as equal to the free population was a gross error, noting that agricultural laborers and artisans in Egypt, Syria, Britain, Gaul, and Spain often maintained themselves without possessing any slaves. However, it is noted that the number of slaves generally remained in proportion to wealth.

In Japanese history, ex-feudatories were reduced to the position of private gentlemen without patents of nobility. While this was a substantial sacrifice, the impact was mitigated because they had not previously possessed the substance of administrative power (which remained with their seneschals) and they retained accumulated funds from former fiefs and a guaranteed income of one-tenth of their feudal revenues.

## Mathematical and Acoustic Proportions
In the analysis of historical texts by Herodotus, proportions are used to reconcile sums of Babylonian talents. A proportion of 78:60 is used to prove the necessity of certain emendations in the text, as it aligns the separate items with the total sum provided (after subtracting 140 talents used for the defense of Kilikia).

In acoustics, the production of musical sound depends on the proportion between lip tension and wind pressure. Increasing lip tension reduces the width of the aperture and the exciting current. If the aperture is narrowed without a proportional increase in wind pressure, the intensity of the tone is reduced or the intonation suffers because the force is insufficient to provide the requisite amplitude and frequency.
