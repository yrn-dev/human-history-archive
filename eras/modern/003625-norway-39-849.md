# Norway

## Historical Figures and Events
The historical records of Norway include the account of Earl Hakon. In the summer of A.D. 1029, Earl Hakon departed Norway for England, where he was well received by the king. The purpose of his travel was to conclude a marriage with a bride in England. He intended to hold the wedding ceremony in Norway and traveled to England to acquire items for the occasion that were difficult to obtain in Norway.

In the autumn of 1029, Earl Hakon prepared to return, though his departure was delayed. During his voyage, his vessel was lost, and no one escaped. While some reports suggest the vessel was seen in a heavy storm north of Caithness with the wind blowing out of Pentland Firth, driving the ship into the ocean breakers, it is known with certainty only that Earl Hakon went missing in the ocean. No wreckage from the ship ever reached land. News of his disappearance was brought to Norway that autumn by merchants. Consequently, Norway remained without a head during that winter. Additionally, records mention an individual named Kalf who returned to Norway to his farm.
