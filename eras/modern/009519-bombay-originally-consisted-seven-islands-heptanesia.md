# Bombay

Bombay is the capital of the Bombay Presidency and the primary seaport of western India, located at 18 deg. 55' N. and 72 deg. 54' E. The city is situated on an island of the same name, which is part of a group now linked to the mainland via causeways. As of 1901, the population of the town and island was 776,006, with an estimate of 977,822 by 1906, making it the second most populous city in the Indian empire after Calcutta.

## Geography and Urban Layout
The Bombay Peninsula consists of a plain approximately 11 miles long and 3 miles broad, bordered by two parallel lines of low hills. Its harbor is characterized by rocky islands and precipices with high peaks. A neck of land extending toward the south-west shelters the harbor on its eastern side, enclosing water between 5 and 7 miles wide. To the south-west lies Back Bay, a shallow basin roughly 2 miles wide that runs inland for about 3 miles.

The nucleus of the city is the fort, located on a raised strip of land between the harbor and the head of Back Bay. West of this point, the land slopes toward a central low-lying plain; prior to the construction of the Hornby Vellard embankment, this area was submerged by the sea at high tide. The city features spacious streets for European commerce and handsome native bazaars where houses reach three or four storeys with elaborately carved front work and pillars. European residences are typically set in large gardens or compounds, with Malabar Hill—a high ridge running into the sea—serving as a favorite suburb. Notable landmarks include the Taj Mahal hotel, a palatial structure built by the Tata family in 1904.

## History
The name Bombay is derived from Mumba, a form of Parvati, the goddess of the Kolis. The Kolis, a race of fishermen and husbandmen, were the earliest known inhabitants, arriving probably at the start of the Christian era. Originally, Bombay consisted of seven islands, referred to by Ptolemy as the *Heptanesia*. The area was an outlying part of the dominions of several successive western Indian dynasties, including the Mauryas, Satavahanas, Rashtrakutas, and Chalukyas. Between 450 and 750, during the Chalukya and Maurya periods, the principal location in the harbor was the city of Puri on Elephanta Island.

The first town built on Bombay Island was Mahikavati (Mahim), founded by King Bhima, likely of the house of the Yadavas of Deogiri, following Ala-ud-din Khilji's 1294 raid into the Deccan. Mahikavati remained under Hindu rule until 1348, when it was captured by a Mahommedan force from Gujarat. The islands remained part of the province or kingdom of Gujarat until 1534, when Sultan Bahadur ceded them to the Portuguese.

## Administration and Government
The government of Bombay is led by a governor appointed from England, who serves as president of a council. This council, appointed by the crown and selected from the Indian civil service, consists of the governor and two ordinary members. Legislative functions are handled by a legislative council comprising the governor, his executive council, and between eight and twenty other persons, at least half of whom must be non-officials.

Local municipal government was established by an 1888 act of the Bombay legislative council. It consists of a town council and a municipal corporation of 72 members. Of these, 16 are government nominees, 36 are elected by ratepayers, 16 by justices of the peace, 2 by the university senate, and 2 by the chamber of commerce. The corporation includes Parsees, Mahommedans, Hindus, and Europeans. The port is managed by a port trust with members nominated from the commercial community.

Justice is administered by a high court in Bombay, featuring a chief justice and seven puisne judges, supported by assistant and district judges. District administration is carried out by collectors, assistant collectors, and supernumerary assistants.

## Education and Military
The Bombay University was established in 1857 as an examining body based on the model of the University of London. Key educational institutions include the government law school, the government Elphinstone College, the Grant medical college, the Victoria Jubilee technical institute, the Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy school of art, and two missionary colleges, St Xavier and Wilson.

In 1904, Lord Kitchener rearranged the Indian army, abolishing the old Bombay command and replacing it with the Western army corps under a lieutenant-general. This corps was split into three divisions. The 6th division, headquartered at Poona, includes brigades at Aden, Ahmednagar, and Bombay, and covers the Bombay district south of the Tapti, the Poona district, the Dharwar and Aurungabad districts, and the Belgaum district north of the Tungabhadra.

## Industry and Trade
Bombay is a railway center and a chief seaport with a monopoly on the cotton industry. The city is also known for "Bombay blackwood furniture," manufactured in Bombay, Ahmedabad, and Surat. This furniture uses Shisham (blackwood), a dark, hard-grained timber. While some pieces are considered clumsy due to original Dutch models, smaller ornamental items are highly prized for their lace-like carving. These are often produced by workmen following inherited family traditions. Additionally, "Bombay boxes" are produced, featuring geometrical inlays of ivory, ebony, sapan wood, sandal wood, wire, and stags' horns.
