# BARRINGTON, JOHN SHUTE, 1ST VISCOUNT (1678-1734)

John Shute Barrington was an English theologian and lawyer who rose to prominence as a leader of the dissenters and a member of the Irish peerage. Born in 1678 at Theobalds, Hertfordshire, he was the son of a merchant named Benjamin Shute.

## Education and Early Career
Barrington's education was partly completed at the university of Utrecht. Upon his return to England in 1698, he pursued legal studies at the Inner Temple. In 1701, he published several pamphlets advocating for the civil rights of Protestant dissenters, a group to which he himself belonged.

His political utility was recognized by Lord Somers, who recommended Barrington for a mission to induce the Presbyterians in Scotland to support the union of the two kingdoms. For his success in this service, he was appointed to the office of commissioner of the customs in 1708. He held this position until 1711, when he was removed following a change in administration.

## Wealth and Political Rise
During his early career, Barrington's financial standing was significantly improved through the bequest of two large estates. One was left to him by John Wildman of Becket, and the other by Francis Barrington of Tofts. Following the latter bequest, Barrington assumed the name Barrington by an act of parliament.

With his increased fortune and influence, he became the head of the dissenters. Following the accession of George I, he was returned to parliament as the representative for Berwick-upon-Tweed. In 1720, the king granted him a title in the Irish peerage, naming him Viscount Barrington of Ardglass.

## Legal Troubles and Later Life
Barrington's political career suffered a setback in 1723 when he was expelled from the House of Commons. This expulsion resulted from his engagement in the Harburg lottery, which was one of the "bubble speculations" of the era. The punishment was widely viewed as excessively severe and attributed to the personal malice of Walpole.

In his later years, Barrington focused on theological writing. In 1725, he published two works: *An Essay on the Several Dispensations of God to Mankind* and his principal work, *Miscellanea Sacra or a New Method of considering so much of the History of the Apostles as is contained in Scripture*. The latter was published in two 8vo volumes and was later reprinted in three 8vo volumes in 1770 by his son, Shute.

John Shute Barrington died on December 14, 1734.

## Descendants
The 1st Viscount Barrington was succeeded by his eldest son, William Wildman Shute, 2nd Viscount (1717-1793). The 2nd Viscount served in various high-ranking government roles, including secretary at war, chancellor of the exchequer, and treasurer of the navy.

The 1st Viscount also had other sons who achieved distinction:
* **Samuel Barrington (1720-1800):** A British admiral who served in the Seven Years' War and the relief of Gibraltar.
* **Daines Barrington (1727-1800):** A naturalist, antiquary, and lawyer who served as a Welsh judge and second justice of Chester. He wrote on the statutes of England and the language of birds.
* **Shute Barrington (1734-1826):** The youngest son, who became a bishop, serving first at Llandaff in 1769, then Salisbury in 1782, and finally Durham in 1791. He was a vigorous Protestant who advocated for Roman Catholic toleration, provided it did not include political power or establishment.
