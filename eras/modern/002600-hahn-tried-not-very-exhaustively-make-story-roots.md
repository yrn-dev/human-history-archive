# Von Hahn and the Story-Roots of Maerchen

## Comparative Analysis of Ballads
In the study of the identity of plot and incident across the ballads of various lands, Von Hahn attempted to create comparative tables focusing on the "story-roots" of *Maerchen*. While these efforts are described as not very exhaustive, the archive suggests that similar tables could be developed using the introductions and learned notes provided by Prof. Child in his 1898 work, *English and Scottish Popular Ballads*.

## Common Plot Patterns
Certain recurring plots appear across different cultures and collections. One such common plot involves a faithful leman whose lord returns home with "a braw new bride," though the leman eventually recovers the lord's affection at the eleventh hour. This narrative is found in the Danish *Skiaen Anna* and the Scottish ballad of Lord Thomas and Fair Annie, and it appears twice in M. Fauriel's collection of Romaic songs.

Another frequent plot involves a girl who pretends to be dead so that she may be carried on a bier to meet her lover. This story is present in Scotland, as well as in the popular songs of Metz (collected by Puymaigre) and Provence (collected by Damase Arbaud). In the versions from Provence and Metz, the story includes an incongruous sequel in which the lover attempts to murder his bride, but she proves too cunning and drowns him.

## Symbolic Imagery in Folk Songs
A familiar feature in these traditions is the imagery of the bush and briar, or two rose trees. These plants are depicted as meeting and plaiting over the graves of unhappy lovers. This sight prompts passers-by in the Provencal tradition to say, "Diou ague l'amo Des paures amourous."
