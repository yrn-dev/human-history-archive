# Sanjak of Novibazar

The Sanjak of Novibazar was a Turkish political division located within the Balkan Peninsula. As of 1909, the region covered an area of 2,840 square miles and had a population of approximately 153,000 people.

## Diplomatic and Political Context
In the early 20th century, the Sanjak of Novibazar became a point of contention in the geopolitical struggles of the Balkan Peninsula. In 1903, a temporary agreement known as the "February Programme" was reached between Russia and Austria, which was later supplemented in October by the "Muerzsteg Programme." While Count Goluchowski observed the terms of the Muerzsteg programme during his tenure, his successor, Baron von Aerenthal, shifted the policy of the Habsburg monarchy.

By January 1908, Baron von Aerenthal deliberately undermined the existing Austro-Russian agreement. He sought and obtained a concession from the sultan for the construction of a railway. This proposed railway was intended to run from the Bosnian frontier, through the sanjak of Novibazar, to the Turkish terminus located at Mitrovitza.

## International Reactions
The railway concession in the Sanjak of Novibazar caused significant diplomatic friction:
* **Russia:** The move was made despite the expressed wishes of Russia.
* **Servia:** The project roused "helpless resentment" in Servia. The Servians viewed the railway as another link in a chain forged for them by the Habsburg empire, a sentiment exacerbated by Servia's economic dependence on the Dual Monarchy following a war of tariffs in 1906.
* **Great Powers:** Several great powers were offended by the concession, perceiving it as a sign that Austria-Hungary had abandoned its interest in Macedonian reforms.

Baron von Aerenthal was able to pursue this policy due to the support of Germany. The two powers had maintained intimate relations, notably during the Algeciras Conference on March 3, 1906, where Austria-Hungary was the only power to side with Germany in a dispute with France regarding Morocco.

## Related Regional Administrations
The Sanjak of Novibazar existed alongside other complex administrative arrangements in the region. For example, following the Congress of Berlin, Austria undertook the occupation and administration of Bosnia and Herzegovina. This operation began on July 29 with 60,000 troops under General Philippovich, eventually requiring 200,000 men to crush strenuous resistance from Mahommedans in the mountainous terrain. 

While the Sanjak of Novibazar remained Turkish, the occupation of Bosnia and Herzegovina was recognized by the Porte in 1879, provided that the sovereignty of the sultan was acknowledged. These provinces were placed under the control of the joint minister of finance rather than being attached to either half of the empire.
