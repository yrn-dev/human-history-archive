# De Regeering van Koning Lodewijk Napoleon (1806-1810)

*De Regeering van Koning Lodewijk Napoleon (1806-1810)* is a historical work written by L. Wichers and published in Utrecht in 1892. The text focuses on the period during which Louis Bonaparte served as the King of Holland.

## Historical Context of the Reign
Following the victory at Austerlitz on December 2, 1805, Napoleon began planning a network of allied states to surround the French empire. As part of this strategy, Napoleon designated his brother Louis for the throne of Holland, proclaiming him king on June 6, 1806.

The reign of Louis was characterized by increasing tension between the new king and his brother, the Emperor. Napoleon frequently criticized Louis for being too lenient with his subjects and for placing too much importance on courting popularity.

## Conflicts and the Continental System
The relationship between the two brothers deteriorated further due to the continental system. The increasing rigour of this system led to an open rupture. Additionally, the relationship was strained by a violent jealousy Louis felt toward his wife.

In 1808, Napoleon offered Louis the vacant throne of Spain. Louis refused to accept the position, and the honor was subsequently given to Joseph. Throughout this period, the dispute between Louis and the Emperor continued.

## Annexation and the End of the Reign
By the latter part of 1809, Napoleon decided to virtually annex Holland to stop the Dutch from secretly conducting trade with England. At the end of that year, Louis traveled to Paris in an attempt to secure better terms for Holland and to obtain a divorce from Hortense; however, he failed in both objectives.

In January 1810, Napoleon annexed the island of Walcheren. This action was based on the allegation that Louis had failed to defend French interests during the British Walcheren expedition of 1809. French troops also occupied Bergen-op-Zoom and Breda. Although Louis acquiesced to these points, the crisis was only postponed.

Following the collapse of negotiations with Great Britain in the spring of 1810, Napoleon increased the pressure on Louis and eventually dispatched French troops against the Dutch capital. Despairing of any possibility of resistance, Louis fled his kingdom and eventually settled in Bohemia at Toplitz. On July 9, 1810, Napoleon officially annexed Holland into the French empire.

## Later Life of Louis Bonaparte
After his reign, Louis lived separately from his wife. In 1815, he gained custody of his eldest son. He spent much of his remaining life in Rome, where he focused on philosophic and literary studies and the lives of his sons. He took great pleasure in the devotion his sons showed toward the democratic and national cause in Italy between 1830 and 1831, though this was overshadowed by the death of his elder son, Napoleon Louis, during the spring campaign.
