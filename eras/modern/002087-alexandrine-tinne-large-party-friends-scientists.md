# Alexandrine Tinne

## Exploration of the Bahr-el-Ghazal
In 1863, Miss Alexandrine Tinne led an expedition to ascend the Ghazal river. The primary objective of this journey was to determine the extent to which the basin of the Nile extended toward the west. This exploration took place during a period of increasing geographic interest in the region; prior to this, Greek geographers had speculated that the Nile's source lay westward toward Lake Chad. By 1772, the French cartographer d'Anville had published the first map indicating the course of the Ghazal with relative accuracy.

The ascent of the White Nile by Egyptian expeditions between 1839 and 1842 preceded the exploration of the river. Other notable figures had worked in the Ghazal region during the mid-19th century, including John Petherick, a Welsh mining engineer who explored the Jur and the main stream between 1833 and 1865, and the Frenchman Lejean, who surveyed the main river and published a map in 1862. Additionally, the Venetian Giovanni Miani had penetrated the southern regions of the Ghazal basin in 1859, providing the first reports of the Welle, a great river flowing west beyond the Nile watershed.

## Composition and Impact of the Expedition
Miss Tinne traveled with a large party consisting of friends and scientists. Among the members of the party were her aunt and her mother. The scientific leadership of the expedition was provided by two Germans: Hermann Steudner and Theodor von Heuglin.

The expedition resulted in considerable additions to the existing knowledge of the region. However, the journey was marked by significant loss of life; of the nine white members of the party, five died as a result of blackwater fever.

## Subsequent Regional Exploration
Following Tinne's expedition, further exploration of the southern district occurred between 1869 and 1871, when Georg Schweinfurth traversed the area and discovered the Welle by crossing the watershed. Later, between 1879 and 1881, Gessi Pasha, the Italian governor under General C. G. Gordon, explored the river and its tributaries as part of efforts to destroy the slave trade in the Ghazal province. During a similar period, Wilhelm Junker explored the southern tributaries of the Ghazal.

The Jur (also known as the Sue) was eventually followed throughout its entire course by Lieutenant A. H. Dye and other members of a French mission led by Colonel (then Captain) J. B. Marchand. This mission, which had crossed from the Congo in October 1897, reached Fashoda on the White Nile in July 1898.
