# Ezekiel Holliman

## Role in the Providence Settlement
Ezekiel Holliman was a figure associated with the early religious developments in the Providence settlement on Narragansett Bay. He had previously been with Roger Williams at Plymouth and shared Williams' separatist views. 

## Formation of the First Baptist Church
In March 1639, Roger Williams and eleven other individuals decided to form a church composed of baptized believers and to restore the practice of believers' baptism. This decision was rooted in Williams' conviction that churches of Christ should consist exclusively of regenerate members, leading him to view the baptism of infants as a perversion of a Christian ordinance and as valueless.

During the establishment of this community, Ezekiel Holliman performed the first baptism for Roger Williams. Subsequently, Williams baptized the remaining members of the company. This group founded what is recognized as the first Baptist Church in America.

## Context of the Providence Commonwealth
The church was established within a commonwealth founded by Roger Williams, who had fled to the wilderness in January 1636 to avoid deportation to England. Williams had been sentenced to banishment in October 1635 by the Massachusetts Court due to his persistence in advocating separatist views that were considered dangerous and unsettling. In the wilderness, Williams was hospitably entertained by natives who provided him with a tract of land for his settlement. In this new commonwealth, Williams combined civil democracy with absolute religious liberty.

While Williams initially participated in the restoration of believers' baptism with Holliman and others, he eventually withdrew from the church to become a seeker. Williams came to believe that apostolic church organization and ordinances had been lost during a general apostasy, and he felt it was presumptuous to attempt their restoration without a special divine commission. Despite this withdrawal, Williams remained on friendly terms with the Baptists of Providence and maintained the conviction that their practices were closer to the original practice of Christ than those of other communities.
