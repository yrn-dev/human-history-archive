# Administration of Public and State Affairs

The administration of public affairs varies across history and geography, ranging from the specialized joint ministries of dual monarchies and the centralized reforms of colonial governors to the shifting dynamics of power between senates and the general populace in ancient republics.

## Ancient and Early State Administration
In Carthage, the republic achieved significant power when the people spontaneously left the care of public affairs and the chief administration to the senate. However, this shifted as the people, emboldened by conquests and wealth, sought a share in the government and seized most of the power. This transition led to public affairs being handled by factions and cabals, which Polybius cited as a primary cause of Carthage's ruin. Similarly, in Athens, Pericles is credited with introducing a form of corruption by providing pecuniary gratification to individuals attending assemblies where state affairs were discussed. This resulted in members of the republic selling their care in the administration of government, treating sovereign functions as servile employments.

In Japan, the Dadka system (A.D. 645) established an elaborate polity based on Tang models. Over time, however, the Imperial exchequer became exhausted, and administrative posts were treated as assets to be bought and sold. This led to a proliferation of offices and increased red tape. In contrast, Yoritomo and his councillors at Kamakura rejected this model, adopting brevity and simplicity as their guiding principles for polity.

## Colonial and Imperial Administration
In India, Governor-General Dalhousie implemented extensive administrative reforms. He established an imperial system of post-offices with postage stamps and created the first department of public instruction. His administrative efforts included placing gaols under proper inspection, abolishing the branding of convicts, and ending the practice of human sacrifices known as *meriahs*. Dalhousie also inaugurated a system of administrative reports, enlarged the legislative council, and improved the civil service by forbidding trading interests and establishing a training system for civilians in *mufasal* stations. His comprehensive view of the Indian government's machinery was detailed in a minute dated October 13, 1852.

In the Dual Monarchy, common affairs were managed by three joint ministries: finance, war, and foreign affairs. The minister of foreign affairs handled international relations and treaties, though commercial or state treaties involving territorial changes or state burdens required parliamentary assent from both states. The minister of war administered military affairs, though the monarch held supreme command of the army. The minister of finance managed the joint budget and state debt, and until 1909, administered the provinces of Bosnia and Herzegovina (excluding war matters). A joint supreme court of accounts audited these ministries.

## Transition of Power in Korea
Following conflicts with Russia, Japan assumed control over Korean affairs, arguing that Japanese interests in Korea were paramount. Initially, Japan limited itself to advisory supervision of internal administration, employing Japanese experts as counsellors whom the Korean Government could choose to accept or reject. However, under a July 1907 convention, the powers of the Japanese resident-general increased significantly; laws and administrative measures required his consent, and he controlled the appointment of high officials. Japanese vice-ministers were placed in state departments, and Japanese chiefs were appointed to the bureaus of customs and police. By August 22, 1910, the emperor of Korea ceded all rights of sovereignty to the emperor of Japan, and the resident-general, now titled governor-general, took over the entire direction of the administration.

## Political Administration in the Americas
In the early 19th century, Simón Bolivar focused his care on administering freed provinces. In December 1824, he convoked a constituent congress, which invested him with dictatorial power for one year due to the unsettled state of the country. In May 1826, Bolivar presented a project for a constitution for Bolivia. A controversial feature of this code was the proposal to place executive authority in a president for life who would be without responsibility and have the power to nominate a successor. This proposal alarmed republicans in Chile and Buenos Aires. In Peru, Bolivar was accused of intending to unite Peru, Bolivia, and Colombia into a single state under his perpetual dictatorship.

## Individual Administrative Training and Global Perspectives
Some figures entered administration through structured education and experience. Louis (1778-1846) entered the council of state in 1804 specifically to perfect his knowledge of administrative affairs, later serving as the governor of Paris.

On a broader scale, there is a growing perspective that mankind is becoming a single community, necessitating world-wide control over certain affairs. This includes the comprehensive direction of the planet's natural resources, financial and monetary expedients, the management of infectious diseases, and the regulation of population migrations. Such global concerns, along with the destructive nature of modern war, are seen as requiring authorities of a greater range and comprehensiveness than any government that has previously existed.
