# BONDE, GUSTAF, COUNT (1620-1667)

Gustaf Bonde was a Swedish statesman who served during the mid-17th century. He is distinguished in history as a persistent advocate for a pacific policy during an era when the prevailing sentiment among Swedish politicians was to engage in war at the slightest provocation.

## Political Philosophy and Policy
Count Bonde consistently championed a policy of peace, economy, and the development of trade. His primary objective was to make Sweden entirely independent of foreign subsidies. To achieve this, he advocated for strict economy and the recovery of royal domains that had been alienated or fallen into the hands of the nobility. 

Bonde's commitment to peace was evident in his strenuous opposition to the popular Polish adventure undertaken by Charles X. However, Bonde demonstrated a pragmatic approach to governance; once the decision to proceed with the Polish venture had been made, he materially assisted the king in securing the means necessary to carry it out.

## Government Service and Regency
In 1655, Bonde was appointed as a member of the land-recovery commission under Charles X, a role in which his natural partiality for his fellow-peers became apparent. He later ascended to the position of lord high treasurer in 1659, succeeding Herman Fleming. 

During the minority of Charles XI, Bonde served as one of the members of the council of regency appointed to govern Sweden. In 1661, he presented a comprehensive plan to the senate designed to foster independence from foreign subsidies through the aforementioned policies of trade-development, peace, economy, and the further recovery of alienated estates. He framed a budget in 1662 based on these same principles, which later served as an invaluable guide for Charles XI.

## Opposition and Decline
Despite his tenacity of purpose, Bonde faced significant opposition from the majority of his co-regents and the senate. Many of these officials preferred the more adventurous and ultimately ruinous methods championed by the chancellor, Magnus de la Gardie. 

Bonde's efforts were further undermined by the ambitions of oligarchs and the jealousy of numerous monopolists who rose in arms against his economic policies. The financial stability of the state was eventually shattered by a costly and useless expedition against Bremen in 1665, an action that had been undertaken contrary to Bonde's advice.

In his final years, Bonde's ability to resist these opposing forces was weakened by sickness and the mortification he felt regarding the triumph of reckless extravagance. Consequently, he practically retired from government service some time before his death in 1667.
