# Austria and Hungary

The relationship between Austria and Hungary has been characterized by periods of conflict, strategic integration, and eventual separation. This history spans from the early Habsburg acquisitions to the establishment of the Republic of Austria.

## Early Habsburg Rule and Turkish Conflict
In 1519, the Habsburg dominions in eastern Germany included the county of Tirol, Carniola, Styria, Carinthia, and the duchies of Lower and Upper Austria. Emperor Charles V resigned these lands, as well as claims to Hungary and Bohemia, to his brother Ferdinand. Following the battle of Mohacs on August 28, 1526, Suleiman the Magnificent defeated and killed King Louis of Bohemia and Hungary. This victory allowed the Turks to conquer and retain the majority of Hungary until the peace of Karlowitz in 1699. Ferdinand spent much of his life attempting to secure Hungary while combating the Turks, who besieged Vienna in 1530 and launched several invasions of Austria and Hungary. Eventually, Ferdinand agreed to pay an annual tribute to Suleiman for a small portion of Hungary, measuring approximately 12,228 square miles.

## Internal Stability and Governance
During a period of simmering racial rivalries, the government utilized the army to maintain order by garrisoning regiments in opposing territories: Austrians were stationed in Hungary, while Hungarian regiments were placed in Italy. To prevent the infiltration of "revolutionary" ideas from outside the dominions, the government erected a "Chinese wall" of censors and tariffs, though this was largely unsuccessful. Internally, the growth of Liberalism was countered through the re-establishment of clerical control over education and rigid supervision of the press. 

Administrative stagnation was common, and the system of provincial diets and estates—revived in various regions between 1816 and 1828—was not representative. Eligibility was limited to nobles and clergy, with a few town delegates, and their functions were primarily restricted to local police matters and registering imperial decrees regarding taxation or recruiting. This era was marked by economic stagnation, where trade was strangled by internal *octrois* and high frontier tariffs, and agriculture was hampered by the feudal privileges of a noble caste.

## The Dual Monarchy and the Ausgleich
The principle of Dualism led to a change in imperial titles in 1868; the monarch became the "Emperor of Austria, King of Bohemia, &c., and Apostolic King of Hungary," signifying the separation of the two districts. The realm became known as the "Austrian-Hungarian Monarchy" or "Austrian-Hungarian Realm." This division extended to the administration: there were three ministries of finance—the *Kiralye* for Hungary, the *Kaiserlich-Koeniglich* (K.K.) for Austrian affairs, and the *Kaiserlich und Koeniglich* for joint affairs.

Financial and economic reforms were managed through a general settlement every ten years known as the *Ausgleich* (compact or compromise). These negotiations were often fraught with anxiety and national jealousy, as concessions in commercial policy were often traded for gains in financial agreements. For example, in 1877, Austria granted Hungary a share in the management of the bank in exchange for a favorable arrangement regarding the bonus on exported sugar.

## Economic Crisis and the 1907 Treaty
By October 1907, a severe economic crisis in Hungary led to a new "customs and commercial treaty." Hungarian opinion was sobered by the realization that separation from Austria would result in commercial ruin or distress, while Austria recognized that separation would deprive its manufacturers of their best market and increase the cost of living in Cisleithania. Key terms of the agreement included:
* The establishment of separate but identical customs tariffs for each state.
* The facilitation of railway communication between Vienna and Dalmatia (to be completed by 1911) and between Hungary and Prussia.
* The reform of Hungarian Stock Exchange and produce laws to prevent agrarian speculation.
* The abolition of trade impediments for sugar.
* The creation of a court of arbitration consisting of four judges from each state, with the presidency decided by lot.
* Provisions for Hungary to redeem its share of the old Austrian debt at a rate of 4.325% within ten years.

## Political Upheaval and Independence
The fall of Metternich triggered widespread unrest. In Hungary, the government was forced on March 31 to consent to a new constitution that virtually made Hungary an independent state. Similarly, a separate constitution was promised to Bohemia on April 8. 

Following later conflicts, the Republic of Austria was recognized as an independent republic. Under the terms of its treaty, Austria was required to recognize the complete independence of Hungary, as well as the independence of Jugoslavia and Czecho-Slovakia. Austria was forced to demobilize its naval and aerial forces, surrender aviation material, and renounce all extra-European rights. Economic conditions and freedom of transit were established similar to those in the German treaty, and Austria was granted access to the Adriatic.
