# The Landwehr and Honveds of the Dual Monarchy

The military structure of the Austro-Hungarian Dual Monarchy was composed of several distinct layers: the common army (*K. und K.*), the special national armies, and the *Landsturm* (levy-in-mass). Among the special armies were the Austrian *Landwehr* (which retained the old designation *K.K.*) and the Hungarian *Honveds*. These two entities functioned as separate national institutions.

## Organization and Composition
The Austrian *Landwehr* was organized into eight divisions of varying strength. The "Royal Hungarian" *Landwehr*, known as the *Honveds*, was organized into seven divisions. Both the Austrian and Hungarian *Landwehr* were equipped with their own artillery and cavalry, specifically hussars and Uhlans.

In terms of peace-time strength as of 1905, the Austrian *Landwehr* consisted of 2,053 officers and 25,766 men, with 1,456 horses. The Hungarian *Honveds* comprised 2,648 officers and 25,319 men, with 3,772 horses. When broken down by branch, the Austrian *Landwehr* infantry included 1,883 officers and 23,905 men (with 174 horses), while its cavalry consisted of 170 officers and 1,861 men (with 1,282 horses). The Hungarian *Honveds* infantry included 2,258 officers and 21,149 men (with 262 horses), and its cavalry consisted of 390 officers and 4,170 men (with 3,510 horses).

## Administration and Recruitment
The administration of the *Landwehr* and *Honveds* differed from that of the common army. While the common army was managed by the joint minister of war, the special armies were under the administration of the respective ministries of national defence for Austria and Hungary. However, the supreme command of all armed forces, including the special armies, was vested in the monarch.

Recruitment for these forces was based on the principle of universal and personal obligation of the citizen to bear arms, a system established in 1868. The annual contingent of recruits was determined by military bills voted on by the Austrian and Hungarian parliaments, generally based on census population data. In 1905, 10,000 men were annually allotted to the Austrian *Landwehr* and 12,500 to the Hungarian *Honveds*.

The term of service for a citizen involved various stages: two years with the colours (three years for cavalry), seven or eight years in the reserve, and two years in the *Landwehr*. Men not drafted into the active army spent an equivalent total period of service in special reserves.

## Role in War and Total Strength
It was probable that in the event of war, a *Landwehr* or *Honveds* division would become part of each army corps. An exception existed for the Vienna corps, which maintained three divisions during peace.

The estimated war strength for these forces was significant. The *Landwehr* and *Honveds* together were expected to yield 219,000 infantry and 18,000 cavalry. When combined with the first line army and other reserves, the approximate total strength of the armed forces (excluding the *Landsturm*) was 1,147,000.

## The Landsturm
Men of military age up to 42 who were not in the active army, reserves, or *Landwehr* formed the *Landsturm*. This levy-in-mass included many individuals who, in other military systems, would have been classified as *Landwehr*. The specific war strength and numbers of the *Landsturm* were not published.
