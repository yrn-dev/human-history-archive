# Barratry

Barratry is a term used in different legal contexts, most notably within English criminal law, Scottish law, and the field of marine insurance. The term originates from the Old French words *bareter* or *barater*, meaning to cheat or barter.

## Marine Insurance
In the context of marine insurance, barratry is defined as any form of fraud committed by a ship's master against the insurers or the owner of the vessel. For an act to be classified as barratry under this definition, the master must act with the specific intention of benefiting himself at the expense of the owners or insurers.

Continental jurists apply a broader interpretation of the term. In their view, barratry encompasses any willful act performed by the crew or the master—regardless of the motive—that results in the charterers or owners being damnified. Due to these risks, it is common practice in bills of lading to except barratry from the liability of the shipowners.

## English Criminal Law
In English criminal law, the offense is more commonly referred to as "common barratry." This crime consists of the act of constantly stirring up and inciting quarrels in a manner that disturbs the peace, whether this occurs in courts or other locations. Common barratry is recognized as an offense under both statute and common law, and those found guilty are subject to imprisonment and fines.

To prove the commission of common barratry, it must be demonstrated that the act was committed at least three times, as it is considered a cumulative offense. A statute enacted in 1726 further stipulated that any person belonging to the legal profession who was found guilty of common barratry would be disabled from practicing law in the future.

While there was nearly a two-century period without a recorded indictment for this offense, a case occurred in 1889 at the Guildford summer assizes (*R. v. Bellgrove*). However, the charge was not proceeded upon because the defendant was convicted of a different offense.

## Scottish Law
In Scotland, the definition of barratry differs significantly from the English legal tradition. In the Scottish jurisdiction, barratry is the specific crime committed by a judge who allows himself to be induced by bribery to pronounce a judgment.
