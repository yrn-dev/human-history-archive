# University of Pennsylvania

The University of Pennsylvania, formerly known as the College of Philadelphia, is an American educational institution that has hosted various professors and academic expeditions across diverse fields of study, including medicine, linguistics, and archaeology.

## Academic Appointments in Medicine and Natural History

The institution has a history of appointing scholars to chairs in botany, natural history, and medicine. Benjamin Smith Barton (1766–1815), an American naturalist born in Lancaster, Pennsylvania, was appointed as the professor of botany and natural history in 1789. In 1795, Barton was made professor of *materia medica*. Following the death of Dr. Benjamin Rush in 1813, Barton obtained the chair of practical medicine. Barton, who had studied at Edinburgh and graduated from Goettingen, was also a strong supporter and president of the American Philosophical Society (chosen in 1802) and authored the well-known *Elements of Botany* in 1803.

## Linguistics and Archaeology

The university has also been a center for the study of ancient languages and the exploration of early civilizations. Rev. John Punnett Peters, Ph.D., D.D., a Canon Residentiary of the Cathedral of New York, previously served as the Professor of Hebrew at the University of Pennsylvania. In addition to his teaching role, Peters was in charge of a University of Pennsylvania expedition that conducted excavations at Nippur between 1888 and 1895.

Furthermore, Daniel Garrison Brinton (1837–1899) served as a professor at the university. Brinton, an American archaeologist and ethnologist born in Thornbury, Pennsylvania, held the position of professor of American linguistics and archaeology from 1886 until his death on July 31, 1899. Brinton's academic background included graduation from Yale in 1858 and studies at Jefferson Medical College, as well as further studies in Paris and Heidelberg. He was a prolific author of works such as *The Myths of the New World* (1868) and *Religions of Primitive People* (1897), and he edited the eight-volume *Library of American Aboriginal Literature* (1882–1890).
