# Basses-Pyrenees

Basses-Pyrenees is a department located in south-western France at the angle of the Bay of Biscay. Formed in 1790, the department was created using three districts of Gascony—Labourd, Soule, and Basse-Navarre—and two-thirds of its territory from Bearn. The districts of Basse-Navarre, Soule, and Labourd constitute the Basque region of France and are situated in the west of the department.

## Geography and Boundaries
The department is bounded to the north by Gers and Landes, to the east by Hautes-Pyrenees, to the south by Spain, and to the west by the Atlantic Ocean. Hautes-Pyrenees maintains two enclaves within Basses-Pyrenees, which form five communes.

The southern portion of the department is entirely occupied by the lower and western summits of the Pyrenees. The height of these mountains increases gradually from west to east. In the south of the valley of Mauleon, the peak of Orhy reaches 6,618 ft., while the peak of the Rhune, located south of St Jean de Luz, rises to 2,950 ft. On the border of the Basque country, the mean height of the summits is not significantly greater than that of the Rhune. 

Higher elevations are found beyond the peak of Anie (8,215 ft.) on the meridian of Orthez, which serves as the boundary of Bearn. These include the southern peak of Ossau at 9,465 ft. and Mourrous, which reaches 9,760 ft. on the border of Hautes-Pyrenees. The crest-line of the main range generally defines the frontier between France and Spain.

Outside of the southern mountains, the department's terrain consists of hills divided by numerous fertile valleys to the west of the Gave de Pau, and a region of plateaus and heaths to the northeast of the same river.

## Hydrography
The rivers of Basses-Pyrenees generally flow toward the north-west. Most streams meet in the Adour via the Nive, the Bidouze, and the Gave de Pau. In the north-east, the two Luys flow directly into the Adour, joining it in Landes. In the south-west, the Bidassoa and the Nivelle flow directly into the sea.

The Gave de Pau is a larger stream than the Adour. The lower course of the Adour serves as the boundary between Basses-Pyrenees and Landes. The Adour enters the sea a short distance below Bayonne over a shifting bar that has frequently altered the position of the river's mouth.

## Infrastructure and Demographics
Important passes into Spain run along the Aspe and Nive upper valleys, which are guarded by forts. As of 1906, the population of the department was 426,817, and its total area was 2,977 sq. m.
