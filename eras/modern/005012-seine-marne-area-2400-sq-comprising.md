# Brie

## Geography and Boundaries
Brie is an agricultural district located in northern France to the east of Paris. Its boundaries are defined by the Seine to the west and south, and the Marne to the north. The district covers an area of 2,400 sq. m. and encompasses the majority of the department of Seine-et-Marne, as well as portions of the departments of Aube, Marne, Aisne, Seine, and Seine-et-Oise.

The region is divided into two primary portions: the *Brie française* in the west and the *Brie champenoise* in the east. Topographically, the Brie is a plateau characterized by few eminences. Its altitude varies between 300 and 500 ft. in the western section and between 500 and 650 ft. in the eastern section.

## Geology and Natural Features
The surface soil of the Brie consists of clay containing embedded fragments of siliceous sandstone, which are utilized for constructional purposes and the creation of millstones. The subsoil is composed of limestone.

The landscape is varied by several forests of significant size, most notably the Forêt d'Armainvilliers, the Forêt de Crécy, and the Forêt de Senart. The region is not abundantly watered, with annual rainfall measuring between 20 and 24 inches. The primary rivers of the district are the Yères, a tributary of the Seine, and the Grand Morin and Petit Morin, which are tributaries of the Marne.

## Economy and Production
The Brie is renowned for its dairy products, particularly its cheeses, as well as its grain.

## Regional Context
The Brie is situated within the broader Seine basin. To the south of the Marne, the heights of Brie reach altitudes of 700 to 800 ft. and slope gently toward the west. These heights command the plain of Champagne-Pouilleuse via a steep descent to the east. 

Historically, the area is associated with the old district of Île-de-France, which was bounded by the Marne, the Seine, the Oise, the Thève, and the Beuvronne. Following the Revolution, the territory of Île-de-France was used to form several departments, including the greater part of Seine-et-Marne and the department of the Seine.
