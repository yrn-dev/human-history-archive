# Johann Franz Buddeus

## Early Life and Education
Johann Franz Buddeus was a German Lutheran divine born in Anklam, a town located in Pomerania. His father served as a pastor in the same town. Buddeus pursued his higher education at Wittenberg and Greifswald, where he studied with great distinction. During his academic tenure, he focused specifically on the study of history, theology, and languages.

## Academic Career
Buddeus began his professional academic career in 1692 when he was appointed as a professor of Latin and Greek at Coburg. The following year, in 1693, he became a professor of moral philosophy at the university of Halle. In 1705, he transitioned to Jena, where he was appointed professor of theology. Buddeus was held in high esteem at Jena, leading to his appointment in 1715 as a member of the Consistory and the Primarius of his faculty. He remained active in his field until his death in 1729.

## Principal Works
Throughout his career, Buddeus authored several significant volumes across various disciplines:
*   **Philosophy and Law:** He wrote the three-volume *Elementa Philosophiae Practicae, Instrumentalis, et Theoreticae* in 1697 and *Selecta Juris Naturae et Gentium*, published in Halle in 1704.
*   **Theology and History:** His works include the four-volume *Historia, Ecclesiastica Veteris Testamenti* (Halle, 1709) and the *Leipzig, allgemeines historisches Lexikon* (Leipzig, 1709 ff.).
*   **Later Publications:** In 1727, he published the two-volume *Isagoge Historico-Theologica ad Theologiam Universam, singulasque ejus partes* and the three-volume *Miscellanea Sacra* in Jena.
