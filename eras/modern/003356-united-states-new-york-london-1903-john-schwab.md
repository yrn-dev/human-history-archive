## Educational and Philosophical Contributions
John Bascom (1827–1903) was a prominent American philosophical writer and educationalist born in Genoa, New York. Bascom's career included serving as a professor of rhetoric at Williams College (1855–1874) and as the president of the University of Wisconsin, where he also taught mental and moral philosophy from 1874 to 1887. In his later years, he returned to Williams College, serving as a lecturer in sociology (1887–1891 and 1901–1903) and as a professor of economics (1891–1901). Bascom retired in 1903. His extensive bibliography includes works such as *Growth of Nationality in the United States* (1899) and *God and His Goodness* (1901).

## Notable Figures and Estates
The Astor family maintained significant influence in New York. William Backhouse Astor (1792–1875), known as the "Landlord of New York," increased his fortune through real estate and contributed approximately $550,000 to the Astor library. His son, John Jacob Astor (1822–1890), continued this philanthropic support. The subsequent generation, William Waldorf Astor (1848–), served as the United States minister to Italy from 1882 to 1885 and built the Waldorf and New Netherland hotels in New York. Astor eventually moved to England in 1890, became a naturalized citizen in 1899, and acquired the *Pall Mall Gazette* in 1893.
