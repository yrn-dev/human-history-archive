# Buckingham

The name Buckingham is associated with several historical figures, most notably the Dukes of Buckingham and the Earls of Buckinghamshire, as well as the town and county of the same name.

## George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham

George Villiers, Duke of Buckingham, was a prominent political and literary figure. His political career was characterized by a degree of levity and shifts in allegiance. Around 1620, during a period of high interest in the Bohemian revolution and the threat of a Spanish invasion of the Palatinate, Buckingham initially supported the popular movement. However, by the end of the summer of 1620, he shifted his position to align closely with Gondomar, the Spanish ambassador, following injuries inflicted upon English sailors by the Dutch in the East Indies. This change may have been influenced by his marriage to Lady Katherine Manners, daughter of the 6th Earl of Rutland, who was a Roman Catholic despite her outward conformity to the English Church.

Buckingham's actions were often driven by vanity or passion, though he frequently persuaded himself that he was performing his duty to his country. In one instance, he was involved in a dispute with Bacon regarding the marriage of Bacon's daughter to Sir John Villiers, the marquess's penniless brother. Although Bacon initially opposed the match, the indignation of both the king and Buckingham forced him to apologize and advance the marriage.

The career of the Duke ended in tragedy. While at Portsmouth to command a final expedition for the relief of La Rochelle—a task he had begun to realize was beyond his powers—he was assassinated. On the morning of August 23, John Felton, a former servant at Ré who felt disappointed by a lack of promotion and unpaid services, stabbed Buckingham to the heart as he left his breakfast room. Buckingham is survived by three sons and one daughter; his second son, George (1628–1687), succeeded him as duke.

## Literary Works of Buckingham

Buckingham possessed undeveloped but evident poetical gifts, producing occasional verses and satires. He is most noted as the author of *The Rehearsal*, a clever satire on the heroic drama and specifically on Dryden. First performed on December 7, 1671, at the Theatre Royal and published in 1672, the play was later imitated by Sheridan in *The Critic* and Fielding in *Tom Thumb the Great*.

His other published works include:
*   *The Chances* (1682), an adaptation of a play by Fletcher.
*   *The Restoration or Right will take place* (1714), adapted from Beaumont and Fletcher's *Philaster*.
*   *The Battle of Sedgmoor* and *The Militant Couple* (both published in 1704).

Additionally, Wood mentions a work titled *A Demonstration of the Deity*, though no trace of it currently exists.

## The Earls of Buckinghamshire

The titles of the Earls of Buckinghamshire passed through several generations of the Hobart family. The 4th earl left no sons, and the title passed to his nephew, George Robert Hobart (1789–1849), the 5th earl. In 1824, the 5th earl inherited the Hampden family estates in Buckinghamshire and adopted the name Hampden.

Upon the 5th earl's death in February 1849, he was succeeded by his brother, Augustus Edward Hobart (1793–1884), the 6th earl, who took the name Hobart-Hampden in 1878. The 6th earl was succeeded by his grandson, Sidney Carr Hobart-Hampden (born 1860), the 7th earl, who added the name Mercer-Henderson. Other descendants of the 6th earl include Augustus Charles Hobart-Hampden, known as Hobart Pasha, and two sons, Vere Henry (governor of Madras from 1872) and Frederick John, both of whom predeceased their father.

Historically, until 1784, the 2nd earl of Buckinghamshire signed himself simply as "Buckingham," which has led to some historical confusion, as George Grenville, Earl Temple, was not created marquess of Buckingham until that year.

## Biographical and Historical Sources

The life of the Duke of Buckingham has been documented by various authors. Key biographies include the work of Lady Burghclere (1903) and C.H. Firth in the *Dictionary of National Biography* (1899). Other accounts are found in Wood's *Athenae Oxon* (Bliss), *Biographia Britannica*, and works by Brian Fairfax and the author of *Hudibras*. Early accounts include the *Life of Buckingham* by Sir Henry Wotton (1642) and characters written by the Earl of Clarendon (1706).

Regarding the town and county of Buckingham, the standard history is G. Lipscomb's *History and Antiquities of the County of Buckingham* (1831–1847). Other regional sources include works by Browne Willis, R. Gibbs, and the *Victoria County History*.
