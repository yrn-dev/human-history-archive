# General Fairfax

General Fairfax was a prominent military figure during the English Civil Wars, commanding forces that engaged several high-ranking Royalist officers and suppressed regional insurrections.

## Military Engagements and Victories
Fairfax's army was involved in several decisive actions against Royalist forces. At the battle of Marston Moor, Fairfax's Yorkshire horse were swept before them by Sir Charles Lucas. However, in a later engagement at Colchester in 1648, Fairfax's army invested the city. Following a desperate resistance, the city fell, leading to the court martial and execution of Royalist officers Sir Charles Lucas and Sir George Lisle.

In the west, Fairfax received the surrender of Baron Ralph Hopton on February 16, 1646, after Hopton's last stand at Torrington. Additionally, in 1648, Fairfax was responsible for suppressing a widespread insurrection organized on behalf of Charles II in the county of Kent.

## Headquarters and Administration
During the civil wars, Fairfax utilized Bradenburg House as his headquarters in 1647. Bradenburg House was a riverside mansion located in Fulham, built by Sir Nicholas Crispe during the reign of Charles I.

## Interaction with the Nobility
The actions of the parliamentarian forces under Fairfax had significant financial consequences for Royalist figures. George Villiers, the 2nd Duke of Buckingham, participated in a rebellion in July 1648. As a result, his lands—which had been restored to him in 1647 due to his youth—were confiscated. A considerable portion of these lands passed into the possession of Fairfax. Following this confiscation, the Duke refused to compound.
