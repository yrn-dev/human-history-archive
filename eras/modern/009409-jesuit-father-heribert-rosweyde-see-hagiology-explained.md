# Heribert Rosweyde

Heribert Rosweyde was a Dutch Jesuit father known for his scholarly contributions to hagiology and the publication of ecclesiastical texts.

## The Acta Sanctorum
Rosweyde is credited with conceiving the original idea for the *Acta Sanctorum*, a comprehensive collection of the biographies and legends of saints. These accounts were arranged according to the calendar by day. In 1607, Rosweyde issued a prospectus for the project titled *Fasti sanctorum quorum vitae in Belgicis Bibliothecis manuscriptae*. 

His stated intention was to compile the lives of the saints from manuscripts and publish them in eighteen volumes, accompanied by sober notes. By the time of his death in 1629, Rosweyde had gathered a significant amount of material, although he had not yet begun the actual publication of the work. The project was subsequently carried on by another Jesuit father, John Bolland, who was sent to Antwerp to continue amassing material and expanding the scope of the collection.

## Scholarly Works and Annotations
Beyond the foundation of the *Acta Sanctorum*, Rosweyde produced extensive work regarding the *Vitae Patrum*. He published a large volume of this work intended for use in convents and created two copious indexes to the text. These indexes serve as guides to various subjects, including scattered passages on obedience and descriptions of "infernal scenes."

Rosweyde also authored an *Onomasticon*, which provides definitions and measurements for ancient terms. For example, he identifies the "arura" as a square measure of one hundred Egyptian cubits and defines "paximacia" as small loaves or biscuits weighing six ounces each. His annotations are cited as resources for understanding ancient Latin versions of texts, such as those by Evagrius.

## Other Activities
In addition to his hagiographical and philological work, Rosweyde was involved in religious polemics. He is identified as one of the Jesuit pamphleteers who launched open attacks against the scholar Casaubon after Casaubon committed himself to Anglicanism.
