# BARNET

Barnet is a residential district located in England within the mid or St Albans parliamentary division of Hertfordshire. Situated 10 miles north of London, the district is served by the branches and main line of the Great Northern railway.

## Divisions of Barnet

The district is composed of three primary divisions:

**Chipping or High Barnet**
This area is an urban district and market town with a population of 7,876 as of 1901. The name "High Barnet" refers to its location on a hill, while the name "Chipping Barnet" is derived from a market granted there by Henry II to be kept by the abbots of St Albans. Historically, the town served as an important coaching station on the Great North Road. It continues to host a large annual fair for cattle and horses.

**East Barnet**
Located 2 miles southeast of Chipping Barnet, this area includes the East Barnet Valley urban district, which had a population of 10,094. It features an ancient parish church that contains Norman portions, although the structure has been enlarged in modern times.

**New Barnet**
This division is situated 1 mile east by south of Chipping Barnet.

## Historical Significance

The vicinity of Chipping Barnet is the site of a decisive battle fought in 1471 between the houses of Lancaster and York. The conflict took place near a point marked by an obelisk; the battle resulted in a total defeat for the Lancastrians and the death of the earl of Warwick.

## Related Localities

While not part of the Hertfordshire residential district of Barnet, Friern Barnet is located 3 miles south of Chipping Barnet. It is situated in the Enfield parliamentary division of Middlesex and is characterized as a residential district adjoining Whetstone to the south and Finchley to the north. As of 1901, the urban district of Friern Barnet had a population of 11,566. The name "Friern" is a reminder of the former lordship of the manor, which was possessed by the friary of St John of Jerusalem in Clerkenwell, London.
