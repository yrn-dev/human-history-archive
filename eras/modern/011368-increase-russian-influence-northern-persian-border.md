# Russian Influence on the Northern Persian Border

The expansion of Russian influence along the northern borders of Persia and its subsequent movement southward toward Seistan triggered a series of strategic diplomatic and administrative responses from Great Britain. To counter this influence, the British appointed a consul at Kirman, which served as the dominating town of southern Khorasan and maintained a direct connection to Meshed in the north. Additionally, Great Britain secured the rights of administration for the Nushki district, a move intended to protect trade between Quetta and Seistan via the new Helmund desert route.

## Territorial Boundaries and the Oxus
The Russian advance toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts necessitated the establishment of a formal boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and Russia's trans-Caspian conquests. This was addressed by the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission between 1884 and 1886. The resulting boundary, which is largely artificial in construction, was fixed between the Oxus and the Hari Rud (the river of Herat). 

Key geographical markers of this boundary include Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs where the line leaves the Hari Rud, and the most southerly point where the boundary crosses the Kushk, situated about 60 miles north of Herat. From Khamiab—located roughly 150 miles above the Russian Trans-Caspian railway crossing at Charjui—the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, extending to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## Strategic Military and Infrastructure Developments
Russia's presence in the region was bolstered by significant infrastructure and military installations. Annentkov is noted for bridging the Trans-Caspian deserts with a railway. In the Afghan province of Badghis, the river Kushk forms part of the boundary between Russian and Afghan territory. The Kushkinski Post (or Kushk) serves as a fourth-class Russian fortress. This post is connected to Merv via a Russian branch railway, with a terminus at Chahil Dukteran. This terminus is located only 66 miles from Herat, positioning it as a potentially important base for a Russian advance in the event of war. The area is served by both the Orenburg-Tashkent and Transcaspian railways.

## Regional Geography and Administration
In the Persian province of Khorasan, the town of Bujnurd (historically Buzinjird) is situated in a fertile plain. Its northern boundary is defined by Russian Transcaspia. The population of this district is diverse, consisting of Persians, Garaili Turks, Goklan Turkomans, and Kurds (specifically the Zafranlu and Shadillu groups).

Beyond the Afghan border, the Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 demarcated a line extending to the glaciers and snowfields overlooking the Chinese border. The Sarikol range separates the Russian Pamirs from Chinese Turkestan, though the actual dividing line remains indefinite.

## Russian Exploration in Asia
During the 19th century, several Russian explorers conducted scientific expeditions that expanded knowledge of Central Asian topography. Following the work of Prjevalsky, explorers Pevtsov and Roborovski conducted expeditions in 1889-1890 and 1894, focusing on the northern borders of Tibet and western Chinese Turkestan. Other prominent figures included Potanin, Lessar, and P.K. Kozlov. 

Lieutenant K.P. Kozlov specifically investigated the region between Arghan and Tikkenlik, attempting to prove that the Chinese Lob-nor is the Kara-Koshun (Black district). His research, published in a separate pamphlet and the *Isvestia* of the Russian Geographical Society, analyzed the movement of the Koncheh-daria and the receding humidity of the region, which allowed moving sands to conquer the land and create desert conditions.
