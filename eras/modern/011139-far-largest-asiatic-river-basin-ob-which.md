# River Basins of Asia

The hydrographic layout of Asia is divided into three primary divisions based on drainage patterns. These divisions encompass a vast array of river basins, with the northern division including drainage that falls into the Mediterranean, the Aralo-Caspian depression, and the Arctic Sea.

## The Northern Division
The northern division of Asia covers an area of approximately 6,394,500 sq. m. This total is comprised of the Mediterranean basin (268,500 sq. m.), the Aralo-Caspian basin (1,759,000 sq. m.), and the Arctic river basins, which account for 4,367,000 sq. m.

Within this region, the Ob river basin is distinguished as the largest river basin in Asia, with an extent that exceeds 1,000,000 sq. m. Other significant rivers in the northern and eastern reaches include the Lena, which flows through Siberia into the Arctic Sea. The Yablonoi range serves as a geographic divider between the waters of the Lena and those of the Amur.

## Southern and Interior Divisions
The southern division of Asia is nearly equal in extent to the northern division, totaling 6,514,000 sq. m. This area is split between the Indian Ocean drainage (2,873,000 sq. m.) and the Pacific drainage (3,641,000 sq. m.). The Amur river, which falls into the North Pacific, has a basin embracing no less than 776,000 sq. m. Other major basins in these divisions include the Yang-tsze-kiang at 685,000 sq. m., the Ganges at 409,500 sq. m., and the Indus at 370,000 sq. m.

The interior or inland basins cover approximately 3,141,500 sq. m., which is about half the size of the other two divisions. These inland basins include:
* The Syro-Arabian inland basin
* The Gobi depression
* The Tibetan plateau
* The basin of Asia Minor
* The Iranian (or Perso-Afghan) uplands
* Lacustrine regions located south of the Arctic watershed

## Regional Geographic Features
In the eastern extremity of Asia, the Stanovor mountain range reaches heights of 4,000 to 5,000 ft. and runs from Kamchatka to the borders of Manchuria. The basin of the Amur river and its affluents constitute Manchuria. To the east of the Khingan range, drainage falls into the Yellow Sea and the Amur, while to the west lies Mongolia, a region characterized by an inclination toward the central area of the continent and described as almost rainless.

In the northern part of the continent, between the Seas of Okhotsk and Bering and the Arctic Ocean, the land is a barren plateau between 1,000 and 2,000 ft. high. This area is deeply indented by long fiords—such as the Gulf of Anadyr, Kolyuchin Bay, and the Bays of Ghizhiga and Penzhina—and by the rivers of the Anadyr basin. Further south, the basin of the Uda is located between the Bureya and Stanovoi mountains, characterized by marshes and forests.

## Geological Definition of Basins
In a geological context, a "basin" is defined as a broad shallow syncline, a structure pertaining to the bed rock of a district. This structural basin is formed by rock beds that exhibit a centroclinal dip. This differs from a physiographic river basin, although the two may occasionally coincide. Other geological variations include "rock-basins," which are steep-sided depressions scooped from solid rock by glaciers in mountainous regions, and lake basins, which may be formed by crustal movements or the solution of soluble constituents like rock salt in the underlying strata.
