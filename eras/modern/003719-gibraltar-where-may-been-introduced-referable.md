# Gibraltar

Gibraltar is a location characterized by a prominent rock and fortress that overlooks Algeciras Bay. It is situated on the Straits of Gibraltar, a channel that serves as a boundary for the maritime province of Cadiz in the extreme south of Spain.

## Geography and Natural History
The rock of Gibraltar is one of the habitats of the Barbary ape (*Macacus inuus*), a tailless monkey that also inhabits Morocco and Algeria. This monkey is light yellowish-brown on its upper side and yellowish-white below, featuring a flesh-coloured naked face. While it is referable to the Asiatic group of macaques, it represents the subgenus *Inuus*. On Gibraltar, the Barbary ape is entirely terrestrial in its habits and travels in droves; however, it is noted that the species may have been introduced to the rock.

The surrounding region, known as the Campo de Gibraltar, is characterized by picturesque, well-wooded mountain ranges that abound in game. To the south of the European mainland, Punta Marroqui stands as the southernmost promontory on the straits.

## Ancient History and Trade
In antiquity, the Straits of Gibraltar were referred to as the Pillars of Hêraklês. The region was a focal point for early Phoenician commerce. The colony of Gadês, located outside the strait, served as a center for extensive trade reaching as far as Britain, the Scilly Islands, and the western coast of Africa. Phoenician factories and small trading towns were established along the western coast of what is now Morocco. One such established dépôt for merchandise, the island of Kernê, was located twelve days' sail from the strait.

The south-western coast of Spain, extending from the Strait of Gibraltar to the territory beyond Cadiz, was known as Tartessus. This region was a source of brass (or copper) that was brought to Greece in large quantities by the Phocæans from King Arganthonius.

Historical accounts also mention an alleged circumnavigation of Africa ordered by Nekos, in which Phoenicians claimed to have sailed from the Red Sea, around the Cape of Good Hope, and to the Straits of Gibraltar. These sailors reported having the sun on their right hand during the voyage, a claim that Herodotus rejected as incredible because he believed a journey from the Red Sea to the Straits of Gibraltar would place the sun on the left hand.

## Political and Administrative History
By January 1914, Gibraltar was classified as a Crown colony of the British Empire. In this capacity, it was ruled by the British Home Government through the Colonial Office and was administered by a governor, a system of rule that verged on autocracy.

In September 1914, the Straits of Gibraltar were a point of military concern. Winston S. Churchill inquired into the responsibility for allowing a merchant ship from Buenos Ayres, carrying 400 German reservists, to pass through the Straits and proceed to Italian ports rather than being arrested as prisoners of war.
