# Bangor

Bangor is a city and port of entry located in the United States in the state of Maine. It serves as the county-seat of Penobscot county and is situated at the confluence of the Kenduskeag stream with the Penobscot river. Positioned approximately 60 miles from the ocean and about 75 miles northeast of Augusta, the city sits at the head of navigation on the Penobscot.

## Demographics and Connectivity
The population of Bangor has shown steady growth at the turn of the century, recorded at 19,103 in 1890, 21,850 in 1900, and 24,803 according to the 1910 census. As of 1900, the population included 176 negroes and 3,726 foreign-born residents. 

The city is connected to Brewer (which had a population of 5,667 in 1910) by a bridge across the Penobscot measuring approximately 1,300 feet in length. Transportation is facilitated by the Eastern Steamship line and the Maine Central railway, which serves the city directly. Several important branches radiate from Bangor, and the Maine Central connects near the city with the Washington County railway and the Bangor & Aroostook railway, the latter of which maintains its general offices in Bangor.

## Geography and Economy
The business district of the city is located on both sides of the Kenduskeag and extends for about three miles along the western bank of the Penobscot river, where the bank is quite low. In contrast, many fine residences are situated on the hillsides further back.

Bangor serves as the trade center for an extensive agricultural district. The city's economy is heavily tied to its geography; the Kenduskeag provides good water-power, and the Penobscot is navigable for large vessels due to a tide that rises to a height of 17 feet. Bangor is recognized as one of the largest lumber depots in the United States and also ships significant quantities of ice. The city engages in foreign trade of some importance, with imports valued at $2,720,59 in 1907.

## Institutions and Public Facilities
Bangor is the seat of three state institutions: the Eastern Maine insane hospital, the Eastern Maine general hospital, and the law school of the University of Maine. Additionally, the city is home to the Bangor Theological Seminary (Congregational). This seminary was incorporated in 1814, opened in Hampden in 1816, and moved to Bangor in 1819; it was empowered to confer degrees in divinity in 1905.

The city's infrastructure includes several public parks, a public library, and various charitable institutions, such as a deaconesses' home, a home for aged women, a home for aged men, and a children's home. Principal buildings in the city include the city hall, the opera house, the Federal building, and the county court house.

## Culture and Events
Bangor hosts two annual events: the Eastern Maine State Fair and the Eastern Maine Music Festival, the latter of which is held every October.
