# J.B. Gail and the Chair of Greek at the Collège de France

The chair of Greek at the Collège de France is a distinguished academic position that has been held by several prominent scholars. The institution itself originated as the Collegium Trilingue, which was founded by Francis I. after he was persuaded to do so by the French scholar Guillaume Budé and Jean du Bellay, the bishop of Narbonne.

## Succession of the Chair
In 1828, the chair of Greek at the Collège de France was held by J.B. Gail. He was succeeded in this position by Jean François Boissonade de Fontarabie.

## Jean François Boissonade de Fontarabie
Jean François Boissonade de Fontarabie (1774–1857) was a French classical scholar born in Paris on August 12, 1774. His career began in the public service in 1792 under the administration of General Dumouriez. Although he was driven from this service in 1795, he was later restored by Lucien Bonaparte, serving as the secretary to the prefecture of the Upper Marne.

Following his resignation from public employment, Boissonade devoted himself to the study of the Greek language. His academic progression included the following appointments:
*   **1809:** Deputy professor of Greek at the faculty of letters in Paris.
*   **1813:** Titular professor, following the death of P.H. Larcher.
*   **1828:** Professor in the chair of Greek at the Collège de France, succeeding J.B. Gail.

In addition to his professorship, Boissonade held the office of perpetual secretary of the Académie des Inscriptions and served as the librarian of the Bibliothèque du Roi. He died on September 8, 1857.

## Scholarly Contributions of Boissonade
Boissonade focused his scholarly attention primarily on later Greek literature. His published works include:
*   **Philostratus:** *Heroica* (1806) and *Epistolae* (1842).
*   **Marinus:** *Vita procli* (1814).
*   **Tiberius Rhetor:** *De Figuris* (1815).
*   **Nicetas Eugenianus:** *Drosilla et Charicles* (1819).
*   **Herodian:** *Partitiones* (1819).
*   **Aristaenetus:** *Epistolae* (1822).
*   **Eunapius:** *Vitae Sophistarum* (1822).
*   **Babrius:** *Fables* (1844).
*   **Tzetzes:** *Allegoriae Iliados* (1851).

He also produced a 24-volume *Collection of Greek Poets*. Furthermore, his works *Anecdota Graeca* (1829–1833) and *Anecdota Nova* (1844) are noted for their importance to Greek grammarians and Byzantine history.

## Institutional Context of the Collège de France
The Collège de France operated under different regulations than the university of Paris. While the university of Paris had undergone reforms that closed its doors to anyone who was not Catholic, the chairs of the Collège de France were not governed by those same university statutes. However, historical records indicate that public opinion regarding heresy was once so violent that Henry IV. refrained from appointing a Calvinist to a chair, despite the lack of statutory prohibition.
