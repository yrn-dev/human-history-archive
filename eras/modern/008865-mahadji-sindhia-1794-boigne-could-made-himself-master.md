# Benoit de Boigne and Mahadji Sindhia

## Early Career of Benoit de Boigne
Benoit de Boigne, Count, was born on March 8, 1751, in Chambery, Savoy, to a father who was a fur merchant. His early military experience began in 1768 when he joined the Irish Brigade in France. He later entered Russian service, during which time he was captured by the Turks. Driven by reports of the wealth of India, Boigne traveled to the region. After a brief period of service with the East India Company, he resigned his position.

## Military Service under Mahadji Sindhia
In 1784, Boigne joined Mahadji Sindhia with the specific objective of training Sindhia's troops in European methods of warfare. His value to the Mahratta army was demonstrated during the battles of Chaksana and Lalsot; while the rest of the Mahratta army suffered defeat by the Rajputs, Boigne and his two battalions successfully held the field.

Boigne's military contributions continued to elevate the fortunes of the Mahrattas. In 1788, he fought in the battle of Agra, where he restored Mahratta fortunes and established Mahadji Sindhia as the undisputed master of Hindostan. Following this success, Boigne was granted command of a brigade consisting of ten infantry battalions. He led this force to victories at Merta and Patan in 1790. As a result of these achievements, Boigne was appointed commander-in-chief of Sindhia's army and was permitted to raise two additional brigades of disciplined infantry. In 1793, he achieved a victory over Holkar's army at the battle of Lakhairi.

## The Transition of 1794
The year 1794 marked a significant turning point due to the death of Mahadji Sindhia. At this juncture, Boigne possessed sufficient power and influence that he could have made himself the master of Hindostan had he desired to do so. However, he chose not to seize power and instead remained loyal to Daulat Rao Sindhia.

## Later Life and Retirement
Boigne's active military career in India ended shortly after the transition of power. In 1795, his health began to decline, leading him to resign his command. He returned to Europe the following year, bringing with him a fortune of £400,000. During the era of Napoleon, Boigne lived in retirement, though he later received great honor from Louis XVIII. He died on June 21, 1830.
