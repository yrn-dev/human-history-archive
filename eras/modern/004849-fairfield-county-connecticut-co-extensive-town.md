# Fairfield County, Connecticut

Fairfield County is located in the south-west part of the state of Connecticut, U.S.A. It contains several significant municipalities, including the cities of Bridgeport and Danbury, as well as the township of Greenwich.

## Bridgeport
Bridgeport is a city, a port of entry, and serves as one of the county-seats of Fairfield County. It is co-extensive with the town of Bridgeport and is situated on Long Island Sound at the mouth of the Pequonnock river, approximately 18 miles south-west of New Haven. The city's population grew from 27,643 in 1880 to 48,866 in 1890, reaching 70,996 by 1900 and 102,054 by 1910. Of the 1900 population, 22,281 were foreign-born, including 5,974 from Ireland, 3,172 from Hungary, 2,854 from Germany, 2,755 from England, and 1,436 from Italy.

The city is served by the New York, New Haven & Hartford railway, as well as coast steamers and steamers traveling to Port Jefferson and New York City. Its harbor is formed by Yellow Mill Pond and the river estuary. East Bridgeport is a peninsula located between these two bodies of water and houses some of the city's largest manufacturing establishments. The main portion of the city lies west of the river and harbor, featuring a wholesale section along the bank and a retail section further back, with numerous factories extending westward along the railway line.

Notable landmarks include Beardsley Park in the extreme north and Seaside Park along the Sound. Seaside Park contains a soldiers' and sailors' monument and statues of P.T. Barnum—a showman who lived in the city after 1846 and contributed significantly to East Bridgeport—and Elias Howe, who established a large sewing-machine factory in 1863. Principal buildings include the Bridgeport and St Vincent's hospitals, the Protestant orphan asylum, the United States government building (housing the customs house and post-office), and the Barnum Institute, which is used by the Bridgeport Medical Society and the Bridgeport Scientific and Historical Society.

## Danbury
Danbury is another city and county-seat of Fairfield County, located in Danbury township on the Still river, a tributary of the Housatonic. The city's population was 16,552 in 1890, 16,537 in 1900 (with 3,702 foreign-born), and 20,234 in 1910. The total population of the township was 19,474 in 1900 and 23,502 in 1910.

Transportation is provided by the Danbury & Harlem electric railway (connecting to the New York Central at Goldens Bridge, New York), an electric line to Bethel, and three divisions of the New York, New Haven & Hartford railway. The city is known for the annual Danbury Fair and Lake Kenosia, a pleasure resort located about 2.5 miles from the city center. Educational and social facilities include a home for destitute and homeless children under private unsectarian control and a state normal school opened in 1904.

The municipality owns its water works and possesses good water-power. The primary industry is the manufacture of felt hats, which began in 1780. By 1905, this industry involved approximately thirty factories with a yearly product value of $5,798,107, representing 15.8% of all felt hats produced in the United States and 71.9% of the city's factory product value. In 1900, Danbury ranked first in the country for this industry, and second in 1905. Silver-plated ware, largely produced by Rogers Bros., is also a significant product.

## Greenwich
Greenwich is a township located in the extreme south-west of the state on Long Island Sound, about 28 miles north-east of New York City. It comprises a borough of the same name and the villages of Riverside, Sound Beach, and Cos Cob. The township has electric railway and steamboat connections to New York City and is served by the New York, New Haven & Hartford Railway. The township's population was 12,172 in 1900 (including 3,271 foreign-born) and 16,463 in 1910; the borough's population was 3,886 in 1910.

Greenwich serves as a summer resort, primarily for residents of New York. Notable residents have included the landscape painter John Henry Twachtman, Edwin Thomas Booth, and Henry Osborne Havemeyer (1847-1907), who founded the American Sugar Company. The borough contains a hospital, a public library, the Brunswick School for boys (founded 1901 and united with Betts Academy of Stamford in 1908), and the co-educational Greenwich Academy (founded 1827). In Sound Beach, the Rev. William H. H. Murray (1840-1904), author of *Camp Life in the Adirondack Mountains* (1868), once served as pastor.

The township's manufactures include iron and gasoline motors, woollens, belting, and tinners' hardware, and it ships oysters.

## History and Notable Figures
The township of Greenwich was first settled in 1640 by people from the New Haven Colony. However, the Dutch laid claim to the area following Adrian Blok's 1614 exploration of Long Island Sound. Due to a lack of assistance from New Haven, the settlers joined New Netherland in 1642, and Greenwich became a Dutch manor. A 1650 treaty fixed the boundary between the New Haven Colony and New Netherland, leading the Dutch to relinquish their claim. The inhabitants refused to submit to the New Haven Colony until October 1656, and six years later, Greenwich was among the first New Haven towns to submit to Connecticut. During the War of Independence, the township suffered from the invasion by the British and the frequent quartering of American troops.

In Danbury, the township was settled in 1684 by emigrants from Norwalk and named in 1687. During the War of Independence, resident Enoch Crosby is believed to be the original for the character Harvey Birch in J. F. Cooper's *The Spy*. In April 1777, Governor William Tryon of New York raided Danbury, destroying private property and a military supply depot established in 1776. During his retreat on April 26, Tryon was attacked at Ridgefield by Americans under General David Wooster (1710-1777).

Other notable figures from the county include Joel Barlow (1754-1812), an American politician and poet born in Redding. Barlow graduated from Yale in 1778 and served as a chaplain in a Massachusetts brigade during the revolutionary war. He was a member of the "Hartford Wits" and published *The Vision of Columbus* in 1887. His later career included serving as the agent for the Scioto Land Company in France, becoming a citizen of France in 1792, serving as American consul at Algiers (1795-1797), and acting as American plenipotentiary to France.
