# African Mainland and Related Geography

## Geography and Continental Proximity
The African mainland serves as a primary geographic reference point for various Atlantic island groups and coastal explorations. For instance, the Azores archipelago, which belongs to the kingdom of Portugal, is situated such that the nearest point on the African mainland, Cape Cantin, is more than 900 m. distant. In comparison, the Azores are closer to Cape da Roca on the Portuguese coast (830 m. east of St Michael's) than they are to the African mainland or the American headland of Cape Race in Newfoundland, the latter of which is more than 1000 m. away.

## Exploration of the West Coast
During the 15th century, the West African coast was the subject of extensive voyages, notably those of the Venetian navigator Alvise Cadamosto. Sailing in the service of Prince Henry the Navigator, Cadamosto embarked on an expedition on March 22, 1455. His travels took him along the West Sahara, where he documented trade-routes and tribes, eventually reaching the Senegal river, the lower course of which had already been explored by the Portuguese to a distance of 60 m.

Cadamosto's records provide detail on the "negro lands" south of the Senegal, including the people of Budomel, located approximately 50 m. beyond the river under a friendly chief. He further explored the Gambia, though he encountered extreme hostility from the natives there. In 1456, during a second voyage under Prince Henry, Cadamosto doubled Cape Blanco and discovered the Cape Verde Islands (specifically Boavista and Santiago) after being driven out to sea by contrary winds. He subsequently returned to the African mainland to visit the Geba, Rio Grande, and the Gambia.

## Peoples and Cultures of the West Coast
Among the inhabitants of the West Coast of Africa are the Krumen (also known as Krooboys, Krus, or Croos). They reside in villages along the coast of Liberia, extending from below Monrovia to Cape Palmas. The Krumen are a robust, muscular race characterized by blue-black skin and woolly hair; the women are noted as being a lighter shade than negro women generally.

The Krumen are traditionally recognized as daring fishermen and skillful seamen, and were the first West African people to serve on European vessels. They are described as honest, brave, and proud, with a passionate devotion to freedom that has led some to starve or drown rather than be captured. Notably, they have never trafficked in slaves. 

Politically, the Krus are organized into small commonwealths led by hereditary chiefs who represent the people to strangers. Actual governance is held by elders, who are identified by iron rings worn on their legs. The head fetish-man serves as president and guardian of national symbols, and his home acts as a sanctuary for offenders. Both land and personal property are held communally by families.

## Fauna of the African Continent
The African mainland and its southern regions support a variety of distinct animal species:

*   **Antelopes:** The klipspringer is a small mountain-antelope with gold-spangled coloring and blunt rounded hoofs. Its range extends from the Cape through East Africa to Abyssinia and Somaliland.
*   **Zebras:** South Africa was home to the now-extinct true quagga, which featured dark stripes on a brown ground limited to the shoulders, neck, and head. The Burchell's zebra (or bonte-quagga) was more common in the great plains of South Africa, ranging from the Orange river to the borders of Abyssinia. In north-east Africa, the *E. burchelli granti* variety exhibits stripes that extend to the hoofs. Additionally, the mountain zebra is found in the mountain ranges of Cape Colony and in south-west Africa as *E. zebra penricei*.
*   **Primates:** The African pottos, along with the Indo-Malay lorises, comprise the section *Nycticebinae* of the family *Nycticebidae*.

## Southern African Urban Centers
In the Cape province of South Africa, the town of Kimberley serves as the center for the Griqualand West diamond industry. Located 4012 ft. above sea level on the bare veld between the Vaal and Modder Rivers, the town is 647 m. northeast of Cape Town and 310 m. southwest of Johannesburg by rail. 

Kimberley grew from camps surrounding diamond mines, resulting in an irregular town plan. Key landmarks include the market square, law courts, and the De Beers mine, which is located one mile east of the Kimberley mine. Other significant mines in the area include Wesselton, Du Toits Pan, and Bultfontein. The town also contains monuments and statues, including those of Cecil Rhodes and Queen Victoria, as well as a monument to those who fell during the siege of 1899-1900.
