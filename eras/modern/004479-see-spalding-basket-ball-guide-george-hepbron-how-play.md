# Basket-Ball

## Origins and Development
Basket-ball was invented in 1891 by James Naismith, who served as an instructor in the gymnasium of the Young Men's Christian Association training-school in Springfield, Massachusetts. The game was created to meet the demand for a gymnasium class activity that could replace baseball and football during the winter months, providing a way to break monotony with a game that was not too rough for indoor play. The initial concept of the game was first published in the school's paper, the *Triangle*. Following its invention, it became a highly popular indoor sport in America for both men and girls, eventually spreading to England and other regions.

## Court and Equipment
The game is typically played on a marked-off space measuring 40 ft. by 60 ft., although dimensions may be larger when played in the open air. The goal consists of a basket—a net suspended from a metal ring 18 in. in diameter—placed 10 ft. above the floor or ground in the middle of each short side. This ring is backed by a back-board measuring 6 ft. long and 4 ft. high, positioned at a distance of 6 in. from the ring. The ball used is leather-covered, inflated, and has a circumference of 30 in.

## Gameplay and Rules
The objective of basket-ball is to propel the ball into the opponents' basket by striking it with the open hands. A match consists of two periods of play separated by an interval of rest; the side that scores the most goals wins. 

The game begins with the two opposing centres standing within a 4-foot ring in the middle of the floor. The referee tosses the ball into the air over their heads, and the centres jump for possession or attempt to bat the ball toward the opposing goal. The ball remains in play until a foul is committed, it passes the boundary lines, or it falls into a basket. After a goal is scored, the referee puts the ball back in play at the centre.

If the ball is thrown across the boundary, a player from the opposing side stands on the line at the point of exit and puts the ball in play by throwing or passing it to a teammate in any direction. There is no off-side rule, a point of similarity to the game of lacrosse.

Movement with the ball is restricted; a player must throw the ball from the spot where they receive it. Running with the ball is prohibited unless the player is continuously bounding it on the floor.

## Scoring and Fouls
Points are awarded based on the nature of the goal:
*   **Two points:** A goal scored while the ball is in play.
*   **One point:** A goal scored from a "free throw."

A free throw is awarded to a player of the opposing side when a foul is called. The throw is taken from a mark 15 ft. from the basket without interference. Fouls include kicking, holding, striking, and hacking. However, a player is permitted to interfere with an opponent who possesses the ball provided they use only one arm and do not hold the opponent.

## Positions and Personnel
While there is no practical limit to the number of players on a side, indoor matches are played by teams of five. These positions mirror those in lacrosse:
*   Two guards (or backs), one right and one left.
*   Two forwards, one right and one left.
*   One centre.

The game is managed by several officials: a referee who provides general supervision and decides if goals are properly scored, an umpire who watches for rule infringements (fouls), a timekeeper, and a scorer.

## Player Qualifications and Variations
Basket-ball is characterized as an extremely fast game requiring a high degree of team-play and combination. The essential qualifications for a successful player include self-control, accuracy, endurance, coolness, and quickness of judgment and movement. Key requisites of the game include passing, throwing, dodging, and team-play. Due to these attributes, it is regarded as excellent winter training for outdoor games. The game is also extremely popular with young women, who play with somewhat modified rules.
