# Napoleon, Mathilde, and Napoleon Joseph Charles

## Napoleon I and the Imperial Era
Napoleon Bonaparte rose to power in France, becoming First Consul in 1799 with enormous powers and later declaring himself Emperor in 1804. His reign was characterized by a "new imperialism" that differed from traditional monarchies; while previous potentates believed they served God and were necessary to mankind, Napoleon viewed himself as an individual man prevailing over others. 

His military and political ambitions led to the overthrow of Prussia at Jena in 1806 and the end of the Holy Roman Empire that same year. Napoleon sought to establish a ring of allied states surrounding the French Empire, often placing family members on foreign thrones. In 1808, he made his brother Joseph the King of Spain. He also proclaimed his brother Louis as the King of Holland on June 6, 1806, though their relationship later deteriorated due to disputes over the continental system and Louis's perceived excessive popularity with his subjects. This conflict eventually led Napoleon to annex Holland to the French Empire on July 9, 1810. Napoleon's power eventually waned following his 1812 retreat from Moscow, leading to his abdication in 1814.

## Jerome Bonaparte and the Kingdom of Westphalia
Jerome Bonaparte (1784–1860), born in Ajaccio, was Napoleon's brother. After serving in the Consular Guard and the navy, Jerome traveled through the United States, where he married Elizabeth Patterson in Baltimore. This marriage annoyed Napoleon, who ordered Patterson to be excluded from his states. Following an imperial decree that annulled the Patterson marriage, Napoleon united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg.

Under the treaty of Tilsit (July 7, 1807), Jerome was raised to the throne of the new kingdom of Westphalia. While Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor for his debts, luxury, and numerous affairs, his rule brought certain benefits to the kingdom: the *Code Napoleon* was introduced, feudalism was abolished, and repressive laws against the Israelites (the Jews) were removed.

## Napoleon Joseph Charles (Prince Napoleon)
Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul (1822–1891), commonly known as Prince Napoleon or "Plon-Plon," was the second son of Jerome Bonaparte and Princess Catherine. Born in Trieste, he became known for his advanced democratic ideas. Following the French revolution of 1848, he was elected to the National Assembly as a representative of Corsica and assumed the name Jerome after the death of his elder brother in 1847.

Although he ostensibly opposed the *coup d'etat* of 1851, he was designated as the successor to the throne should Napoleon III die childless. Prince Napoleon served as a general of division during the Crimean campaign in 1854 and directed the National Exhibition of 1855. In 1858, he was appointed minister for the Colonies and Algeria. In January 1859, he married Princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy. During the war for the liberation of Italy, he commanded the French corps in Tuscany; however, he refused to pressure the inhabitants of the principality, who preferred union with the Italian kingdom. He was also recognized as a great orator in his later years.

## Mathilde Bonaparte
Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine (1820–1904) was the daughter of Jerome and the sister of Prince Napoleon. Born in Trieste on May 20, 1820, she was nearly betrothed to her cousin Louis Napoleon before marrying Prince Anatole Demidov in 1840. The marriage ended in separation within five years, and Tsar Nicholas ensured she received a handsome allowance.

After Louis Napoleon was elected president of the republic, Mathilde moved to Paris, where she managed the honors of the Elysee until his marriage. She remained in Paris until her death on January 2, 1904, where she exerted significant influence as a patron and friend to men of letters and art.

## Other Family Members
The Bonaparte family included several other notable figures. Joseph Bonaparte served as the King of Spain starting in May 1808, though his authority was largely titular and he often functioned as an instrument of military power. Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803–1857), the son of Lucien Bonaparte, was a distinguished scientist and zoologist who published works on ornithology and European fauna. He was also politically active in Italy, advocating for Italian independence and the expulsion of the Austrians before the failure of the 1849 revolution forced him to leave the country.
