# Stowe Manuscripts

The Stowe Manuscripts (Stowe MSS.) are a collection of historical documents and manuscripts. The purchase of these manuscripts was concluded during the tenure of Sir Edward Augustus Bond while he served as the principal librarian.

## Relation to the British Museum
The Stowe MSS. are associated with the British Museum, where Sir Edward Augustus Bond held several leadership roles. Bond began his career at the museum in 1838 as an assistant in the manuscript department. He later served as the Egerton librarian in 1852, assistant keeper of MSS. in 1856, and keeper in 1867. By the time he was appointed principal librarian in 1878, he oversaw the erection of the "White Wing" buildings, which provided space for newspapers, drawings, prints, and manuscripts.

## The Stowe 2 Manuscript
Among the collection is the MS. Stowe 2, which is identified as a "glossed Psalter." This specific manuscript represents the Gallican Psalter and is associated with Spelman's text. It is one of twelve known manuscripts of glossed Psalters from the 9th and 10th centuries.

The Stowe 2 manuscript contains Old English glosses. For example, in the first verse of Psalm c. (Vulg. xcix. 2), the Stowe 2 text reads: "Drymaeth drihtne, eall eorethe; etheowiaeth drihtne on blisse; infaraeth on gesyhethe hys on bliethnysse." This corresponds to the Roman text "Jubilate Deo, omnis terra; servite Domino in laetitia; intrate in conspectu eius in exultatione."

## Connection to Stowe Mansion
The manuscripts share a name with the mansion of Stowe, located near the market town and municipal borough of Buckingham in Buckinghamshire, England. The mansion is situated in an open valley on the upper part of the river Ouse and is characterized by gardens that are very beautifully laid out and a magnificent avenue of elms. Until the title became extinct in 1889, the mansion served as the seat of the dukes of Buckingham.
