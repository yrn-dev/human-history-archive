# Project Gutenberg

## Overview and Origins
Project Gutenberg is a library of electronic works designed to be shared freely with anyone. The concept for this library was originated by Professor Michael S. Hart. For a period of forty years, Professor Hart was responsible for the production and distribution of Project Gutenberg eBooks, operating with the assistance of a loose network of volunteers.

## eBook Production and Copyright
The eBooks provided by Project Gutenberg are frequently developed using several different printed editions. The project ensures that these editions are confirmed to be not protected by copyright within the United States, unless a copyright notice is present. Because of this process, the eBooks are not necessarily kept in compliance with any one specific paper edition.

## Donation Policies and Procedures
Project Gutenberg accepts donations to support its operations. These contributions can be made through various methods, including credit card donations, online payments, and checks. Information regarding current addresses and methods for donating is available on the Project Gutenberg web pages, and donors may visit www.gutenberg.org/donate.

## International Contributions and Legal Constraints
The organization maintains specific policies regarding the solicitation and receipt of funds:

*   **Solicitation:** Project Gutenberg does not solicit contributions from states where it has not met the necessary solicitation requirements.
*   **Unsolicited Gifts:** There is no known prohibition against the organization accepting unsolicited donations from donors in those states who approach the project with offers to donate.
*   **International Donations:** While international donations are gratefully accepted, the organization cannot provide statements regarding the tax treatment of donations received from outside the United States. This limitation exists because the project's small staff is overwhelmed by U.S. laws alone.

## Accessing the Archive
The primary point of entry for most users is the Project Gutenberg website. This site features the main search facility for the library and can be accessed at www.gutenberg.org.
