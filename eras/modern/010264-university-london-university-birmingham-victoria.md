# University of London, University of Birmingham, and the Victoria University of Manchester

## The University of London
The University of London is one of the few institutions in England that provided education on a university footing during a period of intellectual stagnation in the mid-19th century, alongside one institution in Durham. Its model of organization served as the basis for the constitution of Bombay University in 1857, which was established as an examining body.

The university has been associated with various academic achievements and faculty members. In 1853, Robert William Dale, an English Nonconformist divine, placed first in philosophy and won the gold medal in the London University M.A. examination. Additionally, Maximilian Otto Bismarck Caspari has served as a Reader in Ancient History at the institution. In the field of professional education, the London School of Economics, part of the University of London, is noted as one of the few universities in Great Britain to have treated the scientific study of book-keeping seriously and upon adequate lines.

## The University of Birmingham
The foundation of Birmingham University was significantly influenced by Mr. Joseph Chamberlain, whose active interest in the affairs of the town—including his tenure as mayor from 1873 to 1876—was a principal factor in the university's establishment. 

The institution has maintained a focus on commerce and finance. In 1901, William James Ashley was appointed as the professor of commerce and finance, and he subsequently became the dean of the faculty of commerce in 1902. Like the University of London, the University of Birmingham is recognized for treating the subject of book-keeping seriously and upon adequate lines. The university has also employed diverse academic staff, such as Maximilian Otto Bismarck Caspari, who served as a lecturer in Greek from 1905 to 1908.

## The Victoria University of Manchester
The Victoria University of Manchester is identified as one of the few universities in Great Britain that has adopted a serious and adequate approach to the curriculum of book-keeping.
