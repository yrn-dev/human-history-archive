# Ontario (1759-1790)

## Administrative Status and Territorial Association
Between 1759 and 1790, Ontario was associated with Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. During this period, the region was part of a broader British administrative context in North America. This era followed the 1759 capture of Quebec by the British general Wolfe and preceded the later formation of the Dominion of Canada in 1867.

## Regional Context and Related Territories
The broader region encompassing Ontario during the late 18th century included several other territories under British influence or control. New Brunswick had been ceded in 1713, and Prince Edward Island was conquered in 1758. To the east, the Bay of Fundy served as a geographic divider between Nova Scotia and New Brunswick. This body of water, first explored in 1604 by the Sieur de Monts and named La Baye Francaise, is noted for its extreme tidal variations, which can reach 62 feet at the head of the bay.

In the vicinity of Nova Scotia, the town and port of Louisburg served as a significant center for cod-fisheries under the French régime, where it was second only to Quebec in importance. The Louisburg fortress was captured by American colonists in 1745 and subsequently by a British land and sea force in 1758, led by Admiral Boscawen and General Jeffrey Amherst.

## North American Transitions
The period from 1759 to 1790 was marked by significant geopolitical shifts. Britain and France struggled for control of India and America between 1755 and 1763, a conflict known as the Seven Years' War. Following the Peace of Paris in 1763, Canada was ceded to Britain. 

During this time, other settlements were established in North America, such as Saskatchewan in 1766. By 1783, a Treaty of Peace was established between Britain and the newly formed United States of America.

## Related Geographic Designations
The term "Ontario" was also used in different geographic contexts during the late 18th century. In the United States, within the state of New York, the district of Erwin was located in Ontario county. In 1790, the first settlement of what would later become the city of Hornell was established within this district. This settlement was known as Upper Canisteo until 1820.
