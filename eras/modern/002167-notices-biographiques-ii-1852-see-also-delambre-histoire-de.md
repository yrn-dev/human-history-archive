# Notices biographiques, t. ii. (1852)

The publication *Notices biographiques*, t. ii. (1852), serves as a source for biographical information on various historical figures. One notable entry within this volume is a memoir by Arago concerning the life of Bailly.

## Jean-Sylvain Bailly
Bailly was a figure whose studies were interrupted by the cataclysm of the French Revolution. His public life was marked by several high-profile leadership roles: he was elected as a deputy from Paris to the states-general and was chosen as the president of the Third Estate on May 5, 1789. He is further noted for leading the proceedings in the Tennis Court on June 20 and serving as the mayor of Paris from July 15, 1789, to November 16, 1791.

Bailly's political standing shifted after he ordered the National Guard to disperse a riotous assembly in the Champ de Mars on July 17, 1791. This action made him obnoxious to the infuriated populace, leading him to retire to Nantes. During his time in Nantes, he composed *Memoires d'un temoin*, an incomplete narrative of his public life, which was later published in three volumes between 1821 and 1822 by MM. Berville and Barriere.

In late 1793, Bailly left Nantes to join his friend Pierre Simon Laplace at Melun. However, he was recognized and arrested there on November 10, subsequently being brought before the Revolutionary Tribunal in Paris. On November 12, he was guillotined while being insulted by a howling mob. He is described as having met his death with patient dignity, having shared the enthusiasms of his age disastrously, though he took no part in its crimes.
