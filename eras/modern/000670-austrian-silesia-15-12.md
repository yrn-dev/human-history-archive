# Austrian Silesia

Austrian Silesia was a territory of the Habsburg monarchy that became the central point of contention between Austria and Prussia during the mid-18th century. The region's transition from Austrian to Prussian control was a primary driver of the War of the Austrian Succession.

## Territorial Claims and Annexation
The conflict over Silesia was rooted in dynastic claims and treaties. In 1537, Frederick II, Duke of Liegnitz, Brieg, and Wohlau, entered into a treaty with Joachim II, Elector of Brandenburg, stipulating that his duchy should pass to the House of Brandenburg if his line became extinct. When the last duke, George William, died in 1675, Austria refused to recognize the treaty's validity and annexed the duchies. This annexation provided the basis for Frederick II of Prussia to assert his claims to the region in 1740.

## The Prussian Invasion of 1740
In December 1740, Frederick II invaded Silesia without a declaration of war. At the time, Prussia possessed a highly organized army of 100,000 men. While the Austrian forces were disorganized due to the long confusion of the Turkish wars and the lack of a standing army of sufficient size—Charles VI had failed to leave the 200,000-man army suggested by Prince Eugene—the Prussian infantry was drilled to a level of perfection that allowed them to fire five shots for every three fired by the Austrians.

Frederick's army concentrated on the Oder and overran Silesia almost unopposed. Austrian generals were only able to garrison a few fortresses before falling back to the mountain frontiers of Moravia and Bohemia. By the end of the initial campaign, the Prussians held all of Silesia and had invested the strongholds of Neisse, Brieg, and Glogau.

## The War of the Austrian Succession
The "Silesian adventure" expanded into the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748), involving numerous European powers. France, Bavaria, Spain, Saxony, and Sardinia sought to expand at Austria's expense, while England and Holland supported the cause of Maria Theresa.

### Military Campaigns (1741–1742)
In February 1741, the Austrians attempted to reconquer the province with a field army under Count Neipperg. While the Austrians maintained holds in Brieg and Neisse, the Prussians stormed Glogau on March 9, 1741. This period culminated in the Battle of Mollwitz on April 10, 1741. Despite the initial routing of the Prussian cavalry, the discipline of the Prussian infantry secured a victory. This battle signaled the rise of Prussia as a new military power and led France to negotiate an alliance with Frederick.

By 1742, the conflict shifted. While Frederick attempted forays into Moravia and towards Vienna, the Austrians and their allies fought to regain ground. Hungarian irregulars poured into Upper Silesia via the Jablunka Pass. Eventually, Frederick was outmaneuvered by the combined forces of Prince Charles and Marshal Traun, forcing a retreat from Bohemia back into Silesia with heavy losses, though the Austrians failed to establish a foothold within Silesia itself during that specific movement.

## Demographics and Political Identity
Silesia was characterized by a considerable German majority. This demographic makeup influenced its political alignment; because of the large Polish element and the German majority, the Silesian diet refused to recognize the claims of Bohemians who sought to restore the Bohemian monarchy to include Moravia and Silesia.

## Final Cession
The struggle for the province ended with the transfer of Silesia from Austria to Prussia. This cession was formalized by the Treaty of Dresden. The broader War of the Austrian Succession concluded with the Peace of Aix-la-Chapelle (Aachen) in 1748.
