# Oeuvres diverses (1701)

The *Oeuvres diverses*, published in 1701, was the final edition of the works of Boileau published during the poet's own lifetime. Within the study of French literature, this specific publication is recognized as the "favourite" edition of the poet.

## Later Reprints and Scholarly Editions
In 1894, Alphonse Pauly reprinted the *Oeuvres diverses* in two volumes, providing the text with notes and variants. 

Other efforts to establish the critical text of Boileau's works were undertaken by Berriat Saint-Prix, who published *Oeuvres de Boileau* in four volumes between 1830 and 1837. Saint-Prix utilized approximately 350 different editions to establish this text. This version was later edited with notes by Paul Cheron and reprinted by Garnier freres in 1860; this reprint also included an essay by Sainte-Beuve and the *Boloeana* of 1740.

## Literary Significance of Boileau
Boileau is regarded as the first figure in French literature to teach the value of workmanship for its own sake and to reduce the versification of the language to rule. His influence extended to English literature through contemporaries such as Pope, although this influence was less durable than his impact on French letters.

While some of his individual criticisms were characterized by undue harshness, recent writers have rehabilitated his critical work, noting that most of his criticisms were substantially adopted by those who succeeded him. Some scholars suggest that this rehabilitation may have reached the point of exaggeration.

## Personal Character
Boileau is described as a benevolent, honest, and outspoken man possessed of warm and kindly feelings. He was known for his generous actions and his frankness of speech while at court.
