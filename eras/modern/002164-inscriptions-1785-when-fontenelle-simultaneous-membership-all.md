# Jean Sylvain Bailly and the Academies

## Early Life and Scientific Career
Jean Sylvain Bailly was born in Paris on September 15, 1736. Although he was originally intended for a career as a painter, he first turned toward writing tragedies. He eventually shifted his focus to science under the influence of Nicolas de Lacaille. Bailly's early scientific contributions included the calculation of an orbit for the comet of 1759, known as Halley's comet, and the reduction of observations made by Lacaille regarding 515 zodiacal stars.

In 1763, Bailly was elected as a member of the Academy of Sciences. During this year, he presented a memoir to the Academy, which he later expanded into the 1766 publication *Essai sur la theorie des satellites de Jupiter*. He continued his work on this subject with a noteworthy dissertation published in 1771 titled *Sur les inegalites de la lumiere des satellites de Jupiter*.

## Literary Reputation and Academic Memberships
Bailly established a significant literary reputation through his *Eloges* of Leibnitz, Moliere, Corneille, Lacaille, and Charles V. These works were issued in collected forms in 1770 and 1790. His academic standing grew over the following decade; he was admitted to the French Academy on February 26, 1784. 

In 1785, Bailly was admitted to the Academie des Inscriptions. With this appointment, Bailly renewed a distinction previously held by Fontenelle: the simultaneous membership of all three Academies.

## Contributions to the History of Science
Following his admission to the various academies, Bailly dedicated himself to the history of science. He published several works on the subject, including:
* *Histoire de l'astronomie ancienne* (1775)
* *Lettres sur l'origine des sciences* (1777)
* *Lettres sur l'Atlantide de Platon* (1779)
* *Histoire de l'astronomie moderne* (published in three volumes between 1779 and 1782)
* *Traite de l'astronomie indienne et orientale* (1787)

While these works were noted for their erudition, they were also characterized by speculative extravagances.
