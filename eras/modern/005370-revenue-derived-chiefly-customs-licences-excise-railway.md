# Revenue from Customs, Licences, Excise, and Railways

In the administration of various states and protectorates, national revenue is frequently derived from a combination of customs duties, excise taxes, licences, and railway earnings. While the specific composition of these revenues varies by region, these mechanisms serve as primary tools for funding government expenditures, public works, and debt service.

## General Concepts of Revenue and Duties
In a legal and financial context, a "duty" is distinguished from a "tax" by the nature of its application. While a tax is typically levied on individuals—such as an income tax based on a person's earnings—a duty is levied on specific transactions, estates, or commodities. Examples of this include excise-duties, import-duties, and death-or-succession-duties.

## Regional Revenue Systems

### Protectorates and Colonies
In certain protectorates, such as those resembling British crown colonies, revenue is derived chiefly from excise, licences, customs, posts, telegraphs, and railway earnings. Additionally, native populations may be required to pay a hut tax. The completion of infrastructure, specifically the Uganda railway, has been noted to greatly increase both trade and subsequent revenue. For instance, revenue in one such protectorate rose from £64,275 in 1900-1901 to £461,362 in 1906-1907, the latter figure including railway earnings for the first time. In cases where revenue did not cover expenditures, deficiencies were addressed via grants-in-aid from the imperial exchequer.

In Burma, the principal items of the budget's revenue include excise, customs, railways, land revenue, and forests. Total revenue in Burma increased from Rs.7,04,36,240 in the year ending March 31, 1900, to Rs.9,65,62,298 in 1905.

### Latin American Republics
Revenue structures in various republics often rely heavily on import and export duties. In Bolivia, revenues are principally derived from fees and duties on imports, as well as excise taxes on sugar, tobacco, wines, and spirits. Other sources include mining taxes, export duties on coca and minerals (excluding silver), taxes on stock company profits, stamp taxes, postal and telegraph revenues, and fees for patents and licences. While Bolivia is sometimes called a "free-trade country," this is complicated by schedule rates of 10 to 40% ad valorem on imports, consular fees for invoices exceeding 200 bolivianos, and various consumption taxes.

At the departmental level in Bolivia, revenues are derived from land taxes, excise, tithes, mining grants, tolls, inheritance taxes, and stamp taxes. Municipal revenues are further supplemented by cemetery fees, lighting taxes, ground rents, leases, market taxes, licences, and taxes on skins and hides.

In Honduras, revenue is derived chiefly from customs and monopolies on tobacco, gunpowder, and spirits. Between 1901 and 1905, this revenue averaged approximately £265,000. Similarly, in another republic, about half of the national revenue is derived from customs, with the remainder provided mainly by stamps, railways, and monopolies on tobacco and salt.

## Historical and Imperial Perspectives

### The Roman Empire
Historically, the Roman Republic and Empire utilized customs and excise as principal branches of revenue. During the early years of Nero's reign, there was a desire to abolish these "oppressions" to gain popularity. However, senators advised against this, arguing that such a move would dissolve the resources and strength of the republic. Later governors, including Trajan and the Antonines, focused on alleviating the public burden through precise laws that protected subjects from arbitrary interpretations and the "insolent vexation" of revenue farmers.

### Imperial Trade and Tariffs
Within the British Empire, discussions at the 1887 Colonial Conference in London proposed a preferential system that would impose a 2% duty on all foreign goods to maintain the imperial navy. By 1897, Canada introduced an imperial tariff that granted a 25% deduction on customs duties for British goods without requiring reciprocal advantages. This preferential treatment was later adopted by New Zealand, Australia, and various South African states.

## Expenditure and Debt
Revenues are typically allocated toward official salaries, justice, public instruction, and military expenses. In Bolivia, appropriations for 1905 included 2,081,119 bolivianos for war and 2,021,428 for government and fomento. In other regions, significant portions of revenue are dedicated to the service of internal and external debts. For example, in Honduras, the foreign debt reached £22,470,510 by July 1909, much of which was principal borrowed between 1867 and 1870 for railway construction.
