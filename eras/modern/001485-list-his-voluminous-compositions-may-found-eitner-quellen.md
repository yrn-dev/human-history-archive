# Eitner's Quellen Lexikon

The *Quellen Lexikon* (also referred to as *Quellen-Lexikon der Tonkunstler*), authored by Robert Eitner, serves as a reference for the compositions and identities of various musical figures.

## Documentation of Wilhelm Friedermann Bach
The *Quellen Lexikon* provides a complete list of the known works of Wilhelm Friedermann Bach (1710–1784). Bach was the eldest son of Johann Sebastian Bach and was widely regarded as the most gifted among the four sons who became musicians. He was recognized as a complete master of counterpoint, a famous improvisor, and a famous organist. 

Despite his talent, Bach's career was marked by wasted opportunities due to his dissolute and idle habits. His professional history includes an appointment in 1733 as the organist of the Sophienkirche in Dresden, followed by a role in 1747 as the musical director of the Liebfrauenkirche at Halle. He was forced to resign from the latter position in 1764, after which he lived a wandering life. He died in Berlin on July 1, 1784, in a state of great poverty.

The compositions of Wilhelm Friedermann Bach, few of which were printed, consist of instrumental works and many church cantatas. His most notable works include:
* Fantasias, polonaises, and fugues for clavier.
* A sestet for horns, clarinet, and strings.

While the *Quellen Lexikon* lists his works, several of his manuscripts are preserved in the Royal library at Berlin. Additionally, a critical account of his compositions is available in Bitter's *C. P. E. und W. F. Bach und deren Bruder* (Berlin, 1868), which is described as a source of valuable, though ill-arranged, information.

## Other References in the Quellen Lexikon
The *Quellen-Lexikon der Tonkunstler* is also utilized as a source for identifying other musical figures. Specifically, it is cited in relation to Gioseffo Ferlendis, alongside other references such as Gerber's *Lexikon*, Henri Lavoix's *Histoire de l'instrumentation*, and the writings of Pohl and Fetis.
