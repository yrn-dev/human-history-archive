# BRIAND, ARISTIDE

## Early Life and Political Beginnings
Aristide Briand (1862- ) was a French statesman born in Nantes to a bourgeois family. He pursued studies in law and entered the political arena at a young age. During his early career, he aligned himself with the most advanced political movements. This period of his life was marked by his contributions to various publications; he wrote articles for the anarchist journal *Le Peuple* and served as the director of the *Lanterne* for a period of time. He later moved to the *Petite République*, which he eventually left to co-found the publication *L'Humanité* alongside Jean Jaurès.

## Labour Unions and Socialist Leadership
Briand played a prominent role in the movement to establish labour unions. In 1894, during a congress of working men held in Nantes, he was instrumental in securing the adoption of the labour union idea, prevailing over those who adhered to the views of Jules Guesde. Following this success, Briand emerged as one of the primary leaders of the French Socialist party.

## Parliamentary Career and the "Bloc"
After several unsuccessful attempts, Briand was elected as a deputy in 1902. Once in the chamber of deputies, he positioned himself as a strong partisan of the union of the Left, a coalition known as the *Bloc*. His objective in supporting this union was to check the influence of the reactionary deputies of the Right.

## Separation of Church and State
A central focus of Briand's early parliamentary career was the question of the separation of church and state. He was appointed as the reporter of the commission responsible for preparing the relevant law. His report on the matter was described as masterly and established him as a rising leader. Briand was the principal author of the law of separation and successfully guided the project through the legislative process with only slight modifications, managing to do so without dividing the parties that supported him.

## Ministerial Office and Political Shift
Briand sought not only to prepare the law of separation but also to oversee its application. This desire was prompted by the Rouvier ministry, which he believed allowed disturbances to occur during the taking of inventories of church property (a specific clause of the law for which Briand was not responsible). Consequently, in 1906, he accepted the portfolio of public instruction and worship within the Sarrien ministry.

While his efforts were successful within the chamber, his decision to accept a portfolio in a bourgeois ministry resulted in his exclusion from the Unified Socialist party in March 1906. This move highlighted a ideological divide between Briand and Jean Jaurès. Briand contended that Socialists should not stand aloof waiting for the complete fulfillment of their ideals, but should instead co-operate actively with the Radicals on all matters of reform.
