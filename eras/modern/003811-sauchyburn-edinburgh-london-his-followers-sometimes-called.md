# John Barclay (1734-1798)

## Early Life and Education
John Barclay was a Scottish divine born in Perthshire. He pursued his higher education at St Andrews, where he graduated. Following his graduation, he was licensed and began his ecclesiastical career as the assistant to the parish minister of Errol, located in Perthshire.

## Ministerial Career and Conflict
Barclay's tenure in Errol ended in 1763 due to differences with the parish minister. Following his departure, he was appointed as the assistant to Antony Dow in the parish of Fettercairn, Kincardine. While Barclay achieved significant popularity within this parish, his opinions did not satisfy his presbytery.

This tension culminated in 1772 when the presbytery rejected Barclay as the successor to Antony Dow. Furthermore, the presbytery refused to provide him with the testimonials necessary to secure another living. This refusal was subsequently sustained by the General Assembly.

## Foundation of Independent Congregations
As a result of the General Assembly's decision, Barclay left the Scottish church. He proceeded to establish his own congregations in three locations: Sauchyburn, Edinburgh, and London.

## The Bereans
The followers of John Barclay were sometimes referred to as Bereans. This name was derived from Acts xvii. 11, reflecting the group's practice of regulating their conduct through the diligent study of the Scriptures. In terms of their theological beliefs, the Bereans held a modified form of Calvinism.

## Death
John Barclay died in Edinburgh.
