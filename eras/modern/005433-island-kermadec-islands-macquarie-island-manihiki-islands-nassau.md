# Islands of the Pacific and Atlantic: Kermadec, Macquarie, Manihiki, and Nassau

## Kermadec Islands
The Kermadec Islands are a small group of hilly islands located in the Pacific Ocean at approximately 30° S. and 178° W. They were named in 1791 after Huon Kermadec, who served as captain under the French navigator Joseph-Antoine Bruni d'Entrecasteaux. The islands are British possessions and were annexed in 1887, making them politically dependent on the colony of New Zealand.

The largest island in the group is Raoul (also known as Sunday Island), which has a circumference of 20 miles and reaches a height of 1,600 feet. Raoul Island is characterized by thick woodland. The fauna and flora of the Kermadec group largely correspond to those found in New Zealand. Zoogeographers, including Professors Huxley and Newton, categorize the Kermadec Islands as satellites of the New Zealand Subregion. This subregion is noted for its extreme isolation and a history of specialized wildlife, including the now-extinct gigantic moas (Dinornithes) and the unique kiwis (Apteryges).

## Macquarie Island and Manihiki Islands
Macquarie Island is identified as part of the New Zealand Subregion, serving as one of the satellite groups alongside the Kermadec, Norfolk, and Lord Howe islands. Like other islands in this subregion, its avifauna maintains main affinities with Australia.

The Manihiki Islands are listed as one of the various Pacific islands under British possession, appearing alongside other territories such as the Cook Islands, the Phoenix Group, and the Palmyra Island.

## Nassau
The name Nassau refers to two distinct locations within the archive's records: a capital city in the Bahamas and a fort in the Banda Islands.

### Nassau, Bahamas
Nassau is the capital of the Bahamas, an archipelago in the British West Indies consisting of an estimated 29 islands, 661 cays, and 2,387 rocks. Nassau is situated on the north shore of New Providence, the most important island of the archipelago. New Providence measures 19-3/8 miles in length with an area of 85 square miles; as of 1901, it supported a population of 12,534. The town of Nassau is located on a safe harbor sheltered by Hog Island, with a bar depth of 14 feet at low-water spring-tide.

The town features a slightly elevated ridge behind the shore and contains various public buildings and old forts from the late 18th century, including Fort Charlotte. A former gaol from the same century now serves as a public library. Nassau is a popular winter health resort for visitors from Canada and the United States, particularly during February and March, with direct steamer connections to Miami and New York.

Historically, Nassau experienced a period of extreme extravagance and waste between 1861 and 1865 due to the blockade-running trade caused by the closing of southern American ports. In January and February 1865 alone, 20 steamers arrived at Nassau with 14,182 bales of cotton valued at £554,675. While this trade allowed the government to pay a debt of £43,786, it also led to an increase in crime and the prevalence of sickness. Following the end of this trade, the island was struck by a violent hurricane in October 1866, which destroyed fruit trees and damaged the profitable sponge industry.

### Nassau, Banda Islands
In the Dutch East Indies, Nassau is the name of one of two Dutch forts (the other being Belgica) located in the chief town of Banda, situated on the island of Neira.
