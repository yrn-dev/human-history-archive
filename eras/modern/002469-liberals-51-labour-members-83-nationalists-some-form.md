# Political Factions and Nationalist Movements

## British Parliamentary Dynamics
In the early 20th century, the Unionist party found itself in a "miserable minority" within the British parliament, facing an opposition composed of 379 Liberals, 51 Labour members, and 83 Nationalists, while the Unionists numbered 157. This imbalance necessitated a consolidation of Unionist forces. Negotiations between Mr. Chamberlain and Mr. Balfour led to an agreement, confirmed at a meeting at Lansdowne House, which established "fiscal reform" as the party's primary constructive work. The goals of this reform were to secure closer commercial union with colonies and more equal competition terms for British trade. Balfour indicated that while specific methods were not yet prescribed, the imposition of a small duty on foreign corn and a moderate general tariff on manufactured goods—provided they were not used to raise prices or provide artificial protection—were not objectionable in principle.

## Liberalism in Britain
Herbert Henry Asquith was a prominent figure within the Liberal party, coming from a middle-class Yorkshire family with Nonconformist and Liberal views. After a distinguished career at Balliol, Oxford, where he served as president of the Union, Asquith entered the bar and later the House of Commons, winning a seat for East Fife in 1886. His oratorical skills and political judgment attracted Mr. Gladstone, who selected Asquith in August 1892 to move the vote of want of confidence that overthrew Lord Salisbury's government. As home secretary in the subsequent Liberal ministry, Asquith appointed women as factory inspectors and opened Trafalgar Square for Labour demonstrations.

## Nationalist Movements in Ireland
Isaac Butt, an Irish lawyer and Nationalist leader, transitioned from a Protestant Conservative background to a more liberal outlook. Following the disestablishment of the Irish Church, Butt and other Irish Protestants joined with Nationalists who repudiated the English connection. On May 19, 1870, Butt inaugurated the Home Rule movement in Dublin, demanding a parliament for local Irish affairs. By 1871, he led an Irish Home Rule party of 57 members, though his leadership eventually became a nullity due to his dislike of "obstruction" in parliament and the aggressive nature of his followers.

## Political Shifts in Austria and Hungary
In Austria, the political landscape saw the decline of the Liberals. In Vienna, by the time of the 1897 elections, Liberals were largely replaced by Socialists; for example, in Lower Austria, the Liberal representation dropped from 33 out of 37 members in 1879 to a significant disappearance. The 1897 elections resulted in a House that made strong government impossible, featuring anti-Semitic parties and various nationalist factions. These included the German Nationalists, the German Popular party, and the German Radicals, all of whom prioritized questions of nationality. Additionally, the Christian Socialists, led by Dr. Lueger, gained influence in Vienna, though Lueger's nationalist program was primarily directed against Hungarians and Jews.

In Hungary, the Liberal party had been the mainstay of dualism since 1867, but it faced growing "extreme nationalist Magyar spirit." This was evident in demands for the Magyarization of the joint Austro-Hungarian army. In October 1903, Count Stephen Tisza formed a cabinet based on concessions to Magyar nationalist feeling. These concessions included the establishment of military scholarships for Magyar youths, the two-year service system, and the transfer of Hungarian officers from Austrian to Hungarian regiments. While the "programme of the nine" asserted the legal influence of parliament over military prerogatives, the Liberal party adopted an addendum recognizing the king's right to fix the language of service and command in the Hungarian army.

## Nationalist Struggles in Chile
In Chile, Jose Miguel Carrera emerged as a principal leader in the fight for independence. Through a series of coup d'etats, he placed himself at the head of the nationalist government. While he initially worked to organize Chilean forces and promote education, his rivalry with Bernardo O'Higgins led to the defeat of nationalist forces at Rancagua in 1814. Carrera was not part of the 1817 expedition that liberated Chile. He later attempted to organize a rebellion in Argentina after being blocked from returning to Chile by the Argentine government, but he was captured and shot at Mendoza on September 4, 1821.

## Revolutionary Unrest in Prague and Vienna
On March 11, "young Czechs" in Prague drafted a petition containing liberal and nationalist demands. Simultaneously, the diet of Lower Austria petitioned the crown regarding finances. This period was marked by extreme volatility; in Vienna, a mob of students and workmen invaded the diet, adopting Kossuth's speech as a popular programme. The resulting riots evolved into a revolution, forcing Metternich to escape the Hofburg and go into exile in England on March 13.
