## Context within Bacteriology
The research by Sturgis is categorized among studies focusing on the reproductive bodies of Schizomycetes. In the broader study of bacteria, spore-formation is recognized as a characteristic method of reproduction. These minute reproductive bodies serve as a resting stage for the organism. 

The archive distinguishes between two types of spores: arthrospores and endospores. An arthrospore is not considered a true spore, but rather a vegetative cell that separates and enters a state of rest. In contrast, a true spore, or endospore, begins as a minute granule in the protoplasm of a vegetative cell. This granule enlarges over several hours, absorbing all the protoplasm and secreting a resistive, thin envelope to become a ripe ovoid spore that lies loosely within the mother-cell.

## General Properties of Bacterial Spores
The study of organisms like *B. megatherium* contributes to the general understanding of Schizomycetes spores. These spores are typically:
*   **Shape and Size:** Spherical, ovoid, or long-ovoid. They are extremely minute; for instance, those of *Bacillus subtilis* are recorded as 0.0006 mm broad by 0.0012 mm long.
*   **Appearance:** They are highly refractive and colourless, or very dark, which is attributed to their minute size and high index of refraction.
*   **Structure:** The membrane of the spore may be relatively thick and can exhibit strata or shells.

The process of spore formation usually takes place in a bacillar segment, and typically only one spore is formed per cell. In certain instances, the protoplasm involved in spore-formation produces a blue reaction when treated with iodine solutions.
