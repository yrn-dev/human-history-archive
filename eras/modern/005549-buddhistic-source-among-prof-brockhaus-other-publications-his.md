# Hermann Brockhaus and the Study of Buddhistic Sources

## Academic Career and Specialization
Hermann Brockhaus (1806–1877) was a German Orientalist born in Amsterdam on January 28, 1806. Unlike his two brothers, who managed the family business, Brockhaus pursued an academic path. His professional trajectory included an appointment as an extraordinary professor in Jena in 1838. In 1841, he received a similar call to Leipzig, where he eventually became an ordinary professor of ancient Semitic in 1848. He remained at Leipzig until his death on January 5, 1877.

Brockhaus operated as an Oriental scholar in the traditional sense, seeking a broad familiarity with the primary literatures and languages of the East rather than focusing on a single tongue. His linguistic capabilities included the study of Arabic, Persian, and Hebrew. He was further qualified to lecture on Zend, Chinese, Pali, and Sanskrit, the latter of which became his specialty.

## Contributions to the Study of Popular Tales
The most significant work produced by Brockhaus was the *editio princeps* of the *Katha-sarit-sagara*, translated as "The Ocean of the Streams of Story." This work is a substantial collection of Sanskrit stories compiled by Soma Deva during the 12th century.

This publication is credited with providing the first impetus for a scientific approach to studying how popular tales originate and spread. Through this work, Brockhaus enabled other scholars, such as Prof. Benfey, to trace a great bulk of stories from both the West and the East back to an Indian source, and more specifically, to a Buddhistic source.

## Other Publications
In addition to his work on the *Katha-sarit-sagara*, Professor Brockhaus authored several other notable editions:
*   **Prabodhachandrodaya**: An edition of this "curious philosophical play," which translates to "The Rise of the Moon of Intelligence."
*   **Songs of Hafiz**: A critical edition of these poems.
*   **Zend-Avesta**: A publication of the text using Latin letters.
