# Cellular Morphology and Structure

The morphology of cells varies significantly across different biological forms, ranging from minute single-celled organisms to complex epithelial membranes and specialized structures in higher organisms.

## Schizomycetes: Forms and Dimensions
The Schizomycetes consist of single cells or groups of cells that may be filamentous, depending on whether their divisions are completed immediately. These cells exhibit a wide range of sizes; some unicellular forms are less than 1 micron (0.001 mm) in diameter, while others measure 4, 5, 7, or 8 microns in thickness. The length of these cells can vary from the diameter to many times that measurement.

The shape of an individual cell in this group varies from a minute sphere to a cylinder or filament that may be straight, curved, or twisted. These cells are not necessarily uniform in diameter throughout and may possess rounded, flattened, or pointed ends. Based on their shape, they are categorized as follows:
*   **Cocci**: Spherical or spheroidal cells, further classified by size as *Micrococci*, *Macrococci*, and *Monas* forms.
*   **Rods or Rodlets**: Elongated cells that may be biscuit-shaped, cylindrical, or fusiform. Short cylindrical forms are known as *Bacterium*, while longer ones are *Bacillus*. Spindle-shaped forms include *Clostridia*.
*   **Filaments**: Elongated cylindrical cells that remain united end to end after division. These may be simple (e.g., *Leptothrix*) or exhibit false branching (e.g., *Cladothrix*).

## Growth, Division, and Colony Formation
Regardless of their initial shape, the visible results of active nutrition in these cells are elongation and division into two equal halves across the long axis via the formation of a septum. If the cells separate immediately, they maintain the character of the parent cell. If they remain intact, they form filaments. When elongation and division occur in multiple directions, they produce plates or spheroidal colonies.

While the general rule is that cells divide in only one direction (transverse to the long axis), rarer cases of iso-diametric cells divide in two or three directions, creating irregular, flat, or spheroidal colonies of practically unlimited size. In some filamentous forms, "fragmentation" occurs, where multicellular pieces of nearly equal length break away.

## Cell Wall and Membrane
The bacterial cell is enclosed by a definite cell-membrane. Unlike the cell-walls of higher plants, this membrane generally does not react to cellulose, nor does it contain chitin. Instead, it consists of a proteid substance that appears to be a modification of general protoplasm, although *B. tuberculosis* contains a large amount of cellulose. 

In certain instances, the cell-wall swells into a gelatinous mass, creating a clear, transparent space around the cell. When this swollen wall is regular and dense, it is referred to as a "capsule." When vegetative cells become fixed in a matrix of these swollen contiguous cell-walls, they enter a resting state known as zoogloea.

## Spores of Schizomycetes
Spore-formation is a characteristic reproductive method where minute bodies represent a resting stage. A true spore (endospore) begins as a minute granule in the protoplasm of a vegetative cell, which eventually secretes a resistive envelope to become a ripe ovoid spore smaller than the mother cell. 

The ripe spores of Schizomycetes are colorless, highly refractive, and can be spherical, ovoid, or long-ovoid. For example, spores of *Bacillus subtilis* measure 0.0006 mm broad by 0.0012 mm long. The spore membrane may be relatively thick and exhibit strata or shells. Some forms, such as *Micrococcus*, are provisionally called "asporous" as no definite spores have been discovered in them.

## Epithelial Cell Classification
In epithelial membranes, cells are classified by their shape:
*   **Columnar Epithelium**: These cells cover the main part of the intestinal tract (from the end of the oesophagus to the start of the rectum) and line the ducts of many glands.
*   **Cubical Epithelium**: These cells are shorter in height than columnar cells and are found in the middle ear, the choroid plexuses of the brain, and various ducts and glands, such as the kidney.
*   **Squamous or Flattened Epithelium**: These cells are very thin, irregular in outline, and flattened. They occur in the kidney capsule, kidney glomerules, and the covering epithelium of the lung's alveoli.

These cells may form membranes that are only one cell thick, as seen in much of the alimentary canal, or multiple layers, as seen in the epidermis of the skin. When viewed from above, the close apposition of these cells creates a mosaic appearance where each cell area has a polyhedral shape.
