# Hydrographic Systems of Southern Baluchistan

## Overview of Regional Drainage
In the region south-west of the dividing railway lies the great block of Southern Baluchistan. The drainage within this area generally trends toward the west and south, flowing either into the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora or into the Arabian Sea. Several considerable streams serve as important arteries for internal communication in this network, including the Dasht and the Hingol (known anciently as the *Tomerus*), which drain Makran, as well as the Purali (the ancient *Arabus*), which drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela, and the Hab river, which forms the boundary to the west of Karachi.

## The Mulla River System
An exception to the general drainage patterns of the region is found in the Mulla. This river carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin. Due to its geography, the Mulla forms one of the most significant ancient highways leading from the Indus plains to Kandahar.

The Mulla rises among the rugged bands of Jalawan. To reach the Gandava plain, the river breaks through a series of magnificent defiles. The fortress of Kalat is positioned approximately midway between the sources of the Mulla and the Bolan. This fortress sits on the western edge of a cultivated plain surrounded by hills, at an elevation of about 6,800 feet above sea-level, near a small tributary of the Lora (the river of Quetta and Pishin).

## The Kalat Hub and Surrounding Geography
Kalat serves as a "hub" or center from which several affluents radiate, specifically the Mulla, the Bolan, and the southern Lora affluents. To the north of this center are the Sarawan ridges, which enclose narrow fertile valleys and extend south-west toward the edge of the Kharan desert. Routes converging on Kalat from the south typically follow the general strike of denuded hill ridges, running from valley to valley through narrow wooded valleys with easy grades.

While the Mulla and Bolan radiate from the hub, the Lora also drains the Pishin valley to the north. These two systems eventually unite in Shorawak, where they lose themselves in the swamps and desert located to the west of Nushki on the road to Seistan.

## Southern Hydrographic Systems
Beyond the sources of the Mulla, sixty miles south of Kalat, another remarkable hydrographic system commences. This system encompasses all of south-western and southern Baluchistan. To the west of this area lies the Kharan desert, characterized by intermittent river channels that are frequently enclosed or lost.
