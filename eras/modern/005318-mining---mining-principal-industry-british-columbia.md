# Mining

Mining is a global industrial activity involving the extraction of minerals, metals, and fuels. Its development has been characterized by a transition from human labor to mechanical power, as modern civilization has been rebuilt upon cheap mechanical power while labor has become dearer.

## Mining in British Columbia
Mining serves as the principal industry of British Columbia. The region possesses deposits of iron and is rich in coal, lead, copper, silver, and gold. In terms of output value, the principal minerals are gold, copper, coal, lead, and silver. The industry has seen significant growth; from 1894 to 1904, mining output rose from $4,225,717 to $18,977,359, reaching $22,460,295 by 1905.

The history of mining in the province is divided between placer and lode mining. Between 1858—the year of placer discoveries in the Cariboo district and on the Fraser river—and 1882, placer yields were heavy, ranging from one to nearly four million dollars annually, though quartz mining was nonexistent during this time. While placer mining increased again after 1899, the majority of returns have come from lode mining. Chief centers of vein-mining include the Kootenay, Boundary, and Rossland districts, which yield cupriferous and auriferous sulphide ores and silver-bearing lead ores. Additionally, precious metals and copper are worked and prospected on Vancouver Island and along the coast.

The industry is supported by modern machinery and liberal mining laws based on the experience of Western States. Key concentrating plants and smelting and reducing plants are located in the Boundary district (Greenwood and Grand Forks) and the West Kootenay country (Nelson and Trail). Transport is facilitated by the Canadian Pacific railway, which has opened extensive coal fields and crosses a productive mining district via a line running to Midway in the Boundary district. On Vancouver Island, the Esquimalt & Nanaimo railway connects coal fields with southern ports.

## Other North American Mining Centers
In the United States, Butte, Montana, is situated in the center of the world's greatest copper-mining district. The surrounding hills are honey-combed with mines, including the well-known Anaconda mine. The annual value of silver, gold, and copper in the Butte district exceeds $60,000,000, with copper output nearly equaling that of the rest of the country combined. The city supports allied industries such as smelting and quartz crushing, and is home to the Montana School of Mines.

In the Klondike, gold is the primary economic product, though lignite coal has been mined lower on the Yukon and small amounts of tin ore exist. Unlike other placer regions, the Klondike has not developed lode mines because no important quartz veins have been discovered; the gold originates from small stringers of quartz in sericitic and siliceous schists.

## International Mining Operations
### Belgium
The principal mineral produced in Belgium is coal, found in the neighbourhood of Liege and the Borinage district near Mons. In 1906, work began on a new coal-field in the Campine district of the province of Limburg. Belgian coal mines employ nearly 150,000 people, with an average output exceeding 22,000,000 tons. Other minerals include zinc, lead, manganese, and iron. However, iron production has declined, necessitating the annual import of approximately L5,000,000 sterling worth of iron, primarily from French Lorraine.

### India
In the District of Kolar, gold prospecting began in 1876. The region hosts mines operated by the Nandidrug, Ooregum, Champion Reef, and Mysore companies. By the end of 1904, the total value of gold produced was 21 million sterling, with one million paid in royalty to the Mysore state and 9 million paid in dividends. These mines utilize electricity from the falls of the Cauvery as motive power.

### Orange River Colony
In the north-western parts of the Orange River Colony, the town of Kroonstad serves as a center for the coal and diamond-mining industry. A railway line running north-west from Kroonstad to Klerksdorp passes the coal mines at Vierfontein and the Lace diamond mine.
