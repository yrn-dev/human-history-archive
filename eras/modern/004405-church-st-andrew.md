## Historical Conversions in Japan
In the Arima fief of Japan, missionary records describe a period of rapid Christian conversion during the 16th century. In 1576, the Arima baron accepted baptism and became known in these records as "Prince Andrew." 

Following his baptism, Prince Andrew converted the chief temple of his capital into a church. He assigned the temple's revenues to support the missionaries and the maintenance of the building. He further sought to establish similar conversions in other towns within his fief, aiming to eliminate idolatry in his states. During the two years Prince Andrew survived after his baptism, twenty thousand converts were made in Arima. However, his successor was a believer in Buddhism and subsequently ordered the destruction of the Christian churches and the removal of crosses, while requiring converts to return to Buddhism.
