# Parto de Tregna of the 4th of April 1884

The Parto de Tregna of the 4th of April 1884 was a diplomatic instrument central to a long-standing dispute between Chile and Bolivia. The conflict concerned the Chilean occupation of the former Bolivian provinces of Arica and Tacna.

## Diplomatic Challenges and Negotiations
Arranging a satisfactory resolution to the dispute under the Parto de Tregna proved difficult. By 1895, there appeared to be a possibility of a resolution in which Chile might concede an outlet on the sea to Bolivia. In exchange, Bolivia would recognize Chilean ownership of the provinces of Arica and Tacna.

However, this conciliatory attitude from Chile changed following the discovery of secret negotiations conducted between Argentina and Bolivia. This development shifted the diplomatic landscape, leaving Bolivia in a position where it could neither compel Chile to make concessions nor venture into hostilities. The situation for Bolivia was further complicated when the boundary dispute between Chile and Argentina reached a final settlement, which removed the hope that Bolivia could obtain Argentine support.

## The Treaty of 1905
Recognizing the necessity of yielding to reach a settlement, President Pando and his successor, Ismail Montes (who assumed the presidency in 1904), sought the best possible terms. This resulted in a treaty ratified in 1905.

Under the terms of the 1905 treaty, Bolivia was compelled to cede the maritime provinces that Chile had occupied since the war of 1881, effectively leaving the republic without a seaport. Despite this loss, the treaty provided several advantages for Bolivia:

*   **Infrastructure:** The government of Chile agreed to construct a railway from Arica to the Bolivian capital, La Paz, at Chile's own expense.
*   **Transit Rights:** Chile granted Bolivians free transit through Chilean territory to specific towns located on the coast.
*   **Financial Assistance:** Chile agreed to pay a cash indemnity to Bolivia and provide pecuniary assistance for the construction of other railways necessary to open up the country.
