# The Dugong (Halicore)

The dugong, classified under the genus *Halicore*, is one of two existing generic representatives of the Sirenia, an order of herbivorous aquatic mammals. Within the marine orders of Sirenia and Cetacea, the dugong is found exclusively in the Indian Ocean.

## Physical Characteristics
Dugongs are distinguished from their cousins, the manatis, by several anatomical features. The most prominent difference is found in the upper jaw of the male, which possesses a pair of large tusks; in females, the growth of these tusks is arrested, leaving them concealed. Their dental structure is limited to a maximum of five molar teeth on each side of either jaw, totaling no more than twenty teeth, all of which feature flat grinding surfaces.

The animal's tail is broad and crescent-shaped, contrasting with the rounded tail of the manati. Additionally, their flippers lack nails. The bones of the dugong are firm and hard, capable of taking a polish comparable to that of ivory. To facilitate their feeding habits, they possess truncated snouts and thick, obtuse lips.

## Habitat and Behavior
Dugongs frequent the shallow waters of tropical seas. Their range extends from the shores of the Indian, Malayan, and Australian seas, reaching as far as the east coast of Africa north of the mouth of the Zambezi. In these environments, they are often observed browsing on submarine pastures of seaweed or basking on the water's surface.

These mammals are gregarious and tend to feed in large numbers in areas where they are not frequently disturbed. The female produces a single offspring per birth and is noted for exhibiting great affection toward its young. This maternal bond is so strong that capturing a young dugong typically makes the capture of the mother straightforward.

## Species and Distribution
Three species of dugong are commonly recognized:

*   **The Indian dugong (*Halicore dugong*):** This species is abundant along the shores of the Indian Ocean. It generally reaches a length of 8 to 10 feet.
*   **The Australian dugong (*H. australis*):** A larger species that can sometimes attain a length of 15 feet. It is found along the Australian coast, specifically from Cape York to Moreton Bay.
*   **The Red Sea dugong (*H. tabernaculi*):** Recognized as one of the three common species.

## Human Interaction and Hunting
In the Indian Ocean, the Indian dugong is captured in large numbers by the Malays, who consider its flesh a great delicacy. Europeans also regard the lean portions of the animal, particularly those from young specimens, as excellent eating. 

The primary method of capture is spearing. Hunters aim to raise the animal's tail out of the water, a maneuver that renders the dugong perfectly powerless. Similarly, the natives of Australia hunt the Australian dugong with spears and highly value its flesh.
