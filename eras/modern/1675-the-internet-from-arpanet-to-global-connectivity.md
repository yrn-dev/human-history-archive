# The Internet: From ARPANET to Global Connectivity

## Overview
The evolution of the internet is not the story of a single "eureka" moment, but rather a decades-long convergence of military strategy, academic collaboration, and commercial ambition. It began during the Cold War as a project of the U.S. Department of Defense’s Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA). The primary goal was to create a decentralized communication network that could withstand partial destruction—such as a nuclear strike—by utilizing "packet switching," a method of breaking data into small chunks that could travel independently across various routes to their destination.

By the 1970s and 80s, the focus shifted from mere connectivity to standardization. The development of the Transmission Control Protocol and Internet Protocol (TCP/IP) by Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn provided a universal language that allowed disparate networks to communicate, effectively creating a "network of networks." However, the internet remained the domain of scientists and military personnel until 1989, when Tim Berners-Lee invented the World Wide Web at CERN. By introducing HTML, HTTP, and the first web browser, Berners-Lee transformed a complex command-line system into a visual, navigable experience accessible to the general public.

Historians often debate the "inventor" of the internet. While the U.S. government provided the funding and framework, some scholars argue that the conceptual foundations laid by British and French researchers (such as Donald Davies and Louis Pouzin) were equally critical. This debate highlights the tension between viewing the internet as a strategic American military achievement versus a global collaborative scientific effort.

## Key Facts
*   **Packet Switching:** Unlike traditional circuit-switching (used in telephony), packet switching allows data to be routed dynamically, ensuring network resilience.
*   **Decentralization:** The architecture was intentionally designed without a central "hub" to prevent a single point of failure.
*   **The Web $\neq$ The Internet:** The internet is the underlying infrastructure (hardware, wires, protocols), while the World Wide Web is an application that runs *on* that infrastructure.

## Did You Know?
1. **The First Message:** The first message sent over ARPANET in 1969 was intended to be "LOGIN," but the system crashed after the first two letters, making "LO" the first transmitted data.
2. **DNS Revolution:** Before the Domain Name System (DNS) was introduced in 1983, users had to maintain a local text file (`HOSTS.TXT`) containing the numerical IP addresses of every computer on the network.
3. **CERN's Gift:** In 1993, CERN placed the World Wide Web software in the public domain, ensuring it remained free and open for everyone to develop.
