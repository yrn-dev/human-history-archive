# Germany

## Cities and Industrial Centers
Germany contains several significant urban and industrial centers with diverse economic specializations. Königsberg is recognized as one of the most important commercial cities in the country due to its protected harbor. It serves as a naval and military fortress of the first order, featuring a system of fortifications that began in 1843 and were completed in 1905. These works include an inner wall, twelve detached forts (six on the left and six on the right bank of the Pregel), and two large forts: Friedrichsburg, located on an island in the Pregel, and the Kaserne Kronprinz to the east. To stimulate trade, a new channel was created between the city and its port, Pillau, which is 29 miles away on the outer side of the Frische Haff, allowing vessels drawing 20 feet of water to reach the quays. Additionally, Königsberg is celebrated for producing the best marchpane (marzipan), a sweetmeat made of sugar and pounded sweet almonds.

In the Prussian province of Silesia, Königshütte is a town situated in the Upper Silesian coal and iron district. Incorporated with neighboring villages in 1869, its population grew from 4,495 in 1852 to 57,919 by 1900. Nearly half of its population consists of Poles. The town is home to the largest iron-works in Silesia, which includes zinc-works, rolling-mills, and puddling works. It also maintains trades in coal, wood, glass, and brick manufacture.

In the Prussian province of Westphalia, Königborn is a spa located north of the town of Unna. It is known for large saltworks that produce over 15,000 tons annually. The area features brine springs with a temperature of 93° F, which are used in a hydropathic establishment to treat scrofula, rheumatism, and skin diseases.

## Scholarship and Education
German scholarship has made significant contributions to classical studies and textual criticism. Hermann Bonitz (1814–1888), a scholar born in Saxony, held various academic positions, including professorships at the university of Vienna and the gymnasium at Stettin, and served as director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium. Bonitz was instrumental in the system of examination and teaching used in Prussian high schools after 1882. He is best known internationally as a commentator on Aristotle and Plato. His major works include *Disputationes Platonicae Duae* (1837), *Platonische Studien*, *Aristotelische Studien*, and *Index Aristotelicus*. He also wrote on Thukydides, Sophokles, and the origin of homerischen poems.

In the field of biblical studies, scholars such as Bousset and Schmidtke have worked to identify local texts within manuscript groups. Bousset argued that certain readings in the Pauline epistles represent the text used by Pamphilus. He also connected a group headed by "B" with the recension of Hesychius, a theory that has been widely accepted within Germany. Building on this, Schmidtke suggested a connection between manuscript B and a Gospel harmony identified as that of Ammonius, though other scholars, including Nestle, disagree.

## International Relations and Colonial Ambitions
During the late 19th and early 20th centuries, Germany maintained a complex relationship with Great Britain. Despite trade rivalry, a strong commercial connection developed, with the two nations becoming each other's best customers in Europe. British policy initially viewed German colonial expansion with indifference. Even during the Boer War, when Germany expressed rage against England and attempted to form a European coalition, the British Foreign Office proposed a Triple Alliance including Germany in 1901.

However, Germany harbored extensive colonial aspirations. Plans were discussed to unite a vast array of territories under German control to create a colonial empire comparable to those of France, Russia, America, and England. These desired territories included:
*   **Africa:** Central Africa, South-west Africa, German East Africa, Kamerun, Nigeria (with the port of Lagos), Senegambia, Sierra Leone, the Gold Coast, Dahomey, the Katanga ore district, Northern Rhodesia, Nyasaland, Mozambique, Delagoa Bay, and Zanzibar.
*   **Islands:** Madagascar, the rich islands of San Thomé and Principe, and the Azores (specifically the coaling station of Ponta Delgado and Horta).

Such an empire would encompass over 100,000,000 inhabitants and provide immense prospects for trade, settlement, and natural treasures.
