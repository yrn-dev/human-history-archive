# Baden-Baden

Baden-Baden is a town and fashionable watering-place located in the grand-duchy of Baden, Germany. Situated on a hill 600 feet high within the valley of the Black Forest, it lies 23 miles south by west of Karlsruhe. The town is connected to Karlsruhe via a branch of the Mannheim and Basel railway.

## Geography and Demographics
The town is positioned on the right bank of the Oos river, though the primary resorts for visitors are located on the left bank. The area is characterized by extensive promenades, gardens, and pleasure-grounds. The resident population has grown steadily over several decades, recorded at 12,779 in 1885, 14,862 in 1895, and 16,238 in 1905. Despite the resident population, the town attracts more than 70,000 annual visitors. While French nationals were the prevailing visitors until the war of 1870, more recent trends show a higher number of English, Russian, and American visitors.

## Thermal Springs and Balneotherapy
Baden-Baden is renowned for its hot springs, which number twenty-nine. These springs flow from the castle rock at a rate of 90 gallons per minute, with temperatures ranging from 37° to 54° R. (115° to 153° Fahr.). The water is distributed through the town via pipes to supply various baths, including two chief bathing-establishments regarded as the most elegant in Europe.

The waters of Baden-Baden are considered specific for the treatment of several conditions, including:
* Chronic rheumatism and gout
* Neuralgia and paralysis
* Skin diseases
* Internal complaints, specifically uric acid and stone

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town features several significant buildings, including a library, a reading-room, a picture-gallery, a theatre, a *Trinkhalle* (pump-room), and a *Conversationshaus*. While public gaming-tables were once a striking feature of the town, they have since been abolished.

The town's antiquarian interest is centered on its castles and its parish church, which dates from the 15th century and houses the tombs of several margraves. The religious landscape includes a Lutheran church, a Russian church featuring beautiful frescoes, and an English church built in the Norman architectural style. Additionally, the Greek chapel on the Michaelsberg, distinguished by a gilded dome, was erected over the tomb of a son of the Rumanian prince Michel Stourdza, who died in 1863.

## History
The springs of Baden were known to the Romans; an inscription of doubtful authenticity attributes the town's foundation to Emperor Hadrian, and it was named *Aurelia Aquensis* in honor of Aurelius Severus. Roman remains, including well-preserved vapour baths discovered in 1847, are located below the New Castle.

From the 14th century until the end of the 17th century, Baden served as the residence for the margraves. The margraves initially resided in the Old Castle at the summit of a hill before moving in 1479 to the New Castle, which is noted for its subterranean dungeons. The town suffered significant damage during the Thirty Years' War, particularly from French forces who pillaged the town in 1643 and burned it in 1689. In 1706, Margrave Louis William moved the residence to Rastatt. Since the start of the 19th century, the government has actively encouraged the town's growth.

## Regional Context
Baden-Baden is one of the most frequented mineral springs in the grand-duchy of Baden, alongside others such as Petersthal, Freiersbach, Griesbach, Antogast, and Badenweiler. The broader region of Baden experiences extreme temperature variations due to its physical configuration; the Rhine valley is the warmest district in Germany (mean temperature approximately 50° F.), while the high table-land of the Black Forest is much colder (mean temperature 43° F.).
