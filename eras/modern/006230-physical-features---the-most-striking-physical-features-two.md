## Regional Geography

### Bulgaria
The most prominent physical features of Bulgaria are two mountain chains. The Balkans, also known as ancient Haemus, run east and west through the heart of the country and run almost parallel to the Danube. The Rhodope chain forms the southern boundary for a considerable distance. The Balkans represent the southern half of the anti-Dacian system, a great semicircular range where the Carpathians form the northern portion. This chain is divided at the Iron Gates by the passage of the Danube. The two parts of this system share similarities in their flora, geological formation, aspect, and outline.

### Louisiana
Geologically, Louisiana is a recent creation belonging to the "Coastal Plain Province." Its surface is composed mostly of soils or rocks formed as submarine deposits, while the southernmost and easternmost areas are true river deposits. The state's average elevation is approximately 75 ft. above the sea; areas exceeding 400 ft. are limited to hills in Vernon, Claiborne, and Sabine parishes. The physiographic features are simple and consist of five essential elements: "pine-hills," "bluffs," prairies, coast marshes, and diluvial plains. These elements represent successive stages of the geologic process that continues to modify the state. Except for the prairies, these features are found in the "Florida parishes" east of the Mississippi and spread from north to south west of the Mississippi.

### Ashanti
A significant portion of Ashanti is covered by almost impenetrable primeval forest. This region is characterized by hardwood and silk-cotton trees, with the bombax reaching heights over 200 ft. Lianas, or monkey-ropes, hang in immense festoons from tree to tree, contributing to a weird aspect; some species of these lianas produce a type of commercial rubber. Mimosa trees in this region reach heights of 30 to 60 ft. In the north-eastern districts, the primeval forest transitions into park-like country featuring plains of high coarse grass dotted with small trees, such as fan palms, dwarf date, shea-butter, wild plum, and occasional baobabs.

### West African Littoral
The littoral of the old Slave Coast is sandy, very low, and obstructed by a bar. A line of lagoons—including Grand Popo, Whydah, and Porto Novo (or Lake Nokue)—lies behind the seashore. The Weme is the most important river running south, extending 300 m. from the Bariba country to Porto Novo, where it enters the lagoon; its upper course is known as the Ofe. The Zu is an affluent of the Weme to the west. Further west, the Kuffu (150 m. long) broadens into the Aheme lagoon, which measures 20 m. long by 5 m. broad, before entering the Whydah lagoon. The Kergigoto and Makru rivers flow north-east into the Niger. In the section forming the colony's north-east frontier, the Niger is navigable only for small vessels and with great difficulty.

## Geological and Physical Sciences

### Hornfelsing and Rock Structure
In certain districts, hornfelsed rocks acquire a schistose structure through shearing, creating transitions to gneisses and schists. Examples include andalusite and kyanite mica schists, cordierite and sillimanite gneisses, and schistose calc silicate rocks known as cipolins. These are generally admitted to be sediments that underwent thermal alteration. The essential features of hornfelsing are attributed to permeating vapours, pressure, and heat, which regenerate rock mass without large-scale fusion. While some argue that chemical change occurs due to matter introduced from granite—citing the formation of new felspar as evidence—most current authorities view these changes as purely physical rather than chemical.
