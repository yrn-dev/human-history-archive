# Sturgeons and Salmonidae

## Classification and Physical Characteristics
Sturgeons are classified within the order of Ganoids, characterized by angular bony scales covered with a thick stratum of enamel. In terms of internal anatomy, sturgeons are categorized as lower gnathostomatous fishes. A defining feature of their digestive system is the presence of a spiral valve within the intestine. This structure consists of a shelf-like projection into the lumen that follows a spiral course, guiding food during digestion. This is considered an archaic structure, as it is absent in higher fishes, though it remains in a reduced state in some species such as *Amia* and *Lepidosteus*.

The *Salmonidae* are classified as Cycloids, possessing smooth scales that lack denticulation on the hind margin. Regarding their internal anatomy, the spiral valve typically found in lower fishes has disappeared in *Salmonidae*, although it may exist as a faint vestige.

## Geographic Distribution
The distribution of these fish varies significantly by region:

*   **Northern and Arctic Regions:** *Salmonidae* exist in rivers flowing into the northern Pacific and the Arctic Ocean. Specimens of char (*Salmo arcturus* and *Salmo naresii*) have been obtained from latitudes as high as 82° N.
*   **Asia:** *Salmonidae* are entirely absent from the waters of southern Asia. However, trout are found in streams flowing into the Caspian, beyond the watershed of the Indus. Sturgeons are found in Asiatic Russia, and an allied form extends into southern China.
*   **Europe and Western Asia:** Sturgeons abound in the Caspian and Black Seas, where they ascend the rivers that flow into these bodies of water.
*   **North America:** In British Columbia, salmon are a primary product of the sea. Various types run in these waters at different seasons, including the quinnat (spring salmon), the sockeye, the silver salmon (cohoe), and the dog-salmon.

## Lake Baikal and Associated Fauna
Lake Baikal contains waters that swarm with *Salmonidae* and sturgeons. The lake's herring, *Salmo omul*, serves as the chief product of the local fisheries, although catches have notably decreased over the last forty to fifty years. Additionally, the small Lake Frolikha, which communicates with Lake Baikal via a river of the same name, is home to a peculiar species of trout, *Salmo erythreas*, which is not found anywhere else.

## Economic Importance and Fisheries
Both groups hold significant economic value in various regions:

*   **The Caspian Sea:** This inland sea is exceptionally rich in fish, particularly near the mouths of the Kura, Terek, Ural, and Volga rivers. Four species of sturgeon are found here and are of the greatest economic value due to their yield of isinglass and caviare. Salmon and herring are also key components of the Caspian fisheries.
*   **British Columbia:** Salmon is the chief product of the region's sea commerce. The quinnat is regarded as the best table fish. The sockeye, which runs in enormous numbers up the Skeena and Fraser rivers, is preferred for canning due to its uniform size and bright color. The dog-salmon is caught in large numbers by the Japanese for salting and export to the Orient.

## Paleontological Observations
Research into palaeontology has noted structural similarities between living sturgeons and fossil genera. Specifically, the structure and prolongation of the upper caudal lobe in sturgeons show a close resemblance to the fossil *Palaeoniscus*.
