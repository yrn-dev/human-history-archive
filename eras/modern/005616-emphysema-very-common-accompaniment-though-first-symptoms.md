# Emphysema

Emphysema is a medical condition characterized by the dilatation of the air-tubes. Within the archive's records, it is identified as a complication or accompaniment to several other respiratory ailments.

## Relationship to Bronchitis and Asthma
Emphysema frequently occurs in connection with chronic inflammatory conditions of the bronchial tubes. In cases of long-standing chronic bronchitis, the nutrition of the lungs becomes impaired, which results in the dilatation of the air-tubes (emphysema). This condition, along with other complications, leads to breathlessness that is more or less constant.

A similar progression is noted in pediatric cases of asthma. While asthma in children is usually periodic, it tends to become more erratic as the child ages. Although it may begin as "pure" asthma, it later in life becomes attended by chronic bronchitis, which in turn gives rise to emphysema.

## Association with Bronchiectasis
Emphysema is described as a very common accompaniment to bronchiectasis. Bronchiectasis is the dilatation of the bronchi and can occur in connection with many lung diseases, including phthisis, acute pneumonia, broncho-pneumonia, and both acute and chronic bronchitis. 

While the symptoms of bronchiectasis may initially resemble those of bronchitis, they eventually become distinctive. These include a severe, intermittent, and markedly paroxysmal cough. The patient's posture significantly affects these symptoms; lying on the affected side may stop the cough throughout the night, whereas rising, bending forward, or turning to the sound side results in the expulsion of large quantities of bronchial secretion. Depending on whether the dilatation is cylindrical or saccular, this expectoration may be foetid or gush from the mouth with suddenness.

## Related Respiratory Conditions
The archive provides context on the primary conditions that may lead to the structural changes associated with emphysema:

*   **Acute Bronchitis:** This inflammatory affection typically arises from exposure to cold and damp or sudden atmospheric temperature changes. Early symptoms include a short, painful, dry cough, wheezing respiration, and a feeling of tightness in the chest and rawness in the throat.
*   **Chronic Bronchitis:** This condition involves the thickening or ulceration of the mucous membrane of the bronchial tubes. It can arise secondarily to other ailments, such as syphilis, gout, or heart and kidney diseases (specifically Bright's disease). Occupational factors also play a role; for example, weavers, cotton factory workers, millers, and grain-shovellers are especially liable to it due to the inhalation of vegetable dust and the growth of organisms carried by that dust.
