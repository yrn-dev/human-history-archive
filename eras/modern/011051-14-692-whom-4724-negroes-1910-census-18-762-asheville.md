# Asheville

## Geography and Location
Asheville is a city located in the United States, serving as the county-seat of Buncombe county, North Carolina. It is situated in the west part of the state within the mountainous Blue Ridge region, approximately 210 miles west of Raleigh. The city is positioned on a high terrace on the east bank of the French Broad river, at the mouth of the Swannanoa, at an elevation of about 2,300 feet above the sea.

## Population
The population of Asheville has shown steady growth over the late 19th and early 20th centuries. In 1890, the population was 10,235. By 1900, this number increased to 14,692, of whom 4,724 were negroes. According to the 1910 census, the population had grown further to 18,762.

## Economy and Tourism
Asheville is recognized as one of the most popular pleasure and health resorts in the south. Due to its beautiful scenery and an equable, dry climate, it serves as a winter resort for those from the north and a summer resort for southerners. The city's attractiveness is enhanced by an extensive system of suburban and city parks connected by a series of beautiful drives, and it is surrounded by great forests.

The city is a transportation hub, situated at the junction of three branches of the Southern railway.

## Public Institutions and Education
The city contains several public buildings, including an auditorium, a public library, a Federal building, the court house, and the city hall.

Educational institutions in or near the city include:
* **The Bingham school**: Founded in 1793 by William Bingham (who died in 1826) at Pittsboro, N.C., and moved to its current site 3 miles north-west of Asheville in 1891.
* **The Asheville school for boys**: Located 5 miles west of the city.
* **Presbyterian Church Institutions**: Under the control of the Woman's Board of Home Missions of the Presbyterian Church, the city hosts a collegiate and normal institute for young women (established 1892) and a home industrial school for girls (established 1887), both of which occupy the same campus.
* **The Asheville farm school for boys**: An industrial school for negroes.

## Social Life and Biltmore Estate
Asheville's social scene includes several clubs, specifically the Swannanoa Country club, the Tahkeeostee, the Elks, the Asheville, and the Albemarle.

Located about 2 miles south-east of the city is Biltmore, the estate of George W. Vanderbilt. Spanning 125,000 acres, it is considered probably the finest country place in the United States. The estate's central feature is a French Renaissance design chateau, measuring 375 by 150 feet, modeled after the famous chateau at Blois, France. Mr. Vanderbilt also maintains a model village in the neighborhood, which includes a church, a hospital, an industrial school for whites, and an elementary school. Both the village and the chateau were designed by Richard M. Hunt.
