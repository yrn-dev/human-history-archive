# AVEIRO

Aveiro is a seaport, episcopal see, and the capital of an administrative district in Portugal. It is situated on the river Vouga and is served by the Lisbon-Oporto railway. As of 1900, the population of the city was 9,979.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city is built on the southern shore of a marshy lagoon that measures approximately 15 miles from north to south and has an average breadth of about 1 mile. This lagoon contains many small islands. To provide access to the Atlantic Ocean, an artificial canal known as the Barra Nova was constructed between 1801 and 1808, reaching a depth of about 33 feet.

## Economy and Industry
The local economy is supported by several primary industries, including the gathering of aquatic plants known as *molico*, the preparation of sea-salt, and the catching and curing of fish, with a particular emphasis on oysters and sardines. Additionally, there is a brisk trade in fruit, oil, and wine. The broader Aveiro district is characterized by much good pasture-land and contains mines for lead and copper.

## History
Aveiro is believed to be the Roman Talabriga. During the 16th century, the city became a duchy and saw the construction of its cathedral. It was also the birthplace of Joao Affonso, who was among the first navigators to visit the fishing-grounds of Newfoundland. Following this, Aveiro became famous for a fleet of more than sixty vessels that sailed to Newfoundland annually, returning with loads of dried codfish.

The title of "duke of Aveiro" eventually became extinct in 1759, when the last holder of the title, Dom Jose Mascarenhas e Lancaster, was burned alive for high treason.

## Administrative Context
The administrative district of Aveiro covers 1,065 square miles and had a population of 303,169 in 1900. This district coincides with the north-western portion of the province of Beira. Within the popular division of Beira, Aveiro and Coimbra together comprise the section known as Beira Mar, or Maritime Beira, which corresponds to the former coastal province of Douro.

The province of Beira itself is an ancient province and principality of central and northern Portugal, bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Spanish provinces of Estremadura and Leon to the east. The coastline of this region is uniformly flat, featuring marshland, heath, and long stretches of sandy pine forest. The lagoon of Aveiro is noted as one of the most conspicuous features of this coastline.

## Regional Connections
Aveiro is one of several small seaports along the coast of Beira, alongside Ovar, Murtosa, and Figueira da Foz. Nearby, located 3 miles southwest of Aveiro on the lagoon, is the seaport of Ilhavo. Ilhavo, which had a population of 12,617 in 1900, is primarily inhabited by fishermen and is known for the Vista-Alegre, a celebrated manufactory of porcelain and glass. While tradition suggests Ilhavo was founded by Greek colonists around 400 B.C., this claim is considered to be of doubtful validity.
