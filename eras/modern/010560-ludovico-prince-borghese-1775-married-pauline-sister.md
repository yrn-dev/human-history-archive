# Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese

## Family Origins and Rise to Nobility
The Borghese family is a noble Italian house of Sienese origin, with the first mentions of the family dating back to 1238. The family's prominence in Rome began when Marcantonio Borghese settled in the city; he was the father of Camillo Borghese (1550–1620), who was elected pope in 1605 under the name Paul V. 

During his papacy, Paul V elevated his family's status, creating his nephew a prince of Vivero on November 17, 1609. Additionally, in 1610, Philip III of Spain conferred the title of prince of Sulmona upon him. The family solidified its position among the higher Roman nobility in 1614 through the marriage of the prince's son, Paolo, to Olimpia, who was the heiress of the Aldobrandini family.

## Life and Career of Prince Ludovico
Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese, was born in 1775. In 1803, he married Pauline, the sister of Emperor Napoleon and the widow of General Leclerc. Following this marriage on August 23, 1803, Pauline went to reside with him in Rome.

In 1806, Prince Ludovico was made the duke of Guastalla. For a period of several years, he served as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces. Following the fall of Napoleon, he established his residence in Florence, where he remained until his death in 1832.

## Marriage to Marie Pauline
Marie Pauline (1780–1825), described as the most beautiful and gayest member of her family, was born in Ajaccio on October 20, 1780. Before her marriage to Prince Borghese, she had married General Leclerc, a staff officer of Napoleon, at the age of seventeen. She accompanied Leclerc to St Domingo, where he died of yellow fever in 1802.

After returning to Paris, she married Prince Camillo Borghese. However, she soon grew tired of him and returned to Paris, where she engaged in whims that caused some scandal. Like her husband, she received the title of duchess of Guastalla in 1806. In 1810, Pauline was removed from court due to her offhand treatment of the new empress, Marie Louise. Despite this, she traveled to Elba in 1814 with "Madame Mere" and is said to have expressed a desire to share Napoleon's exile on St Helena. She died of cancer in 1825. She is the subject of a well-known statue by Canova, which depicts her as Venus reclining on a couch.

## The Borghese Legacy in Rome
The Borghese family is associated with the Borghese palace in Rome, which is regarded as one of the city's most magnificent buildings. The palace once housed a splendid gallery of pictures. Most of these artworks were later transferred to the Villa Borghese, located outside the Porto del Popolo. This site, now known as Villa Umberto I, is the property of the Italian government.
