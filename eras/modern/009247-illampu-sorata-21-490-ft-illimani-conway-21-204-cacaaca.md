# Illimani and Sorata

## Geographic Context
Illimani and Sorata are prominent summits located within the eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes. These mountains are part of a larger Andean system that comprises approximately two-fifths of Bolivia's total area. The cordilleras in this region are characterized by two great parallel chains with various spurs and flanking ranges extending toward the Brazilian highlands.

## Geological Composition
The geological makeup of the eastern ranges, which include the summits of Illimani and Sorata, consists of Palaeozoic rocks. These formations are further characterized by the presence of other intrusions, specifically granitic ones. This composition distinguishes them from the volcanoes of Bolivia, which are located almost entirely within the Western Cordillera and are primarily composed of Jurassic and Cretaceous beds, as well as volcanic ashes and lavas.

## Regional Physiography
The mountains exist within a complex landscape of intersecting ridges and elevated plateaus. To the north-east of Lake Titicaca lies the Nudo de Apolobamba, a knot of lofty ridges containing some of the highest peaks in South America. Between the eastern and western ranges lies a great Bolivian tableland, or *alta-planicie*, which averages an elevation of about 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca. This plateau serves as a lacustrine basin that once held an inland sea, which previously had an outlet to the east via the La Paz gorge.
