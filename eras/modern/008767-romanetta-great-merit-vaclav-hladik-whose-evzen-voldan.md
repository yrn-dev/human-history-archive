# Bohemian Literature and Authors of the Late 19th Century

The development of Bohemian literature during the latter part of the 19th century reached an extent that few had foreseen, a growth particularly accelerated following the foundation of the national university in 1882. This period saw a proliferation of talented writers across various genres, including poetry, prose, drama, and historical novels.

## Notable Poets and Epic Writers
Among the significant figures of this era was John Neruda (1834-1891), a talented and fruitful writer of both prose and poetry. His philosophical epic, *Kosmicke basne* (cosmic poems), is regarded as perhaps the most valuable of his works. Julius Zeyer (1841-1901) also contributed extensively to both verse and prose; his novel *Jan Maria Plojhar* achieved great success, and his epic poem *Vysehrad* is noted for its value in celebrating the ancient glory of the acropolis of Prague.

Later Bohemian poets of high standing include Adolf Heyduk, Jaroslav Vrchlicky, and Svatopluk Cech. Cech's work was heavily inspired by national enthusiasm, with his most notable poems being *Basne otroka* (the songs of a slave) and *Vaclav z Michalovic, Lesetinsky Kovar* (the smith of Lesetin). Jaroslav Vrchlicky, who wrote under the pseudonym Emil Frida, shared strong patriotic feelings but adopted a more catholic approach to his subjects. His lyric poem collections of particular value include *Sonety Samotare* (sonnets of a recluse), *Poute k Eldoradu* (pilgrimages to Eldorado), and *Rok na jihu* (a year in the south). Additionally, Vrchlicky is recognized as a brilliant dramatist.

## Novelists and Dramatists
The number of Bohemian novelists grew significantly during this period. Jacob Arbes is noted for his *Romanetta*, which are described as having great merit. Vaclav Hladik is another prominent figure who, like many of his Bohemian contemporaries, was a copious author of dramas. Hladik is specifically recognized for his work *Evzen Voldan*, which provides a very striking representation of life in modern Prague. Other distinguished figures in these fields include Alois Jirasek, who was a distinguished dramatic author as well as a novelist. Earlier writers of note include Benes Trebizky, who authored many historical novels, and Bozena Nemceva, whose work *Babicka* has been translated into numerous languages.

## Historical Writing and Scholarship
Bohemia also produced a fruitful array of historic writers. Wenceslas Tomek (1818-1905) authored many historical works, the most important of which is *Dejepis miesta Prahy* (history of the town of Prague). Jaroslav Goll (b. 1846) wrote extensively on history, with a particular focus on the community of the Bohemian Brethren. In 1895, Goll jointly founded the *Cesky Casopis Historicky* (Bohemian Historical Review) with Dr. Anton Rezek, who also produced important historical studies in the Journal of the Bohemian Museum.

Other scholarly contributions include those of Professor Joseph Kalousek, who wrote on the early history of Bohemia and produced a valuable study of the ancient constitution (*Statni pravo*) of Bohemia. More recent scholarship includes the work of Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, who published studies on the writings and life of John Huss, and the learned archaeological studies on the earliest period of Bohemian history published by Professors Niederle and Pic.

## Literary Historiography
The study of Bohemian literature has been documented in several key texts. In English, these include *A History of Bohemian Literature* (1899) by Count Lutzow and *Slavonic Literature* (1883) by W.R. Morfill. Additionally, A.N. Pypin and V.D. Spasovic wrote a *History of Slavonic Literature* in Russian, which was later translated into German by Trangott Pech in two volumes between 1880 and 1884. Modern histories of the literature written in the national language have been produced by Mr. Jaroslav Vlaek, Dr. Vaclav Flajshans, and Dr. Karel Tieftrunk.
