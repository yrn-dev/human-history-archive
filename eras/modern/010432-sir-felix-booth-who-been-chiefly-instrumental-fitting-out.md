# Sir Felix Booth

## Contribution to Arctic Exploration
Sir Felix Booth was a figure of significance in the history of British North American exploration. He was chiefly instrumental in the fitting out of the expedition conducted by Captain (later Sir James) Ross between 1829 and 1833. 

## Boothia Peninsula
In recognition of Sir Felix Booth's role in supporting the expedition, the peninsula discovered by Sir James Ross was named Boothia. Located in the Franklin district of British North America, the Boothia Peninsula is situated between 69 deg. 30' and 71 deg. 50' N. latitude and 91 deg. 30' and 97 deg. W. longitude. The peninsula covers an area of 13,100 sq. m. 

The peninsula is characterized by the following geographical and scientific features:
* **Extreme Points:** Its northernmost promontory, Murchison Point, serves as the northernmost point of the American mainland.
* **Waterways:** Boothia forms the western side of the Boothia Gulf. It is nearly separated from the main continent by inlets and lakes. It is connected to the Franklin Strait via the Bellot Strait—a narrow channel that also separates the peninsula from North Somerset Island (discovered by Sir E. Parry in 1819). Additionally, it is connected to the Fox Channel by the Fury and Hecla Strait, and to Lancaster Sound and Barrow Strait by the Prince Regent Inlet.
* **Bays:** The western portion of the peninsula contains Lord Mayor bay, while the southern portion contains Pelly and Committee bays.
* **Scientific Importance:** The peninsula is of scientific note because Sir James Ross first distinctly localized the north magnetic pole on its western side at 70 deg. 5' N., 96 deg. 47' W.

The region is further noted for its connection to the Franklin expedition and the subsequent search for Franklin.
