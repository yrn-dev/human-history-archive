# Bacteria

## General Characteristics
Bacteria are organisms that are predominantly saprophytic or parasitic in nature, a characteristic resulting from their lack of chlorophyll. While the majority of bacteria are colourless, a small number of species are capable of secreting colouring matters other than chlorophyll. 

In terms of physical dimensions, bacterial cells typically measure approximately 0.001 mm (1 micron) in diameter. Their length is generally between two and five times their diameter, although both smaller and larger cells are known to exist.

## Morphology and Structure
Bacteria assume various shapes and structural forms. Documented examples include:
*   *Bacillus subtilis* and *Bacillus typhi*
*   *Spirillum undula* and *Spirillum rubrum*
*   *Microspira Comma*
*   *Planococcus citreus*
*   *Pseudomonas pyocyanea*, *P. macroselmis*, and *P. syncyanea*
*   *Bacillus vulgaris*

The structural study of bacteria includes the examination of the cell membrane, the nucleus, and the presence of cilia.

## Biological Interactions and Distribution
Bacteria exhibit specific interactions with plant life, notably the invasion of leguminous roots. In the root of a pea, an "infection thread" (zoogloea) can be observed pushing through the cell walls of the epidermis. Bacteria also gather in masses at the free end of pea root-hairs and push upward in thin streams. In the lupin, bacteria form root nodules; longitudinal sections of these nodules reveal bacterial tissue and cells where the protoplasm is filled with bacteria. Specific forms known as bacteroids have been identified in *Lupinus albus* and *Vicia villosa*.

Regarding their distribution in time, fossil evidence indicates that bacteria existed during the Devonian and Carboniferous periods, and likely even earlier. While some claims suggest that geologically immured forms have been found still living, such statements are dismissed as absurd.

## Academic Authorities and Research
The study of bacteria is supported by various specialized research areas and authorities:
*   **General Structure and Functions:** Fischer (*The Structure and Functions of Bacteria*) and Migula (*System der Bakterien*).
*   **Water Bacteriology:** Frankland and Marshall Ward have provided reports on the bacteriology of water, including specific studies on the river Thames and the biology of *B. ramosus*.
*   **Cellular Components:** Research on the cell membrane and cilia has been conducted by Butschli, Fischer, and Rowland. Studies on the bacterial nucleus include work by Wager, Vejdovsky, and Mencl.
*   **Spores:** Research into spores and soil bacillus (such as *B. megatherium*) has been conducted by Marshall Ward, Sturgis, and Klein.
*   **Classification:** The classification of Schizomycetes has been addressed by Marshall Ward, as well as Lehmann and Neumann.
*   **Fossil Bacteria:** Research on fossilized bacteria has been conducted by Renault and van Tieghem.
