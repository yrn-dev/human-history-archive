# BONA FIDE AND SIR FREDERICK WILLIAM BORDEN

## Bona Fide
*Bona fide* is a Latin term meaning "in good faith." In a legal context, it refers to an action or state implying the absence of unfair dealing, fraud, or acting. The term is typically used in conjunction with a noun to define specific legal statuses:

*   **Bona fide purchaser:** An individual who purchases property from its legal owner, pays the required consideration, and takes a legal conveyance without having notice of any trust that affects the property.
*   **Bona fide holder:** In the context of a bill of exchange, this is a person who takes a bill that is regular and complete on its face before it is overdue. This person must act in good faith, provide value, and have no notice of any defect in the title of the person negotiating the bill to them.
*   **Bona fide traveller:** Under licensing acts, this is defined as a person whose lodging place during the previous night was at least 3 miles away from the location where they are requesting a supply of liquor, with the distance measured by the nearest public thoroughfare.

Outside of strict legal definitions, the term is used to describe the authenticity of documents or the legitimacy of intentions. For example, in the middle of the 19th century, a series of fabricated letters attributed to Shelley and Byron were published as *bona fide* documents. In diplomatic and military contexts, such as the 1914 discussions regarding the transfer of vessels to the Turkish flag, the term is used to specify that a transference must be permanent and genuine. Similarly, during conflicts involving the Venizelist army, the term was used by Allies to distinguish genuine (*bona fide*) Venizelists from spies posing as deserters.

## Sir Frederick William Borden
Sir Frederick William Borden (born May 14, 1847) was a Canadian statesman. He was born in Cornwallis, Nova Scotia. His education took place at Harvard University and King's College in Windsor.

Before entering politics, Borden practiced medicine for several years in Canning, Nova Scotia. His political career began in 1874 when he was elected to the Canadian parliament as a Liberal member representing King's county. Later, in 1896, he served as the minister of militia and defence within the Liberal ministry.
