# Pierre Napoleon

## Early Life and Adventures
Pierre Napoleon was born in Rome on September 12, 1815, the son of Lucien Bonaparte. He began a life of adventure at fifteen years of age, joining insurrectionary bands in the Romagna between 1830 and 1831. His travels subsequently took him to the United States to join his uncle Joseph, and later to Colombia in 1832, where he was with General Santander. Upon returning to Rome, he was taken prisoner by order of the pope from 1835 to 1836, after which he sought refuge in England.

## Political Career in France
Pierre Napoleon returned to France during the revolution of 1848 and was elected as the deputy for Corsica to the Constituent Assembly. During this period, he identified as an "out-and-out republican," voting alongside the socialists and expressing support for the national workshops and opposition to the *loi Falloux*. These political stances helped generate popular confidence in his cousin, Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III).

Although Pierre Napoleon disapproved of the *coup d'etat* carried out by Napoleon III on December 2, 1851, he was soon reconciled with the emperor. He accepted the title of prince, an action that led the republicans to abandon him immediately.

## Later Life and Legal Controversy
Following his break with the republicans, Pierre Napoleon lost his political importance and led a debauched life, turning his attention to the publication of mediocre poems. He returned to public prominence in January 1870 due to a violent controversy with Paschal Grousset. Grousset sent two journalists to provoke Pierre Napoleon into a duel; during a violent discussion that followed, Pierre Napoleon used a revolver to kill one of the men, Victor Noir.

This killing caused significant excitement within the republican press, which demanded a trial. While the High Court acquitted him, the verdict led to criticism of the government.

## Personal Life and Death
Pierre Napoleon married Justine Eleanore Ruffin, the daughter of a working-man from Paris. Before their marriage, he had two children:
* **Roland Napoleon** (born May 19, 1858), who served in the army until being excluded in 1886, subsequently focusing on scientific explorations and geography.
* **Jeanne**, who became the wife of the marquis de Vence.

Pierre Napoleon died in obscurity at Versailles on April 7, 1881.
