# Sir Oswald Walters Brierly

## Early Life and Education
Sir Oswald Walters Brierly (1817–1894) was an English marine painter born in Chester to an old Cheshire family. His formal artistic training began at Sass's art-school in London. In addition to his art studies, Brierly studied naval architecture at Plymouth. He first exhibited drawings of ships at the Royal Academy in 1839.

## Maritime Travels and Experiences
Driven by a passion for the sea, Brierly undertook several significant voyages that informed his work. In 1841, he traveled around the world aboard the ship "Wanderer" with Benjamin Boyd, a man later known as a great Australian squatter. Upon reaching New South Wales, Brierly resided at Auckland for ten years; Brierly Point is named in his honor.

He continued to expand his sea experiences through further voyages, serving on H.M.S. "Rattlesnake" in 1848 and on the "Meander" in 1850 with Sir Henry Keppel. Brierly returned to England on the "Meander" in 1851 and subsequently provided illustrations for a book regarding Keppel's cruise, published in 1853.

## Professional Career and Royal Patronage
Brierly accompanied Sir Henry Keppel again during the Crimean War. In 1855, he published a series of lithographs titled "The English and French fleets in the Baltic." This work brought him to the attention of Queen Victoria and other members of the royal family. 

Brierly was subsequently attached to the suites of the prince of Wales and the duke of Edinburgh during their tours by sea. These experiences resulted in the creation of further marine pictures by the artist. His relationship with the monarchy culminated in 1874 when he was appointed marine-painter to the queen.

## Artistic Works and Legacy
While Brierly exhibited his work at the Royal Academy, he exhibited more extensively at the Royal Water-colour Society. Among his most important works are historical paintings, specifically "The Loss of the Revenge" (1877) and "The Retreat of the Spanish Armada" (1871). Although he led an active and prosperous life and was knighted in 1885, he is not regarded as a great artist. His best paintings are located in Sydney and Melbourne.

Sir Oswald Walters Brierly died on December 14, 1894. He was married twice during his lifetime.
