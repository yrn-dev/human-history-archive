# Prince Napoleon (Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul)

Napoleon Joseph Charles Paul, commonly known as Prince Napoleon or by the sobriquet "Plon-Plon," was a prominent figure in the French Empire during the 19th century. Born on September 9, 1822, in Trieste, he was the second son of Catherine, princess of Wurttemberg, and Jerome Bonaparte, the king of Westphalia.

## Political Ideology and Early Career
Prince Napoleon established early popularity through the expression of advanced democratic ideas. Following the French revolution of 1848, he was elected to the National Assembly as a representative of Corsica. After the death of his elder brother, Jerome Napoleon Charles, in 1847, he assumed the name of Jerome. 

Although he appeared to oppose the *coup d'etat* of 1851, he was designated as the successor to the throne should Napoleon III die without children upon the establishment of the Empire. While he received a liberal dotation, he was not permitted to participate in public affairs. Privately, he associated with men of advanced political opinions and viewed himself as the representative of the democratic aspect of the Napoleonic tradition.

## Role at Court and Military Service
Within the imperial court, Prince Napoleon acted as the representative of the Liberal party in opposition to the empress Eugenie. 

In 1854, he served as a general of division during the Crimean campaign. Although his conduct at the battle of the Alma led to accusations regarding his personal courage, these claims appear to have been groundless. Upon his return to France, he demonstrated significant capacity while undertaking the chief direction of the National Exhibition of 1855.

## Administrative and Diplomatic Activities
In 1858, Prince Napoleon was appointed as the minister for Algeria and the Colonies. While his administration initially generated great hopes, his focus shifted following his marriage in January 1859 to the princess Marie Clotilde of Savoy, the daughter of Victor Emmanuel. This marriage served as a prelude to the war for the liberation of Italy.

During this conflict, Prince Napoleon commanded the French corps that occupied Tuscany. Despite expectations that he would become the ruler of the principality, he refused to apply pressure to the inhabitants, who expressed a preference for union with the Italian kingdom. In the subsequent years, he became known as a great orator through a series of remarkable speeches.
