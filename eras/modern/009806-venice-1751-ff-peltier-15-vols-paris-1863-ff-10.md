# Venice, 1751 ff.

The archive contains records of various publications and scholarly editions produced in Venice during the 18th century and subsequent periods, spanning theological, hagiographical, and literary works.

## Theological and Hagiographical Publications

Venice served as a site for the publication of significant theological works, most notably those of the "Seraphic Doctor," Bonaventura. Bonaventura is characterized as a dogmatic theologian of high rank and a meditative thinker. His works address scholastic thought, including the principle of individualism, matter, universals, and the *intellectus agens*. He viewed theology as a practical science intended to influence affections. A specific edition of his works was published in Venice starting in 1751, consisting of 13 volumes.

Additionally, Venice was the location for an edition of the *Acta Sanctorum*. This specific Venice edition concluded at volume v. of September, with a publication date range spanning from 1734 to 1770.

## Literary and Historical Works

The city of Venice also produced a variety of literary texts and translations. Works attributed to Boiardo published in Venice include:
* *Cinque Capitoli in terza rima* (published in either 1523 or 1533).
* *Apulejo dell' Asino d'Oro* (published in 1516 and 1518).
* *Asino d'Oro de Luciano tradolto in volgare* (published in 1523).
* *Erodoto Alicarnasseo istorico, tradotto di Greco in Lingua Italiana* (published in 1533 and 1538).

In the realm of poetic theory, Thomas Corraeus published *De toto eo poematis genere quod epigramma dicitur* in Venice in 1569.

## Later Venetian Scholarship

In the 19th century, Venice continued to be a center for historical and genealogical research. Notable publications from this era include:
* *Storia documentata di Venezia*, volume iv., written by S. Romanin and published in 1855.
* *Le antichita dei Bonaparte*, published in 1857 by F. de Stefani, which included an introduction by L. Beretta.
* *Caterina Cornaro e il suo regno*, a work by A. Centelli published in 1892.
