# Arrigo Boito and the Cantata Le Sorelle d'Italia

## Early Life and Education
Arrigo Boito was an Italian musical composer and poet born on February 24, 1842, in Padua. During his youth, he pursued musical studies at the Milan Conservatoire. From these early stages of his education, Boito exhibited a divided allegiance between the fields of music and literature, devoting an equal amount of his time to both disciplines. This duality became a defining characteristic of his life's history.

## Collaboration at the Conservatoire
While studying at the Milan Conservatoire, Boito engaged in a musical collaboration with Franco Faccio. Together, they wrote and composed a cantata titled *Le Sorelle d'Italia*. This specific work was performed with success.

## Musical Career and Operatic Works
Following the completion of his studies, Boito spent several years traveling before returning to Italy and settling in Milan. In Milan, he balanced his professional life between music and journalism. 

In 1868, Boito conducted the first performance of his opera *Mefistofele* at the Scala theatre in Milan. The production was a complete failure and was withdrawn after its second performance. However, the work was revived in 1875 at Bologna in an abbreviated and much altered form, where it achieved unquestionable success. The opera was later performed in London in 1880 with success. Despite frequent revivals, *Mefistofele* never firmly established itself in popular favor. In composing this work, Boito approached the Faust legend with a spirit more closely aligned with the conception of Goethe than the version found in Gounod's *Faust*; however, the opera is noted for lacking dramatic interest and cohesion, despite containing many isolated beauties.

## Libretti and Literary Contributions
Boito eventually devoted much of his energy to the composition of libretti. His principal works in this area include:
* *Otello* and *Falstaff*, which were set to music by Verdi.
* *La Gioconda*, set by Ponchielli.
* *Amleto*, set by Faccio.
* *Ero e Leandre*, set by Mancinelli and Bottesini.

These libretti are recognized for their uncommon literary value and a rare knowledge of the requirements of dramatic poetry. Beyond his musical and theatrical contributions, Boito authored a novel titled *L'Alfier Meno* and published a book of poems.

## Later Life and Honors
In 1866, Boito participated in military action, fighting under Garibaldi. His academic and professional achievements were recognized internationally in 1893, when the university of Cambridge conferred upon him the degree of doctor of music.
