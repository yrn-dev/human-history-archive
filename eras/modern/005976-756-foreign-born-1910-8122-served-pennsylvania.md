# Selected Municipalities of Pennsylvania and Ohio

The following entries detail various boroughs and cities located within the states of Pennsylvania and Ohio, focusing on their demographics, industrial foundations, and historical development.

## Municipalities of Pennsylvania

### Allegheny County
**Carnegie** is a borough situated in the valley of Chartiers Creek within a state district known for natural gas and coal. Formed in 1894 through the union of the boroughs of Mansfield and Chartiers, it was named after Andrew Carnegie. The borough contains St Paul's orphan asylum and a Carnegie library, and is noted for alkaline and lithia mineral springs. Its manufactures include glass, lead, steel, enamel- and tin-ware, and ploughs. As of 1910, the population was 10,009, with 1,816 foreign-born residents recorded in 1900.

**McKees Rocks** is a borough located on the Ohio river, approximately 3 miles northwest of Pittsburg. First settled in 1830 and incorporated in 1892, the area contains an ancient Indian mound. The local economy is supported by the shipping of lumber and coal, with bituminous coal and natural gas found nearby. The population grew from 1,687 in 1890 to 14,702 by 1910.

**McKeesport** is a city located 14 miles southeast of Pittsburg at the confluence of the Youghiogheny and Monongahela rivers. The first white settler, David McKee, established a ferry there in 1769, and his son John laid out the town in 1795. Growth accelerated following the 1830 discovery of coal. The city is a major center for the manufacture of steel, iron, and terne and tin plate, the latter being one of the most significant plants in the United States. In 1900, the city's factory products were valued at $36,058,447, falling to $23,054,412 in 1905. The population reached 42,694 by 1910.

### Other Pennsylvania Boroughs
**Ashland** is a borough in Schuylkill county, situated on the slope of Locust Mountain. Laid out in 1847 and incorporated in 1857, it was named after the home of Henry Clay in Lexington, Kentucky. Its primary industry is anthracite coal mining. The area is home to a state hospital for injured persons of the Anthracite Coal Region, which opened in 1883 at Fountain Springs.

**Bristol** is a borough in Bucks county on the Delaware river. One of the first Pennsylvania settlements after William Penn's 1681 charter, it served as the county seat of government until 1725. It was laid out in 1697 and incorporated as a borough in 1720. The borough is known for its chalybeate waters at Bath Springs and various mills producing worsted, hosiery, yarn, and carpets, as well as factories for patent leather and wallpaper.

**Dubois** is a borough in Clearfield county, located on the west slope of the Alleghany Mountains. Founded in 1872 and named for John Dubois, it was incorporated in 1881. It serves as a lumber and coal center, featuring iron works, blast furnaces, locomotive works, and a glass factory. Its population was 12,623 according to the 1910 census.

**Dunmore** is a borough in Lackawanna county, adjoining Scranton. Settled in 1783 and incorporated in 1862, its growth was spurred by the 1863 establishment of railway shops. The borough is the seat of the state oral school for the deaf and focuses on anthracite coal mining. In 1905, railway repair shops employed 48.9% of all manufacturing wage-earners.

## Municipalities of Ohio

### Crawford and Coshocton Counties
**Bucyrus** is the county-seat of Crawford county, located on the Sandusky river. First settled in 1817 and incorporated as a city in 1885, it sits at an elevation of 1000 feet. The surrounding country is suited for stock-raising and agriculture. Its manufacturing sector includes structural steel, machinery, furniture, copper products, and carriages. The population grew from 5,974 in 1890 to 8,122 in 1910.

**Coshocton** is the county-seat of Coshocton county, located at the confluence of the Muskingum, Walhonding, and Tuscarawas rivers. The site was originally a chief village of the Turtle tribe of the Delawares, which was destroyed in 1781. White settlement began in 1801, and the town was laid out in 1802 as Tuscarawas before adopting its current name in 1811. It is a commercial center for an agricultural district, producing glass, paper, china-ware, and advertising specialities.

### Belmont County
**Bellaire** is a city on the Ohio river, 5 miles south of Wheeling, West Virginia. Settled around 1795 and chartered as a city in 1874, it serves as the shipping center for the Belmont county coalfield. In 1907, this field produced 19.3% of the state's total coal output. The city manufactures glass, steel, iron, and agricultural implements. The value of its factory products rose from $8,837,646 in 1900 to $10,712,438 in 1905.
