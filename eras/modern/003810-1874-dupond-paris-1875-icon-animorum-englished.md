# John Barclay (1582–1621)

John Barclay was a Scottish Latin poet and satirist born on January 28, 1582, at Pont-a-Mousson. His father, William Barclay, held the chair of civil law there, and his mother was a Frenchwoman of good family. Barclay received his early education at the Jesuit College, where he wrote a commentary on the *Thebaid* of Statius at the age of nineteen.

## Life and Travels
In 1603, Barclay traveled to London with his father. Despite his French surroundings, he had persistently maintained his Scottish nationality, and he likely viewed the accession of James I as an opportunity. He did not stay in England for long; he is believed to have published the first part of his *Satyricon* there, but by 1605 he was in Paris. Prior to this, he had spent time in Angers and married a French woman named Louise Debonaire.

Barclay returned to London with his wife in 1606 and published his *Sylvae*, a collection of Latin poems. He remained in London until 1616. During this period, in 1609, he edited *De Potestate Papae*, an anti-papal treatise written by his father, who had died the previous year. In 1611, he issued an *Apologia*, referred to as a "third part" of the *Satyricon*, which served as a response to attacks from the Jesuits and others who were likely embittered by the tone of his earlier satirical work. While it is said that James I was attracted to Barclay's scholarship, specific details regarding his life in London are unavailable.

In 1616, for reasons that remain unexplained, Barclay went to Rome, where he lived until his death on August 15, 1621. During his time in Rome, he appeared to be on better terms with the Church and specifically with Bellarmine. In 1617, he issued *Paraenesis ad Sectarios* from a press in Cologne, which was an attack on the position of Protestantism.

## Literary Works
Barclay's works have never been collected. His most well-known work, the romance *Argenis*, was completed approximately a fortnight before his death. It has been suggested that his death was hastened by poison. The romance was printed in Paris in 1621. Other notable works include:

*   ***Satyricon***: A satirical work published in multiple parts. A "fifth part" of the *Satyricon* appears in most editions, written by Alethophilus (Claude Morisot).
*   ***Icon Animorum***: A work appearing in 1614 as a so-called "fourth part" of the *Satyricon*. It was translated into English by Thomas May in 1631 under the title *The Mirrour of Mindes, or Barclay's Icon Animorum*.
*   ***Sylvae***: A collection of Latin poems published in 1606.

The best account of Barclay's life is found in the preface by Jules Dukas in his bibliography of the *Satyricon* (Paris, 1889), which supersedes the account previously found in Bayle's *Dictionary*.

## Other Figures Named John Barclay
The archive also contains records of a different John Barclay (1734–1798), a Scottish divine born in Perthshire and deceased in Edinburgh. He graduated from St Andrews and served as an assistant to the parish minister of Errol in Perthshire. Following differences with the minister, he left in 1763 to become an assistant to Antony Dow of Fettercairn, Kincardine. 

Although popular in his parish, his opinions were not satisfactory to his presbytery. In 1772, the presbytery rejected him as Dow's successor and refused him the testimonials necessary to obtain another living. This refusal was sustained by the General Assembly. Consequently, Barclay left the Scottish church and established congregations in London and Sauchyburn, Edinburgh. His followers, who regulated their conduct through the diligent study of the Scriptures, were sometimes called Bereans. They adhered to a modified form of Calvinism.
