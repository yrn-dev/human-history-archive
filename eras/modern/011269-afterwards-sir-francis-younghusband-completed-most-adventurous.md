# Sir Francis Younghusband

Sir Francis Younghusband was a British explorer and officer known for his adventurous journeys and geographical missions across Central Asia and Tibet during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Central Asian Explorations
In 1886, then Captain Younghusband conducted a significant expedition across the heart of the Asian continent. This journey involved crossing the Muztagh, which served as the great mountain barrier between Kashmir and China. 

During his travels, Younghusband contributed to the natural history of the region. Before departing the Altai Mountains, he collected several heads of *Ovis Poli*, an animal referred to by the Mongols as Argali. Younghusband noted that the specimens he found in the Gobi were different from those he later encountered at Yarkand (which had been brought from the Pamir); specifically, the Gobi specimens featured horns that were shorter in length, had a lesser degree of curve, and were considerably thicker at the base. In later years, Younghusband frequently mentioned the sport of shooting *Ovis Poli*.

## Mission to Lhasa
In 1904, Sir Francis Younghusband led a mission to Lhasa. This expedition had significant geographical and cartographic results, including the extension of the Indian system of triangulation, which allowed for the final determination of the geographical position of Lhasa. 

The mission also facilitated a highly valuable reconnaissance of the Indus and Upper Brahmaputra valleys, which was carried out by Captains C.G. Rawling and C.H.D. Ryder.

## Context of Central Asiatic Geography
Younghusband's work followed a series of European efforts to map the regions of Central Asia and the highlands of the Pamirs. Prior to the 1870 mission of Sir Douglas Forsyth to Yarkand, very little was known of these areas, despite the desires of various Indian viceroys to establish lucrative trade between Central Asia and India. 

Other pioneers and missions that contributed to the knowledge of the region included:
* **Robert Barkley Shaw and George Hayward**: European pioneers who arrived at Yarkand in 1868.
* **Henry Trotter**: Produced the first maps of Chinese Turkestan during Forsyth's 1870 mission.
* **The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission (1884-1886)**: Mapped the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan through the work of Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich.
* **Ney Elias**: Crossed from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs, identifying the "Dragon Lake" (Rangkul).
* **Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart**: Crossed the Hindu Kush into Wakhan.
* **Colonel Woodthorpe**: Verified the explorations of W.W. M'Nair and determined the conformation of the Hindu Kush.
* **Arthur Douglas Carey and Andrew Dalgleish**: Contributed new data to the map of Asia in 1885 by following the tracks of Prjevalsky.

Later advancements in the region were made by others, such as Dr M.A. Stein and Captain C.G. Rawling in western Tibet, Ellsworth Huntington on the Alai range and Tian-shan plateau in 1903, and Sven Hedin, whose journeys between 1899 and 1908 revolutionized knowledge of the region north of the upper Tsanpo.
