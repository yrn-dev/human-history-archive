## Linguistic Analysis in Travelogues
In the study of the travels of Marco Polo, "G.T." is used as a reference marker for specific passages in a French original text, which are then compared to Italian (Crusca) and Latin (G.L.) translations. These excerpts highlight various translation peculiarities and errors:

*   **Religious and Ethnic Groups:** In G.T. 23 (I. 69), the text describes a caliph sending for all the Christians who were in his land. The Latin translation (G.L. 316) misinterpreted the Italian phrase *di là* as "beyond the river."
*   **Botany and Industry:** G.T. 198 (II. 313) mentions *sosimain* (sesamum), from which oil is produced. The Latin translation (G.L. 448) erroneously rendered this as *turpes manus* ("dirty hands").
*   **Geography:** G.T. 124 (II. 36) refers to a province that is still on the border of Mangi. The Latin version (G.L. 396) translates this as a province called "Anchota."
*   **Culture and Attire:** G.T. 146 (II. 119) describes women wearing gold and silver bracelets of great value on their arms and legs.
*   **Environmental Descriptions:** G.T. 32 (I. 97) describes a plain as *chaue* (chaude/hot), which the Italian translation (Crusca 35) rendered as *cavo*.

## Ballistic Calculations and Direct Fire
Within the archive's technical records on ballistics, specific formulas are used to calculate range tables for "direct fire." Direct fire is officially defined as fire from guns using a full charge at an elevation that does not exceed 15 degrees. 

In these calculations, the vertical component of air resistance is considered insensible and is ignored. Consequently, the actual velocity and its horizontal component (or the component parallel to the line of sight) are treated as indistinguishable. The equations of motion are measured in feet:
*   $d^2x/dt^2 = -r = -gp/C$
*   $d^2y/dt^2 = -g$

The integration of these equations allows for the determination of time ($t$) and distance ($x$). The formula $y = 1/2gt(T - t)$ is identified as Colonel Sladen's formula, which is utilized for plotting the ordinates of a trajectory. At the vertex (A), where $y = H$, the formula is simplified for practical purposes (taking $g = 32$) to $H = 4T^2$ or $(2T)^2$.
