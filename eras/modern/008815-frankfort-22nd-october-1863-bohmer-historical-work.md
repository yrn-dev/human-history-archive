# Johann Friedrich Bohmer

Johann Friedrich Bohmer (1795–1863) was a German historian whose professional life and scholarly contributions were centered largely in the city of Frankfort. Born in Frankfort-on-Main on April 22, 1795, he was the son of Karl Ludwig Bohmer, who died in 1817.

## Education and Career
Bohmer received his education at the universities of Gottingen and Heidelberg. During his early years, he developed an interest in art, which led him to visit Italy. Upon returning to Frankfort, however, he shifted his focus toward the study of history. 

He held several significant administrative and scholarly positions within his home city, serving as the librarian and the archivist of Frankfort. Additionally, he served as the secretary of the *Gesellschaft fur altere deutsche Geschichtskunde*.

## Personal Views and Affiliations
Bohmer's personal convictions influenced his perspective on history. He possessed a strong affection for the Roman Catholic Church and Austria, though he was not himself a member of the Catholic Church. Conversely, he held a great dislike for the Protestant faith and Prussia. While some suggest his critical sense may have been somewhat warped, his research is regarded as being of great value to students of history.

## Historical Works and Contributions
Bohmer's historical work focused primarily on the collection and tabulation of imperial documents and charters from the middle ages. His bibliography is extensive, consisting largely of *Regesta* (abstracts) and collections of original authorities.

### The Regesta Series
Bohmer edited a significant series of *Regesta*, which included introductions and explanatory passages. Key publications in this series include:
* *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica regum atque imperatorum Romanorum 911-1313* (Frankfort, 1831).
* *Regesta chronologico-diplomatica Karolorum. Die Urkunden samtlicher Karolinger in kurzen Auszugen* (Frankfort, 1833).
* *Regesta imperii* for the period 1314-1347 (Frankfort, 1839), which was later followed by three supplementary volumes.
* *Regesta* for the period 1246-1313 (Frankfort, 1844), followed by two supplementary volumes.
* *Regesta* for the period 1198-1254 (Stuttgart, 1849).

Additionally, Bohmer and J. Ficker edited the *Regesta imperii* 1314-1347 (Innsbruck, 1865) and the *Acta imperii selecta* (Innsbruck, 1870).

### Other Major Collections
Bohmer edited several other critical collections of historical documents:
* *Die Reichsgesetze 900-1400* (Frankfort, 1832).
* *Codex diplomaticus Moeno-Francofurtanus. Urkundenbuch der Reichsstadt Frankfurt* (Frankfort, 1836).
* *Wittelsbachische Regesten von der Erwerbung des Herzogtums Bayern bis zu 1340* (Stuttgart, 1854).
* *Fontes rerum Germanicarum* (Stuttgart, 1843-1868), a collection of original authorities for German history during the 14th and 13th centuries. The final fourth volume of this work was edited by A. Huber following Bohmer's death.

## Death and Legacy
Johann Friedrich Bohmer died unmarried in Frankfort on October 22, 1863. His life, letters, and smaller writings were later detailed in a work by J. Janssen titled *J.F. Bohmers Leben, Briefe und kleinere Schriften*, published in Freiburg in 1868.
