## Babylonian Chronology
In the study of ancient Babylonia, the dating of royal dynasties has been a subject of historical discussion. Between 4000 and 2000 B.C., various monuments and inscriptions of kings are known. Prior to 1907, available data led some authorities to assign high dates to Khammurabi, the sixth king of the first Babylonian dynasty; these included the dates 2376-2333 B.C. (as cited by Sayce) or 2285-2242 B.C. (as cited by Johns).

However, the discovery of recent inscriptions demonstrated that the second dynasty was partly contemporaneous with the first and third dynasties, proving the earlier dates were too high. Consequently, the dates B.C. 2130-2088 were adopted by Thureau-Dangin in 1908, as well as by Ungnad in 1908 and Gressmann in 1909.
