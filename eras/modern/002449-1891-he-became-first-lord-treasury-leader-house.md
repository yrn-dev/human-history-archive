# Arthur Balfour

Arthur Balfour was a British statesman who held several high-ranking government positions, including the leadership of the House of Commons and the premiership. His career was marked by significant involvement in domestic parliamentary management, Irish governance, and foreign diplomacy.

## Early Political Roles and Leadership of the Commons
In 1888, Mr. Balfour served on the Gold and Silver Commission, where he focused on academic subjects such as currency problems from the perspective of bimetallism. Following the death of Mr. W. H. Smith in 1891, Balfour became the leader of the House of Commons and first lord of the treasury. In this capacity, he introduced a Local Government Bill for Ireland in 1892, though the project failed as the Conservative government was then "at the end of its tether."

For the subsequent three years, Balfour led the opposition with address and skill. When the Unionists returned to power in 1895, he resumed the leadership of the House. While his initial management of education proposals in 1896 was viewed by some supporters as showing a disinclination for the "continuous drudgery" of modern parliamentary management, he eventually regained his standing with the country and the House.

## Domestic and Irish Policy (1895–1900)
Between 1895 and 1900, Balfour was active in various domestic and foreign debates. He successfully saw the passage of a bill to provide Ireland with an improved system of local government. His other significant activities during this period included:
*   The championship of voluntary schools.
*   The parliamentary handling of the "crisis in the Church" resulting from the Protestant movement against ritualistic practices.
*   The proposal of a scheme for a Roman Catholic university for Ireland, a move that drew adverse criticism from his party and colleagues.

## Foreign Affairs and Diplomacy
Balfour's responsibilities expanded during the illness of Lord Salisbury in 1898 and during Salisbury's absences abroad. During these times, he was in charge of the foreign office. He was specifically tasked with conducting critical negotiations with Russia regarding the issue of railways in North China, where he was noted for being both firm and conciliatory.

## Premiership and Cabinet Reconstitution
On July 11, 1902, Mr. Balfour succeeded Lord Salisbury as prime minister with the cordial approval of all sections of the Unionist party. Shortly after taking office, he reconstituted the cabinet. In this new arrangement:
*   Balfour himself served as lord privy seal and first lord of the treasury.
*   The duke of Devonshire served as leader of the House of Lords and remained lord president of the council.
*   Lord Lansdowne remained foreign secretary.
*   Mr. (later Lord) Ritchie replaced Sir Michael Hicks-Beach as chancellor of the exchequer.
*   Mr. J. Chamberlain remained colonial secretary, while his son, Austen, became postmaster-general with a cabinet seat.
*   Other appointments included Mr. G. Wyndham as chief secretary for Ireland, Lord Selborne at the admiralty, Mr. St John Brodrick as war minister, Lord George Hamilton as secretary for India, and Mr. Akers-Douglas as home secretary.
*   Lord Balfour of Burleigh remained secretary for Scotland, Lord Dudley became lord lieutenant of Ireland, and Lord Londonderry was appointed president of the Board of Education.
*   Gerald Balfour, who had been chief secretary for Ireland from 1895 to 1900, remained president of the Board of Trade.

## Fiscal Policy and Later Cabinet Changes
A crisis later developed among free-trade Unionists. On October 1, Mr. Balfour delivered a speech at Sheffield where he reiterated his views on retaliation and free-trade. He declared his intention to lead and expressed readiness to reverse traditional fiscal policy by removing the axiom that import duties should only be levied for revenue purposes. While the National Union of Conservative Associations and those sympathetic to Mr. Chamberlain received this enthusiastically, free-traders disagreed with the formula.

This led to the resignation of the duke of Devonshire. During a subsequent reconstitution of the ministry:
*   Austen Chamberlain became chancellor of the exchequer.
*   Mr. Alfred Lyttelton was appointed colonial secretary.
*   Mr. Brodrick became secretary for India.
*   Mr. H. O. Arnold-Forster became war minister.
*   Lord Stanley was appointed postmaster-general.
*   Mr. Graham Murray became secretary for Scotland.
*   Lord Londonderry became president of the council.
*   Lord Lansdowne became leader of the House of Lords.
*   Lord Salisbury (son of the late premier) joined the cabinet as lord privy seal.

## Academic and Personal Life
Balfour was highly educated and held numerous honorary degrees. He was made LL.D. of Edinburgh University (1881), St Andrews University (1885), Cambridge University (1888), and Dublin and Glasgow Universities (1891), as well as a D.C.L. of Oxford University (1891). He served as lord rector of St Andrews University (1886) and Glasgow University (1890), and was chancellor of Edinburgh University in 1891. He was also a member of the senate of London University (1888) and a fellow of the Royal Society (1888).

His publications include *Essays and Addresses* (1893) and *The Foundations of Belief, being Notes introductory to the Study of Theology* (1895). Personally, he was a cultured musician and an enthusiastic golfer, serving as captain of the Royal and Antient Golf Club of St Andrews from 1894 to 1895. In 1904, he served as president of the British Association.
