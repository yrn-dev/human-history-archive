# Northern Nigeria

Northern Nigeria is a British protectorate established following a period of treaty, conquest, and settlement under royal charter between 1884 and 1886. In 1900, the territory was divided into North and South Nigeria after the transfer of chartered company territories to the crown.

## The Province of Bassa
The province of Bassa is located at the angle where the Benue river meets the Niger. It covers an area of 7,000 square miles and has an estimated population of approximately one and a half million people. Its boundaries are defined by the Benue to the north, the Niger to the west, the province of Muri to the east, and the frontier of Southern Nigeria to the south.

The region is characterized by heavy forests and is considered one of the richest provinces in the protectorate regarding natural products. Because of this wealth, the province hosts trading establishments from Messrs Holt and the Niger Company on the Benue and Niger, and it has been penetrated by colonies of native traders from the north.

## Administration and Conflict in Bassa
Nominal British control of the districts that now form Bassa began in 1900, though administrative authority was limited to the western half until 1903, with the capital located at Dekina. In December 1903, a military expedition was dispatched following a disturbance that resulted in the murder of the British resident. This operation extended British control to the borders of the Munshi country.

While the western portion of the province—occupied by peaceful and friendly tribes on the Niger—is organized under the protectorate's general administrative system with operative courts of justice and peaceful tax collection, other areas remain turbulent. Specifically, the Okpoto and their neighbors, the Munshis, are described as unruly.

## Trade and Smuggling
Certain prohibitions in Northern Nigeria have led to illegal trade across the border from Southern Nigeria. Spirits, the importation of which is forbidden in Northern Nigeria, are freely smuggled across this border, as are powder and arms. Additionally, the slave-trade persists in the district; it is believed that an overland route for slaves has been established through eastern Bassa leading to the Benue.

## Ethnography and Culture of Bassa
The province has not been penetrated by Moslem influence and is largely inhabited by pagans described as unruly and warlike. The Bassas themselves are a remarkable pagan race found in small colonies throughout nearly every province of the Northern Nigeria protectorate; they are characterized as industrious, naturally peaceful, and clever agriculturists.

Historically, in the early 16th century, the Igbira (also known as Ibo or Okpoto) were among the most powerful pagan peoples in Nigeria, with their capital at Iddah. Over time, the Bassas conquered the western portion of the state and the Munshis conquered the eastern portion. The Okpoto maintained control of the south and a wedge-shaped district that partially divides the Munshis and Bassas. Like the Okpoto, the Munshis are considered a warlike and unruly race, despite being good agriculturists.

## Other Regions and Towns
### Bida
Bida is a town and administrative district serving as the capital of the province of Nupe. Founded in 1859 upon the establishment of Fula rule in Nupe, the town is walled and of considerable size. It is located 25 miles north-by-east of Muraji on the Niger. Bida is known for its glass factory and flourishing industries in the dyeing of cloths and the preparation of indigo. The inhabitants, mostly Hausa, engage in extensive trade and are noted for elegant embossed copper and brass work, specifically "Bida goblets" which blend the two metals. In 1909, Bida was connected by railway to Baro, the Northern Nigeria railway's river terminus.

### Bussa
Bussa is a town on the west bank of the Niger and serves as the capital of northern Borgu. It is situated just above the rapids that limit the navigability of the Niger by steamer from the sea. Between 1894 and 1898, possession of Bussa was disputed between France and Great Britain. France acknowledged the British claim in a convention in June 1898, granting Britain control of the lower Niger. Historically, the town is noted as the site where Mungo Park was attacked by inhabitants and drowned in 1806 during his second expedition to trace the Niger's course.

## The Fula People
The Fula (also known as Fulbe, Follani, Fellatah, Fullan, or Fulde on the Benue) are a people whose name—including the Kororofa name "Abate" meaning "white"—likely refers to their light reddish hue. Their language is akin to the Hamito-Semitic group in its rudiments and uses suffixes that resemble Negro dialects. The language features two grammatical genders: human and non-human.
