# LOCH AWE

Loch Awe is the longest freshwater lake in Scotland. It is situated in mid-Argyllshire at an elevation of 116 feet above the sea. The lake covers an area of nearly 16 square miles and extends for a total length of 23 miles from Ford to Kilchurn Castle.

## Geography and Topography
The loch follows a south-west to north-east direction. Its breadth varies across its length, ranging from one-third of a mile to 3 miles at its upper end. At this upper extremity, the lake takes the shape of a crescent; one arm of this crescent extends toward Glen Orchy, while the other leads to the point where the river Awe exits the lake.

The character of the scenery differs significantly between the two ends of the loch. The lower half is described as tame and pastoral, whereas the scenery at the upper extremity is majestic.

## Notable Landmarks and Islands
Loch Awe contains numerous islands. The most well-known among these is Inishail, which houses the ruins of a convent and a church that was suppressed during the Reformation.

At the extreme north-eastern end of the lake is an islet that becomes part of the mainland when water levels are low. This islet is the site of the imposing ruins of Kilchurn Castle. Due to its romantic surroundings, the castle has become a favorite subject for landscape painters.

## Surrounding Area and Natural Resources
The river Awe issues from the north-western horn of the loch and is noted for providing excellent fishing for salmon and trout. Approximately 2 miles from the loch is the village of Dalmally, which is considered one of the pleasantest villages in the Highlands and experiences great vogue during midsummer.

## Historical Associations
The family of Sir Colin Campbell of Loch Awe, an ancestor of the duke of Argyll, held the hereditary keepership of Dunoon Castle, which was conferred upon them by Robert Bruce. Additionally, Sir Duncan Campbell of Loch Awe founded a Collegiate chapel in 1442 at Kilmun, located on the northern shore of Holy Loch; this chapel served as the burial-ground for the Argyll family. In Loch Tay, a ruined castle located on an islet once belonged to the Campbells of Lochawe.
