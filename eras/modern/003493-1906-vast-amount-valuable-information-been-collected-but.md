# Statistical Records and Data Collection

The collection and analysis of statistical data have been employed across various disciplines, including national finance, demographics, linguistics, and banking, to establish trends and provide a basis for administrative and academic research.

## National Finance and Taxation
In the context of income tax, historical rates have fluctuated significantly, reaching as high as 16d. between 1855 and 1857, and dropping as low as 2d. from 1874 to 1876. By the 1906-1907 period, it was estimated that each penny of the tax produced a revenue of £2,666,867. 

Efforts to adjust inequalities in the income tax led to several reports from select committees in 1851-1852 and 1861, as well as a Departmental Committee in 1905. A 1906 select committee investigated the practicability of graduating the income tax and differentiating between precarious and permanent incomes. Their report concluded that graduation via the extension of the existing system of abatements was practicable. However, they noted that this could not be applied to all income levels with satisfactory results. The committee suggested that the limits of prudent extension would be reached if the rate of tax collected at the source increased to a point where the excess collected over what was retained caused serious inconvenience to individual taxpayers and trade and commerce. They determined these limits would not be exceeded by raising the abatement allowance to £1000 or more.

## Demographics and Geography
The use of public registers for births, deaths, and marriages has provided a foundation for population estimates. For example, records from the *Contrôlee General* at Paris for the years 1770 to 1774 showed an annual average of 928,918 children (479,649 boys and 449,269 girls) across the kingdom. In the province of French Hainault, which had an average of 257,097 inhabitants based on enumerations from 1773 to 1776, there were 9,906 births. Using a proportion of 1 to 26, the population of France was inferred to be 24,151,868; using a more moderate proportion of 1 to 25, the population was estimated at 23,222,950.

In the field of geography, Büsching is regarded as the creator of modern Statistical Geography. His major work, *Erdebeschreibung*, was published in seven parts. The first four parts, covering Europe, appeared between 1754 and 1761, while the fifth part, published in 1768, focused on Asia, specifically Arabia and Asiatic Turkey.

## Banking and Financial Statistics
Trustworthy statistics for banking in the United Kingdom are limited, with few extending back more than fifty years. English banks were historically reticent, and Scottish banks did not generally publish accounts until 1865. While Irish joint-stock bank cash balances and deposits were published collectively starting in 1840 by Dr. Neilson Hancock, general positions remained estimates until recently.

A 1851 paper by W. Newmarch provided an early basis for estimates, suggesting total deposits in Ireland, Scotland, England, and Wales (including the Bank of England) were between £250,000,000 and £360,000,000. By 1872, Palgrave's *Notes on Banking* estimated this amount at £430,000,000 to £450,000,000, excluding bank capitals and deposits in discount houses. By the close of 1906, total deposits (including acceptances) reached approximately £997,000,000, with total resources—including note circulation of £177,500,000, reserves, and capitals—recorded for that year.

Regarding cheque turnover, the London Clearing House provides a primary metric. In 1839, clearings totaled £954,401,600 across 29 banks. This rose to £9,150,269,000 in 1899 (19 banks) and reached £12,711,334,000 in 1906 (18 banks). The total yearly turnover in cheques is estimated to be at least twice the amount registered by the London Clearing House when accounting for other exchanges and branch settlements.

## Linguistic Data Collection
Extensive information has been gathered regarding various language groups. In the study of Bantu languages, Carl Meinhof collected a vast amount of information in his 1899 essays and his 1906 work, *Grundzuege einer vergleichenden Grammatik der Bantusprachen*, though his deductions sometimes differ from other authorities. Other contributors include Dr. W. I. Bleek, R. N. Cust, Father J. Torrend, and Sir H. H. Johnston. Specialized dictionaries, such as Dr. L. Krapf's *Swahili-English Dictionary* (1882) and Rev. D. C. Scott's *An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mananja Language of British Central Africa* (1891), have preserved invaluable linguistic data, including obsolete words and prefixes.

Similarly, the study of the Basque language has been documented in works such as Julien Vinson's *Essai d'une bibliographie de la langue basque* (1891, 1898), which is described as a mine of information on principal works. Other significant contributions include the 1906 *Diccionario vascongado espanol-francais* by R. M. de Azkue and the *Grammaire basque* by L'Abbe Ithurry (1895-1906).
