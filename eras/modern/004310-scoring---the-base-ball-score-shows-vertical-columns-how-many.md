# Scoring

Scoring systems vary significantly across different sports and games, ranging from the detailed statistical tracking of base-ball to the point-accumulation methods used in pool, billiards, and bagatelle.

## Base-Ball Scoring
In base-ball, the score is recorded in vertical columns to track a variety of player and game statistics. These columns include the number of times a player has been at bat—excluding sacrifice-hits and bases taken on balls—as well as the number of runs scored and base-hits made. The score also tracks sacrifice-hits, the number of opponents put out, and "assists," which occur when a player helps put out an opponent, such as throwing a ground ball to first-base.

Additionally, the score records errors, though balks, bases on balls, and "passed balls" (those not held by the catcher) are not counted as errors and are placed in regular columns. Other recorded data include stolen bases, double and triple plays, extra long hits, the number of men struck out or hit by the ball for each pitcher, the name of the umpire, and the total time of the game.

These records are used to calculate averages for professional and amateur players:
*   **Batting Record:** Calculated by dividing the number of hits made by the number of times at bat.
*   **Fielding Record:** Calculated by dividing the number of accepted chances (put outs plus assists) by the total chances. For example, a player with 1188 put outs, 64 assists, and 15 errors has a fielding average of 1252 divided by 1267 (where 1000 represents perfect fielding).

## Pool and Billiards Scoring
Scoring in pool and billiards often involves the aggregate value of balls pocketed or specific maneuvers.

**Pool Variations**
*   **Pyramid Pool:** The winner is the player who pockets all fifteen balls and achieves the greatest aggregate of the numbers on those balls.
*   **Chicago Pool:** The player scoring the highest aggregate of numbers in a frame wins. Players must strike the lowest-numbered ball remaining; failing to do so, or pocketing their own ball, results in forfeiting the points represented by that ball. Missing the ball played at or committing other fouls results in a forfeit of three points (or sometimes the number on the ball).
*   **Bottle Pool:** This game uses a cue-ball, the 1 and 2 balls, and a leather bottle. Points are awarded for caroms on two balls (2 points), pocketing the 1-ball (1 point), pocketing the 2-ball (2 points), upsetting the bottle from a carom (5 points), or upsetting the bottle to a standing position (10 points). The game is typically played to 31 points; scoring more than this means the player is "burst" and must restart.
*   **Thirty-Four:** Scoring is done via caroms or pocketing two object-balls; a player is "burst" if they exceed exactly 34 points.

**Billiards and Snooker**
In "billiards proper," players compete for 100 or more points. Red hazards score three points, while white hazards and cannons score two. Opponents score points when a player commits a "coup" (running a ball off the table or into a pocket without hitting another ball) for 3 points, or an ordinary miss for 1 point.

In Snooker Pool, pyramid balls count for one point, while colored balls have higher values (yellow 2, green 3, and so on). A player must pocket a red ball before playing a colored ball. If a player misses a ball or hits a ball of higher value before the proper one, the value of that ball is deducted from their score.

## Bagatelle Scoring
Bagatelle features several varieties with distinct scoring rules:
*   **Bagatelle Proper:** Players aim to play eight balls into holes after touching a black ball. The black ball counts double. Balls that miss the black ball, roll back across the baulk-line, or are forced off the table are considered "dead."
*   **Sans Egal:** Two players use red and white balls. The goal is to hole the black ball and their own balls. If a player holes an opponent's ball, it counts for the opponent. Games are decided by rounds or by reaching 21 or 31 points.
*   **The Cannon Game:** A cannon (carom) counts for 2 points. Holing the white object-ball scores 2 and the black scores 3, provided a cannon was made in the same stroke; otherwise, the score goes to the adversary. Missing the white object-ball gives 1 point to the adversary, and missing the black gives 5. In the Irish Cannon Game, all pocketed balls count for the adversary.
*   **Mississippi and Trou Madame:** In Mississippi, balls must strike a cushion before going through numbered arches; the black ball counts double. If a ball goes through an arch without hitting a cushion, the score goes to the adversary. In Trou Madame, the cushion requirement is removed, and any ball falling into a cup scores for the opponent.

## General Scoring and Handicapping
Some games utilize specific paper-based scoring systems. One method uses two vertical columns divided by a horizontal line, with honours scored above and tricks below. A more precise method uses four columns (two for tricks and two for honours) with a separate line for each hand to easily rectify discrepancies.

To balance unequal players, handicapping is used. This may include giving the weaker player a starting number of points, requiring the stronger player to "owe" points before they begin reckoning their own score, or agreeing that the better player will not count breaks below a certain figure.
