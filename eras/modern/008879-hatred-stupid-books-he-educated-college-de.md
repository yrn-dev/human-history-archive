# Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux

## Early Life and Character
Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux was a French critic and poet born on November 1, 1636, in Paris on the rue de Jerusalem. He was the fifteenth child of Gilles Boileau, who served as a clerk in the parlement. The name Despreaux was taken from a small property located near Villeneuve Saint-Georges at Crosne. 

Boileau's early years were marked by difficulty; his mother passed away when he was two years old. Due to a delicate constitution and a perceived lack of care during this period, he developed a hard and unsympathetic outlook. According to Sainte-Beuve, this disposition was a result of both the uninspiring circumstances of his early childhood and the general character of the era in which he lived. Boileau is described as having lived without illusions from a young age, driven by a single, defining passion: "the hatred of stupid books."

## Education and Professional Transitions
Boileau's formal education began at the College de Beauvais. Following this, he was sent to the Sorbonne to pursue the study of theology. However, he eventually abandoned theology in favor of law. He was called to the bar on December 4, 1656.

His experience in the legal profession was brief and unsuccessful. Boileau recoiled from the practice of law in disgust, expressing bitter complaints regarding the level of chicanery that occurred under the guise of justice and law.

## Literary Career
The death of Boileau's father in 1657 provided him with a small fortune. This financial independence allowed him to leave the legal profession and devote himself entirely to the pursuit of letters.

## Family Background
Boileau came from a family with academic and ecclesiastical connections. Two of his brothers achieved notable distinction: Jacques Boileau, who contributed to church history and became a canon of the Sainte-Chapelle, and Gilles Boileau (1631-1669), who authored a translation of Epictetus.
