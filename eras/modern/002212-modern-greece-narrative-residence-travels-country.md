# Modern Greece, A Narrative of a Residence and Travels in that Country

*Modern Greece, A Narrative of a Residence and Travels in that Country* is a published work authored by Henry Martyn Baird.

## Author Biography
Henry Martyn Baird (1832–1906) was an American educationalist and historian born on January 17, 1832, in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. He was the son of Robert Baird, a Presbyterian author and preacher who advocated for temperance in Europe and the United States. During his early youth, Baird spent eight years living with his father in Geneva and Paris. 

Baird's academic background included graduation from New York University in 1850 and the Princeton Theological Seminary in 1856. Between 1853 and 1855, he was a student at the Union Theological Seminary in New York City. His professional career included a four-year tenure as a tutor at the College of New Jersey (which later became Princeton University). From 1859 until his death on November 11, 1906, in New York City, he served as a professor of Greek literature and language at New York University.

While Baird is most recognized for his high-order scholarship and judicial temper in his three-part history of the Huguenots—covering the rise of the Huguenots of France, the Revocation of the Edict of Nantes, and Henry of Navarre—he authored several other works. These include a biography of his father, *The Life of the Rev. Robert Baird, D.D.* (1866), and *Theodore Beza, the Counsellor of the French Reformation* (1899).

## Context of the Work
Baird published *Modern Greece, A Narrative of a Residence and Travels in that Country* in 1856. The work was informed by his personal experiences living in Greece and Italy for two years following his graduation from New York University in 1850.
