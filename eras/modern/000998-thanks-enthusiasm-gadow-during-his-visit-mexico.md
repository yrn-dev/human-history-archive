# H. Gadow and the Study of the Axolotl in Mexico

## Field Research in Mexico
In the summer of 1902, H. Gadow visited Mexico, where his enthusiasm for the region's fauna led to a better understanding of the environmental conditions of the axolotl near Mexico City. During his visit, Gadow conducted a careful examination of two specific lakes: Xochimilco and Chalco. He found that axolotls occur in abundance in these two lakes and are collected there for the market.

Gadow's research provided three primary findings. First, he determined that axolotls are entirely absent from the Lago de Texcoco, a discovery that disposed of the Weismannian explanation. Second, he confirmed a statement made by A. Duges regarding the existence of a second species of *Amblystoma* near Mexico. This second species is found at a higher altitude and undergoes normal metamorphosis, which may explain observations by Velasco that regularly transforming *Amblystomas* exist near the city. Third, he documented the specific habitats of the axolotl in Lakes Chalco and Xochimilco.

## The Environment of Lakes Chalco and Xochimilco
Gadow described Lakes Chalco and Xochimilco as a "paradise," noting that they are situated approximately 10 feet higher than Lake Texcoco and are separated from it by several hills. The geography of the area includes high mountains sloping down to the southern shores, featuring a belt of fertile pastures, trees, shrubs, and various ravines, rocks, and little streams. Gadow observed that there are thousands of opportunities for newts to leave the lake should they wish to do so.

In Lake Xochimilco, Gadow noted the presence of powerful springs. Away from these springs, the water is dark and muddy, containing decomposing and fresh suspended vegetable matter. The lake is teeming with worms, *Daphniae*, larvae of insects, fish, and axolotls.

## Life Cycle and Behavior of the Axolotl
According to Gadow's account and the knowledge of native fishermen, axolotls breed at the beginning of February. The eggs are fastened to water plants, after which thousands of small larvae swarm. These larvae grow rapidly; by June, they have developed into "big, fat creatures" ready for the market. 

Gadow reported that in the later part of the summer, the axolotls are said to take to the rushes, and they become scarce during the autumn. Crucially, Gadow noted that no axolotls have ever been known to metamorphose or leave the water, and no perfect *Amblystomas* are found in the vicinity of Lakes Chalco and Xochimilco.

## Other Contributions of H. Gadow
Beyond his work in Mexico, H. Gadow has contributed to the field of myology (the study of muscles). His work includes a volume titled *Voegel* within Bronn's *Klassen und Ordnungen des Tierreichs*, which contains lists of myological literature. He also authored a paper in 1880 titled *Zur vergl. Anat. der Muskulatur des Beckens und der hinteren Gliedmasse der Ratiten*, focusing on the comparative anatomy of the pelvic musculature and hind limbs of ratites. Additionally, Gadow published findings in the journal *Nature* in 1903.
