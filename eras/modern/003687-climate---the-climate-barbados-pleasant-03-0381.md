# Regional Climates and Environmental Factors

The study of global climates reveals a vast array of temperature ranges, precipitation patterns, and health impacts across different geographical regions. These conditions are influenced by natural topography, oceanic proximity, and, increasingly, human intervention.

## Caribbean and Pacific Islands

In the West Indies, Barbados is characterized by a pleasant climate with seasons divided into wet and dry. The dry season, which also serves as the cold season, extends from December to the end of May. Temperatures typically range between 70° F. and 86° F., and rarely drop below 65° F. The island receives an average annual rainfall of approximately 60 inches, with September being the wettest month. For eight months of the year, the tropical heat is tempered by invigorating N.E. trade winds. Due to the porous nature of the soil, the extent of cultivation, and an absence of swamps, the island is free from miasma and fever. While the climate is noted for arresting the decay of vital power in old age and benefiting pulmonary diseases in early stages, elephantiasis (known as "Barbados leg") and leprosy among negroes are present.

Grenada, located 140 m. S.W. of Barbados, also possesses a good climate with equable temperatures. In the low country, the average yearly temperature is 82° F., though it is cooler in the highlands. Rainfall is very heavy, reaching 200 inches in some areas; specifically, the average annual rainfall is 79.07 inches at St Georges and 164 inches at Grand Etang. The rainy season lasts from May to December. Because of its excellent climate and sea-bathing, Grenada serves as a health resort for neighboring islands, particularly Trinidad.

In the southern Pacific Ocean, Lord Howe island is described as having a pleasant climate.

## Asian Climates

The climate of Calcutta is hot and damp, though it features a pleasant cold season from November to March. The monsoon months, spanning June to October, are marked by malaria and damp heat. The mean annual temperature is 79° F., with a mean maximum of 102° F. in May and a mean minimum of 48° F. in January. Historically, the city's climate was very unhealthy, but modern drainage and sanitation have led to great improvements.

In Korea, the climate is described as superb for nine months of the year. The three months characterized by heat, rain, and damp are not considered injurious to health, although Koreans suffer from malaria. In Seoul, the summer mean temperature is approximately 75° F. and the winter mean is 33° F. The average annual rainfall is 36.3 inches. Rains occur in July and August on the north-east and west coasts, and from April to July on the south coast.

Other varied climates are found in regions bordering the Black Sea. On the plateau, winters are long, cold, and snowy, while summers are very hot with cool nights. The north coast experiences cold winters with heavy snow and rain driven by winds from the Russian steppes. The south coast has mild winters with occasional frosts and very great summer heat. The west coast is moderate, though Smyrna feels the influence of cold north winds. A notable summer feature is the *inbat*, a north wind that blows from the sea almost daily from noon until near sunset.

## North American Regional Variations

In the United States, Idaho exhibits several distinct climate zones. Between 1898 and 1903, the mean annual temperature for the state was 45.5° F. The mildest regions are the valley of the Boisé, Payette, and Weiser rivers, and the Snake river basin between Lewiston and Twin Falls; in 1902, this area had a mean annual temperature of 52° F., a maximum of 106° F., and a minimum of -13° F. Conversely, the Lost river regions, Camas prairie, and Upper Snake basin are much colder, with a 1902 low of -35° F. and a high of 101° F. The mean annual rainfall for the state in 1903 was 16.60 inches, ranging from a low of 5.69 inches at Garnet to a high of 37.70 inches at Murray.

## Human Influence on Climate

Human activity serves as a powerful influence on the environment. Through the use of the axe, plough, and fire, humans alter the world. The destruction of forests makes seasons more extreme, a phenomenon observed in the northeastern states of the United States. This deforestation also leaves soil unprotected from rain, leading to it being washed away and leaving barren rock, as has occurred in Dalmatia, Spain, and ancient South Arabia. Conversely, irrigation can mitigate climate and restore deserts to life, a process currently occurring in Australia and Northwest India.
