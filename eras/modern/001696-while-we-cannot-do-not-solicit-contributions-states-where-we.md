# Project Gutenberg

## Origins and Concept
Project Gutenberg was originated by Professor Michael S. Hart. The core concept of the project was to create a library of electronic works that could be shared freely with any person. For a period of forty years, Professor Hart was responsible for the production and distribution of Project Gutenberg eBooks, operating with the assistance of a loose network of volunteers.

## eBook Production and Copyright
The eBooks provided by Project Gutenberg are frequently developed using several different printed editions. The project ensures that these editions are confirmed to be not protected by copyright within the United States, provided that a copyright notice is not included. Because of this process, the electronic books are not necessarily kept in compliance with any one specific paper edition.

## Donation Policies and Legal Constraints
Project Gutenberg accepts donations through various methods, including credit card donations, online payments, and checks. While the project gratefully accepts international donations, it cannot provide statements regarding the tax treatment of funds received from outside the United States. The organization notes that its small staff is overwhelmed by U.S. laws alone.

Regarding domestic contributions, the project does not solicit contributions from states where it has not met specific solicitation requirements. However, there is no known prohibition against the project accepting unsolicited donations from donors in those states who approach the organization with offers to donate.

## Access and Information
The primary point of entry for most users is the Project Gutenberg website, which houses the main search facility at www.gutenberg.org. Current addresses and methods for donating can be found on the project's web pages, or specifically at www.gutenberg.org/donate.
