# Chronic Bronchitis and Structural Alterations of the Bronchial Tubes

Chronic bronchitis is a condition characterized by the inflammation of the mucous membrane of the bronchial tubes. It may develop independently or as a result of repeated attacks of acute bronchitis. While it can affect individuals of any age, it occurs more frequently in those advanced in life. In children, the condition may appear later in life as a consequence of asthma, which in turn can lead to emphysema.

## Structural Alterations and Complications
The progression of chronic bronchitis leads to significant alterations in the structure of the affected bronchial tubes. The mucous membrane of these tubes may become ulcerated or thickened. In some instances, a permanent dilatation of the bronchi occurs, a condition known as bronchiectasis. This dilatation is typically cylindrical, though it can rarely be saccular, and generally affects the medium and smaller sized tubes. This structural change is often accompanied by profuse foetid expectoration.

In cases where the disease is long-standing, the nutrition of the lungs becomes impaired. This leads to further complications, including the dilatation of the air-tubes, referred to as emphysema, which results in more or less constant breathlessness.

## Symptoms and Clinical Presentation
The typical history of chronic bronchitis involves a cough that recurs during the colder seasons, often departing entirely in the summer, earning it the name "winter cough." However, in advanced cases, the cough is seldom completely absent and may be triggered by slight causes, such as changes in weather. The cough is generally more troublesome in the morning than during the day.

Patients typically experience free and copious expectoration; when this is exceptionally abundant, it is termed bronchorrhoea. While the symptoms and auscultatory signs are similar to those of acute bronchitis, the pain and febrile disturbance are much less marked.

## Causes and Risk Factors
Chronic bronchitis can arise secondarily to other ailments, serving as a serious complication in cases of gout, syphilis, heart disease, and Bright's disease of the kidneys. 

Occupational exposure also plays a significant role. The inhalation of vegetable dust can produce bronchitis through the growth of organisms carried by the dust and the irritation caused by the dust particles. Consequently, those employed as grain-shovellers and millers are especially liable to the condition, followed by workers in cotton factories and weavers.

## Treatment and Management
Treatment strategies for chronic bronchitis are determined by the patient's age, the severity of the case, and whether complications are present. Prime importance is placed on general health, particularly for those whose work involves exposure. Recommended interventions include:

*   **Supplements:** The use of tonics combined with cod-liver oil.
*   **Protection:** The use of a respirator during damp or very cold weather.
*   **Environmental Changes:** In aggravated cases where cold air triggers fresh attacks, patients may be advised to remove to a more genial climate during winter or remain confined to a warm room.
