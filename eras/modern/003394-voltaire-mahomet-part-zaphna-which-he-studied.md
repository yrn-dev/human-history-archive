# Voltaire's Mahomet and the Role of Zaphna

The play *Mahomet*, written by the French author Voltaire (François Marie Arouet, 1694–1778), is a tragedy that presents a fictionalized account of the life of the prophet. The work is noted for its departure from historical accuracy in favor of dramatic effect and the author's specific philosophical perspectives.

## The Character of Zaphna and John Bannister
In the theatrical history of the play, the character of Zaphna was portrayed by John Bannister (1760–1836). Bannister, who had initially studied to be a painter before pursuing a career on the stage, made a formal appearance in James Miller's version of *Mahomet* at Drury Lane in 1778. For this production, Bannister studied the part of Zaphna under the guidance of Garrick. The cast of this particular production also included Mrs. Robinson, who performed the role of Palmira ("Perdita").

## Narrative and Historical Accuracy
Voltaire's depiction of the events following the conquest of Mecca is characterized by the portrayal of "the most horrid crimes." The author of the play admitted that his narrative was not supported by the truth of history. Voltaire justified this creative choice by alleging that anyone who wages war against their own country in the name of God is capable of anything. 

This representation of the tragedy was viewed critically by some; for instance, a Turkish ambassador in Paris was reported to have been much scandalized by the play's depiction of these events.

## Voltaire's Influence and Perspective
Voltaire was a prolific author of enormous industry, with works spanning seventy to ninety-four volumes depending on the edition. He dealt extensively with public affairs and history, maintaining correspondences with prominent figures such as Frederick the Great of Prussia, Catherine the Great of Russia, and Louis XV. 

His intellectual influence extended to other historians, including Gibbon, whose outlook was described as cosmopolitan and French. Voltaire's approach to history often involved a preference for the Turks over Christians in certain historical disputes. He also expressed admiration for *le Philosophe Turc*, though critics have noted that he may have been an intolerant bigot in his own way.

## Literary Legacy
Voltaire's style and approach to writing had a significant impact on European literature. In the Netherlands, writers such as the baroness Juliana Cornelia de Lannoy and Onno Zwier van Haren—the latter of whom wrote a series of tragedies in the manner of Voltaire—were associated with his school of writing.
