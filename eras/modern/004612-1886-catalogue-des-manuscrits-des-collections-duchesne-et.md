# 1886 and the Catalogue des manuscrits des collections Duchesne et Bréquigny

## Academic and Literary Publications of 1886
The year 1886 saw the publication of several scholarly works across various fields of history, theology, and linguistics. In the realm of ecclesiastical history, the French scholar and ecclesiastic Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne published the first volume of his learned edition of the *Liber pontificalis*. This work served as a continuation and completion of the results Duchesne had previously attained in his French thesis.

In other academic disciplines, the year 1886 is associated with the publication of *Obichai i pesni turetskikh Serbov* by Yastreboff in St Petersburg. Additionally, within the study of the Babi sect, Baron Victor Rosen published volume iii of the *Collections scientifiques de l'Institut des Langues orientales* (pp. 1-51).

## The Catalogue des manuscrits des collections Duchesne et Bréquigny
The *Catalogue des manuscrits des collections Duchesne et Bréquigny* is a bibliographic record of manuscript collections housed in the Bibliothèque Nationale. This catalogue was authored by René Poupardin and published in 1905. It serves as a reference for the collections associated with Duchesne and Louis George Oudard Feudrix de Bréquigny.

## Louis George Oudard Feudrix de Bréquigny
Louis George Oudard Feudrix de Bréquigny was a researcher and supervisor of historical documents. His work included contributions to the *Table chronologique des diplômes, chartes, lettres, et actes imprimés concernant l'histoire de France*, for which he collaborated with Mouchet between 1769 and 1783. Bréquigny was also tasked with the supervision of a large collection of documents pertaining to French history, similar to Rymer's *Foedera*, and he published the first volume, *Diplomatat. Chartae*, in 1791.

Beginning in 1776 at the request of the minister Bertin, Bréquigny started a collection titled *Mémoires concernant l'histoire, les sciences, les lettres, et les arts des Chinois*; however, the French Revolution interrupted this project after fifteen volumes had been released.

## Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne
Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne (born September 13, 1843) was a French ecclesiastic and scholar. His early career involved scientific missions to Asia Minor in 1876 and Mount Athos in 1874, which initially directed his interests toward the ancient history of the Christian churches of the East. He later shifted his focus to the Western church.

Duchesne earned his *docteur es lettres* in 1877 with two theses: an *Etude sur le Liber pontificalis* and a dissertation *De Macario magnete*. He served as a professor at the Catholic Institute in Paris for eight years, where he taught church history using scientific criticism, though his course was eventually suspended due to the views of traditionalist exegetists. In November 1885, he became a lecturer at the Ecole Pratique des Hautes Etudes.

Beyond the *Liber pontificalis*, Duchesne authored several other significant works:
* *Origines du culte chretien, etude sur la liturgie latine avant Charlemagne* (1889).
* *Memoire sur l'origine des dioceses episcopaux dans l'ancienne Gaule* (1890).
* *Fastes episcopaux dans l'ancienne Gaule* (Vol. i, 1894; Vol. ii, 1899).
* *Catalogues episcopaux de la province de Tours* (1898).

Duchesne was elected to the Academie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in 1888 and later served as the director of the French school of archaeology at Rome. He also co-authored the *Martyrologium Hieronymianum* with J.B. de Rossi, which preceded the second volume of the *Acta Sanctorum* in 1894.
