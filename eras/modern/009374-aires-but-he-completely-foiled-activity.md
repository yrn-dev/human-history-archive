# Buenos Aires and the War of Independence in Upper Peru

The patriots of Buenos Aires played a central role in the War of Independence as it pertained to Upper Peru, a region later known as Bolivia. After successfully liberating the interior provinces of the Rio de la Plata from Spanish dominion, the patriots directed their military efforts against enemies holding Upper Peru.

## Course of the Conflict
The warfare in this region was nearly uninterrupted from July 1809 until August 1825. The conflict was characterized by alternating successes between the South American patriot forces and the Spanish royalist forces. The primary scene of action for these engagements lay between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta.

The initial phase of the war saw a successful invasion of Upper Peru by the army of Buenos Aires. Under the command of General Balcarce, this army defeated Spanish troops twice and celebrated the first anniversary of independence near Lake Titicaca in May 1811. However, the patriot army soon became disorganized due to the negligence and dissolute conduct of its leaders. Consequently, in June 1811, the Spanish army, led by Gey fol Goyeneche, attacked and defeated the patriots, driving them back into Jujuy.

## Major Battles and Insurrections
For four years, victory shifted between the Spaniards and the patriots. This period concluded in 1815 with a battle between Oruro and Potosi, which resulted in a total rout of the patriot forces. Following this defeat, an insurrection occurred involving Creoles and Indians from the southern provinces of Peru, who sought the independence of the entire country. This revolt was quickly suppressed by the royalists.

## The Role of Gaucho Troops
In 1816, the Spanish general Laserna was appointed as the commander-in-chief of Upper Peru. Laserna attempted to invade the Argentine provinces with the intention of marching on Buenos Aires. However, he was completely foiled in this objective by the activity of irregular *gaucho* troops from Jujuy and Salta, which forced his retirement.

## Guerrilla Warfare and Later Expeditions
Following the failure of Laserna's invasion, the patriots of Upper Peru maintained a guerrilla warfare for six years. These forces took refuge primarily in the mountains of the province of Yungas, from which they frequently harassed royalist troops. In June 1823, an expedition was prepared with great activity and zeal by General Santa Cruz.
