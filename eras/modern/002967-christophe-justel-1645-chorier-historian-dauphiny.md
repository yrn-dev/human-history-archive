# Christophe Justel and the Forged Genealogies of the Maison d'Auvergne

## The Works of Christophe Justel
Christophe Justel was the author of the *Histoire de la maison d'Auvergne*, published in 1645. This work is noted for the production of spurious genealogies, which were created because authentic documents to support certain pretensions could not be located.

## Chorier and the Expansion of Forgeries
Following the work of Justel, Chorier, a historian of Dauphiny, contributed to the fabrication of family records. In 1672, Chorier included a forged deed in the second volume of his history. This specific document was designed to establish a connection between the La Tours of Auvergne and the La Tours of Dauphiny.

## The Organization of Forged Documents
The creation of false records eventually evolved into a regular manufactory of forged documents. This operation was organized by Jean de Bar, who was an intimate companion of the cardinal de Bouillon. The individuals involved in these forgeries were sufficiently skillful to deceive prominent scholars. On July 23, 1695, a unanimously favorable report was issued regarding these documents by experts including Baluze, Dom Thierry Ruinart, and Dom Jean Mabillon, the latter of whom was the founder of Diplomatics.

## Legal Consequences and the Role of Baluze
The activities of the forgers led to a war of pamphlets initiated by the enemies of cardinal de Bouillon. In March 1698, Baluze wrote a *Letter* in response, though it proved nothing. By 1700, Jean de Bar and his accomplices were arrested. Following a long and searching inquiry, they were declared guilty in 1704.

Despite the legal verdict, Baluze remained obstinate, convinced that the incriminated documents were genuine. With the financial encouragement and support of cardinal de Bouillon, Baluze attempted to redo the work of Justel. He produced a *Table genealogique* in 1705, followed by the *Histoire genealogique de la maison d'Auvergne* in 1709. This later work included "Proofs" that contained the deeds already pronounced spurious.

## Later Life of Baluze
In 1710, Baluze suffered a sudden disgrace alongside his patron, the cardinal de Bouillon. He was exiled from Paris and stripped of his pensions, benefices, and appointments. During his exile, he continued his scholarly work, publishing the *Historiae Tutelensis libri tres*, a history of his native town, in 1717. Baluze eventually returned to Paris, where he died on July 28, 1718, remaining unconvinced of his errors. Analysis of his correspondence with the cardinal suggests that he may have been the victim of clever cheats rather than an accomplice.
