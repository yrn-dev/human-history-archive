# Broach and the British Acquisition of Bombay

## Overview of Broach
Broach, also known as Bharuch, is an ancient city and modern district located in the northern division of Bombay in British India. The city is situated on the right bank of the Nerbudda river, approximately 203 miles north of Bombay and 30 miles from the sea. The city area, including its suburbs, covers 2-1/6 square miles. As of 1901, the population of the city was 42,896, while the wider district had a population of 291,763, representing a 15% decrease from the previous decade.

Historically, Broach is identified as the Barygaza of Arrian and Ptolemy, as well as the Barakacheva of the Chinese traveller Hsüan Tsang.

## Political History and Transfers of Power
Broach was part of the kingdom formed upon the Mahommedan conquest of Gujarat. In 1572, after Akbar overthrew that state, Broach was annexed to the Mogul empire and governed by a Nawab. By 1685, the Mahrattas became the masters of the city, holding it in subordination to the peshwa until 1772. At that time, it was captured by a force led by General Wedderburn, who died during the assault.

The city's status shifted frequently between the late 18th and early 19th centuries:
* **1782–1783:** Following the first collision between the English and the Mahratta power, the treaty of Salbai (1782) resulted in Salsette being ceded to the British, while Broach was handed over to Sindhia. In 1783, the British ceded Broach to Sindhia in acknowledgment of certain services.
* **1803:** During the second Mahratta war, a detachment commanded by Colonel Woodington stormed the city. Under the treaty of Sarji Anjangaom, Sindhia finally ceded Broach to the East India Company.

## Geography and Economy of the Broach District
The District of Broach covers an area of 1467 square miles, consisting primarily of a rich, highly cultivated alluvial plain at the mouth of the Nerbudda river. The region is well-supplied with rivers, including the Kim to the south and the Mahi to the north. While the land lacks forests, it is not without trees.

The population of the district is composed of several distinct races or castes who speak the Gujarati dialect but live in separate villages. These include Kunbi (or Voro/Bora) and Koli villages, as well as lands cultivated by high castes such as Parsees, Brahmans, and Rajputs.

The chief industry is dealing in cotton, with dealers organized into a guild. The district features several cotton ginning and pressing factories, some on a very large scale, as well as cotton mills in Broach city. Other industries include large flour-mills, though handloom-weaving is nearly extinct. The principal crops grown are pulse, wheat, millet, and cotton. The district was severely impacted by the famine of 1899-1900. Infrastructure is supported by the Bombay & Baroda railway, which utilizes an iron-girder bridge of 67 spans to cross the Nerbudda opposite the city.

## The Development of the Bombay Presidency
The broader administrative context of Broach involves the growth of the Bombay presidency. The East India Company established its first settlement in the region in 1618 with a factory at Surat, protected by a charter from Emperor Jahangir. Early attempts to acquire the island of Bombay failed in 1626, and purchase proposals were suggested in 1653.

In 1661, Bombay was ceded to the English crown as part of the dower of the infanta Catherine of Portugal upon her marriage to Charles II. Due to poor administration by crown officers and a low estimation of the acquisition in England, Bombay was transferred to the East India Company in 1668 for an annual payment of L10. This transfer included powers for the administration of justice and defence. A European regiment was enrolled, and fortifications were built, which successfully deterred a Dutch attack in 1673.

Bombay became the head of all Company possessions in India in 1687, though it later became subordinate to the government of Calcutta in 1753. By 1800, Surat had been annexed, and following the second Mahratta war in 1803, the East India Company received the districts of Kaira and Broach.
