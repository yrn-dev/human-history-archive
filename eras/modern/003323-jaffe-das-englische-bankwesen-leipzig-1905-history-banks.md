# History of Banking and Financial Literature

The study of banking and financial systems is documented through a vast array of historical texts, manuals, and theoretical treatises. These sources cover the evolution of banking practices from early monetary theories to the specialized national systems of the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## British Banking and Monetary Theory
The literature on British banking spans several centuries, beginning with early treatises such as *De Monetarum Augmento, variatione et diminutione, Tractatus varii* (1509) and a 1697 proposal regarding the supply of money to the King by A. D. of Grey's Inn and associates. By the 18th century, works like Hayes' *Negociators' Magazine of Monies and Exchanges* (1730) provided insight into the era's financial exchanges.

The 19th century saw a proliferation of theoretical and practical works. Lord King wrote *Thoughts on Bank Restrictions* (1804), followed by *The Theory of Money with considerations on the Bank of England* (1811). William Cobbett contributed a two-volume work in 1815 titled *Paper against Gold and Glory against Prosperity*. Later in the century, Walter Bagehot's *Lombard Street* (1873) provided a detailed depiction of the city's financial center, which was later countered by A. S. Cobb in *Threadneedle Street, a reply to "Lombard Street"* (1891).

Practical guides for the profession include George Rae's *The Country Banker* (1886) and J. George Kiddy's *The Country Banker's Handbook* (1903). Other significant contributions include T. B. Moxon's *English Practical Banking* (10th ed., 1899) and J. W. Gilbart's *The History, Principles and Practice of Banking* (1882).

## International Banking Systems
Banking practices in other nations are documented through specialized histories and legal manuals.

### Germany and Central Europe
German banking literature includes Dr. W. Scharling's *Bank Politik* (1900) and a history of the operations of the Reichsbank from 1876 to 1900. Comparative studies exist, such as Dr. Adolf Weber's *Depositenbanken und Spekulationsbanken*, which compares German and English banking (1902). Other focused works include Dr. Felix Hecht's study of the Mannheimer Banken from 1870 to 1900 and Dr. Riesser's 1905 work on the development of German Grossbanken and their concentration efforts. Additionally, G. M. Boissevain wrote on German and English deposit banks in 1905. In Austria, the *Finanzielles Jahrbuch fuer Oesterreich-Ungarn* serves as a financial yearbook.

### Other Global Systems
In the British colonies, Edward B. Hamilton authored several manuals, including *A Manual of the Law and Practice of Banking in Australia and New Zealand* (1880) and *Banking in Australasia* (1883), as well as a work comparing the Canadian system with the national banking system of the United States (1890). Japanese banking is represented by *The House of Mitsui* and the 1903 *Law and the By-Laws of the Nippon Kogyo Ginko* (The Industrial Bank of Japan). Other national references include *La Banca Popolare di Milano* (1881) for Italy, and works by Annuaire-Chaix and A. Raffalovich regarding the French financial market in 1905.

## Money Markets and Statistics
The technical aspects of the money market are detailed in various specialized publications. A. Crump's *The Key to the London Money Market* (1872) and W. Y. Duncan's multi-volume *Notes on the Rate of Discount in London* (published between 1856 and 1877) analyze market rates. R. H. Inglis Palgrave provided broader comparative data in *Bank Rate and the Money Market in England, France, Germany, Holland and Belgium, 1844-1900* (1903). Statistical data for the British sector was compiled by John Dun in *British Banking Statistics* (1876).
