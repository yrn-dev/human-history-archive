# Egypte and La Colonne de la grande armee

The works *Egypte* and *La Colonne de la grande armee* are significant engraved records of the military campaigns of Napoleon Bonaparte. These works were produced through the skill of Louis Pierre Baltard, a French engraver and architect.

## Louis Pierre Baltard
Louis Pierre Baltard (1764–1846) was born in Paris on July 9, 1764. Although he began his career as a landscape painter, his travels through Italy inspired him to change his profession to architecture after he was struck by the beauty of Italian buildings. In this field, Baltard achieved great success and was commissioned to create plans for several of the largest public edifices in Paris.

Despite his architectural achievements, Baltard's reputation is primarily based on his mastery of engraving. His notable works include the two-volume folio *Paris et ses monuments* (1803), the series *Grand prix de l'architecture*, and various portrait engravings. Among his most distinguished contributions to historical documentation were the engravings for Denon's *Egypte* and the illustrations of Napoleon's wars titled *La Colonne de la grande armee*. Baltard died in Paris on January 22, 1846.

## The Egyptian Campaign
The illustrations in *Egypte* relate to Napoleon's expedition to Egypt, which began in May 1798. Napoleon persuaded the Directory to allow the campaign, partly because they were dazzled by his previous exploits in Italy. The expedition was conceived as an imitation of Julius Caesar's return from Gaul, with Egypt and India serving as Napoleon's equivalent to Gaul.

The campaign was also a strategic move against the British East India Company, which had established a military and political power in India following the break-up of India after the death of Aurangzeb in 1707. Napoleon's expedition to Egypt was a direct attack on the empire of this company.

The French Armada departed from Toulon in May 1798, captured Malta, and arrived at Alexandria after evading the British fleet. Napoleon landed his troops and became the master of Egypt following the battle of the Pyramids. During this campaign, General Geraud Christophe Michel Duroc, who served as Napoleon's aide-de-camp, was seriously wounded at Aboukir. Napoleon eventually returned to France in 1799.

## Napoleonic Wars and Military Legacy
The illustrations in *La Colonne de la grande armee* document the broader scope of Napoleon's wars. Napoleon's military career saw him rise from First Consul in 1799 to Emperor in 1804. His campaigns spanned Europe, including the overthrow of Prussia at Jena in 1806 and the retreat from Moscow in 1812, leading to his abdication in 1814.

To honor military and civil merit during these conflicts, Napoleon instituted the Legion of Honour on May 19, 1802. This order was open to citizens regardless of rank, birth, or religion, provided they had 25 years of service or had been conferred "swords of honour." Members swore to support the principles of equality and liberty. The order was organized into 15 cohorts, each with its own hospitals for sick and infirm legionaries, and was administered by a grand council of seven grand officers under Napoleon as grand master.
