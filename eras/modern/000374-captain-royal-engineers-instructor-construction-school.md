# E. N. Stockley

## Professional Role and Affiliations
E. N. Stockley is a Captain in the Royal Engineers. He serves as an Instructor in Construction at the School of Military Engineering, located in Chatham. In addition to his instructional duties, Stockley was in charge of the Barracks Design Branch of the War Office for a period of time. His professional focus is associated with the subject of barracks.

## The Royal Engineers and Military Infrastructure
The Royal Engineers are responsible for the maintenance of buildings within barracks, as well as the systems of mains, pipes, and roads located within those facilities. At military stations, an engineer office is required, which includes workshops and a building yard to manage the ordinary duties related to the upkeep of property belonging to the War Department. In larger stations, such as Aldershot, these engineering requirements may extend to the implementation of special works for the supply of electricity or gas for lighting, the disposal of sewage, and water supply schemes.

## History of Barrack Construction in Great Britain
The development of barrack construction in Great Britain evolved as the needs of the military changed. Initially, when troops were levied specifically for a particular war, fortifications sufficed, with the exception of those required for the royal bodyguard. As the standing army grew, the military often utilized billeting—the practice of quartering soldiers among the town populations. However, this method was found to be prejudicial to military efficiency, subversive of discipline, and burdensome to the civilian population, while also exposing soldiers to temptations of disloyalty.

Consequently, barracks were gradually established in areas where the population was too sparse to support billeting or where the concentration of troops was necessary due to disaffection among local inhabitants. The earliest recorded barracks in England were those for the foot guards, built in 1660. Other early examples include the Royal Barracks at Dublin, which date from 1700. While several barracks were built in Ireland during the 18th century, the majority of early English barracks were constructed between the end of the 18th century and the beginning of the 19th century.

## Administration and Evolution of Design
The responsibility for barrack construction originally fell to the King's Engineers (later the Corps of Engineers), who operated under the master-general of the ordnance, as these buildings were primarily connected to fortresses. A shift in administration occurred around 1796 with the formation of a special civil department under the commissioners for the affairs of barracks to manage facilities separate from fortifications. By 1816, a warrant appointed a civilian comptroller of the barrack department to oversee the upkeep and erection of barrack hospitals and barracks located outside of fortified places.

## Components of Military Stations
Modern military stations incorporate various specialized structures beyond the primary barracks:
*   **Army Schools:** These are designed to provide accommodation slightly more liberal than the minimums set by the Board of Education. They are typically situated between the barracks and married quarters to provide education for the children of married soldiers and instruction for men seeking educational certificates.
*   **Garrison Churches:** These are constructed when troops cannot easily attend divine services at nearby places of worship.
*   **Detention Facilities:** While only two military prisons remain (Dover and Curragh) for those discharged with ignominy, ordinary sentences are served in branch detention barracks or detention barracks attached to districts and military commands, built according to Home Office regulations.
*   **Support Facilities:** All barracks require expense stores for utensils and bedding provided by the Army Service Corps. Large stations may also require a supply depot for provisions, featuring an attached bakery and abattoir.
*   **Administrative Offices:** District and brigade offices are used for the administration of large units, and quarters are sometimes provided for the headquarters staff and the general officer commanding.
