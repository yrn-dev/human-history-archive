# Potosi to the Argentine Frontier

The region extending from the department of Potosi to the Argentine frontier is characterized by high-altitude plateaus, rugged Andean cordilleras, and significant mineral wealth. This area forms a critical geographic and commercial link between the Bolivian highlands and the neighboring Argentine provinces.

## Physiography and the High Plateaus
The south-western corner of Bolivia is dominated by the Andean cordilleras, which project east toward the Brazilian highlands. This system consists of two great parallel chains that enclose elevated plateaus, broken by deep ravines and transverse ranges. A primary feature of this region is the great Bolivian tableland, or *alta-planicie*, which extends from the Vilcanota knot in Peru south to the Serrania de Lipez on the Argentine frontier. This plateau is approximately 500 miles long and 80 miles broad, covering about 40,000 square miles.

The plateau's elevation averages 12,650 feet near Lake Titicaca, descending by about 1,000 feet toward its southern extremity. The northern section is known as the *puna*, while the southern portion is referred to as the "desert of Lipez," which is characterized as part of the great Puna de Atacama. This landscape is described as bleak, inhospitable, and arid, featuring saline depressions that fill with water during the rainy season. The highest peak in this area is the Cerro de Tahua, reaching 17,454 feet. To the west, the plateau is overlooked by several snow-clad peaks, including Sajama (21,047 ft.), Huallatiri (21,654 ft.), and Parinacota (20,918 ft.).

## The Desert of Atacama
The south-western corner of the Potosi department merges into the Desert of Atacama, an arid and saline region that also covers the Argentine territory of Los Andes and Chilean provinces. The higher elevations of this desert, the Puna de Atacama, range from 7,000 to 13,500 feet. While the region is generally rainless and barren, occasional rainfall on the eastern side and streams from snow-clad peaks provide limited fertility. 

Despite its desolate appearance, the region is a significant source of mineral wealth. It contains productive copper and silver mines and is abundant in iron, lead, nickel, cobalt, borax, and nitrates.

## Urban Centers and Industry in Potosi
The department of Potosi contains several towns that are prominent due to their commercial or industrial roles:

*   **Tupiza:** Located near the Argentine frontier, Tupiza serves as a commercial and mining center. It is the terminus for the extension of the Argentine railway into Bolivia.
*   **Uyuni:** This town serves as a junction for several important highways and projected railways, specifically where the Pulacayo branch meets the Oruro and Antofagasta railway.
*   **Pulacayo:** A mining center known for the Huanchaca silver mining company, which employs 3,200 men in its surface works and mines.

According to 1900 census data, the department of Potosi had an area of 48,801 square miles and a population of 325,615.

## Transport and Communications
Efforts to connect this isolated region to the outside world have involved several railway projects. Argentina undertook the extension of its northern railway from Jujuy to the frontier town of Tupiza. Within Bolivia, the Antofagasta and Oruro line (totaling 574 miles, with 350 miles in Bolivian territory) and a 26-mile private branch to the Pulacayo mines provided essential transport. By 1907, further lines were projected to connect Uyuni to Potosi, Sucre, and Tupiza.

## Military History of Upper Peru
The region, historically referred to as Upper Peru, was a primary theater of the War of Independence from July 1809 to August 1825. Warfare occurred frequently between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta. 

A significant turning point occurred in 1815, when the patriot forces suffered a total rout in a battle located between Oruro and Potosi. Following this, royalist forces under General Laserna attempted to invade the Argentine provinces to march on Buenos Aires in 1816, but were foiled by irregular *gaucho* troops from Jujuy and Salta. For several years, patriot guerrilla warfare continued in the mountains, particularly in the province of Yungas.
