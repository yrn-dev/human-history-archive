# United Kingdom

## Trade and Commerce
The United Kingdom maintains extensive international trade involving the import and export of a wide variety of goods. In the early 20th century, the United Kingdom engaged in significant trade with several regions. For example, between 1904 and 1905, trade figures associated with the United Kingdom were recorded at 7,498,301 and 13,690,869 respectively.

The United Kingdom also maintains specific trade relations with various nations. In the case of Bulgaria, exports to the United Kingdom were valued at £239,665 in 1900, increasing to £989,127 by 1904. Conversely, imports from the United Kingdom to Bulgaria rose from £301,150 in 1900 to £793,972 in 1904.

## Agricultural Imports
The United Kingdom imports significant quantities of dairy and fruit products to supplement home production.

### Dairy Products
Between 1886 and 1900, the United Kingdom imported large volumes of butter, margarine, and cheese. Total imports of butter and margarine grew from 2,431,540 cwt in 1886 to 4,298,932 cwt in 1900. Cheese imports during the same period rose from 1,734,890 cwt in 1886 to 2,711,805 cwt in 1900. 

Regarding cheese, Canada dominates the colonial import market. In 1900, Canada provided the vast majority of the 128,600 tons of colonial cheese imported, while New Zealand contributed a comparatively insignificant 4,000 tons. Australia sent small quantities in several seasons since 1891, though these amounts were not considered significant enough to quote. Additionally, condensed milk imports were recorded at 352,332 cwt in 1888.

### Fruit Imports
The United Kingdom imports both raw fruits that can be produced domestically and those that cannot be grown on an industrial scale in the British Isles. In 1908, the expenditure on imported raw fruits that could be produced at home totaled £4,195,654. This expenditure included:
* Apples: £2,079,703
* Pears: £515,914
* Grapes: £728,026
* Plums: £428,966
* Cherries: £235,523
* Currants: £121,852
* Apricots and peaches: £60,141
* Gooseberries: £25,529

Further spending included approximately £280,000 on "unenumerated" raw fruit and £560,000 on nuts other than almonds (such as filberts and walnuts), both of which are produced domestically.

In 1908, the United Kingdom spent over £5,000,000 on fruits that cannot be grown industrially in the British Isles, consisting of:
* Oranges: £2,269,651
* Bananas: £1,769,249
* Almond-nuts: £560,301
* Lemons: £471,713
