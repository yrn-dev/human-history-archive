# BADAJOZ

Badajoz is a city in western Spain and the capital of the province of the same name. It is situated on the Madrid-Lisbon railway and on the left bank of the river Guadiana, positioned close to the frontier of Portugal. As of 1900, the city had a population of 30,899.

## City Geography and Fortifications
The city is situated on a slight eminence that is overlooked by the ruins of a Moorish castle. Its appearance is one of great strength, characterized by a broad moat, outworks, bastions, and a strong wall, supplemented by forts located on the surrounding heights. The river Guadiana flows between the castle-hill and the powerfully armed fort of San Cristobal. A magnificent granite bridge crosses the river; this structure was originally built in 1460, underwent repairs in 1597, and was rebuilt in 1833.

The city's history of conflict is reflected in its architecture. The cathedral, which was built in 1258, possesses massive embattled walls that give it the appearance of a fortress.

## History
Badajoz is not mentioned by any Roman historian and first gained importance under Moorish rule. In 1031, it served as the capital of a small Moorish kingdom. Although the Portuguese held the city temporarily in 1168, it remained independent until 1229, when it was captured by Alphonso IX of Leon.

Due to its location as a frontier fortress, the city was subject to numerous sieges. It was beleaguered by the Portuguese in 1660 and by the Allies in 1705 during the War of the Spanish Succession. During the Peninsular War, the French attacked the city unsuccessfully in 1808 and 1809. However, on March 10, 1811, the Spanish commander Jose Imaz was bribed into surrendering the city to the French forces led by Marshal Soult. A British army under Marshal Beresford attempted to retake the city; while they defeated a relieving force at Albuera on May 16, the siege was abandoned in June.

## Administration, Culture, and Economy
Badajoz serves as the official residence of the captain-general of Estremadura and is the see of a bishop. The city is the birthplace of the painter Luis de Morales (1509-1586) and the statesman Manuel de Godoy, duke of Alcudia (1767-1851). The cathedral preserves two paintings by Morales, though these have been retouched in modern times.

The economy of the city benefits from a considerable transit trade with Portugal due to its geographic position. Local industries include the manufacture of pottery as well as leather, woollen, and linen goods.

## The Province of Badajoz
The province of Badajoz was formed in 1833 from districts taken from the province of Estremadura. It is the largest province in the kingdom, covering an area of 8,451 square miles with a population of 520,246 in 1900. It is bounded by Portugal to the west, Caceres to the north, Cordova and Ciudad Real to the east, and Huelva and Seville to the south.

The terrain consists largely of a monotonous, flat, or slightly undulating desolate plain, though some districts contain low ranges of hills. The primary river is the Guadiana, which traverses the north of the province from east to west. The river-beds only fill with significant water during certain seasons, often allowing the river to be forded easily. The climate is characterized by extreme cold in winter—with fierce north and north-west winds—and extreme heat in summer. In the Guadiana valley, intermittent fevers are prevalent during the hot months.

Agriculture is in a backward condition because rainfall is scanty in average years and only an insignificant portion of the land is irrigated. Much of the land is covered by forest, thin bush, or used for pasture. Consequently, the province possesses more livestock than any other province in Spain. It is particularly noted for its acorn-fed swine, celebrated for their bacon and hams, as well as large herds of goats and sheep.

The province's mineral resources, consisting of lead and small quantities of copper, are hindered by a lack of communication and water; in 1903, only 26 of nearly 600 registered mines were operational. Local industries include the production of oils, soaps, cork, leather, and coarse cotton and woollen stuffs. Commercial interests are more significant than industrial ones, facilitated by seven custom-houses that manage transit trade with Portugal.
