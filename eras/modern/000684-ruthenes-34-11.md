# Ruthenes

The Ruthenes are one of the various nationalities and races that composed the Habsburg dominions. They are identified as a Slav race and have historically maintained a presence within the Austrian monarchy, particularly in the province of Galicia.

## Demographics and Regional Presence
In Galicia, the Ruthenes constituted a large minority, comprising approximately 43% of the population. In this region, they existed alongside a Polish majority and a small German-speaking population consisting primarily of railway servants, government officials, and Jews. 

## Political Representation and Electoral History
The political representation of the Ruthenes has undergone significant changes due to electoral reforms and the structure of the Austrian parliamentary system.

### Parliamentary Seats
In earlier parliamentary records, the Ruthenes were represented by 11 seats, divided between the "Ruthenes" (6 seats) and the "Young Ruthenes" (5 seats). Following a redistribution of constituencies—which occurred around the general election of 1907—Ruthene representation was trebled, increasing to 34 seats. Despite this increase, archive sources note that this representation remained far from being proportioned to their actual numbers.

### Electoral Structure
The allotment of constituencies was based on race and tax-paying capacity. In mixed districts, separate registers were established so that electors could only vote for candidates of their own race. This system ensured that Ruthenes voted for Ruthene candidates, preventing other groups, such as the Poles, from achieving electoral victory over them.

## Inter-Ethnic Relations and Political Alliances
The Ruthenes have been involved in various political tensions and alliances within the monarchy.

### Relations with Poles
In Galicia, the Poles sought to maximize autonomy for their own province. While the Poles were generally satisfied if Austria surrendered the Ruthenes to their influence, they actively opposed measures that would increase Ruthene influence. For example, the Poles opposed a reform bill introduced by Taaffe in October 1893 because it proposed to give more influence to the Ruthenes.

### Alliances for Centralization
The Ruthenes, along with a few Italians, provided support to the Liberal German party, which advocated for centralization. This party sought to create a common Austrian nationality and promote the German language through schools to maintain the unity of the monarchy and the ascendancy of German culture.
