# The Great Depression: The Global Economic Cataclysm of the 1930s

## Overview
The Great Depression was the most severe economic downturn in the history of the industrialized Western world. While it is often associated with the dramatic stock market crash of October 1929, the Depression was not a single event but a decade-long collapse characterized by deflation, massive unemployment, and a breakdown in international trade. 

The crisis began in the United States but rapidly spread globally due to the interconnected nature of post-WWI debts and the gold standard. In the U.S., GDP fell by nearly 30%, and unemployment peaked at roughly 25% in 1933. The era was defined by "Hoovervilles" (shanty towns) and the ecological disaster of the Dust Bowl, which displaced thousands of farmers in the Great Plains.

Historians and economists continue to debate the primary cause of the collapse. The "Monetarist" school, led by Milton Friedman, argues that the Federal Reserve exacerbated the crisis by failing to provide liquidity to banks, allowing the money supply to collapse. Conversely, Keynesian economists argue that a drop in aggregate demand and under-consumption triggered the spiral.

## Key Facts
*   **The Banking Crisis:** Between 1929 and 1933, thousands of American banks failed because they had invested depositors' money in the stock market or held bad loans. This led to "bank runs," where panicked citizens attempted to withdraw all their cash simultaneously.
*   **The Dust Bowl:** A combination of severe drought and poor farming practices led to massive soil erosion in the American Midwest, forcing a migration of "Okies" toward California.
*   **The New Deal:** President Franklin D. Roosevelt implemented a series of programs (the "Alphabet Agencies" like the CCC and WPA) designed to provide *Relief, Recovery, and Reform*.
*   **Global Impact:** Germany was hit particularly hard, as the withdrawal of American loans crippled the Weimar Republic, creating the economic instability that contributed to the rise of Adolf Hitler.

## Did You Know?
1. **The "Forgotten" Recovery:** While the New Deal provided essential relief, many historians argue that it was the industrial mobilization for World War II, rather than policy alone, that fully eliminated unemployment.
2. **Deflationary Spiral:** Prices didn't just stop rising; they plummeted. This meant that while goods were cheaper, wages fell faster, and debts became effectively more expensive to pay back.
3. **Gold Standard Constraints:** Many economists believe that countries that abandoned the gold standard earlier (such as Great Britain in 1931) recovered faster than those that clung to it.
