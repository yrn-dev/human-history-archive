# Foreign Mercantile and National Communities in Bucharest

## The Mercantile Community of Bucharest
In the city of Bucharest, which serves as the winter residence of the royal family and the seat of various high-level government institutions—including the parliament, the national bank, the bank of Rumania, and the supreme court (_Curtea de Casatie_)—there exists a significant mercantile community. This commercial sector is largely composed of individuals from several European nations, specifically Austrians, Frenchmen, Germans, Greeks, and Swiss. These groups are noted for forming exclusive colonies within the city.

## Economic and Industrial Context
The presence of these mercantile groups coincides with Bucharest's role as a center for extensive transit trade, particularly in agricultural produce such as maize and wheat, as well as timber and petroleum. The city's industrial base is diverse, encompassing the extraction of vegetable oils, petroleum-refining, the manufacture of machinery, textiles, and leather goods, among other trades such as tanning, cabinet-making, and brandy-distilling. Additionally, the city hosts an important annual fair that lasts for seven days.

## Infrastructure and Military Status
The city is supported by a comprehensive telephone system that connects it to Sinaia, Jassy, Galatz, and Braila, while railway lines provide access to all regions. Beyond its commercial importance, Bucharest is a first-rank fortress and the headquarters of the II. army corps. Its fortifications, designed by the Belgian engineer General Brialmont in 1883 and constructed between 1885 and 1896, consist of 36 batteries and armoured forts. The perimeter of these defenses is approximately 48 m., with the forts situated at a mean distance of 4 m. from the city. The military infrastructure includes three military academies, an arsenal, a military hospital, and barracks capable of housing more than 30,000 infantry and cavalry.
