# Independency, Brownisme, Antinomy, Familisme, &c., a sermon

## Overview
*Anabaptism, the true Fountain of Independency, Brownisme, Antinomy, Familisme, &c.* is a sermon authored by an individual whose writings were documented in D. Laing's edition of the *Letters and Journals of Robert Baillie* (1637-1662). This work is listed among several other publications by the author, which include *Ladensium* (Greek: *autokatakrisis*), an answer to *Lysimachus Nicanor*; *An Historical Vindication of the Government of the Church of Scotland*; and *The Life of William (Laud) now Lord Archbishop of Canterbury Examined*, published in London in 1643. The author also wrote *A Parallel of the Liturgy with the Mass Book, the Breviary, the Ceremonial and other Romish Rituals* in 1661.

## Related Religious Movements
The sermon's title references several movements, including Independency and Brownism. Historical records indicate that the modern Baptist movement in England emerged from a break with Brownism. John Smith (also known as Smyth, d. 1612) was an ordained clergyman appointed as the preacher of the city of Lincoln in 1600. By 1605 or 1606, Smyth became a separatist and emigrated to Amsterdam with the Gainsborough Independents due to persecution.

In Amsterdam, Smyth joined the "Ancient" church alongside Morton and Thomas Helwys (ca. 1560–ca. 1616). Following exposure to Mennonite teaching in 1609, Smyth separated from the Independents and baptized himself, earning the designation "Se-baptist." Helwys and others followed, likely utilizing the Mennonite or Anabaptist practice of pouring.

## The First English Baptist Church
The group formed by Smyth, Helwys, and others established the first English Baptist Church. In 1611, they published a "declaration of faith of English people remaining at Amsterdam in Holland." This confession outlined several core tenets:

*   **Baptism:** The church taught that members should be received via baptism only upon the confession of their faith and sins, as wrought by the preaching of the gospel. They asserted that baptism—defined as washing with water—is an outward manifestation of walking in newness of life and dying unto sin. Consequently, they maintained that baptism does not appertain to infants and that any church constituted otherwise is not according to Christ's testament.
*   **Church Governance:** They held the belief that no single church should claim any prerogative over another.
*   **Liberty of Conscience:** The confession stated that the magistrate should not compel individuals into any specific form of religion, nor meddle with matters of conscience or religion. This is recognized as the first known expression of absolute liberty of conscience in a confession of faith.

## Legal and Conceptual Antinomies
While the sermon title references "Antinomy" in a religious context, the term also appears in legal history. In the context of the Code and Pandects, antinomies refer to opposite laws. These contradictions have served as both a cause and an excuse for the "glorious uncertainty" of civil law.
