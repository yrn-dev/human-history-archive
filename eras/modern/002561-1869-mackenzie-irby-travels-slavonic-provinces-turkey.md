# Travels in the Slavonic Provinces of Turkey

*Travels in the Slavonic Provinces of Turkey* is a work published in London in 1866, authored by Mackenzie and Irby. The text serves as a historical record of the Balkan Peninsula, specifically detailing the conditions of the region prior to the Treaty of Berlin.

## Regional Background
The region described in the text—the Balkan Peninsula—underwent significant ethnographic changes starting in the 3rd century A.D. due to a great Slavonic immigration that lasted for four centuries. This movement occurred while the Byzantine empire held nominal or actual possession of the regions south of the Danube. 

The immigration patterns affected various groups:
* **Thracians:** The progenitors of the Vlachs, who shifted from being an agricultural people to nomad shepherds in mountainous districts.
* **Illyrians:** In Albania, the aboriginal Illyrian element maintained its ancient language in the mountains and eventually pushed back the immigrant race.
* **Greeks:** These populations were driven toward fortified towns, the islands, and the sea-coast.
* **Serbo-Croats:** In the 7th century, they invaded north-western regions, including Bosnia, Herzegovina, Montenegro, Servia, and Croatia, where they assimilated or expelled the Illyrian population.
* **Bulgars:** A Turanian race that crossed the Danube at the end of the 7th century, subjecting the Slavonic inhabitants of Thrace and Moesia before being assimilated by them.
