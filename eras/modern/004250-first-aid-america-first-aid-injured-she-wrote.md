# Clara Barton

## Early Life and Career
Clara Barton was an American philanthropist born in 1821 in Oxford, Massachusetts. She received her education at the Clinton Liberal Institute, located in Clinton, New York. At the age of sixteen, Barton entered the teaching profession, though she was eventually forced to leave this career due to ill-health. Between 1854 and 1857, she worked as a clerk in the Patent Office in Washington.

## Civil War and Post-War Relief
During the American Civil War, Barton focused her efforts on the relief of wounded soldiers, distributing large quantities of supplies. Following the conclusion of the war, she established a bureau of records in Washington to assist in the search for missing men. This work continued for approximately four years, during which time Barton identified and marked the graves of more than twelve thousand soldiers located in the National Cemetery at Andersonville, Georgia.

## International Experience and the Red Cross
In 1869, Barton traveled to Switzerland for health reasons. While in Geneva, she was visited by members of the International Committee of the Red Cross, who requested her cooperation in their society's work. At that time, the United States had declined to join the treaty of Geneva that served as the foundation for the Red Cross Society. 

When the Franco-Prussian War broke out, Barton traveled to the seat of hostilities with members of the Red Cross society to assist in the organization of military hospitals. Her subsequent relief work included superintending the distribution of aid to the poor in Paris in 1872 and in Strassburg in 1871. For these services, the German emperor decorated her with the Iron Cross.

## Establishment of the American Red Cross
Barton returned to the United States in 1873 and began working to organize a United States branch of the Red Cross and to bring the country into the treaty of Geneva. These efforts were successful between 1881 and 1882. Barton served as the first president of the American Red Cross, a position she held until 1904.

In her capacity as a representative of the United States, she attended several International conferences in Geneva (1884), Karlsruhe (1887), Rome (1892), Vienna (1897), and St Petersburg (1903). Additionally, she authored the American amendment to the Red Cross constitution, which expanded the society's mandate to distribute relief not only during war but also during other calamities.
