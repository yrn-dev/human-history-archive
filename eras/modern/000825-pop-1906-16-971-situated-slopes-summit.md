# Auxerre

Auxerre is a town located in central France and serves as the capital of the Yonne department. It is situated 38 miles south-south-east of Sens on the Paris-Lyon railway, positioned between Nevers and Laroche. As of 1906, the population of the town was 16,971.

## Geography and Layout
The town is situated on the left bank of the Yonne river, occupying the summit and slopes of an eminence. Two bridges cross the river to connect the town with suburbs located on the right bank. The layout of Auxerre is irregular, characterized by steep and narrow streets. However, the town is surrounded by wide boulevards lined with trees, which were constructed to replace the city's ancient fortifications.

## Religious Architecture
Auxerre is home to several notable churches:

**St Etienne**
Formerly the cathedral, St Etienne is a majestic Gothic building constructed between the 13th and 16th centuries. The church features a nave with aisles, lateral chapels, a choir, a transept, and a deambulatory situated at a slightly lower level. The choir is a fine example of early Gothic architecture and sits above an 11th-century crypt containing 12th-century mural paintings. The building is entered via three richly sculptured portals; the largest and central portal is topped by a rose window. While a massive tower rises over the north portal, the tower intended for the south portal remains unfinished. The church is further noted for its fine stained glass, pictures, and other works of art, with lateral entrances featuring arches and tympana decorated with statuettes.

**St Germain**
The abbey church of St Germain was built during the 13th and 14th centuries. Much of its nave has disappeared, leaving an imposing Romanesque tower standing apart from the main structure. The abbey's 9th-century crypts contain the tombs of the bishops of Auxerre. The abbey was once a fortified site, and a cylindrical tower and a high wall still remain. The 18th-century buildings of the abbey are currently used in part as a training college and a hospital.

**Other Churches**
The church of St Eusebe dates from the 12th, 13th, and 16th centuries. Additionally, the church of St Pierre, built in the 16th and 17th centuries in the Renaissance style, is distinguished by the elaborate ornamentation found on its west facade.

## Civic Buildings and Culture
The ancient episcopal palace is located behind the cathedral and currently serves as the prefecture; it retains a Romanesque gallery dating to the 12th century. The town's museum, which houses a library and a collection of paintings and antiquities, is located within the old law-court. In the center of the town, there is a gateway.
