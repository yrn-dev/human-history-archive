# MARIE LOUIS ANTOINE GASTON BOISSIER

## Early Life and Education
Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier (1823–1908) was a French classical scholar and secretary of the French Academy. He was born in Nimes on August 15, 1823. From an early age, Boissier was attracted to the study of ancient history, specifically influenced by the Roman monuments located in his native town. He focused his academic interests on epigraphy.

## Academic Career
Boissier began his professional career at the age of twenty-three when he became a professor of rhetoric at Angouleme, a position he held for ten years. His career advanced after a travelling university inspector heard him lecture, leading to his appointment as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne in Paris.

In 1861, Boissier was appointed professor of Latin oratory at the College de France. He also became an active contributor to the *Revue des deux mondes*. His early literary contributions included a thesis on the poet Attius, published in 1857, and a study regarding the life and work of M. Terentius Varro, published in 1861.

## Major Works and Scholarship
Boissier is noted for his ability to reproduce the characteristics of ancient Roman society and its manners with exquisite vivacity. In 1865, he published *Ciceron et ses amis* (translated into English by A.D. Jones in 1897), a work of erudition that achieved significant success.

His later publications focused on the religious and political landscapes of antiquity:
* **La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins (1874):** A two-volume work analyzing the great religious movement of antiquity that occurred before the acceptance of Christianity.
* **L'Opposition sous les Cesars (1875):** A work providing a picture of the political decadence of Rome during the era of the early successors of Augustus.

## Honors and Later Years
By 1875, Boissier had earned the universal respect of men of letters and scholars. Following the death of H.J.G. Patin (the author of *Etudes sur les tragiques grecs*) in 1876, Boissier was elected as a member of the French Academy. He eventually reached the position of perpetual secretary of the Academy, to which he was appointed in 1895.
