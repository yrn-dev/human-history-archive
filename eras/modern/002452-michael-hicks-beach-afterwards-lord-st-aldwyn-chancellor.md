# Michael Hicks-Beach (Lord St Aldwyn)

Sir Michael Hicks-Beach, later known as Lord St Aldwyn, was a prominent figure in the Unionist party who served in several high-ranking government positions during the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

## Government Service and the Chancellorship
Sir Michael Hicks-Beach served as the chancellor of the exchequer until July 1902. Following the resignation of Lord Salisbury on July 11, 1902, Mr. Balfour succeeded him as prime minister. Within a few weeks of this transition, Mr. Balfour reconstituted the cabinet, and Mr. (afterwards Lord) Ritchie took the place of Sir Michael Hicks-Beach as the chancellor of the exchequer.

## Other Political Roles
Prior to his time as chancellor, Sir Michael Hicks-Beach held the position of chief secretary for Ireland. He eventually found it desirable to resign from this post due to an affection of the eyes. Following his resignation, Lord Salisbury appointed his nephew, Mr. Balfour, to succeed him as chief secretary for Ireland.

## Political Alignments and Tariff Reform
During a period of internal division within the Conservative party regarding "Protection," also known as Tariff Reform, Sir Michael Hicks-Beach emerged as a significant figure of support for the Free Trade Unionists. His active sustenance of this faction, alongside the Duke of Devonshire, was indirectly endorsed by Lord Salisbury from his retirement. This period of political strife saw the resignation of six ministers and the withdrawal of support from fifty Conservative or Unionist members.
