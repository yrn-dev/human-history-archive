# Rainfall and Precipitation in Asia

## General Rainfall Patterns in Northern and Central Asia
Rainfall across the greater part of Asia varies significantly by region. In northern Asia, between the Lena and the Volga, rainfall is generally equal, ranging from 19 to 29 inches. A more considerable increase in precipitation is noted in Japan, Siam, and Korea. 

In other regions of the north and center, the figures are lower. At Tiflis, the yearly fall is 22 inches, while on the Sea of Aral it is 5 or 6 inches, and on the Caspian it is about 7 or 8 inches. In south-western Siberia, rainfall is 12 or 14 inches, but this diminishes as one proceeds eastward, falling to 6 or 7 inches at Barnaul and further to 5 or 6 inches at Urga in northern Mongolia. Eastern Siberia records rainfall of approximately 15 to 20 inches.

## Precipitation in China and Southeast Asia
In China, the rainfall at Peking is about 23 inches. However, at Canton, where the region of the south-west monsoon is entered and the location lies nearly on the northern tropic, the quantity increases to 78 inches. 

Similar levels are found in Southeast Asia, with Batavia in Java recording a fall of about 78 inches and Singapore reaching nearly 100 inches. On the coast of the Malay peninsula, the quantity increases considerably in areas that are not sheltered from the south-west by Sumatra. Even higher levels are registered on the Burmese and Tenasserim coast, where falls exceed 200 inches and are nowhere less than 75 or 80 inches.

## Rainfall in the Indian Peninsula and Bengal
The eastern part of the delta of the Ganges has an average rainfall of about 75 or 80 inches, while Calcutta stands at approximately 64 inches. The hills flanking Bengal to the east experience very great falls; specifically, on the Khasi hills at an elevation of about 4500 feet, the ten-year average is more than 550 inches. Records for this area include 610 inches in one year and as much as 150 inches in a single month.

On the west coast of the Indian peninsula, rainfall at sea-level varies from 75 to 100 inches. At certain mountain elevations, more than 250 inches is commonly registered. Conversely, the east coast receives far less rain, with quantities never rising to 50 inches and reducing to 25 or 30 inches toward the southern apex of the peninsula. Ceylon shows rainfall between 60 and 80 inches.

## Inland Diminution of Rainfall
As one recedes from the coasts, the amount of rainfall diminishes. At the head of the Gangetic plain, the fall is reduced to about 25 or 30 inches. In the region along the Indus, the land is almost rainless until within 60 or 80 miles of the Himalaya.
