# Landsturm

The Landsturm refers to a military reserve force utilized by the German Empire and Austria-Hungary, primarily consisting of men of middle age who are mobilized to support the regular army during times of war or emergency.

## Organization in Austria-Hungary
In Austria-Hungary, the creation of the Landsturm was established by a law passed in 1886 by either parliament. This legislation provided for the organization and arming of the entire male population up to the age of forty-two in the event of an emergency. Within the military structure of the Dual Monarchy, the Landsturm is composed of the remaining men of military age (up to 42) who are not already serving in other capacities. The archive notes that the Landsturm in this region includes many individuals who might be classified as Landwehr in other systems.

While the peace strength of the active army—including the two Landwehrs, the gendarmerie, and various guards—is approximately 350,000 officers and men, the specific numbers for the Landsturm and the total war strength of the armed forces are not published. Estimates for the first line army in war include 460,000 infantry, 49,000 cavalry, 78,000 artillery, and 21,000 engineers, with the Landwehr and Honved contributing an additional 219,000 infantry and 18,000 cavalry. Other reserves provide 223,000 men, bringing the approximate total to 1,147,000, a figure that does not include the Landsturm.

## Roles and Duties in the German Empire
In the German Empire, the Landsturm is heavily utilized for the security of vital infrastructure and lines of communication. Their primary responsibility is the guarding of railway lines, which are essential for transporting troops to fighting zones. Landsturm sentries are stationed at short intervals along the lines and are particularly concentrated at bridges and viaducts. These positions are considered critical, as the destruction of a bridge could sever a communication artery and potentially result in the loss of a battle.

Beyond railway security, the Landsturm is employed in other security and enforcement operations. This includes providing detachments to guard huts and performing arrests in villages where inhabitants are suspected of firing on soldiers or spreading rumors. In such operations, Landsturm infantry may be used to search houses and arrest populations, while Landsturm cavalry squadrons are used to occupy street exits to prevent flight.

## Appearance and Composition
Landsturm soldiers in the German Empire are typically men of middle age, with some nearing the age of fifty. Their uniform consists of a dark-blue tunic featuring a red collar and light-blue shoulder-straps, paired with black trousers that have a narrow red stripe. They wear peakless blue caps with a red band. During the evenings and at night, they wear grey overcoats. All Landsturm men are armed with rifles.

## Operational Presence
The Landsturm is present both in the interior of the empire and near the front lines. They are seen guarding bridge-heads in locations such as Halle and patrolling areas near the French frontier. Their presence is marked by a disciplined routine; sentries pace their posts with arms crossed and rifles held vertically inside the left arm until they are relieved by comrades. When approached by officers, they are noted for coming briskly to attention with the rifle to the order.
