# Ney Elias and the Exploration of the Pamirs

## The Mission of Ney Elias
Ney Elias was a central figure in the expansion of European geographical knowledge regarding Central Asia during the late 19th century. His most significant contribution occurred during the period of the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission between 1884 and 1886. During this time, Elias conducted a journey crossing from China through the Pamirs and Badakshan to reach the camp of the commission. A key achievement of this expedition was his identification of Rangkul, described as the great "Dragon Lake," which he encountered along his route. His findings were later detailed in his work, "Explorations in Central Asia," published in the *Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society* between 1886 and 1887.

## Geography of the Pamirs and Badakshan
The regions traversed by Elias are characterized by complex mountain systems and high plateaus. The Pamir plateau is described as a broad, rounded ridge extending north and south, featuring thick mountain chains and elevated valleys. These valleys generally slope gently toward the east but are narrow and confined with a rapid fall toward the west. The watershed of the region extends from the Tághdúngbásh Pamir, down the range dividing it from the Little Pamir, and along the Neza Tásh mountains to the Kizil Art Pass.

Badakshan, a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan, adjoins Russian territory. Its boundaries were largely decided by an Anglo-Russian agreement in 1873, which acknowledged the province and its dependent district, Wakhan, as belonging to the amir of Kabul. The province is described as having a "bottle-shaped" arm that reaches eastwards into the Pamirs, narrow at the neck—represented by the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush—and swelling out to include portions of the great and little Pamirs.

## Regional Topography and Trade Routes
The topography of Badakshan is defined by its relationship to the Hindu Kush and the Oxus river. The southern boundary of the province follows the crest of the Hindu Kush. To the north, the plateau slopes toward the Oxus, dropping from an average altitude of 15,000 feet to 4,000 feet near Faizabad. 

Several key geographical features facilitate movement through this rugged terrain:
* **The Kokcha River:** This river traverses Badakshan from south-east to north-west. The course of the Kokcha provides a high road across the province from the Oxus (1,000 ft.) to Faizabad (4,000 ft.) and Zebak (8,500 ft.).
* **The Panja:** From Ishkashim, the Panja leads through the Pamirs. This route is recognized as a continuation of what was once a heavily traversed trade route connecting China and Kashgar with Afghan Turkestan, serving as one of the great continental high-roads of Asia.
* **The Dorah Pass:** This pass connects Zebak and Ishkashim at the bend of the Oxus with the Lutku valley leading to Chitral.

## Context of Central Asian Exploration
The journey of Ney Elias was part of a broader era of European exploration into the "central dominion of Kashgar" and the highlands of the Pamirs. Prior to the 1870 mission of Sir Douglas Forsyth to Yarkand, very little was known of these regions. Early pioneers included Robert Barkley Shaw and George Hayward, who arrived at Yarkand in 1868. Following Forsyth, Henry Trotter produced the first maps of Chinese Turkestan.

Other contemporaneous explorers contributed to the mapping of the region. Between 1884 and 1886, Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich, Colonel St George Gore, and Sir Adelbert Talbot mapped the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan. Captain William Lockhart crossed the Hindu Kush into Wakhan, while Colonel Woodthorpe verified previous explorations by W.W. M'Nair to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush. In 1885, Andrew Dalgleish and Arthur Douglas Carey added new data to the map of Asia, and in 1886, Captain Francis Younghusband crossed the Muztagh, the mountain barrier separating Kashmir and China.
