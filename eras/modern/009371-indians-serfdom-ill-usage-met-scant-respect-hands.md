# Treatment of Native Populations in Colonial Bolivia

## Colonial Administration and the Native Population
The region that currently constitutes the republic of Bolivia, named in honor of the liberator Simon Bolivar, was originally a part of the empire of the Incas of Peru. Following the Spanish conquest of Peru during the 16th century, the native populations were subjected to significant oppression and tyranny.

## Serfdom and Legislative Efforts
The Spanish home government attempted to implement legislative measures to protect the Indians from ill-usage and serfdom. However, these humane and protective regulations, established by the council of the Indies, were met with scant respect by the mine-owners and settlers in the distant colonies. These settlers bid defiance to the home government's wishes, treating the native populations as little more than beasts of burden.

## Population Decline and Forced Labour
There have been claims that approximately eight million Indians perished as a result of forced labour within the mines; however, such figures are considered gross exaggerations. While there was undoubtedly a very great annual diminution in the Indian population, this decline was attributed far more to the indulgence in alcohol and the impact of European epidemics than to the effects of hard work.

## Insurrections and Independence
Between 1780 and 1782, an insurrection led by the Inca Tupac Amaru took place. This movement was not a general rising, but was instead directed against the tyranny of the Creoles rather than against Spanish rule. 

The Indians experienced heavy losses during this outbreak. This history, combined with a general distrust and dislike of the colonial Spaniard, resulted in the native populations viewing the colonial revolt of 1814 with comparative indifference. This later revolt against Spain eventually led to the independence of the South American states.
