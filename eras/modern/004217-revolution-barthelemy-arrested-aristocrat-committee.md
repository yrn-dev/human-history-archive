# Jean Jacques Barthelemy

Jean Jacques Barthelemy (1716–1795) was a French numismatist and writer. Born on January 20, 1716, in Cassis, Provence, he received his early education at the college of the Oratory in Marseilles, followed by studies at the Jesuit college in the same city. Although he initially intended to enter the priesthood, Barthelemy focused his studies on classical antiquities—specifically numismatics—and oriental languages. He was introduced to the study of antiquities by his friend, M. Gary of Marseilles.

## Career in Paris and Archaeological Research
In 1744, Barthelemy moved to Paris using a letter of introduction to M. Gros de Boze, who served as the keeper of the royal collection of medals and the perpetual secretary of the Academy of Inscriptions and Belles-lettres. Barthelemy served as an assistant to de Boze and succeeded him as the keeper of the medals upon de Boze's death in 1753.

In 1755, Barthelemy accompanied M. de Stainville (later the duc de Choiseul), the French ambassador, to Italy. He spent three years conducting archaeological research during this period. Upon returning to France, Barthelemy became an inmate of Choiseul's house and received various valuable preferments from him. In 1789, following the publication of his work *Voyage du jeune Anacharsis*, he was elected as a member of the French Academy.

## Experience During the Revolution
During the French Revolution, Barthelemy was arrested on the grounds of being an aristocrat. However, he was released immediately after the Committee of Public Safety was informed of his arrest by the duchess of Choiseul. 

In 1793, he was nominated to be the librarian of the Bibliotheque Nationale. Barthelemy refused this specific appointment, choosing instead to resume his previous role as the keeper of medals. In this capacity, he continued to enrich the national collection through many valuable accessions.

## Death and Legacy
Jean Jacques Barthelemy died on April 30, 1795. His written works and correspondence have been preserved in several volumes. His *Oeuvres completes* (1821) include a notice by Villenave, and his *Oeuvres*, collected with those of Mery in 1831, include a notice by L. Reybaud. 

His extensive correspondence includes letters to Mme du Deffand, with whom he was on intimate terms, and to the comte de Caylus; the latter were published as *Voyage en Italie* in 1801. His correspondence with Paolo Paciaudi, focusing primarily on antiquarian subjects, was edited by Ch. Nisard in 1877. Additionally, Barthelemy authored his own memoirs, *Memoires sur la vie de l'abbe Barthelemy*, published in 1824 with a notice by Lalande.
