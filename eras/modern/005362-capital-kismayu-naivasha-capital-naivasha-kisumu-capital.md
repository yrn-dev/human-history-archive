# East Africa Protectorate: Provinces and Towns

The East Africa protectorate is organized into several provinces, each further divided into sub-districts and districts. These provinces include Seyyidie on the south coast (capital Mombasa), Ukamba in the center (capital Nairobi), Kenya in the district of Mt. Kenya (capital Fort Hall), and Tanaland to the north of Seyyidie and Ukamba (capital Lamu). Other provinces include Jubaland in the northern region (capital Kismayu), as well as Naivasha (capital Naivasha) and Kisumu (capital Kisumu).

## Provincial Transitions and Administration
The provinces of Naivasha and Kisumu both adjoin the Victoria Nyanza. Originally, these two areas formed the eastern province of Uganda, but they were transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902. 

Administrative leadership for the protectorate was established in Nairobi, which was chosen as the administrative capital in 1907. Nairobi is located 327 miles from Mombasa and 257 miles from Port Florence.

## Coastal Settlements and Ports
Mombasa serves as the chief port of the protectorate and has a population of approximately 30,000. Its harbor, located on the south-west side of Mombasa island, is called Kilindini and serves as the terminus for the Uganda railway. 

Opposite Mombasa town on the mainland is Freretown (known by natives as Kisaoni), a settlement for freed slaves. Freretown serves as the headquarters for the Church Missionary Society in East Africa and is the residence of the bishop of the diocese of Mombasa, featuring a mission house and a fine church.

Further north-east, 150 miles from Mombasa, is Lamu. Located on an island of the same name, Lamu is an ancient settlement and the headquarters of the coast Arabs; the site contains Portuguese ruins and a large Arab city buried in the sands. Other notable coastal towns include Patta, Kipini, and Malindi. Malindi is the site of a pillar erected by Vasco da Gama during a visit in 1498, though its harbor is very shallow.

Kismayu is the northernmost port of the protectorate, situated 320 miles north-east of Mombasa. It is considered an invaluable harbor of refuge and is the last sheltered anchorage on the east coast.

## Interior Towns and Geography
The development of flourishing towns has been driven by the Uganda railway. Naivasha is located 64 miles north-north-west of Nairobi at an elevation of 6,230 feet above sea level. It is situated in the rift-valley close to Lake Naivasha.

Lake Naivasha is a small fresh-water lake measuring approximately 13 miles in each direction, with a surface 6,135 feet above the sea. The lake's basin is closed to the north by the ridge of Mount Buru. To the west of the rift-valley, opposite Lake Naivasha, the Mau Escarpment rises to over 8,000 feet, with its crest covered by a vast forest.

## Infrastructure and Communications
The protectorate has been opened up through the construction of roads and railways. A trunk road runs from Mombasa, passing through Lake Naivasha and Kibwezi in the upper Sabaki basin, ending at Berkeley Bay on Victoria Nyanza.

The most significant engineering project was the Uganda railway, which runs from Mombasa to Victoria Nyanza. Preliminary surveys began in 1892, and construction started in 1896. The railway is a metre (3.28 ft.) gauge mountain line spanning 584 miles, built by the British government at a cost of £5,331,000 (approximately £9,500 per mile). 

The line reaches the Kikuyu Escarpment 346 miles from Mombasa at an altitude of 7,600 feet. After descending to the valley floor (6,011 feet), it ascends the Mau escarpment to its highest point of 8,321 feet. The line eventually reaches Port Florence on the Kavirondo Gulf at an altitude of 3,738 feet. The first locomotive arrived at Victoria Nyanza on December 26, 1901, and the permanent way was largely completed by March 1903.

The construction of the railway had two major impacts: it provided a "final death-blow" to the slave trade in the region by eliminating the need for the carriage of goods by men, and it facilitated the development and occupation of Uganda by reducing the prohibitive cost of transporting goods from the coast, which had previously cost £60 per ton.
