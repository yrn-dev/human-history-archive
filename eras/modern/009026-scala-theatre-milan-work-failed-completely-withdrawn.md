# Scala Theatre, Milan

The Scala theatre in Milan has served as a prominent venue for the production and performance of Italian operatic works by various composers throughout the 19th century.

## Productions and Successes
The theatre has hosted works that established the international reputations of several composers. In 1827, Vincenzo Bellini, a composer of the Italian school born in Sicily, wrote the opera *Il Pirata* for the Scala. The work was a success, aided by the "splendid rendering" of the music by great Italian singers such as Rubini and Tamburini, which helped establish Bellini's reputation across Europe.

Other notable productions at the Scala include *Enrico Quarto* by the Irish composer Michael William Balfe, who was engaged at the theatre to sing alongside Malibran. Additionally, the composer Donizetti achieved a "brilliant reception" in Milan for his work *Anna Bolena*, a success that extended his fame beyond his own country.

## The Case of Mefistofele
Not all productions at the Scala were successful. In 1868, Arrigo Boito, an Italian poet and composer born in Padua, conducted the first performance of his opera *Mefistofele* at the theatre. The work failed completely and was withdrawn from the program after only a second performance. 

Boito's *Mefistofele* treated the Faust legend in a manner more closely aligned with Goethe's conception than the version by Gounod. Despite possessing many isolated beauties, the work was noted for a lack of dramatic interest and cohesion. The opera did not find success until it was revived in 1875 at Bologna in an abbreviated and much altered form. While it was later performed with success in London in 1880, it never firmly established itself in popular favour despite frequent revivals.
