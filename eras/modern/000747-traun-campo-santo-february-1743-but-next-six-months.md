# Traun at Campo Santo

## The Engagement at Campo Santo
On February 8, 1743, a military engagement took place at Campo Santo in Italy. During this conflict, Spanish forces positioned on the Panaro achieved a victory over Traun. This victory is characterized as a Pyrrhic victory.

## Subsequent Military Developments
Following the engagement at Campo Santo, the next six months were characterized by inaction. This period of inactivity ended when Lobkowitz joined Traun, bringing reinforcements from Germany. Together, they succeeded in driving the enemy back to Rimini.

## Broader Context of the Conflict
While the events at Campo Santo and Rimini unfolded, other military actions continued in the region. In the Alps, a war between the Spanish and Piedmontese persisted without achieving significant results. The only notable incident in that theater was a combat at Casteldelfino, which was won by the king of Sardinia in person.

## Career of Marshal Traun
Marshal Traun was a significant military figure who served in various capacities across different theaters of war. In addition to his actions in Italy, he was summoned from the Rhine to Bohemia, where he held the king in check. 

Traun later succeeded Khevenhuller in 1744. During this time, he held Count Leopold Josef von Daun in high regard, entrusting Daun with the rearguard of the Austrian army during its escape from the French to launch an attack against Frederick the Great. Traun also collaborated with Prince Charles; together, their united forces completely outmaneuvered Frederick, forcing a Prussian retreat to Silesia with heavy losses.
