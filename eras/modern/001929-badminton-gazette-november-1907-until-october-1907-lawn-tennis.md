# Badminton Gazette

## Overview and Official Status
The *Badminton Gazette* is a publication associated with the sport of badminton. In November 1907, it became the official organ of the Badminton Association. Prior to this date, until October 1907, the official organ of the Badminton Association was a publication titled *Lawn Tennis and Badminton*.

## Related Publications and Literature
The *Badminton Gazette* has served as a medium for sporting instruction and information. In its November 1907 issue, the publication reprinted an article by S. M. Massey, which had originally appeared in the *Badminton Magazine* in February 1907; the version in the *Gazette* was slightly revised. For comprehensive details regarding the laws of the game, readers are directed to the *Laws of Badminton and the Rules of the Badminton Association*, which is published annually in London.

## The Game of Badminton
Badminton is a fast-paced game played with rackets and shuttlecocks. The name of the sport is derived from the seat of the duke of Beaufort in Gloucestershire. While the game appears to have first been played in England around 1873, it was played in India before that time and remains very popular there.

### Governance and Rules
The Badminton Association in England was established in 1895. The association's laws were developed from a code of rules created in 1887 for the Bath Badminton Club, which were themselves based on the original Poona rules of 1876. 

According to the rules, a shuttle is considered "in play" from the moment it is struck by the server's racket. It remains in play until one of the following occurs:
* It touches the ground.
* It touches the net without crossing over.
* A "fault" is committed.

### Gameplay and Technique
In England, the game is typically played in a covered court and can be played using either daylight or artificial light. The sport consists entirely of volleying and is noted for requiring more staying power in a single game than a single in lawn tennis. 

The game can be played in two formats:
* **Two-handed or single game:** One player on each side.
* **Four-handed or double game:** Two players on each side.

The sport allows for significant skill and judgment, specifically through techniques such as "smashing" (hitting the shuttle with a hard downward stroke) and "dropping" (hitting the shuttle gently so it lands just over the net).

### Competitive History
The All England championships were introduced in stages:
* **1899:** Championships for mixed doubles, ladies' doubles, and gentlemen's doubles were instituted.
* **1900:** Championships for ladies' singles and gentlemen's singles were instituted.
* **1904:** The first championship between Ireland and England took place.
