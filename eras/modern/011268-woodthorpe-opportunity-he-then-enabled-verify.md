# Colonel Woodthorpe and Central Asiatic Exploration

## Exploration of the Pamirs and Central Asia
For many successive Indian viceroys, the establishment of a lucrative trade route between India and central Asia remained a primary goal. While efforts were made to improve the northern approaches to Simla, the regions of the great central depression and the highlands of the Pamirs remained largely unknown until the 1870 mission of Sir Douglas Forsyth to Yarkand. 

Prior to this, Robert Barkley Shaw and George Hayward acted as European pioneers of geography in the central dominion of Kashgar, both arriving at Yarkand in 1868 within a few weeks of one another. Shaw later joined the 1870 mission led by Forsyth, during which time Henry Trotter produced the first maps of Chinese Turkestan.

## The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission
A significant increase in geographical knowledge of central Asia occurred during the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission from 1884 to 1886. During this period, Sir Adelbert Talbot, Colonel St George Gore, and Colonel Sir T.H. Holdich mapped the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan. Simultaneously, Ney Elias traveled from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs to reach the commission's camp, identifying Rangkul, known as the "Dragon Lake," during his journey.

## Colonel Woodthorpe's Contributions
During the same period as the Boundary Commission, a mission led by Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart crossed into Wakhan via the Hindu Kush and returned to India through the Bashgol valley of Kafiristan. This mission provided the opportunity for Colonel Woodthorpe to determine the conformation of the Hindu Kush and verify the results of previous explorations conducted by W.W. M'Nair.

## Subsequent Asian Mapping
Following these events, further contributions to the map of Asia were made in 1885 by Andrew Dalgleish and Arthur Douglas Carey, who followed the tracks of Prjevalsky to varying degrees. In 1886, Captain (later Sir Francis) Younghusband completed an adventurous journey across the heart of the continent, crossing the Muztagh, which serves as the great mountain barrier between Kashmir and China.
