# Barcelona

Barcelona is a flourishing city and the principal seaport of Spain, located on the Mediterranean Sea in eastern Spain. It is situated on the sloping edge of a small plain between the Llobregat river to the south and the Besos river to the north. The city's geography is bounded to the north-east by the Montanas Matas, to the west by heights culminating in Tibidabo (1745 ft.), and to the south-west by the fortified hills of Montjuich, which reach an altitude of 650 ft. Since 1833, Barcelona has served as the capital of the province of Barcelona, and it was formerly the capital of Catalonia.

## History and Development
Traditionally founded in the 3rd century B.C. by the Carthaginian Hamilcar Barca, the city was known as Barcino. Following the Roman conquest, Augustus gave the city the status of a Roman colony and renamed it Julia Faventia (later Augusta and Pia). The Roman city was located on Monte Taber and became a leading mart of the western Mediterranean by the 2nd century A.D., competing with Massilia and Tarraco. 

The bishopric of Barcelona was established in 343. The city served as a temporary capital for the Visigoths in 415 and 531, and hosted church councils in 540 and 599. In 713, the city (then called Barcinona or Bardjaluna) was captured by the Moors, before passing to the dominion of the Franks in 801. From 874, the counts of Barcelona ruled as independent monarchs. The city reached its peak of wealth and fame after the 1149 union between Aragon and Catalonia. During this era, Barcelona's merchant ships traded as far east as Alexandria and as far west as the Baltic and North Seas. In 1258, James I of Aragon authorized the *Consulado del Mar*, a maritime law code recognized by various European states.

The city's commercial supremacy declined after the 1492 discovery of America, as trade shifted to western Spanish ports. Political tensions with the "Castilian" government led to a rebellion in 1640 against Philip IV, during which the city sought French protection. It returned to its allegiance in 1652 but was captured by the duke of Vendome in 1697.

## Urban Structure and Fortifications
Barcelona was previously enclosed by a strong line of ramparts and a citadel on the north-east, which was erected in 1715 by Philip V. Because these fortifications restricted the city's natural development, local authorities abolished them in 1845 despite opposition from the central government. The ground previously occupied by the citadel was converted into gardens, and the walls of the moat were used as cellars for houses built on the former site of the ramparts.

The city is now divided between an old town and a new town. The old town is characterized by narrow, irregular streets and buildings primarily made of brick, typically four to five storeys high with flat roofs and other oriental features. The main axis of the old town is the Rambla, which features a promenade planted with plane-trees and houses the city's principal theatres and hotels. In contrast, the new town was built according to a premeditated scheme of symmetry and precision, following a royal sanction for new districts in 1860. Architecture in the new town frequently employs hewn stone and modern English styles.

## Population and Economy
As of 1900, the population of Barcelona was 533,000. Excluding its most important suburbs—which include Sans, San Martin de Provensals, San Gervasio de Cassolas, San Andres de Palomar, Horta, Las Corts de Sarria, and Gracia—the city contained approximately 334,000 inhabitants. This figure represented an increase of nearly 150,000 since 1857, driven largely by immigrant artisans attracted by industrial development in the latter half of the 19th century.

The city is a major industrial hub and seaport. To the east, on the tongue of land forming the port, is the suburb of Barceloneta. Established around 1754 through the efforts of the marquis de la Mina, Barceloneta is regularly laid out with uniform brick houses. Additionally, the town of Badalona, located 6 m. N.E. of the city on the Mediterranean Sea, serves as an industrial suburb. Badalona contributes significantly to Barcelona's export trade through its fishing, boat-building, and various manufacturing works, including chemical, gas, and mineral-oil plants.

## Religious and Cultural Institutions
Barcelona is the see of a bishop and contains a large number of ecclesiastical buildings, although it possesses fewer than it did in the past. The city's history and topography have been documented in various works, including those by A. A. P. Arimon, J. Artigas y Feiner, and S. Sampere. Economic and political histories have been detailed by A. Capmany and T. Tomich, while M. Gil Maestre has written on the spread of the revolutionary movement and criminality within the city.
