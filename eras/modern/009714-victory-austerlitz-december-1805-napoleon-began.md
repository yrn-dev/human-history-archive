# Napoleon After the Victory of Austerlitz

Following the victory at the Battle of Austerlitz on December 2, 1805, Napoleon Bonaparte initiated a series of strategic political and military maneuvers to consolidate his power across Europe. His efforts focused on the creation of a network of allied states, the expansion of the French Empire, and the systematic neutralization of opposing European powers.

## Political Restructuring and the Ring of States
After Austerlitz, Napoleon began planning the formation of a ring of states that would surround the French Empire and remain in close alliance with it. A primary component of this strategy involved placing family members on foreign thrones. On June 6, 1806, Napoleon proclaimed his brother Louis as the King of Holland. 

However, the relationship between the two brothers was strained. Napoleon frequently reproached Louis for being too lenient with his subjects and for pursuing popularity too aggressively. This tension was further exacerbated by a violent jealousy Louis felt toward his wife. By 1808, when the throne of Spain became vacant, Napoleon offered it to Louis; upon Louis's refusal, the position was given to Joseph.

The relationship between Napoleon and Louis continued to deteriorate, particularly as the rigour of the "continental system" increased. By late 1809, Napoleon resolved to annex Holland to stop secret Dutch trade with England. Following the failure of negotiations with Great Britain in the spring of 1810, Napoleon sent French troops against the Dutch capital. Louis fled his kingdom and settled in Bohemia at Toplitz. On July 9, 1810, Napoleon officially annexed Holland to the French Empire.

## Military Campaigns and the Fall of Prussia
While Napoleon had defeated the Austrian and Russian armies at Ulm and Austerlitz, Prussia entered the war against him shortly thereafter. In 1806, Prussia was utterly defeated and broken at the Battle of Jena. 

Napoleon's focus then shifted toward Russia, an antagonist he continued to fight through 1807. This period included the Polish campaign, during which Napoleon was "roughly handled" at Pultusk and Eylau. Eventually, the Russians were defeated at Friedland in 1807. This victory led to an alliance with the young Tsar Alexander I. The two emperors met on a raft in the middle of the Niemen at Tilsit to reach an understanding.

## Administrative and Diplomatic Appointments
Napoleon relied on a circle of trusted officials and generals to manage the administration of his expanding territories and the logistics of his armies. 

*   **Daru:** Following Austerlitz, Daru participated in drafting the Treaty of Presburg and became the intendant-general of Napoleon's military household. He served in the campaigns of 1806–1807 against Russia and Prussia, helped draw up the Treaty of Tilsit on July 7, 1807, and supervised the financial and administrative duties of the French army occupying Prussian fortresses.
*   **Geraud Christophe Michel Duroc:** After commanding the grenadiers at Austerlitz, Duroc was tasked with several high-level negotiations. These included dealings with the elector of Saxony in December 1806, the incorporation of states into the Confederation of the Rhine, and the conclusion of the armistice of Znaim in July 1808.
*   **Vincenzo Dandolo:** In 1805, Napoleon appointed Dandolo as the *provediteur general* (governor) of Dalmatia. Dandolo worked to improve the region by introducing better agricultural methods and draining pestilential marshes.

## Territorial and Imperial Shifts
The period following Austerlitz saw significant shifts in the map of Europe. In 1806, Francis II dropped the title of Holy Roman Emperor, bringing the "Holy Roman Empire" to an end. Napoleon's dominance extended across most of Italy and Spain, and he came to dominate all of Europe west of Russia. 

Despite these land victories, Napoleon was unable to secure command of the sea from the British, following the conclusive defeat of his fleets by Admiral Nelson at the Battle of Trafalgar in 1805.
