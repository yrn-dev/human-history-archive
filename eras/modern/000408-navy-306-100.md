## British Naval Forces and Logistics
During the period surrounding the Great War, the British navy experienced significant growth in both personnel and capacity. In August 1914, the navy consisted of 145,000 officers and men with a total tonnage of 2,500,000. By August 1918, these figures had increased to 450,000 men and 8,000,000 tons.

The operational scale of the British navy was extensive. In a single month during 1918, British warships covered 1,000,000 sea miles within home waters, while auxiliary vessels traveled 6,000,000 miles, a distance equivalent to 250 times the circuit of the globe. Throughout the war, the navy managed the transport of 20,000,000 men—of whom 2,700 were lost to enemy action—as well as 2,000,000 mules and horses. Logistics included the movement of 130,000,000 tons of food and other materials, 51,000,000 tons of fuel and oil, and 25,000,000 tons of explosives, all while engaged in constant warfare against enemy submarines.
