# Avifauna of the Islands

The distribution and characteristics of bird species across various island groups reveal significant patterns of specialization, colonization, and regional boundaries. These peculiarities often manifest as variations in plumage, anatomy, and the presence of endemic families.

## The Moluccas and Celebes
In the Molucca Islands and Celebes, the avifauna exhibits distinct regional transitions. Birds of the Indian region disappear abruptly at a specific boundary known as Wallace's line, while many Australian forms reach this line but do not cross it. Additionally, Celebes is home to bird species that are akin to those found in Africa.

## The Papuan and Oriental Subregions
The Papuan Subregion, which includes the Moluccas, Celebes, the Timor group, and primarily New Guinea, is characterized by its brilliant or metallic plumage. The most distinctive feature of this area is the presence of birds of paradise, which are almost peculiar to the subregion and are more highly specialized than the Australian bower-birds (_Chlamydodera_). Other characteristic birds include the great crowned pigeons (_Goura_), various Megapodes, racquet-tailed kingfishers (_Tanysiptera_), and the largest and smallest parrots (_Nasiterna_ and _Calyptdrhynchiis_). The region is also noted for the extraordinary development of cassowaries, as well as specialized pigeons, parrots, honey-suckers, and kingfishers. However, it lacks several groups found in Australia, such as emeus, diurnal birds of prey, weaver birds (_Ploceidae_), babblers (_Timeliidae_), and the _Platycercinae_ parrots.

The Oriental Subregion, located between the Australian and Palaearctic areas, contains over seventy families. While only one family—the broadbills (_Eurylaemidae_)—is peculiar to this region, it is highly significant as it represents all the _Subclamatores_. Other characteristic birds include sunbirds (_Nectariniidae_), drongos (_Dicruridae_), cuckoo shrikes (_Campephagidae_), bulbuls (_Pycnonotidae_), pheasants, _Gallus_, and _Pavo_.

## The Malagasy and Ethiopian Regions
The Malagasy province, comprising Madagascar and the Seyehelle, Comoro, and Mascarene islands, is considered one of the most peculiar areas on earth. Madagascar alone has 238 species, 129 of which are peculiar to the island, including 35 peculiar genera. Notable examples include _Mesites_ (positioned between Gruiformes and Galli), _Philepitta_ of the Clamatores, and _Euryceros_ of the Oscines. Other abundant birds include the Coraciine _Geobiastes_, _Atelornis_, and _Brachypteracias_. The region also featured the extinct tall Ratite _Aepyornis_.

While Madagascar has unique forms, it also hosts birds identical to those in Britain, such as the common quail, barn owl, little grebe, and pigmy rail. Other islands in the region had specific extinct species: Mauritius had the dodo, _Aphanapteryx_, and _Lophopsittacus_; Rodriguez had the solitaire, _Necropsar_, and _Necropsittacus_; and Bourbon (Reunion) had _Fregilupus_. Additionally, the Ethiopian region's islands host peculiar kestrels, such as _T. punctatus_ in Mauritius, _T. newtoni_ in Madagascar, and _T. gracilis_ in the Seychelles.

## New Zealand and Polynesia
The New Zealand Subregion is highly isolated and shows strong Australian affinities. Its most extraordinary features were the gigantic moas (_Dinornithes_) and the weird-looking kiwis (_Apteryges_). Other peculiar developments include the takahe (_Notornis_), woodhens (_Ocydromus_), the wrybill (_Anarhynchus_), and the owl-parrot (_Stringops_). The region also had the recently extinct rapacious _Harpagornis_ and a large flightless goose, _Cnemiornis_.

Polynesia contains specialized forms resulting from long-term isolation. Examples include the kagu (_Rhinochetus_) of New Caledonia, which has affinities pointing to South America, and the toothbilled pigeon (_Didunculus_) of Samoa. The Sandwich Islands are entirely devoid of Coraciiformes and Psittaci but show an extraordinary development of the _Drepanidae_ family. Other residents include the Sandwich island goose (_Bernicla sandvicensis_), a peculiar Meliphagine genus (_Acrulocercus_), a coot, a raven, and various thrushlike birds and flycatchers.

## The Mediterranean Province
The islands of the Azores, Madeira, and the Canaries belong to the Mediterranean province. Birds here tend to vary from their continental counterparts, typically possessing stronger bills and legs and darker plumage. Specific examples include:
* **The Azores:** The bullfinch (_Pyrrhula murina_) has a mouse-coloured breast instead of the ruddy breast seen in _P. vulgaris_.
* **The Canary Islands:** The peculiar chaffinch (_Fringilla teydea_) has a sombre hue.
* **Madeira:** Home to the peculiar pigeon (_Columba trocaz_) and the golden-crested wren (_Regulus maderensis_).
* **Shared Species:** The chaffinch _F. tintillon_ is found across the Azores, Madeiras, and Canaries; it is closely allied to the Mauritanian _F. spodogenia_.

Research by F.D. Godman indicates that the avifauna of these islands is the result of colonization occurring over a long period.
