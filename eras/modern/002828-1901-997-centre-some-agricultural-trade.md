# Agricultural Trade Centres

Across various regions of Ireland, England, and Australia, several towns serve as primary hubs for agricultural trade, facilitating the movement of produce, livestock, and specialized goods from the surrounding districts to larger markets.

## Agricultural Centres in Ireland

In County Sligo, Ballymote is a market town located 14 miles south of Sligo. As of 1901, its population was 997. The town serves as a centre for some agricultural trade and maintains carriage-building works. Historically, it is noted for the remains of a strong castle built in 1300 by Richard de Burgh, the powerful earl of Ulster, and ruins of a 13th-century Franciscan foundation.

In County Antrim, Ballymoney is a market town situated 53 miles north-north-west of Belfast. With an urban district population of 2,952 in 1901, it is a centre for local agricultural trade. The town's economy is diverse, involving brewing, distilling, tanning, and the manufacture of tobacco, candles, and soap, alongside the linen manufacture common to the county.

Dundalk, a seaport in County Louth with an urban district population of 13,076 in 1901, maintains a brisk trade primarily in dairy and agricultural produce. Its port is a significant seat for the trade of live stock and agricultural produce, while also serving as a centre for salmon fisheries and a sea-fishery district.

Other Irish market towns with significant agricultural trade include Carrickmacross in County Monaghan, which is also known for its famous lace manufacture, and Enniscorthy in County Wexford. Enniscorthy, which had an urban district population of 5,458 in 1901, acts as the agricultural trade centre for its district, supported by water communication with Wexford and the presence of horse-fairs and important fowl markets.

## Agricultural Centres in England

In Bedfordshire, Biggleswade is a market town located 41 miles north by west of London. Its urban district population in 1901 was 5,120. Situated on the east bank of the Ivel, the town lies in a flat plain where vegetables are grown extensively for London markets, and the town serves as a centre for this trade. Due to its position on the Roman road to the north, it also became an agricultural centre for the surrounding district. Historically, the town held annual fairs, with those for horses being noted as famous by Camden.

In Shropshire, Market Drayton is a market town on the river Tern and the Shropshire Union canal. The population of the civil parish of Drayton-in-Hales was 5,167 in 1901. The town is a centre of agricultural trade and also contains large iron foundries.

## Agricultural Centres in Australia

In Victoria, Australia, Kyneton is a town in Dalhousie county located on the river Campaspe, 56 miles north-north-west of Melbourne. With a population of 3,274 in 1901, it serves as the centre of a prosperous pastoral and agricultural district. The town hosts an annual exhibition of stock as well as important stock sales.

## Regional Agricultural Production and Education

In the insular county of Buteshire in Scotland, agricultural produce is a primary export, with the staple crops being potatoes and oats. The region also rears horses, sheep, and cattle, and maintains an extensive seed-growing industry. To support these activities, the county council provides subsidies for technical education in agriculture at Kilmarnock and Glasgow.

Similarly, in another Scottish region where the chief towns include Dumfries and Annan, the county committee on secondary education uses grants to provide science and technical classes. These classes embrace practical chemistry, dairying (specifically at the Kilmarnock Dairy school), and agriculture.
