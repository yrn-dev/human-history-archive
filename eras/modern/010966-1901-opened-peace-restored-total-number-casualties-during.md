# Peace and Casualties in Turn-of-the-Century Conflicts

## The Restoration of Peace in 1901
The beginning of 1901 marked the restoration of peace following a campaign to crush a rebellion. This effort followed the relief of Kumasi and involved a force led by Colonel Willcocks. The force consisted of 134 British officers, 35 British non-commissioned officers, and 3,368 natives, including Sikhs from Central Africa and Yaos, as well as Ashanti levies. 

The decisive engagement occurred on September 30, when the Kumasi were completely beaten at Obassa. Following this defeat, many rebel chiefs surrendered, and the final two remaining in the field were captured on December 28. As a result of these actions, 1901 opened with peace restored. For his leadership, Colonel Willcocks was promoted and created a K.C.M.G.

## Regional Health and Population Data (1891–1901)
During the decade between 1891 and 1901, Bengal largely avoided the plague and famine that affected western and central India. While a drought occurred in 1896–1897, it did not cause actual famine, though distress was acute in the remote hills of Chota Nagpur and the densely populated districts of northern Behar.

Plague appeared sporadically in Calcutta in April 1898. Until April of the following year, the total deaths from plague in the province were fewer than 1,000, a stark contrast to the 191,000 deaths recorded for Bombay. However, a serious recrudescence occurred at the start of 1900 in Calcutta, alongside a malignant outbreak in the Patna district that caused 1,000 deaths per week. The plague reappeared in these regions in the early months of 1901.
