# Online Distributed Proofreading Team

The Online Distributed Proofreading Team is a collaborative group associated with the production of eBooks for Project Gutenberg. The team operates via the website https://www.pgdp.net (also referenced as http://www.pgdp.net) and is credited with the production of various digitized historical and academic texts.

## Role in Project Gutenberg
The Online Distributed Proofreading Team provides production credits for numerous Project Gutenberg eBooks. Their work involves transforming source materials into digital formats available for use at no cost and with minimal restrictions. This process often involves the use of existing scans of public domain materials, such as those provided by the Robinson Curriculum, or images made available by the American Libraries and The Internet Archive.

## Notable Projects and Collaborations
The team has been instrumental in the digitization of several large-scale works, most notably the 11th Edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica. Their contributions include various volumes of this encyclopedia, such as:
* "Austria, Lower" to "Bacon"
* "Baconthorpe" to "Bankruptcy"
* "Banks" to "Bassoon"
* "Bible" to "Bisectrix"
* "Coquelin, Benoît Constant" to "Costume"
* "Home, Daniel" to "Hortensius, Quintus"
* "Demijohn" to "Destructors"

Additionally, the team is credited with producing multiple volumes of George Grote's *History of Greece*, including Volume 01, Volume 02, and Volume 03.

## Technical Processes and Standards
The production process involves specific transcription standards to maintain the integrity of the original texts. In certain volumes of the Encyclopaedia Britannica, the team and its collaborators have implemented the following conventions:
* **Subscripts:** Numbers following letters without a space (e.g., C2) indicate original subscripts. When letters are subscripted, they are preceded by an underscore (e.g., C_n).
* **Superscripts:** Characters following a carat (^) are used to represent original superscript text.
* **Formatting:** Side-notes from the original print may be relocated to serve as titles for their respective paragraphs.
* **Corrections:** In some instances, typographical errors are corrected and underlined in the HTML versions of the texts.
