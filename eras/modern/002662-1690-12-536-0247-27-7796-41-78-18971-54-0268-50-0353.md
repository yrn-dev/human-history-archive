# 1690

The year 1690 is marked in the archive's records by significant events in colonial expansion, military conflicts, and political and intellectual developments across Europe and Asia.

## Colonial and Urban Foundations
A pivotal event in the history of India occurred on August 24, 1690, with the founding of Calcutta. Job Charnock of the English East India Company established the settlement after previous attempts to secure a location. In 1686, English merchants under Charnock's leadership had been forced to leave their factory due to a rupture with Mogul authorities, retreating approximately 26 miles down the river to the village of Sutanati. Following temporary occupations of Sutanati in December 1686 and November 1687, Charnock permanently occupied the site on August 24, 1690. This settlement eventually expanded along the river bank to the village of Kalikata, evolving from a cluster of hamlets into a major town.

## Military Conflicts and Naval Engagements
In Europe, the year saw various military actions. In Gornja Tuzla, Austrian forces routed the Turks. This victory led to the removal of Roman Catholics, including Franciscan friars numbering approximately 3,000, who were relocated into Slavonia.

In naval history, a commander in the royal navy, who had previously received a full pardon, died on June 21, 1690. He fell while leading an assault on St Christopher's. Additionally, the period is associated with the Siege of Londonderry, as evidenced by historical narratives published in later years, including a 1690 account by J. Mackenzie.

## Political and Economic Figures
Nicholas Barbon, an English economist and the probable son of Praise-god Barbon, was active during this period. Born in London and educated in medicine at Leiden and Utrecht, Barbon is credited as the institutor of fire-insurance in England, a venture he began around 1680. In 1690, Barbon served as the Member of Parliament (M.P.) for Bramber. He also published a work titled *A Discourse of Trade* in 1690. Barbon's intellectual contributions extended to the rebuilding of London after the fire of 1666 and the foundation of a land bank that was later united with that of John Briscoe in 1696.
