# The Baptiste Family of Actors

The Baptiste family was a prominent group of French theatrical performers active during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. The family was led by Joseph Francois Anselme, a popular actor, and his wife, who performed leading roles in tragedies. The couple enjoyed the friendship of Lekain and the protection of Voltaire.

## Nicolas Anselme Baptiste (Baptiste, aine)
Nicolas Anselme Baptiste (1761–1835) was the elder son of Joseph Francois Anselme. Born in Bordeaux on June 18, 1761, he made his first stage appearance as de Belloy in the production *Gaston et Bayard*, likely under the auspices of Lekain. 

Early in his career, using the stage name Baptiste, he signed a contract in Arras to perform roles as a young lover, though he also appeared in pantomime and opera. Following three successful years in Rouen, his reputation reached Paris. He was summoned to a new theatre founded by the comedian Langlois-Courcelles.

## Family Engagements and Stage Names
Upon joining the theatre in Paris, Nicolas Anselme succeeded in securing engagements not only for himself but for his entire family. To distinguish the family members in playbills, specific labels were used:
*   **Baptiste, aine**: Nicolas Anselme (the elder son).
*   **Baptiste pere**: Joseph Francois Anselme (the father).
*   **Baptiste cadet**: Paul Eustache Anselme (the younger brother).
*   **Madame Baptiste mere**: The mother.
*   **Madame Baptiste bru**: The daughter-in-law (wife of Nicolas).

The presence of so many family members with the same stage name led to a pun in which a play featuring the entire group was referred to as *une piece de baptistes*.

## Career and Success of Nicolas Anselme
Nicolas Anselme gained public favor through his role in La Martelliere's *Robert, chef de brigands* and as Count Almaviva in Beaumarchais' *La Mere coupable*. His performance in the latter was so highly regarded that the directors of the Theatre de la Republique sought his services in 1793. To secure him immediately, the directors paid a 20,000 franc forfeit to cover the cost of breaking his existing contract.

Throughout his career, Nicolas took all leading roles in tragedy and comedy. As he aged, he specialized in roles portraying noble fathers. He served the stage for thirty-five years of uninterrupted service before retiring in 1828.

## Later Years and Legacy
Following the revolution of 1830, the Theatre Francais faced significant difficulties. Nicolas and his brother, Paul Eustache Anselme, returned to the stage to assist the theatre in regaining its prosperity. Nicolas Anselme died in Paris on December 1, 1835. Both Nicolas and his younger brother eventually achieved the status of *societaire*.
