# Gironde

Gironde is a department in south-western France. Its capital is the city of Bordeaux.

## Bordeaux
Bordeaux is described as one of the most extensive and finest cities in France. It is located on the west (or left) bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea. The city is situated within a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district. At this location, the Garonne river forms a semicircle, which serves to separate the city proper on the left bank from La Bastide, an important suburb located on the right bank.

As of 1906, the population of Bordeaux was 237,707.

### Infrastructure and Transport
The city is connected to other major hubs via railway and road. It is located 359 miles south-south-west of Paris via a main line of the Orleans railway, and 159 miles north-west of Toulouse via the main line of the Southern railway. A railway bridge connects the Southern company station on the left bank with the Orleans railway company station in La Bastide. Additionally, the river is crossed by the Pont de Bordeaux, a stone structure from the early 19th century that measures 1,534 feet in length.

### City Layout and Landmarks
The city's quays are wide and busy, featuring a background of mansions, factories, and lofty warehouses, with steeples and towers rising behind them. Near the center of these quays is the Place des Quinconces. This area is planted with plane trees and features two huge statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne, as well as two rostral columns that function as lighthouses. On the west side of the Place des Quinconces stands a monument dedicated to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed in 1793 under the convention.

At the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces is the Place de la Comedie, which houses the 18th-century Grand Theatre. The city's central life is concentrated in the narrow, winding streets surrounding the Place des Quinconces. In contrast, the streets outside this central quarter are quiet and narrow, characterized by low white houses rather than the high tenements found in other large French towns.

The city is encircled by a semicircle of boulevards. Beyond these boulevards are the suburbs of Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat. The primary promenades are located near the city center and include the Place des Quinconces, the allees de Tourny, and the public garden.

## Regional Geography and Borders
The department of Gironde shares a border with the inland department of Dordogne to the east. In the neighboring department of Dordogne, the Isle river—a chief affluent of the Dordogne river—crosses the center of that department before flowing into the Dordogne at Libourne, which is located within the department of Gironde.
