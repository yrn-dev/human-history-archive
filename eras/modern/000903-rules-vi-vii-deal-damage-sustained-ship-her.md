# Damage to Ships and Appliances in General Average

In the context of maritime law and the rules governing General Average (G.A.), specific provisions address the damage sustained by a vessel and its appliances during efforts to save the ship and its cargo from peril. Rules VI and VII specifically focus on the damage incurred when a ship has stranded and efforts are made to force her off the ground.

## Damage from Refloating Efforts
When a ship is stranded, the efforts required to force her off the ground or drive her higher up the ground for the common safety often involve an abnormal use of the vessel's equipment. This abnormal use is likely to cause damage to the ship's appliances, specifically the engines, boilers, sails, and spars. Under the established rules, such damage is treated as an act of sacrifice.

According to Rule VI, any loss of or damage to spars and sails caused by these efforts to move the ship for the common safety shall be made good as G.A. However, this does not apply if the ship is already afloat; in such instances, no loss or damage caused by carrying a press of sail is admissible as G.A.

Rule VII addresses the machinery of a vessel. Damage caused to the boilers and machinery of a ship that is ashore and in a position of peril is allowed in G.A., provided it is demonstrated that the damage arose from an actual intention to float the ship for the common safety, accepting the risk of such damage.

## Legal Precedents and Interpretations
The application of these rules is consistent with English law. In the 1895 case of *The Bona*, the court of appeal determined that both the coal and stores consumed during refloating efforts, as well as the damage sustained by the engines while they were worked to get the ship off the ground, were subjects for G.A. contribution at common law.

## Related Sacrifices and Intentional Stranding
The rules for damage during refloating are distinct from those governing the initial act of stranding. Under Rule V, the conditions for voluntary stranding are considered. If a ship is intentionally run on shore because it would otherwise inevitably sink or drive onto rocks or shore, no loss or damage to the freight, cargo, or ship resulting from that intentional act is made good as G.A. Conversely, in all other cases where a ship is intentionally run on shore for the common safety, the resulting damage or loss is allowed as G.A.

## Additional Costs and Cargo Damage
Beyond the ship's physical structure and machinery, other expenses and damages associated with a stranded vessel may be admitted as G.A. under subsequent rules:

*   **Lightening the Ship:** Rule VIII and Rule IX state that when a ship is ashore, the loss or damage sustained by discharging cargo, bunker coals, and ship's stores to float the vessel—including the extra cost of reshipping, lighter hire, and lightening—shall be admitted as G.A.
*   **Cargo Damage:** Rule XII provides that damage or loss of cargo necessarily caused by the acts of storing, reloading, stowing, and discharging shall be made good as G.A., provided the costs of those measures are also admitted as G.A.
*   **Fuel Consumption:** Rule IX allows for the admission of cargo, stores, or ship's materials burnt for fuel for the common safety during a time of peril, provided an ample supply of fuel had been provided. In these cases, the estimated quantity of coal that would have been consumed (calculated at the price of the last port of departure) is credited to the G.A. and charged to the shipowner.

## Repairs and Adjustments
When damage is allowable as G.A., certain rules govern the financial adjustment:

*   **Temporary Repairs:** Under Rule XIV, no "new for old" deductions are made from the cost of temporary repairs for damage allowable as G.A.
*   **Permanent Repairs:** Rule XIII specifies that repairs are subject to "new for old" deductions based on the age of the ship. For iron or steel ships, those up to one year old are allowed repairs in full, with the exception of bottom coating or painting, from which one-third is deducted.
*   **Valuation:** Rule XVI stipulates that the amount made good for sacrificed goods is based on the loss sustained by the owner, using market values at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival.
