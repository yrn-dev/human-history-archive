# De Sapientia Veterum and Redargutio Philosophiarum

The works *De Sapientia Veterum* and *Redargutio Philosophiarum* are philosophical and scholarly writings attributed to Francis Bacon, produced during a period of his life characterized by a tension between active public service and his project to reorganize natural science.

## Context and Composition
During the early 17th century, Bacon was heavily involved in active life, including legal and commercial disputes regarding the proposed Union and the naturalization of the *Post Nati*. Despite these obligations, he continued his efforts to develop a new system of science. Following the groundwork laid in *The Advancement of Learning*, Bacon produced several shorter pieces. 

In 1609, Bacon authored the panegyric *In felicem memoriam Elizabethae* and the work *De Sapientia Veterum*, which is described as an ingenious and curiously learned piece. During this same period, he completed the *Redargutio Philosophiarum*.

## Redargutio Philosophiarum
The *Redargutio Philosophiarum* is a highly finished work composed in the form of an oration, likely written around 1608 or 1609. It is a treatise focused on the "idols of the theatre." The text provides a detailed exploration of concepts that Bacon later expanded upon in the first book of the *Novum Organum* in connection with the *Idola Theatri*. Within the archive's classification of Bacon's minor philosophical writings, it is noted as a piece that contains material later taken up in more elaborate works, though it remains significant for its specific method of treatment and point of view.

## Related Philosophical Works
The *De Sapientia Veterum* shares a thematic approach with another of Bacon's tracts, *De Principiis atque Originibus secundum Fabulas Cupidinis et Caeli*. In both works, Bacon utilizes the disguise of old mythological stories to uncover deep truths. In the latter tract, he discusses the nature of primitive matter, the origin of things, and the limits of science, while providing notices of the modern philosopher Telesio and the ancient philosopher Democritus.

Other contemporary minor works from this period include:
*   ***Cogitata et Visa***: A 1607 tract written in sonorous Latin that contains the substance of the first book of the *Organum*.
*   ***Valerius Terminus***: Composed probably around 1603, providing an obscure outline of the first two parts of the *Instauratio*.
*   ***Temporis Partus Masculus***: A fragment noted for an arrogant and offensive style, where the term *masculus* suggests the fruit-bearing power of the new philosophy compared to the imperfect "births of time" that preceded it.
*   ***Descriptio Globi Intellectualis***: A work that details the general classification of the sciences.
