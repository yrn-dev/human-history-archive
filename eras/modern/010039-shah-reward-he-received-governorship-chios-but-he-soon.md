# Shah

The title of Shah is associated with several historical figures and dynasties across Persia, Hindustan, and Afghanistan, appearing in the archive's records primarily in relation to military conquests, the administration of provinces, and the establishment of regional sovereignty.

## Nadir Shah
Nadir Shah was a Persian monarch who advanced from Persia to invade Hindustan. During his campaigns, he established authority in the province of Baluchistan while stationed at Kandahar, where he dispatched several detachments. 

Nadir Shah's influence extended to the appointment of regional governors. He ordered that Abdulla Khan continue in the government of Baluchistan. Later, after the death of the tyrannical Haji Mahommed Khan (the eldest son of Abdulla Khan), Nadir Shah received a report of the events from Nasir Khan, the second son of Abdulla Khan. Nasir Khan had accompanied Nadir Shah to Delhi and had earned the monarch's confidence. In response, Nadir Shah issued a firman in 1739 appointing Nasir Khan as the *beglar begi* (prince of princes) of all Baluchistan.

The military reach of Nadir Shah also intersected with European adventurers. Claude Alexandre, Comte de Bonneval—a French soldier who had previously served in the Austrian army and later converted to the Mahommedan faith as Ahmed Pasha—was appointed by the sultan to organize and command the artillery. In this capacity, Ahmed Pasha rendered valuable services to the sultan during wars involving both Russia and the "famous" Nadir Shah.

## Ahmad Shah (Durani)
Following the death of Nadir Shah in 1747, Ahmad Shah, known as the king of Kabul, rose to prominence. His reign was marked by conflicts over the independence of regional leaders, most notably Nasir Khan of Kalat. 

In 1758, Nasir Khan declared himself entirely independent of Kabul. Ahmad Shah responded by dispatching a force under one of his ministers, but this army was totally routed by the khan. Ahmad Shah then personally led strong reinforcements into a pitched battle, in which he defeated Nasir Khan. Although the king invested the city of Kalat with his entire army, he was unable to take the city by surprise or storm. 

The conflict ended in a treaty of peace with several stipulations:
* The king was to marry the cousin of Nasir Khan.
* Nasir Khan was exempt from paying tribute.
* Nasir Khan was required to furnish troops to assist the king's armies when called upon, receiving a cash allowance equal to half of the troops' pay.

As a reward for his subsequent services in the wars of Kabul, Ahmad Shah bestowed several districts upon Nasir Khan in entire and perpetual sovereignty.

## Other Figures and Titles
The archive mentions other figures using the title "Shah" or associated with imperial governance:
* **Shah Jahan:** Under this ruler, the seat of the governorship of Budaun (a town in British India) was transferred to Bareilly, leading to a decline in the importance of Budaun.
* **Malik-Shah:** A sultan of the Seljuks whose vizier, Nizam-ul-Mulk, was a contemporary of Hassan ibn Sabbah. Hassan attempted to supplant the vizier in the favor of Malik-Shah but was outwitted and forced to leave Persia.
