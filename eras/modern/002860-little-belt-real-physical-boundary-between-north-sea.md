# Little Belt

The Little Belt is a narrow channel of water located in Denmark that serves as one of the communication links between the Baltic proper and the Kattegat. It is specifically situated between the island of Fünen (also known as Fyen) and the Schleswig coast of Jutland.

## Physical Characteristics and Geography
The Little Belt varies in width along its course. In the north, the channel is not half a mile wide. However, the average width between the island of Fünen and the Schleswig coast is 10 miles. The town of Middelfart is located near the narrowest reach of the channel.

Several islands of limited extent are located off the west coast of Fünen within the Little Belt. To the south of Fünen, there is an archipelago enclosed by the long, narrow islands of Langeland (32 miles in length) and Aerö (16 miles in length). This archipelago includes a triangular area of shallow sea containing the islands of Turö, Dreiö, Avernakö, and Taasinge.

## Regional Context and Boundaries
The Little Belt is part of a larger system of narrow channels—including the Great Belt and the Sound—that allow the Baltic Sea to communicate with the Kattegat. The Kattegat itself is the eastern portion of a winding channel between the Cimbrian peninsula and the south of Scandinavia; the part of this channel west of a line joining Christiania fjord with the Skaw is known as the Skagerrak.

While the Little Belt is a primary channel, the real physical boundary separating the North Sea from the Baltic Sea is formed by a plateau. This plateau encompasses the islands of Laaland, Funen, and Zealand, and extends from the islands of Moen and Falster to the coasts of Ruegen and Mecklenburg.

## Associated Settlements and Landmarks
The western coast of Fünen features the town of Assens, which serves as a port for crossing the Little Belt into Schleswig. Assens still retains traces of fortifications that were stormed in 1535 by John of Ranzau. Other coastal towns on Fünen include:
* **Odense**: The principal town, located near Odense Fjord behind the Hindsholm peninsula.
* **Nyborg**: An eastern port providing a steam-ferry connection to Korsör in Zealand.
* **Svendborg**: A town overlooking the southern archipelago.
* **Faaborg**: Located to the south-west on a fjord of the same name.
* **Bogense**: A small port situated on the north coast.

Additionally, the island of Langeland is home to the great castle of Tranekjaer, with records dating back to the 13th century. The ports of Rudkjöbing on Langeland and Aeröskjöbing on Aerö are noted as considerable ports.
