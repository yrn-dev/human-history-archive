# Brest-Litovsk

Brest-Litovsk is a strongly fortified town located in Russia within the government of Grodno. It is situated at 52° 5' N. latitude and 23° 39' E. longitude, positioned at the intersection of railways arriving from Moscow, Kiev, Warsaw, and East Prussia. Geographically, the town lies at the junction of the Bug and the navigable river Mukhovets.

## History
The town was first mentioned at the beginning of the 11th century. Its history is marked by several periods of destruction and rebuilding:
* **1241:** The town was laid waste by the Mongols and remained unbuilt until 1275.
* **1379:** The Teutonic Knights burned the suburbs of the town.
* **Late 15th Century:** The khan of the Crimea destroyed the entire town.

During the reign of the Polish king Sigismund III, Brest-Litovsk served as a location for diets. In 1594 and 1596, the town hosted two remarkable councils of the bishops of western Russia. The town was captured by the Swedes twice, first in 1657 and again in 1706. In 1794, it was the site of a victory by Suvarov over the Polish general Sierakowski. Brest-Litovsk was added to the Russian empire in 1795.

## Demographics and Religion
As of 1867, the population of the town was 22,493, which grew to 42,812 by 1901. In the 1901 census, more than one-half of the population were Jews. The town contains a Jewish synagogue that was considered the first in Europe during the 16th century. Additionally, Brest-Litovsk is the seat of a Greek Catholic bishop and an Armenian bishop; the latter holds authority over Armenians throughout the entire country.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Brest-Litovsk is a center for extensive trade in several commodities, including leather, tar, wood, hemp, flax, and grain. 

The town is integrated into a larger waterway system via the King's canal (also known as the Brest-Litovsk canal). This canal is 50 miles long and utilizes the Bug and Mukhovets rivers to create a link connecting the Vistula with the Dnieper. The town is located 137 miles by rail south of the city of Grodno.
