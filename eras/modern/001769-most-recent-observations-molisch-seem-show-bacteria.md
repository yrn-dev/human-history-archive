# Observations of Molisch on Bacteria

The research conducted by Molisch has contributed significant findings to the understanding of bacterial assimilation, mineral oxidation, and the phenomenon of luminosity in organic matter.

## Assimilation and Pigmentation
Molisch's observations indicate that bacteria possessing bacteriopurpurin exhibit a specific type of assimilation: the assimilation of organic material under the influence of light. In these red-purple bacteria, the coloring matter is located within the protoplasm of the cell. This differs from most chromogenic bacteria, where the pigment is either formed by their action in the medium or occurs as excreted pigment between and on the cells.

The solubility of bacterial pigments varies by species. While the pigments of red-purple sulphur bacteria, as well as the violet pigments of *B. janthinus* and *B. violacens* and the crimson and scarlet pigments of *B. ruber* and *B. prodigiosus*, are not soluble in water, they are readily extracted by alcohol. Some forms, such as *B. berolinensis* and the yellow *Micrococcus cereus flavus*, yield their color to neither water nor alcohol. In contrast, certain fluorescent bacteria, including *B. fluorescens* and *B. pyocyaneus*, form a blue-green fluorescent body that is water-soluble.

## Iron and Manganese Bacteria
In the study of bacteria found in chalybeate waters and marsh pools near ironstone—such as those in the genera *Leptothrix*, *Cladothrix*, and *Crenothrix*—there is a debate regarding the liberation of energy. Winogradsky posits that these organisms liberate energy by oxidizing ferric and ferrous oxide within their protoplasm, a view that H. Molisch does not accept. 

These bacteria absorb hydrocarbonate from soluble conditions, such as the soluble bicarbonate of the protoxide found in chalybeate springs. The resulting ferric hydroxide accumulates in the sheath and eventually becomes insoluble ferric oxide. Molisch has demonstrated that in some bacteria, iron can be replaced by manganese, a process that contributes to the formation of manganese ores, just as the oxidation of iron results in the creation of ochre and enormous deposits of bog-iron ore.

## Bacterial Luminosity
Molisch has also researched the occurrence of luminosity in organic materials. While phosphorescence in sea fish and other animals is attributed to marine bacteria (studied by researchers such as Fischer, Beyerinck, and Cohn), Molisch's more recent researches have shown that the luminosity of ordinary butcher's meat is a common occurrence under appropriate conditions. 

In a study of meat samples purchased in Prague and stored in a cool room for approximately two days, Molisch found that luminosity was present in 52% of beef samples, 50% of veal samples, and 39% of live samples.
