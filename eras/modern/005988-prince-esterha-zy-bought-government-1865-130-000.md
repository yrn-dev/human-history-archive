# Prince Esterházy and the National Gallery

## The National Gallery Collection
The national gallery was formed by Prince Esterházy. In 1865, the government purchased this collection for the sum of £130,000. Before its current installation, the gallery was housed in the academy.

## Integration into the Museum of Fine Arts
The national gallery was installed in 1905 within the museum of fine arts. This museum was constructed between 1900 and 1905 and is located in the Andrássy Ut. The national gallery was placed there alongside a collection of modern pictures sourced from the national museum.

## Surrounding Architecture of the Andrássy Ut
The museum of fine arts is situated on the Andrássy Ut, a street that contains several other notable institutions. These include the academy of music, the national drawing school, and both the old and new exhibition buildings. Additionally, the street features an opera-house, built between 1875 and 1884 in the Italian Renaissance style. At the end of the Andrássy Ut stands one of many monuments created throughout the country to commemorate the thousandth anniversary of the foundation of the kingdom of Hungary.

## Other Notable Regional Edifices
The broader urban landscape includes various significant structures. Fronting the left bank of the Danube are the Houses of Parliament, a late Gothic limestone edifice covering 3¾ acres (erected 1883-1902), and the Academy, built in the Renaissance style from 1862-1864. The Academy contains a library, a botanic collection, a historic picture gallery, and a lofty reception room. Other nearby structures include the Redoute buildings, designed for social purposes and balls in a mixed Moorish and Romanesque style, as well as an extensive custom-house.

Other remarkable buildings in the area include:
* The national museum (1836-1844).
* The town-hall (1869-1875), built in the early Renaissance style.
* The university library (opened 1875), a Renaissance building, and the university, which features a baroque façade rebuilt in 1900.
* The palace of justice (1896), located near the Houses of Parliament and the palatial buildings of the ministries of agriculture and justice.
* The Austro-Hungarian bank (1904) and the exchange (1905).
* The art-industrial museum (1893-1897), which features oriental style and Hungarian ornamentations.
