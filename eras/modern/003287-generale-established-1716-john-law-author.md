# Banque Générale

## Establishment and Early History
The Banque Générale was the first bank of issue established in France. It was founded in 1716 by John Law, who was also the author of the *Systeme* and the *Mississippi Scheme*. In 1718, the institution was converted into the Banque Royale. During this period, the notes issued by the bank were guaranteed by King Louis XV. The bank came to an end in 1721.

## Subsequent Attempts and the Bank of France
Following the end of Law's bank, an attempt was made to reconstruct a bank in 1767, but this new establishment was suppressed in 1793. While other banks continued to operate and some issued notes, they achieved only limited success and never attained real power.

In 1796, there were numerous negotiations regarding the establishment of a bank. Although financial difficulties at the time prevented an immediate result, the advice provided by those involved in these plans assisted Napoleon I. In 1800, Napoleon I, with the aid of his minister Mollien, founded the Bank of France.

## Functions and Evolution of the Bank of France
The Bank of France was established to supply loanable capital at a moderate charge and to support French industry and trade. It has exercised these functions with great judgment and vigour, eventually extending its reach across the entire country through its branches and attached towns.

Initially, the operations of the Bank of France did not cover all of France. Under a law adopted in 1803, departmental banks with the privilege of issue were formed; by the end of 1847, nine such banks existed in the larger towns. These departmental banks were absorbed into the Bank of France in 1848, granting the Bank of France an exclusive privilege of issue. Later, in 1863, the Bank of France took over the Bank of Savoy following the union of that province with France. From its founding until the present, the Bank of France has remained the most powerful financial institution in the country.
