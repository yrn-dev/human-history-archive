# St Petersburg

St Petersburg has served as a significant center for international diplomacy, scholarship, the arts, and literature, attracting a diverse array of figures from across Europe and Asia.

## Academic and Scientific Contributions
The city has been a hub for linguistic and philological study. Otto von Bohtlingk, a distinguished German Sanskrit scholar born in St Petersburg in 1815, studied Oriental languages—specifically Sanskrit, Persian, and Arabic—at the university of St Petersburg between 1833 and 1835. After further studies in Germany, Bohtlingk returned to St Petersburg in 1842 to join the Royal Academy of Sciences, where he was elected an ordinary member in 1855. 

Bohtlingk's scholarly output in the city was extensive. He published *Vopadevas Grammatik* (1847), *Uber die Sprache der Jakuten* (1851), and *Indische Spruche* (1870-1873). He also produced translations of the *Brihadaranyaka-upanishad* and *Chhandogya-upanishad* in 1889. His most significant work, the seven-volume *Sanskrit-Worterbuch*, was completed over twenty-three years and published in St Petersburg between 1853 and 1875, with a new edition appearing between 1879 and 1889.

## Literary and Artistic Life
St Petersburg has hosted numerous poets, dramatists, and fabulists. The Russian poet Jewgenij Abramovich Baratynski was educated at the royal school in the city. Later, a collected edition of his poems was published in St Petersburg in two volumes in 1835. Ivan Andreevich Kriloff, the national fabulist of Russia, moved to the city with his mother to seek a government pension and held a post in the civil service until 1788. Kriloff later served from 1812 to 1841 in the Imperial Public Library, eventually becoming the head of the Russian books department. A statue of Kriloff in the Summer Garden is regarded as one of the city's finest monuments.

The city's theatrical scene attracted international talent. Jean Baptiste Prosper Bressant, a French actor, arrived at the French theatre in St Petersburg in 1838. He spent eight years there playing important parts and building a reputation before returning to Paris in 1846.

The city also played a role in the life of the writer Dostoievsky, who settled there in 1865. During this period, he was involved in journalistic enterprises related to Slavophil interests, though he suffered severe financial losses. After a period of exile in Italy and Germany to escape creditors, he returned to Russia in the early seventies and served as the director of *The Russian World*. Dostoievsky spent the final eight years of his life in St Petersburg in comparative prosperity until his death on February 9, 1881.

## Diplomacy and International Residents
The city's court and administration attracted various foreign nationals. August Friedrich Ferdinand von Kotzebue, a German dramatist, served as the secretary to the governor-general of St Petersburg through the influence of Graf Görtz, the Prussian ambassador to the Russian court. 

The city also provided refuge for political figures. Pierre Etienne Louis Dumont, a French political writer and pastor from Geneva, moved to St Petersburg following political troubles in his hometown in 1882. He served as the pastor of the French church in St Petersburg for eighteen months before moving to London in 1885.

## Exploration and Medicine
St Petersburg served as a starting point for early 18th-century diplomatic and medical missions. In 1714, the Scottish traveller and medical doctor John Bell arrived in the city. He was appointed as the medical attendant to Valensky for a Persian embassy (1715-1718). Bell also attended Peter the Great during an expedition to the Caspian Gates and Derbend. After a mission to Constantinople, Bell returned to St Petersburg in May of an unspecified year before eventually retiring to Scotland.
