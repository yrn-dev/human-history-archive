# Zeitschrift für vergleichende Sprachforschung

The *Zeitschrift für vergleichende Sprachforschung auf dem Gebiete der Indogermanischen Sprachen* is recognized as the standard periodical regarding the study of Indo-Germanic languages. It has served as a primary venue for research into comparative philology and the linguistic history of the Indo-Germanic peoples.

## Connection to Comparative Mythology
The periodical was intimately connected with the work of Franz Felix Adalbert Kuhn (1812–1881), a German folklorist and philologist. Kuhn founded a new school of comparative mythology that was based upon comparative philology. His research focused on the language and history of the Indo-Germanic peoples as a whole, specifically seeking to account for the earliest civilization of these peoples before they separated into different families. Kuhn utilized the comparison and analysis of original stems and words common to different languages to support his theories. He maintained that the most essential factors in the origin of myths were homonymy and polyonymy, arguing that the roots of myths were to be found within the domain of language.

## Contributions to Indo-Aryan and Indo-European Studies
The *Zeitschrift* has published significant research concerning the evolution and structure of Indo-European languages. In 1903, G. A. Grierson authored an article titled "On Certain Suffixes in the Modern Indo-Aryan Vernaculars" (pp. 473 seq.), which provided detailed information regarding various postpositions.

The publication has also been cited in the context of identifying the separation of the Aryan branch from other Indo-European languages. Research published in the *Zeitschrift für vergleichende Sprachforschung* in 1908 (vol. 42, pp. 24 ff.) by E. Meyer relates to discoveries made by German excavators at Boghaz Keui in Cappadocia. These discoveries involved treaties from the beginning of the 14th century B.C. between the king of the Hittites and the king of Mitanni. These texts appear almost certainly to contain the names of Indra, Varuna, and Mitra, which are deities belonging to early Aryan mythology.
