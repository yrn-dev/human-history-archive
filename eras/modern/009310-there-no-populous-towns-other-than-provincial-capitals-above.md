# Urban Population Distribution and Provincial Centers

The distribution of populous towns across various global regions often reveals a concentration of inhabitants within provincial capitals or specific commercial and industrial hubs. In several territories, the provincial capitals serve as the primary centers of population, while other towns remain small or specialized in specific industries.

## Urban Centers in South America and Central America

In Bolivia, the total area is 515,156 sq. m. (according to Gotha computations with corrections for territory lost to Brazil in 1903). The region is characterized by a lack of populous towns outside of the provincial capitals. Four of these capitals—Oruro, Cochabamba, La Paz, and Sucre (or Chuquisaca)—have functioned as the national capital. While Sucre was originally chosen, the capital shifted to La Paz following the revolution of 1898, as La Paz is more accessible and serves as the commercial metropolis. 

Other prominent smaller towns in Bolivia are often defined by their commercial or industrial roles, particularly in the department of Potosi. These include the silver mining center of Pulacayo (pop. 6512), where 3,200 men work in surface works and mines; Tupiza (pop. 1644), a mining and commercial center near the Argentine frontier; and Uyuni (pop. 1587), a highway convergence point and railway junction. Other notable locations include Viacha (pop. 1670), a railway station 14 m. from Alto de La Paz; Tarata (4681) and Totora (3501), which are trading centers in the department of Cochabamba; and Ascension (pop. 4784), a large mission station in the Chiquitos hills of the department of Santa Cruz.

In Honduras, the official 1905 estimate for the aggregate population was 500,136, though census accuracy is hindered by Indian populations avoiding the process due to fears of taxation or military conscription. The population is concentrated on the Pacific slope. The capital, Tegucigalpa, had a population of approximately 35,000 in 1905. Other important towns include Jutigalpa (18,000), Comayagua (8,000), and the seaports of Trujillo (4,000), Amapala (4,000), and Puerto Cortes (2,500). Additionally, the towns of Santa Rosa, Choluteca, La Esperanza, and Nacaome each have more than 10,000 inhabitants.

## Urbanization in Asia

In Korea, the inland city of Seoul serves as the capital with a population of nearly 200,000. Other major cities include Phyöng-yang, which had a population of 60,000 prior to the war of 1894, and Songdo (Kaisöng), a walled city with 60,000 inhabitants that served as the capital from approximately 910 to 1392. Songdo is a center for the grain trade, wooden shoes, fine matting, coarse pottery, and the sole center for ginseng manufacture. Outside of Fusan, Wön-san, Tung-nai, Chöng-ju, and Kang-hwa, it is doubtful if any other Korean towns reach a population of 15,000. Many cities and provincial capitals are walled, and the larger towns are mostly located in the fertile southern provinces.

In Siam, Ayuthia (known to the Siamese as *Krung Kao* or "the Old Capital") is the chief town of a rich agricultural provincial division and the headquarters of a high commissioner. With a population of about 10,000, the city is situated on the river Me Nam and is characterized by floating houses. It serves as a hub where paddi is sent by rail and river to Bangkok in exchange for cloth and other goods.

## European Urban Distributions

In the grand-duchy of Luxemburg, the capital, Luxemburg (or Lutzelburg), had a population of 20,984 in 1905. The town is situated on the Alzette river and was once considered the strongest fortress in Europe after Gibraltar before being dismantled in 1867.

In Baden, the population in 1905 was 2,009,320, with about half classified as rural (living in communities of fewer than 2,000 inhabitants). The capital is Karlsruhe. Major towns include Mannheim (162,607), Heidelberg (49,439), Pforzheim (59,307), Freiburg (74,102), and Constance (24,818). Other important towns include Lahr, Bruchsal, Baden-Baden, and Rastatt.

In the Basque Provinces, specifically Biscay, Bilbao is the principal port and capital with a population of 83,306. Baracaldo (15,013) is an important industrial town, and Sestao (10,833) is the only other town exceeding 10,000 inhabitants. Other notable locations include the fishing station of Bermeo (9061), Durango (4319), and the village of Guernica (3250).

In the canton of Lucerne, the capital is Lucerne. Other towns include Kriens (pop. 5951), Willisau (4131), Ruswil (3928), Littau (3699), Emmen (3162), and Escholzmatt (3127).
