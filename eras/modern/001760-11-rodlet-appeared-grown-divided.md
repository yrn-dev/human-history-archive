# Growth and Development of Bacillar Rodlets

In the study of Schizomycetes, particularly within the family Bacillaceae, the vegetative cells often take the form of straight, cylindric structures known as rodlets. These organisms are characterized by division planes that always run perpendicular to the long axis of the cell.

## Colony Formation and Division
The process of colony formation in certain varieties of *Bacillus (Proteus) vulgaris* has been observed in hanging drops, revealing a rapid sequence of growth and division. Observations indicate that a single rodlet may appear at 11 A.M.; by 4 P.M., this rodlet grows and divides, breaking up into eight separate rodlets. Further development continues through the evening, with subsequent stages recorded at 8 P.M. and 9:30 P.M.

## Characteristics of Specific Bacilli
Different species of bacilli exhibit distinct growth patterns and morphologies:

*   ***Bacillus megaterium***: This species can appear as a chain of motile rodlets that are actively growing and dividing. When treated with an alcoholic iodine solution, a rodlet in this state may appear divided into four segments.
*   ***Bacillus anthracis***: In cultures of aqueous humour, these rodlets grow out into long filaments resembling *leptothrix*. These filaments later become septate, and spores develop within the segments.
*   ***Bacillus subtilis***: This species is associated with motile rodlets and the development of spores.

## Classification of Rodlet-Forming Organisms
The archive classifies various sporogenous rodlets based on their shape and motility:

*   **Bacilleae**: This sub-family includes cylindric sporogenous rodlets that do not alter in shape. This group includes the non-motile *Bacillus*, the monotrichous *Bactrinium* (possessing one polar flagellum), the lophotrichous *Bactrillum* (possessing a terminal tuft of cilia), and the peritrichous *Bactridium* (possessing cilia over the entire surface).
*   **Clostridieae**: This sub-family consists of spindle-shaped sporogenous rodlets, such as the peritrichous motile *Clostridium*.
*   **Plectridieae**: This sub-family consists of drumstick-shaped sporogenous rodlets, such as the peritrichous motile *Plectridium*.

## Spore Formation and Germination
Spores in Schizomycetes are typically minute, highly refractive, and colorless, though they can appear very dark. For example, spores of *Bacillus subtilis* measure 0.0006 mm in breadth and 0.0012 mm in length. These spores are spherical, ovoid, or long-ovoid, and their membranes may be thick or exhibit strata.

The formation of a spore usually occurs within a bacillar segment, and typically only one spore is formed per cell. In some instances, the protoplasm involved in spore formation reacts blue when exposed to iodine solutions. Spores may develop even while cells are actively swarming. Some organisms, referred to by older writers as "Koepfchenbacterien," are actually bacterioid segments with a spore at one end, where the mother cell-wall has adapted to the spore's outline.

Germination patterns vary by species:
*   In *B. subtilis*, the germinal rodlet's axis of growth is at a right angle to the long axis of the spore.
*   In *Clostridium butyricum*, the axis of growth coincides with the long axis of the spore.

## Environmental and Pathogenic Factors
The distinction between pathogenic and non-pathogenic Schizomycetes is not sharp. The virulence or pathogenicity of these organisms can be increased or diminished through definite treatment or alterations in the environment. Furthermore, many species are facultative parasites, meaning they can function as either parasites or saprophytes depending on the circumstances. Some bacteria that are not observed as parasites still produce poisons or toxins through the fermentation and decomposition of organic substances.
