# German Explorers and Missionaries in the 19th and 20th Centuries

The activities of German missionaries and explorers during the mid-19th century and into the early 20th century contributed significantly to the geographical and linguistic documentation of various regions, particularly in Africa, the Middle East, and the polar regions.

## Exploration of East Africa
In the middle of the 19th century, the interior of the region now known as British East Africa was first made known through the efforts of German missionaries Johannes Rebmann and Ludwig Krapf, as well as Baron Karl von der Decken. These explorations were often perilous; Baron von der Decken and three other Europeans were murdered by Somali in October 1865 at a town called Bardera while they were exploring the Juba river.

Beyond geographical discovery, German scholarship focused heavily on the linguistic structures of the continent. Carl Meinhof collected a vast amount of information regarding existing languages in South-East and East Africa, as well as the original structure of the Bantu mother-speech. His findings were published in the *Mittheilungen des Seminaers fuer Orientalische Sprachen* in Berlin (also issued through Brockhaus, Leipzig, in 1899) and in his 1906 work, *Grundzuege einer vergleichenden Grammatik der Bantusprachen*. While valuable, some of Meinhof's deductions were not in agreement with those of other authorities.

## Surveys of the Middle East
German explorers also conducted detailed studies of the Levant and surrounding areas. In 1860, J. G. Wetzstein published *Reisebericht ueber Hauran und die Trachonen* in Berlin. Later, in 1900, an important survey of the Hauran and neighboring regions was directed by Dr. Enno Littmann under American auspices. The results of this survey began to be published in 1906.

## Polar Expeditions
German exploration extended to the Arctic regions of Greenland. In 1870, a second German polar expedition was led by Carl Christian Koldewey. This expedition reached Cape Bismarck at 77 deg. N. Although the expedition identified a feature they called the Dove Bay, it was later ascertained in 1905 by the duke of Orleans that this was actually a strait and that Cape Bismarck was located on an island.

## Other 1860s Contexts
While not exclusively German, the year 1860 marked various global milestones in exploration and settlement. In the United States, the settlement of Duluth had only 80 inhabitants in 1860. In the Pacific, the colony in the Marquesas Islands—which had been formally possessed by France in 1842 after French Roman Catholic missionaries prepared the way—was practically abandoned between 1860 and 1870. Additionally, the year 1860 saw the publication of *The Sources of the Nile* by Beke and *Lake Regions of Equatorial Africa* by Richard Francis Burton.
