## Literary and Poetic Authorship
The archive documents several individuals recognized as authors of specific works. Alexander Brome (1620–1666), an English attorney and poet, authored a comedy titled *The Cunning Lovers* (1654) and published *Songs and other Poems* in 1661. This collection included political songs, epigrams, translations, elegies, epitaphs, ballads, and epistles. Brome also authored a translation of Horace (published in 1666) and served as the editor for two volumes of plays by Richard Brome.

## Classical and Ancient Attribution
The archive contains discussions regarding the authorship of ancient Greek texts, where the identity of the author is often a subject of critical debate. Regarding Homer, one perspective suggests he is not an individual man but rather the divine or heroic father of the gentile Homêrids. In this view, Homer is the author of the *Iliad*, the *Odyssey*, the *Thebaïs*, the *Epigoni*, the *Cyprian Verses*, and the *Proœms* (or Hymns), though these compositions may have emanated from different individuals among the Homêrids.

Furthermore, the archive notes the role of "recent editors" who act as secondary authors or revisers of classical texts. For example, editors of Thucydidês have changed the final words of certain passages based on the authority of various manuscripts (MSS). Similarly, editors Schneider and Krüger revised the text of Xenophon's *Anabasis*, changing the reading of a passage regarding the Euphrates river from *παρὰ* to *ἐπὶ*.

## Legal and Regulatory Authorship
Authorship also extends to the creation of "by-laws" (or "bye-laws"). These are regulations authored by corporations, companies, boards, or councils, typically under statutory power. These regulations are designed for the preservation of good government and order within a specific jurisdiction. 

When authored under the authority of a statute, these by-laws must be submitted to a confirming authority for approval before they can operate. To be valid, they must be reasonable, not retrospective, and not contrary to the general law of the land. In the case of corporate bodies, the authors of by-laws must ensure the regulations are in harmony with the society's objects and do not limit the duties or powers of its officers.

## Professional Application in Architecture
In the professional sphere, the architect acts as the author of building designs. The architect may be required to design and superintend the erection of a wide variety of structures, including:
*   Domestic buildings and dwelling-houses (including those for the working class).
*   Public buildings such as hotels, libraries, hospitals, schools, and churches.
*   Commercial and industrial sites, including shops, factories for all general trades, electric power stations, studios, and cold storage buildings.
*   Specialized facilities such as slaughterhouses and stables.

The architect's design process is governed by the by-laws of different districts or towns. In England, these are carried out by district or borough authorities via the Public Health Acts and By-laws. These authorities appoint inspectors to ensure that buildings—particularly those used for the slaughter of beasts for human consumption or the making of patent foods—meet sanitary arrangements and avoid defects that could affect the clean preparation of food.
