# Saxons in Military and Regional History

The history of the Saxons is characterized by their involvement in various European conflicts, their role in the foundation of early English kingdoms, and their colonization of Eastern European regions.

## Early Migration and the Foundation of England
According to the *Anglo-Saxon Chronicle*, the Saxons were one of three nations of invaders who settled in Britain. While the Jutes occupied southern Hampshire and Kent, the Saxons founded the kingdoms of Essex, Sussex, and Wessex. The kingdom of Sussex is attributed to the landing of Ella (or Ælle) in 477, while Wessex is said to have originated with Cerdic, who arrived approximately eighteen years later. However, these dates are regarded with suspicion. While some evidence suggests the royal family of Essex was of Saxon origin, the West Saxon royal family claimed a stock shared with Bernicia and maintained connections with the Angli.

## Colonization of Kolozsvár
In 1178, Saxons colonized the site of a Roman settlement known as *Napoca*. Following this colonization, the settlement received the German name *Klausenburg*, derived from the word *Klause*, meaning "mountain pass." This town later served as the capital of Transylvania and the seat of the Transylvanian diets. Between 1545 and 1570, the Saxon population of the town decreased significantly due to the introduction of Unitarian doctrines.

## Military Engagements in the Thirty Years' War
During the Thirty Years' War, the Saxon army was involved in several major movements and conflicts in central Germany. In the 1630s, the Swedish field-marshal Johan Baner entered Bohemia with 16,000 men and combined his forces with the Saxon army to march on Prague. This advance was eventually halted by the defeat of Bernhard of Saxe-Weimar at the first battle of Noerdlingen. Later, in 1639, Baner overran northern Germany and defeated the Saxons at Chemnitz before invading Bohemia.

## Conflicts in the 18th and 19th Centuries
During the mid-18th century, Saxony was frequently aligned with various powers in conflicts involving Prussia and Austria.

### The Campaign of 1742
In 1742, a combined operational plan was formed between the French, Saxons, and Prussians to rescue Linz. During this period, Saxon forces joined with Frederick of Prussia. However, the Saxons became demoralized and discontented, eventually marching away to their own country.

### The Campaign of 1745
On January 8, a Quadruple Alliance was concluded at Warsaw, consisting of Saxony, England, Holland, and Austria. By the end of May 1745, Prince Charles commanded 85,000 Austrians and Saxons behind the Riesengebirge near Landshut. On June 4, these combined forces suffered a complete defeat at the battle of Hohenfriedberg (also known as Striegau) at the hands of Frederick, who commanded approximately 65,000 men.

### Subsequent Prussian-Austrian Conflicts
In later engagements, diplomatists successfully won Saxony over to the Austrian side. This left Frederick isolated and exposed to the combined attacks of the Saxons and Austrians. The united forces of Traun and Prince Charles eventually outmanoeuvred the Prussians, forcing a retreat from Prague back to Silesia with heavy losses.

### The Battle of Dennewitz
In the 19th century, Saxons were again involved in large-scale European warfare. On September 6, 1813, at the village of Dennewitz in the Prussian province of Brandenburg, an army of 58,000 French, Poles, and Saxons under Marshal Ney was defeated by 50,000 Prussians led by Generals Tauentzien and Bülow.
