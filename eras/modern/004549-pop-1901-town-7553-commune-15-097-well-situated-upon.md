# Bassano

## Geography and Demographics
Bassano is a city located in the province of Vicenza within Venetia, Italy. It is situated at the foot of the Venetian Alps and is well positioned upon the Brenta river, which is spanned at this location by a covered wooden bridge. The city is accessible by rail, located 30 miles north of Padua and 24 miles northeast of Vicenza. As of 1901, the population of the town was 7,553, while the population of the commune was 15,097.

## History and Governance
Bassano apparently came into existence around A.D. 1000. In the 11th century, a German named Eccelin obtained fiefs in the district from Conrad II, founding the Ezzelini family. This family became prominent in the history of North Italy during the 13th and 14th centuries.

The possession of the city was disputed between Vicenza and Padua. It was briefly under the power of Gian Galeazzo Visconti of Milan, who provided fortifications for the city. At the start of the 15th century, Bassano passed to Venice. Under Venetian government, the city's industries flourished, specifically its manufacture of majolica—which continues to this day—and its printing-press.

In later history, the city was the site of a military action on September 8, 1796, fought between the Austrians and the French, resulting in a French victory.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The upper portion of the town, located above the river, contains a castle erected by the Ezzelini in the 13th century. A tower built by a member of the same family remains a conspicuous feature of the landscape.

The city's religious architecture includes the church of S. Francesco. Construction of this church began in the 12th century in the Lombard Romanesque style and was continued during the 13th century in the Gothic style. Additionally, some houses in the city retain traces of paintings on their facades.

## Arts and Culture
The cathedral, the museum, and several other churches in Bassano house paintings by the da Ponte family, who were active in the 16th and early 17th centuries and were surnamed Bassano after their place of birth. Jacopo is noted as the most eminent member of this family. Furthermore, the museum contains letters and drawings by the sculptor Antonio Canova.
