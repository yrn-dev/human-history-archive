# Prussia and the Armistice of Giurgevo

The diplomatic and military interactions between Prussia, Austria, and Turkey during the late 18th century were characterized by shifting alliances and a struggle for the European balance of power. A pivotal moment in this period occurred under the leadership of the Habsburg ruler Leopold II, whose statesmanship resolved a conflict with Prussia and facilitated a peace agreement with Turkey.

## The Conflict Between Austria and Prussia
The tensions between Austria and Prussia were rooted in the ambitions of Joseph II, Leopold II's predecessor. In 1784, Joseph II attempted to acquire Bavaria by negotiating an exchange of the Netherlands with the elector Charles Theodore. This plan was opposed by the duke of Zweibruecken, the heir to the Bavarian throne. In response to this appeal, Frederick the Great of Prussia formed the *Fuerstenbund* (a confederation of German princes) on July 23, 1785, to oppose Austrian preponderance.

This move formally established Prussia as the protector of the German states against the ambitions of Austria. Prussia subsequently became the center of an anti-Austrian alliance that included Poland, Sweden, and the maritime powers.

## The War with Turkey
While dealing with Prussian opposition, Joseph II entered a war with Turkey in 1788, acting in alliance with Russia. The initial campaign, conducted by Joseph II personally, was a dismal failure. The Austrian army was disorganized by disease and pursued across the Danube by Turkish forces. Although Marshal Loudon later achieved some successes, these were offset by a strategic blow on January 31, 1790, when Prussia and Turkey concluded an alliance. Joseph II died shortly thereafter, on February 20, 1790.

## Leopold II and the Treaty of Reichenbach
Upon ascending to power, Leopold II faced a complex geopolitical landscape. Domestically, he reverted to traditional Habsburg methods, restoring the supremacy of the Church and the old machinery of estates and diets to ensure stability. In foreign policy, he sought to resolve the empire's external conflicts.

Leopold II utilized his skill in negotiations to end the quarrel with Prussia. These efforts culminated in the Treaty of Reichenbach, signed on August 15, 1790. The resolution of the conflict with Prussia was a critical prerequisite that paved the way for the armistice of Giurgevo with Turkey, which was signed on September 10. Following these diplomatic successes, Leopold II was able to restore order in the Low Countries. The conflict with Turkey was definitively settled on August 4, 1791, with the signing of a peace treaty at Sistova, which largely established the *status quo*.

## The Role of Great Powers in the Low Countries
The broader European balance of power also influenced the region. England, Holland, and Prussia had previously encouraged the Patriots in their revolt against Austria in Belgium. This support was not based on a desire for Belgian independence, but rather on a strategy to weaken Austria and prevent her policy of extension toward Constantinople in the east of Europe. 

However, these powers eventually betrayed the young Belgian republic. At the conference held at Reichenbach—which included Prussia, England, Holland, and Austria—it was decided to restore Austrian rule in Belgium. This was later finalized by the Treaty of the Hague in 1790, allowing Emperor Leopold II to reoccupy the "Austrian Netherlands."
