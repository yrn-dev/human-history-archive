# Commerce

Commerce across various global regions is characterized by the production and exchange of agricultural staples, specialized industrial goods, and natural resins. Trade patterns vary from localized industrial suburbs to major international seaports and regional hubs.

## Agricultural and Regional Trade
In certain island regions, the production of grain, fruit, wine, and oil forms the basis of the local economy. These islands maintain an active trade with Barcelona, specifically in fresh fish and large quantities of lobsters. While most agricultural products are sent to the Peninsula, items such as rice, almonds, lemons, figs, wine, and marble are exported to Africa and Europe. In these regions, shoemaking is noted as one of the most prosperous industries. Because direct trade with foreign countries is limited, principal imports—including coal, timber, sugar, petroleum, leather, and cotton—are introduced via Barcelona.

Similarly, in the Portuguese archipelago of the Azores, the seaport of Horta serves as a center for profitable fisheries, including tunny, bonito, mullet, and whale. The port's exports include grain, wine, fruit, and sperm-oil. Between 1904 and 1897, the port accommodated approximately 140 vessels annually, totaling 220,000 tons, primarily of Portuguese or British nationality.

In other regions, such as those served by the Transcaspian railway (built 1886-1888), trade is promoted through the export of raw cotton, silk, carpets, and fruits to Russia, while imports from India include indigo, shawls, tea, and cottons. In these areas, goods are often transported via horses, donkeys, or camels in hilly tracts due to a lack of roads.

## Port Cities and Industrial Hubs
Major ports facilitate extensive international commerce. Bordeaux, the fourth port in France, maintains commercial relations with the United States, Portugal, Spain, Great Britain, and Argentina. Its exports include fruit, potatoes, other vegetables, oil-cake, pitwood, salt-fish, chemicals, cotton and woollen goods, sugar, hides, skins, and brandy. Wine is a central component of its trade; sales of wine account for about one quarter of its 13.5 million sterling in exports. The city also supports subsidiary industries such as cooperage, wine-mixing, and the manufacture of bottles, corks, capsules, wooden cases, and straw envelopes. Bordeaux is also a center for ship-building, constructing merchant vessels, torpedo-boats, ironclads, and railway carriages.

In Spain, the town of Badalona serves as an industrial suburb to Barcelona. Its fertile plains produce fruit, wine, and grain, while the town itself manufactures sugar, biscuits, glass, cotton and woollen goods, and brandy. It also houses mineral-oil, chemical, and gas works, and is known for its boat-building and fishing trades.

## Specialized Commodities and Natural Resins
The commerce of resins, specifically "dragon's blood," involves various plant species across different geographies. In the Eastern Archipelago and Further India, the *Calamus draco* palm produces a resin from its round, scaly fruit. The highest quality, known as *jernang* or *djernang*, is purified by melting the resin via boiling water or sun heat and moulding it into quills or sticks. An inferior version is extracted by boiling. This resin is used in China for red facing on writing paper and in Europe for lacquers and varnishes. Other sources include *Dracaena cinnabari* from Socotra, *Dracaena draco* from the Canary Islands (procured from cracks in the trunk and leaf surfaces), and *Croton draco* from Mexico, where it is used as an astringent and vulnerary.

## Land Tenure and Production
In some regions, commerce is tied to specific land tenure systems, such as the *colonia perpetua*. Under this system, tenants pay rent in kind based on a proportion of the produce. For example, in the production of olives, the portion due to the landlord is determined by valuators who estimate the yield just before the fruit ripens. In Corfu, some high-quality olive trees can yield up to 2 gallons of oil. While vineyards are worked with broad heart-shaped hoes and the vintage begins on September 26 (O.S.), the wines of Corfu are not widely exported.

## General Exports and Imports
Broad commercial inventories in various archives list a wide array of traded goods. Exports often include raw materials and luxury items such as opium, gum tragacanth, liquorice root, mohair, silk, sponges, olive oil, and various minerals. Corresponding imports frequently consist of manufactured goods, including hardware, glass-ware, petroleum, salt, sugar, and various cotton and woollen cloths.
