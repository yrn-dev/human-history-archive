# France et la question d'Autriche (76, 1902); Rene Henry, Questions

## Overview of the Austrian Question
The "Austrian question" has been a subject of significant diplomatic and political analysis, particularly regarding its developments at the turn of the 20th century. Literature on this topic examines the intersection of European powers and the internal stability of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.

## Diplomatic and Political Perspectives
Recent developments concerning the Austrian question are addressed in several key works from the early 1900s. Andre Cheradame explored these issues in *L'Europe et la question d'Autriche au seuil du XX^e siecle* (1901) and in *L'Allemagne, la France et la question d'Autriche* (1902). Additionally, Rene Henry authored *Questions d'Autriche-Hongrie et question d'orient* (1903), a work featuring a preface by Anatole Leroy-Beaulieu. Further analysis of the future of the region is provided by "Scotus Viator" in *The Future of Austria-Hungary* (1907).

Other diplomatic histories of Austria cover earlier periods, such as A. Beer's studies on Austrian politics from 1801 to 1810 and the empire's oriental politics since 1774. Other scholars, including A. Fournier and F. von Demelitsch, have focused on the roles of Gentz, Cobenzl, and Metternich in the early 19th century.

## Racial and Linguistic Issues
Beyond diplomacy, the Austrian question encompasses a "Racial Question," involving extensive literature on race and languages within the empire. Legal aspects of these issues are detailed in the *Oesterr. Staatswoerterbuch* by Josef Ulbrith and Ernst Mischler. Other contributions to this discourse include:
* Bertrand Auerbach's *Les Races et les nationalites en Autriche-Hongrie* (1898).
* L. Leger's *La Save, le Danube et le Balkan* (1884).
* Bressnitz von Sydacoff's *Die panslavistische Agitation* (1899).
* Works by Hainisch and Herkner regarding the future of the Deutsch-Oesterreicher.
