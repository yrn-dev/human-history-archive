# Jerome Bonaparte

Jerome Bonaparte was a member of the Bonaparte family and served as the King of Westphalia. He is the subject of various historical works and the progenitor of a branch of the family that settled in the United States.

## Biographical Works and Literature
Several chief works relate to the life and reign of Jerome Bonaparte. These include the seven-volume *Memoires et correspondance du roi Jerome et de la reine Catherine* (1861-1866) and *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* (1883), both written by Baron Albert du Casse. Other significant texts include *Konig Jerome Napoleon* by M.M. Kaisenberg, *The Bonaparte-Patterson Marriage* by W.T.R. Saffell, and *Jerome Napoleon, roi de Westfalie* (1902) by A. Martinet.

The correspondence between King Jerome and Queen Catherine of Westphalia and King Friedrich of Wurttemberg was published by August von Schlossberger (1886-1887) and later supplemented by Baron du Casse in *Corresp. inedite de la reine Catherine de Westphalie* (1888-1893). Additional accounts are found in P.W. Sergeant's *The Burlesque Napoleon* (1905) and F. Masson's four-volume work, *Napoleon et sa famille* (1897-1900).

## The Bonapartes of Baltimore
Jerome Bonaparte established a branch of the family in America through his union with Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of a Baltimore merchant named William Patterson. Elizabeth was born in 1785 and was likely descended from Robert Paterson, the inspiration for Sir Walter Scott's "Old Mortality." 

The marriage took place in Baltimore on December 24, 1803. While the union was valid under American law and Pope Pius VII refused to declare it void, Napoleon greatly disliked the marriage and refused to recognize its legality. Under pressure from his brother, Jerome was forced to separate from Elizabeth. After a period in England, Elizabeth (referred to as Madame Patterson or Madame Bonaparte) returned to Baltimore, where she lived until her death in 1879.

## Descendants
Jerome and Elizabeth had one child, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805-1870). Born in England, he resided primarily in Baltimore and was noted for his strong resemblance to his uncle, the emperor. He maintained a relationship with his father, who provided him with a large allowance for a time, and the two met occasionally.

The first Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte had a son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832-1893). This grandson of the King of Westphalia entered the French army and served in Italy and the Crimea.

Another grandson, Charles Joseph Bonaparte, was the younger son of the first Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte. Born in Baltimore on June 9, 1851, and educated at Harvard University, Charles Joseph became a lawyer in 1874. He achieved a distinguished career in American politics, serving as the president of the National Municipal League. In President Roosevelt's cabinet, he served as secretary of the navy from July 1905 to December 1906, and subsequently served as the attorney-general of the United States until March 1909.

## Baron Albert du Casse
Baron Pierre Emmanuel Albert Ducasse (1813-1893) was a French historian born in Bourges. In 1849, he served as aide-de-camp to Prince Jerome Bonaparte, the ex-king of Westphalia, who was then the governor of the Invalides. On Jerome's commission, Ducasse wrote *Memoires pour servir a l'histoire de la campagne de 1812 en Russie* (1852). 

Ducasse also served as an attaché to Jerome's son, Prince Napoleon, during the Crimean War. This role led to the publication of a *Precis historique des operations militaires en Orient* (1857) and a later volume, *La Crimee et Sebastopol de 1853 a 1856*. Ducasse was also employed by Prince Napoleon to work on the *Correspondance* of Napoleon I. While his publications, including *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* and the *Journal de la reine Catherine de Westphalie*, provided significant light on Napoleon I and his entourage, some critics noted they were edited with little care and lacked complete trustworthiness.
