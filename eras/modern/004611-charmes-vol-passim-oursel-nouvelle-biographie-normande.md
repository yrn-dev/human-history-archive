### Military and Political Figures
Ambroise de Loré (1396–1446) was a French commander and the baron of Ivry in Normandy. Born at the château of Loré in the arrondissement of Domfront (Orne), he was active in the warfare against the English. His military career included participation in the battle of Agincourt in 1415 and battles at Patay, Meung-sur-Loire, and Jargeau in 1429. He utilized his fortress of Saint Céneri as a base of operations and conducted an incursion to the walls of Caen, where he captured three thousand prisoners. After being exchanged for Talbot in 1433, he continued his service, helping the earl of Richmond expel the English from Paris in 1436. He served as the provost of Paris starting in February 1437 and was appointed "judge and general reformer of the malefactors of the kingdom" in 1438.

### Scientific Figures
Eugène Eudes-Deslongchamps (1830–1889) was a French palaeontologist. He served as a professor of zoology at the faculty of sciences at Caen, succeeding his father around 1856. By 1861, he had expanded his role to become dean and a professor of geology. His scientific contributions include the *Prodrome des Téléosauriens du Calvados*, a memoir on Teleosaurs completed after his father's death in 1867. He also produced works for the Société Linnéenne de Normandie, covering the geology of Calvados (1856–1863) and the department of La Manche (1856), as well as studies on Jurassic brachiopods, the *Terrain callovien* (1859), and the lower Jurassic stages of Normandy (1864). He also wrote on *Nouvelle-Calédonie* (1864) and issued the incomplete work *Le Jura normand* between 1877 and 1878.

### Literary Figures
Charles Dufresny, Sieur de la Riviere (1648–1724), a French dramatist born in Paris, wrote a play titled *La Reconciliation normande* in 1719. This work is included in the six volumes of his *Theatre* (1731).
