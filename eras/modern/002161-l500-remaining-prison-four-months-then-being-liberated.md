# Robert Baillie of Jerviswood

## Early Life and Legal Conflict
Robert Baillie, known as Baillie of Jerviswood, was the son of George Baillie of St. John's Kirk, located in Lanarkshire. He was described by his cousin, Bishop Burnet, as a man of great virtue and piety who adhered to presbyterian principles. Baillie was noted for being learned in languages, mathematics, and the law. He married a sister of Lord Warriston, Sir Archibald Johnston.

Baillie first drew the resentment of the Scottish government in June 1676. This conflict arose when he rescued his brother-in-law, a Presbyterian minister named Kirkton, who had been illegally confined in a house and seized by an informer named Carstairs. As a result of these actions, Baillie was fined L500. He remained in prison for four months and was eventually liberated after paying one-half of the fine to Carstairs.

## Political Activity and Arrest
By 1683, Baillie became despondent regarding the state of his country and attempted to emigrate to South Carolina, though this plan failed. In the same year, he and several friends traveled to London to seek redress, entering into communications with Russell, Monmouth, and their party. 

Following the discovery of the Rye House Plot, Baillie was arrested. During questioning by the king, Baillie denied any knowledge of the conspiracy. However, he truthfully admitted that he had been consulted regarding a potential insurrection in Scotland.

## Imprisonment and Execution
Despite the lack of evidence connecting him to the plot, Baillie was kept in close confinement and fined L6000. During this period of imprisonment, he was loaded with irons. His health declined, and he entered a languishing state.

On December 23, 1684, Baillie was brought before the high court on charges of treason. He was found guilty the following day. On the afternoon of December 24, 1684, he was hanged at the market cross in Edinburgh. The execution was carried out with all the usual barbarities. His treatment is remembered as one of the worst crimes perpetrated by the Stuart administration in Scotland.

## Legacy and Family
Baillie left behind a son named George. George sought refuge in Holland before eventually returning to Scotland with William III, at which point he was restored to his estates.
