# Sodium Hydroxide and Hydrolytic Processes

## Chemical Properties and Hydrolysis
Sodium hydroxide is characterized as a "strong base" that is highly ionized, producing a comparatively large amount of hydroxyl ions in solution. This property is demonstrated in the behavior of borax in aqueous solution. Boric acid is a weak acid that is scarcely ionized and provides only a small quantity of hydrogen ions. When a concentrated aqueous solution of borax is treated with just enough acetic acid to turn litmus red, the subsequent addition of a large volume of water changes the litmus color back to blue. This occurs because the dilution of the solution occasions hydrolysis, producing sodium hydroxide. The resulting excess of hydroxyl ions creates an alkaline reaction, which causes the litmus to turn blue.

## Applications in Industrial Dyeing
In the production of certain colors for cotton-dyeing, sodium hydrate (sodium hydroxide) is utilized in the creation of "Iron Buff." This process involves impregnating cotton with a solution of ferrous sulphate. The material is squeezed and then passed through a solution of sodium carbonate or sodium hydrate. Finally, the cotton is either exposed to air or passed through a dilute solution of bleaching powder. The resulting color is virtually iron-rust or oxide of iron, which is fast to washing and light, though it can be removed by acids.

## Related Hydrolytic Reactions in Organic Chemistry
Hydrolysis is a recurring mechanism in the preparation of various organic compounds, often involving the use of alkalies or specific esters:

*   **Acids:** Isobutyric acid can be artificially prepared through the hydrolysis of isopropylcyanide using alkalies. Normal butyric acid may be prepared via the hydrolysis of ethyl acetoacetate.
*   **Ketones:** Ketones can be produced by the hydrolysis of ketonic acids or their esters using baryta water or dilute alkalis. Additionally, the hydrolysis of alkyl derivatives of acetone dicarboxylic acid yields ketones.
*   **Other Compounds:** Acetonyl acetone can be obtained through the hydrolysis of diaceto-succinic ester. In the production of hydrindene, a hydrindene dicarboxylic ester (obtained by the action of sodiomalonic ester on ortho-xylylene bromide) undergoes hydrolysis to yield the corresponding acid. Furthermore, nitriles that combine with hydrocyanic acid furnish hydroxyacids upon hydrolysis.

## Comparison with Other Hydroxides
The archive distinguishes sodium hydroxide from other basic hydroxides, such as cadmium hydroxide. Cadmium hydroxide is obtained as a white precipitate when potassium hydroxide is added to a solution of a soluble cadmium salt. While cadmium hydroxide is soluble in ammonia, it is not soluble in an excess of dilute potassium hydroxide; this specific property is used to distinguish cadmium hydroxide from zinc hydroxide.
