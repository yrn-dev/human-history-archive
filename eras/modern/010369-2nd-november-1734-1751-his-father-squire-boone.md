# Daniel Boone

## Early Life and Family
Daniel Boone was an American pioneer and backwoodsman of English descent. He was born on November 2, 1734 (N.S.), near the city of Reading, Pennsylvania. Around 1751, Boone's father, Squire Boone, settled his family in the Yadkin Valley, located in what is now Davie County, North Carolina, which was the frontier at that time. During his early years, Daniel worked on his father's farm and spent a significant amount of his time trapping and hunting.

## Military Service and Early Exploration
In 1755, Boone served as a blacksmith and wagoner during Braddock's expedition against the Indians, an effort described as disastrous. He later visited Florida in 1765 and made his first visit to the Kentucky region in 1767. Between 1769 and 1771, Boone spent two years roaming through the area that is now Kentucky. He was accompanied by several companions, including John Finley, who had visited the region as early as 1752. During this period, Boone collected deer, beaver, and bear skins and encountered numerous adventures, including conflicts with roving bands of Indians.

## Settlement of Kentucky
Boone served in Lord Dunmore's War in 1774. In 1775, he led a party of settlers to Kentucky to found Boonesborough, which became an important settlement for a long period. 

On February 7, 1778, Boone and the party he had led were captured by a band of Shawnees. Boone was taken to Detroit and adopted into the Shawnee tribe. He eventually escaped and undertook a perilous 160-mile journey, reaching Boonesborough within four days. His timely arrival provided a warning regarding a formidable attack by his captors. Boone played a conspicuous part in repelling this attack, which took place from September 8 to September 17. Later, in 1782, he participated in the "Battle of Blue Licks," which is characterized as sanguinary.

## Political and Civil Career
During the period when Kentucky was part of Virginia, Boone represented the settlers in the Virginia legislature. He also held several official positions in Fayette County—one of the three counties Kentucky was divided into at the time—serving as county lieutenant, sheriff, and deputy surveyor.

## Later Years and Relocations
Due to carelessness regarding titles, Boone lost all of his land. Consequently, he moved to Point Pleasant, Virginia (now West Virginia), in 1788. Around 1799, he relocated again, this time to a location in territory then owned by Spain, approximately 45 miles west of St. Louis in what is now Missouri. In this region, he was appointed as the district's syndic and received a land grant of 1000 arpents, which is approximately 845 acres.
