# Thomas Erskine Holland

Thomas Erskine Holland was a distinguished legal scholar, author, and Bencher of Lincoln's Inn. He held several prestigious academic and professional titles, including K.C., D.C.L., and LL.D. He was a Fellow of All Souls' College, Oxford, as well as a Fellow of the British Academy. In his academic career, he served as the Professor of International Law at the University of Oxford.

## Literary Works
Holland authored several significant legal texts. Among his primary works is *Studies in International Law*. His other publications include:

* *Alberici Gentilis de jure belli*
* *The Laws of War on Land*

## The Role of Bencher and Lincoln's Inn
As a Bencher of Lincoln's Inn, Holland was part of the governing body of one of the four ancient societies known as the Inns of Court in England. These societies—which also include Gray's Inn and the Inner and Middle Temples—have functioned as schools of law since the 13th century. Their origins are attributed to a clause in the Magna Carta that fixed the Common Pleas at Westminster, bringing professors of law together in London.

The benchers of the Inns of Court hold significant disciplinary and administrative authority. They possess the right to reject applicants for membership and may refuse to call a student to the bar or expel members (barristers or other benchers) for sufficient reasons. Such decisions are subject to appeal to the common-law judges, who serve as visitors of the Inns. Benchers may take cognizance of any misconduct, whether professional or otherwise, that they deem unworthy of a barrister's rank.

Historically, the benchers' involvement in the systematic teaching of law declined between the 16th and 19th centuries. However, a modern system of legal education was established in 1852 through the Council of Legal Education. This body, appointed by the four Inns of Court, consists of twenty barristers and judges who control the education of students and administer examinations in subjects such as Common law, Equity, Roman law, Evidence, Procedure and Criminal law, Constitutional law, legal history, and Real and Personal Property.

## Professional Context of the Bar
In the English legal system, the term barrister refers to the highest class of lawyers, who maintain exclusive audience in all superior courts. The grade of barrister includes ordinary practitioners (sometimes called "utter barristers") and king's counsel. The heads of the profession are the solicitor-general and the attorney-general, both of whom hold office at the will of the current government.

The process of becoming a barrister involves being "called to the bar," a privilege held exclusively by the Inns of Court. While the benchers maintain disciplinary power, the General Council of the Bar (which succeeded a bar committee formed in 1883) was established in 1894 to address professional etiquette, conduct, and legal reforms. Although the General Council does not have disciplinary powers, it serves to alert the benchers to gross violations of professional etiquette.
