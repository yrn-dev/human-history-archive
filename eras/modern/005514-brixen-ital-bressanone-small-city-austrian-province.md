# BRIXEN

Brixen, known in Italian as Bressanone, is a small city located in the Austrian province of Tirol. It serves as the chief town of the administrative district of Brixen. As of 1900, the city had a population of 5,767.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city is situated in the valley of the Eisack, specifically at the point where the Eisack stream meets the Rienz. It is a station on the Brenner railway, located 24 miles north-east of Botzen and 34 miles south-east of the Brenner pass. Approximately 5½ miles to the north of the city lies the great fortress of Franzensfeste, which was constructed between 1833 and 1838 to protect the route over the Brenner and the eastward path up the Pusterthal.

## History and Administration
Brixen may have originated as a Roman station and later became a royal estate under the name *Prichsna*. In 901, Louis the Child gave the estate to the bishop. The city was enclosed by walls in 1030. In 1525, Brixen was the site of the first outbreak of the great peasants' revolt.

The city's administrative status was significantly altered in 1027 when Emperor Conrad II granted the bishop extensive temporal powers. The bishop maintained these powers until 1803, at which point they were lost to Austria. In later historical settlements regarding the dismemberment of Austria, property of predominant historic interest belonging to the episcopal principality of Bressanone could be transferred without payment.

## Ecclesiastical Significance
The city possesses a strongly ecclesiastical character. It continues to serve as the see of a bishop and is home to an episcopal palace, a seminary, twelve churches, five monasteries, and a cathedral church dating from the 18th century. 

The see itself was founded at Säben, located on rocky heights above the town of Klausen to the south of Brixen, at the end of the 8th century (though possibly as early as the 6th century). The see was transferred to Brixen in 992. One notable figure associated with the see was Poppo, the bishop of Brixen, who was raised to the papacy by Emperor Henry III as Pope Damasus II, serving from July 17 to August 9, 1048.
