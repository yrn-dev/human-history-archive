# Commercial Centers of Corn and Cattle Trade

Across various global regions, several towns and provinces have established significant commercial importance through the production and trade of corn and cattle. These centers often leverage their geographic positioning—whether as inland agricultural hubs or port cities—to facilitate the movement of these commodities.

## South American Agricultural Hubs

In Brazil, the inland town of Barbacena, located in the state of Minas Geraes, is noted for its considerable commercial importance. Situated approximately 3500 feet above sea-level and 150 miles north-northwest of Rio de Janeiro, the town is surrounded by a chiefly agricultural district that produces cattle, Indian corn, sugar-cane, and coffee. Barbacena is also recognized for its healthiness and its large sanatorium, which attracts convalescents from Rio de Janeiro during the hot season. While it previously served as a principal distributing center for the mining districts of Minas Geraes, this status was lost following the extension of the railways.

In Venezuela, the town of Calabozo serves as the capital of the state of Guárico and was formerly the capital of the province of Caracas during the colonial period. Located on the left bank of the Guárico river in an extensive *llano*, the town is a place of considerable commercial importance due to its location within a rich cattle-raising country. Originally an Indian town, it became a trading station for the Compañia Guipuzcoana in 1730.

In Ecuador, the province of Azuay (formerly called Cuenca) is an elevated mountainous district. Its diverse climates allow for the production of various goods, including cattle and Indian corn, alongside silver, quicksilver, wool, barley, cinchona, and straw hats.

## European Trade Centers

In Austria, the town of Kremsier in Moravia is situated in the fertile region of the Hanna on the March river. The town maintains a considerable trade in cattle, corn, fruit, and manufactures, supported by local industries such as corn-milling, brewing, and iron-founding.

Budapest, the most important town in Hungary, derives great commercial importance from its location on the Danube. The introduction of steamboats in 1830 provided a stimulus to its corn trade, which was further promoted by a revolutionized flour-milling industry. Budapest is considered one of the world's greatest milling centers. Its trade includes corn, cattle, horses, pigs, flour, wool, hides, and wines.

In England, the town of Dunster historically possessed commercial importance through its port, which maintained a considerable trade in cattle, corn, and wool with Ireland. While the harbor has since silted up and the local cloth manufacture has disappeared, the town's earlier commercial prominence was rooted in these trades.

## Asian and Siberian Markets

In the Philippine Islands, several locations are central to the production of Indian corn and livestock. Balayan, a town and port of entry in the province of Batangas on Luzon, is located in a fertile district with volcanic soil. This region produces Indian corn, rice, cane-sugar, coffee, cacao, pepper, cotton, native dyes, and various fruits. In Balayan, cattle and horses are raised for market in considerable numbers.

Similarly, Dagupan is the most important commercial center of the province of Pangasinan on Luzon. It serves as the chief point of exportation for a rich province that produces Indian corn, rice, sugar, indigo, and copra.

In West Siberia, the town of Kolyvañ, located in the government of Tomsk on the Chaus river, is described as a wealthy town. Its merchants engage in a considerable export trade involving corn, cattle, tallow, hides, and fish.

## Regional Production and Constraints

While many regions thrive on these commodities, others face specific challenges. In certain areas, Indian corn is grown exclusively for home use. In Texas and adjoining states, the cattle industry has suffered great damage due to "charbon" and "Texas fever." In these regions, dairying interests are not largely developed, and forage crops are seldom grown despite favorable soil conditions.
