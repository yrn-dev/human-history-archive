# Migula and Fischer: Bacteriological Research

The works of Migula and Fischer represent significant contributions to the study of bacteria, particularly regarding their structure, classification, and biological interactions. Their research is cited as general authority in the field of bacteriology, with specific focus on the morphology and internal composition of bacterial cells.

## Structural and Morphological Studies
Fischer and Migula have conducted extensive research into the physical makeup of bacteria. Fischer's work, including *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (Oxford, 1900, 2nd ed.) and its German counterpart (Jena, 1903), as well as *Unters. ueber den Bau der Cyanophyceen und Bakterien* (Jena, 1897), examines the cell membrane and the general construction of these organisms. 

Specific attention has been given to the study of cilia, with Fischer publishing "Unters. ueber Bakterien" in *Pringsh. Jahrb.* vol. xxvii. Additionally, the study of the bacterial nucleus is a recurring theme in the works of both Migula and Fischer. Migula's *System der Bakterien* (Jena, 1897) serves as a primary reference for the systemic organization of these microorganisms.

## Interaction with Leguminous Plants
Research attributed to Fischer, specifically in *Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien* (2nd ed., Jena, 1902), documents the invasion of leguminous roots by bacteria. This process involves several stages:

*   **Infection Threads:** Bacteria utilize "infection threads" (zoogloea) to push through the cell walls of the root epidermis, as seen in the root of the Pea.
*   **Root-Hair Entry:** Bacteria gather in masses at the free end of root-hairs and push their way upward in a thin stream.
*   **Nodule Formation:** In plants such as the lupin, bacteria form root nodules. These nodules contain bacterial tissue (w) and fibro-vascular bundles (g). Within these tissues, cells exhibit a nucleus and protoplasm filled with bacteria.
*   **Bacteroids:** The research identifies specific forms of bacteria, including normal undegenerate forms from lupin nodules and bacteroids found in *Lupinus albus* and *Vicia villosa*.

## Academic Context and Related Authorities
The research of Migula and Fischer is situated within a broader network of bacteriological study. Other authorities cited in conjunction with their work on the cell membrane include Butschli and Rowland. Regarding the bacterial nucleus, their findings are grouped with those of Wager and Vejdovsky, the latter of whom published "Ueber den Kern der Bakterien und seine Teilung" (1904) and studies on the bacteria of the Prager Wasserleitung (1905).

Their contributions are part of a vast body of literature that includes works on pathogenic microorganisms by Kolle and Wassermann, as well as general principles of bacteriology by authors such as Abbott, Crookshank, and Hueppe.
