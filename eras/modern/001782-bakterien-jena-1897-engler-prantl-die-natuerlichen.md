# Bacteriological Literature and Authorities

The study of bacteria and related microorganisms has produced an extensive body of literature, comprising general treatises, specialized research on cellular structure, and comprehensive manuals on pathogenic organisms.

## General Authorities and Classification
Several foundational works provide a general overview of bacteria. These include Migula's *System der Bakterien* (Jena, 1897) and Fischer's *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (Oxford, 1900, 2nd ed.), which also had a German edition published in Jena in 1903. Other significant general references include Engler and Prantl's *Die naturlichen Pflanzenfamilien* (I. Th. 1 Abt. a), Lafar's *Technical Mycology* (London, 1898), and Mace's *Traite pratique de bakteriologie* (5th ed. 1904).

Regarding the classification of these organisms, Marshall Ward published "On the Characters or Marks employed for classifying the Schizomycetes" in the *Ann. of Bot.* (1892, vol. vi.). Additional classification research is found in the works of Lehmann and Neumann.

## Cellular Structure and Morphology
Research into the physical composition of bacteria is divided into several specialized areas:

*   **Cell Membrane and General Structure:** Key studies include Butschli's *Weitere Ausfuhrungen ueber den Bau der Cyanophyceen und Bakterien* (Leipzig, 1896) and Fischer's *Unters. ueber den Bau der Cyanophyceen und Bakterien* (Jena, 1897). Rowland also contributed "Observations upon the Structure of Bacteria" in the *Trans. Jenner Institute* (1899).
*   **Cilia:** The study of cilia is addressed in Fischer's "Unters. ueber Bakterien" (*Pringsh. Jahrb.* vol. xxvii), as well as in the works of Migula and Fischer.
*   **Nucleus:** Research on the bacterial nucleus and its division includes works by Wager (*Ann. Bot.* vol. ix), Migula, and Fischer. Vejdovsky published "Ueber den Kern der Bakterien und seine Teilung" in *Cent. f. Bakt.* (1904) and a study on bacteria from the Prager water pipes in the same journal (1905). Mencl also wrote on the structural conditions of *Bacterium gammari* in *Archiv f. Protistenkunde* (1907).
*   **Spores:** The biology of *B. ramosus* was examined by Marshall Ward (1895), while Sturgis studied a soil bacillus of the type of de Bary's *B. megatherium*. Further research on spores was conducted by L. Klein in 1889.

## Specialized Bacteriological Studies
The archive identifies several niche areas of bacteriological research:

*   **Fossil Bacteria:** Renault's "Recherches sur les Bacteriacees fossiles" (*Ann. des Sc. Nat.*, 1896) focuses on this field.
*   **Bacteria in Water:** Frankland and Marshall Ward provided "Reports on the Bacteriology of Water" in the *Proc. R. Soc.* (vol. li, liii, and lvi). Additionally, papers concerning the bacteria of the river Thames were published in *Ann. of Bot.* (vol. xii and xiii).

## Pathogenic Bacteria and Immunity
Due to the vast amount of literature on infective diseases, certain comprehensive works are highlighted as primary authorities. Kolle and Wassermann's *Handbuch der pathogenen Mikroorganismen* (Jena, 1904) is noted as the most important work on the subject, with ongoing supplements. Other significant manuals include:

*   Abbott, *Principles of Bacteriology* (1905)
*   Crookshank, *Bacteriology and Infective Diseases* (1896)
*   Duclaux, *Traite de microbiologie* (1899-1900)
*   Eyre, *Bacteriological Technique* (1902)
*   Fluegge, *Die Mikroorganismen* (1896)
*   Fischer, *Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien* (1902)
*   Guenther, *Einfuehrung in das Studium der Bakteriologie* (1906)
*   Hewlett, *Manual of Bacteriology* (1902)
*   Hueppe, *Principles of Bacteriology* (1899)
*   Klein, *Micro-organisms and Disease* (1896)
*   McFarland, *Text-book upon the Pathogenic Bacteria* (1906)
*   Muir and Ritchie, *Manual of Bacteriology* (1908)
*   Park, *Pathogenic Micro-organisms* (1906)
*   Sternberg, *Manual of Bacteriology* (1896)
*   Woodhead, *Bacteria and their products* (1891)

For the bacteriology of infective diseases, the *System of Medicine* edited by Clifford Allbutt (1907) is cited. Research on immunity is primarily represented by Ehrlich's *Studies in Immunity* (1906) and the works of Metchnikoff.

## Reference Sources
Researchers are directed to the *Centralbl. fuer Bakter. u. Parasitenk.* (Jena) and the *Index Medicus* for further references. Additionally, the New Sydenham Society published a collection of translated French and German essays titled *Microparasites in Disease: Selected Essays* in 1886.
