# Islay's Lea har na Feinne (London, 1872)

## Overview and Context
*Lea har na Feinne*, published in London in 1872 by Campbell of Islay, is a significant work within the study of ballads. It is noted for its specific role in the documentation of Gaelic balladry, serving as the sole entry for the Gaelic people within the extensive bibliography of ballad collections compiled by Professor Child.

## Relation to the Work of Professor Child
The importance of *Lea har na Feinne* is highlighted in the context of *English and Scottish Popular Ballads*, a work published between 1897 and 1898 by Professor Child. Child's work is considered an indispensable resource for students of ballads. While Child was unrivalled in his knowledge of the ballads of various European peoples, his bibliography of collections contains approximately four hundred titles. 

Within this comprehensive list, the most prolific ballad makers are identified as the English, Scots, French, Italian, Danish, Slavic, and German peoples. In contrast, the Gaelic contribution to this bibliography is represented only by Campbell of Islay's *Lea har na Feinne*.
