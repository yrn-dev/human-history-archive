# Philipp Emanuel Bach

Philipp Emanuel Bach was a musician and the son of the German composer Johann Sebastian Bach. He is described as having been "brilliantly successful," and his achievements had a significant impact on the musical landscape of his time.

## Appointment and Influence
The brilliant successes of Philipp Emanuel led to his appointment as court-composer to the king of Prussia. This appointment resulted in a significant event in 1747, when his father, Sebastian Bach, was summoned to visit Frederick the Great at Potsdam. Sebastian Bach regarded this visit as the culmination of his career. During this visit, Bach played on the organs of the churches of Potsdam and on numerous newly invented pianofortes produced by Silbermann, which the king had purchased. 

During the visit, Frederick the Great provided Bach with a splendid theme for extemporization; this theme later served as the basis for the composition *Das musikalische Opfer*.

## Musical Style and Legacy
Philipp Emanuel Bach was a proponent of a new style of music. Johann Sebastian Bach noted that this new style had advanced to "great heights" and observed that the "old style of music no longer pleases our modern ears." This new style, championed by Philipp Emanuel, was soon to eclipse the polyphonic art of his father for the next hundred years.

In addition to his musical compositions, Philipp Emanuel contributed to the preservation of his family's history. He completed the family genealogy that had been drawn up by his father. This genealogy identifies Veit Bach—a miller and baker—as the founder of the family, and Hans Bach ("*der Spielmann*") as the first professional musician in the lineage.

## Family Context
Philipp Emanuel was one of the children of Johann Sebastian Bach and his first wife. While his brother Friedermann was viewed by Philipp Emanuel as being more capable of replacing their father than any other family member, Friedermann ultimately led a dissolute and unproductive life, attaining a disreputable old age.
