# Grant Medical College, the Government Law School, and the Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy School of Art

The Grant Medical College, the government law school, and the Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy school of art are among the primary educational institutions located in the city of Bombay.

## Educational Context in Bombay
These institutions operate within a broader educational landscape in Bombay City that includes the government Elphinstone College and the Victoria Jubilee technical institute. Additionally, the city is served by two missionary colleges, specifically St Xavier and Wilson. The overarching academic framework for the city was established in 1857 with the constitution of the Bombay University, which was created as an examining body based on the model of the university of London.

## Local Governance and Administration
The environment in which these institutions exist is managed by a specific municipal and port structure. The port of Bombay, including its warehouses and docks, is overseen by a port trust with members nominated by the government from the commercial community. 

The city's municipal government was established via an act passed by the Bombay legislative council in 1888. This government is composed of a town council and a municipal corporation. The corporation consists of 72 members, with the following distribution of representation:
* 16 members nominated by the government.
* 36 members elected by ratepayers.
* 16 members elected by the justices of the peace.
* 2 members elected by the chamber of commerce.
* 2 members elected by the senate of the university.

The town council serves as the standing committee for the corporation and is made up of 12 members; 4 are nominated by the government, while the remaining 8 are elected by the corporation. The membership of the corporation is diverse, including Parsees, Mahommedans, Hindus, and Europeans.
