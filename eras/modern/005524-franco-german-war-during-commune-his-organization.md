# Franco-German War

The Franco-German War, also referred to as the Franco-Prussian War of 1870, was a significant conflict between France and Germany. The war is noted for its impact on the subsequent development of Germany and its long-term influence on international relations, contributing to a lasting Franco-German feud.

## Military Operations and Key Events
The conflict involved various military engagements and movements across Europe. One of the early stages of the war saw the firing of the first shots in the town of Saarbrücken. During the course of the hostilities, the battle at Sedan resulted in the capture of various personnel; for instance, the French author and politician Paul Déroulède, who enlisted as a private, was wounded and taken prisoner there before being sent to Breslau and eventually escaping.

Following his escape, Déroulède served under Bourbaki and Chanzy, participating in a retreat to Switzerland that is described as disastrous. In the aftermath of the war, the German emperor decorated the American philanthropist Clara Barton with the Iron Cross for her services during the conflict.

## Medical and Humanitarian Efforts
The war necessitated significant organization of medical care for the wounded and the poor. Clara Barton, working with members of the International Committee of the Red Cross, traveled to the seat of hostilities to assist in the organization of military hospitals. Her humanitarian work extended beyond the immediate conflict, as she superintended the distribution of relief to the poor in Strassburg in 1871 and in Paris in 1872.

Additionally, Paul Broca, a French surgeon and anthropologist, rendered distinguished service during the Franco-German War. His contributions were particularly notable during the Commune, where he was responsible for the administration and organization of public hospitals.

## International Perspectives and Reporting
The conflict drew international attention and was documented by foreign observers. Henry Kingsley, an English novelist, served as a war correspondent during the Franco-German War, an experience that preceded his eighteen-month tenure as the editor of the *Edinburgh Daily Review*.

## Aftermath and Political Legacy
The conclusion of the war had profound political ramifications. In France, the period following the war was marked by internal strife, including the fighting against the Commune in Paris. The sense of loss led to the rise of patriotic movements; Paul Déroulède founded the *Ligue des patriotes* in 1882 with the specific goal of furthering France's "revanche" (revenge) against Germany. This patriotic fervor also led Déroulède to become an early advocate for a Franco-Russian alliance, traveling to Russia as early as 1883 to promote the objective.

From the German perspective, the period following 1870 was one of continued development. However, the "Franco-German Feud" remained a point of apprehension for Bismarck, leading to the establishment of various precautions and alliances during the Bismarckian period and system to maintain the balance of power.
