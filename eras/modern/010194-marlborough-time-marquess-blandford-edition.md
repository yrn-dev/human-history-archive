# Marlborough (Marquess of Blandford)

In the history of the English peerage and the collection of rare books, the title of Marquess of Blandford serves as the courtesy title held by the eldest son of the Duke of Marlborough during his father's lifetime. Two distinct figures associated with this title are noted in the archive's records: one as a prominent 19th-century politician and another as a notable bibliophile of the early 19th century.

## John Winston Spencer-Churchill
John Winston Spencer-Churchill (1822–1883) later became the 7th Duke of Marlborough. He was a prominent politician of the Conservative party and served as the lord-lieutenant of Ireland from 1876 to 1880. During the period in which he held the courtesy title of Marquess of Blandford, he was responsible for the "Blandford Act" of 1856. This specific act of legislation enabled populous parishes to be divided for the purposes of Church work. He was the grandfather of Charles Richard John Spencer-Churchill (born 1871), who became the 9th Duke of Marlborough in 1892.

## Bibliographical Competition and the Roxburghe Club
The title of Marquess of Blandford is also associated with a significant event in the history of book collecting during the reign of George III. In 1812, the books of the Duke of Roxburghe were sold at auction. This sale was marked by intense competition between Lord Spencer and the Duke of Marlborough, who was at that time the Marquess of Blandford. 

The two men competed specifically for an edition of Boccaccio that had been printed by Valdarfer in Venice in 1471. The Marquess of Blandford won the auction, paying £2,260 for the volume. This event and the subsequent commemorative dinner led to the formation of the Roxburghe Club.

The Boccaccio volume remained in the Duke of Marlborough's collection until 1819, when his books were sold. At that time, the volume was purchased by Earl Spencer (who died in 1834) for £750. The book eventually passed to Mrs. Rylands in 1892 and was gifted to the John Rylands library at Manchester in 1899.
