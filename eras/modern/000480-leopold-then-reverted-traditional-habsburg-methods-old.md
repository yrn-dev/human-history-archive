# Leopold and the Habsburg Dynasty

The name Leopold is associated with several distinct figures across the history of the Habsburg dynasty and the development of the Austrian monarchy, ranging from early margraves to Holy Roman Emperors and later European sovereigns.

## Early Origins and the Babenbergs
Before the rise of the House of Habsburg, the duchy of Austria was held by the Babenbergs, a Franconian family. In 976, Leopold, a count in the Donnegau and member of this family, was described as the margrave of the East Mark. This district, located on the eastern frontier of Bavaria and measuring approximately 60 miles in breadth, eventually grew into the duchy of Austria. Leopold likely received this mark as a reward for his fidelity to Emperor Otto II during the Bavarian rising of 976. He expanded the area's territory at the expense of the Hungarians before being succeeded in 994 by his son, Henry I.

## Early Habsburg Rulers
Following the establishment of the Habsburgs in Austria, Leopold III was one of the brothers who divided their lands in 1379. While his brother Albert III retained Carniola and Austria proper, Leopold III received Tirol, Carinthia, and Styria. Leopold III was killed at the battle of Sempach in 1386. His two eldest sons, William and Leopold, died early in the 15th century.

In the early 17th century, Archduke Leopold of Austria, who served as the bishop of Passau, intervened in Bohemia in 1611. Acting likely in connivance with his cousin King Rudolph, Leopold invaded and obtained possession of part of Prague. However, his army was defeated by troops raised by the Bohemian estates, forcing his departure from the region.

## Leopold I and Imperial Consolidation
Leopold I focused on the internal consolidation and expansion of the Austrian monarchy. He completed the edifice of the monarchy through the conquest of Transylvania and Hungary. Following the death of Archduke Sigismund in 1665, Leopold gained Tirol and funds that he used to buy back the Silesian principalities of Ratibor and Oppeln, which had been pledged to the Poles by Ferdinand III.

Administratively, Leopold I strengthened the central government. While the old estates survived, he maintained effective power in his own hands, initiating a system of centralized bureaucracy. He also established the Austrian standing army, maintaining regiments raised in 1672 despite opposition. Intellectually, he patronized learning and helped establish the universities of Olmuetz and Innsbruck. Under his auspices, Vienna evolved from a frontier fortress into a brilliant European capital after the defeat of the Turks in 1683, though he remained intolerant toward religious dissent.

## Leopold II and the Reaction to Revolution
Leopold II ascended to the throne in 1792, succeeding his son Francis II. Previously, as the grand-duke of Tuscany, Leopold had been known as a liberal and enlightened ruler. However, upon taking power, he found that "Josephinism" had not produced justified results, and the progress of the French Revolution prompted a shift toward reaction.

Leopold II reverted to traditional Habsburg methods to ensure stability. He restored the supremacy of the Church, viewing it as the empire's only effective bond, and resolved the *Einheitsstaat* back into its elements, reinstating the old abuses and machinery of estates and diets. This policy of stability preceded the era of Metternich and lasted until 1848.

On the international stage, Leopold II acted as the executor of the Empire's ban against the insurgent Liegeois and positioned himself as a champion of order. He skillfully negotiated the treaty of Reichenbach (August 15, 1790), ending a quarrel with Prussia, and the armistice of Giurgevo (September 10) with Turkey. A definitive peace with Turkey was signed at Sistova on August 4, 1791, establishing the *status quo*.

As Roman emperor, Leopold II faced direct antagonism with revolutionary France. He protested the Constituent Assembly's decree of August 4, which abolished feudal rights and affected German princes' *enclaves* in Lorraine and Alsace. While he initially maintained a moderate attitude, his refusal to disperse the *emigres* on the French frontier and his insistence on the rights of dispossessed princes led to a crisis. On January 25, 1792, the French Assembly decreed that war would be declared if no satisfactory reply was received by March 1.

## Leopold II of Belgium
In a separate dynastic line, Leopold II served as the King of the Belgians, succeeding his father, Leopold I, who died on December 10, 1865. Born on April 9, 1835, Leopold II married Archduchess Marie-Henriette of Austria in 1853. Their only son, the Count of Hainaut, died in 1869 at age ten. Leopold II is noted for possessing sound common sense, unusual tact, an acute knowledge of men and things, and respect for his role as a constitutional sovereign.
