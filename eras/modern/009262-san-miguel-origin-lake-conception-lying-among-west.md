# San Miguel

The San Miguel is a river located in Bolivia. It is one of several rivers, including the Guapore, Guapay, Mamore, Yacuma, Beni, and Madre de Dios, that traverse an extensive region of the country.

## Geography and Region
The San Miguel river flows through a region characterized by the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos. This area is situated north of an elevation that served as the southern shore of the ancient Mojos Lake. The region was once entirely covered by the great Mojos Lake and continues to contain large undrained areas, such as the area of Lake Rojoagua (also referred to as Roguaguado).

The surrounding landscape consists of extensive open plains and rich agricultural districts. Since the era of the Jesuit missions in the region, cattle-raising has been successfully practiced on these plains.

## Regional Context
The San Miguel is part of a broader geographical area in Bolivia that challenges the popular conception of the country as being exclusively rugged and mountainous. In reality, three-fifths of Bolivia is composed of gently undulating forest regions, great swamps, flooded bottomlands, and low alluvial plains, including the Chiquitos region.

While the San Miguel flows through the northern llanos, other parts of the country feature different terrains, such as the Bolivian Chaco and the llanos of Manzo in the extreme south. To the north-west, where the Beni and Madre de Dios rivers traverse the land, the lower slopes of the Andes are covered with heavy forests, creating one of the richest districts in Bolivia capable of sustaining a large population.
