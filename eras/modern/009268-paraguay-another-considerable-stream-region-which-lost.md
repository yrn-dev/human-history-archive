# Paraguay

Paraguay is a republic in South America. It is bordered to the north and east by Brazil, and to the south by Argentina. It also shares a border with Bolivia.

## Geography and Hydrography
The Paraguay river is a central feature of the region. In Bolivia, the south-east drainage basin discharges into the Paraguay; this basin extends from the Cordillera Oriental in the east to the Paraguay river, and from the Sierras de Chiquitos in the north to the Argentine frontier. 

The Bolivian tributaries of the upper Paraguay are generally small and unimportant. The Otuquis, formed by the Tucabaca and San Rafael (which drain the Cerro Cochii range), is the southernmost of this group, though it is lost in great marshes 50 meters from the Paraguay. The Parapiti, rising on the eastern slopes of the Sierra de Misiones, flows north-east for approximately 150 meters before being lost in the marshy districts of the Bolivian plain.

Other significant water systems in the broader region include the Pilcomayo, which rises on the east slopes of the Cordillera Oriental and flows through the Bolivian Chaco. Because the country is nearly level, the Pilcomayo has a sluggish current and obstructed channels. In the sierra region, its largest tributaries are the Mataca, Cachimayo, and the Pilaya (or Camblaya), the latter of which is formed by the San Juan and Cotagaita.

## Asuncion
Asuncion (Nuestra Senora de la Asuncion) is the capital of the republic and serves as a city and port. It is located on the left bank of the Paraguay river at 25 deg. 16' 04" S., 57 deg. 42' 40" W., situated 970 meters above Buenos Aires and 253 feet above sea level. Founded by Ayolas in 1335, it is the oldest permanent Spanish settlement on the La Plata.

The city is built on a low sandy plain that rises to hillsides overlooking the Bay of Asuncion and the wooded country of the Chaco on the opposite shore. Asuncion was laid out on a regular plan, largely credited to Dictator Francia. The city features paved principal streets with gas and electricity lighting, as well as street-car and telephone services.

Historically, Asuncion was the seat of Spanish rule in the region and was later the site of a struggle between Jesuits and church authorities. Following the declaration of independence in 1811, the city was ruled despotically by Dr. Francia, and subsequently by the elder and younger Lopez. The most prominent building in the city is a palace begun by the younger Lopez, which currently houses a bank. Other city features include a public library, a national college, and numerous religious edifices, as the city has been the seat of a bishopric since 1547.

As of 1900, the estimated population of Asuncion was 52,000. While some residences possess architectural merit, a majority are mud-walled cabins with thatched roofs. The climate is described as healthful and hot, with a mean annual temperature of approximately 72 deg. F.

## Trade and Communication
Asuncion's only means of trade communication with the outer world are regular lines of river steamers connecting the port to Montevideo and Buenos Aires. Additionally, a railway operated by an English company connects the capital to the inland town of Villa Rica, located 95 meters away.

In the neighboring Argentine Republic, the city of Corrientes serves as a vital river port on the left bank of the Parana river, 20 meters below the junction of the Paraguay and the Upper Parana. Corrientes practically controls the trade of extensive tributary regions, as freight for the Chaco and Upper Parana rivers is transhipped there.

For Bolivia, the Paraguay river and its affluents provide essential outlets for trade. Key connections to the upper Paraguay include Puerto Suarez on Lake Caceres—which handles the bulk of Bolivian trade in that direction—Puerto Pacheco on Bahia Negra, and the projected port of Puerto Quijarro on Lake Gaiba.

## Flora and Fauna
The region is home to diverse wildlife. Alligators are found in the lagoons and tributaries of the Paraguay river. The area is also inhabited by various species of snakes, including boa-constrictors and rattlesnakes, as well as numerous turtles and lizards.

The avian population includes the macaw (genus *Ara*). With one exception, known species of *Ara* inhabit the American mainland from Mexico to Paraguay. These birds are particularly abundant in Bolivia.
