# BUFF

Buff is a type of leather that was originally produced from the skin of the buffalo. In later periods, the skins of other animals were also used in its manufacture. The material is characterized by a dull pale yellow colour.

## Applications and Military Use
Historically, buff leather was utilized to create the jerkin or the buffcoat, which served as a leathern military coat. Due to the distinct yellow or buff-colour of their facings, certain regiments in the British army adopted the name in their titles. These include the old 3rd Foot regiment of the line (which later became the East Kent Regiment), referred to as the "Buffs," and the old 78th Foot (now the 2nd battalion Seaforth Highlanders), known as the "Ross-shire Buffs." In contemporary usage, the term "buff" is commonly applied to the colour alone.

## The Buffalo
The leather is derived from the buffalo, a name that properly refers to an aberrant species of cattle. This species has been domesticated since time immemorial in Egypt and India, and was introduced from Egypt into southern Europe. While the native home of this species is India, the term "buffalo" is now used to include all closely related animals.

Buffaloes are described as heavily built oxen with broad muzzles, large ears, massive angulated horns, and sparsely haired skin. They possess long, tufted tails and 13 pairs of ribs, a feature that makes them resemble typical oxen. 

### Regional Variations
There are distinct differences between the Asiatic and South African species:
* **Asiatic Buffaloes:** These are characterized by a more elongated and narrow head, less thickly fringed ears, and a large size with wide separation of the horns. Crucially, the hair on the fore-part of their backs is directed forwards.
* **African Buffaloes:** In contrast, all African buffaloes have the hair of the back directed backwards.

Within the Indian variety, specific local races have been identified. In Assam, the *B. bubalis macrocercus* was known for horns of immense size that directed mainly outwards rather than curving upwards. Another Assam race, *B. bubalis fulvus*, is distinguished by having a tawny hide and hair instead of black.

### Natural History and Behavior
The typical Indian buffalo (*Bos (Bubalus) bubalis*) is primarily restricted to Ceylon and India, though local races may exist in the Islands and Malay Peninsula. They frequent grass-jungles near swamps where the grass can exceed 20 feet in height; in these environments, they create covered pathways that keep them completely concealed.

Buffaloes are fond of wallowing in mud in marshes during the day. They are not shy animals and are known to cause significant damage to crops. Their rutting season occurs in autumn, during which a single male is followed by several females. The gestation period lasts ten months, resulting in the birth of one or two calves. The bull is noted for its strength, being capable of overthrowing an elephant and generally proving a match for the tiger.
