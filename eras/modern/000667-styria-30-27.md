# Styria

Styria is a duchy and crown-land of Austria. Its geography includes the north Styrian Alps, where the village of Mariazell is located in the valley of the Salza. The region shares a threefold boundary with Carinthia and Carniola at the peak of the Grintovc (also known as Grintouz), which reaches an elevation of 8,393 feet.

## Medieval Political Transitions
In the mid-13th century, the duchy of Styria was claimed as a vacant fief of the Empire by Emperor Frederick II, who entrusted its government to Otto II, Duke of Bavaria. During this period of instability, Pope Innocent IV bestowed the duchy upon Hermann VI, Margrave of Baden. Following the deaths of Hermann and the emperor in 1250, the region experienced further disorder. While the estates of Austria chose Ottakar, son of Wenceslaus I of Bohemia, as their duke in 1251, Styria was claimed and conquered by Bela IV, King of Hungary.

Ottakar eventually interfered in the affairs of the duchy due to Styrian resentment of Hungarian rule. After a war with Hungary, a treaty signed in March 1261 recognized Ottakar as the Duke of Styria. However, in 1276, Ottakar submitted to Rudolph, Count of Habsburg (the German king), and renounced the duchy. On December 27, 1282, Rudolph invested his sons, Rudolph and Albert, with the duchies of Styria and Austria. Albert undertook the government of the region until his election as German king in 1298.

## Habsburg Dynastic Divisions
Throughout the 14th and 15th centuries, Styria was frequently divided or shared among Habsburg family members. In 1379, Leopold III received Styria, Carinthia, and Tirol in a land division with his brother Albert III. Following Leopold's death in 1386, the lands were ruled in common by his nephews. In 1406, a further division occurred between Ernest and Frederick; Ernest became the Duke of Styria and Carinthia. He was succeeded in 1424 by his sons, Frederick and Albert.

Later, Ferdinand divided the German Habsburg inheritance among his three sons, granting Styria, along with Carinthia and Carniola, to Archduke Charles.

## Religious History
During the reign of Emperor Maximilian II (1564–1576), nearly the whole of Styria was mainly Lutheran. This changed with the accession of the fanatical Spanish Catholic Rudolph II (1576–1612), who encouraged the Jesuits to pursue the counter-Reformation. Later, Ferdinand II, while serving as Archduke of Styria, crushed Protestantism within the duchy before becoming king of Bohemia and emperor.

In more recent history, the Roman Catholic Church's alliance with Slav parties led to political friction. In 1898, an agitation against the Church emerged among Germans in Styria. This "Los von Rom" movement resulted in large numbers of Germans leaving the Catholic Church for either Old Catholicism or Protestantism.

## Ethnic and Political Composition
Styria has been a site of tension between different racial and political factions. The "kernel" of the empire—the purely German district—included all of Styria except for its southern districts. Politically, the Liberal party was dominant in Styria and sought to develop central institutions. This was opposed by a Conservative and Clerical party that supported local institutions to protect against the influence of a centralized bureaucracy and parliament.

The region also contains Slovene populations. The government recognized Slovenian as an official language for the district of Cilli in Styria. While the diet of Carniola became a center for Slovene agitation, the diet of Styria served as the center for the German interest.

## Notable Sites
The village of Mariazell, located 89 miles north of Graz, is home to the most frequented sanctuary in Austria. It is visited annually by approximately 200,000 pilgrims who venerate a miracle-working image of the Virgin, carved from lime-tree wood and measuring about 18 inches high. This image was presented to the site in 1157. The current large church was erected in 1644, expanding a smaller church built by King Louis I of Hungary following a victory over the Turks in 1363. The Alpine lake of Erlafsee is located near the village.
