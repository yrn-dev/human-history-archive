# Jan van Broekhuizen

Jan van Broekhuizen, also known as Janus Broukhusius, was a Dutch poet and classical scholar who lived from 1649 to 1707. He is recognized for his contributions to both Dutch and Latin literature, as well as his scholarly editions of classical texts.

## Early Life and Military Career
Born in Amsterdam on November 20, 1649, Broekhuizen lost his father at a very young age. During his youth, he spent several years living and working with an apothecary, an employment he did not enjoy. Consequently, he entered the army. In 1674, he was sent to America as part of a regiment in the fleet commanded by Admiral de Ruyter, though he returned to Holland within the same year.

In 1678, Broekhuizen was stationed at a garrison in Utrecht. During this period, he formed a friendship with the celebrated Graevius. While in Utrecht, he became deeply involved in a duel; under the laws of Holland, this action resulted in the forfeiture of his life. He was granted a pardon after Graevius wrote to Nicholas Heinsius on his behalf. Following these events, Broekhuizen served as a captain of one of the companies in Amsterdam.

## Later Life and Literary Pursuits
Following the peace of Ryswick in 1697, Broekhuizen's company was disbanded. He retired to a country house near Amsterdam on a pension, which allowed him the leisure to pursue his literary and classical studies. He died on December 15, 1707.

## Literary Works and Scholarship
Broekhuizen's literary output spanned several languages and genres:

**Dutch Poetry**
Broekhuizen wrote Dutch poems following the model of Pieter Hooft. These were first published in 1677. A later edition was released in 1712, which included a biography written by D. van Hoogstraten. The final edition of his Dutch poems was edited by R.A. Kollewijn in 1883.

**Latin Poetry**
His Latin poems, titled *Carmina*, were published in 1684. A subsequent edition, titled *Poemata*, was published in 1711 and edited by D. van Hoogstraten.

**Classical Scholarship**
Broekhuizen's reputation as a classical scholar rests primarily on his editions of two authors: Propertius, published in 1702, and Tibullus, published in 1707.

**Correspondence**
His *Select Letters* (*Jani Browkhusii Epistolae Selectae*) were published in 1889 and 1893. These letters were edited by J.A. Worp, who also authored a biography of Broekhuizen in 1891.
