# Smashing

In the context of sports and military history, the term "smashing" or the act of smashing refers to the application of hard, forceful impact to achieve a specific objective, ranging from tactical maneuvers in racket sports to the destruction of enemy fortifications and weaponry.

## Smashing in Badminton
In the game of badminton—a fast-paced sport played with rackets and shuttlecocks—smashing is identified as a specific technique that allows for the application of skill and judgment. It is defined as the act of hitting the shuttle with a hard downward stroke. 

Badminton is a game consisting entirely of volleying. It can be played as a "two-handed" (single) game with one player on each side, or a "four-handed" (double) game with two players on each side. The game requires significant staying power, often more than a single at lawn tennis. Along with "dropping," which involves hitting the shuttle gently just over the net, smashing is one of the primary methods used by players to manipulate the shuttle during play.

## Military Applications of Smashing
The term is also used to describe the forceful destruction of military targets using heavy weaponry.

During the Great War, the use of specialized shells was employed to smash enemy equipment. In one engagement involving a U-boat and British drifters, the vessel *Gipsy* utilized a 12-pounder gun. After achieving two hits with common pointed shell, the *Gipsy* deployed eight out of nine lyddite shells, which smashed the last gun of the German U-boat and set the vessel on fire forward, leading to the crew's surrender.
