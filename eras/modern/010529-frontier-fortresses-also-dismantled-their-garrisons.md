# Dismantling of Frontier Fortresses and Garrison Reductions

Throughout history, the dismantling of frontier fortresses and the reduction of their garrisons have served as instruments of political transition, peace treaties, and strategic shifts in territorial control. These actions often signal the end of hostilities or the establishment of new diplomatic orders between sovereign powers.

## Anglo-Scottish Border Transitions
Following the accession of James I of England in 1603, a concerted effort was made to erase the memory of historical conflicts between England and Scotland. As part of this policy, James I sought to replace the term "Borders" with the designation "Middle Shires." To further this goal of peace, frontier fortresses were ordered to be dismantled and their garrisons were reduced to nominal strength. 

The long-term result of this policy varied by region. While some English strongholds, including Naworth, Ford, Chillingham, and Alnwick, were eventually modernized, others such as Warkworth, Wark, and Norham became picturesque ruins. In contrast, the majority of Scottish fortresses were demolished; their sites were either built over or reduced to grass-grown mounds.

## European Diplomatic Treaties and Neutrality
In the 19th century, the status of the fortress of Luxemburg was a point of significant international contention. Under the treaty of Vienna, the garrisoning of the fortress had been placed in Prussian hands. This arrangement continued until 1867, despite the dissolution of the German Confederation in the previous year. Tensions rose when King William III proposed selling his rights over the grand-duchy to France, an act that irritated the Prussians.

The matter was resolved through the treaty of London, signed on May 11, 1867. This agreement mandated that the Prussian garrison be withdrawn and the fortress dismantled, a process completed in 1872. This action coincided with the great powers guaranteeing the neutrality of the grand-duchy, allowing Luxemburg to become a sovereign and independent state.

Similarly, in the frontier town of Maastricht, the capital of the province of Limburg in Holland, the ramparts of the former strong fortress were dismantled between 1871 and 1878.

## Post-War Demilitarization and Control
The dismantling of fortifications has also been used as a strict condition of peace following major conflicts. In one such instance, all field works, fortresses, and fortified works located in German territory within a zone of fifty kilometers east of the Rhine were required to be dismantled within three months. Furthermore, the construction of any new fortifications in this zone was forbidden, although works on the eastern and southern frontiers were permitted to remain.

To ensure these provisions were executed, interallied commissions of control were established. These commissions were authorized to establish headquarters at the German seat of Government and travel to any part of Germany. Germany was required to provide complete facilities and cover all expenses related to the execution of the treaty, including the materials and labor necessary for the destruction or demolition of war equipment.

## Strategic Dismantling in Classical Antiquity
In ancient Greece, the dismantling of outlying fortresses was used to maintain political dominance. The Thirty, acting to ensure Lacedæmonian predominance, dismantled all outlying fortresses in Attica. This strategic move left frontier fortresses, such as Phylê in the mountains north of Attica, without garrisons. Consequently, when Athenian emigrants led by Thrasybulus, Anytus, and Archinus struck from Thebes, they were able to seize Phylê without resistance.

Additionally, the Lacedæmonians engaged in the targeted destruction of enemy fortifications to neutralize threats. During a campaign in Arcadia against the Parrhasians, the Lacedæmonians sought to demolish the fort of Cypsela, which had been built and garrisoned by the Mantineans to annoy the district of Sciritis in Laconia. After laying waste to the Parrhasian country, the Lacedæmonians successfully razed the fortress.
