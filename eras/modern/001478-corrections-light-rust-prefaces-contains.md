# Wilhelm Rust and the Bach-Gesellschaft

## Overview of the Bach-Gesellschaft
The *Bach-Gesellschaft* (B. G.) produced the only complete modern edition of the works of Johann Sebastian Bach. The project was initiated in 1850, marking the centenary of Bach's death, with the goal of ensuring the correct publication of all his remaining works. This effort was part of a broader rediscovery of Bach, championed by figures such as Mendelssohn and Schumann, and preceded by the publications and performances of Crotch and Wesley in England.

The quality of the *Bach-Gesellschaft* volumes is described as unequal. The initial editors are noted for a lack of understanding regarding Bach's musical language; they often dismissed details of ornamentation, figured bass, and variants between parts and scores, particularly in recitatives and arias, which they viewed as archaic.

## The Editorship of Wilhelm Rust
Wilhelm Rust, the grandson of a 19th-century composer of the same name known for polyphonic fantasias and suites, is regarded as the "Porson of Bach-scholarship." Rust served as the editor of the *Bach-Gesellschaft* for fourteen years, during which he introduced scholarly methods starting with the 9th *Jahrgang*. 

Rust is credited with a rivalled insight into Bach's style. Through reasoning and research, he restored harmonies of significant value from incomplete texts. His contributions to the *Bach-Gesellschaft* are highlighted by his prefaces, which are considered among the most valuable works of criticism for 18th-century music.

## Comparative Scholarship and Editions
The scholarship of Rust and Kroll is identified as the highest standard within the *Bach-Gesellschaft*. Kroll edited the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier* (*Jahrgang* xiv.), a text he also made accessible in the *Edition Peters*. This was a significant improvement over the previously accepted *Edition Peters* version by Czerny, which is described as a result of corrupt tradition.

Following Rust's death, the quality of the *Bach-Gesellschaft* deteriorated. His immediate successor (*Jahrgang* xxix.) focused on reprinting early versions of works rather than digesting Rust's materials, and *Jahrgang* xxx. contained errors where the violin and bass were separated by a bar for an entire line. However, the final ten volumes returned to a satisfactory standard. In *Jahrgang* xliv., the English and French suites were re-edited, and the B minor mass was partially reworked. Additionally, the British Museum autograph was used to supplement Kroll's text of the *Wohltemperirtes Klavier*.

Outside of the *Bach-Gesellschaft*, the Steingraeber edition of clavier works, edited by Dr. Hans Bischoff, is cited as the best available. It provides a straightforward text while using footnotes to list variants and distinguishing the editor's phrasing and nuances from Bach's own rare indications.

## Related Bach Publications and Biographies
Other publications of Bach's work include the *Edition Peters* vocal scores of cantatas, which are considered superior to the complete series issued by Haertel and Breitkopf in conformity with the *Bach-Gesellschaft*. The English vocal scores from Novello are also noted as being of very good quality, specifically the Christmas oratorio, which features an analytic preface by Sir George Macfarren.

In terms of biographical scholarship, P. Spitta's biography of Johann Sebastian Bach (published in 1873 and 1880) is described as having superseded all previous works. When corrected in light of Rust's *B. G.* prefaces, Spitta's work is said to contain everything worth knowing about Bach, excluding the music itself.

## The Neue Bachgesellschaft
The *Neue Bachgesellschaft* was established with the objective of making the results of the first *Bach-Gesellschaft* more generally accessible. It achieved this by issuing practical, inexpensive editions and organizing frequent Bach festivals. These activities, alongside a movement to restore Bach's vocal music to the Lutheran Church, are viewed as having a salutary effect on the future of music.
