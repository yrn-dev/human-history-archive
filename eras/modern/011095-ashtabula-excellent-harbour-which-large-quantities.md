# Ashtabula

Ashtabula is a city located in Ashtabula township within Ashtabula county, Ohio, U.S.A. Situated on Lake Erie and the Ashtabula river, the city is positioned 54 miles northeast of Cleveland. The city is constructed on the high bank of the river, approximately 75 feet above the lake, providing views of diversified scenery.

## History and Governance
The first settlement in the area was established around 1801. Ashtabula township was created in 1808, and the townships of Sheffield, Plymouth, and Kingsville were subsequently formed from it. The village of Ashtabula was incorporated in 1831 and later received a city charter in 1891. The name "Ashtabula" is derived from an Indian word originally applied to the river, meaning "fish river."

## Demographics and Culture
The population of Ashtabula has shown steady growth over the turn of the century, recorded at 8,338 in 1890, increasing to 12,949 in 1900, and reaching 18,266 by the 1910 census. In 1900, 3,688 residents were foreign-born. The city and the wider county possess a large population of Finnish birth. This community is served by the *Amerikan Sanomat*, a Finnish weekly established in 1897 that is among the most widely read of its kind in the country. The city also maintains a public library.

## Transport and Infrastructure
Ashtabula is served by inter-urban electric lines and several railways, including the New York, Chicago & St Louis, the Lake Shore & Michigan Southern, and the Pennsylvania railways.

The city possesses an excellent harbour used for the shipment of large quantities of coal and iron ore. The port is a primary hub for iron ore, receiving more annually than any other port in the country, and likely the world. This ore is transported by rail from the port to iron manufacturing centres such as Youngstown and Pittsburg. In 1907, the port shipped 2,632,027 net tons of soft coal and received 7,542,149 gross tons of iron ore.

## Economy and Industry
The city's manufacturing sector includes machine shop and foundry products, agricultural implements, worsted goods, and leather. In 1905, the total value of factory products was $1,895,454, which represented a 114.3% increase over the previous five years.

Additionally, there are large greenhouses located in and near the city. Under glass, quantities of tomatoes, cucumbers, and lettuce are raised and shipped to large cities, including Pittsburg.
