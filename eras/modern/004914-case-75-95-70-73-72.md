# Criminal Investigations Involving Jeffrey Epstein

The legal and investigative history of Jeffrey Epstein involves multiple federal and state agencies, spanning several years and various jurisdictions. Documentation from the FBI and the Department of Justice (DOJ) details a series of criminal cases, investigations into child exploitation, and the subsequent handling of victim notifications.

## Early Investigations and Agency Coordination

In 2006, a West Palm Beach FBI Special Agent, referred to as "the case agent," informed Assistant United States Attorney (AUSA) Villafafia during casual conversations that the Palm Beach Police Department (PBPD) was investigating a wealthy man in Palm Beach for recruiting minors for sexual activity. The case agent noted that the PBPD had contacted the FBI because the State Attorney's Office was considering either allowing the defendant to plead to a misdemeanor charge or not charging the case at all.

While AUSA Villafafia suggested meeting with the PBPD, the case agent clarified that the PBPD preferred to wait and see how the State Attorney's Office decided to charge Epstein before formally presenting the case to the FBI. In May 2006, the lead Detective from the state's investigation met with the FBI case agent and Villafafia to summarize the information gathered. At that time, neither the case agent nor Villafafia had prior knowledge of Epstein or his background.

## FBI Case Management and Documentation

FBI investigative files contain extensive records regarding the submission of victim and witness work files. Records from 2006, 2007, and 2008 indicate numerous entries for the submission of these work files to the case file. These documents often utilize form FD-340 (Rev. 4-11-03) for the field office acquiring evidence.

The FBI's process for managing victim information involves collecting contact data during the investigation stage and storing it in a case management system. A Victim Specialist then exports this data into the VNS database, where it is linked to a specific VNS case number. Through the VNS, the FBI Victim Specialist can generate seven types of notification letters:
1. Initial notification
2. Notice that the case is under investigation
3. Notice of the defendant's arrest
4. Declination of prosecution
5. Other
6. Advice of victim rights
7. Notice that the investigation is closed

Once a charging document is filed and the "prosecution stage" begins, responsibility for victim notification shifts to the USAO's Victim Witness Specialist. The USAO imports data from its own management system into the VNS, linking it to the FBI's data to generate letters regarding arraignments, proposed plea agreements, change of plea hearings, sentencing hearings, and sentencing results.

## Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA) and Victim Notification

Following the signing of a Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA), the FBI case agent and co-case agent began notifying victims. However, after speaking with three victims, the case agent expressed concern that informing victims about the NPA and its monetary damages provision could create "potential impeachment material" for the victims and the agent if Epstein breached the agreement, leading to an indictment and trial. The agent stated to the Office of Professional Responsibility (OPR) that they were not comfortable telling victims they could sue Epstein for money.

Following consultations between the case agent, Villafafia, and the USAO's Professional Responsibility Officer, the agents stopped notifying victims about the NPA and their ability to pursue monetary damages. Despite these actions, email records from the case agent to Villafafia indicate that at least one victim understood the federal case had been resolved and expressed unhappiness with that resolution.

## Archive of Related Criminal Cases

The Department of Justice has produced a wide array of materials related to Epstein and associated individuals, available at justice.gov/epstein. These materials include:
* **FBI New York Files:** Records for the 2018 Epstein criminal case regarding child sex trafficking, the 2019 Maxwell criminal case, the 2019 Epstein death investigation, and a threat made against one of Epstein's victims.
* **FBI Miami Files:** Records for the 2006 Epstein criminal case for child prostitution and the 2009 Alfredo Rodriguez criminal case for obstruction of justice.
* **Other Materials:** Investigative materials for the OIG's June 2023 report on Epstein's death, Bureau of Prisons (BOP) records from the Metropolitan Correctional Center New York (MCC New York)—including count slips, commissary records, and visitor logbooks—and grand-jury materials from the SDNY and SDFL cases.
