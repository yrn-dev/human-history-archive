# Spanish Colonies in America and Related Trade

## Territorial Extent and Early Exploration
By the end of the third quarter of the eighteenth century, Spain maintained extensive control over the Americas. Except for Brazil, which was Portuguese, and various small areas held by the Dutch, British, French, and Danish, Spain controlled Florida, Louisiana, California, and all of America to the south. This vast reach was established following the Papacy's division of the American continent between Portugal and Spain. 

Early Spanish exploration included the crossing of the Isthmus of Panama to sight the Pacific Ocean. In 1519, the Portuguese sailor Magellan, acting in the employment of the Spanish King, coasted the south of South America and passed through the Strait of Magellan to enter the Pacific.

## Trade and Commercial Relations
The Spanish colonies and the Canary Islands were significant partners in international trade, particularly with the city of Bristol. During the 16th century, Bristol engaged in trade with Spain, the Canaries, and the Spanish colonies in America. During this same period, Bristol merchants exported cloth to Spain, France, and the Levant.

In Spain, the city of Cadiz served as a major marine center for colonial commerce. However, the city experienced a decline in local trade due to the Napoleonic wars and the acquisition of independence by many Spanish colonies in the early 19th century. Later, the Spanish-American war of 1898 further damaged local commerce, specifically ruining trade with Porto Rico and Cuba.

## Revolts and Independence
The Spanish colonies eventually followed the example of the United States by revolting against the European Great Power system. This movement gained momentum in 1810 when Napoleon placed his brother, Joseph, on the Spanish throne. General Bolivar is described as the "Washington of South America" during this struggle.

Unlike the Thirteen Colonies, the Spanish colonies did not achieve a union among themselves because they were separated by forests, deserts, great mountainous chains, and the Portuguese Empire of Brazil. Consequently, they became a constellation of republican states that were initially prone to revolutions and internal wars. Central America attained its independence between 1819 and 1822.

## International Intervention and the Monroe Doctrine
Spain was unable to suppress the colonial revolts on its own. In accordance with the spirit of the Holy Alliance, Austria suggested that European monarchs assist Spain in the struggle to maintain its colonies. This proposed monarchist restoration was opposed by Britain in Europe.

In 1823, President Monroe of the United States issued a warning that the United States would view any extension of the European system in the Western Hemisphere as a hostile act. This "Monroe Doctrine" established that there should be no extension of extra-American government in America. This policy kept the Great Power system out of America for nearly a hundred years, allowing the new states of Spanish America to determine their own destinies.
