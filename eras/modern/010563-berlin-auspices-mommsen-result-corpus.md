# The Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum

The *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* is a comprehensive collection of the Latin inscriptions of the Roman world. The project was initiated based on a design conceived by the Italian antiquarian Bartolommeo Borghesi (1781–1860). Borghesi, who had studied in Rome and Bologna and served as the podesta of the republic of San Marino, had established a reputation as a numismatist and antiquarian through his contributions to Italian archaeological journals and his work *Nuovi Frammenti dei Fasti Consolari Capitolini* (1818–1820), which provided positive bases for the chronology of Roman history.

## Development in Berlin
Following the design conceived by Borghesi before his death, the task of publishing the collection of Latin inscriptions was taken up by the Academy of Berlin. The project was carried out under the auspices of Mommsen. The resulting work, the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*, serves as a critical resource for the study of Roman antiquity.

## Scholarly Application and Contributions
The *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* has been utilized by scholars to differentiate and analyze historical sites. For example, in the study of Roman villas, Mommsen used volume x of the *Corpus* (published in Berlin in 1883) to argue for the differentiation of two locations that some had suggested were a single villa.

The work has also expanded through supplements. A supplement to volume iii of the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* was published in Berlin in 1893, providing data relevant to the antiquities of Macedonia.

## Related Academic Context
The production of the *Corpus* occurred within a broader environment of German classical scholarship in Berlin. During this era, the city was a center for philology and history, hosting figures such as August Immanuel Bekker (1785–1871), a professor of philosophy at the university of Berlin who edited a vast array of classical authors, and Johann Wilhelm Adolf Kirchhoff (1826–1908), a professor of classical philology in Berlin and an epigraphist. While Kirchhoff focused on other collections, such as the *Corpus Inscriptionum Graecarum* and the *C. I. Atticarum*, his work mirrored the systematic epigraphical approach seen in the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*.

Additionally, Mommsen contributed to other major historical projects of the time. He was responsible for the first three volumes of *Römisches Staatsrecht*, which formed part of the expanded *Handbuch der römischen Alterthümer* continued by Joachim Marquardt.
