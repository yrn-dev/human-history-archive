# Simon Bolivar

## Early Life and Education
Simon Bolivar (1783–1830) was born on July 24, 1783, in Caracas, Venezuela. He was born to Maria Concepcion Palacios y Sojo and Juan Vicente Bolivar y Ponte, both of whom came from noble Venezuelan families. To pursue his studies, Bolivar was sent to Europe, where he lived in Madrid for several years. Following the completion of his education, he traveled throughout the south of Europe and visited Paris, where he witnessed some of the final scenes of the Revolution.

## Personal Life and Return to Venezuela
In 1801, Bolivar married the daughter of Don N. Toro, who was the uncle of the marquis of Toro in Caracas. The couple traveled to Venezuela, where Bolivar intended to focus on the improvement of his extensive estate. However, his young wife died prematurely due to yellow fever, an event that prompted Bolivar to return to Europe.

In 1809, while returning home, Bolivar passed through the United States. This journey provided him with his first opportunity to observe the operations of free institutions.

## Revolutionary Activity and Independence
Shortly after arriving in Venezuela, Bolivar became involved in the cause of independence, a movement that had been active in the Spanish colonies for several years. He was one of the promoters of the April 1810 insurrection at Caracas. As a result, the revolutionary junta granted him a colonel's commission. Bolivar was subsequently associated with Louis Lopez Mendez on a mission to the court of Great Britain.

Venezuela officially declared its independence on July 5, 1811. The following year, the war intensified as Spanish troops, led by Monteverde, advanced. Bolivar was given command of the critical post of Puerto Cabello; however, he was forced to evacuate the position because he lacked necessary support. Due to the inaction of Miranda, the Spaniards were able to regain control of the country.
