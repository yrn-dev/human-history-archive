# Brutus (Payne's), Bertuccio, and Ruy

The figures of Brutus (Payne's), Bertuccio, and Ruy are identified in the archive's records as theatrical roles performed by the American actor Edwin Booth.

## Edwin Booth and His Dramatic Roles
Edwin Booth was a prominent actor known for his "eloquent sensibility" and "extraordinary grace and beauty," qualities that particularly suited him for his most famous role as Hamlet. Booth is noted for having played Hamlet more frequently than perhaps any other actor before or since. His repertoire was extensive, encompassing a wide variety of parts including Macbeth, Lear, Othello, Iago, Shylock, Wolsey, Richard II, Richard III, Benedick, Petruccio, Richelieu, and Sir Giles Overreach.

Among his other roles were the characters of Ruy Blas, Don Cesar de Bazan, Bertuccio—appearing in Tom Taylor's play *The Fool's Revenge*—and Brutus (Payne's).

## Professional Career and Theatres
Between 1868 and 1869, Booth established his own venue, Booth's theatre, located in New York at the corner of 6th Avenue and 23rd Street. He organized a high-quality stock company at this location, producing several plays including *The Merchant of Venice*, *Much Ado about Nothing*, *Macbeth*, *Julius Caesar*, *The Winter's Tale*, and *Romeo and Juliet*. Booth was distinguished by his commitment to using the true text of Shakespeare, a reform that occurred in his practice many years before similar changes took place in England.

While Booth's theatrical ventures were almost always successful, his management was not economical due to his "confiding nature" and generosity. In 1874, he lost his entire fortune along with the grand dramatic structure he had built. However, through arduous toil, he was able to accumulate wealth once more.

## Later Life and Legacy
Booth demonstrated his generous nature by converting his large residence in Gramercy Park, New York, into The Players' club. This club was intended for the elect of the acting profession as well as members of other professions. He donated the house, his works of art, his books, and various invaluable mementos of the stage to the club, retaining only a single apartment for himself.

Booth's career included international success; he visited London in 1851, 1880, and 1882, where he performed with brilliant success at the Haymarket theatre. He also visited Germany in 1882, where his acting was received with the highest enthusiasm. His final stage appearance took place in 1891 in Brooklyn, where he played Hamlet.

Booth was married twice: first to Mary Devlin in 1860 (who died in 1863) and later to Mary F. McVicker in 1869 (who died in 1881). He had one daughter from his first marriage, Edwina Booth Grossman, who published *Edwin Booth: Recollections* in 1894. Edwin Booth died on June 7, 1893.
