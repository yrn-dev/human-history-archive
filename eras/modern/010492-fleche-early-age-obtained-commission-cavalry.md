# La Flèche and Associated Military and Academic Figures

La Flèche is a location associated with several notable educational institutions and individuals of academic and military distinction.

## Educational Institutions at La Flèche
The archive records multiple schools located at La Flèche. One such institution was founded and endowed by Henry IV for the Jesuits. This school was attended by Descartes (known as Du Perron) from 1604 to 1612. During his time there, Descartes was granted exceptional privileges due to his feeble health, which excused him from morning duties and allowed him to develop a lifelong habit of reflection in bed. While at the school, he began to distrust his teachers and the authority of tradition. Two years before his departure, he was among twenty-four individuals selected to receive the heart of Henry IV as it was transported to its resting place at La Flèche.

Additionally, La Flèche hosted a military school specifically for the sons of officers. This institution was attended by Paul Margueritte, the son of General Jean Auguste Marguerite.

## Notable Figures Associated with La Flèche
Several individuals who studied at La Flèche went on to achieve prominence in science and the arts:

*   **Jean Charles Borda (1733–1799):** A nautical astronomer and mathematician born in Dax. After studying at La Flèche, he obtained a commission in the cavalry at an early age. Borda became a member of the Academy of Sciences in 1756 following the presentation of his *Memoire sur le mouvement des projectiles*. His career included service at the battle of Hastembeck and a subsequent transition to the naval service, where he created a map of the Canary Islands and the Azores. In 1782, he was captured by a British squadron and taken to England, though he was soon released on parole. Borda is noted for his contributions to marine engineering through hydrodynamics and his improvements to repeating and reflecting circles for nautical astronomy. He also invented the majority of the instruments used in an attempt to determine an arc of the meridian, a project he pursued with P.F.A. Mechain and J.B.J. Delambre.
*   **Paul Margueritte (1860–):** A French novelist born in Algeria. After attending the military school at La Flèche, he served as a clerk to the minister of public instruction starting in 1880. He later transitioned to literature and the arts, designing pantomimes such as *Colombine pardonnée* and *Pierrot assassin de sa femme*, in which he played the role of Pierrot. He authored numerous novels, including *La Confession posthume* and *L'Eau qui dort*. Though he began as a realistic novelist, he later signed a manifesto against Zola's *La Terre* and became known for sober studies of sentiment.

## Military Commissions and Cavalry
The archive contains various records regarding the attainment of military commissions and the nature of cavalry service.

### Cavalry and Dragoons
The term "dragoon" originally referred to mounted infantrymen of the 17th and late 16th centuries who were trained to fight on foot. They were named after the "dragon," a short musket or carbine. Unlike true cavalry, they were organized into companies rather than squadrons, and their officers held infantry titles. Over time, dragoons improved their armament and horsemanship to meet cavalry standards, eventually becoming "medium cavalry" by the early wars of Frederick the Great. In the British service, dragoons are classified as heavy cavalry, while Dragoon Guards are medium. The "Dragonnades" were specific operations involving mounted troops used to enforce the decrees of Louis XIV against Protestants.

### Ranks and Roles
Within the cavalry, infantry, and artillery, the rank of corporal is a non-commissioned officer ranking below a sergeant. In the 16th and 17th centuries, the cavalry utilized corporals but not sergeants. This tradition persists in the three British household cavalry regiments, where the rank of sergeant is replaced by "corporal of horse" and the sergeant-major by "corporal-major."

### Other Military Careers
The archive notes several other figures who entered military service at an early age:
*   **François Marie, Duc de Broglie (1671–1745):** He became a lieutenant-colonel of the king's regiment of cavalry at age twenty-three. He served in the War of the Spanish Succession and was later appointed director-general of cavalry and dragoons in 1719. He eventually became a marshal of France in 1734.
*   **Antoine Adolphe Marcelin de Marbot (1781–1844):** He entered the army at an early age and obtained a commission during the revolutionary wars. He served as aide-de-camp to Bernadotte and later to Marshal Davout.
*   **Victor Margueritte (1866–):** He entered the 1st chasseurs d'Afrique in 1888 and served until resigning his commission in 1896.
