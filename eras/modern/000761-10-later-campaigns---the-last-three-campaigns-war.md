# Later Campaigns of the War in the Netherlands

The final three campaigns of the war in the Netherlands were characterized by the fully developed military genius of Marshal Saxe. Following the events at Fontenoy, French forces achieved significant momentum, carrying all before them. This French advance was aided by the withdrawal of the majority of English forces, who returned home to assist in suppressing the "Forty-Five rebellion," leaving their allies in a helpless state.

## French Advances and the Battle of Roucoux
By 1746, the French had driven the Austrians and the Dutch back toward the line of the Meuse. During this period, the French successfully captured most of the important fortresses in the region. A pivotal engagement occurred on October 11, 1746, at the battle of Roucoux (also referred to as Raucourt) near Liege. In this conflict, the French forces under Marshal Saxe secured a victory over the allied forces commanded by Prince Charles of Lorraine.

## The Invasion of the United Provinces
By April 1747, Saxe's army had conquered the Austrian Netherlands up to the Meuse. He then shifted the French focus toward the United Provinces. The frontier fortresses of Holland offered only slight resistance to the advance. On July 2, 1747, the duke of Cumberland and the prince of Orange suffered a severe defeat at Lauffeld (also known as Lawfeld or Val).

Following this victory, Saxe secretly dispatched a corps under Marshal Loewendahl to besiege Bergen-op-Zoom. The French successfully stormed Bergen-op-Zoom on September 18, 1747. In the final year of the war, the combined forces of Loewendahl and Saxe attacked Maestricht, which surrendered on May 7, 1748.

## Russian Involvement and the General Peace
During the conflict, Russia allied herself with Austria in 1746, following the settlement of the quarrel between Russia and Sweden via the peace of Abo in 1743. Eventually, a large Russian army marched from Moscow to the Rhine to join the allies on the Meuse. While this movement held military significance and served as a prelude to the great invasions of 1815 and 1813-1814, the army arrived too late to be of use in the campaign.

The war concluded with the signing of the general peace of Aix-la-Chapelle (Aachen) on October 18, 1748.
