# Bacillus subtilis

*Bacillus subtilis* is a species of Schizomycetes characterized by its rod-like form and its ability to produce spores. It is categorized within the genus *Bacillus*, one of the three primary genera that encompass the majority of described bacterial species.

## Morphology and Motility
The organism exists as motile rodlets. In its motile stage, *B. subtilis* possesses peritrichous cilia. However, this state is temporary; within a few hours after the commencement of growth, the organism transitions from this motile stage into one of filamentous growth, a process preceded by the casting off of its cilia.

## Spore Formation
*Bacillus subtilis* utilizes spore-formation as a characteristic method of reproduction. These spores serve as reproductive bodies that represent a resting stage for the organism. The species is capable of producing two different types of spores:

*   **Endospores (True Spores):** The process begins with the appearance of a minute granule in the protoplasm of a vegetative cell. This granule enlarges over several hours, absorbing all the protoplasm and secreting a resistive, thin envelope. The resulting ripe ovoid spore is smaller than the mother cell and lies loosely within it.
*   **Arthrospores:** These are not true spores but are ordinary vegetative cells that separate and enter a condition of rest.

In *B. subtilis*, spores are typically formed in a bacillar segment, and usually, only one spore is produced per cell. The spore may be formed in long or short segments, with the cell wall changing form to accommodate the contents. In certain instances, the protoplasm responsible for spore-formation produces a blue reaction when treated with iodine solutions. Spores can develop even in cells that are actively swarming, as the process of formation does not interfere with their movements.

Older literature refers to "Koepfchenbacterien," which are identified as bacterioid segments containing a spore at one end, where the mother cell wall has adapted to the spore's outline.

## Spore Characteristics and Germination
The ripe spores of *B. subtilis* are colorless (or very dark due to their minute size and high index of refraction), highly refractive, and spherical, ovoid, or long-ovoid in shape. According to Zopf, these spores measure 0.0006 mm in breadth and 0.0012 mm in length. The membrane of the spore may be relatively thick and can exhibit strata or shells.

During germination, the spore develops a germinal rodlet. In *B. subtilis*, the axis of growth for this germinal rodlet is positioned at right angles to the long axis of the spore. As germination progresses, the remains of the spore stay attached to the germinal rodlets.
