# The Deposition of President Melgarejo and Subsequent Bolivian Political Transitions

## The Revolution of 1871
In January 1871, President Melgarejo was deposed and driven from Bolivia. This change in leadership was the result of a revolution led by Colonel Augustin Morales, who subsequently assumed the presidency. Morales's tenure ended in November 1872 when he was murdered. He was succeeded by Colonel Adolfo Ballivian, who remained in power until his death in 1874.

## Diplomatic Relations and the Chilean Treaties
During the presidency of Colonel Adolfo Ballivian, Bolivia entered into a secret agreement with Peru. The motivations for this alliance were rooted in previous arrangements with Chile. Under a treaty signed in 1866, Bolivia had established the 24th parallel as the boundary of Chilean territory. This instrument granted Chile full trading facilities and a half share of the customs on the coast between the 23rd and 24th parallels. Additionally, Chile was permitted to export products and mine in this district without hindrance or tax from Bolivia.

Further concessions occurred in 1870, when Bolivia granted an Anglo-Chilean company the right to work specific nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel in exchange for a sum of $10,000. These compacts led to significant discontent within Bolivia due to the wealth passing into Chilean hands. Similarly, Peru was displeased by the establishment of Chilean capital in the rich district of Tarapaca.

## The Secret Agreement with Peru
On February 6, 1873, Bolivia and Peru entered into a secret agreement. While the ostensible purpose of this pact was mutual defense against exterior aggression and the preservation of their territorial integrity, the agreement was specifically aimed at preventing further encroachment by Chile.

## Later Political Instability and the War with Chile
Bolivia later experienced further political upheaval during a period of double revolution affecting both Peru and Bolivia. In Peru, Colonel Pierola was proclaimed dictator after General Prado was deposed. In Bolivia, troops at Tacna, citing the cowardice or incompetence of their president, removed him from command and elected Colonel Camacho to lead them. Simultaneously, a revolution in La Paz proclaimed General Narciso Campero as president; he was officially elected to the post in June of that year via the ordinary constitutional procedure.

During 1880, the conflict between Chile and Peru was primarily a naval struggle, with Bolivia playing little to no part. Following the battles of Miraflores and Chorillos in January 1881, which involved savage excesses and heavy slaughter by Chilean troops, Lima and Callao surrendered. While a provisional government under Peruvian admiral Montero continued to resist in the interior, the Peruvians eventually signed a peace treaty with Chile on October 20, 1883.

## The Peace Treaty of December 1883
Following the Peruvian surrender, Admiral Montero fled to Bolivia for refuge, though he was received coldly and eventually withdrew to Europe. Negotiations between Bolivia and Chile concluded on December 11, 1883, with the signing of a peace treaty. As a result of this agreement, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, including the port of Cobija.
