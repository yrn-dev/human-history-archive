# BASHKALA

## Geography and Location
Bashkala is the chief town of a sanjak within the vilayet of Van, located in Asiatic Turkey. The town serves as a military station and is situated at an elevation of 7,500 feet above sea-level. It is positioned in the valley of the Great Zab river, specifically on the east slope of lofty bare mountains. From this location, the town overlooks a wide valley, with the Zab river flowing on the farther side.

## Demographics and Infrastructure
The population of Bashkala numbers approximately 10,000. While the inhabitants are principally Kurds, the population also includes 1,000 Jews and 1,500 Armenians. 

The town's infrastructure is characterized by wide and fairly clean streets. The houses are constructed of sun-dried brick and are described as well built. On a knoll situated above the town, there is a ruined fortress that was formerly occupied by a Kurdish Bey.

## Economy and Trade
Bashkala is an important center for the Hakkiari sanjak, a difficult mountain district located to the south-west. This region contains numerous tribes of Kurds and Nestorian Christians, as well as various Kurdish tribes located along the Persian frontier.

The local economy includes a small bazaar, though a thriving trade is conducted with the surrounding mountain districts. The town is known for producing good work in carpentry and smithing. Agriculture on the plain consists of the cultivation of sesame, millet, barley, and wheat. However, due to the environment, vegetables and fruit must mostly be imported from Persia.

## Climate and Transport
The high elevation of Bashkala results in a short summer and an extremely severe winter. 

The town is connected by roads leading to Mosul through the Nestorian country, as well as to Van and Urmia in Persia. In the wilder areas of the Hakkiari Mountains, the Nestorian and Kurd tribes are subject to slight government control; they are granted a large degree of self-government and are permitted to pay tribute.
