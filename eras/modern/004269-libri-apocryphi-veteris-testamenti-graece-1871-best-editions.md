# Libri Apocryphi Veteris Testamenti Graece (1871)

The *Libri Apocryphi Veteris Testamenti Graece*, published in 1871, is a work by Fritzsche. It is categorized as one of the most convenient editions of the Greek text.

## Inclusion of the Apocalypse of Baruch
The *Libri Apocryphi Veteris Testamenti Graece* is notable for its reproduction of the Apocalypse of Baruch. This specific apocalypse had been long lost until its discovery by Ceriani, who found a 6th century manuscript in the Milan library. The text of the apocalypse survived only in a Syriac version.

Ceriani first published a Latin translation of the work in 1866 within *Monumenta Sacra* (I. ii. 73-98). Fritzsche subsequently reproduced this material in the 1871 *Libri Apocryphi V. T.* on pages 654-699.

## Related Editions and Works
While Fritzsche's 1871 work provides a convenient Greek text, other editions are cited as the best for specific books. For the book of Baruch, these include:
* Kneucker's *Das Buch Baruch* (1879).
* Gifford's edition found in the *Speaker's Apoc.* ii.

Other convenient editions of the Greek text include those produced by Tischendorf (found in the second volume of his Septuagint) and Swete (found in volume iii).

## Context of the Apocalypse of Baruch
The Apocalypse of Baruch contains sections that were known long before Ceriani's discovery of the full text. Chapters lxxviii through lxxxvi consist of Baruch's epistle to the nine and a half tribes in captivity. These specific chapters have been published in various forms:
* In Syriac and Latin within the Paris and London Polyglots.
* In Syriac from a single manuscript in Lagarde's *Libri V. T. Apocryphi Syr.* (1861).
* From ten manuscripts by Charles in *Apocalypse of Baruch* (1896, pp. 124-167).

Further translations of the entire book include an English translation by Charles (pp. 1-167) and a German translation by Ryssel, published in Kautzsch's *Apok. und Pseud.* (1900, ii. pp. 413-446).
