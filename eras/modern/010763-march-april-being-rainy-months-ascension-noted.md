# Ascension Island

## Geography and Topography
Ascension is a volcanic island located in the Atlantic Ocean, situated between 7 deg. 53' and 8 deg. S. latitude and 14 deg. 18' and 14 deg. 26' W. longitude. It is positioned approximately 800 miles northwest of St Helena. The island is a volcanic mass built upon a submarine platform and is characterized by numerous cones. It measures about 7-1/2 miles in length and 6 miles in breadth, covering an area of 38 square miles with a circumference of roughly 22 miles.

The principal elevation of the island is Green Mountain, a huge elliptical crater that reaches 2820 feet above sea level. Surrounding this peak are table-lands or plains that vary in height from 1200 to 2000 feet. These plains sweep gradually down toward the shore on the north side, whereas on the south, they end in lofty and bold precipices. The landscape is further intersected by rugged and steep ravines that open into small coves or bays on the shore, which are fenced with cellular and compact masses of lava.

## Climate and Vegetation
The island is under the immediate influence of the south-east trade-wind. The climate is described as remarkably healthy despite high temperatures; the average day temperature is 85 deg. F. on the shore and 75 deg. F. on Green Mountain. The air is light and clear. Average annual rainfall is approximately 20 inches, with the rainy months occurring in March and April.

Originally, the island lacked vegetation except for the summit of Green Mountain, which remains verdant due to frequent mists. However, grasses and shrubs have since been planted on the lower hills.

## Flora and Fauna
Ascension possesses no indigenous vertebrate land fauna. The island is frequented in large numbers by "wideawake" birds, whose eggs are collected for food. Other animals found on the island include goats, wild cats, a few deer, some cattle, and numbers of bred sheep.

The shores are noted for a high number of turtle eggs and turtles, with the season lasting from December to May or June; these turtles are caught and kept in large ponds. The surrounding coasts provide a variety of high-quality fish, most notably the "soldier," the conger-eel, the cavalli, and the rock-cod.

## Scientific and Historical Notes
The island's lee side is subject to "rollers" that break on the shore with great violence. In 1877, Sir David Gill used Ascension to determine the solar parallax.
