# Marshal Belleisle

Marshal Belleisle, also known as Louis Charles Armand Fouquet (1693–1746), was a French soldier and diplomatist. Throughout his career, he served in various capacities as a military commander and a political representative of the French government.

## Early Career and Diplomatic Missions
The Chevalier de Belle-Isle began his military service as a junior officer during the War of the Spanish Succession. By the campaign of 1734, he served as a brigadier on the Moselle and Rhine, where he attained the rank of *marechal de camp*. Between 1741 and 1742, he was employed in political missions in Swabia and Bavaria under his brother. During this period, he rose to the rank of lieutenant-general.

In 1741, during the Silesian Campaign, France dispatched Marshal Belleisle to the camp of Frederick to negotiate an alliance. This diplomatic effort contributed to the transition of the "Silesian adventure" into the broader War of the Austrian Succession.

## Campaigns in Germany and Bohemia
Belleisle served as the political head of French affairs in Germany and was described as a very capable general. He accompanied Broglie throughout the operations in Bohemia. By late June 1742, the French forces under Broglie were nearly surrounded at Prague by the army of Prince Charles. Although the French government eventually sent reinforcements from the lower Rhine under Marshal Maillebois to assist, a disagreement in objectives arose. Belleisle and Broglie believed Maillebois was tasked with regaining a permanent foothold in Bohemia, whereas Maillebois viewed his mission as a demonstration to cover Broglie's retreat.

Following these events, Broglie was sent to take over command from Maillebois on the Danube, east of Regensburg. Simultaneously, Belleisle took charge of the army at Prague. In this position, Belleisle commanded a force that included Saxe and the best of the younger French officers. This army was opposed by a force under Prince Lobkowitz, which was only slightly superior to Belleisle's troops in terms of quality and number.

## Later Years and Death
Between 1742 and 1743, Belleisle continued to fight in the Rhine countries, Bavaria, and Bohemia. In 1744, he was arrested and sent to England along with the marshal. Following his release, he was assigned a command in the Army of Piedmont. Marshal Belleisle died on July 19, 1746, during the action of Exilles (Col de l'Assiette), falling as a victim to his "romantic bravery."
