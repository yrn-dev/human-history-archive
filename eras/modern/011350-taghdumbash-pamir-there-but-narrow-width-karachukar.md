# Taghdumbash Pamir

The Taghdumbash Pamir, also referred to as the Supreme Head of the Mountains, is a high-altitude region located in the Pamirs. It serves as a critical geographical and political landmark in the demarcation of boundaries between Afghanistan, Russia, and China.

## Geography and Hydrography
The Pamir region is described not as a single steppe, but as a variety of "Pamirs," which are lofty valleys situated between hill ranges. These valleys feature floors that are more or less flat, typically measuring no more than five or six miles in width, and provide luxuriant summer pasture. The Pamir plateau itself is characterized as a broad, rounded ridge extending north and south, intersected by thick mountain chains.

The Taghdumbash Pamir is a significant point of water parting. While most waters in the plateau collect in the Oxus, the eastern flow from the Taghdumbash Pamir finds its way into the Yarkand river. Consequently, the watershed extends from the Taghdumbash Pamir, moving down the range that divides it from the Little Pamir and continuing along the Neza Tash mountains to the Kizil Art Pass.

## Political Boundaries and Demarcation
The Taghdumbash Pamir is central to the definition of Afghan territory in the north-east. The extension of Afghan territory reaches a point slightly short of 75 deg. E., from which it follows the water-divide to the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir. From this point, the boundary is defined by the water-parting of the Hindu Kush.

In the province of Badakshan, which includes Wakhan, the boundary skirts around the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir before merging into the Pamir boundary and turning westwards to follow the course of the Oxus. This configuration results in Badakshan extending an "arm" into the Pamirs that is bottle-shaped—narrow at the neck, formed by the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush, and swelling out eastwards to include portions of the Great and Little Pamirs.

## Regional Connectivity and Routes
The Taghdumbash Pamir is a key waypoint for travel between different regions of Central Asia. According to itineraries comparing the routes of Marco Polo and Captain Younghusband, a path from Wakhan to Kashgar passes through the following landmarks:
* The Wakhijrui Pass, where the Pamir-i-Wakhan joins Little Pamir at Bozai-Gumbaz.
* The old fort of Kurgan-i-Ujadbai, located at the junction of the two branches of the Tagh-dum-bash Pamir.
* The Tagh-dum-bash Pamir itself, followed by Tash Kurgán, Bulun Kul, and the Gez Defile before reaching Kashgar.

## Geological Context
The region is linked to broader mountain systems. The Shandur Range, which is part of a stupendous water-divide hedging in the Chitral and Kunar valleys, unites with the Hindu Kush near the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir.

## Etymology and Classification
The term "Pamir" is identified as a Khokandi-Turki word meaning "wilderness"—a place that is waste, abandoned, or depopulated, yet still capable of habitation. This is evidenced by the ruins of villages and burial-grounds left by the Kirghiz who formerly inhabited the area. Geographers recognize several distinct Pamirs, including:
* Pamir Alichur
* Pamir Khurd (Little Pamir)
* Pamir Kalan (Great Pamir)
* Pamir Khargosi
* Pamir Sares
* Pamir Rang-kul

Some sources suggest that "Pamir" is a generic term applied to all high plateaux within the Thian Shan.
