# Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres

The Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres is a French learned society and academic institution. It maintains a role in the scientific direction of the French Ecole d'Athenes, which was founded in 1846. Under this direction, the school has conducted extensive and costly excavations at Delphi and Delos, as well as investigations into monuments of the Byzantine epoch.

## Membership and Notable Members

Membership in the Académie has included a variety of scholars, scientists, and historians. 

**Jean Sylvain Bailly (1736–1793)**, a French orator and astronomer, was admitted to the Académie des Inscriptions in 1785. His admission occurred at a time when the simultaneous membership of all three Academies, previously held by Fontenelle, was renewed in him. Bailly was also a member of the Academy of Sciences (elected 1763) and the French Academy (admitted February 26, 1784).

**Anatole Jean-Baptiste Antoine de Barthelemy (1821–1904)**, a numismatist and archaeologist, was elected as a member of the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in 1897. He was known for his exhaustive work on the ancient bishoprics of Brittany and his various manuals and monographs on numismatics, including works on Merovingian coinage and the monnaie parisis.

**Joseph Marie Noel Valois** is listed as a member of the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in Paris. In addition to this membership, Valois has served as the Honorary Archivist at the Archives Nationales and was previously the President of the Société de l'École des Chartes and the Société de l'Histoire de France.

## Institutional Roles and Administration

The Académie has historically employed specialists to manage royal and national collections. Jean Jacques Barthelemy (1716–1795), a writer and numismatist, served as an assistant to M. Gros de Boze, who was the keeper of the royal collection of medals and the perpetual secretary of the Academy of Inscriptions and Belles-lettres. Upon the death of de Boze in 1753, Jean Jacques Barthelemy succeeded him as the keeper of the medals.

## Publications and Research

The Académie produces scholarly records and research papers, most notably the *Memoirs of the Academy of Belles Lettres* (also referred to as *Mémoires de l'Académie des Inscriptions*). These publications have covered a wide range of historical and linguistic topics:

*   **Ancient History:** The *Memoirs* have included dissertations by M. Freret and Abbé Banier concerning the Assyrian empire.
*   **Etymology and Linguistics:** The *Memoirs* (specifically tom. xx) have provided research on the name of Clovis, noting that the "Ch" in the Latin *Chlodovechus* or *Chlodovoeus* represents a German aspiration, making the true name similar to "Lewis."
