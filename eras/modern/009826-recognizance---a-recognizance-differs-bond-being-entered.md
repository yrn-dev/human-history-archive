# Recognizance

## Definition and Legal Nature
A recognizance is a legal obligation that is distinguished from a bond by the manner of its entry. Specifically, a recognizance is entered into before a court of record, which results in the agreement becoming an obligation of record.

## Historical Applications in Trade
In English history, recognizances were used as a regulatory tool for itinerant food dealers known as badgers. Badgers were individuals who purchased food—such as corn, victuals, butter, cheese, or fish—in one location to sell in another. While an English statute of 1552 exempted badgers from penalties regarding regrating, forestalling, and engrossing (provided they were licensed by three justices of the peace), later legislation increased restrictions. 

A statute passed in 1562-1563 required that badgers be householders of three years' standing in their licensed county and be licensed for only one year. As part of this regulatory process, these dealers were required to enter into recognizances pledging that they would not forestall or engross. These statutes remained in effect until an act of 1844 abolished the offence of badgering and repealed the associated laws.

## Use in Ancient Athens
In the habits of ancient Athens, recognizances were utilized during legal proceedings. It was not the custom in Athens to maintain the long postponement of a trial when an accused party was under recognizance, nor was it common to keep an accused party in prison for a long duration before their trial. Historical accounts mention the flight of Kallixenus from his recognizances, an event that occurred prior to the death of Kleophon.

## Modern Legal Context
In contemporary legal proceedings, such as those in the Circuit Court of the Fifteenth Judicial Circuit in Palm Beach County, Florida, a defendant may be released on their own recognizance (OA). Failure to appear at a scheduled hearing under these conditions can result in the forfeiture of the bond and the issuance of a capias for the individual's arrest.
