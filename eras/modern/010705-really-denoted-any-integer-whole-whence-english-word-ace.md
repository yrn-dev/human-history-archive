# As

The term *as* was a Roman unit of weight and measure. In its most general sense, *as* denoted any integer or whole. This conceptual meaning is the origin of the English word "ace."

## Units of Measure and Weight
As a unit of measure, the *as* was divided into 12 *unciae*, a division that provided the basis for both the English "inch" and "ounce." The Roman system utilized various fractions of the *as*, including:
* **Deunx**: 11/12
* **Dextrans**: 5/6
* **Dodrans**: 3/4
* **Bes**: 2/3
* **Septunx**: 7/12
* **Semis**: 1/2
* **Quincunx**: 5/12
* **Triens**: 1/3
* **Quadrans**: 1/4
* **Sextans**: 1/6
* **Sescuncia**: 1/8
* **Uncia**: 1/12

Specific applications of the *as* varied by the type of measurement being taken. For weight, the unit was the *libra* (pound), which equals approximately 11-4/5 oz. avoirdupois. For length, the unit was the *pes* (foot), measuring approximately 11-3/5 in. Surface area was measured by the *jugerum*, which is about 2/3 of an acre. Liquid measure was denoted by the *amphora* (approximately 5-3/5 gal.), and dry measure was denoted by the *modius* (approximately 9/10 peck).

## Legal and Financial Applications
Beyond physical measurements, the term *as* was used to signify a whole inheritance. This terminology is reflected in legal descriptions of heirs: a *heres ex asse* was an heir to the whole estate, while a *heres ex semisse* was an heir to half of the estate. Additionally, the term was employed in the calculation of rates of interest.
