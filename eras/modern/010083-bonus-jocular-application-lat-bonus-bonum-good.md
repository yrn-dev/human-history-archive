# BONUS

## Definition and Financial Application
The term "bonus" is a jocular application of the Latin word *bonus* (used in place of *bonum*), meaning "a good thing." In a general sense, it refers to any remuneration or payment that exceeds what was promised or is due.

In the context of joint-stock companies, a bonus is a sum paid to shareholders as an addition to the ordinary dividend. These payments are generally sourced from exceptional transactions or accumulated profits. Insurance companies also use the term to describe an addition made to a policy's amount through a *pro rata* distribution of surplus or accumulated profits.

## Linguistic and Historical Occurrences
The word appears in various historical and literary contexts across different languages and eras:

**Latin Usage**
In Latin texts, *bonus* is used to describe positive attributes or individuals:
*   **Leadership and Character:** In the writings of Sallust, a person is described as being *bonus consilio* (good in counsel). Other texts refer to a *dux bonus* (a good leader) and the concept of a *vir bonus* (a good man), though one source notes that such a man is as rare as a phoenix, born only once every five hundred years.
*   **Comparative Descriptions:** In a verse regarding three rivals, the phrase *bonus Afer* is used to describe Niger.
*   **Religious Context:** In the *Gesta Francorum*, King Clovis is recorded as exclaiming that St. Martin is *bonus in auxilio* (good in help), though he added that the saint is *carus in negotio* (expensive in business) after a miracle involving the redemption of a war-horse.
*   **Moral Observations:** An anonymous author of the *Expositio totius Mundi*, writing in "barbarous Latin" about Carthage, observed that a *bonus* (good person) is difficult to find among its inhabitants, though a few good people may exist among many.

**Other Languages**
In the context of mixed languages and Orientalisms, the term *Bonus* is used to represent the Persian word *abnús*, meaning "ebony."
