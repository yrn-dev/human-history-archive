# The Fomana Treaty and Ashanti-British Relations

The Fomana treaty was a diplomatic agreement concluded between the British and the Ashanti kingdom. Its subsequent violation and the failure of the Ashanti to adhere to its terms contributed to escalating tensions and diplomatic crises between the two powers in the late 19th century.

## Context and the Fomana Treaty
The Fomana treaty followed a period of conflict and negotiation. While the specific terms of the treaty's initial drafting are not detailed in the archive, the agreement included a stipulated indemnity of 50,000 oz. of gold to be paid by the Ashanti. 

Following the war of 1873-74, the Ashanti deposed their leader, Kofi Karikari, and placed his brother, Mensa, on the golden stool, which served as the symbol of sovereignty. During his reign, Mensa broke almost every article of the Fomana treaty. The indemnity payment was largely ignored; of the 50,000 oz. of gold stipulated, only 4,000 oz. were actually paid, and the British did not even demand the remaining balance. Mensa's rule was characterized as tyrannous and was marked by repeated human sacrifices.

## Subsequent Political Instability in Ashanti
The instability of Mensa's reign led to a revolution in 1883, which displaced him. He was succeeded by Kwaka Dua II, a young man who died in June 1884, only a few months after his election. The former king, Kofi Karikari, also died in June 1884. These deaths threatened the Ashanti with disruption, leading to a desolating civil war.

The conflict ended on March 26, 1888, when Prince Prempeh was chosen as king, taking the name Kwaka Dua III. This election occurred with the sanction and presence of an officer from the Gold Coast government, as the colonial government had been forced to intervene due to the troubles the dispute caused in the Gold Coast. Under Prempeh, the Ashanti experienced a period of peace and prosperity.

## The 1893 Crisis and British Intervention
Peace was interrupted in 1893 when fresh trouble arose between the Ashanti and the tribes of the protectorate. Prempeh ordered the closure of roads to traders. Because the country had been included in the British sphere of influence by international agreement, the British government intervened.

A mission was dispatched to Prempeh with two primary demands:
1. That he fulfill the terms of the 1874 treaty.
2. That he accept a British protectorate and receive a resident at Kumasi.

Prempeh refused to treat with the governor of the Gold Coast. Instead, he sent informal agents to England, but the secretary of state refused to receive them. Prempeh provided no reply to the British mission's demands within the three-week grace period, which expired on October 31, 1895.

## Historical Background of Ashanti-British Conflict
The tensions surrounding the Fomana treaty were rooted in a long history of territorial and financial disputes. In the early 18th century, Osai Tutu founded the Ashanti power, extending the empire through conquests. By 1807, conflict erupted when the Ashanti invaded the country of the Fanti to capture fugitive Assin chiefs. This led to the destruction of a town near Anamabo and the slaughter of 8,000 inhabitants.

Throughout the 19th century, disputes frequently centered on "custom-notes" and ground rents for forts. For example, the Ashanti regarded the Elmina fort as their territory and received an annual payment from the Dutch. When the British took over Elmina fort on April 2, 1872, the Ashanti resented the occupation. This event, along with the Ashanti captivity of four Europeans (including members of the Basel mission and a French trader), served as the immediate cause of the war in 1873.

Earlier, in 1820, a consul named Joseph Dupuis had negotiated a treaty in Kumasi that acknowledged Ashanti sovereignty over Fanti territory, though this was later disowned by the governor of Cape Coast, Mr. J. Hope Smith. In 1824, Sir Charles M'Carthy was defeated by the Ashanti at Essamako; he and eight other Europeans were killed, and the governor's skull was used as a royal drinking-cup in Kumasi.
