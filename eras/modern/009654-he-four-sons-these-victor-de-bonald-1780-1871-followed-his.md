# LOUIS DE BONALD AND FAMILY

## Louis de Bonald
Louis de Bonald, the vicomte de Bonald, was a writer whose work is characterized by a style described as pure, vigorous, and ornate. While his writings contain many fruitful thoughts, his system is regarded as lacking the qualities of a formal philosophy; he is characterized as a dilettante in abstract thought, with his primary strengths being the sincerity and vigour of his statements rather than the cogency of his reasoning.

The vicomte published several works, including *Essai analytique sur les lois naturelles de l'ordre social* (1800), *Du divorce considere au XIXe siecle* (1801), and *Legislation primitive* (1802). In 1818, he published the two-volume *Recherches philosophiques sur les premiers objets de connaissances morales*, followed by *Melanges litteraires et politiques, demonstration philosophique du principe constitutif de la societe* in 1819 (and 1852). A first collected edition of his works was released in 12 volumes between 1817 and 1819, and a later edition was published by the Abbe Migne in three volumes in 1859.

## Victor de Bonald
Louis de Bonald had four sons, one of whom was Victor de Bonald (1780–1871). Victor accompanied his father during his exile. Following the restoration, he served as the rector of the academy of Montpellier, a position he lost during the Hundred Days. He regained the post at the second restoration but eventually resigned in 1830.

As an author, Victor de Bonald wrote *Des vrais principes opposes aux erreurs du XIXe siecle* (1833) and *Moise et les geologues modernes* (1835). He also wrote a biography of his father, titled *Notice sur M. le Vicomte de Bonald* (1841, edited in Avignon, 1853).

## Other Sons of Louis de Bonald
The other three sons of Louis de Bonald pursued diverse paths in church, state, and journalism:

*   **Louis Jacques Maurice (1787–1870):** He became a cardinal in 1841. He faced condemnation by the council of state due to a pastoral letter in which he attacked the *Manuel de droit ecclesiastique* written by Dupin the elder. In 1848, he conducted a memorial service dedicated to those who had fallen in the defense of religious and civil liberty. However, by 1851, he used his position in the senate to advocate for the use of arms to maintain the temporal power of Rome.
*   **Henri (d. 1846):** He was a contributor to journals associated with the legitimists.
*   **Rene:** He served as the interim prefect of Aveyron in 1817.
