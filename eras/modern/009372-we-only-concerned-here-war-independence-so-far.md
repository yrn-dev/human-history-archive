# The War of Independence in Upper Peru

The War of Independence, specifically as it pertained to Upper Peru (the region known in later days as Bolivia), was a prolonged conflict characterized by a struggle between Spanish royalist forces and South American patriot forces. The conflict began after the patriots of Buenos Aires had successfully liberated the interior provinces of the Rio de la Plata from Spanish dominion and subsequently directed their military efforts against the enemies holding Upper Peru.

## Timeline and Early Campaigns
The warfare in the region was almost uninterrupted, spanning from July 1809 until August 1825. The primary theater of action for these hostilities was located between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta.

The initial phase of the war saw a successful invasion of Upper Peru conducted by the army of Buenos Aires under the leadership of General Balcarce. After achieving two victories over the Spanish troops, the patriot army celebrated the first anniversary of independence near Lake Titicaca in May 1811. However, this early success was short-lived. Due to the negligence and dissolute conduct of its leaders, the patriot army became disorganized. In June 1811, the Spanish army, commanded by Gey fol Goyeneche, attacked and defeated the patriots, forcing them to retreat into Jujuy.

## Mid-War Fluctuations and Insurrections
For the following four years, the conflict was marked by alternating victories for both the Spaniards and the patriots. This period of instability concluded in 1815 with a decisive battle taking place between Oruro and Potosi, which resulted in a total rout of the patriot forces.

Following this defeat, an insurrection broke out among the Indians of the southern provinces of Peru. This revolt was joined by numerous Creoles, as the objective of the movement was the independence of the entire country. This uprising was, however, quickly suppressed by the royalists.

## Royalist Offensives and Guerrilla Warfare
In 1816, the Spanish general Laserna was appointed as the commander-in-chief of Upper Peru. Laserna attempted to invade the Argentine provinces with the intention of marching on Buenos Aires. This offensive was completely foiled by the activity of irregular *gaucho* troops from Jujuy and Salta, forcing the Spanish general to retire.

In the aftermath of Laserna's failure, the patriots of Upper Peru transitioned to a strategy of guerrilla warfare. For six years, these forces took refuge primarily in the mountains of the province of Yungas, from which they frequently harassed the royalist troops. This period of irregular conflict continued until June 1823, when an expedition was prepared with great activity and zeal by General Santa Cruz.
