# Lake Baikal

## Overview and Geography
Lake Baikal is a lake located in East Siberia. It is the largest fresh-water basin in Eurasia and ranks as the sixth largest lake in the world. The lake stretches from the southwest to the northeast, situated between 51° 29' and 55° 50' N. latitude and 103° 40' and 110° E. longitude. Geographically, it serves as a separator between the government of Transbaikalia and the government of Irkutsk.

The lake's southern extremity extends into the high plateau of Asia, and the entire body of water is situated within the Alpine zone that fringes the north-west of that plateau. Baikal has a total area of 13,200 square miles, a size nearly equivalent to that of Switzerland. Its dimensions include a length of 386 miles and a width that varies from 20 to 50 miles. The coast-line measures 640 miles along the eastern shore and 525 miles along the western shore.

## Altitude and Water Levels
The altitude of Lake Baikal has been the subject of various estimates. Chersky estimated the altitude at 1,587 feet, while Suess estimated it at 1,679 feet. According to *Zapiski Russ. Geog. Soc.* xv. (1885), the lake is 118 feet above the level of the Angara at Irkutsk. However, a more correct altitude of 1,500 feet was suggested in *Izvestia East Sib. Branch*, xxviii. 1 (1897).

The water level of the lake is subject to slight oscillations. A notable instance occurred in 1869 following five weeks of heavy rain, during which the lake rose 7 feet, resulting in the submergence of an immense territory at the mouth of the Selenga.

## Nomenclature and Regional Context
The lake is known by different names among various regional groups; the Turkish tribes refer to it as *Bai-kul*, while the Mongols call it *Dalai-nor*.
