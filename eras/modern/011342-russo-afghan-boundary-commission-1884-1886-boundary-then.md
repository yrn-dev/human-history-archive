# Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886

The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission of 1884-1886 was a diplomatic and geographical undertaking established to determine a definite boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and the trans-Caspian conquests of Russia. This necessity arose from the Russian advance toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts.

## Boundary Demarcation and Geography
The commission fixed the northern limits of Afghanistan from the Hari Rud in the west to the Sarikol mountains in the east. The resulting boundary between the Oxus and the Hari Rud is described as being almost entirely artificial in its construction. 

Key geographical markers of this boundary include:
* **The Hari Rud:** The boundary leaves the Hari Rud at Zulfikar, located approximately 70 miles south of Sarakhs.
* **The Kushk:** The most southerly point of the boundary occurs where it crosses the Kushk, roughly 60 miles north of Herat.
* **The Oxus:** The boundary joins the Oxus at Khamiab, situated about 150 miles above the Russian Trans-Caspian railway crossing-point at Charjui. From this junction, the main channel of the Oxus river serves as the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia, and continues to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir.

## Mapping and Scientific Contributions
The work of the commission represented a significant extension of scientific geographical mapping in the region. The mapping of the Oxus regions and Afghan Turkestan was carried out by Sir T.H. Holdich, Colonel St George Gore, and Sir Adelbert Talbot. 

During this period, other explorers contributed to the geographical knowledge of the area. Ney Elias traveled from China through Badakshan and the Pamirs to reach the commission's camp, identifying the "Dragon Lake" (Rangkul) during his journey. Additionally, L. Griesbach conducted researches in the Oxus basin, reporting the existence of a longitudinal ridge or anticlinal extending through the great Balkh plain of Afghan Turkestan. Griesbach noted that this ridge effectively prevents the northern affluents of that basin from joining the river, providing evidence of a gradual process of upheaval.

## Regional Impact: Badghis
The commission surveyed the district of Badghis in 1885. Badghis, known as the "home of the winds," is located in north-west Afghanistan between the Hari Rud and Murghab rivers, extending north to the edge of the Sarakhs desert. The Russo-Afghan boundary runs through the Chul formations within this district. Following the survey, the amir largely settled the region with purely Afghan tribes.

## Context within Indian and Central Asian Frontiers
The Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission was part of a broader series of geographical efforts to map the frontiers of India, Baluchistan, and Persia. These efforts, which included the Afghan war of 1878-80 and the occupation of Chitral and Gilgit, resulted in professional maps extending from the Euphrates to the Indus and from the Indus to the Oxus. 

The 1884-1886 commission provided a major accession to the knowledge of Central Asiatic geography, complementing other contemporary explorations, such as the mission of Captain (later Sir William) Lockhart across the Hindu Kush into Wakhan and the journeys of Arthur Douglas Carey, Andrew Dalgleish, and Captain (later Sir Francis) Younghusband.

## Subsequent Boundary Adjustments
The work of the 1884-1886 commission was followed by further demarcations. In 1895, an Anglo-Russian Commission defined the line beyond Victoria Lake to the glaciers and snowfields overlooking the Chinese border. Afghanistan's southern and eastern boundaries were later defined by minor commissions based on the Kabul agreement of 1893, concluding with the Mohmand settlement in 1897.
