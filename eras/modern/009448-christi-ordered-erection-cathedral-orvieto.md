# Corpus Christi

The term Corpus Christi refers to a religious festival in the Catholic tradition, a city in the United States, and is associated with specific historical events and architectural commissions in Italy.

## The Religious Festival
The festival of Corpus Christi was instituted by Pope Urban IV to commemorate the "miracle of Bolsena" which occurred in 1263. This miracle involved a Bohemian priest who held skeptical views regarding the doctrine of transubstantiation. While consecrating the host, the priest was convinced of the doctrine's truth when drops of blood appeared on the host. In addition to establishing the festival, Pope Urban IV ordered the construction of the cathedral of Orvieto.

During the Reformation, the festival was suppressed in reformed Churches due to the rejection of the doctrine of transubstantiation. Martin Luther, despite his own belief in the Real Presence, abolished the festival, viewing it as the most harmful of all medieval festivals. This suppression was further intensified by the reformers' abhorrence of the traffic in indulgences that had become closely linked to the event.

Consequently, the festival became a means for adherents of the "old religion" to demonstrate Catholic orthodoxy. The procession of the Host on Corpus Christi day served as a public manifestation of faith against Protestantism and, in later periods, against religious Liberalism. Because of these tensions, the procession is currently forbidden in most countries where religious opinion is sharply divided, even if Catholicism is the dominant religion. In England, the Italian community in London has been tolerated in holding an annual procession. However, an attempt to hold a public procession of the Host in 1908 in connection with the Eucharistic Congress at Westminster was abandoned following the personal intervention of the prime minister.

## Corpus Christi, Texas
Corpus Christi is a city and the county-seat of Nueces county in Texas, U.S.A. It is located on Corpus Christi Bay, opposite the mouth of the Nueces river, approximately 150 miles south-southeast of San Antonio and 192 miles west-southwest of Galveston. The city was chartered in 1876.

The population of the city grew from 4,387 in 1890 to 4,703 in 1900 (which included 460 negroes and 963 foreign-born residents) and reached 8,299 by 1910. The city is served by several railways: the San Antonio & Aransas Pass, the St Louis, Brownsville & Mexico, and the National of Mexico.

Geographically, the city is a winter and summer resort characterized by a very dry, equable climate with an average annual mean temperature of 70.2 deg. F. It features the horseshoe beach of Corpus Christi Bay, which is popular for bathing. The city maintains an extensive coasting trade and exports oysters, fish, early vegetables, and fruit.

Historically, a small Spanish settlement existed in the area at an early date, though American settlement did not occur until after the Mexican War. In 1846, General Zachary Taylor used Corpus Christi as the base for his forward movement to the Rio Grande. In 1908, the Federal government began a project to create a channel 75 ft. wide at the bottom and 8-1/2 ft. deep at low water to connect Aransas Pass with the harbour of Corpus Christi, utilizing a natural depression between the two bays.
