# Officers' Quarters

Officers' quarters are a component of military and naval barracks, which are permanent structures of brick or stone—or sometimes corrugated iron hutments—used for the accommodation of forces. In military settings, the officers' quarters are typically situated at a distance from the barracks used by the men.

## The Officers' Mess
The officers' mess is generally located at a distance from the men's barracks and usually overlooks a cricket ground or the parade. The building's layout includes an entrance hall featuring a band alcove for guest nights. The mess-room (which serves as the dining room) and the anteroom (the reading room) are positioned on opposite sides of this hall. To allow for the arrangement of lantern lights, the kitchen and billiard room are located at the rear of the building.

The facility includes a mess office and various accessories for the mess-man's department and the mess waiters' department, such as a plate-closet, pantry, and cellarage. Additionally, quarters are provided for the mess-man.

## Living Accommodations
The quarters for officers are typically arranged in a story above the mess itself and in wings that extend the frontage of the mess building. Standard accommodation for an officer consists of a large room, with a portion partitioned off to serve as a bedroom. Field officers are allotted two rooms. Each officer is assigned a soldier servant, who is provided with a small room for cleaning purposes. Bathrooms are centrally located and receive hot water from the mess kitchen.

Specific housing is provided for high-ranking officials:
* **Commanding Officer:** Provided with a detached house containing a kitchen, servants' hall, bathroom, seven bed- and dressing-rooms, and three sitting-rooms.
* **Quartermaster:** Provided with a smaller house containing a kitchen, bath, four bedrooms, and two sitting-rooms.

Other married officers of the regiment are not provided with quarters and must arrange their own housing, though they are usually granted a lodging allowance.

## Naval and International Variations
In naval barracks, such as those at Devonport, Chatham, and Portsmouth, the officers' messes feature splendid public rooms. However, the officers' quarters in naval barracks are not as large as those found in military barracks; this is attributed to the naval officer's familiarity with the small cabins of shipboard life.

In other countries, the arrangement of quarters varies:
* **Germany and France:** Many barracks are constructed as a single large block of three or four storeys that houses all personnel, including officers, in one building. While accommodation for officers is not usually provided unless the barracks are on a country site, the German army provides every regiment with a fine officers' mess-house furnished at public expense.
* **United States:** American barracks typically follow a separate block system centered around a soldiers' club or post-exchange.

## Administrative and Support Facilities
In large military stations, further administrative quarters may be required, including offices for the brigade and district, as well as quarters for the headquarters staff and the general officer commanding. The upkeep of these buildings, along with the roads and pipes within the barracks, is maintained by the Royal Engineers.
