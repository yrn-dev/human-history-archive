# Colonel Charles George Gordon

Colonel Charles George Gordon was a British officer and explorer who conducted geographical research and expeditions in Central Asia. He is noted for his work with the exploring party detached by Sir Douglas Forsyth and for his contributions to the understanding of the Pamir region.

## Exploration and Natural History
During his expeditions, Colonel Gordon focused on the study of regional wildlife and geography. He successfully brought back the head of an *Ovis Poli* (known as Argali by the Mongols). According to a letter Gordon wrote to a friend, the specimen featured one perfect horn measuring 65½ inches on the curves, while the other, which was broken at the tip, measured 64 inches. The straight line between the tips of the horns was 55 inches. This finding supported previous accounts of the species having horns "good 6 palms in length," or approximately 60 inches. Gordon provided a full description of the *Ovis Poli*, including a large plate drawing of the horns, in his work titled *Roof of the World*.

## Geographical Research in the Pamirs
Colonel Gordon contributed significant linguistic and geographical data regarding the "Pamir" region. Based on information obtained from an intelligent guide on the Great Pamir, Gordon noted that the term "Pamir" appears to mean a wilderness—a place that is waste, abandoned, and depopulated, yet remains capable of habitation. 

He explained that in earlier times, when the area was inhabited by Kirghiz, the valley was not referred to as "Pamir" in its entirety. Instead, it was known by specific village names. This is contrasted with the country beyond Sirikol, which is currently occupied by Kirghiz and is known by names such as Bas Robát and Chárling. Gordon concluded that the designation "Pamir" is applied specifically when a place is deserted. This explanation was corroborated by Sir T. D. Forsyth, who received the same information at Yangi-Hissar and identified the term as a Khokandi-Turki word.

## Investigations into Bolor
Colonel Gordon also conducted inquiries into the geographical nomenclature of the region to the west of Yarkand. While some sources, including the Chinese traveller and Humboldt, referred to a place called "Bolor," Gordon sought to verify the existence of this name. He made repeated enquiries with Wakhis, Kirghiz, and Fatteh Ali Shah, the Mír of Wakhán. Gordon reported that all those questioned professed perfect ignorance of "Bolor" as a name for any place, country, or mountain.
