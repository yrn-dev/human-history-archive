# Sophus Bugge

## Early Life and Education
Sophus Bugge (1833–1907) was a Norwegian philologist. He was born on January 5, 1833, in Laurvik, Norway. His academic preparation took place across several cities, as he was educated in Christiania, Copenhagen, and Berlin.

## Academic Career
In 1866, Bugge attained the position of professor of Old Norse and comparative philology at Christiania University. Throughout his career, he authored a vast number of books focusing on the fields of folklore and philology.

## Research and Contributions
Bugge's scholarly work spanned several linguistic and cultural areas. He made significant contributions to the study of the following languages:
* Celtic
* Romance
* Oscan
* Umbrian
* Etruscan

Additionally, he wrote on Runic inscriptions and dedicated effort to the collection of Norwegian traditions and folk-songs.

## Principal Works and Theories
Bugge's primary work was a critical edition of the elder Edda, titled *Norroen Fornkvoedi*, which was published in Christiania in 1867. In his analysis, Bugge maintained that the earlier sagas and the songs of the *Edda* were largely based on Latin and Christian traditions that had been imported into Scandinavian literature via England.

His other published writings include:
* *Gamle Norske Folkeviser* (1858), a collection of Old Norse folk-songs.
* *Bidrag til den aeldste skaldedigtnings historie*, published in Christiania in 1894.
* *Helge-digtene i den Aeldre Edda*, published in Copenhagen in 1896 (later translated into English as *The Home of the Eddic Poems* in 1899).
* *Norsk Sagafortaelling op Sagaskrivning i Island*, published in Christiania in 1901.
* Various publications regarding Runic inscriptions.

Sophus Bugge died on July 8, 1907.
