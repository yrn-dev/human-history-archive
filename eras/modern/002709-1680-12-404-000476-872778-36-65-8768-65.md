# 1680

The year 1680 was marked by significant political transitions in Japan, legal and territorial developments in Scotland, and the deaths of several notable European figures.

## Political Transitions in Japan
In 1680, the Tokugawa Bakufu entered a new period beginning with the accession of the fifth shogun, Tsunayoshi. This transition occurred after the fourth shogun, Ietsuna, became dangerously ill. During a council of chief Bakufu officials to determine a successor, the prime minister, Sakai Tadakiyo, suggested following the example of Kamakura by inviting an Imperial prince to the office of shogun. This proposal was vehemently opposed by Hotta Masatoshi, a junior minister, who argued that the dignity belonged solely to Tsunayoshi, as he was the only brother of Ietsuna and the legitimate son of the late shogun, Iemitsu. Following this confrontation, Sakai Tadakiyo retired from the council chamber.

## Events in Scotland and England
In Scotland, 1680 saw the rise of Graham, a supporter of Charles II and an advocate for rigorous measures and calculated terrorism against ringleaders. Early in the year, Graham received a royal grant of the barony of the outlawed Macdougal of Freuch, which was later confirmed by orders upon the exchequer in Scotland. By April 1680, the privy council withdrew his roving commission. Consequently, Graham was not involved in the severe measures that followed the Sanquhar Declaration on June 22, 1680.

In England, the year marked the end of the life of William Bedloe, an informer born in Chepstow. Bedloe had gained notoriety in 1678 by providing the government with an account of a supposed popish plot and receiving £500 for his details regarding the murder of Sir E.B. Godfrey. Bedloe died in Bristol on August 20, 1680; his dying depositions, taken by Sir Francis North, the chief justice of the common pleas, were found to reveal nothing of importance.
