# Margaret Fuller

## Early Life and Education
Margaret Fuller, later known as the Marchioness Ossoli (1810–1850), was an American authoress born on May 23, 1810, in Cambridgeport, Massachusetts. She was the eldest child of Timothy Fuller (1778–1835), a politician and lawyer of some eminence. 

Fuller's education was managed by her father, who sought to accelerate her intellectual development. According to Fuller, this approach was a mistake, as it led to a "premature development of brain." While this made her a "youthful prodigy by day," it also left her vulnerable to somnambulism, nightmare, and "spectral illusions" by night. Despite these struggles, she began reading Latin at the age of six and developed a passion for reading. Her early favorite authors included Moliere, Cervantes, and Shakespeare. She eventually became familiar with the masterpieces of Spanish, Italian, and French literature, and in 1833, she began studying German, reading the works of Schiller, Novalis, Korner, and Goethe within a year.

## Literary Works and Memoirs
Fuller's literary legacy includes a variety of published works and posthumous collections. In 1855, her *Collected Works* were printed, and in 1860, a supplementary collection of poems and essays titled *Life Without and Life Within* was published in Boston.

Several biographical accounts and memoirs provide further insight into her life. These include the *Autobiography of Margaret Fuller Ossoli* (1852), which features additional memoirs written by W.H. Channing, R.W. Emerson, and J.F. Clarke. Other significant works include Julia Ward Howe's 1883 biography, *Margaret Fuller (Marchesa Ossoli)*, part of the "Eminent Women" series, and Thomas Wentworth Higginson's 1884 work, *Margaret Fuller Ossoli*, which was published in the "American Men of Letters" series and relied heavily on unedited material. Additionally, *The Love Letters of Margaret Fuller, 1845-1846* was published in 1903 in London and New York, featuring an introduction by Julia Ward Howe.
