# Lord Palmerston

Lord Palmerston was a prominent English statesman who served as the British foreign minister. His career was marked by his diplomatic approach to European affairs and his interactions with the British monarchy.

## Relations with the British Court
During his tenure as foreign minister, Palmerston encountered suspicion from Queen Victoria and Baron Stockmar. Stockmar, a servant and friend of King Leopold of Belgium, acted as the "keeper of the international conscience" for the Queen and her consort. This group, consisting of people from Saxe-Coburg-Gotha, held a generous enthusiasm for a liberal unity and well-being in Germany and were alarmed by the revival of Bonapartism.

In contrast to the Queen's circle, Palmerston was friendly with the "usurper" from the outset. He caused offense to Queen Victoria by sending amiable despatches to the French President without submitting them for her examination. This action deprived the Queen of the time necessary to consult with Stockmar, and as a result of these actions, Palmerston was obliged to resign.

## Cabinet and Later Career
Palmerston later led his own cabinet. In early 1855, Fox Maule Ramsay (the 2nd Baron Panmure) joined Lord Palmerston's cabinet, taking on the newly created office of secretary of state for war. Panmure held this position until February 1858, serving during the concluding period of the Crimean War.

## Historical Records
The life of the statesman was documented in a work titled *Life of Viscount Palmerston*. The first two volumes of this biography were published in 1870, with a third volume appearing four years later. However, the work remained a fragment as it broke off abruptly; it was eventually completed by Evelyn Ashley.
