# Hogarth, Bartolozzi, John Pine, George Vertue, and Landscape-Plates

## Allegorical Book-Plates
In the history of English book-plates, certain styles of composition were prevalent during specific periods. While many older and modern plates were essentially pictorial—including genres such as "book-piles" and "library interiors"—there was a specific tradition of allegories. Notable artists who engraved these allegorical plates include Hogarth, Bartolozzi, John Pine, and George Vertue. In these particular examples, the armorial element typically plays a secondary role to the pictorial composition.

## Francesco Bartolozzi
Francesco Bartolozzi (1725–1815) was an Italian engraver born in Florence. Although he was originally intended to follow his father's profession as a silver- and gold-smith, his skill in designing led him to study painting under two Florentine artists for three years. He later moved to Venice to study engraving, where he executed several works of importance, and spent a short time in Rome completing a set of engravings on the life of St Nilus.

In 1764, Bartolozzi moved to London, where he resided for nearly forty years. During this period, he produced a vast number of engravings, including contributions to Boydell's *Shakespeare Gallery*. His most highly regarded works include the *Virgin and Child* (after Carlo Dolce) and *Clytie* (after Annibale Caracci), with a significant portion of his output based on the works of Angelica Kauffmann and Cipriani. His art is described as graceful, though not entirely English. In 1802, he became the director of the National Academy in Lisbon, Portugal, where he remained until his death. His son, Gaetano Stephano (1757–1821), was also an engraver.

## William Hogarth and English Illustration
William Hogarth is recognized as the founder of many new conditions in both painting and illustration in England. His influence was followed by other distinct geniuses, such as Reynolds and Bewick. Hogarth's *Works*, which he engraved himself, were published with descriptions by J. Nichols in 1822 and contain 153 plates.

## Landscape-Plates and Wood-Engraving
Landscape-plates in the context of book-plates were produced by wood engravers of the Bewick school. Thomas Bewick is credited with the beginning of a wonderfully consistent development in English illustration. He was a practical inventor of the "white line" and "tint" methods of wood-cutting. 

Bewick's artistic approach focused on providing texture and local colour without the use of shadow, which ensured a precision of outline. This technique allowed him to introduce an element of colour into black-and-white illustration that had been absent from the Italian and German schools of the 15th and 16th centuries. Because of this method, some of his best designs possess the quality of a specimen plate.
