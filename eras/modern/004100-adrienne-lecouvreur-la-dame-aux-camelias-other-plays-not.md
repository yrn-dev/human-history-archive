# La Dame aux camélias and Related Dramatic Works

## La Dame aux camélias
*La Dame aux camélias* originated as a novel written in 1848 by a son of Dumas. The author, who had previously published a book of verse in 1847 titled *Peches de jeunesse*, wrote the novel as a reflection of the world in which he had been living. Following the success of the novel, the author produced *Le Roman d'une femme* (1848) and *Diane de Lys* (1851), though these works did not relieve him of an odious load of debt.

In 1849, the author dramatized *La Dame aux camélias*. However, the play could not be produced immediately, primarily due to the rigour of censorship. It was eventually produced at the Vaudeville on February 2, 1852, following the intervention of Morny, the all-powerful minister to Napoleon. The play achieved lasting success on the stage, attributed less to an inherent superiority over other plays than to the significant opportunities it provided for actresses of genius.

## Notable Performances and Interpretations
The play became a vehicle for several leading actresses of the nineteenth century. Sarah Bernhardt became famous in the role. Later, the Italian actress Eleanora Duse (1859– ) performed in Italian versions of the play during her chief triumphs in the 1880s and early 1890s. 

The interpretations of the play by Bernhardt and Duse highlighted a contrast in acting traditions. While Bernhardt represented the theatrical emotionalism of the French tradition, Duse avoided "make-up" and relied on poignant intellectuality, sympathetic force, and intense naturalness. This difference in approach led admirers of the two actresses to form two rival schools of appreciation.

## Wilson Barrett and the Court Theatre
In 1879, the English actor, playwright, and manager Wilson Barrett (1846–1904) took over the management of the old Court theatre. During his tenure there, he introduced Madame Modjeska to the London stage. The productions introduced through Modjeska included *La Dame aux camélias*, *Adrienne Lecouvreur*, an adaptation of Schiller's *Maria Stuart*, and other plays.
