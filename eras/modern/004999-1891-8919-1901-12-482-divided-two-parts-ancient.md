# Bridlington

Bridlington is a seaside resort, municipal borough, and market town located in England within the East Riding of Yorkshire. It is situated in the Buckrose parliamentary division, approximately 31 miles north-northeast of Hull, and is accessible via a branch of the North Eastern railway.

## Geography and Layout
The town is divided into two distinct parts. The ancient market town is located about one mile from the coast. In contrast, the modern area of Bridlington Quay, which serves as a watering-place, fringes the shore of Bridlington Bay. The coastline to the south of the town is low, while the coast to the north is steep and very fine, characterized by the eastward projection of the great spur of Flamborough Head.

## History and Architecture
The old town of Bridlington is home to the church of St Mary and St Nicholas. This structure includes Early English portions and a fine nave in the Perpendicular and Decorated styles, originating from an Augustinian priory church founded during the time of Henry I. The priory was established around 1114 by Walter de Gaunt. The institution flourished until 1537, at which time the last prior was executed for his involvement in the Pilgrimage of Grace. A Perpendicular gateway from this period remains and currently serves as the town-hall.

In terms of other religious history, a Congregational society was established in 1662. Its old church, which dated from 1702, remained until 1906.

## Amenities and Infrastructure
Bridlington Quay offers excellent sea-bathing facilities. The area features ornamental gardens and a parade that provide pleasant promenades for visitors. Extensive works have been carried out along the sea front, and the town possesses a chalybeate spring. The harbor is equipped with two stone piers and provides good anchorage within the bay.

## Administration and Population
The municipal borough of Bridlington covers an area of 2,751 acres. Its administration is conducted under a mayor, 18 councillors, and 6 aldermen. The population of the town has seen growth over the turn of the century, recorded at 8,919 in 1891 and increasing to 12,482 by 1901.
