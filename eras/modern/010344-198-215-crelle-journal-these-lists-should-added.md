# George Boole

## Overview and Public Reputation
George Boole was recognized by the public primarily as the author of several standard works and numerous abstruse papers focused on mathematical topics. His total body of work consists of a few separate publications and approximately fifty scattered articles.

## Early Work and Professional Relationships
Boole's first published paper, titled "Theory of Analytical Transformations," appeared in the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* in 1839. This publication led to a friendship with D.F. Gregory, the journal's editor, which continued until Gregory's premature death in 1844.

## Published Contributions and Journals
Boole contributed extensively to various scientific and mathematical journals:
* **Cambridge Mathematical Journals**: He wrote twenty-two articles for the *Cambridge Mathematical Journal* and its successor, the *Cambridge and Dublin Mathematical Journal*.
* **Philosophical Magazine**: He published sixteen papers across the third and fourth series of this magazine.
* **Royal Society**: The Royal Society printed six of his important memoirs in the *Philosophical Transactions*.
* **Other Publications**: His memoirs appear in the *Transactions of the Royal Society of Edinburgh* and the *Transactions of the Royal Irish Academy*. In 1862, a piece was published in the *Bulletin de l'Academie de St-Petersbourg* under the name G. Boldt (vol. iv. pp. 198-215). He also published in *Crelle's Journal*.

Additionally, in 1848, Boole published a paper in the *Mechanic's Magazine* regarding the mathematical basis of logic.

## Archival Records of Works
A comprehensive list of Boole's detached papers and memoirs on mathematical and logical topics is available in the *Catalogue of Scientific Memoirs* published by the Royal Society. Further records can be found in the supplementary volume on *Differential Equations*, which was edited by Isaac Todhunter.
