# The Madre de Dios River

The Madre de Dios, also known as the Mayu-tata, is a significant river located in northern Bolivia. It serves as the principal affluent of the Beni river, joining it approximately 120 miles above the Beni's mouth.

## Geography and Course
The Madre de Dios rises in the eastern Cordilleras, situated about 35 miles east of Cuzco. From its source, the river flows in a north-east and east direction through northern Bolivia. The region through which the river and the Beni traverse is characterized by heavy forests on the lower slopes of the Andes, particularly toward the north-west. This area is noted as one of the richest districts in Bolivia and possesses the capacity to sustain a large population.

## Tributaries and Navigation
The Madre de Dios is considered a more important river than the Beni itself in terms of its size and the length of its tributaries. Its primary tributaries include two large rivers, the Paucartambo and the Inambari, as well as the Tambopata, Marcapata, and Chandless.

Regarding navigation, the Madre de Dios is navigable during the wet season as far as the foot of the Andes, which is 180 miles from Cuzco.

## Regional Context
The river is part of a larger hydrographic system in north-eastern Bolivia. This region was once covered by the great Mojos Lake and currently includes the llanos of Mojos and Guarayos. This extensive area is traversed by several rivers and their tributaries, including the Beni, Mamore, Yacuma, Guapay, San Miguel, and Guapore. 

The surrounding landscape consists of low alluvial plains, flooded bottomlands, and great swamps. The region contains rich agricultural districts and open plains where cattle-raising has been practiced since the era of the Jesuit missions.
