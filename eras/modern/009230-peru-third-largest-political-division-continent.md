# Peru

## Early Conquest and Colonial Period
Peru was described as a "wonder-land" that was subjugated by Pizarro, who crossed the Isthmus of Panama in 1530. This conquest was part of a broader period of Spanish expansion in America, during which Cortez also conquered the Neolithic empire of Mexico. At the time, these events were viewed by Europe primarily as a means of providing a stimulating and useful influx of silver into the Spanish treasury. During the colonial era, the region was organized under the Spanish vice-royalty of Peru, which included the province of Charcas, also known as Upper Peru.

## Independence and Political Transition
The struggle for independence in the region involved prolonged conflict between royalist Spanish forces and South American patriot forces. In the area of Upper Peru, warfare occurred from July 1809 until August 1825, with significant action taking place between the shores of Lake Titicaca and the Argentine provinces of Jujuy and Salta. This period saw various shifts in power, including a revolt by Indians in the southern provinces of Peru, joined by numerous Creoles, which was eventually suppressed by royalists.

In September 1826, Bolivar departed from Lima, having entrusted the government to a council led by Santa Cruz. Bolivar's subsequent political movements in Bogota and Venezuela were later impacted by events occurring in Peru and the southern departments, which were described as striking at the foundation of his power.

## Regional and Geographic Classifications
In terms of biological and regional subdivision, some suggestions for the Neotropical region categorize part of Peru within the "Patagonian" subregion, while the rest of the continent and Central America are classified as "Columbian." Geographically, Peru shares a border with Bolivia to the east.

## The Chile-Peruvian War
Peru was involved in a conflict known as the Chile-Peruvian War. During this war, the Chilean naval officer Patricio Lynch led a destructive and brilliant naval raid. This was followed by the final campaign of Miraflores and Chorrillos in 1880, where Lynch led a division under Baquedano. Following the battle of Chorrillos, Lynch was appointed to command the Army of Occupation in Peru. During his tenure in this post, he placed the Peruvian president, Garcia Calderon, under arrest and eventually invested Iglesias with supreme power in Peru before returning to Chile in 1883.
