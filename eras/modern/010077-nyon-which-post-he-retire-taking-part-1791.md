# Nyon

In the historical records of the late 18th century, Nyon is noted as a location of administrative appointment for Swiss officials, specifically in the context of the Bernese bailiwick system.

## Administrative History and Charles Victor de Bonstetten

Nyon served as a post for the Bernese bailiffs. In 1887, Charles Victor de Bonstetten (1745–1832) was transferred to Nyon to serve in this capacity. Bonstetten was a member of a prominent patrician family from Bern and was characterized as a liberal patrician who was more French than Swiss, representing the Gallicized nature of 18th-century Bern.

Bonstetten's tenure in Nyon ended in 1791. His retirement from the post was the result of his participation in a festival held to celebrate the destruction of the Bastille. This event aligned with the liberal sentiments Bonstetten had imbibed during his earlier education in Geneva (1763–1766), where he had been influenced by Charles Bonnet and Rousseau.

## Context of the Era

The political climate surrounding the administration of Nyon during this period was heavily influenced by the French Revolution. Bonstetten's removal from his post in 1791 reflects the tension between the established Bernese authorities and the revolutionary ideals spreading through Europe. Following his time in Nyon, Bonstetten continued to hold various roles, including governing the Italian-speaking districts of Mendrisio, Locarno, Lugano, and Val Maggia for the Swiss Confederation between 1795 and 1797.
