# Jerome Bonaparte

Jerome Bonaparte (1784–1860) was a French prince and the king of Westphalia, known for his impetuous nature and his complex relationship with his brother, the emperor Napoleon. Born in Ajaccio on November 15, 1784, Jerome shared his family's fortunes during the early years of the French Revolution and received an education at Juilly. In 1800, he was called to join his brother, who was then the First Consul of France.

## Early Military Career and Naval Service
While serving in the Consular Guard, Jerome fought a duel with the younger brother of General Davout, during which he was wounded. Following this event, he was transferred to the navy. During his naval service, he cruised in the West Indies. While there, he was blockaded by a British cruiser, leading him to leave his ship and travel through the United States.

## Marriage and Conflict with Napoleon
While in Baltimore, Jerome fell in love with and married Miss Elizabeth Patterson. Because Jerome was a minor at the time, this marriage was viewed by Napoleon as a disregard for the laws of France and military discipline, which greatly annoyed the emperor. When Jerome brought his wife to Europe in 1805, Napoleon ordered that she be excluded from his states. Jerome attempted to change his brother's mind during an interview at Alexandria, but these efforts were in vain.

In May 1805, Jerome was given command of a small squadron in the Mediterranean. His wife remained in Camberwell, where she gave birth to a son. By November, Jerome was sailing in a squadron under the command of Admiral Willaumez with the intent to ravage the West Indies, though a storm eventually scattered the squadron. After damaging British commerce in the North Atlantic, Jerome returned to France safely in August 1806.

## Political Rise and the Kingdom of Westphalia
Upon his return, Napoleon named Jerome a prince of France and assigned him command of a division of South Germans for the 1806 campaign. Following the battle of Jena, Jerome accepted the surrender of several Prussian towns. 

Jerome's personal life shifted when an imperial decree annulled his marriage to Elizabeth Patterson. The emperor then united Jerome with Princess Catherine of Wurttemberg. Under the terms of the treaty of Tilsit, signed on July 7, 1807, Jerome was raised to the throne of the newly created kingdom of Westphalia.

## Governance of Westphalia
As king of Westphalia, Jerome was frequently rebuked by the emperor. He became known for his fondness for luxury, his numerous *amours*, and for accruing deep debts. Despite these personal tendencies, his connection to France brought certain benefits to the kingdom. Jerome introduced the *Code Napoleon*, abolished feudalism, and provided an impulse to higher education. Additionally, repressive laws affecting the Jews were removed.
