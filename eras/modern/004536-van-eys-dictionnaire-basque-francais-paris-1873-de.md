# W. J. Van Eys and Basque Linguistic Works

The scholarly study of the Basque language and its dialects has been documented through a variety of grammatical and lexicographical works. Among these contributions are the publications of W. J. Van Eys, who authored both a dictionary and a comparative grammar.

## Works of W. J. Van Eys
W. J. Van Eys produced two significant texts regarding the Basque language. The first is the *Dictionnaire basque-francais*, published in Paris in 1873. Following this, Van Eys published the *Grammaire comparee des dialectes basques* in 1879, which was released in Paris, London, and Amsterdam.

## Related Linguistic Authorities
The literature concerning the Basque language is described as restricted, though several other key authorities have contributed to the field. Julien Vinson provided a practically exhaustive resource in the *Essai d'une bibliographie de la langue basque* (Paris, 1891), which included a volume of corrections and additions in 1898. Vinson also authored *Les Basques et le pays basque* (1882), a survey intended for the general reader, and *Le Folk-Lore du pays basque* (1883), which covers the whole of the region's folk-lore and the Pastorales.

Other notable linguistic works include:
* **Early Vocabularies:** The first Basque vocabulary is found in *Le Codex de Saint-Jacques de Compostella*, lib. iv. (Paris, 1882), written by J. Vinson and R. P. F. Fita.
* **Grammars and Manuals:** Fl. Lecluse published the *Manuel de la langue basque* in Toulouse (1826), and L'Abbe Ithurry produced the *Grammaire basque* between 1895 and 1906.
* **Comparative and Analytical Studies:** Prince L. L. Bonaparte wrote *Le Verbe basque en tableaux* (London, 1864-1869). Dr H. Schuchardt authored *Die Entstehung der Bezugsformen des Baskischen* (Wien, 1893). C. Ribary's *Essai sur la langue basque* (1866) was translated into French by Julien Vinson in 1877. A. Oihenart's *Notitia utriusque Vasconiae* was published in Paris in 1638 and 1656.
* **Dictionaries:** In addition to the work of Van Eys, R. M. de Azkue published the *Diccionario vascongado espanol-francais* in Tours in 1906.

## Context of the Basque Provinces
The linguistic studies of these authors pertain to the Basque Provinces (*Provincias Vascongadas*), a division of north-eastern Spain. This area comprises the provinces of Guipuzcoa, Alava, and Biscay (also known as Vizcaya). As of 1900, the population of this region was 603,596 across an area of 2,739 square miles.

Geographically, the territory is a triangle bounded by the Bay of Biscay and France to the north, Navarre to the east, and the provinces of Logrono, Burgos, and Santander to the south and west. The French Pays Basque is situated within the arrondissements of Mauleon and Bayonne. Among the Spanish provinces, Guipuzcoa is the only one that is entirely Basque, while Alava is the least so.

Historically, the boundaries of the ancient tribes—the Vascones, Varduli, Caristi, and Autrigones—and their differing dialects likely corresponded more closely to the ancient dioceses of Calahorra, Bayonne, and Pamplona than to the current civil provincial divisions.

## Other Regional Studies
Beyond linguistics, the region has been the subject of anthropological and cultural study. T. de Aranzadi authored *El Pueblo Euskalduna. Estudio de Antropologia* (1889) and *Existe una raza Euskara? Sus caracteres antropologicos* (1905). Additionally, *La Tradition au pays basque* (Paris, 1899) serves as a collection of papers written by local authorities.
