# Governance of the Evangelical Church

The governance of the Evangelical Church, specifically within the context of the state of Baden, is structured under the authority of the grand-duke, who is a Protestant. The church is administered through a combination of a nominated council and a synod.

## The Governing Council and Synod in Baden
In Baden, the Evangelical Church is governed by a nominated council and a synod. The composition of the synod includes the "prelate," along with 48 elected members and 7 nominated members, who are drawn from both the clergy and the laity.

## General State Church Administration
Within the broader context of state churches in Germany, the sovereign continues to be regarded as the *summus episcopus*. The sovereign appoints a minister of public worship, who then nominates the members of the governing body. Depending on the region, this body may be known as the *Consistorium*, the *Directorium*, or the *Oberkirchenrath*. This council is responsible for managing ecclesiastical matters, including patronage and property.

While the central council handles these broader issues, individual parishes elect their own councils to manage parochial affairs, such as ecclesiastical assessments. These parish councils hold a legal status. The *Landessynode* is formed by delegates from these various parish councils. When consultation is required between the two bodies, the Synod and the *Consistorium* appoint committees to confer.

## Variations in Evangelical and Lutheran Organizations
Different Evangelical and Lutheran bodies maintain distinct organizational structures and memberships:

*   **Evangelical Lutheran Church of Prussia:** Also known as the Old Lutherans, this church was established in 1817 and received complete state recognition in 1845. Its affairs are managed by an *Oberkirchencollegium*, which consists of two lay members and four ordained members.
*   **State Evangelical United Church:** Known as the *Evangelische unirte Landskirche*, this state entity exists separately from various "free churches," such as the Evangelical Lutheran Free Church of Saxony, the Hermannsburg Free Church, and the Evangelical Lutheran Community in Baden.
*   **American Organizations:** In the United States, the Evangelical church exists in three separate organizations. The General Synod of the Evangelical Church of the United States (organized in 1820) utilizes a liberal interpretation of the *Augsburg Confession*. The General Council (organized in 1867) accepts the "unaltered" *Augsburg Confession* and uses other Lutheran symbols as explanatory. The Synodical Conference of North America (organized in 1872) requires its pastors to subscribe to all nine creeds found in the *Book of Concord*.

## Theological and Educational Context
The governance of these churches is influenced by a divergence in theology, where rationalist (liberal) and orthodox (conservative) views exist side by side. This is partly due to the fact that theology taught in universities is often divorced from the practical religious life of church members. In Baden, the government maintains total control over public education, which includes the Protestant university at Heidelberg.
