# BORGHESI, BARTOLOMMEO (1781-1860)

## Early Life and Education
Bartolommeo Borghesi was an Italian antiquarian born on July 11, 1781, at Savignano, located near Rimini. He pursued his higher education in Rome and Bologna. During his early career, Borghesi focused on the study of documents from the middle ages; however, this pursuit weakened his eyesight, leading him to shift his academic attention toward the fields of numismatics and epigraphy.

## Professional Career and Contributions
While residing in Rome, Borghesi was commissioned by Pius VII to catalogue and arrange several collections of coins, including those held by the Vatican. He gained a reputation as an antiquarian and numismatist through his contributions to various Italian archaeological journals.

Between 1818 and 1820, he produced a monumental work titled *Nuovi Frammenti dei Fasti Consolari Capitolini*. This publication was highly regarded by the learned world for providing positive bases for the chronology of Roman history.

## Later Life and Political Activity
Following the disturbances of 1821, Borghesi retired to San Marino. While he remained primarily an enthusiastic student of history, he also served for a period as the podesta of the small republic. He remained in San Marino until his death on April 16, 1860.

## Legacy and Posthumous Works
Before his death, Borghesi developed a plan to publish a comprehensive collection of all Latin inscriptions from the Roman world. This design was later adopted by the Academy of Berlin under the auspices of Mommsen, resulting in the creation of the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*.

Additionally, Napoleon III ordered the publication of a complete edition of Borghesi's works. This edition consisted of ten volumes; the first volume was published in 1862, and the full set was not completed until 1897.
