## Thomas Hill Green: Life and Career
Thomas Hill Green (1836–1882) was a prominent English philosopher and a primary representative of the Neo-Hegelian (or Neo-Kantian) school of thought. Born on April 7, 1836, in the village of Birkin in the West Riding of Yorkshire, he was the son of a rector and a descendant of Oliver Cromwell. Green was educated at home until the age of fourteen, after which he attended Rugby for five years. 

In 1855, Green entered Balliol College, Oxford, as an undergraduate and was elected a fellow of the college in 1860. He dedicated his subsequent career to university teaching, serving first as a college tutor and later, from 1878 until his death on March 26, 1882, as Whyte's Professor of Moral Philosophy at Oxford.

## Major Works and Contributions
The core of Green's positive constructive teaching is found in two primary works based on his professorial lectures: *Prolegomena to Ethics* and *Lectures on the Principles of Political Obligation*. Because these were not published during his lifetime, his views were initially known indirectly through an *Introduction* to the standard edition of Hume's works, co-authored with T. H. Grose (who died in 1906), which provided an exhaustive examination of empirical or "English" philosophy.

*Prolegomena to Ethics*, considered Green's most important treatise, was nearly complete in manuscript form at the time of his death. It was published the following year under the editorship of A. C. Bradley, with a fourth edition released in 1899. His other works were later compiled in a three-volume set edited by R. L. Nettleship. This collection included:
* **Volume I:** Reprints of Green's criticisms of Lewes, Spencer, and Hume.
* **Volume II:** Lectures on Logic, Kant, and the *Principles of Political Obligation*.
* **Volume III:** Miscellanies and a full memoir by the editor.

The *Principles of Political Obligation* was eventually published as a separate volume.

## Critical Reception and Influence
Green's Neo-Hegelianism has been the subject of various critical analyses. Andrew Seth (Pringle Pattison) provided a criticism of the school in *Hegelianism and Personality*. Other scholarly evaluations of Green's work appeared in the *Philosophical Review* (by S. S. Laurie in 1897), the *Academy* (by S. Alexander), and *Mind* (by Henry Sidgwick and A. J. Balfour in 1884).

Further studies on Green's philosophy and political teachings include:
* *Philosophy of T. H. Green* by W. H. Fairbrother (1896).
* *The Principles of State Interference* by D. G. Ritchie (1891).
* *The Service of the State: Four Lectures on the Political Teaching of T. H. Green* by J. H. Muirhead (1908).
* *English Rationalism in the XIXth Century* (vol. ii.) by A. W. Benn (1906).
