# Brewing in the 9th Edition of the Encyclopaedia Britannica

## Perspectives on Industry Development
In the 9th edition of the *Encyclopaedia Britannica*, the author of the article on Brewing held the view that the brewing industry had reached its maximum development. At the time of this assessment in 1875, the industry was producing approximately 25,000,000 barrels.

## Subsequent Growth and Production
Later data indicates that the industry continued to expand beyond the projections made in the 9th edition. The brewing trade in the United Kingdom eventually reached vast proportions, achieving a maximum production of 37,090,986 barrels in 1900. Although a steady decline followed that peak, production figures for 1905-1906 remained high at 34,109,263 barrels, which exceeded the production of any year prior to 1897.

## Industry Statistics (1905-1906)
By the period of 1905-1906, the scale of the industry was characterized by the following data:
*   **Market Participants:** There were 5,180 brewers for sale.
*   **Major Producers:** Messrs Guinness owned the largest brewery in the world, producing more than two million barrels and paying roughly one million sterling to the revenue. Three other firms each produced close to or more than one million barrels.
*   **Revenue:** For the year ending September 30, 1905, the exchequer received £13,156,053 in beer duty.
*   **Technical Specifications:** The average specific gravity of the beer produced during 1905-1906 was 1053.24.

## Materials and Consumption
The production of beer during the 1905-1906 period utilized a wide array of materials, including:
*   **Malt:** 51,818,697 bushels.
*   **Hops:** 62,360,817 lb.
*   **Sugar:** 2,746,615 cwt.
*   **Rice, flaked maize, and similar materials:** 1,348,558 cwt.
*   **Unmalted corn:** 125,671 bushels.
*   **Hop substitutes:** 49,202 lb.

Regarding trade, 520,826 barrels of beer were exported and 57,194 barrels were imported, figures which had remained nearly stationary for thirty years. The majority of production was consumed within England, which accounted for 28,590,563 barrels of the total consumption in 1905-1906, compared to 3,265,084 barrels in Ireland and 1,648,463 barrels in Scotland.

The financial scale of the liquor trade in the United Kingdom grew significantly over time. In 1871, Professor Leone Levi calculated the invested capital at £117,000,000; by 1908, this figure may have safely doubled. Additionally, a 1906 entry in the *Brewers' Almanack* estimated that capital invested specifically in limited liability breweries alone reached £185,000,000.
