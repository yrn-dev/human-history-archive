# The Hundred Days and the Career of François Barbe-Marbois

## The Hundred Days
The "Hundred Days" refers to the period during which Napoleon returned to France to reclaim power after eleven months of exile on the island of Elba. Having evaded the British ships monitoring his island, Napoleon reappeared at Cannes and conducted a triumphal procession to Paris, during which he walked on white Bourbon cockades. 

His return presented a dilemma for honest Frenchmen, who found themselves choosing between a restored "dull weight of old kingship" under the Bourbons and an adventurer who had betrayed the republic. Because the allies refused to consider further experiments in republicanism, much of France supported Napoleon. Upon his return, Napoleon professed to be a changed man, claiming he would respect the constitution régime and that there would be no more despotism.

## François Barbe-Marbois
François, Marquis de Barbe-Marbois (1745–1837), was a French politician born in Metz. His early career began under the old regime as the intendant of San Domingo. Following his return to France at the end of 1789, he served the revolutionary government. In 1791, he was sent to Regensburg to assist the French ambassador, de Noailles, in negotiations with the diet of the Empire regarding the possessions of German princes in Alsace and Lorraine.

Barbe-Marbois experienced periods of political instability. He was arrested upon his return from Regensburg on suspicion of treason, though he was later released. In 1795, he was elected to the Council of the Ancients. His moderate stance, particularly his opposition to the exclusion of nobles and the relations of *emigres* from public life, led to suspicions that he was a royalist, despite the fact that he pronounced a eulogy for Bonaparte following his success in Italy. He was arrested again during the *coup d'etat* of the 18th Fructidor (September 4, 1797) and transported to French Guiana.

## Service Under Napoleon
After being transferred to Oleron in 1799, Barbe-Marbois owed his liberty to Napoleon following the 18th Brumaire. He subsequently rose through the imperial administration, becoming a councillor of state and director of the public treasury in 1801, and a senator in 1802. 

In 1803, he negotiated the treaty for the cession of Louisiana to the United States, for which the First Consul rewarded him with a gift of 152,000 francs. His ascent continued in 1805 when he was named a count and a grand officer of the legion of honour. By 1808, he had become the president of the *cour des comptes*. In exchange for these favors, Barbe-Marbois addressed Napoleon with servile compliments.

## Transition and Later Career
Despite his previous servility, Barbe-Marbois assisted in drawing up the emperor's act of abdication in 1814. During the invasion of France by the allies, he declared to the *cour des comptes* that they had not been so free for a long time as they were in the presence of the foreigner in arms, who was united for the "most beautiful of causes." In June 1814, Louis XVIII confirmed him as president of the *cour des comptes* and named him a peer of France.

During the Hundred Days, Napoleon deprived Barbe-Marbois of his positions. Following this period, in August 1815, he was appointed minister of justice in the ministry of the duc de Richelieu, though he was unsuccessful in his attempts to gain confidence in that office.

## Other Figures During the Hundred Days
Several other individuals were involved in the events surrounding Napoleon's brief return:
* **Letizia**: Napoleon's mother joined him on Elba and was privy to his escape plans, returning to Paris during the Hundred Days.
* **Daru**: Having retired to private life after the first abdication in 1814, Daru aided Napoleon during the Hundred Days.
* **Joseph Bonaparte**: Napoleon's brother played an insignificant role during the Hundred Days. Four days after Waterloo, Napoleon urged Joseph to inspirit the Chamber of Deputies to encourage national resistance, but Joseph primarily focused on furthering the emperor's plans to escape to America.
* **Bignon**: He returned to Napoleon's service during the Hundred Days. After Waterloo, as minister of foreign affairs under the executive commission, Bignon signed the convention of July 3, 1815, which handed Paris over to the allies.
