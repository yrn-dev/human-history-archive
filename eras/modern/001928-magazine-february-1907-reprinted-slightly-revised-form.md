# Publications and Periodicals in the Archive

The Human History Archive contains records of various magazines and periodicals across diverse fields, including sports, natural history, astrology, and botany. These publications often served as official organs for associations or as venues for the initial publication of scholarly articles that were later reprinted in revised forms or collected into books.

## Sports Periodicals
In the early 20th century, the Badminton Association utilized specific publications as its official organs. Until October 1907, the official organ was *Lawn Tennis and Badminton*. This was succeeded in November 1907 by the *Badminton Gazette*. 

The archive notes a specific instance of cross-publication regarding the laws of the game: an article written by S. M. Massey appeared in the *Badminton Magazine* in February 1907. This piece was subsequently reprinted in a slightly revised form within the *Badminton Gazette* in November 1907.

## Natural History and Science
Periodicals have historically been used to disseminate zoological and botanical research. William John Broderip (1789–1859), an English naturalist and metropolitan police magistrate, contributed a series of zoological articles to *Fraser's Magazine*. These articles were later collected and reprinted in 1848 under the title *Zoological Recreations*, followed by *Leaves from the Note-book of a Naturalist* in 1852.

In the field of botany, *The Botanical Magazine*, published by Lovell, Reeve & Co, provided illustrations of plant life, including the fruit of the pine-apple (*Ananas sativa*) and Spanish moss (*Tillandsia usneoides*).

## Astrology and Specialized Journals
The archive's bibliography on the history of astrology mentions several periodicals used for the study of the subject's affinities with astronomy and popular belief. R. Garnett (writing as "A.G. Trent") published a piece titled "The Soul and the Stars" in the *University Magazine* in 1880. This article was later reprinted in 1893 in the work *Natal Astrology* by Dobson and Wilde.

## Military and Colonial Records
The writings of Sir Henry Yule demonstrate the use of journals and colonial magazines for military and regional reports. Yule published several "Notes on the Iron of the Kasia Hills" and "Notes on the Kasia Hills and People" in the *Jour. Asiatic Soc. Bengal* between July and December of 1842 and 1844. Additionally, his work *The African Squadron vindicated* was reprinted in the *Colonial Magazine* in March 1850, which then served as the basis for a French translation, *L’Escadre Africaine vengée*, published in the *Revue Coloniale* in May 1850.
