# Regensburg

Regensburg is a city located on the Danube river. Historically, it was classified as one of the Swabian free cities.

## Political and Administrative Role
Regensburg served as a site for the imperial diet. In 1700, the diet of Regensburg decreed that the Protestant states of Germany should adopt the Gregorian calendar and the Gregorian correction of the intercalation, moving away from the Julian calendar. In implementing this change, it was resolved that the paschal moon and the equinox would be determined through astronomical computation using the Rudolphine tables. This specific method was eventually abandoned in 1774 at the request of Frederick II, the king of Prussia, due to numerous inconveniences.

## Military History
During the Thirty Years' War, Regensburg was the target of an audacious *coup-de-main* on the Danube. In mid-winter—an event described as very rare for the 17th century—the Swedish field-marshal Johan Baner broke camp and united his forces with the French under the comte de Guebriant. They surprised Regensburg while the diet was sitting. However, the capture of the city was prevented solely by the break-up of the ice. Following this failed attempt, Baner was forced to retreat to Halberstadt.

## Navigation and Commerce
Regensburg is a significant point of navigation on the upper Danube. The river is navigable from Vienna to Regensburg, though the draught is somewhat lower than the limit between Turnu Severin and Vienna. Barges with a register of 600 tons can be towed from the lower Danube to Regensburg. 

The city has served as a hub for the transport of goods and passengers. The Societe Imperiale et Royale Autrichienne of Vienna, which began operations in 1830, transports passengers and goods between Regensburg and Galatz. Additionally, the South Danube German Company provides a combined service with the Rumanian State Navigation Company to transport goods from Pest to Regensburg. In 1898, the first consignment of Rumanian petroleum, conveyed in tank boats, arrived in Regensburg after a six-week voyage from Giurgevo; petroleum tanks were constructed in the city for the storage of this resource.
