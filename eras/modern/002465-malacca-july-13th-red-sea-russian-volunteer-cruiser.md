# The Stopping of the Malacca

## Incident Overview
On July 13th, 1904, the P. & O. steamer *Malacca* was stopped in the Red Sea by the Russian volunteer cruiser *Peterburg*. This event occurred during a period of significant tension between Great Britain and Russia, specifically regarding the exercise of the right of search over British ships by Russian warships.

## Diplomatic and Commercial Impact
The stopping of the *Malacca* caused a "storm of indignation" and contributed to great irritation within English commercial circles. This incident was part of a series of maritime confrontations during 1904. Shortly after the *Malacca* incident, on July 24th, the Vladivostok squadron sank the *Knight Commander*, which further intensified negative feelings. 

The tensions escalated further on October 23rd, when the Russian Baltic fleet fired upon the English fishing-fleet off the Dogger Bank in the North Sea. This particular event nearly resulted in war. The crisis was only mitigated on October 28th, when Mr. Balfour announced at Southampton that the Russian government had expressed regret and that an international commission would be established to investigate the facts and punish those responsible.

## Political Context in Great Britain
At the time of these maritime incidents, the British government, led by Mr. Balfour, was facing internal pressures regarding fiscal questions and army reform. Despite Mr. Chamberlain's desire for an early appeal to the electors, he maintained loyalty to Mr. Balfour. 

The government viewed a change in leadership as undesirable due to the critical nature of foreign affairs, which were being conducted by Lord Lansdowne. The administration feared that a "swopping of horses" during this period would be dangerous, as the Russo-Japanese War threatened to lead to further complications. Additionally, the government was engaged in important negotiations for the revision or renewal of a treaty with Japan. Consequently, the government sought to avoid a premature dissolution of parliament unless it lost its majority.
