# Punjnud and the Indus

The Punjnud and the Indus are significant watercourses in India, particularly associated with the geography of the Punjab and the province of Sind. The Indus, also referred to as the Sind, serves as a primary drainage system for various regions, including the highlands of Baluchistan and the plains of the Punjab.

## The Punjab and the Five Rivers
The Punjab is a country watered by five great streams that join the Indus. The name "Pendj-ab" is derived from Persian, meaning "five rivers." These five rivers are:
*   The Hydaspes (known as the Chelum, Behni, or Bedusta; Sanscrit: Vitashà).
*   The Acesines (the Chenab; Sanscrit: Chandrabhágâ).
*   The Hydraotes (the Ravey or Iraoty; Sanscrit: Irâvatî).
*   The Hyphasis (the Beyah; Sanscrit: Vepâsà).
*   The Satadru (the Sutledj; Sanscrit: the Hundred Streamed).

## Bahawalpur State
Bahawalpur (or Bhawalpur) is a native state of India located within the Punjab. It is characterized as the principal Mahommedan state in the Punjab, ranking second only to Patiala. The state stretches for more than 300 miles along the left bank of the Indus, the Punjnud, and the Sutlej. Geographically, Bahawalpur is bounded to the south by the Rajputana desert, and to the north and east by the Punjab and Sind.

## The Indus in Sind
The Indus is the central feature of Sind, which is described as the lower valley of the Indus. This region is very flat with scanty vegetation and relies entirely on irrigation for its productiveness. The plain of Sind is covered by wind-blown sand and alluvium.

### Climate and Environment
The climate of Upper Sind is characterized by extreme heat and dryness, resembling the sultry deserts of Africa. In Upper Sind, temperatures have been known to reach 130 degrees in the shade. In Lower Sind, the mean maximum temperature during the six hottest months is 98 degrees in the shade, and the water of the Indus reaches "blood heat." Unlike other coastal regions, the monsoon rains exert little influence in Sind, making travel easier there from June to October.

### Hydrography and Geography
The Indus supports various features and settlements:
*   **Manchar Lake:** Situated on the right bank of the Indus, this lake reaches a length of 20 miles and a breadth of 10 miles during inundations, covering an estimated 180 square miles.
*   **Kotri:** A town in the Karachi district of Sind located on the right bank of the Indus. It serves as a railway junction with a bridge crossing the river and has historically been a center for river transit trade and the headquarters of the Indus steam flotilla.
*   **Khairpur:** A native state in the Sind province of Bombay. The state is watered by five canals drawn from the Indus, as well as the Eastern Nara, which follows an old bed of the Indus. Khairpur town is located 15 miles east of the Indus.

## Regional Drainage and Connectivity
The Indus acts as a destination for drainage from the highlands of Baluchistan. North of the railway line from the Indus to Quetta, drainage either runs north to the Gomal or bursts eastwards through limestone hills to reach the Indus via the shortest transverse route. To the south, drainage from the Jalawan highlands of the Kalat plateau passes eastwards into the plains of Sind.
