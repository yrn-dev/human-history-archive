# Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache

The *Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache* is a scholarly work on the Sanskrit language authored by the German philologist Franz Bopp. Published in 1827, the text was the result of work Bopp had been engaged in since 1821.

## Authorship and Development
Franz Bopp (1791–1867) was born in Mainz and educated at the Lyceum in Aschaffenburg. His interest in the languages and literature of the East was stimulated by the lectures of Karl J. Windischmann, as well as the influence of G.F. Creuzer, J.J. Gorres, the brothers Schlegel, and specifically Fr. Schlegel's 1808 book, *Uber die Sprache und Weisheit der Indier*. 

To further his studies, Bopp traveled to Paris in 1812 under the sponsorship of the Bavarian government. During four years of study in Paris, he utilized the Sanskrit books from the Serampore and Calcutta presses and the extensive collection of manuscripts at the Imperial library, many of which had been brought from India by Father Pons in the early 18th century. He also associated with scholars such as L.M. Langles, S. de Sacy, A.L. Chezy, and Alexander Hamilton.

## Publication History
Following his initial research, Bopp published *Uber das Conjugationssystem der Sanskritsprache in Vergleichung mit jenem der griechischen, lateinischen, persischen und germanischen Sprache* in 1816. This early work established the trajectory of his subsequent philological research, focusing on tracing the common origin of grammatical structures among Sanskrit, Persian, Greek, Latin, and German.

In 1821, on the recommendation of Humboldt, Bopp was appointed to the chair of comparative grammar and Sanskrit at Berlin. It was during this tenure that he produced the *Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache* (1827). The work saw further iterations and related publications, including:
* A new edition written in Latin, which began in 1828 and was completed in 1832.
* A shorter version of the grammar published in 1834.
* A Sanskrit and Latin glossary compiled in 1830, with second and third editions (1847 and 1867) that incorporated cognate languages.

## Relation to Comparative Grammar
While the *Lehrgebaude* focused on the Sanskrit language, Bopp's primary activity centered on his *Comparative Grammar* (*Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen*). This larger work appeared in six parts between 1833 and 1852. 

The *Comparative Grammar* was preceded by a series of monographs in the *Transactions of the Berlin Academy* from 1824 to 1831 titled *Vergleichende Zergliederung des Sanskrits und der mit ihm verwandten Sprachen*. Bopp's objective in these comparative studies was threefold: to investigate the origin of grammatical forms, to trace phonetic laws, and to describe the original grammatical structure of the languages through intercomparison.
