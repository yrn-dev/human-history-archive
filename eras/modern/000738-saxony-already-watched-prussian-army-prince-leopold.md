# Military and Political History of Saxony and Related Campaigns

## The Silesian Campaign of 1741
In February 1741, the Austrians, under Count Neipperg, prepared to reconquer Silesia. During this period, the Prussians, led by Prince Leopold (the younger) of Anhalt-Dessau, executed the storming of Glogau on the night of March 9. The operation was completed in one hour and was noted for its mathematical precision. 

The conflict progressed into a war of manoeuvre between Neisse and Brieg. On April 10, 1741, the first pitched battle fought by Frederick and his army took place at Mollwitz. Although the Prussian right wing of cavalry was quickly routed, the infantry's tenacity and discipline secured the field, as the Austrian infantry gradually melted away despite the efforts of Neipperg and his officers. Frederick himself had participated in the cavalry melee but was persuaded by Field Marshal Schwerin to ride away when the battle seemed lost, narrowly avoiding capture by Austrian hussars. Following the victory, Frederick secured Brieg, and Neipperg retreated to Neisse. This "Silesian adventure" eventually evolved into the War of the Austrian Succession after France sent Marshal Belleisle to negotiate an alliance with Frederick.

## Prussian Advances and the Shift in Alliances
During subsequent operations, Prussian forces advanced via three columns: one through Lusatia, one through Silesia, and another through Saxony. This movement led to the reaching of Prague on September 2 and the subsequent surrender of the Austrian garrison eight days later. 

However, the political landscape shifted as diplomatists won Saxony over to the Austrian side. Frederick eventually found himself isolated and exposed to the combined attacks of the Saxons and Austrians. Under the pressure of the united forces of Prince Charles and Marshal Traun, Frederick was outmanoeuvred and forced to retreat from Bohemia back to Silesia with heavy losses, although the Austrians failed to gain a foothold within Silesia itself.

## The Campaign of 1745
By January 8, 1745, a Quadruple Alliance was concluded at Warsaw between Saxony, Austria, Holland, and England. This left Frederick isolated once more, as no assistance was available from France, which was focused on the Flanders campaign. 

In May, Frederick established a camp at Frankenstein with approximately 65,000 men. He faced Prince Charles, who commanded 85,000 Austrian and Saxon troops behind the Riesengebirge. On June 4, the battle of Hohenfriedberg (or Striegau) occurred, resulting in a complete defeat for Prince Charles and marking one of the greatest victories of Frederick's career. The remainder of the summer was characterized by manoeuvres between the two leaders on the upper Elbe.

## Regional Geography and Administration in Prussian Saxony
The archive identifies several key locations within Prussian Saxony. Lützen, located in the circle of Merseburg, is noted as the site of a major battle on November 6/16, 1632, between the Imperialists under Wallenstein and the Swedes under King Gustavus Adolphus. Burg is a town on the river Ihle known for boot-making and cloth manufactures. Originally belonging to the lordship of Querfurt, Burg passed to the archbishops of Magdeburg in 1496, was ceded to Saxony in 1635, and finally ceded to Brandenburg in 1687.

Additionally, the archive notes Corvey in the Prussian province of Westphalia. Founded around 820 by emperor Louis the Pious, it became a center of Christianity in Saxony. The bones of St Vitus, the patron saint of Saxony, were reportedly moved there in 836. The abbey was secularized in 1803, and its lands were given to Prussia in 1815.
