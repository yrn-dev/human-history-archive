# La Robe rouge

*La Robe rouge* is a play written by the French dramatist Eugène Brieux and produced in 1900. The work was specifically directed against the injustices of the law.

## Author and Artistic Intent
The play was authored by Eugène Brieux (born January 19, 1858, in Paris), who was born to poor parents. Brieux's body of work is characterized as essentially didactic, with his plays designed to target specific iniquities or weaknesses within the social system. 

## Context within Brieux's Works
*La Robe rouge* is part of a broader series of social commentaries written by Brieux between the late 19th and early 20th centuries. His other works addressed various societal issues, including:
* **Education and Class:** *Blanchette* (1892) examined the evil results of the education provided to girls of the working classes, while *Les Trois Filles de M. Dupont* (1897) studied the miseries that the French system of dowry imposed on poor middle-class girls.
* **Political and Social Corruption:** *L'Engrenage* (1894) focused on political corruption, and *M. de Réboval* (1892) was directed against pharisaism.
* **Social Customs and Beliefs:** *Les Bienssaiteurs* (1896) targeted the frivolity associated with fashionable charity, and *L'Évasion* (1896) satirized an indiscriminate belief in the doctrine of heredity.
* **Labor and Domestic Life:** *Le Résultat des courses* (1898) illustrated the evil results of betting among Parisian workmen, and *Les Remplaçantes* (1901) addressed the practice of putting children out to nurse.
* **Other Studies:** *Petite amie* (1902) described the life of a Parisian shop-girl. Brieux also wrote *Les Avariés* (1901), which was read privately at the Théâtre Antoine after being forbidden by the censor due to its medical details.

Brieux's early career began with a one-act play titled *Bernard Palissy*, written with M. Gaston Salandri and produced in 1879. However, he did not obtain another hearing for eleven years until *Ménage d'artistes* was produced by Antoine at the Théâtre Libre in 1890. His later works include *La Couvée* (1903), *Maternité* (1904), *La Déserteuse* (1904)—written in collaboration with M. Jean Sigaux—and the three-act comedy *Les Hannetons* (1906).
