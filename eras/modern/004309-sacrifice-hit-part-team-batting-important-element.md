# Sacrifice-Hit

The sacrifice-hit is a specific element of team batting in base-ball, which is defined as the co-operation between a base-runner and a batsman. In close games, the sacrifice-hit is considered an important strategic component of this team play.

## Definition and Mechanics
A sacrifice-hit occurs when there is a runner on base and the batsman hits the ball in a manner that results in the batsman's own retirement, but allows the runner to advance to the next base. 

There are two primary methods of executing a sacrifice-hit:
*   **The Bunt:** This is the most frequent form of a sacrifice-hit. A bunt involves the batsman blocking the ball and allowing it to rebound from a loosely held bat. This method is preferred because it provides the batsman with the best opportunity to reach first-base safely while ensuring the advancement of the runner.
*   **The Long Fly:** A sacrifice-hit can also be achieved by hitting a long fly ball to the outfield. In this scenario, a runner on third-base (or any other base) must remain on that base until the ball is caught. However, because the distance from the home-plate to the outfield is so great, a fast runner can generally score their run before the ball is returned.

## Fielding the Sacrifice-Hit
When a batsman bunts, the ball commonly rolls slowly toward the third-baseman. To prevent the runner from advancing or to put the runner out, the third-baseman must run toward the batsman, pick up the ball—usually with one hand—and "snap" it to the first-baseman. A "snap" is an underhand throw performed without the player taking the time to return to an erect position. Additionally, the pitcher or the catcher may field bunts that drop dead in front of the home-plate.

## Scoring and Records
In the official base-ball score, which is recorded in vertical columns, sacrifice-hits are tracked as a specific statistic. The score records how many sacrifice-hits a player has made. However, when calculating the batting record of a player—which is found by dividing the number of hits made by the number of times at bat—sacrifice-hits are not counted as times at bat.
