## Honoré de Balzac and the Comédie humaine
Honoré de Balzac (1799–1850) was a French novelist born in Tours. He is best known for his massive literary project, the *Comédie humaine*, which he began organizing into a huge structure in 1842. This work is divided into several sections, including *Scenes de la vie parisienne* and *Etudes philosophiques*. 

Balzac's approach to writing was characterized by "furious energy." He often worked from midnight until the following day, sometimes in stretches of sixteen hours, continuing this process for weeks. He was known to constantly alter and reshape his work, frequently expanding small printed originals into long novels during the proofreading stage.

## Literary Output and Chronology
Balzac's career began with a "garret-period" from 1820 to 1822, during which he wrote tragedies (such as *Cromwell*) and early prose fiction. His early novels, known as *Oeuvres de jeunesse*, were often published under pseudonyms like "Lord R'hoone" or "Horace de Saint Aubin" and were sometimes written in collaboration with other writers.

Following this period, Balzac produced a vast array of works. Notable publications include:
* **1830–1831:** *La Peau de chagrin*, which centers on the "Nemesis of accomplished desire," and *Le Chef-d'oeuvre inconnu*.
* **1832:** *Louis Lambert*, described as philosophic and autobiographical, and *Le Colonel Chabert*.
* **1833:** *Eugenie Grandet*, considered a universally admitted masterpiece, and *L'Illustre Gaudissart*.

In addition to his novels, Balzac wrote political pamphlets, miscellaneous articles, and several dramas, including *Mercadet le faiseur*, which found some success after his death.

## Biographical Context
Balzac's life was marked by a tension between his vocation as a man of letters and a recurring "fancy for business." He engaged in several disastrous business ventures, including working as a printer, type-founder, and publisher, which left him with a significant load of debt.

His personal life was dominated by his long-term relationship with Madame Hanska. After a prolonged engagement and various meetings across Europe in cities such as Rome, Brussels, and Baden, the two were married on March 14, 1850, at her estate in the Ukraine. Balzac's health had been severely ruined by his intense labor and the Russian cold. He died on August 17, 1850, in Paris.

## Critical Reception and Study
The study of Balzac's work evolved significantly after his death. Early examinations by contemporaries like Sainte-Beuve and Gautier were often biased by personal friendship or enmity. More objective, comprehensive studies began with Taine's Essay in 1858. 

The publication of the *Edition definitive* allowed for a more complete analysis of his work. Subsequent scholars have focused on different aspects of his legacy:
* **Paul Flat:** Focused on tracing types of character.
* **Edmond Bire:** Provided new biographical details and clarified the spelling of the author's name.
* **A. Cabanes:** Investigated Balzac's interest in occult studies.
* **A. Le Breton and Ferdinand Brunetiere:** Produced critical examinations of the man and his oeuvre in the early 20th century.
