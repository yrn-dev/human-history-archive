# Saxony and Sardinia

## Saxony in Medieval and Early Modern Europe
Saxony is associated with various political figures and entities throughout European history. In the 11th century, King Henry II of Saxony provided military aid to Pope Benedict VIII, assisting him in defeating an anti-pope named Gregory. Following this victory, Henry II was crowned emperor in 1014. 

In later periods, the region was represented by the Elector of Saxony. During the 18th century, Saxony became involved in the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). Following the Prussian victory at the Battle of Mollwitz on April 10, 1741, Saxony joined other powers—including France, Bavaria, and Spain—that were ambitious to expand at the expense of Austria. However, the archive also notes that Saxony served as one of the most important allies to Austria at various intervals during this conflict.

## Sardinia and Early Settlements
Sardinia's history includes periods of external settlement and strategic involvement in European conflicts. During the pontificate of Pope Benedict VIII (1012–1024), Saracens began attacking the southern coasts of Europe and established a settlement in Sardinia.

By the 18th century, Sardinia's role shifted toward the geopolitical struggles of the War of the Austrian Succession. Like Saxony, Sardinia's allegiances fluctuated; it was identified as one of the most important allies of Austria from time to time, yet it was also listed among the powers, alongside France, Spain, Bavaria, and Saxony, that sought expansion at Austria's expense following the 1741 victory at Mollwitz.

## The War of the Austrian Succession
The involvement of both Saxony and Sardinia is closely tied to the War of the Austrian Succession, which began in 1740 when Frederick II of Prussia invaded Silesia. This conflict saw nearly all European powers involved after 1741, primarily centered on the struggle between Prussia and Austria for control of Silesia. 

While France and Prussia were constantly allied with Bavaria and Spain, and Austria was supported by Holland and England, Saxony and Sardinia acted as intermittent allies to the Austrian cause. The war eventually concluded with the peace of Aix-la-Chapelle (Aachen) in 1748. The resulting treaties saw Silesia transferred from Austria to Prussia via the treaty of Dresden, and cessions made in Italy at the expense of the house of Habsburg.
