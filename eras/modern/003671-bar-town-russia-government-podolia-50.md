# BAR

## Geography and Population
Bar is a town located in Russia within the government of Podolia. It is situated on an affluent of the Bug river, approximately 50 miles northeast of Kamenets. As of 1897, the town had a population of 10,614.

## History and Nomenclature
The town was formerly known as Rov. Its current name, Bar, was bestowed by Bona Sforza, the consort of Sigismund I of Poland, in memory of Bari in Italy, where she was born. Bona Sforza rebuilt the town following its destruction by the Tatars in 1452.

The town's administration changed several times over the centuries. From 1672 to 1699, Bar remained in the possession of the Turks. In 1768, the town served as the site where a confederation of Polish nobles was formed against the Russians. Shortly after the formation of this group, the town was taken by storm. Bar did not become finally united to Russia until the partition of 1793.

## The Confederation of Bar
In 1768, a famous confederation of the gentry and nobles of Poland was established at the little fortress of Bar in Podolia. The primary objective of this group was to defend the external and internal independence of Poland against the aggressions of the Russian government, which was represented in Warsaw by Prince Nicholas Repnin. The individuals responsible for originating the confederation were Michael Krasinski, Osip Pulawski, and Adam Krasinski, the bishop of Kamenets.

While King Stanislaus initially attempted to mediate between Russia and the confederates, he eventually found this impossible. He dispatched a force under two generals and the grand hetman Ksawery Branicki, who successfully captured Bar. Despite this, the confederation extended throughout Lithuania and the eastern province of Poland, aided by a simultaneous outbreak of a *jacquerie* in Little-Russia.

## Military and Diplomatic Struggles
The confederates sought foreign assistance, which contributed to the outbreak of war between Turkey and Russia. The situation became so severe that Catherine was advised by Frederick II to reach terms with the confederates. Under the leadership of Prince Charles Radziwill, Michael Pac, and Ignaty Malchewsky, the confederate bands won several engagements against the Russians and ravaged the land. Ignoring their king, they sent their own envoys to the principal powers of Europe.

In 1770, the Council of the Confederation moved its seat from Silesia to Hungary to conduct diplomatic negotiations with Turkey, Austria, and France to form a league against Russia. The court of Versailles appointed Dumouriez as the commander-in-chief of the confederates. However, Dumouriez did not distinguish himself as a politician or soldier. He committed a significant blunder by labeling King Stanislaus a traitor and a tyrant at a time when the king was nearly ready to accede to the Confederation. This caused the king to return to the Russian faction, resulting in the Confederation losing the confidence of Europe. Despite this, the army, which Dumouriez had thoroughly reorganized, continued a hopeless struggle for several years.
