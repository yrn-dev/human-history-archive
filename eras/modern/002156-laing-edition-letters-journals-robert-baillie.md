# Laing's Edition of the Letters and Journals of Robert Baillie

## Overview of the Publication
The *Letters and Journals of Robert Baillie* (1637-1662) is a three-volume work edited by D. Laing. Published in Edinburgh between 1841 and 1842, the edition was produced for the Bannatyne Club. This collection is noted for providing a full notice of the writings of Robert Baillie, as well as a complete memoir of his life.

## Subject of the Edition: Robert Baillie
The edition focuses on the life and works of Robert Baillie (1602-1662), a Scottish divine born in Glasgow. Baillie graduated from Glasgow in 1620 and dedicated himself to the study of divinity. His career included several prominent roles:
* **Ecclesiastical and Academic Appointments:** After his ordination, he served as a regent at the university before being appointed to the living of Kilwinning in Ayrshire in 1631. In 1642, he became a professor of divinity at Glasgow, and by 1661, he was appointed principal of Glasgow University.
* **Political and Religious Involvement:** Baillie was a member of the Glasgow Assembly in 1638 and served as a preacher or chaplain accompanying the Scottish army and Leslie. In 1643, he was one of five Scottish clergymen selected for the Westminster Assembly. In 1649, he served as a commissioner to Holland to settle the terms of admission for Charles II to the Scottish government.

Baillie is described as a man of ability and learning whose views were not extreme. While he played a secondary role in the major events of his era, he remained active in the various minor disputes of the church. He died in August 1662, a death likely accelerated by his mortification regarding the apparent firm establishment of episcopacy in Scotland.

## Historical Significance and Contents
The *Letters* of Robert Baillie are regarded as being of first-rate historical importance, as they provide a lively depiction of the period in which he lived. In addition to the letters and journals, the archive notes several other works authored by Baillie:
* *Ladensium* [Greek: *autokatakrisis*], which served as a response to *Lysimachus Nicanor*. The latter was an attack on the system and person of Laud, responding to claims that the Covenanters were associated with Jesuitry.
* *The Life of William (Laud) now Lord Archbishop of Canterbury Examined*, published in London in 1643.
* *A Parallel of the Liturgy with the Mass Book, the Breviary, the Ceremonial and other Romish Rituals*, published in London in 1661.
* A sermon titled *Anabaptism, the true Fountain of Independency, Brownisme, Antinomy, Familisme, &c.*
* *An Historical Vindication of the Government of the Church of Scotland*.
