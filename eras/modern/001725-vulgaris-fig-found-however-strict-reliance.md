# Vulgaris (Species Designation)

The term *vulgaris* is utilized across various biological classifications in the archive's records to designate a wide array of species, ranging from microscopic bacteria and fungi to plants, fish, and prehistoric fossils.

## Botanical Species
Several plant species carry the *vulgaris* designation, spanning diverse forms such as cacti, grasses, and insectivorous herbs.

**Opuntia vulgaris**
Also known as the Indian fig cactus or prickly pear, *O. vulgaris* is part of a large group of approximately 150 species found in the West Indies, North America, and warmer regions of South America, reaching as far as Chile. These fleshy shrubs feature rounded, woody stems and succulent branches. The branches are typically composed of compressed, often suborbicular or elliptic joints, which are dotted with small, caducous leaves in spiral lines. Within the axils of these leaves are areoles containing hooked or barbed spines. The species produces yellow or reddish-yellow flowers followed by egg-shaped or pear-shaped fruits with a soft, fleshy rind and tufts of small spines. The fruits of *O. vulgaris* are esteemed for their cooling properties and are eaten as prickly pears. This species is cultivated for its fruit in northern Africa, the Canaries, and Southern Europe, and it is hardy in the south of England.

**Bambusa vulgaris**
*Bambusa vulgaris* is a type of bamboo that grows to a height of 20 to 50 feet. In Oriental countries, the plant is used extensively. Its soft, succulent shoots are harvested as they begin to spring and served as a vegetable, similar to asparagus. These shoots are sometimes earthed over to extend their fitness for consumption, or they are preserved as candied treats in sugar, salted and eaten with rice, or prepared as pickles. As the plant matures, a fluid is secreted in the hollow joints, leading to the development of a siliceous concretion known as *tabascheer* or *tabaxir*. While this substance was highly valued in the East for medicinal qualities and possesses peculiar optical properties, the bamboo itself is considered inert as a medicinal agent and is not included in the European materia medica.

**Pinguicula vulgaris**
Commonly called butterwort, *P. vulgaris* is a small insectivorous herb that grows in boggy, wet land. It is characterized by a rosette of pale, fleshy, oblong leaves (1 to 3 inches long) that are appressed to the ground. The leaves have a sticky surface that captures small insects. A digestive enzyme within the viscid excretion renders the insect's nitrogenous substances soluble for absorption by the leaf. The leaves possess two sets of glands: smaller, almost sessile glands and larger ones borne on usually unicellular pedicels. Upon capturing a fly, the excretion becomes strongly acid, and the leaf margins curve further inward to ensure complete contact with the insect. This plant is distributed throughout the north temperate zone and extends into the arctic zone.

## Microorganisms and Fungi
The designation *vulgaris* is also applied to bacteria and fungal structures.

**Bacillus (Proteus) vulgaris**
*Bacillus vulgaris* (also associated with the name Hauser and Migula) is a form of bacteria. Observations of this variety in a hanging drop demonstrate the stages of colony formation: a single rodlet appearing at 11 A.M. grows and divides into eight rodlets by 4 P.M., with further development continuing through the evening.

**Berberis vulgaris**
In the study of *Puccinia graminis*, the leaf of *Berberis vulgaris* is noted for containing spermogonia and aecidium fruits, as well as a peridium.

The term is further used to identify specific animal species and fossilized organisms.

**Lota vulgaris**
*Lota vulgaris*, known as the burbot, is a fish characterized by the presence of jugular ventral fins.
