# Ministry of Finance

The term "Ministry of Finance" refers to various governmental departments across different nations and eras responsible for the management of state funds, budgets, and debt.

## The Dual Monarchy (Austria and Hungary)
In the Dual Monarchy, the Ministry of Finance was one of three joint ministries established to administer common affairs, alongside the Ministry of War and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The authority of the joint ministers was strictly limited to these common affairs; they were prohibited from exercising influence over government matters that affected only one half of the monarchy.

### Responsibilities and Administration
The Minister of Finance was tasked with the following duties:
*   Managing the finances of common affairs.
*   Preparing the joint budget.
*   Administering the joint state debt.
*   Administering the provinces of Bosnia and Herzegovina (until 1909), except for matters that fell under the exclusive jurisdiction of the Minister of War.

To ensure oversight of these common finances, a joint supreme court of accounts was appointed to audit the accounts of the joint ministries.

### The Joint Budget
The Dual Monarchy maintained a common budget alongside the individual budgets of each state. This joint budget covered expenditures for the army, foreign affairs, and the Ministry of Finance. Revenues for this budget were derived from the joint ministries, net proceeds from customs, and proportional contributions (the quota) from the two states.

The quota for these contributions was fixed for several years, typically coinciding with the duration of the commercial and customs treaty. The distribution of these contributions shifted over time:
*   **Until 1897:** Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30% of the joint expenditure remaining after common revenue deductions.
*   **1897 to July 1907:** The quota was adjusted to 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary.
*   **1907:** Hungary's contribution was increased to 36.4%.

Additionally, 2% of total charges were debited to Hungary due to the incorporation of the former military frontier into that state.

### Financial Data (1875–1905)
Expenditure for the Ministry of Finance showed a gradual increase over three decades, rising from £154,200 in 1875 to £177,100 in 1905. In the 1905 budget estimates, the Ministry of Finance provided £4,870 in revenue. During this same period, the general debt borne jointly by Austria and Hungary decreased slightly from £232.41 million in 1875 to £224.31 million in 1905.

## France
In French history, the role of the finance minister has been held by several prominent statesmen.

### Nicolas Desmarets
Nicolas Desmarets (1648–1721) served as a financial expert and statesman under Louis XIV. After serving as an intendant of finances in 1678 and later providing economic memoirs to controllers-general, he was appointed as a director of finances in 1703. He eventually succeeded Chamillart as the minister of finance in 1708. Desmarets faced a grave financial situation upon taking office; in 1708, ordinary revenues were 81,977,007 livres, while expenses to meet were 200,251,447 livres. He was recognized for his honesty and talent, being regarded as the greatest finance minister of Louis XIV after Colbert.

### Baron Louis
Joseph Dominique Louis (1755–1837) was a financier and statesman who studied the financial system of Pitt while in England. He served as the Minister of Finance during several periods:
*   1814–1815.
*   1815 (resuming the portfolio).
*   1818 (during the Decazes ministry).
*   Under the government of Louis Philippe, where he served as the first minister of finance.
*   1831–1832.

## Denmark
In Denmark, the Ministry of Finance became a focal point of constitutional struggle between the two chambers of the Rigsdag: the Folketing (Lower House) and the Landsting (Upper House). In 1872, the "United Left" rejected the budget and passed a vote of want of confidence against the government. When J. B. Estrup became prime minister in 1875, he retained the Ministry of Finance in his own hands, anticipating that the political conflict would be primarily financial. He used this position to strengthen national armaments and establish political parity between the Landsting and the Folketing.
