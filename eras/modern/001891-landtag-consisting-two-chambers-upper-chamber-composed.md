# Landtag of Baden

The *Landtag* is the representative assembly of Baden, which shares legislative authority with the grand-duke. The government of Baden is structured as an hereditary monarchy where executive power is vested in the grand-duke. The *Landtag* is organized into two distinct chambers.

## The Upper Chamber

The upper chamber of the *Landtag* is composed of a diverse group of appointed, hereditary, and elected members. Its composition includes:

*   **Royalty and Nobility:** All princes of the reigning family who have reached full age, the chiefs of the mediatized families, and eight members elected by the territorial nobility for four-year terms.
*   **Religious and Academic Leaders:** The archbishop of Freiburg, the president of the Protestant Evangelical church, and a deputy representing each of the universities as well as the technical high school.
*   **Economic and Civic Representatives:** Three representatives from the chamber of commerce, two from the chamber of agriculture, and one from the chamber of trades. Additionally, the chamber includes two mayors of municipalities, one burgomaster of lesser towns, and one member of a district council.
*   **Grand-Ducal Appointments:** Eight members nominated by the grand-duke, two of whom must be legal functionaries.

## The Lower Chamber

The lower chamber consists of 73 popular representatives. These members are divided by the region they represent: 24 are elected by the burgesses of specific towns, and 49 are elected by rural communities.

### Suffrage and Election Process
The right to vote is granted to every citizen who is 25 years of age, provided they have not been convicted and are not a pauper. The election process is indirect; citizens first nominate *Wahlmaenner* (deputy electors), who then proceed to elect the representatives.

### Terms and Meetings
Members of the lower chamber are elected for four-year terms. To ensure continuity, half of the members retire every two years. Both the upper and lower chambers of the *Landtag* meet at least every two years.

## Executive and Judicial Context

The legislative work of the *Landtag* exists alongside an executive branch consisting of four state departments:
1.  Finance.
2.  Interior.
3.  Foreign affairs and the grand-ducal house.
4.  Justice, ecclesiastical affairs, and education.

The state's revenue is derived from domains, railways (which are worked by the state), and both direct and indirect taxes. The public debt is approximately 22 million sterling, all of which is attributed to the railways.

Judicially, the duchy maintains supreme courts of justice in Mannheim, Constance, Waldshut, Mosbach, Heidelberg, Offenburg, Freiburg, and Karlsruhe. Appeals from these courts are directed to the *Reichsgericht* (the supreme tribunal of the empire) located in Leipzig. Additionally, under a convention established with Prussia in 1871, the army of Baden is integrated as a portion of the Prussian army.
