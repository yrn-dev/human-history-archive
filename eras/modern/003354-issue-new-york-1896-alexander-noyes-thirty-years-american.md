# Alexander D. Noyes and Financial Literature of the Late 19th Century

## Alexander D. Noyes
Alexander D. Noyes was an author of financial history. He published the work *Thirty Years of American Finance* in New York in 1898. His writing is cited as an authority on the subjects of banking and money within the historical record of the United States.

## Contemporary Works on Banking and Issue
The late 19th and early 20th centuries saw a significant body of literature regarding the theory and history of banking and currency. In 1896, Charles A. Conant published *A History of Modern Banks of Issue* in New York. Conant later expanded his contributions to the field with the two-volume set *The Principles of Money and Banking*, published in 1905.

Other authoritative texts from this era include:
*   **William G. Sumner**: Published *A History of American Currency* (1884) and *A History of Banking in the United States* (1896), the latter being the first volume of a larger work titled *A History of Banking in All the Leading Nations*.
*   **Albert S. Bolles**: Produced a three-volume *Financial History of the United States* between 1884 and 1886.
*   **Charles F. Dunbar**: Wrote *Chapters on the Theory and History of Banking*, published in New York and London in 1891.
*   **John Jay Knox**: Published a revised edition of *History of Banking in the United States* in 1900.
*   **Horace White**: Authored *Money and Banking* (1902).
*   **Davis Rich Dewey**: Published *Financial History of the United States* in 1903.
*   **Amos Kidder Fiske**: Wrote *The Modern Bank* (1904).

## Other Financial and Political Records
The archive contains various other specialized financial and political histories. David Kinley published *The Independent Treasury of the United States* in 1893, and R. C. H. In the realm of political history, several works analyze the evolution of American politics and party organization. These include the *Writings* of Thomas Jefferson (1892-1899), *The Jeffersonian Cyclopaedia* (1900), and *The Democratic Speaker's Handbook* (1868).
