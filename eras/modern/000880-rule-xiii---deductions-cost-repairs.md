# RULE XIII.--DEDUCTIONS FROM COST OF REPAIRS

Rule XIII is a component of the York-Antwerp Rules, which were established to introduce uniformity, certainty, and clearness to the principles used in adjusting General Average (G.A.) losses. Specifically, Rule XIII provides a definite scale to regulate deductions from the cost of repairs based on the concept of "new for old." This scale replaced previous customary rules that were often uncertain and varied depending on the place of adjustment, while simultaneously adapting the deductions to modern shipbuilding conditions.

## General Principles of Deductions
Under Rule XIII, deductions are generally regulated by the age of the ship rather than the age of the specific part being repaired. However, there are exceptions to this principle regarding boilers, machinery, and provisions and stores. 

Certain items are exempt from these deductions:
*   No deduction is made for old material that is repaired without being replaced by new material.
*   Provisions and stores that have not been in use are not subject to deductions.
*   For all ships, the expense of straightening bent ironwork—including the labor for its removal and replacement—is allowed in full.
*   Graving dock dues, including the use of stages, shears, graving dock materials, and expenses related to cartages and removals, are allowed in full.

## Deductions for Iron or Steel Ships
For iron or steel ships, deductions are calculated from the date of the original register to the date of the accident according to the following age brackets:

*   **Up to 1 year old (A):** All repairs are allowed in full, with the exception of the coating or painting of the bottom, from which one-third is deducted.
*   **Between 1 and 3 years (B):** A deduction of one-third is applied to the renewal and repair of painting, upholstery, furniture, crockery, glassware, metal, sails, rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers (excluding chain and wire), covers, and awnings, as well as woodwork of the hull, masts, and spars. A deduction of one-sixth is applied to chain cables, chains, wire ropes, wire rigging, wire hawsers, steam winches, donkey engines, and steam cranes, including their connexions. Other repairs are allowed in full.
*   **Between 3 and 6 years (C):** Deductions follow the same rules as bracket B, except that a deduction of one-sixth is applied to machinery (including their mountings and boilers) and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 6 and 10 years (D):** Deductions follow the same rules as bracket C, except that a deduction of one-third is applied to all rigging, ropes, sheets, hawsers, the renewal and repair of all machinery (including boilers and mountings), and the ironwork of masts and spars.
*   **Between 10 and 15 years (E):** A deduction of one-third is applied to all renewals and repairs, except for cementing, the ironwork of the hull, and chain cables, which are subject to a one-sixth deduction. Anchors are allowed in full.
*   **Over 15 years (F):** A deduction of one-third is applied to all renewals and repairs, except for anchors, which are allowed in full, and chain cables, which are subject to a one-sixth deduction.

Additionally, painting of the bottom is not allowed if the bottom had not been painted within the six months preceding the accident.

## Deductions for Wooden or Composite Ships
For wooden or composite ships, the rules differ based on the age of the vessel at the time of the accident:
*   **Under one year old:** No "new for old" deductions are made.
*   **After one year:** A general deduction of one-third is applied, with specific exceptions:
    *   Anchors are allowed in full.
    *   Chain cables are subject to a deduction of only one-sixth.
    *   Provisions and stores not in use receive no deduction.
    *   Metal sheathing is handled by allowing the full cost of a weight equal to the gross weight of the stripped metal sheathing, minus the proceeds from the old metal.
    *   Labour metalling, felt, and nails are subject to a one-third deduction.

## Related General Average Rules
Rule XIII exists alongside other regulations governing G.A. claims. For instance, Rule XIV specifies that no "new for old" deductions are made for temporary repairs. Rule XII states that damage or loss of cargo caused by discharging, storing, reloading, and stowing is made good as G.A. only when the costs of those specific measures are admitted as G.A.

Furthermore, Rule X allows for the expenses of entering and leaving a port of refuge if necessary for common safety, as well as the cost of discharging cargo to enable necessary repairs for the safe prosecution of the voyage. Rule XI allows for the wages and maintenance of the master, officers, and crew during the extra period of detention in such ports. However, these wages and maintenance are not admitted as G.A. if the ship is condemned or the voyage is abandoned.
