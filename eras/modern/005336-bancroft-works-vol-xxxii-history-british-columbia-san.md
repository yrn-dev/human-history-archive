## Author Biography
Hubert Howe Bancroft (1832– ) was an American historical writer born on May 5, 1832, in Granville, Ohio. Between 1852 and 1868, he operated as a bookseller in San Francisco. During his tenure as a bookseller, Bancroft accumulated an extensive library of historical materials. He eventually left the book trade to focus on the publication of several large-scale historical projects.

These projects included *Native Races of the Pacific States* (5 volumes, 1874–1876) and the *History of the Pacific States of North America* (21 volumes, 1882–1890). To gather the necessary data for these works, Bancroft employed a corps of assistants. Because of this method of collection, his publications are characterized as an encyclopaedia rather than a unified history, though they are noted for their enduring value as a storehouse of material. In 1905, the University of California acquired Bancroft's vast collection. He also wrote a work titled *Literary Industries* (1890), which provides an account of his working methods.

## Historical Context of British Columbia
The *History of British Columbia* contributes to a body of literature documenting the region's development. According to archive records, the discovery of British Columbia occurred in 1774 by the Spaniard Perez, with geographical exploration of the coast beginning in 1778 during the visit of Cook. Between 1792 and 1794, Vancouver conducted surveys of almost the entire coast for the British government.

The interior was entered around the same time by traders of the N.W. Company and Mackenzie. In 1821, the N.W. Company amalgamated with the Hudson's Bay Company, which ruled the territory for twenty-eight years. Vancouver Island became a British colony in 1849. The mainland was established as a colony named British Columbia in 1858 following the discovery of gold and an influx of miners. In 1866, the mainland and Vancouver Island were united under the name British Columbia.

The province entered the confederation and became part of the Dominion of Canada in 1871. A primary condition of this entry was the rapid construction of the Canadian Pacific railway. The failure to fulfill this promise by 1876 led to strained relations with the government in Ottawa, which were later improved when construction of a transcontinental road commenced. Other significant events in the province's history include the founding of the city of Vancouver by the C.P.R., the establishment of Canadian steamship lines to Australia, Japan, and China, the discovery of the Boundary and Kootenay mining districts, and disputes with the United States regarding pelagic sealing.
