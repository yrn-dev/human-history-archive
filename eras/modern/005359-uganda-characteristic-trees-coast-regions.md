# Uganda

## Overview and Nomenclature
Uganda is a protectorate located in British East Africa, a region that encompasses all territory under British influence on the eastern side of Africa between Abyssinia and the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan to the north and German East Africa to the south. Within the archive's linguistic records, it is noted that "Uganda" and "Unyoro" are popular mis-spellings of the countries Buganda and Bunyoro, respectively; the corresponding languages are Luganda and Runyoro.

## Geography and Flora
The geography of the region is characterized by its proximity to the Victoria Nyanza. In districts bordering the Victoria Nyanza, the flora is similar to that found in the East Africa protectorate. The region's landscape is part of a broader system of high ground and volcanic belts, culminating in the Ruwenzori range.

The flora varies by region. In the coast regions, the characteristic trees are the coco-nut palm and the mangrove. Ebony is found growing in the scrub-jungle. In the forests and near the coast, two varieties of the valuable rubber-vine are present: *Landolphia Kirkii* and *Landolphia florida*.

## Linguistics and Population
The Uganda-Unyoro group of languages includes dialects spanning a wide area: from the Victoria Nile and Busoga in the north and east, to the Congo Forest and the Ruwenzori range in the west, and from the south coast of the Victoria Nyanza to the east coast of Tanganyika and the Malagarazi river in the south and south-east. This group includes the district of Busoga and the inhabited islands of the Victoria Nyanza as far east as Bukerebe.

The languages of the west coast of Lake Victoria, the Sese Islands, and Busoga are closely related to the language of the kingdom of Uganda. A distinct but allied sub-section is the Unyoro group, which includes dialects such as *Ru-toro*, *Ru-tusi*, *Ru-iro*, *Ru-songora*, and *Uru-hima* (the latter spoken by the Hima, described as the Hamitic aristocracy of these equatorial lands), as well as kindred dialects of Businja, Bukerebe, Busiba, and *Ruanda*. Additionally, *Lu-konjo* is spoken on the southern flanks of the Ruwenzori Range, extending south to Lake Kivu and the eastern limits of the Congo Forest.

The dialects of Uganda, Unyoro, and Ruwenzori are considered among the most archaic Bantu dialects existing today. Regarding the population, a large proportion of the people in Uganda are Arab and are relatively light-skinned.

## Administrative and Regional Connections
Uganda is linked to the East Africa protectorate through various means of communication and administration. An inland system of telegraphs connects the chief towns of the East Africa protectorate with one another and with Uganda. 

Historically, the districts of Naivasha and Kisumu, which adjoin the Victoria Nyanza, were originally part of the eastern province of Uganda. However, these were transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902. Furthermore, the Uganda railway terminates at Kilindini, the harbour on the south-west side of Mombasa island.
