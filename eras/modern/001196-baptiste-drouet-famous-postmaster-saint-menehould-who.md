# Postmaster of Sainte Menehould

The postmaster of Sainte Menehould is identified in historical accounts as the individual responsible for the recognition and subsequent interception of the King of France during his attempted flight to Varennes.

## The Recognition at Sainte Menehould
During the King's attempt to flee, he was recognized at Sainte Menehould by the landlord of the post house. Following this identification, messengers were dispatched across the eastward roads to rouse the countryside and attempt to intercept the fugitives.

## The Interception at Varennes
The postmaster from Sainte Menehould played a central role in the King's capture. After the King and his party had encountered delays in the lower village of Varennes—where the King, disguised as a valet, had disputed with his postillions—the postmaster arrived on the scene. He had ridden past the party while the postillions were wrangling and had gathered a number of "worthy republicans" from Varennes.

The postmaster and the gathered republicans awaited the royal party at the bridge connecting the two parts of the town. The bridge had been barricaded, and the party was stopped by individuals with muskets who demanded their passports.

## Surrender of the King
Upon being intercepted at the bridge, the King surrendered without a struggle. He was taken into the house of a village functionary, where he remarked that he was hungry and later commended the wine as "quite excellent wine." While royalist troops were in the vicinity, no rescue attempt was made. To prevent surprise, the village "illuminated itself" and the tocsin began to ring.

## Geography of Sainte Menehould
Sainte Menehould is located within the Marne department. Historically, it served as the capital of the Argonne and was once an important fortress. In more recent records, it is listed as one of the five arrondissements of the department, with a population of 3,348.
