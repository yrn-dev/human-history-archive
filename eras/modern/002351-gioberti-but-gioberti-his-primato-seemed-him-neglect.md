# Gioberti

Vincenzo Gioberti was an influential figure in the political and intellectual discourse regarding the unification and independence of Italy during the 19th century. His ideas centered on the organization of the Italian states and the role of the papacy in achieving national stability.

## Political Vision and the Italian Confederation
Gioberti advocated for an Italian confederation under papal auspices. This vision of a league of separate states under the supremacy of the pope was viewed as an ostensible ideal by some of his contemporaries. This specific political framework was shared by other Italian figures of the era, including the writer and statesman Cesare Balbo and the statesman and author Marquis d'Azeglio (Massimo Taparelli).

## The Primato and Critiques by Cesare Balbo
Gioberti detailed his theories in his work titled *Primato*. While Cesare Balbo shared Gioberti's genuine ideal of a confederation under the pope, he believed that Gioberti's *Primato* failed to address a critical requirement for the region's success. Specifically, Balbo felt that Gioberti neglected the "first essential of independence."

In response to this perceived omission, Balbo wrote his own work, *Speranze* (or *Hopes of Italy*). In this text, Balbo emphasized the necessity of military and moral preparation, as well as patience and alertness. To achieve the independence he sought, Balbo suggested that Austria should be encouraged to seek compensation in the Balkans to offset the inevitable loss of its Italian provinces.

## Influence and Contemporary Context
Gioberti's ideas resonated with a broader movement of Italian intellectuals seeking to end foreign control. Marquis d'Azeglio, who had previously used historical novels such as *Niccolo dei Lapi* and *Ettore Fieramosca* to reawaken national feeling and highlight the evils of foreign domination, also aligned with Gioberti's belief in a papal-led confederation. This shared perspective was particularly evident during the period of general enthusiasm surrounding the perceived liberalism of Pope Pius IX.
