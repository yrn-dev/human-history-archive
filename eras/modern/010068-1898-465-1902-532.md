# Historical Records of 1898 and 1902

The years 1898 and 1902 appear across various archival records, documenting advancements in botany, maritime history, international trade, and academic scholarship.

## Botany and Exploration
Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland, a French botanist and traveller born in La Rochelle in 1773 (whose real name was Goujand), contributed significantly to the classification of plants. After studying under J.N. Corvisart in Paris and serving as a surgeon in the French army, Bonpland spent five years traveling through Colombia, Mexico, and districts bordering the Amazon and Orinoco with A. von Humboldt. During these explorations, he classified approximately 6,000 plants, most of which were previously unknown in Europe. These findings were later described in *Plantes equinoxiales* (1808-1816). 

Following his return to Paris, Bonpland published *Monographie des Melastomees* (1806) and *Description des plantes rares de Navarre* (1813), while also overseeing the gardens at Malmaison. In 1816, he traveled to Buenos Aires with various European plants and served as a professor of natural history. His subsequent exploration of central South America led to his arrest in 1821 by order of Dr. Francia, the dictator of Paraguay. Bonpland remained detained until 1831. He later lived in the province of Corrientes at San Borga before moving to Santa Anna in 1853, where he died on May 4, 1858.
