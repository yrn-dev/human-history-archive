# Sigismund Korybutovic and the Bohemian Conflicts

## Deposition of Sigismund and Provisional Governance
In the early 15th century, royal authority in Bohemia ceased following a meeting of the diet at Caslav on June 1, 1421, where Sigismund was deposed. The diet determined that a Polish prince should be selected as the sovereign. Until such a leader could be chosen, a provisional government was established, consisting of twenty men representing various parties.

## The Regency of Sigismund Korybutovic
In 1422, Sigismund attempted to reclaim Bohemia by invading, but he suffered a decisive defeat at Nemecky Brod (Deutschbrod) at the hands of Zizka. Following this, a Polish prince named Sigismund Korybutovic arrived in Bohemia. He was recognized as regent by a large majority of the population. However, his first tenure was brief; due to the influence of the papal see, the rulers of Poland recalled him after only a few months.

## Civil War and Reunification
The departure of Prince Korybutovic triggered the first outbreak of civil war between two Hussite factions: the advanced Taborite party and the moderate Hussites, who were also known as Utraquists or Calixtines. While isolated disturbances had occurred between these groups previously, this period marked a more formal conflict.

The Bohemians were eventually reunited by two primary factors: the return of Prince Korybutovic and the menace of a German invasion. This unification allowed the Bohemians to achieve a decisive victory over the Germans at Aussig in 1426.

## Departure and the End of the Slav Dynasty
Although Sigismund Korybutovic participated in the victory at Aussig, he subsequently incurred the dislike of the extreme Hussites. This animosity forced him to leave Bohemia. His departure signaled the end of all hopes to establish an independent Slav dynasty within the region.

## Subsequent Crusades and Bohemian Offensives
In 1427, several German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites. This invading force included 1000 English archers who served as the bodyguard to Henry Beaufort, the bishop of Winchester and papal legate. The crusaders experienced sudden panic at Tachau and Mies (Stribro) upon approaching the Hussites, leading to a hurried flight across the mountains into Bavaria.

Despite the recurrence of internal disturbances, the Bohemians utilized this success to transition to the offensive, repeatedly invading the German states and Hungary.
