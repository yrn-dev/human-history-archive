# Carey and Dalgleish

Arthur Douglas Carey and Andrew Dalgleish were English explorers who contributed to the geographical understanding of Central Asia during the late 19th century. Their work was part of a broader period of intense scientific enterprise directed toward Asiatic inquiry, which sought to define the orography of the vast central region encompassing Mongolia and Tibet.

## Explorations in Asia
In 1885, Carey and Dalgleish conducted explorations in Asia, following the tracks of the Russian explorer Prjevalsky to a certain extent. Their efforts provided new information that contributed to the mapping of the continent. Specifically, they were among the investigators whose work necessitated the revision and partial recasting of earlier geographical descriptions of the Kuen-lun mountain system. 

The Kuen-lun system had been previously defined by Baron von Richthofen in 1876 as a complex arrangement of several parallel ranges running from the Pamirs (76° E.) eastwards to 118° E. The subsequent investigations by Carey, Dalgleish, and other explorers—including Russians such as N. M. Przhevalsky and Swedes such as Sven Hedin—helped refine the details of this system.

## Geographical Determinations
Carey and Dalgleish provided specific longitudinal data for Sachu (also known as Saitu) in Mongolia. Sachu is considered an obligatory point in modern map construction. Carey and Dalgleish recorded the longitude of Sachu as 94 deg. 48' E. of Greenwich. 

This value differs from other recorded measurements for the same location, such as those provided by Krishna (94 deg. 23'), Littledale (94 deg. 49'), and Kreitner (94 deg. 58'). It also differs from the values provided by Prjevalsky, who recorded 94 deg. 26' during his second and third explorations and a revised value of 94 deg. 54' in the map accompanying his fourth exploration.
