# Comparative Grammar and the Work of Franz Bopp

## The Objectives of Comparative Grammar
In the field of linguistics, specifically within the work of Franz Bopp, the task of comparative grammar was defined as being threefold. The primary objective was to investigate the origin of grammatical forms. To achieve this, two subservient goals were established: first, to provide a description of the original grammatical structure of languages as deduced through their intercomparison, and second, to trace their phonetic laws.

## The Contributions of Franz Bopp
Franz Bopp's academic career in this field began in 1821 when, upon the recommendation of Humboldt, he was appointed to the chair of comparative grammar and Sanskrit at Berlin. He was elected to the Royal Prussian Academy the following year. Bopp's early work included the *Ausfuhrliches Lehrgebaude der Sanskrita-Sprache*, which he worked on from 1821 and published in 1827. This was followed by a Latin edition completed in 1832 and a shorter grammar in 1834. In 1830, he also compiled a Sanskrit and Latin glossary, with later editions in 1847 and 1867 incorporating cognate languages.

Bopp's primary focus was the development of his *Comparative Grammar* (*Vergleichende Grammatik des Sanskrit, Zend, Griechischen, Lateinischen, Litthauischen, Altslavischen, Gothischen, und Deutschen*). This extensive work was published in six parts between 1833 and 1852. The maturation of this project was evidenced by a series of monographs titled *Vergleichende Zergliederung des Sanskrits und der mit ihm verwandten Sprachen*, printed in the *Transactions of the Berlin Academy* from 1824 to 1831. 

The scope of the *Comparative Grammar* evolved over time; for instance, Old-Slavonian was included starting from the second part. A second revised German edition (1856–1861) expanded the study to include Old-Armenian. The work reached a wider audience through an English translation by E.B. Eastwick in 1845 and a French translation by Professor Michel Breal in 1866. Additionally, Bopp published two essays on "Numerals" in 1835 following the first part of the *Comparative Grammar*.

## English Grammar and Education
Beyond comparative studies, the teaching of English grammar and composition was influenced by other scholars, such as Bain. Appointed to the chair of logic and English at the university of Aberdeen in 1860, Bain sought to address deficiencies in these subjects in northern Scotland. He produced several textbooks, including *Higher English Grammar* (1863), *A First English Grammar* (1872), and the *Companion to the Higher Grammar* (1874).

The study of the English language also saw various historical and philological treatments in the 19th century. Notable works include the *Historische Grammatik der englischen Sprache* by C.F. Koch (1863), the *Englische Grammatik* by Eduard Matzner (1860–1865), and the *Comparative Grammar of the Anglo-Saxon Language* by F.A. March (1870). Other specialized studies include E.A. Abbott's *A Shakespearian Grammar* (1872) and the works of the Rev. R. Morris on historical English accidence and grammar (1873–1874).

## Regional Grammars and Dialects
Grammatical studies have also been applied to various regional languages and dialects. In India, G.A. Grierson produced *Seven Grammars of the Dialects and Sub-dialects of the Bihari Language* (1883–1887) and a grammar for the Maithili language of North Bihar (1881). S.H. Kellogg's *A Grammar of the Hindi Language* (1893) treated High Hindi as well as colloquial dialects such as Maithila, Magadha, and Bhojpur. Regarding Bhojpuri, works include J. Beames' "Notes on the Bhojpuri Dialect of Hindi spoken in Western Bihar" (1868) and A.F. Hoernle's *A Grammar of the Eastern Hindi compared with the other Gaudian Languages* (1880).

In other regions, Nathan Brown's *Grammar* (1848) served as the primary study for Assamese, while G.F. Nicholl provided a supplement to his Bengali grammar. For Oriya, grammars were produced by E. Hallam (1874), T. Maltby (1874), and J. Browne (1882), though these were considered mere sketches of the language.
