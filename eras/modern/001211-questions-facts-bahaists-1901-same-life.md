# Baha'i Literature and Related Works

## Overview of Baha'i Literature
The literature associated with the Baha'i sect is described as being very voluminous, although a significant portion of it exists primarily in manuscript form. Rare manuscripts are preserved in several major European public collections, specifically in Paris at the Bibliothèque Nationale, in London at the British Museum, and in St Petersburg. The collection in Paris includes rare manuscripts gathered by Gobineau, which feature the history of Hajji Mirza Jani of Kashan, a contemporary of the Bab.

## Key Texts and Authors
Among the most important works of Baha is the *Kitab-i-Aqdas*. An edition of this text, featuring an introduction, notes, and a Russian translation, was produced by Captain A. G. Toumansky and published in St Petersburg in 1899. 

Other notable contributions to the literature include:
*   **'Abbas Efendi**: The son of Baha, who composed a Persian work titled *Traveller's Narrative, written to illustrate the Episode of the Bab*. This work was edited, translated, and annotated by E. G. Browne and published in 1891.
*   **Mirza Muhammad Mahdi Khan (Za'imu'd-Duwla)**: A Persian author who wrote an Arabic history of the Babis, printed in Cairo between A.D. 1903 and 1904. While described as unsympathetic, the work is noted as being well-informed.

## Works for American Converts
Several works were composed in English specifically for converts in America. These include:
*   ***Baha'u'llah* (The Glory of God)**: Written by Ibrahim Khayru'llah with the assistance of Howard MacNutt and published in Chicago in 1900.
*   ***The Three Questions*** (n.d.) and ***Facts for Bahaists*** (1901): Both authored by Ibrahim Khayru'llah.
*   ***Life and Teachings of 'Abbas Efendi***: Written by Myron H. Phelps and published in New York in 1903, featuring a preface by E. G. Browne.
*   ***The Revelations of Baha'u'llah, in a Sequence of Four Lessons***: Published by Isabella Brittingham in 1902.
*   ***Some Answered Questions Collected***: Authored by Laura Clifford Burney.

## Scholarly Research and Bibliographies
Academic study of the movement is reflected in various catalogues and historical analyses. E. G. Browne published *The New History of the Bab* in 1893 and a "Catalogue and Description of the 27 Babi Manuscripts" in the *Journal of R. Asiat. Soc.* during 1892. Other scholarly contributions include Andreas's *Die Babi's in Persien* (1896) and several volumes of *Collections scientifiques de l'Institut des Langues orientales* by Baron Victor Rosen, published between 1877 and 1891. Additionally, Captain A. G. Toumansky contributed articles to the *Zapiski vostochnava otdyeleniya Imperatorskava Russkava Archeologicheskava Obshchestva* in St Petersburg between 1890 and 1900.
