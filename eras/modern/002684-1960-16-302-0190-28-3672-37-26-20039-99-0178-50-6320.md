The Department developed policies in 1959 and 1960 to guide federal prosecutors in the use of their charging discretion. These guidelines are part of the "Petite policy," which federal prosecutors use when deciding whether to pursue federal charges against defendants who have already been prosecuted for state or local offenses.

This policy operates under the principle that the Constitution does not prohibit the federal government from prosecuting defendants who were previously charged, acquitted, or convicted on state charges for the same criminal conduct. This is based on the principle that the prohibition against double jeopardy does not apply to prosecutions brought by different sovereigns. A relevant judicial precedent cited in this context is *Petite v. United States*, 361 U.S. 529 (1960).
