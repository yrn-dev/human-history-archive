# Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland

## Early Life and Education
Aime Jacques Alexandre Bonpland, born as Goujand on August 22, 1773, in La Rochelle, was a French botanist and traveller. His early professional training included serving as a surgeon within the French army and pursuing studies in Paris under J.N. Corvisart.

## Scientific Explorations
Bonpland spent five years traveling through Colombia, Mexico, and the districts bordering the Amazon and Orinoco rivers in the company of A. von Humboldt. During these explorations, he classified and collected approximately 6,000 plants, the majority of which were previously unknown in Europe. These findings were later described in his work *Plantes equinoxiales*, published between 1808 and 1816. Upon his return to Paris, Bonpland was granted a pension and appointed as the superintendent of the gardens at Malmaison. During this period, he published *Monographie des Melastomees* in 1806 and *Description des plantes rares de Navarre* in 1813.

## Travels in South America and Detention
In 1816, Bonpland departed for Buenos Aires, bringing various European plants with him. While there, he was elected as a professor of natural history, though he soon resigned from this position to explore central South America. 

In 1821, while journeying to Bolivia, Bonpland was arrested by order of Dr Francia, the dictator of Paraguay. He was detained by the Paraguayan government until 1831.

## Later Years
After regaining his liberty in 1831, Bonpland resided at San Borga, located in the province of Corrientes. In 1853, he moved to Santa Anna, where he remained until his death on May 4, 1858.
