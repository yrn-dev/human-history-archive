# The Russo-Japanese War

The Russo-Japanese War was a conflict that took place between 1904 and 1905. The struggle was preceded by a need for Japan to secure a safe avenue for its troops over the sea to the continent of Asia, as Russia possessed the potential to gain an overwhelming advantage by controlling these lines of communication. At the outbreak of hostilities, Russian squadrons assembled in the East or en route there consisted of 18,000 men and 59 fighting ships mounting 1,350 guns, though this total included a Mediterranean squadron that abandoned its journey to the Pacific upon the start of the war.

## Military Leadership and Campaigns
Japan's strategy during the campaign was guided by the genius of Count Gentaro Kodama, who served as chief of staff to Field Marshal Oyama. Count Itei Kuroki, a general born in Satsuma, commanded the I. Army. Kuroki won the war's opening battle at the Yalu river and subsequently participated in the battles of Mukden, Shaho, and Liao-Yang after advancing through the mountains. 

## International Complications and British Involvement
The war created a critical international environment that influenced British domestic politics. Prime Minister Mr. Balfour maintained a policy of firmness in support of Japan. The British government viewed the situation as too dangerous to risk a change of government or a premature dissolution of parliament, partly due to the importance of seeing the Russo-Japanese War through and the existence of negotiations regarding the revision or renewal of a treaty with Japan.

During 1904, tensions rose between Britain and Russia due to the Russian warships exercising the right of search over British ships. This caused significant irritation in English commercial circles. Specific incidents included the stopping of the P. & O. steamer "Malacca" in the Red Sea by the Russian volunteer cruiser "Peterburg" on July 13, and the sinking of the "Knight Commander" by the Vladivostok squadron on July 24. Tensions nearly led to war on October 23, when the Russian Baltic fleet fired upon the English fishing-fleet off the Dogger Bank in the North Sea. War was averted after Mr. Balfour announced on October 28 that the Russian government had expressed regret and that an international commission would investigate the facts to punish those responsible.

## Conduct of War and Allegations
The conflict involved allegations regarding the use of "dum-dum bullets"—expanding bullets that create severe wounds. The Japanese government issued a protest over the alleged use of these bullets by Russian troops. While such bullets had been used in India and the Sudan, and were manufactured at the Dum-Dum foundry, they were not used by the British during the Boer War. Following the Russo-Japanese conflict, proposals at the second Hague Conference to forbid these bullets were accepted by all powers except the United States and Great Britain.

## The Treaty of Portsmouth
The war concluded with the Treaty of Portsmouth, which established several political and territorial arrangements:
* **Korea and Manchuria:** Japan's "paramount political, military, and economic interests" in Korea were recognized. Both parties agreed to a simultaneous evacuation of Manchuria, though they were permitted to maintain guards (not exceeding fifteen per kilometer) to protect their respective railways.
* **Territorial Transfers:** Russia transferred the lease of the Russian railways south of Kwanchengtsz and the lease of the Liaotung peninsula to Japan, along with collateral mining and other privileges. The southern half of Saghalien was ceded to Japan, with the boundary set at the fiftieth parallel of latitude.
* **Rights and Payments:** Japanese subjects secured fishing rights along the coasts of the seas of Bering, Okhotsk, and Japan. Russia reimbursed Japan approximately four million sterling ($20,000,000) for the maintenance of Russian prisoners, minus the costs Russia incurred for Japanese prisoners.
* **Restrictions:** Both powers pledged not to use Manchurian railways for strategic purposes and to abstain from military measures on the Russo-Korean frontier that might menace Korean or Russian territory. Additionally, they promised not to build fortifications on Saghalien or adjacent islands or take measures impeding the Gulf of Tatary and the Strait of La Pérouse.
