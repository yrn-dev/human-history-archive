## Mathematical Functions and Rationality
In the context of mathematical analysis, the archive discusses various types of functions:

**Integral and Meromorphic Functions**
A single valued function is classified as an integral function if it possesses no singular points except at $z = \infty$. Examples include integral polynomials (which have a pole at $z = \infty$) and the function $\exp(z)$ (which has an essential singularity at $z = \infty$). A function that has no singular points for finite values of $z$ other than poles is termed a meromorphic function.

**Rational Functions**
A meromorphic function is considered a rational function if it also possesses a pole at $z = \infty$. For a rational function $R(s, z)$, the "order" is defined as the number of times the function takes any value (including $\infty$) on the algebraic construct. This order is equal to the sum of the values of $m$ where the function vanishes (zeros) or the sum of the values of $\mu$ where the function is infinite.

When considering a rational function $f(s, z) = 0$ in the neighbourhood of a place $(c, d)$, substitutions $z = c + P(t)$ and $s = d + Q(t)$ result in the form $t^m H(t)$. If the integer $m$ is positive, the function vanishes to order $m$; if $m$ is negative (where $m = -\mu$), the function is infinite to order $\mu$.

## Physical and Mechanical Applications

**Bending Moment and Shearing Force**
In the study of girders, the distribution of shearing force and bending moment is analyzed based on load:
*   **Fixed Load:** For a girder of span $l$ with a fixed load $W$ at distance $m$ from the right abutment, the reactions are $R_1 = Wm/l$ and $R_2 = W(l-m)/l$. The bending moment $M$ at the load is $R_2m$ or $R_1(l-m)$, and its distribution is represented by the ordinates of a triangle.
*   **Uniform Load:** For a girder with a uniform load $w$ per foot, the total load is $wl$ and reactions are $R_1 = R_2 = \frac{1}{2}wl$. The greatest bending moment occurs at the center ($M_c = \frac{1}{8}wl^2$), and the bending moment at any point $x$ from the abutment follows the parabolic equation $M = \frac{1}{2}wx(l-x)$.
