# Charles Melchior Artus, Marquis de Bonchamps

Charles Melchior Artus, Marquis de Bonchamps (c. 1760–1793), was a French military leader and a prominent figure during the insurgencies in the Vendée. Born in Jouverteil, Anjou, Bonchamps began his military career during the American War of Independence. Following his return to France, he served in the French army, where he attained the rank of captain of grenadiers.

## Role in the French Revolution
Bonchamps was a staunch supporter of the monarchy. Due to these convictions, he resigned his military command at the outbreak of the French Revolution and retired to his chateau located at St Florent.

## Leadership in the Vendée
In the spring of 1793, Bonchamps was selected as a leader by the insurgents of the Vendée. He is credited with contributing significantly to the success of the peasants' arms through his counsels. During his leadership, he was present at the capture of Thouars, Bressuire, and Fontenay; it was at Fontenay that he sustained a wound.

Despite these early successes, the insurgent forces were weakened by dissensions among their leaders. This internal instability contributed to a severe defeat for the Vendeans at the bloody battle of Cholet in October 1793.

## Death and Legacy
During the battle of Cholet, Bonchamps was mortally wounded and died the following day. According to historical accounts, his final act was one of clemency: he pardoned five thousand republican prisoners. This act occurred despite the fact that his own troops had sworn to kill these prisoners in revenge for his death.

Bonchamps is commemorated by a statue created by David d'Angers, which is located in the church of St Florent.
