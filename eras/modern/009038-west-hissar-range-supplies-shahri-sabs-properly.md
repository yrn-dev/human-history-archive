# Bokhara and the Hissar Region

## The State of Bokhara
Bokhara (also pronounced Bukhara) is a state located in central Asia under Russian protection. It is positioned on the right bank of the middle Oxus, situated between 37 and 41 degrees North latitude and 62 and 72 degrees East longitude. The state is bounded by Afghanistan to the south, the Pamirs to the east, the Transcaspian territory and Khiva to the west, and the Russian governments of Ferghana, Samarkand, and Syr-darya to the north. Including the khanates of Darvaz and Karateghin, the total area of the state is approximately 85,000 square miles.

The geography of the state varies by region. The western portion consists of a plain watered by the Zarafshan river and various irrigation canals. The north is marked by an intrusion of the Kara-kum sand desert, while the east contains the Karnap-chul steppe, which is covered with grass during the early summer. Cultivation is primarily limited to very fertile oases watered by irrigation. The middle portion of the state consists of high plateaus reaching altitudes of about 4,000 feet, which slope from the Tian-shan and are intersected by numerous rivers flowing toward the Oxus. This middle region is densely populated and characterized by extensive cattle-breeding and agriculture in its fertile valleys. Notable towns in this area include Guzar (or Huzar), Chirakchi, Shaar, Kitab, and Karshi.

## Hydrography and the Hissar Range
The Oxus, also known as the Amu-darya, serves as the chief river of Bokhara, flowing along its south-west border and separating the state from Afghanistan to the south. Steamboats operate on the Oxus from the mouth of the Surkhan up to Karki, near the Afghan frontier. Another significant river is the Zarafshan, which is 660 meters long; its waters are used extensively for irrigation, though the river is lost in the sands 20 meters before it can reach the Oxus.

The Hissar range is a westward continuation of the Alai Mountains and spans approximately 200 meters in length. It serves as a geographic barrier separating the Zarafshan from the Vakhsh, Kafirnihan, and Surkhan tributaries of the Oxus. The range is characterized by a dry climate with scarce rain, resulting in a lack of Alpine meadows and forests. However, when snowmelt coincides with heavy rain, the watercourses become furious torrents. The range's passes are extremely difficult, reaching altitudes between 12,000 and 14,000 feet, which is 1,000 to 3,000 feet below the surrounding peaks.

The Kashka-darya river flows westwards from the glaciers of Hazret-sultan, located west of the Hissar range. This river provides water to the Shaar-sabiz (properly Shahri-sabs) oasis, although it eventually disappears in the desert west of Karshi. Additionally, the southern foot-hills of the Hissar range are covered with loess, forming the fertile valleys of the Vakhsh and Hissar.

## The Capital City of Bokhara
The capital, Bokhara (Bokkara-i-Sherif), is situated in a fertile plain on the left bank of the Zarafshan and the Shahri-rud irrigation canal. It is located at 39 deg. 47' N. lat. and 64 deg. 27' E. long., 162 miles west of Samarkand by rail. The city is enclosed by a stone wall 8 miles long and 28 feet high, featuring semicircular towers and eleven gates.

The current city was established in A.D. 830 on the site of an earlier settlement, destroyed by Jenghiz Khan in 1220 and subsequently rebuilt. The city is known for its religious and educational architecture, containing 360 mosques and 140 madrasas (theological colleges), where approximately 10,000 pupils are educated. Notable structures include the 16th-century Mir-Arab mosque with its lecture halls and the Mejid-kalyan (or Kok-humbez) mosque. Adjacent to the latter is a 203-foot brick minaret used for throwing state criminals until 1871. The principal square is the Raghistan, which houses the citadel. The citadel is built on an artificial eminence 45 feet high and is surrounded by a 1-mile wall containing the prison, water-cisterns, the palace of the emir, and the houses of chief functionaries. Most residential houses are flat-roofed, single-storey structures made of unburned bricks.

## Darvaz
Darvaz is a small vassal state of Bokhara located on the Panj river at a point where the river bends sharply westwards. It is a mountainous region where agriculture is only possible in the lower valley sections. The population is approximately 35,000, consisting primarily of Galchas and Moslem Tajiks. Its chief town is Kala-i-khumb, situated on the Panj at an altitude of 4,370 feet.
