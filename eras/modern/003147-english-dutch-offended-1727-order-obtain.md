# The Ostend Company and Bankipur

## Formation and Settlements
The Ostend Company was established between 1722 and 1723 as a significant effort by Germany to establish a presence in India. Operating with a capital of less than one million sterling, the Company founded two primary settlements. The first was located at Coblom (Covelong) on the Madras coast, situated between the Dutch settlement of Sadras and the English settlement of Madras. The second settlement was established at Bankipur, an ancient village on the Hugli river in the Bengal Presidency. Bankipur was located near modern Palta, above Barrackpore, and was positioned between the English settlement of Calcutta and the Dutch settlement of Chinsura.

## Diplomatic Pressures and Suspension
The presence of the Ostend Company caused offense to both the English and the Dutch. In 1727, the court of Vienna sought a European guarantee for the Pragmatic Sanction. To obtain this guarantee, the court resolved to sacrifice the Ostend Company and suspended its charter. Despite this suspension, the Company continued to exist for several decades, eventually becoming bankrupt in 1784 and ceasing to exist entirely in 1793.

## The Fall of Bankipur
The decline of German interests in India was accelerated by the actions of the English and Dutch. In 1733, these two powers stirred up a Mahommedan general at Hugli to initiate a conflict. The general attacked Bankipur, where the garrison consisted of only fourteen persons. Following the attack, the garrison set sail for Europe, marking the disappearance of German interests from the region. Bankipur has since disappeared from the map.
