# Spanish Home Government and Colonial Administration

The Spanish home government exercised authority over various overseas territories, implementing legislative frameworks intended to regulate the conduct of settlers and the treatment of indigenous populations. The effectiveness of these laws often varied depending on the region and the distance from the central administration.

## Legislative Efforts in the Americas
In the regions that now comprise the republic of Bolivia, which was originally part of the Inca empire of Peru, the Spanish home government attempted to implement humane and protective regulations. Following the Spanish conquest of Peru in the 16th century, the native populations faced significant tyranny and oppression. The home government made legislative efforts to protect the Indians from ill-usage and serfdom; however, these efforts were frequently met with scant respect. 

The regulations established by the council of the Indies were often defied by mine-owners and distant settlers, who treated the native populations as beasts of burden. While there are claims that eight million Indians died due to forced labor in the mines, such figures are considered gross exaggerations. Although the Indian population diminished greatly, the primary causes were European epidemics and the indulgence in alcohol rather than hard work.

## Administration of Louisiana
Spain formally took control of Louisiana on August 18, 1769, replacing French officialdom with the Spanish tongue and law. Despite this transition, the colony remained essentially French in character. The Spanish rulers in Louisiana adopted a liberal and conciliatory policy, aiming to govern wisely. This included showing great complaisance toward the colony's profits, even when such actions contradicted Spanish colonial commercial regulations.

The administration in Louisiana focused on several key improvements:
* **Judicial and Official Standards:** The judicial system was improved, and a higher grade of officials was appointed.
* **Integration:** French Creoles were appointed to office, and high officials encouraged intermarriages between the Spanish, French, and English.
* **Leadership:** Bernardo de Galvez, who served as governor, was noted as one of the most popular and liberal of the Spanish rulers. During the American War of Independence, Galvez conducted energetic campaigns between 1779 and 1781, capturing important posts in the British colony of West Florida.

## Territorial Influence and Diplomacy
Spanish administration also extended to the management of western territories and international relations. Under Governor Estevan Miro, who succeeded Galvez in 1785, the Spanish government engaged in intrigues to expand its influence. In 1787, Miro attempted to persuade James Wilkinson of Kentucky to secede from the United States and join the Spanish, though this effort was unsuccessful.

By 1794, facing pressure from France and Great Britain, Spain entered into a treaty with the United States. Through this agreement, Spain recognized the Mississippi River as the western boundary of the United States, separating it from Louisiana and allowing for free navigation. Additionally, earlier Spanish divisions for religious purposes influenced later administrative structures in the region; for example, the "parishes" established in 1807 were based on these earlier Spanish divisions, which is why the names of saints appear in parish nomenclature.
