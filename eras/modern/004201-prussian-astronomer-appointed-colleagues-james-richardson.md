# Adolf Overweg and the Sahara Expedition

## Appointment and Mission
At the instance of scientists, including Bunsen, Adolf Overweg, a Prussian astronomer, was appointed as a colleague to James Richardson. Richardson was an explorer of the Sahara who had been selected by the British government for the purpose of opening up commercial relations with the states of the central and western Sudan.

## The Expedition of 1850
The party departed from Tripoli in early 1850. The mission faced significant losses during its course; James Richardson died in March 1851, and Adolf Overweg died in September 1852. These deaths left Barth to continue the mission alone.

## Legacy of the Mission
Following the deaths of Richardson and Overweg, Barth continued the expedition, which is described as one of the most fruitful ever undertaken in inner Africa. Barth traversed the country from Timbuktu in the west to Lake Chad and Bagirmi in the east, and as far south as Cameroon. During this time, he made prolonged sojourns in Timbuktu and the emirates or sultanates of Gando, Sokoto, Nupe, Kano, and Bornu. 

Barth conducted minute studies of the resources, civilizations, history, and topography of the regions he visited. His findings were published in a five-volume work titled *Travels and Discoveries in North and Central Africa* (1857-1858), published simultaneously in German and English. This work is considered a standard authority on these regions, a great part of which later came under British rule as Nigeria.
