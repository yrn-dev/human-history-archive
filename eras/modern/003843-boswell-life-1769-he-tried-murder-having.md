# James Boswell and the Life of Johnson

James Boswell was a biographer described as a "biographical genius" and the "first and most fatuous of all interviewers." He is noted for employing a deliberate and artificial method of revealing himself to the reader, as well as a unique "recipe" for disclosing personality that differed from that of Sterne.

## The Life of Johnson
Boswell's most significant work is the *Life of Johnson* (1791). This text is characterized as a "magnificent type" of biography, surpassing other portraitists and purveyors of "Johnsoniana" in its mastery of portraiture, courage, and picturesqueness. The work reflects the 18th-century interest in discussing hard cases of conscience, though the era generally remained suspicious of technical systems for dealing with such moral difficulties.

The influence of Boswell's model was extensive; once his approach became familiar to other biographers, the biographies of the 19th century were more or less founded upon the type established by the *Life of Johnson*.

## Literary Connections and Sources
The *Life of Johnson* serves as a record of various historical figures and correspondences. For instance, the letters of Birch, who was engaged in numerous literary undertakings and maintained an extensive correspondence with leading men of his time, appear within the work. Additionally, the Italian critic Giuseppe Marc' Antonio Baretti—who served as secretary to the Royal Academy of Painting and was acquainted with Garrick and Johnson—is mentioned repeatedly in Boswell's *Life*.
