# The Habsburg Empire under Charles VI

## Imperial Composition and Governance
The Habsburg dominions were characterized by a complex structure, described as a congeries of nationalities. This diverse empire included Germans, Italians, Flemings, Czechs, Magyars, Ruthenes, and Rumanians. Because the territories were separated by many miles, the ruler of these lands required administrative skill, patience, tolerance, and a comprehensive understanding of European diplomacy to govern successfully.

Charles VI, who ruled until his death in 1740, was noted for lacking these specific qualities. Consequently, the scattered nature of the empire and its inherent weakness made it a target for the cupidity of other continental powers upon his passing.

## Impact of the War of Spanish Succession
The hereditary dominions of the Austrian crown suffered significant financial and material strain due to the War of Spanish Succession, which proved to be a heavy drain on its resources. 

## Military Successes in Eastern Europe
Despite the depletion of resources caused by the War of Spanish Succession, Charles VI managed to compensate for these losses through military achievements in eastern Europe. In 1716, Charles VI entered into an alliance with Venice and declared war on the Turks. This conflict saw a victory by Eugene at Peterwardein, which resulted in the conquest of the banat of Temesvar. This was followed in 1717 by the capture of Belgrade.

These military successes were formalized in a treaty signed at Passarowitz on July 21, 1718. Under the terms of this treaty, the Habsburg monarchy annexed the northern districts of Servia, Belgrade, and the banat, the latter of which served to round off Hungary.
