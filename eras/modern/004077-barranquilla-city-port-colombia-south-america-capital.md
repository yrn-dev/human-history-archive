# BARRANQUILLA

Barranquilla is a city and port located in South America within the country of Colombia. It serves as the capital of a province of the same name, which is situated in the department of Atlantico.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The city is positioned on the left bank of the Magdalena river, approximately 7 miles above the river's mouth. It is located 18-1/2 miles by rail from its associated seaport, Puerto Colombia. The city is built upon a low plain and features a regular layout. While the city contains many residences, public buildings, and fine warehouses, a larger portion of the city consists of thatched rushes and mud-walled cabins supported by bamboo framework, known as *guadua*.

The city's water supply is drawn from the Magdalena river. Modern amenities available in the city include electric light, telephone, and tram services.

## Commercial Importance and Shipping
Barranquilla is the principal commercial centre of the republic. This status is due to a dangerous bar located at the mouth of the Magdalena river, which necessitates that much of the trade from the river's extensive tributary territory—representing about 60% of the entire country's trade—pass through Barranquilla and its seaport.

Historically, Savanilla served as the seaport until approximately 1890. At that time, drifting sands created shoals that forced the relocation of the port to Puerto Colombia, situated a short distance to the west. To facilitate the handling of freight, a steel pier measuring 4,000 feet in length was constructed at Puerto Colombia.

Navigation on the Magdalena river is conducted using light-draught steamboats. These vessels ascend to Yeguas, located 14 miles below Honda. At Yeguas, goods are transhipped via rail to Honda, and from there they are transported to Bogota by pack animals or moved further up the river using smaller boats.

## History and Population
Barranquilla was originally founded in 1629. However, it did not attract attention as a commercial centre until the middle of the 19th century, when efforts began to secure the trade that had previously passed through Cartagena.

As of an estimate from 1902, the population of the city was 31,000. The population is described as being of the mixed type common to the lowlands of Colombia. Because the surrounding country is subject to periodical inundations, there is very little cultivation in the area, and the majority of the population is not engaged in any settled productive occupation.
