# Trieste and Territory

Trieste is a city and territory that has historically been a point of significant political contention and administrative reorganization, primarily between the Austrian Empire and Italy.

## Administrative History and the Küstenland
Trieste is part of a region known as the Küstenland (coast-land or littoral), which comprises three Austrian crown-lands: Trieste, Istria, and Görz and Gradisca. As of 1900, the combined population of these three lands was 755,183, covering an area of 3,084 square miles. For specific administrative purposes, these territories are united under the governor of Trieste, and the financial and legal authorities of Trieste exercise jurisdiction over the entire littoral.

In the allotment of constituencies for the Reichsrath, Trieste and its territory were assigned 5 representatives. Within the broader political structure of the Austrian territories, the Delegation—which settled budget points and represented the various kingdoms and lands—included one representative from Trieste.

## Napoleonic Era and the Kingdom of Illyria
The political status of Trieste shifted during the Napoleonic period. In 1809, the name "Illyrian Provinces" was revived to describe a territory that included Trieste, along with Istria, Carniola, Dalmatia, Fiume, Görz and Gradisca, and parts of Croatia and Carinthia. These lands were ceded by Austria to Italy at the peace of Schönbrunn on October 14, 1809. Under the governance of Napoleon, the provinces were occupied by French troops; the republic of Ragusa was later annexed to them in 1811.

By the end of 1813, French occupation became ineffective, and the provinces reverted to Austria. In 1816, the Kingdom of Illyria was constituted as a kingdom of the Austrian crown, consisting of Trieste, Istria, Carniola, Görz and Gradisca, and Carinthia. This arrangement lasted until 1849.

## Italian Irredentism and Territorial Disputes
Trieste became a focal point for the "Irredentists," a large party in Italy that demanded the annexation of Italian-speaking districts under Austrian rule, specifically Trieste, Istria, and South Tirol. This movement led to public riots in Italy, including the tearing down of the Austrian flag from the consulate in Venice and insults directed at the embassy in Rome. Similar riots occurred within Trieste itself. In response, the Austrian government moved troops into Tirol to signal its determination not to surrender territory.

Following the Prussian and Italian victories over Austria in 1866, Italy gained the province of Venetia. However, Trieste, along with Rome and several small frontier towns, remained under Austrian control.

## Military Conflict and Strategic Importance
During the First World War, Trieste was described as a "coveted city" by the Italians. From the perspective of Monfalcone, the town was visible ten miles away across an empty gulf. Italian military operations in the region included the laboriously equipped Bainsizza plateau, which served as a taking-off place for attacks intended to drive Austrians back up the Vippaco valley toward Laibach, described as one of the "back-doors" to Trieste.

## Notable Residents
Trieste served as a place of residence for Maria Annunciata Caroline (1782–1839), the former queen of Naples and sister of Elisa. After retiring to Austrian territory and spending time under some measure of restraint following the movements of her husband, Joachim Murat, in 1814–1815, she lived in Trieste until her death on May 18, 1839.
