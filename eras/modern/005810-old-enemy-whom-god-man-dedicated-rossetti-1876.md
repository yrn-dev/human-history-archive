# Robert Williams Buchanan and the "Old Enemy"

## Life and Early Career
Robert Williams Buchanan (1841–1901) was a British dramatist, novelist, and poet. Born on August 18, 1841, in Caverswall, Staffordshire, he was the son of Robert Buchanan (1813–1866), a journalist and Owenite lecturer. After his father moved from Manchester to Glasgow, Buchanan received his education at the university and the high school. During this time, he formed a close friendship with the poet David Gray, a relationship later detailed in an essay Buchanan contributed to the *Cornhill Magazine*. In 1860, the two traveled to London together in pursuit of fame.

Following a period of disappointment and struggle, Buchanan began publishing his poetic work. His first volume, *Undertones*, appeared in 1863. This was followed by several other collections: *Idyls and Legends of Inverburn* (1865), *London Poems* (1866), and *North Coast and other Poems* (1868). These works demonstrated a sympathetic insight into humble life conditions and a faculty for poetic narrative. In 1870, he attempted a more ambitious study in mysticism titled *The Book of Orm: A Prelude to the Epic*.

## Conflict with D.G. Rossetti
Buchanan became known for his contributions to periodical literature, specifically gaining notoriety through an article published in the October 1871 issue of the *Contemporary Review*. Writing under the pseudonym Thomas Maitland, he authored a piece titled "The Fleshly School of Poetry," which he later expanded into a pamphlet in 1872. 

This work provoked strong responses from other literary figures. D.G. Rossetti replied in a letter to the *Athenaeum* on December 16, 1871, titled "The Stealthy School of Criticism," and Mr. Swinburne responded in *Under the Microscope* (1872). Buchanan eventually expressed regret regarding the violence of his attack. The tension between the two men was such that Buchanan referred to Rossetti as the "old enemy," and it was to Rossetti that Buchanan dedicated the work *God and the Man*.

## Later Works and Dramaturgy
In 1876, Buchanan published *The Shadow of the Sword*, which served as the first in a long series of novels and is considered one of his best. In addition to his poetry and novels, Buchanan wrote several successful plays. These include *Lady Clare* (1883), *A Man's Shadow* (1890), *The Charlatan* (1894), and *Sophia* (1886), the latter of which was an adaptation of *Tom Jones*.
