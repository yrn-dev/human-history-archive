## Profile of Francois, Marquis de Barthelemy
One of the primary subjects of Garsou's study is Francois, Marquis de Barthelemy (1747 or 1750–1830), a French politician. Barthelemy's early life and career were shaped by his uncle, the abbe Jean Jacques Barthelemy, who educated him for a career in diplomacy. He served as secretary of legation in England, Switzerland, and Sweden. Later, he was appointed as minister plenipotentiary in Switzerland, where he was responsible for negotiating the treaties of Basel with Spain and Prussia in 1795.

Barthelemy's political trajectory was marked by significant volatility:
* **The Directory:** In May 1797, he was elected as a member of the Directory through royalist influence. However, he was arrested during the *coup d'etat* of the 18 Fructidor on September 17, 1797, and subsequently deported to French Guiana.
* **Exile and Return:** After escaping deportation, he traveled to England and the United States. He returned to France following the 18 Brumaire.
* **The Empire:** In February 1800, he entered the senate. He played a role in the establishment of the empire and the consulship for life.
* **Restoration and Later Years:** In 1814, Barthelemy abandoned Napoleon and assisted in the drawing up of the constitutional charter, which led to his appointment as a peer of France. During the Hundred Days, he lived in concealment. Following the second Restoration, he was granted the title of marquis. In 1819, while serving in the chamber of peers, he introduced a motion intended to make the electoral law more aristocratic.
