# Charlotte Honorine Josephine

## Family and Lineage
Charlotte Honorine Josephine was a member of the Bonaparte family. She was the daughter of Zenaide Bonaparte and Charles Lucien Jules Laurent, the prince of Canino. Her father, born in 1803 and deceased in 1857, was the son of Lucien Bonaparte. Rather than pursuing a career in politics, the prince of Canino was a scientist who gained a reputation for his work in zoology. He published a four-volume work titled *American Ornithology* in Philadelphia between 1825 and 1833, followed by several other catalogs and studies on European birds, fish, and mammals.

Charlotte Honorine Josephine's mother, Zenaide Bonaparte, was the daughter of Joseph Bonaparte. Zenaide and the prince of Canino married in 1822.

## Marriage and Siblings
Charlotte Honorine Josephine married Count Primoli. She was one of eight children who survived her father. Her siblings included:
* Joseph Lucien Charles Napoleon, the prince of Canino (1824–1865), who died without heirs.
* Lucien Louis Joseph Napoleon (born 1828), who entered holy orders in 1853 and attained the rank of cardinal in 1868.
* Julie Charlotte Zenaide Pauline Laetitia Desiree Bartholomee, who married the marquis of Roccagiovine.
* Marie Desiree Eugenie Josephine Philomene, who married the count Campello.
* Auguste Amelie Maximilienne Jacqueline, who married Count Gabrielli.
* Napoleon Charles Gregoire Jacques Philippe (born 1839), who married the princess Ruspoli and had two daughters.

## Historical Context of the Bonaparte Family
The broader family history of the Bonapartes includes the reign of Napoleon I. Napoleon I had previously been married to Madame Josephine de Beauharnais, a charming young widow with influence with Barras. The couple was jointly crowned by the pope in Paris, where they received imperial ornaments including crowns, rings, and scepters.

However, Napoleon I later divorced Josephine because she was childless, a move intended to ensure the continuity of his dynasty. Following this divorce, Napoleon I married the arch-duchess Marie Louise of Austria, the daughter of Emperor Francis I and Princess Theresa of Naples. This marriage took place by proxy in Vienna on March 11, 1810, and was followed by civil and religious contracts in Paris in April. Marie Louise gave birth to a son on March 20, 1811, who was given the titles "king of Rome" and "Napoleon II."
