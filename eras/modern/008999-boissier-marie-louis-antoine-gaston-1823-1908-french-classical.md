# BOISSIER, MARIE LOUIS ANTOINE GASTON (1823-1908)

Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier was a French classical scholar and the perpetual secretary of the French Academy. Born on August 15, 1823, in Nimes, Boissier developed an early interest in ancient history, sparked by the Roman monuments located in his native town.

## Early Career and Academic Appointments
Boissier specialized in the study of epigraphy. At twenty-three years of age, he was appointed as a professor of rhetoric in Angouleme, a position he held for ten years. His career advanced when a travelling university inspector heard him lecture, leading to his appointment as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne in Paris.

In 1861, Boissier was appointed professor of Latin oratory at the College de France. During this period, he also became an active contributor to the *Revue des deux mondes*.

## Literary Works and Scholarship
Boissier began his literary output with a thesis on the poet Attius in 1857, followed by a study on the life and work of M. Terentius Varro in 1861. He gained significant recognition in 1865 with the publication of *Ciceron et ses amis* (translated into English by A.D. Jones in 1897), a work of erudition that achieved rare success. Boissier was noted for his ability to reproduce the characteristics of ancient Roman society and its manners with "exquisite vivacity."

His later major works include:
*   ***La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins*** (1874): A two-volume analysis of the great religious movement of antiquity that occurred before the acceptance of Christianity.
*   ***L'Opposition sous les Cesars*** (1875): A study providing a picture of the political decadence of Rome during the reign of the early successors of Augustus.

## Honors and Later Life
By the mid-1870s, Boissier had earned the universal respect of men of letters and scholars. Following the death of H.J.G. Patin—the author of *Etudes sur les tragiques grecs*—in 1876, Boissier was elected as a member of the French Academy. He later served as the academy's perpetual secretary, an appointment he received in 1895.
