# Banjaluka

## Geography and Infrastructure
Banjaluka is the capital of a district of the same name located in Bosnia. The city is situated on the river Vrbas at the head of a narrow defile, which is enclosed by steep hills to the east and west. To the north, the landscape expands to meet the valley of the Save. A small stream known as the Crkvina flows into the Vrbas from the north-east.

The city serves as the terminus for a military railway that connects with the Hungarian state line at Jasenovac, located 30 miles north-north-west of the city.

## Demographics and Administration
As of 1895, the population of Banjaluka was 13,666, with approximately 7,000 of these residents being Moslems. Administratively, the city is the seat of a district court and houses an Austrian garrison. It is also the seat for both Orthodox and Roman Catholic bishops.

## City Landmarks and Architecture
The citadel and barracks are located in the angle formed by the Vrbas and the Crkvina stream. Within the city, there are more than 40 mosques, the largest and most beautiful of which is the 16th-century Ferhadiya Jamia. 

The city contains several other noteworthy buildings, including:
*   Franciscan and Trappist monasteries.
*   A real-school.
*   A Turkish bazaar.
*   A girls' school operated by the Sisterhood of the Sacred Blood of Nazareth.

Banjaluka is known for its hot springs, which are utilized by modern baths. The city also contains ruins of Roman baths, though one massive domed building from the 6th century remains in use.

## Economy and Natural Resources
The hills adjoining the city contain deposits of silver, iron, coal, and other minerals. The city's industrial base is supported by the two rapid rivers and includes:
*   A government tobacco factory.
*   A brewery.
*   Cloth-mills and gunpowder-mills.
*   Many corn-mills.
*   A model farm.

## History
Banjaluka is believed to be the Roman fort identified as *Castra* in the *Tabula Peutingeriana*, located on the road from Salona on the Adriatic to Servitium in Pannonia on the river Urbanus. The origin of the name Banjaluka, which means "Baths of St Luke," is not certain.

The city rose rapidly in importance during the 15th century following the fall of Jajce, a rival stronghold located 22 miles to the south. Consequently, Banjaluka became a site for numerous encounters between Turks and Austrians, specifically in 1737, 1688, and 1527. During the second half of the 18th century, no other Bosnian city possessed greater importance or prosperity. In 1831, Hussein Aga Borberli, known as *Zmaj Bosanski* ("the Dragon of Bosnia"), departed from Banjaluka to lead a holy war against sultan Mahmud II.
