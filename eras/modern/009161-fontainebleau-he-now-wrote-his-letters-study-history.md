# Fontainebleau

## The Library at Fontainebleau
The library at Fontainebleau was established through the influence of the French scholar Guillaume Budé (1467–1540). Budé, who had gained a significant reputation for his treatise on ancient coins and measures, *De Asse et Partibus* (1514), was held in high esteem by Francis I. Along with Jean du Bellay, the bishop of Narbonne, Budé persuaded the king to found the library. This institution was later removed to Paris, where it served as the origin of the Bibliothèque Nationale.

## Bolingbroke and the Chateau of Argeville
In June, Bolingbroke retired to France after becoming baffled and disappointed by political fray. He resided primarily at the chateau of Argeville, located near Fontainebleau. During this period of retirement, he authored the work *True Use of Retirement* and his *Letters on the Study of History*. The latter was printed privately before his death and subsequently published in 1752.

Bolingbroke's time in France was interspersed with visits to England. In 1738, he became a leading adviser and friend to Frederick, prince of Wales, who led the opposition. For this purpose, Bolingbroke wrote *The Patriot King*, as well as *The Spirit of Patriotism* and *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I*. These works were entrusted to Pope and remained unpublished until the poet secretly printed 1,500 copies of *The Patriot King*. This discovery led Bolingbroke to publish a correct version in 1749.

Following a failed attempt to obtain a share in politics, Bolingbroke returned to France in 1739 and later sold Dawley. He visited England again in 1742 and 1743, during which time he quarreled with Warburton. He eventually settled in Battersea in 1744 with Hugh Hume, the 3rd earl of Marchmont. In his final years, Bolingbroke assisted in negotiations for the "broad bottom" administration and recommended a tutor for the future George III. He died on December 12, 1751, and was buried in the parish church at Battersea alongside his wife, who had died in 1750.

## Literary Works Associated with Fontainebleau
The location of Fontainebleau is also linked to biographical scholarship. Specifically, the work *Biographie du général Kosciuszko* was published in Fontainebleau in 1837. This biography concerns Jozef Kosciuszko, a democrat influenced by the school of Lafayette and Jefferson, who believed that a republic could only be regenerated through equality before the law and absolute liberty.
