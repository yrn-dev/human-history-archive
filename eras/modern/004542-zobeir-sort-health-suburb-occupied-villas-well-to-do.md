# Zobeir

## Overview and Characteristics
Zobeir is described as a modern town that serves as a sort of health suburb. It is characterized by the presence of villas occupied by the well-to-do inhabitants of Basra.

## Geographic and Historical Context
Zobeir is situated near the ruin mounds that mark the location of the original city of Basra. The original city was founded in A.D. 636 by the caliph Omar on an ancient canal that is now dry, located approximately 8 miles south-west of the present site of Basra, on the edge of the pebbly and stony Arabian plateau.

In its period of prosperity, the original city of Basra was comparable in size and wealth to Wasit and Kufa, and it gained fame through the tales of the *Arabian Nights*. However, the city's importance declined following the decay of the Abbasid caliphate's power. This decline was marked by the neglect of canals and the severance of communication with the Persian Gulf, which eventually led to the site being abandoned entirely.

## History of the Present City
The present city of Basra was conquered by the Turks in 1668 and subsequently became a site for numerous revolutions. In 1777, the Persians, led by Sadik Khan, took the city after a siege lasting eight months. Within approximately one year, the Turks regained control, though they were later deprived of the city by the sheikh of the Montefik (Montafiq) Arabs. Control was recovered by Suleiman Pasha in the following October after he encountered the sheikh on the banks of the Euphrates and put him to flight. Since that event, the city has remained under Turkish control.
