# Philippines

## Political History and Administration
The Philippine Islands were acquired by the United States following a brief war in 1898, which occurred during the American intervention in Cuba's insurrection against Spain. Following this conflict, the Philippines were established under a special form of government. This administrative structure consists of a popularly elected lower house and an upper body composed of members appointed by the United States senate.

The transition to American administration was marked by tension. While Cuba and Porto Rico welcomed the intervention, there was a demand in the Philippine Islands for immediate and complete freedom after the Spanish war. This led to considerable resistance against the American military administration. In February 1899, American troops captured Iloilo, after which Filipino insurgents burned much of the town. Some observers have characterized the United States' actions in the Philippines as being nearest to the imperialism of the Great Power type.

## Geography and Urban Centers
The Philippines comprises several islands and provinces with various economic centers:

*   **Iloilo:** Located on the southeast coast of Panay, Iloilo is a town, port of entry, and the capital of the province of Iloilo. It is situated at the mouth of the Iloilo river and possesses a harbor protected by the island of Guimaras, capable of accommodating ships with a 15 ft. draught. In 1903, its population was 19,054, though this number more than doubled later that year through the addition of the municipalities of Molo, Mandurriao, La Paz, and Jaro (though Jaro became a separate town again in 1908). Iloilo is the second most commercially important Philippine city after Manila.
*   **Negros Island:** This island contains the provinces of Negros Occidental and Negros Oriental. 
    *   **Cadiz:** A town on the north coast of Negros Occidental, located approximately 53 m. N.N.E. of the capital, Bacólod. As of 1903, its population was 16,429. The town is known for lumber products and is home to a saw-mill said to be the largest in the Philippines.
    *   **Dumaguete:** The capital town of Negros Oriental, located on Tanon Strait. Its 1903 population was 14,894, which increased after the annexation of the town of Sibulan (population 8,413). The town features substantial public buildings, including a belfry and a watch-tower.

    *   **Dumanjug:** A town on the west coast of Cebu at the mouth of the Dumanjug river, roughly 40 m. S.W. of the town of Cebu. Its 1903 population was 22,203, following the annexation of the town of Ronda (population 9,662).

## Economy and Natural Resources
The Philippine economy is characterized by diverse agricultural and industrial activities. The region surrounding Iloilo is fertile and produces rice, tobacco, and sugar in abundance. Iloilo's manufactures include coconut oil, vinegar, lime, jusi, piña, and various items crafted from palm wood. In the province of Cebu, specifically near Dumanjug, sugar-cane and Indian corn are grown successfully, and the town maintains an important coast trade. On the island of Negros, Dumaguete is situated within a fertile agricultural district where shops are primarily operated by Chinese merchants.

The islands also possess unique biological specimens, such as the *Scalpellum rostratum* identified by Darwin.

## Religious and International Relations
In the 17th century, the Philippines had diplomatic and religious interactions with Japan. In 1624, the shogun Hidetada refused to grant an audience to a Philippine embassy. This decision followed a report from an envoy to Europe that was unfavorable to Christianity. Consequently, the shogun ordered the deportation of all Spaniards from Japan and decreed that Japanese Christians were forbidden from traveling to the Philippines.

In more recent historical data, the Philippines is noted in records of the Disciples of Christ. According to one archive listing, there were 4 churches and 425 members in the Philippines.
