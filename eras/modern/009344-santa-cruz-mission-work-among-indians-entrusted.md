# Santa Cruz

The term Santa Cruz refers to several distinct entities across different geographical and historical contexts, including a department in Bolivia, a former political leader of Bolivia, and geographical features in the Pacific and South America.

## Department of Santa Cruz, Bolivia
Santa Cruz is one of the eight departments of the republic of Bolivia. According to the census of 1900, the department had a population of 209,592 and an area of 141,368 square miles. Its capital is Santa Cruz de la Sierra, which had a population of 15,874 at the time of the 1900 census. 

The geography of the region is varied. Southern Santa Cruz contains low-lying plains and extensive swamps that are subject to periodical inundations, making them of little value for pastoral or agricultural purposes. However, some areas above the flood lines provide rich grazing lands. The region is also associated with the Chiquitos hills, where Ascension (population 4,784) serves as a large mission station.

In terms of education, Santa Cruz is home to a university that provides instruction in law.

## Religious Administration and Mission Work
The republic of Bolivia is divided into one archbishopric and three bishoprics; Santa Cruz is the seat of one of these three bishoprics. The state recognizes and supports the Roman Apostolic Catholic religion, and the domesticated Indians of the region profess this faith, though it is influenced by the superstitions of their ancestors.

Mission work among the Indians is entrusted to the *Propaganda Fide*. This organization maintains five colleges and a large number of missions, receiving a small subvention from the state. It is estimated that these missions are responsible for as many as 20,000 Indians.

## Marshal Santa Cruz
Marshal Santa Cruz was a prominent political and military figure in Bolivian history. He was chosen as president by a congress formed at Chuquisaca in April 1828. Following a period of disorder caused by a revolution led by General Blanco, quiet was restored in 1831. At that time, Santa Cruz promulgated a code of laws bearing his name and organized the country's financial affairs, while also establishing a treaty of commerce with Peru.

In 1835, Santa Cruz intervened in a power struggle within Peru. After defeating General Gamarra in the spring of 1836, he pacified the country and named himself its protector, intending to create a confederation between Bolivia and Peru. This led to active interference from the government of Chile. After three years of conflict, Santa Cruz was defeated at the battle of Jungay in June 1839 and subsequently exiled. Despite his exile, the Santa Cruz party remained a strong force within Bolivia and eventually revolted to install General Ballivian in power.
