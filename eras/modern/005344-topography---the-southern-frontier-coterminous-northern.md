# Topography and Frontiers

The study of topography involves the delineation of boundaries, the analysis of physical terrain, and the mapping of natural features such as rivers, mountains, and coastlines. Historical and geographical records provide detailed accounts of these features across various global regions, from the frontiers of Africa and Asia to the landscapes of Europe and the Americas.

## African Territories and Boundaries

In East Africa, one protectorate is bounded to the north by Italian Somaliland and Abyssinia, and to the west by Uganda. Its eastern boundary is the Indian Ocean, with a coastline extending approximately 400 miles. The southern frontier is coterminous with the northern frontier of German East Africa; this line runs northwest from the mouth of the Umba river (4° 40' S.) to Victoria Nyanza (1° S.), though it deviates to ensure Mount Kilimanjaro remains entirely within German territory. This region covers an area of about 240,000 square miles and has an estimated population between 2,000,000 and 4,000,000, which includes 3,000 Europeans and 25,000 Indians.

In West Africa, Ashanti is a British possession described as an irregular oblong with a triangular projection southward known as the country of the Adansi. It is bounded to the west by the French colony of the Ivory Coast and to the north by the British Protectorate of the Northern Territories of the Gold Coast. Its eastern boundary is the river Volta, which separates Ashanti from the German colony of Togoland. The southern frontier is conterminous with the northern frontier of the British Gold Coast colony. As of 1907, Ashanti had an estimated population of 500,000 and an area of 23,000 square miles.

## Asian Frontiers and Military Topography

In British India, the town and district of Kohat is located in the Peshawar division of the North-West Frontier Province, 37 miles south of Peshawar via the Kohat Pass. Kohat serves as the military base for the southern Afridi frontier, while Peshawar serves the northern frontier of the same tribe. In 1897-98, Kohat was the starting point for Sir William Lockhart's expedition against the Afridis and Orakzais.

The boundary between Afghanistan and Baluchistan begins at Domandi, where the Kundar and Gomal rivers meet. The boundary follows the Kundar stream southwest for 40 miles before diverging northwards toward Lake Ab-i-Istada. After 100 miles, it reaches the Kadanai river and turns the northern spurs of the Toba plateau. The boundary then follows the western edge of the Toba plateau, which reaches a height of 8,000 feet, near the New Chaman railway station. From 40 miles south of Chaman, the border is defined by hill ranges extending south to Nushki. The eastern boundary of this northern section of Baluchistan is the "red line" at the foot of the frontier hills, marking the border of British India. This creates a buffer system of independent tribes. While the Indian government maintains a similar level of non-interference with tribal chiefs in the Suliman Range as it does with northern chiefs, the British maintain a line of posts on the Zhob river. This allows them to command the hills and control communication with Afghanistan, promoting order on the southern frontier.

## European Landscapes and Borders

In Spain, the province of Burgos in the north is bounded by Santander (NW), Biscay and Álava (NE), Logroño (E), Soria (SE), Segovia (S), and Valladolid (SW). It includes the county of Treviño, which is entirely surrounded by Álava. The province's topography varies from mountainous districts in the north and northeast—including the Cantabrian chain and the Sierra de Neila—to the elevated plateau of Old Castile in the center and south. The highest peaks are in the Sierra de la Demanda, featuring the 6,995-foot Cerro de San Millan. The Pancorbo Pass, known as the "Iron Gates of Castile," provides a gap through low ranges for roads and railways. The region is noted for cold winter winds and snow. Key waterways include the non-navigable Ebro in the north and the Douro in the south, along with the Arlanzón and Pisuerga rivers.

In Italy, the Apennines dominate the landscape as a group of lofty masses. In the southern Apennines, Monte Pollino reaches 7,325 feet just within the frontier of Lucania. The mountains slope gradually toward the Gulf of Tarentum. The northern boundary is constituted by the Silarus (Sele) river, while the southern limit of the province is formed by the mouth of the Crathis.

In Central Europe, peace treaties have dictated the cession of Posen, West Prussia (left bank of the Vistula), and the greater part of Upper Silesia from Germany to Poland. A Field Boundary Commission of seven members was tasked with delimiting this boundary. Regarding East Prussia, plebiscites were used to fix the eastern and southern frontiers touching Poland, specifically in the regency of Allenstein and areas including the circles of Rosenberg, Stuhm, and parts of Marienwerder and Marienburg east of the Vistula.

## Classical and American Topography

In Greece, the topography of Pylus and Sphakteria has been a subject of historical debate. Thucydidês described the northern entrance to the bay of Navarino as admitting only two triremes and implying significant depth. However, modern observations show a width of about 150 yards with a sand bar limiting water depth to 18 inches. The southern entrance is currently 1,300 to 1,400 yards wide with depths ranging from five to thirty-three fathoms, whereas Thucydidês claimed it was only wide enough for eight or nine triremes abreast. In Athens, the Aglaurion is located on the north side of the acropolis, a position supported by Leake and Forchhammer, contradicting Siebelis who placed it on the eastern side.

In Central America, the topography of the region between the mountains and the sea limits the formation of navigable rivers. The San Juan river drains Lake Nicaragua, running 95 miles mostly along the frontier to the Colorado Mouth in Costa Rica. The Reventazon flows from the central plateau to the Caribbean Sea. On the Pacific coast, rivers are rapid and prone to flooding; the Tempisque river enters the Gulf of Nicoya, where it contributes alluvial deposits to a shallow inlet of 5-10 fathoms. The depression in the central plateau that separates the northern and southern mountains is indicated by the headwaters of the Reventazon and the Rio Grande de Tarcoles.
