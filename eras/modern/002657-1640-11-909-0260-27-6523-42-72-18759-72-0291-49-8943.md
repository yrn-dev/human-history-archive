## Political Events in England
In 1640, King Charles faced significant financial and military pressures. Following a revolt in Scotland—where Presbyterianism had been established as the national church—and the mutiny of English levies raised to fight the Scotch, Charles experienced insolvency. This financial state was described as the natural result of a "spirited" foreign policy.

Due to a lack of money and trustworthy troops, Charles summoned a Parliament in 1640, known as the Short Parliament, which he dismissed within the same year. He also attempted a Council of Peers at York in 1640. Subsequently, in November of that year, he summoned his final Parliament.

## Biographical Records
John Ball, a scholar and puritan divine born in Cassington, Oxfordshire, died on October 20, 1640. Ball had previously served as a tutor in Cheshire and a curate in Whitmore, Staffordshire, before being deprived of his position by John Bridgeman, the high church bishop of Chester. He later earned a reputation for piety and scholarship as a schoolmaster.
