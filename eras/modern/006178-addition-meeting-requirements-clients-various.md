# Requirements and Regulations in Professional Services

Professional services across architecture, law, and brokerage are governed by a combination of client requirements, statutory laws, and professional ethical standards. These regulations ensure that construction is safe, legal representation is competent, and financial transactions are transparent.

## Architectural Planning and Construction Regulations
In the field of architecture, buildings must be planned and constructed on clearly defined lines that satisfy both the requirements of the clients and the rules of controlling authorities. Different types of edifices are subject to different governing bodies:
*   **Public Schools:** In England, these are governed by the board of education.
*   **Churches:** These are governed by various financing societies, with the Incorporated Church Building Society exercising the strongest control.
*   **Factories:** In both France and England, factories must be erected to meet separate acts specific to those buildings.
*   **Dwelling-houses:** In London, building acts dictate the thickness of walls, drainage, roof inclination, materials used, and means of escape from fire. These laws specifically forbid the use of timber-framed buildings. However, in certain English districts where model by-laws are not in force, such as Letchworth, Herts, buildings can be erected with sound materials without being trammeled by by-laws.

Special requirements also apply to combined premises (shop and dwelling-house) in London; if the area exceeds 10 squares or 1,000 square feet, the stairs and a large portion of the building must use fire-resisting materials. Similarly, stairs and corridors in London flats must be fire-resisting under certain conditions, whereas timber buildings are permitted in parts of New York. Public buildings and theatres in New York, Paris, and London must conform to regulations regarding seating, exits, and the stage, including the flies and scenery dock.

Furthermore, fire insurance companies set requirements based on the building's size and the specific trade for which it is intended, adjusting premium rates accordingly.

## Architectural Process and Site Management
Once an architect has secured approval from the client and building authorities, they may face disputes with adjoining owners regarding party walls, air space, or ancient lights. In the city of London, these issues can lead to long suspensions of work and significant financial loss for the client. 

For large sites or those with uncertain soil, trial holes should be sunk immediately after sketch plans are approved. If the property is leasehold, the architect must negotiate approval from freeholders and senior lessors before serving notices regarding party walls. Contract plans must be meticulously prepared, including elevations, sections, and levels from a datum line, with larger scale details provided for principal portions of the building. If existing buildings are on the site, accurate detail plans and surveys are necessary, particularly regarding rights of adjoining owners and easements.

## Legal Ethics and Client Representation
Attorneys are bound by professional ethics standards requiring the exercise of diligence and competence. According to FRPC 4-1.1 and 4-1.3, competent representation generally necessitates the preparation, skill, thoroughness, and legal knowledge reasonably necessary for the case. 

Diligence requires a lawyer to act with promptness and "zeal" in advocating for a client, pursuing a matter despite personal inconvenience, obstruction, or opposition. However, this does not mandate that an attorney press for every possible advantage for the client. An attorney may achieve a flawed result but still be found to have met the requirements of the standard if sufficient competence and diligence were exercised throughout the representation.

Additionally, FRPC 4-4.1 mandates candor in dealing with others, prohibiting lawyers from knowingly making false statements of material fact or law to third persons. This includes omissions or partially true statements that are misleading. Government attorneys are further subject to the local federal court rules and state laws of the states where they perform their duties.

## Legal Proffers and Government Agreements
In specific legal contexts, such as proffer meetings between a client and the government, strict understandings are established. For example, a proffer is not a cooperation agreement. While a client may provide information for the government to evaluate for prosecutive decisions, the government is not obligated to enter into a plea agreement, immunity, or non-prosecution agreement.

Under such agreements, statements made during the meeting are generally not offered in the government's case-in-chief or sentencing proceedings, with exceptions for:
*   Prosecutions involving perjury, obstruction of justice, or false statements made during or after the meeting.
*   Instances where the client becomes a fugitive from justice.
*   The purpose of cross-examination if the client testifies.
*   Rebutting evidence or arguments offered by the client at any stage of criminal prosecution, including sentencing and bail.

## Brokerage and Market Rules
Contracts within the market are subject to rules that modify ordinary law regarding rights and liabilities. A significant modification occurs for brokers who are members of an exchange; between themselves, all members are considered principals, and agents are not recognized. A broker employed by a non-member is treated as buying for himself and is personally liable on the contract. 

In futures contracts, brokers must follow weekly settlement rules. If a principal fails to take delivery, the broker must compensate the selling member for the difference between the market price and the contract price. While brokers may negotiate specific terms of employment, exchanges often provide printed contract forms stating the contract is subject to exchange rules and that the broker acts as the principal.
