# Conservatoire

The term conservatoire refers to specialized institutions of higher learning dedicated to the arts, particularly music and drama. These institutions serve as centers for professional training, the preservation of musical instruments, and the cultivation of artistic talent across various European nations.

## Music Conservatoires in Belgium and Italy
In Belgium, the state provides royal conservatoires located in Ghent, Liege, Antwerp, and Brussels. In addition to these four royal institutions, there are sixty-nine minor conservatoires throughout the kingdom.

In Italy, conservatoires have historically served as primary training grounds for composers. For example, Gaetano Donizetti entered the conservatoire of his native city, Bergamo, where he studied under the operatic composer Simon Mayr. Similarly, Arrigo Boito studied music at the Milan Conservatoire. During his time there, Boito collaborated with Franco Faccio to write and compose a cantata titled *Le Sorelle d'Italia*, which achieved success.

## The French Conservatoire
The Conservatoire in France has functioned as a training center for both musicians and dramatic artists.

### Musical Training and Leadership
The institution has provided rigorous training for composers, such as Francois Clement Theodore Dubois, who studied under Ambroise Thomas. Dubois's tenure at the Conservatoire was extensive; he became a professor of harmony in 1871 and later a professor of composition in 1891, succeeding Leo Delibes. Upon the death of Ambroise Thomas in 1896, Dubois was appointed director of the Conservatoire.

### Dramatic Arts
The Conservatoire also trained actresses for the stage. Augustine Susanne Brohan entered the institution at age eleven, winning the second prize for comedy in 1820 and the first prize in 1821. Her daughter, Josephine Félicité Augustine Brohan, was also admitted to the Conservatoire at a very young age and twice received the second prize for comedy.

## Conservatoire Museums and Instrument Preservation
Conservatoires often maintain museums that preserve rare and historical musical instruments, serving as archives for the evolution of instrument design.

### The Paris and Brussels Collections
The Conservatoire National de Musique in Paris houses a museum containing rare instruments, including two flutes measuring 73 cm and 94 cm. Additionally, the museum of the Conservatoire in Paris holds two of the only three known existing specimens of a specific curious instrument marked with a trefoil.

The Brussels Conservatoire museum preserves various early musical specimens, including:
* A bass clarinet from the early period of the instrument's development, originally belonging to M. de Coussemaker or Adolphe Sax.
* A specimen of a bass clarinet created by Nicolas Papalini of Pavia, which features a serpentine bore pierced through two slabs of pear-wood and nineteen finger-holes.
