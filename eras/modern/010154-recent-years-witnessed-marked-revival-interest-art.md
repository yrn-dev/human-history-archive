# Revivals in Art, Literature, and Industry

The history of human development is characterized by various periods of revival, where interest in previous artistic methods, literary traditions, or intellectual forces is renewed. These revivals occur across diverse cultures and disciplines, ranging from the technical arts of bookbinding to the scholarly pursuit of classical literature.

## The Art of Bookbinding
In recent years, there has been a marked revival of interest in the art of bookbinding. This resurgence has occurred alongside a significant development in technical processes driven by the invention of delicate and ingenious machinery. These machines can now efficiently execute tasks that were previously performed laboriously by hand, including:
* Folding printed sheets and sewing them together on bands.
* Rounding the backs of books after sewing.
* Creating outer cases, covering them with leather or cloth, and stamping designs upon them.

While larger trade binders utilize these machines extensively to save time and labor, high-class trade binders generally do not depart from older methods. Private binders typically avoid machinery entirely. The older hand-labor methods are noted for being safer and stronger, and less likely to bruise or injure the paper, though they are slow and require highly skilled labor.

Modern binders face two primary difficulties: the prevalence of bad paper overladen with wood pulp and clay, and the use of modern leathers treated with sulphuric acid, which eventually rots the fiber. To address these "evils," the Society of Arts has appointed expert committees to report on the issues. Additionally, the decoration of covers (approximately 11-1/2 by 8 inches) has been successfully achieved using large metal dies or blocks.

## Literary and Intellectual Revivals
Revivals of literature and intellectual thought have appeared in various global contexts:

**Japan:** In 1689, Tsunayoshi encouraged Japanese classical studies by inviting Kitamura Kigin and his son Shuncho to Yedo, granting the former the title of Hoin and a revenue of five hundred koku. This initiated a vigorous revival of Japanese literature in the Bakufu capital. Simultaneously, in Osaka, the scholar Keichu Ajari published annotations of the Kokin-shu and Manyo-shu anthologies, which earned him the admiration and consideration of Tokugawa Mitsukuni, baron of Mito. These figures served as predecessors to Motoori and Kamo.

**Holland:** The final years of the 18th century saw a general revival of intellectual force in Holland. This period was influenced by the German romantic movement, which led German lyricism to replace French classicism in Dutch literature. Pieter Nieuwland (1764-1794) is identified as a feeble forerunner of this revival; although he recognized the inevitable transition, his indifferent powers and short life limited his influence. He published one volume of poems in 1788 and another posthumously in 1797.

## Art and Style in the Byzantine Era
The transition to the Byzantine style, the first mature Christian style, is viewed as an "eastern leaven" within Roman society and art. From the beginning of Byzantine art (approximately 450) to the Moslem conquest of Constantinople in 1453, the millennium is divided into three periods:

1. **The Classical Epoch:** This period includes the mature phase under Justinian (centered around 550) and lasted until approximately 850.
2. **The Orientalizing Period (850-1200):** Marked by orientalizing influences, particularly in architecture, due to isolation from the West. However, paintings and manuscripts (MSS.) experienced a distinct and successful classical revival. This era is represented by the works of Basil the Macedonian (867-886) in the imperial palace of Constantinople.
3. **The Return of Western Influence:** This final period was influenced by the establishment of Cistercian monasteries, though the art remained essentially Oriental-Christian.

In Byzantine painted books, such as the Joshua Roll of the Vatican and the *Natural History* of Dioscorides of Vienna (c. 500), classical survivals like personified rivers are evident. Later schools of art showed a marked classical revival in the 10th century, while others displayed Persian influence through intricate arabesques resembling eastern rugs.

## Other Historical Revivals
Revivals have also been noted in political prestige and industry:

**Egypt:** Following a period of decline—evidenced by the fact that the "petty monarch" Solomon could marry a daughter of a Pharaoh of the XXIst Dynasty on equal terms—there was a revival during the XXIInd dynasty. The founder, Pharaoh Shishak, took advantage of the cleavage between Judah and Israel to loot Jerusalem and subjugate Philistia.

**Incubation Industry:** Artificial incubation as an industry in America and Europe is a recent development. Around 1870, a marked revival of interest in fancy poultry, combined with population growth and the introduction of the breech-loading gun (which increased the scarcity of game birds), led to the creation of various successful incubation appliances. Earlier attempts include a 1824 incubator by Walthew, which used a double-walled metal box connected to a kitchen boiler or a special fire grate to circulate steam.
