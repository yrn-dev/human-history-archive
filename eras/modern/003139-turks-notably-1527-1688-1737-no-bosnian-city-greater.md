# Turks

The history of the Turks is characterized by extensive territorial conquests across the Balkans and Asia, prolonged military conflicts with European powers, and internal migrations.

## The Turkish Conquest of the Balkans
The Turkish expansion into the Balkan Peninsula began in 1340 with the ravaging of the Maritza valley. This was followed by the capture of Philippopolis in 1362 and Sofia in 1382. In 1366, the last Bulgarian tsar, Ivan Shishman III, was forced to become a vassal of Sultan Murad I and send his sister to the conqueror's harem. 

The fate of the peninsula was largely decided in 1389 following the rout of the Bosnians, Croats, and Servians at the field of Kossovo. Subsequently, the Turks attacked Ivan Shishman, capturing, burning, and sacking Trnovo in 1393 after a three-month siege. The final remnant of Bulgarian independence vanished in 1396 with the fall of Vidin, where Ivan's brother, Strazhimir, had established himself.

## Conflicts with Austria and the Balkans
The Turks were involved in long-term struggles with the Austrian monarchy. Between 1662 and 1664, a war occurred due to Ottoman aggression in Transylvania. This conflict ended on August 1, 1664, with a victory for Montecuculi over the grand vizier at St Gothard on the Raab. The resulting peace of Vasvar, signed on August 10, left the Turks in possession of the fortress of Ersekujvar and Nagyvarad, while Transylvania was recognized as an independent principality.

A subsequent war broke out in 1683 when the Turks, invited by Hungarian malcontents and encouraged by Louis XIV, overran Hungary and reached the walls of Vienna. On September 12, 1683, John Sobieski gained a victory over the Turks, saving the Austrian capital. This began a series of successes that drove Ottoman power from Hungary between 1683 and 1699.

In Bosnia, the city of Banjaluka became a site of frequent encounters between the Turks and Austrians, most notably in 1527, 1688, and 1737. By the last half of the 18th century, no other Bosnian city possessed greater importance or prosperity. Later, in 1831, Hussein Aga Borberli (known as the "Dragon of Bosnia" or *Zmaj Bosanski*) departed from Banjaluka to wage a holy war against Sultan Mahmud II.

## Other Regional Engagements
The Turks also engaged in conflicts with Venice. In 1688, during a war against the Venetians, the Turks sent Albanian troops into Attica, where they put the region to "fire and blood." This forced the inhabitants of Athens to seek refuge in Corinth, Ægina, and Salamis; many were unable to return to their fields and towns for three years.

In the early 16th century, the Turks were also opponents of Charles and Henry VIII.

## The Kizilbashes
The Kizilbashes, a term meaning "Red-Heads" in Turkish, are Shiitic Turkish immigrants from Persia. They were settled by Turkish sultans in districts ranging from Kara-Hissar along Amasia and Tokat to Angora during wars with Persia. Described as persianized Turks, they speak pure Persian and are known as industrious people working as scribes, physicians, and in trade. 

Kizilbashes are also found in Afghanistan, having immigrated during the time of Nadir Shah in 1737. In Afghanistan, they constitute the bulk of the amir's cavalry. Their name originates from Persia, referring to the red caps worn by Shiites.

## 20th Century Developments
In 1908, Austria annexed the Yugo-Slav provinces of Bosnia and Herzegovina from the Turk with the support of the German emperor. This annexation estranged Italy and contributed to a defensive understanding between Russia, France, and Britain.

During the same year, a military revolt occurred in the Monastir district led by two junior officers, Niazi Bey and Enver Bey. This movement, known as the Young Turk or constitutional party, proclaimed the restoration of the 1876 constitution on July 24, 1908.
