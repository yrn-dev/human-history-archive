# Practical Applications and Approximations in History and Science

Across various disciplines—including ballistics, structural engineering, diplomacy, and law—the concept of "practical" utility often necessitates the use of approximations, landmarks, or negotiated agreements when absolute precision is unattainable or unnecessary.

## Ballistics and Cordite Combustion
In the study of powder-gas and cordite, calculations are often based on assumed limits to reach a functional estimate. For instance, if a limit is fixed at 16 tons per sq. in. (corresponding to a G.D. of 0.2), the powder-gas occupies a volume of 1825 cub. in., with the shot advancing 3.225 ft. Given an average pressure of 8 tons per sq. in., the shot acquires a velocity of approximately 1020 f/s, meaning the time elapsed over 3.225 ft. at an average velocity of 510 f/s is about 0.0063 sec.

When this time is compared to experimental values of cordite burning, a closer approximation can be sought. However, if the agreement between these values is considered close enough for practical requirement, the combustion of the cordite is viewed as complete at stage P. In subsequent expansion, it is assumed that the gas follows an adiabatic law where pressure varies inversely as some $m^{th}$ power of the volume. For cordite, an average value of $m = 1.2$ is experimentally found to be a good standard.

## Structural Engineering of Bridges
In the design of railway bridges, the precise magnitude and distribution of loads are unknown because rolling stock varies significantly. Consequently, engineers assume a set of loads likely to produce straining more severe than any probable actual rolling loads.

For short bridges, the curve of maximum bending moments is drawn for a typical set of assumed loads. For longer bridges, the funicular polygon is used. Because extreme accuracy is unnecessary, a parabola can often be used to include the curve of maximum moments. Experience indicates that a parabola agrees closely enough for practical designing if it has the same ordinate at either the centre of the span or at one-quarter span as the curve of maximum moments.

## Diplomacy and International Agreements
Practical political and territorial control is often established through treaties and agreements. In the late 19th century, the "hinterland doctrine" was accepted by Germany and Great Britain in July 1887, placing Uganda within the British "sphere of influence." Despite German maps assigning the territory to Germany and Karl Peters leading a raiding expedition in 1889, an Anglo-German agreement on July 1, 1890, officially assigned Uganda to the British sphere.

Similarly, in Korea, Japan resumed its position as administrative adviser on February 23, 1904, through an agreement that guaranteed the country's integrity and the position of the imperial house. This was followed by a treaty on November 17, 1905, which gave Japan control over Korea's relations and foreign affairs.

In religious history, a practical agreement was patched up in 1436 between the moderate Hussites and the Council of Basle. This agreement allowed the Bohemian church to maintain certain distinctions from general Catholic practice until the sixteenth-century German Reformation.

## Legal and Historical Landmarks
In the study of history, certain events are used as "assumed landmarks" for convenience. For example, the edict of Justinian in A.D. 529, which formally closed schools of heathen philosophy, is used as a landmark for the transition of literary Hellenism, even though such landmarks do not always mark a definite transition from one order to another.

In legal contexts, practical arrangements may involve expedited processes. Benjamin, a legal professional in London, was called to the bar on June 6, 1866, after the influence of English judges allowed him to dispense with the usual three years of study.

In modern legal negotiations, practical deadlines are used to force conclusions. In the case of Mr. Epstein, a deadline of 5:00 the following day was proposed for a signed agreement to ensure sufficient time for negotiation before a Jewish holiday and the filing of charges on Tuesday, September 25. A proposed deal included two federal obstruction charges with a 24-month cap and a nonbinding recommendation for 18 months, followed by pleas to state offenses including those against minors and one year of community confinement or house arrest.
