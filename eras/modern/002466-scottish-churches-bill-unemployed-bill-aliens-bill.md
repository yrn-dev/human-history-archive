# Scottish Churches Bill, Unemployed Bill, and Aliens Bill

The Scottish Churches Bill, the Unemployed Bill, and the Aliens Bill were pieces of legislation passed during the parliamentary session of 1905. Their passage occurred during a period of significant political instability for the government led by Mr. Balfour.

## Political Context of 1905
The session of 1905 was characterized by a determined effort by the opposition to initiate debates in the House of Commons regarding the fiscal question. Mr. Balfour was equally determined to avoid these debates. This strategy of avoidance reached a peak when Mr. Balfour and his supporters departed from the House, which allowed a motion hostile to tariff reform to be passed *nem. con*.

## Legislative Outcomes
Despite the prevailing political tensions and the tactics used to avoid fiscal debates, the government successfully passed three specific measures: the Scottish Churches Bill, the Unemployed Bill, and the Aliens Bill.

However, other legislative efforts during this time were unsuccessful. A "complete fiasco" occurred regarding redistribution proposals. These proposals failed to please any party and were eventually withdrawn due to a procedural blunder.

## Government Instability and Decline
The passage of these bills did not secure the stability of the government. On July 17, a meeting of the party at the foreign office produced verbal assurances of loyalty. Nevertheless, two days later, the government was caught in a minority of four on the estimates for the Irish Land Commission. Following this event, there was a period of uncertainty regarding whether the government would dissolve or resign, though it was initially decided to hold on.
