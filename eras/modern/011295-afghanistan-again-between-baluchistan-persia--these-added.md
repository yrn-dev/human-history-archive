# Afghanistan and Baluchistan: Frontiers and Geography

The regions of Afghanistan and Baluchistan, situated between the Indus and the Persian frontier, have been the subject of extensive scientific geographical mapping and boundary demarcation since the late 19th century. This process has resulted in a series of professional maps extending from the Oxus to the Indus and from the Indus to the Euphrates.

## Boundary Demarcation and Political Status
Since approximately 1880, Asia has entered an era of defining politico-geographical limits and government responsibilities. Russia, Persia, Afghanistan, Baluchistan, India, and China have all revised their borders to establish a more assured basis for political relations.

Afghanistan is regarded as a distinct entity among Asiatic nations. Its north-eastern territory in the Pamirs reaches a point just short of 75 deg. E., following the water-divide to the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir and the water-parting of the Hindu Kush. The boundary leaves the Hindu Kush near the Dorah Pass, dividing Kafiristan from Bajour and Chitral, and crossing the Peshawar-Kabul route at Lundi-Khana. This demarcation creates a broad belt of independent territory—including Bajour, Swat, and part of the Mohmand territory north of the Kabul river—between Afghanistan and British India.

The principle of maintaining neutral intervening territory continues along Afghanistan's eastern borders down to the Gomal river. From the Gomal, Baluchistan serves as the intervening state between Afghanistan and British India.

## Administration of Baluchistan
Baluchistan is a country within the borders of British India, named after its dominant race. It extends from the Gomal river to the Arabian Sea, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. Unlike Afghanistan, Baluchistan is not a fully distinct entity; its independence is qualified by British control.

The region is divided into two main divisions:
*   **British Baluchistan:** A portion of British India under a chief commissioner, covering 9,403 sq. m. This area consists primarily of tracts ceded by Afghanistan under the 1879 treaty of Gandamak and formally declared part of British India in 1887. It officially includes the districts of Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali.
*   **Foreign Territories:** Administered or supervised by the same officer acting as agent to the governor-general. This area includes:
    *   **Directly-administered districts:** Including the eastern portion of the Quetta tahsil, the Bolan Pass, and the whole of the Chagai and Zhob political agencies, totaling 36,401 sq. m. Some of these are held on lease from the khan of Kalat.
    *   **Native States:** Including Las Bela and Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran), covering 79,382 sq. m.
    *   **Tribal Areas:** Covering 7,129 sq. m. in the possession of the Bugti and Marri tribes.

The Baluchistan Agency extends from the Persian frontier and the Gomal to the Arabian Sea. While some districts within the agency are as independent as those in Afghanistan, the overall political status of the province resembles that of the native states of the Indian peninsula, with supreme control exercised by the agent to the governor-general of India.

## Regional Geography and Flora
Afghanistan and Baluchistan form part of a great plateau extending from the Mediterranean to the Indus. This eastern half of the plateau is characterized as poor and unproductive, with a sparse, frequently nomadic population. The climate is extremely hot in summer and very severe in winter, with very scanty rainfall.

The flora of the less elevated parts of Afghanistan and Baluchistan is similar to that of southern Persia and the mountainous south-western regions of Arabia (Arabia Felix). This vegetation partakes of the character of the hotter Mediterranean region, where aromatic shrubs are abundant. Common trees include *Celtis*, *Pistacia*, *Dodonaea*, poplars, and the date palm. Dry hills are covered by prickly forms of *Astragalus* and *Statice*. In spring, bulbous plants and herbaceous vegetation appear, some of which are identical to those of the Syrian region and extend to the Himalaya.

Additionally, these regions are part of a continuous rainless desert flora that extends from the coast of Senegal and the Cape Verde Islands to the 75 deg. E. meridian, encompassing the shores of the Persian Gulf, south Persia, and the peninsula of Arabia.

## Strategic and Commercial Developments
The demarcation of the boundary between Baluchistan and Afghanistan begins at Domandi, at the junction of the Kundar and Gomal rivers. The line follows the Kundar stream south-west for 40 m. before diverging northwards toward Lake Ab-i-Istada. It eventually winds through the plains west of the Kojak, following the western edge of the Toba plateau (which reaches 8,000 ft.) near the New Chaman railway station.

The "red line" at the foot of the frontier hills defines the border of British India, creating a buffer system of independent tribes. While the Indian government interferes little with the internal jurisdiction of tribal chiefs in the Suliman Range, the occupation of a line of posts on the Zhob river allows the British to command the hills and control communications with Afghanistan.

The completion of surveys in Makran and Baluchistan, along with the settlement of Afghanistan's southern boundaries, has reopened at least one of the ancient trade routes between India and Seistan.
