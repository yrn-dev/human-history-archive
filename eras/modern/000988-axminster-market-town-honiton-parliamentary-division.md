# AXMINSTER

Axminster is a market-town located in the Honiton parliamentary division of Devonshire, England. It is situated on the river Axe, approximately 27 miles east by north of Exeter, and is accessible via the London & South-Western railway. As of 1901, the population of the town was 2,906.

## History and Origins
The town's name is derived from the river Axe and an old minster or abbey church, which is said to have been built by King Aethelstan. Evidence of very early settlement is indicated by the presence of numerous hill-fortresses and earthworks in the surrounding area, as well as the town's location at the intersection of two major ancient roads: the Fosse Way and Iknield Street.

According to Domesday, the king held Axminster. In 1246, the lord of the manor, Reginald de Mohun, founded a Cistercian abbey at Newenham within the parish of Axminster. De Mohun granted the abbey a fair on Midsummer day and a Saturday market; the midsummer fair continues to be held. In 1247, the manor and hundred of Axminster were made over to the monks from Beaulieu. The abbey was eventually dissolved in 1539. Local tradition also suggests that the battle of Brunanburh took place in the valley of the Axe and that the Danish princes who died during that action were buried in the Axminster church.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The minster, which is dedicated to St Mary the Virgin, features architectural styles ranging from the Perpendicular to the Norman. Within the chancel, there are three sedilia, a piscina under arches, and two freestone effigies that may date to the 14th century.

Located approximately 2 miles from the town is Coaxdon House, formerly known as St Calyst. This site is noted as the birthplace in 1602 of the Puritan historian Sir Symonds d'Ewes.

## Industry and Economy
Axminster was long renowned for the production of hand-woven carpets of admirable quality, crafted similarly to tapestry. This industry was established in 1755. Although the name "Axminster" is still preserved in relation to these carpets, the industry's seat was moved to Wilton near Salisbury.

Following the departure of the carpet industry, the inhabitants of Axminster found employment in various other sectors, including:
*   Iron foundries
*   Timber yards
*   Corn mills
*   Brush factories

Additionally, the town produces leather, cotton, drugget, tapes, and gloves.

## Transport and Connectivity
In addition to the London & South-Western railway, Axminster serves as the starting point for a light railway that terminates at the market town and watering-place of Lyme Regis.
