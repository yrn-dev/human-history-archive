# Theodore Faullain de Banville

## Early Life and Education
Theodore Faullain de Banville was a French miscellaneous writer and poet born on March 14, 1823, in Moulins, located in the Bourbonnais. He was the son of a captain in the French navy. Banville described his boyhood years as being spent cheerlessly at a lycee in Paris; while he noted that he was not treated harshly, he did not participate in the amusements of his peers. Upon completing his schooling, he possessed only slender means of support and consequently dedicated himself to the pursuit of letters.

## Literary Career and Poetry
Banville began publishing his verse in 1842 with his first volume, *Les Cariatides*, which was followed in 1846 by *Les Stalactites*. Although these early works faced some adverse criticism, they earned him the friendship and approbation of Jules Janin and Alfred de Vigny. 

Following these publications, Banville devoted his life to criticism and literary production. He published further volumes of poetry, including the *Odes funambulesques* (Alencon, 1857). These poems were dedicated to Victor Hugo, who provided them with unstinted praise. Banville's claim to remembrance is based primarily on his contributions to poetry.

## Prose and Dramatic Works
From 1853 onward, Banville produced a significant amount of prose. His industrious writing included sketches of well-known persons (*Camees parisiennes*, &c.), studies of Parisian manners, and a series of tales such as *Contes heroiques* and *Contes bourgeois*. Most of these prose works were republished in his collected works between 1875 and 1878. Additionally, he served as a dramatic critic for several newspapers and wrote extensively for reviews.

In the realm of drama, several of his verse comedies were produced on various stages, including the Theatre Francais. While these plays are noted for their refinement and distinction, they are considered deficient in dramatic power. Similarly, while his stories demonstrate fertility of invention, they are generally characterized as unreal and conventional. Much of his prose is described as being that of a journalist in substance, if not in manner.

## Personal Life and Honors
Banville spent the majority of his life in Paris, where his cultivated mind and genial character allowed him to form friendships with the leading men of letters of his era. He also maintained an intimate friendship with other famous actors, including Frederick-Lemaitre.

In recognition of his achievements, Banville was decorated with the legion of honour in 1858. He was later promoted to the rank of officer of the order in 1886.

## Death
Theodore Faullain de Banville died in Paris on March 15, 1891. At the time of his death, he had just completed his sixty-eighth year.
