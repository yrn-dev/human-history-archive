# Badenoch and the Highlands Deer Country

## Geography and Landscape of Badenoch
Badenoch is a district located in south-east Inverness-shire, Scotland. Its geographical boundaries are defined by the Monadhliath mountains to the north, the Cairngorms and Braemar to the east, the Grampians and Atholl to the south, and Lochaber to the west. While its total area is somewhat undefined, it is estimated to measure 15 miles from north to south and 36 miles from south-east to north-west.

The region is characterized as a wild mountainous tract, with many hills reaching heights exceeding 3,000 feet, except for the great glens and the valley of the Spey. The district is abundantly watered by the Spey and its various tributaries, and its principal lakes are Loch Ericht and Loch Laggan. The Highland railway traverses the area from Boat of Garten to Dalnaspidal.

## Deer Country and Natural Resources
Within the forests of Feshie, Gaick, Drumochter, and Alder, Badenoch contains some of the best deer country in the Highlands. Population centers in the district are few, grouping primarily at Kingussie and other locations near or on the Spey, and there are very few industries in the region.

## History of the Lordship
The lordship of Badenoch has a documented history of ownership involving several prominent figures and families:
* **The Comyns:** Owned the lordship from 1229 to 1313.
* **Alexander Stewart:** In 1371, Robert II granted the lordship to his son, Alexander Stewart (1343-1405), who was known as the "Wolf of Badenoch."
* **The Earls of Huntly:** After the lordship reverted to the crown, it was bestowed upon the 1st earl of Huntly in 1452. The title of lord of Badenoch continues to be held by the marquess of Huntly.
