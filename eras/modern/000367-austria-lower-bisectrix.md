# AUSTRIA LOWER to BISECTRIX

## Lower Austria
Lower Austria, known in German as *Niederoesterreich* or *Oesterreich unter der Enns* ("Austria below the river Enns"), is a crownland and archduchy of Austria. It is bounded to the north by Moravia and Bohemia, to the east by Hungary, to the west by Upper Austria and Bohemia, and to the south by Styria. The province covers an area of 7,654 square miles.

### Geography and Topography
The Danube river divides the province into two parts, entering at the westernmost point and exiting near Pressburg at the eastern extremity. The northern region, known as the *Waldviertel*, consists of low hilly country that continues the Moravian and Bohemian plateau. Notable elevations in this area include the Peilstein in the Weinsberger Wald (3,478 ft.), the Gfoehler Wald (2,368 ft.), and the Manhartsgebirge (1,758 ft.). Near Vienna, the Bisamberg reaches 1,180 ft.

The southern division is primarily hilly and mountainous, characterized by the Lower Austrian Alps. The highest summit in the province is the Schneeberg at 6,806 ft. Other principal groups include the Raxalpe (6,589 ft.), the Oetscher (6,205 ft.), the Duerrenstein (6,156 ft.), and the Voralpe (5,802 ft.). East of the Semmering ridge are the Leithagebirge (1,674 ft.) and the Wechsel (5,700 ft.). The Wiener Wald, which reaches 2,929 ft. at the Schoepfl, ends northwest of Vienna at the Leopoldsberg (1,380 ft.) and Kahlenberg (1,404 ft.).

### Hydrography and Climate
Lower Austria is part of the Danube watershed. Except for the Lainsitz, which flows into the Moldau, all rivers in the province are received by the Danube. Navigable rivers include the Danube, the March, and the Enns. Right-bank affluents include the Leitha, Fischa, Schwechat, Wien, Traisen, Pielach, Erlauf, Ybbs, and Enns. Left-bank affluents include the March, Goellersau, Kamp, Krems, and Isper. Notable small Alpine lakes include the Lunzer See and the Erlaufsee. The province is also known for various mineral springs: the thermal springs of Voeslau, the iron springs of Pyrawarth, the iodine springs of Deutsch-Altenburg, and the sulphur springs of Baden. The climate is generally healthy and moderate, though it is subject to rapid temperature changes.

### Economy and Industry
The province's land use consists of 43.4% arable land, 34.2% woods, 13.1% meadows and gardens, 3.2% pastures, and 2% vineyards. Vineyards on the sunny slopes of the Wiener Wald produce good wine. While cattle-rearing is not well developed, fish and game are plentiful. Mining is of slight importance, with graphite found near Muehldorf and small amounts of iron-ore and coal extracted in the Alpine foothill region.

Lower Austria is a leading industrial province alongside Moravia and Bohemia. Vienna serves as the center of commerce and industrial activity. Many factories are located in the region from the Wiener Wald to the Semmering to utilize water for motive power. Key industries include:
*   Textile and metallurgic industries
*   Chemicals, brewing, and milling
*   Paper, silk, and leather
*   Physical and musical instruments
*   Foodstuffs, tobacco, and sugar
*   Millinery, cloth, and *objets de luxe*

As of 1900, the population of Lower Austria was 3,100,493.

### Political History
In the late 19th century, political shifts occurred in Vienna, specifically the disappearance of the Liberals. In 1879, Liberals held 33 of the 37 seats returned in Lower Austria, but they were later largely replaced by Socialists. This shift affected the government's ability to maintain a strong party of moderate constitutionalists. During this period, Lueger was elected burgomaster after a two-thirds majority in the town council. Although the emperor initially refused to confirm the election and requested Lueger's withdrawal, Badeni later advised confirmation following the 1897 elections, which increased Lueger's followers in the Reichsrath. This era was marked by violent antipathy between German Nationalists and Christian Socialists.

## George Linnaeus Banks
George Linnaeus Banks (1821–1881) was a British miscellaneous writer born in Birmingham on March 2, 1821. After working in various trades, he began contributing to newspapers at age seventeen. He became a playwright, writing several lyrics, a couple of burlesques, and two plays. Between 1864 and 1848, he edited several newspapers, including the *Dublin Daily Express* and the *Birmingham Mercury*. He also published various volumes of verse and miscellaneous prose. He died in London on May 3, 1881.

## Bisectrix
In geometry, a bisectrix (from the Latin *bisector*, meaning to cut in two) is the same as a bisector. It refers to a line that divides an angle into two equal parts or a point that divides a line into two equal parts. In the field of crystallography, the term denotes the bisector of the angle between the optic axes.
