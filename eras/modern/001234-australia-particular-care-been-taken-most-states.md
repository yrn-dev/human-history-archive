# Protection of Infant Life and Child Welfare in Australia

## State Legislation and Oversight
In Australia, most states have implemented specific measures to prevent the evils associated with baby-farming. These efforts are characterized by state-level legislation designed to provide supervision over the care of infants who are boarded out by their mothers.

In South Australia, the State Children Act of 1895 established the State Children's Council. This body is granted extensive powers to oversee infants under the age of two who are boarded out. Similarly, New South Wales enacted the Children's Protection Act of 1892, which was later amended in 1902. This act requires state supervision of homes where children are boarded out. Victoria also passed an act in 1890 specifically for the purpose of making better provision for the protection of infant life.

## Regulation of Foster-Mothers
The systems in South Australia and New South Wales utilize the licensing of "foster-mothers," the term used for women who take in infants as boarders. Under the South Australian system, these licenses fix the number of children a foster-mother is authorized to keep. 

Licensed foster-mothers are required to maintain a detailed register. This register must include the following information for every child received:
* The child's name, age, and place of birth.
* The names, addresses, and descriptions of the parents, or any other person from whom the child was received or to whom the child was delivered.
* The specific dates of receipt or delivery.
* Particulars regarding any illness or accident involving the child.
* The name of the medical practitioner who attended the child, if applicable.

## Regional Context
Similar legislative efforts to protect infant life and regulate the adoption of children are found in New Zealand, specifically through the Adoption of Children Act 1895 and the Infant Life Protection Act 1896.
