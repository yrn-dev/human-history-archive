## English Political History
In 1640, King Charles faced significant political and financial instability. Following a revolt in Scotland—where Presbyterianism had been established as the national church in opposition to the half-Catholic, half-Protestant characteristics of the Church of England—and the mutiny of English levies raised to fight the Scotch, Charles encountered insolvency. 

Due to a lack of money and trustworthy troops, Charles summoned a Parliament in 1640, known as the Short Parliament, which he dismissed within the same year. He subsequently attempted a Council of Peers at York in 1640, and in November of that year, he summoned his final Parliament.
