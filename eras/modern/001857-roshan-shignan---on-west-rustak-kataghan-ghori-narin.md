# Roshan and Shignan

Roshan and Shignan are districts located within the broader region of Badakshan, a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan. While they are omitted from the list of chief provincial subdivisions of Badakshan—which include areas such as Rustak, Kataghan, Ghori, Narin, and Anderab to the west; Darwaz, Ragh, and Shiwa to the north; and Charan, Ishkashim, Zebak, and Wakhan to the east—they remain distinct geographical and cultural entities within the mountainous tracts of the region.

## Geography and Demographics
The districts of Roshan and Shignan are situated in the more mountainous tracts of Badakshan. The broader province is characterized by the Hindu Kush, which forms its southern edge as a great central upheaval or plateau. This plateau breaks into long spurs southwards, creating deep, rugged, and snow-capped altitudes that isolate the valleys of Kafiristan. To the north, the plateau slopes toward the Oxus river.

The population of the mountainous tracts, including Roshan and Shignan, consists of Tajiks of the Shiá creed. This distinguishes them from the population of "Badakshan Proper," which is composed of Sunnis—specifically Turks, Arabs, and Tajiks—who follow the orthodox doctrines of Mahomedan law.

## Language
The inhabitants of these districts possess their own separate provincial dialects or languages. Specifically, the dialect known as *Shighnáni* (also referred to as *Shighni*) is spoken in both Shignan and Roshan. This dialect differs materially from other regional languages, such as *Ishkáshami* (spoken in Ishkásham), *Wakhi* (spoken in Wakhán), *Minjáni* (spoken in Minján), and *Sanglichì* (spoken in Sanglich and Zebák). While the people of these mountainous areas use their own dialects, the inhabitants of the principal places often combine this knowledge with the Persian language.

## Historical and Legendary Pedigree
The chiefs of Roshan and Shignan, along with the chiefs of Wakhán, Darwáz, Karátegín, Gilgít, Chitrál, Swát, and Khapolor in Bálti, have claimed a legendary pedigree tracing their ancestry back to Alexander the Great. This claim is noted by earlier Eastern authors and by Baber.

## Regional Associations
The region has been associated with various nomadic and political groups over time. The Kara-Kirghiz, a nomadic people who have occupied the uplands along the Russo-Chinese frontier since at least the 13th century, have utilized the area. Their range extends from the Ala-tau range and the Issyk-kul basin to the uplands draining toward the Oxus and Jaxartes, specifically including Shignan and Karateghin southwards to the Pamir table-land, which they visit during the summer months.
