# Territories and Areas of Asia

## Territorial Distribution and Area
The total area of Asia, encompassing both continental and insular regions, is estimated to be somewhat over 16,819,000 square miles, although various authorities provide differing detailed estimates. This vast region is divided among several extra-Asiatic powers, independent territories, and nominally independent states.

## Population Distribution
The total population of Asia is roughly 823,000,000. The largest concentrations of this population are found in Chinese territory, with 330,000,000 inhabitants, followed by British territories with 302,000,000, and Russian territories with 25,000,000.

## Regional Geography and Plateaus
A significant portion of Asia west of British India (excluding Syria and Arabia) consists of an extensive plateau. This region is as large as Tibet, though it exists at a lower altitude. This high region is nearly conterminous with Armenia, Asia Minor, Iran (or Persia), Baluchistan, and Afghanistan.

The boundaries of this plateau are defined by the following:
*   **Southern Border:** Runs along the Tigris, the Persian Gulf, and the Arabian Sea, extending westward to the north-east angle of the Levant.
*   **Northern Border:** Follows nearly 36 deg. N. to the Black Sea, the Sea of Marmora, and the southern shore of the Caspian.
*   **Eastern Flank:** Defined by a line of hills near the Indus between the Himalaya and the river's mouth, roughly on the 72nd meridian. While these hills generally do not exceed 5,000 ft., some summits reach 10,000 ft. or more.
*   **Southern and South Western Face:** Follows the Persian Gulf coast from the mouth of the Indus. Further west, it is formed by a mountain scarp that flanks the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, rising in many points to 10,000 ft. This scarp extends through Armenia and Kurdistan to nearly the 40th meridian before turning along the Taurus range.

In the north east, the Afghan table-land meets Tibet and the Himalaya, forming a continuous mountain mass between the 34 deg. and 36 deg. N. latitudes and the 71st and 72nd meridians. An unbroken range of mountains stretches from the intersection of the 36th parallel and 71st meridian toward the Caspian in the west and the northern slope of the Tibetan plateau in the north east. This range separates the headwaters of the Indus and its Kabul tributary (which flow into the Arabian Sea) from the headwaters of the Oxus (which flow to the Aral Sea).

## The Khanate of Khiva
Khiva, formerly an important Asian kingdom, is now a reduced khanate dependent upon Russia. It is located in the delta of the Amu-darya (Oxus). The territory is 200 miles long from north to south and reaches a maximum width of 300 miles. Its frontiers are defined by the left bank of the Amu from 40° 15´ N., extending to Lake Aral, then along the south coast of the lake for about 40 miles, and finally southwards along the Ust-Urt plateau escarpment.

The khanate is divided into two primary areas:
*   **The Khiva Oasis:** Covering 5210 sq. m. with an estimated population of 400,000. This area is very fertile due to the Amu's water and a thin layer of deposited ooze. Crops include cotton, vine, rice, wheat, barley, millet, oats, flax, hemp, peas, madder, vegetables, and especially melons.
*   **The Steppes:** Estimated at 17,000 sq. m. with a nomadic population of 400,000. Nomads in this region extensively breed cattle, horses, camels, and sheep.

## Historical Cartographic Perspectives
Historical records indicate periods of confusion regarding Asian geography. A work printed in 1533 described territories beyond Ptolemy’s Tables, attributing the discovery of countries beyond 180° of East Longitude to others and the Venetian Marco Polo. This text erroneously grouped various regions under the part of Asia, including Cathay, Tangut, the Desert of Lop, the realm of Mexico (which it claimed older travellers called Quinsay), Florida, the Bachalaos (Newfoundland), Paria, Uraba, and the countries of the Canibals. Additionally, a 1589 dedication by Robert Parke identified Japan and China with regions written about long ago by Sir John Mandeuill and Paulus Venetus.
