# President Melgarejo and the Political History of Bolivia

The political landscape of Bolivia has been characterized by periods of instability, military revolutions, and shifting diplomatic relations with neighboring republics. The presidency of Melgarejo and the subsequent administration of Colonel Augustin Morales represent a specific era of transition within this history.

## The Presidency and Fall of Melgarejo
President Melgarejo held power until January 1871. At that time, he was deposed and driven from the country following a revolution. This uprising was headed by Colonel Augustin Morales, who succeeded Melgarejo as president. Morales's tenure was short-lived, as he was murdered in November 1872. Following the death of Morales, Colonel Adolfo Ballivian assumed the presidency, serving until his own death in 1874.

## Diplomatic Relations and the Secret Agreement with Peru
Under the presidency of Adolfo Ballivian, Bolivia entered into a secret agreement with Peru. This pact was designed for the mutual defense of both countries and the preservation of their territorial integrity, specifically against the possibility of further encroachment by Chile.

The motivations for this agreement were rooted in previous treaties and economic discontent. In 1866, a treaty between Chile and Bolivia established the 24th parallel as the boundary of Chilean territory. Under this instrument, Chile was granted full facilities for trading on the coast between the 23rd and 24th parallels and received a half share of the customs in that region. Additionally, Chile was permitted to export products and mine in this district without hindrance or tax from Bolivia.

Further tension arose in 1870 when Bolivia granted the right to work certain nitrate deposits north of the 24th parallel to an Anglo-Chilean company in exchange for $10,000. The transfer of great wealth into Chilean hands caused significant discontent within Bolivia. Similarly, Peru was displeased with the establishment of Chilean capital in the rich district of Tarapaca. These factors culminated in the secret agreement signed with Peru on February 6, 1873.

## Earlier Political Transitions and Conflicts
The instability seen in the late 19th century mirrored earlier struggles for power in the republic. Following the independence of the country, the government faced repeated risings. In 1827, General Sucre and Colombian troops were driven from La Paz. A new congress formed at Chuquisaca in April 1828 modified the constitution provided by Bolivar and appointed Marshal Santa Cruz as president. However, a revolution led by General Blanco overturned the government a year later.

Order was restored in 1831 when Santa Cruz promulgated a code of laws and organized the country's financial affairs. During this period, he concluded a commerce treaty with Peru. In 1835, Santa Cruz intervened in a power struggle in Peru, defeating General Gamarra and naming himself protector of Peru in 1836 with the intent of creating a confederation of the two nations. This prompted interference from Chile, which supported Gamarra. After three years of fighting, Santa Cruz was defeated in June 1839 at the battle of Jungay and subsequently exiled.

Following Santa Cruz's exile, General Velasco became the provisional chief of Bolivia, but he was soon overthrown by the Santa Cruz party, which installed General Ballivian in power. In August 1841, General Gamarra attempted to annex the province of La Paz, but his army was routed and he was killed in a battle with Ballivian. Ballivian remained president until 1848, after which General Belzu led a successful military revolution.
