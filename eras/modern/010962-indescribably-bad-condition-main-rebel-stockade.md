# The Relief of Kumasi and the Capture of the Rebel Stockade

## The Garrison at Kumasi
Following a period of conflict, a force consisting of 100 Hausa soldiers and three white men—Dr. Hay, Mr. Ralph, and Captain Bishop—were stationed at the Kumasi fort. This garrison was provided with rations intended to last for three weeks. As the situation developed, the garrison became war-worn, and by the time they were reached by relief forces, most of the defenders were too weak to stand. The environment surrounding the fort was devastated, characterized by putrid bodies and houses that had been burnt down.

## The Relief Expedition
Colonel James Willcocks organized a relief expedition at Cape Coast, arriving there from Nigeria on May 26. The mission faced appalling difficulties, including a lack of local food supplies and a scarcity of carriers. The operation was further hindered by the peak of the rainy season, which rendered the bush almost impenetrable and turned all roads into deep mire. Additionally, the opposing forces were described as cunning and brave, utilizing concealed stockades for their defense.

Colonel Willcocks was unable to advance to Fumsu until July 2. Upon learning of the straits of the Kumasi garrison and the escape of the governor the following day, he determined to relieve the fort. On July 9, he reached Bekwai, where the local king had remained loyal.

## Strategic Maneuvers and Assault
To divert the enemy, Colonel Willcocks spread a report stating that he would attack Kokofu, located east of Bekwai, on July 13. This deception successfully drew several thousand enemy combatants away from Kumasi. After performing a feint toward Kokofu, the Colonel suddenly marched west.

Following smart fighting on July 14, the relief column continued its progress. On July 15, after marching since daybreak over roads described as being "in indescribably bad condition," the forces encountered the main rebel stockade. The stockade was carried at the point of the bayonet by Yoruba troops, who demonstrated an *elan* and capability equal to that of the Hausa.

## Entry into Kumasi
The relief force entered Kumasi on the evening of July 15. As the column came into view, a bugler from the garrison sounded the "general salute." The relieving force consisted of 1,000 fighting men, all of whom were West Africans, supported by 60 non-commissioned and white officers. Their armament included six Maxims, four seven-pounder guns, and two 75-millimetre guns.
