# Johann Bernhard Basedow and Philanthropinism

## Biography of Johann Bernhard Basedow
Johann Bernhard Basedow (1723–1790) was a German educational reformer. Born in Hamburg on September 11, 1723, he was the son of a hairdresser. His early education took place at the Johanneum in Hamburg, where he was influenced by the rationalist H. S. Reimarus, the author of the *Wolfenbuetteler Fragmente*. 

In 1744, Basedow moved to Leipzig to study theology, though he eventually devoted himself entirely to the study of philosophy. This period of study initially led him toward sceptical notions. However, after a more profound examination of sacred writings and related materials, he returned to the Christian faith. Despite this return, Basedow formed his beliefs according to his own ideas in retirement, resulting in views that were far from orthodox.

## Professional Career and Theological Conflict
Between 1749 and 1753, Basedow served as a private tutor for a nobleman's family in Holstein. During this time, he demonstrated remarkable abilities as an educator of the young. His distinction in this field led to his appointment in 1753 as a professor of belles-lettres and moral philosophy at the academy of Soro in Denmark.

Basedow's career was frequently impacted by his theological opinions. In 1761, he was removed from his post in Soro and transferred to Altona. In Altona, several of his published works caused him to fall into great disfavour with the orthodox clergy. While he was forbidden from providing further instruction, he was permitted to keep his salary.

## Educational Reform and Philanthropinism
Toward the end of 1767, Basedow abandoned the study of theology to focus his efforts on education. He conceived a project for a general reform of education within Germany. In 1768, he published *Vorstellung an Menschenfreunde fuer Schulen, nebst dem Plan eines Elementarbuches der menschlichen Erkenntnisse*. This work was strongly influenced by Rousseau's *Emile*.

Through this publication, Basedow proposed the following reforms:
* The reform of schools.
* The reform of common methods of instruction.
* The establishment of an institute dedicated to qualifying teachers.

To support these goals, Basedow solicited subscriptions for the printing of his *Elementarwerk*, a work intended to illustrate and explain his principles at length using plates. He successfully raised 15,000 Talers (£2250) for this purpose. In 1774, he published the *Elementarwerk* in four volumes, which presented a complete system of primary education designed to develop intelligence.

## Related Scholarship
The relationship between Basedow and Rousseau, as well as the broader movement of philanthropinism, has been the subject of various historical and biographical studies. Notable works include G. P. R. Hahn's *Basedow und sein Verhaltnis zu Rousseau* (Leipzig, 1885), A. Pinloche's *Basedow et le philanthropinisme* (Paris, 1890), and C. Goessgen's *Rousseau und Basedow* (1891). Other biographical accounts include works by H. Rathmann (1791) and J. C. Meyer (1791-1792).
