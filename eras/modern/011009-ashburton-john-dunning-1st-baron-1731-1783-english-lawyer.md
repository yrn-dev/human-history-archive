# ASHBURTON, JOHN DUNNING, 1ST BARON

John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was a prominent English lawyer and politician. Born on October 18, 1731, at Ashburton, Devonshire, he was the second son of John Dunning, an attorney. He received his early education at the free grammar school in his native town.

## Legal Career
Dunning began his professional training as an articled clerk to his father. At the age of nineteen, he was admitted to the Middle Temple and was subsequently called to the bar in 1756. His early entry into practice was slow; for several years, he worked the western circuit without receiving a single brief. During this period of limited employment, he was known to have a friendship with Lloyd Kenyon (later 1st Baron Kenyon), who became known to attorneys through answering Dunning's cases.

Dunning's reputation changed in 1762 when he was hired to draft a defense for the British East India Company against grievances memorialized to the crown by the Dutch East India Company. The "masterly style" of this document brought him both emolument and reputation. In 1763, he further distinguished himself as counsel for Wilkes. His reputation was firmly established following his powerful argument against the validity of general warrants in the case of *Leach v. Money* on June 18, 1763. Following this success, his practice grew significantly, and by 1776, it is reported that he earned nearly £10,000 per annum.

## Political Career and Public Office
Dunning entered public service in 1766 when he was chosen as the recorder of Bristol. In December 1767, he was appointed solicitor-general, a position he held until May 1770, when he retired alongside his friend Lord Shelburne. In 1771, he was granted the freedom of the city of London.

His parliamentary career began in 1768 when he was elected as the member for Calne, a borough he represented until his promotion to the peerage. Dunning became a regular member of the opposition and was noted for delivering many able speeches in parliament. In 1780, he carried a motion by a majority of eighteen stating that the influence of the crown "had increased, was increasing, and ought to be diminished."

While Dunning strongly opposed the system of pensions and sinecure officers, the archive notes that his probity did not prevent him from utilizing the system himself. In 1782, under the prime ministry of the marquis of Rockingham, Dunning was appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster, which was a rich sinecure. Around this same time, he was advanced to the peerage.

## Death and Legacy
John Dunning died in 1783. He is memorialized by a tablet in the church of St. Andrew in Ashburton, which features an inscription written by Dr. Johnson. The title of Baron Ashburton was later held by Alexander Baring (1774–1848), who was the husband of Dunning's niece.
