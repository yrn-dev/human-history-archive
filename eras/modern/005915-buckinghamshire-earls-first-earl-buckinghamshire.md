# EARLS OF BUCKINGHAMSHIRE

The earldom of Buckinghamshire is a title held by the Hobart family, distinct from the earldom of Buckingham. The lineage is rooted in a family of legal professionals; the first earl was a descendant of Sir James Hobart (d. 1507), who served as attorney-general to Henry VII, and Sir Henry Hobart (d. 1625), who was attorney-general and chief justice of the common pleas under James I and was made a baronet in 1611.

## Early Earls and the Hobart Lineage
John Hobart (c. 1694–1756), a son of the 4th baronet Sir Henry Hobart, was created Baron Hobart of Blickling in 1728. He later served as lord-lieutenant of Norfolk in 1740 and was created earl of Buckinghamshire in 1746. His sister, Henrietta Howard, countess of Suffolk, was the mistress of George II. Upon his death on September 22, 1756, he was succeeded by his eldest son, John (1723–1793), the 2nd earl.

The 2nd earl served as the comptroller of the royal household and as a member of parliament for Norwich. His career included diplomatic and administrative roles, serving as ambassador to Russia from 1762 to 1766 and as lord-lieutenant of Ireland from 1776 to 1780, though he was considered hardly equal to the difficulties of the latter position. Because he died without sons on August 3, 1793, the title passed to his half-brother, George (c. 1730–1804), who became the 3rd earl. However, the 2nd earl's Norfolk estates and Blickling Hall passed to his daughter, Henrietta (1762–1805), who married William Kerr, the 6th marquess of Lothian.

## Robert Hobart, 4th Earl
The 4th earl, Robert Hobart (1760–1816), was the eldest son of the 3rd earl. A soldier and member of both the Irish and English Houses of Commons, he served as chief secretary to the lord-lieutenant of Ireland from 1789 to 1793, where he worked to prevent concessions to Roman Catholics. In 1793, while known by the courtesy title of Lord Hobart, he was appointed governor of Madras, though he was recalled in 1798 following serious differences with the governor-general of India, Sir John Shore.

Returning to British politics, Robert Hobart was called to the House of Lords in 1798 and succeeded to the earldom in 1804. He supported the union between Ireland and England and held several high offices: secretary for war and the colonies from March 1801 to May 1804 (the city of Hobart Town in Tasmania is named after his family), chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster in 1805, and joint postmaster-general. From 1812 until his death on February 4, 1816, he served as president of the Board of Control.

## Later Successions and the Hampden Connection
The 4th earl left no sons, and the title passed to his nephew, George Robert Hobart (1789–1849), the 5th earl. George Robert Hobart was the son of George Vere Hobart (1761–1802), who had been the lieutenant-governor of Grenada. In 1824, the 5th earl inherited the Buckinghamshire estates of the Hampden family. This connection stemmed from an earlier marriage around 1655 between his ancestor, Sir John Hobart, 3rd baronet, and Mary Hampden. Consequently, the 5th earl took the name of Hampden.

Following the death of the 5th earl in February 1849, he was succeeded by his brother, Augustus Edward Hobart (1793–1884), the 6th earl, who adopted the name Hobart-Hampden in 1878. The 6th earl was survived by his grandson, Sidney Carr Hobart-Hampden (b. 1860), who became the 7th earl and added the name Mercer-Henderson to his own. The 6th earl's two sons, Frederick John Hobart (1821–1875) and Vere Henry, Lord Hobart (1818–1875)—who served as governor of Madras starting in 1872—both predeceased their father. Another son of the 6th earl was Augustus Charles Hobart-Hampden, known as Hobart Pasha.

## Distinctions and Confusions
Historical records indicate some confusion between the earldom of Buckinghamshire and the earldom of Buckingham. Until 1784, the 2nd earl of Buckinghamshire frequently signed himself as "Buckingham," and was known by that name by his contemporaries. This confusion is further complicated by the separate creation of the marquess of Buckingham in 1784 for George Grenville, Earl Temple.
