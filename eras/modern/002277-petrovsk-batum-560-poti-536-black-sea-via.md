# Petrovsk

## Geography and Location
Petrovsk is a port located on the west shore of the Caspian Sea. It is situated within a region of Russian Transcaucasia that serves as a transit point for goods and resources moving between the Caspian and Black Seas.

## Transportation and Connectivity
Petrovsk is a key node in the rail network connecting the Caspian region to other parts of the empire. It is part of a rail line that connects Baku to the south Russian railway system at Beslan. This route runs via Petrovsk and Derbent, eventually reaching Vladikavkaz, which is located 400 miles away.

Furthermore, the rail system extends from the Caspian Sea through Tiflis and the Suram tunnel to Kutais. From Kutais, the line continues to the Black Sea ports of Poti and Batum. This infrastructure allows for the transport of heavy oils in tank railway-cars from the petroleum fields of Baku to Poti, Batum, and Novorossiysk.

## Regional Trade and Logistics
As a port on the west shore of the Caspian, Petrovsk contributes to the maritime traffic of the region. This traffic was significantly increased by the opening of the Transcaspian railway in 1886 and the development of the petroleum industry on the Apsheron peninsula. 

The broader network involving ports like Petrovsk, Baku, and Derbent facilitates the movement of raw cotton from Ferghana, which is shipped for use in mills in central and southern Russia and other western countries. Additionally, the Russian empire utilizes these sea routes to draw supplies of petroleum for liquid fuel and lighting.
