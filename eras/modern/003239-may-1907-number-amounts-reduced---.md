# Banking and Economic Developments of 1907

The year 1907 was marked by significant shifts in financial circulation, legislative changes in corporate and agricultural sectors, and a major financial crisis in the United States. These events spanned various regions, including Ireland, the United Kingdom, and several American states.

## Irish Bank Circulation
In May 1907, the number and amounts of bank issues in Ireland were reduced. The authorized and actual issues for private and joint-stock banks were as follows:
*   **Private Banks:** 12 banks with an authorized issue of £482,744 and an actual issue of £122,536.
*   **Joint-Stock Banks:** 17 banks with an authorized issue of £1,084,836 and an actual issue of £437,693.

The actual circulation of these banks remained significantly lower than the authorized issue because their circulation was limited to very small areas. Notes from country banks were largely unknown outside the immediate vicinity of their issuance; while they were payable in London, cashing them often presented considerable difficulty.

## The Panic of 1907 in the United States
A financial panic occurred in 1907, precipitated by over-speculation by a group of national banks. This led to the suspension of the Knickerbocker Trust Company on October 22, which held deposits of $48,000,000. Subsequent runs on other companies followed, and in the week of November 2, New York banks faced a deficit in required reserves totaling $38,838,825. To combat this, arrangements were made to import foreign gold, with amounts approaching $100,000,000.

The crisis caused a severe strain on the cash resources of New York City banks, though it did not result in as many failures as the panic of 1893. On October 28, 1907, the payment of cheques in currency was suspended in New York, a state that continued until approximately the beginning of 1908. 

During the panic, currency added to or disappeared from circulation amounted to more than $275,000,000, nearly one-tenth of the country's usual circulation volume. This included a $72,000,000 transfer from the treasury to the banks and an autumn increase of about $77,000,000 in national bank circulation. By December 28, 1907, total bank-note circulation had risen to an abnormal level of $687,340,835, which was reduced during the spring of 1908.

## National Banking and Reserves
Under the national banking system, reserves against deposits were required at a rate of 25% in "reserve cities" and 15% in "country banks" outside those cities. New York, Chicago, and St. Louis served as the three central reserve cities where cash was required. In 1908, there were forty other reserve cities where banks could deposit half their cash in national banks within central reserve cities; country banks were permitted to deposit three-fifths of their cash in any reserve city.

The gold standard law of March 14, 1900, allowed banks to issue circulation to the full par-value of deposited bonds. It also reduced the tax on circulation from 1% to 1/2 of 1% for circulation secured by 2% refunding bonds. Consequently, the volume of notes outstanding secured by bonds rose steadily from $207,920,774 on October 31, 1899, to $562,727,614 by 1907.

## Regional Industry and Legislation
In 1907, several legislative and industrial milestones were reached in the United States:
*   **Transportation:** A law went into effect establishing a maximum railway fare of two cents a mile. In Chicago, the movement toward municipal ownership of street railways appeared to lose momentum after municipal ownership candidates were defeated in the city's elections.
*   **Agriculture:** Illinois remained a leader in agricultural production. In 1907, it ranked first in the production of Indian corn, producing 9,521,000 bushels out of a national total of 99,931,000. However, it was surpassed by Iowa in the production of oats, with the Illinois crop totaling 101,675,000 bushels.
*   **Mining:** In Kentucky, the total value of mineral products in 1907 was $19,294,341. The state ranked eighth among coal-producing states, with a total output of 10,753,124 short tons. The western district produced 6,295,397 tons, while the eastern district produced 4,457,727 tons. Kentucky also led the Union in cannel coal production, with an output of 77,733 tons in 1907 (including 4,650 tons of semi-cannel coal).
*   **Legal Rulings:** In 1907, the Supreme Court of Kansas ruled that giving a hat-pin to each purchaser was not an illegal "gift enterprise" because it lacked chance or lot. Conversely, in Oklahoma, it was held that contracts for payment where the return value depended on chance constituted a lottery.
