# Sir Harry Hamilton Johnston

Sir Harry Hamilton Johnston (D.Sc., G.C.M.G., K.C.B.) was a British explorer, linguist, and official who played a significant role in the exploration and political organization of Africa during the late 19th century.

## Exploration and Diplomacy in Africa
Sir H.H. Johnston conducted extensive exploration in East and Central Africa. In 1884, he explored Kilimanjaro. During that same year, he concluded the first treaties with native chiefs in the Kilimanjaro region on behalf of a British company. Although these concessions in the Kilimanjaro district were initially disregarded, they occurred during a period of increasing competition for influence in the region, particularly as Germans acquired numerous concessions on the East African coast between 1884 and 1885.

In 1889, Johnston was dispatched to address a dispute between the African Lakes Corporation and a number of Arabs who had established themselves at the north end of Lake Nyasa. His mission was to attempt a possible arrangement to resolve this conflict and to ensure that friendly native chiefs were protected from Portuguese aggression beyond a specific point. The treaties he negotiated and the efforts he undertook resulted in the creation of a British sphere of influence and protectorate north of the Zambezi.

## Linguistic and Anthropological Studies
Beyond his diplomatic and exploratory work, Sir Harry Johnston contributed to the study of languages and anthropology. He authored a book titled *The Opening up of Africa*, published in the Home University Library, which discusses the subject of philological history. He also wrote *Comparative Study of the Bantu and Semi-Bantu Languages*. In the field of linguistics, Johnston provided observations on the Keltic group of languages, suggesting that they combined a Berber (or Iberian) grammar with an Aryan vocabulary.

Johnston also documented the characteristics of various indigenous peoples. He provided descriptions of the Bambute, a race of pygmies located in the Semliki Forest on the western borders of the Uganda Protectorate. Johnston noted that the Bambute possess a skill for drawing, using sharpened sticks to sketch birds and beasts in mud or sand. He also observed that the skin of the Bambute emits a rancid odor, which he characterized as being between the smell of a negro and a monkey.
