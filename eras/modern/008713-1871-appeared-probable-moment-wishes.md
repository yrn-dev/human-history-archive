# Political and Cultural Events of 1871

The year 1871 was marked by significant diplomatic efforts in Japan, constitutional negotiations within the Habsburg Empire, and various literary publications across Europe.

## Bohemian Constitutional Negotiations
In 1871, there was a brief period where it seemed probable that the desires of the Bohemians would be fulfilled. The Bohemians sought the re-establishment of their ancient constitution in a modernized form. Count Karl Hohenwart, the new Austrian prime minister, took office with a firm intention to reach an agreement between Bohemia and other regions of the Habsburg empire. 

This led to prolonged negotiations aimed at creating a constitutional system that would satisfy Bohemian claims while maintaining a firm connection to the other lands ruled by the house of Habsburg. On September 14, 1871, an imperial message was sent to the diet of Prague. In this message, the sovereign recognized the rights of the kingdom of Bohemia, citing the constant fidelity of its population and the power and glory the Bohemian crown had provided to his ancestors. The sovereign further expressed a willingness to confirm this assurance by taking the coronation oath.

Despite these efforts, the attempt to reconcile Bohemia with Austria failed due to various influences. By 1872, a government with a pronounced German tendency took office in Vienna, leading the Bohemians to temporarily refuse attendance at the parliamentary assemblies of Prague and Vienna.

## Japanese Treaty Negotiations
Following the Meiji Restoration, the Japanese government sought to revise early treaties that had provoked indignation among conservative statesmen in Kyoto. Specifically, Japan aimed to remove the clause regarding consular jurisdiction and restore the customs tariff to the level it had been before the naval demonstration at Hyogo by Sir Harry Parkes.

The Japanese government was entitled to raise these issues in 1871, as the treaties were textually subject to revision that year. Consequently, an embassy was dispatched to the Occident to negotiate these changes. However, the embassy failed because the conditions that had originally necessitated extraterritorial jurisdiction had not changed by 1871 to justify its abolition. During this time, the consular courts were criticized; while some Western powers had expert judicial officials, others entrusted consular duties to merchants who lacked legal training and were often involved in the same commercial transactions they were required to adjudicate.

## Literary Publications of 1871
The year 1871 saw the release of several notable literary works and autobiographical accounts:

*   **Grosse:** The author published *Maria Mancini* (a novel) and released six volumes of *Erzahlende Dichtungen* in Berlin between 1871 and 1873.
*   **Benedix:** An autobiography of Benedix was published in the *Gartenlaube* for 1871.
*   **Kriloff:** A second edition of W. R. S. Ralston's English prose version of Kriloff's *Fables* was published in 1871.
