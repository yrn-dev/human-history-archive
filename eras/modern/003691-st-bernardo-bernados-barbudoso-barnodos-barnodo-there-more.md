# St Bernardo, Bernados, Barbudoso, Barnodos and Barnodo

The names St Bernardo, Bernados, Barbudoso, Barnodos, and Barnodo appear as various renderings of the name of the island of Barbados on maps from the 16th century.

## Etymology and Early History of Barbados
Opinions regarding the derivation of the name "Barbados" differ. It may be derived from a species of bearded fig-tree, or it may be the Spanish word for the hanging branches of a vine that strike root in the ground. The island contains more numerous traces of the Carib Indians than any other of the Antilles.

It is thought that the Portuguese first visited the island. Barbados is characterized by a process of peaceful colonization, as it was acquired without conquest and has remained in British possession since its acquisition. In 1605, the British ship "Olive Blossom" touched the island; the crew found it uninhabited and took possession in the name of James I.

## British Settlement and Governance
The first actual settlement occurred in 1625 under the patent of Lord Leigh (later the earl of Marlborough), directed by Sir William Courteen. In 1627, following a compromise with Lord Marlborough, a grant was obtained by the earl of Carlisle, whose claim was based on a 1624 royal grant of all Caribbean islands. In 1628, Charles Wolferstone, a native of Bermuda, was appointed governor, and the present capital was founded when sixty-four settlers arrived at Carlisle Bay.

During the English Civil War, Barbados became a refuge for many Royalists. Under Lord Willoughby, who had leased the island from the earl of Carlisle, these Royalists resisted the forces of the Commonwealth, though Willoughby was eventually exiled after his defeat. Following the Restoration, the crown took over the government of the island and abolished the proprietary or patent interest to appease planters who were uncertain of their estate titles. To satisfy the claims of the patentees, a duty of 4-1/2% was imposed on all exports. A census conducted in 1684 under Governor Sir Richard Dutton recorded a population of 20,000.

## Other Notable Individuals Named Bernardo
The archive contains records of several other historical figures named Bernardo:

### Arts and Literature
*   **Bernardo Castello (1557–1629):** A historical and portrait painter from Genoa born at Albaro. He was an intimate friend of Tasso and designed the figures for the 1592 publication of *Gerusalemme Liberata*. He worked in Rome, Genoa, and the court of the duke of Savoy, often employing a superficial and rapid style. He was the father of Valerio Castello.
*   **Bernardo Gatti:** Ranked as the best pupil of the painter Correggio.
*   **Bernardo Bibbiena:** One of the speakers in the discussion regarding the perfect courtier as described in Castiglione's treatise *Il Cortegiano*.
*   **Bernardo Diaz del Castillo:** Author of a 1632 work on the conquest of Mexico, noted for its truth and simplicity compared to the "pompous elegance" of Antonio de Solis.

### Politics and Military
*   **Bernardo O'Higgins:** A leader in the liberation of Chile. He led an expedition in 1817 alongside Jose de San Martin. His rivalry with Jose Miguel Carrera contributed to the defeat of nationalist forces at Rancagua in 1814.
*   **Bernardo de Galvez:** Served as governor during the period of Spanish domination (1777–1785).

### Science and Law
*   **Bernardo Ramazzini (1633–1714):** Suggested the diagonal barometer, a form in which the upper part of the tube is inclined to the lower part.
*   **Bernardo (b. 1428):** A jurist and the father of the Italian statesman Niccolò Machiavelli. He held landed property valued at approximately £250 per year.
