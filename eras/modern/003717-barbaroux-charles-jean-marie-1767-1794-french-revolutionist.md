# BARBAROUX, CHARLES JEAN MARIE (1767-1794)

Charles Jean Marie Barbaroux was a French revolutionist who lived from 1767 to 1794. His early education was provided by the Oratorians of Marseilles, after which he pursued the study of law and established himself as a successful advocate.

## Early Political Career
Barbaroux served as the *greffier* (secretary) to the commune of Marseilles. In 1792, he was commissioned to travel to the Legislative Assembly to demand the accusation of the directory of the department of Bouches-du-Rhone, who were alleged accomplices in a royalist movement located in Arles. While in Paris, Barbaroux entered into relations with the Rolands and J. P. Brissot and was received into the Jacobin club. He is credited with instigating Marseilles to send a battalion of volunteers to Paris, a force that contributed to the insurrection against the king on August 10, 1792.

Upon his return to Marseilles, Barbaroux was involved in the repression of two opposing movements: an ultra-Jacobin movement in Marseilles and a royalist movement in Avignon. He was subsequently elected as a deputy to the Convention, receiving 775 out of 776 possible votes.

## Activities in the Convention
From the beginning of his tenure in the Convention, Barbaroux positioned himself as an opponent of the Mountain. His political actions included attacking Marat, proposing the breakup of the commune of Paris, and accusing Robespierre on September 25, 1792, of aiming for a dictatorship. 

Despite these oppositions, he secured the adoption of the act of accusation against Louis XVI. During the trial of the king, Barbaroux voted for his death, specifying that it should be carried out "without delay and without appeal."

## Conflict and Flight
During the final struggle between the Mountain and the Girondists, Barbaroux refused to resign his position as a deputy. He also rejected an offer from the sections of Paris to provide hostages in exchange for the representatives who had been arrested.

Barbaroux succeeded in escaping and first fled to Caen, where he organized a civil war. He later moved to Saint-Emilion, near Bordeaux. During his time there, he wrote his *Memoires*, which were later published by his son in 1822 and re-edited in 1866.

## Death
After being discovered, Barbaroux attempted to commit suicide by shooting himself. He was only wounded in the attempt and was taken to Bordeaux. Once his identity was established, he was executed by guillotine.
