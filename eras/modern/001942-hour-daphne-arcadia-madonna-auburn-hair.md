# Hour, Daphne, In Arcadia, and Madonna with the Auburn Hair

## The Miniatures of William Jacob Baer
William Jacob Baer (1860–) was an American painter born on January 29, 1860, in Cincinnati, Ohio. Between 1880 and 1884, he pursued studies in Munich. Baer played a significant role in the revival of miniature-painting within the United States, a medium to which he turned in 1892. He served as the first president of the Society of Painters in Miniature in New York. Among his noted works in the medium of miniatures are "The Golden Hour," "Daphne," "In Arcadia," and "Madonna with the Auburn Hair."

## Daphne in Mythology and Legend
In Greek mythology, Daphne—a name meaning "laurel tree"—is identified as the daughter of either the Thessalian Peneus, the Laconian Amyclas, or the Arcadian river-god Ladon. According to Ovid, she was beloved by Apollo; while being pursued by him, her mother Gaea transformed her into a laurel tree, which became sacred to the god. 

Peloponnesian legends provide an alternative account involving Leucippus, the son of Oenomaus of Pisa. Leucippus sought to join Daphne's companions by disguising himself as a girl. However, his true sex was discovered while bathing, and he was subsequently slain by the nymphs.

## The Village and Temple of Daphne
The Macedonian kings of Syria established a place of devotion to Apollo at Daphne, located five miles from Antioch. This site featured a magnificent temple containing a colossal figure of the god of light, adorned with gems and gold. The deity was depicted in a bending posture, pouring a libation from a golden cup, as if supplicating for the "cold and beauteous Daphne." This imagery reflects how Syrian poets adapted the amorous tale of Daphne from the banks of the Peneus to those of the Orontes.

The area was characterized by a thick grove of cypresses and laurels extending ten miles in circumference, providing cool shade during sultry summers. The site was further distinguished by:
*   The Castalian fountain, from which flowed a stream of prophecy that rivaled the Delphic oracle.
*   A stadium, established via a privilege purchased from Elis, where Olympic games were celebrated.
*   An annual public expenditure of thirty thousand pounds sterling for public pleasures.

Over time, the influx of spectators and pilgrims led to the creation of the populous village of Daphne. While it emulated the splendor of a provincial city, it never officially acquired that title. The grove was regarded as a paradise of health, joy, and luxury, though it was avoided by philosophers and soldiers due to its sensual temptations.

## Other References to Daphne and the Hour
The name Daphne appears in other historical and geographical contexts. In the writings of Josephus, a place called Daphne is mentioned near the exit of Little Jordan into Great Jordan, though some sources, such as Reland, suspect the text should read "Dan" instead.

Regarding the concept of the "Hour," the Qur'an refers to the "Hour (of Reckoning)." It teaches that on the day this Hour is established, transgressors will be deluded, swearing that they had tarried for only an hour.
