# BIBLIOGRAPHY

In the Human History Archive, bibliographies serve as curated guides to the literature of specific regions, academic disciplines, and historical figures. These records identify foundational texts, specialized monographs, and official reports used to construct historical and scientific knowledge.

## Regional and National Bibliographies

### The Baleares and Costa Rica
For a general account of the Baleares islands, valuable sources include *Die Balearen geschildert in Wort und Bild* by Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria (1896), G. Vuillier's *Les Iles oubliees* (1904)—translated as *The Forgotten Isles* (1896)—and P. Pifferrer's 1423-page illustrated volume *Islas Baleares* (1888). Other notable works include an 1841 article by George Sand in the *Revue des deux mondes*. Specialized monographs on the islands include Sir C. R. Markham's *The Story of Majorca and Minorca* (1908), M. Willkomm's *Illustrationes florae insularum Balearium* (1881-1892), and E. Cartailhac's *Monuments primitifs des iles baleares* (1892). Historical perspectives are provided by J. Dameto and V. Mut in *La Historia general del regno balearico* (1632-1650), A. Campaner y Fuertes' *Bosquejo historico de la dominacion islamita en las islas Baleares* (1888) regarding Moorish rule, and A. Lecoy de la Marche's treatise *Les Relations de la France avec le royaume de Majorque* (1892). Commercial movements are tracked in the *British Foreign Office Reports for the Consular District of Barcelona*.

Regarding Costa Rica, general information is found in works by G. Niederlein (1898), R. Villafranca (1895), L. Z. Baron (1894), H. Pittier (1890), P. Biolley (1889), and M. M. de Peralta (1873). Statistical data on commerce and immigration are available via J. Schroeder (1894), the Bureau of American Republics, British Diplomatic and Consular Reports, U.S.A. Consular Reports, and Reports of the Ministries. History is covered by L. Z. Baron (1894), F. M. Barrantes (1892), and J. B. Calvo (1890), the latter of which provides a government-authorized partisan account of finance, trade, and politics. Frontier issues are addressed by Col. G. E. Church in the *Journal of the Royal Geographical Society* (1897) and Dr. E. Seler in *Petermann's Mittheilungen* (1900). A detailed bibliography is provided by D. J. Maluquer in *Republica de Costa Rica* (1890).

### East Africa, Ashanti, and Kentucky
The East Africa Protectorate is comprehensively detailed in Sir Charles Eliot's *The East Africa Protectorate* (1905), annual governor reports since 1896, and *Drumkey's Year Book for East Africa* (1908). Historical information is found in the War Office's *Précis of Information* (1901), P.L. McDermont's *British East Africa or Ibea* (1895), and J.W. Gregory's *The Foundation of British East Africa* (1901). Other specialized works include Bishop A.R. Tucker's account of missionary labors (1908), Joseph Thomson's *Through Masai Land* (1886), and A. Chapman's *On Safari* (1908). Geological data is provided by J.W. Gregory's *The Great Rift Valley* (1896), Hans Meyer (1890), and H.B. Muff (1908).

For Ashanti and Jaman, general surveys include R.A. Freeman's *Travels in Ashanti and Jaman* (1898), C.P. Lucas's *Historical Geography of the British Colonies* (1900), and Colonial Office *Annual Reports* (from 1906). Ethnology is covered by Col. A.B. Ellis (1887), while history is detailed by Col. A.B. Ellis (1893) and C.C. Reindorf (1895). Early accounts include works by T.E. Bowdich (1819) and J. Dupuis (1824).

In Kentucky, physical and natural resource data are found in reports from the Kentucky Geological Survey, the U.S. Geological Survey, and the Bureau of Agriculture, Labor and Statistics. Early descriptions include Gilbert Imlay's 1797 work, which reprints John Filson's 1784 text. Social life in mountain districts is described by William H. Haney (1906). Administrative and legal history is documented in the *Official Manual for the Use of the Courts*, the proceedings of constitutional conventions (1849, 1890), B. H. Young's *History and Texts of Three Constitutions of Kentucky* (1890), and the *General Statutes of Kentucky* (1877). History is further detailed by R. M. McElroy (1909), N. S. Shaler (1885), and John M. Brown (1889).

### China
Literature on China includes James Legge's translation of the Confucian Canon in *The Chinese Classics*, which comprises the Book of History, the Odes, the Book of Rites, the Book of Changes, the Annals of Confucius' native State, and the discourses of Confucius and Mencius. F. Hirth's *The Ancient History of China* sketches history up to 221 B.C. E. H. Parker's *China* provides commercial and statistical data and trade-route maps. H. A. Giles' *A Chinese Biographical Dictionary* contains 2579 short lives of various classes, including women, from early times to the present. L. Richard provides *A Comprehensive Geography of the Chinese Empire*.

## Specialized Subject Bibliographies

### Natural Sciences and Book-keeping
Literature on bees, particularly the genus *Apis*, includes classical observations by Reaumur (1740-1742) and F. Huber (1792), and more recent works by J.H. Fabre (1879-1891) and M. Maeterlinck (1901). Solitary and parasitic species are discussed by F. Smith (1853-1859), H. Friese (1891), J. Perez (1895), and C. Verhoeff (1892). Social species are covered by E. Hoffer (1881) and H. von Jhering (1903). Controversy regarding parthenogenesis in hive bees is found in works by J. Perez (1878), F. Dickel (1901, 1902), A. Petrunkevich (1901), and A. Weismann (1901). General guides include F.R. Cheshire's *Bees and Bee-keeping* (1885-1888), T.W. Cowan's *Honey Bee* (1904), and D. Sharp's summary in the *Cambridge Natural History*.

The bibliography of book-keeping is anchored by the catalogue of the library of the Institute of Chartered Accountants in England and Wales. Key texts include works by G. van de Linde (1898), L.R. Dicksee (1905, 1906), the *Encyclopaedia of Accounting* (1903), the *Accountants' Library* (1901), J.W. Heaps (1898), and R. Brown (1905).

## Classical and General Bibliographies

### Lucian of Samosata
The works of Lucian include the *Editio princeps* (1496) and noted editions by T. Hemsterhuis and J. F. Reitz (1743-1746), J. T. Lehmann (1822-1831), C. Jacobitz (1886-1888), and J. Sommerbrodt (1886-1899). H. Rabe edited the scholia (1906). English translations include a 1711 version, T. Francklin (1780), W. Tooke (1820), and a 1905 colloquial translation by H. W. Fowler and F. G. Fowler. French translations include those by P. L. Courier, E. Talbot (1866), and Belin de Ballu (1789). Critical studies on Lucian were written by M. Croiset (1882), E. Egger (1862), C. Martha (1866), H. W. L. Hime (1900), and Sir R. C. Jebb (1907).

### General Bibliographical Works
"Bibliographies of bibliographies" include Dr. Julius Petzholdt's *Bibliotheca bibliographica* (1866), Henri Stein's *Manuel de bibliographie generale* (1898), Ch. V. Langlois' *Manuel de bibliographie historique* (1901), and W.P. Courtney's *A Register of National Bibliography* (1905). Other resources include the British Museum's *List of Books of Reference in the Reading-Room* (1889) and G.K. Fortescue's *Subject-index of the Modern Works added to the Library of the British Museum in the years 1881-1900*. Professor Ferguson's *Some Aspects of Bibliography* (1900) also provides an eclectic list.
