# Buildings Fronting the Left Bank of the Danube

The left bank of the Danube is characterized by a long range of substantial buildings, featuring a variety of architectural styles and serving diverse civic, cultural, and commercial purposes.

## Major Civic and Government Edifices
Among the most prominent structures is the Houses of Parliament, a massive limestone building constructed between 1883 and 1902. This edifice is designed in the late Gothic style and covers an area of 3¾ acres. Located not far from the Houses of Parliament is the palace of justice, a magnificent building erected in 1896. The surrounding neighborhood also contains palatial buildings that house the ministries of agriculture and justice.

Other significant administrative and financial buildings in the area include the town-hall, built between 1869 and 1875 in the early Renaissance style, and the central post and telegraph office. Financial institutions present on the left bank include the Austro-Hungarian bank (1904) and the exchange (1905).

## Educational and Cultural Institutions
The left bank hosts several major academic and artistic institutions. The Academy, erected from 1862 to 1864 in the Renaissance style, contains a library, a botanic collection, a historic picture gallery, and a lofty reception room. The university features a baroque façade (which was rebuilt in 1900), while the university library, opened in 1875, is a handsome building in the Renaissance style.

Cultural landmarks include the national museum, constructed between 1836 and 1844, and the art-industrial museum (1893-1897), which is built in an oriental style and incorporates ornamentations characteristic of Hungary.

## The Andrássy Ut and Associated Buildings
The Andrássy Ut is home to several notable structures, including the academy of music and the national drawing school. The opera-house, built between 1875 and 1884, is designed in the Italian Renaissance style. The street also contains the old and new exhibition buildings and the museum of fine arts (1900-1905). 

The museum of fine arts houses the collection of modern pictures from the national museum as well as the national gallery. The national gallery was originally formed by Prince Esterházy and was purchased by the government in 1865 for £130,000; it was housed in the academy before its installation in the museum of fine arts in 1905. At the terminus of the Andrássy Ut stands one of several monuments erected throughout the country to mark the thousandth anniversary of the foundation of the kingdom of Hungary.

## Other Notable Structures
The waterfront and surrounding area include the Redoute buildings, a large structure used for social purposes and balls, built in a mixed Moorish and Romanesque style. At the lower end of the quays is an extensive custom-house. The area is further populated by several insurance offices and fine hotels. 

Additional infrastructure and facilities mentioned include:
* Two great railway termini.
* A central market, established in 1897.
* Large barracks.
* Technical and secondary schools.
* Several handsome theatres.
