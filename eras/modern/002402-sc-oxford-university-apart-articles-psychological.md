# Honorary D.Sc. of Oxford University

The honorary Doctor of Science (D.Sc.) degree from Oxford University is an academic distinction awarded to individuals of significant achievement in their respective fields. 

## Recipient: James Mark Baldwin

One notable recipient of the honorary D.Sc. from Oxford University was James Mark Baldwin (1861– ). An American philosopher born in Columbia, S.C., Baldwin received his education at Princeton and various universities in Germany. His academic career included several prestigious professorships: he served as the professor of philosophy at the University of Toronto in 1889, the professor of psychology at Princeton in 1893, and later, in 1903, the professor of philosophy and psychology at Johns Hopkins University.

Baldwin was a prominent figure among experimental psychologists and was among the founders of the *Psychological Review*. His leadership in the field is further evidenced by his role as vice-president of the International Congress of Psychology held in London in 1892, and his tenure as president of the American Psychological Association from 1897 to 1898. In addition to the honorary degree from Oxford, he was awarded a gold medal from the Royal Academy of Arts and Sciences of Denmark in 1897 and served as the honorary president of the International Congress of Criminal Anthropology in Geneva in 1896.

## Literary and Academic Contributions of James Mark Baldwin

Beyond his contributions to the *Psychological Review*, Baldwin authored several significant works, including:
* *Handbook of Psychology* (1890)
* *Elements of Psychology* (1893)
* *Mental Development in the Child and the Race* (1896)
* *Social and Ethical Interpretations in Mental Development* (1898)
* *Story of the Mind* (1898)
* *Thought and Things* (published in London and New York, vol. i., 1906)

He also provided a translation of Ribot's *German Psychology of To-day* (1886). Furthermore, Baldwin served as the editor-in-chief of the *Dictionary of Philosophy and Psychology* (1901-1905), to which he contributed extensively.
