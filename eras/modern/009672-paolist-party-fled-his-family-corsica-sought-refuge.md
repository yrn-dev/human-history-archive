# The Flight of the Bonaparte Family from Corsica

## Political Conflict in Corsica
During the late 18th century, Corsica was marked by a sharp political divide between two opposing factions. One side was the Paolist party, led by Paoli, which represented a more conservative viewpoint. This party sought to establish an alliance with England to oppose the regicide republic of France. Opposing them were the democrats, who embraced the French side.

Among those who joined the democratic movement were the Bonaparte brothers: Joseph, Napoleon, and Lucien. Lucien, in particular, became a prominent figure in this struggle, serving as the leading speaker for the Jacobin club in Ajaccio. He urged his brothers to break ties with Paoli and the conservative party. Lucien furthered this cause by heading a Corsican deputation to France, where he sought aid for the democrats and denounced Paoli.

## Exile to France
The political struggle eventually shifted in favor of the conservative faction. As the Paolists gained the upper hand, the Bonaparte family was forced to leave the island. Joseph, along with his family, fled Corsica and sought refuge in France. Similarly, the other Bonapartes left the island to join Lucien at Toulon.

Following their arrival in France, the family members pursued different paths. Joseph spent a brief period in Paris, though he became disgusted by the excesses of the Jacobins. He eventually settled in Marseilles, where he married Julie Clary, the daughter of a local merchant. Meanwhile, Lucien remained active in the Jacobinical cause in the south of France, operating in the small town of St Maximin (which had been renamed Marathon), where he was known as "Brutus."

## Efforts for Recovery
Despite their exile, the Bonapartes did not abandon their connection to their homeland. The family moved from place to place primarily to coordinate measures intended to recover Corsica. Joseph participated in these efforts, including a mission to Genoa in 1795.

In 1796, Joseph accompanied Napoleon during the early stages of the Italian campaign. He was involved in negotiations with Sardinia that resulted in the armistice of Cherasco on April 28, and he was the one who delivered this news to the French government. Joseph later traveled to Leghorn and participated in a French expedition aimed at the recovery of Corsica. Working alongside Miot de Melito, a commissioner of the French Republic, Joseph assisted in the reorganization of the island.

## Subsequent Political Careers
Following the efforts in Corsica, Joseph's career transitioned into diplomacy and governance. In March 1797, the Directory appointed him as minister to the court of Parma, and he later served in the same capacity in Rome. During his time in Rome, Joseph was ordered by the French Directory and Napoleon to encourage revolutionary movements. After a disturbance on December 28, 1797, which resulted in the death of General Leonard Duphot, Joseph left Rome. He then entered parliamentary life in Paris as a member for Corsica in the Council of Five Hundred, though he retired from the chamber in 1799.

Lucien also entered the Council of Five Hundred in Paris in 1798, having previously served as a commissioner in the French army in Germany after a period of imprisonment and a brief post at St Chamans.
