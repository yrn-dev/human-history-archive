# Congo

The Congo is a region in Equatorial Africa characterized by the vast basin of the Congo river. It has transitioned through various political statuses, from an independent state to a Belgian colony.

## Political History and Administration
The region was formerly known as the Independent State of the Congo (or *Etat Independent du Congo*). Its creation was driven by the personal interest of King Leopold II of Belgium in the commercial development and exploration of Equatorial Africa. In 1878, the *Comite des Etudes du Haut Congo* was formed, which later developed into the International Association of the Congo, presided over by Colonel M. Strauch.

Through the efforts of H.M. Stanley in Africa and King Leopold II in Europe, the International Association was recognized by global powers as an independent state between 1884 and 1885. On February 23, 1885, declarations were exchanged between the Association and the Belgian government. By April of that year, the Belgian chambers authorized King Leopold II to serve as the chief of this new state. While the union between Belgium and the state was initially declared to be purely personal, the state's European headquarters were located in Brussels, and its officials became almost exclusively Belgian over time.

In 1889, King Leopold II announced his intention to bequeath the state to Belgium, and by 1890, the Belgian government acquired the right of annexation in exchange for financial assistance. Despite this, immediate annexation was delayed due to the disinclination of both the king and the Belgian public. However, reports of misgovernment in the Congo led to strong agitation for reform in the United States, Great Britain, and other nations. This shift in public opinion resulted in a new treaty of cession in November 1907, and the territory was annexed to Belgium in 1908, becoming the Belgian Congo.

## Geography and Infrastructure
The Belgian Congo occupies the majority of the Congo river basin. Boma (properly *Mboma*), located on the north bank of the river Congo approximately 60 miles from its mouth, serves as the administrative capital with a population of about 5,000. Boma has a long history as a trade center; European traders established stations there in the 16th and 17th centuries, and it became a well-known mart for slaves and an entrepot for commerce in the lower Congo. Trade in Boma was primarily managed by Dutch merchants, though Portuguese, French, and British firms also maintained factories there. In 1884, the natives of Boma granted a protectorate to the International Association of the Congo.

Communication and transport have been critical for the development of the region. The Congo river, along with the Nile and various lakes, provides natural links to the seaboard. During the war, two railways were constructed in the Congo territory to extend communications from the center to the south and from east to west. These developments allow for travel from the Indian Ocean at Dar-es-Salaam via the German Central Railway to Ujiji on Lake Tanganyika, then by steamer to Albertville, by train to Kabalo, by steamer to Kongolo, by train to Kindu, and finally by rail and steamer down the Congo to the Atlantic Ocean.

## Linguistics and Ethnic Groups
The Congo basin is home to various Bantu language groups:
*   **Bangala-Bobangi-Liboko:** These are commercial languages of the Upper Congo (including *Ngala*, *Bangi*, *Liboko*, *Poto*, *Ngombe*, and *Yanzi*). They are spoken along and around the main stream from the Sanga junction in the west to the Lomami and Rubi rivers in the east, extending north to the limits of the Bantu domain.
*   **Bakuba:** This assemblage of Central Congo dialects (including *Songe*, *Shilange*, and *Babuma*) likely encompasses all Bantu languages between the Kwa-Kasai and Upper Kwilu in the west and the Lomami river in the east.
*   **Rua-Luba-Lunda-Marungu:** This group (including *Kanyoka*, *Lulua*, and *Ki-tabwa*) occupies much of the south central basin, situated between the Zambezi watershed to the south, the Bakuba country to the north, the Kasai and Kwango streams to the west, and Lake Tanganyika to the east.
*   **Balolo:** This group consists of languages in the Northern Congo bend and potentially corrupt dialects of the Northern Kwango, Kwilu, and Kasai.
