## Military History
During the American Civil War, Don Carlos Buell, a major-general of volunteers, was sent to Kentucky in November 1861 to succeed General William T. Sherman in command. Buell focused his efforts on the training and organization of the Army of the Ohio, which was subsequently known as the Army of the Cumberland. This military body was noted for maintaining a standard of efficiency and discipline that was surpassed only by the Army of the Potomac throughout its career.

## Regional Customs and Law
In the region of Cumberland, specific local traditions and legal roles have been documented:

*   **Educational Customs:** At Bromfield school in Cumberland, a practice known as "barring-out" was formerly common. According to William Hutchinson, students would depose and exclude their master from the school premises for three days starting at Fasting's Even (the beginning of Lent). During this time, the boys armed themselves with mock weapons and barricaded the doors. If the master successfully re-entered, the boys performed extra tasks as a penalty. The event concluded on the third day with terms of capitulation, typically written in Latin verse, which granted the students the right to engage in cockfights and football.
*   **Legal Administration:** Richard Burn (1700-1785), an English legal writer and vicar of Orton in Westmorland, served as a justice of the peace for both Cumberland and Westmorland. Burn was a noted authority on law, authoring *Justice of the Peace and Parish Officer* (1755), *Ecclesiastical Law* (1760), *Digest of the Militia Laws* (1760), and *A New Law Dictionary* (1792).

## Notable Individuals
The archive mentions two distinct figures associated with the name Cumberland:

*   **Richard Gibson:** Described as a "Cumberland pygmy," Gibson began his career as a page for a "gentle" family and later the royal family. He served as a drawing-master to the daughters of James II (Queens Mary and Anne) and was a miniature painter to Charles I. He was married to Anne, another dwarf of Henrietta Maria; the couple together measured just over 7 feet and had nine children, five of whom survived and were of ordinary stature. Gibson died in 1690 at the age of seventy-five and is buried in St Paul's, Covent Garden.
*   **The Duke of Cumberland:** Records indicate that in May 1765, the duke of Cumberland attempted to form an administration, though this effort resulted in failure.
