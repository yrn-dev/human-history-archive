# Camelids of Bolivia

The most interesting animals found in Bolivia are the members of the Camelidae family: the guanaco (*Auchenia huanaco*), the llama (*A. llama*), the alpaca (*A. pacos*), and the vicuna (*A. vicugna*). These animals share the habits and structure of the African camel, though they are smaller in size and lack a hump. They are adapted to mountainous regions rather than sandy plains, specifically inhabiting the steppes of Patagonia and the semi-arid and arid plateaus of the Andes. A notable characteristic of these animals is their ability to survive for long periods without food or drink.

## Species and Distribution

The guanaco is considered the original type among the four species. It is the largest of the group and possesses the widest geographic range, extending from Peru to Tierra del Fuego. While the llama and alpaca were domesticated long before the discovery of America, the vicuna and guanaco exist only in a wild state.

## Economic Use and Industry

The rearing of alpacas and llamas is an established industry in the Bolivian highlands. This industry is managed entirely by the Indians, who are the only ones who appear to understand the peculiarities and habits of these animals.

Each species provides specific materials used by the native populations:
*   **Llamas:** These animals serve as pack animals in Peru and Bolivia. Their coarse wool is utilized to manufacture garments for the natives.
*   **Alpacas:** Highly prized for their fine wool, which serves as a staple export for Bolivia. However, the alpaca is difficult to rear, and the production of its wool cannot be significantly increased.
*   **Vicunas:** Also celebrated for their wool, which natives use to weave other wearing apparel and beautiful, expensive blanket cloaks known as *ponchos*.
*   **Guanacos:** These animals are hunted for their skins, which are dressed to create robes or attractive rugs.

## Conservation Status

The populations of the wild camelids are currently under threat. The slaughter of the vicuna and the guanaco is rapidly reducing their numbers.
