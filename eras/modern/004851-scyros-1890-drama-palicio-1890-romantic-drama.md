# Achilles in Scyros and Palicio

*Achilles in Scyros* and *Palicio* are dramatic works written in 1890 by the English poet Robert Bridges.

## Author Background
Robert Bridges (born October 23, 1844) was an English poet educated at Eton and Corpus Christi College, Oxford. Before his literary pursuits, he studied medicine in London at St Bartholomew's hospital and served as a physician at the Great Northern hospital and as an assistant physician at the Children's hospital, Great Ormond Street. He retired from medical practice in 1882 and married Mary, the daughter of Alfred Waterhouse, R.A., two years later.

Bridges is noted for a distinct theory of prosody, maintaining that poetry should follow the rules of natural speech and that English prosody is dependent on the number of "stresses" in a line rather than the number of syllables. His work is characterized by precision, purity, restraint, and delicacy combined with strength of expression. While his poetry was initially printed privately and known only to a small circle of admirers, he exerted a great influence within a select circle.

Other dramas produced by Bridges in 1890 include *The Return of Ulysses*, a five-act drama, and *The Christian Captives*, a tragedy based on the same subject as Calderon's *El Principe Constante*.

## Other Literary Contributions
Beyond the dramas of 1890, Bridges authored several other significant works. His early poetry includes *Prometheus* (1883), described as a "mask in the Greek Manner," and *Eros and Psyche* (1885), which is a version of Apuleius. He also wrote a series of sixty-nine sonnets titled *The Growth of Love*, printed for private circulation in 1876 and 1889.

His historical tragedy *Nero* was published in 1885, with its second part appearing in 1894. In 1889, he wrote *The Feast of Bacchus*, which was partly translated from Terence's *Heauton-Timoroumenos*. Later works include the comedy *The Humours of the Court* (1893), based on Lope de Vega's *El Perro del hortelano* and Calderon's *El secreto á voces*, and *Hymns from the Yattendon Hymnal* (1899).

In the field of literary criticism, Bridges published *John Keats, a Critical Essay* (1895) and *Milton's Prosody* (1893), the latter of which compiled two earlier essays from 1887 and 1889. A complete six-volume edition of his *Poetical Works* was published between 1898 and 1905.
