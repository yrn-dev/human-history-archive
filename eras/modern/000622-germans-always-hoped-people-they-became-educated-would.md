# German Educational Aspirations and National Identity

## Language and Education in the Austrian Empire
In the context of the Austrian Empire, education was a matter of significant political importance. For a long period, German served as the ordinary language of government, the army, and the central public offices in Vienna. This created a network of German-speaking officialism across the monarchy, where proficiency in German was a prerequisite for any share in government.

The Germans maintained a firm belief that German was the *Kultur-sprache*. Based on this conviction, they had always hoped that as the people became educated, they would cease to use their own particular languages. This hope was tied to a broader ambition to Germanize the entire empire.

However, this goal faced opposition from Slav populations. A law passed in 1867 required elementary education in Slav districts to be conducted in Slovenian or Czech. Furthermore, the minister of education under Count Taaffe, Prazak, granted the demand of Slav minorities in towns to have their own schools rather than being compelled to attend German ones. Despite economic factors that drove Slavs toward western manufacturing districts and towns—which might have been expected to lead to Germanization—the movement of population instead spread Slav languages. This led to demands for special schools and the appointment of nationals of their own ethnicity to government offices in regions such as Styria, Bohemia, and Vienna. The introduction of Slavonic into higher and middle schools was viewed by Germans as a strike at their most sensitive point. To illustrate the perceived inadequacy of other languages for higher education, Count A. Auersperg once entered the diet of Carniola carrying the entirety of Slovenian literature to argue that it could not substitute for German.

## Educational Control and Nationalism in the Hohenzollern Era
Following the victories of 1871, the mental growth of educated Germans was shaped by a period of rapid wealth accumulation and military success. This environment fostered patriotic vanity, which was systematically exploited by the Hohenzollern dynasty through the control of the press, literature, and educational institutions.

The school and college systems were utilized to instill a belief in the racial, physical, intellectual, and moral superiority of Germans over all other peoples. Professors and teachers were expected to preach an inevitable destiny for Germany to lead the world under the Hohenzollern dynasty; those who failed to do so faced obscurity and failure. This system resulted in the systematic falsification of human history in teaching, where Prussians were depicted as the regenerators and leaders of mankind, while other nations were characterized as decadent and incompetent.

This indoctrination was pervasive, appearing in school-books, church teachings, and literature. The influence was so strong that lectures in subjects such as mathematics or biology would frequently be interrupted by royalist patriotic rhetoric. Consequently, a conception was built in the German mind of the emperor and the nation as a godlike entity in "shining armour," predominant over a world of inferior and ill-disposed peoples.

## Scientific Inquiry and Academic Organization
While the British and French were initially the leaders in the proliferation of knowledge, Germans eventually overhauled them through pertinacity and zeal in scientific inquiry. This progress was aided by the state of education in other nations; British universities had become the preserve of the gentry and nobility, dominated by an uncritical textual knowledge of Greek and Latin classics. Similarly, French education was dominated by the classical traditions of the Jesuits.

These conditions allowed Germans to organize a body of investigators that, while small in absolute terms, was large compared to the small groups of experimentalists and inventors in Britain and France.
