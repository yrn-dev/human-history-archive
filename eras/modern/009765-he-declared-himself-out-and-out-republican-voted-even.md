# Pierre Napoleon

## Early Life and Adventures
Pierre Napoleon (1815–1881) was born in Rome on September 12, 1815. He was the son of Lucien Bonaparte. At the age of fifteen, he began a life of adventure by joining insurrectionary bands in the Romagna between 1830 and 1831. His travels subsequently took him to the United States to join his uncle Joseph, and to Colombia in 1832, where he was with General Santander. Upon returning to Rome, he was taken prisoner by order of the pope from 1835 to 1836, after which he sought refuge in England.

## Political Career in France
Pierre Napoleon returned to France during the revolution of 1848 and was elected as the deputy for Corsica to the Constituent Assembly. During this period, he declared himself an out-and-out republican and voted in alignment with the socialists. He expressed support for the national workshops and opposed the *loi Falloux*.

His political stance helped generate popular confidence in his cousin, Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III). However, Pierre Napoleon disapproved of the *coup d'etat* carried out by Napoleon III on December 2, 1851. Despite this disapproval, he was soon reconciled with the emperor and accepted the title of prince. This acceptance led the republicans to abandon him, and he subsequently lost his political importance.

## Later Life and Legal Controversy
Following his decline in political influence, Pierre Napoleon turned to literature and published several mediocre poems; he was also noted for leading a debauched life. In January 1870, he became prominent again due to a violent incident stemming from a controversy with Paschal Grousset. Grousset sent two journalists to provoke Pierre Napoleon into a duel. During a violent discussion with these men, Pierre Napoleon drew his revolver and killed one of them, a man named Victor Noir.

This killing caused significant excitement within the republican press, which demanded a trial. Although the High Court acquitted him, the government faced criticism as a result.

## Personal Life and Death
Pierre Napoleon married Justine Eleanore Ruffin, the daughter of a working-man from Paris. Before their marriage, he had two children with her:
*   **Roland Napoleon** (born May 19, 1858), who served in the army until being excluded in 1886, after which he focused on scientific explorations and geography.
*   **Jeanne**, who became the wife of the marquis de Vence.

Pierre Napoleon died in obscurity at Versailles on April 7, 1881.
