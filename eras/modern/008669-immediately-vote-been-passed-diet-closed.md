# The Imperial Diet and Legislative Procedures

The Imperial Diet is a legislative body consisting of two houses: the House of Peers and the House of Representatives. The House of Peers is composed of Nobles, members of the Imperial Family, and Deputies nominated by the Emperor. The House of Representatives consists of members elected by the people according to the Law of Election. It is prohibited for any individual to serve as a member of both houses simultaneously.

## Legislative Functions and Voting
Every law requires the consent of the Imperial Diet. Both houses are empowered to vote on projects of law brought forward by the Government, and they may also independently bring forward their own projects of law. If a bill is rejected by either house, it cannot be reintroduced during the same session. Additionally, both houses may make recommendations to the Government regarding laws or other subjects; however, if such recommendations are not adopted, they cannot be made a second time within the same session.

For a debate to be opened or a vote to be taken in either house, a quorum of at least one-third of the total membership must be present. Votes in both houses are decided by an absolute majority. In the event of a tie vote, the President is granted the casting vote.

## Sessions and Convocation
The Imperial Diet is convoked annually, with standard sessions lasting three months. If necessary, the duration of a session may be prolonged by Imperial order. In cases of urgent necessity, an extraordinary session may be convoked, with its duration determined by Imperial order.

The opening, closing, and prorogation of the Diet, as well as the prolongation of its sessions, occur simultaneously in both houses. If the House of Representatives is ordered to dissolve, the House of Peers is prorogued at the same time. Following the dissolution of the House of Representatives, new members are ordered to be elected by Imperial decree, and the new House must be convoked within five months of the dissolution date.

## Governance and Public Proceedings
The deliberations of both houses are generally held in public. However, they may be held in secret sittings if requested by the Government or by a resolution of the House. Both houses have the authority to receive petitions from subjects and may present addresses to the Emperor. Furthermore, they may enact rules necessary for the management of their own internal affairs, beyond what is already provided by law and the constitution.

## Financial Oversight and Emergency Measures
The Imperial Diet holds significant roles in financial governance. The Government may seek the consent of the Diet for a continuing expenditure fund for a fixed number of years to meet special requirements. To address unavoidable deficits or requirements not provided for in the Budget, a reserve fund is established.

In situations where the Imperial Diet cannot be convoked due to the state of foreign relations or domestic affairs, and there is an urgent need for measures to maintain public safety, the Government may take necessary financial measures via an Imperial Ordinance. Such actions must be submitted to the Imperial Diet for approval during its next session. If the Diet has not voted on the Budget, or if a Budget has not been brought into actual existence, the Government is to carry out the Budget from the preceding year. Finally, the Board of Audit verifies and confirms the final account of State revenue and expenditure, which the Government then submits to the Imperial Diet along with the Board's verification report.

## Political Dynamics
Historically, the proceedings of the Diet have been characterized by party politicians in the lower chamber launching successive attacks on holders of power. This occurred during a period where the Cabinet relied on the sovereign's prerogative and remained aloof from alliances, while party politicians worked to discredit the Cabinet. During these struggles, the House of Peers consistently supported the Cabinet. The reputation of the Diet was enhanced during the war with China (1894-1895), as all political parties set aside differences to vote for funds to prosecute the campaign. Later, Prince Ito, serving as prime minister, successfully obtained funds from the Diet—despite opposition in the House of Representatives—to increase the navy and raise the army to thirteen divisions.
