# Howell Cobb and Jacob Thompson

## Political Influence in the Buchanan Administration
During the administration of President James Buchanan, Howell Cobb of Georgia and Jacob Thompson of Mississippi held high-ranking cabinet positions. Cobb served as the secretary of the treasury, while Thompson served as the secretary of the interior. Together, these two officials exerted significant influence over the president's policy decisions.

## The Lecompton Constitution and Civil War
Cobb and Thompson were instrumental in convincing President Buchanan that the admission of Kansas into the Union under the pro-slavery Lecompton Constitution was the only viable method to avoid a civil war. This position marked a shift in administration policy; at the start of his term, Buchanan had appointed Frederick P. Stanton of Tennessee as secretary and Robert J. Walker of Mississippi as the territorial governor of Kansas, assuring both of his intent to adhere to the principle of popular sovereignty.

However, Buchanan subsequently used his influence to force the admission of Kansas via the Lecompton Constitution, a move that went against the wishes of the majority of the settlers. This policy shift led to the resignation of Walker, who left in disgust, and the removal of Stanton from office due to his opposition to the scheme.

## Federal Patronage and the Covode Investigation
To advance the Lecompton measure and the compromise English Bill, as well as to prevent the election of Douglas to the Senate in 1858, federal patronage was freely utilized. These actions were later examined during the Covode Investigation of 1860, which was conducted by a committee of the House of Representatives. While the investigation brought some of these facts to light, it was noted as being partisan in character, and questions were raised regarding whether the House possessed the constitutional power to conduct such an investigation unless it served as the basis for an impeachment trial.
