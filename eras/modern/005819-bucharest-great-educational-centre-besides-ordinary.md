# Bucharest: Educational and Cultural Centre

Bucharest, the capital of Rumania and the chief town of the department of Ilfov, is recognized as a great educational centre. Its academic infrastructure is diverse, encompassing elementary schools, gymnasia, lyceums, and ecclesiastical seminaries. The city also provides specialized instruction through training colleges for veterinary surgeons and engineers, as well as institutes for art, science, and commerce.

## The University and Higher Learning
Founded in 1864, the university in Bucharest serves as a cornerstone of the city's intellectual life. It contains faculties dedicated to medicine, pharmacy, science, law, literature, philosophy, and theology. A notable feature of the university is that students are not required to pay fees, with the exception of costs for board. 

The university buildings house several significant institutions, including the meeting-hall of the Rumanian senate and the national library, the latter of which contains many precious Oriental documents. Along with the central girls' school and the Athenaeum—a venue used for music and literary conferences—the university buildings are considered prime examples of modern Rumanian architecture.

## Libraries and Research Institutions
Beyond the university, Bucharest possesses several other important libraries. These include the library of the Academy, which is accompanied by a museum of antiquities and natural history, the central library, and the library of the Nifon seminary. Additionally, the Charles University Foundation (_Fundatiunea universitara Carol_) supports the intellectual community by rewarding scientific or literary merit and endowing research.

## Regional Educational Influence
The educational reach of Bucharest extends beyond the city limits. The ministry of education at Bucharest provides qualified teachers and devotes large sums of money to the maintenance of Rumanian schools in Macedonia. As of the archive's records, there are approximately forty such schools in Macedonia, including two gymnasia. This effort is part of a broader Rumanian movement that gained momentum after the 1861 union of the principalities of Moldavia and Wallachia.

## Philanthropic and Social Institutions
The city's commitment to social welfare is evidenced by various philanthropic institutions. These include the Pantelimon, Philantropia, Maternitate, Brancovan, and Coltei hospitals, as well as the Marcutza lunatic asylum. In 1862, Princess Elena Couza founded the Princess Elena refuge (_Asilul Elena Doamna_) to provide for 230 orphan girls, who utilize a convent in the Transylvanian Alps as a summer home.

## Cultural and Social Life
Bucharest's social progress is described as remarkable, with scientific, literary, and political circles that are on a level with those of most European capitals. The city's cultural offerings include the National and Lyric theatres, the latter of which is primarily patronized by foreign players. The city also features numerous clubs for sporting, social, and political activities, as well as a variety of minor places of amusement, hotels, and restaurants.

## Administrative and Military Significance
As the capital, Bucharest serves as the winter residence for the royal family and the meeting-place of parliament. It is the seat of the ministries, the national bank, the bank of Rumania, a chamber of commerce, and high courts including the supreme court (_Curtea de Casatie_) and an appeal court (_Curtea de Apel_).

The city is also a first-rank fortress and the headquarters of the II. army corps. Its fortifications, constructed between 1885 and 1896 based on a project by Belgian engineer General Henri Alexis Brialmont, consist of 36 batteries and armoured forts. The military infrastructure further includes three military academies, an arsenal, a military hospital, and barracks capable of housing over 30,000 infantry and cavalry.
