# Book-Prices Current

*Book-Prices Current* is an annual publication, first issued in 1887, that serves as a resource for studying the financial aspects of book-collecting. It is cited alongside other works of a similar nature, including Livingston's *American Book Prices Current* and *Auction Prices of Books* (1905).

## Determinants of Book Value
The prices listed in *Book-Prices Current* are influenced largely by fashion, though they are generally considered not to be wholly unreasonable. The valuation of books is determined by three primary factors:

1.  **Interest:** This includes the positive or associative interest found within the book itself.
2.  **Market Availability:** Prices are affected by the infrequency with which copies appear on the market relative to the number and wealth of potential buyers.
3.  **Physical Condition:** Except in instances involving books of extreme rarity and interest, the price is determined by the copy's condition, specifically its size, freshness, completeness, and the absence of stains.

## Context of Book Collecting and Printing
The financial study of books is often linked to a broader interest in the history of printing. Book-lovers frequently show a "religious" interest in the art of printing, which leads local antiquaries to covet the first book printed in any given town. This desire to assess the quality and quantity of work produced by early printers has resulted in the preservation of thousands of books that might otherwise have been lost.

## Historical Trends in Book Pricing
The cost of books has fluctuated significantly across different eras due to various economic and social factors.

### 17th Century
During the 17th century, book prices tended to rise rather than fall. This was attributed to an increase in money, the influence of monopolists, and higher payments made to authors. For example, church Bibles sold for 10s. in 1541, rising to 25s., then 30s., and reaching 40s. by 1641. Other examples include:
*   **Plays:** Single plays in quarto cost 6d. during Shakespeare's time, increasing to 1s. after the Restoration.
*   **Specific Works:** The 1623 Shakespeare folio was published at £1. Izaak Walton's *Compleat Angler* was priced at 1s. 6d. in sheepskin, and *Paradise Lost* was 3s.
*   **Subscription Works:** Bishop Walton's polyglot Bible (six large volumes) was sold to subscribers for £10, though it resulted in a heavy loss. Dryden's *Virgil* was published by subscription at £5:5s.; such high prices for subscription books were often driven by a desire to befriend or honor the author.

### 19th and 20th Centuries
The 19th century saw a diversification of the market. The expansion of the reading public created a demand for cheap reprints of famous works, leading to the production of flimsy editions priced at 1s., 1s. 6d., or even less. 

A significant shift occurred around 1894 in England regarding the pricing of novels. The three-volume edition, which cost 31s. 6d. (based on a price of 10s. 6d. per volume adopted during the popularity of the *Waverly Novels*), was largely substituted by single-volume novels priced at 6s. each, subject to discount.

## Legal Regulations on Pricing
Historically, the English government attempted to regulate book costs. The first English Copyright Act of 1709 allowed any person who believed the published price of a book was "unreasonably high" to lodge a complaint with the archbishop of Canterbury and other named individuals. If the complaint was found to be well-founded, the price would be reduced. Booksellers who charged more than the fixed price were subject to a fine of £5 for every copy sold, although this enactment apparently remained a "dead letter."
