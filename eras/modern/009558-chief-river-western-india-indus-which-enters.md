# The Indus River

The Indus is the chief river of western India. It enters the presidency from the north of Sind and flows south in a tortuous course before falling into the Arabian Sea. The river's discharge into the sea occurs through several mouths, including the Khori, Sir, Bhitiara, Wari, Mall, Vatho, Hajamro, Sisa, Pitiani, Khudi, and Ghizri creeks.

## Hydrology and Seasonal Variation
The Indus exhibits significant changes in depth and current based on the season. During the dry season, the river bed varies in width between 480 and 1,600 yards. The flood season begins in March and continues until September. During this period, the average depth of the river increases from 9 to 24 feet, and the velocity of the current rises from 3 to 7 meters per hour.

## Geography and Regional Impact
The Indus serves as a primary geographical marker across several regions of British India:

*   **The Great Plain:** The Indus forms the western boundary of the great plain of northern India. This plain, which extends from the delta of the Ganges and Brahmaputra in the east to the delta of the Indus in the west, is composed of deposits so finely comminuted that it is possible to travel from the Bay of Bengal, through the Punjab, and down the Indus to the sea—a distance of over 2,000 meters—without encountering a pebble.
*   **Sind:** In the Sind province, the river is flanked by the Hala mountains on its right bank, which separate British India from the dominions of the khan of Kalat. To the east of the river lie ridges of low sandhills. In the native state of Khairpur, the Indus provides water for five canals, as well as the Eastern Nara, a canal that follows an old bed of the river.
*   **Frontier Districts:** In the district of Dera Ghazi Khan, the Indus forms the eastern boundary. The eastern portion of this district benefits from the river's floods. In the district of Dera Ismail Khan, the river formerly divided the area into two nearly equal portions; the tract to the east of the present bed is known as the *Kachi* and is exposed to river action.

## Navigation and Transport
The Indus is navigable by native boats in several sections, including its course of 120 meters within the Dera Ismail Khan district and throughout the district of Dera Ghazi Khan, where the river is not bridged.

In the Karachi district of Sind, the town of Kotri is situated on the right bank of the Indus. Kotri serves as a railway junction where a bridge crosses the river to serve both banks. While the development of railway facilities led to the abolition of the Indus steam flotilla, for which Kotri was the headquarters, the town continues to maintain a considerable general transit trade by river.

## Human and Cultural Associations
The Indus valley has been a corridor for various peoples and scientific classifications:

*   **Dardistan:** Scientists use the term "Dardistan" to describe a tract of country on the north-west frontier. This includes the Indus valley from Bunji to Batera. The "Dard" races are believed by some to be of Aryan origin, having ascended the Indus valley from the plains of the Punjab to reach as far north as Chitral.
*   **Tribal Boundaries:** The river serves as a marker for ethnic distributions. In Dera Ismail Khan, the river historically divided the district, with the trans-Indus tract (allotted to the North-West Frontier Province in 1901) and the cis-Indus tract (remaining in Punjab jurisdiction). In Dera Ghazi Khan, the river marks the eastern limit of a region inhabited largely by Baluch Mahommedans.
