# The Great Depression: The Global Economic Collapse of the 1930s

## Overview
The Great Depression was the most severe economic downturn in the history of the industrialized Western world. While often associated with the U.S. stock market crash of October 1929, the Depression was a complex global phenomenon resulting from a convergence of systemic weaknesses. It was characterized by a catastrophic collapse in GDP, deflation, and unprecedented levels of unemployment—reaching nearly 25% in the United States at its peak.

The crisis was exacerbated by a cycle of bank failures and a contraction in the money supply. As citizens rushed to withdraw their savings (bank runs), financial institutions collapsed, wiping out the life savings of millions. Internationally, the crisis was deepened by protectionist trade policies, most notably the U.S. Smoot-Hawley Tariff Act of 1930, which triggered retaliatory tariffs and caused global trade to plummet.

Historians and economists continue to debate the primary catalyst of the collapse. The "Monetarist" school, led by Milton Friedman, argues that the Federal Reserve's failure to provide liquidity to the banking system turned a typical recession into a depression. Conversely, Keynesian economists argue that a collapse in aggregate demand and under-consumption were the primary drivers, necessitating the massive government spending seen in Franklin D. Roosevelt’s "New Deal."

## Timeline of Key Events

| Year | Event | Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

| 1932 | Peak Unemployment | U.S. unemployment hits approximately 24.9%. |

| 1937 | The "Roosevelt Recession" | A temporary economic dip caused by premature spending cuts. |

## Key Facts
*   **The Dust Bowl:** Environmental catastrophe struck the American Great Plains, where severe drought and poor farming practices led to massive soil erosion, forcing thousands of "Okies" to migrate west.
*   **The New Deal:** A series of programs (e.g., the WPA, CCC, and Social Security Act) designed to provide immediate relief and long-term structural reform to the financial system.
*   **Gold Standard:** Many historians argue that the adherence to the Gold Standard prevented governments from expanding the money supply, prolonging the economic misery.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Bonus Army:** In 1932, thousands of WWI veterans marched on Washington D.C. to demand early payment of their service bonuses; they were forcibly removed by the U.S. Army.
2. **Bank Holidays:** Upon taking office in 1933, FDR declared a "National Bank Holiday" to stop bank runs and restore confidence in the financial system.
3. **Global Reach:** Germany was hit particularly hard, with massive unemployment contributing to the political instability that allowed the Nazi Party to rise to power.
