# Pacific Islands of British Possession

## Overview of Pacific Territories
In the 20th century, Great Britain maintained a variety of island possessions and dependencies across the Pacific Ocean. These territories include a wide array of islands, groups, and reefs. Among these are the Cook Islands, the Phoenix Group, the Purdy Group, the Willis Group, and the Kermadec Islands. Other specific islands under British possession in the Pacific include Bird Island, Bramble Cay, Cato Island, Macquarie Island, Manihiki Islands, Nassau Island, Palmerston Island, Palmyra Island, Rakaanga Island, Rotumah Island, Surprise Island, and Washington or New York Island. Additionally, the archive records the existence of Wreck Reef.

## Other Global Island Contexts
While the aforementioned islands are located in the Pacific, the archive contains records of other island groups across different oceans and regions:

### Indian Ocean and Africa
In the Indian Ocean, British colonies and possessions include Rodriguez, the Chagos Islands, the St Brandon Islands, the Amirante Islands, Aldabra, the Kuria Muria Islands, and the Maldive Islands. In West Africa, the Los Islands (Islas de los Idolos) off the coast of French Guinea were once a seat for pirates and slave-traders. These islands—Tamara, Factory, Crawford, White (or Ruma), and Coral—were garrisoned by British troops between 1812 and 1813. Sir Charles McCarthy, the governor of Sierra Leone, obtained the cession of these islands to Great Britain in 1818, and France recognized this possession in 1882. However, under the Anglo-French convention of April 8, 1904, the islands were ceded to France.

### The Americas and Asia
The Bahamas, an archipelago of the British West Indies, consists of an estimated 29 islands, 661 cays, and 2387 rocks. Key areas include New Providence (containing the capital, Nassau), Andros, Great Abaco, and Great Inagua. 

In Asia, the Bonin Islands (known to the Japanese as Ogasawara-jima) are a volcanic chain of twenty small islands belonging to Japan. These include islands such as Chichi-jima (Father Island), Haha-jima (Mother Island), and others named for family relations (e.g., Elder-brother, Younger-sister). In the Dutch East Indies, Bachian (Batjan) is a volcanic island in the Molucca Sea, known for the production of cloves, sago, and cocoa-nuts, and as the easternmost inhabited point for the black ape known as *Quadrumana*.
