# Education

## The University of Bombay
Established in 1857, the University of Bombay was constituted as an examining body based on the model of the University of London. It is a body corporate composed of fellows, a vice-chancellor, and a chancellor; the governor of Bombay serves as the chancellor *ex officio*. The university's senate also plays a role in local governance, as two members of the municipal corporation of the city are elected by the senate.

## Educational Administration and Institutions in Bombay
The education department is managed by a director of public instruction, who is responsible for administering the department according to the general educational policy of the state. While the government system has generally been adopted by native states, the Kathiawar states and Baroda employ their own inspectors.

In Bombay City, the primary educational institutions include:
*   **Government Institutions:** Elphinstone College and the government law school.
*   **Medical and Technical Education:** Grant Medical College and the Victoria Jubilee technical institute.
*   **Arts:** The Sir Jamsetjee Jeejeebhoy school of art.
*   **Missionary Colleges:** St Xavier and Wilson.

## Regional Statistics and Literacy
As of 1905, there were 10,194 educational institutions with a total of 593,431 pupils. The region contained ten art colleges; five were under private management, three were managed by native states, and two were managed by the government. Despite these institutions, literacy rates remained low; according to the 1901 census, nearly 24 million people were illiterate out of a population of 25-1/2 millions.

## Comparative Educational Systems
Other regions established similar examining bodies in 1857, such as Calcutta University, which was also modeled after the University of London. Calcutta's chief institutions included the Government Presidency College, the Sanskrit College, the Mahommedan Madrasah, the government medical college, and the government engineering college at Sibpur. It also featured the Bethune College and high schools for girls, as well as four unaided native colleges and three aided missionary colleges.

In other parts of the world, such as Baden, public education is entirely managed by the government, featuring a technical college at Karlsruhe and two universities: the Roman Catholic at Freiburg-im-Breisgau and the Protestant at Heidelberg. In Bristol, University College (1876) served as the nucleus for the university of Bristol, which was chartered in 1909. Other Bristol institutions include the technical college of the Company of Merchant Venturers (1885) and Clifton College (opened 1862), the latter of which houses an observatory, a museum, and a physical science school.

## Educational Methods and Figures
The "monitorial" plan of education was developed by Andrew Bell (1753-1832). While serving as superintendent of the male orphan asylum at Madras in 1889, Bell introduced a system of mutual tuition by pupils due to a scarcity of teachers. This system was later improved upon by Joseph Lancaster. Bell's principles eventually led to the 1811 formation of the "National Society for Promoting the Education of the Poor in the Principles of the Established Church."

Other notable figures associated with education and academia include:
*   **Sir George Birdwood:** A professor at Grant Medical College and registrar of the university in Bombay.
*   **N. B. Wagle:** A former lecturer on Sanskrit at the Robert Money Institution in Bombay.
*   **Montague Hughes Crackanthore:** President of the Eugenics Education Society and former member of the Council of Legal Education.
*   **Morris Jastrow:** Professor of Semitic Languages at the University of Pennsylvania.
