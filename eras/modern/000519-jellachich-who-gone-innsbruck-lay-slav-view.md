# Baron Joseph Jellachich

Baron Joseph Jellachich was a prominent political and military figure in the mid-19th century Austro-Hungarian Empire, serving as the ban of Croatia. His career was defined by his role as a representative for the alliance between the southern Slav nationalities and the conservative elements of the empire.

## Appointment and Early Political Activity
Jellachich's appointment as ban of Croatia was confirmed on March 23 by the diet of Agram. This appointment was made as a concession to the agitation occurring among the southern Slavs during a period of widespread national movements and political instability. 

Initially, Jellachich's activities were viewed with suspicion at Innsbruck, where he was seen as another force contributing to the disintegration of the empire. He appeared to align himself with the "Illyrian" party and severed all communications with the Hungarian government. In defiance of an imperial edict issued following the urgency of Batthyani, Jellachich summoned a diet to Agram. On June 9, this diet decreed that the "Triune Kingdom" (comprising Croatia, Slavonia, and Dalmatia) should be separated from Hungary.

## Conflict with the Imperial Government and Hungary
The imperial government, which was hoping for Magyar assistance to combat revolutionists in Vienna, initially repudiated Jellachich's actions. The government accused him of treason and disobedience, subsequently stripping him of his military rank. 

However, Jellachich's strategic objectives were to use the nationalism of the "Illyrians" as a counterweight to the radicalism of the Germans and Magyars, thereby preserving his province for the monarchy. This position was strengthened when the Hungarian government attempted to alter the relationship between the army and the monarchy during a critical campaign in Italy. In May, Hungary established a National Guard and encouraged line soldiers to join for higher pay; by June 1, the garrison of Pest had taken an oath to the Constitution.

In response, on June 10, Jellachich issued a proclamation to Croatian regiments serving in Italy, ordering them to remain and fight for the common Fatherland and the emperor. This act announced his loyalty to the imperial army's traditions and cemented the alliance between the southern Slavs and the army.

## The Slav-Conservative Alliance and the Diet of Agram
Jellachich traveled to Innsbruck to present the Slav view to the emperor. Following this, he was permitted to return to Agram, although he was not immediately reinstated formally. Upon his return, the diet passed a resolution that denounced the dual system and demanded that the union of the empire be restored. This move proclaimed the identity of the conservative point of view with that of the Slavs.

On July 9, Jellachich prorogued the "Illyrian" assembly indefinitely, though he declared it "permanent." He accompanied this action with a "paternal greeting," stating that the safety of the Fatherland now depended more on "physical than upon moral force." The diet that was prorogued on this date never met again.

## Later Position
Having established himself as the absolute master of the forces of the banat, Jellachich remained in a position of power, waiting for the "intractable politicians of Pest" to provide him with the excuse and occasion to set the imperial army in motion against them.
