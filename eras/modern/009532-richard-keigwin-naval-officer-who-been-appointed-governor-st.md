# Richard Keigwin

## Early Career and Appointment to St Helena
Richard Keigwin was a naval officer who gained distinction in the late 17th century. In 1673, he played a part in the capture of the island of St Helena from the Dutch. As a reward for his actions during this operation, he was appointed as the governor of St Helena.

## The Rebellion at Bombay
In 1683, Keigwin became the leader of a formidable movement in Bombay. This unrest occurred during a period when the settlement's position was precarious; the city had been isolated between the rival powers of the Mogul empire and the Mahrattas, and its trade was constantly menaced by Malabar pirates. Furthermore, the English waters had become a battle-ground for rival navies under the weak leadership of the governor Rolt (1677–1682).

The rebellion was fueled in part by the "niggardly policy" of the Company's board of directors, who prioritized peaceful dividends over warlike rule—a stance that was galling to soldiers of fortune. Keigwin was elected governor of Bombay by popular vote. Upon taking power, he issued a proclamation in the name of the king, which declared his government to be under the immediate authority of the crown. In this proclamation, Keigwin cited the "exactions, oppressions and intolerable extortions" of the Company as the justification for his actions.

## Governance and Administration
During his tenure as the elected governor, Keigwin ruled with moderation. He implemented several administrative and economic changes, including:
*   **Taxation:** He reformed the existing system of taxation.
*   **Trade:** He increased the trade of the port by admitting "interlopers."
*   **Diplomacy:** He succeeded in obtaining notable concessions from the Mahrattas.

## Surrender of Power
Despite his successes within the city, Keigwin failed to extend the rebellion beyond the limits of Bombay. His period of independent rule ended when he received a letter under the royal sign manual. The letter ordered him to surrender the fort to Sir John Child, who had been appointed as the captain-general and admiral of the Company's forces. Keigwin obeyed the royal order and surrendered the fort.
