# Extraordinary Military Expenditure

In the records of the Human History Archive, military expenditure is documented across various states and eras, ranging from the specialized "extraordinary" costs of the Dual Monarchy to the systemic funding of ancient Greek city-states and the naval concerns of the early 20th century.

## Expenditure in the Dual Monarchy
Financial records for the Dual Monarchy detail a specific allocation for "Extraordinary Military Expenditure," which amounted to L4,785,500. Additionally, there was a separate allocation for Extraordinary Military Expenditure in Bosnia totaling 315,920. These figures exist alongside the standard expenditures for the Ministry of War, which included the Army (L12,679,160) and the Navy (L2,306,100).

The joint budget for the years 1875 to 1905 shows a steady increase in the Ministry of War's spending (covering both Army and Navy). In 1875, the expenditure was 9,005.4 thousand sterling, rising to 10,085 thousand in 1885, 12,539 thousand in 1895, 13,887.5 thousand in 1900, and reaching 18,087.7 thousand by 1905. During this same period, the general debt borne jointly by Austria and Hungary saw a slight decrease, from 232.41 million sterling in 1875 to 224.31 million sterling in 1905.

## Military Funding in Ancient Athens
In ancient Athens, the peace-establishment included the maintenance of arsenals, larger docks, and more ships of war than any other Greek city. The state spent forty talents annually on the Horsemen, with additional spending on other military forces. These peace-time costs, along with the Theôric expenditure (funds for religious manifestations and the honor of the gods), were defrayed without the use of direct taxation.

Direct taxation was reserved specifically for the "extraordinary cost incident to a state of war." Athens did not maintain an accumulated war-fund to meet these needs. When the war against Philip became serious, the proprietary classes—those on the schedule of assessment—were required to pay a direct tax. Some members of this class attempted to avoid this burden by proposing that the festival-fund be used instead, though such a proposal would have been viewed unfavorably by the general public.

## Early 20th Century European and Global Trends
By 1913, military expenditure had increased significantly across Europe, with the five Great Powers adding over 50 million to their spending. During this period of global increase in the efficiency and number of soldiers, British military strength showed a decline; the regular army dropped by 6,200 men, the Special Reserve was short by 20,000, and the Territorials were short by 65,000. There was a concern that if confidence in naval administration failed, public attention would lead directly to "largely increased expenditure."

In other regions, such as Bolivia, military expenses were listed as one of the chief sources of expenditure. In 1905, the appropriation for war was 2,081,119 bolivianos.

## Military Restrictions and Treaties
Military expenditure and development were sometimes limited by international agreement. In a treaty between Japan and Russia, both governments agreed to exploit railways in Manchuria exclusively for commercial and industrial purposes, not for strategic purposes (though this restriction did not apply to the railway in the territory leased by the Liao-tung Peninsula). 

Furthermore, Japan and Russia agreed not to construct any fortifications or similar military works in their respective possessions on the adjacent islands or the Island of Saghalien. They also committed to avoiding military measures that would impede the free navigation of the Straits of Tartary and La Pérouse.
