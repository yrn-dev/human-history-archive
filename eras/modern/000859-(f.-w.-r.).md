## Differentiable Functions of Complex Variables
A function $[f](z_0)$ is defined as a differentiable function of the complex variable $z_0$ over a specific region if, for every interior and boundary point $z_0$, there exist two definite finite numbers $[f](z_0)$ and $F(z_0)$. For any real positive quantity $\eta$, there must exist a real positive number $\epsilon$ such that the condition $|[f](z) - [f](z_0)| / |z - z_0| - F(z_0) < \eta$ is satisfied for every point $z$ within or upon the boundary of the region where $|z - z_0| < \epsilon$. In this context, $F(z_0)$ is the differential coefficient. Such a function is a continuous function of the real variables $x_0$ and $y_0$ (where $z_0 = x_0 + iy_0$), and $F(z_0)$ is also continuous and a differentiable function of $z_0$.

## Power Series and Taylor's Theorem
Taylor's theorem allows for the representation of a function by an infinite series under specific conditions. A function is considered "analytic" in a domain if it can be represented by the series $[f](x) = [f](a) + \sum_{r=1}^{\infty} \frac{(x - a)^r}{r!} [f]^{(r)}(a)$. This requires that the function has continuous differential coefficients of all finite orders in the interval between $a$ and $a + k$ (excluding the endpoints) and progressive differential coefficients at $a$. Additionally, Cauchy's form of the remainder $R_n$ must have a limit of zero as $n$ increases indefinitely.

Regarding power series, the archive notes the following theorems:
*   A power series converges uniformly in any interval within its domain of convergence, excluding the endpoints.
*   A series that converges uniformly may be integrated term by term.
*   A series can be differentiated term by term if the series of derived functions converges uniformly.
*   A differentiable function $[f](z)$ is expressible by a power series in $z - c$ about every interior point $c$ of its region of definition.

## Classification of Functions
Functions are categorized based on their singular points:
*   **Integral Function:** A single-valued function with no singular points except $z = \infty$. Examples include integral polynomials and $\exp(z)$.
*   **Meromorphic Function:** A function with no singular points for finite values of $z$ other than poles.
*   **Rational Function:** A meromorphic function that also possesses a pole at $z = \infty$.
