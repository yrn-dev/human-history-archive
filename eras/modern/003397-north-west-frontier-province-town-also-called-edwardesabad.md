# North-West Frontier Province

The North-West Frontier Province is a region of British India containing several strategic districts and towns, including Kohat, Bannu, and Dera Ismail Khan. The province is characterized by its military importance, its role as a base for frontier expeditions, and the presence of Pathan tribes.

## Kohat District
Kohat is a town and district located within the Peshawar division of the North-West Frontier Province. Situated in the heart of the Pathan country, the town lies 37 miles south of Peshawar. Access between the two is provided by the Kohat Pass, where a military road was established in 1901. 

As of 1901, the population of Kohat was 30,762. This figure includes 12,670 residents of the cantonment, which houses infantry, cavalry, and artillery. Kohat serves as the military base for the southern Afridi frontier. Historically, it served as the starting point for Sir William Lockhart's expedition against the Afridis and Orakzais during the Tirah campaign of 1897-98.

Within the Kohat district lies the village of Kushalgarh. This location is notable for a bridge across the Indus, which allows for the extension of the strategic frontier railway from Rawalpindi into the Kurram and Miranzai valleys.

## Bannu District
Bannu is a town and district situated in the Derajat division of the North-West Frontier Province. The town, which is also known as Dhulipnagar and Edwardesabad, is located in the valley of the Kurram river in the north-west corner of the district. In 1901, the population of the town was 14,300. Bannu serves as the base for all punitive expeditions directed toward the Waziri frontier and the Tochi Valley.

## Dera Ismail Khan
Located in the Derajat division of the North-West Frontier Province, Dera Ismail Khan is a town and district situated near the right bank of the Indus. For half of the year, the river is crossed here via a bridge of boats. The town's name is derived from Ismail Khan, a Baluch chief who settled in the area toward the end of the 15th century; his descendants continued to rule for 300 years.

The current town was established 4 miles back from the permanent channel of the river after a flood destroyed the old town in 1823. Dera Ismail Khan is home to many Mahommedan gentry and features well-laid-out native quarters with a large bazaar catering to Afghan traders. The town's cantonment can accommodate approximately one brigade of troops. Local industry includes the manufacture of inlaid wood-work and cotton cloth scarves, and there is significant through trade with Afghanistan via the Gomal Pass. In 1901, the population was 31,737.

## Regional Tribes
The province is inhabited by various groups, including the Khattak, an important Pathan tribe. The Khattak inhabit the eastern and south-eastern portions of Kohat, as well as the south-eastern portion of the Peshawar district. Numbering 24,000, the Khattak are described as loyal and quiet subjects of the British government. They are noted for being excellent soldiers and provide many recruits for the Indian army.
