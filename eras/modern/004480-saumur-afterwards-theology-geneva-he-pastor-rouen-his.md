# Jacques Basnage

Jacques Basnage (1653–1723) was a French Protestant divine and diplomat. He was born in Rouen, Normandy, as the eldest son of Henri Basnage, sieur de Franquenay, an eminent lawyer who lived from 1615 to 1695.

## Education and Early Ministry
Basnage pursued his early education in classical languages at Saumur. Following these studies, he moved to Geneva to study theology. Upon completing his education, he returned to his native city of Rouen, where he served as a pastor from 1676 until 1685.

## Exile and Ministry in Holland
In 1685, following the revocation of the Edict of Nantes, Basnage obtained permission from the king to retire to Holland. He first settled in Rotterdam, where he served as a minister pensionary. In 1691, he was chosen as the pastor of the Walloon church in Rotterdam. 

His career later shifted toward civil and diplomatic affairs. In 1709, A. Heinsius, the grand pensionary, secured Basnage's election as one of the pastors of the Walloon church at the Hague. Heinsius intended to utilize Basnage primarily for civil matters. In this capacity, Basnage engaged in secret negotiations with Marshal d'Uxelles, who served as the plenipotentiary of France at the congress of Utrecht. Basnage's success in these negotiations led to his appointment to several other important commissions, which he discharged with great ability.

## Later Diplomatic Activity and Political Views
Basnage continued to be a figure of interest to political leaders. In 1716, Dubois—who was at the Hague on behalf of the regent Orleans to negotiate the Triple Alliance between Holland, Great Britain, and France—sought Basnage's advice. Despite having been denied permission to visit France on a short trip the previous year, Basnage worked to further these negotiations.

The French government also sought his assistance regarding a threatened rising in the Cevennes. While Basnage welcomed the revival of the Protestant church brought about by the zeal of Antoine Court, he remained opposed to active resistance. Adhering to the principles of Calvin, he denounced the rebellion of the Camisards. He detailed these views in his work, *Instructions pastorales aux Reformes de France sur l'obeissance due aux souverains*, published in Paris in 1720. This text was printed by order of the court and distributed throughout the south of France.

Jacques Basnage died on September 22, 1723.
