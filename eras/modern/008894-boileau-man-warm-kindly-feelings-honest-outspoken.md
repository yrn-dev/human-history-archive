# Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux

## Life and Education
Nicolas Boileau-Despreaux (1636–1711) was a French critic and poet born on November 1, 1636, in Paris on the rue de Jerusalem. He was the fifteenth child of Gilles Boileau, who served as a clerk in the parlement. His family background included brothers of some distinction: Jacques Boileau, a canon of the Sainte-Chapelle who contributed to church history, and Gilles Boileau (1631–1669), who authored a translation of Epictetus.

Boileau's early life was marked by the loss of his mother when he was two years old. Due to a delicate constitution, he is believed to have suffered from a lack of care during this period. Sainte-Beuve suggests that the uninspiring circumstances of these early years, combined with the general character of the era, contributed to Boileau's somewhat unsympathetic and hard outlook. He is described as having grown up without illusions, driven by a single passion: "the hatred of stupid books."

His formal education took place at the College de Beauvais, after which he studied theology at the Sorbonne. He eventually shifted his studies to law and was called to the bar on December 4, 1656. However, he soon recoiled from the legal profession in disgust, citing the prevalence of chicanery in the name of justice. Following the death of his father in 1657, Boileau received a small fortune, which allowed him to devote himself entirely to letters.

## Character and Reputation
Boileau is described as a benevolent, honest, and outspoken man characterized by warm and kindly feelings. His generous actions and frankness of speech were frequently noted in anecdotes regarding his time at court.

## Literary Contributions and Influence
Boileau holds a significant position in French literature as the first individual to reduce its versification to rule. He is credited with teaching the value of workmanship for its own sake. His influence extended to English literature through contemporaries such as Pope, although this influence was less durable than his impact on French letters.

While some of his individual criticisms were considered unduly harsh, recent writers have rehabilitated his critical work, noting that most of his criticisms were substantially adopted by those who followed him. Some scholars suggest this rehabilitation may have gone as far as exaggeration in the opposite direction.

## Publication History and Critical Texts
Numerous editions of Boileau's works were released during his lifetime. The 1701 publication *Oeuvres diverses* is recognized as the "favourite" edition of the poet; it was later reprinted in 1894 with notes and variants by Alphonse Pauly in two volumes.

The critical text of his works was established by Berriat Saint-Prix in *Oeuvres de Boileau* (published in four volumes between 1830 and 1837), a project that utilized approximately 350 different editions. This text was subsequently reprinted by Garnier *freres* in 1860, edited with notes by Paul Cheron and including an essay by Sainte-Beuve and the *Boloeana* of 1740.
