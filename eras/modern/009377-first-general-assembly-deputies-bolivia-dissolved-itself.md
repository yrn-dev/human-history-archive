# The First General Assembly of Deputies of Bolivia

## Formation and Independence
The establishment of Bolivia as a distinct nation began with the assembly of fifty-four deputies from various provinces at the capital, Chuquisaca. This body was convened to address a proposal from the government of the Argentine provinces regarding whether the region would remain separate from that country. In August 1825, the deputies decided the matter, declaring that it was the national will for Upper Peru to constitute an independent and distinct nation. Following this decision, the assembly continued its sessions, during which it issued a formal declaration of independence and gave the country the name of Bolivia.

## Dissolution and the New Congress
The first general assembly of deputies of Bolivia dissolved itself on the 6th of October 1825. Subsequently, a new congress was summoned and formally installed at Chuquisaca on the 25th of May 1826. The primary purpose of this new body was to consider a constitution for the new republic that had been prepared by Bolivar. After a committee appointed to examine the document submitted a favourable report, the congress approved it and declared it to be the constitution of the republic, which was then sworn to by the people.

## Presidency of General Sucre
Under the terms of the new constitution, General Sucre was chosen as the president for life. However, Sucre accepted the appointment for a term of only two years. His acceptance was based on the express condition that 2000 Colombian troops be permitted to remain with him. Prior to these constitutional developments, General Sucre had been invested with supreme command in Upper Peru to maintain order until a regular and constitutional government could be established.

## Later Political and Territorial Developments
Bolivia's political history later included periods of instability and conflict. A double revolution occurred in Peru and Bolivia, during which Bolivian troops at Tacna deposed their president due to incompetence or cowardice and elected Colonel Camacho to lead them. Simultaneously, a revolution in La Paz proclaimed General Narciso Campero as president, a position he was formally elected to in June via the ordinary procedure of the constitution.

During the conflict between Chile and Peru, Bolivia played a minimal role in the struggle throughout 1880. Following the surrender of Lima and Callao to Chilean forces, the war lingered until a peace treaty was signed between Chile and Bolivia on 11th December. As a result of this treaty, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, including the port of Cobija.

In an effort to regain access to the sea for commercial prosperity and development, Bolivia signed a treaty with Chile at Santiago on the 18th of May 1895. Under this agreement, Chile pledged to transfer the territories of Tacna and Arica to Bolivia if it acquired permanent sovereignty over them, in exchange for an indemnity of $5,000,000 silver. If this transfer failed, Chile bound itself to cede $5,000,000 silver and the roadstead (caleta) of Vitor, or another analogous port that would satisfy Bolivia's commercial requirements.
