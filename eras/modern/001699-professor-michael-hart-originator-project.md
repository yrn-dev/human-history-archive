# Professor Michael S. Hart and Project Gutenberg

## Origins and Concept
Professor Michael S. Hart was the originator of the concept for Project Gutenberg. The project was envisioned as a library of electronic works designed to be shared freely with anyone. For a period of forty years, Professor Hart was responsible for the production and distribution of Project Gutenberg eBooks, operating with the assistance of only a loose network of volunteer support.

## eBook Production and Copyright
The eBooks provided by Project Gutenberg are frequently developed using several different printed editions. To ensure legality, these editions are confirmed to be not protected by copyright within the United States, provided that no copyright notice is included. Because of this process, the electronic books are not necessarily kept in compliance with any one specific paper edition.

## Access and Search
The primary point of entry for most users is the project's website, located at www.gutenberg.org. This site houses the main search facility for Project Gutenberg.

## Funding and Donations
Project Gutenberg accepts donations through various methods, including credit card donations, online payments, and checks. Those interested in donating can visit www.gutenberg.org/donate. 

Regarding the solicitation of funds, the project does not solicit contributions from states where it has not met specific solicitation requirements. However, there is no known prohibition against accepting unsolicited donations from donors in those states who offer them. While international donations are accepted, the project cannot provide statements regarding the tax treatment of donations received from outside the United States, noting that U.S. laws alone are enough to swamp their small staff. Current addresses and methods for donating are available on the Project Gutenberg web pages.
