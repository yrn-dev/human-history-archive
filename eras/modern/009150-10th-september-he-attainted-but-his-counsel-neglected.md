# Lord Bolingbroke and the Events of September 10

## Political Decline and Exile
Following the accession of George I, Lord Bolingbroke was immediately dismissed from his office. During this period, illuminations and bonfires were held at his house in Golden Square, which were described as "particularly fine and remarkable." Bolingbroke retired to Bucklebury, where it is said he wrote a response to the *Secret History of the White Staff*, a work that had accused him of Jacobitism.

In March 1715, Bolingbroke attempted to defend the previous ministry in the new parliament, but these efforts were unsuccessful. Upon the announcement that Walpole intended to attack the authors of the treaty of Utrecht, Bolingbroke fled to Paris in disguise on March 28, 1715. Before leaving, he sent a letter from Dover to Lord Lansdowne protesting his innocence and challenging his enemies to provide any evidence of criminal correspondence.

## Alignment with the Pretender
By July, Bolingbroke had fully identified himself with the interests of the Pretender and was appointed as the Pretender's secretary. On the 10th of September, Bolingbroke was attainted. Despite his position, his counsel was neglected in favor of Irish priests and ignorant refugees. 

Bolingbroke advised against the expedition of 1715, but the decision to proceed was made regardless of his advice. While he drew up the declaration for James, the priests cancelled the assurances Bolingbroke had included regarding the security of the Church of England.

## Final Break with the Pretender
Bolingbroke remained in Paris and attempted to establish relations with the regent. However, due to jealousies and petty intrigues, he was eventually dismissed from his office upon the return of James. Following this dismissal, Bolingbroke renounced all further efforts on behalf of the Pretender. In a reply to Mary of Modena, who had sent a message deprecating his ill-will, Bolingbroke expressed his resolve by stating that his arm might rot off if he ever used a sword or pen in their service again.
