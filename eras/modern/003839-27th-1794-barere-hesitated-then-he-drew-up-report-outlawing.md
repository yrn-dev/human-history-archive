# Bertrand Barère de Vieuzac

Bertrand Barère de Vieuzac (1755–1841) was a prominent and notorious member of the French National Convention. Born on September 10, 1755, in Tarbes, Gascony, he was the son of a lawyer from Vieuzac. He assumed the name Barère de Vieuzac from a small fief belonging to his father, a title he continued to use even after the renunciation of feudal rights occurred on August 4.

## Early Career and Rise to Prominence
Barère began his professional life as an advocate at the parlement of Toulouse in 1770. He quickly developed a reputation as a skilled orator, and his talent for writing essays led to his election as a member of the Academy of Floral Games of Toulouse in 1788. 

In 1789, the estates of Bigorre elected him as a deputy to the states-general, which convened in May of that year. Having visited Paris for the first time in 1788, Barère found a favorable reception among the provincial deputies due to his liberal opinions, social qualities, manners, and personal appearance.

## Political Evolution and Journalism
During his early political career, Barère aligned himself with the constitutional party. He gained more recognition as a journalist than as a speaker in the Assembly. He published a paper titled *Point du Jour*, which contained reports of the National Assembly's debates. While these reports were not inaccurate, they were characterized as being very dry and incomplete. According to Aulard, the reputation of the *Point du Jour* was bolstered significantly by the painter David, who depicted Barère kneeling in the corner of his painting "Oath in the Tennis Court," recording the proceedings for posterity.

Following the flight of the king to Varennes, Barère shifted his allegiance to the republican party. Despite this shift, he maintained a connection with the duke of Orleans, serving as a tutor to the duke's natural daughter, Pamela.

## Role in the Revolution and Character
Barère's political conduct was noted for a lack of guiding principles or conscience; his actions were driven primarily by a determination to align himself with the strongest faction. After the National Assembly closed, he served as one of the judges of the newly established court of cassation from October 1791.

He later became a member of the French National Convention. His legacy is associated with the period of the Reign of Terror, though he is often viewed as a figure of opportunism.

## Historical Records and Memoirs
The *Memoires de B. Barere*, published between 1824 and 1844 by H. Carnot and David (d'Angers), are considered false. However, they are noted to contain valuable information. A separate "Notice historique" written by Carnot and published in 1842 is regarded as very good. Other assessments of Barère vary; for instance, an essay by Macaulay in the *Edinburgh Review* is described as eloquent but incorrect.
