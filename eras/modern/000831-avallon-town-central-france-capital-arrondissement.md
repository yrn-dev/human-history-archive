# AVALLON

## Geography and Location
Avallon is a town located in central France. It serves as the capital of an arrondissement within the department of Yonne. The town is situated 34 miles south-south-east of Auxerre and is accessible via a branch of the Paris-Lyon railway. Geographically, Avallon is positioned on a promontory; the southern base of this elevation is washed by the Cousin, while small streams flow to the east and west.

## Urban Features and Architecture
The town is characterized by wide streets and picturesque promenades. Several notable architectural structures and historical remains are present:

*   **Church of St Lazare:** Dating from the 12th century, this is the town's chief building. Its two western portals feature sculpture in the ornate Romanesque style. The tower located on the left of the facade was rebuilt during the 17th century.
*   **Tour de L'Horloge:** A structure from the 15th century that contains a museum on its second floor. It is pierced by a gateway through which the Grande Rue passes.
*   **Fortifications:** Remains of the town's ancient fortifications are still visible, including seven of the flanking towers.
*   **Statuary:** The town features a statue of the military engineer Vauban.

## Administration and Education
Avallon houses several public institutions, including a subprefecture, a communal college, and a tribunal of first instance.

## Economy and Industry
The local economy is supported by various manufacturing sectors and trade. Industrial activities include the production of leather, farm implements, gingerbread, and biscuits. Additionally, there is significant trade involving wine, wood, and the agricultural produce and live-stock originating from the surrounding countryside.

## History
In the middle ages, Avallon (referred to as *Aballo*) served as the seat of a viscounty. This viscounty was dependent on the duchy of Burgundy. Following the death of Charles the Bold, the town passed under royal authority.

As of 1906, the population of Avallon was 5,197.
