# Carlo Buonaparte and Letizia Ramolino

## Family Origins and Marriage
Carlo Buonaparte (1746–1785), the father of Napoleon I, was a law graduate from the university of Pisa. In 1764, he married Letizia Ramolino, who was fourteen years old at the time of the marriage. Letizia was described as a beautiful and high-spirited girl from a well-connected family that had been domiciled in Corsica since the middle of the 15th century. 

The couple's first two children, born in 1765 and 1767, died during infancy. Their first surviving son, Joseph, was born in 1768, followed by Napoleon in 1769.

## Professional Life and Character of Carlo Buonaparte
Following the French conquest of Corsica, Carlo Buonaparte served as an assessor to the royal court of Ajaccio and the surrounding districts. He is characterized as having a restless and dissatisfied nature, which drove him to intrigue for different posts and engage in risky business ventures. These enterprises resulted in the compromise of his family's fortune for many years. 

In terms of his personal convictions, Carlo was a Voltairian and a schemer by nature. These traits led him to seek and point the way toward careers in France, a move that ultimately molded the destinies of Europe and the fortunes of his family. Carlo Buonaparte died in Montpellier in 1785 from cancer of the stomach.

## Character and Influence of Letizia Ramolino
Letizia Ramolino is described as having a firm and courageous disposition, which became evident during the troubles following the French conquest of Corsica, a period during which she accompanied her husband on several escapes and journeys. In her personal life, she was devout in thought and manner, and maintained simple and frugal tastes.

While Carlo looked toward France for the family's advancement, Letizia helped to bind her children to the life of Corsica. Her career is noted as having been singularly varied.
