# Bukovina

## Geography and Environment
Bukovina is a crownland and duchy of Austria with a total area of 4,035 square miles. It is bordered to the north by Galicia, to the east by Russia and Rumania, to the south by Rumania, and to the west by Hungary and Transylvania. The region's terrain is characterized by the offshoots of the Carpathians, particularly in the southern areas, where the Giumaleu reaches an altitude of 6,100 feet. Key mountain passes include the Borgo Pass and the Radna Pass.

The region's hydrology is defined by the Danube watershed, with the exception of the Dniester river which runs along the northern border. Other primary rivers include the Sereth—along with its affluents the Bistritza, Moldava, and Suczawa—and the Pruth. The climate is described as healthy but severe, particularly during winter, though it remains generally milder than the climate of Galicia; for example, the mean annual temperature in Czernowitz is 46.9° F.

The name "Bukovina" is derived from the abundance of beech trees. Woodland covers 43.17% of the total area. Of the remaining land, 27.59% is arable land, 12.68% consists of meadows, 10.09% is pasture, and 0.78% is dedicated to gardens.

## Economy and Industry
The soil in Bukovina is fertile, leading to significant progress in agriculture. The primary agricultural products include barley, oats, rye, wheat, maize, hemp, flax, and potatoes. Cattle-rearing also serves as a major source of revenue. The government operates a salt mine at Kaczyka, as salt is the principal mineral found in the region. The chief industries are milling, distilling, and brewing. Commerce is primarily handled by Armenians and Jews and is mostly limited to raw products, including wood, wool, cattle, and agricultural produce.

## Demographics and Society
As of 1900, Bukovina had a population of 729,921, resulting in a population density of 181 inhabitants per square mile. The population is ethnically diverse:
*   Ruthenians: over 40%
*   Rumanians: 35%
*   Jews: 13%
*   Other groups: Germans, Russians, Poles, Armenians, and Hungarians.

The official language used for instruction in the university, as well as in the law-courts and administration, is German.

Religious affiliation is predominantly Orthodox, with nearly 70% of the population belonging to the Greek Orthodox Church under the jurisdiction of the metropolitan or archbishop of Czernowitz. Other religious groups include Roman Catholics (11%), the Greek United Church (3.25%), and Protestants (2.5%).
