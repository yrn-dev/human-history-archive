# Population Density and Distribution

Population density, often measured as inhabitants per square mile (sq. m.), varies significantly across different global regions, provinces, and administrative districts. Data from the early 20th century reveals a wide spectrum of population concentrations, ranging from sparsely populated territories to densely inhabited provinces.

## European Population Densities

In Europe, population density varies by region and province. In Spain, the province of Biscay exhibited a high density of 372.4 persons per sq. m. in 1900, a figure attributed to a uniformly high birth-rate, a low death-rate, and minimal loss through emigration. In contrast, the administrative district of Beja in Portugal—the largest and most thinly populated district in that country—had a population of 163,612 in 1900 across an area of 3,958 sq. m., resulting in 41.3 inhabitants per sq. m.

In the region of Carniola, the population in 1900 was 508,348, which corresponds to 132 inhabitants per sq. m. The population of Carniola was nearly 95% Slovenes and 5% Germans. Within this province, the capital, Laibach, had a population of 36,547. Other notable populations included Oberlaibach (5,882), Idria (5,772), Gurkfeld (5,294), Zirknitz (5,266), Adelsberg (3,636), Neumarktl (2,626), Krainburg (2,484), and Gottschee (2,421).

In the Canary Islands, the island of Fuerteventura had a population of 11,669 in 1900. Its largest town, Oliva, had a population of 2,464, while the administrative capital, Betancuria, had 586 inhabitants, and the chief port, Cabras, had 1,000.

## Other Global Regions

In Asia, Korea (a tributary state of India transferred from Bengal to the Central Provinces in 1905) had a population of 35,113 in 1901. With an area of 1,631 sq. m., its density was only 22 persons per sq. m.

In Central America, an official 1905 estimate placed the aggregate population at 500,136, though a complete census was difficult as Indian populations and other "ignorant masses" avoided the process due to fears of taxation or military conscription. In 1905, there were fewer than 11 inhabitants per sq. m. The population was concentrated on the Pacific slope, while the Atlantic declivity remained uninhabited or sparsely occupied by Indian tribes. The capital, Tegucigalpa, had a population of approximately 35,000 in 1905. Other towns with over 10,000 inhabitants included Nacaome, La Esperanza, Choluteca, and Santa Rosa.

## Birth Rates and Demographics in Scotland

Demographic data for Scotland in 1906 indicates a total of 132,005 births, with 122,699 legitimate and 9,306 illegitimate. This represents a birth rate of 27.93 births per 1,000 of the population.

The percentage of illegitimate births relative to total births in Scotland showed a general decline from 1860 to 1902, falling from 9.22% in 1860 to 6.28% in 1902, before rising slightly to 7.05% by 1906. 

When analyzed by district in 1906, the rate of illegitimate births per 1,000 of the population varied:
*   **Highest rates:** Elgin (or Moray) at 15.66, Banff at 12.93, and Wigtoun at 12.79.
*   **Lowest rates:** Dumbarton at 4.14, Renfrew at 4.46, and Fife at 4.56.

Across different town types, the rate of illegitimate births per 1,000 of the population was 7.14 for principal towns, 5.58 for large towns, and 6.23 for small towns. Rural areas showed higher rates, with mainland-rural at 9.08 and insular-rural at 5.88.
