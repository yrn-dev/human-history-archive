# Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien

*Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien* is a scientific work authored by Fischer. The text is cited within historical and scientific archives as a source for the study of bacteriology, specifically regarding the structure and functions of bacteria and their interactions with plant life.

## Bacterial Invasion of Leguminous Roots
The work provides detailed observations on how bacteria invade the roots of leguminous plants. In the case of the Pea, bacteria enter through the root-hair; particles of earth are found at the free end of the hair, while a mass of bacteria gathers on the left. These bacteria push their way up into the hair in a thin stream. 

Further into the root, bacteria utilize an "infection thread," also referred to as zoogloea, to push through the cell-walls of the root epidermis.

## Root Nodules and Bacterial Tissue
Fischer's research describes the formation and composition of root nodules, such as those found in the lupin. A longitudinal section of a root and its nodule reveals the presence of a fibro-vascular bundle and specialized bacterial tissue. 

Within these bacterial tissues, cells contain a nucleus and protoplasm that is filled with bacteria. The work identifies different forms of bacteria associated with these plants, including the normal, undegenerate form found in lupin nodules, as well as bacteroids found in *Lupinus albus* and *Vicia villosa*.

## Publication and Academic Context
*Vorlesungen ueber Bakterien* was published in Jena, with a second edition released in 1902. It is categorized among the important books of recent bacteriological literature, alongside other foundational texts on the subject. 

Fischer's contributions to the field extend beyond this specific volume. He also authored *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (published in Oxford in 1900, 2nd edition, and in Jena in 1903) and *Unters. ueber den Bau der Cyanophyceen und Bakterien* (Jena, 1897). Additionally, Fischer has published research on cilia in *Pringsh. Jahrb.* (vol. xxvii).
