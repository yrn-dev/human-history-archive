# Queen Eleanor Cross Commemorating the Countess of Loudoun

## Location and Description
The Queen Eleanor cross commemorating the countess of Loudoun is located in Ashby-de-la-Zouch, a market town in the Bosworth parliamentary division of Leicestershire, England. The town is situated 118 miles north-west by north from London and is accessible via the Leicester-Burton branch of the Midland railway.

## Artistry and Design
The monument was created by Sir Gilbert Scott. It stands as a commemorative structure within the town, which is also known for other historical sites, including the extensive remains of Ashby Castle located to the south of the town.

## Context of Ashby-de-la-Zouch
The cross is one of several notable features in Ashby-de-la-Zouch. The town's church, St Helen, is a fine Perpendicular building that underwent enlargement and restoration in 1880; it houses monuments of the Huntingdon family and an old finger-pillory used for punishing misbehaviour within the church. 

Additionally, the town is noted for the Ivanhoe baths, established in 1826. These baths are frequented for saline waters containing bromine, which are utilized for the treatment of rheumatic and scrofulous complaints. The springs for these baths are located at Moira, three miles to the west. The surrounding district is characterized by extensive coal-mines, including those at Moira, from which the Ashby-de-la-Zouch canal extends south to connect with the Coventry canal.
