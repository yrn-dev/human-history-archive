# BUCKETSHOP

## Definition and Operations
A bucketshop is a slang financial term used to describe the business or office of a stockbroker of an inferior class. Such brokers are characterized by the fact that they are not members of any official exchange. Instead of conducting legitimate trades, the bucketshop broker carries out speculative operations for clients who provide a cover or deposit a margin.

In practice, these operations do not involve any actual purchase or sale of stocks. Instead, the transactions are essentially simple wagers or bets placed between the client and the broker. While the term is sometimes applied loosely and wrongfully to any stockbroker who lacks membership in a recognized local exchange, its specific meaning refers to this betting-based speculative business.

## Origins and Etymology
The term "bucketshop" is American in origin. According to the *New English Dictionary*, it is believed to have originated in Chicago. The etymology is linked to the regulations of the Chicago Board of Trade, which prohibited dealings in grain "options" of less than 5,000 bushels.

In response to these restrictions, an unauthorized exchange—referred to as an "Open Board of Trade"—was established on a neighboring street, located below the rooms of the official Board of Trade. This unauthorized exchange catered to small gamblers. The term "bucketshop" is said to have arisen from the practice of members of the Board of Trade sending a lift down to the Open Board to bring up a "bucketful" of smaller speculators during periods when business was slack.
