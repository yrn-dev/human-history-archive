# Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider and the University of Wittenberg

## Life and Academic Career of Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider
Karl Gottlieb Bretschneider (1776–1848) was a German theologian and scholar born in Gersdorf, Saxony. His academic journey began in 1794 at the university of Leipzig, where he spent four years studying theology. Following a period of hesitation, he decided to seek ordination and passed the examination for *candidatus theologiae* with great distinction in 1802. This achievement brought him to the attention of F.V. Reinhard, the court-preacher at Dresden and author of *System der christlichen Moral* (1788–1815), who served as Bretschneider's patron and friend for the rest of his life.

Between 1804 and 1806, Bretschneider served as a *Privat-docent* at the university of Wittenberg. During his tenure there, he delivered lectures on theology and philosophy. This period was marked by significant literary output, most notably his 1805 work on the development of dogma, *Systematische Entwickelung aller in der Dogmatik vorkommenden Begriffe nach den symbolischen Schriften der evangelisch-lutherischen und reformirten Kirche* (which saw a fourth edition in 1841). He also produced an edition of Ecclesiasticus featuring a Latin commentary.

## Departure from Wittenberg
The trajectory of Bretschneider's university career was altered by geopolitical events. Upon the advance of Napoleon's French army into Prussia, Bretschneider determined to leave Wittenberg and abandon his career at the university. With the assistance of F.V. Reinhard, he transitioned into ecclesiastical service, becoming the pastor of Schneeberg in Saxony in 1807.

## Later Ecclesiastical and Academic Appointments
In 1808, Bretschneider was promoted to superintendent of the church of Annaberg. In this role, he was responsible for deciding various matters of ecclesiastical law in accordance with the canon law of Saxony. However, he found that the official duties of the office interfered with his theological studies, and the local climate did not agree with him.

Seeking a change, Bretschneider returned to the university of Wittenberg in August 1812 to earn a degree of doctor of theology. His career culminated in 1816 when he was appointed general superintendent at Gotha. He remained in this position until his death in 1848, a period that served as the primary era of his literary activity.

## Other Notable Figures Associated with Wittenberg
The university of Wittenberg served as a center for various scholars and theologians across different eras. In the 16th century, the university was opened in 1502 by Frederick the Wise, elector of Saxony. It became the site where Luther began preaching and eventually succeeded Staupitz as professor of theology after graduating as Doctor of the Holy Scripture. Luther's teaching drew many students to the once obscure institution. Later, in 1540, the Protestant theologian John Macalpine (also known as Maccabeus) matriculated at Wittenberg, proceeding to his doctorate in 1542 before accepting a chair of theology in Copenhagen.

The university also attracted legal scholars such as Benedikt Carpzov (1565–1624). After studying at Wittenberg and other German universities, Carpzov became a doctor of laws at Wittenberg in 1590, joined the faculty of law in 1592, and eventually held the chair *Digesti infortiati et novi* starting in 1601. Similarly, August Carpzov (1612–1683), a diplomatist and son of Benedikt, studied at Wittenberg and was appointed advocate of the court of justice (*Hofgericht*) there in 1637. In the late 18th and early 19th centuries, the philosopher Wilhelm Traugott Krug also studied at Wittenberg under Jehnichen and Reinhard.
