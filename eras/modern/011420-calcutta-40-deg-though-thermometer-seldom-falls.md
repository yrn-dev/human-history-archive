# Calcutta

## Overview and Population
Calcutta is a major city in India and serves as a primary center of commercial prosperity. According to the census of 1901, it was the most populous city in the Indian empire, though it is closely challenged for supremacy in population and commerce by Bombay.

## Climate and Temperature
The climate of Calcutta is characterized by relatively low temperature variation compared to other regions. In northern India, the greatest difference between the hottest and coldest monthly means does not exceed 40 deg. F., but this range falls off to approximately 15 deg. F. at Calcutta.

Regarding absolute temperatures, the thermometer in Calcutta seldom falls to 50 deg. F., and its absolute minimum is approximately 40 deg. F. This is notably warmer than the absolute minimum in northern India at latitude 30 deg., which hardly goes below 32 deg. F.

## Regional Comparisons
The temperature patterns of Calcutta differ significantly from other Asian and global locations:
*   **Other Indian Cities:** The temperature at Bombay rarely falls below 60 deg. F., and at Madras, it rarely falls as low as 65 deg. F. The range between monthly means is about 10 deg. or 12 deg. at both Bombay and Madras.
*   **Southeast Asia:** In Singapore and Batavia, the thermometer very rarely falls below 70 deg. F. or rises above 90 deg. F. The range at Singapore is less than 5 deg. F., similar to the range at Galle in Ceylon and Batavia in Java.
*   **Extreme Variations:** The stability of Calcutta's temperature contrasts sharply with Siberia, where variations between the mean of the hottest and coldest months can exceed 100 deg. F. near the coast and are hardly anywhere less than 60 deg. F.

## Proximity to Darjeeling
Located 367 m. by rail north of Calcutta is Darjeeling, a district and hill station in the Bhagalpur division of Bengal. Darjeeling serves as the summer quarters for the Bengal government and offers a most agreeable climate. Unlike the conditions in Calcutta, temperatures in Darjeeling do not exceed 80 deg. F. in the summer nor fall below 30 deg. F. in the winter.
