# BROEKHUIZEN, JAN VAN [JANUS BROUKHUSIUS]

Jan van Broekhuizen, also known as Janus Broukhusius (1649–1707), was a Dutch poet and classical scholar. Born in Amsterdam on November 20, 1649, his early life was marked by the loss of his father at a very young age. During his youth, he spent several years living and working with an apothecary, though he found he did not like this employment.

## Military Career
Seeking a different path, Broekhuizen entered the army. In 1674, he was sent to America as part of a regiment in the fleet commanded by Admiral de Ruyter, though he returned to Holland within the same year. By 1678, he had been stationed at the garrison in Utrecht. It was during his time there that he formed a friendship with the celebrated Graevius.

While at Utrecht, Broekhuizen became deeply involved in a duel. Under the laws of Holland, this action resulted in the forfeiture of his life. However, he was granted a pardon after Graevius wrote to Nicholas Heinsius on his behalf. Following these events, Broekhuizen served as a captain of one of the companies based in Amsterdam. His military service concluded after the peace of Ryswick in 1697, when his company was disbanded.

## Literary and Classical Studies
Upon his retirement from military life, Broekhuizen moved to a country house near Amsterdam, supported by a pension. This period of leisure allowed him to dedicate himself to literary and classical studies. 

His contributions to Dutch poetry were first published in 1677. In these works, he utilized the model of Pieter Hooft. Later editions of his Dutch poems appeared in 1712, featuring a biography by D. van Hoogstraten, and in 1883, edited by R.A. Kollewijn.

Broekhuizen also produced Latin poetry, titled *Carmina*, which was published in 1684. A subsequent edition, titled *Poemata*, was published in 1711 by D. van Hoogstraten. Additionally, his *Select Letters* (*Jani Browkhusii Epistolae Selectae*) were edited by J.A. Worp in 1889 and 1893. Worp also authored a biography of Broekhuizen in 1891.

## Classical Scholarship
Broekhuizen's reputation as a classical scholar is primarily based on his scholarly editions of two Latin poets:
* Propertius (published in 1702)
* Tibullus (published in 1707)

Jan van Broekhuizen died on December 15, 1707.
