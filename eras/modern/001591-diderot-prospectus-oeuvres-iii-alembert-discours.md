# Diderot's Prospectus and d'Alembert's Discours

Diderot's *Prospectus* (found in *Oeuvres*, iii.) and d'Alembert's *Discours* (found in *Oeuvres*, i.) are foundational texts associated with the great French *Encyclopedie*. These works outline the intellectual framework and organizational structure of the massive undertaking.

## Classification of the Sciences
The division of the sciences utilized within the French *Encyclopedie* was not an arbitrary arrangement but was founded upon the classification established by Bacon. This systemic approach to organizing knowledge served as the basis for the *Encyclopedie*'s structure. In the history of intellectual organization, this scheme is noted as a precursor to later attempts at similar classifications by figures such as Comte, Cournot, Ampere, and Herbert Spencer.

## Scope and Content of the Encyclopédie
The *Encyclopedie*, edited by Diderot, was characterized by its comprehensive approach to both theoretical knowledge and practical arts. A notable example of this is found in the 1772 edition, which contains elaborate descriptions of the art of coach-building. These entries provided detailed information on the tools and workshops used in the craft, accompanied by plates illustrating the various types of carriages in use during the period.

## Intellectual Context
The authors and editors of the *Encyclopedie* were part of a broader circle of French men of letters. Diderot and d'Alembert maintained close associations with other prominent intellectuals of the era, including D'Holbach and Baron von Grimm. This network extended to figures such as Louise Florence Petronille Tardieu d'Esclavelles d'Epinay, who was an acquaintance of both Diderot and d'Alembert. 

The intellectual output of this group was often documented in the *Correspondance litteraire*, a collection involving the writings of Grimm, d'Alembert, Diderot, and others. Additionally, the literary environment of the time was influenced by the *salons* of Paris, such as that of Madame Doublet de Persan, where the criticism of literature and art frequently occurred.
