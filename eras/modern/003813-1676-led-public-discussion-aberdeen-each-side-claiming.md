# Robert Barclay

## Early Life and Education
Robert Barclay (1648–1690) was a prominent writer associated with the Society of Friends, also known as Quakers. He was born in 1648 in Gordonstown, Morayshire. His father had a complex history, having served under Gustavus Adolphus and navigated a "tortuous course" during the troubles of the civil war. Barclay was sent to Paris to complete his education, where he was at one time inclined toward the Roman Catholic faith.

## Conversion and Early Quakerism
In 1667, Barclay followed his father's example and joined the recently established Society of Friends. He married Christian Mollison of Aberdeen, a Quaker lady, in 1670. Described as a man of considerable mental powers and warm feelings, Barclay became an ardent student of theology. He quickly rose to prominence as the leading apologist for the new doctrine, establishing his reputation through a controversy with William Mitchell.

## Theological Works and the Aberdeen Discussion
In 1676, Barclay published fifteen *Theses Theologiae*. This publication sparked a public discussion in Aberdeen, with both sides claiming victory. The most significant of these theses concerned immediate revelation, in which Barclay sharply stated that the "inner light" was superior to both reason and scripture.

Following the *Theses*, Barclay published his greatest work, *An Apology for the True Christian Divinity*, in Latin at Amsterdam in 1676. This work served as an elaborate statement of the grounds for the fundamental positions he had established in his *Theses*. Barclay later translated the *Apology* into English in 1678. The text is regarded as one of the most impressive theological writings of the century and remains perhaps the most important manifesto of the Quaker Society, characterized by a spirit of large tolerance.

## Public Life and Political Influence
Barclay's commitment to the Society of Friends led to his experience with the persecutions faced by the group, resulting in him being imprisoned several times. He traveled extensively throughout Europe, accompanying George Fox and Penn on one occasion, and held several interviews with the princess palatine, Elizabeth.

In his later years, Barclay exerted significant influence over James II. While James was still the duke of York, he granted a patent for the province of East New Jersey to twelve members of the Society of Friends, appointing Barclay as governor from 1682 to 1688. As the arrival of William of Orange became imminent, Barclay is said to have visited James II in an attempt to establish terms of accommodation.

Robert Barclay died on October 3.
