# Clotilde de Lusignan, ou Le Beau Juif; Le Centenaire, ou Les Deux

## Overview of Early Works
*Clotilde de Lusignan, ou Le Beau Juif* and *Le Centenaire, ou Les Deux Beringheld* are novels written by Balzac during his early creative period. These works are categorized as *incunabula* and are included in the collection titled *Oeuvres de jeunesse*. 

These specific titles were part of a group of six novels published in 1822. This group also included *L'Heritiere de Birague*, *Jean Louis, ou La Fille trouvee*, *Le Vicaire des Ardennes*, and *Le Tartare, ou Le Retour de l'exile*. These followed an initial set of productions from 1821, which consisted of *Les Deux Hector, ou Les Deux Families bretonnes* and *Charles Pointel, ou Mon Cousin de la main gauche*.

## Publication and Authorship
The early novels of Balzac, which originally filled approximately two score volumes, were not always published under his own name. Instead, they appeared under various pseudonyms, including "Horace de Saint Aubin" and "Lord R'hoone" (the latter being an anagram of "Honore"). Additionally, some of these works were produced in actual collaboration with two or three other writers.

## Literary Quality and Significance
From a critical perspective, these early works are described as containing only the "faintest dawn" of the true Balzac. It is noted that none of these novels are good as a whole, and very few individual parts are considered good even with significant qualification. Despite these shortcomings, they are regarded as deserving of more study than they typically receive, as it is difficult to fully apprehend the true Balzac without having studied these early efforts.

## Cessation of Early Writing
The production of these types of novels ceased for a period. This pause was not caused by the author's conviction that the works were bad—although he did not hold any serious delusions regarding their quality—nor was it due to a lack of financial success in terms of money return. Instead, the cessation occurred because Balzac entered into business ventures. These endeavors, which proved to be the most prolonged and disastrous of his "dabblings," involved working as a printer, a type-founder, and to some extent, a publisher. These business experiences resulted in a general failure and burdened him with a load of debt.
