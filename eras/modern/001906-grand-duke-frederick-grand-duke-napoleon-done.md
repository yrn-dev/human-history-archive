# Grand-Duke Frederick of Baden

Grand-Duke Frederick of Baden was a sovereign prince known for his statesmanlike moderation and his role in the integration of Baden into the German Empire. His reign was marked by a preference for German nobility and unity over particularist ambitions, a stance that earned him universal esteem.

## Origins of the Grand-Duchy
The state of Baden was created in 1803 by Napoleon, who established it to reward a relative of the emperor of Russia, the "little margrave of Baden." Following the battle of Austerlitz, Napoleon further aggrandized the margravate at the expense of Austria, transforming it into a sovereign principality and subsequently raising it to a grand-duchy. Through the processes of secularization and the dismemberment of Wuerttemberg, Napoleon provided the grand-duke with 500,000 new subjects. While Napoleon believed this artificial ethnical formation would ensure security for France, the state eventually joined a coalition against France in 1813. The Parisian paper *Temps* later characterized the history of the grand-duchy as a "sterile paradox of the Napoleonic work," noting that Grand-Duke Frederick, as a ruler created by Napoleon's will, "has done France all the harm he could."

## Accession and Early Political Career
Frederick succeeded his father, Grand-Duke Leopold, as regent following Leopold's death on April 24, 1852. He assumed this role because the eldest son, Louis, was incapable of ruling. Between 1850 and 1866, Baden remained a consistent supporter of Austria in German politics. During the war of 1866, Badenese contingents fought the Prussian army of the Main in two sharp engagements. 

Although Frederick had been opposed to the war with Prussia from the start, he initially yielded due to popular resentment regarding Prussia's policy in the Schleswig-Holstein question. However, on July 22 (two days before the affair of Werbach), the second chamber petitioned the grand-duke to end the conflict and seek an alliance with Prussia. This led to the resignation of the ministry, Baden's withdrawal from the German confederation, and the signing of a treaty of peace and alliance with Prussia on August 17.

## Integration into the German Empire
Frederick played a pivotal role in the unification of Germany. While Bismarck initially prevented Baden from joining the North German confederation to avoid giving Napoleon III an excuse to intervene, Baden's subsequent opposition to a South German confederation made the ultimate union inevitable. During the war of 1870, Badenese troops participated conspicuously. In the historic assembly of German princes at Versailles, Grand-Duke Frederick was the first to hail the king of Prussia as the German emperor.

Under the treaty that made Baden an integral part of the German empire, Frederick reserved only the exclusive right to tax spirits and beer. He placed the conduct of foreign relations, the post-office, the army, and the railways under the effective control of Prussia.

## Personal Life and Influence
Frederick's influence in the councils of Berlin was bolstered by his marriage in 1856 to Louise, the only daughter of Emperor William I. On September 20, 1906, Frederick celebrated both his golden wedding anniversary and the jubilee of his reign. This occasion was honored across Europe; King Edward VII sent the order of the Garter via the duke of Connaught to mark the event.
