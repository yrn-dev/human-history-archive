# ASHTABULA

Ashtabula is a city located in Ashtabula township, Ashtabula county, Ohio, U.S.A. It is situated on the Ashtabula river and Lake Erie, positioned 54 miles northeast of Cleveland. The city is built on the high bank of the river, approximately 75 feet above the lake, providing views of diversified scenery.

## History and Governance
The first settlement in the area was established around 1801. Ashtabula township was created in 1808, and the townships of Sheffield, Plymouth, and Kingsville were subsequently formed from it. The village of Ashtabula was incorporated in 1831 and later received a city charter in 1891. The name "Ashtabula" is derived from an Indian word originally applied to the river, meaning "fish river."

## Population and Culture
The population of the city has shown steady growth over the turn of the century, recorded at 8,338 in 1890, 12,949 in 1900, and 18,266 according to the 1910 census. In 1900, 3,688 residents were foreign-born. The city and the wider Ashtabula county contain a large population of Finnish birth. This community is served by the *Amerikan Sanomat*, a Finnish weekly established in 1897 that is among the most widely read of its kind in the country. The city also maintains a public library.

## Transportation and Infrastructure
Ashtabula is served by several railway lines, including the New York, Chicago & St Louis, the Lake Shore & Michigan Southern, and the Pennsylvania railways. Additionally, the city is connected by inter-urban electric lines.

## Economy and Industry
The city possesses an excellent harbour used for the shipment of coal and iron ore. Ashtabula is a primary hub for iron ore, receiving more annually than any other port in the country, and potentially the world. This ore is transported by rail to iron manufacturing centres such as Youngstown and Pittsburg. In 1907, the port handled 7,542,149 gross tons of iron ore and shipped 2,632,027 net tons of soft coal.

Manufacturing in the city includes the production of worsted goods, leather, foundry and machine shop products, and agricultural implements. The total value of factory products in 1905 was $1,895,454, which represented a 114.3% increase over the previous five years.

The region is also known for its large greenhouses. Under glass, quantities of tomatoes, cucumbers, and lettuce are raised and shipped to large cities, including Pittsburg.
