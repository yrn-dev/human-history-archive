# Place des Quinconces

The Place des Quinconces is a central location in Bordeaux, a city in south-western France. Situated on the left bank of the Garonne river, the square is positioned almost at the center of the city's line of quays.

## Urban Layout and Surroundings
The Place des Quinconces serves as a focal point for the city's urban structure. Surrounding the square are narrow, winding streets where the life of the city is concentrated. This specific quarter contains most of the city's important buildings. In contrast, the streets located outside of this quarter are quiet and narrow, characterized by low white houses rather than the high tenements typically found in other large French towns.

The square is part of a cluster of principal promenades located near the city center, which also include the allees de Tourny and a beautiful public garden. To the south-west, the Place des Quinconces opens into the Place de la Comedie, which houses the 18th-century Grand Theatre.

## Features and Monuments
The Place des Quinconces is planted with plane trees. Within the square stand two large statues depicting Montesquieu and Montaigne. The square terminates at the quays, where it is marked by two rostral columns that function as lighthouses. Additionally, the west side of the square features a monument dedicated to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed under the convention in 1793.

## Regional Context
Bordeaux is the capital of the department of Gironde and is located in a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district. The city is situated approximately 60 miles from the sea, where the Garonne river forms a semicircle. This river separates the city proper on the left bank from the suburb of La Bastide on the right bank. The two banks are connected by a railway bridge and the Pont de Bordeaux, a stone structure from the early 19th century measuring 1534 feet in length.

The wider city is encircled by a semicircle of boulevards. Beyond these boulevards lie several suburbs, including Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat.
