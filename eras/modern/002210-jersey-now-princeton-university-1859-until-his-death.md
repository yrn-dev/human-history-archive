# Princeton University

Princeton University, located in New Jersey, was formerly known as the College of New Jersey. The institution has a long history of academic leadership in philosophy, theology, and the humanities, and has been associated with various prominent American and international scholars.

## Institutional Leadership and Administration

The university has been led by several notable presidents. Jonathan Dickinson served as the first president of the College of New Jersey. He was followed later by the Rev. Aaron Burr, who served as the second president from 1748 to 1757. In the late 19th century, James McCosh, a Scottish philosophical writer and former professor at Queen's College, Belfast, was chosen as president and professor of philosophy in 1868. McCosh was noted for his effectiveness in college administration and his Hamiltonian philosophical method, which separated religious and philosophical data from physical data. He resigned the presidency in 1888, though he continued to lecture on philosophy until 1894.

In a later period, the university was presided over by Woodrow Wilson. Before becoming the Democratic Governor of New Jersey in 1910 and subsequently the President of the United States in 1913, Wilson was a teacher and student of political sciences, constitutional law, and history.

## Academic Faculty and Scholarship

The university and its affiliated Theological Seminary have hosted numerous distinguished scholars. William Henry Green, a Hebrew scholar born in New Jersey, served as an instructor in Hebrew at the Princeton Theological Seminary from 1846 to 1849. From August 1851 until his death in 1900, he was a professor of Biblical and Oriental Literature (a chair renamed "Oriental and Old Testament Literature" in 1859). Green was a leader of the orthodox wing of the Presbyterian church in America and authored a *Grammar of the Hebrew Language* (1861, revised 1888). Despite being offered the presidency of Princeton College in 1868, he refused the position, though he served as the acting head of the Theological Seminary as its senior professor.

Other faculty members include James Mark Baldwin, who served as a professor of psychology at Princeton in 1893. Baldwin was a founder of the *Psychological Review* and a prominent experimental psychologist. Additionally, Richard Webster served as a Fellow in Classics at the university.

## Student Body and Alumni

The university has produced alumni who achieved prominence in law, politics, and social reform. Aaron Burr, the son of the university's second president, graduated in 1772 before pursuing law and later serving in Washington's army during the War of Independence. James Gillespie Birney, who graduated in 1810, became a lawyer and a leader of the conservative abolitionists in the United States between 1835 and 1845.

The institution also provided early academic training for educators such as Henry Martyn Baird. After graduating from the Princeton Theological Seminary in 1856, Baird served as a tutor at the College of New Jersey for four years before becoming a professor of Greek language and literature at New York University.

## Athletics and Extracurriculars

Princeton was an early participant in organized amateur base-ball. In 1879, it became a member of the first college league, alongside Brown, Amherst, Dartmouth, and Harvard. A year later, Yale joined this league. In 1887, Princeton joined the Eastern College League, which also included Yale, Harvard, and Columbia. This league was eventually dissolved, and the university later operated independently of such leagues.
