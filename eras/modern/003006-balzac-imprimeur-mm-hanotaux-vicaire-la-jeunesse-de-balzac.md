# Balzac imprimeur

The phrase *Balzac imprimeur* refers to a specific area of study regarding the early professional life of the French novelist Honoré de Balzac, specifically his ventures into the printing and publishing industries. This subject is notably addressed in the first part of the work *La Jeunesse de Balzac* (1903), authored by MM. Hanotaux and Vicaire.

## The Printing Business and Personal Associations
According to the research presented by Hanotaux and Vicaire, the period of Balzac's life designated as *Balzac imprimeur* focuses on his involvement in the printing business. In addition to detailing these commercial activities, this portion of their study examines Balzac's intimacy with Madame de Berny.

## Context of Business Ventures
Balzac's foray into the printing trade occurred after a period of early literary experimentation. Between 1820 and 1822, he lived in a garret, and from 1821 to 1823, he produced a series of early novels known as the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*. These works—which included titles such as *Les Deux Hector*, *Charles Pointel*, and *Le Vicaire des Ardennes*—were often published under pseudonyms like "Lord R'hoone" or "Horace de Saint Aubin."

Balzac eventually ceased the production of these early novels not because of a lack of financial return or a personal conviction of their poor quality, but because he became preoccupied with business. This transition marked the beginning of what is described as the most prolonged and disastrous of his "dabblings in business." During this time, he acted as a printer and type-founder, and to some extent, as a publisher.

## Outcomes of the Venture
The archive's sources indicate that Balzac's experiences in the printing and publishing sectors ended in general failure. The primary consequence of these business attempts was that they hampered the novelist by burdening him with a load of debt.
