# London

London is a center of publication and scholarly production, serving as the site of origin for a wide array of historical, linguistic, artistic, and biographical works. The city's publishing history is evidenced by a diverse range of texts spanning several centuries, from early academic histories to modern critical analyses of art and religion.

## Academic and Historical Publications
London has been a significant location for the publication of academic and institutional histories. In the 16th century, the city saw the release of several works related to the University of Cambridge, including the *History of the University of Cambridge* (published in 8vo in 1568 and in 4to in Latin in 1574). Other early London publications from this period include *De Mendeti Methodo* (1556), *De Libris propriis* (1570), *Of some Rare Plants and Animals* (1570), and *De Pronunciatione Graecae et Latinae Linguae* (1574). Additionally, the *Account of the Sweating Sickness in England* (also titled *De Ephemera Britannica*) was printed in London in 1556 and later in 1721.

In more recent history, London has hosted the publication of extensive works on the papacy. Notable examples include M. Creighton's *History of the Papacy*, vol. 1 (1899), and the translation of L. Pastor's *History of the Popes*, vol. i. (1899), translated by F. I. Antrobus.

## Art and Cultural Studies
The city is a hub for the study of art and the publication of critical accounts of artists. Works concerning the painter Camille Corot have been published in London, including *Corot* by David Croal Thomson (1892), *Ingres and Corot in Modern Painting* by Geo. Moore (1893), and *Corot* by Ethel Birnstigl and Alice Pollard (1904). 

London also serves as a point of translation for broader art histories, such as the London translation of R. Muther's *The History of Modern Painting* (1896). In the realm of biography and gender studies, Austin Dobson's *Four Frenchwomen* was published in London in 1890.

## Linguistic and Specialized Research
London has been involved in the publication of specialized linguistic research, particularly concerning the Basque language. W. J. Van Eys published the *Grammaire comparee des dialectes basques* in 1879, a work released in London, Paris, and Amsterdam. Other Basque-related publications produced in London include Prince L. L. Bonaparte's *Le Verbe basque en tableaux* (1864-1869) and Wentworth Webster's *Basque Legends* (1877 and 1879).

Furthermore, the city has contributed to the documentation of musical instruments. Captain C.R. Day's *Descriptive Catalogue of Musical Instruments* was published in London in 1891.

## Book-Plate Studies
London is a center for the study of book-plates (ex-libris). The city is the location of the *Ex-libris Series*, which treats the composition of book-plates. Various authorities on the subject have published their findings in London, including:
*   Hon. J. Leicester Warren (Lord de Tabley), *A Guide to the Study of Book-plates* (1880)
*   Egerton Castle, *English Book-plates* (1892)
*   H.W. Fincham, *Artists and Engravers of British and American Book-plates* (1897)
