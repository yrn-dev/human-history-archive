# Charles Powlett, 3rd Duke of Bolton

Charles Powlett, the 3rd Duke of Bolton (1685–1754), was a British nobleman, soldier, and public official. He was the eldest son of Charles, the 2nd Duke of Bolton (1661–1722), who had served as a supporter of William of Orange, lord-lieutenant of Hampshire and Dorset, governor of the Isle of Wight, lord chamberlain of the royal household, and twice as a lord justice of the kingdom.

## Political and Public Career
Charles Powlett entered politics as a member of parliament, serving from 1705 until 1717. His tenure in the House of Commons ended in 1717 when he was granted a peerage as Baron Pawlet of Basing. Following this elevation, he occupied many of the same public offices that had previously been held by his father.

His career was marked by fluctuations in political favor. In 1733, after displeasing Sir Robert Walpole, he was stripped of several of his offices. However, some of these positions were later restored to him.

## Military Service
In addition to his administrative and political roles, the 3rd Duke attained a high rank within the British army. His military contributions included raising a regiment in 1745 for service against the Jacobites.

## Personal Life
The 3rd Duke was known as a famous gallant. He married Lavinia Fenton, a singer who had previously been his mistress, as his second wife. Lavinia Fenton died in 1760.

Charles Powlett died in August 1754. He was succeeded as the 4th Duke of Bolton by his brother, Harry (c. 1690–1759). Harry had been a member of parliament for forty years and also served as the lord-lieutenant of Hampshire.

## The Dukedom of Bolton
The title of Duke of Bolton was held by the family of Powlett (or Paulet) between 1689 and 1794. The lineage began with Charles Powlett, the 1st Duke (c. 1625–1699), who had been the 6th Marquess of Winchester. The 1st Duke served as a member of parliament for Winchester and Hampshire from 1660 to 1675. After supporting the claim of William and Mary to the English throne in 1688, he was restored to the office of lord-lieutenant of Hampshire and the privy council, and was created Duke of Bolton in April 1689. He was described as an eccentric man who was hostile to Marlborough and Halifax; it is noted that in 1687 he traveled with 100 horsemen and four coaches, spending his days sleeping and his nights providing entertainments.
