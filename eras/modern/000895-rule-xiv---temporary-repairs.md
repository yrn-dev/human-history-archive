# RULE XIV.--TEMPORARY REPAIRS

In the context of maritime law and the adjustment of General Average (G.A.), Rule XIV specifically addresses the financial treatment of temporary repairs. According to this rule, no deductions based on the principle of "new for old" are to be made from the cost of temporary repairs of damage that is allowable as General Average.

## General Average and the York-Antwerp Rules
The York-Antwerp Rules were established to introduce uniformity and certainty into the principles used to adjust G.A. losses, replacing previous customary rules that varied by location. These rules provide a definite scale for deductions from repair costs regarding "new for old," adapting these scales to modern shipbuilding conditions.

While Rule XIV prohibits "new for old" deductions for temporary repairs, Rule XIII governs the deductions for other repairs. For iron or steel ships, these deductions are based on the time elapsed from the date of the original register to the date of the accident. For ships up to one year old, all repairs are allowed in full, with the exception of the coating or painting of the bottom, from which one-third is deducted.

## Port of Refuge and Necessary Repairs
Under Rule X, a broad view is adopted regarding ports of refuge. If it is necessary for common safety to enter such a port, the expenses associated with entering and subsequently exiting (provided the ship sails again with some or all of its original cargo) are allowed as G.A. 

Rule X (b) further specifies that the cost of discharging cargo is admissible as G.A. if it is necessary to enable repairs to the ship to be conducted, regardless of whether the damage was caused by accident during the voyage or by sacrifice. This applies to repairs conducted at a port of call as well as a port of refuge, provided the repairs are necessary for the safe prosecution of the voyage.

## Associated Costs and Cargo Handling
When the cost of discharging cargo is admitted as G.A., subsequent related expenses are also covered under Rule X (c). These include:
* The cost of storing the cargo on shore.
* The cost of reloading and stowing the cargo back on board after repairs are complete.
* Any damage or loss incidental to these operations, as specified in Rule XII.

However, if the ship is condemned or does not proceed on its original voyage, storage expenses incurred after the date of abandonment or condemnation are not admitted as G.A.

## Crew Wages and Maintenance
Rule XI provides that the wages of the master, officers, and crew, along with their maintenance costs, are allowed as G.A. during the period of detention in a port or place for the purposes of repairs mentioned in Rule X. This allowance continues until the ship is, or should have been, ready to proceed on its voyage.

This rule represents a significant concession to shipowners, as common law and English law often do not allow for the wages and maintenance of a crew at a port of refuge, particularly when the detention is for the repair of accidental damage. It is noted, however, that this protection does not extend to shippers. For example, in the case of *Anglo-Argentine &c. Agency v. Temperley Shipping Co.* (1899), it was determined that a shipper of cattle could not claim G.A. for extra fodder consumed by the cattle or the extra wages and provisions of cattlemen during a stay at a repairing port.

## Contributory Values and Loss
Rule XVII dictates that contributions to a G.A. are based on the actual values of the property at the termination of the adventure, plus the amount made good as G.A. for sacrificed property. Certain deductions are made from the shipowner's passage-money and freight at risk, specifically port charges and crew wages that would not have been incurred had the ship and cargo been totally lost at the date of the sacrifice or G.A. act. 

Regarding cargo, Rule XVI states that the amount made good for goods sacrificed is based on the loss sustained by the owner, calculated using market values at the termination of the adventure or the date of the vessel's arrival. Additionally, Rule XV stipulates that loss of freight resulting from the loss of or damage to cargo is made good as G.A. when caused by a G.A. act.
