# Ashburton

## Overview and Geography
Ashburton is a market town located in the Ashburton parliamentary division of Devonshire, England. It is situated 24 miles north-west by west of Plymouth. The town lies within a valley surrounded by hills and is positioned a short distance from the river Dart. The surrounding scenery, particularly in the vicinity of Holne Chase and Buckland, as well as the views toward Dartmoor, is described as unsurpassed within the county.

## Infrastructure and Economy
The town is served by a branch of the Great Western railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 2,628. 

Ashburton is a center for the manufacture of serge. Other local industries include saw-mills, breweries, and paint factories. The area around the town is known for a large deposit of umber that is worked in the neighborhood. Historically, copper and tin mines, as well as slate quarries, were of significant value to the region.

## Architecture and Education
The town's church, St. Andrew, is a cruciform structure featuring a lofty tower. Built early in the 15th century, the church contains a fine old oak roof over its north aisle. It also houses a tablet dedicated to the memory of the 1st Baron Ashburton, John Dunning (1731-1783), who served as solicitor-general; the inscription on this tablet was written by Dr. Johnson.

Ashburton is also home to a grammar school. This institution was founded in 1314 as a chantry and served as the place of education for Lord Ashburton.

## Surrounding Area
The urban district of Buckfastleigh is a neighboring center for the serge industry, located 3 miles south-south-west of Ashburton; its population in 1901 was 2,520. 

Situated between Ashburton and Buckfastleigh is Buckfast Abbey. It is said to have been a Benedictine house before the Conquest and was refounded for Cistercians in 1137. In 1882, a French Benedictine community restored the abbey to use. While various parts of the abbey have been rebuilt according to original lines, the fine Perpendicular abbot's tower remains.
