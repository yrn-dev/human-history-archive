# Inambari

The Inambari is a large river located in South America. It is identified as one of the principal tributaries of the Madre de Dios river.

## Geographic Context and Hydrography
The Inambari flows into the Madre de Dios, which is itself a tributary of the Beni river. The Beni river rises in the elevated Cordilleras near the city of La Paz, where it is initially known as the Rio de La Paz. The Beni flows in an east and north-east direction, eventually forming a junction with the Mamore at 10 deg. 20' S. latitude to create the Madeira river.

The Madre de Dios, into which the Inambari flows, is described as a more important river than the Beni in terms of its size and the length of its tributaries. The Madre de Dios rises in the eastern Cordilleras, approximately 35 miles east of Cuzco, and flows through northern Bolivia. In addition to the Inambari, the principal tributaries of the Madre de Dios include the Paucartambo, as well as the Tambopata, Marcapata, and Chandless rivers. During the wet season, the Madre de Dios is navigable to the foot of the Andes, a distance of 180 miles from Cuzco.

## Regional Characteristics
The river system associated with the Inambari is part of the broader geography of northern Bolivia. The lower course of the Beni river, which eventually receives the waters of the Madre de Dios and Inambari, passes through open plains and forest-covered hills. This region was historically the site of several old Indian missions.
