# The Works and Influence of Cibber

The name Cibber is associated with several significant contributions to 18th-century English literature, biography, and the theatrical arts. These contributions range from the documentation of the lives of poets and actors to the management of major London theaters.

## Biographical Works
Theophilus Cibber (1703–1758) is credited with the *Lives of the Poets*, published in 1753. While the work bore his name, it was primarily written by Robert Shiels, who died in 1753. This text is noted for providing a substantial amount of valuable information regarding the personal adventures of writers. Specifically, the *Lives of the Poets* serves as a source for accounts of figures such as the writer Dennis (found in volume iv) and the translator Budgell (found in volume v). Budgell, who translated Theophrastus's *Characters* in 1714, left a natural daughter named Anne Eustace who became an actress at Drury Lane.

In addition to the *Lives of the Poets*, the archive references a work titled *Lives and Characters of the most eminent Actors and Actresses*, published in 1753.

## Theatrical Management and Actors
Cibber was involved in the management of prominent London theaters, specifically the Haymarket and Drury Lane. He was associated in this management with others, including the English actor Thomas Doggett (d. 1721). Doggett, a Dublin-born actor who first appeared in London in 1691, was highly regarded by his contemporaries and played significant roles in works such as Congreve's *Love for Love*.

The influence of Cibber also extends to the historical record of early English actresses. According to Cibber, the establishment of "benefits" for actors—which had previously been reserved exclusively for authors—was first instituted by the command of James II on behalf of Elizabeth Barry (1658–1713). Mrs. Barry was a celebrated actress who created over 100 parts and was praised by Dryden as "always excellent."

## Later Theatrical Associations
The legacy of the Cibber name continued into the mid-to-late 18th century through associations with other leading performers. Spranger Barry (1719–1777), a British actor born in Dublin, moved from Drury Lane to Covent Garden in 1750. He was accompanied in this move by Mrs. Cibber, who performed the role of Juliet opposite his Romeo. This transition led to a period of rival performances of *Romeo and Juliet* between the two theaters, with critics preferring Barry's impersonation over that of Garrick.

## Context of 18th-Century Biography
The works of Cibber and his contemporaries emerged during a period when English biography was often viewed as a highly artificial and rhetorical endeavor. Critics like William Oldys (1696–1761) attacked the superficiality of biographers who omitted defining characteristics of their subjects. While the *Lives of the Poets* provided valuable personal details, the art of biography saw a major advancement later with Mason's *Life and Letters of Gray* (1774), which pioneered the use of familiar correspondence to illustrate a writer's real character—a method later improved upon by Boswell in his 1791 *Life of Dr Samuel Johnson*.
