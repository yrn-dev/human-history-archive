# The Bonapartes and Julie Clary

## Joseph Bonaparte and Julie Clary
Joseph Bonaparte (1768–1844) was born on January 7, 1768, in Corte, Corsica. After receiving an education at the college at Autun in France, he returned to his homeland in 1784 following the death of his father. He subsequently studied law at the university of Pisa and became a barrister at Bastia in June 1788. He was also elected as a councillor of the municipality of Ajaccio.

Along with his brothers, Lucien and Napoleon, Joseph embraced the democratic or French side of the political divide. Following the victory of the Paolist party, Joseph and his family fled Corsica to seek refuge in France. After a brief period in Paris, where he found the excesses of the Jacobins disgusting, he settled in Marseilles. It was there that he married Mlle Julie Clary, who was the daughter of a merchant from that town.

## Political and Diplomatic Career of Joseph
Following his marriage, the Bonaparte family moved frequently, primarily to coordinate efforts to recover Corsica. Joseph participated in these activities, including a mission to Genoa in 1795. In 1796, he accompanied Napoleon during the early stages of the Italian campaign and was involved in negotiations with Sardinia that resulted in the armistice of Cherasco on April 28.

Joseph later traveled to Leghorn and assisted Miot de Melito, a commissioner of the French Republic, in the reorganization of Corsica during a French expedition for the island's recovery. In March 1797, the Directory appointed Joseph as minister to the court of Parma, and he later served in the same capacity in Rome. During his time in Rome, Joseph was ordered by the French Directory and Napoleon to encourage revolutionary movements amidst discords between the French Republic and the Vatican. After a disturbance on December 28, 1797, which resulted in the death of General Leonard Duphot, Joseph left Rome. He subsequently entered parliamentary life in Paris as a member for Corsica in the Council of Five Hundred, though he retired in 1799 after making no mark in the chamber.

## Lucien Bonaparte and the Prince of Canino
Lucien Bonaparte (1775–1840), the prince of Canino, was born in Ajaccio. Initially destined for the church due to defective sight that barred him from the army, he attended the seminary at Aix in Provence in 1886. However, he soon embraced anti-clerical and democratic movements during the Revolution of 1789. In Corsica, he became a leading speaker for the Jacobin club in Ajaccio and urged his brothers to break with the conservative leader Paoli.

After the Paolists gained power, the Bonapartes joined Lucien at Toulon. On May 4, 1794, Lucien married Mlle Catherine Boyer without family consent, which led to a period of penury and disgrace. Following the coup d'etat of Thermidor, he was imprisoned for a time before Napoleon's influence secured his release and a post as a commissioner in the army in Germany. Lucien eventually returned to Corsica and later entered the Council of Five Hundred in Paris in 1798.

Lucien's son, Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803–1857), the prince of Canino, was a scientist. In 1822, he married his cousin Zenaide Bonaparte, the daughter of Joseph. Charles Lucien Jules Laurent published extensive works on zoology and ornithology, including *American Ornithology* and *Iconographia della fauna Italica*. He was a member of various academies and, in 1848, entered the Junto of Rome. After the failure of the revolution in 1849, he left Italy for Holland and France. He was survived by eight children, including a son who became a cardinal in 1868 and several daughters who married counts and a marquis.

## The Bonapartes of Baltimore
A branch of the family settled in America through Jerome Bonaparte and his union with Elizabeth Patterson, the daughter of Baltimore merchant William Patterson. They married in Baltimore on December 24, 1803. This marriage was not recognized as legal by Napoleon, though it was valid under American law and was not declared void by Pope Pius VII. Jerome was eventually forced by his brother to separate from Elizabeth.

The couple had one child, Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1805–1870), who was born in England but lived mostly in Baltimore. He had a son, also named Jerome Napoleon Bonaparte (1832–1893), who served in the French army in Italy and the Crimea. Another grandson of Jerome, Charles Joseph Bonaparte (born 1851), became a prominent American politician. Educated at Harvard, he served as the attorney-general of the United States until March 1909 and was secretary of the navy in President Roosevelt's cabinet from July 1905 to December 1906.
