# Czech Political and National Struggles in Austria

The Czech population, comprising over three-fifths of the population in Bohemia—the wealthiest part of the Austrian dominions—engaged in a prolonged struggle for national recognition, linguistic equality, and political autonomy. This conflict was primarily fought against German influence in Bohemia and Moravia, where the two racial groups were nearly equal in number.

## National Aspirations and Political Goals
Czech national feeling was rooted in an appeal to historic law. A significant political party, led by Rieger and Palacky, sought the restoration of the Bohemian monarchy in its fullest extent, which included Silesia and Moravia. Their vision included the crowning of the emperor as king of Bohemia in Prague, following the tradition of his predecessors, and securing a position within the monarchy similar to that of Hungary. 

The Czechs maintained a policy based on the indissoluble unity of the Bohemian kingdom, arguing that German and Czech should be recognized as equal and parallel languages throughout the territory. This stance put them in direct opposition to German residents of Bohemia, who feared Czech control and became ardent advocates of centralization. While the Czechs were supported by some nobles and fellow-countrymen in Moravia, they faced opposition in Bruenn and Silesia, where Germans held a considerable majority.

## Internal Political Divisions
The Czech political landscape was marked by internal conflict, particularly between the Old Czechs and the Young Czechs. The Old Czechs, led by Rieger, maintained an alliance with the clergy and aristocracy and pursued a policy of passive opposition. In contrast, the Young Czechs (also known as Radicals) rejected the alliance with the aristocracy and clergy, recalled Hussite traditions, and advocated for universal suffrage. The Young Czechs sought to take their seats in the diet to pursue political reform, leading to violent attacks on Rieger's leadership and a period where the two Czech factions were as focused on opposing each other as they were on opposing the Germans.

## The Language Conflict and Ordinances
A central point of contention was the use of the Czech language in official capacities. The government took several steps to increase the position of Czech:
* **1880:** An ordinance determined that business brought before government offices or law courts in Bohemia and Moravia should be dealt with in the language in which it was introduced.
* **1882:** Czech classes were introduced at the University of Prague, an act viewed by Germans as a desecration of the oldest German university.
* **1886:** To avoid frequent translations, business introduced in Czech was permitted in the high courts of Bruenn and Prague.

The conflict peaked with the "celebrated ordinances" published by Badeni in May 1897. These ordinances mandated that all correspondence and documents regarding matters brought before government officials in Bohemia be conducted in the language of their introduction. Furthermore, it was decreed that after 1903, no one could be appointed to a government post in Bohemia without passing an examination in Czech.

## German Reaction and Parliamentary Paralysis
The Badeni ordinances fulfilled the worst fears of the Germans, leading to widespread agitation and serious disturbances in Aussig and Eger. German Nationalists and Radicals resorted to obstruction in parliament, bringing repeated motions to impeach ministers, which eventually forced the proroguing of parliament in June 1897.

Later, under Dr. von Koerber, attempts to find a compromise in 1902 and 1903 failed. Koerber proposed dividing Bohemia into ten districts (five Czech, three German, and two mixed) and dividing 234 district tribunals similarly. The Czechs rejected this, demanding that both languages be placed on an equal footing throughout Bohemia for all official purposes. The Germans refused this as it would recognize Czech as a language of internal service.

Consequently, the Czechs blocked parliamentary business with "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction—including one sitting that lasted 57 hours without interruption. In response, various German parties (including the Christian Socialists, Populists, Progressists, and Constitutional Landed Proprietors) formed a joint executive committee to protect German racial interests.

## Impact on Governance
The feud between Germans and Czechs paralyzed parliamentary life in Austria at the close of the 19th century. Because the parliament was unable to vote on budgets (such as those for 1903, 1904, and 1905), the government was forced to rely on paragraph 14 of the constitution, an emergency clause that allowed estimates to be sanctioned by imperial ordinance. This shifted power away from the parliament, making ministers responsible to the emperor rather than the legislature.
