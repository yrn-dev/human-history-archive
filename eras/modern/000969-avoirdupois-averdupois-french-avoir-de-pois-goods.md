# AVOIRDUPOIS

Avoirdupois, also referred to as averdupois, is a system of weights utilized in America and Great Britain. The term is derived from the French phrase *avoir de pois*, which translates to "goods of weight." This system is applied to all commodities with the exception of medicines, gems, and precious metals.

## Foundation and Units of Measure
The foundation of the avoirdupois system is the grain. In this system, a cubic inch of water is measured at 252.458 grains. The pound avoirdupois is composed of 7,000 grains. 

The pound is further subdivided into 16 ounces, and each of these ounces is divided into 16 drachms. The progression of weights within the system is structured as follows:
* 1 drachm = 27.3 grains
* 1 ounce = 16 drachms (437.5 grains)
* 1 pound = 16 ounces (7,000 grains)
* 1 stone = 14 pounds (98,000 grains)
* 1 quarter = 2 stone (196,000 grains)
* 1 hundred = 4 quarters (112 lb)
* 1 ton = 20 hundred (2,240 lb)

## Application in Assaying
The avoirdupois system is used in specific customs regarding the assaying of ores containing silver or gold. These ores are typically assayed via the dry way, involving fusion with appropriate fluxes and the separation of elements in metallic form. 

Rather than using percentages of the ore's weight, the results of such assays are expressed as ounces to the ton. In this specific context, the ton is defined as 2,000 avoirdupois pounds, while the ounce used is the troy ounce. To facilitate chemical calculations using the metric system, the "assay ton" (A.T.) has been established at 29.166 grammes. The assay ton maintains the same relationship to the 2,000 lb. avoirdupois ton as one milligram does to the troy ounce; consequently, one milligram of silver or gold discovered in one assay ton of ore represents one ounce to the ton.
