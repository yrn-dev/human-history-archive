# Charles Powlett, 2nd Duke of Bolton

Charles Powlett, the 2nd Duke of Bolton (1661–1722), was an English nobleman and statesman who held several high-ranking administrative and royal offices during his career. He was the elder son of Charles Powlett, the 1st Duke of Bolton, who had previously served as the 6th Marquess of Winchester.

## Political Career and Public Office
The 2nd Duke of Bolton served as a member of parliament for Hampshire. Throughout his life, he was a supporter of William of Orange. His administrative contributions to the state included serving as a commissioner tasked with arranging the union between England and Scotland. Additionally, he was appointed twice as a lord justice of the kingdom.

## Regional and Royal Appointments
The Duke held significant authority over several regions and royal departments. He served as the lord-lieutenant of both Hampshire and Dorset. His roles within the royal administration included serving as the lord chamberlain of the royal household. Furthermore, he was appointed as the governor of the Isle of Wight and held the position of lord-lieutenant of Ireland for two short periods.

## Personal Life
The Duke's third wife was Henrietta, who died in 1730. She was a natural daughter of James, Duke of Monmouth. In contemporary accounts, the writer Swift characterized the Duke as "a great booby."

## Succession
Upon his death in 1722, he was succeeded by his eldest son, Charles, the 3rd Duke of Bolton (1685–1754). The 3rd Duke continued the family's tradition of public service, filling many of the same offices his father had held, while also attaining a high rank in the British army.
