# Lucas (Oxford, 1900) and the Annual Reports, Ashanti

## Overview of Bibliographic Sources
In the study of the region of Ashanti, several key texts are cited for providing general surveys and historical data. Among these is the work *Historical Geography of the British Colonies*, volume iii, "West Africa," authored by C.P. Lucas and published in Oxford in 1900. Additionally, the Colonial Office in London has issued *Annual Reports, Ashanti* from 1906 onward, which serve as primary administrative records for the territory.

## Administrative History of Ashanti
Ashanti was formally annexed to the British dominions via an order in council dated September 26, 1901. Under this arrangement, the territory was granted a separate administration, though it remained under the control of the governor of the Gold Coast. In the absence of the governor, a chief commissioner represents him, supported by a staff consisting of four assistant commissioners and four commissioners.

The order in council functioned as the first constitution granted to Ashanti by the British. It mandated that the governor, when issuing ordinances regarding the raising of revenue, the administration of justice, or other matters, must respect native laws regulating the civil relations of populations, tribes, or chiefs, provided those laws are not clearly injurious to the welfare of the natives or incompatible with British sovereignty.

## Governance and Social Relations
Following the 1901 annexation, relations between the governed and the governing power showed steady improvement. By 1906, F.C. Fuller, who had succeeded Sir Donald Stewart as chief commissioner in early 1905, reported that suspicion regarding the ulterior motives of the "white man" was quickly diminishing among the Ashanti. 

The British administration sought to win the confidence of the people—who were naturally suspicious and mindful of their exiled king—by supporting lawful chiefs and maintaining the tribal system. Furthermore, the natives demonstrated faith in the impartial treatment of the British-established criminal and civil courts by showing a marked preference for using them.

## Economic Activity and Infrastructure
The economy of Ashanti is characterized by various vegetable products and mineral resources. The rubber tree (*Funtumia elastica*) is the most commercially significant vegetable product. Other notable exports include gum copal and the nut of the kola tree, which is in great demand. Since 1905, cocoa plantations have been established, particularly in the eastern districts, while tobacco is grown in the northern regions.

Gold is found in the western districts, and between 1895 and 1901, several companies were formed to operate mines. The Obuassi mines provided most of the gold exported from the Gold Coast in 1902 and subsequent years. In 1905, the gold output from Ashanti was valued at £254,790, totaling 68,259 oz. 

Infrastructure development included the completion of a railway in 1903 running from Sekondi to Kumasi, which passes through the auriferous region. Kumasi serves as the distributing center for the hinterland and the whole of Ashanti.

## Trade and Revenue
Ashanti maintains trade with its neighbors in Germany and France, as well as with the Niger countries to the north. Trade moving southward through British territory is included in the figures for the Gold Coast. Previously, some trade had been diverted to the French port of Assini due to wars between England and the Ashanti; however, following the suppression of the 1900 revolt, measures were implemented to improve trade between Cape Coast and Kumasi.

The financial records of Ashanti, including expenditure and revenue, are included in those of the Gold Coast. Revenue is primarily generated from the following sources:
* Contributions from gold-mining companies
* Rents from government land
* Liquor licences
* Caravan taxes

## Military Presence
To maintain order and administration, a battalion of the Gold Coast regiment is stationed within the country, with its headquarters located at Kumasi.
