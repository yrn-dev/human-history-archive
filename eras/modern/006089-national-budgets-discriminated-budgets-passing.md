# National Budgets

National budgets are categorized into two primary types: those that are emitted on executive authority and those that pass through parliamentary debate and scrutiny from year to year. In most constitutional countries, the actual procedure exists as a middle ground between these two extremes.

## Comparative Parliamentary Procedures
The methods of budget scrutiny vary significantly across different constitutional systems:

*   **United Kingdom:** The executive places the budget before the whole House. Aside from the cabinet, no previous examination occurs. The House then scrutinizes the budget while sitting as a committee.
*   **United States:** The budget effectively emanates from Congress due to the lack of connection between the legislative and executive departments. The secretary of the treasury submits estimates prepared by executive departments to the House of Representatives. Two committees then handle these estimates: the committee on appropriations manages expenditure (utilizing various sub-committees for specific departments or subjects), and the committee on ways and means manages taxation.
*   **Other Constitutional Countries:** In the majority of countries, a specially selected committee performs a preliminary examination of the budget and possesses the authority to make drastic changes to the executive's proposals.

## Global Implementation and Financial Years
The practice of budgeting has been adopted by constitutional countries such as France and the United States, as well as autocratic powers. Russia began publishing annual budgets in 1866, followed by Turkey (via an imperial decree in 1875) and Egypt. While all countries utilize a yearly period, the start dates differ:
*   **April 1st:** United Kingdom, Germany, and Denmark.
*   **January 1st:** Austria, Belgium, and France.
*   **July 1st:** Canada, Italy, Spain, and the United States.

Historically, the English financial year ran from January 1st to December 31st until 1832. Notable variations in the United Kingdom include a budget introduced by Disraeli in the autumn of 1852, one introduced on February 10, 1860, and a year (1880) featuring two separate budgets—one in March under Disraeli and another in June under Gladstone.

## Budgetary Frameworks in Specific Nations

### Japan
Under the Japanese system, the annual budget for state revenue and expenditure requires the consent of the Imperial Diet, with the budget first being laid before the House of Representatives. The Imperial Diet must also consent to the contracting of liabilities or national loans not provided for in the budget. 

Specific rules apply to different types of spending:
*   **Imperial House:** Expenditure is defrayed annually from the National Treasury at a fixed amount and does not require Diet consent unless an increase is necessary.
*   **Fixed and Legal Obligations:** Expenditures based on the Emperor's supreme powers, law, or legal government obligations cannot be reduced or rejected by the Imperial Diet without government concurrence.
*   **Special Funds:** The government may seek consent for a continuing expenditure fund for a fixed number of years to meet special requirements. Additionally, a reserve fund is established to cover unavoidable deficits or unprovided requirements.
*   **Emergency Measures:** If the Imperial Diet cannot be convoked due to foreign relations or domestic affairs, financial measures for public safety may be taken via Imperial Ordinance, provided they are submitted to the Diet at the next session.
*   **Budgetary Failure:** If the Budget is not brought into existence or voted on, the government implements the budget from the preceding year. 

The Board of Audit verifies and confirms the final account of state revenue and expenditure, which the government then submits to the Imperial Diet.

### Austria-Hungary (The Dual Monarchy)
The Dual Monarchy maintains a common budget alongside the individual budgets of each state. This common budget covers the ministry of finance, the army, and foreign affairs. Revenues for this joint budget are derived from the joint ministries, net proceeds of customs, and proportional contributions (quotas) from the two states.

These quotas are fixed for several years, often coinciding with commercial and customs treaties. Historically, Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30% until 1897. From 1897 to July 1907, the quota was 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary. In 1907, Hungary's contribution rose to 36.4%. Additionally, 2% of total charges are debited to Hungary due to the incorporation of the former military frontier. In 1905, the total revenue for the common administration was L20,762,410.

## Political and Local Budgetary Dynamics
In federal governments—including the Argentine republic, the German empire, and the United States—knowledge of finances requires consulting both federal budgets and the budgets of the several states. Furthermore, greater countries typically maintain local and municipal expenditures and taxations.

In Denmark, budgetary disputes have historically driven political crises. Between 1872 and 1894, the *Folketing* (Lower House) and *Landsting* (Upper House) struggled for power. Prime Minister J. B. Estrup utilized provisional financial decrees to maintain government functions when the *Folketing* rejected the budget. Later, in 1897, the *Folketing* voted for a budget that was subsequently rejected by the *Landsting*, leading to the resignation of the ministry. In 1898, the *Folketing* rejected the military budget by a majority of 85 to 12.

## Etymology
The term "budget" was historically applied to a small coffer or leather-covered case (translated from the French *bougette*). It became a common term for a despatch box used for official papers. This origin is reflected in the phrase "open his budget," used when the chancellor of the exchequer delivered his annual statement.
