# Ashburton Markets and Fairs

## Market Rights and Administration
Ashburton, an ancient stannary town and borough by prescription, was historically governed by a bailiff and a portreeve. These officials were elected on an annual basis during the court leet, which was presided over by the lord of the manor. The town's ownership transitioned over time; according to Domesday, it was held in chief by Osbern, the bishop of Exeter, and rendered geld for six hides. In 1552, the bishop sold the two manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough, after which they became property of the crown. Eventually, the Clinton family acquired the manors in moieties, and the joint lordship of the manor is currently held by Sir Robert Jardine and the present Lord Clinton.

## The Feasts of St Laurence and St Martin
During the reign of Edward II, Bishop Stapledon secured the right to hold a Saturday market in Ashburton. Additionally, he obtained the right to hold two annual fairs, each lasting three days. These fairs were scheduled to coincide with specific feasts: the feast of St Laurence on August 10 and the feast of St Martin in winter on November 11.

## Later Commercial Grants
The commercial activity of the town expanded through further grants in the 17th and 18th centuries. In 1672, John Ford was granted a Tuesday market specifically for the sale of woollen goods and wool, provided they were made from English yarn. Later, in 1705, Andrew Quicke obtained the right to hold two annual fairs for the sale of merchandise, corn, and cattle. These fairs were held on the first Thursdays of June and March.

## Parliamentary Representation
Ashburton's political status was reflected in its parliamentary returns. The town returned two members in 1407 and 1298. From 1407 until 1640, it returned only one member, before returning to two members. This representation was reduced by the Reform Act of 1832, which deprived the town of one member, and subsequently by the Reform Act of 1885, which deprived it of the remaining member.
