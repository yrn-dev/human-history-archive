# Bordeaux and La Bastide

Bordeaux is a major city located in south-western France and serves as the capital of the Gironde department. It is situated on the left (west) bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea. The city is positioned within a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district.

## Geography and Layout
The Garonne river forms a semicircle at this location, which serves to separate the city proper on the left bank from La Bastide, an important suburb located on the right bank. The city is characterized by a crescent of wide, busy quays. The background of these quays consists of mansions, factories, and lofty warehouses, with steeples and towers rising behind them.

The city's layout features a central area where life is concentrated, consisting of narrow, winding streets surrounding the Place des Quinconces. Outside of this central quarter, the streets are quiet and narrow, bordered by low white houses. This architectural style differs from other large French towns, which are typically characterized by high tenements. A semicircle of boulevards surrounds the entire city, beyond which are the suburbs of Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat.

## River Crossings
The Garonne is crossed at this point by two primary structures:
* **The Pont de Bordeaux:** A fine stone structure built in the early 19th century. It measures 1534 feet in length.
* **The Railway Bridge:** This bridge connects the Southern company station on the left bank with the Orleans railway company station located in La Bastide.

## Public Spaces and Monuments
The city's principal promenades are located near the center and include the Place des Quinconces, the allees de Tourny, and a beautiful public garden. 

The Place des Quinconces is planted with plane trees and features two huge statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne. The square terminates at the quays with two rostral columns that function as lighthouses. Additionally, the west side of the square contains a monument dedicated to the Girondin deputies who were proscribed in 1793 under the convention. At the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces, the area opens into the Place de la Comedie, which houses the 18th-century Grand Theatre.

## Regional Connectivity
Bordeaux is connected to other major cities via railway. It is located 159 miles north-west of Toulouse on the main line of the Southern railway and 359 miles south-south-west of Paris via the main line of the Orleans railway. As of 1906, the population of the city was 237,707.
