# Ayacucho

The department of Ayacucho is located across the great plateau of central Peru. It is positioned between the departments of Apurimac and Huancavelica, with Ica to the west and Cuzco to the east. The region covers a mountainous and broken landscape that is partially barren at its higher elevations, though it is traversed by fertile, warm, and deep valleys.

## Geography and Administration
The department has an area of 18,185 square miles and was divided into six provinces as of 1896, with a population of 302,469. Originally known as Guamanga, the name of the department was changed to Ayacucho by a decree issued in 1825.

## The City of Ayacucho
The capital of the department and the province of Guamanga is the city of Ayacucho. Founded by Pizarro in 1539, the city was also known as Guamanga until 1825. It is situated on an elevated plateau 8,911 feet above sea level, located between the central and western Cordilleras. The city lies on the main road connecting Cuzco and Lima, situated 394 meters from Lima via Jauja.

The city is characterized by a temperate and agreeable climate and is regularly built. It serves as the seat of a superior court of justice and a bishopric. While its conventual establishments have been closed, the city is distinguished by its numerous churches. As of 1896, the city had a population of 20,000 and maintains considerable commercial importance.

## History and the Plain of Ayacucho
The department formed part of the original home of the Incas and once supported a large population. A significant historical site is the small plain of Ayacucho (a Quichua term meaning "corner of death"), located near the village of Quinua. This plain sits in an elevated valley 11,600 feet above sea level. In 1824, a decisive battle took place here between the Spanish viceroy La Serna and General Sucre. The defeat of La Serna in this engagement resulted in the independence of Peru.

## Economy and Natural Resources
Ayacucho is an important region for mining, with a large number of silver mines currently in operation. The agricultural output varies by elevation:
*   **Colder regions:** These areas produce potatoes, Indian corn, and other cereals.
*   **Low tropical valleys:** These areas produce mandioca (_Jatropha manihot_, L.), sweet potatoes, and tropical fruits.
