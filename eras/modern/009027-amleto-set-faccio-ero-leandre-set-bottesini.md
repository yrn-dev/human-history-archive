# Amleto and Ero e Leandre

The works *Amleto* and *Ero e Leandre* are musical compositions based on libretti written by the Italian poet and composer Arrigo Boito.

## Arrigo Boito
Arrigo Boito (1842– ) was born on February 24, 1842, in Padua. His life was characterized by a divided allegiance between music and literature. He studied music at the Milan Conservatoire, where he collaborated with Franco Faccio to write and compose a successful cantata titled *Le Sorelle d'Italia*. 

Following his studies, Boito traveled for several years before settling in Milan, where he worked in music and journalism. In 1866, he fought under Garibaldi. In 1868, he conducted the first performance of his opera *Mefistofele* at the Scala theatre in Milan; although it initially failed and was withdrawn, a revised and abbreviated version performed in Bologna in 1875 was successful. The work was later performed with success in London in 1880, though it never firmly established itself in popular favor. Boito's approach to the Faust legend was more closely aligned with the conception of Goethe than that of Gounod, though the opera is noted for lacking dramatic interest and cohesion despite isolated beauties.

Boito was later primarily devoted to the composition of libretti. His principal works in this field include *Otello* and *Falstaff* (set by Verdi), *La Gioconda* (set by Ponchielli), *Amleto* (set by Faccio), and *Ero e Leandre* (set by Bottesini and Mancinelli). These libretti are recognized for their uncommon literary value and a rare knowledge of the requirements of dramatic poetry. Additionally, Boito wrote a novel, *L'Alfier Meno*, and a book of poems. In 1893, the university of Cambridge conferred upon him the degree of doctor of music.

## Amleto
*Amleto* is a work for which Arrigo Boito provided the libretto and which was set to music by Franco Faccio. Faccio had previously collaborated with Boito during their time at the Milan Conservatoire on the cantata *Le Sorelle d'Italia*.

## Ero e Leandre
*Ero e Leandre* is a work featuring a libretto by Arrigo Boito, set to music by Mancinelli and Giovanni Bottesini. 

Giovanni Bottesini (1822–1889) was regarded as the greatest virtuoso on the double bass the world has ever known. He was celebrated not only for his tone and technical perfection but also for his exquisite taste in phrasing and the delicacy of his style.
