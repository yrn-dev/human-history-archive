# University of Pennsylvania

The University of Pennsylvania is an American educational institution located in Philadelphia. Formerly known as the College of Philadelphia, it has served as a center for higher education and research across various disciplines, including medicine, botany, linguistics, and archaeology.

## Academic Faculty and Appointments

The university has hosted a variety of distinguished professors and scholars. In 1789, Benjamin Smith Barton was appointed as the professor of botany and natural history. Barton later expanded his roles at the institution, becoming the professor of *materia medica* in 1795 and eventually obtaining the chair of practical medicine in 1813 following the death of Dr. Benjamin Rush.

In the field of linguistics and archaeology, Daniel Garrison Brinton served as the professor of American linguistics and archaeology from 1886 until his death in 1899. Additionally, the university has employed experts in Semitic languages, including Morris Jastrow, Jr., Ph.D., and Rev. John Punnett Peters, Ph.D., D.D., who previously served as a Professor of Hebrew.

## The Babylonian Expedition

The University of Pennsylvania conducted significant archaeological work in the Near East through its Babylonian Expedition. From 1888 to 1895, Rev. John Punnett Peters was in charge of the university's expedition conducting excavations at Nippur. Peters later detailed these efforts in his work, *Nippur, or Explorations and Adventures on the Euphrates* (1897).

The activities of the expedition are documented in the writings of H. V. Hilprecht, specifically in *The Babylonian Expedition of the University of Pennsylvania* (1893 and following).

## Notable Alumni and Students

The university has been attended by individuals who achieved prominence in law, politics, and finance. Joseph Hopkinson graduated from the University of Pennsylvania in 1786; he later served as a Federal judge of the Eastern District of Pennsylvania and a member of the national House of Representatives.

Nicholas Biddle, an American financier and president of the Bank of the United States, attended the university and nearly completed its prescribed course of study before transferring to Princeton to graduate in 1801.
