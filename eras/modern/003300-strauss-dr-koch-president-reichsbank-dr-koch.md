# Strauss and Dr R. Koch

## Banking Criticisms in Germany
In the German banking sector, a growing proportion of resources has been directed toward industrial concerns, including some located outside the boundaries of the empire. This practice has drawn criticism from Caesar Strauss and Dr R. Koch, the president of the Reichsbank. 

The primary motivation for banks to employ funds in this manner is to secure a higher rate of interest than what can be obtained through making loans at home or discounting bills. However, such actions are viewed as being opposed to banking tradition and regular rules of business, which typically avoid making fixed investments of any large portion of a bank's resources. Dr Koch specifically highlighted the necessity for Germany to develop powerful banks that remain unconnected to this type of speculative business. Despite these concerns, Dr Koch observed that the risks associated with the "reserve system" mentioned by Bagehot are not a fear for Germany.

## Geographical and Historical Research of Professor Koch
A Professor Koch has conducted systematic investigations into the regions of Armenia and the route of the Greeks as described by Xenophon. His research is noted for its authority due to his personal investigations in these areas.

### The March of the Greeks
Professor Koch provided analysis on the progress of the Greeks, including the distance from Trebizond to the crossing of the Eastern Euphrates. He suggested that various embarrassments occurred during the march that Xenophon did not explicitly state. Koch also proposed that the river Xenophon identified as the Harpasus is likely the Tchoruk-su, a view shared by Colonel Chesney.

Regarding the crossing of the Eastern Euphrates during mid-winter, Professor Koch differed from the views of Colonel Chesney and Mr. Ainsworth, affirming that the river would be fordable at approximately latitude 39° 3′, slightly above its confluence with the Tscharbahur. This placement aligns the Greeks' path with the road from Mush to Erzerum, which is considered the only mountain pass open throughout the winter. Additionally, Professor Koch identified a warm spring mentioned by Xenophon in the district of Wardo, near the village of Bashkan, stating it is the only such spring south of the Bingöldagh mountain range.

### Route Identifications and Topography
Professor Koch followed Mr. Ainsworth in identifying the Teleboas as the Kara-Su. He proposed that the Greeks crossed the Kentritês higher up, near the town of Sort or Sert, and marched north-east toward Bitlis to bypass the Bitlis-Su river. He further suggested they traveled near the Lake of Van over a saddle separating the lake from the mountain Ali-Dagh, which serves as the water-shed between the affluents of the Eastern Euphrates and the Tigris.

In other topographical assessments, Professor Koch noted that the sea cannot be discerned from the mountain Kop-Dagh. He also affirmed, along with other modern travelers, that the ridge called Tekieh-Dagh is neither high enough nor close enough to the sea to allow for the view described by Xenophon. Furthermore, Koch identified the neighborhood of Jezireh ibn Omar (ancient Bezabde) as the likely location where the Greeks left the Tigris for the Karduchian mountains.
