# Gar-fish and Flying Fishes

## Distribution and Prevalence
The gar-fish and flying fishes are described as being numerous, with their distribution extending into the seas of Europe. They are categorized among the Physostomi, a group of fishes that also includes the siluroids, which are found abundantly in muddy waters and estuaries.

## Related Marine Species of South Asia
The seas of south Asia contain a diverse array of marine life. Within the Physostomi, the siluroids exhibit remarkable habits, such as males carrying ova in their mouths until the young hatch. Other members of this group include the gelatinous *Harpodon*, also known as bumalo, from the small *Scopelidae* family.

The *Clupeidae*, or herrings, are most abundant in these waters, while sardines (or anchovies) appear in shoals at irregular and uncertain intervals. In the Indian seas, marine eels (*Muraenidae*) are less numerous than they are toward the Malay Archipelago. Other common forms include:
* **Gymnodontes:** This group includes globe-fish, sun-fish, pipe-fishes (*Syngnathus*), fife-fishes (*Sclerodermus*), and sea-horses (*Hippocampus*).
* **Chondropterygii (Cartilaginous fishes):** Hammer-headed sharks and true sharks are numerous. The dog-fish is also present, with one species extending from the Cape of Good Hope to the Indian seas.
* **Other Cartilaginous forms:** Ordinary rays, skates, electrical rays (*Torpedinae*), and saw-fishes (*Pristidae*) are found in considerable numbers.

## Spiny-Rayed and Scaly-Finned Fishes
Among the Acanthopterygii (spiny-rayed fishes), the *Percidae* (perches) are largely represented. The genus *Serranus* is very numerous in Asia and features very large forms; this genus has only one species in Europe. Other allied genera extend from eastern Africa to the Indian seas.

The Squamipennes (scaly-finned fishes) are primarily found near coral reefs in the seas of southern Asia. This group includes:
* **Mullidae:** Red mullets represented by genera that differ from those found in Europe.
* **Polynemidae:** These range from the Pacific through the Indian Ocean to the Atlantic. One species, the mango fish, is considered a great delicacy and inhabits the seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal. These fishes are used to prepare isinglass.
* **Sciaenidae:** These extend from China to the Bay of Bengal, though they are not known to the westward.
* **Stromateidae:** Known as pomfrets, these extend to the Pacific and China and resemble the Mediterranean dory.

Other fishes distributed through the seas of south Asia include scabbard fishes (*Trichuridae*), lancet fishes (*Acanthuridae*), sword fishes (*Xiphidae*), as well as mullets, blennies, gobies, and various genera of mackerels.

## Fresh-Water and Migratory Fishes
The fresh waters of southern Asia differ from the marine environments, as they are deficient in typical Acanthopterygii. Instead, they are inhabited by spined or simple eels, siluroids, carp, and climbing or walking fishes. The latter are peculiar to Africa and south-eastern Asia; they are organized to breathe outside of water, allowing them to survive in regions of rain and periodical drought.

The *Cyprinidae* (carp) are largely represented in southern Asia and reach sizes unknown in Europe; for example, a *Barbus* in the Tigris was recorded at 300 lb. The *Clupeidae* (herrings) also occur in numerous forms in Asiatic waters and can ascend rivers for many hundred miles; the hilsa is a well-known Indian fish of this family.

Other notable fresh-water distributions include:
* **Salmonidae:** Entirely absent from southern Asian waters, though they exist in the northern Pacific and rivers flowing into the Arctic Ocean. Trout are found in streams flowing into the Caspian, beyond the Indus watershed, but are unknown in Indian rivers.
* **Sturgeons:** These abound in the Caspian and Black Seas and are found in Asiatic Russia, with an allied form extending to southern China.
* **Siluridae:** These reach their chief development in tropical regions. A single species of *Silurus* extends across Africa, southern Asia, and Europe.
