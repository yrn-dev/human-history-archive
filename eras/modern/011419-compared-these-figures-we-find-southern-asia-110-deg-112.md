# Temperatures of Asia

The Asian continent exhibits some of the most extreme temperature variations on Earth, ranging from some of the lowest recorded temperatures in the north to some of the highest in the south. These variations are driven by the continent's vast size, its geographical boundaries, and the influence of both oceanic proximity and inland elevation.

## Regional Temperature Variations

In Siberia, temperature variations are very great. Near the coast, the difference between the mean of the hottest and coldest months exceeds 100 deg. Fahr., with even greater differences between the extreme temperatures of those months. In most of Siberia, the difference between the means of the hottest and coldest months is rarely less than 60 deg. Fahr. Specific regional differences include 80 deg. Fahr. on the Sea of Aral and more than 50 deg. at Astrakhan on the Caspian. In Tiflis, the difference is 45 deg., while in northern China at Peking it is 55 deg.

As one moves south, these variations decrease. In Canton, the difference is reduced to 30 deg., and in Manila to 20 deg. In northern India, the greatest difference does not exceed 40 deg., falling further to approximately 15 deg. at Calcutta and 10 deg. or 12 deg. at Bombay and Madras. Similar ranges are found at the head of the Persian Gulf (comparable to northern India) and at Aden (comparable to Madras). In Singapore, Batavia in Java, and Galle in Ceylon, the range is approximately 5 deg. or less.

## Extreme Temperatures

The extremes of heat and cold are starkly divided between the northern and southern regions of the continent. In Siberia, maximum extreme temperatures are considered to be between 80 deg. and 90 deg. Fahr., while minima range between -40 deg. and -70 deg. Fahr. Near the Aral and Caspian Seas, heat rises to nearly 100 deg. Fahr., while cold falls to -20 deg. Fahr. or lower.

In contrast, southern Asia reaches some of the highest temperatures on the globe. A maximum of 110 deg. or 112 deg. Fahr. is hardly ever exceeded in this region. Absolute minimums in southern Asia are significantly higher: in northern India (at lat. 30 deg.), the temperature hardly drops below 32 deg.; at Calcutta, it is about 40 deg. (though it seldom falls to 50 deg.); at Bombay, it rarely falls below 60 deg.; and at Madras, it rarely falls as low as 65 deg. In Batavia and Singapore, the thermometer very rarely rises above 90 deg. or falls below 70 deg. At Aden, the maximum does not much exceed 90 deg., and the minimum is a few degrees below 70 deg.

## Factors Influencing Climate

Several geographical factors contribute to these temperature patterns:

*   **Oceanic Influence:** The proximity of the ocean provides a moderating effect, particularly along the eastern and southern parts of Asia where the land consists of peninsulas and islands.
*   **Elevation and Surface:** The table-lands of Persia and Afghanistan, along with the central part of Asia, have great elevations that tend to exaggerate winter cold. Conversely, the sterility of the surface and small rainfall in these regions increase summer heat. Because the dry soil cannot release solar heat through evaporation, heat accumulates on the surface in summer. In winter, the long, clear nights lead to a prolonged loss of heat by radiation, an effect intensified by air with little vapour or surfaces covered in snow.
*   **Permafrost:** In northern Asia, specifically near Yakutsk, the soil is permanently frozen to a depth of more than 380 ft., where the temperature remains 5 deg. or 6 deg. Fahr. below the freezing point of water. Summer heat only thaws the surface to a depth of about 3 ft.

## Mean Annual Temperatures

The mean temperature of the north coast of eastern Siberia is only a few degrees above 0 deg. Fahr. Isothermals of mean annual temperature generally follow regular curves across northern Asia. From the north down to 30 deg. N, a reduction of 1 deg. of latitude corresponds approximately to a rise of 1/2 deg. Fahr. At 30 deg. N, the mean temperature is about 75 deg. Fahr. South of this point, the increase in temperature is slower, with the highest mean temperature attained in southern Asia not much exceeding 82 deg. Fahr.
