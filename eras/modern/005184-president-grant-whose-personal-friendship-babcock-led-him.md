# President Grant and the Whisky Ring

## The Whisky Ring Scandal
Between 1870 and 1876, the Federal government was defrauded of a large portion of its rightful revenue derived from the distillation of whisky. This illicit operation, known as the "Whisky Ring," was headquartered in St. Louis. The scheme involved the cooperation of revenue officers and distillers across several cities, including Cincinnati, Milwaukee, and St. Louis.

In St. Louis alone, the illicit gains from this operation were estimated to be more than $2,500,000 over the six-year period. The revenue officers involved levied assessments on the distillers, claiming these funds were for a Republican campaign fund intended to further the re-election of President Grant. These gains were then divided between the officers and the distillers.

## Benjamin Helm Bristow
Benjamin Helm Bristow served as the Secretary of the Treasury from 1874 to 1876. A lawyer and politician born in Kentucky in 1832, Bristow had previously served as a state senator, a district-attorney for the Louisville district, and the first appointed solicitor-general of the United States. 

During his tenure as Secretary of the Treasury, Bristow vigorously prosecuted the Whisky Ring. His efforts resulted in the indictment of more than 200 men. While a number of these individuals were convicted and imprisoned for several months, they were eventually pardoned.

## The Role of Orville E. Babcock
Orville E. Babcock served as the private secretary to President Grant. Babcock was identified as a prominent alleged accomplice of the Whisky Ring in Washington. 

President Grant maintained a personal friendship with Babcock. This relationship led the President to engage in indiscreet interference regarding the prosecution of the ring.
