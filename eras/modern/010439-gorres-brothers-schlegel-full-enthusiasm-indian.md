# Indian Wisdom and the Influence of the Schlegel Brothers

## Intellectual Enthusiasm for Indian Philosophy
During the early 19th century, a circle of scholars in Germany exhibited a profound enthusiasm for Indian philosophy and wisdom. This group included J.J. Gorres, G.F. Creuzer, the brothers Schlegel, and Karl J. Windischmann. The influence of these figures was particularly evident in the education of the German philologist Franz Bopp. While attending the Lyceum in Aschaffenburg, Bavaria, Bopp was introduced to the literature and languages of the East through the eloquent lectures delivered by Windischmann.

## The Influence of Friedrich Schlegel
A significant catalyst for the interest in Indian studies among German historians and philosophers was the work of Fr. Schlegel. His book, *Uber die Sprache und Weisheit der Indier*, published in Heidelberg in 1808, exerted a powerful influence on the intellectual climate of the time. This specific text stimulated Bopp's interest in the sacred language of the Hindus, which eventually led him to pursue the study of Sanskrit.

## Franz Bopp's Philological Studies
Driven by this interest, Bopp traveled to Paris in 1812, funded by the Bavarian government, to devote himself to the study of Sanskrit. During his four years in Paris, he associated with eminent scholars, most notably Alexander Hamilton, who had acquired knowledge of Sanskrit while in India. Hamilton, along with L.M. Langles, had produced a descriptive catalogue of Sanskrit manuscripts held in the Imperial library.

At the Imperial library, Bopp utilized a rich collection of Sanskrit manuscripts, many of which had been brought from India by Father Pons in the early 18th century. He also studied Sanskrit books produced by the Serampore and Calcutta presses. In 1816, Bopp published the first result of these studies in Frankfort-On-Main, titled *Uber das Conjugationssystem der Sanskritsprache in Vergleichung mit jenem der griechischen, lateinischen, persischen und germanischen Sprache*. This work, which featured a preface by Windischmann, focused on tracing the common origin of the grammatical systems of Sanskrit, German, Latin, Greek, and Persian, as the common parentage of these languages had already been established.

## Other Contributions and Scholarly Works
The Schlegel brothers' engagement with Indian and linguistic studies extended into other areas of scholarship. A.W. Schlegel was known for his insistence on strict philological methods and the investigation of the laws of language, particularly the correspondence of sounds. His severe review in 1815 of the *Altdeutsche Walder*—a periodical published by the two brothers—criticized lawless etymological combinations and is said to have influenced the studies of Grimm.

Additionally, the *Indische Bibliothek* by Schlegel is cited as a source in various bibliographies, including those concerning ethnography and the identification of ancient Indian sites. For instance, Schlegel and Mr. Wilson concurred in identifying the site of Palimbothra with the Patna of the moderns and the Patalipara of the Indians.
