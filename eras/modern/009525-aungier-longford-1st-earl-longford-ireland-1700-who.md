# Francis Aungier, 1st Earl of Longford

Francis Aungier served as the 3rd Lord Aungier of Longford and the 1st Earl of Longford in Ireland. He died in the year 1700.

## Family and Relations
Francis Aungier was the brother of Gerald Aungier (also spelled Angier), who was instrumental in the foundation of the modern city of Bombay. While Francis held the titles of Lord Aungier of Longford and Earl of Longford, his brother Gerald pursued a career in administration and governance in India.

## The Work of Gerald Aungier
Gerald Aungier succeeded Sir George Oxenden as the president of Surat in 1669. During his tenure, he faced significant threats to Bombay, including pressures from the Dutch and Malabar pirates at sea, as well as the Mahrattas from the inland. Additionally, the Portuguese, who occupied the island of Salsette, had established a customs-barrier in the channel between the shore and Bombay, effectively cutting the city off from the mainland.

Despite a "niggardly policy" from the court of directors—who refused to pay for skilled engineers—Gerald Aungier successfully fortified the shore and the town. He established a military force consisting of regulars and militia; the regulars were primarily Germans, as they were considered more trustworthy than the individuals recruited by the Company's crimps in London.

## Administration of Bombay
In 1672, Gerald Aungier moved his headquarters to Bombay. After deterring an imposing Dutch fleet that had attempted to surprise the island in 1670, he began a comprehensive reorganization of the settlement. His administration was guided by a belief in the traditions of English liberty and a desire to move away from the oppressive nature of previous Portuguese rule, which had been characterized by religious intolerance, cruel treatment of natives, and oppressive fiscal policies.

Gerald Aungier implemented several key reforms:
* **Fiscal Reform:** With the approval of a general assembly of the chief representatives of the people, he replaced the burdensome land tax with a fixed money payment.
* **Religious Freedom:** He protected all castes in the celebration of their religious ceremonies.
* **Labor Rights:** He forbade the forced compulsion of natives to carry burdens against their will.

## Impact on Population and Urban Growth
These reforms led to a rapid increase in the population of Bombay, which grew from 10,000 to 60,000 within eight years. The city became a haven for various groups seeking freedom for their religion and trade, including Armenians and Parsees. Additionally, a specific quarter was designated for the banya, the Hindu capitalist class. However, this rapid concentration of people in an unwholesome location resulted in the prevalence of disease due to appalling sanitary conditions.

Gerald Aungier died in 1677.
