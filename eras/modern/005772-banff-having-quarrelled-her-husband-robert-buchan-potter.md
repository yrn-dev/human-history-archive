# Banff

Banff is a seaport and the capital of Banffshire, Scotland. It is a royal, municipal, and police burgh situated on high ground on the left bank of the mouth of the Deveron. Located 50 miles northwest of Aberdeen via the Great North of Scotland railway, the town is described as being beautifully situated.

## History and Governance
Banff is a place of great antiquity. Its first charter was granted in 1163 by Malcolm IV. Subsequent privileges were conferred by Robert Bruce in 1324 and Robert II in 1372. The burgh is governed by a council and a provost. For parliamentary representation, Banff unites with Peterhead, Kintore, Inverurie, Cullen, and Elgin to return a single member to parliament.

## Architecture and Public Edifices
The town's landscape includes the modern Banff Castle, which belongs to the earl of Seafield; this structure was erected on the site of an old castle on the hill by the sea where Archbishop Sharp was born, though scarcely any trace of the original castle remains. 

Other significant public buildings include:
*   **The Town Hall:** Featuring a spire that reaches 100 feet in height.
*   **The Academy:** A modern building designed in the Grecian style. It includes an extensive museum that was originally part of the defunct Banff Institution before being taken over by the town council. The museum contains examples of the early mechanical genius of the astronomer James Ferguson. Thomas Edward, known as the "working naturalist," served as the museum's curator for several years.
*   **Chalmers Hospital:** Founded by Alexander Chalmers of Clunie, a shipowner and merchant from the town.
*   **County Buildings** and a masonic hall of tasteful design.

Additionally, the town is adjacent to Duff House.

## Economy and Trade
The economy of Banff is supported by a variety of industries and the fishing trade. Principal manufactures include sails, rope, leather, and woollens. The town also hosts brick-yards, timber-yards, iron foundries, breweries, and distilleries. There is also some ship-building activity.

The fishing trade is of significant importance, with the fishery districts centering in Buckie and Banff. White fishing occurs year-round, while the herring season runs from September to June. The town's imports consist primarily of timber and coal, while its exports include potatoes, dairy produce, fish, cattle, and grain.

## Infrastructure and Regional Context
Banff is connected by rail via a station at the harbour (communicating with Keith and Portsoy) and another at Bridge of Banff (communicating with Aberdeen via Inveramsay). 

The town is located one mile west of Macduff, a police burgh and seaport on the right bank of the Deveron. Macduff is connected to Banff by a stone bridge. While Macduff's harbour is considered more accessible and safer than that of Banff, both serve as centers for the regional fishing trade.

## Local Benefactors
The poor of the town benefit from the "Cassie Gift," which originated from a bequest of £20,000 left by Alexander Cassie of London, a native of Banff. The interest from this sum is divided twice annually.
