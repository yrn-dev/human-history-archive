# Causeries du lundi

*Causeries du lundi* is a series of works by the critic Sainte-Beuve. The texts are cited across various historical and literary contexts within the archive, serving as a source of critical analysis, biographical notices, and historical commentary.

## Analysis of Balzac
In volume ii of *Causeries du lundi*, Sainte-Beuve provides a postmortem examination of the novelist Balzac. The relationship between the two men had been strained, as Sainte-Beuve and Balzac fell out, leading the novelist to preserve a "furious diatribe" against the critic. Consequently, the analysis found in volume ii is described as being hardly cordial, although it is noted as not being unfair.

## Literary and Biographical Notices
Sainte-Beuve utilizes the *Causeries du lundi* to examine a wide array of figures and works:

*   **Military History:** Volume vi contains a long and careful notice regarding the *Mémoires* of Marmont. These memoirs, published in eight volumes after Marmont's death in 1856, serve as a personal defense of the author and provide value for the military history of his time.
*   **Poetry and Drama:** Volume iv is cited in relation to the works of P. Dupont. Additionally, volume vi is referenced in the study of the works of Ducis, whose plays include *Hamlet*, *Macbeth*, *Oedipe chez Admete*, and *Abufar*.
*   **Administrative Figures:** Volume ix contains three articles concerning Daru, an administrator of the Napoleonic empire whom Napoleon praised for his intellect, judgment, and "body and mind of iron."
*   **Other Figures:** The series includes discussions on Duclos (volume ix) and other contemporary literary figures (volume xiv).

## Historical Applications
The *Causeries du lundi* are also referenced as a resource for studying the reign of Louis XIV. This period is noted for its richness in memoirs, such as those by Saint-Simon and Madame de Motteville, as well as the letters of Madame de Sévigné and Madame de Maintenon. Sainte-Beuve's work is listed alongside other historical sources, such as the *Cambridge Modern History* and the *Histoire générale* of Lavisse and Rambaud, as a means of exploring this era.
