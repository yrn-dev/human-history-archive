# Peace Treaty of December 11 between Chile and Bolivia

The peace treaty signed on December 11 followed a period of prolonged conflict and political instability involving Chile, Peru, and Bolivia. The resolution of this struggle was preceded by internal revolutions in both Peru and Bolivia and a series of decisive military engagements.

## Political Instability and Revolutions
Prior to the peace negotiations, double revolutions occurred in Bolivia and Peru. In Peru, Colonel Pierola was proclaimed dictator after General Prado was deposed. Bolivia followed the example of its allies. In Bolivia, troops stationed at Tacna, feeling indignant over the incompetence or cowardice of their president, removed him from command and elected Colonel Camacho to lead them. Simultaneously, a revolution in La Paz proclaimed General Narciso Campero as president, a position he was formally elected to in June via the ordinary constitutional procedure.

## Military Conflict and the Fall of Peru
Throughout 1880, the war was primarily a naval struggle between Chile and Peru, with Bolivia playing little to no part. The conflict escalated in January 1881 with the battles of Miraflores and Chorillos, which were characterized by heavy slaughter and savage excesses committed by Chilean troops. These battles led almost immediately to the surrender of Callao and Lima, effectively making the Chileans the masters of Peru.

Despite these losses, the war lingered in the interior of Peru, where Admiral Montero had established a provisional government. In September 1882, a conference was held between Admiral Montero and President Campero of Bolivia, where it was decided that they would continue to hold out for better terms. However, the Peruvians eventually grew wearied of the useless struggle. On October 20, 1883, Peru concluded a peace treaty with Chile. Following this, the troops at Arequipa under Admiral Montero surrendered the town. Montero subsequently fled to Bolivia for refuge, where he was received coldly, before eventually withdrawing to Europe.

## The Treaty of December 11
By November 9, the Chilean army of occupation had concentrated at Arequipa, while the remnants of the Bolivian army were located at Oruro. Following the opening of negotiations, a peace treaty was signed between Chile and Bolivia on December 11. Under the terms of this treaty, Bolivia ceded its entire sea-coast to Chile, which included the port of Cobija.
