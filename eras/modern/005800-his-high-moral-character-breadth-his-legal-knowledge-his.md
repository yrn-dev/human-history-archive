# James Buchanan

## Character and Qualifications
James Buchanan possessed a high moral character and a breadth of legal knowledge. His professional background was extensive, having served as a diplomat, a cabinet member, and a congressman. These attributes and experiences were such that, in ordinary times, he would have been an excellent president. However, he lacked the self-reliance, moral courage, and soundness of judgment required to manage a crisis.

## Administration and the Kansas Conflict
At the start of his presidency, Buchanan appointed Frederick P. Stanton of Tennessee as secretary and Robert J. Walker of Mississippi as the territorial governor of Kansas. He assured both men of his intention to adhere to the principle of popular sovereignty. 

Despite these initial assurances, Buchanan began using his influence to force the admission of Kansas into the Union under the Lecompton Constitution, which favored pro-slavery interests. This action was contrary to the wishes of the majority of settlers in the territory. This shift in policy was attributed to timidity rather than a calculated effort to gain the favor of the Southern Democracy for the purpose of re-election.

## Influence and Political Maneuvers
The president's convictions regarding the necessity of the Lecompton measure to avoid civil war were influenced by Jacob Thompson of Mississippi (secretary of the interior) and Howell Cobb of Georgia (secretary of the treasury). 

To advance the Lecompton measure and the compromise English Bill, federal patronage was used freely. Additionally, this patronage was employed to prevent the election of Douglas to the Senate in 1858.

## The Covode Investigation
In 1860, a committee of the House of Representatives conducted the Covode Investigation, which brought some of these facts to light. However, the investigations were noted for being partisan in character. Furthermore, there are reasons to doubt whether the House possessed the constitutional power to conduct such an investigation, unless it served as the basis for an impeachment trial.
