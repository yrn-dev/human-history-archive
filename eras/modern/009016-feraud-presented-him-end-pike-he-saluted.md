# Jean Feraud and the Events of the Year III

## Political Career of Boissy d'Anglas
Francois Antoine de Boissy d'Anglas (1756-1828) was a French statesman who began his career in literature and as an advocate to the parlement in Paris. In 1789, he was elected as a deputy to the states-general by the third estate of the *senechaussee* of Annonay. He played a role in the proclamation of the National Assembly on June 17, 1789, and expressed approval for the taking of the royal family to Paris in October 1789, as well as the capture of the Bastille.

During his time in the Legislative Assembly, Boissy d'Anglas served as *procureur-syndic* for the directory of the department of Ardeche. Later, as a member of the Convention, he was positioned in the center, known as "*le Marais*." In the trial of Louis XVI, he voted for the king's detention until deportation was deemed expedient for the state. He was also dispatched on a mission to Lyons to investigate frauds related to the supplies of the army of the Alps.

## Role in the Committee of Public Safety
While Boissy d'Anglas initially supported Robespierre during the Terror, he was eventually won over by members of the Mountain who were hostile to Robespierre. This shift in support from Boissy d'Anglas and other leaders of the *Marais* facilitated the events of the 9th Thermidor. Following this, he was elected to the Committee of Public Safety, where he was responsible for the superintendence of the provisioning of Paris. He also presented the report that supported the decree of the 3rd Ventose of the year III, which established liberty of worship.

## The Insurrections of Germinal and Prairial
Boissy d'Anglas demonstrated significant courage during the critical days of Germinal and Prairial of the year III. On the 12th Germinal, while reading a report on food supplies in the tribune, the hall of the Convention was invaded by rioters; once the rioters withdrew, Boissy d'Anglas quietly resumed his report from where he had been interrupted.

On the 1st Prairial, Boissy d'Anglas presided over the Convention. During this session, he remained unmoved despite facing menaces and insults from insurgents. It was during these events that the head of the deputy, Jean Feraud, was presented to him on the end of a pike.
