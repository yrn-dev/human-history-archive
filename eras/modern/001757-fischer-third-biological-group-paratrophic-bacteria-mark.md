# Fischer's Classification of Bacteria

The biologist Fischer proposed a revised biological classification for bacteria, suggesting that the traditional division between parasites and saprophytes be replaced by a system that accounts for specific peculiarities in bacterial nutrition. His system categorizes bacteria into three primary biological groups: Prototrophic, Metatrophic, and Paratrophic.

## Prototrophic Bacteria
Fischer defines Prototrophic bacteria as primitive forms of life. These organisms are characterized by their ability to work up non-living materials into living materials, playing a significant role in the evolution of life on the globe. Prototrophic bacteria are monotrophic, meaning they can carry out only one particular series of decompositions or fermentations. Because they can obtain carbon or nitrogen from inorganic sources, they require no organic food materials. Examples of bacteria in this group include those that are nitrogen-fixing, nitrifying, and those that process iron or sulphur.

## Metatrophic Bacteria
The Metatrophic group encompasses the vast majority of bacteria, many of which are ordinarily referred to as saprophytes. These bacteria require various kinds of organic materials, which they obtain from the surfaces of the bodies of other organisms or from dead remains. Fischer further distinguishes these based on their metabolic activity:
*   **Saprogenic:** Bacteria that bring organic material to a putrefactive state.
*   **Saprophilous:** Bacteria that live best within putrefying materials.
*   **Zymogenic:** Bacteria whose metabolic products can induce other toxic effects or blood-poisoning; these are considered facultative parasites, though they are not true parasites.

Metatrophic bacteria may be Polytrophic, meaning they can utilize and decompose organic materials in various ways, or they may be monotrophic, though in the latter case, they remain unable to work up inorganic materials.

## Paratrophic Bacteria
Fischer established the third biological group, the Paratrophic bacteria, to categorize true parasites (referred to as obligate parasites by de Bary). This designation marks the importance of their specific mode of life, as Paratrophic bacteria live and multiply within the interior of living organisms. Specifically, they are found in the tissues, juices, or blood of their hosts.
