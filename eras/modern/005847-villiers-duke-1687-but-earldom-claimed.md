# The Dukedom of Buckingham

The title of Duke of Buckingham has been held by several distinct lineages and creations throughout English history, originating with the Villiers family and later appearing in the houses of Normanby and Grenville.

## The Villiers Lineage
In 1617, King James I created George Villiers as earl. This was followed by his creation as marquess in 1618 and duke of Buckingham in 1623. The dukedom and marquessate continued until the death of the 2nd Duke of Buckingham in 1687, at which point both titles became extinct.

The 2nd Duke, George Villiers (1628–1687), was the son of the 1st Duke. He was educated at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he earned an M.A. in 1642, and was raised by King Charles I alongside the king's own children. During the Civil War, he fought for the king, participating in the attack on Lichfield Close in April 1643. Following this, he and his brother Francis lived in Rome and Florence under the care of the earl of Northumberland.

During the Second Civil War in July 1648, George Villiers joined the earl of Holland in Surrey. While his brother Francis was killed near Kingston, the duke escaped to Holland after being surprised at St Neots on the 10th. His participation in the rebellion led to the confiscation of his lands, a significant portion of which went to Fairfax. Despite this, Charles II admitted him to the privy council on April 6, 1650, and conferred the Garter upon him on September 19, 1649. 

The 2nd Duke's political career was marked by his support for an alliance with Scottish presbyterians and his alliance with Argyll, which led him to dissuade Charles II from joining a royalist plot in October 1650. He served as general of the eastern association in England and was commissioned to raise forces abroad. He was present at the battle of Worcester on September 3, 1651, and escaped to Rotterdam in October. His influence later waned due to his negotiations with Cromwell's government and his courtship of the king's widowed sister.

## Claims to the Earldom
Following the extinction of the dukedom in 1687, the earldom was claimed by a collateral line through the special remainder in the 1617 patent. These claimants, whose legitimacy was considered doubtful, claimed descent from John Villiers, 1st Viscount Purbeck. The title was used by John Villiers, who styled himself earl of Buckingham until his death in 1723. The claim finally ended with the death of a clergyman named George Villiers in 1774.

## Subsequent Creations
Other distinct titles using the name Buckingham were created in later years:

*   **The Normanby Creation:** In 1703, John Sheffield, marquess of Normanby, was created "duke of the county of Buckingham and of Normanby." This title passed to his son Edmund, but became extinct upon Edmund's death in October 1735.
*   **The Grenville Creation:** A later title of marquess and duke of Buckingham was established within the Grenville family. Unlike the Normanby title, this was derived from the town of Buckingham rather than the county. It began in 1784 when the 2nd Earl Temple was created marquess of Buckingham "in the county of Buckingham." This title was later elevated to the dukedom of Buckingham and Chandos for his son in 1822.
