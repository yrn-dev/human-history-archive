# Praise-God Barbon

Praise-God Barbon (c. 1596–1679) was an English leather-seller, minister, and political figure associated with the Fifth Monarchy men. He was admitted as a freeman of the Leathersellers Company on January 20, 1623, and later became a liveryman on October 13, 1634.

## Religious Life and Ministry
Barbon served as a minister for a congregation that met at his own residence, known as "The Lock and Key," located in Fleet Street. His preaching was noted for attracting large audiences. While his exact religious views are not entirely clear, his enemies described him as an Anabaptist and a Brownist (likely a Baptist). However, Barbon wrote two books in support of paedobaptism, and his congregation had separated from a larger Baptist group specifically over this controversy. He later became affiliated with the sect of Fifth Monarchy men.

Due to his beliefs, Barbon was frequently the target of ridicule and abuse from opposing parties. His meetings were often disrupted by riots, and on December 20, 1641, a mob stormed his house, an event from which he narrowly escaped with his life.

## Political Career and the Barebone's Parliament
A man of substantial property, Barbon was summoned by Oliver Cromwell on June 6, 1653. He was appointed as a member for London to the assembly of nominees. This assembly was referred to in derision as "Barebone's Parliament," named after him. Although his name appears occasionally in the records of this parliament, he does not appear to have participated in the debates.

## Opposition to the Restoration
In 1660, Barbon was highly active in attempting to prevent the Restoration. He published a book by Needham titled *News from Brussels in a Letter from a Near Attendant on His Majesty's Person ...*, which contained unfavorable anecdotes regarding the morals of Charles. 

On February 9, Barbon presented a petition to Parliament proposing that all officials should abjure the Stuarts. The petition further suggested that anyone publicly proposing the Restoration should be considered guilty of high treason. These actions led to several attacks from royalists. Consequently, on March 31, Barbon was forced to sign an engagement with the council promising not to disturb the peace.

## Arrest and Later Life
On November 26, 1661, Barbon was arrested along with James Harrington and John Wildman. He was imprisoned in the Tower until July 27, 1662, when he was released on bail.

Barbon was the author of *A Discourse tending to prove ... Baptism*. He was married and died in 1679, with his burial taking place on January 5, 1680.
