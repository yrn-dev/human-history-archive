# Republics and Historical Archives

## The Development of Republics in Europe and the Americas
The transition to republican forms of government occurred across several continents during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. In France, the nation became a republic in 1792 following the declaration of war between France and Austria, and the subsequent declaration of war by Prussia against France. This period saw the beheading of Louis XVI in 1793 and the end of the Jacobin republic in 1794 with the execution of Robespierre. The French government then transitioned to the rule of the Convention and later the Directory in 1795. By 1804, Napoleon Bonaparte, who had previously served as First Consul, became Emperor.

In the Americas, the United States of America issued a Declaration of Independence in 1776 and established a Treaty of Peace with Britain in 1783. The Federal Government of the United States was set up by the Constitutional Convention of Philadelphia in 1787, and the First Federal Congress met at New York in 1788. Later, in 1810, Spanish America became republican.

## The Republic of Bolivia
The republic of Bolivia experienced a period of instability following the purchase of its independence. In 1827, General Sucre and Colombian troops were driven from La Paz. A new congress formed at Chuquisaca in April 1828, which modified the constitution provided by Bolivar and appointed Marshal Santa Cruz as president. Although a revolution led by General Blanco briefly overturned the government, order was restored by 1831. During this time, Santa Cruz established a code of laws and a treaty of commerce with Peru.

In 1835, Santa Cruz intervened in a power struggle within the republic of Peru, defeating General Gamarra and naming himself protector of Peru in 1836 with the intent of creating a confederation between the two countries. This prompted intervention from the government of Chile. Following three years of conflict, Santa Cruz was defeated at the battle of Jungay in June 1839 and exiled. While General Velasco became the provisional chief in Bolivia, the Santa Cruz party eventually revolted and installed General Ballivian in power.

General Ballivian's presidency was marked by a conflict with Peru's President Gamarra, who attempted to annex the province of La Paz in August 1841. Ballivian routed Gamarra's army, resulting in Gamarra's death. Ballivian remained president until 1848, when he retired to Valparaiso. He was succeeded by General Belzu following a military revolution.

## Historical Archives and Documentation
The preservation of republican and colonial history has been facilitated by various archives and scholarly works. In Bolivia, the *Archive Boliviano. Collecion de documentes relativos a la historia de Bolivia* was published by Vicente de Ballivian y Rizas in Paris in 1872. Other historical studies of the region include works by C. Wiener, E. Mossbach, and Theodore Child, as well as Ramon Sotomayor Valdes's study of the administration of General don Jose Maria Acha.

In the United States, John Romeyn Brodhead, an American historical scholar, focused on the colonial history of New York. In 1839, he became an attaché of the American legation at the Hague to access Dutch archives regarding early Dutch settlements in America. His research revealed that Dutch archives contained extensive material on the early history of New York. This led the state legislature to fund the gathering of transcripts from European archives in Holland, France, and England. These documents, comprising some eighty manuscript volumes, were published between 1853 and 1883 as *Documents relating to the Colonial History of New York*.

In the Netherlands, Guillaume Groen van Prinsterer, a politician and historian born in 1801, served as secretary to King William I of Holland. He is noted for editing the *Archives et correspondance de la maison d'Orange*, a twelve-volume work published between 1835 and 1845.
