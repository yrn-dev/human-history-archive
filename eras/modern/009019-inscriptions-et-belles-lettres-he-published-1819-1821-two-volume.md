# Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres

The Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres is a scholarly institution to which various distinguished French archaeologists, numismatists, and ecclesiastics have been elected.

## Notable Members and Scholars

Several scholars specializing in history, archaeology, and the church have been associated with the Academy. Louis Marie Olivier Duchesne, a French ecclesiastic and scholar born in 1843 in Saint Servan, Brittany, was elected as a member of the Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres in 1888. Duchesne was a professor at the Catholic Institute in Paris and later a lecturer at the Ecole Pratique des Hautes Etudes. He is recognized for his scientific criticism of church history and his work on the *Liber pontificalis*, as well as his studies on the origins of the Christian cult and episcopal dioceses in ancient Gaul.

Anatole Jean-Baptiste Antoine de Barthelemy (1821–1904) was also elected to the Academy in 1897. A French numismatist and archaeologist born in Reims, Barthelemy established his reputation primarily through his numismatical research. His contributions include the *Nouveau manuel complet de numismatique ancienne* (1851, revised 1890) and the *Nouveau manuel complet de numismatique du moyen age et moderne* (1853). He also authored specialized works such as *Numismatique merovingienne* (1865) and *Essai sur la monnaie parisis* (1874), and edited a volume on *La Numismatique de la France* in 1891 for the Comite des travaux historiques et scientifiques.
