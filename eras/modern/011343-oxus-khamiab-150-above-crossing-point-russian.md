# Oxus at Khamiab and the Russo-Afghan Boundary

The Oxus river serves as a critical geographical and political marker in Central Asia, particularly in the demarcation of boundaries between Russia and Afghanistan. A significant point of reference in this boundary is Khamiab, where the junction of the boundary with the Oxus is located approximately 150 miles above the crossing-point of the Russian Trans-Caspian railway at Charjui.

## Boundary Demarcation and Political Geography
The necessity for a definite boundary between the kingdom of Afghanistan and Russia's trans-Caspian conquests arose from the Russian advance toward the Oxus and the Turkoman deserts. This boundary was established in the north-west by the Russo-Afghan Boundary Commission between 1884 and 1886. The resulting line is described as being almost entirely artificial in its construction.

The boundary begins at Zulfikar, where it leaves the Hari Rud (the river of Herat), situated about 70 miles south of Sarakhs. The most southerly point of this boundary occurs where it crosses the Kushk, approximately 60 miles north of Herat. From the junction at Khamiab, the main channel of the Oxus river becomes the northern boundary of Afghanistan, separating it from Russia. This river boundary continues eastward to its source in Victoria Lake of the Great Pamir. Beyond this point, the Anglo-Russian Commission of 1895 demarcated a line extending to the glaciers and snowfields overlooking the Chinese border.

## The Province of Badakshan
Badakshan, which includes the district of Wakhan, is a province on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan that adjoins Russian territory. According to an Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873, Badakshan and Wakhan were acknowledged as fully belonging to the amir of Kabul, with the boundary limited to the southern or left bank of the Oxus.

The province's geography is characterized by its relationship to the Oxus and the Hindu Kush. Its northern boundary follows the Oxus stream under the northern slopes of the Hindu Kush; in some areas, the province is only 8 to 10 miles wide, separated by the length of these slopes from the southern boundary along the crest of the Hindu Kush. Badakshan extends an "arm" into the Pamirs that is bottle-shaped—narrow at the neck and swelling eastwards to include parts of the little and great Pamirs.

## Regional States and Russian Expansion
The Oxus basin has been a focal point of Russian exploration and territorial acquisition. The state of Bokhara (or Bukhara), which is under Russian protection, is situated on the right bank of the middle Oxus between 37 and 41 degrees North latitude. It is bounded by Afghanistan to the south and the Pamirs to the east.

Russia also established relations with Khiva in the 17th century. In 1717, Peter the Great dispatched a military force to Khiva after hearing of auriferous sand in the bed of the Oxus and seeking to open mercantile relations with India through Turan. Later, in 1873, a large-scale expedition under General Kaufmann occupied Khiva. Following this, Russia annexed all territory on the right bank of the Oxus, totaling 110,000 souls and 35,700 square miles.

## Geological and Physiographic Theories
The relationship between the Oxus and the Caspian Sea has been a subject of scientific investigation. Earlier theories suggested an ancient connection between the two. However, Russian scientists have displaced this theory with a hypothesis that the Caspian Sea extended eastwards into Trans-Caspian territory during the post-Pleiocene age. 

Support for this hypothesis is found in the discovery of shells—currently living in the Caspian—located about 100 miles inland at altitudes between 140 and 280 feet above the current Caspian level. Furthermore, the Kara-kum sands appear to be a purely marine formation, lacking the fluviatile deposits that would be expected if they were delta deposits of the Oxus. Additionally, the Uzboi depression between the Great and Little Balkhan ranges, once thought to be an old bed of the Oxus, is now considered to have been a gulf of the Caspian.
