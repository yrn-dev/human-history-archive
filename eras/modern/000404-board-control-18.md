# Board of Control

The term "Board of Control" refers to various administrative and regulatory bodies across different historical and geographical contexts, ranging from ancient Athens to modern state governance and colonial administration.

## Colonial and National Administration
In the context of the British Empire, the Board of Control for India was created in 1784. This occurred during a period when the home government was managing colonial affairs; for example, in 1791, Pitt granted representative institutions to Canada, though the home government maintained responsible control.

In the United Kingdom, the Board of Trade serves as a public department of the state directly responsible to parliament. Following the act of 1883, the Board of Trade assumed administrative functions previously handled by the courts, specifically regarding commercial legislation and the equitable distribution of a debtor's assets. Under this act, the bankruptcy department of the Board of Trade was created, headed by an inspector-general in bankruptcy. This department provides general supervision over administrative work and possesses extensive control over the appointment of trustees, including the power to audit their accounts and remove them for unfitness, neglect, or misconduct. 

The Board of Trade also appoints and removes official receivers who manage the estates of bankrupts and monitor their conduct. Additionally, the Board of Trade may direct official receivers to summon meetings to review the resolutions of a committee of inspection or the acts of a trustee. It is required to inquire into creditor complaints and may direct local investigations of a trustee's books and accounts.

## State and Municipal Boards
In various United States state governments, boards of control are utilized for the management of public institutions. One such example is a bipartisan board of control of state institutions consisting of four members. 

In other jurisdictions, control of public services is managed through specialized boards:
* **Health and Sanitation:** Louisiana organized a state board of health in 1855, the first effectively organized in the United States.
* **Infrastructure:** The Louisiana Board of Levee Commissioners was organized in 1865.
* **Municipal Governance:** In Louisville, the mayor appoints the members of two principal executive boards—the board of public safety and the board of public works—subject to the approval of the board of aldermen.
* **Prison Industries:** In some regions, a Board of Prison Industries is composed of the managers of the state reformatory and the commissioners of the penitentiaries.

## Professional and Social Control
Control boards are also employed to regulate professional licensing and social welfare. In the Dominion of Canada, provinces generally control the licensing of physicians. Quebec is unique in having a separate homoeopathic board of examiners, known as the College of Homoeopathic Physicians and Surgeons of Montreal, which is under the control of the Montreal Homoeopathic Association.

Historically, in Belgium, the state took over the realm of charity from the church and private entities. Local governments took over charitable institutions, where finances were controlled by the *échevins* and boards of trustees were appointed by municipalities. Under this system, the state controlled beggars, forcing them to work and requiring licenses for those under twelve, over sixty, or mothers without work and with numerous children.

## Ancient Athens
In ancient Athens, a board known as the *Πρόβουλοι* (Probûli) existed for a short period. It was established for a temporary purpose during a time of peculiar pressure and discouragement. Rather than acting as a hindrance or a board destined merely for control, it was intended to encourage the suggestion of new measures from experienced men of sense. This board was eventually merged into the Oligarchy of Four Hundred and was not reconstituted after the deposition of that oligarchy.
