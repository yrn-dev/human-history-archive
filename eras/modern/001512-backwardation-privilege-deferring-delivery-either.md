# Backwardation

Backwardation, often referred to simply as "back," is a technical term used within the context of the London Stock Exchange. It refers to a specific financial arrangement involving the loan of stock between accounts and the deferment of delivery.

## Definition and Application
In stock exchange operations, backwardation represents the amount charged for the loan of stock from one account to another. This payment is made by the seller to the purchaser on a bear account. The primary purpose of this payment is to allow the seller to defer the delivery of the stock.

## Mechanics of the Bear Account
The use of backwardation typically arises from the strategies of a seller who has sold stocks or shares for delivery on a specific date, despite likely not possessing them at the time of the sale. The seller operates on the hope that they can purchase the shares at a lower price before the fixed delivery date, thereby earning a profit.

If, upon the settling-day, the prices of the stocks have not decreased as the seller expected, the seller must pay the purchaser an agreed amount of interest, which is the backwardation. This payment is made for the privilege of deferring the delivery. The seller may seek this deferment for two primary reasons:
1. To provide more time to procure the necessary stock.
2. In the hope that a subsequent shrinkage in price will eventually occur, allowing the seller to realize a profit.

## Relation to Carrying Over
Backwardation is also associated with the process of "carrying over" or "continuation." This is a stock exchange operation where the settlement of a bargain—whether transacted for money or for a given account—is postponed from one settling day to another in exchange for a consideration. This consideration is termed either a "backwardation" or a "contango."

Such a continuation is functionally equivalent to a purchase "for the day" followed by a resale for the succeeding account, or a sale "for the day" followed by a repurchase for the succeeding account. The price used to adjust these specific transactions is known as the "making-up" price of the day.
