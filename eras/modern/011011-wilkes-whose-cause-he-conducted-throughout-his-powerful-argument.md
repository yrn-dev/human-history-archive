# John Dunning and the Cause of Wilkes

## Early Life and Legal Education
John Dunning, later known as the 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783), was an English lawyer born on October 18, 1731, in Ashburton, Devonshire. He was the second son of John Dunning, an attorney. Dunning received his early education at the free grammar school in his hometown. At the age of nineteen, he was admitted to the Middle Temple, having first been articled to his father. He was called to the bar in 1756.

## Professional Ascent
Dunning's early legal career progressed slowly; for several years, he worked the western circuit without receiving a single brief. His professional reputation began to change in 1762 when he was hired to draft a defense for the British East India Company. This document was written in a masterly style to address grievances memorialized to the crown by the Dutch East India Company, and it brought Dunning both emolument and reputation.

## The Case of Wilkes and General Warrants
In 1763, Dunning distinguished himself as counsel for Wilkes, a cause he conducted throughout. A pivotal moment in his career occurred on June 18, 1763, in the case of *Leach v. Money*. In this matter, Dunning presented a powerful argument against the validity of general warrants. This specific legal victory established his reputation and led to a gradual increase in his practice. By 1776, it is reported that his annual income had reached nearly £10,000.

## Political Career and Public Office
Dunning's rising prominence led to several significant appointments. In 1766, he was chosen as the recorder of Bristol, and in December 1767, he was appointed solicitor-general. He held the position of solicitor-general until May 1770, when he retired alongside his friend Lord Shelburne. In 1771, he was granted the freedom of the city of London.

Dunning entered parliament as a member for Calne in 1768, representing the borough until his promotion to the peerage. During this time, he became a regular member of the opposition and was noted for delivering many able speeches. In 1780, he successfully carried a motion by a majority of eighteen, stating that the influence of the crown had increased, was increasing, and ought to be diminished.

## Views on Sinecures and Later Appointments
Dunning was a strong opponent of the system of pensions and sinecure officers. However, the archive notes that his probity was not sufficient to prevent him from utilizing the system himself. In 1782, following the appointment of the marquis of Rockingham as prime minister, Dunning was appointed chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster, which was a rich sinecure.
