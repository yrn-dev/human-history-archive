# Speranze d'Italia

The *Speranze d'Italia* (Hopes of Italy) is a literary work authored by Count Cesare Balbo (1789–1853), an Italian statesman and writer. The work was produced during a period when Balbo sought to influence the destiny of his country through literature, having been denied public service following the revolution of 1821.

## Author Background
Cesare Balbo was born in Turin on November 21, 1789, to Prospero Balbo, a noble Piedmontese and mayor of the capital, and a mother from the Azeglio family. After spending time in Paris starting in 1798, Balbo served the Napoleonic empire between 1808 and 1814 in various locations, including Paris, Rome, Florence, and Illyria. Following the fall of Napoleon, he entered the service of his native country, joining the army and undertaking political missions to London and Paris.

Although suspected of sympathizing with the revolution of 1821, Balbo actually disapproved of it. Despite this, he was forced into exile. Upon his return to Piedmont, he was barred from public service, leading him to turn to literature as his primary means of political influence. This transition resulted in a body of work characterized by a practical and sometimes temporary element, often appearing fitful or incomplete.

## Core Objectives and Ideals
The primary goal of Balbo's intellectual labors was to assist in securing Italian independence from foreign control. His vision for the region was not one of true Italian unity, for which he had neither desire nor expectation. Instead, he was devoted to the house of Savoy, believing it was destined to change the fate of Italy.

Balbo's genuine ideal was the establishment of a confederation of separate states under the supremacy of the pope. While this was also the ostensible ideal of Gioberti, Balbo believed that Gioberti's work, *Primato*, neglected the first essential requirement for achieving independence.

## Teachings of Speranze d'Italia
In *Speranze d'Italia*, Balbo addressed the necessity of removing foreign influence to achieve independence. A central suggestion in the text is that Austria should seek compensation for the inevitable loss of its Italian provinces by looking toward the Balkans. To achieve this goal, Balbo emphasized the importance of:
*   Military and moral preparation.
*   Alertness.
*   Patience.
