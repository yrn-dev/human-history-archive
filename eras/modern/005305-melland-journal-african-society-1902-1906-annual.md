# Frank Melland and the Journal of the African Society

## Contributions to African Geography
Frank Melland is an author associated with the *Journal of the African Society*. Between the years 1902 and 1906, he wrote articles for this publication focusing primarily on North Eastern and North Western Rhodesia. These writings serve as part of a broader body of literature concerning the geography and administration of the region.

## Related Regional Documentation
The work of Melland exists alongside various other accounts of the African interior and British Central Africa. These include the *Narrative of an Expedition to the Zambezi* (1865) by David and Charles Livingstone, as well as the *Last Journals of David Livingstone* edited by the Rev. Horace Waller in 1874. Other relevant regional texts include *Adventures in Nyasaland* (1891) by L. Monteith Fotheringham, *Tropical Africa* (1891) by Henry Drummond, and *The Shiré Highlands* (1885) by John Buchanan.

Administrative and descriptive accounts of the area are further provided by Sir H.H. Johnston in *British Central Africa* (2nd ed., 1898), H.L. Duff in *Nyasaland under the Foreign Office* (1903), and J.E.S. Moore in *The Tanganyika Problem* (1904). Additionally, the Colonial Office published annual *Reports* on British Central Africa.

## Linguistic and Ethnographic Studies
The study of the region's people and languages is documented in several works. Miss A. Werner wrote *The Natives of British Central Africa* (1906) and produced various linguistic works. Other linguistic contributors include the Rev. Govan Robertson, Dr. R. Laws, A.C. Madan, Monsieur E. Jacottet, and Father Torrend. 

Specifically, the Rev. D.C. Scott published *An Encyclopaedic Dictionary of the Mang'anja Language, as spoken in British Central Africa* in 1891. Father J. Torrend authored the *Comparative Grammar of the South African Bantu Languages* (1894), which focused largely on the languages of the Central Zambezi, though it is noted as being incomplete and erroneous in some of its deductions.
