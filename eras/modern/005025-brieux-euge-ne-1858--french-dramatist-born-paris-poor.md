# BRIEUX, EUGÈNE

## Early Life and Career
Eugène Brieux was a French dramatist born in Paris on January 19, 1858, to parents of poor means. His entry into the theatrical world began in 1879 with the production of a one-act play titled *Bernard Palissy*, which he wrote in collaboration with M. Gaston Salandri. Following this initial work, Brieux experienced a period of eleven years before obtaining another hearing for his work. This gap ended in 1890 when Antoine produced his play *Ménage d'artistes* at the Théâtre Libre.

## Thematic Focus and Didacticism
The plays of Eugène Brieux are essentially didactic in nature. His writing was specifically aimed at addressing various iniquities or weaknesses found within the social system. Through his dramas, he sought to highlight social evils and systemic failures across a variety of contexts, including education, politics, and law.

## Major Works and Social Critiques
Between 1892 and 1902, Brieux produced a series of plays targeting specific societal issues:

*   **Education and Class:** *Blanchette* (1892) focused on the evil results associated with the education of working-class girls. Later, *Les Trois Filles de M. Dupont* (1897) provided a powerful and somewhat brutal study regarding the miseries that the French system of dowry imposed on girls from the poor middle class.
*   **Morality and Social Conduct:** *M. de Réboval* (1892) was directed against pharisaism, while *Les Bienssaiteurs* (1896) targeted the frivolity found in fashionable charity.
*   **Politics and Law:** *L'Engrenage* (1894) addressed corruption in politics, and *La Robe rouge* (1900) was directed against the injustices of the law.
*   **Science and Health:** *L'Évasion* (1896) served as a satire of an indiscriminate belief in the doctrine of heredity. In 1901, Brieux wrote *Les Avariés*, which was forbidden by the censor due to its medical details; consequently, the author read the play privately at the Théâtre Antoine.
*   **Labor and Domestic Life:** *Le Résultat des courses* (1898) illustrated the evil results of betting among Parisian workmen, and *Petite amie* (1902) described the life of a shop-girl in Paris. Additionally, *Les Remplaçantes* (1901) was directed against the practice of putting children out to nurse.

## Later Productions
In the early 20th century, Brieux continued to produce work. These later plays include *La Couvée* (1903), which had been acted privately in Rouen in 1893, and *Maternité* (1904). In 1904, he collaborated with M. Jean Sigaux on *La Déserteuse*. His 1906 work, *Les Hannetons*, was a comedy written in three acts.
