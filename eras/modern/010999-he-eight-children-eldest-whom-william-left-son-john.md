# The Ashburnham Lineage

The Ashburnham family history is characterized by a succession of titles and the establishment of a notable private library. The lineage includes a figure who had eight children, the eldest of whom was William. William had a son named John (1656–1710), who was created Baron Ashburnham in 1689.

## The Barons and Earls of Ashburnham

The title of Baron Ashburnham passed from the first John to his second son, also named John (1687–1737). This second John became the 3rd Baron Ashburnham upon his brother's death in 1710. In 1730, he was further elevated in rank, being created the earl of Ashburnham and Viscount St Asaph.

The family line continued through direct descendants, including the 5th earl, who was born in 1840. Another prominent member of the lineage was Bertram (1797–1878), the 4th earl. Bertram is recognized as the collector of the famous Ashburnham library, though this collection was dispersed in 1883 and 1884.

## Published Works and Historical Records

Several documents related to the family and their political conduct have been preserved in the archives. In 1648, a publication titled *A Letter from Mr Ashburnham to a Friend* was released, which served to defend the conduct of John Ashburnham regarding the king. Additionally, a *Letter to W. Lenthall* from 1647 exists, which repudiates accusations that the king had violated his parole; this document is held in the *Thomason Tracts* at the British Museum (E 418 [4]).

A more extensive *Narrative* was published later, in 1830, by George, the 3rd earl of Ashburnham. However, George's championship of his ancestor in this work is described as being entirely unconvincing and uncritical.
