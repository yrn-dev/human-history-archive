# Kherson and Podolia

## The Government of Kherson
Kherson is a government located in south Russia along the northern coast of the Black Sea. It is bounded to the west by the governments of Podolia and Bessarabia, to the north by Poltava and Kiev, and to the south by Taurida and Ekaterinoslav. The government covers an area of 27,497 square miles.

The landscape, particularly in the southern regions, consists of open steppe and is almost entirely devoid of forest. The territory is drained by several minor streams as well as the Ingul and the Bug. Its boundaries are marked by the Dnieper to the south-east and the Dniester to the west, with extensive lagoons stretching along the shoreline.

## Geography and Natural Resources
The region's principal minerals include salt, kaolin, and iron. In the government of Kherson, the town of Krivoy Rog serves as the center of a district rich in minerals. This area features Tertiary deposits overlying crystalline schists, from which graphite, slate, lithographic stone, brown coal, copper ores, and colors are obtained. Notably, nearly 2,000,000 tons of iron ore—containing 60% to 70% iron—are extracted annually.

## Economy and Agriculture
Agriculture is well developed in Kherson, with 9,000,000 acres (51.1% of the land) under crops. The use of agricultural machinery is extensive, and the sector has been significantly improved by approximately seventy German colonies. Common crops include hemp, flax, mustard, maize, and ordinary cereals. Tobacco is both grown and manufactured in the region. Fruit cultivation is also prominent, specifically the mulberry, apricot, peach, plum, and cherry. Additionally, the vine is widely grown, producing 1,220,000 gallons of wine each year.

Livestock farming is pursued on a large scale, including horse-breeding, cattle-breeding, and sheep-farming; some sheep farmers possess 30,000 to 40,000 merinos each. Fishing is another important occupation. Industrial activities include chemical works, steam flour-mills, sugar factories, iron-works, and the manufacture of machinery (especially agricultural machinery), leather, hemp, and wool.

Land ownership is distributed as follows:
* Peasants: Nearly 45% (who rent an additional 1,730,000 acres from landlords)
* Nobility: 31%
* Other classes: 12%
* Public institutions, municipalities, and the crown: 12%

## The City of Kherson and Regional Trade
The town of Kherson, the capital of the government, is situated on a hill above the right bank of the Dnieper, approximately 19 miles from the river's mouth. It was founded in 1778 by the courtier Potemkin as a seaport and naval station. By 1786, it had 10,000 inhabitants. Although the removal of naval establishments to Nikolayev in 1794 and the rise of Odessa slowed its progress, the town's population reached 73,185 by 1900.

The city is a regular, substantially built town. Its cathedral serves as the burial place of Potemkin, and the remains of the English philanthropist John Howard, who died in 1790, are located nearby. The town's name was derived from the belief that it was the site of the Greek city Chersonesus Heracleotica, founded by the Dorians of Heraclea.

Trade on the Dnieper at Kherson primarily involves hides, cattle, cereals, wool, and timber. Local industries include flour-milling, brewing, tallow-melting, soap-boiling, wool-dressing, and tobacco manufacture. Major commercial outlets for Russia include the ports of Odessa, Nikolayev, Ochakov, and Kherson. Other important inland traffic hubs include Tiraspol, Olviopol, Voznesenask, Elisavetgrad, Alexandriya, and Berislav.

## Demographics
The population of the Kherson government grew from 1,661,892 in 1871 to 2,744,040 in 1897 (of which 785,094 lived in towns and 1,332,175 were women). By 1906, the estimated population was 3,257,600. The population includes Germans (123,453), as well as Bohemians, Bulgarians, Greeks, Rumanians, and Great and Little Russians.

## Podolia and the Southern Bug
Podolia is a government that borders Kherson to the west and Kiev to the south-west. Historically, the city of Lublin served as the primary trade center for Volhynia, Red Russia, and Podolia during the rule of the Jagellon dynasty. In October 1676, the treaty of Zurawno between Turkey and Poland resulted in the Turks retaining Podolia, Kaminiec, and the greater part of the Ukraine.

The Southern Bug (sometimes simply called the Bug) is a river that rises in the south of the government of Volhynia. It flows generally south-east through the governments of Kherson and Podolia. At Nikolayev, it picks up the Ingul from the left before entering a lagoon (liman) where the Dnieper also discharges. The river is 470 meters long. While its upper section is characterized by rapids, its lower section is of little navigational value due to blocks of rock and numerous sandbanks.
