# Auxonne

Auxonne is a town located in eastern France within the department of Cote d'Or. It is situated 19 miles east-south-east of Dijon on the Paris-Lyon railway leading to Belfort. As of 1906, the population of the town was 2,766, while the population of the commune was 6,307.

## Geography and Layout
The town is described as quiet and is positioned in a wide plain on the left bank of the Saone river.

## Architecture and Landmarks
Auxonne retains several historical structures:
*   **Fortifications:** The town preserves remains of ramparts and a 16th-century stronghold characterized by cylindrical towers. It also features a sculptured gateway dating from the 15th century. In the latter half of the 17th century, Vauban restored these fortifications and constructed the arsenal, which currently serves as a market.
*   **Religious Buildings:** The church of Notre-Dame dates back to the 14th century. Its triple porch is surmounted by two towers, though only the southern tower is finished. Additionally, a third tower over the crossing is topped by a lofty spire.
*   **Civic and Residential Buildings:** The hotel de ville dates from the 15th century. The town also contains several houses from the Renaissance period of architectural interest.

## History and Commemoration
The town commemorates Napoleon I, who stayed in Auxonne from 1788 to 1791. A statue of him as a sub-lieutenant has been erected to mark these sojourns.

## Administration and Economy
Auxonne houses a communal college and a tribunal of commerce. While the town's industries are considered unimportant, it maintains a large trade in vegetables, which are produced by numerous market gardens located in the vicinity.
