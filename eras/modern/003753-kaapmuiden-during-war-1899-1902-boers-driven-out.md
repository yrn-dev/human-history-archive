# Kaapmuiden

## Geography and Infrastructure
Kaapmuiden is a location in South Africa that serves as a critical junction for the regional railway system. It is the point where the main Lourenco Marques-Pretoria trunk railway connects with a 35-mile branch line. This branch line runs northeast through a mountainous landscape to reach the town of Barberton.

## Relation to Barberton
Kaapmuiden's significance is closely linked to the nearby town of Barberton, located in the Transvaal. Barberton was established in 1886 following the discovery of gold in the Kaap valley. The town is situated 2,825 feet above sea level on the side of the De Kaap valley, beneath a prominent headland of the Drakensberg. 

Barberton is positioned 136 miles west-northwest of Delagoa Bay and 283 miles from Pretoria by rail (or 175 miles in a direct line). The town features several fine public buildings, which are primarily grouped around President Square. As of 1904, the population of Barberton was 2,433, of whom 1,214 were whites.

## The War of 1899-1902
During the war of 1899-1902, the Boers were driven out of Barberton on September 13, 1900, by General French (who later became Sir John French). 

This conflict was part of a broader three-year war between Great Britain and two independent republics established by Boers (Dutch settlers) in the Transvaal and the Orange River district. The war was enormously costly for the British people and concluded with the surrender of the two republics. In the aftermath, the former republics eventually became associates with Natal and Cape Colony in a Confederation of South African states as a self-governing republic under the British Crown in 1907.
