# Bahia

## Geography and Boundaries
Bahia is an Atlantic state located in Brazil. Its borders are defined by several neighboring states and the ocean: to the north, it is bounded by the states of Pernambuco, Sergipe, and Piauhy; to the east by the Atlantic and Sergipe; to the south by Minas Geraes and Espirito Santo; and to the west by Goyaz and Minas Geraes. The state encompasses an area of 164,650 square miles.

The terrain is characterized largely by a barren, arid plateau known as a *chapada*. This plateau has a general elevation ranging from 1,000 to 1,700 feet above the Sao Francisco river, or between 2,300 and 3,000 feet above sea level. On the western side, the *chapada* reaches an elevation of 2,300 feet and spans 60 miles in breadth, serving as the water-parting between the Tocantins and the Sao Francisco. The drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river traverses the state from the south toward the north and northeast.

## Regional Divisions
East of the Sao Francisco river, the state is divided into three distinct regions:
*   **Limestone Plateau:** A rough area that rises gradually toward the culminating ridges of the Serra da Chapada.
*   **Gneissose Plateau:** An area featuring extensive exposures of bare rock that dip slightly toward the coast.
*   **Sandy Plateau:** A narrower region covered with compact sandy soil that descends toward the coastal plain.

The first two regions, each approximately 200 miles wide, are inhospitable, barren, and arid, except for the dividing ridges where sea clouds provide some moisture. The third zone becomes less arid and more vegetated as it nears the coast. The coastal plain itself varies in character, appearing in some areas as swampy, low, and sandy—filled with canals and lagoons—while other areas are rolling, elevated, and very fertile.

## Climate and Industry
The climate of Bahia mirrors its surface features. The interior is hot and dry, while the coastal regions are hot and humid, and in some instances, unhealthy.

Historically, cattle-raising was the primary industry in the interior. However, this has been nearly extinguished due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts, which were exacerbated by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve grass. In the agricultural regions, the state produces coffee, tobacco, cacao, sugar, tropical fruits, cotton, and mandioca. Other exports from the state include rum, diamonds, cabinet woods, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, and hides.

## Population
As of 1890, the population of Bahia numbered 1,919,802. The population is described as being largely of a mixed and unprogressive character.
