# Geography and Geopolitics of Africa

## Physical Geography and Climate
Certain regions of Africa are characterized by a notable absence of desert country, benefiting instead from abundant rainfall. In these areas, the average annual rainfall is approximately 50 inches, a figure mirrored in the Shire Highlands of British Central Africa, a region that currently attracts a high number of European settlers. Rainfall levels vary by topography; in the vicinity of the highest mountains, averages may reach 75 inches, and in parts of Mount Mlanje, they may frequently reach 100 inches per year. Conversely, the driest areas are found in the lower Shiré district and the land to the north-east and east of Lake Mweru, where annual rainfall likely does not exceed an average of 35 inches.

From a geological perspective, evidence suggests that the south of Africa was once part of a great land mass that likely extended across the Indian Ocean. This land mass was united with a region including the peninsula of India, which has been land since the Carboniferous period, as indicated by the presence of terrestrial and freshwater deposits of the Gondwana series and a lack of marine fossils except near its borders.

## Linguistic Groups
The African continent features diverse language families. One prominent group is the Bantu, which extends from a short distance north of the equator to the southern extremity of the continent. Other linguistic complexes exist across the center of the continent.

In Portuguese East Africa, specifically away from the Lake Nyasa watershed, several language genera are present. The Yao genus stretches from behind the coast of the Lindi settlements in German East Africa, south-westward across the Ruvuma river to the north-east shores of Lake Nyasa, and further to the region east of the Shire river between Lake Nyasa, the Shire highlands, and Mt. Mlanje. The Yao language expanded its territory south of Lake Nyasa starting in the middle of the 19th century, though Nyanja-speaking colonies remain within its domain. To the east of the Yao domain is the Makua genus, which includes dialects such as Anguru, I-lomwe, I-medo, and I-tugulu, as well as unnamed dialects in the Angoji coast-region. The Angoji region also contains strong colonies of Swahili-speaking people, while the Ci-cuambo occupy the southern part of the Makua domain in the Quelimane district.

## Colonial Perspectives and Geopolitics
Different European powers have held contrasting views on the administration and strategic use of Africa. British policy has been characterized by a tendency to study the interests of natives and regard their point of view with favor, often to the disappointment of white settlers. This approach has avoided the militarization of natives, with British statesmen relying on long African experience to implement a policy regarded as beneficial to the gradual civilization of the natives.

In contrast, German strategic thought has viewed Africa through the lens of world power. Franz Kolbe described a vision where Germany would possess the entire West African coast from the mouth of the Orange River to the mouth of the Cross River. Such fortification would be significant for Germany and England; if the Turks were to close the Suez Canal to England, all traffic to South Africa, Australia, and India would be forced around the Cape of Good Hope, passing the coast of German Central Africa. This would compel England to station a considerable fleet in South Africa to protect trade, thereby weakening English forces in European waters. Additionally, German East Africa was viewed as a means to achieve a balance of power in the East.

## Resources and Trade
While there are believed to be large deposits of oil in West Africa, the regions of Africa where manufacture is currently progressing—specifically Egypt and South Africa—rely on imported liquid fuel. These supplies are sourced from oil-fields in Texas and Borneo. Fuel is transported from Borneo to Cape Town via Singapore, and from Texas to Alexandria via the Mediterranean.

## Historical Influence
Africa has played a role in the movement of populations and power between continents. The Carthaginians are described as Phoenicians who transplanted themselves to Africa. Later, the Turks and Arabs were able to successfully establish footings in Europe because they first conquered the parts of Africa and Asia that adjoined Europe, ensuring the invaders remained in touch with Asiatic settlements.
