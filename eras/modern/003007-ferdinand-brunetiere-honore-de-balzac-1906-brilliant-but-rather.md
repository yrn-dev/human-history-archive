# Honore de Balzac (1906) by Ferdinand Brunetiere

## Overview
*Honore de Balzac* is a study of the French novelist Honore de Balzac, published in 1906 by the critic Ferdinand Brunetiere. Within the broader context of Balzac scholarship, it is categorized as one of the two most important studies of the author written in the French language. Brunetiere's work is characterized as being brilliant, although it is also described as rather one-sided.

## Context of Balzac Scholarship
The study by Brunetiere emerged during a period of extensive critical activity regarding Balzac's life and work. Early assessments of the novelist were often influenced by personal relationships; for instance, the critic Sainte-Beuve and Balzac had a falling out, leading to a "furious diatribe" from the novelist and a postmortem examination by Sainte-Beuve in *Causeries du lundi* that was not cordial. Conversely, Gautier, a trusty and intimate friend, produced a personal study reprinted in *Portraits contemporains*. Other contemporaries such as Champfleury, Lemer, and Gozlan also provided contributions.

The transition toward more objective, comprehensive studies began with Taine's 1858 Essay (reprinted in 1865), which was based on the totality of Balzac's work rather than personal bias. However, full study of Balzac's man and work was only made possible after the completion of the *Edition definitive*, which reunited his scattered originals and *Correspondance*.

## Related Critical Works
Brunetiere's 1906 volume is part of a lineage of critical examinations that followed the *Edition definitive*. Other notable works include:
* **L'Oeuvre de Balzac (1890):** Written by M. Marcel Barriere as an official critical introduction to the *Edition definitive*, though it focuses heavily on elaborate analyses of individual books.
* **Essais sur Balzac (1893-1894):** Two volumes by M. Paul Flat that focus specifically on tracing types of character.
* **Honore de Balzac (1897):** A work by M. Edmond Bire that provided new biographical details, including the correct spelling of the author's name.
* **Balzac ignore (1899):** A study by A. Cabanes focusing on Balzac's interest in occult studies.
* **La Jeunesse de Balzac (1903):** A work by MM. Hanotaux and Vicaire; its first part, *Balzac imprimeur*, examines Balzac's intimacy with Madame de Berny and his involvement in the printing business.
* **Balzac, l'homme et l'oeuvre (1905):** A well-informed and critical examination by M. A. Le Breton, described as being somewhat severe.

## The Subject of the Study
The subject of Brunetiere's analysis, Honore de Balzac (1799-1850), was a novelist born in Tours. His literary career effectively began in 1829 with the publication of *Physiologie du mariage* and *Les Chouans* (originally titled *Le Dernier Chouan*). Balzac is noted for a unique blending of "observation and imagination," a characterization delivered by Victor Hugo in his funeral discourse. 

His vast production, which he termed the "Comedy," was often treated as a single entity in his mind, leading to constant reshaping and re-connecting of different parts. This body of work includes the *Comedie humaine* (first issued in sixteen volumes between 1842 and 1846) and various miscellaneous works and correspondence.
