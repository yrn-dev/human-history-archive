# Census and Population Estimates

The practice of conducting censuses and population estimates varies widely across different regions, eras, and subjects, ranging from the enumeration of human populations and urban growth to the counting of biological species and livestock.

## National Population Censuses
Population data for various nations often fluctuate between official census returns and subsequent estimates.

### Bolivia
In 1900, a roughly-taken census of Bolivia recorded a total population of 1,816,271, which included the Litoral department (49,820), though the Litoral now belongs to Chile. This census estimated 91,000 wild Indians in forest regions. The demographic breakdown was 50.7% Indians, 26.8% *mestizos* (a mixture of Indians and Caucasians of European origin), 12.8% whites, 9.4% unknown, and 0.3% negroes, who are descendants of colonial slaves. By 1904, an official estimate increased the population to 2,181,415, including the Litoral (59,784). The Indian population, totaling 920,860, consists largely of the "civilized tribes" of the Andes—primarily Aymaras south of Lake Titicaca—and those of the Chiquitos and Mojos regions, who were organized into industrial communities by Jesuits in the 17th century. Other groups include the Chiriguanos, a detached tribe of the Guarani race in the southern Bolivian plain.

### China
The population of China has never been exactly ascertained. In A.D. 156, the total population was returned as slightly over fifty millions. More modern census methods involve serving house-tickets to the head of every household; however, because there is no fixed return date, results remain approximate. In 1842, Sacharoff estimated the population at over four hundred millions, and a 1902 census reportedly yielded four hundred and ten millions, though some suggest three hundred millions is a more just estimate.

### Korea
The first complete census in Korea was taken in 1897, returning a population of 17,000,000 (with females in the majority), though it was later estimated at a maximum of 12,000,000. The population is described as absolutely homogeneous. There is a foreign population of approximately 65,000, of whom 60,000 are Japanese.

### Other Regions
In a country where the capital is Tegucigalpa, the first census in 1791 recorded 95,500 people. By 1905, an official estimate placed the aggregate population at 500,136, though a satisfactory census is difficult because Indians and "ignorant masses" avoid the process due to associations with taxation or military conscription.

## Urban and Regional Growth
Censuses are also used to track the rapid expansion of cities. Buenos Aires, for example, had a population of 177,767 during its first authentic census in 1869. By 1887, it was estimated at 404,000 (excluding the annexed suburbs of Flores and Belgrano, which had 28,000 people). A national census in 1895 recorded 663,854 residents, and a 1904 municipal census increased this to 950,891. By the end of 1905, the national statistical office estimated the population at 1,025,653, representing about one-fifth of the republic's total population.

## Historical and Biblical Censuses
Ancient records and religious texts provide various accounts of population counts.

### Ancient Rome and Greece
The census was a significant institution in Rome. Niebuhr rejected the census of Servius Tullius as unhistoric, arguing that the census included all confederate cities with the right of Isopolity. In Athens, the number of citizens has been a subject of study by Bœckh and Fynes Clinton.

### Biblical Accounts
The Gospel of St Luke records that the birth of Christ occurred during a general census of the empire ordered by Augustus, conducted when Quirinius was governor of Syria. Some historians argue this is a confused recollection of a later census in A.D. 6, while others, such as Sir W. M. Ramsay, suggest it refers to a periodic fourteen-year census.

Additionally, records regarding King David indicate he took a census of his dominions, which stretched from the Euphrates to the Mediterranean and from Lebanon to the frontiers of Egypt. Different sources provide different numbers: the lowest figures cite 800,000 men fit to bear arms in Israel and 500,000 in Judah. Other accounts suggest a muster of 1,300,000 or 1,574,000 fighting men, which could imply a total population of thirteen millions.

## Non-Human Censuses
Census methods are also applied to biology and agriculture.

### Fungi
Accurate censuses of fungi are difficult due to insufficient data. In 1889, Saccardo estimated 32,000 species, including 8,500 *Fungi imperfecti*, 650 Schizomycetes, and 400 Myxomycetes. In 1892, Schroeter estimated 20,000 species. Other estimates vary wildly, including De Bary's 1872 estimate of 150,000 species, Cooke's 1895 estimate of 40,000, and Massee's 1899 estimate of over 50,000.

### Livestock
In the United States, cows were not censused until 1840. By the close of the 19th century, it was estimated there was one milch cow for every four persons, totaling approximately 17,500,000 cows. These were concentrated in dairy states, with Iowa leading with 1,500,000 cows.
