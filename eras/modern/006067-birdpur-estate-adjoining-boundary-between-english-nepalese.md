# Birdpur Estate

The Birdpur estate is a location situated adjoining the boundary between Nepalese and English territory. It has gained historical and archaeological significance due to discoveries that provide insight into the early period of the region and the activities of the Sakiya clan.

## Archaeological Discoveries
A pre-eminent discovery made on the Birdpur estate by Mr. William Peppé is a stupa, also referred to as a cairn. This structure was erected by the Sakiya clan to house their portion of the ashes from the cremation pyre of the Buddha. 

In the vicinity of this site, other significant markers have been identified:
* **Lumbini Garden Pillar:** Approximately 12 meters to the north-east of the stupa, an inscribed pillar was found. This pillar was placed by Asoka to record his visit to the Lumbini Garden, identifying it as the place where the future Buddha was born. Although the inscription dates to more than two centuries after the birth it commemorates, it serves as strong evidence for the location of the garden.
* **Konagamana Pillar:** To the north-west of the garden site, another Asoka pillar was discovered. This pillar records Asoka's visit to a cairn erected by the Sakyas over the remains of Konagamana. Konagamana is described as one of the previous teachers or Buddhas, and Gotama the Buddha had claimed to be a follower of his.

## The Sakiya Republic
These archaeological finds have allowed researchers to determine the district occupied by the Sakiya republic during the 6th and 7th centuries B.C. While the exact boundaries of the republic are unknown, it is estimated that the clan spread 30 miles or more southwards over the plains and 30 miles or more along the lower slopes of the Himalayas.

## Environmental History
The region has been characterized as abandoned jungle since the 3rd century A.D., or possibly earlier. Because of this state of abandonment, the numerous ruined sites throughout the district have remained undisturbed, leading researchers to believe that further discoveries may be expected in the area.
