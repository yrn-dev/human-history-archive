# Industrial and Demographic Trends (1890–1910)

The turn of the 20th century was marked by significant demographic shifts and industrial expansion across various regions, characterized by rapid population growth in urban centers and a diversification of manufacturing outputs.

## Urban Population and Demographics
Population growth was a prominent feature of the era. In one city, the population rose steadily from approximately 2,000 following the first census enumeration in 1820 to 352,387 by 1900, further increasing by 20% to reach 423,715 in 1910. As of 1900, this city's population included 248,135 native-born and 104,252 foreign-born residents. The racial composition in 1900 consisted of 350,586 white individuals and 1,801 coloured persons, of whom 1,698 were negroes. Among the native-born whites, 155,716 had at least one foreign-born parent, and 93,256 members of the total population were of unmixed German parentage. Foreign-born residents included 36,720 Germans, with other significant groups being Polish, Canadian, Irish, and other British. Immigrants from Italy, Hungary, and Poland occupied various sections of the city's poorer areas.

Other municipalities saw similar growth. Danville, Illinois, grew from a population of 11,491 in 1890 to 16,354 in 1900 (including 1,435 foreign-born) and reached 27,871 by 1910. Bellaire, Ohio, had a population of 9,934 in 1890, 9,912 in 1900 (with 1,159 foreign-born), and 12,946 in 1910. Cortland, New York, grew from 8,590 in 1890 to 9,014 in 1900, 11,272 in 1905, and 11,504 by 1910.

On a state level, Idaho's population increased from 14,999 in 1870 to 32,610 in 1880, 88,548 in 1890, 161,772 in 1900 (an 82.7% increase), and 325,594 in 1910 (a 101.3% increase). In 1900, 15.2% of Idaho's inhabitants were foreign-born, including 3,943 from England, 2,974 from Germany, 2,822 from Sweden, 2,528 Canadian English, and 1,633 from Ireland. The coloured population in Idaho in 1900 was 4.5%, consisting of 4,226 Indians, 1,467 Chinese, 1,291 Japanese, and 293 negroes.

## Manufacturing and Industrial Value
Manufacturing interests expanded greatly during the final decades of the 19th century. In one state, capital invested, the cost of materials, and the value of output all increased between 1890 and 1900, with output value reaching $121,181,683 in 1900. The value of factory products in this state rose from $111,397,919 in 1900 to $186,379,592 in 1905. About two-thirds of the manufacturing value in 1900 and 1905 was derived from rice cleaning and polishing, cotton-seed oil and cake, lumber and timber products, and sugar and molasses refining.

In Kentucky, the total value of manufactured products rose from $126,719,857 in 1890 to $154,166,365 in 1900. Between 1900 and 1905, factory-made products alone increased from $126,508,660 to $159,753,968. Flour and grist mill products became the top-ranked product by value in 1905 ($18,007,786), while chewing and smoking tobacco and snuff fell from first to third rank. Other significant Kentucky manufactures in 1900 and 1905 included:
* Lumber and timber products: $13,338,533 (1900) to $14,539,000 (1905)
* Iron and steel: $5,004,572 (1900) to $6,167,542 (1905)
* Packed meats: $5,177,167 (1900) to $5,693,731 (1905)
* Men's clothing: $3,420,365 (1900) to $6,279,078 (1905)

Birmingham served as a principal manufacturing center in its state, particularly for iron production in the southern states. Between 1890 and 1900, the value of Birmingham's manufactory products increased 78.9%, from $7,064,248 to $12,581,066. However, products from establishments under the "factory system" saw a decrease of 11.7% between 1900 ($8,599,418) and 1905 ($7,592,958).

## Localized Industry and Infrastructure
Various cities developed specialized industrial bases. Danville, Illinois, utilized its proximity to the Grape Creek coalfield for a large coal trade and manufactured bricks, foundry products, glass, flour, hominy, and railway cars. Its factory product value increased by 72.7% between 1900 and 1905, reaching $3,304,120. Bellaire, Ohio, acted as a shipping center for the Belmont county coalfield and produced glass, iron and steel, stoves, agricultural implements, and enamelled and galvanized ware. Its factory product value rose from $8,837,646 in 1900 to $10,712,438 in 1905. Cortland, New York, produced clothing, wall-paper, iron and steel, cabs, carriages, wagons, and wire-cloth and netting. Its factory product value increased 49.3% from $3,063,828 in 1900 to $4,574,191 in 1905.

## Agriculture and Land
In southern Californian fruit country, land prices remained high, attracting well-to-do immigrants. Between 1900 and 1910, the assessed valuation of property in one such city increased more than threefold, reaching $276,801,517 by 1910. 

Agricultural data from 1892 to 1901 shows fluctuating quantities of fruit. For example, apple quantities peaked in 1896 at 6,177 thousand cwts before dropping to 2,129 thousand cwts in 1900. Grapes showed a general increase in quantity from 762 thousand cwts in 1892 to 1,158 thousand cwts in 1899, though they fell to 593 thousand cwts in 1900. By 1908, the United Kingdom spent £4,195,654 on imported raw fruits that could be produced domestically.
