# Banda

## Geography and Topography
Banda is a town and district located in the Allahabad division of the United Provinces in British India. The town is situated near the right bank of the river Ken, 95 miles southwest of Allahabad. The district covers an area of 3,061 square miles and is characterized as the most backward and barren portion of the province.

The landscape of the district is varied. Some areas consist of elevated plains and irregular uplands featuring detached granite rocks, while other regions are marshy lowlands that frequently remain underwater during the rainy season. The country sloping toward the bank of the Jumna is marked by numerous ravines. To the southeast, the district is naturally bounded by a low range of hills, not exceeding 500 feet in height, where the Vindhya chain originates.

## Hydrography and Transport
The principal river in the district is the Jumna, which flows from the northwest to the southeast. It serves as the boundary of the district along its northeast edge for a distance of 125 miles.

The district is served by two railway lines. The East Indian main line to Jubbulpore crosses the district, and a branch railway from Manikpur to Jhansi traverses its length.

## Population and Demographics
In 1901, the population of the town of Banda was 22,565. The total population of the district in 1901 was 631,058. This figure represented a decrease of 11% over the preceding decade, a decline attributed to the effects of famine.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The district's black soil supports the production of several crops, most notably millet, pulse, rice, cotton, oil-seeds, and other food-grains. Cotton from Banda is held in high repute within the market.

The town of Banda contains 168 Hindu temples and 65 mosques. While it previously served as a military cantonment, it no longer holds that status.
