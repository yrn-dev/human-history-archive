# Religious Demographics and Institutions in Budapest and Europe

## Religious Composition of Budapest
In the city of Budapest, religious affiliation is diverse. According to recorded data, the largest religious group consists of 445,023 Roman Catholics. Other Christian denominations include 5,806 Greek Catholics and 4,422 Greek Orthodox. Protestantism is represented by 67,319 individuals of the Helvetic Confessions and 38,811 of the Augsburg Confessions. Additionally, the population includes 168,985 Jews, with the remaining inhabitants belonging to various other creeds.

## Religious and Educational Institutions in Germany and Belgium
In Germany, the two primary religions are the Roman Catholic and the Evangelical (Lutheran), comprising 39% and 58% of the population respectively. These proportions vary due to migratory habits, with Roman Catholics increasing in Württemburg, Saxony, Bavaria, and Prussia, while Evangelicals increase in other districts and large cities. In Baden, the government manages public education, which includes two universities: a Roman Catholic university at Freiburg-im-Breisgau and a Protestant university at Heidelberg. The Roman Catholic archbishop of Freiburg serves as the metropolitan of the Upper Rhine.

In Belgium, the constitution ensures absolute liberty of conscience and establishes no state religion. Despite this, the population is predominantly Roman Catholic. Estimates indicate there are 5,000 Jews and 10,000 Protestants (half of whom are English), while the rest are Catholics. The Belgian government provided nearly 7,000,000 francs in 1904 to support the benevolent institutions and religious establishments of the Roman Church, while other cults received 118,000 francs. The hierarchy of the Roman Church in Belgium includes the bishops of Namur, Tournai, Bruges, Ghent, and Liege, as well as the archbishop of Malines.
