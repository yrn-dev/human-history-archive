# British East Africa and Uganda

British East Africa is a term used in its broadest sense to describe all territories under British influence on the eastern side of Africa. This region is situated between Abyssinia and the Anglo-Egyptian Sudan to the north and German East Africa to the south. It encompasses the protectorates of Uganda, Zanzibar, and East Africa.

## Geography and Topography
The continental area of the region consists primarily of the great plateau of East Africa. Aside from a narrow belt of coastline, the elevation rarely falls below 2,000 feet, with some extensive sections reaching heights of 6,000 to 8,000 feet. The landscape transitions from coastal lowlands through a series of steps and intervening plateaus to a broad zone of high ground characterized by abundant volcanic action.

This upland is marked by the eastern "rift-valley," created by the subsidence of its floor and containing lakes without outlets. To the west, a basin of lower elevation is partially occupied by Victoria Nyanza, which drains north toward the Nile. Further inland, the terrain rises again to a second volcanic belt, which culminates in the Ruwenzori range.

The East Africa protectorate specifically covers an area of approximately 240,000 square miles. Its southern frontier is coterminous with German East Africa, running northwest from the mouth of the Umba river to Victoria Nyanza, though it deviates to ensure Mount Kilimanjaro remains entirely within German territory. Its eastern boundary is the Indian Ocean, with a coastline of about 400 miles. It is bounded by Italian Somaliland and Abyssinia to the north and Uganda to the west.

## Provinces and Urban Centers
The East Africa protectorate is divided into several provinces, each with its own capital:
*   **Seyyidie:** The south coast province, with its capital at Mombasa.
*   **Ukamba:** Located in the center of the protectorate, with its capital at Nairobi.
*   **Kenya:** The district of Mt. Kenya, with its capital at Fort Hall.
*   **Tanaland:** Located north of Seyyidie and Ukamba, with its capital at Lamu.
*   **Jubaland:** The northern region, with its capital at Kismayu.
*   **Naivasha:** Capital at Naivasha.
*   **Kisumu:** Capital at Kisumu.

Naivasha and Kisumu, both adjoining Victoria Nyanza, were originally part of the eastern province of Uganda before being transferred to the East Africa protectorate on April 1, 1902.

Mombasa serves as the chief port, with a population of approximately 30,000. Its harbor, Kilindini, located on the southwest side of Mombasa island, is the terminus of the Uganda railway. Nearby on the mainland is Freretown (known natively as Kisaoni), a settlement for freed slaves. Lamu, located 150 miles northeast of Mombasa, is an ancient settlement and the headquarters of the coast Arabs, containing Portuguese ruins and a buried Arab city. Other notable coastal towns include Patta, Kipini, Malindi—where Vasco da Gama erected a pillar in 1498—and Kismayu, the northernmost port and a vital harbor of refuge.

Nairobi, located 327 miles from Mombasa, was selected in 1907 as the administrative capital of the protectorate.

## Population and Infrastructure
The population of the East Africa protectorate is estimated to be between 2,000,000 and 4,000,000 people. This includes approximately 25,000 Indians and 3,000 Europeans, the latter of whom include several hundred Boer families and many emigrants from South Africa.

Transportation is facilitated by the Uganda railway, which runs from Mombasa on the Indian Ocean to Victoria Nyanza. This railway has spurred the growth of flourishing towns along its route. Regarding broader regional communications, travel is possible from Alexandria by rail and river to Khartoum, and then by steamer up the Nile to Rejaf near the Uganda border. From Rejaf to Nimule, a distance of 150 miles, the Nile is unsuitable for river transport, necessitating the construction of a railway. From Nimule, the river is navigable again up to Lake Albert.

## Missionary Activity and Religion
In Uganda, Christianity was disseminated by British Anglican and French Catholic missionaries. This introduction of European civilization was marked by intense doctrinal disputes; in Mengo, the capital of Uganda, conflicts between "Protestants" and "Catholics" resulted in deaths.

In the East Africa protectorate, the Church Missionary Society is headquartered at Freretown. This location serves as the residence for the bishop of the diocese of Mombasa and includes a mission house and a church.
