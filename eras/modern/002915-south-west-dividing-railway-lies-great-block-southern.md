# Southern Baluchistan

Southern Baluchistan is a large block of territory located south-west of the dividing railway. This railway, along with its associated plains, passes, and the dominant hills surrounding Quetta, serves as a geographical divider that splits Baluchistan into two distinct parts.

## Geography and Demographics
The region south of the railway consists of a square block of territory measuring roughly 300 m. by 300 m. It is primarily the home of the Baluch and the Brahui. This area is characterized by a diverse range of environments, containing representatives of half of the great races of Asia and almost every conceivable phase of climate.

## Hydrography and Drainage
Within Southern Baluchistan, drainage generally trends toward the west and south, flowing either into the Arabian Sea or into the central swamps of Mashkel and Lora. Several considerable streams serve as important arteries for internal communication:
* **The Hab river**: Forms the boundary to the west of Karachi.
* **The Purali (ancient Arabus)**: Drains the low-lying flats of Las Bela.
* **The Hingol (ancient Tomerus) and the Dasht**: Both drain the Makran region.

An exception to this general drainage pattern is the Mulla, which carries floods from the Kalat highlands into the Gandava basin. This river constitutes one of the most important ancient highways leading from the Indus plains to Kandahar.

## The Kalat Region
The fortress of Kalat is situated approximately 6800 ft. above sea-level on the western edge of a cultivated plain. It is located midway between the sources of the Mulla and the Bolan, near a small tributary of the Lora (the river of Quetta and Pishin). Kalat acts as a "hub" or center from which the southern Lora affluents, the Mulla, and the Bolan radiate.

The surrounding landscape of Kalat includes:
* **To the north**: The Sarawan ridges, which enclose narrow fertile valleys and extend south-west toward the edge of the Kharan desert.
* **To the east and south**: The rugged bands of Jalawan. The Mulla rises among these bands and reaches the Gandava plain by breaking through a series of magnificent defiles.

Routes converging on Kalat from the south typically follow the general strike of denuded hill ridges, running from valley to valley through narrow wooded valleys with easy grades.

## Regional Systems and Borders
Sixty miles south of Kalat, beyond the sources of the Mulla, begins another remarkable hydrographic system encompassing all of south-western and southern Baluchistan. To the west of this system lies the Kharan desert, characterized by intermittent river channels that are often lost.

Additionally, the elevated highlands of the Kalat plateau, known as Jalawan, feature drainage that cuts deep gorges into the hills before passing eastwards into the plains of Sind. In areas beyond and south of the Jalawan hydrographical area, streams either curve westwards to disappear into prominent inland swamps or run in long straight lines to the Arabian Sea, north of Karachi. A narrow section of the coast districts discharges water directly into the Arabian Sea.
