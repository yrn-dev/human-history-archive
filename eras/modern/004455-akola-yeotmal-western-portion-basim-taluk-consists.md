# Basim

## Administrative History
Basim is a town located in the Akola district of Berar, India. It is situated 52 miles south-south-east of the Akola station of the Great Indian Peninsula railway. Until the year 1905, Basim served as the headquarters for the district of Basim, which encompassed an area of 2,949 square miles. In 1905, this district was abolished, and its component *taluks* were divided between the districts of Yeotmal and Akola.

## Geography and Climate
The Basim *taluk*, which forms the western portion of the former district, is characterized by a fertile tableland approximately 1,000 feet above sea level. This land slopes downward toward the south and west into the rich valley of the Penganga. In contrast, the eastern portion of the former district, consisting of the *taluks* of Pasud and Mangrul, is primarily composed of a succession of low hills covered with poor grass. However, the Pasud *taluk* contains wide stretches of woodland and peaks that reach heights of 2,000 feet, offering scenery that is described as very beautiful, particularly during the rainy season.

The climate in this locality is noted as being superior to that of other districts in Berar. During the summer months, the hot winds that blow throughout the day are succeeded by a cool breeze at night.

## Economy and Industry
The region's primary agricultural output consists of oilseeds, cotton, pulse, wheat, millet, and other food grains. Industrial activity in the town of Basim includes ginning factories and the manufacture of blankets and cotton-cloth.

## Population
As of 1901, the population of the town of Basim was 13,823. The population of the broader district in 1901 was 353,410. This figure represented a decrease of 11% over the preceding decade, a decline attributed to the famine of 1899-1900, which was severely felt within the district.
