# Johann Christian Bach and the Milan Cathedral

## Early Life and Education
Johann Christian Bach (1735–1782) was the eleventh son of his father, born in Leipzig. Following his father's death in 1750, he moved to Berlin to become a pupil of his brother, Emanuel. In 1754, Bach traveled to Italy to further his musical education, where he studied under Padre Martini.

## Tenure at Milan Cathedral
Between 1760 and 1762, Johann Christian Bach held the position of organist at the Milan cathedral. During his tenure in this post, he composed several works, including a *Te Deum*, a *Requiem*, and two Masses.

## Career in London
By 1762, Bach had established a reputation as a composer of opera, which led to an invitation to London. He spent the remainder of his life in England, where he became the most popular musician for a period of twenty years. His dramatic works were produced at the King's theatre and were received with great cordiality. 

During his time in London, Bach was appointed as the music-master to the queen. He also partnered with Abel to give concerts at the Hanover Square rooms, which became highly fashionable public entertainments.

## Musical Legacy and Style
Johann Christian Bach is of historical significance as the first composer to prefer the pianoforte over older keyed instruments. While his works are described as pleasing and elegant, they were ephemeral in character and have since been deservedly forgotten. 

Detailed accounts of his career and compositions can be found in the fourth volume of Burney's *History of Music* and in a catalogue by Max Schwarz published in the *Sammelbaende* of the *Internationale Musik-Gesellschaft* (Jhrg. ii. p. 401).
