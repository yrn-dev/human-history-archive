# Baltimore

## Overview and Geography
Baltimore is a city and seaport serving as the metropolis of Maryland in the United States. Geographically, it is situated at the head of tide-water on the Patapsco river and its north-west and middle branches, which form an estuary 12 miles from the entrance of their waters into Chesapeake Bay. The city is located at latitude 39deg 17' N. and longitude 76deg 37' W.

In terms of distance from other major locations, Baltimore is approximately 172 miles by water from the Atlantic Ocean. By rail, it is 40 miles north-west of Washington and 26 miles north-by-west of Annapolis. It is also located 97 miles south-west of Philadelphia and 184 miles from New York.

## Population and Demographics
Baltimore is the 7th city in population in the United States. Census data from the late 19th and early 20th centuries shows a steady increase in its population:
*   **1890:** 434,439
*   **1900:** 508,957
*   **1910:** 558,485

As of 1900, the population included 79,258 negroes and 68,600 foreign-born residents. Among the foreign-born population, the largest group was natives of Germany (33,208), followed by those from Russia (10,493), Ireland (9,690), England (2,841), Poland (2,811), Bohemia (2,321), and Italy (2,042).

## Transportation and Trade
The city is a significant hub for both rail and maritime transport. It is served by several railway lines, including:
*   The Baltimore & Ohio
*   The Philadelphia, Baltimore & Washington (the Pennsylvania system)
*   The Baltimore & Annapolis Short Line
*   The Baltimore, Chesapeake & Atlantic
*   The Northern Central
*   The Western Maryland
*   The Maryland & Pennsylvania railways

Additionally, Baltimore is connected by steamship lines that run directly to the more important ports on the Atlantic coast of the United States, as well as to Brazil and the West Indies. Its international maritime reach extends to various European ports, including London, Liverpool, Southampton, Bristol, Leith, Glasgow, Dublin, Belfast, Havre, Antwerp, Rotterdam, Bremen, and Hamburg.
