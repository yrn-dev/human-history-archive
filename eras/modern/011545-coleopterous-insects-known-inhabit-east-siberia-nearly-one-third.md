# Coleopterous Insects of East Siberia

## Distribution and Regional Affinities
The distribution of coleopterous insects in east Siberia exhibits a significant connection to western Europe. Nearly one-third of the coleopterous insects known to inhabit east Siberia are also found in western Europe. 

Within the broader geographical context of Asia, insect groups are categorized by regional similarities. The insects of southern Asia—which includes China, Siam, the Malayan Islands, and India south of the Himalaya—form one group. In this southern group, species and genera are often identical on opposite sides of the Bay of Bengal. Japan is categorized as part of the Europeo-Asiatic group.

## Geographic Boundaries of Insect Types
The distribution of these insect forms is generally divided by latitude. European forms appear to extend south to approximately 30 deg. N. South of this line, Indo-Malayan types are encountered. Conversely, northern forms generally extend from the Levant to the Caspian, and along the south coast of the Mediterranean up to the border of the great desert.

## General Entomological Context
The study of insects, known as entomology, treats animals included in the class Hexapoda of the phylum (or sub-phylum) Arthropoda. Beetles, referred to as Scarabaei, are among the insects that have been the subject of ancient study, appearing in some of the oldest sculptured works of the Egyptians.
