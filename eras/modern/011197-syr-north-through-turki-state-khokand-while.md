# The Syr-Darya and the Khanate of Khokand

## Geography and Hydrology
The Syr, also known as the Jaxartes or Syr-darya, is a river that flows to the north from the Pamir region. The Pamir is located at the north-western extremity of the elevated Tibeto-Himalayan mountain plateau, situated approximately at 39 deg. N. and 73 deg. E. This region possesses the characteristics of the highest regions of Tibet and is referred to by the Russians as a steppe. While the Oxus (or Amu) flows west from this plateau, the Syr-darya flows north through the Turki state of Khokand.

## The Khanate of Khokand
The khanate of Khokand was a powerful state that emerged in the 18th century. While its early history is not well documented, the town of Khokand was founded in 1732 by Abd-ur-Rahim under the name Kali-i-Rahimbai or Iski-kurgan, though this name likely referred specifically to the fort. Arab travelers from the 10th century had previously mentioned a location called Hokand or Hovakend, which has been identified with the site of Khokand.

In the late 18th century, specifically 1758-1759, the Chinese conquered East Turkestan and Dzungaria, leading the rulers (begs) of Ferghana to recognize Chinese suzerainty. By 1807 or 1808, Alim, the son of Narbuta, consolidated authority over all the begs of Ferghana and captured Chimkent and Tashkent. However, Alim was unsuccessful in his attacks on the Bokharan fortress of Ura-tyube and was killed in 1817 by supporters of his brother, Omar.

## Expansion and Conflict
Omar, a patron of learning and a poet, expanded the kingdom by taking the sacred town of Azret (Turkestan). To defend Ferghana against raids by nomad Kirghiz, Omar constructed fortresses on the Syr-darya. These fortifications subsequently served as bases for the Khokand people to raid into Kirghiz land, sparking a conflict with Russia.

Following Omar's death in 1822, the khanate experienced forty-five years of continuous warfare between the nomad Kipchaks and the settled Sarts. During this period, Khokand occasionally fell under the suzerainty of Bokhara. From 1858 to 1866, Bokhara supported Khudayar-khan, the representative of the Kipchak party. Conversely, Alim-kul represented the Sarts and led a *gazawat* (Holy War) proclaimed in 1860, fighting the Russians until his death at Tashkent in 1865.

## Russian Conquest and Administration
Starting after 1847, the Russians engaged in several petty wars to destroy Khokand's forts and secure the Syr-darya region and the Ili (and thus Dzungaria). In 1866, following the occupation of Jizakh and Ura-tyube, the khanate of Khokand was separated from Bokhara.

The town of Khojent, located in the province of Syr-darya on the left bank of the Syr-darya (Jaxartes), served as a critical point of contention between Bokhara and Khokand. Historically, all commerce between the two khanates passed through Khojent. The town was stormed by the Russians in 1866 and played a significant role in the war with Khokand in 1875.

## Urban Centers and Economy
The town of Khokand, located in the province of Ferghana, is situated 20 miles south of the Syr-darya. As of 1900, it had a population of 86,704 and sat at an altitude of 1375 feet. The town is the center of a fertile irrigated oasis and features a citadel enclosed by a wall nearly 12 miles in circuit, alongside broad streets, large squares, and a handsome bazaar. Its most notable building is the former palace of the khans, which reflects the architecture of the mosques of Samarkand. Khokand is a major trade center in Turkestan; its primary exports are silk and raw cotton, while it imports manufactured goods from Russia. The town's coins, known as *khokands*, bear the inscription "Khokand the Charming."

In Khojent, the economy is based on the manufacture of cotton and silk goods, the rearing of silkworms, and the production of a coarse ware imitating Chinese porcelain. The surrounding district is characterized by vineyards, fruit gardens, and cotton plantations. The majority of Khojent's inhabitants are Tajiks.
