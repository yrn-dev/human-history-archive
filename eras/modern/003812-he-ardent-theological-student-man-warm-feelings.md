# Robert Barclay

## Early Life and Education
Robert Barclay was born in 1648 at Gordonstown in Morayshire. His father had a complex history, having served under Gustavus Adolphus and navigated a "somewhat tortuous course" during the troubles of the civil war. To complete his education, Robert was sent to Paris. During his time there, it appears he was inclined at one point to accept the Roman Catholic faith.

## Affiliation with the Society of Friends
In 1667, Barclay followed his father's example and joined the recently-formed Society of Friends, also known as the Quakers. He married a Quaker lady from Aberdeen, Christian Mollison, in 1670. Described as a man of considerable mental powers, warm feelings, and an ardent theological student, Barclay quickly became a prominent figure and the leading apologist for the new doctrine. He established his reputation through a controversy with William Mitchell.

## Theological Works and Doctrines
In 1676, Barclay published fifteen *Theses Theologiae*, which sparked a public discussion in Aberdeen where both opposing sides claimed victory. The most significant of these theses concerned immediate revelation, in which Barclay sharply stated that the "inner light" is superior to both scripture and reason.

Following the theses, Barclay published his greatest work, *An Apology for the True Christian Divinity*, in Latin at Amsterdam in 1676. This work served as an elaborate statement of the grounds for the fundamental positions he had established in his *Theses*. He later translated the work into English in 1678. The *Apology* is regarded as one of the most impressive theological writings of the century and remains perhaps the most important manifesto of the Quaker Society, characterized by a spirit of large tolerance.

## Public Life and Political Influence
Barclay's commitment to the Society of Friends led to his experience with the persecutions directed at the group, resulting in him being imprisoned several times. He traveled extensively throughout Europe, including trips with George Fox and Penn, and held several interviews with Elizabeth, princess palatine.

In his later years, Barclay exerted significant influence over James II. While James was duke of York, he granted a patent for the province of East New Jersey to twelve members of the Society of Friends, appointing Barclay as governor from 1682 to 1688. As the arrival of William of Orange became imminent, Barclay is said to have visited James II in an attempt to establish terms of accommodation.

Robert Barclay died on October 3rd.
