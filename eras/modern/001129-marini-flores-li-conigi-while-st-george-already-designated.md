# Early Cartographic Designations of the Azores

The Azores, also known as the Western Islands, are an archipelago in the Atlantic Ocean belonging to the kingdom of Portugal. The islands are organized into three distinct groups: the south-eastern group (comprising St Mary, St Michael's, and Formigas), the central group (consisting of Terceira, Graciosa, St George, Pico, and Fayal), and the north-western group (consisting of Flores and Corvo).

## Early Knowledge and Mapping
While it is not apparent that the ancient Romans or Greeks had knowledge of the archipelago, the discovery of Carthaginian coins on Corvo suggests the islands may have been visited by Carthaginians. Later, Arabian geographers such as Ibn-al-Wardi in the 14th century and Edrisi in the 12th century described nine islands in the Western Ocean located north of the Canaries. These writers claimed the islands were populous and contained cities of some magnitude, though they noted that intestine warfare had greatly reduced the population.

The islands first appeared distinctly on a map dated 1351. In this document, the western group was called the Brazil Island (*De Brazi*), a term then used for any red dye-stuff. The middle group was designated as the Wind or Dove Islands (*De Ventura sive de Columbis*), and the southern group was known as the Goat Islands (*Cabreras*).

## The Catalan Map of 1375
A Catalan map produced in 1375 provides more specific designations for individual islands. In this record, the island of Corvo is identified as *Corvi Marini*, and the island of Flores is listed as *Li Conigi*. Additionally, the island of St George is already designated on this map as *San Zorze*.

## Discovery and Portuguese Administration
There has been conjecture that the discoverers of the islands were Genoese, though evidence for this is insufficient. While a Flemish man named van der Berg is sometimes credited with discovery after being driven to the islands in 1432, this is considered secondary. According to one account, the navigator Gonzalo Velho Cabral was sent to prosecute the discovery after the news reached the court of Lisbon. An alternative version suggests that Prince Henry the Navigator of Portugal possessed a map of the islands and dispatched Cabral based on the accuracy of that map.

## Physical and Administrative Geography
The archipelago is volcanic in nature, characterized by elevated, undulating outlines and precipitous coastlines. The highest point is found on Pico at 7,612 feet, while the lowest peak is on Corvo at 350 feet. Volcanic activity has been frequent, particularly on St Michael's, though Flores, Corvo, and Graciosa have remained exempt from eruptions.

Administratively, the islands are divided into three districts named after their chief towns:
*   **Ponta Delgada:** The capital of St Michael's, which includes St Mary.
*   **Angra (Angra do Heroismo):** The capital of Terceira, which includes St George and Graciosa.
*   **Horta:** The capital of Fayal, which includes Pico, Flores, and Corvo.
