# De Utililate Scientiarum

*De Utililate Scientiarum* is the designation sometimes given to Part I (pages 1-22) of the *Opus Majus*, a major work by the 13th-century author Bacon. The *Opus Majus* is regarded by Whewell as both the Organum and the Encyclopaedia of the 13th century, and it marks the beginning of Bacon's real activity.

## The Four Offendicula
The primary focus of *De Utililate Scientiarum* is the examination of the four *offendicula*, which are defined as the causes of error. These four causes are:
*   Authority
*   Custom
*   The opinion of the unskilled many
*   The concealment of real ignorance with a pretence of knowledge

Among these, the pretence of knowledge to hide real ignorance is identified as the most dangerous error and is considered, in a sense, the cause of all the other *offendicula*. While some have viewed these classifications as an anticipation of the *Idola* described by Francis Bacon, the two systems of classification have little in common.

## Scientific Unity and Context
Bacon's perception of the necessity for encyclopaedic treatment and the unity of science is clearly demonstrated in the summary of this part, which is contained within the *Opus Tertium*. 

The *Opus Majus* stands as a central point in Bacon's bibliography. Prior to its composition, Bacon wrote several tracts, including some unimportant smaller works focused on alchemy. Other earlier works include a short treatise titled *De Secretis Operibus*, which may have been composed before 1249 if its dedication is authentic, and an unpublished work on chronology titled *Computus Naturalium*, likely dating to the year 1263. The relationship between these various works can only be determined through internal evidence.
