# The Bequest of James Dick for Higher Learning

## Overview of the Bequest
James Dick (1743-1828) was a West India merchant who provided a significant financial endowment to support the educational advancement of teachers in specific Scottish regions. He left a bequest of over L110,000 specifically intended to promote the higher learning of schoolmasters within the shires of Banff, Aberdeen, and Elgin.

## Administration and Distribution
The endowment generates an annual income of L4000. These funds are distributed among "dominies" who have successfully qualified to become beneficiaries through a process of examination.

## Regional Context of Banffshire
The bequest serves teachers in areas including Banffshire, a county with a population of 61,684 in 1891 and 61,488 in 1901, representing a density of 97 persons per square mile. As of 1901, the county's chief towns were Banff (population 7,161), Buckie (6,549), and Keith (4,753), followed by Cullen (1,936), Portsoy (1,878), and Dufftown (1,823). 

In this region, most schools operate under the jurisdiction of school boards. Some of these institutions earn grants for higher education. Additionally, the county council utilizes a "residue grant" to provide subsidies for classes in various practical and scientific fields, including:
* Veterinary science
* Agriculture
* Navigation
* Cookery and laundry work

## Governance and Legal Structure
Banffshire is part of a sheriffdom that includes the shires of Kincardine and Aberdeen. A resident sheriff-substitute is based in Banff, though this official also sits in Dufftown, Buckie, and Keith. Politically, the county returns one member to parliament, while the royal burghs of Cullen and Banff are part of the Elgin group of parliamentary burghs.
