# Lady Anne Barnard

## Early Life and Marriage
Lady Anne Barnard (1750–1825) was born on December 12, 1750, at Balcarres House in Fife. She was the eldest daughter of James Lindsay, the 5th earl of Balcarres. In 1793, she married Andrew Barnard, who was a son of the bishop of Limerick.

## Tenure at the Cape of Good Hope
Following her marriage, Lady Anne obtained an appointment for her husband as colonial secretary at the Cape of Good Hope through Henry Dundas (the 1st Viscount Melville). The Barnards traveled to the Cape in March 1797. Lady Anne remained at the Cape until January 1802. During her time there, she wrote a remarkable series of letters to Dundas, who served as the secretary for war and the colonies. These letters were later published in 1901 under the title *South Africa a Century Ago*.

## Later Years and Death
In 1806, the British reconquered the Cape, and Andrew Barnard was reappointed as colonial secretary. Lady Anne did not accompany him on this occasion. Andrew Barnard died in 1807. Lady Anne spent the remainder of her life in London, where she died on May 6, 1825.

## Literary Works
Lady Anne was the author of the ballad "Auld Robin Gray," which she wrote in 1772. The music for the ballad was composed by the Rev. William Leeves (1748–1828), a fact he admitted in 1812. Although the work was published anonymously in 1783, Lady Anne did not acknowledge her authorship of the lyrics until two years before her death, when she wrote to Sir Walter Scott. Scott subsequently edited the work for the Bannatyne Club, adding two continuations.
