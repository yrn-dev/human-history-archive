# Bolivar

## Geography and Territory
Bolivar is an inland state located in Venezuela. Its geographical boundaries are defined by the Orinoco and Apure rivers to the north, the Caroni river to the east (which forms the boundary with the Yuruari territory), and the Amazonas territory and Brazil to the south. The state is characterized by a landscape of numerous rivers, great forests, and extensive plains known as *llanos*. These plains are covered with nutritious grass during the rainy season, though this vegetation disappears entirely during the dry season.

The state comprises large areas of uninhabited territory. Due to frequent political changes within Venezuela, the outlines and size of Bolivar have undergone various modifications. Specifically, its area has been largely reduced since 1894.

## Population and Administration
In 1894, the population of Bolivar was recorded as 135,232. The capital of the state is Ciudad Bolivar, which was formerly known as Angostura. Situated on the right bank of the Orinoco, the capital is located approximately 240 meters above the river's mouth and had a population of 11,686.

## Economy and Trade
Trade is a significant activity in the region, facilitated by the Orinoco river, which can be easily ascended by vessels of light draught up to the capital. The state's exports consist of various forest products as well as agricultural and livestock goods, including:
* Cocoa
* Sugar
* Cotton
* Hides
* Jerked beef
