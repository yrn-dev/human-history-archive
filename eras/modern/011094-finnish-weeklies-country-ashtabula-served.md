# Finnish Weeklies in the United States

The presence of Finnish-born populations in various regions of the United States has led to the establishment of Finnish-language weekly publications to serve these communities.

## Publications in Ohio and Oregon

In Ashtabula, Ohio, there is a large population of Finnish birth residing within both the city and Ashtabula county. This community is served by the *Amerikan Sanomat*, a Finnish weekly established in 1897. The publication is noted as being one of the most widely read Finnish weeklies in the country.

Similarly, in the state of Oregon, the city of Astoria—which serves as the county-seat of Clatsop county and a port of entry—has a population that includes many Finns. Among the 3,779 foreign-born residents recorded in 1900, many were of Finnish origin, and a Finnish weekly was established in the city in 1905.

## Regional Context of Ashtabula

The city of Ashtabula, where the *Amerikan Sanomat* is based, is located in Ashtabula township on Lake Erie and the Ashtabula river, situated 54 miles northeast of Cleveland. The city is a significant industrial and transport hub, served by inter-urban electric lines and several railways, including the New York, Chicago & St Louis, the Lake Shore & Michigan Southern, and the Pennsylvania railways.

Ashtabula is particularly prominent for its harbour, which receives more iron ore annually than any other port in the country, and possibly the world. In 1907, the port received 2,632,027 net tons of soft coal and 7,542,149 gross tons of iron ore, which is transported by rail to iron manufacturing centres such as Youngstown and Pittsburg. The city's economy is further supported by the manufacture of foundry and machine shop products, agricultural implements, worsted goods, and leather. Additionally, the area is known for large greenhouses that produce tomatoes, cucumbers, and lettuce for shipment to large cities.

## Regional Context of Astoria

Astoria, the site of the 1905 Finnish weekly, is the oldest American settlement in the Columbia Valley, founded in 1811 by John Jacob Astor as a fur trade depot. Located on the Columbia river eight miles from its mouth, the city serves as the port of entry for the Oregon Customs District. 

The city's economy is heavily centered on the water, functioning as a packing and fishing centre for halibut, cod, and salmon. In 1905, the value of Astoria's factory products was $3,092,628, with $1,759,871 of that value coming from canned and preserved fish. Other local industries include dairy interests, terra-cotta and pressed brick factories, and an extensive lumber trade.
