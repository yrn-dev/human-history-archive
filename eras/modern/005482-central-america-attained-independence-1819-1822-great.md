# British Interests and Political Transitions in Central America (1819-1901)

Following the period between 1819 and 1822, during which Central America attained its independence, Great Britain took steps to secure its position in the region. This was achieved by incorporating the provisions of a 1786 treaty into a new treaty with Mexico in 1826. Additionally, these provisions were included in the drafts of treaties with the United States of Central America in 1831 and New Granada in 1825.

## British Sovereignty and the Belize Settlement
The legal status of British sovereignty in the region was a subject of historical debate. Some argued that the defeat of a Spanish attempt in 1798 established permanent sovereignty as an act of conquest. However, other perspectives note that a new treaty with Spain in 1814 revived the provisions of an earlier treaty. Furthermore, the British government did not claim rights based on the successful defense of 1798 for many years; acts of parliament between 1817 and 1819 continued to describe Belize as "a settlement, for certain purposes, under the protection of His Majesty."

By 1836, the British claimed the territories located between the Sarstoon and Belize rivers. Sovereignty over the entire colony, including these 1836 claims, was eventually confirmed through several developments:
* The peaceful progress of the country under British rule.
* The 1850 Clayton-Bulwer Treaty, which specifically excepted Belize from the provision forbidding Great Britain and the United States from colonizing or fortifying any point on the Central American mainland.
* The settlement of boundary disputes with Guatemala in 1859.

In that same year, 1859, the Bay Islands were recognized as part of the republic of Honduras.

## Internal Conflict and Border Security
Between 1849 and 1901, the northern and north-western marches of the British colony faced occasional attacks from rebel bands. These bands consisted of Indians who had risen against their Mexican rulers beyond the Hondo in 1849. These groups were finally subjugated in 1901, though the last serious raid occurred in 1872, where it was foiled.

## Political Evolution of Costa Rica
Costa Rica's political trajectory during the 19th century was marked by shifting alliances and internal strife. On September 15, 1821, Costa Rica and other Central American provinces revolted and joined the Mexican empire under the dynasty of Iturbide. This arrangement lacked popularity. When a Mexican republic was established in 1823, conflict erupted between Liberals, who sought an independent republic, and Conservatives, who wished to maintain the union. The Liberals were victorious in a meeting near the Ochomogo Pass, leading to the transfer of the seat of government from the old capital, Cartago, to the Liberal headquarters in San Jose.

From 1824 to 1839, Costa Rica was part of the Republic of the United States of Central America, though the central government's authority was largely nominal. The country's political status was not definitively assured until 1848, when an independent republic was proclaimed.

## Later Conflicts and Diplomatic Awards
Costa Rica experienced a war with the adventurer William Walker between 1856 and 1860. Aside from this, the state's history was generally free of political disturbances, with the exception of occasional threats of revolution caused by unsatisfactory economic conditions and various boundary disputes. In 1885, an attempt by J. R. Barrios, the president of Guatemala, to restore federal unity to Central America failed and had little impact on Costa Rican affairs. Later, in 1897, Costa Rica joined the Greater Republic of Central America (established in 1895 by Salvador, Nicaragua, and Honduras), but this union dissolved in 1898.

Boundary disputes were settled through international arbitration:
* **Nicaragua:** The boundary question was referred to the president of the United States, who issued an award in 1888 confirming an 1858 treaty. Further demarcation issues were resolved by a treaty in 1896.
* **Panama:** At the time a province of Colombia, the boundary with Panama was fixed on September 15, 1900, via an award from the French president.
