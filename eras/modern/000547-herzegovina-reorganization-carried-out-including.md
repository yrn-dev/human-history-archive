# Herzegovina

## Historical Rule and Conquest
Herzegovina was conquered by the Turks in 1483, following the fall of Constantinople (1453), Servia (1459), and Bosnia (1463). This expansion brought the Ottoman frontiers into contact with those of Venice, leading to incessant border wars. While the city of Ragusa sought safety through friendship with the invaders, the interior of the region became part of a Turkish province.

Following a series of conflicts, the peace of Carlowitz in 1699 granted the coast of Herzegovina to Venice. This delimitation was later confirmed by the treaty of Passarowitz in 1718, though it excluded the domains of Ragusa and the surrounding Ottoman territory. Over time, these boundaries were modified as Austria-Hungary substituted for both Venice and Turkey.

## Military Administration and Reorganization
Under Austrian administration, the military system underwent significant changes. In 1882, the system of compulsory military service was extended to include Herzegovina and Bosnia. This extension was accompanied by a reorganization based on the Prussian plan, which introduced local organization and army corps. These measures were intended to facilitate speedy mobilization, although there was a perceived risk that national and local spirits might influence the army.

## Trade and Communications
Herzegovina maintains a lucrative transit trade with Montenegro, Turkey, Bosnia, and various Mediterranean and Adriatic ports. The region's sea-borne trade is primarily handled by vessels that steam up the Narenta to Metkovic.

Communications in the region have been described as defective, with some interior areas accessible only via rough mountain roads. However, southern railway lines unite Herzegovina with Dalmatia, terminating at Metkovic, Ragusa, and Castlenuovo on the Bocche di Cattaro.

## Geography and Mapping
