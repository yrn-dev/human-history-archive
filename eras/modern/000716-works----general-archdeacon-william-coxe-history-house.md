# Works of Archdeacon William Coxe

Archdeacon William Coxe was a prolific author whose historical works covered a diverse range of subjects, including European royal houses, military figures, and the administration of foreign states.

## History of the House of Austria
One of Coxe's most significant contributions to historical literature is the *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792*. Published in London in 1817 across three volumes, this work remained the only general history of Austria available in the English language for a considerable period. The work was later expanded with a continuation by W. Kelly, published in London in 1853, with a subsequent new edition appearing in 1873.

While it served as a primary English resource, the *History of the House of Austria* was eventually superseded by more recent research. Following Coxe's era, a vast amount of scholarship was devoted to Austrian history, including works by J. Majlath, Count F. von Hartig, and A. von Arneth. Notably, Count F. von Hartig's *Genesis der Revolution in Oesterreich im Jahre 1848* was translated and included as an appendix to the 1853 edition of Coxe's work.

## Biographies and Memoirs
Coxe also produced biographical works, most notably regarding the Duke of Marlborough. His *Lives* of Marlborough, published between 1818 and 1819, are cited as one of the chief works on the subject, alongside the biographies written by Lediard, Sir Archibald Alison, and Viscount Wolseley.

In addition to his work on Marlborough, Coxe wrote regarding the Spanish monarchy. His 1815 London publication, *Memoirs of the Kings of Spain of the House of Bourbon*, provides an account of the administration of the Bourbon kings, including information regarding Ensenada.
