# Napoleon

## Imperial Appointments and Literary Connections
Napoleon's influence extended to the appointment of intellectuals to high office. In 1804, Carlo Giovanni Maria Denina, an Italian historian born in Piedmont in 1731, was invited by Napoleon to Paris to serve as the imperial librarian. Denina had previously been educated at Turin and Saluzzo and had spent many years in Berlin at the invitation of Frederick the Great. While residing in Paris under Napoleon's patronage, Denina published his work *Tableau de la Haute Italie, et des Alpes qui l'entourent* in 1805. He remained in Paris until his death on December 5, 1813.

## The Bonaparte Family and the Kings of Holland and Westphalia
The history of Napoleon's reign is closely tied to the roles of his family members, specifically his brothers. Louis Bonaparte served as the King of Holland from 1806 to 1813. The administration and court of Louis Bonaparte have been the subject of various historical accounts, including A. Garnier's *La Cour de Hollande sous le regne de Louis Bonaparte, par un auditeur* (published in Paris and Amsterdam in 1823), and works by T. Jorissen, V. Loosjes, and L. Wichers.

Jerome Bonaparte served as the King of Westphalia. His life and correspondence, as well as that of Queen Catherine, have been documented in several volumes by Baron Albert du Casse, as well as in works by M.M. Kaisenberg, A. Martinet, and P.W. Sergeant. Joseph Bonaparte also held significant roles, including a period in America and involvement in Southern Italy. His political and military memoirs were compiled in a ten-volume set by Baron A. du Casse in 1854.

## Historiography of the Napoleonic Era
A significant body of literature exists regarding Napoleon and the Bonaparte family. Baron A. du Casse authored *Les Rois freres de Napoleon* in 1883, which addresses the lives of Napoleon's brothers. Other comprehensive studies include F. Masson's four-volume work *Napoleon et sa famille* (published between 1889 and 1900) and F. Rocquain's 1875 study, *Napoleon I^er et le Roi Louis, d'apres les documents conserves aux archives nationales*.

Modern historical analysis of the period also includes the contributions of John Holland Rose, a lecturer on Modern History at Cambridge University, who authored *Life of Napoleon I.* and *Napoleonic Studies*.
