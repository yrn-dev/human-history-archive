# Atmospheric Pressure and Temperature in Asia

## Effects of Summer Heat on Air Pressure
The great summer heat causes the air to expand upwards, which disturbs the level of the planes of equal pressure. This process results in an outflow of the upper strata of air from the heated area. Consequently, there is a diminution of barometric pressure across Asia during the summer months. This effect is the opposite of what occurs during the winter, when cold produces an accumulation of air over the cold area, leading to an increase in barometric pressure. These fluctuations are the results of the alternate cooling and heating of the air over the continent.

## Characteristics of Asian Air Temperatures
Air temperatures in Asia vary significantly by region and latitude. In the northern portion of the continent, winters are characterized by extreme severity and long duration. Even as far south as the circle of 35 deg. N. lat., minimum temperatures frequently drop almost as low as zero degrees Fahrenheit. While summers in the northern latitudes are short, they are hot, with maximum heat levels that are only comparatively little less than those observed in tropical countries further south.

The proximity of the ocean provides a moderating effect on temperatures in the eastern and southern parts of Asia, particularly where the land consists of peninsulas or islands.

## Geographic Influences on Temperature
Several physical factors contribute to the exaggeration of temperature extremes in central Asia:

*   **Elevation:** The table-lands of Persia and Afghanistan, as well as the great elevations of central Asia above sea-level, tend to exaggerate the cold of winter.
*   **Surface Sterility:** Due to small rainfall in these regions, the surface is sterile. This operates to increase summer heat because a great accumulation of solar heat occurs on the dry surface soil. Because the soil is not covered with vegetation and is not moist, this heat cannot be released upwards through evaporation, nor can it be conveyed away downwards as occurs in the ocean.
*   **Radiation and Snow:** In winter, the prolonged loss of heat by radiation during clear, long nights creates a negative effect. This is further intensified in areas where the air has little vapour or where the surface is covered with snow.

## Subsurface Temperature and Soil
The slow diffusion of heat within the earth's solid crust is exemplified by the frozen soil of Siberia near Yakutsk. In this region, the earth remains permanently frozen to a depth exceeding 380 ft., where temperatures remain 5 or 6 deg. Fahr. below the freezing point of water. At a depth of 50 ft., the temperature is approximately 15 Fahr. below the freezing point. The heat of summer is only capable of thawing the surface to a depth of about 3 ft. Despite these soil conditions, the land is capable of producing other grain and wheat crops from fifteen to forty fold.
