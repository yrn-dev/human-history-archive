# Karateghin and Darvaz

Karateghin and Darvaz are regions associated with the state of Bokhara (also pronounced Bukhara), a central Asian state under Russian protection. When including the khanates of Karateghin and Darvaz, the total area of the state of Bokhara is approximately 85,000 sq. m.

## Geography of Bokhara
The state of Bokhara is situated on the right bank of the middle Oxus, positioned between 37 and 41 degrees North latitude and 62 and 72 degrees East longitude. Its borders are defined by the Russian governments of Ferghana, Samarkand, and Syr-darya to the north, Afghanistan to the south, and the Transcaspian territory and Khiva to the west. To the east, it is bounded by the Pamirs, though the south-eastern frontier in this region remains undetermined except where it meets Russian dominions.

The landscape of the state varies significantly by region:
*   **Western Portion:** This area consists of a plain watered by the Zarafshan river and a vast network of irrigation canals.
*   **Northern Portion:** This area features an intrusion of the Kara-kum sand desert.
*   **Eastern Portion:** This region contains the Karnap-chul steppe, which is covered with grass during the early summer.
*   **Middle Portion:** This area is characterized by high plateaus reaching altitudes of about 4,000 ft. These plateaus slope from the Tian-shan and are intersected by numerous rivers flowing toward the Oxus. This region is densely populated and is used extensively for cattle-breeding and agriculture due to a damper, cooler climate and fertile valleys. Notable towns in this area include Guzar (or Huzar), Chirakchi, Shaar, Kitab, and Karshi.

## Mountains and Waterways
The Hissar range, which is a westward continuation of the Alai Mountains, separates the Zarafshan from the Vakhsh, Kafirnihan, and Surkhan tributaries of the Oxus. The range extends about 200 m. in length. Its passes are extremely difficult, reaching altitudes between 12,000 and 14,000 ft., while the surrounding peaks are 1,000 to 3,000 ft. higher. The ridge is characterized by a lack of Alpine meadows and forests due to scarce rain and a dry climate; however, the melting of snow combined with heavy rain can create furious torrents in the watercourses. The southern foot-hills of the range, covered in loess, form the Vakhsh and Hissar fertile valleys.

The primary river of the state is the Amu-darya (or Oxus), which serves as the south-west border and separates the state from Afghanistan to the south. Steamboats operate on the Oxus from the mouth of the Surkhan up to Karki, near the Afghan frontier. The Zarafshan is the next largest river at 660 m. long; it is used heavily for irrigation but disappears into the sands 20 m. before it can reach the Oxus. Additionally, the Kashka-darya flows west from the glaciers of Hazret-sultan, providing water to the Shaar-sabiz (properly Shahri-sabs) oasis before being lost in the desert west of Karshi.

## The State of Darvaz
Darvaz is a small vassal state of Bokhara. It is located on the Panj river at the point where the river makes a sharp bend toward the west. The region is emphatically mountainous, and agriculture is only possible within the lower parts of the valleys. 

The population of Darvaz is approximately 35,000 people, consisting primarily of Galchas and Moslem Tajiks. The chief town of the region is Kala-i-khumb, which is situated on the Panj at an altitude of 4,370 ft.
