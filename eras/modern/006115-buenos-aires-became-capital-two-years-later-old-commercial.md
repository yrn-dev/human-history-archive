# Buenos Aires

Buenos Aires is the capital of the Argentine Republic, situated on the west shore of the La Plata estuary at 34° 36' 21" S. latitude and 58° 21' 33" W. longitude. The city is located approximately 155 miles above the mouth of the estuary and 127 miles west by north of Montevideo. Built upon an open grassy plain with an average elevation of 65 feet above sea level, the city extends north from the Matanzas (or Riachuelo) river, where the "Boca" port is located.

## Early Foundation and Colonial History
The establishment of a colony at the site of Buenos Aires was attempted three times. The first attempt occurred in 1535, led by Don Pedro de Mendoza. This expedition, though well-equipped, failed completely due to mismanagement and hostility from the Indians. Colonists from this settlement, named "Santa Maria de Buenos Ayres," eventually relocated to Asunción, which had been founded by an expedition sent upriver by Mendoza. A second attempt in 1542 by a portion of a Spanish expedition under Cabeza de Vaca also met with little success.

The permanent foundation of the city dates to 1580, when Don Juan de Garay, the governor of Paraguay, led an expedition down the Paraná. Garay had previously established a post at Santa Fé in 1573. The primary objective of the 1580 expedition was to create a port near the sea to provision ships and receive supplies from Spain, a need keenly felt by colonists in Asunción. Garay constructed a fort and laid out a town in the prescribed Spanish style, naming it "Ciudad de la Santissima Trinidad," while retaining "Buenos Aires" as a descriptive name for the port due to the invigorating and agreeable atmosphere of the area.

## Administrative Evolution
Until 1620, Buenos Aires remained a dependency of Asunción. In that year, the Spanish settlements of the La Plata region were divided into three provinces: Tucuman, Paraguay, and Buenos Aires. Garay's city became the capital of the Buenos Aires province and the seat of a new bishopric. Despite its growing importance in trade and population, the Spanish crown initially showed little interest in the station, as the gold and silver of Peru remained the primary focus. Early supply shipments were limited to one small shipment per year. However, the unprotected coast and proximity to Portuguese settlements in Brazil facilitated smuggling.

In 1820, Buenos Aires became a province of the Confederation. It adopted a constitution in 1854, establishing a government consisting of a legislature of two chambers and a governor, both elected by popular vote. Following an unsuccessful revolt against the national government in 1880, the city of Buenos Aires was federalized and detached from the province by an act of congress. This created a federal district covering 72 square miles. Consequently, La Plata was selected as the new provincial capital.

## Urban Development and Infrastructure
The old city was designed with narrow streets enclosing rectangular blocks of uniform size, running parallel to the cardinal points of the compass. While this initially gave the city a monotonous appearance, subsequent growth in wealth and population brought greater architectural diversity. The city's area has been extended through the reclamation of shallow water frontage and the construction of a new port. Previously, vessels with a draught over 15 feet had to anchor 12 miles from the city in outer roads, relying on small boats and steam tenders to reach landing piers or the beach. Because the estuary is shallow, artificial channels must be constantly dredged to allow vessels to enter the docks.

Notable public buildings include the Casa Rosada (the government-house), which faces the Plaza 25 de Mayo and partially occupies the site of Garay's 1580 fort. Other significant structures include the municipal hall and the congress hall on Avenida de Mayo and Calle Callao, which was completed in 1906 at a cost of approximately £1,300,000. The city also houses a mint, an exchange (bolsa), and a distributing reservoir.

## Economy, Culture, and Education
Buenos Aires is the principal manufacturing center of the republic, with thousands of industrial establishments. Its residents, known as Porteños, frequently refer to the city as the "Paris of America." The population is noted for its interest in the drama, music, and various diversions, including horse-racing, pelota, and Italian opera. The city provides numerous social, athletic, and sporting clubs, as well as several large public baths.

Education and culture are represented by the University of Buenos Aires, which had 3,562 students in 1901 and includes faculties of medicine and law. The city also contains three normal schools, four national colleges, various technical schools, a national museum, a national library, an aquarium, and a zoological garden.
