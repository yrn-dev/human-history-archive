# The Administration of Dr. von Koerber

The administration of Dr. von Koerber was characterized by persistent efforts to resolve ethnic tensions between Germans and Czechs, the use of emergency constitutional powers to bypass parliamentary deadlock, and the implementation of large-scale public works.

## Efforts Toward Compromise
Upon taking office, Dr. von Koerber hoped to arrange a compromise to restore the parliament to normal working conditions. In the early months of 1900, matters appeared more peaceful, but this was interrupted on June 8 when the Czechs, demanding the restoration of their language in the internal service of Bohemia, used noise and disturbance to force the president to suspend proceedings.

Between December 1902 and January 1903, Koerber attempted further negotiations to promote a compromise between the Germans and Czechs. He proposed a division of Bohemia into 10 districts: five Czech, three German, and two mixed. Under this plan, of the 234 district tribunals, 133 would be Czech, 94 German, and seven mixed. These efforts proved futile. The Czechs countered by demanding that the German and Czech languages be placed on an equal footing throughout Bohemia for all official purposes. Because this would require recognizing Czech as a language of internal service in Bohemia, the Germans refused the demand.

## Parliamentary Obstruction and Paragraph 14
Koerber faced significant difficulty in inducing parliament to vote for the budgets of 1903, 1904, and 1905. To maintain government functions, he reverted to a method used by his predecessors: sanctioning estimates by imperial ordinance under paragraph 14 of the constitution. This clause allowed the emperor to proclaim orders in cases of "pressing necessity" if the Reichsrath was not in session, provided they were signed by the whole ministry.

From this point until his fall on December 31, 1904, Koerber governed practically without a parliament. The Chamber was summoned at intervals, though often as a pretext to justify the subsequent use of paragraph 14 rather than to secure legislative assent. The Czechs employed "urgency motions" and noisy obstruction to block business; in one instance, a single sitting lasted 57 hours without interruption. In response to this aggressiveness, several German parties—including the German Populists, German Progressists, Christian Socialists, and Constitutional Landed Proprietors—formed a supreme committee of four members and a joint executive committee to protect German racial interests.

## Public Works and Infrastructure
To overcome obstruction and induce the Chamber to sanction the contingent of recruits, estimates, and other "necessities of state" for 1901 and 1902, Koerber promised the undertaking of large public works of mutual interest to Czechs and Germans. These projects included:
*   A canal from the Danube to the Oder.
*   A ship canal from the Danube to the Moldau near Budweis, with the canalization of the Moldau from Budweis to Prague.
*   A ship canal from the projected Danube-Oder canal near Prerau to the Elbe near Pardubitz, including the canalization of the Elbe from Pardubitz to Melnik.
*   A navigable connection between the Vistula, the Dniester, and the Danube-Oder Canal.

These four canals were estimated to require twenty years for construction, funded by a 4% loan amortizable over ninety years. Additionally, the cabinet secured sanction for a "second railway route to Trieste" to shorten the distance between Salzburg, South Germany, and the Adriatic. This line involved boring tunnels through the Wochein, Karawanken, and Tauern hills, with a completion date set for 1909. While these works were often necessary or desirable, they were frequently begun hastily due to political expediency, and the cost greatly exceeded parliamentary estimates.

## The Szell-Koerber Compact and Resignation
At the end of 1902, Koerber concluded a new customs and trade alliance with the Hungarian premier, Szell. Known as the Szell-Koerber Compact, this agreement established a joint Austro-Hungarian tariff to serve as a basis for commercial treaties with other states, such as Italy and Germany. Although initially greeted as a sign of peaceful development and an end to economic strife, the compact was never fully ratified by either parliament and was eventually discarded.

By the end of 1904, the system of governing via paragraph 14 became unsustainable. The clause could not be used for the sale of state patrimony or the contraction of lasting burdens on the exchequer, yet loans were required for military and other purposes. Because Koerber had become so obnoxious to the Czechs that his removal was viewed as a concession, the emperor accepted his resignation. He was succeeded on January 1, 1905, by Baron von Gautsch.
