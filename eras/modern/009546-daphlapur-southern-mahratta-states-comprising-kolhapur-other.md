# Daphlapur and the Southern Mahratta States

## Administrative Context
Daphlapur is identified as one of the Deccan Satara Jagirs, a group of native states within the Bombay Presidency of British India. The Bombay Presidency is a province administered by a governor, consisting of both British districts and native states. Politically, the presidency is divided into four commissionerships: Sind, the northern (Gujarat), the central (Deccan), and the southern (Carnatic). 

The native states of the presidency are numerous, totaling 353 separate units. These are managed either by collectors of the districts in which smaller states are located or by political agents. Daphlapur belongs to the specific grouping of the Deccan Satara Jagirs, which also includes Aundh, Bhor, Jath, Phaltan, and Akalkot.

## The Southern Mahratta States and Kolhapur
The southern Mahratta states comprise Kolhapur and other states. Kolhapur is the principal state under the political control of the government of Bombay and is ranked as the fourth most important of the Mahratta principalities, following Baroda, Indore, and Gwalior. 

### History and Governance of Kolhapur
The rajas of Kolhapur claim descent from Raja Ram, who was a younger son of Sivaji the Great, the founder of the Mahratta power. The state's relationship with the British government was marked by conflict in the late 18th century, with British expeditions sent against Kolhapur in 1765 and 1792 due to the prevalence of piracy. In the early 19th century, military operations were conducted by the British because of the chief's misgovernment, eventually leading to the appointment of a British officer to manage the state.

In more recent years, Kolhapur has been governed according to the pattern of British administration. Raja Shahu Chhatrapati, G.C.S.I., who is entitled to a 21-gun salute, succeeded to the throne by adoption in 1884, having been born in 1874.

### Geography and Economy of Kolhapur
Kolhapur is located within the Deccan division of Bombay. Together with its feudatories (jagirs), the state covers an area of 3,165 sq. m. As of 1901, its population was 910,011 and its estimated revenue was £300,000. 

The geography of the state varies from the Western Ghats in the west to the Deccan plain in the east. The western region features wild hill slopes and valleys that were previously covered in rich forests and primarily produce timber. This area contains ancient Mahratta strongholds, including Bavda, Panhala, Rungna, and Vishalgarh. The eastern region is a treeless, well-cultivated plain. 

The state's economy is supported by the following:
*   **Agriculture:** Principal products include vegetables, cotton, tobacco, sugar-cane, millets, rice, and safflower.
*   **Mining:** Iron ore is found in the hills. While smelting was once common, it cannot currently compete with European imports. The state also possesses several good stone quarries.
*   **Infrastructure:** The Southern Mahratta company operates the state railway from Miraj junction to Kolhapur town.

## The Town of Kolhapur
The town of Kolhapur, also known as Karvir, serves as the terminus for a branch of the Southern Mahratta railway, located 30 miles from the main line. Its population in 1901 was 54,373. The town contains modern public buildings and evidence of antiquity, having originally served as an important religious center with numerous Buddhist remains discovered nearby.

## Regional Context and the Mahratta People
The Deccan region is the home of the Mahrattas. In 1901, the Mahrattas were the dominating race in the presidency next to Europeans, numbering 3,650,000. This population is composed of 1,900,000 Kunbis, 350,000 Konkanis, and 1,400,000 not otherwise specified. The Mahratti language is spoken in the central division and in Thana, and it is used alongside Kanarese in the southern division.

Historically, the Mahrattas rose against Islam in the southwest and restored Brahminism as the ruling religion, extending their power over the southern triangle of India. Following a period of conflict with Afghans for the rule of North India, the Mahratta power eventually broke into several principalities, including Gwalior, Baroda, and Indore.
