# Hardanger Fjord

The Hardanger fjord is a geographical feature of Norway that appears in both historical chronicles and the records of 19th-century scientific exploration.

## Historical Accounts
In the *Heimskringla*, the Hardanger fjord is mentioned in the context of political and personal movements during the era of the Norwegian kings. Gregorius Dagson, after escaping from King Eystein by traveling through Thelemark and over the mountains, arrived in Hardanger. From there, he proceeded to Studla in Etne, where he was hospitably received by Kristin, the daughter of King Sigurd and wife of Erling Skakke. After receiving a long-ship and necessary supplies from Kristin, Gregorius traveled to Bergen to meet Erling.

The fjord is also associated with a period of severe famine in Norway. During this time, the kings resided on the fjord, and because of the extreme scarcity of food—to the point that the kings could scarcely feed their men-at-arms—the fjord was commonly referred to as Hardanger (Hardacre). This period of hardship influenced political decisions, including the choice of Harald Grafeld to travel to Denmark to accept fiefs from the Danish king in hopes of securing assistance for the people.

## Scientific Exploration
In the 19th century, the Hardanger fjord served as a site for academic research. Peter Christen Asbjornsen, who became a zoologist by profession, conducted a series of investigating voyages along the coasts of Norway. These voyages, which were made with the assistance of the university, focused particularly on the Hardanger fjord.
