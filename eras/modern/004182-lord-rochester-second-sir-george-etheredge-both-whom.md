# Elizabeth Barry

Elizabeth Barry (1658–1713) was an English actress who achieved significant success on the stage despite a difficult start to her career. Early in her professional life, she was unsuccessful to the point of being dismissed from the stage on more than one occasion.

## Career and Coaching
Barry's professional trajectory changed when she was coached by her lover, the Earl of Rochester. Rochester had entered into a wager that he could transform her into a first-rate actress within a short period, a judgment that was ultimately confirmed by the results of his coaching. 

Throughout her career, Mrs. Barry is said to have created over 100 parts. She found particular success in the plays written by Thomas Otway. Her talent was highly regarded by her contemporaries; Dryden described her as "always excellent," and Betterton noted that her acting brought success to plays that would otherwise "disgust the most patient reader."

## Royal Patronage
Barry's performances earned the admiration of the monarchy. Her portrayal of Isabella, Queen of Hungary, in the Earl of Orrery's play *Mustapha* delighted Charles II as well as the Duke and Duchess of York. The Duchess of York was so impressed that she took English lessons from Barry. Later, upon becoming queen, the Duchess provided Mrs. Barry with her own coronation robes to wear for the role of Elizabeth in the play *Earl of Essex* by Banks.

Additionally, Cibber provides the authority for the claim that benefits for actors were first established by the command of James II specifically on behalf of Mrs. Barry; prior to this, such benefits had been reserved exclusively for authors.

## Personal Life
Elizabeth Barry had children with two men: Lord Rochester and Sir George Etheredge. Both fathers provided for the children.

Mrs. Barry retired from the stage in 1709 and died on November 7, 1713.
