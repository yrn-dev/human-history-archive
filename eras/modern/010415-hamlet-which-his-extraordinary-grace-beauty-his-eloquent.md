# Edwin Booth

Edwin Booth was a prominent actor and theatre manager known for his extensive repertoire of dramatic roles and his commitment to the original texts of William Shakespeare.

## Early Career and Development
Booth made his first regular appearance on September 10, 1849, at the Boston Museum. He performed as Tressel in Colley Cibber's version of *Richard III*, acting alongside his father. At the time, Booth was described as having a lithe and graceful figure, buoyant spirits, soft and luminous eyes, and dark hair that fell in waving curls. Despite these attributes, his father expressed evident disappointment, and other members of the theatrical profession believed the genius of the elder Booth had not been passed to the son.

His New York debut occurred on September 27, 1850, at the National theatre in Chatham Street, where he played the character of Wilford in *The Iron Chest*. Following his father's illness a year later, Booth took over the role of Richard III. He achieved an unassailable position on the stage only after his father's death. Between 1852 and 1856, Booth performed in the Sandwich Islands, Australia, and California, where his brilliant acting captivated audiences and surprised those who had known him in the east.

## Professional Triumphs and Management
Booth's dramatic successes were widely acknowledged, with his performances as Richelieu, Richard, and Hamlet being pronounced superior to those of Edwin Forrest. His portrayal of Sir Giles Overreach in *A New Way to Pay Old Debts* was noted to have surpassed that of his father.

In 1862, Booth became the manager of the Winter Garden theatre in New York. Between 1864 and 1867, he staged a series of Shakespearian productions of unexampled magnificence, which included *Othello*, *The Merchant of Venice*, and *Hamlet*. 

In 1868-1869, Booth organized an excellent stock company and built his own venue, Booth's theatre, located at the corner of 6th Avenue and 23rd Street in New York. The company produced various plays, including *The Winter's Tale*, *Julius Caesar*, *Macbeth*, *Much Ado about Nothing*, *Romeo and Juliet*, and *The Merchant of Venice*. Booth was noted for using the true text of Shakespeare, a reform that antedated similar movements in England by many years.

## The Role of Hamlet
Hamlet was Edwin Booth's most famous part. He was considered peculiarly fitted for the role due to his eloquent sensibility, beauty, and extraordinary grace. It is probable that Booth played Hamlet more often than any other actor before or since. His final stage appearance took place in 1891 in Brooklyn, where he once again performed as Hamlet.

## Personal Life and Later Years
Booth's career was marked by a period of distress in 1865 when his brother, John Wilkes Booth, assassinated President Lincoln. Prior to this event, in the autumn of 1864, the three Booth brothers—Edwin, John Wilkes, and Junius Brutus (1821-1853)—had performed together in *Julius Caesar*.

While Booth's theatrical ventures were almost invariably successful, his generous and confiding nature led to uneconomical management. In 1874, he lost his entire fortune along with the grand dramatic structure he had built. However, through arduous toil, he accumulated wealth once more. 

Booth demonstrated his generosity by converting his spacious residence in Gramercy Park, New York, into The Players' club. This club was intended for the elect of the acting profession and members of other professions they chose to include. He deeded the house, his works of art, his books, and many invaluable mementos of the stage to the club, retaining only a single apartment for himself.

Booth was married twice: first to Mary Devlin in 1860 (who died in 1863) and later to Mary F. McVicker in 1869 (who died in 1881). His daughter from his first marriage, Edwina Booth Grossman, published *Edwin Booth: Recollections* in 1894. Edwin Booth died on June 7, 1893.

## Other Notable Roles
In addition to Hamlet, Booth's repertoire included:
* **Shakespearean roles:** Lear, Othello, Iago, Shylock, Richard II, Richard III, Benedick, and Petruccio.
* **Other roles:** Wolsey, Richelieu, Sir Giles Overreach, Ruy Blas, Don Cesar de Bazan, Bertuccio (in Tom Taylor's *The Fool's Revenge*), and Brutus (Payne's).
