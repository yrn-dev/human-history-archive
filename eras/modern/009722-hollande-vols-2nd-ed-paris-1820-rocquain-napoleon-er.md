# The Bonaparte Family and Associated Figures

The Bonaparte family, centered around Napoleon I, produced numerous figures who held significant political, administrative, and scientific roles across Europe and beyond. Their history is documented through various biographical accounts and official correspondences.

## Marianne Elisa Bonaparte
Marianne Elisa (1777–1820) was born in Ajaccio on January 3, 1777. Through the efforts of her brothers, she was a "king's scholar" at the establishment of St Cyr near Paris. Following the disruption of this institution by revolutionists in 1792, Napoleon took charge of her and returned her to Ajaccio. 

On May 5, 1797, she married Felix Bacciochi, a well-connected Corsican. Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted her the principality of Piombino and later Lucca. In 1808, she acquired the grand duchy of Tuscany. Because Bacciochi was considered almost a nullity, Elisa's ability and pride allowed her to exert significant influence over Italian affairs and administration. Her relationship with Napoleon was often strained, and between 1813 and 1814, she supported Murat in his enterprises. After the fall of her brother, she lived as the countess of Compignano in Bologna and later Santo Andrea near Trieste, where she died on August 6, 1820.

## Descendants and Later Generations
The family continued through various branches. Prince Napoleon Victor Jerome Frederic (born 1862), the eldest son of his father, became the recognized head of the French Bonapartist party upon his father's death. His brother, Prince Louis Napoleon, served as an officer in the Russian army and was appointed governor of the Caucasus in 1906.

Mathilde Letitia Wilhelmine (1820–1904), the daughter of Jerome and sister of Prince Napoleon, was born in Trieste on May 20, 1820. After a near-betrothal to her cousin Louis Napoleon, she married Prince Anatole Demidov in 1840. The marriage ended in separation within five years, and Tsar Nicholas ensured she received a handsome allowance. Following the election of Louis Napoleon to the presidency of the republic, Mathilde resided in Paris, where she managed the honors of the Elysee until his marriage. She remained in Paris as a patron of letters and art until her death on January 2, 1904.

## Charles Lucien Jules Laurent Bonaparte
The son of Lucien Bonaparte, Charles Lucien Jules Laurent (1803–1857), Prince of Canino, was primarily a scientist. In 1822, he married his cousin Zenaide Bonaparte, the daughter of Joseph. He gained scientific renown starting at age twenty-two with the publication of *American Ornithology* (1825–1833). His extensive zoological works include *Iconographia della fauna Italica*, *Catalogo metodico degli uccelli europei*, and catalogues of European fish and mammals.

He held honorary memberships in the academies of Berlin (1843) and Upsala (1833), and was a correspondent of the Institute of France starting in 1844. In 1847, he engaged in Italian political agitation, advocating for the expulsion of Austrians and Italian independence. He was elected deputy by Viterbo to the national assembly and joined the Junto of Rome in 1848. After the revolution failed, he left Italy in July 1849, traveling through Holland and France to return to his scientific studies. He was survived by eight children, including Lucien Louis Joseph Napoleon, who became a cardinal in 1868.

## Historiography of the Bonapartes
The life and reign of Louis Bonaparte are detailed in several works, including the *Documents historiques et reflexions sur le gouvernement de Hollande* (1820) by le comte de Saint-Leu and *Napoleon I^er et le Roi Louis* (1875) by F. Rocquain. Other sources include works by Baron A. du Casse, A. Garnier, T. Jorissen, V. Loosjes, L. Wichers, and F. Masson.

General histories of the Bonaparte family include *Storia genealogica della famiglia Bonaparte* (1846), *Le antichita dei Bonaparte* (1857), and *La Famille imperiale* (1860). Specific accounts of Joseph Bonaparte include the *Memoires et correspondance politique et militaire du roi Joseph* and the *History of Joseph Bonaparte* by J.S.C. Abbott.
