# National Budgets and Financial Administration

The budget is a financial instrument used by constitutional countries, and in some instances, autocratic powers, to manage annual expenditure and revenue. While the practice of taking a yearly period for budgeting is universal, the specific dates of commencement vary by nation.

## International Variations in Budgetary Cycles
In the United Kingdom, Germany, and Denmark, the financial year begins on April 1st. In contrast, France, Belgium, and Austria begin their financial years on January 1st, while Canada, the United States, Italy, and Spain begin theirs on July 1st. In the United Kingdom, the financial year previously ran from January 1st to December 31st prior to 1832.

The adoption of annual budgets has expanded over time. Russia began publishing annual budgets in 1866, followed by Egypt and Turkey, the latter by an imperial decree in 1875. In federal systems, such as the Argentine republic, the German empire, and the United States, financial knowledge requires consulting both the federal budgets and the budgets of the individual states.

## Budgetary Processes and Authority
National budgets are categorized as either those emitted on executive authority or those passing under parliamentary scrutiny and debate. 

In the United Kingdom, the executive places the budget before the whole House without prior examination, except by the cabinet, and it is then scrutinized by the House sitting as a committee. In the United States, the budget effectively emanates from Congress due to the lack of connection between the legislative and executive departments. The secretary of the treasury submits estimates from executive departments to the House of Representatives, where two committees manage the process: the committee on appropriations handles expenditure, and the committee on ways and means handles taxation.

In other constitutional countries, a middle ground is often adopted where a specially selected committee performs a preliminary examination and may make drastic changes to executive proposals.

## The Budget in the United Kingdom
The term "budget" was originally applied to a leather-covered case or small coffer (from the French *bougette*), which became a common term for the despatch box used to hold official papers. Consequently, the chancellor of the exchequer was described as "opening his budget" during the annual statement.

The timing of the budget's introduction in the UK has fluctuated due to political and diplomatic events. For example, in 1860, the budget was introduced on February 10th following the ratification of a commercial treaty with France. In 1859, a change in administration delayed the budget until July 18th. In 1880, two separate budgets were introduced: one in March under Disraeli's administration and another in June under Gladstone's administration.

## Political Impact and Administration
Budgets have served as pivotal points in English political history. In the autumn of 1852, Disraeli introduced a budget that resulted in his defeat. This defeat led to the resignation of Lord Derby's minority ministry by the middle of December 1852.

Regarding the composition of the cabinet, a principle was introduced suggesting that representatives of "spending departments"—specifically the first lord of the admiralty and the secretary of state for war—should be members of the House that votes the supplies. While Disraeli followed this precedent, it was later disregarded. For instance, in Sir H. Campbell-Bannerman's 1905 cabinet, there were thirteen ministers in the House of Commons and six in the House of Lords. Later, in 1908, Mr. Asquith personally introduced a budget in May that featured the inauguration of a system of old-age pensions.

## Specialized Budgetary Systems
### The Dual Monarchy (Austria and Hungary)
The Dual Monarchy utilized a common budget alongside the budgets of each individual state. This joint budget covered the ministry of finance, the army, and foreign affairs. Revenues for this budget were derived from the joint ministries, the quota (proportional contributions from the two states), and net proceeds from customs. 

The quota was fixed for several years. Until 1897, Austria contributed 70% and Hungary 30%. From 1897 to July 1907, the proportions shifted to 66-46/49 for Austria and 33-2/49 for Hungary. In 1907, Hungary's contribution rose to 36.4%. In 1905, the total budget estimates for the common administration were £20,762,410.

### The Imperial Diet of Japan
Under the rules of the Imperial Diet, annual expenditure and revenue must receive the Diet's consent in the form of an annual budget, which is first laid before the House of Representatives. If the Diet has not voted on the budget, the government carries out the budget from the preceding year. 

Specific provisions exist for various financial needs:
* **Reserve Funds:** Established to meet requirements not provided for in the budget or to supply unavoidable deficits.
* **Continuing Expenditure Funds:** The government may ask for consent for a fixed number of years to meet special requirements.
* **Imperial Ordinances:** In urgent cases regarding public safety or foreign relations where the Diet cannot be convoked, financial measures may be taken via Imperial Ordinance, subject to approval at the next session.
* **Imperial House Expenditure:** This is defrayed annually from the National Treasury and does not require Diet consent unless an increase is necessary.
* **Fixed Expenditures:** Expenditures based on the Emperor's supreme powers or legal obligations cannot be reduced or rejected by the Diet without government concurrence.
