# Hermann Bonitz

Hermann Bonitz (1814–1888) was a German scholar born on July 29, 1814, in Langensalza, Saxony. He is recognized primarily for his contributions as a commentator on the works of Aristotle and Plato, as well as his influence on the Prussian educational system.

## Education and Academic Career
Bonitz pursued his higher education at two major centers of learning. He studied at Leipzig under G. Hermann and subsequently at Berlin, where he studied under Bockh and Lachmann. 

Following his studies, Bonitz held a series of teaching and administrative positions across various cities:
* **Dresden:** He served as a teacher at the Blochmann institute beginning in 1836.
* **Berlin:** He became an Oberlehrer at the Friedrich-Wilhelms gymnasium in 1838 and at the Graues Kloster in 1840. Later, in 1867, he served as the director of the Graues Kloster gymnasium.
* **Stettin:** He held a professorship at the gymnasium in 1842.
* **Vienna:** In 1849, he became a professor at the university of Vienna.

His professional standing was further marked by his appointment as a member of the imperial academy in 1854 and his membership in the council of education in 1864. Bonitz retired in 1888 and died in Berlin on July 25 of that same year.

## Educational Contributions
Beyond his philological work, Bonitz maintained a significant interest in higher education. He was the primary figure responsible for the system of examination and teaching implemented in Prussian high schools after 1882.

## Philological Works and Scholarship
Bonitz is best known outside of Germany for his commentaries on the classical philosophers Plato and Aristotle. His scholarly output in this area includes several critical observations and studies:
* **On Plato:** He authored *Disputationes Platonicae Duae* (1837) and *Platonische Studien* (the third edition of which was published in 1886).
* **On Aristotle:** His work on Aristotle is extensive, including *Observations Criticae in Aristotelis Libros Metaphysicos* (1842), *Observationes Criticae in Aristotelis quae feruntur Magna Moralia et Ethica Eudemia* (1844), *Alexandri Aphrodisiensis Commentarius in Libras Metaphysicos Aristotelis* (1847), and *Aristotelis Metaphysica* (1848–1849). He also wrote *Uber die Kategorien des A.* (1853), *Aristotelische Studien* (1862–1867), and the *Index Aristotelicus* (1870).

In addition to his work on philosophy, Bonitz wrote on other classical subjects, including *Uber den Ursprung der homerischen Gedichte* (5th ed., 1881), as well as contributions to the explanation of Sophokles (1856–1857) and Thukydides (1854). He contributed frequently to the *Zeitschrift fur die osterreichischen Gymnasien* on educational and classical topics.
