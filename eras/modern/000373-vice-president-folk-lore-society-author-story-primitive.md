## Context of Folk-Lore
The society of which Clodd served as Vice-President deals with "folk-lore," a term derived from the Old English *lár*, as well as the Dutch *leer* and German *Lehre*. This root refers to the act of receiving or imparting knowledge, as seen in the words "learning" and "learn." 

While it was suggested in the *Gentleman's Magazine* in June 1830 that "lore" be used as a scientific termination to replace the Greek derivative "-ology," this change was not adopted. Instead, the term is frequently used to describe traditional stories and beliefs associated with specific subjects, such as bird-lore or legendary lore. In a broader historical and scientific context, folk-lore is viewed as a form of "mental fossilization" that, along with contemporary savage customs and psycho-analysis, helps scientific men attempt to reconstruct the primitive mentality of humans from four hundred centuries ago.
