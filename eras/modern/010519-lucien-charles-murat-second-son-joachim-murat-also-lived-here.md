# Lucien Charles Murat

Prince Napoleon Lucien Charles Murat was the second son of Joachim Murat. He is noted for having resided for many years in Bordentown, a city located in Burlington County, New Jersey, United States.

## Residence in Bordentown
Bordentown is situated on the east bank of the Delaware River, approximately 6 miles south of Trenton and 28 miles northeast of Philadelphia. The city is positioned on a broad, level plain 65 feet above the river. During the period associated with the residence of the Prince, Bordentown was home to several other notable figures. From 1817 to 1832 and again from 1837 to 1839, it was the residence of Joseph Bonaparte, the ex-king of Spain. Bonaparte lived on a magnificent estate known as "Bonaparte's Park," where he hosted various distinguished guests, including Lafayette. 

The presence of Bonaparte in the region was significant enough that the New Jersey legislature passed a special law allowing him, as an alien, to own real property; it is suggested that this legal arrangement contributed to the state receiving the nickname "Spain." In addition to Prince Lucien Charles Murat and Joseph Bonaparte, the estate known as "Ironsides" served for a long time as the home of Rear-Admiral Charles Stewart.

## Family Background
Prince Lucien Charles Murat was the son of Joachim Murat and Maria Annunciata Caroline. His mother, born in Ajaccio in 1782, married Joachim Murat in early 1800. Through her ambitions, she supported her husband's ascent through several high-ranking positions, including governor of Paris, marshal of France (1804), and grand duke of Berg and of Cleves (1806). In 1808, Joachim Murat served as the lieutenant of the emperor in Spain before becoming the king of Naples later that summer.

Joachim Murat's career also included military leadership during the retreat of the Grand Army. While Napoleon and his staff returned to Germany with only a handful of guards, the Grand Army under Murat reached Königsberg in a disciplined state, though its numbers had been reduced from six hundred thousand to approximately one thousand. From Königsberg, Murat retreated to Posen. Additionally, in 1813, Joachim Murat founded a large lunatic asylum in Aversa, a town in Campania, Italy.

The marriage of Joachim Murat and Caroline was marked by tension between Caroline and Napoleon, partly due to her displeasure regarding the distance of Naples from Paris. Caroline associated herself with her husband's equivocal movements in 1814 and 1815. Following Joachim Murat's death at Pizzo on October 13, 1815, Caroline lived in Austrian territory under some restraint before eventually residing in Trieste with her sister, Elisa. Caroline died on May 18, 1839.
