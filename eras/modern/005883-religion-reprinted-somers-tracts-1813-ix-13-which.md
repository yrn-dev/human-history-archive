# Religion

The archive's records regarding religion primarily consist of the published works, theological disputes, and personal convictions of various historical figures, as well as the documentation of religious dissent and conversion efforts.

## The Works of the Duke of Buckingham
The Duke of Buckingham published a pamphlet in 1685 titled *A short Discourse on the Reasonableness of Man's having a Religion*. This work, which was later reprinted in *Somers Tracts* (1813, ix. 13), focused on the main subject of the reasonableness of religion and the author's favorite topic, religious toleration. The publication prompted several rejoinders; it was defended by William Penn and by the Duke himself in a 1685 piece titled *The Duke of Buckingham's Letter to the unknown author of a short answer to the Duke of Buckingham's Paper*.

During the reign of James, the Duke was sent a priest in an attempt to convert him to Roman Catholicism, though Buckingham responded to the priest's arguments with ridicule. At the end of his life, Buckingham expressed great repentance, stating that he felt "forsaken by my God" and "despised by my country."

## Protestant Dissent and Theology
John Shute Barrington, 1st Viscount (1678–1734), was an English theologian and lawyer who belonged to the class of Protestant dissenters. In 1701, he published several pamphlets advocating for the civil rights of this group. Barrington was later employed, via the recommendation of Lord Somers, to encourage Presbyterians in Scotland to support the union of the two kingdoms.

Barrington authored two significant theological works in 1725:
*   *Miscellanea Sacra or a New Method of considering so much of the History of the Apostles as is contained in Scripture* (published in two volumes, and later reprinted in three volumes in 1770 by his son, Shute).
*   *An Essay on the Several Dispensations of God to Mankind*.

## Religious Controversies and Literature
The archive notes several other works and controversies related to religious themes:
*   **The Martin Marprelate Controversy:** Documentation of this controversy includes tracts and related documents discussed in E. Arber's *Introductory Sketch to the Martin Marprelate Controversy* (1880) and H. M. Dexter's *Congregationalism* (1880). Some of these tracts were reprinted in Arber's *English Scholar's Library* and Petheram's *Puritan Discipline Tracts* (1842–1860).
*   **Samuel Butler:** Works ascribed to Butler include *The Morning's Salutation, or a friendly conference between a puritan preacher and a family of his flock*, which was reprinted in Dublin in 1714, and *The Geneva Ballad, or the occasional conformist display'd* (1674).
