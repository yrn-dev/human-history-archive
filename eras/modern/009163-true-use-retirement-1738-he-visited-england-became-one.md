# True Use of Retirement

The *True Use of Retirement* is a written work authored by Bolingbroke. He composed this piece during a period of his life spent in France, where he resided primarily at the chateau of Argeville, located near Fontainebleau, after having retired from political activity feeling disappointed and baffled. During this same period of residence in France, Bolingbroke also wrote his *Letters on the Study of History*, which was published in 1752 after being printed privately before his death.

## Political Activities and Later Life

Following his period of retirement in France, Bolingbroke visited England in 1738. During this visit, he became an adviser and one of the leading friends of Frederick, the prince of Wales, who was then the head of the opposition. For this purpose, Bolingbroke wrote *The Patriot King*. This work, along with *The State of Parties at the Accession of George I.* and an essay titled *The Spirit of Patriotism*, were entrusted to Pope and remained unpublished at that time.

Bolingbroke returned to France in 1739 after failing to secure a share in politics, and he subsequently sold Dawley. He made further visits to England in 1742 and 1743, during which time he quarrelled with Warburton. In 1744, he settled permanently at Battersea with Hugh Hume, the 3rd earl of Marchmont. While at Battersea, he was present when Pope died in May. 

## Publications and Disputes

A conflict arose when it was discovered that Pope had secretly printed 1,500 copies of *The Patriot King*. This prompted Bolingbroke to publish a correct version of the work in 1749. This event led to further altercations with Warburton, who defended Pope against the bitter aspersions Bolingbroke leveled at him. Bolingbroke's conduct during this time was generally reprehended, and he published a piece titled *Familiar Epistle to the most Impudent Man Living*.

In addition to these works, Bolingbroke wrote an unfinished pamphlet around 1749 titled *Present State of the Nation*. His writing also influenced others; for example, the future king George III was inspired by the high notions of the duties and powers of the sovereign found in Bolingbroke's *Patriot King*.

## Final Years and Death

In 1744, Bolingbroke was heavily involved in assisting negotiations to establish the new "broad bottom" administration. He did not show sympathy for the Jacobite expedition of 1745 and recommended the tutor for Prince George (the future George III).

Bolingbroke died on December 12, 1751; his wife had died previously in 1750. Both are buried in the parish church at Battersea. A monument was erected to their memory featuring inscriptions and medallions composed by Bolingbroke himself. According to Lord Chesterfield, Bolingbroke's final words were: "God who placed me here will do what He pleases with me hereafter and He knows best what to do."
