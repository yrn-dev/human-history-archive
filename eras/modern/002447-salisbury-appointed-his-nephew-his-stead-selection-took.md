# Lord Salisbury and the Appointment of Mr. Balfour

## Early Administrative Roles of Mr. Balfour
During his first administration, which lasted from June 1885 to January 1886, Lord Salisbury appointed Mr. Balfour as the president of the Local Government Board. In his second administration, formed in July 1886, Lord Salisbury appointed him as the secretary for Scotland and granted him a seat in the cabinet. These specific offices were regarded as an apprenticeship to departmental responsibilities, as they offered few opportunities for distinction.

## Appointment as Chief Secretary for Ireland
A shift in Mr. Balfour's career occurred when Sir Michael Hicks-Beach, the chief secretary for Ireland, resigned due to an affection of the eyes. Lord Salisbury appointed his nephew, Mr. Balfour, to take his place. This selection was met with criticism and took the political world by surprise. Irish Nationalists responded with contemptuous ridicule, as they did not suspect Mr. Balfour's debating power, his ability to attack, his immense strength of will, or his capacity to disregard criticism.

## Governance of Ireland
Mr. Balfour's tenure as chief secretary occurred during one of the most exciting and turbulent periods of Irish administration and parliamentary history. He implemented a policy of "coercion," which involved the fearless administration of the Crimes Act combined with remedial legislation. He pursued both paths regardless of insults within the House or the risk of outrage outside of it. 

In parliament, Mr. Balfour waged successful warfare against the entire Home Rule party and withstood the assaults of the Irish Nationalists. He is noted for having combined a mastery of facts unsurpassed by his predecessors in the secretaryship with an obstinacy of will. Through his remorseless vigour and unfaltering courage, he broke down the Plan of Campaign in Ireland. His performance eventually convinced the country and the House of Commons that he was in the right place as chief secretary.

## Later Political Activity and Fiscal Policy
Mr. Balfour later became a prominent Conservative politician, second only to Lord Salisbury in terms of being admired and attacked. By October 1st, during a crisis involving free-trade Unionists, Mr. Balfour spoke at Sheffield regarding retaliation and free-trade. He declared his intention to lead and expressed readiness to reverse traditional fiscal policy by removing the axiom that import duties should only be levied for revenue purposes. 

While this speech was enthusiastically received by the National Union of Conservative Associations—who were predominantly in sympathy with Mr. Chamberlain—it was disliked by free-traders. This tension led to the resignation of the duke of Devonshire. Mr. Balfour resented this step, believing his efforts to conciliate the duke had been in vain.

## Other Appointments by Lord Salisbury
Lord Salisbury's selections extended beyond his family. Following the retirement of Sir William Baker from the India Council in the autumn of 1875, Lord Salisbury selected Yule for the vacant seat. Yule followed Lord Salisbury with conviction and steady confidence. Additionally, when Lord Salisbury became prime minister in 1885, Sir Stafford Henry Northcote (the 1st Earl of Iddesleigh) was included in the cabinet as first lord of the treasury.
