# Cubic Symmetry and Related Measurements

The term "cubic" appears across various scientific and technical disciplines, referring to geometric symmetry in mineralogy, cellular structures in biology, volumetric measurements in waste and fuel management, and mathematical laws of physics.

## Cubic Symmetry in Mineralogy
In the study of crystals, specifically boracite, certain characters may appear to be strictly in accordance with cubic symmetry. However, when these crystals are examined using polarized light, they are revealed to be doubly refracting, a phenomenon first observed by Sir David Brewster in 1821. Further examination of thin sections reveals a division into optically biaxial areas and twin-lamellae.

It can be proven through the cutting of sections in suitable directions that a rhombic dodecahedral crystal is actually composed of twelve orthorhombic pyramids. In this structure, the bases coincide with the faces of the pseudo-cubic compound crystal, and the apices meet at the center. 

The optical characters of these crystals change when heated. At a temperature of 265 degrees, the crystals suddenly become optically isotropic, though the complexity of the internal structure reappears upon cooling. These "optical anomalies" have been attributed by some to internal strains or alteration. However, evidence suggests there are two distinct crystalline modifications of boracite: an orthorhombic (or monoclinic) modification stable at lower temperatures, and a cubic modification stable above 265 degrees. This is considered strictly analogous to silver iodide (iodyrite), which possesses both rhombohedral and cubic modifications at different temperatures, although only pseudo-cubic crystals of boracite have been encountered in nature.

## Biological Cubical Structures
In anatomy, the epithelium of various glands in the body is characterized by cells that are either conical or cubical in shape. These cells contain granular protoplasm and distinct oval nuclei. Within the protoplasm, spherical granules of varying chemical composition and size accumulate; these granules serve as stores of material to be discharged as secretions.

The physical appearance of the cubical cell varies based on its activity. A cell at rest contains many granules, which increase its size and largely hide the nucleus. Conversely, a cell that has been actively secreting appears shrunken with clearer protoplasm and a more obvious nucleus due to the extrusion of the granules.

## Volumetric and Cubic Measurements
The term "cubic" is frequently used to denote volume in the context of materials and taxation:

*   **House Refuse:** In the study of house refuse, volume is measured in cubic yards. Ordinary house refuse weighs between 12¼ and 15 cwt per cubic yard. Shop refuse, which often contains light wastes such as straw and paper, is lighter, sometimes weighing as little as 7¼ cwt per cubic yard.
*   **Maritime Fuel:** In warships, the space efficiency of fuel is a critical factor. According to usual ship bunkering allowances, 36 cubic feet of stored oil are equal in practical calorific value to 67 cubic feet of coal.
*   **Taxation:** In Holland, taxes on beer are levied on the cubic contents of the Mash-Tun or on quality.
*   **Ancient Architecture:** Historical records of an immense edifice in Egypt describe a structure with a square base of 110 fathoms and a perpendicular height of 77-3/4 fathoms, resulting in solid contents of 313,590 cubical fathoms.

## Mathematical Cubic Laws
In the field of gunnery and the study of projectiles, the ancient theory attributed to Galileo ignored air resistance, treating trajectories as parabolas. To provide more practical value, theorists such as Newton and Euler proposed hypotheses regarding resistance that varies as a simple power of velocity. This includes the "cubic law," where resistance varies as the cube of the velocity. Such theoretical assumptions lead to results of great analytical complexity and are primarily used for provisional extrapolation at high or low velocities until further experimental data is obtained.
