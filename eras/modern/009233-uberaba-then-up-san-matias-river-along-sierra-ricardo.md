# Uberaba, the San Matias River, and Sierra Ricardo Franco

The geographical area encompassing Uberaba, the San Matias river, and the Sierra Ricardo Franco forms a critical segment of the international boundary between Bolivia and Brazil. This frontier is the result of colonial limits established by the treaties of San Ildefonso (1777) and Madrid (1750), which were subsequently modified by treaties in 1867 and 1903.

## Boundary Course and Landmarks
The boundary line begins at the outlet of Bahia Negra into the Paraguay river (lat. 28 deg. 08' 35" S.). From there, the line ascends the Paraguay river to a point on the west bank located 9 kilometres below Fort Coimbra. It then moves inland 4 kilometres to a specific point at lat. 19 deg. 45' 36" S. and long. 58 deg. 04' 12.7" W.

From this coordinate, the boundary follows an irregular course moving North and East of North. This path leads to Lakes Gaiba (or Gahiba), Mandiore, and Uberaba. Following these landmarks, the boundary line proceeds up the San Matias river and continues North along the Sierra Ricardo Franco. This trajectory persists until it reaches the headwaters of the Rio Verde, which is a tributary of the Guapore.

## Territorial Modifications
The specific routing of the boundary in this region was designed to turn inland from the Paraguay river. This adjustment ensured that several settlements on the west bank, including Corumba and Fort Coimbra, remained within Brazilian jurisdiction. 

Further modifications occurred in 1903, when approximately 1158 sq. m. of territory were ceded to Bolivia. This change was implemented to provide improved commercial facilities on the Paraguay river.

## Regional Hydrology and Connectivity
The boundary continues from the Rio Verde to follow the Guapore, Madeira, and Mamore rivers down to the mouth of the Abuna (approximately lat. 9 deg. 44' S.), as determined by the treaty of 1903. 

The broader river systems of Bolivia are divided into three regions: the Central Plateau, La Plata, and the Amazon. The Amazon system, which includes the rivers mentioned in the boundary's course, covers more than one-half of the republic. The Mamore and Beni rivers are the two principal rivers of this system, uniting at lat. 10 deg. 20' S. to form the Madeira.
