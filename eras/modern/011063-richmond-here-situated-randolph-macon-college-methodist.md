# Richmond

The archive's records identify Richmond as a central geographic reference point for several cities and institutions across Virginia and Kentucky.

## Virginia Geography and Connections
Richmond serves as a primary point of distance and transit for various Virginia municipalities. The village of Ashland, located in Hanover county, is situated 17 miles northwest of Richmond and is a popular resort for those traveling from the city via the Richmond, Fredericksburg & Potomac railway. Further west-southwest, the city of Lynchburg in Campbell county is located approximately 125 miles from Richmond on the James river. Additionally, the city of Danville, located in Pittsylvania county on the Dan river, is approximately 140 miles southwest of Richmond by rail. Danville is connected to Richmond by a branch of the Southern railway.

## Historical Events
Richmond is noted in the archives in relation to the end of the American Civil War. Following the evacuation of Richmond on April 2, 1865, the archives of the Confederacy were transported to the city of Danville. During this period, President Jefferson Davis paused in Danville for several days while continuing his flight southward.

## Educational Institutions in the Region
Several educational institutions are located in the vicinity of Richmond, most notably those associated with the "Randolph-Macon System of Colleges and Academies." This system is governed by a single board of trustees to correlate the work of its preparatory schools with its colleges.

The Randolph-Macon College (Methodist Episcopal, South) is located in Ashland. It is recognized as one of the oldest Methodist Episcopal colleges in the United States. After receiving its charter, it opened in 1832 near Boydton in Mecklenburg county before being moved to Ashland in 1868. During the 1907-1908 period, the college maintained a faculty of 16 and a student body of 150. The institution publishes an endowed historical series known as *The John P. Branch Historical Papers of Randolph-Macon College*.

Other institutions within the Randolph-Macon System include:
*   **Randolph-Macon Woman's College:** Opened in 1893 in Lynchburg, this college is conducted by a self-perpetuating board under the auspices of the Methodist Episcopal Church, South. In 1907-1908, it had an enrolment of 390 students.
*   **Randolph-Macon Institute:** A school for girls located in Danville, which was admitted into the system in 1897.
*   **Randolph-Macon Academy:** Two boys' academies established in 1890 at Bedford City and in 1892 at Front Royal, Virginia.

## Other Regional References
The archive also contains records of a "Central University" that opened at Richmond, Kentucky, in 1874. This institution later consolidated with Centre College (which opened in Danville in 1823) in 1901 to form the Central University of Kentucky, a Presbyterian institution.
