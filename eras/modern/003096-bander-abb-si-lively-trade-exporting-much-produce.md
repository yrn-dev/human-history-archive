# Bander Abbasi

Bander Abbasi is a Persian town located on the northern shore of the Persian Gulf at 27deg 11' N. and 56deg 17' E. It has a population of approximately 10,000 people and is characterized by bad water and an insalubrious climate. Administratively, it is part of the "Persian Gulf ports" division, which is overseen by a governor residing at Bushire.

## History and Nomenclature
Until 1622, the town was known as Gombrun (also referred to as Gombroon, Gamaroon, or Cambarao and Comorao by Portuguese writers). It was renamed Bander Abbasi, meaning the "port of Abbas," in honor of Shah Abbas I. The Shah had destroyed a fort built by the Portuguese in 1612 and expelled the Portuguese from the area in 1614.

Following these events, the English were permitted to establish a factory in the town, and the Dutch obtained similar privileges around 1620. In 1622, the English and Persians captured the island of Hormuz (Ormus), which resulted in a large portion of that island's trade being transferred to Bander Abbasi. While traffic remained considerable for the rest of the 17th century, prosperity declined during the 18th century, and much of the trade shifted to Bushire. In 1759, the French destroyed the English factory; although it was later re-established, it has long since been abandoned, and its ruins remain west of the current town.

## Political Administration and Territorial Disputes
The town and district, including the town of Minab and the fort of Shamil, as well as the islands of Larak, Hormuz, and Kishm, were granted to the Arab tribe of the Beni Ma'ini by Nadir Shah around 1740 in exchange for a yearly tribute or rent. Approximately 40 years later, Sultan bin Ahmad, the ruler of Muscat, occupied the town after the Arab inhabitants appealed to him for assistance against Persian misrule. The Persian government confirmed this possession via a firman, provided a yearly rent of a few thousand tomans was paid. The islands were also considered the property of Muscat.

In 1852, Persians expelled the Muscat authorities, but they later retired when Muscat agreed to pay an increased rent. A 1856 treaty between Persia and Muscat stipulated that the islands and the Bander Abbasi town and district were Persian territory leased to Muscat for an annual rent of 14,000 tomans (L6000). Although intended to last twenty years, the Persians used the assassination of the sultan of Muscat, Seyed Thuweini, in 1866 to install their own nominee as governor of the district.

## Trade and Commerce
Bander Abbasi maintains a lively trade, serving as a hub for importing goods into Khorasan and other districts, while exporting much of the produce from south-eastern and central Persia. Between 1890 and 1905, the average annual value of total imports and exports was approximately L660,000. This was comprised of exports averaging L260,000 (L155,000 British) and imports averaging L400,000 (L340,000 British).

The port is supported by telegraph and post offices. Mail steamers from the British India Steam Navigation Company call at the port on a weekly basis. International representation is present in the town via consuls from Russia and Great Britain. British shipping maintains a dominant presence; of the 255,000 tons of shipping that entered Bander Abbasi in 1905, 237,000 tons were British.
