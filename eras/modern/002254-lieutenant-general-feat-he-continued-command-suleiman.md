# Lieutenant-General

The rank of lieutenant-general is a high-level military position found across various national armies and historical periods. Officers holding this rank have commanded divisions, armies, and specialized military districts, often serving as key strategic leaders during major conflicts.

## British Military Officers
Several British soldiers achieved the rank of lieutenant-general through distinguished service in various global campaigns. Sir Rufane Shaw Donkin (1773–1841) became a lieutenant-general in 1821, following a career that included service in the West Indies, Denmark, and Portugal, as well as a period administering the colony at the Cape of Good Hope. William Cadogan, 1st Earl (1675–1726), was promoted to lieutenant-general on January 1, 1709, after serving as a confidential staff officer and quartermaster-general under Marlborough, notably organizing the march of the allies to the Danube.

Valentine Baker (1827–1887), though initially a British soldier who served in the Kaffir war and the Crimean War, later entered the service of Turkey during its war with Russia. After sustaining a brilliant rearguard action at Tashkessan with a division of infantry, he was promoted to the rank of *Ferik* (lieutenant-general). In this capacity, he continued to command Suleiman's rearguard with distinction before moving into an administrative post following the peace.

## French Military Officers
The rank of lieutenant-general was prominent within the French military hierarchy. François Marie, the first comte de Broglie (1611–1656), died as a lieutenant-general during the siege of Valenza on July 2, 1656. His son, Victor Maurice, Comte de Broglie (1647–1727), attained the rank in 1688 before eventually becoming a marshal of France in 1724.

Another member of the family, François Marie, later the Duc de Broglie (1671–1745), was made lieutenant-general in 1710. He served with Villars during the final campaign of the War of the Spanish Succession and fought at the battle of Denain. He later held several high-ranking positions, including director-general of cavalry and dragoons, and was eventually named marshal of France in 1734.

## Other National and Modern Commands
In Belgium, Henri Alexis Brialmont (1821–1903), a military engineer and general, was promoted to lieutenant-general in 1877. Brialmont served as the inspector-general of fortifications and the corps of engineers and commanded the Antwerp military district. His expertise led him to advise the government of Greece on defense and the government of Rumania on the fortification of Bucharest.

In more recent military history, the rank was utilized in the organization of large-scale army structures. During the constitution of a second army, the immediate command of the First Army was assigned to Lieutenant General Hunter Liggett, while the command of the Second Army—whose divisions were positioned in the Woevre—was given to Lieutenant General Robert L. Bullard. Bullard had previously served as the commander of the Third Corps and the First Division.
