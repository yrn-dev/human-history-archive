# Libraries and Specialized Collections of the Archive

The Human History Archive contains records of various significant libraries and specialized bibliographic collections across Europe, North Africa, and the Middle East. These institutions are characterized by their vast holdings of printed volumes and manuscripts (MSS.), often focusing on oriental literature, hagiography, and regional histories.

## University and State Libraries in Germany

Several German cities house extensive libraries integrated into their educational and governmental infrastructures. In Breslau, the university—founded in 1702 by Emperor Leopold I as a Jesuit college and expanded in 1811 via the incorporation of the university of Frankfort-on-Oder—maintains a library containing 4000 MSS. and 306,000 volumes. This library includes a valuable collection of oriental literature known as the *Bibliotheca Habichtiana*.

In Darmstadt, the capital of the grand-duchy of Hesse-Darmstadt, the state's scientific and art collections were moved from the palace to a new museum erected in 1905. This complex houses a library consisting of 4000 MSS. and 600,000 volumes, alongside the state archives and various antiquity museums.

## The Khedivial Library and Arab Museum

Located in a building opened in 1903 at a cost of £66,000, the khedivial library contains approximately 64,000 volumes. Over two-thirds of these holdings consist of MSS. and books in Syriac, Turkish, Persian, Arabic, and Amharic. The library is particularly noted for its Arabic section, which holds a unique collection of 2,677 korans, while the Persian section is distinguished by its illuminated MSS. The adjacent Arab museum preserves treasures of Saracenic art, including objects relocated from mosques for security.

## The Bibliotheca Orientalis and Vatican Collections

The Vatican library has been the site of extensive efforts to collect and edit oriental manuscripts. Joseph Simon, a Maronite of Mount Lebanon born in 1687, was transferred to the Vatican library and later served as its librarian and the titular archbishop of Tyre. Between 1717 and 1735, he conducted two expeditions to Syria and Egypt, returning with approximately 150 choice MSS. and a subsequent, more valuable collection.

Simon executed a plan to publish the Vatican's MS. treasures in a work titled *Bibliotheca Orientalis Clementino-Vaticana*, published between 1719 and 1728. This work was intended to be divided into four parts:
1. Orthodox, Jacobite, and Nestorian Syrian and allied MSS.
2. Christian and Mahommedan Arabian MSS.
3. Turkish, Persian, Aethiopic, and Coptic MSS.
4. Arabian and Syrian MSS. that were not distinctively theological.

Only the first three volumes were completed, though extensive preparations were made for the remaining sections. Joseph Simon Assemannus, a learned Maronite sent by Pope Clement XI in 1715 to Egypt and Syria, also contributed to the *Bibliotheca Orientalis Clementino-Vaticana*. His four folio volumes (published 1719–1728) included a 950-page folio volume dedicated to a dissertation on the state of the Nestorians.

## The Escorial and the Bibliotheca Arabico-Hispana

The library of the Escorial in Spain contains significant Arabic holdings. Miguel Casiri (1710–1791), a Maronite who served as the principal librarian at the Escorial, published the *Bibliotheca Arabico-Hispana Escurialensis* (1760–1770). This two-volume catalogue identifies over 1,800 Arabic MSS. classified by subject. The second volume focuses on historical and geographical MSS. providing information on the wars between Christians and Moors in Spain. Additionally, the Escorial library contains paper MSS. dating back to the 4th or 5th century of the Hegira.

## Hagiographic Research and the Bollandists

Since 1882, the Bollandists have applied historical methods to the study of hagiography. They established a quarterly review, *Analecta Bollandiana*, which by 1906 had reached its 25th volume. This publication includes dissertations, inedited texts, and a *Bulletin des publications hagiographiques* (since 1892). 

The Bollandists analyzed hagiographic MSS. in major libraries and published various catalogues, including those of Venice, Messina, Ghent, Namur, and Chartres. They produced separate volumes for Latin MSS. in the Bibliotheque Royale at Brussels (1886–1889), Latin and Greek MSS. in the Bibliotheque Nationale at Paris (1889–1896), Greek MSS. in the Vatican (1899), and Latin MSS. in Roman libraries (1905 onwards). Their inventories include the *Bibliotheca hagiographica latina* (1899), the *Bibliotheca hagiographica graeca* (1895), and the *Bibliotheca hagiographica Orientalis*.
