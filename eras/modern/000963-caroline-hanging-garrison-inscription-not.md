# Caroline

The name Caroline appears in historical records across several centuries, associated with European royalty, political figures, and specific historical periods.

## Queen Caroline of Great Britain and Ireland
Caroline (1683–1737) was the wife of King George II of Great Britain and Ireland. Born on March 1, 1683, at Ansbach, she was the daughter of John Frederick, margrave of Brandenburg-Ansbach. She spent much of her youth in Berlin and Dresden, where she developed a close friendship with Sophie Charlotte, the wife of Frederick I of Prussia. In September 1705, she married George Augustus, the electoral prince of Hanover, and spent the early years of her marriage in Hanover.

Caroline maintained a correspondence with Leibnitz and held a friendly relationship with the old electress Sophia. In October 1714, she moved to London following her husband and father-in-law, King George I. As the princess of Wales, she was popular and tactful in her courtly duties. However, a serious quarrel between the prince of Wales and the king in 1717 led to Caroline and her husband being driven from court and deprived of the custody of their children. During this period, they resided at Richmond and Leicester House, where Caroline cultivated a circle of literary figures, including Pope, Gay, Lord Hervey, and Lord Chesterfield.

Following a formal reconciliation with George I in 1720, Caroline and George II were crowned in October 1727. As queen, she exercised significant political influence, primarily in support of Sir Robert Walpole, whom she helped keep in power and in control of church patronage. She was noted for her tolerance, appointing bishops based on learning rather than orthodoxy. On four occasions, she served as regent of the kingdom during the king's absences from England.

## Maria Annunciata Caroline
Maria Annunciata Caroline (1782–1839) was born in Ajaccio on March 25, 1782. In early 1800, she married Joachim Murat. Described as having an ambitious and intriguing nature, she worked to advance her husband's career. Murat held several high-ranking positions, including marshal of France (1804), governor of Paris, grand duke of Cleves and Berg (1806), and lieutenant of the emperor in Spain (1808). In the summer of 1808, he became the king of Naples.

Caroline's relationship with Napoleon became strained due to her displeasure with the distance between Naples and Paris. She associated herself with her husband's equivocal movements between 1814 and 1815. Following Murat's death at Pizzo on October 13, 1815, Caroline retired to Austrian territory, where she was placed under some restraint. She spent her final years at Trieste with her sister Elisa and died on May 18, 1839.

## Fort Caroline and the Spanish Conflict
Fort Caroline was a French post in Florida established by Huguenots. In 1565, Philip II of Spain sought to prevent other powers from possessing Florida due to its location on the trade route from Mexico. He entered into an *asiento* (contract) on March 20, 1565, with a seaman named Aviles, providing 15,000 ducats for Aviles to establish a colony.

Aviles arrived in Florida on July 28, 1565, with 1,500 men, ten sloops, and one 600-ton vessel. After naming and establishing a fort at the Bay of St Augustine on August 28, Aviles captured Fort Caroline on September 20, 1565. In October, he exterminated a group of Frenchmen led by Jean Ribault who had come to relieve the colony. Aviles slew nearly all the prisoners and hung their bodies from trees with the inscription, "Not as Frenchmen but as Lutherans." Aviles viewed the Frenchmen as heretics and pirates.

In 1568, a French sea-captain named Dominique de Gourgues sought revenge for the massacre. He captured the fort, which the Spanish had renamed Fort San Mateo, and hung the garrison with the inscription, "Not as Spaniards but as murderers." Aviles remained in Florida until 1567, returned for one more voyage, and died on September 17, 1574.
