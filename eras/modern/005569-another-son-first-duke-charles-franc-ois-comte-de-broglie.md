# CHARLES FRANÇOIS, COMTE DE BROGLIE

Charles François, Comte de Broglie (1719-1781), was a prominent French diplomat and soldier who served during the reign of Louis XV. He was a son of the first duke of the noble French family de Broglie.

## Military and Diplomatic Career
The Comte de Broglie spent several years serving in the army before transitioning into diplomacy. He eventually became one of the most distinguished diplomatists in the service of King Louis XV. 

He is primarily remembered for his involvement with the *Secret du Roi*. This was a private diplomatic service maintained by Louis XV, which operated independently and distinctly from the official diplomatic channels of the French state. Within this private service, Charles François was regarded as the most important and ablest member.

## Family Background
The de Broglie family was originally Piedmontese and emigrated to France in 1643. The family's head at the time of emigration, François Marie (1611-1656), assumed the title of comte de Broglie and served as a lieutenant-general, dying during the siege of Valenza on July 2, 1656. 

He was succeeded by his son, Victor Maurice, Comte de Broglie (1647-1727), who served under commanders such as Turenne and Condé during the era of Louis XIV. Victor Maurice rose through the military ranks, becoming a *maréchal de camp* in 1676, a lieutenant-general in 1688, and eventually a marshal of France in 1724.

## Descendants
Charles François was the father of Victor Claude, Prince de Broglie (1757-1794). Like his father, Victor Claude served in the army, where he attained the rank of *maréchal de camp*. He also served in America alongside Rochambeau and Lafayette.

Victor Claude adopted revolutionary opinions and became a member of the Jacobin Club and the Constituent Assembly, where he consistently voted on the Liberal side. He held the position of chief of the staff for the Republican army on the Rhine. However, during the Terror, he was arrested and denounced, leading to his execution in Paris on June 27, 1794. Before his death, he admonished his young son to remain faithful to the principles of the Revolution, regardless of whether those principles were ungrateful or unjust.
