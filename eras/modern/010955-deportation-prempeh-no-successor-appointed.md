# The Administration of Ashanti After the Deportation of Prempeh

## Political Transition and British Administration
Following the deportation of Prempeh, no successor was appointed to the throne of Ashanti. In the wake of this vacancy, a British protectorate was established and Prempeh was deposed. The British government installed Captain Donald W. Stewart, a British resident, at Kumasi to oversee the region. 

While the other states within the Ashanti confederacy were permitted to retain their tribal systems and their respective kings, the affairs of Kumasi were administered by chiefs under the guidance of the British. To secure the area, a fort was constructed in Kumasi and garrisoned with the Gold Coast constabulary. During this period, missionaries returned to the city, including Mr. and Mrs. Ramseyer, who had previously been imprisoned for four and a half years by King Kofi Karikari.

## The Golden Stool and Rising Tensions
Despite an outward appearance of submission, the chiefs of Kumasi remained unreconciled to British rule. Central to this tension was the golden stool, the symbol of sovereignty, which the Kumasi had kept hidden since 1896. 

On March 28, 1900, Sir F. Hodgson, the governor of the Gold Coast, held a public palaver at Kumasi. During this meeting, he announced that the Ashanti chiefs were required to pay the British government 4000 oz. of gold annually. Sir F. Hodgson further reproached the chiefs for failing to deliver the golden stool to him.

## The Rebellion of 1900
A serious rebellion broke out in 1900, driven in part by the Kumasi's desire to erase the dishonour of allowing Prempeh to be deported without a fight. The rebellion involved the Kokofu, Adansi, and Kumasi tribes, although other tribes within the Ashanti confederation remained loyal to the British. The rebel forces were reported to number 40,000.

Hostilities escalated three days after the public palaver when Kumasi warriors attacked a party of Hausa who had been sent specifically to discover the location of the golden stool; this followed a failed secret attempt to seize the stool in January of that year. The rebels subsequently threatened the fort at Kumasi.

## Military Conflict and Reinforcements
As the situation deteriorated, Sir F. and Lady Hodgson, along with the Basel missionaries and Mr. Ramseyer, took refuge inside the Kumasi fort. Urgent requests for reinforcements were made, leading to the following military arrivals:
* **April 18:** 100 Gold Coast constabulary arrived.
* **April 29:** The Kumasi launched a forceful attack but were repulsed. On this same day, 250 Lagos constabulary reached Kumasi, though they arrived with little ammunition after fighting their way up.
* **May 15:** Major A. Morris arrived from the British territory north of Ashanti with an additional 250 men.

By this time, the garrison numbered 700 personnel, which included 29 Europeans and four women.

## Historical Context of the Ashanti Monarchy
The instability leading up to the British protectorate followed a period of internal volatility. After the war of 1873-74, several states dependent on Ashanti sought British protection, which was initially refused. Following the war, the Ashanti deposed Kofi Karikari and placed his brother, Mensa, on the golden stool. Mensa's reign was characterized by tyranny and repeated human sacrifices; he also failed to pay the stipulated indemnity, paying only 4000 oz. of the 50,000 oz. of gold required by the Fomana treaty.

Mensa was displaced by a revolution in 1883 and succeeded by Kwaka Dua II, who died in June 1884. The former king, Kofi Karikari, also died in June 1884, leading to a desolating civil war. On March 26, 1888, Prince Prempeh (taking the name Kwaka Dua III) was chosen as king with the sanction of a Gold Coast government officer. While Prempeh initially brought peace and prosperity, fresh trouble arose in 1893 when he ordered the closure of roads to traders. This led to British interference, as the region was included in the British sphere of influence by international agreement. Prempeh eventually declined to treat with the governor of the Gold Coast and failed to respond to British demands to accept a protectorate by October 31, 1895.
