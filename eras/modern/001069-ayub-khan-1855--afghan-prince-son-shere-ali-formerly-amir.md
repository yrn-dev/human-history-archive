# AYUB KHAN

Ayub Khan (born c. 1855) was an Afghan prince and the son of Shere Ali, the former amir of Afghanistan. He was also a cousin of the amir Abdur Rahman.

## Early Life and Rise to Power
During the reign of his father, Shere Ali, there is little recorded information regarding Ayub Khan. However, following the expulsion of Shere Ali from Kabul by the English and his subsequent death in January 1879, Ayub Khan took possession of Herat. He maintained his position there until June 1881.

## Military Campaigns and Conflict with the British
In June 1881, Ayub Khan invaded Afghanistan to assert his claims to sovereignty. His primary objective was to gain possession of Kandahar, which was then occupied by the British. On July 27, he encountered a British force commanded by General Burrows at Maiwand. Ayub Khan won this engagement, marking one of the few pitched battles won by an Asiatic leader over an army under European direction.

This victory was short-lived. While Ayub Khan hesitated to launch an assault on Kandahar, he was attacked on September 20, 1880, by Sir Frederick (later Lord) Roberts, who had completed a memorable march from Kabul. Ayub Khan was utterly discomfited in this encounter and retreated to Herat, where he remained for a period without being molested.

## Conflict with Abdur Rahman and Exile
In the summer of 1881, Ayub Khan invaded Afghanistan once more. On the anniversary of the battle of Maiwand, he achieved a signal victory over the lieutenants of Abdur Rahman, a success attributed mainly to the defection of a Durani regiment. Although Kandahar fell into his hands, Abdur Rahman personally took the field, totally defeated Ayub Khan, and expelled him from Herat.

Following this defeat, Ayub Khan took refuge in Persia. For a time, he lived quietly while receiving an allowance from the Persian government.

## Final Attempts at Power and Captivity
In 1887, internal troubles within Afghanistan prompted Ayub Khan to make another attempt to seize the throne. This effort failed; he was defeated and driven into exile. After wandering through Persia for some time, he surrendered himself to the British agent at Meshed in November. He was subsequently sent to India, where he lived as a state prisoner.
