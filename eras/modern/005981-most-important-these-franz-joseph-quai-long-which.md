# Franz Joseph Quai

The Franz Joseph Quai is a significant waterfront thoroughfare located in Budapest. It is part of a line of broad quays situated on the left bank of the Danube, which extend for a distance of 2½ miles from the custom-house to the Margaret bridge.

## Characteristics and Importance
The Franz Joseph Quai is 1 mile in length and is considered the most important of the quays on the left bank. It serves as a favorite promenade for the public and is home to the city's most fashionable hotels and cafés. The area is characterized by the presence of imposing buildings lining the waterfront.

## Urban Context and Connectivity
The quai is situated within the broader urban layout of Budapest, which covers an area of 78 square miles. The city is divided into ten municipal districts. The left bank, which contains the Franz Joseph Quai, belongs to Pest and is the industrial and commercial center of the town, as well as the hub for intellectual and nationalistic life. 

The surrounding infrastructure of the inner town includes the Innere Ring-Strasse, a circle of wide boulevards built on the site of the old wall. This inner ring is connected via the Váczi Körut (Waitzner-Ring) to the Grosse Ring-Strasse. The Grosse Ring-Strasse consists of a succession of boulevards forming a semicircle that begins at the Margaret bridge and ends at the Boráros Platz, located near the custom-house quay.

Other notable streets in the vicinity include the Király Utcza, the Kerrepesi Ut, and the Üllöi Ut, which act as lines of demarcation between different districts. Additionally, the Andrássy Ut, a 1½ mile long thoroughfare, connects the Váczi Körut with Városliget (Stadtwäldchen), the favorite public park of the city.

## Regional Infrastructure
The Franz Joseph Quai exists within a city connected by six bridges spanning the Danube. These include two suspension bridges: the Schwurplatz bridge (completed in 1903) and the Ketten-Brücke (constructed between 1842 and 1849). The Ketten-Brücke is one of Europe's largest, measuring 410 yards long and 39 feet broad. Other crossings include the Margaret bridge, the Franz Joseph bridge, and two railway bridges.
