# Health and Climate of European Settlements

The health and viability of European settlements in various global regions have historically been closely tied to local climate, geography, and environmental conditions. Records indicate a wide variance in health outcomes, ranging from areas described as decidedly healthy to those where fever and humidity led to the abandonment of colonies.

## West African Climates
In West Africa, the healthfulness of a location often depends on its proximity to the coast and the presence of specific weather patterns. In the region of Ashanti, the climate is noted as being unsuited for the prolonged residence of Europeans; however, it is considered less unhealthy than the climate found in the coast towns of West Africa. This area is characterized by a mean temperature at Kumasi of 76 deg. F. and a rainy season that lasts from the end of May until October, featuring frequent and violent storms.

Conversely, some coastal towns offer better conditions. Kotonu, a town laid out by Europeans on a definite plan, is described as being decidedly healthy for white men in comparison with other coast towns. This is attributed to an almost continuous, cool, westerly sea-breeze.

## The Americas
European colonization efforts in the Americas encountered significant health challenges in certain environments. In the early 16th century, Spanish adventurer Lucas Vasquez de Ayllon established a town called San Miguel (located either near the mouth of the Pedee river or where Jamestown, Va., was later built). The site was unhealthy, and fever resulted in the deaths of many colonists, including Ayllon himself, who died on October 18, 1526. Following his death and subsequent rebellions by some of the African slaves, the town was abandoned in December.

In Colombia, the Pacific port of Buenaventura is described as having a very unhealthy climate that is oppressively hot and humid. The town is situated on the island of Cascajal at the head of a broad estuary.

## South Africa and Other Regions
In South Africa, the city of Durban serves as a principal seaport and the largest city of Natal. While its climate is generally humid and hot, it is recorded as not being unhealthy. 

In other regions, such as the Shiré Highlands, European settlements were established at higher elevations. Blantyre, founded in 1876 by the Church of Scotland mission, is situated at a height of approximately 3000 ft. above the sea. Other European sites in the region include Zomba, Port Herald, Chiromo, and various forts and townships such as Fort Anderson, Fort Johnston, and Fort Jameson.
