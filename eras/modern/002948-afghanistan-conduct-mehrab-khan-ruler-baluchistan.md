# Mehrab Khan and the British Advance in Baluchistan

## The 1839 Campaign and the Assault on Kalat
In 1839, the British army advanced through the Bolan Pass toward Afghanistan. During this operation, the conduct of Mehrab Khan, the ruler of Baluchistan, was perceived by British authorities as dangerous and treacherous. This assessment led to the conclusion that retribution against the chieftain was necessary to establish future security in the region.

In response, General Willshire was detached from the army of the Indus with a force of 1,050 men to assault Kalat. The British forces used field-pieces to knock in a gate, allowing them to storm the citadel and the town within a few minutes. The assault resulted in the deaths of over 400 Baluches, including Mehrab Khan, and the capture of 2,000 prisoners.

## Subsequent Inquiries and the Role of Mahommed Hussein
Following the events at Kalat, subsequent inquiries revealed that the perceived treachery toward the British had not been committed by Mehrab Khan. Instead, the responsibility lay with his vizier, Mahommed Hussein, and a group of allied chiefs. These individuals had instigated the plundering of British convoys as they passed through the Bolan Pass and Kach Gandava.

The vizier, Mahommed Hussein, had manipulated "too credulous" British political officers into believing that Mehrab Khan was the party to blame. His objective was to bring his master to ruin so that he could seize all power in the state for himself, knowing that the successor to Mehrab Khan was only a child.

## Political Aftermath and Succession
The instability following the death of Mehrab Khan continued into the following year. The governor and a "feeble garrison" established by the British were overpowered, and Kalat changed hands. By the end of that same year, the British reoccupied the area under the command of General Nott.

In 1841, the British recognized Nasir Khan II, the youthful son of the slain Mehrab Khan, as the ruler. Shortly after this recognition, the British evacuated the country.

## Geographic and Political Context of Baluchistan
Baluchistan is a region located within the borders of British India, extending from the Arabian Sea to the Gomal river, and from the borders of Afghanistan and Persia to those of Sind and the Punjab. It serves as an intervening state between British India and Afghanistan.

The territory is divided into British Baluchistan—which includes districts such as Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali—and foreign territories. The latter consists of tribal areas, directly administered districts, and native states, including Las Bela and the state of Kalat (which includes Kharan and Makran). The state of Kalat is ruled by a khan, a title of respect in Mahommedan countries derived from the Turki word *khaqan*, meaning sovereign or emperor.
