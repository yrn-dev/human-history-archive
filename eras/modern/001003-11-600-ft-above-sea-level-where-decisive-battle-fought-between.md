# The Battle of Ayacucho

## Geography and Location
The decisive battle of 1824 took place in an elevated valley situated 11,600 ft. above sea-level. This valley is located near the village of Quinua and is associated with the small plain of Ayacucho, a name derived from Quichua meaning "corner of death." 

The region is part of central Peru. The city of Ayacucho, which serves as the capital of the province of Guamanga and the department of the same name, is situated on an elevated plateau at an altitude of 8,911 ft. above sea-level. This plateau lies between the central and western Cordilleras and is positioned on the main road connecting Cuzco and Lima, 394 m. from the latter via Jauja.

## The Decisive Battle of 1824
The elevated valley at 11,600 ft. above sea-level served as the site for a critical military engagement between the forces of General Sucre and the Spanish viceroy La Serna. The conflict resulted in the defeat of La Serna and led to the independence of Peru.

## The City of Ayacucho
Founded by Pizarro in 1539, the city was known as Guamanga until 1825. It is characterized by a temperate and agreeable climate and is regularly built. The city holds significant commercial importance and serves as the seat for a superior court of justice and a bishopric. Architecturally, it is noted for its numerous conventual establishments and churches, though the conventual establishments have since been closed. As of 1896, the population of the city was 20,000.
