# Napoleon Eugene Louis Jean Joseph

## Early Life and Military Service
Napoleon Eugene Louis Jean Joseph was born in Paris on March 16, 1856. He was the only son of Empress Eugenie and Emperor Napoleon III. Described as a delicate boy, he was sent by his mother to the army during the outbreak of the war of 1870. This decision was intended to help the prince win popularity, and government journals at the time praised his bravery. However, following the first defeats of the conflict, he was forced to flee France alongside the empress. The pair settled in England at Chislehurst, where the prince completed his military education at Woolwich.

## Proclamation as Napoleon IV
Following the death of his father on January 9, 1873, Imperialists proclaimed Napoleon Eugene as Napoleon IV. In this capacity, he became the official Pretender. While he was naturally inactive, he was subject to the influence of his mother and various Bonapartist leaders in France. These leaders believed that the prince should secure his crown through the acquisition of military prestige.

## Expedition to Zululand and Death
Persuaded by his advisors to seek military glory, Napoleon IV attached himself as a volunteer to an English expedition to Zululand in February 1879. This decision is characterized as a blunder that resulted in tragedy. On June 1, 1879, while conducting a reconnaissance with a small group of troopers, the prince and his party were surprised by Zulus. The Prince Imperial was killed during the encounter. His body was subsequently returned to England and buried at Chislehurst.
