# Jaz-morian

## Overview and Geography
Jaz-morian is a *hamun*, or depression, located in the province of Baluchistan in Persia. It measures 50 meters in length. The depression serves as the terminus for the Bampur river, which flows from east to west and empties into the Jaz-morian approximately 70 meters to the west of the town of Bampur.

## Regional Context
The Jaz-morian is situated within Persian Baluchistan, a province with an area of about 60,000 square miles. This region is bounded by Kerman to the west, Seistan and the central Persian desert to the north, and British and independent Baluchistan to the east, along the northern shore of the Arabian Sea. The landscape of the province is characterized by arid, waterless plains, barren hills, mountains, and deserts consisting of moving sands or stony ground. 

Water is scarce in the region, and only a small portion of the land is under cultivation. The Bampur river and the Mashkid are the principal rivers of the province; both flow away from the sea and are eventually lost in depressions known as *hamuns*.

## Characteristics of Hamuns
The *hamuns* of Persia and south-west Baluchistan, including the Helmund *hamuns* and the Jaz-morian, are typical of lakes that are relics of an earlier geological period. These occurred when land areas recently upheaved from the sea were spread at low levels and experienced alternate inundations of fresh and salt water. 

These bodies of water are characterized by the following:
* **Seasonal Variability:** They change their extent and form from season to season.
* **Composition:** Many are impregnated with saline deposits from the underlying strata.
* **Drainage:** They are part of a central section of the Asian continent where drainage is lost in swamps or *hamuns*, with an average level likely not exceeding 2,000 feet above sea level.

## Administrative and Local History
The town of Bampur, located at an elevation of 1,720 feet (27deg 12' N., 60deg 24' E.), serves as the capital of the province of Baluchistan. Bampur is one of twenty districts within the administrative division of the province of Kerman. 

Historically, the province was practically independent until Persian troops occupied Bampur in 1849. Persian supremacy was not recognized over some of the extreme eastern districts until 1872. 

In Bampur, an old citadel once stood on an elevation about 100 feet high, 3 meters north of the river. After this citadel fell into ruins, a new fort called Kalah Nasseri was constructed in the eighties at Fahraj, located 15 meters further east. Consequently, Fahraj (also known as Puhra, Paharu, or Pahura) has become more important than Bampur. Some identify Fahraj as the Poura where Alexander the Great halted during his march from India, though others suggest Bampur itself or another Fahraj near Bam.
