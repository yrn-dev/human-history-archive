# Magnificat

The term *Magnificat* refers to distinct musical settings composed by Johann Sebastian Bach and his son, Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach.

## The Magnificat of Johann Sebastian Bach
Johann Sebastian Bach's *Magnificat* is written in D. It is described as one of the most profound and terse works the composer ever produced. The piece is noted for its subtle inspirations and the presence of playfulness, such as the quaint musical representation of the words "dimisit inanes."

The work features specific instrumental and vocal arrangements to convey emotion. For instance, the words "quia respexit humilitatem ancillae suae" are set as a soprano solo accompanied by the oboe d'amore, which is one of Bach's favorite instruments. Following this, the tone shifts to a quiet joy during the sentence "ecce enim beatam me dicent."

From a technical perspective, the *Magnificat* serves as an example of *stretto*—the overlapping of the answer and the subject. This musical resource is specifically exemplified in the setting of the words "omnes generationes."

## The Magnificat of Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach
Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach, the third son of Johann Sebastian Bach, also composed a setting of the *Magnificat* in 1749. This work was written during his residence in Berlin, where he served in the royal household of the crown prince of Prussia (who became Frederick the Great in 1740). 

In this particular composition, Karl Philipp Emanuel Bach displays more traces of his father's influence than is usual for his work. This piece was composed during a period of high productivity in Berlin, during which he also wrote an Easter cantata (1756), several concerted works and symphonies, and various volumes of songs, including *Sing-Oden* (1766), *Oden mit Melodien* (1762), and *Geistliche Oden und Lieder* (1758).
