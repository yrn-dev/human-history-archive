# Bucharest

Bucharest serves as a central administrative and economic hub in Rumania. It is the meeting-place of parliament, the winter residence of the royal family, and the seat of several high-level governmental and judicial bodies, including the ministries, the national bank, the bank of Rumania, the supreme court (_Curtea de Casatie_), and an appeal court (_Curtea de Apel_). The city also hosts a chamber of commerce and various smaller credit establishments.

## Commerce and Industry
The city maintains a very large transit trade, specifically in timber, petroleum, and agricultural produce, with a primary focus on maize and wheat. Its industrial sector is diverse, encompassing the extraction of vegetable oils and petroleum-refining. Other manufacturing activities include:
*   **Metal and Construction:** The production of machinery, wire, nails, metal-ware, and cement.
*   **Chemicals and Consumer Goods:** The manufacture of soap, candles, paste, starch, paper, cardboard, pearl buttons, and confectionery.
*   **Textiles and Leather:** The production of textiles, leather goods, and tanning.
*   **Other Goods:** Cabinet-making, brandy-distilling, glucose production, preserved meats and vegetables, and the manufacture of army supplies and ropes.

The mercantile community in Bucharest is largely composed of exclusive colonies of Swiss, Germans, Frenchmen, Austrians, and Greeks. Additionally, the city hosts an important annual fair that lasts for seven days.

## Infrastructure and Communications
Bucharest is well-connected via railway lines that meet at the western limit of the city, providing access to all parts of the region. The city's telephone system is internally complete and provides communication links to Sinaia, Jassy, Galatz, and Braila.

## Military Significance
Bucharest is a fortress of the first rank and serves as the headquarters of the II. army corps. The city's fortifications were constructed between 1885 and 1896 based on a project drafted in 1883 by General Brialmont, a Belgian engineer. These defenses, which are considered technically important for embodying the Brialmont system, consist of 36 batteries and armoured forts. The perimeter of these defenses is approximately 48 m., with the forts situated at a mean distance of 4 m. from the city. Military infrastructure includes an arsenal, a military hospital, three military academies, and barracks capable of housing over 30,000 infantry and cavalry.
