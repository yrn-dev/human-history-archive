# Arya Samaj

The Arya Samaj is a Hindu religious association characterized by reforming tendencies. It was founded by Dayanand Saraswati, a Guzerati Brahman born into a Saivite family around 1825. In his early adulthood, Dayanand became dissatisfied with idol-worship and undertook various pilgrimages. He studied Vedic philosophy to seek a solution to the problem of how to attain final liberation and alleviate human misery, a problem attributed to the Buddha.

## Foundations and Philosophy
Around 1866, Dayanand began gathering disciples and teaching. During this period, he encountered the Christian scriptures, which he vehemently assailed, and the *Rig Veda*, which he exalted. However, the Christian scriptures remained highly influential in his ultimate conception of God. 

Dayanand's approach to the *Vedas* was distinctive, as he read his own beliefs and modern scientific discoveries into the texts. For example, he interpreted the *Yajna* (sacrificial cult) not as traditional sacrifice, but as the application of chemistry, physics, and the arts of peace, the instruction of the people, the purification of air, and the nourishment of vegetables through meteorology (referred to in Sanskrit as *Agni-Notri*). While he denied that the *Vedas* warranted the caste system, he advocated for the retention of four grades as orders of learning, which should be accessed via examination.

## Organization and Growth
Dayanand established *Samajes* across various parts of India, founding the first in Bombay in 1875. He selected the name "Arya" because he considered it more dignified than the term "Hindu," which he viewed as slightly contemptuous. Before his death at Ajmere in 1883, Dayanand conducted a series of successful tours where he engaged in public debates with Christian missionaries and orthodox pundits.

The movement appealed to educated natives whose faith in current Hinduism had been undermined but who remained predisposed against foreign religious influences. These followers were drawn to a vision of India's original faith that appeared to harmonize with the ethics of European civilization and the discoveries of modern science. This group also supported Dayanand's polemics against European rationalist and agnostic literature.

The association saw significant growth, reaching 40,000 members by 1890 and over 92,000 by 1900. Membership requires an admission age of eighteen, and members are expected to contribute at least 1% of their income to the funds of the Samaj.

## Creed and Reform
The Arya Samaj is not an eclectic system; it differs from the Brahma Samaj, which seeks a common basis among all great religions. The Arya Samaj's narrower scope and intensity of conviction contributed to its strength. Its creed consists of ten articles:

1. God is the source of all true knowledge.
2. God is described as boundless, almighty, just, merciful, all-pervading, omniscient, and the cause of the universe; he is the support and Lord of all, and worship is due to him alone.
3. The *Vedas* are the medium of true knowledge.
4. Truth must be accepted.
5. Truth must become the guiding principle.
6. The objective of the Samaj is to benefit the world by improving its moral, intellectual, social, and physical conditions.
7. Conduct should be guided by justice and love.
8. Knowledge must be spread.
9. The good of others must be sought.
10. Members should subordinate themselves to the good of others in general interests, while retaining independence in personal interests.

The sixth clause of the creed encompasses a broad reform program, which includes the promotion of female education, the abolition of idolatry and caste, marriage reform, physical exercise and cleanliness, and abstinence from animal food and spirituous liquors.

## Internal Divisions
The movement experienced internal cleavages, most notably a split between the *Mansi* (flesh-eating) and *Ghasi* (vegetarian) sections. The *Mansi* section included Rajputs who sympathized with the movement but refused to follow the tenet forbidding the consumption of animal food and the destruction of animal life.
