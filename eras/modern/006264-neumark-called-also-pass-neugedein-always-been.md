# Neumark

The pass of Neumark, also referred to as the pass of Neugedein, is a significant geographical and historical landmark located within the Bohmerwald (Bohemian Forest). It serves as the dividing point between the two parts of the Bohmerwald range: the northern section, known in Czech as *Cesky Les*, and the southern section, known as *Sumava*.

## Geography and Location
The pass of Neumark has historically served as the principal approach to Bohemia from Germany. Geographically, it stretches toward the east, situated above the small town of Taus (referred to in Czech as *Domazlice*, and formerly known as *Tuhost*, meaning "the Fortress"). 

The surrounding region of Bohemia is described as an irregular rhomb, bordered by several mountain ranges. While the Bohmerwald is one such border to the southwest, other ranges include the Erzgebirge (Ore Mountains) to the northwest and the Riesengebirge (Giant Mountains) and other Sudetes ranges to the northeast. Within the Bohmerwald, the southern part is noted as the most picturesque and highest area on the Bohemian side, featuring peaks such as the Plockenstein (4,513 ft.) and the Osser (4,053 ft.), although the highest peak of the range is the Arber (4,872 ft.), located in Bavaria. The landscape is characterized by blue lakes, crystalline torrents, and extensive forests of pine and oak.

## Military History
Due to its position as a primary entrance into Bohemia, the pass of Neumark has been the site of several significant and bloody battles:

*   **7th Century:** In the first half of the 7th century, Samo utilized the pass to repel invading hordes of Avars who threatened the independence of the Slavonic inhabitants who had recently settled in the region.
*   **11th Century:** On August 22 and 23, 1040, Wratislas II defeated the German emperor Henry III in a battle lasting two days.
*   **15th Century:** The pass was the site of a major victory for the Hussites in 1431 against a German army of crusaders. Later, another German army was vanquished at this location by George of Podebrad.
