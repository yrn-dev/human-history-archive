# Ornithology of Northern Asia

## Regional Affinities and Distribution
The ornithology of northern Asia exhibits a close alliance with that of Europe, a connection that is even more pronounced than the relationship between the mammal fauna of the two regions. This affinity is evidenced by the fact that nearly three fourths of the well-known bird species found in Europe extend through Siberia and into the islands of the Japanese empire.

The distribution of bird life in this region is heavily dependent on climatic conditions. Northern Asia is characterized by a total absence of tropical forms. Instead, there is a significant development of bird groups that are characteristic of temperate and cold regions.

## Specialized Avian Genera
Certain bird groups are restricted to the specific environments of northern and north eastern Asia:

*   **Phasianus:** This genus consists of splendid birds that are restricted to northern Asia in their wild state.
*   **Thaumalea and Crossoptilon:** The gold pheasants (_Thaumalea_) and eared pheasants (_Crossoptilon_), which are described as being more magnificently clad, are confined to specific districts within the mountains of north eastern Asia.
*   **Passeres:** Within the _Passeres_, several forms are well developed and exhibit numerous species, specifically the grosbeaks, linnets, finches, stone chats, and larks.

## Migratory Species and Global Connections
Certain species, such as the greenshank (_Totanus glottis_), demonstrate the broad reach of northern Old World ornithology. The greenshank is native to the northern parts of the Old World and likely breeds in the more northern or elevated districts of Finland, Sweden, and Norway, and probably extends from there to Kamchatka. While it breeds in these northern regions, it wanders far south during the winter, appearing regularly in India, the Indo-Malay Archipelago, and the Cape of Good Hope.

On a broader scale, the Holarctic region—which encompasses North America and the extratropical landmass of the Old World—is characterized ornithologically by the Oscines, _Gallidae_ (or Alectoropodous Galli), _Alcidae_, and Colymbi. In this region, the Oscines have reached their highest level of development. Conversely, non-Oscine Passeres (with the exception of _Conurus carolinensis_ and _Tyrannidae_ in North America), Psittaci, Tinami, and Ratitae are absent.

## Environmental Context
The avian distribution is mirrored by the regional flora and climate. The area comprising Mongolia, Turkestan, western Manchuria, and central Asia (located between the southern border of Siberia and the temperate alpine zone of north China and the Himalaya) is an almost rainless region with intense summer heat and extreme winter severity. The plants and animals of this specific area possess a special character suited to these conditions and are more closely allied to the adjacent northern Siberian tract than to other bordering regions.

Furthermore, while much exploration of eastern Asia and China remains to be done, it is established that some special forms of this region extend to the Himalaya, while others indicate a clear connection with North America.
