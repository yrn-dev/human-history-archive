# Donaghadee

Donaghadee is a market town located in County Down, Ireland. Situated on the Irish Channel near the south of Belfast Lough, it is positioned 25 miles East by North of Belfast. As of 1901, the town had a population of 2,073.

## Geography and Infrastructure
Donaghadee is the nearest port in Ireland to Great Britain, located 21-1/2 miles Southwest of Portpatrick in Wigtownshire. While the harbour can admit vessels up to 200 tons, there is no regular passenger route between Donaghadee and Portpatrick due to the unsuitability of Portpatrick.

The town is accessible via a branch of the Belfast and County Down railway. This railway system serves the eastern part of the county with a main line running from Belfast to Newcastle and Dundrum Bay, including various branches such as those from Belfast to Bangor, Comber to Newtowntownards, and Comber to Donaghadee.

## Communications and Engineering
The town serves as a critical point for maritime communications. Both telephone and telegraph cables connect Donaghadee to Portpatrick. In 1853, Sir Charles Tilston Bright, an English telegraph engineer, superintended the laying of the first cable between Great Britain and Ireland, which ran from Portpatrick to Donaghadee. 

The experiments conducted during this project convinced Bright of the practicability of establishing an electric submarine cable connection between Ireland and America. Following discussions in 1855 with J. W. Brett and Cyrus Field—who controlled the Newfoundland Telegraph Company—Bright helped organize the Atlantic Telegraph Company in 1856 as engineer-in-chief to realize this goal.

## Local Landmarks and Economy
Donaghadee is frequented during the summer months as a seaside watering-place. The town's landmarks include:
* A parish church dating from 1626.
* Two holy wells.
* A rath or encampment on the north-east side of the town, standing 70 feet high, which houses a powder magazine.

## Regional Context
Within County Down, Donaghadee is listed as one of the principal towns, alongside others such as Newry, Downpatrick (the county town), and Bangor. The county is divided into sixty-four parishes and fourteen baronies. It falls under the Protestant diocese of Down, as well as the Roman Catholic diocesies of Dromore and Down. Politically, the county returns four members to parliament representing the east, west, south, and north divisions.
