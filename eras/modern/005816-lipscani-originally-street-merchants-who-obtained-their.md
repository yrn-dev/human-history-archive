# Lipscani

## Overview and Origin
Lipscani is one of the three main thoroughfares that skirt the left bank of the river in Bucharest. The city is frequently referred to as "The Paris of the East," a designation attributed partly to a supposed social resemblance and partly to the presence of its numerous avenues and boulevards. 

Originally, the Lipscani served as the street for merchants who obtained their wares from the annual fair held at Leipzig. This specialization was part of a broader urban organization in Bucharest where almost all gilds or crafts—with the exception of tavern-keepers and bakers—were long confined to separate quarters. This historical arrangement is still reflected in the survival of old names for various districts, such as those for money-changers, furriers, and musicians.

## Urban Geography and Connectivity
The Lipscani serves as a central point from which other major roads radiate. Specifically, the Calea Victoriei (the "Avenue of Victory") and the Plevna radiate north and east, respectively, from the Lipscani. The Calea Victoriei commemorates the Rumanian success at Plevna in 1877. 

These thoroughfares eventually meet a broad road that surrounds the city of Bucharest on all sides, except for the north-west. Further north, continuous with the Calea Victoriei, is the Kisilev Park. This park is traversed by the Chausée, a favorite drive that leads to the Baneasa race-course, where meetings are held during the autumn and spring. Additionally, the Cismegiu (or Cismigiu) Park, which has a circumference of approximately 1 meter, is situated between the Calea Victoriei and the Plevna road. The city also contains zoological and botanical gardens.
