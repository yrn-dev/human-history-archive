# Bahia

## Geography and Boundaries
Bahia is an Atlantic state located in Brazil. Its borders are defined by the states of Piauhy, Pernambuco, and Sergipe to the north, and Sergipe and the Atlantic Ocean to the east. To the south, it is bounded by Espirito Santo and Minas Geraes, while its western boundary is formed by Minas Geraes and Goyaz. The state covers an area of 164,650 sq. m.

The landscape of Bahia is characterized by a general elevation of 2,300 to 3,000 ft. above sea-level (or 1,000 to 1,700 ft. above the Sao Francisco river). A significant portion of the state consists of an arid, barren plateau known as a *chapada*. This plateau, which is 60 m. broad with an elevation of 2,300 ft., serves as the western boundary of the state and acts as the water-parting between the Tocantins and the Sao Francisco. The drainage basin of the Sao Francisco river traverses the state from the south toward the north and northeast.

## Regional Divisions
East of the Sao Francisco river, the state is divided into three distinct regions:
*   **The Limestone Plateau:** A rough plateau that rises gradually toward the culminating ridges of the Serra da Chapada.
*   **The Gneissose Plateau:** An area featuring extensive exposures of bare rock that dip slightly toward the coast.
*   **The Sandy Plateau:** A narrower plateau covered in compact sandy soil that descends toward the coastal plain.

The first two regions, each approximately 200 m. in breadth, are inhospitable, barren, and arid, except at the dividing ridges where sea clouds deposit moisture. The third zone becomes less arid and possesses better vegetation as it nears the coast. The coastal plain itself varies in character, ranging from low, sandy, or swampy areas filled with canals and lagoons to more elevated, rolling, and fertile terrain.

## Climate and Industry
The climate of Bahia mirrors its geography, remaining hot and dry throughout the interior and hot and humid—and in some areas unhealthy—along the coast. 

Historically, cattle-raising was the primary industry in the interior. However, this has been nearly extinguished due to increasing aridity and devastating droughts, which were caused by the practice of annually burning over the *campos* to improve grass. In the agricultural regions, the state produces coffee, cacao, tobacco, sugar, mandioca, tropical fruits, and cotton. Other exports include rum, diamonds, cabinet woods, piassava fibre, mangabeira rubber, and hides.
