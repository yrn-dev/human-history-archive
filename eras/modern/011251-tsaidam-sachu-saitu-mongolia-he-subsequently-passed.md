# Geographical Exploration of Tsaidam and Sachu

The modern period of scientific geographical exploration in Asia has been characterized by prolific expeditions directed toward the central depression north of the Tibetan table-land and the great highland plateau. This plateau extends from the Himalaya northwards to Chinese Turkestan and from the frontier of Kashmir eastwards to China.

## The Tsaidam Region
Tsaidam is a remarkable region from which the great rivers of Burma, Siam, and China emanate. The Russian explorer Nicolai Prjevalsky first defined the geography of Tsaidam and mapped its hydrography between 1871 and 1873. 

The topography of the area includes the Tsaidam depression and the plain or flat basin of Särtäng, which serves as a north extension of Tsaidam. The region is bordered to the southwest by the Nan-shan mountains, which consist of broad plains dotted with lakes and short irregular chains at altitudes between 8,800 and 9,000 feet. To the north and south are various mountain ranges:
* **The Akato-tagh (or Altun Range):** A range built of schists and granite with a denuded crest. It is separated from the Astin-tagh by the Kakir valley, a sterile expanse of clay.
* **The Chimen-tagh:** This range is equated in the east with the Tsaidam chain of Przhevalsky. It acts as a "breakwater" to clouds, condensing moisture carried north by south winds. Consequently, water is abundant and grass and animal life are more prevalent here than on the Akato-tagh or Astin-tagh.

## Sachu and Regional Mapping
Sachu (also referred to as Saitu) in Mongolia is considered an obligatory point in modern map construction. Because of its importance, various explorers have provided different longitude values for the location:
* **Prjevalsky:** 94 deg. 54' E. (revised value from his fourth exploration), as well as 94 deg. 26' from his second and third explorations.
* **Krishna:** 94 deg. 23'
* **Carey and Dalgleish:** 94 deg. 48'
* **Littledale:** 94 deg. 49'
* **Kreitner:** 94 deg. 58'

Another obligatory mapping point is Darchendo (or Tachienlu), located on the borders of China on the high road between Peking and Lhasa. Its longitude is accepted by the Royal Geographical Society as 102 deg. 12", though other explorers provided values ranging from 102 deg. 5" (Kreitner) to 102 deg. 18" (Baber).

## Principal Expeditions
Several key explorers contributed to the knowledge of these regions:

**Pundits Nain Singh and Krishna**
Native surveyors attached to the Indian survey, these explorers added significantly to the knowledge of the great plateau. Nain Singh explored the Upper Brahmaputra and the sources of the Indus from 1865 to 1867. In 1874-1875, he traveled from the eastern frontiers of Kashmir to Lhasa, passing through the Tengri Nor lake. 

Krishna conducted a remarkable journey from 1879 to 1882. His route extended from Lhasa northwards through Tsaidam to Sachu in Mongolia. He then passed through eastern Tibet to Darchendo. After failing to reach India via Upper Assam, he returned to the vicinity of Lhasa and crossed the Himalayas using a more westerly route.

**Nicolai Prjevalsky**
The Russian explorer Prjevalsky conducted multiple journeys into Central Asia. After his initial work in Tsaidam (1871-1873), he visited Lop Nor in 1876 and discovered the Altyn Tagh range. In 1879, he traversed the Gobi desert from Hami to Sachu—which served as a point of junction between his travels and those of Krishna—and visited the sources of the Salween and the Hwang-ho (Yellow river). His fourth journey (1883-1885) took him to the trade center of Sining, through northern Tibet, and via the Cherchen-Keriya trade route to Khotan and Aksu.
