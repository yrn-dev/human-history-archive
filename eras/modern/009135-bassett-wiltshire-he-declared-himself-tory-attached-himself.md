## Geological Features
In the field of geology, Wootton Bassett is noted as a location where good exposures of the Kimeridgian can be found. The Kimeridgian is the basal division of the Upper Oolites within the Jurassic system. The term is derived from the hamlet of Kimmeridge (or Kimeridge) near the coast of Dorsetshire, England. The name was first suggested by T. Webster in 1812, and Buckland used the term "Kimeridge Clay" in 1818.

From the cliffs of Gad Cliff to St Alban's Head on the Dorsetshire coast, the Jurassic outcrop follows a line through Wiltshire. In Wiltshire, there is a broad expanse of this formation between Devizes and Westbury. The Kimeridgian generally occupied broad valleys, such as the vale of Aylesbury. In addition to Wootton Bassett, good exposures of this formation occur at Calne, Seend, Swindon, Abingdon, Faringdon, Culham, Brill, Shotover Hill, Ely, and Market Rasen. Traces of this formation extend as far north as Helmsdale and Eathie on the east coast of Sutherland and Cromarty.
