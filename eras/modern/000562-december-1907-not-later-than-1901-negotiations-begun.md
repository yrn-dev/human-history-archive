## International Diplomacy and Borders
In East Africa, a boundary agreement was reached following long negotiations to define the frontier between the lake and the river Juba. This agreement was signed at Adis Ababa on December 6, 1907. The resulting boundary assigned Lake Rudolf to the British, with the exception of its north-eastern extremity, while Lake Stefanie was assigned to Abyssinia. The frontier was further defined from approximately 4° 20' N., where the Daua meets the Juba. Following this agreement, an Anglo-Abyssinian commission, led by British representative Major C.W. Gwynn, delimited the frontier during 1908-1909.

## Austro-Hungarian Economic Agreements
During this period, Austria and Hungary maintained a working agreement to preserve their Union. A key component of their arrangements involved a Customs Union; while the reciprocal and binding treaty governing this union had lapsed, both parties continued it in practice. They promised to maintain this practice until December 31, 1907. 

The terms of their alliance stipulated that negotiations for renewal were to begin no later than 1901, with a goal of renewing the alliance from 1903 to coincide with the expiration of commercial treaties. Had this renewal occurred, tariffs would have been revised before new commercial treaties were established. Without such a renewal, no new treaties extending beyond 1907 would be made, allowing each party to determine its own policy independently after 1907. Additionally, the Bank charter was renewed until 1910, providing Hungarians with a larger share in direction than they previously held.

## United States Infrastructure and Agriculture
In Idaho, the development of agriculture was significantly advanced by federal irrigation projects. The Minidoka project, located in Cassia and Lincoln counties, saw surveys begin in March 1903 and construction commence in December 1904. This project was completed in 1907, creating an irrigable area of between 130,000 and 150,000 acres. This followed the completion of the Twin Falls project (1903-1905), which irrigated over 200,000 acres. These developments were part of a broader period of irrigation introduced by the Carey Act of 1894 and the Federal Reclamation Act of 1902.
