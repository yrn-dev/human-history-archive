# Cracow, Przemysl, and Komárom

Within the defensive architecture of the Austro-Hungarian monarchy, several key fortifications served as strategic anchors. Among the most significant were the Galician strongholds of Cracow and Przemysl, and the Hungarian fortress of Komárom, which functioned as the center of the inland fortifications.

## Komárom
Komárom (known in German as Komorn) is the capital of the county of Komárom in Hungary, located 65 miles west-north-west of Budapest by rail. The town is situated at the confluence of the Waag and the Danube, at the eastern end of the island Grosse Schütt (or Csallóköz). Since 1896, the market-town of Uj-Szöny on the opposite bank of the Danube has been incorporated into the town. As of 1900, the population was 16,816.

One of Hungary's oldest towns, Komárom received its charter in 1265. It is primarily celebrated for its fortifications, which form the center of the monarchy's inland defenses. These works were initiated by Matthias Corvinus and were later strengthened during the Turkish wars between 1526 and 1564. Further construction occurred in 1663, with significant enlargements taking place between 1805 and 1809. The town was beleaguered by the Turks in 1543, 1594, 1598, and 1663. In 1751, it was elevated to a royal free town.

During the revolutionary war of 1848-49, Komárom was a primary site of military operations. It was besieged by Austrian forces, who suffered defeats there on July 11, 1849, by General Görgei, and on August 3 by General Klapka. The fortress eventually capitulated on honorable terms on September 27 and was evacuated by Hungarian troops on October 3 and 4. Later, in 1866, the treasure of the Austrian national bank was moved from Vienna to Komárom when the Prussian army threatened the capital. Economically, the town maintains a brisk trade in fish, wine, timber, and cereals.

## Przemysl and Cracow in Galicia
In the region of Galicia, the principal fortifications were located at Cracow and Przemysl. Both cities served as centers for the military organization of the Dual Monarchy; Cracow was the seat of the I army corps, while Przemysl was the seat of the X army corps.

Przemysl played a critical role during the conflict with Russia. Russian armies, pressing westward, captured the great fortress of Przemysl on March 22, 1915, after having taken Lemberg on September 2, 1914. However, following a series of heavy blows delivered by German forces using massed artillery, Przemysl was retaken on June 22.

Cracow's history includes periods of political instability and military action. It was once a little independent republic and the site of a Polish nationalist movement in 1846. During this period, the Austrian government sought to master the revolution by winning over Ruthenian peasants through the abolition of feudal dues and the recognition of their language. This culminated in the bombardment of insurgent Cracow into submission on April 26.

## Military Context of the Fortifications
The fortifications at Komárom, Cracow, and Przemysl were part of a broader defensive network. Other principal fortifications included Petervarad, Temesvar, and O-Arad in Hungary; Mostar, Bilek, and Serajewo in Bosnia-Herzegovina; and the naval harbor of Pola on the Adriatic coast, along with Trieste, Zara, and Cattaro. In the Alpine frontiers, particularly in Tirol, the center of fortifications was formed by Franzensfeste and Trent, while defensive works were established on military roads leading into Carinthia at Predil Pass and Malborgeth. Notably, the capitals of Vienna and Budapest were not fortified.

The active army's peace strength consisted of approximately 350,000 officers and men, including the Hungarian crown guards, the gendarmerie, the Austrian "K.K." guards, and two Landwehrs. In times of war, the first line army was estimated to include 460,000 infantry, 78,000 artillery, 49,000 cavalry, and 21,000 engineers. Additional reserves from the Honved and Landwehr provided another 219,000 infantry and 18,000 cavalry, with other reserves totaling 223,000 men, bringing the approximate total strength to 1,147,000, excluding the Landsturm.
