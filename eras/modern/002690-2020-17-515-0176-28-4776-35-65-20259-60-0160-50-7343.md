# 2020 Legal Proceedings and the CVRA

## The Mandamus Petition of Jane Doe I
In September 2019, a petitioner identified as "Jane Doe I" filed a petition for a writ of mandamus in the United States Court of Appeals for the Eleventh Circuit. The petitioner sought a review of a district court order that had denied all of her requested remedies. This legal action centered on the application of the Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA).

The government's responsive brief argued that legal obligations under the CVRA do not attach before the government charges a case. Consequently, the government maintained that the CVRA was not triggered in the Southern District of Florida because no criminal charges had been brought. However, during oral arguments, the government conceded that the USAO had not been "fully transparent" with the petitioner and had mistakenly led her to believe the case was ongoing when a Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA) had actually been signed.

## Court Rulings and Rehearings
On April 14, 2020, a divided panel of the Court of Appeals denied the petition. The court concluded that the CVRA does not apply before the commencement of criminal proceedings and therefore did not provide the petitioner with judicially enforceable rights. In reaching this decision, the court analyzed the statute's language, legislative history, and previous court decisions. It distinguished a case known as *In re* as "dictum" that lacked analysis of the CVRA's structural underpinnings, text, or history.

The court further noted that its interpretation aligned with implementing regulations and a 2010 OLC opinion regarding victim standing. Additionally, the court cited separation of powers concerns, suggesting that a different interpretation of victim standing would interfere with prosecutorial discretion.

Despite the denial, the court was highly critical of the government's conduct. It stated that the government seemingly deferred to Epstein's lawyers regarding information provided to victims about the NPA, and that the government's actions had moved from "passive nondisclosure" to something close to "active misrepresentation." The court remarked that it "seems obvious" the government should have consulted with the petitioner and other victims before executing Epstein's NM, though it found no legal obligation to do so.

A dissenting judge issued a strongly worded opinion, arguing that the majority's interpretation was "contorted" because the plain text of the CVRA contains no post-indictment temporal restriction.

Following this, a petition for rehearing en bane was filed on May 5, 2020. On August 7, 2020, the court granted this petition and vacated the panel's opinion. Oral argument for the rehearing was scheduled for December 3, 2020.

## The Crime Victims' Rights Act (CVRA)
The CVRA was presented as a compromise measure by Senators after they determined that a proposed amendment to the Bill of Rights was unlikely to be approved. The act defines a "crime victim" as any person directly and proximately harmed by the commission of an offense in the District of Columbia or a Federal offense.

During the period relevant to the federal Epstein investigation, the CVRA afforded crime victims eight specific rights:
1. The right to reasonable protection from the accused.
2. The right to reasonable, accurate, and timely notice regarding any release or escape of the accused, or any public court or parole proceeding involving the crime.
3. The right not to be excluded from public court proceedings, unless the court finds by clear and convincing evidence that the victim's testimony would be materially altered by hearing other testimony.
4. The right to be reasonably heard at any public proceeding in the district court involving sentencing, plea, parole, or release.
5. The reasonable right to confer with the government's attorney in the case.
6. The right to full and timely restitution as provided by law.
7. The right to proceedings free from unreasonable delay.
8. The right to be treated with respect for the victim's dignity and privacy, and with fairness.
