# Barcelona

Barcelona, known in antiquity as Barcino, is a city in Spain with a history rooted in the 3rd century B.C., when it was traditionally founded by the Carthaginian Hamilcar Barca. Following the Roman conquest, the city was named Julia Faventia by Augustus (27 B.C.–A.D. 14) and granted the status of a Roman colony. By the 2nd century A.D., it had become a leading mart of the western Mediterranean, rivaling Massilia and Tarraco. The Roman city was situated on Monte Taber, and remains of this era, including magnificent columns, are still found in the Calle del Paradis, though they are currently hidden by surrounding buildings.

## Historical Development
The bishopric of Barcelona was established in 343. During the early medieval period, the Visigoths selected the city as their temporary capital in 415 and 531, and church councils were convened there in 540 and 599. The city, then called Barcinona or Bardjaluna, was captured by the Moors in 713 before passing to the dominion of the Franks in 801. Starting in 874, the counts of Barcelona ruled as independent monarchs.

The city reached the zenith of its wealth and fame following the 1149 union between Aragon and Catalonia. During this era, Barcelona's merchant ships traded as far east as Alexandria and as far west as the Baltic and North Seas, competing with Venice, Ragusa, and Genoa. In 1258, James I of Aragon authorized the city to issue the *Consulado del Mar*, a code of maritime law that became authoritative across many European states. Barcelona was also an early adopter of marine insurance and maintained consuls at major Mediterranean commercial centers.

The city's commercial supremacy declined after 1492, as the discovery of America shifted trade to the ports of western Spain, a process accelerated by the 1479 union of Aragon and Castile. This led to political tension with the "Castilian" government. In 1640, Barcelona became the center of a Catalonian rebellion against Philip IV and sought French protection. Although it returned to its allegiance in 1652, the city was captured by the duke of Vendome in 1697.

## Port and Infrastructure
Originally, the port of Barcelona consisted of an open beach sheltered by hills. In 1438, Don Alphonso V granted a license to build a mole, and the Moll de Santa Creu was officially started in 1474. Despite these efforts, travelers continued to describe the city as lacking a harbor until satisfactory works were completed in the 17th century. For a long period, a sand-bank blocked large vessels from entering.

Modern improvements included the construction of a mole from the foot of Montjuich and an extension of the former mole, allowing heavy battleships to find shelter. Systematic improvements began in 1873, adding new quays and storage. By 1903, the port featured an inner harbour (18 to 30 ft. deep at low tide) and an outer harbour (20 to 35 ft. deep). In 1904, nearly 5,000,000 tons of cargo entered the port via 8,075 vessels. The city is connected by rail and tramway lines that run from the port to the chief suburbs, including Gracia.

## Civic and Educational Institutions
Barcelona has a long history of important educational and charitable institutions. The *Universidad Literaria* was founded in 1430 by the city's magistracy and confirmed by Pope Nicholas V in 1450; after being suppressed in 1714, it was restored in 1841. Other institutions include a college of medicine and surgery (confirmed by Benedict XIII in 1400), an academy of fine arts, a theological seminary, a normal school, and an academy of natural sciences. The Board of Trade provides high-order gratuitous instruction to over 2,000 pupils. Charitable foundations include the foundling hospital, the hospital general (dating from 1401), and the Casa de Caridad.

Notable civic buildings include:
* **Casa Consistorial:** A Gothic hall built between 1369 and 1378.
* **Lonja (Exchange):** Built in 1383.
* **Aduana (Custom-house):** Built in 1792.
* **Atarazanas (Arsenals):** Finished around 1243, though partly demolished in the 19th century.
* **Royal State Remains:** Found in the Palacio de la Reina and the Palacio Real of the kings of Aragon.

For recreation, the city offers numerous restaurants and public courts for the Basque game of pelota. Its two most important theatres are the Teatro Principal and the Teatro del Liceo, the latter built in 1845 on the site of a Trinitarian monks' convent.

## Regional Influence and Literature
The town of Badalona, located 6 miles northeast of the city on the river Besos, serves as an industrial suburb. It contributes to Barcelona's export trade through the production of glass, sugar, brandy, cotton and woollen goods, and chemical and mineral-oil works.

Literature regarding the city is extensive. General descriptions are provided by A. A. P. Arimon (*Barcelona antigua y moderna*, 1850) and J. Artigas y Feiner (*Guia itineraria de Barcelona*, 1888). S. Sampere wrote on the city's antiquities in *Topografia antigua de Barcelona* (1890). Economic and political history is covered by A. Capmany in *Memorias historicas sobra la marina, comercio, y artes de la antigua ciudad de Barcelona* (1779-1792), T. Tomich in *Historias e conquestas dels comtes de Barcelona* (1888), and the *Coleccio de documents inedits del Arxin municipal de la ciutat de Barcelona* (1892). The revolutionary movement and anarchism in the city were traced by M. Gil Maestre in *El Anarquismo en Espana, y el especial de Barcelona* (1897) and *La Criminalidad en Barcelona* (1886). Additionally, José R. de Luanco published works in Barcelona regarding alchemy, including *Ramon Lull, considerado como alquimista* (1870) and *La Alquimia en España* (1889-1897).
