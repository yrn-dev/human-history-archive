# Lakes and Lacustrine Basins of Bolivia

The lacustrine features of Bolivia are distributed across several distinct hydrographic regions, ranging from the high-altitude plateaus of the Andes to the low-lying alluvial plains of the north and south-east. While many Bolivian lakes are not well known, they play a significant role in the country's geography and transport.

## The Central Plateau (Alta-Planicie)
The central plateau, or *alta-planicie*, is one of the most elevated lacustrine basins in the world. This region, which once contained an inland sea that drained eastward through the La Paz gorge, now possesses no surface outlet. The drainage of this extensive district is believed to be absorbed by evaporation and the dry soil of the desert.

The two principal lakes of this region are Lake Titicaca and Lake Pampa Aullaguas (also known as Poopo). Lake Titicaca is situated near the north end of the plateau at an elevation of 12,644 feet above sea level. It is characterized by numerous coves and bays, with a maximum length of 138 miles and a maximum breadth of 69 miles. A 1904 survey by Dr. M. Neveau-Lemaire estimated its water surface at 1,969 square miles (excluding peninsulas and islands) and its maximum depth at 892 feet. The lake's level fluctuates seasonally, rising approximately 5 inches in the summer. Shared by Bolivia and Peru, the lake is navigated by steamers connecting Peruvian railway ports, such as Puno, with Bolivian ports, including Guaqui (or Huaqui).

Water from Lake Titicaca exits via the Desaguadero, a sluggish river that flows south for 184 miles. The Desaguadero is navigable for small craft and empties into Lake Pampa Aullaguas. This lake, in turn, has a small outlet called the Lacahahuira, which flows west for 60 miles into the Cienegas de Coipasa, or salt-swamps.

## The Northern Lacustrine Basin
Between the Mamore and Beni rivers lies a great lacustrine basin containing several lagoons and lakes. Two of these are of significant size: Lake Rogagua (also referred to as Rojoagua), which drains into the Beni via the Rio Negro, and the Roguaguado marshes and lagoon. The Roguaguado covers a large area near the Mamore and has an estimated area of about 580 square miles; its elevation is nearly level with that of the Mamore river. Additionally, Lake Conception, located in the Chiquitos mountains, belongs to this same hydrographic area. This northern region, along with the south-east, is subject to periodical inundations from November to March or May, during which time boat travel becomes the only means of traffic.

## South-Eastern Lakes and Swamps
The south-east region contains several large, shallow lakes that occupy slight depressions. These lakes are caused by defective drainage and obstructed outlets, and their size and character vary by season. Near the Paraguay river, examples include Bahia Negra, Caceres, Mandiore, Gaiba, and Uberaba, some of which are deep enough for small craft to navigate. Above these lakes lie the great Xarayes swamps, which are occasionally described as a lake.

## Commercial and Transport Significance
Due to the loss of its maritime department, Bolivia relies on its lake and river ports for international trade. The ports of Lake Titicaca, particularly Guaqui, are vital for trade with Peru. In the south-east, Bolivian trade is conducted through ports connected to the upper Paraguay, including Puerto Pacheco on Bahia Negra, Puerto Quijarro on Lake Gaiba, and Puerto Suarez on Lake Caceres. The latter is the primary conduit for the bulk of Bolivian trade in that direction.
