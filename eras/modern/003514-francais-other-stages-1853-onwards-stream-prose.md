# Theodore Faullain de Banville

Theodore Faullain de Banville (1823–1891) was a French miscellaneous writer and poet. Born on March 14, 1823, in Moulins in the Bourbonnais, he was the son of a captain in the French navy. Banville spent his boyhood at a lycee in Paris; by his own account, this period was passed cheerlessly, as he did not participate in the amusements of his peers, although he was not treated harshly.

## Literary Career and Poetry
Upon leaving school with limited means of support, Banville dedicated himself to letters. He published his first volume of verse, *Les Cariatides*, in 1842, followed by *Les Stalactites* in 1846. While these early poems faced some adverse criticism, they earned him the friendship and approbation of Jules Janin and Alfred de Vigny.

Banville's life thereafter remained steadily devoted to criticism and literary production. He produced further volumes of verse, including the *Odes funambulesques* (Alencon, 1857), which were dedicated to Victor Hugo and received unstinted praise from him. Banville's claim to remembrance is based primarily on his poetry.

## Prose and Dramatic Works
From 1853 onwards, Banville's industrious pen produced a stream of prose. This body of work included sketches of well-known persons (*Camees parisiennes*, &c.), studies of Parisian manners, and a series of tales, such as *Contes bourgeois* and *Contes heroiques*. Most of these prose works were republished in his collected works between 1875 and 1878. In substance, if not in manner, much of his prose is characterized as that of a journalist; his stories are noted for their fertility of invention but are generally considered unreal and conventional.

In addition to his poetry and prose, Banville wrote several comedies in verse. These were produced on various stages, including the Theatre Francais. While these plays were written with refinement and distinction, they are regarded as deficient in dramatic power.

## Professional Life and Honors
Banville spent the majority of his life in Paris, where his cultivated mind and genial character allowed him to form friendships with the leading men of letters of his era. He maintained an intimacy with other famous actors, including Frederick-Lemaitre. Beyond his own creative writing, he acted as a dramatic critic for more than one newspaper and wrote freely for reviews.

In recognition of his contributions, Banville was decorated with the legion of honour in 1858 and was later promoted to an officer of the order in 1886.

## Death
Theodore Faullain de Banville died in Paris on March 15, 1891, having just completed his sixty-eighth year.
