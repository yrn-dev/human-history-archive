# The Bonaparte Family

The Bonaparte family consists of Napoleon I and his siblings, as well as their descendants. The family's history is documented through various genealogical and biographical works, including those by F. Masson, A. Kleinschmidt, and others.

## Siblings of Napoleon I

Napoleon I had several brothers and sisters, listed here in order of age:

**Joseph Bonaparte**
Joseph is the first listed among Napoleon's siblings. Various works detail his life, including his correspondence with Napoleon and his time in America.

**Lucien Bonaparte**
Lucien lived in Italy, primarily in Rome, where he exhibited marked hostility toward the emperor. In December 1807, Napoleon attempted to reach an arrangement where Lucien would become a French prince if he annulled his marriage; Lucien refused. After spending time at his estate of Canino, from which he took the title of prince of Canino, he traveled to America. He was captured by a British ship and taken to Malta and then England, where he remained under surveillance until the peace of 1814. 

Lucien returned to Rome and offered his help to Napoleon during the Hundred Days in 1815, standing by him at the "Champ de Mai" in Paris. He was the final person to defend Napoleon's prerogatives during the second abdication. He spent his remaining years in Italy and died in Rome on June 29, 1840. He fathered four sons and six daughters and authored the epic *Charlemagne, ou l'Eglise delivree* (1814), as well as *La Verite sur les Cent Jours* and unfinished *Memoirs*.

**Marianne Elisa (1777–1820)**
Born in Ajaccio on January 3, 1777, Elisa entered the establishment of St Cyr near Paris as a "king's scholar" through the efforts of her brothers. After revolutionists disrupted the establishment in 1792, Napoleon took charge of her and returned her to Ajaccio. She married a well-connected Corsican, Felix Bacciochi, on May 5, 1797. 

Following the foundation of the French empire in 1805, Napoleon granted her the principality of Piombino and later Lucca. In 1808, she obtained the grand duchy of Tuscany. Due to Bacciochi being "almost a nullity," Elisa's ability and pride significantly influenced Italian affairs and administration. Her relationship with Napoleon was often strained, and in 1813–1814, she supported Murat's enterprises. After Napoleon's fall, she lived as the countess of Compignano in Bologna and later Santo Andrea near Trieste, where she died on August 6, 1820.

**Louis Bonaparte**
Louis's life and reign are documented in various historical documents, including those concerning the government of Hollande between 1806 and 1813.

**Maria Annunciata Caroline (1782–1839)**
Born in Ajaccio on March 25, 1782, Caroline married Joachim Murat in early 1800. She used her "ambitious and intriguing nature" to advance his career. Murat served as the governor of Paris, marshal of France (1804), grand duke of Berg and Cleves (1806), and lieutenant of the emperor in Spain (1808), eventually becoming the king of Naples in the summer of 1808. 

Caroline was displeased by the distance between Naples and Paris, leading to strained relations with Napoleon. She associated herself with her husband's "equivocal movements" in 1814–1815. She retired to Austrian territory under restraint before Murat's death at Pizzo on October 13, 1815. She spent her final days at Trieste with her sister Elisa and died on May 18, 1839.

**Jerome Bonaparte**
Jerome's life is detailed in various memoirs and correspondences, including those involving his wife, Queen Catherine.

## Descendants of the Family

The fortunes of the family continued through the descendants of Jerome and Lucien.

**Pierre Napoleon (1815–1881)**
The son of Lucien Bonaparte, Pierre was born in Rome on September 12, 1815. At age fifteen, he joined insurrectionary bands in the Romagna (1830–1831). He later traveled to the United States to join his uncle Joseph and spent time in Colombia with General Santander in 1832. Upon returning to Rome, he was imprisoned by the pope from 1835 to 1836, after which he sought refuge in England.

During the revolution of 1848, Pierre returned to France and was elected as a deputy for Corsica to the Constituent Assembly. He identified as an "out-and-out republican," voting with socialists and supporting national workshops while opposing the *loi Falloux*. This stance helped provide popular confidence to his cousin, Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III). Although Pierre disapproved of the *coup d'etat* on December 2, 1851, he eventually reconciled with the emperor and accepted the title of prince, causing republicans to abandon him.

In his later years, Pierre led a debauched life and lost political influence, turning instead to the publication of "mediocre poems." In January 1870, he became prominent again following a controversy with Paschal Grousset. After Grousset sent two journalists to provoke him to a duel, Pierre killed one of them, Victor Noir, with a revolver during a discussion. While the republican press demanded a trial, the High Court acquitted him. Pierre died in obscurity at Versailles on April 7, 1881. He had two children with Justine Eleanore Ruffin: Roland Napoleon (born May 19, 1858), who studied geography and scientific explorations after being excluded from the army in 1886, and Jeanne, the wife of the marquis de Vence.
