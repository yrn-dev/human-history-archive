# Charles Annibal Fabrot

## Publication of the Basilica
Charles Annibal Fabrot was the editor responsible for publishing an edition of the sixty books of the Basilica. This work was a significant undertaking of considerable merit, although it is noted to have been a very imperfect representation of the original code. The edition was published in seven volumes.

## Patronage of Louis XIII
The publication of the Basilica was conducted under the patronage of Louis XIII, the King of France. To support the editor during the publication process, Louis XIII provided two specific forms of assistance:
* He assigned Fabrot an annual stipend of two thousand livres.
* He placed the royal printing-press at Fabrot's disposal.

## Context of the Basilica
The Basilica is a body of law consisting of sixty books. For a period, the existence of any portion of the code appeared to have been ignored by jurists in western Europe. This changed in 1533 when Viglius Zuichemus brought the importance of the code to the study of Roman law to their attention in a preface to his edition of the Greek *Paraphrase of Theophilus*. 

A century passed after Zuichemus's work before Fabrot's edition was published. Later, between 1833 and 1870, a more complete and newly-restored text of the sixty books was published in six volumes at Leipzig, edited by G. E. Heimbach and K. W. E. Heimbach.

The archive's sources indicate that the Basilica did not survive in its integrity. A preserved letter from Mark, the patriarch of Alexandria, to Theodoras Balsamon reveals that copies of the work were very scarce in the 12th century, as the patriarch was unable to obtain one. The difficulty in multiplying copies was attributed to the great bulk of the code. Additionally, the need for full copies was largely superseded by the creation of *encheiridia* and synopses of the contents by eminent jurists.
