## Literary Contributions of the Duke of Buckingham
The Duke of Buckingham was a writer of satires and occasional verses. While his poetical gifts are described as undoubted, they are also characterized as undeveloped. A collection of these works was first published by Tom Brown in 1704, though this collection included many pieces that were not actually written by Buckingham.

Buckingham is the author of *The Rehearsal*, a clever and amusing satire focusing on the heroic drama and specifically targeting Dryden. First performed at the Theatre Royal on December 7, 1671, and published in 1672, the play became popular and was later imitated by Sheridan in *The Critic* and by Fielding in *Tom Thumb the Great*.

His other published works include:
*   *The Chances* (1682), which was altered from a play of the same name by Fletcher.
*   *The Restoration or Right will take place* (1714), adapted from Beaumont and Fletcher's *Philaster*.
*   *The Battle of Sedgmoor* (1704).
*   *The Militant Couple* (1704).

Additionally, Wood mentions a work titled *A Demonstration of the Deity*, although no trace of this text currently exists. The most recent edition of Buckingham's works was produced by T. Evans in 1775, consisting of two 8vo volumes.
