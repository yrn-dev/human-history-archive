# Project Gutenberg

## Origins and Concept
Project Gutenberg was originated by Professor Michael S. Hart. The core concept of the project was to establish a library of electronic works that could be shared freely with any person. For a period of forty years, Professor Hart was responsible for the production and distribution of Project Gutenberg eBooks, operating with the assistance of a loose network of volunteers.

## eBook Creation and Copyright
The process of creating Project Gutenberg eBooks often involves the use of several different printed editions. To ensure legality, these editions are confirmed to be not protected by copyright within the United States, unless a copyright notice is present. Because eBooks may be synthesized from multiple sources, they are not necessarily kept in compliance with any one specific paper edition.

## Access and Search
The primary point of entry for most users is the Project Gutenberg website. This site hosts the main search facility for the archive and can be accessed at www.gutenberg.org.

## Funding and Donations
Project Gutenberg accepts donations to support its operations. These contributions can be made through various methods, including credit card donations, online payments, and checks. Information regarding current addresses and methods for donating is available on the project's web pages, specifically at www.gutenberg.org/donate.

The project accepts international donations, although it cannot provide statements regarding the tax treatment of funds received from outside the United States. Regarding domestic contributions, the project does not solicit donations from states where it has not met specific solicitation requirements; however, there is no prohibition against accepting unsolicited donations from donors in those states who offer them. The project's small staff is currently overwhelmed by the complexities of U.S. laws.
