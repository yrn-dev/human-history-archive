# Exploraciones practicadas en el Alto Paraguay y en la Laguna Gaiba

The work *Exploraciones practicadas en el Alto Paraguay y en la Laguna Gaiba*, published in Buenos Aires in 1901 by Enrique Bolland, is cited as an authority regarding the geography and industrial aspects of Bolivia. It is part of a broader body of literature used to document the region's natural resources and territorial characteristics.

## Regional Geography and Navigation
The regions discussed in Bolland's work are situated within a complex network of river systems. In the area of the Upper Paraguay, several key ports facilitate trade and movement. These include Puerto Pacheco on Bahia Negra and Puerto Suarez, which is located on Lake Caceres and is approximately 1,600 miles from Buenos Aires by river. Puerto Suarez is noted as the point through which the bulk of Bolivian trade in that direction passes. Additionally, there is Puerto Quijarro, located on Lake Gaiba; this is a projected port described as being more accessible than any other port in the region.

The broader navigational context of the area is influenced by the geography of the Paraguay and Paraná rivers. For example, the city of Corrientes in the Argentine Republic serves as a critical river port on the left bank of the Paraná, situated 20 miles below the junction of the Upper Paraná and Paraguay. Corrientes effectively controls the trade of extensive tributary regions, as it serves as the starting point for the navigation of the Bermejo and Upper Paraná rivers.

## Bolivian Commercial Outlets
The importance of the ports mentioned in Bolland's explorations is highlighted by Bolivia's limited maritime access. Following the loss of its maritime department, Bolivia relies on the ports of Lake Titicaca (specifically Guaqui, which trades with Puno in Peru), as well as the affluents and rivers of the Paraguay and Madeira. 

Because these routes often require transhipment in foreign territory, transport costs are increased, and neighboring nations can exclude Bolivia from direct commercial intercourse with other countries. While the Madeira river provides an outlet to the Amazon, navigation is interrupted by falls. The port of Villa Bella, located at the confluence of the Mamore and Beni, requires a costly and long portage to reach the lower river and is similarly shut off from navigable rivers above by the falls of the Beni and Mamore.

## Connectivity and Future Development
The archival records indicate that the development of the region depends heavily on infrastructure. A railway to be built by Brazil is expected to remedy the unfavorable situation of north-eastern Bolivia, providing a better outlet and promoting rapid development in the region above the falls of the Beni and Mamore, which possesses an admirable system of navigable rivers.

In the south, the potential for direct river communication with Montevideo and Buenos Aires exists, provided that trade becomes significant enough to justify the expense of opening a navigable channel in the Pilcomayo.
