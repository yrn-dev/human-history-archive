# The Mountain Range of the 36th Parallel

The 36th parallel of latitude north serves as a significant geographical marker in Asia, particularly regarding the distribution of high-altitude plateaus and an extensive, unbroken mountain system that acts as a natural line of division across a large portion of the continent.

## The Asian High Region
A vast plateau exists in the portion of Asia west of British India, excluding Syria and Arabia. This region is comparable in size to Tibet, although it is situated at a lower altitude. The northern boundary of this high land follows nearly the 36th degree North, extending to the Black Sea, the Sea of Marmora, and the southern shore of the Caspian. This high region is nearly conterminous with several areas, including Armenia, Asia Minor, Iran (or Persia), Baluchistan, and Afghanistan.

The southern and southwestern boundaries of this region are defined by the Persian Gulf, the Arabian Sea, and the Tigris. Further west, a mountain scarp flanks the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, rising in many points to 10,000 feet. This scarp extends through Armenia and Kurdistan nearly to the 40th meridian before turning along the north-eastern angle of the Mediterranean and the Taurus range.

## The Unbroken Mountain Range
A critical geographical feature begins at the intersection of the 36th parallel of latitude and the 71st meridian. From this point, an unbroken range of mountains stretches in two directions:
* **North-east:** The range extends toward the crest of the northern slope of the Tibetan plateau.
* **West:** The range extends nearly due west as far as the Caspian.

The north-eastern portion of this range is characterized by great altitude. It serves as a divide between the headwaters of the Indus and its tributary, the Kabul—which unite below Peshawar and flow south into the Arabian Sea—and the headwaters of the Oxus, which flow toward the Aral Sea. The western section of this range was known to the ancients as Paropamisus Mons; its height diminishes west of the 65th meridian.

## Connection to the Tibetan Plateau
Between the 71st and 72nd meridians and between 34 and 36 degrees North, the north-eastern portion of the Afghan table-land abuts Tibet and the Himalaya, forming a continuous mass of mountain. Tibet itself is a rugged table-land, narrow in proportion to its length, and is broken up by mountain ranges that generally follow the direction of the table-land's length and often reach regions of perpetual snow. 

The Tibetan plateau's eastern termination is marked by snowy mountains flanking China on the west, located between the 35th and 27th parallels of latitude and approximately 103 degrees east. To the west, the table-land's physical features continue beyond political limits to about 70 degrees east. The ranges of perpetual snow extend west as far as Samarkand before diminishing in height and ending in low hills north of Bokhara.

## Other Regional Topography
While the 36th parallel is central to the Asian mountain systems, other mountainous regions exist across different latitudes:
* **Greece Proper:** Located between the 36th and 40th parallels of north latitude, this area extends from Mount Olympus to Cape Tænarus.
* **The Afghan-India Border:** The mountain barrier separating the Afghan highlands from the Peshawar plains differs from the barrier further south. West of Peshawar, the mountain systems strike roughly from west to east. The Safed Koh range, a long line of rugged peaks, serves as the southern wall of the Kabul River basin. Near the 71st meridian, it sends a spur north-east known as the Shandur Range, which unites with the Hindu Kush near the head of the Taghdumbash Pamir.
* **Eastern Asia:** The Great Khingan range serves as the eastern border ridge of an immense plateau extending from the Tian-shan Mountains to the Khingan Mountains and from the Himalaya to the Bering Strait.
