# Sierra Leone

## Demographics and Regional Context
Sierra Leone is located in West Africa. According to estimated population data, the region has a population of 1,000,000 people, who are characterized as being chiefly black. Within the broader context of West Africa, Sierra Leone is listed alongside other territories such as Nigeria (which includes Lagos and has an estimated 15,000,000 black and brown inhabitants), the Gold Coast and hinterland (2,700,000), and Gambia (163,000).

## Colonial Administration and Governance
The administration of Sierra Leone has involved various British officials. In December 1826, Dixon Denham, an English traveller and lieutenant-colonel, arrived in Sierra Leone to serve as the superintendent of liberated Africans. He was later appointed governor of the colony in 1828. However, his tenure as governor lasted only five weeks; he died of fever at Freetown on May 8, 1828. Other administrative figures include Sir Charles McCarthy, who served as governor of Sierra Leone and, in 1818, obtained the cession of the Los Islands to Great Britain from the chiefs of the Baga country.

## The Los Islands
The Los Islands (also known as Islas de los Idolos) are a group of volcanic islands situated off the coast of French Guinea in West Africa. They are located approximately 80 miles north-northwest of Freetown, Sierra Leone, between 9° 25´ and 9° 31´ N. and 13° 46´ and 13° 51´ W. 

The archipelago consists of five principal islands: Tamara, Factory, Crawford, White (or Ruma), and Coral. Tamara is the largest, measuring some 8 miles long by 1 to 2 miles broad, and contains the principal settlement and a good harbour. Tamara and Factory islands form part of a ruined crater, with Crawford Island serving as the cone. The highest point in the group is a knoll in Tamara reaching 450 feet above sea level.

The islands are inhabited by approximately 1,500 immigrants of the Baga tribe of Senegambian negroes, who are primarily farmers. The Church of England maintains a flourishing mission there with a native pastorate. Historically, the islands were a center for pirates and slave-traders. To combat these activities, British troops garrisoned the islands between 1812 and 1813, though they eventually withdrew due to the unhealthiness of the climate.

After their cession to Great Britain in 1818, the islands served as headquarters for several traders from Sierra Leone. France recognized the islands as a British possession in 1882, but they were eventually ceded to France under article 6 of the Anglo-French convention on April 8, 1904. France sought the islands due to their strategic geographical position; the capital of French Guinea, Konakry, is located on an islet only 3 miles from Factory Island, leaving it vulnerable to long-range artillery placed on the island.

## Geopolitical Aspirations
Sierra Leone has been identified in historical strategic planning as a territory of interest for colonial expansion. In discussions regarding the potential unification of various African territories under a single ownership, Sierra Leone was listed alongside Senegambia, the Gold Coast, Dahomey, Nigeria, Kamerun, and various other regions in Central, East, and South-West Africa. Proponents of such a union argued that these territories, which collectively possessed over 100,000,000 inhabitants, offered immeasurable prospects due to their richness in men, trade possibilities, natural treasures, and settlement opportunities.
