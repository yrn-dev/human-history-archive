# Jean Jacques Barthelemy

Jean Jacques Barthelemy (1716–1795) was a French writer, numismatist, and scholar who produced a variety of learned works on antiquarian subjects. Born on January 20, 1716, in Cassis, Provence, he received his early education at the college of the Oratory and later at the college of the Jesuits, both located in Marseilles. During his studies for the priesthood, Barthelemy focused on oriental languages and was introduced to the study of classical antiquities, specifically numismatics, by his friend M. Gary of Marseilles.

## Professional Career and Patronage
In 1744, Barthelemy moved to Paris, utilizing a letter of introduction to M. Gros de Boze, who served as the keeper of the royal collection of medals and the perpetual secretary of the Academy of Inscriptions and Belles-lettres. Barthelemy served as de Boze's assistant and succeeded him as keeper of the medals upon de Boze's death in 1753.

In 1755, Barthelemy accompanied M. de Stainville (later the duc de Choiseul), the French ambassador, to Italy. He spent three years engaged in archaeological research during this period. Upon returning to France, Barthelemy became an inmate of Choiseul's house and received significant preferments from him.

During the French Revolution, Barthelemy was arrested on the grounds of being an aristocrat. However, he was released immediately after the duchess of Choiseul informed the Committee of Public Safety of his arrest. In 1793, he was nominated as the librarian of the Bibliotheque Nationale; though he refused this specific post, he resumed his duties as keeper of medals and added many valuable accessions to the national collection. He died on April 30, 1795.

## Major Works and Scholarship
Barthelemy's most famous work is the *Voyage du jeune Anacharsis en Grece, vers le milieu du quatrieme siecle avant l'ere chretienne*, published in four volumes in 1787. This work was the result of thirty years of labor, having been started in 1757. Following its publication in 1789, Barthelemy was elected a member of the French Academy.

The *Voyage* is written from the perspective of a young Scythian, a descendant of the philosopher Anacharsis, who travels to Greece in his youth for instruction. The narrative follows the hero as he tours islands, colonies, and republics, eventually returning to his native land in old age to write the book after a Macedonian hero had overturned the Persian empire. The text is structured like the accounts of modern travellers, detailing the antiquities, government, and customs of the visited regions. It includes a copious introduction for historical details and various dissertations covering the literature of the Athenians, Greek music, and the manners, pursuits, economy, and ruling passions of surrounding states.

While modern scholarship has superseded many of the details in the *Voyage*, the author did not intend the work to be a register of accurately ascertained facts. Instead, his goal was to provide his countrymen with knowledge of Greek civilization in an interesting format. This approach is compared to the *Charicles* of W. A. Becker; while the latter is considered superior in scholarship, it is noted to lack the charm of style found in Barthelemy's work.

## Other Contributions and Correspondence
Beyond his major publication, Barthelemy contributed a concise and instructive memoir to the *Mémoires de l’Académie des Inscriptions* (tom. xxviii. p. 585), in which he used an original act from the archives of Rome to mention an agreement between factions of the 14th century regarding the Coliseum (*de Tiburtino faciendo*).

His personal and professional life is further documented through various published correspondences and memoirs:
* **Correspondence with Paolo Paciaudi:** Chiefly concerning antiquarian subjects, these were edited by Ch. Nisard in 1877.
* **Letters to the comte de Caylus:** Published in 1801 as *Voyage en Italie* by Antoine Serieys.
* **Letters to Mme du Deffand:** Published in a three-volume set in 1866, edited by the marquis de Sainte-Aulaire.
* **Autobiographical works:** *Memoires sur la vie de l'abbe Barthelemy, ecrits par lui-meme* (1824), which includes a notice by Lalande.
* **Collected works:** His *Oeuvres completes* was published in four volumes in 1821, containing a notice by Villenave.
