# Francis Asbury

## Early Life and Conversion
Francis Asbury was born on August 20, 1745, at Hamstead Bridge in the parish of Handsworth, near Birmingham, in Staffordshire, England. Born to poor parents, Asbury received a brief education at the village school of Barre. At fourteen years of age, he began an apprenticeship with a maker of tongues, also known as "buckle chapes." 

Asbury's entry into Methodism occurred in his thirteenth year, and it is probable that his parents were among the early converts of Wesley. By the age of sixteen, he had become a local preacher. Described as a fluent and simple speaker, Asbury's success in preaching led John Wesley to enroll him as a regular itinerant minister in 1767.

## Missionary Work in America
In 1771, Asbury volunteered for missionary work in the American colonies. Upon his arrival in Philadelphia in October of that year, Methodism had been present in the colonies for only three years, and the number of converts was fewer than 300. Asbury provided new life to the movement, and within one year, the membership across several congregations more than doubled. 

In 1772, John Wesley appointed Asbury as "general assistant" to oversee the work in America. Although Thomas Rankin, an older preacher, superseded him in 1773, Asbury remained practically in control of the operations.

## The War of Independence and Exile
During the outbreak of the War of Independence, the Methodist population had grown to several thousand. The group faced unjust suspicions of Loyalism, primarily because they refused to take the prescribed oath. While many ministers, including Thomas Rankin, returned to England, Asbury remained in the colonies due to his sense of duty and sympathy. 

Despite being arrested once and facing frequent threats, Asbury continued his itinerant preaching. Eventually, hostility from the authorities in Maryland forced him into a two-year exile in Delaware, where he remained active but quiet. In 1782, he was reappointed to supervise the Methodist congregations in America. In 1784, John Wesley took the radical step of appointing Francis Asbury and the Rev. Thomas Coke as "bishops" or superintendents of the church in the United States, an action taken in disregard of the authority of the Established Church.

## Legacy and Commemorations
Francis Asbury died in 1816. His *Journals*, published in three volumes in New York in 1852, serve as both a history of his life's work and a valuable commentary on the industrial and social history of the United States during its first forty years.

Several institutions and locations bear his name:
* **Asbury Park:** A city in Monmouth County, New Jersey, founded in 1869 and named in honor of the Rev. Francis Asbury. It was incorporated as a borough in 1874 and chartered as a city in 1897.
* **De Pauw University:** Located in Greencastle, Indiana, this Methodist Episcopal institution was founded in 1837 as Indiana Asbury University. It was renamed in 1884 in honor of Washington Charles de Pauw. The university includes the Asbury College of Liberal Arts.
