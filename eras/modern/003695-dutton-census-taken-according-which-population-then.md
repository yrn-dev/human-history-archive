# Census and Population Records

Census data across various global regions provide insights into demographic shifts, ethnic compositions, and the challenges of official population estimation. These records range from ancient biblical accounts to early 20th-century administrative surveys.

## European and Balkan Demographics
In united Bulgaria, the first census was conducted in 1888, recording a total population of 3,154,375. Subsequent records show a steady increase, with 3,310,713 inhabitants in January 1893 and 3,744,283 in January 1901. By the census of January 12, 1906, the population of united Bulgaria reached 4,028,239, with a density of 88 persons per square mile. This total was comprised of 2,853,704 people in northern Bulgaria and 1,174,535 in Eastern Rumelia.

The 1901 Bulgarian census provided detailed breakdowns of the population by sex and occupation. There were 1,909,567 males and 1,834,716 females. Occupationally, 74.85% (2,802,603 persons) were engaged in agriculture, while 360,834 worked in productive industries, 148,899 in commerce, and 118,824 in liberal professions or government service. The census also attempted to categorize the population by language:
* Bulgars: 2,888,219
* Turks: 531,240
* Gipsies (Tziganes): 89,549
* Rumans: 71,063
* Greeks: 66,635
* Spanish-speaking Jews: 33,661
* Tatars: 18,884
* Armenians: 14,581
* Other nationalities: 30,451

## Asian Population Surveys
In China, the census is described as a very old institution. A record from A.D. 156 indicated a total population of slightly over fifty millions. In more modern times, the process involved distributing house-tickets to the head of every household. However, because there was no fixed return date for these tickets, results remained approximate. A census taken in 1902 reportedly yielded a total of four hundred and ten millions, though some estimates suggest three hundred millions may be more accurate.

In Korea, the first complete census took place in 1897, returning a population of approximately 17,000,000, with females in the majority. This figure was later estimated at a maximum of 12,000,000. The population includes a foreign presence of about 65,000, of whom 60,000 are Japanese.

## Americas and African Territories
In Central America, an official estimate from 1905 placed the population of one region at 500,136, though a satisfactory census was difficult to achieve because Indians and other "ignorant masses" avoided the process due to fears of taxation or military conscription. The first census in 1791 had recorded only 95,500 people. In this region, the Spanish population is concentrated on the Pacific slope, while the Atlantic declivity is sparsely occupied by Indian tribes of unknown numbers. In 1905, the population density was fewer than 11 inhabitants per square mile.

In British Honduras (formerly Balize or Belize), the population in 1905 was 40,372. In British South Africa, the 1904 census recorded a white population of 1,132,226.

In Griqualand West, census data shows growth over several decades. In 1877, the population was 45,277 (including 12,347 whites); by 1891, it rose to 83,215 (29,602 whites); and by 1904, it reached 108,498 (32,570 whites).

## Historical and Specialized Records
Ancient records describe a census taken by David of his dominions, which stretched from the Euphrates to the Mediterranean and from Lebanon to the frontiers of Egypt. Depending on the source, the number of men fit to bear arms was either 800,000 in Israel and 500,000 in Judah, or a higher muster of 1,300,000 to 1,574,000 fighting men.

Other specialized census data include school populations. For the 1908-1909 period, a school census recorded 739,352 students, with 587,051 residing in rural districts. In the preceding 1907-1908 year, the school population was 734,617, with 441,377 enrolled in public schools.
