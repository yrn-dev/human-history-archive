# Kraftmasses (1876) and the Kritik der reinen Erfahrung (1888-1890)

The works *Philosophie als Denken der Welt gemaess dem Princip des kleinsten Kraftmasses* (1876) and *Kritik der reinen Erfahrung* (1888-1890) are the chief publications of the German philosopher Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius (1843-1896). Through these texts, Avenarius sought to establish a philosophical framework that could co-ordinate action and thought.

## Philosophical Foundations and Economy of Thinking
In developing his theories, Avenarius adopted a starting point similar to that of Mach, utilizing the principle of economy of thinking. This approach is central to his attempt to synthesize the relationship between human thought and practical action.

## Kritik der reinen Erfahrung
In the *Kritik der reinen Erfahrung*, published between 1888 and 1890, Avenarius focused on the explanation of pure experience. He examined this concept specifically in relation to the environment and knowledge. His analysis led to the discovery that pure experience is constituted by statements that are dependent upon the environment.

## Empirio-criticism
The philosophy presented in these works is identified as Empirio-criticism. This system is characterized as an idealistic dualism. The archive's sources specify that Empirio-criticism is not a form of materialism, nor is it a realistic philosophy.

## Author Biography
Richard Heinrich Ludwig Avenarius was born in Paris on November 19, 1843. He pursued his education in Berlin and Zuerich, eventually graduating from the university of Leipzig in 1876. During his time at Leipzig, he served as the first editor of the *Vierteljahrsschrift fuer wissenschaftliche Philosophie* and was among the founders of the *Akademisch-philosophische Verein*. 

Following his graduation, Avenarius became a professor of philosophy in Zuerich in 1877, a position he held until his death on August 18, 1896. In addition to his chief works on the principle of the smallest *Kraftmasses* and pure experience, he published an essay regarding the Pantheism of Spinoza in 1868.
