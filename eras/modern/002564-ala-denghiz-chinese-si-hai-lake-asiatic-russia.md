# Lake Balkhash

Lake Balkhash, also known as *Ak-denghiz* or *Ala-denghiz* by the Kirghiz and *Si-hai* by the Chinese, is a lake located in the Kirghiz steppes of Asiatic Russia. It is situated between the governments of Semiryechensk and Semipalatinsk, positioned between 45° to 47° N. and 73° 30' to 79° E. The lake is located approximately 600 miles east of Lake Aral.

## Physical Characteristics
Balkhash is the fourth largest lake in Eurasia, covering an area of 7,115 square miles and sitting at an altitude of 900 feet. The lake is shaped like a broad crescent, extending about 430 miles from the west-southwest to the east-northeast, with its concave side facing south. Its width varies between 36 and 53 miles.

The lake's depth reaches a maximum of 35 feet along its north-western shore. While the lake has no outlet, its waters remain relatively fresh. It typically freezes from November through April. The north-western shore is bordered by a dreary plateau known as the Famine Steppe (*Bek-pak-dala*). Conversely, the south-east shore is low; evidence suggests this shore previously extended as far as the Ala-kul and the Sasyk-kul. In 1903, the Kirghiz reported that the lake's surface had been rising steadily for ten years, following a previous period of decline.

## Hydrology and Geography
The primary feeder of Lake Balkhash is the Ili river, which originates in the Tian-shan Mountains' Khantengri group. The Ili is a major Central Asian river with a total length exceeding 900 miles. Its head-stream, the Tekez, rises at 11,600 feet on the western slopes of mount Kash-katur. After meeting the Kungez, the river becomes the Ili, flowing between the Boro-khoro and Talki ranges to the north and the Trans-Ili Ala-tau mountains to the south. Upon reaching Iliysk, the river turns north-west and enters Lake Balkhash through at least seven mouths, although only the southern arm of the delta carries water permanently.

Other rivers entering the lake from the south-east include the Aksu, the Karatal, and the Lepsa, while the Ayaguz enters from the north-east. The Karatal, Aksu, and Lepsa struggle to flow through reeds and sands that were covered by the lake in the recent past.

## Fauna and Geological History
The fauna of Lake Balkhash and its tributaries was explored by Nikolsky. The findings indicate that the fauna is more similar to that of the rivers in the Tarim basin than to those of Lake Aral; notably, the lake does not contain the common frog. 

Based on these observations, it is considered probable that Lake Balkhash was formerly connected to the lake that filled the Lukchun depression (located at 42-1/2° N. lat. and 89-1/2° E. long.) via the lakes Ayar (Telli-nor) and Ebi-nor. However, research suggests that a connection with Lake Aral was improbable in recent times. The lake has also been the subject of investigation by L. S. Berg.
