# Gamalitis, Sogana, and Trachonitis

The regions of Gamalitis, Sogana, and Trachonitis were historical districts located within the broader territory of Bashan. The boundaries of Bashan are partially derived from early historical records, which note that Og dwelt at Ashteroth and fought the Israelites at Edrei. Other markers of this region include the region of Argob, containing threescore cities, and Mount Hermon, which served as a northern limit.

## Gaulanitis: Gamalitis and Sogana
According to the historian Josephus, Bashan was composed of four provinces: Gaulanitis, Trachonitis, Auranitis, and Batanaea. Gaulanitis, which likely took its name from the city of refuge known as Golan, corresponds to the modern Jaulan. This province extended from the Haj Road to the Jordan lakes.

Josephus further specifies that Gaulanitis was divided into two distinct sections: Gamalitis and Sogana. Sogana was categorized as part of the Upper Gaulanitis, while Gamalitis was part of the Lower Gaulanitis. The city of Gamala, situated on the borders of Agrippa's kingdom, was located in the Lower Gaulanitis. Gamala was noted for its difficult geography, situated on a high mountain ridge with a middle "neck" that gave the city a figure resembling a camel, which contributed to its name. The city featured thick, closely packed houses built on a straight acclivity and was characterized by abrupt edges ending in deep valleys.

## Trachonitis
Trachonitis was one of the four provinces of Bashan and adjoined the territory of Damascus as well as the provinces of Auranitis and Batanaea. In the Gospel of Luke (iii. 1), Trachonitis is mentioned as being part of the territory governed by Philip the tetrarch. 

This region corresponds to the *Trachones* described by Strabo and the modern district of the Leja. Evidence for this identification is found in inscriptions discovered in the Leja that list "Trachon" as the former name of the area.

## Related Provinces of Bashan
The other two provinces of Bashan were Auranitis and Batanaea. Auranitis, referred to as the Hauran in the writings of Ezekiel and by modern Arabs, is located north of Gilead and south of the Jaulan. While some sources apply the name loosely to the entire country east of the Jaulan, it is locally restricted to the narrow strip on the west and the plain south of the Leja.

Batanaea lies east of the Hauran plain and the Leja. This province includes the Hauran mountain, also known as Jebel ed-Druz, and is still remembered in the name *'Ard el-Bathaniyeh*.
