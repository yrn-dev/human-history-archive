# Asa of Judah

Asa was a king of Judah, identified as the son (or potentially the brother) of Abijah, who was the son of Rehoboam. His reign was characterized by religious reforms, conflicts with the kingdom of Israel, and a significant military encounter with a foreign force. During his time on the throne, he was a contemporary of the Israelite kings Baasha, Zimri, and Omri.

## Conflicts with Israel and Baasha
During Asa's reign, Baasha of Israel fortified the city of Ramah, located five miles north of Jerusalem. Fearing the potential mischief this army could cause to his country, Asa sought external assistance. He sent ambassadors to the king of the Damascenes, offering gold and silver to secure a league. Asa reminded the king of Damascus of the friendship that had existed between their forefathers.

The king of Damascus accepted the payment and broke his alliance with Baasha. He dispatched his own forces to attack the cities under Baasha's dominion, resulting in the burning and spoiling of several locations, including Dan, Ijon, and Abelmain. Consequently, Baasha ceased the fortification of Ramah and returned to assist his own people. Asa utilized the building materials left behind at Ramah to construct two strong cities to the immediate north of Jerusalem: Geba and Mizpah.

## The Campaign of Zerah
The Book of Chronicles and other accounts describe a sensational defeat of Zerah, identified as the "Cushite" or the king of the Ethiopians (and possibly Egypt). According to one account, Zerah's army consisted of three hundred chariots of war and one million men. Asa marched against this multitude, relying on his God and praying for help, stating that it was nothing for the Lord to help whether with many or those without power. The narrative records that God struck the Ethiopians with terror, leading to their irrevocable defeat and destruction.

Regarding the identity of Zerah, some interpretations suggest "Cushite" may designate an Arabian, as Cush is described as the father of the Sabaeans. Some scholars argue that if Zerah were a Sabaean or Ethiopian prince, the narrative's difficulties are removed, as no king named Zerah of Ethiopia is known from this period. Additionally, there is a suggestion that the invaders may have been desert hordes in league with Philistine towns, with the battle potentially located at Mareshah.

## Succession and Legacy
Asa's reign also included a great religious revival involving both Judah and Israel. He was eventually succeeded as king by his son, Jehoshaphat.
