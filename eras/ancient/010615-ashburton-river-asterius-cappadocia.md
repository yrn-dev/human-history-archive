# ASHBURTON (RIVER) AND ASTERIUS (OF CAPPADOCIA)

## Ashburton (River)
The Ashburton is a river located in Western Australia. It originates in the mountains situated to the west of the Great Sandy Desert and flows in a north-westward direction for a distance of 400 miles before emptying into the Exmouth Gulf. 

The river's geography is characterized by two distinct regions. In its upper reaches, the Ashburton flows through a rich gold-bearing district that shares the river's name. As it nears its mouth, the river traverses a vast tract of fine pastoral country. The port of Onslow, located at the mouth of the river, serves as the outlet for both the pastoral and gold-bearing districts. Additionally, several pearl-fishing stations are situated near the port of Onslow. The Ashburton river is not navigable.

## Asterius of Cappadocia
Asterius of Cappadocia was a teacher of rhetoric and a sophist in Galatia. Around the year 300, he converted to Christianity and became a disciple of Lucian, who founded the school of Antioch. However, during the persecution led by Maximian in 304, Asterius relapsed into paganism. Although he was later received back into the church by Lucian and received support from the Eusebian party, he never attained an ecclesiastical office.

Asterius is most recognized as an able defender of the semi-Arian position; Athanasius referred to him as the "advocate" of the Arians. His literary output included many works, most notably the *Syntagmation*, as well as commentaries on the Psalms, Romans, and the Gospels. He participated in numerous synods, with the last record of his activity occurring at the synod of Antioch in 341.
