# Kaiserreichs

The term *Kaiserreich* appears across various historical and academic contexts within the archive's records, referring to different imperial entities, legal frameworks, and scholarly catalogs of art and history.

## The Carolingian Empire
Several historical records focus on the *Kaiserreich* during the Carolingian period. Scholarly works such as E. Mühlbacher's *Die Regesten des Kaiserreichs unter den Karolingern* (Innsbruck, 1881) and *Die Regenten des Kaiserreichs unter den Karolingern* (Innsbruck, 1881) provide documentation on the regents and registers of this empire. 

Associated historical sources for this period include the *Annales Fuldenses*, *Annales Bertiniani*, and the *Historiarum Libri* by Nithard. Other relevant texts include the *Vita Hludowici* (both the version by Thegan and the one attributed to Astronomus), and Ermoldus Nigellus's *In honorem Hludowici imperatoris*. Further study of the East Frankish realm is detailed in E. Dümmler's *Geschichte des ostfränkischen Reichs* and B. Simson's *Jahrbücher des deutschen Reiches unter Ludwig dem Frommen* (or *Jahrbücher des fränkischen Reichs unter Ludwig dem Frommen*).

## The Roman Empire and Legal Frameworks
The term also relates to the Roman Empire, specifically regarding the administration of law in its eastern provinces. L. Mitteis's work, *Reichsrecht und Volksrecht in den ostlichen Provinzen des romischen Kaiserreichs*, examines the relationship between imperial law (*Reichsrecht*) and folk law (*Volksrecht*), including contributions to the understanding of Greek law.

## The Latin Empire of Constantinople
The archive contains references to the *lateinischen Kaiserreiches von Konstantinopel* (the Latin Empire of Constantinople), as discussed in the work of Gerland (*Geschichte des lateinischen Kaiserreiches von Konstantinopel*, part i., 1905). Other related accounts of this era include Villehardouin's *La Conquete de Constantinople* and Robert de Clari's *La Prise de Constantinople*.

## Art and Architecture of the Austrian Empire
In the context of art history, the *Kaiserreich* refers to the Austrian Empire. Dr. G. Heider and others authored the work *Kunstdenkmale des oesterreichischen Kaiserreichs*, which serves as a reference for architectural monuments. 

One such monument is the basilica of Parenzo in Istria, dating to approximately A.D. 542. This small basilica, measuring 121 ft. by 32 ft., is noted for having sustained very little alteration. Its architectural features include:
*   A cloistered atrium and a baptistery that is square on the outside and octagonal within.
*   A nave divided from the aisles by nine pillars, some of which were borrowed from earlier structures.
*   Byzantine capitals.
*   A choir occupying the three easternmost bays.
*   An apse containing a bishop's throne and a bench for presbyters.
*   Walls inlaid with mother-of-pearl and rich marble, accompanied by gorgeous mosaics.
