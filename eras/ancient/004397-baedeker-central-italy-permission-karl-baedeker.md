# Karl Baedeker

Karl Baedeker (1801–1859) was a German publisher best known for creating a series of influential guide-books. Born on November 3, 1801, in Essen, Baedeker was the son of a book-shop and printing establishment owner. He eventually established his own independent business in Coblenz, where he began issuing the first of his series of guide-books.

## Development of the Guide-Books
The guide-books produced by Baedeker were initially based on the model of an English series established by John Murray. Over several years, Baedeker developed these guides to cover the majority of the civilized world. While originally published in German, the guides were later issued in French and English as well. Following Karl Baedeker's death, his son Fritz continued the business, which was transferred to Leipzig in 1872.

## Influence and Usage
The Baedeker guides became widely recognized sources of information for travelers. In the early 20th century, they were used as references for various global locations:
* **Cairo:** Baedeker's guides are noted for containing accurate and useful information regarding the city of Cairo.
* **Syria and Palestine:** The *Handbook to Syria and Palestine* (Baedeker-Socin) served as an authority for information on the region.
* **Egypt:** Baedeker's *Egypt* is cited as a source for information regarding the ruins of the temple of Louxor.
* **Belgium:** In the 1910 edition of the guide, the *Majestic Hotel* was listed under the name *Grand Hotel des Bains*. Additionally, Baedeker provided a testimonial for the town of Ostend, describing it as perhaps the most elegant watering-place in Europe at that time.

## Historical Context and References
The guides were utilized by a variety of individuals, though their comprehensiveness was sometimes debated. In one account from the West during wartime, a guide to a cathedral was contrasted with the knowledge of a local named Dahlgren, with the author noting that "Baedeker" did not know one-tenth of what Dahlgren knew. 

Furthermore, Baedeker's work provided architectural and geographical context for historians and researchers. For instance, plans of the Basilica Julia in Rome were sourced from Baedeker's *Central Italy*.
