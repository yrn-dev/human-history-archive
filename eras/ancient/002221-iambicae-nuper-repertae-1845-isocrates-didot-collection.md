# Iambicae nuper repertae (1845) and Isocrates in the Didot Collection

The *Fabellae Iambicae nuper repertae* (1845) and the edition of Isocrates in the Didot collection of classics (1846) are scholarly works associated with the Swiss philologist and textual critic Johann Georg Baiter.

## Johann Georg Baiter
Johann Georg Baiter (1801–1877) was born in Zurich on May 31, 1801. He received his early education in Zurich before attending the university of Tuebingen in 1818. Due to a lack of funds, he returned to his hometown to work as a private tutor for several years. Between 1824 and 1829, Baiter pursued further studies under several scholars: Friedrich Thiersch at Munich, Georg Dissen at Goettingen, and Christian Lobeck at Koenigsberg.

From 1833 until 1876, Baiter served as an *Oberlehrer* at the gymnasium in Zurich, where he remained until his death on October 10, 1877. Baiter specialized in textual criticism, focusing primarily on the Attic orators and Cicero. He was noted for his accuracy in collations and his ability to locate the best manuscript authorities.

## Collaborative Works and Publications
Baiter frequently collaborated with other scholars, most notably Orelli, who considered Baiter to be his "right-hand man." Their joint efforts included:
*   **Babrius**: The publication of *Fabellae Iambicae nuper repertae* in 1845.
*   **Isocrates**: An edition of Isocrates produced for the Didot collection of classics in 1846.
*   **Cicero**: Baiter was associated with Orelli's extensive work on Cicero, assisting with the *Onomasticon Tullianum* (1836–1838) and *Ciceronis Scholiastae* (1833). He also assisted in the second edition of the Cicero with Orelli and Halm.

In addition to his work with Orelli, Baiter collaborated with Sauppe to edit Lycurgus' *Leocratea* (1834) and the *Oratores Attici* (1838–1850). He also worked with Sauppe to translate Leake's *Topography of Athens*. With Orelli and Winckelmann, he produced a critical edition of Plato (1839–1842) that utilized two new manuscripts. Other independent or collaborative achievements include the *Fasti Consulares* and *Triumphales*, for which he was solely responsible, and an edition of Cicero for the Tauchnitz series (1860–1869) produced with Kayser. He also provided new editions of Horace and Orelli's Tacitus.

## Isocrates and the Didot Collection
Isocrates was an orator whose works were edited by Baiter for the Didot collection of classics in 1846. Baiter had previously edited Isocrates' *Panegyricus* in 1831. 

In the broader context of historical and rhetorical study, Isocrates and Lycurgus are noted for their discussions regarding the recitation of Homer at the Panathenaea. However, these orators do not mention the poems being collected and arranged at Athens. Furthermore, the treatises of ancient rhetoricians, including those preceding the inventor Tisiâ, were eventually superseded by Aristotle, who improved their expositions and collected them "nominatim."

The Didot collection also served as a vehicle for other fragments of ancient texts. For example, fragments of the historian Theopompus were printed by M. Didot. One such fragment, preserved by Theodorus Metochita, was categorized by Didot and another editor, Wichers, as a *Fragmentum Spurium* based on the authority of Plutarch.
