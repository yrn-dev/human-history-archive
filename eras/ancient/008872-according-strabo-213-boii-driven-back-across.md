# The Boii

The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. Known to the Romans at least by the time of Plautus—who referred to them contemptuously in the *Captivi*—the Boii split into two primary groups early in their history. One group migrated into Germany, while the other moved into Italy. Some members of the tribe remained in their original territory; for example, during the Second Punic War, a Boian prince named Magalus offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the Carthaginian leader had crossed the Pyrenees.

## Migration and Settlement in Italy
The group of Boii that immigrated to Italy crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the Po valley. Upon finding the region already occupied, they crossed the river and drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans. They established their presence as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna. According to Cato, as cited in Pliny's *Natural History*, the Boii consisted of as many as 112 different tribes. Evidence from tombs at La Tene, Hallstatt, and other sites suggests they were fairly civilized.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii engaged in several wars with the Roman Republic. They suffered a defeat alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake in 283. Later, in 224, they were forced to submit following the battle of Telamon in Etruria. Despite this submission, the Boii maintained a hatred for Rome. This animosity was exacerbated by the foundation of the Roman colonies of Placentia and Cremona. Consequently, during the Second Punic War in 218, they provided valuable assistance to Hannibal. The Boii continued their struggle against Rome until 191, when P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica finally subdued them, resulting in the Boii losing nearly half of their territory.

## Displacement and the Danube Settlements
Following their defeat, Strabo records that the Boii were driven back across the Alps. They settled on the Danube on the land of the Taurisci, their kinsmen, in an area adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia. However, most authorities believe the Boii had established settlements on the Danube from a very early period. This included part of modern Bohemia, which was known as *Boiohemum*, meaning the "land of the Boii." 

The Boii also left traces of their name in Bohemia and penetrated the Black Forest. In the first century, they were subdued by the Marcomanni, a Teutonic tribe. Those Boii who settled in Noricum were later mingled with the Lombards and became known as the Boiovarii or Boio Arii (Bavaria), with "var" appearing in some German dialects to mean descendants or remains.

## Later Migrations and Decline
Around 60 B.C., some Boii migrated to Pannonia and Noricum. During this period, 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte.

In the earliest historical period, the Boii inhabited part of the land that now bears their name, with their capital called Boiohemum. According to ancient traditions accepted by modern historians of Bohemia, the tribe was weakened by constant warfare with neighboring tribes. They were eventually subdued by the Marcomanni around 12 B.C. Subsequently, the Marcomanni were expelled by other Teutonic tribes, and Bohemia was eventually conquered by Slavic tribes, most notably the Cechs, who became the masters of the country in the 5th century.
