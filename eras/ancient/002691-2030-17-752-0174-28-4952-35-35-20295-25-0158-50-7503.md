## Ancient Military History

The archive contains accounts of various ancient conflicts and strategic movements.

### The Battle of the Trebia
Consul Sempronius, following orders from the senate, traveled from Sicily to Ariminum. He then marched to the Trebia, a small river in Lombardy that flows into the Po above Placentia. There, he joined forces with Scipio. Hannibal's army was positioned across the small river from the Roman camp. Frequent skirmishes occurred between the two forces; in one instance, Sempronius led a body of horse to gain a trifling advantage over the Carthaginians, which increased his own opinion of his merit.

### Theban and Athenian Engagements
Records describe a tactical operation by Epaminondas, who organized a column of infantry and a column of cavalry for an attack on his left, while placing a reserve body on rising ground to the right against the Athenians. The enemy observed these movements but did not understand them or heed them until Epaminondas gave the command to "take up arms." This resulted in surprise and tumultuous movement among the opposing force, with hoplites running to their places and horsemen rushing to bridle their horses and put on breast-plates. The archive suggests Epaminondas may have used cavalry movements in the front to conceal his preparations.

## Geography and Urban Centers

### Damanhur
Damanhur is a town located in Lower Egypt, situated 38 miles east-southeast of Alexandria by rail. It serves as the capital of the province of Behera. Known in ancient times as Timenhor ("town of Horus"), it was the capital of a nome during Ptolemaic times and was located on the Canopic branch of the Nile. The town's Greek name, Hermopolis Parva, suggests Thoth as the local god, though its other name implies the worship of Horus (equated with Apollo) in the same form as at Edfu. As of 1907, the population of Damanhur was 38,752. The town is known for several cotton manufactories and a fair that occurs three times a year.
