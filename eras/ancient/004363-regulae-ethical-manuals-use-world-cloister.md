# Regulae

The *Regulae* are ethical manuals authored by Basil, designed for specific applications in different spheres of existence. These texts are categorized by their intended use: one serves as a guide for those living "in the world," while the other is intended for those in "the cloister."

## Context and Authorship
The *Regulae* are among the principal writings of Basil, whose work reflects a strong inclination toward asceticism. In addition to these ethical manuals, Basil authored the *Moralia*, as well as theological works such as *De Spiritu Sancto* and three books written against Eunomius, the primary exponent of Anomoian Arianism. Basil was also recognized as a famous preacher, producing an exposition of the psalter and a series of lenten lectures on the *Hexaemeron*.

Beyond his written manuals, Basil's efforts as a reformer were focused on the reformation of the monastic orders of the East and the improvement of the liturgy. His personal character, as revealed in his three hundred letters, is described as observant, tender, optimistic, and occasionally playful, despite facing ecclesiastical unrest and ill-health.

## Purpose and Application
The *Regulae* function as practical guides for ethical conduct. By providing distinct manuals for the world and the cloister, the texts address the different moral requirements and behavioral standards necessary for secular life versus the secluded, disciplined life of a monastic community. This distinction mirrors a broader historical tension between the "wisdom of the man of the world" and the "saintliness of the cloister," a balance exemplified by figures such as Marcus Aurelius, whose precepts combined these two modes of existence.

## Related Ethical Manuals and Traditions
The use of manuals to codify ethics and professional standards is a recurring theme across various historical and cultural contexts:

*   **Buddhist Ethics:** In the Buddhist tradition, the *Dhamma Sangani* is an ancient manual for students dating to the 4th century B.C. It provides a classified exposition of the Dhamma (doctrine) and is based on psychological conceptions that underpin Buddhist ethics.
*   **Medieval Encyclopedism:** In the early 11th century, the monk Byrhtferth, a pupil of Abbo of Fleury, produced an encyclopaedic manual. This work compiled knowledge on several sciences, including ethics, rhetoric, arithmetic, astronomy, chronology, and metre.
*   **Legal and Professional Standards:** In modern legal contexts, manuals such as the United States Attorneys' Manual (USAM) serve as guides for federal prosecutors. The USAM provides general principles and desirable practices to ensure that prosecutorial authority is exercised rationally, objectively, and in a manner that promotes the fair administration of Federal criminal laws.
*   **Class-Based Ethics:** In Japanese history, the ethics of the *bushi* (samurai) were criticized by the philosopher Dr. Inouye Tetsujiro for focusing exclusively on private morality while ignoring public morality.
