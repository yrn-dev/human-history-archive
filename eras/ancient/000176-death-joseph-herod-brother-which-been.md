# The Death of Joseph, Brother of Herod

In the history of the reign of Herod the Great, multiple individuals bore the name Joseph. To avoid confusion, historical records distinguish between Herod's uncle (the second husband of Salome), Herod's treasurer, Herod's nephew (the husband of Olympias), and Joseph, the brother of Herod.

## Role and Administration
Joseph, the brother of Herod, served as a key military and administrative figure for the king. He was entrusted with the administration of the public affairs of the kingdom, a role that required him to be frequently in the presence of Queen Mariamne. During these interactions, Joseph often spoke of Herod's great affection and kindness toward the queen. He went so far as to mention a specific charge he had received from the king, suggesting that Herod was unable to live without Mariamne and could not endure separation from her even after death. While Joseph intended these comments to demonstrate the king's inclinations, the women—particularly Alexandra—interpreted them as evidence of Herod's tyrannical nature, fearing they could not escape his severe usage even after his passing.

In addition to his administrative duties, Joseph was utilized for strategic military placements. Herod sent Joseph to Idumea with four hundred horsemen and two thousand footmen to maintain control of the region and prevent any innovations by Antigonus.

## Military Campaign and Death
The death of Joseph occurred during a conflict with Antigonus in Judea. While Herod was away visiting Mark Antony, Joseph was tasked with managing affairs in the region. After pitching his camp in the mountains, Joseph received five regiments from Machaerus. He proceeded hastily to Jericho to reap the corn belonging to that area.

The army Joseph led consisted largely of Roman regiments that had been recently raised in Syria; these soldiers were unskillful in war. Consequently, the enemy attacked Joseph in a location of geographical difficulty. Joseph was slain while fighting bravely, and his entire army was lost, resulting in the deaths of six regiments.

## Aftermath and Treatment of the Body
Following the victory, Antigonus treated the body of Joseph barbarously. Despite the fact that Joseph's brother, Pheroras, offered fifty talents to redeem the body, Antigonus refused and cut off Joseph's head.

The defeat had significant political repercussions in the region. In Galilee, those of Herod's party were seized by revolting Galileans and drowned in the lake. Much of Judea became seditious, while Machaerus fortified the place known as Gitta in Samaria.

Upon learning of these events, Herod's reaction was one of severe indignation. He eventually identified Pappus as the murderer of his brother. In an act of vengeance, Herod cut off the head of Pappus and sent it to his other brother, Pheroras.

## Family Context
Joseph was part of a complex royal family. His brother Pheroras was a partner in all affairs of the kingdom except for the diadem and served as a tetrarch of the land beyond Jordan. Joseph's lineage continued through his son, also named Joseph. This son of Joseph (and nephew of Herod the king) married Olympias, the daughter of Herod and a Samaritan woman. This union produced a daughter, Mariamne, who later married Herod, the brother of Agrippa.
