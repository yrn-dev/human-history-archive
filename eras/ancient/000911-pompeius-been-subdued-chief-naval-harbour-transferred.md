# Misenum and the Naval Harbours of Campania

## The Transition to Misenum
In the history of Roman naval infrastructure in Campania, Italy, a significant shift in strategic operations occurred following the defeat of Sextus Pompeius. After Pompeius had been subdued, the chief naval harbour was transferred to Misenum.

## Lake Avernus and the Portus Iulius
Prior to the move to Misenum, naval operations were centered around Lake Avernus. Lake Avernus is an old volcanic crater located approximately 1 1/2 miles north of Baiae. The lake is nearly 2 miles in circumference, reaches a depth of 213 feet, and sits 3 1/2 feet above sea level with no natural outlet. In ancient times, the area was characterized by dense forests and served as the setting for various legends; it was viewed as the abode of the Cimmerii and the entrance used by Aeneas and Odysseus to descend into the infernal regions. Its Greek name, *Aornos*, was interpreted to mean that no bird could fly across it.

In 37 B.C., Agrippa converted Lake Avernus into a naval harbour known as the *Portus Iulius*. This was achieved by joining the lake to the Lacus Lucrinus via a canal. To further connect the site to the sea and reduce the distance to Cumae, Agrippa bored a tunnel through the hill on the north-west side of the lake. This tunnel, which measures over 1/2 mile in length, is currently referred to as the Grotta della Pace.

## Associated Structures and Later Works
Several architectural remains are located around Lake Avernus. On the east side of the lake, there are remains of baths, which include a brickwork octagonal hall from the 1st century known as the Temple of Apollo. On the south side, the Grotto of the Cumaean Sibyl consists of a rock-cut passage with vertical apertures for ventilation; it is possible that this structure was part of the works associated with the naval harbour.

Later, in A.D. 64, Nero proposed the construction of a canal from Baiae to the Tiber. Works for this project appear to have commenced near Lake Avernus. One theory suggests that the Grotta della Pace may actually be a portion of Nero's proposed canal.

## Geography of the Region
The area surrounding the former naval center is marked by volcanic activity and water bodies. To the south of Lake Avernus lies the Lacus Lucrinus. To the south-east is the Monte Nuovo, a volcanic hill that was upheaved in 1538 and contains a deep extinct crater at its center.
