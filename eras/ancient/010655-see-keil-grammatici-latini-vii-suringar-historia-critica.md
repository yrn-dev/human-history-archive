# Grammatici Latini and Related Latin Grammatical Works

The study of Latin grammar in antiquity and the subsequent preservation of these works are documented through various treatises, commentaries, and critical editions. A significant resource for these texts is *Grammatici Latini*, edited by H. Keil, which contains the works of several ancient grammarians and philologists.

## Arusianus Messius
Arusianus Messius (also referred to as Messus) was a Latin grammarian who flourished during the 4th century A.D. He authored a small work titled *Exempla Elocutionum*, which was dedicated to Probinus and Olybrius, the consuls for the year 395. This work consists of an alphabetical list, primarily focusing on verbs that allow for more than one construction. To illustrate these points, Messius utilized examples from four specific writers: Cicero, Terence, Sallust, and Virgil. The writer Cassiodorus is the only known author to mention Arusianus, referring to the *Exempla Elocutionum* by the term *Quadriga*.

## Aemilius Asper
Aemilius Asper was a Latin grammarian who possibly lived in the 2nd century A.D. He is noted for writing commentaries on Virgil, Sallust, and Terence. Fragments of his work on Virgil indicate that he possessed taste and good judgment as both a commentator and a critic. 

Distinct from the 2nd-century commentator, there are two short grammatical treatises extant under the name of Asper. These works are considered to be of very little value and date to a much later period, specifically the 6th century during the time of Priscian.

## Other Grammatical and Philological Works
Various other authors contributed to the body of Latin grammatical and literary study:

*   **Cassiodorus:** In his ninety-third year, he produced *De Orthographia*, a compilation derived from the works of twelve grammarians, concluding with his contemporary, Priscian.
*   **Donatus:** The *Ars* and its associated commentaries by others, including Servius, are preserved in *Grammatici Latini*. Additionally, Tiberius Donatus wrote a commentary on the *Aeneid* of Virgil.
*   **Dositheus:** The *Grammatica* of Dositheus is included in *Grammatici Latini* and was also published separately in 1871.

## Critical Sources and Historiography
The study of these grammarians and the history of Latin philology has been supported by several critical works. Suringar's *Historia Critica Scholiastarum Latinorum* (published 1834–1835) is cited as a source for the works of both Arusianus Messius and Aemilius Asper. Other relevant scholarly works include Grafenhan's *Geschichte der klassischen Philologie im Alterthum* and Schanz's *Geschichte der romischen Litteratur*.
