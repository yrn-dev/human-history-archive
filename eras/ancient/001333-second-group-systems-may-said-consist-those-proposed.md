# Systems of Chronological Emendation

In the study of historical chronology, specifically regarding the dynasties of the Kings' List, various systems have been proposed to resolve discrepancies in the data. These systems are categorized into three primary groups based on their methodology for handling conflicting information.

## Classification of Chronological Systems

The first group of systems obtains results by selecting specific data to rely upon while ignoring other available information. This group is noted for producing higher dates than the second group.

The second group consists of systems proposed by Rost, Peiser, Marquart, and Lehmann-Haupt. Unlike the first group, these writers attempted to resolve discrepancies in the data by emending the figures provided by the inscriptions. Generally, the second group produced higher dates than the third group.

The third group includes systems proposed by Niebuhr and Hommel. This group is distinguished by its specific proposals regarding the treatment of Dynasty II to effect reductions in the dates assigned to Dynasty I.

## The Second Group: Emendation of Figures

The writers of the second group sought to reconcile conflicting data by altering specific figures. In 1891, Peiser proposed an emendation to the length of Dynasty III to ensure the total duration of the dynasties aligned with the statement of Nabonidus regarding the date of Khammurabi and the chronological system of Berossus. He suggested that the Kings' List reading of "9 soss and 36 years" (totaling 576 years) was a scribal error for "6 soss and 39 years." This change reduced the length of Dynasty III by 177 years, which consequently reduced the dates assigned to Dynasties I and II.

In 1897, Rost further reduced the figure suggested by Peiser. However, he partially offset this reduction by emending a figure engraved on the rock at Bavian regarding Sennacherib's date for Marduk-nadin-akh's defeat of Tiglath-pileser I; Rost argued that the engraved figure "418" was a mistake for "478." By 1900, Rost abandoned the emendation of Sennacherib's figure and decreased his reduction of Dynasty III, resulting in a change of only one year for the start of Dynasty I.

Lehmann-Haupt's first system (1898) initially resembled those of Maspero, Delitzsch, Winckler, Rogers, Sayce, and Oppert by accepting the figures of the Kings' List without emendation. However, he achieved a low date for the close of Dynasty III by reducing Sennacherib's figure in the Bavian inscription by one hundred years. Lehmann-Haupt's influence is evident in the system published by Marquart in 1899. Additionally, Lehmann-Haupt arrived at a slightly reduced figure for the beginning of Dynasty I by incorporating information from the first published date-list.

## The Third Group: Treatment of Dynasty II

The third group focused on the role of Dynasty II to adjust the chronology of Dynasty I. In 1886, Hommel proposed reversing the order of the first two dynasties of the Kings' List, placing Dynasty II before Dynasty I, primarily to reduce the date of Khammurabi. In subsequent systems (1895 and 1898), and an alternative scheme in 1901, Hommel abandoned this reversal. Instead, he adopted a suggestion from Halevy that Dynasty III followed Dynasty I immediately. Hommel suggested that Dynasty II was either mainly apocryphal or synchronized with Dynasty I.

Niebuhr modified Hommel's second theory. Rather than ignoring Dynasty II entirely, Niebuhr reduced its independent existence to 143 years and proposed that it overlapped Dynasty I by 225 years.

Hommel's 1898 system produced extremely low dates because he combined his own elimination of Dynasty II with Peiser's emendation of the length of Dynasty III. By 1901, Hommel abandoned Peiser's emendation and offered two alternative schemes: one that assigned Dynasty II an independent existence of approximately 171 years to reconcile the Kings' List with Berossus, and another that essentially mirrored his 1895 theory.
