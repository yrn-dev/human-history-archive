# Herod's Veteran Soldiers and Regional Disturbances

The period following the reign of Herod was marked by significant instability, characterized by internal military revolts, the rise of opportunistic claimants to power, and persistent banditry across the region.

## Military Unrest and Claims to Kingship
During a period of widespread disturbances in the country, many individuals attempted to establish themselves as kings. In Idumea, two thousand of Herod's veteran soldiers assembled, arming themselves to fight against the king's party. This conflict was met by Achiabus, the king's first cousin, who conducted his defense from some of the most strongly fortified locations to avoid engaging the veteran soldiers in direct conflict on the plains.

Similar unrest occurred in the city of Sepphoris in Galilee. A man named Judas, the son of the arch-robber Hezekias—who had previously overran the country before being subdued by King Herod—gathered a large multitude. Judas broke into the royal armory to arm his followers and launched attacks against those seeking to gain dominion.

## The Trachonite Robberies
The region of Trachonitis was a center of persistent lawlessness. Originally, Zenodorus hired the "house of Lysanias," but seeking higher revenues, he partnered with the robbers inhabiting Trachonitis. These inhabitants lived in a "mad way," pillaging the country of the Damascenes with the cooperation of Zenodorus. Because they possessed no cities or lands, the Trachonites lived in common with their cattle in large underground dens and receptacles with narrow entrances, utilizing granaries and pools of water to resist attackers.

Cæsar eventually ordered the destruction of these "nests of robbers" and granted the land to Herod to ensure the neighboring countries would no longer be disturbed. Under Herod's initial rule, the inhabitants were forced to plough the land and live quietly, as Herod would not permit them to rob.

## Conflict with Arabia
While Herod was sailing to Rome to commit Antipater to Cæsar's protection and accuse his son Alexander, the Trachonites spread a report that Herod was dead. They revolted and returned to robbing their neighbors. Although Herod's commanders subdued them in his absence, approximately forty principal robbers fled to Arabia. They were granted a place of strength and protection by Sylleus, who had previously failed in his attempt to marry Salome.

From this secure retreat, the robbers overran Judea and all of Celesyria. Upon his return from Rome, Herod found his dominions greatly suffered. Unable to reach the robbers in Arabia, Herod instead slew the relations of the robbers within Trachonitis. This provoked the robbers further, as their law required them to avenge the murderers of their relations by all possible means, leading to continued impunity and destruction throughout Herod's dominion. Herod later sought the punishment of these robbers through Saturninus and Volumnius.

## Other Military and Civil Conflicts
The instability of the era extended to other military engagements. In one instance, Herod's brother Joseph was given full power in Judea but was charged not to attack Antigonus until Herod's return. Joseph ignored this command and marched toward Jericho with five cohorts provided by Machaerus to seize corn during the summer. Joseph and the entire Roman cohorts were destroyed in a mountain ambush. These cohorts consisted of new-raised men from Syria; they lacked the presence of veteran soldiers who might have supported those unskillful in war.

Additionally, internal strife led to violent clashes in the market-place and temple. During a conflict involving Herod's party and those supporting Antigonus, sixty men were stationed as guards in houses adjoining the temple. Tumultuous crowds burnt these guards, prompting Herod to respond with rage, resulting in a cycle of daily ambushes and slaughters.
