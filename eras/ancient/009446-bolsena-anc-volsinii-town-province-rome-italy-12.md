# BOLSENA

Bolsena, known in ancient times as Volsinii, is a town located in the province of Rome, Italy. It is situated on the north-east bank of the lake of Bolsena, approximately 12 miles west-south-west of Orvieto by road. As of 1901, the town had a population of 3,286.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town is dominated by a picturesque castle dating from the medieval period. A significant religious site in the town is the church of S. Christina, which dates from the 11th century. According to legend, S. Christina was martyred in 278 by drowning in the lake. The church contains frescoes that may belong to the school of Giotto and features some high-quality terra cottas by the Della Robbia. Around 1500, Cardinal Giovanni de' Medici (who later became Pope Leo X) constructed a fine Renaissance facade for the building.

Beneath the church are catacombs containing the tomb of the saint, which was discovered in 1880.

## The Miracle of Bolsena
In 1263, a miracle occurred at one of the altars in the crypt of the church of S. Christina. A Bohemian priest, who held skeptical views regarding the doctrine of transubstantiation, became convinced of its truth when drops of blood appeared on the host he was consecrating. In commemoration of this event, Pope Urban IV instituted the festival of Corpus Christi and ordered that the cathedral of Orvieto be erected. This miracle was later depicted in a celebrated fresco by Raphael located in the Vatican.

## Lake Bolsena
The town is situated on the Lake of Bolsena (ancient *Lacus Volsiniensis*), which is almost circular in shape. The lake is 71 square miles in area, reaches a depth of 480 feet, and sits 1,000 feet above sea level. While it was the central point of a large volcanic district, it is probably not an extinct crater itself. The lake's sides feature fine basaltic formations in certain areas. Although the lake abounds in fish, its banks are somewhat deserted and are not free from malaria.

The lake contains two islands:
*   **Bisentina**: Home to a church constructed by Vignola.
*   **Martana**: Contains the remains of a castle where Amalasuntha, the daughter of Theodoric, was imprisoned and strangled.

## Historical Geography
According to the generally adopted theory, the Roman Volsinii was transferred to Bolaena. Meanwhile, the Etruscan Volsinii occupied the site of Orvieto, which led to Orvieto being called *Urbs vetus* during medieval and late classical times.
