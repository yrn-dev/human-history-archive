# Tribun

The term "tribun" (or tribune) appears across various historical contexts in the archive, referring to political offices of the people, judicial bodies, and specific publications used for political agitation.

## The Roman Tribunate and its Legacy
In ancient Rome, the office of the tribune was associated with the plebeians. The archive records that the nobility often opposed candidates for the tribunate; for example, Caius faced opposition from all men of distinction during his campaign, though he was eventually elected fourth. Once in office, Caius used his position to speak freely, reminding the people of ancestral customs where tribunes were held in high regard—noting that in former times, Romans went to war on behalf of a tribune who had been insulted and condemned a citizen to death for failing to make way for a tribune in the Forum.

Later Roman history shows the tribunate as a tool for political struggle. Publius Furius, a tribune and son of a former slave, used his year of office to oppose the return of Metellus. Following his term, Furius was prosecuted by another tribune, Caius Canuleius, and was subsequently torn to pieces in the Forum by the people.

In the middle ages, there were beliefs that the commonwealth had been restored in the tenth century through the revival of the name and office of the tribunes of the commons by plebeian magistrates. However, critical analysis suggests these were merely titles bestowed by emperors or assumed by powerful citizens to denote rank and patrician descent, rather than functioning orders of government.

## Rienzi and the Reformation of Rome
In a later period, the tribune Rienzi enacted a sudden and transient reformation of Rome. Rienzi established a tribunal that was accessible to strangers and the poor, applying impartial rigor regardless of birth, dignity, or church immunities. He abolished private sanctuaries and privileged houses that had previously protected offenders from justice. 

Under Rienzi's leadership, high-ranking figures were held accountable: the lord of the Ursini family was fined four hundred florins for negligence regarding a stolen mule, and Peter Agapet Colonna, a former senator, was arrested for debt or injury. The most notable example of Rienzi's inflexibility was the trial and execution of Martin Ursini, who was led to the gallows after being stripped of his mantle and bound, despite his status as the purple of two cardinals. Following the death of Rienzi, the poet Petrarch, who had initially viewed the tribune as a plebeian hero, shifted his hopes for the restoration of a free republic toward the king of the Romans.

## The Tribun du Peuple
In the context of the French Revolution, the *Tribun du peuple* was a publication founded by Babeuf. Originally titled the *Journal de la liberte de la presse* on September 3, 1794, the name was changed to *Le Tribun du peuple* on October 5. Through this journal, Babeuf—who called himself "Gracchus"—attacked the economic outcomes of the Revolution based on his socialistic theories and defended the men of Thermidor.

Babeuf's political trajectory was marked by arrests and imprisonment. While imprisoned at Arras, he was influenced by terrorist prisoners such as Lebois. Upon his release, Babeuf became a confirmed terrorist. In No. 33 of the *Tribun*, he proclaimed a Utopia which he believed could only be realized through the restoration of the constitution of 1793. This position placed him in conflict with public opinion. In February 1795, Babeuf was arrested again, and the *Tribun du peuple* was publicly burnt in the Theatre des Bergeres by the *jeunesse doree*.

## Athenian Tribunals
In ancient Greece, the tribunals at Athens served as a mechanism for the administration of the Athenian empire. These tribunals provided a means for various small communities to have their disputes peaceably redressed. They also functioned as a refuge for those seeking redress against wrongs committed by individual Athenian citizens.

The archive also notes the role of the "democratical tribunal" in legal sentences. For instance, Alkibiadês was subject to a sentence of banishment passed by the democratical tribunal on grounds of treason and irreligion. His eventual restoration required a vote of security from the armament, as the soldiers were hesitant to reverse a sentence solemnly passed by the tribunal.
