# The Death of Salome and the Cities of Herod and Philip

## The Life and Legacy of Salome
Salome was the sister of Herod the Great. Throughout her life, she was a figure of significant influence and wealth within the Herodian dynasty. She was married first to Costobarus, and after his execution for adultery, she was married again to Pheroras. During her time, she faced accusations from Pheroras, who claimed she had agreed to marry Silleus, the procurator of Obodas, the king of Arabia, who was an enemy of Herod. However, Salome was eventually pardoned for these and other accusations.

Salome played a critical role during the transition of power following the death of Herod the Great. Before the soldiers were informed of the king's death, Salome and Alexas dismissed prisoners held in the hippodrome, claiming the king had ordered them to return to their own lands. Subsequently, Salome and Alexas gathered the soldiery in the amphitheater at Jericho to announce the king's death and read his final letters and testament.

In terms of her estate, Caesar granted Salome the royal palace of Ascalon and made her the mistress of Ashdod, Phasaelis, and Jamnia, providing her with a revenue of sixty talents. Upon her own death, Salome bequeathed her toparchy, Jamriga, and her palm tree plantation in Phasaelis to Julia, the wife of Augustus.

## The Tetrarchies of Philip and Herod Antipas
Following the death of Herod the Great, Caesar divided the kingdom. One half was given to Archelaus as Ethnarch. The remaining half was split into two tetrarchies for two other sons of Herod: Philip and Herod Antipas.

Herod Antipas was granted the tetrarchy of Galilee and Perea, which produced a revenue of two hundred talents. Philip was given the tetrarchy of Batanea, Trachonitis, Auranitis, and certain parts of Zeno's house near Jamnia, with a revenue of one hundred talents. 

Philip the tetrarch is characterized as the best of Herod's posterity due to his love of justice and peace. He remained in his tetrarchy through the transition of the Roman empire from Augustus—who reigned for fifty-seven years, six months, and two days—to Tiberius, the son of Julia.

## City Construction by Herod and Philip
The tetrarchs and their father were active in the construction of cities across their territories. Philip built the city of Cesarea in the region of Paneas and at the fountains of Jordan. He also established the city of Julias in the lower Gaulonitis.

Herod Antipas constructed the city of Tiberius in Galilee. Additionally, he built another city also named Julias in Perea, located beyond the Jordan.

## Family Relations and Conflicts
The Herodian family was marked by complex marriages and internal strife. Salome's daughter, also named Salome, was the child of Herodias and Herod-Philip (a son of Herod the Great). Herodias later divorced Herod-Philip while he was still alive to marry her husband's brother, Herod Antipas, the tetrarch of Galilee. This marriage was described as adulterous and incestuous.

John the Baptist reproved Herod Antipas for this union. In response to this reproof, the younger Salome—the daughter of Herodias—occasioned the unjust beheading of John the Baptist.
