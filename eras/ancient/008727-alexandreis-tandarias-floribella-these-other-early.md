# Alexandreis and Tandarias a Floribella

## Overview of Early Bohemian Literature
The earliest records of the Bohemian or Czech language are described as very ancient. While some documents, specifically the manuscripts of Kralodvur (Koniginhof) and Zelena Hora (Gruneberg), are almost certainly forgeries created during the early part of the 19th century, there are genuine early documents. These authentic records include several legends—most notably those of St Dorothy and St Catherine—as well as various hymns.

## Epic Fragments
Among the preserved remnants of early Bohemian writing are several ancient epic fragments. Two specific examples of these fragments are the *Alexandreis* and *Tandarias a Floribella*. Following the revival of Bohemian literature in the 19th century, these and other early writings from the Bohemian tradition have been printed.

## The Chronicle of Dalimil
Another significant piece of early Bohemian literature is a rhymed chronicle. Although it is generally known as the chronicle of Dalimil, this attribution is considered wrong. The author of this work likely lived during the reign of King John, who ruled from 1310 to 1346. 

The chronicle provides a historical record of Bohemian events starting from the earliest period and continuing through the reign of King Henry of Carinthia, who was the immediate predecessor to King John. A defining characteristic of this chronicle is the presence of a strong feeling of racial antipathy toward the Germans.
