# Ma-vera-un-nahr

Ma-vera-un-nahr (also written as Ma wara 'l Nahr) is a region of Central Asia known in classical titles as Transoxiana. The name refers to the land located beyond the river.

## Geography and Early History
The region was known to ancient peoples as Sogdiana. Because of its eastern location, it was never brought under the dominion of Rome. The foundation of its capital is attributed to the great Persian hero Efrasiab. Following the conquests of Alexander the Great, Sogdiana became part of the empire of the Seleucidae, sharing a similar historical trajectory with the neighboring region of Bactria.

Later, the Yue-chi, a nomad people, migrated from the east into the valley of the Oxus. They gradually established themselves as a settled territorial power within Sogdiana and Bactria. Under the rule of King Kadphises I, who is believed to have ascended the throne around A.D. 45, the dominions of the Yue-chi extended from Bokhara to the Indus.

## Persian and Islamic Influence
The district was eventually reconquered by Persia under the Sassanian dynasty. Records indicate the presence of Nestorian Christians in Samarkand during the 6th century. Islam was introduced to the region shortly after the Arab conquest of Persia, which took place between 640 and 642. The faith quickly became the dominant religion of the area.

During the early centuries of Mahommedan rule, the region—then called Sughd—was one of the most celebrated and flourishing districts in Central Asia. It was characterized by two primary cities:
* **Samarkand**: Generally serving as the seat of government.
* **Bokhara**: Highly regarded as a center of learning and religion.

## Later Conquests and Dynasties
At the end of the 11th century, Malik Shah, the third ruler of the Persian Seljuk dynasty, crossed the Oxus and subdued the entire country watered by the Jaxartes and the Oxus rivers. In 1216, Bokhara was subdued by Mahommed Shah Khwarizm, though this conquest was later seized by Jenghiz Khan in 1220. 

While the region was devastated by the fury of Jenghiz Khan, it regained some prosperity under his son, Ogdai Khan, who is described as benevolent and humane. His descendants maintained control of the region until 1369. At that time, Timur (also known as Tamerlane) conquered the territory and established his capital at Samarkand.
