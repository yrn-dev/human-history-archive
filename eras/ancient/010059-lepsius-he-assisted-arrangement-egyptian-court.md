# Lepsius

The archive contains records of several individuals identified as Lepsius, most notably a prominent figure in the field of Egyptology and a professor emeritus who served during a period of war.

## Contributions to Egyptology
Lepsius was a leading figure in the study of ancient Egypt, overseeing significant exploration and documentation efforts. Between 1842 and 1844, he led a Prussian government exploration expedition to Egypt. During this expedition, he was joined by Giuseppe Bonomi, a sculptor and illustrator who provided drawings of monuments.

Lepsius's work involved the reproduction of ancient Egyptian art and the documentation of historical records. He reproduced a painting from the tombs of Beni Hassan dating to the XIIth Dynasty, which depicts a Semite carrying a seven or eight-stringed lyre (or a cithara in transition). Additionally, the archive references a work titled *Urkundenbuch* authored by Lepsius, specifically citing Plate XXII in discussions regarding cartography.

His influence extended to the training of other scholars. Johannes Dumichen, a German Egyptologist born near Grossglogau who studied theology and philology in Berlin and Breslau, became a pupil of Lepsius and Brugsch. Dumichen subsequently devoted himself to the study of Egyptian inscriptions and eventually became a professor of Egyptology at Strassburg in 1872.

## Military and Academic Activity
