# The Four Noble Truths and the Middle Path

The Four Noble Truths constitute the foundation of Buddhist asceticism, which is described as an ethical and philosophical approach to achieving deliverance from life and its inevitable suffering. According to the teachings of the Buddha (the Blessed One), the long history of wandering and transmigration is a result of failing to understand and grasp these four truths. Once they are grasped, the craving for existence is rooted out, the process leading to renewed existence is destroyed, and birth ceases.

## The Nature of Suffering and Its Origin
The first Noble Truth is the truth of suffering. This teaching asserts that birth, decay, disease, and death are all attended with pain. Suffering is further defined as the pain found in union with the unpleasant, separation from the pleasant, and any craving that remains unsatisfied. In summary, the conditions of individuality, referred to as the five aggregates of clinging, are painful.

The second Noble Truth identifies the origin of this suffering. It is attributed to "craving thirst," which causes the renewal of becomings. This craving is accompanied by sensual delights and seeks satisfaction in various forms: the gratification of the senses, the desire for prosperity, or the craving for a future life.

## The Cessation of Pain and the Middle Path
The third Noble Truth concerns the passing away of pain. This is achieved through the total emancipation from craving thirst, meaning the giving up, getting rid of, and harboring no longer of such passions.

The fourth Noble Truth is the way that leads to the cessation of pain, known as the Noble Eightfold Path. This path is presented as a "Middle Path" discovered by the Tathagata. It is designed to open the eyes, bestow understanding, and lead to insight, higher wisdom, peace, and Nirvana. The Middle Path explicitly rejects two extremes:
1. Devotion to attractions based on passions, which is characterized as an ignoble, unprofitable, and low pagan ideal fit for the worldly-minded.
2. The practice of asceticism, which is described as painful, ignoble, and unprofitable.

The Noble Eightfold Path consists of Right Views, Right Aspirations, Right Speech, Right Conduct, Right Mode of Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, and Right Rapture.

## Implications of the Truths
The Buddha taught that an elect disciple who possesses this way of Truth can be assured of final salvation. Such a person may claim that they are no longer liable to be reborn in a state of suffering and that rebirth as a ghost, an animal, or in any place of woe—including hell—has been destroyed.
