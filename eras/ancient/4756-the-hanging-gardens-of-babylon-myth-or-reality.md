# The Hanging Gardens of Babylon: Myth or Reality?

The Hanging Gardens of Babylon stand as one of the most enduring enigmas of the ancient world. Listed as one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, they are described as a breathtaking ascending series of tiered gardens, featuring a diverse array of flora supported by complex irrigation systems. However, unlike the Great Pyramid of Giza, the gardens leave behind a frustrating void of physical evidence.

### The Historical Debate
The central tension in the study of the gardens is the total absence of Babylonian records mentioning them. While the Neo-Babylonian king Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BCE) left extensive inscriptions detailing his architectural achievements, he never once mentioned the gardens. Conversely, the gardens are described in detail by Greek and Roman historians, such as Berossus and Diodorus Siculus, who wrote centuries after the event.

This discrepancy has led historians to two primary theories. The first is that the gardens were a poetic exaggeration or a complete myth created by Hellenistic writers to romanticize the "exotic" East. The second, more recent theory proposed by Dr. Stephanie Dalley of Oxford University, suggests a geographical error. Dalley argues that the gardens actually existed, but not in Babylon. Instead, she posits they were built by the Assyrian King Sennacherib in Nineveh (modern-day Mosul, Iraq) around 700 BCE. Sennacherib’s own records describe a "palace without parallel" and a sophisticated system of aqueducts that could have supported such a feat.

### Key Facts
*   **Engineering:** If they existed, the gardens would have required advanced hydraulic engineering (likely Archimedes' screws or chain pumps) to lift water from the Euphrates to the upper terraces.
*   **Botanical Diversity:** Ancient texts describe the gardens as containing plants from various regions, suggesting an early attempt at a botanical garden.
*   **Archaeological Silence:** Despite extensive excavations of Babylon, no structural remains consistent with the gardens have been identified.

### Did You Know?
1. **The "Hanging" Misnomer:** The word "hanging" is a mistranslation of the Greek word *kremastos*, which more accurately means "overhanging" or "tiered," referring to the terraces rather than plants suspended in mid-air.
2. **The Love Story:** Tradition holds that Nebuchadnezzar built the gardens for his wife, Amytis of Media, because she missed the green mountains of her homeland.
3. **Nineveh's Evidence:** Unlike Babylon, Nineveh contains ruins of massive aqueducts and reliefs depicting lush gardens, supporting the theory that the "Wonder" was actually Assyrian.
