# The Final Days of Gotama

## Journey to Kusinara
The final days of the life of Gotama, the teacher, are documented in accounts that follow the more legendary notices of his earlier journeyings. At eighty years of age, Gotama traveled toward the town of Kusinara, which is situated approximately 80 miles due east of Kapilavastu and about 120 miles north-north-east of Benares. 

During this journey, the teacher rested briefly in a grove at Pawa. This grove had been presented to the society by a local goldsmith named Chunda, who also provided a mid-day meal for the mendicants. After this meal, the Buddha continued his journey toward Kusinara. He was forced to rest shortly after departing and requested water to drink from Ananda. Between the two towns flows the river Kukushta, where Gotama rested again and bathed for the final time.

## Final Instructions and the River Hiranyavati
Aware that he was dying, Gotama expressed concern that Chunda might be reproached by others or by himself for the meal provided. He instructed Ananda to inform Chunda that he would receive a very great reward in a future birth because the Buddha was about to die after eating the food he gave. Gotama identified Chunda's gift and a previous gift from Sujata, given before he attained wisdom under the Bo tree, as the two gifts that would be blest above all others.

After several more halts, the party reached the river Hiranyavati, located close by Kusinara. This was the site of the teacher's final rest. Lying under Sal trees with his face toward the south, he engaged in a long and earnest conversation with Ananda regarding his burial and the rules the society was to observe following his death. During this evening conversation, Ananda wept, prompting the Buddha to comfort him with the promise of Nirvana and a reminder of the impermanence of all things, stating that all must eventually part from what they hold most dear.

## The Encounter with Subhadra
Around midnight, a brahmin philosopher of Kusinara named Subhadra arrived to ask the Buddha questions. Ananda initially refused him entry, fearing the discussion would be too taxing for the sick teacher. However, Gotama heard them and requested that Subhadra be admitted.

Subhadra questioned whether the six great teachers knew all laws or if there were some they knew only partially. The Buddha responded that it was not the time for such discussions, asserting that there is only one way to true wisdom: the path laid down in his system. He stated that those who follow this path and conquer the anger, pride, and lust of their own hearts become free from wrong belief, doubt, and ignorance, entering a calm state of universal kindliness and reaching Nirvana. Gotama noted that he spoke from experience, having striven for pure and perfect wisdom since the age of twenty-nine.

Although the society had a rule requiring a four-month probation for followers of rival systems, Gotama granted Subhadra's request for immediate admission.

## Final Words to the Disciples
In his final addresses to his disciples, the Buddha told them that although he would pass away, he would not truly leave them. He instructed them to let his laws, his explanations of the deep things of truth, and his words serve as their guide. He further urged his followers to show reverence to one another and rebuked a disciple who spoke indiscriminately about everything that occurred to him.

Towards morning, the Buddha asked if anyone had any remaining doubts regarding the society, the law, or the Buddha himself, offering to clear them up. When no one answered, Ananda expressed surprise that there were no doubts among so many. The Buddha responded by emphasizing the final perseverance of the saints.
