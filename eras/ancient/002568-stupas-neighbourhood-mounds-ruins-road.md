# Ruins and Mounds of Balkh

## Overview of the City
Balkh is a city located in Afghanistan, situated approximately 46 miles south of the Oxus and 100 miles east of Andkhui. Historically identified as Zainaspa or ancient Bactra, the city currently exists largely as a mass of ruins and many acres of debris on the right bank of the Balkh river, at an elevation of 1,200 feet above the sea. The modern settlement consists of a small bazaar, a colony of Jews, and about 500 houses of Afghan settlers.

## Fortifications and Architecture
The city's outer walls are in a state of utter disrepair, measuring between 6-1/2 and 7 meters in perimeter. On the south-eastern borders, these walls are set high on a rampart or mound, which indicates a Mongol origin. To the north-east, a moated and walled fort and citadel are built upon a barren mound, though only the remains of a few pillars currently survive.

Entering through the west (Akcha) gate, visitors pass under three arches that are likely the remnants of a former Jama Masjid. Other notable structures include the Masjid Sabz, featuring a green-tiled dome and said to be the tomb of Abul Narsi Parsar, a Khwaja. Additionally, there is a Madrasa of which only the arched entrance remains.

## Buddhist Remains
The archive notes that earlier Buddhist constructions in Balkh have proven more durable than the Mahommedan buildings. Two significant structures are highlighted:

*   **Top-i-Rustam:** A circular building 50 feet high, measuring 50 yards in diameter at its base and 30 yards at the top. Its base is constructed of sun-dried bricks (approximately 2 feet square and 4 to 5 inches thick). The interior contains four circular vaults, which are likely accessed via four passages pierced from the outside.
*   **Takht-i-Rustam:** A structure with a wedge-shaped plan and uneven sides, apparently constructed of pise mud (puddled mud mixed with straw).

It is possible that these ruins represent the Nan Vihara described by the Chinese traveller Hsuan Tsang.

## Surrounding Neighbourhood
The area surrounding the city contains the remains of many other topes, or stupas. Furthermore, on the road leading to Mazar-i-Sharif, there are mounds of ruins that likely represent the site of a city even older than the mounds upon which modern Balkh is situated.

The region is garrisoned by a few hundred kasidars, while the regular Afghan Turkestan troops are stationed at Takhtapul, near Mazar-i-Sharif. To the north-east, the gardens contain a caravanserai.
