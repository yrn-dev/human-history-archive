# Albanopolis and Urbanopolis

Albanopolis and Urbanopolis are locations cited in traditional accounts as the sites of the martyrdom of Saint Bartholomew, one of the twelve apostles.

## Saint Bartholomew and His Martyrdom
Saint Bartholomew is identified in some beliefs as Nathanael, with Bartholomew serving as a patronymic meaning "son of Tolmai." Other Syrian traditions suggest his original name was Jesus, which he reportedly dropped because it was the name of the Master. In the synoptic gospels, he is mentioned only in the lists of the apostles, appearing after Philip.

Following the ascension of the Lord, Bartholomew is said to have conducted a missionary tour of India—a term that served as a wide geographical designation at the time. According to Eusebius, he left a copy of St Matthew's gospel in India.

Traditional accounts of his death state that he was crucified with his head downwards after being flayed alive. The location of this event is subject to differing reports: one account places it at Albanopolis in Armenia, while Nicephorus places it at Urbanopolis in Cilicia.

## Iconography and Commemoration
In works of art, Saint Bartholomew is typically represented with a large knife, which was the instrument used in his martyrdom. In Michelangelo's "Last Judgment," he is depicted with his own skin hanging over his arm. The festival celebrating Saint Bartholomew is observed on the 24th of August.

## Historical Context of the Regions
The regions mentioned in the accounts of Bartholomew's death, Armenia and Cilicia, were areas of significant geopolitical transition. Armenia, in particular, was the first nation to embrace Christianity. Historically, the region underwent various partitions; for instance, around A.C. 390, it was divided into a Western Roman division and an Eastern Persian division, the latter being four times larger than the former.

Armenia's political structure often linked religious and royal authority. According to the institution of St. Gregory, the Apostle of Armenia, the archbishop was always a member of the royal family, effectively uniting the crown with the mitre.
