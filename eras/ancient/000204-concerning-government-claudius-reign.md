# The Government of Claudius

The historical records of the archive contain accounts of two different figures named Claudius who held imperial power. One account details the rise of Claudius following the death of Caius, while another describes a later emperor named Claudius who campaigned against the Goths.

## The Succession of Claudius After Caius

Following the reign of Caius, who ruled for three years and eight months before being slain by treachery, the Roman government entered a period of instability. The senate, acting on the reference of consuls Pomponius Secundus and Sentis Saturninus, sought to oppose Claudius by force. This opposition was rooted in the barbarous treatment the senate had experienced under Caius. The senators resolved to either restore the nation to an aristocracy, as it had been governed in the past, or to elect an emperor by vote who was worthy of the position.

During this transition, Claudius was seized from his house and brought to the camp by the armies at Rome. At the time, Claudius was perceived as a man who had never meddled in public affairs and had survived significant dangers during the reign of Caius. While some guards initially feared he was being taken to execution, many soldiers grew glad to see him. Their support was driven by their affection for his brother, Germanicus, who possessed a vast reputation. The soldiers also distrusted the covetous nature of the senate's leading men and feared the dangers of a government led by a single person they had not helped advance.

The senate eventually sent an embassage to Claudius, consisting of tribunes Veranius and Brocchus. They begged him not to throw the city into war and, recognizing the superior number of soldiers guarding him compared to the forces of the consuls, urged him to accept the government as a gift from the senate. Upon taking power, Claudius commanded that the murderers of Caius be slain.

## Administration and Relations with Judea

During his government, Claudius maintained a relationship with King Agrippa. Claudius restored the kingdoms of Agrippa's grandfathers to him and augmented his dominions through a published edict. Following these grants, Agrippa returned to Judea, where he began to encompass Jerusalem with a wall and provided benefits to the inhabitants of Berytus.

Claudius also intervened in regional disputes, putting an end to differences that had arisen between the Jews and the Samaritans. Following the death of Agrippa, Claudius determined that Agrippa Junior was too unskilled and youthful to govern. Consequently, Claudius sent Cuspius Fadus to serve as the procurator of the entire kingdom and of Judea.

## The Later Emperor Claudius and the Goths

Separate records describe a different Claudius of modest birth from a province bordering the Danube. This Claudius spent his youth in arms and gained the confidence of Decius. He was eventually declared general and chief of the Illyrian frontier, commanding troops in Dalmatia, Pannonia, Dacia, Mæsia, and Thrace. He also held appointments as the præfect of Egypt and the establishment of the proconsul of Africa.

This Claudius achieved victories over the Goths, for which the senate honored him with a statue. However, his success and his just contempt for the dissolute sovereign Gallienus led to tension. Gallienus, fearing the fury of the Dacian troops and the imprudence of Claudius, attempted to appease him with presents and secret negotiations.

The reign of this Claudius lasted two years. He was eventually killed by a pestilence that had also decimated the barbarians. Before his death at Sirmium, he recommended his general, Aurelian, as the most deserving successor to the throne. He is remembered in some accounts for his justice, temperance, valor, and affability.

## Succession and Later Governance

Following the death of the first Claudius mentioned, Nero succeeded him in the government. The archive notes that during the subsequent period, while Felix and Festus served as procurators of Judea, the region saw the rise of impostors, murderers, and robbers. Later, under the procuratorship of Albinus, James was slain. The period of Roman administration in the region eventually led to the procuratorship of Florus, whose actions necessitated the Jews to take up arms against the Romans.
