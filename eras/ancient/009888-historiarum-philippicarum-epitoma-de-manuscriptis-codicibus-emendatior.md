# Historiarum Philippicarum epitoma de manuscriptis codicibus emendatior

The *Historiarum Philippicarum epitoma de manuscriptis codicibus emendatior et prologis auctior* is a scholarly work published in Paris in 1581. It consists of an abridgment of Justin's own abridgment of the history of Trogus Pompeius.

## Authorship and Publication
The work was written by Jacques Bongars (1554–1612), a French diplomatist and scholar. Bongars was born in Orleans and raised in the reformed faith. His academic background included early education at Jena and Marburg, followed by further studies at Bourges and Orleans. Before his diplomatic career, he spent time in Rome and visited eastern Europe.

## Context of the Author's Career
Jacques Bongars served as a diplomat for Henry of Navarre (later Henry IV of France). His service began under Segur Pardaillan, and by 1587, Bongars was dispatched on missions to various princes in northern Europe. He also visited England to seek assistance from Queen Elizabeth for Henry of Navarre. 

In 1593, Bongars became the representative of the French king at the courts of the imperial princes. In this capacity, he worked vigorously to limit the power of the house of Habsburg, dedicating significant personal funds and health to the cause. He continued these labors until the murder of the king in 1610. Following this event, Bongars returned to France and died in Paris on July 29, 1612.

## Other Works by Jacques Bongars
Beyond the abridgment of the history of Trogus Pompeius, Bongars was involved in several other editorial and historical projects:

*   **Gesta Dei per Francos**: Published in Hanover in 1611, this was a collection of works by several contemporary French writers describing the crusades.
*   **Rerum Hungaricarum scriptores varii**: A collection published in Frankfort in 1600.
*   **Epistolae**: His letters were published in Leiden in 1647, with a French translation appearing in Paris between 1668 and 1670.

## Archival Preservation
The professional and personal papers of Jacques Bongars are distributed across several locations. A significant portion of his papers was presented to the library at Bern in 1632, and a comprehensive list of these documents was compiled in 1634. Additionally, copies of instructions and other papers are held in the British Museum and various libraries in Paris.
