# The Noble Eightfold Path

The Noble Eightfold Path is a Middle Path discovered by the Tathagata, designed to open the eyes, bestow understanding, and lead the practitioner toward peace, insight, higher wisdom, and Nirvana. This path serves as the specific way that leads to the passing away of pain.

## The Four Noble Truths
The Eightfold Path is situated within a broader framework of Four Noble Truths regarding the nature of existence. The first truth is the Noble Truth as to suffering, which posits that birth, decay, disease, and death are all attended with pain. Pain is also found in union with the unpleasant, separation from the pleasant, and any unsatisfied craving. In summary, the five aggregates of clinging—the conditions of individuality—are painful.

The second truth identifies the origin of suffering as "craving thirst." This thirst causes the renewal of becomings and is characterized by a search for satisfaction in sensual delights, the craving for prosperity, or the craving for a future life. The third truth is the Noble Truth as to the passing away of pain, which is achieved through the emancipation from and the giving up of this craving thirst so that no passion remains. The fourth truth is the way that leads to this passing away of pain: the Noble Eightfold Path.

## The Elements of the Path
The path consists of eight elements. The first, Right Views, emphasizes the stern examination of ideas and an insistence upon truth. This involves a rejection of tawdry superstitions and the destructive analysis of the idea of an enduring individual soul, as well as the condemnation of beliefs in the transmigration of souls.

Following Right Views are Right Aspirations. This element does not seek the total destruction of desire, but rather the change of desire. It involves suppressing evil desires, idle excitements, low ideals, and useless cravings by cultivating lofty aspirations. Examples of Right Aspirations include the wish not to injure any living thing, the desire for emancipation from sensuality, the attainment of love for others, and the promotion of right dispositions within one's own heart. Such aspirations may manifest as a love for the service of others, a desire to secure justice, or a devotion to art and science, provided these aims are free from the craving for fame or jealousy.

The path further includes Right Speech, Right Conduct, and Right Mode of Livelihood. These are followed by Right Effort, which requires the disciple to maintain a keenly critical eye on their activities, as good intentions are not sufficient without diligent application. Right Mindfulness is the seventh element, serving as a constant guard against lapsing into personal feeling or glory regarding one's actions. Finally, Right Rapture is aimed against witless gloryings and pointless ecstasies.

## Application and Historical Implementation
The Eightfold Path is presented as a Middle Path that avoids two extremes: devotion to attractions based on passions (described as a low, pagan, and unprofitable ideal) and the practice of asceticism (described as painful and ignoble).

Historical records indicate that this way of living is compatible with beneficent and useful activities. One notable example is a monarch who, after joining the Buddhist community as a layman and later becoming a full member of the Order, devoted himself to Nirvana via the Eightfold Path. His career was distinguished by Right Aspiration, Right Effort, and Right Livelihood. His practical applications of these principles included:
* The organization of the planting of trees for shade and the digging of wells in India.
* The foundation of public gardens, hospitals, and gardens for medicinal herbs.
* The appointment of officers to supervise charitable works.
* The creation of a ministry to care for subject races and aborigines.
* The provision of education for women.
* The attempt to educate the general population into a common way of life and a common view of its ends.

This monarch further supported the teaching of Gautama by making vast benefactions to teaching orders, stimulating the study of their literature, and erecting long inscriptions of the teachings across the land. He also dispatched missionaries to spread these teachings to the Ptolemies, the Seleucids, Ceylon, and Kashmir.
