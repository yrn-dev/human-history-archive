# Cunningham

The name Cunningham appears in the archive's records across several distinct contexts, including the fields of archaeology and history, Scottish geography and genealogy, and literary biography.

## Archaeological and Historical Contributions
General A. Cunningham was an eminent archaeologist who provided analysis on the geography of ancient India. Regarding the route taken by Prince Nogodar during an incursion into India, Cunningham addressed the identity of "Ariora." He proposed that "Ariora" is a representation of the Sanskrit *Harhaura*, referring to the Western Panjáb. According to Cunningham, Harhaura served as the North-Western Division of the *Nava-Khanda* (the Nine Divisions of Ancient India), situated between Madra in the north (Eastern Panjáb) and Sindhu-Sauvira in the west (Sind). He further suggested that the name Harhaura is preserved in the Haro River and noted that the Sind-Sagor Doab was part of the kingdom of Kashmir.

Cunningham also contributed to the study of the Greeks in India, specifically through his work "The Greeks of Bactriana, Ariana and India" published in the *Numismatic Chronicle*. His research is cited alongside other scholars in the study of Graeco-Bactrian and Indo-Scythian kingdoms.

## Scottish Geography and Genealogy
In Scotland, Cunningham is associated with the town of Kilmaurs in Ayrshire. The town is located on the Carmel, approximately 21½ miles south by west of Glasgow. The family name Cunningham was likely adopted from the manor that the earls of Glencairn acquired during the 12th century. These earls were the leading personages of the district for several centuries, and some held the title of Lord Kilmaurs. 

The town of Kilmaurs was established as a burgh of barony in 1527 by the earl of that period. The estate was later sold in 1786 by the thirteenth earl—who was the patron of the poet Robert Burns—to the marchioness of Titchfield.

## Literary Associations
The name Cunningham appears in the history of the publication of the works of Robert Burns. Alexander Cunningham, along with John Syme, requested that Dr. Currie edit the *Works of Robert Burns; with an Account of his Life, and a Criticism on his Writings*, which was published in 1800 for the benefit of the poet's family. Additionally, Allan Cunningham authored a biography of the poet in the eight-volume set *The Works of Robert Burns, With his Life by Allan Cunningham*, published in London in 1834, which provided significant biographical material and additions to the poet's works.
