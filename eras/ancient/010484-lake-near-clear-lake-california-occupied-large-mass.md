# Borax Lake

## Overview and Composition
Borax Lake, located in Lake County, California, near Clear Lake, is characterized by a bed occupied by a large mass of crystallized borax. This natural deposit is of sufficient purity that it is fit for use by the assayer without requiring any preliminary purification. 

The substance found here, borax (sodium pyroborate or sodium biborate, $\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7$), exists in commerce in two primary forms: "common" or prismatic borax ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$) and "jewellers'" or octahedral borax ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 5\text{H}_2\text{O}$).

## Mineralogical Characteristics
In the mud of the lake and the surrounding marshy soil, fine isolated crystals of borax are frequently discovered. At Borax Lake in Lake County, as well as at Borax Lake in San Bernardino County, California, these crystals have been found in large numbers, with some reaching up to 7 inches in length and weighing one pound each.

## Geological Origins
The boron found in solution within salt lakes is likely supplied by solfataras and hot springs of volcanic origin. This process is similar to the action of the lagoons in Tuscany, which are charged with boric acid. The resulting deposits, formed through evaporation from salines, marshes, or lakes, consist of mixtures of various alkaline salts—including chloride, sulphate, and sodium carbonate—as well as borates and gypsum.

## Commercial Context
While Borax Lake provides high-quality crystallized borax, the primary supply of the substance is derived from the boric acid of Tuscany. This is processed by fusing the acid with half its weight of sodium carbonate in a reverberatory furnace, after which the mass is extracted with warm water. Alternatively, sodium carbonate is dissolved in steam-heated, lead-lined pans, and boric acid is added gradually until the solution concentrates and the borax crystallizes. Borax can also be prepared by fusing calcium borate with soda ash, followed by water extraction and concentration.

In more recent times, the chief commercial sources of boron compounds have shifted to extensive borate deposits (primarily calcium borates) located in the Atacama desert of South America and the Mohave desert on the borders of Nevada and California.
