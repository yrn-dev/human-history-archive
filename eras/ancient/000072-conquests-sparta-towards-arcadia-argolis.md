# CONQUESTS OF SPARTA TOWARDS ARCADIA AND ARGOLIS

The territorial expansion of Sparta toward Arcadia and Argolis established the state as the commanding power in Hellas by approximately 560–540 B.C. During this period, Sparta reached the maximum extent of its territorial possessions, achieving full acquisition of the southern Peloponnese from sea to sea.

## Expansion into Arcadia
Before the establishment of Megalopolis, the most powerful towns in Arcadia were Mantineia and Tegea. Sparta sought to expand its influence upon the southern boundary of Arcadia, though its initial attempts against Tegea were unsuccessful. Following the direction of an oracle, the Spartans were instructed to bring the bones of the hero Orestês to Sparta. Subsequent operations against Tegea became more successful, although Tegea managed to maintain its independence.

In later periods, Sparta's control over the territory north of the city and between Sparta and Arcadia was challenged. The Skiritæ, hardy mountaineers of Arcadian race who had previously been dependent on Sparta and served as a valuable military contingent, became independent and hostile to the Spartan state. Similarly, the location of Sellasia became independent, though it was retaken by the Lacedæmonians four or five years later.

## Conquest of Argolis and Thyreatis
Spartan progress toward Argolis involved the conquest of Thyreatis. The possession of this region was decided through a battle between Sparta and Argos involving three hundred select champions. This engagement featured the valor of Othryades and resulted in Thyreatis coming into the possession of Sparta. Despite subsequent efforts by the Argeians to recover the territory, Sparta maintained control. This specific conflict contributed to a change in general Grecian opinion regarding the practice of using select champions to resolve disputes.

In Argolis, the Kynurians occupied the frontier district of Laconia and Argolis where Thyrea is situated, as well as the north-western portion of Argolis under the Lyrkeium ridge. Herodotus describes the Kynurians as being of Ionic race, but "Dorized" due to long subjection to Argos, which governed them as Periœki.

## Military Institutions and Superiority
The recognized superiority of Sparta during its early expansion was attributed to its rigorous military institutions and personal training, which existed at a time when other states lacked such systems. Spartan military organization featured minute subdivisions, such as the Enômoties, which were distinct from civil divisions and subjected to careful drilling. This differed from other Grecian cities, where no such distinction between military and civil divisions existed.

## Regional Context and Rivalries
While Sparta expanded, Argos struggled to recover the headship of Greece. Argos had previously conquered Kleônæ, Tiryns, and Mykenæ, and was associated with the Nemean games. In the broader region, Achaia consisted of twelve or perhaps more autonomous towns, though it remains little known.

Traditional accounts of the Dorian invasion provide varying perspectives on these conquests. One tradition, followed by Pausanias and Sosibius, views the conflicts in Arcadia as a gradual southward advance, concluding with King Alcamenes' conquest of Helos (c. 770 B.C.) and King Teleclus' conquest of Amyclae (c. 800 B.C.). Another tradition regarding Argolis suggests the Argive Dorians arrived by sea from the Maliac Gulf, accompanied by survivors of the Dryopes. In Argos and other Argolian cities, the descendants of Achaean chiefs were integrated into political partnerships, though traditions of race-feuds persisted into historic times.
