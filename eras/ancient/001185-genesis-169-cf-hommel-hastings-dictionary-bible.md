# The Tower of Babel and Related Traditions

## Origins and Babylonian Influence
The story of the tower of Babel is characterized as being polytheistic and anthropomorphic in its appearance in Genesis. While the specific origin of the story has not been located in Babylonia, it is believed that the tower was inspired by the temple towers of Babylon. Specifically, W. A. Bennet suggests that the E-Saggila, the great temple of Merodach (also known as Marduk), served as the suggestion for the tower.

The narrative's themes of the dispersion of mankind and the variety of languages were viewed as a curse. Professor Cheyne suggests that an ancient North Semitic myth likely existed to explain these phenomena, and the event was subsequently localized in Babylon.

## Diverse Cultural Accounts
Various historical and mythological sources provide differing accounts of the tower's fate and the scattering of humanity:

*   **Classical and Arabic Sources:** Cornelius Alexander and Abydenus record that the tower was overthrown by winds. Similarly, the *Lisan el-'Arab* and Yaqut state that winds swept mankind together into a plain later called "Babil," and that these same winds were responsible for scattering them once again.
*   **Central American Tradition:** A similar tradition exists in Central America involving Xelhua, one of seven giants who survived the deluge. Xelhua constructed the great pyramid of Cholula to storm heaven, but the gods destroyed the structure with fire and confounded the languages of those who built it.
*   **Other Global Legends:** Traces of similar stories have been identified among the Africans of Lake Ngami (according to Dr. Livingstone) and the Mongolian Tharus in northern India. Additionally, the Australian legend regarding the origin of the diversity of speech and the Estonian myth known as "the Cooking of Languages" are considered comparable.
