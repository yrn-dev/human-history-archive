## Political Events and Governance
In 1806, Napoleon Bonaparte orchestrated a significant shift in the governance of southern Italy. Early in the year, Joseph Bonaparte proceeded to Naples with a French force to expel the Bourbon dynasty. While the mainland was quickly conquered, certain areas—specifically Reggio, Gaeta, and the rock of Scylla—resisted for several months. On March 30, 1806, Napoleon issued a decree proclaiming Joseph as the king of Naples, though Joseph maintained his claims to the French throne.

During his tenure in Naples, Joseph sought to improve the state of the people, stating in April 1806 that justice required him to make the population as happy as the "scourge of war" would allow. His administration focused on reorganizing educational, financial, and judicial systems, reforming monastic orders, initiating public works, and abolishing feudal relics. However, his reign faced challenges, including Bourbon plots, a bankrupt treasury, and attacks by British forces from Sicily.

In other European developments, Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese, was appointed duke of Guastalla in 1806. He subsequently served as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces.

## Biographies and Deaths
The archive records the death of Mathurin Jacques Brisson on June 23, 1806, at Croissy near Paris. Brisson was a French natural philosopher and zoologist who had previously served as an assistant to R.A.F. Réaumur. His career transitioned from natural history—marked by the publication of *Ornithologie* (1760) and *Le Règne animal* (1756)—to natural philosophy, where he served as a professor in Paris and Navarre and authored *Poids spécifiques des corps* (1787).
