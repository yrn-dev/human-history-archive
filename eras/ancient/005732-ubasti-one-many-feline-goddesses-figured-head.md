# Ubasti

Ubasti was an Egyptian goddess and one of several feline deities. Her name is derived from the city of Bast, meaning "she of [the city] Bast." In turn, the city's later name, P-ubasti ("place of Ubasti"), was derived from the goddess. The Greeks, confusing the names of the city and the deity, referred to both as Bubastis (or Bubastos in the case of the city).

## Iconography and Representation
The hieratic and most general form of Ubasti featured the head of a lioness. However, a popular representation, particularly in bronze works, depicted her as a woman with the head of a cat. In these figures, she is often shown holding a basket on her left arm and a lion aegis in her right hand; the aegis is described as a broad semicircular pectoral topped with the head of a lioness.

While her general form was lioness-headed, the domestic cat became especially associated with Ubasti during the great development of reverence for sacred animals following the New Kingdom. Because other feline congeners were intractable and scarce, the domestic cat served as the animal for Ubasti as well as all other feline goddesses.

## Worship and Cult Centers
The city of Bubastis served as the capital of the 19th nome of Lower Egypt. Today, this site is represented by Tell Basta near Zagazig, which contains a large temple described by Herodotus and strewn with granite blocks. Monuments found at this site range from the time of Khufu through the thirtieth dynasty, making it one of the most important sites in the Delta, second only to Tanis.

Ubasti's worship received a significant stimulus from the twenty-second (Bubastite) dynasty and grew as Lower Egypt increased in importance in later periods. Her name appears on monuments dating back to the third dynasty. Evidence from the tablet of Canopus indicates the existence of two festivals: a great festival and a lesser one. The lesser festival may have been held at Memphis in the quarter known as Ankhto, which housed a temple to the goddess. Herodotus described the festival of Bubastis as a riotous event attended by thousands of people from across Egypt.

The goddess's cult included a cat cemetery located on the west side of the town. This cemetery contained numerous large brick chambers filled with decayed and burnt mummies, some of which were placed inside cat-shaped cases made of bronze or wood.

## Character and Relations
In contrast to other feline goddesses such as Sokhmi, Ubasti's character is described as essentially playful and mild. She was almost indistinguishable in form from Tafne, the sister of Shöou (Apollo). The Greeks equated Ubasti with their goddess Artemis, though this was a result of confusing her with the leonine Tafne.

Ubasti had a son named Iphthimis (Nfr-tm), also pronounced Eftem. His name may mean "All-good," a detail that suggests why he was identified with Prometheus.
