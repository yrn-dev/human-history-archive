# Basiliken; Huebsch, Die altchristlichen Kirchen; Messmer, Ueber den

## Overview of Architectural Authorities
The study of basilicas and early Christian architecture is supported by a wide array of scholarly authorities. Key texts in this field include *Die altchristlichen Kirchen* by Huebsch and *Ueber den Ursprung, &c., der Basilica* by Messmer. These works contribute to a broader body of research that examines both ancient and Christian basilicas, as seen in the works of Zestermann (*Die antiken u. die christlichen Basiliken*) and Gutensohn and Knapp (*Basiliken des christlichen Roms*).

## Comparative Architectural Studies
The archive's sources list several specialized studies that analyze the development of religious and civic structures across different regions and eras. These include:
* **Ravenna:** Von Quast's *Altchristliche Bauwerke von Ravenna*.
* **Byzantine Architecture:** Works by Texier and Pullan (*Byzantine Architecture*) and Couchaud (*Choix d'eglises byzantines*).
* **The Holy Land and Syria:** Vogue's *Eglises de la Terre Sainte* and *Syrie Centrale, Architecture, &c.*
* **Western Church Architecture:** Dehio and von Bezold's *Die kirchliche Baukunst des Abendlandes*.

## Systematic and Archaeological Approaches
Beyond regional studies, the archive identifies texts that attempt to categorize architecture systematically or through archaeological lenses. Holtzinger's *Die altchristliche Architectur in systematischer Darstellung* provides a systematic presentation of early Christian architecture. Archaeological perspectives are further provided by Leclercq in the *Manuel d'archeologie chretienne* (1907) and Kraus in *Geschichte der christlichen Kunst*.

## Classical Foundations
The study of basilicas also draws upon classical Roman authorities and documentation of ancient sites. These include Vitruvius' *De Architectura* (specifically v. 1 and vi. 3, 9), Huelsen's *The Roman Forum* (1906), and Mau's *Pompeii: its Life and Art*. Other relevant Roman architectural sources include Canina's *Edifizii di Roma Antica*, Ciampini's *Vetera Monimenta*, and the descriptions of the city of Rome provided by Bunsen and Plattner.
