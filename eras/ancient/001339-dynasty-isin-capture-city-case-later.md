# Dynasty of Isin

The Dynasty of Isin was a ruling power in Babylonia that emerged following the collapse of Sargon's empire, a period during which the seat of supreme power shifted southwards to the cities of Ur and Isin.

## Chronology and Duration
According to a fragment of an early dynastic chronicle from Nippur, the Dynasty of Isin succeeded the Dynasty of Ur. While the Dynasty of Ur consisted of five kings and lasted for 117 years, the Dynasty of Isin consisted of sixteen kings and lasted for 225 1/2 years. 

Based on these records, the rise of the Dynasty of Ur is dated to no earlier than 2300 B.C. Additionally, some historical interpretations suggest that the beginning of the historical period of Berossus may be synchronized with an earlier dynasty that had its capital in one of the great cities of Babylonia, such as the Dynasty of Isin.

## The Fall of the Dynasty
The end of the Dynasty of Isin is associated with the capture of the city of Isin by R[=i]m-Sin. This event occurred in the seventeenth year of Sin-muballit, who was the father of Khammurabi. This capture served as an epoch for dating tablets in certain regions of Babylonia, and it is probable that this event marks the fall of the dynasty.

If this identification is correct, the later rulers of the Dynasty of Isin would have been contemporaneous with the earlier rulers of Dynasty I. of the Kings' List.

## Later Dynasty of Isin
Archive records also list a separate "Dynasty of Isin" consisting of 11 kings who ruled for 132 1/2 years. This period is associated with a date of 1203 B.C. and includes rulers such as Nebuchadrezzar I, Bel-nadin-pal, and Merodach-nadin-akhi (who reigned for 22 years). Other figures mentioned in this later period include Merodach-sapik-zer-mati (12 years), Nabu-nadin (8 years), and an usurper named Hadad-baladan.

## Regional Context
During the period when power shifted to Isin and Ur, it is generally assumed that two dynasties reigned at Ur and claimed suzerainty over other Babylonian states, although clear proof of more than one dynasty is lacking. Gungunu is credited with transferring the capital of Babylonia from Isin to Ur. 

Following Gungunu, the ruler Ur-Gur became a significant builder, restoring temples for the Sun-god at Larsa, the Moon-god at Ur, Bel at Nippur, and Ishtar at Erech. Ur-Gur was succeeded by his son Dungi, who reigned for more than 51 years. Under Dungi's rule, Gudea served as the *patesi* (high-priest) of Lagash. Gudea was also a prolific builder who sourced materials from across western Asia, including copper from northern Arabia, cedar from the Amanus mountains, and gold from the desert between Egypt and Palestine.
