# Hamrin and Sinjar

## Geography and Topography
The regions of Hamrin and Sinjar are located within the vast flat of the modern El-Jezireh, a territory approximately 250 miles in length. This area is situated in the district enclosed between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in western Asia. The El-Jezireh plain is interrupted by a single limestone range that rises abruptly from the plain. This range branches off from the Zagros mountains and is known under the names of Sinjar, Hamrin, and Saraz[=u]r.

## Historical Population and Habitation
The level tract of land in this region was once densely populated, as evidenced by the numerous remains of old habitations found there. Despite this history of thick settlement, the area has largely become a wilderness.

## Regional Context and Borders
The broader geography of the area is characterized by a northern division that is more or less mountainous and a southern division that is flat and marshy. To the north of the plateau, there is a well-watered, undulating belt of country featuring low limestone hills. These hills are sometimes arid and other times covered with dwarf-oak. Beyond these hills lie the massive ridges of the Zagros and Niphates ranges, which serve as the source of the Euphrates and Tigris rivers and separate Assyria from Kurdistan and Armenia.

## Modern Demographics and Land Use
In more recent contexts, the area around Jebel Sinjar serves as a southern boundary for the distribution of the Kurd population, who are scattered from Kars in the north to Jebel Sinjar in the south. The nomad Kurds, known as Kocher, utilize the great plain north of Jebel Sinjar during the winter months. To occupy this land, the nomads purchase rights of pasturage from the Shammar Arabs.
