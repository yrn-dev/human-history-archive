# Bacchylides

## Life and Origins
Bacchylides was a Greek lyric poet born in Iulis, located on the island of Ceos. His parentage included a father, whose name was likely Meidon, and a mother who was the sister of Simonides, another native of Iulis. This familial connection made Simonides the uncle of Bacchylides.

## Chronology and Career
Based on the writings of Eusebius, Bacchylides is said to have "flourished" ([Greek: ekmazen]) in Ol. 78. 2, which corresponds to 467 B.C. Because the term *ekmazen* typically refers to a person's physical prime—generally estimated to be around the fortieth year—it is suggested that Bacchylides was born circa 507 B.C.

His age relative to other poets of the era indicates he was approximately fifteen years younger than Pindar and some forty-nine years younger than his uncle, Simonides.

## Dating of Works
The dating of Bacchylides' Odes is established through specific evidence:
*   **Earliest Work:** Ode xii. can be approximately dated to either 479 or 481 B.C.
*   **Latest Work:** Ode vi. has a fixed date of 452 B.C. (Ol. 82. 1), as determined by a recently discovered fragment of the Olympic register.

## Later Life and Reputation
Further records from Eusebius state that Bacchylides "was of repute" ([Greek: egnorizeto]) in Ol. 87. 2 (431 B.C.). Georgius Syncellus uses the same terminology to place him in Ol. 88 (428-425 B.C.). This phrasing suggests that during this period, the poet had reached the full extent of his fame and years. Consequently, it is considered probable that Bacchylides lived to see the beginning of the Peloponnesian war.
