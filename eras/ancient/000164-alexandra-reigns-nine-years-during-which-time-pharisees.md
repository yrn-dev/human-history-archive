# The Reign of Alexandra

Alexandra, the widow of Alexander Jannaeus, reigned for nine years (78–69 B.C.). Her accession marked a complete reversal of the policies pursued by her husband, as she sought to govern in accordance with the ideals of the Pharisees and the ancient customs of her country.

## Accession and Relationship with the Pharisees
Upon taking power, Alexandra worked to gain the goodwill of the people, who viewed her as pious and displeased with the cruelties and violations of law committed by her husband. To pacify the anger of the Pharisees toward Alexander, she followed her husband's suggestions, speaking with them and placing all affairs—including the kingdom's administration and matters regarding the dead body—into their power.

The Pharisees, a sect of the Israelites who appeared more religious than others and were seen to interpret the laws more accurately, joined themselves to Alexandra to assist in her government. Because of her own great piety toward God, Alexandra hearkened to them to an extraordinary degree. Over time, the Pharisees artfully insinuated themselves into her favor and became the real administrators of public affairs. They exercised royal authority, possessing the power to bind, loose, banish, or reduce whom they pleased, while the difficulties and expenses of the crown remained with Alexandra.

## Domestic Administration and Military Growth
Alexandra was described as a sagacious woman in the management of great affairs. While the Pharisees governed her, she focused on the strength of the state, specifically the gathering of soldiers. She increased the army by one half and procured a large body of foreign troops. This expansion rendered her nation not only powerful internally but also terrible to foreign potentates.

Within her family, Alexandra managed her two sons, Hyrcanus and Aristobulus, based on their differing temperaments. She appointed the elder son, Hyrcanus, as high priest due to his age and his inactive, mild, and moderate disposition, which ensured he would not disturb the public. Conversely, she kept the younger son, Aristobulus, as a private person because of his warmth of temper and bold, active nature.

## Conflict and Succession
Toward the end of her reign, Alexandra fell sick. Aristobulus seized this opportunity to take possession of all the fortresses with the help of his many friends and domestics. Using the money found in these fortresses, he hired mercenary soldiers and declared himself king. 

When Hyrcanus complained to his mother, Alexandra showed compassion for his case and placed the wife and sons of Aristobulus under restraint in the fortress of Antonia (formerly called the Citadel), which joined the north part of the temple. However, Alexandra died before she could punish Aristobulus for disinheriting his brother.

## Legacy and Evaluation
Alexandra died at the age of seventy-three after nine years of rule. While she preserved the nation in peace and was loved by the multitude, some accounts suggest her governance was flawed. It is noted that she preferred the power of an imperious dominion above all else, sometimes disregarding what was right or good in favor of the present. Her compliance with those who bore ill-will toward her own family and her reliance on the Pharisees—leaving the administration without the support of great men—resulted in a palace filled with calamities and disturbance after her death. Ultimately, her management of her house contributed to the eventual loss of authority for her family.
