# Assyria

The study of Assyria, particularly regarding its religious practices and beliefs, is documented through a variety of specialized monographs and the analysis of cuneiform texts. Much of the available scholarly material focuses on the intersection of Assyrian and Babylonian religious traditions.

## Religious Scholarship and General Works
Several comprehensive works have been produced to analyze the religion of Assyria and Babylonia. Morris Jastrow, jun. authored *Religion Babyloniens und Assyriens* (Giessen, 1904), which served as an enlarged and rewritten version of his earlier 1898 work, *Religion of Babylonia and Assyria*. Other significant general surveys include L. W. King's *Babylonian Religion and Mythology* (London, 1899) and T. G. Pinches' *Religion of Babylonia and Assyria* (London, 1906).

A. H. Sayce contributed to the field with *The Religion of the Ancient Babylonians* (London, 1887), which was later superseded by his 1902 work, *Religions of Ancient Egypt and Babylonia*. Additionally, Friedrich Jeremias provided analysis in *Die Babylonier und Assyrer*, published in the 1905 third edition of de la Saussaye's *Lehrbuch der Religionsgeschichte*.

## Primary Texts and Cuneiform Records
A significant portion of the evidence for Assyrian religion is derived from cuneiform tablets. The British Museum has published a new series of cuneiform texts from Babylonian tablets starting in 1901, with particular relevance found in volume iv of the earlier *Selections from the Miscellaneous Inscriptions of Western Asia* (edited by H. C. Rawlinson, 2nd ed., 1891), as well as parts v, xii, xv, xvii, xviii, xx, and xxi of the newer series.

Specific textual analyses include:
*   **Creation and Mythology:** L. W. King's *The Seven Tablets of Creation* (London, 1902).
*   **Hymns:** G. A. Reisner's *Sumerisch-babylonische Hymnen* (Berlin, 1896).
*   **Prayer:** J. A. Knudtzon's *Assyrische Gebete an den Sonnengott* (Leipzig, 1893).
*   **General Religious Texts:** J. A. Craig's *Assyrian and Babylonian Religious Texts* (Leipzig, 1895-1897) and Martin's *Textes religieux assyriens et babyloniens* (Paris, 1900 and 1903).

## Divination, Astrology, and Magic
The archive contains numerous references to the Assyrian interest in the occult, divination, and the supernatural.

**Astrology and Omens:**
The reports of astrologers and magicians from Babylon and Nineveh were compiled by R. C. Thompson in *The Reports of the Magicians and Astrologers of Nineveh and Babylon* (London, 1900). A. Boissier produced *Documents assyriens relatifs aux presages* (Paris, 1894-1897) and *Choix de textes relatifs a la divination assyro-babylonienne* (Geneva, 1905-1906). Further astronomical and astrological studies include Virolleaud's *L'Astrologie chaldeenne* (Paris, 1906-) and J. A. Craig's *Astrological-Astronomical Texts* (Leipzig, 1892).

**Magic and Spirits:**
The study of Assyrian magic is represented by Ch. Fossey's *La Magie assyrienne* (Paris, 1902) and L. W. King's *Babylonian Magic and Sorcery* (London, 1896). Research into the supernatural also includes R. C. Thompson's *Devils and Evil Spirits of Babylonia* (London, 1903-1904) and K. L. Tallqvist's work on the Assyrian incantation series, *Die assyrische Beschwoerungsserie Maql[=u]* (Leipzig, 1895).
