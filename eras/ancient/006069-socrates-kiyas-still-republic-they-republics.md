# Socrates

## Historical Context and the Athenian Republic
In the history of Athens, the theatre served as a significant medium for engaging with the affairs of the republic. The Athenian people derived great pleasure from dramatic performances—both comic and tragic—that addressed public transactions. These plays allowed the citizenry to explore their interests, soothe passions, and receive instruction on their duties during complex situations. Consequently, poets often gained influence and credit in public counsels through their work.

Within this cultural environment, the philosopher Socrates became a subject of public and dramatic attention. According to some authors, the playwright Euripides adapted his tragedy *Palamedes* to reflect the sentence passed against Socrates. Through this work, Euripides used an example from antiquity to highlight the innocence of a philosopher who had been oppressed by power, faction, and malignity.

## Comparative Political Structures
The state of Greece during the time of Socrates provides a historical analogy for understanding the social conditions of the eastern valley of the Ganges during the lifetime of the Buddha. In that region, people were divided into clans, many of which were governed as more or less aristocratic republics. 

An example of such a structure was the Sakiya republic, which existed in the 6th and 7th centuries B.C. The Sakiyas were surrounded by other republics to their east and south, though they acknowledged the kingdom of Kosala (modern Oudh) on their western boundary as a suzerain power. In these clan-based republics, titles such as *raja* did not denote a king in the monarchical sense, but rather a position similar to an archon or consul. Real kings were instead referred to as *Maha-raja*.

## The Evolution of Republics
The concept of the republic has appeared in various forms across different eras and regions:

*   **Rome:** The Roman republic transitioned from a population of citizen farmers to a "Republic of Adventurous Rich Men" following the First Punic War and the annexation of Sicily. This shift occurred as military service led to farm debts and the rise of large-scale slave agriculture. Later, the senate of Rome, in a series of repeated ceremonies, refused the resignation of Augustus, conjuring him not to desert the republic he had saved, though he eventually became a perpetual monarch.
*   **France:** The French Revolution released ideals of Republicanism that spread across Europe. France established several "constellations of freedom," including the Batavian Republic (Holland and Belgium), the Ligurian Republic (Genoa and its Riviera), the Cisalpine Republic (north Italy), and the Helvetian Republic (Switzerland), as well as republics in Rome and Naples. While presented as liberators, the French government and private individuals simultaneously exploited the resources of these lands.
*   **South Africa:** In the inland parts of South Africa, independent republics were established by Boers (Dutch settlers) in the Transvaal and the Orange River district. After a costly war beginning in 1899, these republics surrendered, but by 1907, they became part of a self-governing republic under the British Crown, alongside Natal and Cape Colony.

## Development of Political Units
The growth of social and political units has progressed over ten thousand years, moving from small neolithic family tribes to vast united realms. This evolution has seen sovereignty shift from autocratic kings and gods to a more diffused community freedom. Until the Roman republic expanded across all of Italy, free communities were generally limited to the size of city-states, as larger communities were typically governed by monarchs. Modern advancements, such as the railway, telegraph, telephone, and aeroplane, continue to drive the need for even larger political organizations.
