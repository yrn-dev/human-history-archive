# Mount Ida

The name Mount Ida is associated with multiple geographical locations and historical contexts across the Mediterranean, appearing in the records of Asia Minor, the island of Crete, and in the works of later European artists.

## Mount Ida in Asia Minor
In Asia Minor, Mount Ida is a prominent landmark located in the region of the Troad. It is situated east of the plain of Thebe and the cities of Adramytteion and Antandros. According to the accounts of Herodotus, an army marching from Lydia to the land of Ilion passed with Mount Ida on its left hand; while stopped for the night beneath the mountain, the army was struck by lightning and thunder, resulting in the death of a large number of men.

The region surrounding Mount Ida was historically inhabited by various groups. Before the arrival of the Greeks, the area was occupied by Teukrians and Mysians, who showed little ethnical difference. Some sources, such as the poet Kallinus, suggested the Teukrians were emigrants from Crete, while others viewed them as indigenous or from Attica. Additionally, the "Phorônis," an ancient poem dating no later than 600 B.C., identifies the Daktyls of Mount Ida—noted as the great discoverers of metallurgy—as Phrygian. This suggests that Phrygian populations may have extended into the Troad and the region of Mount Ida.

## Æolic Greek Settlements
The region of Mount Ida eventually became "Æolized" through the establishment of Æolic Greek colonies. These settlements emanated from cities such as Lesbos, Kymê, and Tenedos. The territory of Mount Ida is defined in these records as the land west of a line drawn from the town of Adramyttion northward to Priapos on the Propontis. 

The Æolic Greeks found the region particularly valuable for its ship timber and iron. While the Kymæans established themselves in the inland Idæan district at Kebrên and other locations, the Teukrian townships in the area were gradually absorbed. By the time of Herodotus, the "remnant of the ancient Teukrians" were noted as inhabiting Gergis, located in the northern region of Ida.

## Relationship with Crete
There is a noted connection between Mount Ida in Æolis (Asia Minor) and Mount Ida in Crete. This relationship is evidenced by shared legendary tales and religious worship. Historians infer that this connection suggests an ethnographical relationship between the inhabitants of these two regions that existed prior to the period of Hellenic occupation.

## Legendary and Mythological Associations
Mount Ida appears in various heroic narratives. According to the accounts of Hellanikus, the hero Æneas retired to the strongest parts of Mount Ida. Some traditions suggest he remained there for a short time before leaving the country via a convention with the Greeks, while other legends claim he eventually returned to Troy to resume the sceptre.

## Artistic Representations
In the 18th century, the artist Barry produced a painting titled "Jupiter and Juno on Mount Ida," which he exhibited in 1773.
