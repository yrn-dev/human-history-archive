# Sacae and Saka

The Sacae (also referred to as Saka) are identified in historical and ethnographic records as an ancient race associated with the Aryan population. They are recognized as a foundational element of the populations inhabiting several regions of Asia.

## Distribution and Identification
The ancient Sacae, or Scyths, are recognized within the Aryan population. In their purest form, they can be found in the more inaccessible glens and mountains of the central highlands. These people are usually called Tajiks and constitute the underlying population of several regions, including:
* Afghanistan
* Badakshan
* Baluchistan
* Persia

The language of the Tajiks in the central districts of Asia contains words of Sanskrit or Aryan derivation that are not found in the Persian language. While they have largely been dispossessed of their lands due to Turkish conquests and immigration, they are noted for retaining an original intellectual superiority over the mixed tribes and Turkish populations that surround them.

## Historical Geography
In the geographical works of antiquity, the Sacae appear in the records of Eratosthenes. His map, which was later adopted in its main features by Strabo (c. 30 B.C.), extended its information in the extreme east to India, Taprobane (Ceylon), and the Sacae. In these contexts, the Sacae are associated with the Kirghiz.

## Related Populations and Distinctions
The broader ethnographic landscape of the regions inhabited by the Sacae includes various other groups:
* **Turkic Peoples:** The Kirghiz are divided by Russian authorities into the Kara Kirghiz (found primarily in the Altai and Tian-shan mountains) and the Kassak Kirghiz, the latter being more closely allied to the Mongol type. The Kipchaks are identified as a clan of the Kirghiz. The Turkoman is described as the purest form of the Turk element and tongue.
* **Mongolian Peoples:** The Kalmucks are a Buddhist and Mongolian people who originated in Dzungaria, migrated to Siberia, and settled on the Lower Volga before returning to Kulja under the Chinese in the late 18th century.
* **Other Baluchistan Groups:** In Baluchistan, other races exist alongside the underlying Persian (Tajik) population. These include the Naushirwanis (a purely Persian race identical to the Tahukani), the Rekis (nomads possibly of Arab origin), and the Dehwars or Dehkans.
