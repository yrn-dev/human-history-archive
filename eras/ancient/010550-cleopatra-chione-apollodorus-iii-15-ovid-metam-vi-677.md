# Cleopatra and Chione

In Greek mythology, Cleopatra and Chione are identified as the daughters of Boreas, the personification of the north wind. According to the accounts provided by Apollodorus and Ovid, they were born to Boreas and Oreithyia.

## Parentage and Family
Boreas is described as the son of Eos and Astraeus, and the brother of Zephyrus, Notus, and Hesperus. His residence was located at Salmydessus, near the country of the Hyperboreans, or on Mount Haemus in Thrace. 

The mother of Cleopatra and Chione was Oreithyia, the daughter of Erechtheus, who was the king of Athens. Boreas had initially attempted to woo Oreithyia without success. He eventually carried her off to Mount Haemus, where the two lived as the queen and king of the winds. Together, they had four children: two sons, Calais and Zetes, and two daughters, Cleopatra and Chione.

## Boreas and the City of Athens
The abduction of Oreithyia created a lasting connection between Boreas and the people of Athens. In later times, the Athenians relied on the friendliness of Boreas, which was manifested when he sent storms that wrecked the Persian fleet at Sepias and Athos. In gratitude for this assistance, the Athenians established a festival in his honor known as the Boreasmos and erected an altar or sanctuary to him near the Ilissus.

Additionally, the colony of Thurii offered annual sacrifices to Boreas, addressing him as Euergetes because he had destroyed the fleet of Dionysius the elder.

## Artistic Representations
Boreas is typically depicted in art as a powerful, bearded figure with wings, wearing clothing suited for the cold. On the Tower of the Winds in Athens, he is shown holding a shell similar to those blown by Tritons. The event of Boreas carrying off Oreithyia has been a frequent subject in Greek art, appearing on painted vases and in a bronze relief found on the island of Calymna, which is currently held in the British Museum.
