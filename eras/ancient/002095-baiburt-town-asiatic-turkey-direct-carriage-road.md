# BAIBURT

Baiburt (also referred to as Paipurth) is a town located in Asiatic Turkey. It is situated on the direct carriage road that connects Erzerum and Trebizond. The town is positioned on both banks of the Churuk river (also referred to as the R. Charok), which flows through an open cultivated plateau at an altitude of 5,100 feet before turning toward the east.

## Administration and Economy
Baiburt serves as the chief place of a kaza under the jurisdiction of Erzerum. The town's population is 10,000, consisting mostly of Turks with some Armenians. Economically, the town lacks a special industry, and its bazaar is described as poor.

## The Castle and Architecture
The town is characterized by houses that extend up the hillsides on both banks of the river to a considerable height. A prominent feature of the landscape is the old castle, which sits on an isolated mass of rock on the left bank. The castle is situated on a peninsular hill encircled by the windings of the river. 

The fortress was originally built by the Armenians and later restored by the Seljuks. Its extensive walls are now partly ruined. The principal gate, which features several Arabic inscriptions, is located at the southwest corner. Within the castle grounds, there are remains of a mosque, a Christian church, a vaulted chamber, and two covered staircases leading down to the river. From the summit of the castle, there is a fine view of the plain and the Pontic ranges to the north.

## Historical Accounts
Historical perspectives on the origins of the fortifications vary. According to Neumann, the site was an Armenian fortress in the first century, while Ritter suggests that the castle, known as *Baiberdon*, was fortified by Justinian. In the 14th century, the *Masálak-al-absár* mentions a place called *Bajert* (which Quatremère suggests is a reading for Babert or Baiburt) in connection with silver mines.

In more recent history, the town was occupied by Russian forces under General Paskevich during their invasion of 1829. At that time, Baiburt represented the farthest point westward reached by the Russians. Upon their retirement from the town in 1829, the Russians blew up a large portion of the defenses.

## Regional Geography
The region surrounding Baiburt contains mineral resources. Approximately 35 miles northwest of the town are the mines of *Gumish-Khánah* ("Silverhouse"). While these are noted as silver mines, they are more accurately described as lead mines rich in silver and were once worked extensively. Additionally, according to Ramusio’s version, Baiburt served as the third relay for travelers journeying between Trebizond and Tauris, who would pass under the stronghold.
