# Andria

The *Andria* is a dramatic work that has appeared in various contexts throughout history, including as a Roman comedy, a subject of translation, and a catalyst for theatrical careers.

## Roman Origins and Caecilius Statius
The *Andria* was written by Terence and exhibited in 166 B.C. During this period, the Roman comic poet Caecilius Statius—a former slave and contemporary of Ennius—was highly esteemed for his judgment. According to a statement in the life of Terence by Suetonius, Caecilius was ordered to hear the *Andria* read and to provide his professional opinion on the work. 

Caecilius himself supported himself by adapting Greek plays for the Roman stage, drawing specifically from new comedy writers such as Menander. In the development of Roman drama, Caecilius occupied a middle ground between Plautus and Terence; unlike Plautus, he did not confound Roman and Greek elements, and unlike Terence, he did not eliminate every detail that could not be romanized.

## Later Translations and Adaptations
In the 16th century, the *Andria* continued to be a subject of literary interest. Machiavelli produced a translation of the play, which remains extant. This translation exists alongside three original comedies written by Machiavelli, the most powerful of which, *Mandragola*, was first printed in Rome in 1524.

## Influence on Performers
The play also served as an educational tool and a source of inspiration for actors. Barton Booth (1681–1733), an English actor from a good Lancashire family, was educated at Westminster school. It was his success in performing the Latin play *Andria* while at school that gave him an inclination for the stage. 

Although Booth was originally intended for the church, he eventually pursued a theatrical career. After running away from Trinity College, Cambridge, in 1698, he began his professional life in Dublin appearing as Oroonoko. He later achieved significant success in London at Lincoln's Inn Fields and the Haymarket, eventually spending twenty years at Drury Lane. He became a renowned tragedian, particularly noted for the title part in Addison's *Cato*, as well as roles such as Brutus, Hotspur, and the ghost in *Hamlet*.
