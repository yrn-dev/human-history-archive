# HEFELE AND DU CANGE

The works of K. J. von Hefele and Charles de Fresne du Cange serve as significant scholarly references across various historical and ecclesiastical subjects, particularly concerning the history of councils, the early Christian church, and the administration of the Lower Empire.

## The Works of K. J. von Hefele

K. J. von Hefele is the author of *Conciliengeschichte* (also referred to as *Concilien*), a comprehensive study of church councils. His research is cited in relation to several pivotal moments in ecclesiastical history:

**The Donatist Schism and African Synods**
Hefele's work documents the synods resulting from the Donatist schism. This includes a synod of Catholic bishops around 348, which met to record gratitude for the official repression of the "Circumcelliones" (Donatist terrorists). This assembly adopted twelve canons of clerical discipline and declared against the rebaptism of any individual who had already been baptized in the name of the Trinity. Additionally, Hefele provides accounts of the "Conference of Carthage" in 411, an assembly held by imperial command to terminate the Donatist schism, which is noted as one of the most important assemblies in the history of the Christian church and the African church.

**The Council of Ephesus and Nestorius**
Hefele's second edition covers the theological strife involving Nestorius and the patriarch of Antioch, John. The records detail the emperor's intervention to terminate the conflict, the banishment of Nestorius, and the dissolution of the council. While the council was generally received as ecumenical, theological controversy continued despite the 433 "Union Creed" intended to adjust differences between John and Cyril.

**Papal and Medieval History**
Hefele's *Conciliengeschichte* (specifically volume 5, 2nd edition) is utilized as a source for the study of various popes and their political interactions. This includes the papacy of Honorius III and his relationship with Emperor Federico II in the 13th century, as well as the history of Pope Gregory X.

## The Contributions of Charles de Fresne du Cange

Charles de Fresne du Cange is recognized for his supplemental works that provide clarity on the history of the Lower Empire. His most notable contributions include the *Greek Glossary* (also cited as *Glossarium*), *Constantinopolis Christiana*, and *Familiæ Byzantinæ*.

**Linguistic and Historical Analysis**
Du Cange's *Glossary* is employed as a reference for specific terminology and historical definitions. It is cited in the study of early Christian architecture, specifically regarding the "Baptisterium" (baptistery). His work is also used to research the history and mythology of the "Draco" (dragon).

**Byzantine Studies**
The historical notes provided by du Cange have enhanced the value of works by Byzantine authors such as Villehardouin, Cinnamus, and Anna Comnena. His scholarship is credited with diffusing a steady light over the darkness of the Lower Empire.

## Early Christian Baptisteries

Research involving the *Glossary* of du Cange and the works of Hefele intersects in the study of early baptisteries. Archaeological and historical evidence shows the existence of these structures across several regions:

*   **Italy:** Excavations have revealed early baptisteries at Aquileia and Salona. Ravenna possesses two famous baptisteries featuring fine mosaics, dating to the middle of the 5th century and the 6th century. A large mosaic-decorated baptistery also dates to the 6th century in Naples.
*   **The East and Africa:** The metropolitan baptistery in Constantinople remains standing beside the mosque that was once the patriarchal church of St Sophia. Other baptisteries have been identified in Syria and North Africa.
*   **Western Europe:** In France, early examples are found at Riez, Frejus, Aix, and the most famous, St Jean at Poitiers. In England, a detached baptistery was associated with the cathedral of Canterbury.
