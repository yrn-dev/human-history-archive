# Touch of All Meat Where Life Hath Been

The phrase "touch of all meat where life hath been" refers to a specific dietary restriction found in the Orphic mysteries, representing a broader historical pattern of avoiding animal flesh based on spiritual or psychic concerns.

## Orphic and Ancient Dietary Restrictions
In the *Cretans* of Euripides, an individual initiated into the mysteries of Orpheus and who became a "Bacchos" describes a state of purity. This person, robed in white and claiming to be clean from "man's vile birth and coffined clay," declares that the "touch of all meat where life hath been" has been exiled from their lips. This Orphic fast from meat was generally maintained, though it was broken by an annual sacramental banquet. While this banquet may have originally consisted of human flesh, it later consisted of raw bovine flesh.

Similar motivations for avoiding meat are found in other ancient traditions. Apollonius of Tyana and the Jewish Therapeutae practiced vegetarianism due to a fear of imbibing the "irrational soul of animals," which they believed could reinforce the lower instincts and appetites of a human being. The Therapeutae specifically ensured their sacred meals featured tables free from meats containing blood.

Furthermore, early Christians and the Jewish people followed a rule against eating animals that had been strangled, driven by the fear of absorbing the psychic qualities of the animal. In contrast, some Manichaean sects believed that fish were free from the "taint" that pollutes animals produced through sexual connection (*quae copulatione generantur*), because fish are born in and of the waters without such connection. Consequently, fish were considered safe to eat, a belief that served as the origin of the Catholic rule of fasting.

## Dietary Laws in the Qur'an
The Qur'an provides specific guidance on the consumption of meat, emphasizing the importance of the name of Allah. Believers are instructed to eat of the good things provided for them and to be grateful. Specifically, they are told to eat of meats on which the name of Allah has been pronounced. Eating meat where the name of Allah has not been mentioned is described as an abomination or impiety.

The text warns that many people are misled by their own unchecked appetites or low desires through ignorance, leading them to disregard these instructions. However, the Qur'an notes that these rules are explained in detail, and exceptions are made for those compelled by necessity or hunger, provided there is no inclination toward transgression or willful disobedience.

## Forbidden Meats and Methods of Slaughter
The Qur'an explicitly lists several types of meat and methods of killing that are forbidden for food:
*   **Dead meat/Carrion:** Meat from animals that die of themselves.
*   **Blood:** The consumption of blood is prohibited.
*   **Swine:** The flesh of swine is forbidden.
*   **Improper Slaughter:** Meat is forbidden if the animal was killed by strangling, a violent blow, a headlong fall, or by being gored to death.
*   **Predation:** Meat that has been partly eaten by a wild animal is forbidden unless the person is able to slaughter it in the due form.
*   **Sacrificial Prohibitions:** Meat sacrificed on stone altars or that on which a name other than Allah has been invoked is forbidden. Additionally, the division of meat by raffling with arrows is characterized as impiety.

## Meat in the Afterlife
While strict laws govern the consumption of meat in earthly life, the Qur'an describes a different experience for those who believe and whose offspring follow them in faith. In the afterlife, Allah promises to provide them with fruit and meat (or flesh) according to their desires. In this state, they are described as being in a home where toil and weariness do not touch them, and they pass among one another a cup free of vanity, sin, and all taint of ill.
