# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview

In the annals of Ancient Egypt’s New Kingdom, few figures are as provocative or successful as Hatshepsut. While women had held power before, Hatshepsut did something unprecedented: she transitioned from being a queen regent to declaring herself a full-fledged Pharaoh. Ruling during the 18th Dynasty (c. 1473–1458 BCE), she did not merely "act" as a king; she assumed the complete titulary and iconography of the office.

Hatshepsut’s reign was characterized by stability, economic growth, and architectural ambition. Rather than focusing on military conquest, she prioritized the restoration of trade networks, most notably the legendary expedition to the Land of Punt, which brought exotic incense, gold, and ebony to Egypt. Her legitimacy was bolstered by a sophisticated propaganda campaign, claiming she was the daughter of the god Amun, thereby intertwining her political authority with divine will.

Despite her success, her legacy was nearly erased. After her death, her successor and stepson, Thutmose III, attempted to scrub her name and image from monuments. Historians believe this was not necessarily out of hatred, but to ensure the royal succession remained traditionally male and to legitimize his own line.

## Key Facts

*   **Architectural Legacy:** She commissioned the Djeser-Djeseru (the "Holy of Holies"), a stunning terraced mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari that remains one of the most iconic structures of the ancient world.
*   **Gender Fluidity in Art:** To reinforce her authority, Hatshepsut was often depicted in statues wearing the traditional pharaonic kilt and the *nemes* headdress. In later reliefs, she is even shown with the ceremonial false beard of the pharaohs.
*   **Economic Prosperity:** Her reign shifted Egypt's focus from warfare to commerce. By reopening trade routes that had been closed during the Hyksos period, she filled the royal treasuries and improved the standard of living.

## Did You Know?

1. **The Great Obelisks:** Hatshepsut erected two massive obelisks at the Temple of Karnak; one of them remains the tallest surviving ancient obelisk in Egypt.
2. **A Master of PR:** She created a detailed "divine birth" myth, carved into her temple walls, claiming that the god Amun took the form of her father to conceive her, making her rule a divine mandate.
3. **Rediscovered History:** Because her name was erased from the "King Lists," the world largely forgot about her until 19th-century archaeologists decoded hieroglyphics and recognized her unique presence in the royal record.
