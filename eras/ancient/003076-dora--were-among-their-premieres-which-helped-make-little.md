# Dora

In the historical records of the archive, Dora is identified as a coastal location and fortress associated with various military conflicts, regional governance, and geographical landmarks.

## Military Conflicts and Sieges
Dora served as a strategic fortress that was difficult to capture. During a conflict between Antiochus and Trypho, Antiochus pursued Trypho into Phoenicia and besieged him within the fortress of Dora. To support this siege, Antiochus sought a league of friendship and mutual assistance from Simon, the Jewish high priest. Simon accepted the invitation and provided the besieging forces with great sums of money and provisions. Despite these supplies, Trypho eventually fled Dora for Apamia, where he was captured during a siege and put to death after a reign of three years.

## Regional Governance and Control
The region of Dora and the sea-coast were once under the administration of Abinadab, who was an officer appointed over the country and the husband of a daughter of Solomon. 

In later periods, Dora was held by a tyrant named Zoilus, who also possessed Strato's Tower. Zoilus maintained a legion of soldiers and established his own tyranny during a contest between the kings Antiochus Philometor and Antiochus Cyzicenus. When Alexander Janneus conducted an expedition against Ptolemais, Dora and Strato's Tower remained among the few maritime cities yet to be conquered. During the siege of Ptolemais, Zoilus provided the people of that city with some small assistance.

## Geography and Maritime Significance
Dora is situated along the sea-shore. Historical accounts note that the coastline between Dora and Joppa lacked a good haven. This geographical deficiency forced sailors traveling from Phoenicia to Egypt to endure the stormy sea, as south winds frequently raised vast waves that dashed upon the rocks. To remedy this, a king utilized significant expenses and liberal disposal of funds to build a haven larger than the Pyrecum at Athens, including deep stations for ships in the inner retirements of the water.

## Other References
The name Dora also appears in the context of the English stage. *Diplomacy*, a play produced by Sir Squire Bancroft and Effie Marie Wilton at the Prince of Wales's theatre, was an adaptation of a work by Sardou titled *Dora*. This production was one of the premieres that contributed to the fame of the playhouse.
