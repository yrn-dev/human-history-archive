# Ashtaroth and Be-eshterah

Ashtaroth, also referred to as Ashteroth Karnaim and Be-eshterah (Josh. xxi. 27), was an important city located in Bashan. In the biblical record, Genesis xiv. 5 notes that the Rephaim—an aboriginal race distinguished by their lofty stature—were established in Ashteroth-Karnaim. Following the conquest of the land, the territory was allotted to the eastern division of the tribe of Manasseh (Josh. xiii. 29; Numbers xxxiii. 33).

## Geographic Identifications
The precise location of Ashtaroth has been the subject of various proposed identifications, though the archive notes that identifications based solely on the outward similarity of names are often sources of error and that the true site can only be determined through excavation.

One identification that has commanded significant acceptance is the site of Busrah (Bostra). This location contains important Herodian ruins, although there is currently no tangible evidence to prove the site's history extends to such remote antiquity. Due to the similarity of the names, researchers have also sought the city at Tell 'Ashtera and Tell Ashari.

## Religious and Cultural Associations
The site of Ashtaroth-Karnaim is linked to the worship of Syrian deities. There is a reference in 2 Macc. xii. 26 to an Atergateion (a temple of Atargatis) at Carnion in Gilead. This Carnion temple is considered probably identical to the famous temple of Astarte located at Ashtaroth-Karnaim.

This connection reflects a broader fusion of deities. Atargatis, a Syrian "fish-goddess" known to the Greeks as Derketo or Dea Syria, has frequently been identified with Astarte, though their cults were historically distinct. The name Atargatis is a compound; the first part is a form of the Himyaritic *'Athlar*, which is the equivalent of the Phoenician Astarte and the Old Testament Ashtoreth. While the two deities were likely of common origin, they were often fused to the point of being indistinguishable, as exemplified by the temple at Ashtaroth-Karnaim.

## Context of Bashan
Ashtaroth was one of the cities of Bashan, a region noted in poetical and prophetic books for its natural resources. The area was known for its cattle, which were proverbial for their ferocity, and its mountains. Bashan was especially noted for its oaks, which were used to make oars and were frequently coupled with the cedars of Lebanon. 

Historically, the region of Argob in Bashan contained "threescore great cities with walls and brazen bars," which King Solomon assigned to his lieutenant Ben-Geber. Later, during the reign of Jehu, the country was taken from Israel by Hazael, the king of Syria, marking the last historical event related to Bashan in the Old Testament.
