# Observations and Events of June, July, and August

The months of June, July, and August are characterized across various historical and geographical records by specific climatic patterns, trade cycles, administrative movements, and documented events.

## Climatic Conditions and Environmental Effects
In certain regions, these summer months are marked by extreme temperatures. When the south wind, known as the *sherki*, blows, temperatures can reach exceptional heights, with break-of-day readings of 112°F, rising to 122°F shortly before two o'clock, and standing at 114°F at sunset. Ordinarily, summer temperatures average between 75°F at sunrise and 107°F during the hottest part of the day. This heat is mitigated by a constant breeze, typically from the Northwest, and an extremely dry atmosphere, making it feel less intense than lower temperatures in humid conditions. Additionally, nights during this period are almost always cool.

Regional rainfall data for these months varies significantly by location. In Victoria, July is the driest month with 0.4 inches of rain, and in Agassiz, July records 1.55 inches. Conversely, in Kamloops, July is the wettest month with 1.61 inches of rainfall, while Port Simpson experiences its driest period in June with 4.37 inches.

## Imperial Residences and Customs
Historical accounts of the Kaan describe a seasonal distribution of his year to avoid heat. For three months—June, July, and August—the Lord resides at his Park in the city of Chandu, dwelling in either the Cane Palace or the Marble Palace because the location is very cool. On the 28th day of the August Moon, he departs, and the Cane Palace is dismantled. 

The Kaan maintains a stud of over 10,000 pure white horses and mares. The milk of these mares is reserved for the Kaan, his family, and the Horiad tribe. The Horiad were granted this privilege by Chinghis Kaan following their assistance in a victory won long ago. Additionally, the Yakuts are noted to hold festivals in June or July during the foaling of mares, during which they empty immense wooden goblets of *kumíz* and pour the drink for the Spirits to the four quarters of heaven.

## Trade and Imports in the United Kingdom
The months of June, July, and August see specific fluctuations in the import of goods into the United Kingdom.

### Agricultural Imports
Canadian butter begins to arrive in July and reaches its bulk import volume during August, September, and October. In contrast, colonial butter from Australasia arrives during mid-winter months, specifically December, January, and February. 

Tomato imports show a significant peak during this period. Based on data from 1906 to 1908, July consistently recorded the highest import volumes of the year (reaching 238,362 in 1906, 150,907 in 1907, and 171,978 in 1908), followed by August.

### Floral Trade
The trade of fresh flowers sinks to its minimum dimensions from July to October. Records from 1906 to 1908 show that August is the lowest point of the year for flower import values, recording L1,646 in 1906, L1,539 in 1907, and L1,081 in 1908.

### Ancient Military Expeditions
In the study of Greek history, the timing of the out-march of Pelopidas has been debated. One theory by Dodwell places a preceding solar eclipse on June 13, 364 B.C., at five o'clock in the morning. Another theory by Calvisius places the eclipse on July 13 of the same Julian year at a quarter before eleven o'clock. It has been noted that Dodwell's hypothesis suggests only June, July, and August are fit for military expeditions, a view considered unreasonable.

### Security System Failures
In 2019, forensic analysis by the FBI revealed that the DVR 2 system at MCC New York suffered disk failures on July 29, which prevented the system from recording. This failure was not discovered by the BOP until August 8, 2019, when an Associate Warden 1 and a Special Investigative Services (SIS) Lieutenant attempted to review surveillance video. An Electronics Technician subsequently found that approximately half of the institution's 150 cameras assigned to DVR 2 were displaying live feeds but were not recording. This was part of a broader pattern of nine instances between June 2017 and July 2019 where actions were required to address hard drive or camera problems.
