# THE NARA EPOCH

The Nara epoch was a distinct period of Japanese history characterized by the establishment of a capital at Nara in Yamato province. The epoch began with the removal of the capital to Nara in 709 A.D. and lasted until 794 A.D., when the Imperial palace was transferred from Nara to Kyoto.

## Administration and Law
The governance of the Nara epoch was influenced by several legal codes and administrative structures. The Daiho laws of 701 A.D. and the Yoro laws of 718 A.D. provided the legal framework for the era. Under the Daiho laws, the provinces (kuni) were classified and subdivided into kori. The administration of these provinces was handled by provincial rulers; while early administration was conducted by imperial princes (kuni-no-miyatsuko), this later shifted to kokushi.

During the reigns of Koken and Shomu, the administration of the kokushi was marked by abuses, including the use of forced labour to reclaim uplands. In 774 A.D., the term for this forced labour was reduced to five years. Additionally, the archive notes that in 775 A.D., revenues from public lands were appropriated for city officials.

## Economy and Commerce
The Nara epoch saw the development of formal commerce and a standardized currency system. Coinage was established during the Wado era, spanning from 708 to 715 A.D. Markets (ichi) were a feature of the period, and the era was characterized by a specific level of commercial activity. The state also implemented sumptuary laws during this epoch to regulate expenditure and consumption.

## Culture and Society
The Nara epoch was a time of cosmopolitan culture and elegance, particularly within the Imperial palace. This is evidenced by the contents of the Shoso-in, a wooden storehouse constructed during the epoch near the Todai-ji temple in Yamato province. The Shoso-in houses a collection of furniture and ornaments from the Imperial palace, including:
* Polished mirrors with repousse backs in brocaded silk cases.
* Bronze censers and vases.
* Incense-boxes made of Chinese ware or Paulownia wood.
* Two-edged swords tied to the girdle.
* Bamboo flutes and lacquer writing-cases.
* Narrow leather belts decorated with jade or silver.

The presence of utensils from India (some showing Greek influence), Persia, and China highlights the international nature of the palace's culture. 

In terms of literature and arts, the Nara epoch saw the emergence of the Manyo-shu, which is the first Japanese anthology. The period also experienced a vogue for the couplet-tournament (uta awase), a form of verse-making that would later be further developed during the Heian epoch. The prose of the Nara epoch is also noted in the archive's records.

## Religious and Social Life
The epoch was marked by the presence of kokubun-ji (temples) and the creation of the Nara image of Buddha. Socially, the status of Imperial princes underwent changes during this time. The archive also notes the existence of superstition during the Nara epoch.
