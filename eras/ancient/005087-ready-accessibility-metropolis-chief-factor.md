# Brighton

## Overview and Geography
Brighton is a municipal, county, and parliamentary borough located in Sussex, England. It is recognized as one of the most well-known seaside resorts in the United Kingdom. The town is situated on the seaward slope of the South Downs, a position that provides shelter from inclement winds and ensures a generally mild climate. 

The town's geography is defined by its relationship with the English Channel. The sea-front stretches nearly 4 miles, extending from Kemp Town in the east to Hove, which is a separate municipal borough, in the west. Inland, the town extends approximately 2 miles, a distance that includes the suburb of Preston. As of 1901, the population of Brighton was 123,478.

## Accessibility and Popularity
A primary factor in the popularity of Brighton is its ready accessibility from the metropolis. The town is located 51 miles south of London and is connected to the city via the London, Brighton & South Coast railway.

## Coastal Infrastructure
The coastline opposite Brighton is subject to currents in the English Channel that tend to drive shingle eastward. This geographical tendency led to frequent and serious encroachments by the sea. To combat this, a massive sea-wall was constructed beginning around 1830. This structure stands 60 feet high, with a thickness of 23 feet at its base and 3 feet at its summit.

## Architecture and Public Institutions
Brighton features numerous modern churches and chapels, many of which are described as very handsome. Among these is the former parish church of St Nicholas, a Decorated structure that houses a memorial to the great duke of Wellington and a Norman font. Additionally, the Trinity Chapel was once held by the famous preacher Frederick William Robertson between 1847 and 1853.

The town's administrative functions are centered in the parochial offices and the town hall. For the entertainment of visitors, the town offers several music-halls and theatres, as well as an aquarium owned by the corporation. The aquarium houses an excellent marine collection and serves as a winter garden and concert hall, featuring a garden located on its roof.

One of the most remarkable institutions in the town is the Pavilion. Originally built as a residence for the prince regent (who later became George IV), it was remodelled in 1819 by architect John Nash in a grotesque Eastern style of architecture. The town purchased the building in 1849 for £53,000. It is now used for various public purposes and contains picture-galleries, assembly-rooms, and a museum. The former stables, a detached building, have been converted into a fine concert hall; this facility is lit by a vast glazed dome that is similar in dimensions to that of St Paul's cathedral in London.
