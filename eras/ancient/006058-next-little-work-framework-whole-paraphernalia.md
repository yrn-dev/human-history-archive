# Frameworks and Paraphernalia in Human History

The concept of a framework—whether physical, structural, or narrative—appears across various domains of human activity, from the construction of biological organs and industrial crafts to the organization of religious texts and the evolution of global material conditions.

## Narrative and Religious Frameworks
In early Buddhist literature, the "framework" often refers to the prose narratives that accompany poetic utterances. In the work titled *Udāna* ("ecstatic utterances"), the Buddha is depicted as being moved by events, speech, or actions to produce short, enigmatic outbursts of poetry. These "gems," numbering fifty in the translation by General Strong, are charged with religious emotion and often address the Buddhist ideal of life, known as Arahatship.

Similarly, the *Iti Vuttaka* consists of 120 short passages. Each passage leads to a "deep saying" of the Buddha and is introduced by the phrase *Iti vuttam Bhagavalā* ("thus was it spoken by the Exalted One"). While the historical accuracy of these anecdotes is uncertain, they provide evidence of the discussions held by early Buddhists. Another work, the *Thera-therī-gāthā*, contains stanzas attributed to 107 brethren and 73 sisters of the order; in this instance, the prose framework is preserved only within the commentary, which also provides biographies of the authors.

The development of these records suggests an evolutionary process: short suttas (doctrinal statements) were first created, followed by episodes that embodied several suttas. These were then woven into *suttantas*, which were eventually grouped into *Nikāyas*. Because verses were helpful for memory and expressed religious enthusiasm, they were often accompanied by prose comments explaining the context of the utterance. In later canon books, these narrative frameworks shifted from prose to verse, resulting in collections of ballads and, eventually, long epic poems.

## Physical and Industrial Frameworks
In the craft of basket-making, the "framework" refers to the rigid structure of the bottoms and lids of square work, which is constructed using "sticks"—rods of two to three years' growth. The materials used for these structures are categorized as "osier" (*Salix viminalis*) or "fine" (*Salix triandra, S. purpurea*, and other hybrids). Depending on the desired finish, makers use "brown stuff" (unpeeled), "white stuff" (peeled), or "buff" (boiled and stained tawny). These rods are sorted by size into categories such as "luke," "middleboro," and "great." To make the material pliable, brown stuff is soaked for three to seven days, while white and buff rods are soaked for half a day or less.

Other physical frameworks include:
*   **Aviaries:** These outdoor structures consist of wire-netting supported by a framework of woodwork or iron. While hardy birds require only this and some natural shelter, tropical species require a wooden or brick house that can be artificially warmed.
*   **Barbecues:** Originally, a barbecue was a framework on posts placed over a fire to smoke or dry meat. In Cuba, the term refers to an upper floor used for storing grain or fruit.
*   **Biological Structures:** In birds, the *syrinx* (lower larynx) relies on a cartilaginous framework with vibrating membranes and special muscles to regulate tension.

## Ritual Paraphernalia and Material Progress
The term "paraphernalia" is used to describe the equipment associated with specific rituals, professions, or beliefs. In some parts of China, the practice of hypnotism to extract information from spirits involves the paraphernalia of incense, candles, and an altar. Similarly, the "planchette" method of reading the future is performed publicly before a temple altar using a V-shaped instrument with a wooden pencil.

In Japan, the introduction of the Indian creed was viewed as a movement that was "gorgeous in paraphernalia" and impressive in its rites. This influence led to changes in dress, such as the adoption of the veil by women and the stole by men, as well as the construction of massive temples requiring specific timbers for their frames.

In a military context, the term is used to describe the equipment of field services, such as the "paraphernalia" carried by cooks on steaming kitchen wagons during military actions.

## The Material Framework of History
On a global scale, the nineteenth century witnessed a "mechanical revolution" that created a new, gigantic material framework for human affairs. This era saw profound advances in agricultural chemistry, allowing crops to be quadrupled or quintupled compared to the seventeenth century, and significant leaps in medical science that increased the average duration of life. This shift in the material conditions of human existence was more vast than the transitions between the palæolithic stage and the age of cultivation.
