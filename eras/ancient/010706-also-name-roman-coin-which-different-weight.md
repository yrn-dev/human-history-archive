# As (Roman Coin)

The *as* was a Roman coin characterized by significant fluctuations in weight and value across different historical periods. Its introduction is attributed to Servius Tullius.

## Composition and Early Forms
The original *as* was composed of *aes*, a mixed metal alloy consisting of lead, tin, and copper. In its earliest primitive form, known as *aes rude*, the coinage consisted of irregular oblong bars. These bars were stamped with images of a sow, ox, or sheep. The use of these animal figures, combined with the word for money, *pecunia* (derived from *pecus*, meaning cattle), indicates that cattle served as the earliest medium of exchange in Italy.

The value of these early bars was indicated by other marks or little globules and points. The original version of the coin was called the *as libralis* because it nominally weighed 12 ounces (1 lb.), although its actual weight was 10 ounces.

## Evolution of Design and Subdivisions
Following the transition to a round shape, the *as* featured a consistent design: one side was inscribed with the double head of Janus, and the other side depicted the prow of a ship. This ship's prow motif was also utilized on the subdivisions of the *as*, which featured the head of a deity on the reverse side.

## Weight Reductions and Depreciation
The weight of the *as* was reduced several times due to economic pressures and legislative changes:
* **First Punic War:** The treasury was exhausted, leading to a reduction of the *as* to 2 oz.
* **Second Punic War:** The weight was halved again to 1 oz.
* **Papirian Law (89 B.C.):** The coin was further reduced to a diminutive weight of half an ounce.
* **Era of Octavian, Antony, and Lepidus:** The value was reduced further to 1/3 of an ounce.
* **Emperor Severus:** The weight was lowered again to approximately 5/24 of an ounce.

The value of the coin declined sharply over time. Before the introduction of silver coinage in 269 B.C., the *as* was valued at about 6d.; by the time of Cicero, its value had fallen to less than a halfpenny.

## Terminology
During the periods of the Roman empire and the commonwealth, specific terms were used to distinguish between different eras of the coin. *Aes grave* was the term used to denote the old *as* to differentiate it from the depreciated coins then in circulation. As previously noted, *aes rude* referred to the primitive oblong coinage.
