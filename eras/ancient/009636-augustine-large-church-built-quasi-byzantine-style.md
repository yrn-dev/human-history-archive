# Cathedral of St Augustine in Bona

The cathedral of St Augustine is a large church located in Bona, a seaport city in Algeria. The city is situated at the foot of the wooded heights of Edugh and is the chief town of an arrondissement in the department of Constantine.

## Architecture and Location
The cathedral is built in a quasi-Byzantine style. It is situated at the northern end of the Cours Jerome-Bertagna (formerly known as the Cours National), which is a broad, tree-lined promenade running through the center of the town. This promenade serves as the location for the city's principal buildings, including hotels, banks, and a theatre.

## Relics
The church serves as the repository for a specific relic believed to be the right arm of St Augustine. This relic was brought to the cathedral from Pavia in 1842.

## Context of the City
The cathedral is one of several notable structures in Bona. The city's layout includes a modern rampart built outside an older Arab wall. Other significant landmarks include the Grand Mosque, which was constructed using ruins from the ancient Hippo and is located on one side of the Place d'Armes. The town also features a citadel, known as the Kasbah, positioned on a hill to the north-east.

Bona is a major center of commerce, ranking only behind Oran and Algiers in Algeria. It maintains direct steamship communication with Marseilles and manages a trade of importing manufactures and general merchandise while exporting materials such as iron, zinc, phosphates, wool, sheep, barley, cork, and esparto. The city's infrastructure includes an inner harbour of 25 acres and a spacious outer harbour, which was built between 1857 and 1868 and subsequently enlarged from 1905 to 1907.
