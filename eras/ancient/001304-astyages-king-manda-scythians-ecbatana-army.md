# Astyages

Astyages was the final king of the empire based at Ecbatana, referred to in some sources as the Median empire and in Babylonian records as the empire of "the Manda" or Scythians. In the inscriptions of Nabonidus, his name is recorded as Ishtuvegu. He succeeded his father, Cyaxares, who had previously established dominance over the cities of the Assyrian kingdom following the destruction of Nineveh.

## Reign and Administration
Astyages reigned for thirty-five years, with some accounts dating his rule from 584 to 550 B.C. Despite the length of his tenure, historical records provide few particulars regarding his administration. He was married to Aryenis, the daughter of Alyattes of Lydia, making him a brother-in-law to Croesus. 

The empire under his predecessors and himself had ruled Asia above the river Halys for 128 years, though this period was interrupted by a time of Scythian rule. The city of Ecbatana served as the seat of power for the "Manda" princes and later for Astyages.

## Conflict with Cyrus
The decline of Astyages' rule began with a revolt led by Cyrus, the king of Anshan in Elam. According to the inscriptions of Nabonidus, Cyrus began war against Astyages in 553 B.C. (or possibly 549 B.C.). 

Various accounts exist regarding the relationship between the two men. Some narratives describe Cyrus as the grandson of Astyages, born to his daughter Mandane and Cambyses, son of Achemenes. These accounts claim that Astyages, alarmed by a dream, ordered the infant Cyrus to be exposed in the mountains. The order was carried out by Harpagus, who delivered the child to a royal herdsman. In this version of events, Cyrus later sought vengeance against Astyages for this attempted murder.

## The Fall of the Empire
The collapse of Astyages' authority was characterized by the betrayal and rebellion of his own military forces. When the Persians rose up to free themselves from the rule of the Medes, Astyages summoned Cyrus and subsequently armed the Medes to suppress the revolt. He appointed Harpagus as the leader of the army.

During the ensuing conflict, the Median army was dispersed; while some fought, many were willfully slack, fled, or defected to the Persians. Following this defeat, Astyages attempted a final stand by arming the remaining youths and old men within the city. This force was also defeated, and Astyages was taken alive.

In 550 B.C., as Astyages marched against Cyrus, his troops rebelled and he was taken prisoner. Cyrus subsequently occupied and plundered Ecbatana, effectively ending the empire of the Scythians (or Medes).

## Later Life and Death
Following his capture, Astyages was treated fairly by Cyrus. Herodotus records that Cyrus did no harm to him and kept him with himself until his death. Other accounts, such as those from Ctesias, suggest that Astyages was appointed as the satrap of Hyrcania. In this version, he was later slain by Oebares against the wishes of Cyrus, who then provided him with a splendid funeral.
