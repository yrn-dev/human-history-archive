# Beni-Hasan

Beni-Hasan is a location in Middle Egypt known for its tombs, which contain wall paintings and depictions providing historical insight into the visitors and inhabitants of the region during the 19th and 20th centuries B.C.

## Depictions of Semitic Nomads
Wall paintings found in a tomb near ancient Beni-Hasan depict the arrival of a tribe of Semitic nomads in Egypt, dated to approximately 1895 B.C. These depictions highlight the distinctions between the nomadic way of life and the civilizations of river valley cultivators. While agriculturalists lived in permanent settlements, these nomadic peoples lived in constant movement between summer and winter pastures. This lifestyle fostered individuals who were more self-reliant and less of a unit in a crowd, with a greater emphasis on the importance of the leader.

## Clothing and Appearance
The Beni-Hasan tombs provide detailed visual records of the attire worn by the Semites who visited Egypt. While the typical Egyptian loin-cloth of the period was a plain short linen cloth wrapped around the loins and tied in front—worn by peasants, servants, and scribes—the Semites wore larger, colored cloths. These garments were ornamented with parallel stripes of patterns similar to those found on early Palestinian pottery, and featured borders that were either fringed or decorated with bunches of tassels.

In the famous Beni-Hasan tombs, the Semites are shown wearing richly decorated cloth in the form of close-fitting tunics or skirts. The leader of the group is depicted in a particularly magnificent wrapper of red, white, and blue. This garment featured fringed edges and was held in position by a neck-ribbon. In accordance with the customs of the time, the dress for women was longer than that for men, though both sexes left their arms free and exposed the right shoulder.

## Comparative Military Equipment
The archive notes a connection between the imagery at Beni-Hasan and ancient Mesopotamian artifacts. On the "Stele of the Vultures" from Lagash, a scene depicts a king in a chariot holding a curved weapon made of three metal bars bound together by rings. This specific weapon is noted as being similar to one carried by the chief of an Asiatic tribe depicted in a tomb of the 12th dynasty at Beni-Hasan.
