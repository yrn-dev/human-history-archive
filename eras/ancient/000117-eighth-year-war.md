# Eighth Year of the War

The eighth year of the Peloponnesian War is characterized as a turning point in the conflict, featuring events of a more decisive and important nature than those of the preceding years. While earlier operations were often desultory and failed to determine the ultimate outcome of the war despite widespread hardship and fighting, the eighth year saw a shift toward larger and more ambitious strategic operations.

## Athenian Ambitions and Early Hopes
The year opened with sanguine hopes for Athens and dark promises for Sparta. This optimism was primarily driven by the capture of Sphakteria and its prisoners toward the end of the previous summer, as well as the surrender of the entire Lacedæmonian fleet. These events were viewed as imposing by all of Greece and stimulated Athens to pursue a series of ambitious goals. 

The Athenians sought not only to pressure Sparta within her own territory but also to regain the ascendency in Bœotia and Megara that had been lost on or before the thirty years’ truce. Athens launched two considerable enterprises against these regions; however, the results were mixed. While the plan against Megara was partially successful, the operation in Bœotia ended in a ruinous defeat.

## Spartan Counter-Operations and Brasidas
The Athenian successes at Sphakteria intimidated several parties, including the Lacedæmonians, Perdikkas (the king of Macedonia), and the revolted Chalkidic allies of Athens in Thrace. In response, these groups concerted the expedition of Brasidas. 

The career of Brasidas and the subsequent Athenian losses in Thrace, which followed closely after the defeat at Delium, seriously lowered the perception of Athenian power. The success of Brasidas effectively neutralized the advantages Athens had gained from the capture of Sphakteria. Consequently, the year concluded with humiliations for Athens, contrasting sharply with the glowing expectations that had marked its beginning.

## Other Military and Territorial Events
During this year, the Athenian general Lamachus conducted a cross march after losing his triremes. This loss occurred due to a sudden rise of water at the mouth of the river Kalex, located in the territory of Herakleia.

Additionally, the island of Kythêra was occupied by Nikias during the eighth year. This occupation was significant as the Lacedæmonians had previously shown great concern regarding the island, as it served as a point of vulnerability for attacks from ships coming from Libya and Egypt, as well as from pirates.

In the territory of Thyrea on the eastern coast of the Peloponnesus, a separate tragedy occurred involving expelled Æginetans. The Lacedæmonians had assigned these individuals a new abode in Thyrea, but they were subsequently attacked, taken prisoner, and put to death by the Athenians.

## Historical Chronology and Analysis
The events of the eighth year provide a basis for analyzing the composition of historical texts. For instance, the fact that Herodotus does not mention the fate of the expelled Æginetans in Thyrea, nor the occupation of Kythêra by Nikias, suggests to some historians that his history was composed in the earliest years of the Peloponnesian War, before these events took place.

The year's trajectory—from the capture of Sphakteria to the losses in Thrace—eventually led toward the Peace of Nikias, following the renewal of war and the Battle of Amphipolis.
