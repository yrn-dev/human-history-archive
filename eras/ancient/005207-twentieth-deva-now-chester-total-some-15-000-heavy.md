# Roman Military Presence at Deva

## Legionary Forces and Composition
From the time of Hadrian onwards, the province was guarded or coerced by an army that included three primary legions. These were the Second, stationed at Isca Silurum (Caerleon-on-Usk), the Ninth at Eburacum (now York), and the Twentieth at Deva (now Chester). Together, these three legions comprised a total of approximately 15,000 heavy infantry.

In addition to the legions, the army utilized a large but uncertain number of auxiliaries. These troops were considered of the second grade and were organized into cavalry *alae* or infantry cohorts, with each unit consisting of 500 or 1,000 soldiers. Unlike the legions, these auxiliary forces were posted in *castella* located closer to the frontiers.

## Fortifications and Infrastructure
The legionary fortresses, such as the one at Deva, were designed as large rectangular enclosures covering 50 or 60 acres. These sites were enclosed by strong walls. Traces of these structures remain visible today in the lower courses of the north and east town-walls of Chester, as well as on the south side of Caerleon and in the abbey gardens at York.

In contrast, the auxiliary *castella* were significantly smaller, generally ranging from three to six acres depending on the size of the regiment and the requirements for stabling; they were typically less than a tenth of the size of the legionary fortresses. Over 70 of these auxiliary forts are known in England, with approximately 20 more located in Scotland.

While these forts followed a general internal plan, construction materials varied. In forts built primarily before 150 A.D., wood was used extensively, with stone reserved for a few principal buildings. In other forts, the internal buildings were constructed entirely of stone.

## Regional Connectivity and Character
Deva served as a central hub for Roman infrastructure in the region. Roman roads extended from Deva to Mons Heriri (*Tomen y mur*) and to Segontium (Carnarvon). 

Unlike some other Roman sites, Deva remained purely military in character. This distinguishes it from locations like Eburacum, where civil life or a municipality developed outside the fortress walls.
