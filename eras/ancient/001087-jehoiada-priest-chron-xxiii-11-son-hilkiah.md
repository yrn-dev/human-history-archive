# Jehoiada the Priest

Jehoiada was a high priest in Jerusalem who played a central role in the restoration of the house of David to the throne of Judah and the subsequent religious administration of the temple. He is noted for his leadership in deposing the usurper Athaliah and for his long-term influence over the reign of King Jehoash.

## The Preservation of Jehoash
Following the death of King Ahaziah and the subsequent attempt by Athaliah—the daughter of Ahab and wife of Jehoram—to exterminate the royal family of the house of David, a young son of Ahaziah named Jehoash was preserved. Jehoash was saved by his aunt, Jehosheba, who was the wife of Jehoiada. Jehosheba concealed the child, who was not more than a year old, in a secret bed-chamber. For six years, while Athaliah reigned over Jerusalem and the two tribes, Jehoiada and Jehosheba brought up the child privately within the temple.

## The Revolution Against Athaliah
In the seventh year of Athaliah's reign, Jehoiada organized a revolution to assert the kingdom for the child. He first persuaded five captains of hundreds to assist him, securing their loyalty through oaths to prevent discovery. These partners traveled throughout the country to gather the heads of the tribes, the Levites, and the priests, bringing them to Jerusalem.

After requiring these gathered leaders to swear an oath of secrecy and assistance, Jehoiada presented Jehoash to them, identifying him as the king of the house which God had foretold would reign for all time. Jehoiada established a strict security plan for the temple:
* One-third of the group guarded the king inside the temple.
* One-fourth watched the temple gates.
* Another portion guarded the gate leading to the king's palace.
* The remaining multitude stayed unarmed in the temple.

Jehoiada ordered that only priests be armed within the temple, specifically commanding those guarding the king to kill anyone who entered the temple armed. To equip his forces, Jehoiada opened the armory created by David, distributing spears, quivers, and other weapons to the priests, Levites, and captains of hundreds.

Once these arrangements were complete, Jehoiada, the rulers, the captains of hundreds, and the people escorted Jehoash from the temple to the king's palace and placed him on the throne. Jehoash was seven years old at the time of his accession.

## Religious Reforms and the House of Baal
Following the installation of the king, Jehoiada gathered the armed men and the people in the temple to swear an oath of obedience to the king and the safety of his government. In return, Jehoiada required the king to give security by oath that he would worship God and not transgress the laws of Moses.

Under Jehoiada's leadership, the people demolished the house of Baal, which had been built by Jehoram and Athaliah to honor Ahab and dishonor the God of their fathers. During this action, Mattan, who held the priesthood of Baal, was slain. 

Jehoiada then restored the temple's administration according to the appointments of King David. He entrusted the custody of the temple to the priests and Levites, commanding that incense be offered according to the law and that regular burnt-offerings be brought twice daily. Additionally, he appointed porters and Levites to guard the temple to ensure that no defiled person entered.

## Temple Repairs and Later Influence
During the reign of Jehoash, the king desired to repair the temple, which had fallen into decay under Athaliah, Jehoram, and their sons. When the king's initial request for the priests and Levites to collect a half-shekel of silver from every person proved unsuccessful—as Jehoiada concluded that people would not pay willingly—the high priest devised a stratagem.

Jehoiada constructed a wooden chest with a single hole and placed it beside the altar in the temple. He invited the people to cast in whatever they pleased for the temple's repair. This method was well-received, and the people contributed large quantities of gold and silver. Once sufficient funds were collected, Jehoiada and King Joash hired carpenters and masons and purchased high-quality timber to complete the repairs. The remaining funds were used to create bowls, cups, basons, and other vessels, and to provide valuable sacrifices for the altar.

The records indicate that these temple arrangements were maintained as long as Jehoiada lived. Furthermore, during Jehoiada's lifetime, Jehoash remained zealous in the worship of God and careful to keep the laws. Jehoiada also provided two wives for the king when he came of age, who bore him sons and daughters.
