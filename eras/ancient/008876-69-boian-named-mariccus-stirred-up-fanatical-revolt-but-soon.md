# The Boii

The Boii, a Celtic people whose name may mean "the terrible," originally inhabited Gallia Transalpina. They were known to the Romans as early as the time of Plautus, who referenced them with contempt in the *Captivi*. In their early history, the Boii split into two primary groups: one migrated into Germany, while the other entered Italy.

## Migration and Settlement in Italy
The group that migrated to Italy crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the Po valley. Upon finding the region already occupied, they crossed the river and drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans. They established their settlements as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna. According to Cato, as cited in Pliny's *Natural History*, the Boii consisted of as many as 112 different tribes. Evidence from tombs at La Tene and Hallstatt suggests that the Boii were fairly civilized.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii engaged in several wars with the Roman Republic. They suffered a defeat alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake in 283. Later, in 224, they were forced to submit following the battle of Telamon in Etruria. Despite this submission, they maintained a hatred for the Romans.

During the Second Punic War in 218, the Boii were irritated by the establishment of the Roman colonies of Placentia and Cremona. This led them to provide valuable assistance to Hannibal. Earlier, during the same war, a Boian prince named Magalus had offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the Carthaginian general crossed the Pyrenees.

The Boii continued their struggle against Rome from 201 until 191. They were eventually subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica, who deprived them of nearly half of their territory.

## Later Migrations and Settlements
Following their defeat, Strabo records that the Boii were driven back across the Alps to settle on the Danube on the land of the Taurisci, adjacent to Raetia and Vindelicia. However, most authorities believe a settlement of the Boii existed on the Danube from an early period in the region of modern Bohemia (ancient *Boiohemum*, meaning "land of the Boii").

Around 60 B.C., some Boii migrated to Pannonia and Noricum. Additionally, 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte.

## Cultural References
In the writings of Plutarch, the Boii are mentioned in the context of the burial of Tarpeia, a betrayer of the walls of Rome. The text notes that the Boians and other Celtic tribes buried her, though not beside the stream of Po; they flung their shields from their warlike arms as they laid her low.
