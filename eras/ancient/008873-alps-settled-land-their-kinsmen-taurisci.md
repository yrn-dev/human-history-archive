# The Boii and the Taurisci

The Boii and the Taurisci were Celtic peoples who inhabited various regions of Europe, including Gallia Transalpina, Italy, and the lands along the Danube. Their history is characterized by significant migrations, conflicts with the Roman Republic, and eventual settlement in Central Europe.

## The Boii: Origins and Migration
The Boii, whose name may mean "the terrible," originally hailed from Gallia Transalpina. Early in their history, the people split into two primary groups: one migrated into Germany, while the other moved toward Italy. This second group crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the Po valley. Upon finding the area occupied, they drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans, establishing themselves as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna.

The Boii are described as having been fairly civilized, a conclusion drawn from remains found in tombs at La Tene and Hallstatt. According to Cato, the Boii comprised as many as 112 different tribes.

## Conflict with Rome
The Boii maintained a contentious relationship with the Romans. They were defeated alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake in 283 and were forced to submit in 224 following the battle of Telamon in Etruria. Despite this submission, they continued to harbor hatred for Rome.

During the Second Punic War in 218, the Boii were irritated by the establishment of the Roman colonies of Placentia and Cremona. Consequently, they provided valuable assistance to Hannibal. A Boian prince named Magalus had previously offered to guide Hannibal into Italy after the Carthaginian general crossed the Pyrenees. The Boii continued their struggle against Rome until 191, when they were finally subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica and lost nearly half of their territory.

## Settlement on the Danube and Later Movements
Following their defeat, Strabo records that the Boii were driven back across the Alps to settle on the Danube on the land of their kinsmen, the Taurisci. This area adjoined Raetia and Vindelicia. However, other authorities suggest the Boii had maintained a settlement on the Danube from an early date in the region of modern Bohemia, known anciently as *Boiohemum* ("land of the Boii").

Around 60 B.C., some Boii migrated to Pannonia and Noricum. During this period, 32,000 Boii joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they shared in a defeat near Bibracte.

## The Taurisci and the Norici
The Taurisci were a Celtic tribe inhabiting the southern part of the region now known as Austria before the Christian era. They are described as being associated with other Gaulish tribes, such as the Bastarnæ. The Taurisci were later called the Norici and were conquered by the Romans around 14 B.C. 

Following the Roman conquest, their lands were incorporated into the provinces of Noricum and Pannonia. Under this Roman administration, the settlement of Vindobona (modern Vienna) grew in importance.

## Regional Geography and Later Inhabitants
The lands once inhabited by these Celtic groups were later subject to various migrations and conquests. North of the Danube, the Quadi and Marcomanni frequently fought the Romans, particularly during the reign of Emperor Marcus Aurelius, who died in Vindobona in A.D. 180. As the Roman Empire weakened, the region was ravaged by barbarians, most notably the Huns.

Around 590, the district was settled by the Corutanes (or Slovenes), a Slavonic people who were part of the kingdom of Samo and later the kingdom of the Avars. In 791, Charlemagne moved against the Avars after establishing authority over the Bavarians (*Bajuvarii*). By 805, the Avars were subdued and their land was incorporated into the Frankish empire. To defend the eastern border, Charlemagne established a mark known as the East Mark, which was ruled by a series of margraves from 799 to 907.
