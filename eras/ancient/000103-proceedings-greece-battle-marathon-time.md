# Proceedings in Greece from the Battle of Marathon to the Time of the Battle of Thermopylae

The period following the Battle of Marathon was marked by a temporary unification of Greece through fear, followed by a period of Persian preparation and a subsequent large-scale invasion under Xerxes.

## The Battle of Marathon
In 490 B.C., King Darius sent a Persian force to punish the Athenians for interferences in Asia and to restore the tyrant Hippias. Under the generals Datis and Artaphernes, a force of perhaps 50,000 landed at Marathon, a plain on the northeast coast of Attica. The Athenians, following the recommendation of the strategus Miltiades, met this force in the open field with a full levy of 9,000 heavy infantry under the polemarch Callimachus, joined by 1,000 Plataeans.

The Athenians were disappointed by a lack of assistance from Sparta. After observing the Persian army from the head of the Vrana valley, Miltiades drew up the army and charged. While the native Persians initially overbore the Athenian center, the specially deep Athenian wings broke the opposing divisions and closed in on the center. The Persians were driven into the sea; 6,400 Persians were killed, compared to 192 Athenians. Two thousand Lacedemonians arrived in Attica three days after leaving Sparta; though they arrived too late for the battle, they viewed the bodies of the slain and commended the Athenians for their work.

## The Transition of Persian Power
Following the repulse at Marathon, news reached Darius simultaneously with reports of a rebellion in Egypt. Darius died while undecided on which direction to turn. His successor, Xerxes, first addressed the situation in Egypt by establishing a Persian satrap. For the following four years, Xerxes prepared a second attack on Greece. This preparation involved mobilizing nations from across Asia, requiring various contingents to provide ships, land-army soldiers, cavalry, vessels for transporting horses, and ships for provisions and bridges.

## The March of Xerxes
Xerxes marched through subject territory, utilizing magazines laid up in advance for the subsistence of his army. He was joined by additional levied contingents and Thracian volunteers seeking plunder. The Thracians maintained the road he traveled with solemn reverence, protecting it from tillage and intruders even into the days of Herodotus.

Upon reaching the northern boundary of what was properly called Hellas, Xerxes came within sight of Mount Olympus. He was conducted further by the Thessalian Aleuadæ and the Macedonian princes, who were the last of his western tributaries.

## Athenian Development and Context
In the eighty-seven years between the Battle of Marathon and the renovation of the democracy after the Thirty, the Athenian population acquired a larger range of capacities and ideas. While some writers attributed this to the "poison" of the sophists, others argue that the people became both politically and morally better and that their democracy worked toward their improvement.
