# York

## Roman Municipal Status
York is one of five modern cities—alongside Colchester, Lincoln, Gloucester, and St Albans—that are situated on the sites of Roman municipalities. These municipalities were established by the Roman government and provided with special constitutions and charters. While York and the other four cities achieved a considerable level of prosperity, they did not rival the larger municipalities found in other Roman provinces.

## Regional Significance and Surrounding Towns
Within the framework of Romano-British life, York served as a key urban center. To the north-west of York, approximately 12 miles away, lay Isurium Brigantum. This location served as the capital of the Brigantes and was the most northerly town in Romano-British territory. Isurium Brigantum was one of several country towns that likely developed from the capitals of Celtic tribes or market centers prior to the Roman conquest.

## The Reign of Septimius Severus
The city of York is noted as the place where the Emperor Septimius Severus died. Severus, described as the ablest emperor of his age, turned his attention to the island in 208. His arrival followed a period of instability in north Britain; sixteen years after the work of Pius, disorder had broken out between the Derbyshire hills and the Cheviots, requiring four or five years of fighting to repress. Subsequently, between 180 and 185, another war occurred resulting in the Romans losing everything beyond Cheviot. The government of Commodus was unable to repair these losses, and subsequent civil wars in Europe from 193 to 197 provided further opportunities for the Caledonians.

Upon his arrival, Septimius Severus invaded Caledonia and undertook the reconstruction of Hadrian's wall, rebuilding the structure from end to end using stone. Severus died at York in the fourth year of these operations. Despite various uncertain or legendary accounts of his work in Britain, it is established that he designated the line of Hadrian's wall as the substantive frontier. This position was later accepted by his successors, Severus Alexander and Caracalla (211-235), who carried out building and rebuilding projects to increase the efficiency of the frontier's defenses.

## Legacy of the Frontier
The establishment of the frontier by Septimius Severus marked the conclusion of the conquest of Britain. For nearly two hundred years following these events, Hadrian's wall continued to serve as the northern limit of Roman power in the extreme west.
