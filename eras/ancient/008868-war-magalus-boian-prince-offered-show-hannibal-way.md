# Magalus and the Boii

## The Boii People
The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. They are described as having split into two primary groups early in their history: one group migrated into Germany, while the other entered Italy. The group that entered Italy is said to have crossed the Pennine Alps via the Great St Bernard into the Po valley. Upon finding the region already occupied, they drove out the Umbrians and Etruscans, establishing themselves as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna.

According to Cato, the Boii comprised as many as 112 different tribes. Evidence from tombs at La Tene and Hallstatt suggests they were fairly civilized. While some Boii remained in Gallia Transalpina, others eventually settled on the Danube in the land of their kinsmen, the Taurisci, adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia. Most authorities believe a settlement of the Boii existed on the Danube from an early period in the area of modern Bohemia (ancient *Boiohemum*, meaning "land of the Boii"). Later, around 60 B.C., some migrated to Pannonia and Noricum, and 32,000 joined a Helvetian expedition into Gaul, where they were defeated near Bibracte.

## Relations with Rome
The Boii had a contentious relationship with the Romans, characterized by several wars. In 283, the Boii were defeated alongside the Etruscans at the Vadimonian lake. Later, in 224, they were forced to submit following the battle of Telamon in Etruria. Despite this submission, they maintained a hatred for Rome.

During the Second Punic War in 218, the Boii were irritated by the Roman foundation of the colonies of Placentia and Cremona. This led them to provide valuable assistance to Hannibal. Their struggle against Rome continued until 191, when P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica finally subdued them and deprived them of nearly half of their territory.

## Magalus and the Second Punic War
During the Second Punic War, Magalus, a prince of the Boii, played a role in facilitating Hannibal's campaign. After Hannibal had crossed the Pyrenees, Magalus offered to show him the way into Italy. 

Additionally, Hannibal received a deputation from a prince of the part of Gaul situated near the Po. Through an interpreter, this prince assured Hannibal in the name of his subjects that his arrival was impatiently expected and that the Gauls were ready to march against the Romans. The prince offered to conduct Hannibal's army through areas where they would find a plentiful supply of provisions. Hannibal responded to this offer by praising the bravery of his own troops and exhorting them to maintain their glory, which inspired the soldiers to follow him.

## Later Campaigns and Hannibal's Exile
Following his time in Italy, Hannibal's career was marked by shifting alliances and eventual exile. The Carthaginians, fearing that Hannibal's escape would provoke Roman aggression, declared him an exile, sold his goods, and razed his house. Hannibal subsequently sought refuge with Antiochus. 

Hannibal advised Antiochus to make Italy the seat of the war, requesting twelve thousand land forces and one hundred ships to cross into Africa to engage the Carthaginians before descending upon Italy. However, Antiochus eventually slighted and laid aside Hannibal due to the influence of flatterers and envy. These advisors suggested it was imprudent to trust a Carthaginian exile and argued that the king alone should be the general to ensure the glory of success was not ascribed to a foreigner.

In a later council, Hannibal attempted to persuade Antiochus to form an alliance with Philip of Macedon. He argued that had his previous counsels been followed, Liguria and Tuscany would be "in a flame" and he would be in Italy. Despite the applause his speech received, none of his counsels were executed.

In his final years, Hannibal used stratagems to protect his treasure from those who sought it. He placed vessels filled with molten lead covered in gold and silver in the temple of Diana to deceive others, while concealing his actual riches in hollow brass statues. He eventually fled to the court of Prusias, king of Bithynia, where he helped Prusias's troops achieve several victories by land and sea against Eumenes, the king of Pergamus.
