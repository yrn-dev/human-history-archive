# Islamic Festivals: The Lesser and Greater Bairam

In the Perso-Turkish tradition, the term *Bairam* translates to "festival" and refers to the two primary festivals observed in Islam. These celebrations are governed by a lunar calendar, which causes them to shift through the solar year every thirty-two years.

## The Lesser Festival (Id al-Fitr)
The first of the two festivals is known as the "Lesser Festival." In Turkish, it is referred to as *Kutshuk Bairam* ("Lesser Bairam") or *Sheker Bairam* ("Sugar Bairam"). Arabic-speaking Muslims call it *Id al-Fitr* ("Festival of Fast-breaking") or *Al-Id as-saghir* ("Lesser Festival"). 

This festival occurs immediately following Ramadan, the ninth or fasting-month. It takes place during the first three days of the tenth month, Shawwal. Because of its timing, Turks also refer to it as *Ramazan Bairam*. Compared to the Greater Festival, the Lesser Festival is characterized by more outward signs of rejoicing. Common practices include:
*   Holding official receptions and paying private visits.
*   Exchanging congratulations among friends and giving presents.
*   Wearing new clothes.
*   Visiting the graves of relatives.

## The Greater Festival (Id al-Adha)
The second celebration is the "Greater Festival," known in Turkish as *Qurban Bairam* ("Sacrifice Bairam"). Arabic speakers refer to it as *Al-Id al-kabir* ("Greater Festival") or *Id al-adha* ("Festival of Sacrifice"). 

This festival begins on the tenth day of the final month, *Dhu-l-hijja*, and continues for two or three following days. The festival is centered on the act of sacrifice. In the valley of Mina, pilgrims slay a cow, a he-goat, a ram, or a camel to commemorate the ransom of Ishmael with a ram. Throughout the wider Muslim world, those who have the financial means also sacrifice a legal animal. The resulting flesh is either consumed by the participants themselves or distributed to the poor.

Aside from the specific rites of sacrifice, the Greater Festival is celebrated in a manner similar to the Lesser Festival, although it is noted to be observed with less ardour.
