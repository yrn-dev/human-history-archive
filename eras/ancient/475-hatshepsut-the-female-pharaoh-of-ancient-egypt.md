# Hatshepsut: The Woman Who Would Be King

## Overview
In the annals of Ancient Egypt’s New Kingdom, few figures are as provocative or successful as Hatshepsut. While she began her tenure as a regent for her young stepson, Thutmose III, she eventually shattered the traditional boundaries of gender and power by declaring herself Pharaoh. 

Hatshepsut did not simply rule as a queen consort; she assumed the full titulary and regalia of a king. To legitimize her rule in a society where the pharaoh was viewed as the earthly incarnation of the male god Horus, she commissioned art and inscriptions that depicted her with the traditional false beard and muscular physique of a male monarch. However, her reign was not defined by military conquest, but by economic prosperity, architectural ambition, and the restoration of trade networks that had been severed during the Hyksos occupation.

## Key Facts

### The Divine Birth Myth
To solidify her claim to the throne, Hatshepsut promoted a narrative of "divine birth." She claimed that the god Amun-Ra had visited her mother in the guise of her father, Thutmose I, making her the chosen daughter of the supreme deity. This theological maneuver made her rule a divine mandate rather than a political usurpation.

### Architectural Legacy
Hatshepsut was one of the most prolific builders in Egyptian history. Her crowning achievement is her mortuary temple at **Deir el-Bahari**. Carved directly into the limestone cliffs of the West Bank of the Nile, the temple's colonnaded terraces represent a masterpiece of symmetry and classical Egyptian design.

### The Great Trade Expedition
Unlike many pharaohs who sought glory through war, Hatshepsut focused on commerce. Her famous expedition to the **Land of Punt** (likely modern-day Somalia or Eritrea) brought back exotic goods, including myrrh trees, ebony, ivory, and gold, fueling an era of unprecedented wealth.

## Did You Know?

* **The Great Erasure:** After her death, Thutmose III (or his successor) attempted to erase Hatshepsut from history by hacking her name and image off monuments. Historians believe this was done to ensure a clean succession line for the male heirs.
* **The False Beard:** Hatshepsut is often depicted in statues wearing the *postiche* (the ceremonial false beard), a symbol of pharaonic power that transcended gender.
* **A Master of PR:** She used "propaganda" effectively, filling her temples with reliefs that depicted her as a pious servant of Amun, ensuring the powerful priesthood supported her unconventional rule.
