# Periodical Winds and Atmospheric Progress

The movement of periodical winds is governed by the progress of the seasons, though this general law is frequently disguised or made difficult to trace due to the operation of special local influences. These influences modify wind directions across different geographic regions, particularly in the seas and landmasses of Asia.

## The South-West Monsoon
In the Arabian Sea, the south-west monsoon begins with north-westerly and west winds. As the year progresses, these draw round to the south-west, eventually falling back to north and north-west during the autumn. In the Bay of Bengal, the south-west monsoon derives its strength primarily from the south and south-east. Following October, these are succeeded by north-east winds, which then give way to north-westerly and northerly winds as the year advances.

The south-west monsoon does not generally maintain its character as a south-west wind when moving over land. As the current of air flows from the sea, it is dissipated, loses much of its original force, and is gradually diverted toward areas of least pressure. Consequently, winds passing northward over India manifest as easterly and south-easterly winds over the north-eastern Gangetic plain, and as south winds moving up the Indus. By the time these winds reach the northern extremity of the great Indian plain, their northward velocity is almost entirely exhausted. They hardly penetrate the Indus basin or the ranges of the Himalaya and are not felt on the table-lands of Afghanistan. The progress of these winds in their original direction is further prevented by the Himalaya mountains and the ranges that branch off from them into the Malay peninsula.

## Regional Variations and the Equator
The force of the monsoons is significantly interrupted among the islands of the Malay Archipelago. Because this region is positioned on the equator, the directions of prevailing winds are modified. The south-easterly trade winds develop there in the usual manner, while the southerly summer winds found in the Asiatic seas between the tropic and the equator do not extend to the coasts of Java. In contrast, the China Sea is fully exposed to both monsoons; the normal directions of these winds nearly coincide with the center of the channel separating the eastern islands from the continent of Asia.

## Other Observations on Winds and Nature
Beyond the systematic study of monsoons, historical records indicate that prevailing winds in the North Atlantic are westerly. This was noted during the return voyage of the "Mathew," which dropped anchor in Bristol harbour on Sunday, August 6, following a voyage by Cabot.

In the study of early Hellenic religion, some backward communities maintained the Arcadian worship of the winds and thunder. This represents a primitive religious view where natural phenomena were regarded as sacred or divine in their own right, prior to the emergence of personal divinities. Examples include the cult of Zeus "the thunder" at Mantinea and Zeus in Laconia, the latter being associated with a mysterious meteoric stone that falls from heaven.
