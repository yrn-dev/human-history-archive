# Badakshan

Badakshan is a province located on the north-east frontier of Afghanistan, adjoining Russian territory. It includes the district of Wakhan and is bounded to the north by the southern bank of the Oxus river, as established by the Anglo-Russian agreement of 1873. The province's geography is characterized by the Hindu Kush range to the south and the Oxus to the north. Its interior is mountainous, with altitudes ranging from 1,100 feet at Kunduz to an average of 15,000 feet on the plateau, sloping down to 4,000 feet at Faizabad.

## Geography and Administration
The province is divided into several chief provincial subdivisions. In the west are Rustak, Kataghan, Ghori, Narin, and Anderab; the north contains Darwaz, Ragh, and Shiwa; the east comprises Charan, Ishkashim, Zebak, and Wakhan; and the center includes Faizabad, Farkhar, Minjan, and Kishm. 

The region is drained by the Kunduz and Kokcha rivers. The Kokcha river, which flows from south-east to north-west, provides a high road across Badakshan from the Oxus through Faizabad to Zebak. From Ishkashim, the Panja leads through the Pamirs, forming part of a major continental trade route connecting Afghan Turkestan with China and Kashgar.

## Ancient and Medieval History
Badakshan was formerly part of Greek Bactria. The broader region of Bactria, situated between the Oxus and the Hindu Kush with its capital at Bactra, was originally a satrapy of the Persian empire under Cyrus. It later became a province of the Macedonian empire following the conquests of Alexander. Under the Seleucids, Greek towns were established and the Greek language became dominant. Around 255 B.C., Diodotus founded the Graeco-Bactrian kingdom.

The Greek rule was eventually overthrown. Mongolian tribes, including the Tochari (identified by the Chinese as Yue-chi), entered Sogdiana in 159 B.C. and conquered Bactria in 139 B.C. This led to the rise of the "Indo-Scythian" empire and the Kushan dynasty, whose most famous king, Kanishka (ca. 123-153), was a protector of Buddhism. By the 5th century, the Ephthalites (or "white Huns") subjected Bactria, followed by the Turks around A.D. 560. Eventually, the region submitted to the rule of Islam following the Arab overthrow of the Sassanian empire.

In the 7th century, the Chinese pilgrim Hsuan Tsang visited Badakshan in 630 and 644. By the 10th century, Arabian geographers noted the region's large towns, such as Khotl and Waksh, and its mines of lapis lazuli and ruby. In 1272-1273, Marco Polo and his companions stayed in the region.

## Dynastic Rule and Modern History
For several centuries, Badakshan was governed by kings who claimed descent from Alexander the Great. This line ended in the middle of the 15th century with the death of Shah Mahommed, who left only married daughters. In the middle of the 16th century, the Usbegs briefly seized the country before being expelled; subsequently, the region was governed by descendants of the old royal dynasty through the female line.

By the middle of the 18th century, the current dynasty of Mirs replaced the extinct old dynasty. In 1765, the ruler of Kabul invaded and ravaged the country. During the first three decades of the 19th century, the region was depopulated and overrun by Murad Beg and his father Kohan Beg, chiefs of the Kataghan Usbegs of Kunduz. Following the death of Murad Beg, power shifted to another Usbeg, Mahommed Amir Khan.

In 1859, the Kataghan Usbegs were expelled, and Mir Jahander Shah, representing the modern royal line, was reinstated at Faizabad under Afghan supremacy. He was later replaced in 1867 by Abdur Rahman with Mir Mahommed Shah and other family representatives.
