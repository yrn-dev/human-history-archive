# Hephaestus and the Shield of Achilles

## The Fabrication of the Shield
In the *Iliad*, specifically in book XVIII, the narrative describes the creation of new armor for Achilles. Following the death of Patroclus, Thetis, accompanied by the Nereids, promises to obtain this armor from Hephaestus. The process of creating the shield of Achilles is detailed in *Iliad* xviii. 474, where Hephaestus is described as throwing copper, tin, silver, and gold into his furnace to forge the object.

## Metallurgical Implications
The description of Hephaestus's process provides insight into the understanding of metals in the time of the poet. In classical antiquity, bronze typically consisted of copper alloyed with one or more other metals, such as tin, lead, silver, or zinc. While the Greek word *chalkos* was originally the name for pure copper—a usage seen when Homer describes it as *eruthros* (red), *phaennos* (shining), or *aithups* (glittering)—the passage in *Iliad* xviii. 474 suggests that the process of alloying copper with other metals was known to the poet. Because Hephaestus combines copper with tin, gold, and silver, it is not always clear whether the term *chalkos* in Homer's work refers to pure copper or an alloy.

## Other Works of Hephaestus
Beyond the shield of Achilles, Hephaestus is associated with the fabrication of various other items. Under the protection of Thetis, he was employed in creating *daidala* (many ornaments or trinkets), as well as *porpas* and *gnambtas* with spirals (*helixas*). 

## Character and Role in the Iliad
Hephaestus appears in several capacities throughout the *Iliad*. In addition to his role as a divine smith, he is noted for his physical lameness, which is a subject of laughter among the gods in *Iliad* I. His interactions with mortals include his relationship with Dares Phrygius, who is identified in *Iliad* v. 9 as a Trojan priest of Hephaestus. Hephaestus also intervenes directly in the conflict on the plain; for example, he saves Achilles when the Scamander rises against him.
