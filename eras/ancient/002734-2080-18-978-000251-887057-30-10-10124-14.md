# Jamestown

## Overview and Population
Jamestown is a town located within the county of Dumbartonshire. According to population data from 1901, the town had a population of 2,080 residents.

## Regional Context
Jamestown is one of several principal towns in Dumbartonshire. Other towns in the county include Clydebank (21,591), Dumbarton (19,985), Kirkintilloch (11,681), Helensburgh (8,554), Alexandria (8,007), Renton (5,067), Milngavie (3,481), Bonhill (3,333), New Kilpatrick or Bearsden (2,705), and Duntocher (2,122).

The broader population of Dumbartonshire was recorded as 98,014 in 1891 and increased to 113,865 by 1901. Within this 1901 population, 14 individuals spoke only Gaelic, while 3,014 spoke both English and Gaelic.

## Government and Administration
Dumbartonshire is represented by one member in parliament. The county town, Dumbarton, serves as the only royal burgh and is part of the Kilmarnock group of parliamentary burghs. Other municipal and police burghs in the region include Milngavie, Kirkintilloch, Helensburgh, Dumbarton, and Cove and Kilcreggan, as well as Clydebank.

Administratively, the shire is under school-board jurisdiction. It forms a sheriffdom alongside the counties of Clackmannan and Stirling. A resident sheriff-substitute is based in Dumbarton, though this official also sits at Kirkintilloch.

## Education and Funding
While the shire is under school-board jurisdiction, there are several voluntary schools in the area, including St Peter's Roman Catholic College, located in New Kilpatrick. 

The county utilizes a "residue" fund to subsidize technical, art, and science classes. If these funds are insufficient, part of the burgh "residue" may also be used. This same funding source is used to cover the fees and travelling expenses for county students attending Glasgow Technical College, as well as for agricultural lectures.
