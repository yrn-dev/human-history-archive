## Irish Political Conflict (1641–1691)
During the reigns of Charles I, James II, and William and Mary, Irish parties were involved in English political conflicts. In 1641, Irish Catholics carried out a massacre of the English in Ireland, an event that included the killing of women and small children. This was later avenged by Cromwell, who showed no quarter to men found under arms.

Between 1689 and 1691, Ireland experienced another civil war. James II sought support from Irish Catholics against William III. The adherents of James II suffered defeats at the battles of the Boyne in 1690 and Aughrim in 1691. This period concluded with the Treaty of Limerick; however, the English Government failed to keep its promises regarding tolerance for Catholics, leaving a lasting memory of embitterment in Ireland.
