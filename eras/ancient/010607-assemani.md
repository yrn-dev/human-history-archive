# ASSEMANI

Assemani is the name of a Syrian Maronite family noted for their work as Orientalists.

## Contributions to the Study of Ephraim
The family's scholarly work includes the editing of biographies of Ephraim. J.S. Assemani edited a portion of one recension of Ephraim's biography. A second recension was edited in full by S.E. Assemani, appearing in the Roman edition of Ephraim's works (iii. pp. xxiii.-lxiii.).

## The Bibliotheca Orientalis
The *Bibliotheca Orientalis* is a work by Assemani used as a reference for the history of early Christianity. It is cited as a source regarding the presence of a flourishing church in Persia during the time of Constantine.

## Documentation of Eastern Churches
The writings of Assemani provide significant data on various Eastern Christian denominations and their geographical spread:

*   **The Nestorian Church:** Assemani's work (vol. iii. pt. 2) is cited in relation to the Nestorian Church, which maintained a chain of Metropolitans and Bishops extending from Jerusalem to Peking. Specifically, Assemani's records (II. 455–456) mention that Kamul served as the see of a Nestorian bishop, noting the presence of a Bishop of Kamul at the inauguration of the Catholicos Denha in 1266.
*   **The Jacobites:** Assemani's second volume is used as a source for information on the Jacobites (also known as Jacobins). This group, which followed the doctrine of Monophysitism, spread across the East as far as Sístán under the Sassanian Kings.

## Other Historical and Traditional Records
Assemani's works are referenced in the documentation of various traditions and legends:

*   **Christian Traditions:** Assemani (III. pt. 2, 750) is cited regarding a tradition that the Star descended into a well located between Bethlehem and Jerusalem.
*   **Monastic Legends:** In volume ii, Assemani records a story concerning a monastery in Armenia called St. Brassamus. The account claims the monastery housed 300 monks and possessed defenses that would move automatically to shoot back at any enemy attackers.
*   **Mongol History:** Assemani (III. pt. ii. 107) is cited in discussions regarding the religious policies of Kúblái, the grandson of Chinghiz.
