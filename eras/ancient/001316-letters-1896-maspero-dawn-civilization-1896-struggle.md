# G. Maspero and Ancient Civilizations

Gaston Maspero was a prolific author and scholar whose works focused extensively on the history and development of ancient civilizations in the Near East and Egypt. His research contributed to the understanding of early dynastic periods, the evolution of religious structures, and the political struggles of ancient empires.

## Contributions to Ancient History and Chronology
Maspero's writings are cited as authorities on the history of the ancient Orient. His work *Dawn of Civilization* (1892/1896) provides detailed analysis on various regions, including Lydian history, specifically covering pages 232-301. He also authored *Passing of the Empires* (1900) and *Struggle of the Nations* (1897). 

In the study of early dynastic chronologies, Maspero provided specific date ranges for the first three dynasties. According to his 1897 records, Dynasty I spanned from 2416 to 2082 B.C., Dynasty II from 2082 to 1714 B.C., and Dynasty III from 1714 to 1137 B.C. His broader historical research is further documented in *Histoire ancienne des peuples de l'Orient classique*, tome ii.

## Analysis of Ancient Egypt
Maspero’s research into Egypt explored the intersections of religion and state power. In *New Light on Ancient Egypt*, he detailed the conflict between the palace and the temple, specifically the struggle of Pharaoh Amenophis IV against the priesthoods. Maspero highlighted the role of the priests of Ammon Ra, the Lord of Karnak, in this conflict. 

Maspero proposed that the origins of this quarrel may have begun with the priests' opposition to and annoyance of Tii, a beautiful Syrian subject and the mother of Amenophis IV. Maspero suggests that Tii may have inspired her son's fanatical hatred of Ammon Ra. This conflict eventually led Amenophis IV to close Egyptian and Syrian temples, end sectarian worship, and establish the worship of the solar disk, Aton. As part of this transition, the Pharaoh moved his capital from Thebes to Tel Amarna and changed his name to Akhnaton.

## Research on Other Civilizations
Beyond Egypt, Maspero's work touched upon the broader "Orient classique." His writings are referenced in discussions regarding the early history of Babylonia and Assyria, alongside other scholars such as Winckler and Rogers. His research in *Dawn of Civilization* also includes illustrations and detailed discussions on ancient Egyptian religion and mythology.
