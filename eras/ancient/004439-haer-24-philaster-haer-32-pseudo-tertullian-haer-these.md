# Basilides and the Gnostic Tradition

Basilides was a prominent exponent of Gnosticism who lived in Alexandria, likely during the first decades of the 2nd century. While Eusebius's *Chronicle* dates his first appearance to A.D. 133, other evidence suggests his activity may have begun earlier. Agrippa Castor, who lived during the reign of Hadrian (117-138), wrote a polemic against Basilides, indicating his presence prior to 138.

## Writings and Literature
Basilides authored an exegetical work consisting of twenty-four books based on a gospel of his own, though the specific identity of this gospel remains unknown. Further literary contributions associated with his school include:
*   *Ethika*
*   *Exegetika* on the prophet Parchor
*   Writings by his son, Isidorus, titled *Peri prosphuous psuches*

Fragments of these works have been collected and commented upon in Hilgenfeld's *Ketzergeschichte*. A significant fragment from the 13th book of the *Exegetika* was published by L. Traube in 1903, having previously appeared in the *Acta Archelai et Manetis*.

## Historical Accounts of Basilidian Thought
The reconstruction of Basilides' system of thought relies on several early sources, though these accounts often differ. Irenaeus provided a sketch of the school, which may have been derived from the *Syntagma* of Justin. This account is closely related to the one found in the *Syntagma* of Hippolytus, which is preserved in the works of Philaster (*Haer.* 32), Pseudo-Tertullian (*Haer.* 4), and Epiphanius (*Haer.* 24). These descriptions are further supported by various scattered notices found in the *Stromateis* of Clemens Alexandrinus.

In contrast, the *Philosophumena* of Hippolytus presents an essentially different account characterized by a pronounced monistic tendency. There has been a long controversy regarding which of these accounts—the one in the *Philosophumena* or the one shared by Irenaeus and the *Syntagma*—represents the original system of Basilides. Recent scholarly opinion tends to favor the latter, suggesting that in the *Philosophumena*, Hippolytus relied on a Gnostic author's compendium that introduced additions into the systems described.

## Religious Practices and Observances
The Basilidian Gnostics are noted for their specific calendar observances. According to Clement of Alexandria, they celebrated January 6th, though for them, this date served specifically as a celebration of the Baptism.
