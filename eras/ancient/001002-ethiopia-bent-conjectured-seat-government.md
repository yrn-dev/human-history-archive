# ETHIOPIA

## Ancient Origins and Early History
According to historical accounts, Ethiopia was settled by Chus, who was one of the four children of Cham, the second son of Noah. Following the dispersal of Noah's family after the attempt to build the tower of Babel, Cham retired to Africa, and his son Chus established himself in Ethiopia.

In later antiquity, Ethiopia played a role in the politics of neighboring Egypt. Sabachus, a king of Ethiopia, entered Egypt with a large army after being encouraged by an oracle. During his reign in Egypt, Sabachus was noted for his clemency and justice; rather than executing criminals sentenced to death, he required them to repair the causeways of their respective cities. He also constructed several magnificent temples, including one in the city of Bubastus. After reigning for fifty years—the period specified by the oracle—Sabachus voluntarily returned to his kingdom of Ethiopia, restoring the throne to Anysis. It is believed that Sabachus may be the same person as So, who was sought for aid by Hoshea, king of Israel, in a conflict against Shalmanezer, king of Assyria.

## The City of Axum
Axum (also written as Aksum) is an ancient city located in the province of Tigre, Abyssinia, situated at an altitude of 7,226 feet. The city is mentioned in the *Periplus Maris Erythraei* (c. A.D. 67), which describes it as the emporium for ivory brought from the west and the seat of a kingdom. 

The seat of government in Axum has been the subject of historical conjecture. J. T. Bent conjectured that the seat of government was transferred to Axum from Jeha, a location he identified as the ancient Ava. Later, a document quoted by Achille Raffray suggests that the third Christian monarch transferred the seat of government from Axum to Lalibela. Despite this possible transference, the tradition of crowning Abyssinian kings at Axum persisted into the modern era, with King John being crowned there as late as 1871 or 1872.

A. B. Wylde has conjectured that Axum eventually became unsuitable as a royal seat because it acquired the status of a sacred city. This sanctity provided sanctuary to political offenders and criminals within the chief church and the surrounding area, where houses existed to lodge and entertain such persons. Additionally, this sacred status allowed the city to serve as a depository for various goods during times of danger, with the chief church functioning as a type of bank.

The modern town of Axum consists of fewer than a thousand houses and is believed to occupy only a small part of the original ancient city. While the current town lies in a valley or kloof, evidence suggests the old town was built on the western ridge, where traces of well-dressed stones are more frequent.

## International Relations and Trade
Ethiopia appears in various ancient records regarding its interactions with other powers. Josephus mentions the Queen of Ethiopia in the context of her being entertained by Solomon during the fortification of Jerusalem. However, other sources suggest a distinction regarding the Queen of Sheba, noting that she may have been a queen of Sabea in South Arabia rather than of Ethiopia and Egypt.

Additionally, Ethiopia is cited in records concerning the construction of immense edifices in Egypt. It is noted that for a period of ten complete years, stones were hewn in either Arabia or Ethiopia and conveyed to Egypt to be used in the building of a massive pyramid.
