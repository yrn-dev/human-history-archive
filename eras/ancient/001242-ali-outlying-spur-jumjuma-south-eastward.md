## Religious Traditions and the Ali-Illahi
In the regions of Persia and Turkey, specifically within the recesses of the Zagros to the south and the Dersim to the north, there are large half-pagan communities known as the Kizjil-bash or Ali-Illahi. These groups outwardly profess to be Shi'ites, or "followers of Ali."

The Ali-Illahi hold esoteric doctrines and observe secret ceremonies. A central tenet of their belief system is the requirement that a visible manifestation of the Deity must always exist on earth. This manifests in the following ways:
* **Reverence for Incarnations:** They pay reverence to supposed incarnations from ancient times, including Ali, his tutor Salman-ul-Farisi, Moses, David, Christ, and various saints and imams of the Shi'ite tradition.
* **Living Representatives:** Within every Ali-Illahi community, there is a living personage who represents the godhead. These individuals are not necessarily ascetics, yet they receive almost idolatrous honors from the tribesmen.
* **Shrines:** The communities often worship and make vows at the shrines of recent local celebrities. An example is the shrine of Baba Yadgar, located in a gorge of the hills above the old city of Holwan, which is held in supreme veneration by the Gurans of the south.

These institutions are noted as being comparable to the Nosairis and Druses in Syria, as well as the Ismailites in Persia.

## Monuments and Cities
The archive records the existence of the tomb of Ali, which has been enriched with the spoils of the people by various tyrants of Persia between A.D. 977 (Adhad el Dowlat) and A.D. 1743 (Nadir Shah). The tomb features a copper dome with a bright, massy gilding that is visible from several miles away.

Additionally, the archive mentions the city of Meshed Ali. This city is located five or six miles from the ruins of Cufa and 120 miles to the south of Bagdad. In terms of size and form, it is compared to the modern city of Jerusalem.
