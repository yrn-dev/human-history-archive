# Metcalfe and the Alexander Book

The scholarly discourse surrounding the *Alexander Book* and its assumed relationships involves several key researchers and publications. Analysis of these relationships is detailed in the work of G. Neilson, specifically in *John Barbour, Poet and Translator* (1900), which was reprinted from the *Transactions of the Philological Society*. Further examination of these assumed relationships is found in the "Postscript" (pages 156-171) written by J. T. T. Brown. Additionally, the topic was discussed in the *Athenaeum* in issues dated November 17th, December 1st, and December 8th of 1900, as well as the issue from December 9th.

## W. M. Metcalfe and the Legends of the Saints

W. M. Metcalfe is noted for his work regarding the *Legends of the Saints in the Scottish Dialect of the Fourteenth Century*. Published in three volumes by the Scottish Text Society in 1896, Metcalfe's edition serves as a point of scholarly contention. Specifically, Metcalfe disputes the claim that these legends were authored by Barbour. This position contrasts with that of C. Horstmann, who upholds Barbour's authorship and published the text in *Barbours des schottischen Nationaldichters Legendensammlung nebst den Fragmenten seines Trojanerkrieges* (1881-1882), as well as the legend of St Machor in *Altenglische Legenden. Neue Folge* (1881).

Other scholars who have contributed to the discussion regarding these legends include Skeat, Koppel, and P. Buss, the latter of whom wrote *Sind die von Horstmann herausgegebenen schottischen Legenden ein Werk Barberes?* in 1886.

## Gospel-Story Evidence

In addition to his work on the *Legends of the Saints*, W. M. Metcalfe provided evidence concerning the Gospel-story. This evidence is located in volume I, section xxix of his aforementioned work, *Legends of the Saints in the Scottish Dialect of the Fourteenth Century*.

## Related Literary Works and Authorship

The broader context of these studies often intersects with other works attributed to or associated with Barbour. These include:

*   **The Brus**: This work has seen various editions, including those by J. Pinkerton (1790), Jamieson (1820), Cosmo Innes (1856), and W. W. Skeat (1870-1889), the latter of which was reprinted by the Scottish Text Society between 1893 and 1895. J. T. T. Brown also analyzed the recension of Barbour's text in *The Wallace and The Bruce restudied* (1900).
*   **Troy Fragments**: These fragments have been printed by C. Horstmann in his *Legendensammlung*. Other researchers who have examined these fragments include Bradshaw, Skeat, Koeppel, Panton, Donaldson, G. Neilson, and J. T. T. Brown.
