## Administrative and Industrial Records of Konia
Konia is described as a vilayet in Asia Minor. Formed in 1864, it was created by adding the western half of Adana and part of south-eastern Anadoli to the old eyalet of Karamania. The vilayet is divided into five sanjaks: Nigdeh, Konia, Hamid-abad, Buldur, and Adalia.

The population consists of 990,000 Moslems and 80,000 Christians, primarily engaged in pastoral and agricultural work. Industrial activity is limited to the manufacture of cotton and silk stuffs, as well as carpet-weaving. The region possesses mines of rock salt, cinnabar, mercury, chrome, and argentiferous lead. Its principal imports include petroleum, rice, coffee, and cloth-goods, while its primary exports are livestock, wool, cereals, cotton, opium, minerals, and salt. The area is served by the Anatolian railway and contains the railhead of the Ottoman line from Smyrna.

## Topography of the Nan-shan Mountains
The Nan-shan mountains in the south-west are characterized by broad plains and lakes, with short irregular chains. These plains differ slightly in altitude from Tsaidam, ranging between 8,800 and 9,000 feet. A higher terrace exists at an altitude of 12,000 to 13,500 feet.

Ritter's range is the first mountain range on this higher terrace and is partially covered by extensive snow-fields; its passes are located at altitudes of 14,680 feet and 15,990 feet. The Humboldt (or Ama-surgu) range runs from the Astin-tagh in a north-west to south-east direction to approximately 38° N. This range may continue as the southern Kuku (Koko)-nor range, which reaches the Hwang-ho at an elevation of 7,440 feet. Other parallel ranges in this area include the Suess, Semenov, Mushketov, Bain-sarlyk, and Alexander III.
