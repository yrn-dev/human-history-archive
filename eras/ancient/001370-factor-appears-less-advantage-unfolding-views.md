# Views on Life After Death in Babylonian-Assyrian History

## Conceptions of the Afterlife
In the history of the Babylonian-Assyrian periods, certain factors were less advantageous in the unfolding of views regarding life after death. The prevailing conception during these eras was that all dead individuals were gathered in a large, dark cavern located below the earth. This cavern was situated not far from the Apsu, which is described as the ocean that flows underneath and encircles the earth. Within this subterranean space, the dead led a miserable existence characterized by gloom, dust, and inactivity.

## Exceptions and Divine Grace
While the general fate of the dead was one of misery, there were exceptions based on divine grace. Occasionally, a favored individual was permitted to escape the general condition of the dark cavern and was instead placed on a pleasant island. 

Rulers were consistently singled out for such grace. In earlier historical periods, it was believed that rulers possessed a closer proximity to the gods than other mortals. Consequently, kings were deified after their death. In some instances, these divine honors were extended to the kings even during their own lifetimes.

## The Role of Astral Theology
The broader religious framework of Babylonia and Assyria was heavily influenced by astral theology, which served as a theoretical substratum and helped maintain religious rites in a stable condition. This system was a combination of speculation from temple schools and popular beliefs. The central assumption of astral theology was a close link between earthly occurrences and the movements of heavenly bodies.

This system began with the personification of the moon and the sun (Sin and Shamash). Over time, other deities were assigned seats in the heavens, identifying them with planets and stars. By the post-Khammurabic period, this process culminated in the identification of specific deities with specific planets:
*   **Marduk** was identified with Jupiter.
*   **Ishtar** was identified with Venus.
*   **Nergal** was identified with Mars.
*   **Nebo** was identified with Mercury.
*   **Ninib** was identified with Saturn.

This theological structure was rooted in the popular observation that life, guidance, and vegetation depended upon the two great luminaries, leading priests to develop a theory of close correspondence between celestial phenomena and events on earth.
