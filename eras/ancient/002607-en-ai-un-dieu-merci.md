## Related Concepts of Divinity in French History
While the refrain "J'en ai un, Dieu merci" represents a lighthearted expression of gratitude, other French historical and legal texts address the concept of God through different lenses:

### Legal and Criminal Jurisprudence
In French criminal jurisprudence, as detailed in Jousse's *Traité de la Justice Criminelle* (1771), there is a category of crime known as *Leze-Majesté Divine*. These are defined as the most exécrable and atrocious crimes because they attack God immediately. Such offenses include:
* Denying the existence of God.
* Profaning or treading upon holy Hosties.
* Striking images of God with the intent to insult.
* Mutilating or knocking down images consecrated to the Saints, the Holy Virgin, or God.
* Using sacred things for common or bad purposes in derision of the Mysteries.
* Profaning baptismal fonts or the holy Eucharist.

### Legal Philosophy
The French jurisconsult Jean Domat (1625–1696), who was intimate with Pascal and sympathetic to the Port-Royalists, sought to base all law upon religious or ethical principles. His guiding motto was "*L'homme est fait par Dieu et pour Dieu*" (Man is made by God and for God). Domat is primarily recognized for his legal digest, *Lois civiles dans leur ordre naturel* (1689), for which Louis XIV provided a pension of 2000 livres.
