# Septimius Severus

Septimius Severus was a Roman emperor who reigned from A.D. 193 to 211. A Carthaginian by origin, he is identified as one of the few able men to lead the empire during a century of disorder that followed the death of Marcus Aurelius. This era was characterized by the crumbling of frontiers under barbarian pressure and the rule of various inadequate emperors and adventurers.

## Rise to Power and Civil War
Severus emerged as a claimant to the empire while stationed in Pannonia. He declared himself against the murderers of Pertinax, entering a period of civil war against three rivals: Pescennius Niger in Syria, Clodius Albinus in Britain, and Didius Julianus, who had acquired the empire through a public sale by the Praetorian Guards. Severus eventually achieved victory over these rivals.

## Administration of Britain
During his time in Britain, Septimius Severus implemented significant administrative and military changes. He divided the province into two separate entities: Britain Superior and Britain Inferior. The boundary between these two provinces likely ran from the Mersey to the Humber, though the duration of this specific arrangement is unknown.

In terms of military infrastructure, Severus ordered the construction of a stone wall built in the same locality and parallel to the existing rampart of Hadrian. Additionally, the fort at Housesteads, which measures approximately 360 by 600 feet and covers slightly less than 5 acres, may date from his tenure. This fort featured stone ramparts—the northernmost of which coincided with Hadrian's wall—and an interior consisting of stone buildings, including barracks and a central headquarters known as the *Principia*.

## Activities in Africa and the East
Severus's ties to his Carthaginian roots were evident in his personal life; his sister conducted her Roman household in the Punic language, as she was never able to master Latin. In Africa, the inhabitants continued to pay a special tax for the Zaghwan aqueduct—a massive work that delivered over seven million gallons of water daily to the Mapalia reservoirs—during the reign of Severus, a fact noted on the coin-types of the emperor.

In Heliopolis, Severus dedicated two temples. The larger temple was dedicated to Jupiter (Baal), who was identified with the Sun and associated with Mercury and Venus. The smaller temple was built in honor of Bacchus. To commemorate the dedication of these sanctuaries, Severus conferred the *jus Italicum* upon the city.

## Legacy and Historical Record
The reign of Septimius Severus is associated with the first instance of a specific event recorded by Dion Cassius. In the field of cartography, a plan of Rome dating from his reign (A.D. 193-211) is one of only two surviving specimens of Roman cartography, currently housed in the Museo Capitolino.
