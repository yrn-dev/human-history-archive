# Orientgesellschaft

The *Orientgesellschaft* is an organization that dispatched a large-scale German expedition in 1899 for the purpose of exploring the ruins of Babylon. The work of this society has contributed significantly to the modern understanding of the topography of the site.

## Excavations at Babylon
The excavations conducted by the *Deutsche Orientgesellschaft* began in 1899. Through these efforts, the great processional road and the palace of Nebuchadrezzar were laid bare. The resulting knowledge of the area's topography is derived from these excavations, as well as from the inscriptions of Nebuchadrezzar and the writings of classical authors.

The topography identified is specifically that of the Babylon of Nebuchadrezzar, as the older Babylon—which was destroyed by Sennacherib—has left few or no traces behind.

## Topography of the Site
The existing remains of the city are primarily located on the east bank of the Euphrates. The principal remains consist of three vast mounds:
*   **Babil**: Located to the north.
*   **Qasr** (also known as the **Mujelliba** or "Palace"): Located in the center.
*   **Ish[=a]n 'Amran ibn 'Ali**: Located to the south, featuring the outlying spur of the Jumjuma.

Further to the east is the Ish[=a]n el-Aswad, referred to as the "Black Mound," along with three lines of rampart. One of these ramparts encloses the Babil mound on its north and east sides, while a third rampart forms a triangle with the southeast angle of the other two. On the west side of the Euphrates, there are additional ramparts and the remains of ancient Borsippa.

## Related German Expeditions
The *Orientgesellschaft* expedition was part of a broader pattern of German archaeological exploration in the region. Prior to the 1899 Babylon expedition, a German expedition led by Dr. Koldewey explored the cemetery of El Hibba (located immediately south of Tello) between 1886 and 1887, providing the first information regarding the burial customs of ancient Babylonia. Additionally, Dr. W. Andrae subsequently conducted excavations at Qal`at Sherqat, which is the site of Assur.
