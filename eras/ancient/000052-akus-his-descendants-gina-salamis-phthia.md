# ÆAKUS AND HIS DESCENDANTS: ÆGINA, SALAMIS, AND PHTHIA

The heroic genealogy of the Æakids establishes a fabulous connection between the regions of Ægina, Salamis, and Phthia. While the origin of this connection cannot be traced, it is recognized as a fact within the genealogical records of the archive.

## Æakus and His Immediate Offspring
Æakus was the son of Zeus and Ægina. He is credited with offering prayers that procured relief for Greece. Æakus had three sons: Pêleus, Telamôn, and Phôkus. The family history is marked by internal conflict, as Phôkus was killed by his brothers, Pêleus and Telamôn. Following these events, Telamôn was banished and traveled to Salamis, while Pêleus went to Phthia.

## The Line of Pêleus and Phthia
In Phthia, Pêleus married Thetis. Their lineage produced Achilles, who led the Myrmidons from Phthia and Hellas during the expedition to Troy, commanding a force of 50 ships. The descendants of Achilles continued to exert influence in other regions; his son, Neoptolemus, led an army into Epirus, where he obtained possession of the country and founded a dynasty of kings known as the sons of Pyrrhus (Pyrrhus being Neoptolemus's childhood nickname). In Epirus, Achilles is honored like a god and referred to as Aspetus in the local dialect.

Phthia itself has deep legendary roots. Deukaliôn, who was saved during the general deluge and is regarded as the father of Hellên (the eponym of the Hellenic race), eventually settled in Thessaly and ruled the area called Phthiôtis. Some accounts suggest he arrived there by conducting a body of Leleges and Kurêtes into Thessaly, expelling the Pelasgian occupants. The lineage of Deukaliôn is said to have continued in Phthia until the time of Dikæarchus.

## The Line of Telamôn and Salamis
Telamôn settled in Salamis, and his son was Ajax. During the Trojan War, Ajax of Salamis contributed 12 ships to the Greek force assembled at Aulis. Another son of Telamôn, Teukrus, survived the siege of Troy and the voyage home to reach Salamis. However, Telamôn refused to receive him because he had returned without Ajax. 

Consequently, Teukrus was compelled to expatriate and led his followers to Cyprus, where he founded the city of Salamis. The Æakid genealogy diffused through this line; for example, Evagoras was recognized as both a Teukrid and an Æakid during the time of Isokratês.

## Genealogical Disputes and Variations
The archive notes inconsistencies regarding the Æakid lineage. While generally listed as brothers, Apollodôrus records that Pherekydês claimed Telamôn was not the son of Æakus, but rather only a friend of Pêleus. Additionally, a dispute existed between the Megarians and the Athenians regarding the title to the hero Ajax. The Megarians accused Peisistratus of interpolating a line into the Catalogue of the Iliad to support the Athenian claim.

## Related Regional Dynasties
The broader genealogical landscape of the era includes various eponyms. Hellên is described as having offspring including Dôrus, Æolus, and Xuthus. Æolus reigned over regions near Thessaly, while Dôrus occupied territory north of the Corinthian Gulf. Xuthus, through his marriage to Kreüsa (daughter of the indigenous hero Erechtheus), produced Achæus and Iôn, who became founders of their own separate peoples in the Peloponnêsus and Attica.
