# Treaties and Diplomatic Agreements in World History

Throughout human history, treaties have served as formal instruments to resolve conflicts, establish alliances, and define the boundaries of imperial and national power. These agreements range from bilateral peace pacts and defensive alliances to complex international settlements following global conflicts.

## Ancient Greek Alliances and Conventions
In ancient Greece, treaties often created intricate and overlapping networks of association between city-states. One such convention followed hostilities between Athens and Persia; this agreement largely recognized the existing state of affairs without introducing new advantages or disadvantages for either side. While later historians exaggerated the hard conditions imposed on the Great King, the contemporary historian Thucydidês largely ignored the event, suggesting the treaty was of little moment compared to the actual state of Athenian empire.

Later, a complex series of alliances emerged involving Athens, Sparta, and other states. A defensive alliance was concluded between Athens and Sparta for a period of fifty years. Under this treaty, both parties pledged to treat any invaders of the other's territory as enemies and to assist in repelling them. A critical provision for the security of Lacedæmon required the Athenians to provide energetic aid in suppressing any rising of the Helots in Laconia. 

However, these agreements often led to further complication. For example, the oligarchical party in Argos used a treaty as a preface for ulterior measures, eventually persuading the Argeian assembly to renounce its alliance with Athens, Elis, and Mantineia in favor of a special alliance with Sparta. This created a fragmented diplomatic landscape where Athens and Sparta maintained a formal alliance despite Athenian complaints that the treaty had not been fulfilled, while other states like Corinth and Bœotia navigated separate, sometimes secret, agreements.

## Persian and East Asian Diplomacy
In the Persian Empire, Bahr[=a]m V (who reigned from 420-439) ascended to the crown with the assistance of al-Mondhir, the Arabic dynast of Hira. Bahr[=a]m V initiated a systematic persecution of Christians, which resulted in war with the Roman Empire. Following a period of limited success, Bahr[=a]m V concluded a treaty in which both the Persian and Roman empires promised toleration for the worshippers of Zoroastrianism and Christianity.

In more recent history, treaties have been used to settle specific administrative and financial disputes between nations. An agreement between the governments of Japan and Russia detailed the repayment of expenditures incurred for the care and maintenance of prisoners. This treaty required ratification by the Emperor of Japan and the Emperor of all the Russias, with announcements made through the Ambassador of the United States in Saint Petersburg and the French Minister in Tokio. To ensure clarity, the treaty was signed in both English and French, with the French text prevailing in cases of interpretative discrepancy.

## Colonial and Imperial Agreements in Africa
In the 19th century, European powers utilized treaties and protectorates to secure influence in Africa, particularly regarding the kingdom of Dahomey. Under King Glegle, Dahomey's involvement in the slave trade and persecution of native Christians led to tensions with France and Great Britain. In 1861, England annexed the island of Lagos to check the king's aggressive spirit. 

Diplomatic maneuvers continued as the kingdom of Porto Novo accepted a French protectorate in 1863, with boundaries fixed by an Anglo-French agreement in 1864. Later, an arrangement in 1885 confined the German protectorate in Guinea to Togo, with the exception of the town of Little Popo. Portugal also attempted to assume a protectorate over the Dahomeyan coast in 1886, though France induced their withdrawal by December 1887. Finally, an Anglo-French agreement in 1889 resulted in the surrender of Kotonu by Great Britain to France, based on treaties France had concluded with Glegle in 1868 and 1878.

## Modern International Settlements
The conclusion of the Great War saw the implementation of the Treaty of Versailles. While intended to reconstruct the world, the treaty has been viewed as a reminder of national and imperial greed, failing to settle the underlying conflicts that led to the war. The conflict shattered the imperialism of Russia and removed several monarchies and the threat of German imperialism from Europe.

Under the terms of peace and additional conventions, Germany agreed to recognize the decisions regarding the territories of Turkey, Bulgaria, and Austria-Hungary, as well as the validity of treaties concluded by allied and associated powers. Germany further accepted all decrees made by allied or associated prize courts regarding German goods and ships and agreed not to pursue pecuniary claims against signing powers for events occurring before the treaty took effect. Similarly, a preliminary treaty with Austria required the acceptance of the labor charter and the covenant of the League of Nations, alongside the renunciation of extra-European rights.
