# The Aye-Aye

The aye-aye (_Chiromys_ or _Daubentonia madagascariensis_) is described as the most aberrant and remarkable of all the Malagasy lemurs. It is a nocturnal animal that lives either alone or in pairs, primarily inhabiting bamboo forests.

## Physical Characteristics
The aye-aye possesses a superficial resemblance to a dusky-coloured, long-haired cat with unusually large, naked eyes. Its physical structure is characterized by a broad, rounded head and a short face. The animal has large hands with long, thin fingers ending in pointed claws; the third finger is specifically noted for its extreme slenderness. 

The feet of the aye-aye are similar to those of other lemurs, featuring a large opposable great toe with a flat nail, while the remaining toes possess compressed, pointed claws. It has a long, bushy tail and general coloration of dark brown. Its fur consists of a long, rather loose outer layer and a woolly under-coat. The animal has two teats located in the inguinal position.

## Discovery and Captivity
The species was discovered in 1780 by Pierre Sonnerat. The specimen he brought to Paris remained the only one known until 1860, after which many more were obtained. One specimen lived for several years within the gardens of the Zoological Society of London.

## Diet and Behavior
Observations conducted upon captive specimens have led to the conclusion that the aye-aye feeds principally on juices, with a particular preference for sugar-cane. To access these juices, the animal uses its strong incisor teeth to tear open the hard, woody circumference of the cane stalk. 

Additionally, the aye-aye is said to devour certain species of wood-boring caterpillars. It obtains these by using its teeth to cut into their burrows and subsequently picking the insects out of their retreat using the claw of its attenuated middle finger.

## Habitat and Nesting
The aye-aye constructs large, ball-like nests made of dried leaves. These nests are lodged in the fork of the branches of large trees and are designed with an opening on one side.
