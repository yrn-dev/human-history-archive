# Augustus

## Religious Revivals and the Arval Brothers
Augustus was responsible for the revival of the Arval Brothers (*Fratres Arvales*), a priesthood or college that had languished in obscurity during the period of the Roman republic. This college, which was generally believed to have been founded by Romulus, consisted of twelve members elected for life from the highest ranks in Rome. During the empire, the emperor was apparently always included in the membership.

The primary duty of the Arval Brothers was to offer public sacrifices annually to ensure the fertility of the fields. The college conducted a great annual festival in May lasting three days in honor of the anonymous Dea Dia, who was likely identical to Ceres. In the time of Augustus, the organizational structure of the college included:
* A master (*magister*)
* A vice-master (*promagister*)
* A *flamen*
* A *praetor*
* Eight ordinary members

These officials were attended by various servants, specifically including four chorus boys who were the sons of senators and had both parents alive. Members of the college wore a white fillet, a wreath of corn, and the *praetexta*. The president and a *flamen* were elected for one year, and new members were chosen via co-optation based on the president's motion.

## Restoration of the Lupercal
Augustus also undertook the rebuilding of the Lupercal, the cave of Lupercus, which had fallen into a state of decay. The Lupercal was located on the western side of the Palatine hill, close to the Casa Romuli and the Ficus Ruminalis, in front of the Porta Romana. According to legend, this cave was the site where the she-wolf suckled the twins Romulus and Remus. 

The cave was associated with the Lupercalia, an ancient pastoral festival held on February 15th in honor of Lupercus. The festival was overseen by a corporation of priests known as the Luperci. The rites involved the sacrifice of a dog and goats, followed by a ritual where two young men had their foreheads touched with a bloody knife and the blood wiped away with wool dipped in milk. Following a sacrificial feast, the Luperci ran in two bands around the walls of the old Palatine city, striking people with thongs called *februa* cut from the skins of the victims. This act was believed to prevent sterility in women. The overall objective of the festival was to secure the prosperity of the people, the increase of flocks, and the fruitfulness of the land through purification and expiation.
