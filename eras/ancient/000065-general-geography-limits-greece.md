## Physical Features and Terrain
The geography of Greece is characterized by significant irregularity and geological variety. The landscape features the Scardus and Pindus mountains, which extend and disseminate through the Peloponnesus and southern Greece, as well as Ossa and Pelion.

The region's hydrology is marked by several distinct characteristics:
* **Waterways:** The Grecian waters are irregular, and rivers frequently run dry during the summer months.
* **Basins and Drainage:** There are frequent lakes and marshes, and some rivers follow subterranean courses out of land-locked basins.
* **Coastline:** The coastline is heavily indented, which provides universal accessibility by sea.

## Influence of Geography on Society
The physical configuration of Greece had a profound impact on the political and intellectual development of its inhabitants. The difficulty of land transport and communication made sea communication essential, particularly for the colonies and the islands. 

This maritime necessity led to a distinction between the "sea-states" and the "land-states" within Greece. Ancient philosophers observed that these maritime habits and the resulting commerce influenced the political relations and the intellectual growth of the people.
