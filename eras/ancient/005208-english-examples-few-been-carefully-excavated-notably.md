# Roman Fortifications in England

The military presence used to guard or coerce the province of England from the time of Hadrian onwards was composed of three legions and a large, though uncertain, number of auxiliaries. The legions were stationed at Isca Silurum (Caerleon-on-Usk), Eburacum (now York), and Deva (now Chester), totaling approximately 15,000 heavy infantry. The auxiliary troops, categorized as second-grade troops, were organized into cavalry *alae* or infantry cohorts of 500 or 1,000 strong. These auxiliaries were positioned in *castella* located closer to the frontiers than the primary legions.

## Fortress and Castella Architecture

The legionary fortresses were designed as large rectangular enclosures, typically spanning 50 to 60 acres. These sites were enclosed by strong walls; traces of these structures remain visible on the south side of Caerleon, in the abbey gardens at York, and in the lower courses of the north and east town-walls of Chester.

In contrast, the auxiliary *castella* were significantly smaller, generally ranging from three to six acres depending on the size of the regiment and the requirements for stabling. These auxiliary forts were hardly a tenth of the size of the legionary fortresses. While some 20 such forts are known in Scotland, upwards of 70 are known in England.

## Excavated English Examples

A few of the English *castella* have been carefully excavated. Notable examples include:

*   **Gellygaer:** Located between Brecon and Cardiff, this site is considered one of the most perfect specimens of a Roman fort from the end of the 1st century A.D. found anywhere in the Roman empire.
*   **Hardknott:** Situated on a Cumberland moor overhanging Upper Eskdale.
*   **Housesteads:** Located on Hadrian's wall.

## Internal Construction and Planning

The internal arrangements of these forts generally followed a single general plan. However, there were variations in the materials used for internal buildings. In some forts, the buildings were constructed entirely of stone. In others, particularly those that appear to have been built before 150, wood was used freely, with stone reserved for only a few of the principal buildings.
