# BRITANNICUS

Britannicus was a prince of the Roman Empire, the son of the Emperor Claudius and his third wife, Messalina. Born probably in A.D. 41, he was originally named Claudius Tiberius Germanicus. He was later granted the name Britannicus by the senate to commemorate the conquest of Britain, which occurred around the time of his birth.

## Family and Early Life
Britannicus was one of the children born to Claudius and Messalina; he had a sister named Octavia. Claudius had also fathered a daughter, Antonia, by his first wife, Pelina. 

Until the year 48, Britannicus was regarded as the heir presumptive to the Roman throne. However, this status changed following the execution of his mother, Messalina. Claudius subsequently married Agrippina, the daughter of Germanicus and the widow of Domitius Aenobarbus. Agrippina brought her own son from her previous marriage, Lucius Domitius (later known as Nero), into the imperial household.

## Succession and Conflict
Agrippina sought to secure the principality for her own son, fearing that Britannicus would succeed his father in government once he reached man's estate. Through her influence and delusions, she persuaded the "feeble" Emperor Claudius to adopt Lucius Domitius. Following this adoption, Lucius took the name Nero Claudius Cæsar Drusus. To further solidify these ties, Claudius married his daughter Octavia to Nero.

## Death and Legacy
Following the death of Claudius—who had reigned for thirteen years, eight months, and twenty days—Nero ascended to the empire. Agrippina, playing upon Nero's fears, induced him to arrange the death of Britannicus. In A.D. 55, Britannicus was poisoned at a banquet. The poisoning was carried out in a manner intended to keep the act hidden from the multitude.

Despite the circumstances of his death, the Emperor Titus later erected a golden statue in honor of the young prince. In later literary history, Britannicus became the subject of a tragedy written by Racine.
