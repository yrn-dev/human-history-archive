# The Great Pyramid of Giza: Engineering the Eternal

## Overview
The Great Pyramid of Giza, commissioned by the Pharaoh Khufu during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, remains one of the most formidable achievements of human engineering. Built as a monumental tomb to ensure the king's transition to the afterlife, it held the title of the tallest man-made structure in the world for over 3,800 years. 

While the pyramid's scale is awe-inspiring, the primary historical fascination lies in its construction. Composed of approximately 2.3 million limestone and granite blocks, the precision of its alignment to true north is nearly perfect. However, the exact methodology used to lift these massive stones—some weighing up to 80 tons—remains a subject of intense scholarly debate. 

Mainstream Egyptologists generally agree that a massive, organized workforce of skilled laborers (rather than slaves, as previously thought) utilized a system of ramps. The debate persists over whether these were linear ramps, zig-zagging paths, or internal spiral ramps. Recent discoveries of "logbooks" (such as the Diary of Merer) have provided concrete evidence regarding the transport of limestone via boat from Tura to Giza, though the final ascent of the stones remains a mystery.

## Key Facts
*   **Materials:** The core consists of local yellow limestone, while the exterior was originally clad in polished Tura limestone. The King's Chamber is constructed from massive Aswan granite.
*   **Labor Force:** Archaeological evidence from the "Workers' Village" suggests a professional workforce of roughly 20,000–30,000 men who were well-fed and provided with medical care.
*   **Precision:** The base is level to within 15 millimeters, and the sides are aligned almost exactly with the cardinal points of the compass.
*   **Internal Structure:** Unlike most pyramids, it contains a complex system of ascending and descending passages, including the Grand Gallery and three known chambers.

## Did You Know?
1. **Not Built by Slaves:** Excavations of the workers' cemetery revealed that the builders were respected laborers who were buried near the pyramid, a privilege never granted to slaves.
2. **The Missing Casing:** The pyramid once shimmered in the sun because it was covered in polished white limestone; most of these casing stones were stripped away in the 14th century to build mosques and fortresses in Cairo.
3. **The "Air Shafts":** The small shafts leading from the King and Queen's chambers are often debated; while some call them ventilation, many historians believe they were "star shafts" designed to allow the Pharaoh's soul to ascend to the northern stars.
