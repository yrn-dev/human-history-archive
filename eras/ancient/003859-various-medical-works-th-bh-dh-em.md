# Medical Works and Practitioners

The history of medical practice and the documentation of medical knowledge span various eras, cultures, and specialized fields, ranging from ancient Greek treatises and medieval texts to modern military and correctional healthcare.

## Ancient and Classical Medical Practice
In antiquity, there was an intimate union between medical and surgical practice. However, evidence from the Hippokratic treatises suggests a distinction between the physician and the operator. The medical oath found in these collections explicitly states that the physician should not perform operations, such as lithotomy, but should instead leave such tasks to "operators, or workmen." This stands in contrast to other Hippokratic treatises where the *ἰατρός* is depicted performing various operations, including those requiring mechanical or violent dealing.

The works of Hippokratês also include the *Ἐπιδήμιαι* (Notes of visits to different cities), which consists of remarks on particular patients. These records illustrate the itinerant habits of Grecian *ἰατροί*, who traveled as *Periodeutes* (voyageurs). Their observations covered various regions, including Macedonia, Thrace, and Thessaly, as well as Athens, Corinth, Elis, Salamis, the islands of Delos and Syros, and Œniadæ in Akarnania.

## Medieval and Early Modern Contributions
The scholar Bar-Hebraeus is credited with authoring various medical works, alongside his treatises on logic, grammar, astronomy, and philosophy. In the realm of early modern anatomy, G. Rondelet (1507-1557) utilized a medical education from Paris and a complete course of instruction in anatomy under Guentherus of Andernach to inform his studies of aquatic life. This training is evident in his works *Libri de piscibus marinis* (1554) and *Universae aquatilium historiae pars altera* (1555), which provide detailed accounts of the internal and external parts and the economy of 197 marine and 47 fresh-water fishes.

## Specialized Medical Fields and Terminology
Medical terminology extends into the study of parasites. In both veterinary and medical contexts, the infestation of lice is known as phthiriasis. This includes the Mallophaga (bird-lice), which primarily infest birds, though the genus *Trichodectes* is found on mammalia. Key authorities on this subject include works by Denny, Giebel, van Beneden, Piaget, Mégnin, Neumann, Osborn, and Enderlein.

In the field of therapeutics, homoeopathy emphasized the physiological action of drugs and brought attention to powerful substances such as belladonna, nux vomica, and aconite, often utilizing simpler forms of administration. Despite this, homoeopathy has historically been viewed as a dissenting sect within the medical profession. Some practitioners have joined prominent medical associations in the United States by agreeing to remove the name "homoeopathy" from their hospitals, colleges, dispensaries, asylums, and periodicals.

## Institutional and Military Medical Services
Medical roles have been formalized within various governmental and military structures. In historical Japanese codes, specific sections were dedicated to the duties of medical officers attached to the Court. In more recent military history, the American Medical Corps has been recognized for its sanitary proficiency and general effectiveness in both hospital settings and at the front, employing professionals and devoted women.

In correctional settings, such as the MCC New York, medical care involves a multidisciplinary team including Medical Doctors, Clinical Nurses, and Physician Assistants (Mid-Level Practitioners). Standard intake procedures include medical screenings to record vital signs and medical history, including mental health evaluations to assess for suicidal ideation. Comprehensive care in these environments may include blood panel screenings, dental and eye exams, and the prescription of medications for various health needs.
