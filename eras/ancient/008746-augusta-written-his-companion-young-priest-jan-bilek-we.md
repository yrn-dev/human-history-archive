# Augusta and the Account of His Captivity

## Overview of Bishop Augusta
Augusta was a bishop within the community known as the Bohemian Brethren. This community was indirectly founded by Chelcicky and exerted a significant influence on Bohemian literature. The members of the brotherhood were noted for writing the majority of their theological and historical works in their national language, a practice that served to refine and polish the language.

## Literary Contributions and Conflict
Following the death of Brother Gregory in 1480—who was regarded as the patriarch of the brotherhood and authored works such as *On good and evil priests* and *Letters to Archbishop Rokycan*—the community experienced a period of discord. This internal conflict resulted in a surge of literary activity. During this time, Augusta, along with Brothers Lucas, Blahoslav, and Jaffet, produced numerous controversial works.

## The Account of Captivity
Among the records associated with Bishop Augusta, a particularly interesting work is the account of his captivity. This specific narrative was written by Jan Bilek, a young priest who was a companion of the bishop.

## Preservation of Brotherhood Records
Many of the historical works produced by the Bohemian Brethren, including those of Augusta's contemporaries, were lost. In the 17th century, nearly all anti-Roman books written in Bohemia were destroyed. Consequently, only fragments of certain works survived, such as Blahoslav's *History of the Unity*. Other surviving examples of the brotherhood's historical writing include the biographies of Peter and William of Rosenberg, written by the historian Wenceslas Brezan.
