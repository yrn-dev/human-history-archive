## Mathematical Applications of C
In the study of functions and series, the variable $c$ is utilized in several capacities. In the context of Fourier's theorem, the interval between $-c$ and $c$ is referred to as the "periodic interval." Fourier provided rules for determining the coefficients $a_n$ and $b_n$ for a series representing a given function $f(x)$ within this interval. The coefficients are defined as follows: $a_0$ is the integral of $f(x)dx$ from $-c$ to $c$ multiplied by $1/2c$; $a_n$ is the integral of $f(x)\cos(n\pi x/c)dx$ from $-c$ to $c$ multiplied by $1/c$; and $b_n$ is the integral of $f(x)\sin(n\pi x/c)dx$ from $-c$ to $c$ multiplied by $1/c$. This periodic interval can be replaced by any other interval, such as that between 0 and 1, without restricting generality.

Additionally, $c$ appears in the transformation of points and functions. Specifically, points $\zeta = 0, 1, 1 + c$ can become points $z = 0, 1, \infty$. In this framework, the function $(1 - z)^{-1}$ is expressed as $(1 - z)^{-1} = c(c + 1)^{-1}(1 - \zeta)^{-1} + (c + 1)^{-1}$. The function $U$ becomes a polynomial in $z$, specifically $[(c + 1)/c]H - 1/c$, where $H$ is also a polynomial in $z$. Furthermore, the lines $\eta = \pm a$ are expressed as two circles where, if $z = x + iy$, the equation is $c(c + 1)(x + c)^2 + y^2 = \pm \frac{y}{a}$.
