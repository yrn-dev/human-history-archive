# Flourished (Ekmazen)

In historical and biographical records, the term "flourished" (derived from the Greek *ekmazen*) is used to denote a specific period of a person's life or the peak of a particular cultural movement. Within the archive's records, the term is applied to individuals to indicate their physical prime or professional prominence, and to literary genres to indicate their period of prevalence.

## Application to Individuals

When applied to individuals, the term *ekmazen* specifically refers to the physical prime of a person. In historical calculations, this prime was commonly placed at approximately the fortieth year of life. This terminology allows historians to estimate birth dates when an exact date is unknown.

For example, the Greek lyric poet Bacchylides, born at Iulis on the island of Ceos, is recorded by Eusebius as having "flourished" in Ol. 78. 2 (467 B.C.). Based on the definition of *ekmazen* as the fortieth year, it is supposed that Bacchylides was born circa 507 B.C. This dating places him approximately fifteen years younger than Pindar and forty-nine years younger than his uncle, Simonides. 

This differs from the term *egnorizeto* ("was of repute"), which Eusebius used regarding Bacchylides in Ol. 87. 2 (431 B.C.), and which Georgius Syncellus used in reference to Ol. 88 (428-425 B.C.). While *ekmazen* refers to the physical prime, the phrase "was of repute" indicates a period of fullness in both fame and years.

Other figures in the archive are described as having flourished during specific eras or reigns:
* **Lucius Annaeus Cornutus**: A Stoic philosopher and native of Leptis in Libya who flourished during the reign of Nero.
* **Cornutus (Rhetorician)**: A different individual of the same name who flourished between A.D. 200-250, or possibly in the second half of the 2nd century.
* **Autolycus of Pitane**: A Greek astronomer and mathematician who probably flourished in the second half of the 4th century B.C., a date supported by the fact that he instructed Arcesilaus.

## Application to Literature and Eras

The term is also used to describe the period during which a specific style of art, literature, or a city's power reached its peak.

In Roman literature, the *togata*—a species of play that was more realistic, fresher, and coarser in tone than the *palliata*—flourished between 170 and 80 B.C. This genre included the *tabernariae* (comedies dealing with the lower classes), such as those written by Titinius, and the more refined works of L. Afranius.

In a civic context, the term is used to describe eras of prosperity. The reign of Battus the Prosperous is noted as marking a flourishing era for the town of Kyrênê, characterized by a large acquisition of land-dominion and an intimate alliance with Amasis, the king of Egypt.
