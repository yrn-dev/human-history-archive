# Oeuvres

In the context of the archive's bibliographic records, the term *Oeuvres* (French for "works") appears frequently in reference to the collected writings of various authors, scholars, and philosophers. These collections often serve as the primary source for academic citations and historical analysis.

## Collections of Honoré de Balzac
The bibliography of Balzac is described as extraordinarily complicated. A comprehensive account of his works can be found in the *Histoire des oeuvres* (published in 1875 and later), which M. Spoelberch de Lovenjoul attached to the *Edition definitive*. Lovenjoul further supplemented this with smaller works such as *Une Page perdue de Balzac* and *Autour de Balzac*. 

Prior to the *Edition definitive* (which began in 1869), Balzac's works were issued in various installments during his lifetime. The earliest *Comedie humaine* appeared between 1842 and 1846 in sixteen volumes. For a period, the most well-known edition consisted of fifty-five small 24mos published by Michel (Calmann) Levy; though referred to by Browning as "all Balzac's novels fifty volumes long," this edition did not follow the author's last disposition and excluded miscellaneous works, though it did include the *Oeuvres de jeunesse*. 

The *Edition definitive* provides the miscellaneous works in four volumes and an invaluable volume of correspondence. In 1893, the *Repertoire des oeuvres de Balzac* was added, featuring detailed tracking of the personages of the *Comedie* by MM. Christophe and Cerfbeer. Additionally, *Oeuvres posthumes* began appearing in 1899, with the first volume containing an installment of *Lettres a l'etrangere*, followed by a second volume in 1906.

## Scientific and Philosophical Works
The term *Oeuvres* is used in the documentation of several influential thinkers and scientists:

*   **Bacon:** The classification of sciences adopted by the great French *Encyclopedie* was based on Bacon's classification. This is referenced in d'Alembert's *Discours (Oeuvres, i.)* and Diderot's *Prospectus (Oeuvres, iii.)*.
*   **Descartes:** Extensive citations from the *OEuvres* of Descartes are used to document the history of science and mathematics, with references spanning volumes iii through xi.
*   **Eustache Deschamps:** His *OEuvres complètes* were edited between 1878 and 1901 for the *Société des anciens textes français* by Gaston Raynaud and Queux de Saint-Hilaire. This collection includes a supplementary volume with an introduction by G. Raynaud and includes the work "De la prophécie Merlin sur la destruction d'Angleterre qui doit brief advenir" (listed as *Oeuvres*, No. 211).
*   **Other Editions:** An edition of *OEuvres* by Alfred Michiels was published in 1858.
