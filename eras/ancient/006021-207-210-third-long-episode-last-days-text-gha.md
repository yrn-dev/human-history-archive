# The Third Long Episode: The Last Days of the Buddha

The "Third long episode" is a significant narrative segment within the canonical Pāli authorities concerning the life of the Buddha. This specific episode focuses on the last days of the Buddha's life.

## Textual Sources and Location
The primary text for this episode is found in the *Dīgha Nikāya*, specifically within the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta*. In the edited volumes of the *Dīgha Nikāya* by Rhys Davids and Carpenter, this material is located in volume ii, pages 72-168. A translation of this account was provided by Rhys Davids in the work *Buddhist Suttas* (Oxford, 1881), spanning pages 1-136.

Additionally, the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* is identified as the text providing the account of the Buddha's last journey.

## Context within the Pāli Canon
The Pāli books are not books in the modern sense, but are memorial sentences or verses designed to be learned by heart. The structure of these texts is subordinated to the necessity of memorization. Within this system, a *sutta* (Pāli) is typically a very short piece containing a single proposition. When several related suttas are collected into a single dialogue, it is termed a *suttanta*.

The *Dīgha Nikāya* is defined as the "Collection of Long Ones," as it contains the longest of these suttantas. The account of the Buddha's last days is one of three "long episodes" preserved in the canonical Pāli authorities. These long episodes are distinguished from shorter narratives, such as the "Adoration of the babe" or "Youth at home," and other specific accounts like the "Visit to Kapilavastu."

## Relation to Other Buddhist Narratives
The third long episode serves as a concluding narrative arc following other major life events documented in the Pāli canon, including:
*   The Buddha's birth.
*   The "going forth," which includes years of penance, study, the attainment of Buddhahood and Nirvāna, and the conversion of the first five converts.
*   The period from the conversation with the five converts through the end of the first year of teaching.

While other Buddhist Sanskrit texts exist—such as the *Mahāvastu* (likely 2nd century B.C.) and the *Lalita Vistara* (likely 1st century B.C.)—these generally cover earlier periods of the Buddha's life, such as the period leading up to the first sermon or the end of the first year of teaching.
