# Antipater Is Convicted By Bathyllus; But He Still Returns

## The Plot Against King Herod
A conspiracy to assassinate King Herod was uncovered through a series of investigations and tortures. The king's inquiries began after a maid-servant, under torture, identified Antipater's mother as the author of the kingdom's miseries. This led to the discovery of secret meetings between Antipater, his mother, and Pheroras. Further examinations revealed that Antipater's mother had possessed a potion intended for the king. When the king transferred tortures to Antipater's brother and mother, they confessed that the potion had been brought from Egypt by Antipater, having been received from a brother of his who served as a physician in Alexandria.

The investigation expanded as the ghosts of Alexander and Aristobulus were said to act as inquisitors within the palace, leading to the discovery that Mariamne, the daughter of the high priest, and her brothers were conscious of the plot. As a result of this "insolent attempt," the king removed Herod (the son of Antipater's mother) from his testament, where he had previously been named as the successor to Antipater.

## The Testimony of Bathyllus
The conviction of Antipater was finalized through the examination of Bathyllus, who was Antipater's freed-man. Bathyllus had arrived from Rome bringing a second deadly potion composed of the juices of other serpents and the poison of asps. This was intended for Pheroras and Antipater's mother to use in the event that the first potion failed to kill the king.

In addition to the poisoning plot, Bathyllus provided evidence of Antipater's attempts to eliminate his brothers, Archelaus and Philip. While the two youths were being educated in Rome, Antipater sought to remove them so they would not hinder his own hopes. He forged letters in the name of friends in Rome, bribing some to write that Archelaus and Philip grossly reproached their father and expressed unease over being recalled home. Although Antipater wrote to his father regarding these accusations, he attempted to excuse the youths by attributing their words to their youth. To further his own standing, Antipater claimed to have spent two hundred talents on splendid ornaments for great men to gain interest in the affair of Sylleus.

## Return and Trial of Antipater
Antipater returned from Rome unaware of the accusations against him. His lack of knowledge was attributed to the fact that the roads were strictly guarded and that he was widely hated, meaning no one was willing to risk themselves to inform him of the situation in Judea during the seven months prior to his return.

Upon arriving at the palace, Antipater found his friends shut out at the first gate. When he attempted to salute his father, Herod rejected him, calling him a "vile wretch" and a "parricide." Herod refused to touch him until he cleared himself of the charges and appointed Varus, the president of Syria, to serve as his judge. Antipater was initially confounded and unable to answer, but he later received information from his wife and mother regarding the evidence gathered against him.

## The Accusations of Nicolaus
During the legal proceedings, Nicolaus brought a comprehensive accusation against Antipater. Nicolaus characterized Antipater as crafty and ascribed all the wickedness in the kingdom to him. Specifically, he accused Antipater of the murder of his brothers through the use of calumnies and argued that Antipater had laid designs against those brothers who were still alive under the pretext that they were plotting for the succession. Nicolaus questioned how a man who prepared poison for his father could be expected to abstain from harming his brothers. He further accused Antipater of corrupting those closest to the king and attempting to persuade Pheroras to murder his own brother.

## Other Relations and Conduct
The archive notes that Antipater's mother had previously attempted to introduce quarrels between Herod and Pheroras. Herod had urged Pheroras to put away his wife, accusing her of bringing dishonor to the virgins and causing war between the brothers. Despite Herod's warnings and a prohibition against Antipater and his mother having conversation with Pheroras, they continued to hold "merry meetings." Reports also indicated that Antipater had a criminal conversation with Pheroras's wife, facilitated by Antipater's mother.
