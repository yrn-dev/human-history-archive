# Buddhist Literary Collections of the Ganges Valley

## The Khuddaka Nikāya
Following the closure of the Nikāyas, a supplementary collection known as the *Khuddaka Nikāya* was established. This collection provides insight into the literary life of the Ganges valley during the 4th or 5th century B.C., as well as the intellectual state of the Buddhist community immediately following the earliest period. The reasons that drove literary activity during the earliest era continued to influence the creation of these supplementary works.

## The Khuddaka Pātha
The *Khuddaka Pātha* is a brief tract consisting of only a few pages. Its content begins with a profession of faith in the order, the doctrine, and the Buddha. This is followed by a paragraph detailing the thirty-four constituents of the human body, including nerves, blood, and bones. Despite this anatomical focus, the tract also contains several of the most beautiful poems found within Buddhist scriptures. There is no apparent reason for the inclusion of these specific pieces other than their exquisite versification. Historically and currently, this volume has likely served as a first lesson book for young neophytes upon joining the order.

## The Dhammapada
The *Dhammapada* serves as a text book within the Buddhist tradition. It consists of 423 verses gathered from various older sources. These verses are organized around twenty-six selected points of ethics or Buddhist self-training, with each point featuring between ten and twenty stanzas. The collection lacks a strict internal connection beyond the general subject matter, and the collector did not maintain consistency in the number of lines or the metre used across the stanzas.
