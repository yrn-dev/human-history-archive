# The Arrival of Cyrus the Younger in Asia Minor

The arrival of Cyrus, commonly referred to as Cyrus the younger, in Asia Minor is characterized as an event of the greatest importance. This advent opened what is described as the final phase of the Peloponnesian War.

## Political and Military Context
Prior to the events involving Cyrus the younger, the political landscape of Athens had been marked by instability. An oligarchy of the Four Hundred had been installed in the senate-house around February or March of 411 B.C. This regime lasted four or five months, ending in July of the same year with the restoration of the Athenian democracy. Following this restoration, military and naval operations resumed on the Asiatic coast.

## Relations with Lacedæmonian Forces
Upon his arrival, Cyrus the younger established a significant relationship with Lysander, the Lacedæmonian admiral in Asia. Lysander visited Cyrus at Sardis, where he employed a dexterous policy that earned him the peculiar esteem of Cyrus. This alliance resulted in Cyrus providing abundant pay for the Peloponnesian armament. Furthermore, Lysander utilized this position to organize factions among the cities of Asia.

## The Role of Alkibiadês
During this period, Alkibiadês operated in Asia and Thrace. After returning to Athens, he was received with a unanimous welcome, as the Athenians were disposed to overlook his previous wrongs and grant him a new trial. Alkibiadês demonstrated his loyalty by protecting the celebration of the Eleusinian mysteries by land against the garrison of Dekeleia, and he successfully resisted an attempt by Agis to surprise Athens.

However, Alkibiadês' subsequent military ventures in Asia were largely unsuccessful. After an ill-fated attempt at Andros and a failure to secure hopes from Persia, he encountered Lysander at Ephesus. Lysander adopted a cautious policy and refused to fight, which disappointed Alkibiadês. While Alkibiadês went to Phokæa, he left his fleet under the command of Antiochus. During this time, Alkibiadês oppressed the people of Kymê, leading to complaints in Athens. Following the defeat of Antiochus at Notium and further dissatisfaction within the armament, the sentiment in Athens shifted. Alkibiadês was eventually dismissed from his command and retired to the Chersonese, succeeded by ten generals.

## Subsequent Naval Commands
Following the tenure of Lysander, Kallikratidas assumed command. Described as possessing a noble character, Kallikratidas used energy and rectitude to repress internal murmurs and ill-will. He exhibited spirited behavior toward the Persians and appealed to Pan-Hellenic feelings among the Milesians. Kallikratidas successfully fitted out a commanding fleet and achieved success at Lesbos, where he liberated the Athenian garrison and other captives.

## The March of Cyrus the Younger
The movements of Cyrus the younger through Asia Minor are noted in the writings of Xenophon. His march took him through Lycaonia, a large interior region north of Mount Taurus, bounded by Cappadocia to the east, Galatia to the north, and Phrygia and Pisidia to the west.

Cyrus the younger eventually marched toward Kunaxa to oppose his brother, Artaxerxês Mnêmon. In preparation for this invasion, Artaxerxês had constructed a massive defensive ditch extending from the wall of Media to the river Euphratês. This ditch was twelve parasangs (forty-five English miles) long, thirty feet wide, and eight feet deep, with only a twenty-foot wide passage left alongside the river. Despite these defenses, the invading army found the pass undefended and marched through the narrow inlet without resistance. This lack of opposition led Cyrus the younger to believe that Artaxerxês had abandoned the defense of Babylon. However, two days later, Artaxerxês attacked Cyrus on an open plain where neither side held a positional advantage.
