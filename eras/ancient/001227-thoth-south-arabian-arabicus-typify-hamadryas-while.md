# Thoth

In the records of the Human History Archive, the name Thoth (or Sothis) appears in multiple contexts relating to ancient Egyptian religion, astronomy, and the calendar.

## Religious and Mythological Associations
Thoth is identified as an Egyptian deity. In the context of animal worship, the Arabian baboon (*P. hamadryas*), found in Arabia and North Africa, was dedicated by the ancient Egyptians to the god Thoth. Additionally, the archive mentions a deity referred to as Thoth-lunus.

In the town of Damanhur in Lower Egypt (ancient Timenhor), the Greek name for the location, Hermopolis Parva, suggests that Thoth served as the local god, although this exists alongside evidence that Horus was worshipped there.

## Astronomy and the Calendar
The name Thoth (or Sothis) was the Egyptian designation for the "Dog-star," known as Sirius. The heliacal rising of this star in Egypt—which occurred on July 20th of the Julian year between 2700 B.C. and the Christian era—marked the beginning of the Egyptian year.

The Egyptian calendar consisted of twelve months of thirty days each, supplemented by five additional days known as epagomens, totaling 365 days. Because this calendar did not account for the additional six hours of the solar year, the first day of the month of Thoth shifted backward by one day every four years relative to the Julian calendar.

This discrepancy created a cycle known as the Sothiac period (or Sothiac cycle), which spanned 1460 Julian years (equivalent to 1461 Egyptian years). A Sothiac period was reckoned from the year in which the first of the month of Thoth coincided with the heliacal rising of Sirius. According to Censorinus, one such revolution ended in 139 A.D., meaning that specific period began in 1322 B.C., and the period preceding it began in 2782 B.C.

## Related Zoology
The term "baboon" is derived from the French *babuin*, which originates from *Babon*, the Egyptian deity to whom the animal was sacred. The baboons associated with the region include:
*   **The Arabian Baboon (*P. hamadryas*):** Dedicated to Thoth and typifying the *Hamadryas* group along with the South Arabian *P. arabicus*.
*   **The Anubis Baboon (*P. anubis*):** A long-muzzled monkey that ranged from Egypt through tropical Africa. Frescoes indicate that these animals were tamed by ancient Egyptians and trained to pluck sycamore-figs from trees.
