# EDITIONS

The archive contains various references to scholarly editions, translations, and critical works across several disciplines, including classical literature, biblical studies, jurisprudence, and history.

## Classical and Fable Editions
Avianus, a Latin writer of fables who lived at Rome and was likely a heathen, authored 42 fables dedicated to a learned individual named Theodosius. These fables, which became popular as school-books, were likely inspired by the Greek works of Babrius. Avianus produced an elegiac version based on a rough Latin prose paraphrase. Notable editions of Avianus include those by Cannegieter (1731), Lachmann (1845), Froehner (1862), Ellis (1887), and Bahrens in *Poetae Latini Minores*. Additionally, William Caxton produced an English translation of the fables at Westmynstre in 1483.

Regarding the works of Babrius, the archive lists numerous editions, including Boissonade (1844), Lachmann (1845), Schneider (1853), Eberhard (1876), Gitlbauer (1882), Rutherford (1883), Feuillet (1890), Desrousseaux (1890), Passerat (1892), Croiset (1892), and Crusius (1897). Knoell published *Fabularum Babrianarum Paraphrasis Bodleiana* in 1877. Translations of Babrius are available in French by Leveque (1890) and in English by Davies (1860).

## Biblical and Critical Editions
In the field of New Testament studies, C. Tischendorf, a follower of Lachmann, published eight editions of the New Testament between 1841 and 1872. His eighth edition is regarded as a standard critical edition used by scholars worldwide, as it was the first to include the readings of [Hebrew: alef] and featured Prolegomena supplied by C.R. Gregory. While Tischendorf was considered a marvelous collector and publisher of evidence, he is noted for adding little to the principles of Lachmann and failing to appreciate the Griesbachian system of grouping manuscripts.

## Legal and Historical Editions
The archive references the *Collatio Legum Mosaicarum et Romanatum* (tit, vii. No. i), which was edited by Cannegieter. This work is cited in discussions regarding the right of killing nocturnal thieves, a practice declared by the Decemvirs, Solon, and Moses.

In the realm of political memoirs, the *Memoires* of Castelnau, which cover the years 1559 to 1570, were first printed in 1621. A second edition with additions by Le Laboureur appeared in 1659 in two folio volumes, and a third, further enlarged edition by Jean Godefroy was published in 1731 in three folio volumes.

## Dutch Literature and Other Scholarly Works
Various editions of Dutch literary works are recorded, including those edited by J.F. Willems (1836), L.P.C. v. den Bergh (1846-1847), P. Leendertz (1845-1847), C.P. Serrure and Ph. Blommaert (1852-1854), and Dr. E. Verwijs (1868). Additionally, Dr. Jul. Zacher provided an edition in Haupt's *Zeitschrift fur deutsches Altertum* (1842).

Other specialized editions and publications include:
*   **Musicology:** The *De re militari* (Basel, 1532) has seen successive manuscript and printed editions and translations that provide evidence for the evolution of wind instruments.
*   **Greek History:** Lachmann published two Dissertations in the Philological Transactions of the Berlin Academy (1837 and 1841) and a treatise titled *Die Spartanische Staats Verfassung in ihrer Entwicklung und ihrem Verfalle*, which examines the Lykurgean redivision of Sparta.
*   **Biography:** Editions of the life and letters of Lord Bacon include those by J. Spedding (1861, 1878) and the Ellis-Spedding editions featuring Dr. Rawley's *Life*.
