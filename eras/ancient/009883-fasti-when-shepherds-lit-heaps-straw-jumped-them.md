# Fasti and the Tradition of Bonfires

The *Fasti* is a work by Ovid that documents various ancient rites and customs. Among these is the mention of the Palilia, a feast dedicated to Pales, the goddess of the shepherds. During this celebration, shepherds observed a specific ritual in which they lit heaps of straw and jumped over them as the straw burned.

## The Nature and Use of Bonfires
The practice of lighting large open-air fires, or bonfires, has a long history across Europe. These fires have served various purposes over time, including:
*   **Funeral and Sacrificial Rites:** Bonfires have been used as funeral piles for corpses, fires of bones, or for immolation, such as the burning of proscribed books and heretics. There are grounds to believe that human sacrifices were actually performed in bonfires in ancient times.
*   **National and Signal Fires:** Large fires have been lit for occasions of national rejoicing or as signals of alarm, such as the bonfires used to warn England of the approach of the Armada.
*   **Festivities:** In Cornwall, bonfires are lit on St Peter's day and the eve of St John the Baptist. The Cornish term for midsummer, *Goluan*, translates to both "festivity" and "light." In some instances, effigies are burned in these fires, or a pretense is made of burning a living person.

## Historical and Religious Perspectives
The custom of peasants lighting bonfires on specific days of the year to dance around or leap over them is traced back to the Middle Ages, with origins suggesting early heathen Europe. Because of these pagan associations, early Christian synods attempted to suppress the practice during the 7th and 8th centuries.

Two specific ecclesiastical prohibitions are recorded:
*   **The Third Council of Constantinople (A.D. 680):** Through its 65th canon, the council commanded an end to the "ridiculously and foolishly" performed custom of leaping over fires kindled by certain people before their houses and shops on new moons.
*   **The Synodus Francica (A.D. 742):** Under Pope Zachary, this synod forbade all observations of the Pagans, specifically mentioning "sacrilegious fires" referred to as *Nedfri* (or bonefires).

Despite these prohibitions, the early Church eventually made a compromise with heathen traditions, leading to the integration of bonfire lighting into certain Christian festivals.
