# Renunciation of Satan

The renunciation of Satan is a formal act of abjuration historically integrated into the rites of baptism and spiritual purification. In early Christian traditions, this act served as a symbolic and ritualistic break from the influence of evil spirits and the worship of demons.

## Ritual Practices in the Early Church
By approximately the year 300, individuals desiring baptism were admitted to the catechumenate after providing their names to a bishop. These catechumens underwent a period of scrutiny and instruction covering the Christian scheme of salvation, monotheism, and the folly of polytheism. To rid the candidates of the "lingering taint of the worship of demons," they were subjected to repeated exorcisms.

The specific rite of renunciation involved a physical orientation: catechumens turned to the west to pronounce their renunciation of Satan. Following this, they turned to the east to recite the creed. This sequence of turning to the west three times for the renunciation of the "Evil One" and then to the east is noted to have an exact parallel in a water purification rite used by the Malays.

Other elements of the baptismal process included the *traditio* and *redditio* of the symbol (the learning and repeating of the creed by heart), the communication of the Lord's Prayer (*traditio precis*), and an act of unction. In the West, this unction involved the priest applying saliva to the lips and ears of the candidate, accompanied by the formula: "Effeta, that is, be thou opened unto odour of sweetness. But do thou flee, O Devil, for the judgment of God is at hand."

## Theological and Legal Parallels
The developed ceremony of a threefold renunciation has been compared to the Roman legal process of *emancipatio*. In Roman law, a father could extinguish his *patria potestas* (power of life and death) over his son by selling him three times into *mancipium*, with each sale followed by a *manumissio vindicta* (enfranchisement). 

Similarly, the triple *abrenunciatio* of Satan was viewed as freeing the believer from Satan, who was believed to have power of death over the individual by virtue of their birth. Just as legal manumission dissolved agnatic relationships, baptism was seen as a transition where the person gave up physical ties to father and mother to enter a spiritual society of brethren. This concept of adoption as a son and heir of God is attributed to the influence of Roman law on Paul.

## Evolution and Variations of the Rite
The nature of exorcisms and renunciations evolved over time, generally becoming longer. While the *Didache* and Justin focused on fasting to expel evil spirits—based on the belief that demons prefer a well-fed body over an emaciated one—later rites became more formalized. Basil, in *On the Holy Spirit*, noted that the formal renunciation of Satan and his angels, along with the blessing of the baptizand and the water, were handed down through a "silent and sacramental tradition" rather than published teaching.

In later periods, some traditions moved away from these practices. For example, the English prayer-book excluded both the exorcisms and the renunciation of the devil, his angels, pomps, and works.

## Conceptions of Satan in Sacred Texts
The Qur'an presents Satan (also referred to as Shaitan) as a deceptive force that seeks to lead humanity away from God. The texts describe Satan's handiwork as including "abominations" such as intoxicants, gambling, idols, and divining arrows. His primary plan is to excite hatred and enmity between people and to hinder them from prayer and the remembrance of Allah.

The texts detail several instances of Satan's influence:
* **The Fall of Adam:** Satan caused Adam and his wife to "slip" or "deflect" from the garden, expelling them from their state of felicity.
* **Military Failure:** On the day two hosts met, it was Satan who caused some to backslide or make a slip because of deeds they had done.
* **Deception:** Satan is described as making sinful acts seem alluring. In one instance, he promised protection to men, claiming no one could overcome them, only to take flight and declare himself "guiltless" once the armies were in sight, stating his fear of Allah.

To counter these influences, the texts teach that those who fear Allah and are assaulted by a "glamour," "slander," or "thought of evil" from Satan should seek refuge in Allah and bring Him to remembrance, which allows them to "see (aright)."
