# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview
Hatshepsut (r. c. 1479–1458 BCE) stands as one of the most successful and intriguing rulers of the Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt. While women had held power before—such as Sobekneferu—Hatshepsut was unique in her transition from regent to a full-fledged pharaoh. Initially serving as regent for her stepson, Thutmose III, she eventually assumed the complete titulary and regalia of a king.

Her reign was characterized by a shift from the military expansionism of her predecessors toward economic prosperity and architectural grandeur. She is most famous for establishing lucrative trade networks, most notably the expedition to the Land of Punt, which brought exotic goods like myrrh, frankincense, and gold into Egypt. To legitimize her rule in a patriarchal society, Hatshepsut utilized "divine birth" narratives, claiming she was the daughter of the god Amun.

Historians have long debated the motives behind the subsequent attempt to erase her image from monuments. For decades, it was believed that Thutmose III acted out of spite or a desire to "delete" her from history. However, modern scholarship suggests the erasure occurred later in his reign, possibly to ensure a clean line of succession for his own son, Amenhotep II, by removing the precedent of a female king.

## Key Facts
*   **Architectural Legacy:** Her most enduring monument is the *Djeser-Djeseru* (Holy of Holies), a stunning terraced mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari.
*   **Gender Presentation:** In official statuary, Hatshepsut is often depicted with the traditional false beard and muscular physique of a male pharaoh to emphasize her authority.
*   **Economic Focus:** Unlike the "Warrior Kings," she prioritized the restoration of trade routes that had been disrupted during the Hyksos period.
*   **Divine Legitimacy:** She claimed the title "God's Wife of Amun," a role that gave her significant religious and political leverage.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Punt Expedition:** The reliefs at Deir el-Bahari provide some of the earliest detailed visual records of foreign flora and fauna, including incense trees and baboons.
2. **The Obelisks:** She commissioned massive granite obelisks at the Temple of Karnak, one of which remains the tallest surviving ancient obelisk in Egypt.
3. **The Mummy's Identity:** For years, her mummy was unknown; however, in 2007, researchers identified her using a molar tooth found in a wooden box that matched a gap in a mummy discovered in the Valley of the Kings.
