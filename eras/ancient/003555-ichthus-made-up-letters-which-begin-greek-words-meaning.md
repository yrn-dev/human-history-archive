## Related Early Church Concepts
The period surrounding these developments saw the growth of a mythology centered on the concept of re-birth. This included specific views on the baptismal font, which was regarded as the womb of the virgin mother church. In certain congregations, such as those in the early churches of Gaul, the mother church was not viewed as an abstraction but as a divine aeon who sympathized with and watched over the children of her womb, and was the subject of humble supplications and hymns of praise.

Further developments occurred regarding the sponsors, known as *anadochi*. Following the introduction of infant baptism, these individuals became known as god-mothers and god-fathers. They maintained a spiritual relationship with the children they took from the font. Canonists eventually identified this spiritual relation with the blood-tie existing between offspring and real parents. This led to the conclusion that children sharing the same god-parent were considered real brothers and sisters. Consequently, a marriage taboo was created in the Eastern and Latin churches, prohibiting such individuals from marrying one another or the biological children of the god-parent. While the reformed churches have since set aside this fiction, the taboo remained powerful in the Latin and Eastern traditions.
