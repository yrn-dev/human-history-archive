# Brixia

Brixia, known in modern times as Brescia, is a city and episcopal see located in Lombardy, Italy. It serves as the capital of the province of Brescia and is situated at the foot of the Alps, approximately 40 miles west of Verona and 52 miles east of Milan by rail.

## Early History and Romanization
Originally an ancient Celtic town belonging to the Cenomani, Brixia became Roman in 225 B.C. following the submission of the Cenomani to Rome. The city's Roman influence is still evident in its urban planning; the city is rectangular with streets that intersect at right angles. While the area enclosed by medieval walls is larger than the original Roman town, the Roman settlement occupied the eastern portion of the current city.

In 27 B.C., Augustus founded a civil colony at Brixia, rather than a military one. To provide the colony with water, Augustus and Tiberius constructed an aqueduct.

## Roman Architecture and Remains
The site of the Roman forum is marked by the Piazza del Museo. On the north side of this square stands a museum housed within a Corinthian temple featuring three *cellae*. This building is attributed by some to Hercules, though it is more likely the city's Capitolium, erected by Vespasian in A.D. 73. Excavated in 1823, the temple contains a famous bronze statue of Victory, which was discovered in 1826. Other surviving Roman remains include the theatre to the east of the temple and scanty remains of a building on the south side of the forum, identified as either a *curia* or a basilica.

## Medieval and Renaissance Periods
During the Lombard period, Brixia served as the seat of a duchy. By 1167, the city had become one of the most active members of the Lombard League. The city's control shifted several times during the later Middle Ages and Renaissance:
* In 1258, it fell to Eccelino of Verona.
* It subsequently belonged to the Scaligers (della Scala) until 1421.
* In 1421, it came under the control of the Visconti of Milan.
* In 1426, it passed to Venice.

By the early 16th century, Brixia was among the wealthiest cities in Lombardy. However, the city never fully recovered from a sack carried out by the French under Gaston de Foix in 1512.

## Later History and Austrian Dominion
Brixia remained under Venetian rule until 1797, at which point it came under Austrian dominion. The city revolted against this rule in 1848 and again in 1849. In the latter year, it was the only Lombard town to rally to Charles Albert. Following ten days of obstinate street fighting, the city was taken by the Austrians under Haynau.

As of 1901, the population of the town was 42,495, while the commune had a population of 72,731.
