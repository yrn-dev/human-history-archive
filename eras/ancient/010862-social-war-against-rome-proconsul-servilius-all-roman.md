# The Social War and the Siege of Asculum

The Social War, also referred to as the Marsic War due to the active role of the warlike Marsi nation, was a conflict that began in 91 B.C. and did not reach a complete conclusion until 88 B.C. The war was fought between Rome and its Italian allies, known as the *Socii*.

## Causes and Political Context
The immediate cause of the conflict was the rejection of a measure proposed by the tribune M. Livius Drusus. This proposal sought to grant the rights of Roman citizenship to the Italian allies. These allies were subject states of Rome that provided the Roman Republic with money and manpower for its various wars and contributed significantly to Roman victories. In exchange for these burdens, the allies claimed they were entitled to the political rights afforded to Roman citizens.

## Roman Response and Legislation
Initially, the war was unfavorable for the Romans. To address the crisis, the Roman government introduced legislation to grant citizenship to those who remained loyal. In 90 B.C., during the consulship of L. Julius Cæsar, the *Lex Julia* was proposed, offering Roman citizenship to all Italians who had continued to be faithful to Rome, provided they chose to accept it. This was followed in the next year by the *Lex Plautia Papiria*, which extended the grants of citizenship to all allies, with the exception of the Lucanians and the Samnites. The war was eventually finished by Sulla.

## The Events at Asculum
The city of Asculum, the capital of Picenum, occupied a strong position in difficult country and played a prominent role in the Social War. In 90 B.C., the inhabitants of Asculum massacred the Roman citizens within the city walls, as well as the proconsul Q. Servilius (referred to in some records as P. Servilius).

Following this massacre, the city was subjected to a long siege. In 89 B.C., Asculum was captured and burnt by Pompeius Strabo. The aftermath of the capture was severe: the leader of the city, Judacilius, committed suicide, the principal citizens were put to death, and the remaining population was exiled. Pompeius Strabo celebrated a triumph on December 25 of that year for his success against the inhabitants of Picenum and the Asculani.

## Later History of Asculum
Following the Social War, Asculum continued to be a site of strategic importance. After crossing the Rubicon, Caesar occupied the city due to its strong position. The city later received a Roman colony, possibly under the triumvirs. In A.D. 301, it became the capital of Picenum Suburbicarium. 

The city was taken by Totila in 545, though it was later described by Paulus Diaconus as the chief city of Picenum. From the era of Charlemagne until 1185, Asculum was ruled by its bishops, who held the title of prince and possessed the right to coin money. In 1185, it became a free republic, experiencing various struggles with Fermo before coming under more direct papal sway in the 15th century.
