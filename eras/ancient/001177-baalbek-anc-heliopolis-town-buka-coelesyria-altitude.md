# BAALBEK

Baalbek, known in antiquity as Heliopolis, is a town located in the Buka'a region of Coelesyria. It is situated at an altitude of 3,850 feet, positioned east of the Litani river near the point where its waters part from those of the Asi. Geographically, it is located between the Lebanon and Anti-Lebanon mountains.

## Demographics and Infrastructure
The population of Baalbek is approximately 5,000 people. This population includes 1,000 Christians, consisting of Orthodox and Maronite denominations, and 2,000 Metawali. 

The town is connected to the broader region via railway. Since 1902, it has been linked to Rayak (Rejak) on the Beirut-Damascus line, and since 1907, it has maintained a connection with Aleppo.

## Ancient History and Ruins
Baalbek is renowned for its temple ruins dating from the Roman period. While there is no record of the town prior to the Roman era, it is certain that the name Heliopolis is a translation of an earlier native name that included "Baal" as an element. There has been a suggestion that this name refers to the Baalgad mentioned in Joshua xi. 17, though the archive notes this is without good reason.

The ruins are described as vast and imposing due to their immensity, though some accounts characterize them as more imposing than pleasing in their specific details. During its height of glory, Heliopolis stood as a significant site within a Syrian landscape characterized by a "quiltlike pattern" of municipal kingdoms and principalities. The architecture of the period reflected the tastes of artistic and voluptuous Semites, featuring lavish expense and rich ornamentation.

## Historical Documentation and Exploration
The ruins of Baalbek have been documented by numerous travelers and tourists over several centuries. Early discoverers included P. Belon, who wrote *De admirabili operum antiquorum praestantia* (1553) and *Observations* (1555), and M. von Baumgarten, author of *Peregrinatio in ... Syriam* (1594). 

Later significant works include R. Wood's *Ruins of Baalbec* (1757), written before the earthquake of 1759. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the site was further studied by H. Frauberger in *Die Akropolis von Baalbek* (1892) and O. Puchstein, who wrote *Fuehrer durch die Ruinen v. Baalbek* (1905) and *Ansichten* (1905) with Th. v. Luepke. Additional records of the site appear in the *Builder* (11 Feb. 1905) and the *Quart. Stat. Pal. Exp. Fund* (1904).

## Modern Military History
In the context of the Great War, Baalbek appeared in the movements of General Allenby. Following his presence in Beirut on the 8th, Allenby was in Baalbek on the 11th, before proceeding to the final stage of his advance at Aleppo.
