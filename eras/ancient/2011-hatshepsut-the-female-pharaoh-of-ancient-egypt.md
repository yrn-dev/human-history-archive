# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview
Hatshepsut (reigned c. 1479–1458 BCE) stands as one of the most successful and intriguing rulers of the Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt. While women had held power before—most notably Neferusobek—Hatshepsut was unique in that she transitioned from the role of Queen Regent for her stepson, Thutmose III, to a full-fledged Pharaoh.

Her reign was characterized by economic prosperity, the restoration of trade networks, and an ambitious building program. To legitimize her rule in a society where the kingship was traditionally male, Hatshepsut utilized sophisticated propaganda. She claimed divine birth, asserting that the god Amun-Ra had fathered her, and she was frequently depicted in statuary wearing the traditional pharaonic false beard and kilt.

Historians have long debated the motives behind the subsequent attempt to erase her name and image from monuments after her death. While earlier scholars suggested a "vendetta" by Thutmose III, modern consensus suggests that the *damnatio memoriae* was likely a political move to ensure a clean line of succession for Thutmose III's own heirs, rather than a personal grudge.

## Key Facts
*   **The Punt Expedition:** One of her greatest achievements was the maritime expedition to the Land of Punt (likely modern-day Somalia or Eritrea), which brought back luxury goods such as myrrh, frankincense, gold, and exotic animals.
*   **Architectural Legacy:** She commissioned the Djeser-Djeseru ("Holy of Holies"), her magnificent mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari, which is considered a masterpiece of ancient architecture due to its symmetrical terraces.
*   **Divine Right:** She promoted the "Divine Birth" myth, carving reliefs into her temple walls that depicted Amun-Ra visiting her mother in the guise of her father to conceive her.
*   **Economic Focus:** Unlike many of her successors, Hatshepsut focused more on trade and diplomacy than on military conquest, though she did oversee some successful campaigns in Nubia.

## Did You Know?
1. **Gender Fluidity in Art:** Over time, Hatshepsut’s artistic depictions shifted from feminine forms to masculine ones to align with the traditional iconography of the Pharaoh.
2. **The "Forgotten" Queen:** For centuries, her existence was unknown to the modern world until 19th-century archaeologists deciphered the hieroglyphs and realized a woman had ruled as king.
3. **The Obelisks:** She erected massive granite obelisks at the Temple of Karnak, one of which remains one of the tallest surviving ancient obelisks in Egypt.
