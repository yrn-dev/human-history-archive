# Alexander Janneus

Alexander Janneus was a king who reigned for twenty-seven years. He ascended to the throne after the death of Aristobulus, when Aristobulus's wife, Salome (known to the Greeks as Alexandra), released the king's brothers from prison and made Alexander king. He was noted for being elder in age and more moderate in temper than his brothers. According to reports, his father, Hyrcanus, had hated him from birth and kept him away from his sight, having been told in a dream that Alexander would be the heir to all his goods; consequently, Alexander was brought up in Galilee.

## Internal Governance and Family Relations
Upon taking the government, Alexander slew one of his brothers who desired the kingdom. In contrast, he held the other brother in great esteem because that brother preferred a quiet, private life and did not interfere in public affairs.

## Military Expeditions and Territorial Conquests
Alexander Janneus engaged in numerous military campaigns to expand his territory and secure maritime cities. Early in his reign, he conducted an expedition against Ptolemais, defeating the inhabitants in battle and placing the city under siege. At the time, the maritime cities remaining to be conquered were Ptolemais and Gaza, as well as Dora and Strato's Tower, which were held by the tyrant Zoilus.

Alexander also besieged and captured Gadara and Amathus. Amathus was considered the strongest of the fortresses surrounding Jordan and contained the precious possessions of Theodorus, the son of Zeno. This led Theodorus to march against Alexander; while Theodorus recovered his own property and the king's baggage, he also slew ten thousand of the Jews. Alexander recovered from this blow and turned his forces toward the maritime regions, successfully taking Gaza, Raphia, and Anthedon (a city later called Agrippias by King Herod).

## Conflicts with Foreign Powers
Alexander's reign was marked by significant conflict with Ptolemy Lathyrus. Alexander had sent word to Cleopatra to persuade her to make war against Ptolemy, while simultaneously pretending to be in friendship with him. A battle eventually occurred between Alexander and Ptolemy Lathyrus, who had taken the city of Asochis. Although Alexander slew many of his enemies, the victory favored Ptolemy. However, Ptolemy eventually retired to Egypt after being pursued by his mother, Cleopatra.

Alexander also faced conflict with Demetrius Eucerus. After a period where Alexander fled to the mountains, six thousand Jews joined him out of pity. This caused Demetrius to fear and retire from the country.

## Civil Strife and Internal Repression
Alexander faced severe internal opposition from the Jews, who fought against him and introduced foreigners to assist in the war. This conflict reached a point where Alexander was forced to return the land of Moab and Gilead, which he had previously subdued, to the king of Arabia to prevent them from joining the war against him.

After defeating the Jews in several battles, Alexander shut the most powerful of them up in the city of Bethome. Upon taking the city, he brought the captives to Jerusalem and committed an act described as one of the most barbarous in the world. While feasting with his concubines in view of the city, he ordered approximately eight hundred of the captives to be crucified. Simultaneously, he ordered the throats of their wives and children to be cut before their eyes. This action was taken as revenge for the injuries the rebels had caused him, which had placed his life and kingdom in extreme hazard. Due to this brutality, he was referred to as a "Thracian" among the Jews. Following this event, approximately eight thousand soldiers who had fought against him fled and remained fugitives for the duration of his life, allowing Alexander to reign the remainder of his time in tranquility.

## Death and Succession
Alexander Janneus died after a sickness that lasted three years. He left behind two sons, Hyrcanus and Aristobulus, but committed the kingdom to his wife, Alexandra. He provided counsel to Alexandra on how to manage the kingdom, which included suggesting she speak to the Pharisees and grant them power over the affairs of the kingdom and the dead body. This strategy was intended to pacify the anger the Pharisees held toward Alexander. Following his death, Alexandra's actions led the multitude to grieve for him, resulting in a funeral more splendid than that of any king before him.
