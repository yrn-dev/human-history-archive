# Victor Charles Mahillon

Victor Charles Mahillon (born 1841) was a Belgian scholar and historian of music recognized for his contributions to the fields of acoustics and the descriptive cataloging of musical instruments. He is specifically noted for his work associated with the museum of musical instruments belonging to the Brussels conservatoire.

## Academic and Bibliographic Works
Mahillon authored several significant texts concerning the science and history of music. In 1874, he published *Elements d'acoustique musicale et instrumentale* (also referred to as *Éléments d'acoustique*), a work that includes diagrams of mouthpieces and other acoustic illustrations. 

Between 1893 and 1900, he produced a descriptive catalogue of the Brussels conservatoire's museum of musical instruments. A 1896 version of this work is titled *Catalogue descriptif et analytique du musee instrumental du Conservatoire Royal de Musique*. This catalogue provides detailed descriptions of various instruments, including the modern bassoon (No. 999) and other specific entries such as No. 1137 and No. 189.

## Facsimiles and Instrumental Research
Mahillon is noted for creating exact facsimiles of ancient and prehistoric wind instruments to determine their musical properties.

### Prehistoric Horns
Mahillon constructed a facsimile of a prehistoric bronze horn known as a *lur* (or *luur*). These instruments, found in the peat beds of Denmark and preserved in the Museum of Northern Antiquities in Copenhagen, are prototypes of the Roman *cornu*. The original instrument measures 1.91 meters (approximately 6 feet 4 inches) and features a wide conical bore and a U-shaped mouthpiece joint. Through his facsimile, Mahillon determined that the *lur* was in the key of E$\flat$ and could easily produce the first eight harmonics of that key. He noted that while the *lur* stands an octave higher than the modern horn in E$\flat$ (which is roughly 13 feet long), the fundamental E$\flat$ is reachable on the *lur* due to its wider bore calibre.

### Roman Instruments
Mahillon also produced a facsimile of a *buccina*, a brass wind instrument used by the ancient Roman army. The original instruments, three of which were discovered in the ruins of Pompeii and are held in the museum at Naples, consist of a brass tube 11 to 12 feet long with a narrow cylindrical bore. Mahillon's facsimile revealed that the instrument is in G and possesses a harmonic series nearly identical to that of the trumpet and the French horn.

## Descriptions of Rare Instruments
In addition to his reconstructions, Mahillon provided scholarly descriptions of rare musical artifacts. He described a small 18th and 19th-century barrel-organ, known as a bird-organ (*serinette*, *turlutaine*, or *merline*), held in the collection of the Brussels Conservatoire. This specific instrument is shaped like a book titled "*Le chant des oiseaux, Tome vi.*" It contains ten pewter stopped pipes that provide the scale of G, with the addition of A and F$\flat$ two octaves higher. The device measures approximately 8 x 5-1/2 x 2-3/4 inches and is capable of playing eight tunes.
