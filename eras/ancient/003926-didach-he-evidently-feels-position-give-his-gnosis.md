# Didache

The *Didache*, also known as the *Teaching of the Apostles*, is an early Christian text that serves as a significant historical marker for the development of church structure and liturgical practice.

## Church Order and Transition
The *Didache* provides evidence of a pivotal moment of transition within the early Christian movement. During the apostolic age, the church was characterized by a "charismatic" ministry consisting of prophets and apostles. The *Didache* marks the point where this charismatic leadership began to be replaced by a more permanent system of local church officials, specifically deacons, presbyters, and bishops.

## Liturgical Practices
The text is cited as an early example of primitive church practices regarding prayer. Specifically, the *Didache* contains examples of the practice where the local president, or bishop, would offer short prayers at the close of the office. These prayers summed up the supplications of the congregation and served as the precursors to the "collects" found in later church orders. Over time, these improvised prayers were replaced by set forms, eventually leading to the creation of Orationals and Sacramentaries.

## Historical Context and Manuscripts
The *Didache* appears in various ancient manuscripts. One notable 11th-century manuscript contains the *Didache* alongside the "Epistle of Barnabas" and two other writings that served as an appendix to the New Testament in the *Codex Alexandrinus*.
