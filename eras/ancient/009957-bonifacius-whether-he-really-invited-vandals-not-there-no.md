# Bonifacius

## Roman Governor of Africa
Bonifacius (died 432) served as the Roman governor of the province of Africa. He is generally believed to have invited the Vandals into the province between 428 and 429. This action is thought to have been taken in revenge for hostile actions carried out by Placidia, who was ruling on behalf of her son, Emperor Valentinian III. 

Regarding the cause of this hostility, sources differ: Procopius attributes the action to Aetius, a rival of Bonifacius, while the earliest authorities identify Felix, the chief minister of Placidia, as the person who calumniated Bonifacius.

## Conflict with the Vandals
While it is debated whether Bonifacius actually invited the Vandals, he soon turned against them. He bravely defended the city of Hippo from Vandal attacks, utilizing Gothic mercenaries. During this struggle for North Africa, the native population provided no appreciable assistance to the governor. The local nomad tribes either maintained a dubious attitude or used the governor's difficulties to launch predatory expeditions and attacks.

This lack of local support was the result of demoralizing social conditions. Free peasants had become serfs to great landed proprietors, and the landowners themselves were victims of unprecedented extortion by unscrupulous governors. This environment led to repeated bloody insurrections caused by the pressure of taxation, and the senate of large towns was avoided by those with anything to lose, as senators were required to cover frequent and considerable revenue deficiencies.

## Return to Italy and Death
In 432, Bonifacius returned to Italy. He was received back into the favor of Placidia and appointed as master of the soldiery. This promotion was resented by his rival, Aetius. The two men eventually met, possibly in single combat. Although Bonifacius was victorious in the encounter, he received a wound that caused his death three months later.
