# 1893

## Liturgical and Religious Scholarship
In the study of the Roman Breviary, 1893 marks the publication of *L'Histoire du bréviaire romain* by P. Batiffol in Paris, a work that was subsequently translated into English. The Roman Breviary itself serves as a requirement for every member of a religious order and every clerk in orders, who must read the daily allotted services either privately aloud or by joining publicly. These services—which include Matins, Lauds, Prime, Terce, Sext, None, Vespers, and Compline—typically require between four and eight hours in choir, depending on the number of high masses and the amount of music.

## Archaeological and Historical Research

In North Africa, Steph. Gsell began publishing the "Chronique archeologique africaine" in the *Revue africaine* of Algiers in 1893, a series of publications that continued into following years.

## Urban Development in Sweden
In Luleå, a Swedish seaport and the capital of the Norrbotten district, the year 1893 marks the completion of the town's church. This construction was part of a broader rebuilding effort following a fire in 1887 that almost entirely destroyed the town. The church, along with a technical school and the Norrbotten Museum, stands as one of the most important buildings in the city.
