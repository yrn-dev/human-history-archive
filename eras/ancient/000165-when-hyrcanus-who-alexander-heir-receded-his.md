# Hyrcanus and the Succession of Alexander

The transition of power following the reign of Alexander Janneus was marked by a conflict between his two sons, Hyrcanus and Aristobulus, and the intervening governance of their mother, Alexandra. While Hyrcanus was the designated heir to the kingdom, his personal inclinations and the ambition of his younger brother led to a period of instability and political negotiation.

## The Regency of Alexandra
Upon the death of Alexander, the kingdom was committed to Alexandra. She reigned for nine years, during which time she maintained the kingdom by gaining the goodwill of the Pharisees, granting them power over both the affairs of the kingdom and matters regarding the dead body. This strategy pacified the anger the Pharisees had held against Alexander.

During this period, the two sons of Alexander displayed contrasting temperaments. Hyrcanus was described as being unable to manage public affairs and preferred a quiet life. In contrast, Aristobulus was characterized as an active, bold, and magnanimous man.

## The Conflict Between Hyrcanus and Aristobulus
As Alexandra fell sick, Aristobulus seized the opportunity to take control. Utilizing a large number of domestic friends and the warmth of his youth, he took possession of all the fortresses. He used the funds discovered within these fortresses to hire mercenary soldiers and declared himself king.

Hyrcanus complained to his mother regarding this disinheriting. In response, Alexandra showed compassion for Hyrcanus and placed the sons and wife of Aristobulus under restraint in the fortress of Antonia. This site, located at the north part of the temple, was originally called the Citadel but was later renamed Antonia during the time Antony was lord of the East. However, Alexandra died before she could formally punish Aristobulus.

The dispute over the kingdom eventually led to a battle near Jericho. During the conflict, the majority of the forces deserted Hyrcanus to join Aristobulus. Hyrcanus and his remaining supporters fled to Antonia, where he took Aristobulus's wife and children as hostages to ensure his own preservation.

## The Agreement and Receding of the Claim
Before the conflict reached an extreme conclusion, the two brothers reached an agreement. Hyrcanus receded from his claim to the crown and resigned the kingdom to Aristobulus. In exchange, Hyrcanus was permitted to retain all his other dignities as the brother of the king.

The reconciliation was performed publicly in the temple, where the brothers embraced in a kind manner before the people. As part of their arrangement, they exchanged residences: Aristobulus moved into the royal palace, and Hyrcanus retired to the house previously occupied by Aristobulus.

## Subsequent Disputes and Roman Arbitration
The peace between the brothers was temporary. Hyrcanus was later persuaded by Antipater to flee to Aretas. This led to an expedition by Aretas and Hyrcanus against Aristobulus, resulting in the siege of Jerusalem, which was eventually raised by the Roman general Scaurus.

The dispute eventually reached the Roman Republic. Both Aristobulus and Hyrcanus appeared before Pompey to argue over who should possess the kingdom. Following the plight of Aristobulus to the fortress Alexandrium, Pompey led his army against him and ordered the delivery of the fortresses in his possession.

## Legacy and Recognition
Hyrcanus is further recorded as the high priest and ethnarch of the Jews. His status was recognized by Roman authorities; Caius Caesar (the fourth-time dictator and fifth-time consul) spoke of the rights and privileges of Hyrcanus, the son of Alexander, noting that previous imperators had borne witness to him before the senate and people of Rome.

Additionally, Hyrcanus sought to preserve the customs of his forefathers. He sent an ambassador to Dolabella, the prefect of Asia, requesting that the Jews be dismissed from military services. This request was granted because the Jews were not permitted to bear arms, travel on the sabbath days, or procure certain types of food required by their traditions. Dolabella issued an epistle to the Asiatics, specifically the Ephesians, permitting the Jews to assemble for sacred and religious purposes and to collect oblations for sacrifices as their law required.
