# The Basilican Church of St John Studios

## Overview and Architecture
Constantinople preserved until recently a basilican church dating from the 5th century known as St John Studios (463), which currently exists as a ruin. The structure featured a nave and side aisles that were divided by columns. These columns supported a horizontal entablature, while another order supported arches that formed a gallery above the aisles. The church concluded with a standard apsidal termination.

## Regional Characteristics of the Basilica
The basilican form, referred to at the time as *dromical* due to its resemblance to a race-course (*dromos*), was originally as common in the Eastern church as it was in the West. However, a primary distinction between Eastern and Roman basilicas lies in the use of galleries. While this feature is rare in the West—occurring in only a few examples in Ravenna and some questioned instances in Rome—it is a characteristic feature of Eastern churches. These galleries were designed to provide privacy for women, a requirement that was more strictly studied in the East than in the West.

## Evolution of Church Plans in Constantinople
In Constantinople, the basilican or axial type was one of two chief plans in use during the early period of Constantine's church building, the other being the circular or central type. Early examples of the basilican plan in the city included the original churches of the Apostles and St Sophia, though both of these buildings have entirely passed away. 

The basilican form remained prominent until the erection of St Sophia, which introduced a new architectural type that almost entirely superseded the old *dromical* style. Additionally, vaults were applied to the basilican plan in later periods; for example, the 6th-century church of St Irene in Constantinople features a long body covered by two domes. In contrast, the central type is represented by the famous church of the Holy Apostles in Constantinople.

## Related Eastern Basilican Structures
Other notable basilican churches in the East include:
*   **The Church of the Nativity at Bethlehem:** An oriental basilica of the same date as the original churches of St Sophia and the Apostles, though it was rebuilt by Justinian in the 6th century. It contains an oblong atrium, a narthex, double aisles with Corinthian columns, and a transept with apses at each end.
*   **Monastery of St Catherine on Mt. Sinai:** Built by Justinian.
*   **Other regional examples:** The church of Dana (built by Justinian between Antioch and Bir), St Philip at Athens, Bosra in Arabia, Xanthus in Lycia, and the church of St Demetrius at Thessalonica.

## Other Religious Structures in Constantinople
Beyond the basilican churches, Constantinople housed other significant religious architecture. The metropolitan baptistery still stands located at the side of the mosque that formerly served as the patriarchal church of St Sophia.
