# The Basoche

The Basoche was a professional organization of clerks associated with the legal systems of France, most notably those of the Palais de Justice and the Chatelet of Paris. The organization maintained specific legal prerogatives and judiciary powers that were recognized by law.

## Professional Requirements and the "Stage"
To be recognized as a qualified *procureur*, an individual was required to complete a "stage" within the Basoche. This process necessitated that the individual's name be entered on the register of the Basoche for a period of ten years. The archive notes that simply serving as a clerk to a *procureur* and being registered at that specific office was insufficient to meet this requirement.

During the 17th and 18th centuries, this rule led to frequent conflicts between the *procureurs* and the members of the Basoche. While some decisions were made in favor of the *procureurs*, the parlement generally upheld the rights of the Basoche. This support from the parlement was driven by a dread of *certificats de complaisance* issued by the *procureurs*. These specific certificates were particularly problematic because they remained valid in locations where no Basoche existed.

## Judiciary Powers and Jurisdiction
The Basoche possessed law-recognized judiciary powers, which included disciplinary jurisdiction over its own members. It was empowered to decide personal actions in civil law in two specific scenarios:
1. Actions brought by one clerk against another clerk.
2. Actions brought by an outsider against a clerk.

If a judgment in these cases was delivered by a *maitre des requetes*, it was considered authoritative. Such judgments could only be contested through a civil petition presented before the ancient council of the Basoche.

## Organizational Varieties and Expansion
Several different Basoches existed, some of which claimed antiquity or independence:
* **The Chatelet of Paris:** This entity had its own special Basoche, which claimed to be older than the Basoche of the Palais de Justice, leading to disputes between the two regarding certain rights.
* **The Cour des Comptes of Paris:** The clerks of the *procureurs* at this institution had a Basoche of great antiquity known as the "empire de Galilee."
* **Local Basoches:** The Basoche of the Palais de Justice originally held the right to create provostships in localities within the jurisdiction of the parlement of Paris, resulting in the creation of various local basoches.
* **Independent Basoches:** Some organizations were independent in their origin, including the Basoche of the parlement of Toulouse and the "regency" of Rouen.
