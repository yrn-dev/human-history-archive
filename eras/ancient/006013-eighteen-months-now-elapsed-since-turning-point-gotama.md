# The Career of Gotama

## The Turning Point and Early Ministry
The career of Gotama reached a significant turning point during his great struggle under the Bo tree. Eighteen months elapsed following this event before the biographical accounts transition from a strict chronological order to a collection of disconnected stories. These narratives typically focus on the Buddha or the individuals he encountered, often serving to illustrate a specific memorable saying or act. While these accounts are precise regarding the locations of these events, they remain indistinct concerning the exact timing, making a full chronological arrangement of his biography difficult.

Following the start of his mission, Gotama spent forty-five years itinerating within the valley of the Ganges. His travels were generally limited to a radius of approximately 250 miles from Benares. During the rainy months, he typically remained in one location, often staying at *viharas* (homes) that had been provided to his society.

## Disciples and Opposition
During the twentieth year of his mission, Gotama's cousin, Ananda, became a mendicant. From that point forward, Ananda served as a constant companion to the Buddha, providing personal service driven by love and reverence.

Another cousin, Devadatta, the son of the raja of Koli, also joined the society. However, Devadatta became envious of the teacher. He influenced Ajatasattu—who had become the king of Rajagaha after killing his father, Bimbisara—to persecute Gotama. Although the specific details of how the Buddha overcame the devices of Devadatta and Ajatasattu are considered legendary, records indicate that Ajatasattu initially opposed the new sect before eventually converting.

## Teachings and the Path to Nirvana
Gotama taught that there is only one way to achieve true wisdom: the path laid down in his system. He stated that those who follow this path can conquer the anger, pride, and lust of their own hearts, thereby becoming free from wrong belief, doubt, and ignorance. By entering a calm state of universal kindliness, followers can reach Nirvana even during their current life. Gotama asserted that these teachings were based on his own experience, noting that he had striven for pure and perfect wisdom and found Nirvana after following the good path since the age of twenty-nine.

## Final Days and Passing
In his final hours, Gotama was visited by Subhadra, a brahmin philosopher from Kusinara. Subhadra sought to ask if the six great teachers knew all laws or if some remained unknown or only partially known. Gotama informed him that it was not the time for such discussions. Deeply affected by the dying teacher's manner and words, Subhadra requested immediate admission to the society. Although a rule existed requiring a four-month probation for followers of rival systems, Gotama granted Subhadra's request for immediate entry.

As he approached death, Gotama provided final guidance to his disciples. He instructed them that although he would pass away, he would not truly leave them, as they possessed his laws, his explanations of the deep truths, and his words to serve as their guide. He urged his followers to show reverence toward one another and rebuked a disciple for speaking indiscriminately. In his final moments toward the morning, he offered to clear up any remaining doubts regarding the society, the law, or the Buddha. When no one answered, he emphasized the importance of the final perseverance of the saints.
