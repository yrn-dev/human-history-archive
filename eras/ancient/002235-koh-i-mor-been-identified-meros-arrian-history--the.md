# Koh-i-Mor

## Topography and Identification
Koh-i-Mor is a well-known peak located in the Bajour region. It forms the culmination of a mountain spur from the Kunar range that curves eastwards and is visible from the Peshawar valley. In historical and geographical studies, Koh-i-Mor has been identified as the Meros described in the history written by Arrian. According to these accounts, Meros was a three-peaked mountain from which a god issued.

## The City of Nysa
At the foot of Koh-i-Mor, Alexander found the Nysaean colony and the ancient city of Nysa. Tradition states that this colony was founded by Dionysus. 

## The Nysaeans and the Kafir Community
A section of the Kafir community of Kamdesh continues to claim a Greek origin, similar to the claims made by the Nysaeans. This community maintains that they originally migrated from the Swat country to their current habitat in the lower Bashgol. They continue to chant hymns to the god who sprang from Gir Nysa, which is the mountain of Nysa.

Historically, the Kafirs or Nysaeans maintained their presence in the lower Kunar valley and Bajour during a period when Buddhism had spread to Dir, Gilgit, Swat, and Chitral. During this time, the Peshawar valley was recognized as a center for Buddhist learning and Ningrahar was filled with temples and monasteries; however, Buddhism apparently never prevailed in the areas held by the Nysaeans.

## Later Historical Encounters
In the early years of the 16th century, the invader Baber, who wrote extensively about Bajour, likely fought the Nysaeans/Kafirs in this region. This occurred during Baber's journey to establish the Mogul dynasty in India, centuries after Mahmud had crushed Buddhism in northern India.
