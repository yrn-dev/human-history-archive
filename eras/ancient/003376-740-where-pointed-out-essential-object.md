# Bowen v. Foreign & Colonial Gas Company, 22 W.R. 740

## Legal Context and Ruling
*Bowen v. Foreign & Colonial Gas Company*, 22 W.R. 740, is a legal case that addressed the nature of the banker's lien and its application to the discounting of bills. Prior to this ruling, it had been contended that the specific attribute of a banker's lien permitted a banker to hold a customer's funds against liabilities arising from current discounted bills.

The court ultimately disposed of this contention. The ruling pointed out that the essential object of a customer who discounts bills with their banker is to feed the current account. Consequently, the court determined that a possible liability does not constitute a set-off against a debt that already exists.

## Banking Practices Regarding Bills
The discounting of bills is categorized as an incident of ordinary banking practice, alongside the making of advances to customers (via arranged overdraft or definite loan) and the keeping of deposit accounts. In the specific act of discounting bills, there is little that differentiates the position of a banker from that of an ordinary bill-discounter.

Determining whether a particular bill was taken for the purpose of collection or for discount is treated as a question of fact.

## Statutory Protection and Third Parties
Bankers do not possess any statutory protection regarding the collection of bills, similar to the lack of protection provided in the payment of bills. When dealing with third parties, a banker can only rely on their own title as a holder for value or the title of the customer. To do so, the banker must be able to establish a consideration and ensure that no forged endorsement has intervened.
