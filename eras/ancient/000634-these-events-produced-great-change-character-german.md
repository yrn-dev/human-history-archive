# The Evolution of the German Opposition and National Character

The character of the German opposition underwent a significant transformation beginning in 1870, shifting toward a movement that was increasingly and avowedly racial. During this period, the primary objective of this opposition became the preservation and extension of German nationality, which was placed at the forefront of their political programme.

## Origins and the University of Vienna
The roots of this movement can be traced to 1870, when a group of young Germans emerged who expressed little interest in constitutionalism or what they termed "legal mummies." Instead, they focused exclusively on their own nationality. In Austria, this movement found its early center at the university of Vienna. Students there formed a *Leseverein* (reading club) to serve as a point of cohesion for Germans, though this organization was eventually suppressed.

## Political Leadership and Influence
Herr von Schoenerer served as the first representative of this movement within parliament. He openly declared that Germans looked forward to a union with the German empire. The movement was heavily influenced by figures outside of Austria; specifically, Bismarck was regarded as their national hero. The anniversary of Sedan became their political festival, and the desire for approximation to Germany was viewed as more important than the maintenance of Austria.

## The United Left and Linguistic Demands
Following 1878, racial feeling intensified among the Radicals. This culminated in 1881 when all German opposition parties merged into a club known as the United Left. The programme of the United Left emphasized the defence of the position of Germans as a necessary condition for the existence of the state. Furthermore, they demanded that the German language be expressly recognized as the official language.

## Internal Conflict and Fragmentation
The movement experienced internal friction between younger, more ardent spirits and the older constitutional leaders. The younger members complained that the party leadership lacked sufficient decisiveness regarding measures for self-defence. 

A turning point occurred in 1885 when the government forbade great festivities in Graz intended to honor Bismarck's eightieth birthday. The Germans of Styria expressed indignation that the party did not respond to this prohibition with enough energy. Consequently, after the elections of 1885, the Left broke apart into two separate factions.

## Broader Societal and Political Context
The growing national animosity associated with these changes added bitterness to political life. This environment destroyed the possibility of establishing a strong, homogeneous party upon which a government could depend.

Later conflicts, such as the war of 1914, continued to shape the political problems and the fate of the Germanic world.
