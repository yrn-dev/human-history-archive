# ASIATIC DORIANS

The Asiatic Dorians were a group of Dorian Greeks who established settlements on the west coast of Asia Minor. Their presence in the region is characterized by a network of cities, including a specific grouping known as the Hexapolis. While they shared a common ancestral identity with the Dorians of the Peloponnese, their social and political development differed significantly from the militant structures found in European Dorian states like Sparta.

## Settlements and the Hexapolis
The Asiatic Dorians are primarily associated with the Hexapolis, though other Dorian settlements existed outside of this group. Notably, the city of Halikarnassus was excluded from the Hexapolis. The home of these people is simply identified as Caria. 

The distribution of these colonies is linked to various Peloponnesian origins. The rest of Asiatic Doris is traced back to Troezen in Argolis or Epidaurus. Specifically, Kôs, Kalydna, and Nisyrus are described as colonies of Epidaurus. Rhodes and Halikarnassus are frequently treated as colonies of Argos, though Halikarnassus is also sometimes cited as a colony of Troezen, or a joint colony of Argos and Troezen. In the case of Halikarnassus, it is recorded that the colonists expelled the native Leleges and Caras. Additionally, Knidus is identified as a colony of both Sparta and Argos.

## Early History and the Homeric Period
There is evidence to suggest that some Asiatic Dorian settlements were established very early. Rhodes and Kôs are included in the Catalogue of the Iliad, suggesting they were Greek before the Aeolic or Ionic settlements. The Iliad mentions Herakleids from these locations: Tlêpolemus from Rhodes and the brothers Antiphus and Pheidippus from Kôs. 

While the Homeric Catalogue does not explicitly use the term "Dorian"—as the name may not have served as a distinctive class-name at that time—it is believed that Doric migrations by sea to Rhodes and Crete may have occurred prior to the time of the Iliad. This is supported by the possibility that the combat between Tlêpolemus and Sarpêdôn in the epic reflects real historical contests between the Rhodians and their neighbors, the Lykians.

## Social and Political Structure
Unlike the Dorian states in the Peloponnese, the Asiatic Dorians did not maintain the rigid, isolated military camp structure seen in Sparta. Sparta was characterized by a professional soldier aristocracy supported by agricultural and industrial serfs. In contrast, the Asiatic Dorians and other Dorian colonies did not differ appreciably in their social or political history from their Ionian and Aeolic neighbors.

However, some settlements exhibited a significant mixture of populations. Halikarnassus was particularly remarkable for being half Dorian and half Karian, and it was governed by a line of Karian despots.

## Military History and Conquest
During the conquest of south-western Asia Minor by Harpagus, the Doric Greeks of Halikarnassus and Knidus were subdued along with the Karians, Kaunians, and Lykians. The defense of Knidus was described as languid, and the inhabitants submitted easily. This lack of steadfast courage is attributed not to their Dorian identity, but to the mixture of indigenous and Hellenic populations common in Asiatic colonies.

## Historical and Legendary Context
The origin of the Dorians is a subject of historical criticism. Traditional legends describe a Dorian invasion, but the Homeric poems (12th-10th centuries) only mention Dorians in Crete, using the obscure epithet *trichaikes*. Furthermore, the geographical catalogue in the *Iliad* ignores northern Doris entirely.

Some theories suggest that the diagrammatic genealogy of the "sons of Hellen" was a post-Homeric attempt to classify the linguistic and political divisions of the west coast of Asia Minor, where the names Doric, Ionic, and Aeolic correspond to specific territorial divisions. In European Greece, the Dorian states were often ruled by exclusive aristocracies, leading to theories that the Dorians were either Achaean exiles or people of northerly ancestry who gained predominance through violence.
