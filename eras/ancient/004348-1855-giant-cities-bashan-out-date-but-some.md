# Bashan

Bashan is a region located east of the Jordan River toward its source, generally situated north of the territory of Gilead. Its boundaries are not precisely defined, though Mount Hermon is referred to as its northern limit.

## Geography and Physical Characteristics
The region is volcanic in origin, featuring the Jebel ed-Druz, which consists of a group of extinct volcanoes. This volcanic soil is noted for being extraordinarily fertile, with reports that it can yield barley a hundred-fold and wheat eighty-fold. Historically, the area was famous for its oaks, which are mentioned alongside the cedars of Lebanon; these oaks were used to manufacture oars. The region is also noted for its mountains and was once said to be inhabited by lions.

## Historical and Biblical Accounts
The name Bashan first appears in Hebrew history during the wanderings of the Israelites. According to the book of Numbers, after the defeat of Sihon, king of the Amorites, the tribes traveled through the land of Bashan. There, they were met at Edrei by King Og, who was defeated and slain. Biblical accounts describe Og as having a gigantic stature and a notable "bedstead," details which some regard as suggestive of legend. Og is identified as a remnant of the aboriginal Rephaim, a race distinguished by their lofty stature. The Rephaim were established in Ashteroth-Karnaim, an important city of Bashan.

Following the conquest of the land, the territory was allotted to the eastern division of the tribe of Manasseh. During the reign of Solomon, the region of Argob in Bashan—described as containing sixty great cities with brazen bars and walls—was assigned to Ben-Geber, one of Solomon's lieutenants. The last historical event regarding Bashan in the Old Testament occurs during the days of Jehu, when the country was taken from Israel by Hazael, king of Syria.

In prophetic and poetical texts, Bashan is associated with its cattle, which were proverbial for their ferocity. The wealthy women of Samaria who oppressed the poor are referred to as "kine of Bashan."

## Administrative Divisions and Cities
Josephus enumerates four provinces within Bashan:
*   **Gaulanitis:** Represented by the modern Jaul[=a]n, extending from the Jordan lakes to the Haj Road. It was divided into two sections, Sogana and Gamalitis.
*   **Trachonitis:** Adjoining the territory of Damascus, this corresponds to the modern district of the Lej[=a] (formerly known as Trach[=o]n).
*   **Auranitis:** The Hauran of Ezekiel and modern Arabs, located south of the Jaul[=a]n and north of Gilead.
*   **Batanaea:** Located east of the Hauran plain and the Lej[=a], including the Hauran mountain (Jebel ed-Druz).

Among the cities of Bashan, Golan served as one of the cities of refuge established by Moses for those who committed involuntary manslaughter. Other identified or proposed sites include Edrei (equated with Ed-Dera'a), Ashteroth Karnaim (identified by some with Busrah, Tell Ashari, or Tell 'Ashtera), and Salecah (associated with the ruin of Salkhat in the Hauran). Porter also identified Kenath with Kanawat.

## Archaeological and Modern Status
There has been significant dispute regarding the identification of ancient sites. While the "giant cities of Bashan" were once identified with remains in the Hauran and Lej[=a], it is now understood that these sites are Greco-Roman and date no earlier than the time of Herod. Consequently, they have no connection to the more ancient periods of Og or the Israelites. No tangible traces of Og's people or their Israelite supplanters have been found, although the subterranean city at Ed-Dera'a is of an unknown date and may be of great antiquity.

In later history, Bashan became known as a refuge for robbers and outlaws. Strabo mentioned a great cave in Trachonitis capable of holding 4,000 robbers; Arab tradition associates this cave with the home of Job. The region was also the center of the Ghassanid dynasty and remains a principal habitation for the Druses. To prevent the area from remaining a refuge for fugitives avoiding taxation or conscription, the local sub-governor partially destroyed the subterranean city at Ed-Dera'a.
