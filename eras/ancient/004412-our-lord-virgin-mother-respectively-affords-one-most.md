# Our Lord and the Virgin Mother

In the context of early Christian art and architecture, the figures of Our Lord and the Virgin Mother are depicted as the focal points of sacred processions. A primary example of this is found in the basilica of S. Apollinare Nuovo in Ravenna, Italy. This church was originally an Arian structure erected by Theodoric between 493 and 525. The building measures 315 feet in length and 115 feet in breadth, featuring a nave 51 feet wide. 

Within this basilica, a continuous band of mosaic ornamentation is located below the windows. This artwork depicts a stately procession of saintly figures, with males on one side and females on the other. These figures advance toward Our Lord and the Virgin Mother respectively, creating one of the most beautiful examples of mosaic ornamentation found in any church.

## Theological and Historical Perspectives

Historical accounts regarding the family of Jesus Christ reflect varying interpretations of the relationship between his parents and his siblings. Early understandings suggested that the brothers of Jesus—specifically Jude, Simon, and James—were the lawful children of Joseph and Mary. However, a desire to maintain a devout respect for the virginity of the mother of God led to different interpretations. 

The Gnostics and later the orthodox Greeks proposed that Joseph had a second wife. The Latins, following the time of Jerome, asserted that Joseph practiced perpetual celibacy. Under this interpretation, the individuals styled as the brothers of Jesus Christ were instead his first cousins.
