# Third Year of the War—Investment of Plataea—Naval Victories of Phormio

## The Investment and Fall of Plataea
The events surrounding Plataea occurred ninety-three years after the city became an ally of Athens. During the conflict, the Lacedaemonians and their allies conducted a blockade of the city. Prior to this, the Lacedaemonians had invited the Plataeans to remain neutral, citing an original covenant made by Pausanias following the defeat of the Mede. This offer of neutrality was refused by the Plataeans.

Following the siege, the Lacedaemonian judges permitted the Thebans to question the Plataeans individually on whether they had provided any service to the Lacedaemonians and their allies during the war. Upon the Plataeans answering that they had not, they were executed. This massacre included no fewer than two hundred Plataeans and twenty-five Athenians who had been part of the siege. The women of the city were taken as slaves.

The Thebans initially granted the city to surviving Plataeans of their own party and political emigrants from Megara for approximately one year. Subsequently, the city was razed to its foundations. Using the roofs and doors of the Plataeans, the Thebans constructed an inn measuring two hundred feet square on the precinct of Hera. They further dedicated couches made from the brass and iron of the city walls to Hera and built a stone chapel for her measuring one hundred feet square. The land was confiscated and leased to Theban occupiers for ten years. The Lacedaemonians adopted this adverse attitude toward Plataea primarily to appease the Thebans, who were considered useful in the ongoing war.

## Lacedaemonian Mobilization
Immediately following the affair at Plataea, Lacedaemon issued orders to the cities of the Peloponnese and the rest of her confederacy. These cities were commanded to prepare provisions and troops for a foreign campaign intended to invade Attica. The states assembled at the Isthmus, with each city providing a contingent consisting of two-thirds of its total force. The expedition was led by the Lacedaemonian king, Archidamus, who convened the principal officers, generals, and persons of the various states to exhort them.
