## Biography of Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier
Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier (1823–1908) was a French classical scholar and the secretary of the French Academy. Born in Nimes on August 15, 1823, Boissier was early attracted to the study of ancient history by the Roman monuments in his hometown, leading him to specialize in epigraphy.

His professional career began as a professor of rhetoric at Angouleme, where he worked for ten years. Following a lecture heard by a university travelling inspector, he was called to Paris to serve as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne. Boissier's early literary contributions included a thesis on the poet Attius (1857) and a study of M. Terentius Varro (1861). In 1861, he was appointed professor of Latin oratory at the College de France and became a contributor to the *Revue des deux mondes*.

## Major Works of Gaston Boissier
Boissier is noted for his ability to reproduce the characteristics of ancient Roman society with vivacity. In 1865, he published *Ciceron et ses amis*, a work of erudition that achieved significant success. In 1874, he released a two-volume work titled *La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins*, which provided an analysis of the religious movement in antiquity that occurred before the acceptance of Christianity. He also wrote *L'Opposition sous les Cesars* (1875), which depicted the political decadence of Rome during the reign of the early successors of Augustus.

Due to his standing among scholars and men of letters, Boissier was elected a member of the French Academy in 1876 following the death of H. J. G. Patin. He was later appointed as the perpetual secretary of the Academy in 1895.
