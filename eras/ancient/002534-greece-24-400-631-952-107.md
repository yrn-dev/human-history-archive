# Greece

## Geography and Land Use
The kingdom of Greece encompasses a total area of 47,600,000 stremas, which is equivalent to 12,000,000 English acres. The landscape is divided between non-arable and arable land; 26,500,000 stremas consist of forests, lakes, rivers, rocks, and mountains. The remaining 21,000,000 stremas are designated as arable land, which includes currant grounds, olive and vineyard grounds, and land fit for cultivation, though only a relatively small portion of this is currently cultivated. It is noted that the modern kingdom of Greece does not include Thessaly.

Historically, the term "hollow" (κοιλὸς) has been applied to several primary Grecian states, including Lacedaemon, Elis, and Argos. Additionally, the fertility of Boeotia has been recorded in historical fragments and texts.

## Demographics and Political Status
As of 1909, Greece was listed as one of the political divisions of the Balkan Peninsula. During this year, the region had an area of 24,400 square miles and a population of 2,631,952. This resulted in a population density of 107.8 people per square mile.

## Historical Figures and Intellectual Contributions
Greece has been the origin of significant philosophers and political thinkers. Plato is noted for his work *Laws* and his contributions to philosophy. Isokrates, writing around 380 B.C., composed a Panegyrical Oration characterized by patriotic eloquence. This work was intended to encourage Athens and Sparta, acting as the joint chiefs of Greece, to end their internal dissensions and unite in a Pan-hellenic manifestation against a common enemy abroad.

## Military and Political History
The history of Greece involves various expeditions and conflicts with external powers. Following the peace of Antalkidas, there were efforts by leading Spartans and King Agesipolis to organize a new Grecian expedition against Asia, prompted by disaffected subjects of Artaxerxes.

In other eras, Greeks were engaged on both sides during the reconquest of Egypt by Ochus. This period also saw the appointment of Mentor to the satrapy of the Asiatic coast, an event that followed the successful invasion of Egypt. Additionally, the battle of Platea is recorded as a significant event in the region's history.

## Byzantine and Later Influence
In the 10th century, Greek emperors maintained influence over various Mediterranean regions by conferring the title of *upatoV* (or consuls) upon the dukes of Naples, Amalfi, and Venice. 

Later records mention the presence of Greek priests; in one instance, a Greek priest's ignorance and bigotry led him to reject paintings by Titian, claiming the figures were "scandalous" and resembled a "group of statues."
