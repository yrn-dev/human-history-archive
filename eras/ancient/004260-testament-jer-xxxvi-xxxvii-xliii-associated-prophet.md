# Baruch

## Identity and Role
Baruch is a figure appearing in the Old Testament, specifically within the books of Jeremiah (chapters xxxvi, xxxvii, and xliii). The name Baruch is of Hebrew origin and means "Blessed." Within the biblical narrative, he is identified as the spokesman and secretary to the prophet Jeremiah.

## Literary and Linguistic Analysis
The text associated with Baruch has been the subject of significant scholarly debate regarding its original language. Various theories exist:
* Some scholars, including Koenig, Rothstein, Davidson, Kneucker, and Ewald, maintain that the entire book was originally written in Hebrew.
* Others, such as Toy, Schuerer, Gifford, Reuss, Hilgenfeld, and Fritzsche, argue that while sections i. through iii. 8 were originally Hebrew, the remainder of the text was originally Greek.
* Marshall proposes a more complex origin, suggesting that i. through iii. 8 was translated from Hebrew, iii. 9 through iv. 4 from Aramaic, and the rest of the work from Greek.
* A fourth view, held by Noeldeke, Havernick, and Bertholdt, suggests the Greek text is the primitive version, though this view is considered unworkable by some.

Evidence for a Hebrew original in the first sections (i. through iii. 8) is supported by the presence of Hebraisms. For example, the Greek phrase *hou ... ep' autoi* corresponds to the Hebrew *'SHR ... `LYW* (ii. 26), and *hou ... ekei* corresponds to *'SHR ... SHM* (ii. 4, 13, 29; iii. 8).

## Relationship to the Book of Jeremiah
Analysis of the text suggests a close link between the translation of Baruch and the translation of the Book of Jeremiah. Scholars have noted that certain meaningless expressions in the Greek text are actually mistranslations of Hebrew words that appear in both works. 

One such example is found in Baruch i. 9, where the Greek word *desmotes* ("prisoner") is used to translate the Hebrew *MASGEIR*, which should have been rendered as "locksmith." This identical mistranslation is found in Jeremiah xxiv. 1 and xxxvi. (xxix.) 2. Similarly, the Greek *abaton* ("wilderness") in Baruch ii. 4 is a misrendering of the Hebrew *SHMH*, a mistake that occurs several times in Jeremiah. Additionally, the Greek *ergazesthai* is used in Baruch i. 22 and ii. 21, 22, 24 to translate the Hebrew *`BD* in the sense of "serving," where *douleuein* would have been the correct rendering; this same error appears in Jeremiah xxxiv. (xxvii.) 11 and xxxvii. (xxx.) 8. Finally, the Greek *exothen* in the phrase *poleon Iouda kai exothen Ierousalem* is a misrendering of the Hebrew *BCHWTSWT*, as seen in Jeremiah xi. 6, xl.
