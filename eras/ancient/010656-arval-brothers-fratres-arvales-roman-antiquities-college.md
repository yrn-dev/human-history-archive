# ARVAL BROTHERS

The Arval Brothers (*Fratres Arvales*) were a priesthood or college in Roman antiquities. The college consisted of twelve members who were elected for life from the highest ranks of Roman society. During the period of the empire, the membership apparently always included the emperor.

## Duties and Religious Functions
The primary responsibility of the Arval Brothers was to offer public sacrifices annually to ensure the fertility of the fields. Their religious duties were specifically aimed at protecting crops and herds by keeping away enemies of all kinds. In their ancient hymns, they invoked the god Mars, whom they referred to as the "fierce Mars" (*fere Mars*).

The brotherhood conducted a major annual festival in May lasting three days. This festival was held in honor of the anonymous Dea Dia, a deity who was likely identical to Ceres.

## History and Origins
Legend suggests that the college was founded by Romulus. This tradition likely stems from the connection between the Lares and Acca Larentia (the *mater Larum*), as the Lares played a role in the ceremonies of the Arvales. Evidence of the college's high antiquity is found in the verbal forms of a song used in their ceremonies, which remained preserved into late times and suggests the college may have been older than Rome itself.

While the members of the college were always persons of distinction and the office was held in high respect, literary mentions of the brotherhood are scarce; notably, neither Livy nor Cicero mentions them. However, a significant historical record exists in the form of *acta* (minutes of proceedings) inscribed on stone. Excavations conducted between the 16th and 19th centuries in the grove of the Dea Dia, located approximately five miles from Rome, recovered 96 of these records dating from A.D. 14 to 241. These records indicate that the brotherhood likely languished in obscurity during the Roman Republic before being revived by Augustus.

## Organization and Ritual
Under Augustus, the college was organized with a specific hierarchy:
*   A master (*magister*)
*   A vice-master (*promagister*)
*   A *flamen*
*   A *praetor*
*   Eight ordinary members

The president and a *flamen* were elected for one-year terms, and other members were chosen via co-optation on the motion of the president. The priesthood was attended by various servants, most notably four chorus boys. These boys were required to be the sons of senators and must have had both parents alive. During ceremonies, the participants wore a white fillet, a *praetexta*, and a wreath of corn.
