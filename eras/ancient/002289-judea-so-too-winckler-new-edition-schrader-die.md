# Judea

## Geographical Perspectives and the Southern Frontier
In the study of the southern frontier of Judea, scholars have debated the identification of specific geographical markers mentioned in historical and religious texts. A point of contention involves the "River" (*nahar*) associated with Pethor. A. Jeremias, in *Das Alte Testament im Lichte des alten Orients*, supports the view held by Marquart that this "River" is more accurately described as a "Ravine" (*nahal*) of Egypt or Musri, located on the southern frontier of Judea. This perspective is also shared by Winckler in the new edition of E. Schrader's *Die Keilinschriften und das Alte Testament*.

Conversely, traditional interpretations have maintained the ordinary sense of *nahar* as a river, specifically identifying it as the Euphrates. Under this view, Pethor is identified with a *Pitru* located on a tributary of the Euphrates, as mentioned in an inscription of Shalmaneser II. Furthermore, the text of Deut. xxiii. 4 places Pethor within Mesopotamia.
