# Herod's Kingdom and Relations with Caesar

## Confirmation and Expansion of the Kingdom
Herod obtained his kingdom through the Romans and reigned for thirty-seven years from the time he was made king. His relationship with Caesar was characterized by mutual favor and strategic generosity. Caesar confirmed Herod's kingdom and bestowed upon him a diadem, issuing a decree that commended him in a magnificent manner. In return, Herod cultivated this friendship through the provision of magnificent presents.

The bond between the two was further strengthened during Caesar's journey to Egypt through Syria. Herod provided royal and rich entertainments, feasting Caesar and his friends, and ensuring the army was well-supplied with food and water during their march to Pelusium and their subsequent return. Because of this generosity, Caesar and his soldiers believed Herod's kingdom was too small for the scale of his presents. Consequently, after the deaths of Antony and Cleopatra, Caesar enlarged Herod's kingdom. He restored the territories that Cleopatra had taken from him and added Gadara, Hippos, Samaria, and the maritime cities of Joppa, Anthedon, Gaza, and Strato's Tower. Additionally, Caesar provided Herod with a personal guard of four hundred Galls who had previously served Cleopatra.

## Administrative Appointments and Further Territories
Herod's influence extended beyond his immediate kingdom. Caesar appointed him as a procurator of all Syria, a position established such that other procurators could not administer affairs without his advice. 

Following the first games at Actium, Herod's territory was expanded further to include Auranitis, Batanea, and the region of Trachonitis. This expansion occurred after Varro, the president of Syria, cleared Trachonitis of robbers who had been sent by Zenodorus to attack the Damascenes. Caesar bestowed this land upon Herod to prevent it from becoming a receptacle for robbers again. Later, upon the death of Zenodorus, Caesar gave Herod all the land situated between Galilee and Trachonitis.

## Internal Affairs and Security
Despite his prosperous public fortune and the acquisition of power from a private man, Herod was described as a most unfortunate man in his domestic affairs. His reign also involved managing security threats and political intrigues. For example, an Arabian named Sylleus attempted to oppose Herod by bribing Phabatus, Herod's steward. However, Herod offered Phabatus a larger sum, inducing him to abandon Sylleus. Phabatus subsequently revealed that Sylleus had bribed Corinthus, a member of Herod's body guard, to assassinate the king. This led to the arrest and torture of Corinthus and two other Arabians, who confessed to the plot before being sent to Rome after examination by Saturninus, the president of Syria.

## Succession and the Division of the Kingdom
Herod died after reigning for thirty-four years since the slaying of Antigonus. In his testament, he appointed Caesar as the arbitrator to confirm the succession. Following his death, Ptolemy read the king's epistle and testament to the soldiers at the amphitheater in Jericho. The testament designated Philip to inherit the neighboring countries of Trachonitis, while Antipas was to be tetrarch and Archelaus was made king.

The final distribution of the kingdom was determined by Caesar after hearing testimonies from various parties, including accusations against Archelaus brought by Sabinus and Salome. Caesar divided the kingdom as follows:
* **Archelaus:** Appointed as Ethnarch over Idumea, all Judea, and Samaria (though Samaria received a one-quarter tax reduction for not revolting). His territory included Jerusalem, Sebaste, Joppa, and Strato's Tower, with a revenue of four hundred talents.
* **Philip:** Given Batanea, Trachonitis, Auranitis, and parts of Zeno's house near Jamnia, with a revenue of one hundred talents.
* **Antipas:** Given Perea and Galilee, with a revenue of two hundred talents.

Caesar removed the Grecian cities of Hippos, Gadara, and Gaza from the kingdom, adding them to Syria. Salome was made mistress of Phasaelis, Ashdod, and Jamnia, and was given the royal palace of Ascalon, totaling a revenue of sixty talents. Herod's two virgin daughters were granted five hundred thousand drachmae of silver and married to the sons of Pheroras. Caesar also reserved a small portion of the thousand talents bequeathed to him by Herod as presents, distributing the rest among the offspring.

## Legacy and Later Restorations
The lineage of Herod continued to interact with Roman authority. Later, the emperor Claudius confirmed the kingdom of Agrippa (Herod's grandson), restoring to him all the territories over which Herod had once reigned, specifically Samaria and Judea. Claudius further expanded this by granting him Abila of Lysanias and territories at Mount Libanus.
