# History of the District of Craven

The district of Craven is a region in England associated with the manor of Bolton Abbey, located in the West Riding of Yorkshire. The area is characterized by the upper Wharfe, where the valley is closely enclosed by hills and beautifully wooded.

## Bolton Abbey and Bolton Priory
Bolton Abbey is a village situated 5-1/2 miles from Ilkley and 22 miles northwest of Leeds. The village derives its name from Bolton Priory, a great foundation whose ruins are noted for their exquisite situation on the right bank of the upper Wharfe. 

The architecture of the church reflects different periods of construction. The earliest sections are of transitional Norman date, while the nave is described as perfect and is of Decorated and Early English styles. The choir and transepts are currently in ruins, and the remaining domestic buildings are slight.

## Land Ownership and Tenure
The manor of Bolton Abbey, along with the broader district of Craven, was granted to Robert de Romili by William the Conqueror. Although the Domesday survey contains no mention of the manor, Robert de Romili evidently held it in 1086.

In 1120, Cicely de Romili (the heiress of Robert) and her husband, William de Meschines, founded and endowed a priory at Emmesay (or Embsay), located near Skipton. This priory was later moved to Bolton in 1151 by their daughter, Alice de Romili. Alice, the wife of William FitzDuncan, provided the manor to the monks in exchange for other lands.

Following the dissolution of the monasteries, the manor was sold in 1542 to Henry Clifford, the 2nd earl of Cumberland. The manor is currently held by the descendants of Henry Clifford, the dukes of Devonshire.
