# Congress of the Peloponnesian Confederacy at Lacedaemon

The Congress of the Peloponnesian Confederacy at Lacedaemon served as a critical diplomatic forum for the states aligned with Lacedaemon (Sparta) during the period of escalating tensions and subsequent conflict with Athens. These gatherings were used to address grievances, manage alliances, and coordinate military strategies.

## Antecedent Grounds of Complaint
Prior to the outbreak of war, significant friction existed between Athens and the Peloponnesians. Corinth lodged complaints that its colony of Potidæa, along with Corinthian and Peloponnesian citizens residing there, were being besieged. Conversely, Athens accused the Peloponnesians of inciting a member of the Athenian alliance—a town that contributed to Athenian revenue—to revolt. Athens further alleged that the Peloponnesians were openly fighting on the side of the Potidæans. Despite these conflicts, a truce remained in place for a time, as the actions at Potidæa were viewed as a private enterprise by Corinth.

## Composition of the Confederacies
The Lacedaemonian confederacy consisted of all Peloponnesians within the Isthmus, with the exception of the Achaeans and Argives, who initially remained neutral. Among the Achaeans, Pellene was the first city to join the war, eventually followed by others. Outside the Peloponnese, the confederacy included the Phocians, Boeotians, Locrians, Megarians, Anactorians, Leucadians, and Ambraciots. Resource contributions were specialized: the Eleans, Pellenians, Sicyonians, Megarians, Corinthians, Leucadians, and Ambraciots provided ships, while the Locrians, Phocians, and Boeotians provided cavalry. All other member states contributed infantry.

The Athenian confederacy included the Lesbians, Chians, Plataeans, Corcyraeans, Zacynthians, and the Messenians in Naupactus, as well as most Acarnanians. It also encompassed tributary cities in Ionia, the Hellespont, Caria upon the sea (including Dorian neighbors), the Thracian towns, and the Cyclades, excluding Thera and Melos. In terms of resources, Corcyra, Lesbos, and Chios provided ships, while the remaining allies provided money and infantry.

## Diplomatic Maneuvering and the Role of Argos
The city of Argos occupied a complex position in Peloponnesian politics. Fearing they might be left isolated after a separate alliance was formed between Lacedaemon and the Boeotians, and seeing the demolition of Panactum, the Argives grew concerned that the entire confederacy would shift toward Lacedaemon. They feared that Athens—a resource they had previously counted on due to existing dissensions—was no longer an open ally. Consequently, the Argives sent Aeson and Eustrophus as envoys to Lacedaemon to negotiate the best possible treaty to ensure they would be left in peace, fearing the combined opposition of the Athenians, Boeotians, Tegeans, and Lacedaemonians.

Lacedaemon viewed a friendship with Argos as a long-term desire that would facilitate the conduct of war outside the Peloponnese. This was evidenced by the actions of the ephors Cleobulus and Xenares, who privately advised the Corinthians and Boeotians to encourage an alliance between the Boeotians and Argos, and subsequently between the Argives and Lacedaemon.

## Internal Peloponnesian Rivalries
The interior states of the Peloponnese were marked by perpetual rivalry, most notably between Mantineia and Tegea in Arcadia. Tegea, governed oligarchically, maintained a tenacious attachment to Sparta. Mantineia, characterized by a democratic government, was less attached to Lacedaemon, though it remained a member of the confederacy. 

Mantineia had established a small empire of subject allies in Arcadia, a conquest made even during the war with Athens. This created tension, as Mantineia feared Lacedaemonian interference on behalf of these subjects. While Sparta had previously been hindered by embarrassments following the disaster in Sphakteria, it eventually used the pretext of these subjects to march into Arcadia under King Pleistoanax. The Lacedaemonians laid waste to the Parrhasian country, razed the fortress of Cypsela, and made the Parrhasians independent.

## Military Preparations and Strategy
Following the break of the treaty after the affair at Plataea, Lacedaemon ordered its confederates to prepare provisions and troops for an invasion of Attica. The states assembled at the Isthmus, with each city providing two-thirds of its total force. King Archidamus led this expedition, convening the principal officers and generals of the member states.

To bolster its naval strength, Lacedaemon ordered states in Sicily and Italy that had declared for her to build vessels, aiming for a total of five hundred ships. These cities were assigned quotas based on their size and required to provide specific sums of money; until the fleet was ready, they were instructed to remain neutral and permit single Athenian ships into their harbors.
