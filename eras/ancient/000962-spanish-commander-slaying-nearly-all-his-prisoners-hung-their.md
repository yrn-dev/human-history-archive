# The Florida Campaign of Pedro Menéndez de Avilés

## Establishment of the Spanish Colony
In 1565, Philip II of Spain sought to prevent French Huguenots from establishing a colony in Florida. The Spanish claimed the region as their own, viewing the area as strategically vital because its position on the trade route from Mexico made it dangerous if held by another power. To secure the territory, Philip II entered into an *asiento*, or contract, with a seaman named Avilés on March 20, 1565. Under the terms of this agreement, Philip II advanced 15,000 ducats to Avilés, naming him the proprietor of any colony he could establish in Florida, provided the funds were repaid.

Avilés departed for Florida on July 28, 1565, leading a force of 1,500 men accompanied by ten sloops and one vessel of 600 tons. Upon his arrival on August 28, he entered and named the Bay of St Augustine, where he began the construction of a fort.

## Conflict with French Huguenots
The Spanish presence in Florida led to immediate conflict with the French Huguenots. On September 20, 1565, Avilés captured the French post known as Fort Caroline. Following this victory, in October of the same year, Avilés encountered and exterminated a group of Frenchmen led by the Huguenot Jean Ribault, who had arrived on the Florida coast to provide relief to their colony.

After the conflict, the Spanish commander slew nearly all of his prisoners. Their bodies were hung from trees with inscriptions stating, "Not as Frenchmen but as Lutherans." Avilés' own letters indicate that he viewed the Frenchmen as heretics and pirates, and he believed that no honest man could suggest he had gone too far in the massacre.

## Retaliation and Later Years
The massacre was later avenged in 1568 by a French sea-captain named Dominique de Gourgues. Gourgues captured Fort San Mateo—the name the Spanish had given to the former Fort Caroline—and hung the Spanish garrison. He placed inscriptions on the bodies that read, "Not as Spaniards but as murderers."

Avilés remained active in Florida until 1567, when he returned to Spain. He later made one additional voyage to Florida before his death on September 17, 1574. In his personal life, Avilés married Maria de Solis at a young age and fathered three daughters.
