# Academica Priora and the Villa of Hortensius

The *Academica Priora* is a work by Cicero. In the first chapter of its second book, Cicero provides a high eulogium of Lucullus, describing him as a man of letters, a brave soldier, a skilful general, and a person of talent and taste. While Lucullus was known for his mild temper and humanity, he was also fond of luxury and wealth.

## The Villa at Bauli
The second book of the *Academica Priora* features a dialogue that took place at the villa of Hortensius. This villa was located in the resort of Bauli, situated near Baiae. The resort of Bauli derives its name from the Greek *Boaulia* (meaning stalls), referring to the place where Hercules concealed the oxen of Geryon. 

There are differing identifications regarding the modern location of Bauli. Some identify it with the modern village of Bacoli, located 2 miles south-southeast of Baiae, based on a presumed similarity in the names. Others, such as G. B. de Rossi in *Notizie degli scavi* (1888), identify it with the Punta dell' Epitaffio, which is 1 mile northeast of Baiae.

## Ownership and Imperial History
The area of Bauli was home to villas owned by both Pompey and Hortensius. While Pompey's villa was situated on the hills, the villa of Hortensius was located on the shores of the Lacus Lucrinus. The latter was noted for its tame lampreys.

Following its time as the property of Hortensius, the villa became imperial property. It later became the scene of the murder of Agrippina by Nero. Additionally, the region is noted for the bridge of boats constructed by Caligula, which extended from Bauli to Puteoli.
