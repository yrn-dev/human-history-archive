# Regeneration

The term regeneration appears across various disciplines within the archive, encompassing theological concepts of spiritual rebirth, geological processes of rock transformation, biological theories of cellular recovery, and political ideals of national renewal.

## Theological Perspectives
In the context of the New Testament, the idea of regeneration occurs seldom. In the conversation with Nicodemus (John iii. 3-8), spiritual re-birth is insisted upon in verses 3, 6, 7, and 8. While verse 5 mentions "of water and," some analysis suggests these words offend the context, and Justin Martyr appears to omit them when citing the verse. Similarly, in John i. 13, the oldest text represents Christ as being begotten of God, rather than of blood, the will of man, or the will of the flesh, without mentioning water. 

The First Epistle of Peter (i. 3, 23) states that God the Father begat the saints anew unto a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus, and that they were begotten again through the word of God using incorruptible seed rather than corruptible seed. It remains unclear if the writer of these passages intended water baptism or any specific rite as the occasion for this regeneration. Some interpretations suggest the conversation with Nicodemus represents a protest against a late 1st-century tendency to replace the free afflatus of the spirit and the gift of prophecy with formal sacraments.

In early Christian practice, Justin Martyr (c. 140) described a process where those persuaded by the faith fasted and prayed for the remission of past sins. They were then brought to water to be "regenerated" in the name of the Holy Spirit, the Saviour Jesus Christ, and God the Father, the Lord of the universe, receiving a washing with water. Other ancient religions also practiced similar rites; Tertullian noted that the mysteries of Apollo and Eleusis, as well as the votaries of Mithras and Isis (via a font), utilized baptism for regeneration and exemption from the guilt of perjuries.

In later American religious history, specifically during the "Great Awakening" (1740-1743), the "Separate" or "New Light" churches insisted on personal regeneration as a requirement for membership. This conviction led some to conclude that administering baptism to unconscious infants was inconsistent with the requirement for regenerate membership and lacked Scriptural warrant.

## Geological Regeneration
In geology, regeneration refers to the process by which a rock mass is transformed without large-scale fusion. This is particularly evident in hornfelsed rocks, where the action of pressure, heat, and permeating vapours regenerates the rock. This process is often sufficient to replace former minerals with new ones and efface most original structures.

The resulting structure is often characterized as *pflaster* or pavement structure, where small grains fit together like a mosaic. While some have argued that this involves chemical change—such as "felspathization" through the introduction of matter from granite—most current authorities regard these regenerative changes as purely physical. In some districts, hornfelsed rocks acquire a schistose structure through shearing, forming transitions to gneisses and schists, including kyanite mica schists, cordierite and sillimanite gneisses, and cipolins (schistose calc silicate rocks).

## Biological and Technical Applications
In biology, specifically regarding the production of antitoxin, the "side-chain" (*Seitenkette*) theory proposed by Ehrlich describes antitoxin production as an instance of regeneration after loss. According to this theory, living protoplasm (a biogen molecule) consists of a central atom group (*Leistungskern*) with secondary atom groups or side-chains. Toxin molecules attach to these side-chains; because these combined side-chains are lost for the cell's purposes, they are thrown off. The introduction of fresh toxin then repeats the process, leading to the regeneration of side-chains.

In industrial contexts, the term is used in the form of "regenerators." These are air heaters used at modern destructor stations to produce a hot-air blast for furnaces.

## Political Regeneration
In the political sphere, the concept of regeneration has been applied to the state. The democrat Kosciuszko maintained that a republic could only be regenerated through the establishment of absolute equality before the law and absolute liberty.
