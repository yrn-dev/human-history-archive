# Balsaminaceae, Ebenaceae, Jasmineae, and Cyrtandraceae

## Distribution in South India
The plant families Balsaminaceae, Ebenaceae, Jasmineae, and Cyrtandraceae are among the orders that are most largely developed in south India, though they are found more sparingly in other regions. Despite their development in this area, few of these families contain as many as 100 species that are peculiar to India.

## Regional Characteristics of Balsaminaceae
Within the eastern region, the Balsaminaceae are considered rare. In contrast, they are very abundant in the peninsula.

## Cyrtandraceae and Mountain Flora
Various forms of Cyrtandraceae are noted as part of the herbaceous vegetation found in more rainy regions. Some of these forms extend beyond the tropic and ascend into the temperate zones located on the mountains.

## Comparative Flora of the Peninsula and Ceylon
A distinct connection exists between the flora of Ceylon and the peninsula and that of eastern tropical Africa. This is evidenced by the identity of genera and families and the similarity of many truly tropical forms. Most remarkably, the mountain flora of this part of Africa bears a likeness to that of the peninsula, with several species believed to be identical to Abyssinian forms.

This connection is further supported by the absence of cycads, conifers, and oaks from both areas. The absence of the first two families is a notable feature of the flora of Ceylon and the peninsula, particularly because conifers and oaks are abundant to the north and east in mountains that reach similar elevations. Additionally, many of the principal forms found in the eastern flora are either comparatively rare or entirely absent in Ceylon and the peninsula.
