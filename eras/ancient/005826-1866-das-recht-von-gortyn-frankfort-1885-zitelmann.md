# Das Recht von Gortyn and Related Historical Contexts

## Das Recht von Gortyn
*Das Recht von Gortyn* is a work published in Frankfort in 1885, authored by the German classical scholar Franz Bücheler in collaboration with Zitelmann. The work pertains to the study of Greek law, specifically relating to the great inscription of Gortyn. The text of this inscription was first published in 1885 by E. Fabricius in *Ath. Mitth.* ix. (362-384). 

Further scholarly analysis of the Gortyn legal materials includes a contribution by Bucheler and Zittelmann in *Rhein. Mus.* xl. (1885), and a work by Hermann Lipsius titled *Zum Recht von Gortyns*, published in Leipzig in 1909. Other relevant references for the study of these legal inscriptions include Comparetti's *Leggi di Gortyna* (1893) and the *Inscr. juridiques grecques* (1894) by Dareste, Haussoullier, and Th. Reinach. An English translation of the text was provided by Roby in the *Law Quarterly Review* (1886).

## Franz Bücheler
Franz Bücheler (1837-1908) was a successful German classical scholar and commentator. Born on June 3, 1837, in Rheinberg and educated at Bonn, he held a series of professorships: first at Freiburg in 1858, then at Greifswald in 1866, and finally at Bonn in 1870. In 1878, he became a joint-editor of the *Rheinisches Museum für Philologie*.

Bücheler produced numerous editions and original works, including:
* *Frontini de aquis urbis Romae* (1858)
* *Pervigilium Veneris* (1859)
* *Petronii satirarum reliquiae* (1862, with a 3rd edition in 1882)
* *Grundriss der lateinischen Deklination* (1866)
* *Hymnus Cereris Homericus* (1869)
* *Q. Ciceronis reliquiae* (1869)
* *Herondae mimiambi* (1892)

Additionally, he supervised the 1893 third edition of O. Jahn's *Persii, Juvenalis, Sulpiciae saturae*.

## Historical Events of 1866
The year 1866 is associated with significant political changes regarding the city of Frankfort. Historically, Frankfort-on-Main was one of the few cities allowed to keep its *Reichsfreiheit* (holding directly of the Empire) following a decision by the imperial deputation in 1803. In 1815, it was recognized as a free city upon the establishment of the German Confederation. However, as a consequence of its role in the war of 1866, Frankfort lost its independence and was annexed by Prussia.
