# Larentia and the Lares

## Connection to the Arval Brothers
Acca Larentia is identified in Roman antiquities as *mater Larum*. This designation links her to the Lares, who played a role in the religious ceremonies of the Arval Brothers (*Fratres Arvales*). The Arval Brothers were a priesthood consisting of twelve members elected for life from Rome's highest ranks. During the empire, the emperor was apparently always included in this college.

The chief responsibility of the Arval Brothers was to offer public sacrifices annually to ensure the fertility of the fields. While it is generally held that Romulus founded the college, this legend likely stems from the association between the Lares and Acca Larentia as *mater Larum*.

## The Arval Brotherhood and its Rituals
Despite the legend of its founding, there is evidence of the college's high antiquity. This is reflected in the verbal forms of a song that accompanied their ceremonies into late times. Although the duties of the office were held in high respect and the members were persons of distinction, literary allusions to the brotherhood are scarce, with no mentions appearing in the works of Livy or Cicero.

Knowledge of the brotherhood is primarily derived from the *acta*, or minutes of their proceedings, which the members inscribed on stone. Excavations conducted between the 16th and 19th centuries in the grove of the Dea Dia, located approximately five miles from Rome, uncovered 96 of these records dating from A.D. 14 to 241.

## Organization and Festivals
The brotherhood appears to have been revived by Augustus after a period of obscurity during the republic. Under Augustus, the college's structure included:
* A master (*magister*)
* A vice-master (*promagister*)
* A *flamen*
* A *praetor*
* Eight ordinary members

The president and a flamen were elected for one-year terms, and other members were chosen via co-optation on the president's motion. The brotherhood was attended by various servants, including four chorus boys who were sons of senators with both parents still living. During ceremonies, these individuals wore a white fillet, a wreath of corn, and the *praetexta*.

The Arval Brothers conducted a major annual festival in May lasting three days. This festival was held in honor of the anonymous Dea Dia, who was likely identical to Ceres.

## Representation of the Lares
In the context of Roman art, the Lares are represented visually, though the specific artistic details are not elaborated upon in the available records.
