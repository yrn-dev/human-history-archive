# The Earl of Bristol

## Character and Abilities
The Earl of Bristol was a prominent and conspicuous figure of his era, recognized for his brilliant abilities and skill as a great orator. He was noted for his capacity to distinguish himself without effort in any field of activity he chose to pursue. However, these natural gifts were undermined by an instability of character and a restless ambition, traits that were considered fatal to achieving real greatness.

Clarendon described Bristol as the only man he had ever known who possessed such "incomparable parts" yet remained "none the wiser for any experience or misfortune that befell him." He was further recorded as having an extraordinary facility for both making friends and creating enemies.

## Contradictions and Public Life
Horace Walpole characterized Bristol as a "singular person" whose life was defined by contradiction. His public and private actions often stood in direct opposition:
* **Religion:** He wrote against popery but eventually embraced it. Despite being a Roman Catholic, he spoke in favor of the Test Act.
* **Politics:** He acted as a zealous opposer of the court, yet became a sacrifice for it.
* **Legal and Personal Conduct:** While he was conscientiously converted during his prosecution of Lord Strafford, he was described as being most unconscientiously a persecutor of Lord Clarendon.

Walpole further noted that Bristol's "great parts" frequently resulted in harm to himself and his friends, and that despite possessing "romantic bravery," he was consistently an unsuccessful commander. Additionally, on the birthday of true philosophy, he addicted himself to astrology.

## Literary and Intellectual Works
Bristol was a prolific writer and orator. His written contributions include:
* ***Apologie* (1643):** A work found in the Thomason Tracts used to justify his support of the king's cause.
* ***Elvira ... a comedy* (1667):** A play printed in R. Dodsley's *Select Collect. of Old English Plays*.
* ***Worse and Worse*:** An adaptation of a Spanish work that was acted but not printed.
* ***The Adventures of Five Hours* (1663):** A work he is credited with authoring alongside Sir Samuel Tuke.

His eloquent and pointed speeches were frequently printed and can be found in the *Biog. Brit.* and the *Thomason Tracts*. A catalogue of his personal library was published in 1680.

## Personal Life
The Earl of Bristol married Lady Anne Russell, the daughter of Francis, the 4th earl of Bedford. The couple had two daughters and two sons. One son, Francis, died unmarried before his father. The other son, John, succeeded him as the 3rd earl of Bristol.
