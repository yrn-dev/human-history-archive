# Alexandria

Alexandria served as a significant center for early Christian thought, literary production, and the development of scriptural tradition. The city is associated with several prominent early figures and the high valuation of specific religious texts.

## Early Christian Figures and Traditions
Tradition identifies Barnabas as an evangelist in Alexandria, a claim supported by the *Clementine Homilies* (i. 8, ii. 4; i. 9 seq.). Early accounts from Clement of Alexandria also regard Barnabas as one of the seventy. 

Other influential figures associated with the city include Origen (who died in 253) and Dionysius of Alexandria. Origen is noted for his vast range of knowledge and travels, which led him to maintain wider limits for the biblical Canon. Later in the 3rd century, Dionysius of Alexandria utilized acute criticism to justify a specific Alexandrian dislike of the Apocalypse.

## The Epistle of Barnabas
The *Epistle of Barnabas*, an apocryphal book of the New Testament, found its greatest authority in Alexandria. The text was treated with high regard by the Alexandrian Church, which is evidenced by the following:
* Clement commented on the epistle in his *Hypotyposes* in the same manner he did for canonical scriptures.
* Origen cited the work in the same spirit as scripture in *De Principiis* and *C. Celsum*.
* Clement ascribed the work to "the prophet" or "the apostle" Barnabas, specifically referencing Paul's fellow-apostle.

While the epistle appears in the 4th-century *Codex Sinaiticus* and an 11th-century manuscript containing the *Didache*, internal evidence suggests that the ascription to the apostle Barnabas is impossible. It is suggested that the Alexandrian Church attributed the work to him because the writing was so highly favored.

## Theological and Chronological Perspectives
Alexandrian writers contributed to early debates regarding the ministry of Christ and the dating of the Crucifixion. Clement of Alexandria, in *Stromata*, supported the tradition of a single-year ministry (i. 145, vi. 279). Additionally, Clement of Alexandria, along with Origen, identified the year of the Crucifixion as Tiberius 15.

## Literary and Poetic Influence
Beyond religious history, Alexandria was a center for poetry. Alexandrine poets modified older mythical legends; for example, they introduced the motive of love into the story of the temporary servitude of the gods, portraying the servitude of Apollo as voluntary, as seen in the works of Kallimachus.
