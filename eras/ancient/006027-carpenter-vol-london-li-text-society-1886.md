# Sumangala Vilāsinī

## Overview
The *Sumangala Vilāsinī* is a work edited by J. E. Carpenter and T. W. Rhys Davids. Volume I of this text was published in London in 1886 by the Pāli Text Society. It is listed within the archive's records as a source related to the study of Buddhism, a religion followed by the followers of the Buddha that covers a large area in central and east Asia, as well as India.

## Context within Buddhist Literature
The *Sumangala Vilāsinī* is categorized among the authorities and documents used to understand the system of Buddhism. It exists alongside other significant Pāli and Sanskrit texts used to reconstruct the life and teachings of the Buddha. These include the *Mahāvaṃsa*, the *Buddhghosuppatti*, and the "Gandhavaramsa" (published in the *Journal of the Pāli Text Society* in 1886).

The archive notes that the essential doctrines of Buddhism are preserved in official reports of the Buddha's discourses. These discourses, which the Buddha used to explain the main features of his system to the first five men he sought to win over to his faith, are incorporated into two divisions of the sacred books: the *suttas* (which contain the doctrine) and the *Vinaya* (the rules of the order or society he founded). Specifically, this summary is found in *Samyutta*, v. 421 and *Vinaya*, i. 10.

## Related Canonical Authorities
The *Sumangala Vilāsinī* is part of a broader body of Pāli canonical literature that reached its current form before the 4th century B.C. Other primary authorities on the life of the Buddha include:

*   **Majjhima Nikāya**: Contains texts regarding the birth of the Buddha and his "first long episode," which covers his going forth, years of penance and study, the attainment of Buddhahood and Nirvāna, and the conversion of his first five converts.
*   **Anguttara Nikāya**: Contains texts regarding the Buddha's birth and his youth at home.
*   **Sutta Nipāta**: Contains old ballads regarding the adoration of the babe and the "going forth."
*   **Dīgha Nikāya**: Includes a prose account of the "going forth" and the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta*, which details the Buddha's last days.
*   **Vinaya**: Provides accounts of the conversation with the first five converts, the end of the first year of teaching, and a visit to Kapilavastu.

Additionally, Buddhist Sanskrit texts such as the *Mahāvastu* (likely 2nd century B.C.) and the *Lalita Vistara* (likely 1st century B.C.) provide further accounts of the Buddha's life up to his first sermon.
