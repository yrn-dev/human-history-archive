# Asher

## Origins and Etymology
Asher was a tribe of the sons of Israel, named after the son of Jacob and Zilpah, who was the maid of Leah. According to the narrator of Genesis xxx. 12 seq. (J), the name Asher is intended to mean "propitious" or "happy." This naming may serve as an allusion to the fertility of the territory held by the tribe, a connection also suggested in Deuteronomy xxxiii. 24 and Genesis xlix. 20. Alternatively, the name may have originally functioned as a divine title, similar to the name Gad.

## Geography and Population
The territory of the tribe of Asher was located to the north of Zebulun and Issachar, to the south of Dan, and bordered Naphtali. Despite these general placements, the references to the tribe's territory are considered obscure and the boundaries are not definite. 

Because the tribe was situated in close proximity to the Aramaeans and Phoenicians, its population was exceptionally mixed. Evidence from Judges i. 31 seq. regarding the occupation of Palestine indicates that the tribe contained a strong Canaanite element. In the Blessing of Moses, the tribe is bidden to defend itself, which is evidently a reference to protecting the territory against invasion.

## Military History
The historical record of Asher's military involvement is varied. In the account found in Judges v. 17, Asher is blamed for failing to take part in the fight against Sisera. However, other accounts, specifically Judges vi. 35 and vii. 23, state that Asher shared in the defeat of the Midianites alongside Naphtali and Zebulun under Gideon. It is noted that the narrative mentioning Asher's participation in Gideon's victory is not the older of the two accounts of that event; consequently, the inclusion of Asher's name in that specific record is likely the result of a late redactor.
