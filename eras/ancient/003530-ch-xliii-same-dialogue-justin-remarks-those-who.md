# Justin Martyr

Justin Martyr was an early Christian author known for his dialogues and apologies. His writings provide insight into early Christian rites, the relationship between the early church and the Mosaic law, and the legal challenges faced by Christians under the Roman Empire.

## The Dialogue with Trypho
Justin authored a "curious" work titled the *Dialogue with Trypho* (also referred to as the *Dialogue with the Jew Tryphon*). This conference took place at Ephesus during the reign of Antoninus Pius, approximately twenty years after the church of Pella returned to Jerusalem.

In this text, Justin addresses the nature of salvation and the Mosaic law. He notes that some individuals believed the observance of the Mosaic law was necessary for the salvation of heathen converts and refused social interaction with those who did not conform. Justin himself held a different view, freely admitting those who kept the law to Christian communion.

Regarding the authorship of the Gospels, Justin’s *Dialogue with Trypho* contains an early indication of the belief that Luke was the author of the Gospel attributed to him. While Justin typically used the formula "Apostolic Memoirs," in one instance he refers to memoirs composed by "the Apostles and those that followed them."

## Rites of Baptism and Illumination
Justin provides detailed descriptions of the Christian rite of baptism, which was regularly called "illumination" (*photismos*) because those who learned the associated teachings were illuminated in their understandings. 

The process for one who chooses to be born again and has repented of his sins involves being led to the laver by a person who pronounces the name of God the Father and Lord of the universe. Justin asserts that the name of the ineffable God cannot be uttered. The person is washed in the name of the Holy Ghost, who foretold all things about Jesus through the prophets, and in the name of Jesus Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate.

Justin contrasts this practice with the rites of ablution used by the Israelites (Bani Israel), arguing that only Christian baptism can purify those who have repented. He describes Christian baptism as the "water of life," while characterizing the cisterns dug by others as broken and profitless. He argues that baptism which cleanses only the flesh and body is of little use, suggesting instead that one must baptize the soul from hatred, envy, and wrath to achieve true purity of the body. Additionally, Justin remarks that those who approach God through Jesus Christ receive a spiritual circumcision, rather than a carnal one, following the manner of Enoch.

## Legal Persecutions and the Roman State
In his second apology, Justin documents the legal treatment of Christians. He describes instances of legal delay granted to accused Christians, though other accounts suggest such delays were not general practice. 

Justin records a specific instance involving a judge named Urbicus. A Christian named Lucius questioned Urbicus for punishing a man who had committed no crime other than avowing himself a Christian. Upon Lucius admitting that he too was a Christian, the judge ordered his immediate execution. A third person present was condemned to be beaten with rods. Justin expressly charges judges with ordering the execution of the accused without hearing the cause.

## Theological Views
Justin's writings include testimony regarding his own faith and that of his orthodox brethren in the doctrine of a Millennium. This is delivered in a solemn and clear manner in his *Dialogue with Trypho*.
