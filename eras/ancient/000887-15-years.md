# Periods Exceeding Fifteen Years

The historical record contains numerous instances of reigns, lifespans, and chronological cycles that extend beyond fifteen years, spanning the political history of ancient Mesopotamia, the genealogical records of early humanity, and the development of various calendar systems.

## Ancient Mesopotamian Dynasties

The records of Babylonia and Assyria detail several dynasties where individual rulers held power for durations exceeding fifteen years.

### The First Dynasty of Babylon
Within this dynasty, multiple kings had lengthy reigns. Sumu-la-ilu ruled for 36 years, while Khammurabi ruled for 43 years. Other significant reigns include Samsu-iluna (38 years), Samsu-ditana (31 years), Abesukh (25 years), Ammi-ditana (25 years), Ammi-zadoq (21 years), Sin-muballidh (20 years), and Abil-Sin (18 years).

### The Kassite Dynasty
This dynasty consisted of 36 kings who ruled for a total of 576 years and 9 months. Specific rulers with reigns over fifteen years include Agum-sipak (22 years), Bitilyasu I (22 years), Burna-buryas (22 years), Kuri-galzu III (26 years), Nazi-Maruttas (17 years), and Hadad-sum-uzur (30 years).

### Other Babylonian Dynasties
In the Dynasty of Sisku, which lasted 368 years, several kings had extensive reigns: Anman (60 years), Ki-Nigas (56 years), Gul-ki[sar] (55 years), Kirgal-daramas (50 years), [=A]-dara-kalama (28 years), Akur-duana (26 years), and Damki-ilisu (26 years). In the Dynasty of Isin, Merodach-nadin-akhi ruled for 22 years and another Merodach ruled for 18 years. The Dynasty of the Sea-coast included Simbar-sipak, who ruled for 18 years, and the Dynasty of Bit-Bazi included [=E]-Ulmas-sakin-sumi, who ruled for 17 years.

## Early Human Lifespans and Governance

According to the records provided by Josephus, the earliest humans lived for centuries. Adam lived 930 years, and Seth lived 912 years. Enos delivered government to his son Cainan, who lived 905 years. Malaleel lived 895 years, and Jared lived 962 years. 

Governance was also held for extended periods: Mathusela retained government for 969 years, Lamech governed for 777 years, and Noah retained government for 950 years.

## Biblical Chronology of the Israelites

The period from the Exodus to the foundation of the Temple is noted in 1 Kings vi. 1 as consisting of 480 years (or 440 years in the LXX). This timeline includes several periods of independence and oppression.

Specific durations mentioned include:
*   The wilderness period: 40 years.
*   The judgeships of Othniel, Barak, Gideon, and Eli: 40 years each.
*   The judgeship of Ehud: 80 years.
*   The period of Jephthah and five minor judges: 76 years.
*   The judgeships of Samson and Samuel: 20 years each.
*   The reign of David: 40 years.

## Calendar Cycles and Astronomical Periods

Various civilizations developed cycles and systems to track time over many years.

### Solar and Lunar Cycles
The Solar Cycle, or Cycle of the Sun, is a 28-year period that restores the first day of the year to the same day of the week. In the Hebrew calendar, a cycle of 19 years is used to determine the distribution of embolismic years.

### The Mongol and Chinese Cycle
The Mongol and Chinese systems utilize a 60-year cycle of compound names. This is created by combining a 12-year cycle of earthly branches (such as Rat, Ox, and Tiger) with a 10-year cycle of celestial stems (based on elements, colors, cardinal points, or specific terms).

### Calendar Reform and Error
The Julian calendar, which fixed the mean length of the year at 365¼ days, contained an error that resulted in the equinox retrograding. This error amounted to one day every 128 years (though Hipparchus had previously suggested one day in 300 years). By the time of the Gregorian reformation in 1582, the equinox had shifted from March 25th (during Caesar's time) to March 21st (by the council of Nice in 325) and finally to the 11th. Pope Gregory XIII corrected this by suppressing ten days and adjusting the leap year rule for centenary years.
