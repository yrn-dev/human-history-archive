# Winckler

## Chronological Frameworks (1894)
In the study of ancient dynastic timelines, Winckler provided a specific chronological framework in 1894. According to this system, the durations of the first three dynasties are categorized as follows:

*   **Dynasty I:** This period is listed as (2425–2120) B.C.
*   **Dynasty II:** This period spanned from 2120 to 1752 B.C.
*   **Dynasty III:** This period spanned from 1752 to 1177 B.C.

These dates represent one of several competing scholarly interpretations of early dynastic history. For comparison, Winckler's own subsequent work in 1892 proposed a different timeline (2403–2098 B.C. for Dynasty I, 2098–1730 B.C. for Dynasty II, and 1729–1150 B.C. for Dynasty III), and a further revision in 1905 estimated the periods as approximately 2400–2100 B.C., 2100–1700 B.C., and 1700–1150 B.C.

## Comparative Chronologies
The 1894 dates provided by Winckler differ from those proposed by other historians of the era. For instance, Oppert (1888) placed the start of Dynasty I at 2506 B.C., while Sayce (1899 and 1902) suggested 2478 B.C. and 2460 B.C. respectively. Other scholars such as Rogers (1900) suggested a Dynasty I range of 2454–2451 B.C., and Maspero (1897) suggested 2416–2082 B.C.

Regarding the transition between the second and third dynasties, Winckler's 1894 date of 1752 B.C. is shared by the 1891 estimates of Delitzsch and the 1901 estimates of Hommel. This contrasts with the dates provided by Lehmann-Haupt (1898), who placed the transition at 1689/1688 B.C., or Rost (1897), who placed it at 1928 B.C.
