# Herod's Conquest of Sepphoris and the Subjugation of the Cave Robbers

## Military Campaigns in Idumea and Galilee
During a period when the Romans were at rest from war, Herod engaged in several military operations to secure his territories. He first seized Idumea, maintaining control of the region with four hundred horsemen and two thousand footmen, a task he entrusted to his brother Joseph to prevent any innovations by Antigonus. Following this, Herod moved his mother and his relations from Masada to Samaria. Once they were securely settled, he marched to take the remaining portions of Galilee to remove the garrisons established by Antigonus.

## The Capture of Sepphoris
During this time, the country experienced great disturbances, leading many to attempt to establish themselves as kings. In Sepphoris, a city in Galilee, a man named Judas—the son of the arch-robber Ezekias, who had previously been subdued by Herod—gathered a large multitude. Judas broke into the royal armory, armed his followers, and seized the remaining money in an ambitious attempt to obtain royal dignity through injuries and extravagance.

Herod reached Sepphoris during a period of very great snow. He took the city without difficulty, as the guards fled before the assault. Because Sepphoris possessed an abundance of necessaries, Herod's followers were able to refresh themselves after their distress.

## The Battle Against the Cave Robbers
Following the capture of Sepphoris, Herod turned his attention to robbers who inhabited caves and overran much of the country, causing mischief comparable to a war. Herod initially sent one troop of horsemen and three cohorts of footmen to the village of Arbela; he arrived himself with the remainder of his forces forty days later.

The robbers met Herod in arms, possessing the skill of warriors combined with the boldness of robbers. In the resulting pitched battle, the robbers' right wing put Herod's left wing to flight. However, Herod wheeled around from his own right wing to assist, forcing his left wing to return and attacking the pursuers. This maneuver broke the courage of the robbers, who then turned and fled. Herod pursued them, slaying a great number until the survivors were scattered beyond the river Jordan.

## Siege of the Mountain Caves
While the main force of robbers was defeated, some remained concealed in caves, requiring more time to be conquered. These caves were located in abrupt mountains characterized by precipices and sharp rocks, where the robbers lived with their families. Because the ascent was too sharp to climb and the descent too difficult to creep, Herod employed a specialized engineering solution.

He had chests constructed and bound with iron chains, which were lowered from the mountain tops by an engine. These chests contained armed men equipped with provisions and long hooks. The soldiers used darts to kill those at the mouths of the caves and used the hooks to pull out resistors and tumble them down the precipices. Some soldiers even climbed out of the chests to enter the caves directly. Through these means, Herod subdued the caves and the robbers within them.

## Aftermath and Administration
After the victory, Herod distributed the spoils of the robbers to his soldiers, giving each man one hundred and fifty drachmae of silver, with larger sums given to the commanders. He then sent his troops to their winter quarters. He tasked his youngest brother, Pheroras, with providing a market for provisions for the soldiers and rebuilding the desolate city of Alexandrium.

To prevent further sedition in the region, Herod left a portion of his army in the area under the generalship of Ptolemy before returning to Samaria with three thousand footmen and six hundred horsemen to face Antigonus. Following Ptolemy's departure, some who raised tumults in Galilee attacked and killed Ptolemy and laid the country waste. Upon learning of this, Herod returned, destroyed many of the seditious elements, raised the sieges of the fortresses, and exacted a penalty of one hundred talents from his enemies.

Later, Herod further secured the region by building a wall around Sepphoris, establishing it as the metropolis of Galilee.
