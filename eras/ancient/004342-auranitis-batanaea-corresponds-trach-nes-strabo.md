# Auranitis and Batanaea

Auranitis and Batanaea are two of the four provinces of Bashan, as enumerated by Josephus. These regions, along with Gaulanitis and Trachonitis, comprise the historical divisions of the Bashan area.

## Auranitis
Auranitis is identified as the Hauran mentioned in Ezekiel xlvii. 16 and is known to modern Arabs as the Hauran. Geographically, it is situated north of Gilead and south of the Jaul[=a]n. While strangers may loosely apply the name to the entire region east of the Jaul[=a]n, local usage restricts the name to the narrow strip on the west and the plain south of the Lej[=a].

The province's limits meet the desert, and its chief city was Bostra (also referred to as Bosra). Bostra served as the metropolis of a province named Arabia. Auranitis was among the territories bestowed by the emperor Claudius upon Agrippa, in addition to his paternal kingdom and other countries previously granted to Herod by Augustus.

## Batanaea
Batanaea is the fourth province of Bashan. It is located east of the Hauran plain and the Lej[=a]. This region includes the Hauran mountain, also known as Jebel ed-Dr[=u]z. The name of the province is preserved in the modern designation *'Ard el-Bathaniyeh*.

## Related Territories and the Trachones
Auranitis and Batanaea are closely associated with the neighboring province of Trachonitis. Trachonitis adjoined the territory of Damascus as well as Auranitis and Batanaea. This region corresponds to the *Trach[=o]nes* described by Strabo and is represented by the modern district of the Lej[=a]. Evidence for this identification is found in inscriptions within the Lej[=a] that list Trach[=o]n as the former name of the area. Like Auranitis, Trachonitis was among the lands granted to Agrippa by Claudius.

## Historical Context of Bashan
The broader boundaries of Bashan can be deduced from early historical records. These records mention that Og dwelt at Ashteroth and fought the Israelites at Edrei. The "region of Argob," containing threescore cities, is noted within this area, with Mt. Hermon serving as a northern limit and Salecah mentioned as one of the cities. These details are confirmed in the books of Joshua and Deuteronomy.
