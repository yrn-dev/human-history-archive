# La Fille d'Eschyle

*La Fille d'Eschyle* is a tragedy written by the French poet Joseph Autran. The play was performed at the Odeon in 1848, where it achieved great success.

## Author Biography
Joseph Autran was a French poet born in Marseilles on June 20, 1813. His literary career was significantly influenced by the poet Lamartine; in 1832, Autran addressed an ode to Lamartine while the elder poet was visiting Marseilles on a journey to the East. Lamartine persuaded Autran's father to support the young man's poetic ambitions, and Autran remained a faithful disciple of Lamartine thereafter.

Due to the success of two of his early volumes—*La Mer* (1835), which was later remodelled in 1852 as *Les Poemes de la mer*, and *Ludibria ventis* (1838)—Autran was appointed as the librarian of his native town. In 1868, he was elected as a member of the French Academy. He died in Marseilles on March 6, 1877.

## Literary Works
In addition to the tragedy *La Fille d'Eschyle*, Autran produced a diverse body of work:

*   **Rural and Peasant Life:** His work *Vie rurale* (1856) consists of a series of pictures depicting peasant life. He also wrote *Epitres rustiques* (1861).
*   **Military Themes:** Inspired by the Algerian campaigns, Autran wrote verses honoring common soldiers. These include *Milianah* (1842), which describes the heroic defence of that town, and *Laboureurs et soldats* (1854).
*   **Other Poetry:** His other publications include *Paroles de Salomon* (1868) and *Sonnets capricieux*.

A definitive edition of Joseph Autran's collected works was published between the years 1875 and 1881.
