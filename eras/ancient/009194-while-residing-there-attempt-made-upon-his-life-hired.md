# Hired Services and Political Violence

The use of hired individuals for military, political, and criminal purposes has been a recurring feature across various historical contexts, ranging from the employment of mercenaries in ancient warfare to the use of assassins and paid agents for political sabotage.

## Mercenaries in Ancient Greece and Sicily
In the history of Syracuse, the ruler Dionysius relied heavily on hired soldiers to maintain his dominion. Following the flight of Imilkon and the Carthaginians from Syracuse, Dionysius faced significant opposition from his own mercenaries, who threatened his downfall due to a lack of pay. To resolve this, Dionysius seized the Spartan Aristoteles, the commander of the mercenaries. He pacified the disgruntled soldiers by offering them the territory and town of Leontini in exchange for their arrears. Approximately ten thousand mercenaries accepted this bribe and relocated to the fertile soil of Leontini. Dionysius subsequently hired new mercenaries to replace them, trusting the maintenance of his power to these new hires, liberated slaves, and potentially Iberians or Spaniards who had previously served the Carthaginians.

Similarly, in the context of the Peloponnesian War, the Acarnanian Evarchus sought to be restored to Astacus. He persuaded the Corinthians to provide forty ships and fifteen hundred heavy infantry, while he himself hired additional mercenaries to support the effort.

## Hired Assassins and the Fidáwí
In later historical periods, the *Fidáwí*, or Ismailite adepts, operated as hired assassins. Their services were utilized by various leaders; for example, Bibars boasted in a letter to his court at Cairo of employing them when necessary. A Mahomedan author specifically attributes the instigation of an attempt on the life of Prince Edward to Bibars. These assassins were organized under a hierarchy including a "Grand Master" or Shaikh residing at Alamut, with "Grand Priors" overseeing divisions in Syria, ’Irák, and the Kuhistan of Eastern Persia. Other accounts suggest their presence in Asia Minor and Kurdistan, near the Lake of Wan.

## Political Sabotage and Familial Betrayal
The use of hired agents has also extended to personal and political sabotage. In the accounts of Judea, Antipater engaged in a campaign of deception against his own family. While in Judea and prior to traveling to Rome, he paid money to have letters written in Rome against his brethren. To conceal the funds used for hiring these writers, Antipater intentionally confused his financial accounts by purchasing expensive carpets, silver and gold cups, and costly garments. He claimed these expenses, totaling two hundred talents, were related to a law-suit with Sylleus. Despite examinations by torture revealing his attempt to murder his father and the letters revealing his attempt on his brethren, he eventually returned from Rome claiming he had been dismissed with honor by Caesar.

## Economic and Social Concepts of Hire
The concept of "hire" also appears in the social engineering attempts of the French Revolution. Under the Committee of Public Safety and the influence of figures like Robespierre and St. Just, there were efforts to construct a new order of human life. This included attempts to equalize property and abolish profit. In this vision, the labourer was considered worthy of his hire, but was not entitled to an advantage. This period saw harsh laws against "profiteering" in 1793, reflecting a desire to remove the "rude incentive" of profit from human commerce.
