## Ancient Greek Military and Political Affairs

In the history of the Peloponnesian War, the Lacedaemonians proposed a peace treaty to the Athenians, believing the Athenians were desirous of a truce and would accept a peace offer to recover their men. However, the Athenians, who held the men on an island, sought further concessions. Guided by Cleon, son of Cleaenetus—a powerful leader with the multitude—the Athenians demanded that the men on the island surrender themselves and their arms to Athens. Additionally, they required the Lacedaemonians to restore Achaia, Troezen, Pegae, and Nisaea, noting these places had been ceded by Athens during a previous moment of disaster when a truce was more necessary.

During the Olynthian war, Athens faced significant expenses in its conflict against Philip, from the capture of Amphipolis in 358-357 B.C. until the peace of 346 B.C. It is stated that Athens expended at least fifteen hundred talents. Despite reluctance, Athens was obliged to act, though its efforts were described as intermittent and too little, often occurring behind time to produce satisfactory results. Funding for these efforts may have been sourced from a direct property-tax, as a proposition to use the Theôric Fund was ended by the condemnation of Apollodorus. Demosthenes urged the city to provide pay and citizens without delay, utilizing the Theôric Fund as a means of obtaining money.

In other military engagements, such as the battle at Marathon, the Athenians organized their army with the polemarch Callimachos leading the right wing, followed by the tribes in numerical order, with the Plataians on the left wing. To match the length of the Medes' front, the Athenians drew up their middle with few ranks, making the center the weakest point while strengthening the wings.

## Monarchs and Foreign Relations

The records of Herodotus describe the motivations of Croesus, who marched into Cappadocia to acquire land and seek vengeance on Cyrus for Astyages. Cyrus, son of Cambyses, had conquered Astyages, who was the king of the Medes and brother by marriage to Croesus. 

The relationship between the Medes and the Scythians is also noted. Kyaxares, son of Phraortes, initially treated the nomad Scythians well, delivering boys to them to learn the art of shooting with the bow and their speech. However, after a period where the Scythians returned from a chase empty-handed, Kyaxares treated them harshly. In retaliation, the Scythians killed one of the boys being instructed, dressed the flesh as wild game, and served it to Kyaxares and his guests before fleeing to seek protection from Alyattes at Sardis.

In the chronicles of Norway, King Magnus received a foreigner named Giparde, who presented himself as a good knight. Giparde offered his services because he understood there was something to be done in the king's dominions. King Magnus was preparing for a foray into Gautland to repay a disgrace suffered in the spring. Despite warnings from chiefs to turn back, Magnus moved against the Swedish king. During the battle at Foxerne, Giparde was missing from the array, prompting the king to compose verses questioning where the foreign knight was hiding.
