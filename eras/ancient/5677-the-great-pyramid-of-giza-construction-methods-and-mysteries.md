# The Great Pyramid of Giza: Engineering the Eternal

## Overview
The Great Pyramid of Giza, constructed for the Pharaoh Khufu during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, remains one of the most formidable achievements of human engineering. Standing as the only surviving member of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, it served as a monumental tomb designed to facilitate the king's ascent to the afterlife. 

The construction process involved the quarrying and transport of approximately 2.3 million stone blocks, weighing an average of 2.5 to 15 tons each. While the precision of the pyramid's alignment to true north is staggering, the exact methods used to lift these massive stones remain a subject of intense scholarly debate. Most Egyptologists, such as Mark Lehner, argue for the use of a complex system of ramps—either linear, zigzagging, or internal—combined with sledges and lubricated tracks. However, the lack of surviving blueprints or written records from the builders themselves means that these theories remain models rather than settled facts.

## Key Facts
*   **Composition:** The core consists primarily of local limestone, while the interior chambers (such as the King's Chamber) were lined with massive granite slabs transported from Aswan, over 500 miles away.
*   **Precision:** The base is level to within 15 millimeters, and the sides are aligned almost perfectly with the cardinal points of the compass.
*   **Labor Force:** Contrary to the Greek historian Herodotus's claim that the pyramid was built by slaves, archaeological evidence from the "Workers' Village" suggests a highly organized workforce of paid laborers and skilled craftsmen who were well-fed and housed.
*   **The Casing:** Originally, the pyramid was encased in polished Tura limestone, which would have made the structure shine brilliant white under the Egyptian sun.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Internal Void:** In 2017, researchers using cosmic-ray muon radiography discovered a previously unknown large void above the Grand Gallery, the purpose of which remains a mystery.
2. **The Weight of Granite:** Some of the granite beams above the King's Chamber weigh up to 80 tons, posing one of the greatest logistical questions in ancient history.
3. **The Eight Sides:** The Great Pyramid is not actually four-sided; the centers of the four faces are slightly concave, making it an eight-sided structure, a detail only visible from the air during the equinoxes.
