# Brickmaking and the Israelites in Egypt

## The Occupation of the Israelites
During their period of bondage in Egypt, the chief occupation of the Israelites was brickmaking. According to archival records, the Israelites were also tasked with building two store-cities for the Pharaoh, known as Rameses and Pithom.

## Materials and Composition
The bricks produced by the Israelites were likely sun-dried rather than burnt. These "adobes" were constructed using a mixture of water and river mud sourced from the banks of the Nile. Because the Nile mud lacked sufficient cohesion on its own, chopped reeds or straw were added to the mixture to serve as a binding material. 

The addition of these substances served a twofold purpose, primarily by increasing the plasticity of the wet clay. This effect was further enhanced if the mixture was permitted to stand for several days before it was used.

## The Manufacturing Process
The method used to create these sun-dried bricks involved the preparation of a bed or shallow pit. Mud, water, and chopped straw were thrown into this pit in suitable proportions and tramped on until the mass reached the proper consistence and was thoroughly mixed. Once prepared, the mixture was removed in lumps and shaped into bricks, either by hand or through the use of moulds, before being left to dry in the sun.

## Historical Context of the Exodus
The timing of the Israelites' presence and subsequent departure from Egypt is a subject of historical analysis. Excavations by M. Naville indicate that Ramses II of the 19th dynasty was the builder of Pithom, and his name suggests he was also the builder of Rameses. Consequently, the Pharaoh of the Exodus is commonly believed to have been Merenptah, the successor of Ramses II. 

Some conjectures suggest that the Israelites escaped during a period of unsettlement caused by an invasion of the Delta by other foes and a formidable body of Libyans during the fifth year of Merenptah's reign. Other data, such as correspondence from the 18th dynasty (c. 1400 B.C.) between Egyptian governors in Palestine and kings Amenophis III and IV, indicates that Palestine was still an Egyptian province at that time and had not yet come into the occupation of the Israelites.
