# Systems of Power and Inherent Defects

Throughout human history, various political, religious, and technical systems have been established, many of which contained internal flaws or "inherent vices" that contributed to their eventual decline or required significant modification. These defects range from the administrative failures of empires to the social contradictions of economic systems.

## Colonial and Imperial Intolerance
The establishment of foreign power in India saw several waves of conquest. In 1023, Mahmud of Ghazni invaded Gujarat and destroyed the national Hindu idol of Somnath. Later, Mahommed Ghori invaded Gujarat and stationed a garrison in its capital. By 1294, Emperor Ala-ud-din invaded the Deccan, followed by the conquest of Gujarat in 1297. By 1319, the whole of Malabar had fallen to these invaders. While the Delhi sovereigns eventually weakened, leading to the rise of independent kingdoms like the Bahmani kingdom in the Deccan, and later the rise of the Mahrattas in the 17th century, other foreign powers also sought control.

The Portuguese arrived in Calicut in 1498 and established a presence on the coast, capturing Goa (1510), Malacca (1511), and Ormuz (1515). They further occupied Chaul, Bassein (including Bombay), Diu, and Daman between 1531 and 1559. However, the power of the Portuguese was undermined by the inherent vices of their intolerant system before the arrival of English and Dutch rivals.

Similarly, in the Roman Empire, the moderation of conquerors often failed to appease subjects. In Jerusalem, the people's attachment to the law of Moses and their detestation of foreign religions led them to unanimously defeat Caligula's attempt to place his statue in their temple. This highlights a broader observation that intolerance appears inherent in the religious spirit when it is armed with authority.

## Administrative and Political Decay
The Roman Empire under Diocletian faced material disadvantages due to a system that increased taxes and oppressed the people. Diocletian replaced the modest establishment of Augustus and Trajan with three or four magnificent courts, multiplying the number of ministers, officers, and servants. This created a situation where the proportion of those receiving funds exceeded those contributing, leading to an intolerable burden of land taxes and capitation. While these exactions were often blamed on the personal vices of princes, they were more accurately the result of the uniform system of administration.

In later history, the "Great Power system" of Europe faced similar challenges. Between 1848 and 1914, a process of mental and political adaptation occurred as new social, religious, and political ideas entered the European mind. This intellectual process undermined the system of Grand Monarchy in France before 1789 and continued to undermine European systems of government and property liberties through the early 20th century. Additionally, monarchy showed an inherent disposition to return to past conditions, most notably in Spain, where the Inquisition was restored.

## Economic and Intellectual Systems
The transition of economic systems has also been marked by inherent flaws. One early great capitalistic system fell into chaos due to its own "inherent rottenness." Later theories regarding the "class war" suggested that capitalist adventurers, driven by inherent greed and combativeness, would concentrate the means of production into fewer hands. This process was predicted to lead to a social revolution where workers, developing class consciousness, would seize this capital, replacing private owner capitalism with state capitalism.

In the intellectual sphere, the medieval "schoolmen" in universities such as Paris, Oxford, and Bologna engaged in philosophical discussions based on Aristotle's logic. Because the dignitaries of the church were intolerant and watchful for heresy, these scholars had to adopt a severely technical form of expression, hinting at truths they dared not state openly. Their work was a necessary struggle to correct inherent defects of the human mind.

## Technical and Structural Inherent Strengths and Defects
The concept of inherent properties also extends to architecture and engineering. Norman castles utilized the inherent strength of their structures; masonry walls were designed to defy battering-rams and could only be undermined through extensive time and labor.

In music, the valve system of the bombardon possessed an inherent defect: the combination of pistons failed to provide absolutely correct intonation because the tubing was not the theoretical length required. This led to various inventions to remedy the flaw, including the six-valve independent system of Adolphe Sax and the Besson enharmonic valve system.
