# Titus Livius

Titus Livius, referred to in historical records as Livius, is identified in the archive's sources as an author of significant influence on classical education and as a source for early Roman history.

## Influence on Education
The works of Titus Livius served as a primary foundation for the study of the Latin language during the 16th century. According to the records of Roger Ascham, who served as the tutor to the princess Elizabeth for two years (chiefly at Cheshunt), Elizabeth's knowledge of Latin was derived almost entirely from two authors: Cicero and Titus Livius. Ascham noted that Elizabeth read nearly all of Cicero and a great portion of Titus Livius during her studies.

## Historical Accounts
Livius is cited as a source for specific events in early Roman history. In the accounts of Plutarch, Livius is credited with recording the story of Ahala, who served as Magister Equitum to L. Quinctius Cincinnatus, an event dated to 439 B.C.

## Other Figures Named Livius
The archive contains references to several other individuals with the name Livius, distinct from the author Titus Livius:

*   **M. Livius Drusus:** A tribunus plebis in 91 B.C. He had a sister, Livia, who was married first to M. Cato (with whom she had M. Cato Uticensis) and later to Q. Servilius Cæpio (with whom she had a daughter, Servilia).
*   **M. Livius Drusus (Consul):** The father of the aforementioned Livia and the tribunus plebis, who served as consul in 112 B.C.
*   **Livius (Political Figure):** A figure who utilized his tribunitian authority to support the senate. In an effort to outbid Caius for popular favor, he proposed the establishment of twelve colonies, each consisting of 3,000 needy citizens, and sought to relieve these colonists of yearly payments to the treasury. He also proposed a measure to forbid the beating of Latins with rods during their army service. He claimed his measures were in accordance with the wishes of the senate, which helped soften the people's ill feeling toward the senate's most distinguished members.
*   **Livius Andronicus:** A writer who authored tragedies featuring the figure of Danae.
