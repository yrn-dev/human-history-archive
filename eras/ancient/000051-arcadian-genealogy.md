# ARCADIAN GENEALOGY

The genealogy of Arcadia is characterized by a fragmented structure, reflecting the region's historical division into numerous small, independent townships. Because each of these communities maintained its own eponym, the heroic genealogy of the Arcadians appears subdivided and broken up.

## The Line of Lykaôn
In the narratives provided by Pausanias, Lykaôn is a central figure whose fifty sons served as the founders of various Arcadian towns. While some legends suggest Lykaôn's ferocity was punished by the gods, the genealogical tradition focuses on his descendants as the architects of the region's urban landscape. Among the sons of Lykaôn listed as eponyms for Arcadian towns are:
* Pallas
* Orestheus
* Phigalus
* Trapezeus
* Mænalus
* Mantinêus
* Tegeatês

## Arkas and Kallistô
Distinct from the traditions of Lykaôn is the legend of Arkas, the general eponym of Arcadia. According to this narrative, Kallistô was a companion of Artemis in the chase who had taken a vow of chastity. Zeus violated this vow, either through force or persuasion, which drew the anger of Artemis and Hêrê. Hêrê transformed Kallistô into a bear, and Artemis subsequently killed her with an arrow. 

Zeus placed Kallistô among the stars as the constellation of the Bear and preserved her child, Arkas. Arkas was raised by the Atlantid nymph Maia. While some poets, such as Eumêlus, attempted to link these stories by making Kallistô the daughter of Lykaôn, other early sources—including Hesiod, Pherekydês, and Asius—did not acknowledge any relationship between the two lines.

## Regional Subdivisions and Descendants
Geographers have categorized the Arcadians into three primary subdivisions: the Azanes, the Parrhasii, and the Trapezuntii. Azan is identified as the son of Arkas; in the division of his father's inheritance, Azan's lot consisted of seventeen towns. Based on genealogical evidence, Kleitôr appears to be the chief location within Azania. Other figures associated with this region include Pæus (or Päos), located between Psôphis and Kleitôr, and the inhabitants of Phigaleia, whom a Delphian oracle counts among the Azanes.

Other notable figures in the Arcadian genealogical record include:
* **Apheidas, Elatus, and Azan**: Listed as descendants.
* **Aleus, Augê, and Telephus**: Further genealogical connections.
* **Ancæus and Echemus**: Echemus is noted for killing Hyllus, an event associated with the repelling of the Hêrakleids from the Peloponnêsus.

## The Asklêpiads
The genealogy of Arcadia also includes Korônis and Asklêpius. Asklêpius is the subject of numerous legends and extended worship, with temples dedicated to him where sick persons were healed. His descendants, known as the Asklêpiads, include Machaôn and Podaleirius.

## Cultural and Chronological Context
The Arcadians are noted for having a distinct ancient calendar; while some barbarians had three months and Acarnanians had six, the Arcadians had four. 

From a broader historical perspective, the genealogies of the Greeks were designed to connect the community to a common god. These records typically feature historical figures in the lower tiers and non-historical or mythical figures in the higher tiers. Modern analysis suggests that these genealogical names may fall into three categories: real historical names, entirely fictitious names, or the names of clans and races that were converted into the names of individuals. In the case of Arcadia, the "heroic" genealogy often served to provide a foundation myth for the various independent townships.
