# Valentinian

The name Valentinian appears in the archive's records in two distinct contexts: as a series of Roman emperors and as a theatrical production in which the actor Barton Booth appeared.

## The Roman Emperors

### Valentinian and the Division of the Empire
Valentinian was elected emperor and associated his brother, Valens, in power, resulting in the final division of the Eastern and Western Empires. His reign was marked by military campaigns and administrative efforts across various regions, including Germany, Britain, Africa, the East, and the Danube. 

Valentinian's government was characterized by a complex relationship with his subjects. While he was flattered by demonstrations of loyalty from the Illyrian provinces, he was also criticized for the impunity of his ministers, who oppressed subjects without consequence. In one instance, when the deputy of Epirus, a Cynic philosopher named Iphicles, informed the emperor that he had been sent "with tears and groans" by a reluctant people, Valentinian paused but failed to conduct a strict inquiry into his ministers' conduct.

In his military endeavors, Valentinian marched from the banks of the Moselle with forces from Gaul to address calamities in Illyricum. He later advanced into the country of the Quadi, driven by a thirst for blood and revenge following the murder of Gabinius. This resulted in a savage war of extreme devastation and massacre. Following this campaign, he fixed his winter quarters at Bregetio on the Danube, near the city of Presburg.

### Personal Life and Succession
The personal life of Valentinian was subject to historical debate. While an ecclesiastical historian claimed he practiced polygamy by taking Justina as a second wife while married to Severa, other evidence suggests he used the legal permission of divorce to marry Justina after Severa. 

Severa was the mother of Gratian. Before Gratian reached the age of nine, Valentinian granted him the diadem, the purple robe, and the title of Augustus. This election was ratified by the armies of Gaul. Gratian further secured his hereditary rights to the Flavian family by marrying the granddaughter of Constantine. Valentinian eventually expired suddenly in the camp of Bregetio; at the time of his father's death, Gratian was seventeen years old.

### Later Valentinian and the Fall of the Western Empire
A later Valentinian is described as a detested and despicable ruler whose "feeble government" and "inconstant" nature led to widespread resentment. He was particularly despised in Rome, where a republican spirit revived in the senate. This Valentinian was known for "vague and unlawful amours," which caused distress to his wife, Empress Eudoxia.

In a notable act of violation, Valentinian used a debt owed to him by the senator Petronius Maximus to trick Maximus's wife into coming to the Imperial palace, where he violated her. This act, combined with Maximus's own ambition, led Maximus to seek the throne through the suffrage of the Roman senate. Valentinian was eventually punished and killed by two guards of Barbarian race, who were followers of Ætius. Following the death of this Valentinian and the elevation of Maximus to the throne, the Vandal leader Genseric equipped a fleet and anchored at the mouth of the Tyber.

## Theatrical References

In the context of English theatre, *Valentinian* is the title of a play performed at Lincoln's Inn Fields between 1700 and 1704. The English actor Barton Booth, who had previously been educated at Westminster school and Trinity College, Cambridge, made an appearance in this production as the character Maximus. Booth's success in this role was immediate.
