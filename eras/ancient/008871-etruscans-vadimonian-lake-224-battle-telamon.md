# Etruscans: Conflict with Rome and the Boii

The Etruscans were an ancient people who, in their struggles against the Romans, were characterized as being more numerous, more civilized, and better armed than their opponents. Despite these advantages, their power was diminished by two primary disasters: a war with the Greeks of Syracuse in Sicily in 474 B.C., which resulted in the destruction of the Etruscan fleet, and a significant raid by the Gauls from the north.

## Territorial Struggles and the Roman Conquest
The Etruscans engaged in a long and intermittent war with Rome. During this period of conflict, the Romans eventually captured Veii, an Etruscan fortress located a few miles from Rome that had served as a persistent threat and annoyance. This era of struggle is associated with the Etruscan monarchs known as the Tarquins.

The Etruscans also faced pressure from the Boii, a Celtic people originally from Gallia Transalpina. After crossing the Pennine Alps into the valley of the Po, the Boii found the district occupied and subsequently drove out the Umbrians and the Etruscans to establish themselves as far as the Apennines in the modern Romagna.

## Military Defeats at Vadimonian Lake and Telamon
The decline of Etruscan autonomy was marked by several key military defeats in conjunction with other groups. In 283, the Etruscans were defeated alongside the Boii at the Vadimonian lake. 

Later, in 224 (with some sources citing 225), the battle of Telamon took place in Etruria. Following this engagement, the Boii were forced to submit, and the Etruscans continued to suffer the consequences of their weakening position between the Roman forces and the northern Gallic incursions.
