# Takht-i-Rustam and the Ruins of Balkh

## Overview of Balkh
Balkh is a city located in Afghanistan, situated approximately 46 miles south of the Oxus and 100 miles east of Andkhui. Identical to the ancient cities of Zainaspa or Bactra, the site is currently characterized largely by a mass of ruins and many acres of debris. The city sits 1,200 feet above sea level on the right bank of the Balkh river. Its current population consists of a small bazaar, a colony of Jews, and about 500 houses belonging to Afghan settlers.

## City Layout and Architecture
Entry to the city via the west (Akcha) gate leads under three arches, which are likely the remnants of a former Jama Masjid. The outer walls are in a state of utter disrepair, measuring between 6-1/2 and 7 meters in perimeter. On the south-eastern borders, these walls are set high on a rampart or mound, which indicates a Mongol origin. To the north-east, a moated and walled citadel and fort are built on a barren mound, though only the remains of a few pillars survive there.

Other notable structures include:
*   **Masjid Sabz:** Featuring a green-tiled dome, this building is said to be the tomb of Abul Narsi Parsar, a Khwaja.
*   **The Madrasa:** Only the arched entrance of this building remains; traditionally, it is not considered very old.
*   **Buddhist Constructions:** These earlier structures have proven to be more durable than the Mahommedan buildings.

## The Top-i-Rustam and Takht-i-Rustam
The ruins of Balkh include several significant structures. The Top-i-Rustam is a circular building approximately 50 feet high, measuring 50 yards in diameter at its base and 30 yards at the top. Its base is constructed from sun-dried bricks that are approximately 2 feet square and 4 or 5 inches thick. The interior contains four circular vaults, which are likely accessed via four passages pierced from the outside below.

The Takht-i-Rustam is characterized by a wedge-shaped plan with uneven sides. It appears to be constructed of pise mud, which consists of puddled mud mixed with straw.

## Surrounding Region and Historical Context
In the vicinity of these ruins, there are remains of many other stupas (or topes). It is possible that the Nan Vihara described by the Chinese traveller Hsuan Tsang can be recognized among these ruins. Further evidence of ancient habitation exists on the road to Mazar-i-Sharif, where mounds of ruins likely represent a city even older than the site of modern Balkh.

The town is currently garrisoned by a few hundred kasidars. The regular troops of Afghan Turkestan are stationed nearby at Takhtapul, near Mazar-i-Sharif. Additionally, the gardens located to the north-east contain a caravanserai.
