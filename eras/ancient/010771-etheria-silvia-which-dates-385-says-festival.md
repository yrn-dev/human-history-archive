# Feast of the Ascension

The Feast of the Ascension is an oecumenical festival of the Christian Church. In terms of solemnity, it ranks alongside the festivals of Pentecost, Easter, and Christmas. The feast celebrates the ascension of Christ into heaven, which occurred forty days after the resurrection.

## Calendar and Observance
The festival is held forty days after Easter, or ten days before Whitsunday. It always falls on a Thursday, a day referred to as Holy Thursday or Ascension Day.

## Historical Origins
The festival is noted for its great antiquity. While there is no discoverable trace of the feast prior to the middle of the 4th century, subsequent historical references treat it as having been established for a long time. St Augustine mentioned that the feast had been kept from time immemorial and suggested it was likely instituted by the apostles. 

Other early records include:
*   **Antioch:** Chrysostom, in a homily on the ascension, noted a celebration of the festival at the church of Romanesia, located outside Antioch.
*   **Constantinople:** Socrates records that in the year 390, the people of Constantinople celebrated the feast in a suburb of the city according to "old custom" (*ex ethous*).

## Traditions and Locations
Historically, the festival was associated with professional pilgrimages intended to commemorate the passing of the apostles and Christ to the Mount of Olives. In the 7th century, Adamnan, the abbot of Iona, described a procession taking place at Jerusalem, where the feast was celebrated in the church on Mount Olivet.

The *Peregrinatio* of Etheria (Silvia), dating from approximately A.D. 385, states that the festival was held in the Church of the Nativity at Bethlehem. 

In the West during the middle ages, the tradition evolved to include processions outside the church featuring banners and candles. These processions were used as a symbol of the triumphant entry of Christ into heaven.
