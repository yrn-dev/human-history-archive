## Theological Works of Basil
The writings of Basil include *De Spiritu Sancto*, which is described as an edifying and lucid appeal to early Christian tradition and Scripture. Basil also authored three books directed against Eunomius, who was the primary exponent of Anomoian Arianism. In addition to these theological treatises, Basil was a noted preacher whose preserved works include an exposition of the psalter and a series of lenten lectures on the *Hexaemeron*.

Basil's ethical manuals reflect his ascetic tendencies; these are found in the *Regulae*, intended for use in the cloister, and the *Moralia*, intended for use in the world. His personal nature is revealed through three hundred letters, which characterize him as observant, tender, optimistic, and playful, despite facing ecclesiastical unrest and ill-health. As a reformer, Basil focused on the reformation of the monastic orders of the East and the improvement of the liturgy.

## Editions and Bibliographic Records
Various editions of Basil's works have been published over time, including those from Basel (1532) and Paris (1721-1730 by P. Maranus and J. Garnier, and 1839 by L. de Sinner). Specific scholarly editions include *De Spiritu Sancto* edited by C. F. H. Johnston (Oxford, 1892) and *Liturgia* edited by A. Robertson (London, 1894). His works are also contained within Migne's *Patrol. ser. graec.* 29-32. Other relevant scholarly resources include the patrologies of O. Bardenhewer and histories of dogma by F. Loofs and A. Harnack.

## Influence on Monastic Law
The Rules of Basil, alongside the canons of the Councils and the Constitutions of Theodore the Studite, form the primary components of Russian and Greek monastic law. While Greek monachism experienced periods of revival and relaxation over four centuries, the second half of the 8th century was a period of general decadence. Around the year 800, Theodore became the abbot of the monastery of the Studium in Constantinople. Theodore sought to restore the spirit of St Basil in its primitive vigour. To ensure the permanence of this reformation, Theodore created a practical code of laws to supplement Basil's Rules. These constitutions, codified in Migne's *Patrol. Graec.* xcix., 1704-1757, became the standard for life at the Studium monastery and eventually spread throughout the monasteries of the Greek empire.

## Related Ancient Authorities
In the study of ancient works, *De Spiritu Sancto* by Basil is recommended as an authority alongside other texts such as the *Catecheses* of Cyril of Jerusalem, *Orat. 40* by Gregory Nazianzen, and *De Baptismo contra Donatistas* by Augustine. Other cited authorities include the *Constitutiones Apostolicae*, the *Sacramentary* of Serapion of Thmuis, and the *Oratio in eos qui differunt baptismum* by Gregory Nyss.
