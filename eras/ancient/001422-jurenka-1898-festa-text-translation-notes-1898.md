# H. Jurenka and N. Festa (1898)

The works of H. Jurenka and N. Festa, both published in 1898, are recognized as editions of the writings of Bacchylides. These publications are part of a broader history of scholarly efforts to edit and translate the Bacchylides manuscripts.

## Context of the Bacchylides Manuscripts
The editions produced by Jurenka and Festa are based on manuscripts that provide significant linguistic and grammatical data. According to observations by Kenyon, the Bacchylides manuscript is exceptionally well-supplied with accents, a feature not matched by other manuscripts of equal antiquity. The only comparable example is the Alcman fragment held in the Louvre, which dates perhaps to the early part of the 1st century A.D. In the case of the Alcman fragment, the frequent use of accents was likely intended to assist readers who were unfamiliar with Laconian Doric.

In the Bacchylides manuscript, other grammatical or metrical signs, known as *prosoidiai*, are present, though they do not call for extensive special remark. The punctuation provided by the scribe or subsequent correctors is described as very sparse and is not always considered authoritative. Furthermore, the scribe frequently omitted specific signs used to denote the structure of the work, such as the *asterisk* for an ode, the *coronis* for an epode, and the *paragraphus* for a strophe or antistrophe. When these signs were used, they were sometimes employed irregularly or placed incorrectly.

## Chronology of Editions
The 1898 editions by H. Jurenka and N. Festa—the latter of which included a text, translation, and notes—followed the *Ed. princeps* published by F. G. Kenyon in 1897. They preceded the third edition by F. Blass in 1904 and the work of Sir Richard Jebb, who published an edition with a bibliography, translation, notes, and introduction in 1905, followed by a text-only version in 1906. Additional scholarly research on the subject includes the *Studi su Bacchilide, Bibliografia Bacchilidea, 1897-1905*, published by T. Zanghieri in 1905.
