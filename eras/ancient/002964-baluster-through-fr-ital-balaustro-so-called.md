# BALUSTER

## Definition and Etymology
A baluster is a small moulded shaft, which may be circular or square in shape. These shafts are constructed from wood, stone, or occasionally metal. The primary function of a baluster is to support the rail of a staircase or the coping of a parapet. When these shafts are gathered together in an assemblage, the resulting structure is known as a balustrade.

The term is derived from the Italian word *balaustro*, which came to English through French. The Italian term was coined due to a supposed likeness to the *balaustion*, a Greek word for the wild pomegranate flower. In the English language, the word has been corrupted into "banister."

## Historical Development
The earliest known examples of balusters appear in bas-reliefs that represent Assyrian palaces. In these instances, they were utilized as window balustrades and appeared to feature Ionic capitals. While they do not seem to have been known to the Romans or the Greeks, early examples reappear in the balconies of palaces located in Verona and Venice.

Later, Italian revivalists elevated the baluster to a feature of great importance, employing them extensively for roof parapets and window balconies.

## Architectural Variations
In Saxon architecture, the term "baluster shaft" refers specifically to the shaft used to divide a window. Examples of these shafts are located in the south transept of the abbey at St Albans in England; these are believed to have been taken from an old Saxon church. In these specific examples, plain cylindrical Norman shafts were added, along with Norman capitals and bases.

## The Balustrade
A balustrade is a low screen or parapet consisting of a rail or coping supported by balusters. While often functional, the balustrade is sometimes used as a purely decorative element beneath the sill of a window that does not extend to the ground. 

Variations of the balustrade include:
*   The use of flowing foliage in place of the standard parapet.
*   Structures formed from vertical slabs of stone that have been pierced, as seen in the balconies of the minarets at Cairo and at the Ca' d'oro in Venice.
