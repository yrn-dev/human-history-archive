# Leasing-Making and Related Legal Concepts

In historical and legal contexts, the term "leasing-making" and various forms of leasing and assignment appear across different jurisdictions and eras, ranging from criminal charges in 17th-century Scotland to the administration of convict labor in Louisiana.

## Leasing-Making and the Case of Lord Balmerino
In 1634, John, the elder son of Balmerino (who had been permitted to take his father's title in 1613), was imprisoned due to his opposition to Charles I in Scotland. During his legal proceedings, he was found guilty of "leasing-making" by a jury, though the verdict was reached by only a bare majority. This conviction resulted in a sentence of death.

Despite the sentence, there was strong popular sympathy for Balmerino. Intercessions were made on his behalf by others, including the poet Drummond of Hawthornden. Following a period of hesitation, Charles I granted him a pardon. Despite this, Balmerino continued his opposition to the king, becoming a trusted counsellor to the marquess of Argyll and a chief among the Covenanters. He presided over the parliament in Edinburgh in August 1641 and served as one of the Scottish commissioners who visited England in 1644. He died in February 1649.

## Legal Definitions of Demise and Assignment
In Anglo-French legal terminology, a "demise" refers to the transfer of an estate, specifically by lease. In a lease, this term has an operative effect that implies a covenant for "quiet enjoyment." The term is also used in English law regarding the "demise of the crown," which refers to the immediate transfer of sovereignty and its prerogatives to a successor without an interregnum, based on the maxim "the king never dies."

Related to the transfer of interests is the concept of "assignment" (referred to as "assignation" in Scotland). To assign is to make over a property or right, generally through writing rather than actual delivery. The person transferring the interest is the assignor or cedent, while the recipient is the assignee. An assignee may be designated "by deed"—such as when a lessee assigns his lease to another party—or "in law," such as when property is devolved upon an executor.

## Leasing of Convicts in Louisiana
In the context of penal institutions in Louisiana, the leasing of convicts is governed by constitutional and statutory restrictions. According to the state constitution, the leasing or hiring of state convicts is prohibited. However, parish convicts may be hired or leased for various types of labor, including:
* Factory and farm work.
* Work on levees and roads.
* Other public undertakings.

These convicts are subject to classification, and the Board of Control, which supervises state convicts and their places of confinement, has the authority to allow the diminution or commutation of sentences based on exemplary conduct, meritorious services, or good behaviour.
