# Iphigenie en Tauride

*Iphigenie en Tauride* is a title associated with several distinct artistic and literary works, including a musical composition by Gluck, a theatrical production performed at the Comédie Française, and a classical text by Euripides.

## Musical Composition by Gluck
The composer Gluck produced a work titled *Iphigenie en Tauride*. Within this piece, specifically in the aria "Je t'implore et je tremble," Gluck reproduced the first twelve bars of the *Gigue* from Bach's B flat Clavier-Partita, replicating every detail of the figure and harmony. This instance is characterized as plagiarism, whether unconscious or otherwise, which is distinguished from the "profound indebtedness" that typically allows a great artist to achieve originality. It is noted that Gluck's specific training practically deprived him of the direct influence of Bach, which might have otherwise assisted his choral expression and harmonic aims.

## Literary and Classical Origins
The subject matter of *Iphigenie en Tauride* is rooted in classical Greek literature. Euripides authored *Iphigenia in Tauride*, a work that has been the subject of scholarly editions, including one edited by T. Gaisford in 1811. The text has also been cited as a reference in historical contexts, such as in the writings of Gibbon.
