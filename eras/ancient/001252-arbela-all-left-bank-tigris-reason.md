# Arbela and the Geography of the Tigris

The city of Arbela was one of the chief cities of the region historically recognized by writers of antiquity as Assyria. Located in the district enclosed between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers in western Asia, Arbela was situated on the left (eastern) bank of the Tigris.

## Regional Geography and Urban Distribution
The territory between the Tigris and Euphrates is geographically and ethnologically viewed as a single country. This region is divided into two primary sections: a northern area characterized by mountainous terrain and a southern area that is flat and marshy. The two rivers approach one another at a point where the northern undulating plateau drops suddenly into the Babylonian alluvium, a feature that tends to separate the two divisions.

Within this landscape, the original capital of the region was Assur. However, other major cities—specifically Nineveh, Calah, and Arbela—were all established on the left bank of the Tigris. The land east of the Tigris where Arbela and Nineveh were located is referred to in some sources as Aturia, which is considered a dialectic variation of Assyria.

## Reasons for the Eastern Bank Preference
The preference for locating major cities like Arbela on the eastern bank of the Tigris was driven by the availability of resources. The left bank provided an abundant supply of water. In contrast, the great Mesopotamian plain located on the western side of the river was dependent upon the streams that flowed into the Euphrates.

## Surrounding Terrain and Landmarks
The area surrounding these cities is characterized by diverse geological features. To the west lies the modern El-Jezireh, a vast flat approximately 250 miles in length. This plain is interrupted by a single limestone range that branches off from the Zagros mountains under the names of Sinjar, Hamrin, and Sarazur.

To the north of the plateau is a well-watered, undulating belt of country featuring limestone hills. These hills are sometimes arid and other times covered with dwarf-oak, often enclosing fertile valleys and rich plains. Beyond these lie the massive ridges of the Zagros and Niphates ranges, which serve as the source of both the Tigris and Euphrates rivers and act as a geographic barrier separating Assyria from Kurdistan and Armenia.

## Historical and Military Significance
Arbela is noted in historical accounts as a site of strategic importance for the empire of Asia. The Roman emperor Julian, seeking to force a confrontation with the Great King, resolved to advance into the inland provinces with the hope of contending for the empire, potentially in the plains of Arbela.

The region's geography also played a role in the movements of other historical groups. For instance, the Adiabeni occupied a tract on the east side of the Tigris that was apparently part of old Assyria. Additionally, the "Wall of Media," a defensive structure intended to protect Assyria from foreign invasion, is described as having stretched from the Tigris to the Euphrates.
