# Specula Brundusiana

The term *specula Brundusiana* refers to a type of high-quality mirror associated with the ancestors of the Roman era. According to Pliny in *Nat. Hist.* xxxiii. ch. ix. §45, the best mirrors among the ancestors were the Brundusiana, which were composed of a mixture of copper (*aere*) and tin (*stanno*).

## Composition and Material Properties
The *specula Brundusiana* were made of bronze, an alloy consisting primarily of copper and tin in varying proportions. In the context of mirror-making, this specific alloy is referred to as *speculum metal*. The composition of *speculum metal* typically consists of 1 part tin to 2 or 2½ parts copper.

The addition of tin to copper creates a product that is harder and less malleable than pure copper. Furthermore, this alloy is more fusible, which makes it better suited for the process of casting. Other variations of copper-tin alloys exist for different purposes; for example, "bell-metal" contains 1 part tin to 3 to 5 parts copper, while "gun-metal" uses a ratio of 1 part tin to 16 parts copper (soft) or approximately 1 part tin to 8 parts copper (hard).

## Etymology and Nomenclature
There are differing views regarding the etymology of the word "bronze." One connection is made to the same root as the word "brown." However, M.P.E. Berthelot, in *La Chimie au moyen âge*, suggests that the term is a place-name derived from *aes Brundusianum*, citing the aforementioned passage from Pliny's *Natural History*.

## Related Metallurgical Applications
The art of fine casting, as seen in the production of bronze alloys, has endured through various historical periods. Beyond mirrors, bronze alloys have been utilized for:
*   **Statuary:** Often containing 80% to 90% copper, with the remainder being tin, or a combination of tin, zinc, and lead.
*   **Coinage:** The bronze used for French and British copper coinage consists of 95% copper, 4% tin, and 1% zinc.
*   **Machinery:** Copper-tin alloys used for bearings often include zinc to increase hardness. "Anti-friction metals," such as Babbitt's metal (invented by Isaac Babbitt between 1799 and 1862), utilize a small amount of copper combined with tin and antimony.
*   **Ordnance:** Harder gun-metal is employed for bronze ordnance.
*   **Specialized Tools:** Alloys containing 7 parts copper to 1 part tin are sonorous and brittle, and can be tempered to maintain a fine edge.
