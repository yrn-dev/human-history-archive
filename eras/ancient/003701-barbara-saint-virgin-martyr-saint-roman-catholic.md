# BARBARA, SAINT

Saint Barbara is recognized as a virgin martyr and a saint within the Orthodox Eastern Churches and the Roman Catholic Church. Her festival day is observed on December 4th.

## Legend and Martyrdom
According to her legend, Barbara was immured in a tower by her father, who acted out of opposition to her marriage. During this time, she was converted to Christianity by a follower of Origen. Upon discovering her conversion, her father beheaded her. 

The exact location of her martyrdom is subject to varying accounts; sources cite Heliopolis, a town in Tuscany, or Nicomedia in Bithynia. These events are said to have occurred around the year 235.

## Patronage and Veneration
In religious tradition, Saint Barbara serves as the patron saint for gunsmiths and armourers. Additionally, her protection is specifically sought against lightning. 

Her image has also appeared in historical art. For example, in the Esterhazy Gallery in Vienna, there is a painting of the Virgin between Saints Barbara and Catherine.

## Context of Hagiography and Liturgy
The accounts of Saint Barbara's life fall under the category of hagiography, which is the literature concerning the lives of saints and the acts of martyrs. Much of this literature, particularly the *Acts of the Martyrs*, was originally anonymous. In the 10th century, Symeon Metaphrastes acted as an editor for many such texts, revising them according to the linguistic and rhetorical principles of his era and incorporating them into a multi-volume collection that often superseded original texts.

In the Roman Catholic Breviary, the festivals of saints are managed through the *Proprium Sanctorum*, which contains liturgical formularies, psalms, and lessons. This section consists largely of hagiological biography. Furthermore, the *Commune Sanctorum* provides offices for various classes of saints, including categories for virgins and martyrs, to which Saint Barbara belongs.
