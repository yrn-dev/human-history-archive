# Human Nature and the Afterlife

The provided archival texts present a comprehensive vision of human existence, beginning with the physical origins of man and extending to the events of the final judgment and the subsequent states of reward and punishment.

## Human Creation and Nature
According to the texts, man is created from a sperm-drop or drop of seed, after which he is proportioned and moulded. The archive describes the inherent temperament of man as being very impatient, hasty, and anxious. This nature manifests as being fretful or greatly grieved when evil afflicts him, and niggardly or grudging when good reaches him. 

The texts highlight a tendency toward ingratitude and greed. Some individuals, despite being granted ample means, vast riches, and sons, remain greedy and desire more. Others are characterized as having been indulged in luxury and ease, leading them to persist obstinately in "wickedness supreme." In contrast, the texts identify "worshippers" who are constant in their prayer and those who acknowledge a recognized right within their wealth.

## The Natural World as Evidence
The texts encourage reflection on the physical world as a sign of creation. This includes observing the creation of camels, the way the heaven is raised or reared aloft, and the fixing of mountains. The earth is described as a wide or even expanse, with mountains serving as pegs, bulwarks, or projections. The texts further note the creation of humans in pairs, the appointment of sleep for repose, the night as a covering or cloak, and the day as a means for seeking livelihood. Above the earth, seven strong firmaments were built, containing a dazzling lamp or light of splendour.

## The Day of Judgment
The transition from earthly life to the afterlife is marked by several cataclysmic events. These include the sounding of a trumpet, a "Deafening Noise," a "Shout," or a "single Compelling Cry." This day is described as a day of distress, anguish, and violent commotion, where hearts palpitate in agitation and eyes are cast down.

On this day, the social bonds of humanity are severed; a man will flee from his brother, his mother, his father, his spouse, and his children, as every soul will be occupied by its own concerns. The texts describe the unveiling of the world on High, the tearing away of the sky, and the kindling of the Blazing Fire or hell. The "scrolls" or "books" are laid open, and every soul will know what it has prepared or put forward. This includes the questioning of female infants who were buried alive regarding the sin for which they were killed.

## The Companions of the Right Hand
Those identified as the Companions of the Right Hand are described as happy and joyful. Their faces are beaming, bright as dawn, and laughing. They are pleased with their past striving and effort.

Their reward is a high or lofty Garden where they hear no idle speech, vanity, or sinful discourse, hearing only the word "Peace, Peace." This environment features:
*   **Natural Beauty:** Thornless lote-trees, clustered plantains or banana-trees, extended shade, and abundant fruit that is neither forbidden nor out of reach.
*   **Amenities:** Gushing or flowing springs, raised thrones or couches, cushions set in rows, and silken carpets spread out.
*   **Provision:** Goblets placed ready for them.

The texts state that these individuals are made into a new creation, and they are joined by pure, undefiled virgins who are their equals in age and are loving. This group includes a numerous company from both the first and the last generations.

## The Companions of the Left Hand
Conversely, the Companions of the Left Hand are described as wretched. Their faces are stained with dust. They are those who turned away from the truth, fled, and hoarded wealth.

Their state is characterized by:
*   **Physical Torment:** They are placed in the midst of boiling or scalding water and a fierce blast of scorching wind.
*   **Environment:** They dwell in the shade of black smoke, in a place that is neither cool nor refreshing.
*   **The Fire:** The fire of hell is described as eager to roast and dragging the individual by the head or plucking them right to the skull.

## Divine Instruction and Guidance
The texts mention a "Message of instruction" or "admonishment" contained in honored books. These books are described as exalted, purified, and written by the hands of scribes who are noble, righteous, pious, and just. The texts urge the reader to pay heed to this remembrance and to be patient and constant for the sake of the Lord, while shunning all abomination and uncleanness.
