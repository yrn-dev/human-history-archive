# Ravine of Egypt (Musri)

The "Ravine" of Egypt, also referred to as the "Ravine" of Musri, is a geographical feature identified by some scholars as being located on the southern frontier of Judea.

## Scholarly Interpretations of the River
In the context of biblical narratives concerning Balak, the king of Moab, and the prophet Balaam, the text mentions a "River" (*nahar*). Traditionally, this term has been interpreted in its ordinary sense as the Euphrates. Under this traditional view, the location of Pethor—where Balaam resided—is identified with a *Pitru* located on a tributary of the Euphrates, as mentioned in an inscription of Shalmaneser II. This placement in Mesopotamia is further supported by Deuteronomy xxiii. 4.

However, alternative views have been proposed by scholars such as A. Jeremias and Marquart. Jeremias, in *Das Alte Testament im Lichte des alten Orients*, adopts the perspective that the term *nahar* (River) is better understood as *nahal*, meaning "Ravine." According to this interpretation, the location is not the Euphrates but the "Ravine" of Egypt or Musri, situated on the southern frontier of Judea. This view is also supported by Winckler in the new edition of E. Schrader's *Die Keilinschriften und das Alte Testament*.

## Context of the Narrative
The discussion regarding the location of the River or Ravine arises from the account of Balak, the king of Moab. Alarmed by the conquests of the Israelites (Bani Israel), Balak dispatched the princes of Moab to Pethor to seek out Balaam. The objective was for Balaam to come and place a curse upon the Israelites. Despite this request, Balaam, acting under divine inspiration, blessed the Israelites. Even after Balak remonstrated, Balaam reminded the king of his message and blessed the Israelites again before returning home.
