# Balkh

Balkh is a city located in Afghanistan, situated approximately 46 miles south of the Oxus and about 100 miles east of Andkhui. The city is identical to the ancient sites of Zainaspa or Bactra. It is positioned on the right bank of the Balkh river at an elevation of 1,200 feet above the sea.

## City Layout and Current State
Currently, the city consists largely of a mass of ruins and many acres of debris. Within this area are a small bazaar, a colony of Jews, and approximately 500 houses inhabited by Afghan settlers. 

The city's outer walls are in a state of utter disrepair and measure between 6-1/2 and 7 meters in perimeter. On the south-eastern borders, these walls are set high on a rampart or mound, which indicates a Mongol origin. To the north-east, a moated and walled fort and citadel are built upon a barren mound, though only the remains of a few pillars currently survive there.

## Architecture and Landmarks
Entrance to the city via the west gate, also known as the Akcha gate, requires passing under three arches. These arches are believed to be the remnants of a former Jama Masjid. Other notable structures in the city include:

*   **Masjid Sabz:** This building features a green-tiled dome and is said to be the tomb of Abul Narsi Parsar, a Khwaja.
*   **The Madrasa:** Only the arched entrance of this building remains; tradition suggests it is not very old.
*   **Top-i-Rustam:** A circular structure standing about 50 feet high, with a base diameter of 50 yards and a top diameter of 30 yards. Its base is constructed of sun-dried bricks (approximately 2 feet square and 4 or 5 inches thick). The interior contains four sunk circular vaults, which are likely accessed via four passages pierced from the outside.
*   **Takht-i-Rustam:** This structure has a wedge-shaped plan with uneven sides and appears to be built of pise mud (puddled mud mixed with straw).

## Historical and Archaeological Context
The archive notes that earlier Buddhist constructions in the area have proven more durable than the Mahommedan buildings. There are remains of many topes (stupas) in the neighborhood, and it is possible that the ruins of the Nan Vihara, mentioned by the Chinese traveller Hsuan Tsang, can be recognized here. Furthermore, mounds of ruins located on the road to Mazar-i-Sharif likely represent a city site even older than the one upon which modern Balkh stands.

## Administration and Surroundings
The town is garrisoned by a few hundred kasidars. The regular troops of Afghan Turkestan are not stationed in the city but are instead cantoned at Takhtapul, located near Mazar-i-Sharif. To the north-east of the city, the gardens contain a caravanserai.
