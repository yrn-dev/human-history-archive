# ACCA LARENTIA

Acca Larentia is a figure in Roman legend associated with the upbringing of the founders of Rome and the religious traditions of the city.

## Role in the Legend of Romulus and Remus
According to accounts of the children's early life, after they were suckled by a she-wolf and assisted by a woodpecker—both animals sacred to the god Mars—they were brought up by Faustulus. His wife, Acca Larentia, served as the nurse for the children. 

The archive notes that the legend regarding the children's parentage may be linked to the twofold meaning of the word *lupa*. In the Latin language, *lupa* refers both to she-wolves and to unchaste women, a term applied to Acca Larentia.

## Religious Observances and the Laurentia
The Romans offered sacrifices to Acca Larentia. In the month of April, the priest of Mars brings libations to her in a feast known as the Laurentia.

## Connection to the Arval Brothers
Acca Larentia is linked to the Arval Brothers (*Fratres Arvales*), a priesthood of twelve members elected for life from Rome's highest ranks. During the empire, the emperor was apparently always included in this college. The primary duty of the Arval Brothers was to offer public sacrifices annually to ensure the fertility of the fields.

While it is generally held that Romulus founded the college, this legend likely arose from the connection of Acca Larentia to the Lares, as she is identified as *mater Larum*. The Lares played a part in the religious ceremonies conducted by the Arval Brothers.
