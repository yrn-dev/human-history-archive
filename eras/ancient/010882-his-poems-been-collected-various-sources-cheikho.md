# A'SHA (MAIMUN IBN QAIS)

## Biography and Life
A'sha, also known as Maimun ibn Qais, was an Arabian poet born in Manfuha, a village located in al-Yemama in the center of Arabia. His life spanned the era preceding the arrival of Mahomet, and he lived long enough to accept the prophet's mission. 

A'sha lived as a wandering singer, traveling throughout the entirety of Arabia. His journeys extended from al-Hira in the north to Hadramut in the south. During these travels, he frequently attended the annual fair at Okaz (Ukaz).

## Beliefs and Influences
Even before the time of Mahomet, A'sha is said to have been a monotheist who believed in the last judgment and the resurrection. These specific beliefs may have been the result of his interactions with the `Ibadites (Christians) of al-Hira and the bishop of Nejran (Najran).

## Poetic Work and Style
A'sha's poetry was noted for its variety of metre and its skill in the realms of satire and praise. His work is specifically praised for its descriptions of the wild ass and for the praise of wine. In his love poetry, he devoted his verses to the praise of a black female slave named Huraira.

Among his various works, his most well-known poem is a piece written in praise of Mahomet.

## Collections and Editions
The poems of A'sha have been gathered from various sources in the work *Les Poetes arabes chretiens*, published by the Jesuit press in Beirut in 1890 (pp. 357-399) by L. Cheikho. Additionally, his eulogy of Mahomet was edited by H. Thorbecke in the 1875 Leipzig publication *Al Asa's Lobgedicht auf Muhammad*.
