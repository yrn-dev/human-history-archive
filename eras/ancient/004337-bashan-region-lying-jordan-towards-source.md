# Bashan

Bashan is a region located east of the Jordan River toward its source. While its boundaries are not precisely defined, it is generally situated north of the territory of Gilead.

## Biblical History and Conquest
The region first appears in Hebrew history during the wanderings of the Israelites. Following the defeat of Sihon, king of the Amorites, the Israelite tribes traveled through the land of Bashan. There, they were met at Edrei by Og, the king of Bashan, who was defeated and slain. 

The narrative describes Og as being of gigantic stature, a characteristic attributed to his descent from the aboriginal Rephaim, a race known for their height. The Rephaim were established in Ashteroth-Karnaim (also referred to as Ashtaroth). Some scholars regard the details of Og's stature and his "bedstead" as suggestive of legend, noting the remoteness of Bashan from the Israelites' wandering regions and the lateness of the documents recording these wars. However, the story remains a firm part of Hebrew tradition.

Following the conquest, the land was allotted to the eastern division of the tribe of Manasseh. One of the cities of refuge, Golan, was located within Bashan. During the reign of Solomon, the "region of Argob in Bashan"—which contained sixty great cities featuring walls and brazen bars—was assigned to Ben-Geber, one of Solomon's lieutenants. The last historical event concerning Bashan in the Old Testament occurs during the days of Jehu, when the country was taken from Israel by Hazael, king of Syria. Josephus records that Hazael spoiled and burned the eastern parts of the country beyond Jordan, including Bashan and Gilead, while Jehu failed to defend the region.

## Geography and Provinces
The boundaries of Bashan can be partially deduced from early historical texts. Mount Hermon is cited as a northern limit. Josephus enumerates four provinces within Bashan:

*   **Gaulanitis:** Likely named after the city of refuge, Golan. It corresponds to the modern Jaul[=a]n, extending from the Jordan lakes to the Haj Road. Josephus notes it was divided into two sections: Sogana and Gamalitis.
*   **Trachonitis:** This province adjoined the territory of Damascus, Auranitis, and Batanaea. It corresponds to the modern district of the Lej[=a] (the *Trach[=o]nes* of Strabo), where inscriptions have been found using the name Trach[=o]n.
*   **Auranitis:** Identified as the Hauran of Ezekiel and modern Arabs, this region lies north of Gilead and south of the Jaul[=a]n. Some sources restrict the name locally to the narrow strip on the west and the plain south of the Lej[=a].
*   **Batanaea:** Located east of the Hauran plain and the Lej[=a], this province includes the Hauran mountain, or Jebel ed-Dr[=u]z, and is remembered in the name *'Ard el-Bathaniyeh*.

## Natural Resources and Characteristics
In prophetic and poetical books, Bashan is noted for its specific natural products and wildlife. The "Blessing of Moses" suggests the region was inhabited by lions. The area was also proverbial for its cattle, which were noted for their ferocity; consequently, the wealthy women of Samaria who oppressed the poor were referred to as "kine of Bashan." Additionally, the region is recognized for its mountains and its oaks, which are mentioned alongside the cedars of Lebanon. These oaks were used to manufacture oars.

## Archaeology and Site Identification
The identification of ancient sites in Bashan is a subject of ongoing study and dispute. The region of Argob has been equated with the Lej[=a] on philological grounds, though this identification is now generally abandoned. Similarly, the "giant cities of Bashan" have been revealed to be Greco-Roman structures dating no earlier than the time of Herod, meaning they have no connection to the more ancient periods of Og or the Israelites.

Several specific sites have been proposed:
*   **Edrei:** Identified by some as *Ed-Dera'a*. While it contains Greco-Roman remains, it also features a subterranean city of unknown date that may be of great antiquity.
*   **Ashteroth Karnaim (Ashtaroth/Be-eshterah):** Often identified with *Busrah* (Bostra) due to important Herodian ruins, though evidence of more remote antiquity is lacking. Other proposed sites include *Tell 'Ashtera* and *Tell Ashari*.
*   **Salecah:** Identified with the ruin *Salkhat* in the Hauran, which is currently inhabited by Druses.
*   **Kenath:** Identified by Porter as Kanawat.

Despite these proposals, no tangible traces of Og, his people, or the Israelites who supplanted them have yet been found.
