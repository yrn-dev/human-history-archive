# Nānodaya

*Nānodaya* (translated as "the Rise of Knowledge") is a literary work authored by the celebrated Buddhist writer Buddhaghosa. It was written by the author prior to his journey to Ceylon.

## Author and Background
Buddhaghosa was a Brahmin by birth, born in north India approximately A.D. 390 near the great Bodhi tree at Budh Gaya. His father was named Kesi. Under the inducement of his teacher, Revata, Buddhaghosa traveled to Anuradhapura in Ceylon. The purpose of this journey was to translate the commentaries on the scriptures, which had been preserved in the Sinhalese language, into Pāli.

While in Ceylon, Buddhaghosa studied under Sanghapāla. Before the fraternity granted him leave to translate the commentaries, he demonstrated his ability by writing the *Visuddhi Magga* (the Path of Purity), which serves as a summary of Buddhist doctrine.

## Literary Works and Contributions
In addition to *Nānodaya*, Buddhaghosa's body of work includes:
* **Psychological Commentaries:** Before arriving in Ceylon, he began a commentary on the principal psychological manual found in the *Pitakas*. He later rewrote this work while in Ceylon.
* **Sumangala Vilāsinī:** One volume of this work, which is part of the translated commentaries, has been edited.
* **Canon Law:** Buddhaghosa wrote a comment on the Buddhist canon law. While the Pāli edition is based on a comparison with a nearly contemporaneous Chinese translation, the original was discovered in that translation.

Buddhaghosa's industry is described as prodigious. It is known that he wrote books that would fill approximately 20 octavo volumes of about 400 pages each. While other writings are ascribed to him, their authenticity remains unconfirmed.

## Historical Significance
Because the date of Buddhaghosa's life is approximately certain, his voluminous writings are considered of great value for the history of Indian ideas and literature. In a field of history where definite dates are often lacking, his work provides a standard by which the age of other writings can be tested.
