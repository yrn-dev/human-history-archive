# Monumenta Hist. Britann. (1848)

## Overview
*Monumenta Hist. Britann.* is a scholarly collection published in 1848 by Petrie. The work serves as a primary resource for historians and researchers by collecting the chief passages from classical writers that refer to early Britain.

## Content and Classical Authorities
The collection focuses on the principal references to early Britain found within the works of several key classical authors. These include:
*   Julius Caesar
*   Strabo
*   Diodorus
*   The elder Pliny
*   Tacitus
*   Ptolemy
*   Cassius Dio

In addition to these authors, the archive notes that references to early Britain are found in various lists and records, such as the *Notitia Dignitatum* (dating to approximately A.D. 400), the Ravennas (a 7th-century *rechauffé*), and the lists of the Antonine Itinerary, which was edited by Parthey in 1848 and likely dates from A.D. 210-230.

## Related Linguistic and Epigraphic Works
While Petrie's *Monumenta Hist. Britann.* collects chief passages, other works provide complementary data on early Britain. Holder's *Altkeltische Sprachschatz* (1896-1908) organizes similar material alphabetically. 

For those studying Roman inscriptions, the archive points to the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latin.* vii. (1873) collected by Hübner, as well as supplements provided by Haverfield and Hübner in the periodical *Ephemeris epigraphica*. Other specialized collections include Hübner's *Inscript. Britann. Christianae* (1876), though this is noted as being out of date, and the work of J. Rhys regarding Pictish and other inscriptions published in the *Proceedings Soc. Antiq. Scotland* (volumes xxvi and xxxii).

## Note on the Author
The archive contains references to other works by Petrie unrelated to the *Monumenta Hist. Britann.*, specifically in the field of Egyptian chronology. In *Hist. of Egypt* (vol. i, 1903; vol. iii, 1905) and *Researches in Sinai* (1906), Petrie proposes specific dates for Egyptian history, such as B.C. 5510 for Menes and B.C. 4731-4454 for the 4th dynasty. These dates differ from those assigned by other authorities, such as Breasted and Ed. Meyer.
