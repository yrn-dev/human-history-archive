# Pannonia and the Boii Expedition

## The Boii People
The Boii were a Celtic people whose original homeland was Gallia Transalpina. They were known to the Romans as early as the time of Plautus, who referenced them contemptuously in the *Captivi*. Early in their history, the Boii split into two primary groups; one migrated into Germany, while the other entered Italy via the Pennine Alps (Great St Bernard) into the Po valley. After driving out the Umbrians and Etruscans, this group established themselves as far as the Apennines in the region of modern Romagna.

According to Cato, the Boii consisted of as many as 112 different tribes. Evidence from tombs at La Tene and Hallstatt suggests they were fairly civilized. Their relationship with Rome was marked by conflict; they were defeated at the Vadimonian lake in 283 and forced to submit in 224 following the battle of Telamon. Despite this, they maintained a hatred for Rome and assisted Hannibal during the Second Punic War in 218, motivated by the Roman foundation of Placentia and Cremona. Following continued struggle from 201 to 191, they were subdued by P. Cornelius Scipio Nasica and lost nearly half of their territory.

## Migration to Pannonia
Following their conflicts with Rome, the Boii underwent further migrations. Strabo records that they were driven back across the Alps to settle on the Danube among the Taurisci, their kinsmen, in an area adjoining Raetia and Vindelicia. Other authorities suggest a Boii settlement existed on the Danube from an early period in the region of modern Bohemia, known as *Boiohemum* ("land of the Boii").

Around 60 B.C., a portion of the Boii migrated to Pannonia and Noricum. During this period, 32,000 Boii joined an expedition led by the Helvetians into Gaul. This venture ended in defeat for the Boii near Bibracte.

## The Helvetian Expedition
The Helvetian nation originated from a country called Switzerland. At the time of their movement, the nation comprised 368,000 people of every age and sex. In their advance through the part of Gaul subject to the Romans, they were accompanied by the Tigurini. The Helvetians and Tigurini were considered equal in numbers and courage to the Teutones and Cimbri of old, totaling 300,000 people, of whom 190,000 were fighting men.

Before their advance, the Helvetians had destroyed their own infrastructure, burning four hundred villages and twelve cities. Their campaign was opposed by Julius Cæsar. While Labienus defeated the Tigurini near the Arar, Cæsar engaged the Helvetians in a long and difficult contest. The battle culminated in a desperate resistance at the camp and among the chariots, involving the fighting of wives and children, and lasted until midnight.

Following the victory, Cæsar compelled the survivors—numbering over 100,000—to return to their original lands and rebuild the cities they had destroyed. This was done to ensure the country was not left vacant for Germans to cross the Rhenus and occupy.

## Other Populations in Pannonia
Beyond the Boii, other groups were established in the province of Pannonia. The Carpi, a Dacian tribe from the lower Danube, rose to power during the 3rd century A.D. After initial conflicts with the Romans—including an invasion that was beaten back by the emperor Philip and later defeats under Galerius and Diocletian—the Carpi were taken under Roman protection. A significant portion of the Carpi were subsequently established in the provinces of Moesia and Pannonia.
