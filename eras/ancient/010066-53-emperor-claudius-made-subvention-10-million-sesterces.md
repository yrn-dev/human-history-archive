# The Subvention of Emperor Claudius to Bononia

## Overview of the Subvention
In A.D. 53, the emperor Claudius provided a subvention of 10 million sesterces (equivalent to L1,087,500) to the city of Bononia. This financial assistance was granted following a fire that had occurred in the city.

## The City of Bononia
Bononia, known today as Bologna, served as the chief town of ancient Aemilia in Italy. Classical writers attributed its origin to the Etruscans, stating it was founded from Perusia by Ocnus or Aucnus under the name Felsina. However, archaeological excavations have revealed earlier settlements, including approximately 600 ancient Italic (possibly Ligurian) huts and cemeteries from the Umbrian period (800-600? B.C.), which immediately preceded the Etruscan civilization (c. 600-400 B.C.). An extensive Etruscan necropolis was also discovered on the site of the modern cemetery, with others found in the public garden and on the Arnoaldi Veli property.

## Historical Development and Roman Influence
Bononia first appears in historical records in 196 B.C., at which time it was in the possession of the Boii and had likely already changed its name. It became a Roman colony in 189 B.C. The city's importance was solidified by its strategic position within the road system of north Italy:
* In 187 B.C., the Via Aemilia connected Bononia with Placentia and Ariminum.
* A road to Arretium was also constructed in 187 B.C.
* A further road to Aquilelia was likely established around 175 B.C.

The city acquired Roman citizenship in 90 B.C. During the conflict against Decius Brutus in 43 B.C., Mark Antony utilized Bononia as his base of operations and settled colonists there. Later, Augustus added further colonists and constructed a new aqueduct from the Letta, a tributary of the Rhenus.

## Later History and Legacy
Bononia was considered one of the most important cities of ancient Italy. Its resilience was demonstrated in 410 when it successfully resisted Alaric, allowing it to preserve its existence during a period of general ruin. Following this, the city belonged to the Greek exarchate of Ravenna. While no Roman remains exist above ground today, various discoveries have been made within the city walls.
