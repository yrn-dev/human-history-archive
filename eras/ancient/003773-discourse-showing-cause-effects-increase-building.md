# Discourse showing the Cause and Effects of the Increase of Building

## Overview
*Apology for the Builder; or a Discourse showing the Cause and Effects of the Increase of Building* is a work published in 1685. It was written by Nicholas Barbon, an English economist who lived from approximately 1640 to 1698. The text is noted for expressing views on important topics—such as rent, value, and foreign trade—that were considered to be much in advance of his time and closely aligned with modern perspectives.

## Author Biography
Nicholas Barbon was born in London and is believed to be the son of Praise-god Barbon. His academic background included the study of medicine at Leiden, and he graduated with an M.D. from Utrecht in 1661. In 1664, he was admitted as an honorary fellow of the College of Physicians.

Barbon was active in public and financial affairs during the late 17th century. He played a significant role in the rebuilding of London following the great fire of 1666. Around 1680, he started what is considered the institution of fire-insurance in England. His political career included serving as a Member of Parliament (M.P.) for Bramber in both 1690 and 1695. Additionally, he founded a land bank that contemporaries described as fairly successful; this entity was later united with the land bank of John Briscoe in 1696.
