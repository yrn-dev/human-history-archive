# Cassell & Company, Limited

## Origins and John Cassell
The publishing firm known as Cassell & Company, Limited, was founded by John Cassell (1817–1865). Born in Manchester on January 23, 1817, to a father who was a public-house landlord, Cassell was originally apprenticed as a joiner. He was a self-educated man who acquired a knowledge of French and a significant acquaintance with English literature through his own efforts. 

In 1836, Cassell moved to London to work in his trade, though during this period he was primarily focused as an active worker for the cause of temperance. He later established himself as a merchant of coffee and tea in 1847. Shortly thereafter, he began a publishing business intended to provide the working classes with quality literature.

## Development of the Firm
The business evolved over several decades, becoming Messrs. Cassell, Petter, Galpin & Co. in 1859. A defining characteristic of the popular books produced by the firm was their use of illustration. During the Crimean War, Cassell increased the circulation of his *Family Paper* (which began in 1853) by procuring and printing cuts from the Parisian publication *L'Illustration*.

The firm expanded its catalog to include various educational and artistic publications, such as:
* *Cassell's Magazine* (starting in 1852)
* The *Popular Educator* (1852–1855)
* The *Technical Educator* (1870–1872)
* The *Magazine of Art* (1878–1903)

In addition to these periodicals, the firm issued numerous editions of standard works. In 1883, the business was converted into a limited liability company under the name Cassell & Company, Limited. John Cassell passed away in London on April 2, 1865.
