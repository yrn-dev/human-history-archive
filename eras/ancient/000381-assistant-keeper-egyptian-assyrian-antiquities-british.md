# Assistant to the Keeper of Egyptian and Assyrian Antiquities, British

The role of Assistant to the Keeper of Egyptian and Assyrian Antiquities at the British Museum has been held by scholars specializing in the languages and archaeology of the ancient Near East. One notable individual in this position was Leonard William King.

## Leonard William King
Leonard William King, M.A., F.S.A., served as the Assistant to the Keeper of Egyptian and Assyrian Antiquities at the British Museum. In addition to his museum duties, King was a Lecturer in Assyrian at King's College, London. His professional contributions included conducting excavations at Kuyunjik (Nineveh) on behalf of the British Museum.

King was a prolific author of scholarly works, including:
* *Assyrian Chrestomathy*
* *Annals of the Kings of Assyria*
* *Studies in Eastern History*
* *Babylonian Magic and Sorcery*

## Departmental History and Administration
The administration of antiquities at the British Museum underwent structural changes over time. Samuel Birch (1813–1885), an English Egyptologist and antiquary, joined the antiquities department in 1836, initially appointed due to his knowledge of Chinese. As the "cumbrous department" was eventually divided, Birch was appointed to lead the Egyptian and Assyrian branch. 

During his tenure, Birch was responsible for the entire arrangement of the department, as he was one of only two people in the institution—and the only one within his specific department—who possessed knowledge of ancient Egyptian. Birch's work included the creation of a dictionary and hieroglyphical grammar, as well as translations of the Harris papyrus and *The Book of the Dead*.
