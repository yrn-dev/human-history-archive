# The Sermon on the Mount (Bishop Bigandet)

The "Sermon on the Mount" is the name given by Bishop Bigandet to the first set sermon delivered by Gotama to his new disciples. This discourse took place in the solitudes of Uruvel, where Gotama had settled among three brothers who were hermit philosophers and fire-worshippers. These brothers and their gathered scholars had a considerable reputation as teachers; over time, they became believers in Gotama's system, with the elder brother, Kassapa, assuming a principal place among his followers.

## Subject and Imagery
The central subject of the sermon was a jungle-fire that had broken out on the opposite hillside. Gotama used this physical event as a metaphor to warn his hearers against various internal "fires," specifically those of:
* Concupiscence
* Anger
* Ignorance
* Birth
* Death
* Decay
* Anxiety

## Teachings on Sensation
In the course of the sermon, Gotama addressed each of the senses in order. He compared all human sensations to a burning flame. According to this teaching, such sensations appear to be something they are not, producing both pleasure and pain, but they pass away rapidly and ultimately end only in destruction.

## Textual References
In the Pali language, the sermon is known as *aditta-pariyaya*, which translates to the sermon on the lessons to be drawn from burning. The text of this discourse can be found in *Vinaya*, i. 34, as well as *Samyutta*, iv. 19. A literal translation of the text is available in *Vinaya Texts*, i. 134, 135.
