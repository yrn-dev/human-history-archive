# Bashan

Bashan is a region located east of the Jordan River toward its source, generally situated north of the territory of Gilead. Its boundaries are not precisely defined, though Mount Hermon is cited as a northern limit.

## Early History and the Reign of Og
The region first appears in Hebrew history during the wanderings of the Israelites. Following the defeat of Sihon, king of the Amorites, the Israelites encountered Og, the king of Bashan, at Edrei, where Og was defeated and slain. Og is described as having a gigantic stature, a trait attributed to his being part of the remnant of the aboriginal Rephaim, a race known for their height. He dwelt at Ashteroth, and the narrative includes specific details regarding his "bedstead."

The territory of Bashan, specifically the "region of Argob," contained sixty great cities with walls and brazen bars. Following the conquest of the land, this territory was allotted to the eastern division of the tribe of Manasseh.

## Administration under Solomon and Later Events
During the reign of Solomon, the region of Argob in Bashan was assigned to Ben-Geber, one of Solomon's lieutenants, as an administrative district. In later history, during the time of King Jehu, the country was taken from Israel by Hazael, king of Syria. Hazael spoiled the eastern parts of the country beyond the Jordan—including Bashan and Gilead—burning and offering violence to the inhabitants. This event is the final historical occurrence regarding Bashan recorded in the Old Testament.

## Geography and Natural Resources
Bashan is characterized by volcanic origins, including the Jebel ed-Druz group of extinct volcanoes. The resulting friable volcanic soil is noted for being extraordinarily fertile, with reports that it yields barley a hundred-fold and wheat eighty-fold.

The region was historically noted for several specific products and features:
*   **Flora:** The area is famous for its oaks, which are mentioned alongside the cedars of Lebanon. These oaks were used to manufacture oars.
*   **Fauna:** The "Blessing of Moses" suggests the land was inhabited by lions. The region's cattle were proverbial for their ferocity; consequently, the wealthy women of Samaria who oppressed the poor were referred to as "kine of Bashan."
*   **Topography:** The region is noted for its mountains.

## Cities and Provinces
The city of Golan in Bashan was designated by Moses as one of the cities of refuge for those who committed involuntary manslaughter. Other mentioned sites include Edrei, where Og fought the Israelites, and Salecah. Ashteroth-Karnaim (also called Ashtaroth or Be-eshterah) was an important city in the region.

Josephus identifies four provinces within Bashan:
1.  **Gaulanitis:** Likely named after the city of Golan, extending from the Jordan lakes to the Haj Road. It was further divided into Sogana and Gamalitis.
2.  **Trachonitis:** Adjoined the territory of Damascus and corresponds to the modern Leja (formerly known as Trachon).
3.  **Auranitis:** Located south of the Jaulan and north of Gilead, corresponding to the Hauran.
4.  **Batanaea:** Located east of the Leja and the Hauran plain, including the Hauran mountain (Jebel ed-Druz).

## Archaeological and Modern Observations
Modern archaeological analysis has challenged previous identifications of the "giant cities of Bashan." It has been determined that the extensive remains in the Leja and Hauran are Greco-Roman and date no earlier than the time of Herod, meaning they have no connection to the ancient periods of Og or the Israelites. No tangible traces of Og's people or their Israelite supplanters have been found.

Some proposed identifications remain under discussion. Edrei is often equated with Ed-Dera'a, which contains a subterranean city of unknown date. Ashteroth-Karnaim has been linked to Busrah (Bostra), though evidence for its remote antiquity is lacking. Salecah is associated with the ruin of Salkhat in the Hauran, which is currently inhabited by Druses.

In later periods, Bashan became known as a refuge for robbers and outlaws. Strabo mentioned a great cave in Trachonitis capable of holding 4,000 robbers. This cave is regarded by Arab tradition as the home of Job and served as the center of the Ghassanid dynasty. The Hauran remains a principal habitation for the Druse sect.
