# Excerpta ex Theodoto

The *Excerpta ex Theodoto* is a source that provides specific details regarding the theological system of Basilides, particularly concerning the nature of the Saviour and the process of redemption.

## The Nature of the Saviour
Within the system of Basilides, the concept of salvation is introduced as a response to a world where confusion had risen to a significant pitch. To address this, the supreme God dispatched his *Nous*, who is also identified as Christ, into the world. According to the *Excerpta ex Theodoto* (s. 16), the Saviour is referred to as the *diakonos*.

The exact relationship between this Saviour and the historical figure of Jesus of Nazareth is difficult to determine certainly. Basilides acknowledged the existence of an earthly Jesus but denied that he was sinless. While other accounts, such as those from Irenaeus, suggest the Saviour appeared only as a phantasm, the *Excerpta ex Theodoto* (17) teaches that the *Diakonos* descended upon Jesus during his baptism in the form of a dove. Because of this event, the followers of Basilides observed the day of the baptism of Jesus—known as the day of the *epiphaneia*—as a high festival.

## The Process of Redemption
The available sources provide very little information regarding how Basilides envisioned the completion of the redemption process. However, an allusion in the writings of Clemens indicates that the mission of the Saviour initiates a "great separation of the sexes," which is viewed as the restoration and fulfilment of all things. This concept aligns with the initial speculations of Basilides. In this framework, salvation is defined as the act of separating that which had been combined for evil.
