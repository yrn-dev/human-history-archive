# Wales

## Roman Conquest
The subjugation of Wales by Roman forces was a prolonged process characterized by fierce resistance from the local tribes and the wild terrain of the hills. Between A.D. 47 and 79, the region experienced thirty years of intermittent hill fighting. To facilitate the conquest, the Romans established legionary fortresses at Caerleon and Chester, which faced the Welsh hills, as well as at Wroxeter (for a limited time) and Lincoln in the northeast.

The Roman strategy involved placing small detached forts in strategic positions, with each garrison consisting of either 500 or 1,000 men. Early successes included the overrunning of Flintshire, known for its lead mines, and Monmouthshire. By A.D. 60, Suetonius Paulinus had reached Anglesea. Despite these gains, progress was hindered by the revolt of Boadicea, which caused the government to hesitate in moving garrisons forward and contributed to the withdrawal of the Fourteenth Legion around A.D. 67. The final subjugation of Wales was achieved during the decisive decade of A.D. 70-80 under the command of three able generals and an army reinforced by Legio II. Adiutrix.

## Agriculture and Orchards
The distribution of orchard areas in Wales varies significantly by county. The largest orchard areas are found in the two counties that adjoin Hereford: Brecon, which has 1,136 acres, and Radnor, which has 727 acres. In contrast, Anglesey represents the other extreme, with a decreasing orchard area of only 22 acres.

## Demographics and Social Statistics
Data concerning England and Wales indicates a steady decline in the illegitimate birth-rate over many years, both in actual numbers and in proportion to the population. Between 1860 and 1907, the number of illegitimate births in the combined region fell from 43,693 (a proportion of 2.2 per 1,000 of the population) to 36,189 (a proportion of 1.0 per 1,000 of the population).

When calculating the illegitimate birth-rate specifically against the unmarried and widowed female population aged 15-45 years, the rate per 1,000 was 14.4 during the period of 1876-1880, falling to 7.8 by 1907.

Regional variations exist within these statistics. The number of illegitimate children is noted as being comparatively high in North Wales. Additionally, North Wales is cited as a region where both the proportions of illegitimacy and the proportions of married women who signed the marriage register by mark are relatively high.

## Legal and Financial History
In the context of bankruptcy law in England and Wales, the act of 1869 was eventually viewed as a failure. Ten years after its implementation, the comptroller in bankruptcy reported that of 13,000 annual failures, only 1,000 cases (approximately 8%) were subject to the act's more important provisions designed to prevent abuses by professional agents and insolvent debtors. The remaining 12,000 cases (92%) escaped provisions regarding the conduct of agents and the discharge and examination of bankrupts. This lack of effective control and the potential for collusive or fraudulent arrangements led to demands for reform from the business community. Following a highly influential memorial from London bankers and merchants in 1879 and inquiries by Mr. Chamberlain, the President of the Board of Trade, the Bankruptcy Act 1883 was enacted.
