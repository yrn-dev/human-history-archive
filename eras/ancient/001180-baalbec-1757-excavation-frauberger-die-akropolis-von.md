# Baalbec

## Overview of the Ruins
Baalbec is the site of vast ruins characterized by their immensity. While these structures are described as more imposing due to their massive scale than for being pleasing in their specific details, they have been the subject of descriptions by numerous tourists and travellers.

## Historical Documentation and Bibliography
The documentation of Baalbec is divided into several chronological phases based on the state of the ruins and the nature of the research conducted.

### Early Discoveries
The first discoverers of the site include P. Belon, who wrote *De admirabili operum antiquorum praestantia* (1553) and *Observations* (1555), and M. von Baumgarten, author of *Peregrinatio in ... Syriam* (1594).

### Pre-Earthquake and Pre-Excavation Records
Significant records were produced before major geological or archaeological changes occurred at the site. R. Wood published *Ruins of Baalbec* in 1757, providing a record of the site before the earthquake of 1759. Later, H. Frauberger wrote *Die Akropolis von Baalbek* in 1892, which serves as a primary reference for the site before excavation began.

### Post-Excavation Studies
Following the period of excavation, O. Puchstein authored *Fuehrer durch die Ruinen v. Baalbek* (1905). Puchstein also collaborated with Th. v. Luepke on *Ansichten*, published in the same year. Additional data regarding the site can be found in the *Builder* (11 Feb. 1905) and the *Quart. Stat. Pal. Exp. Fund* (1904, pp. 58-64) by R. Phene Spiers.

## Local Legends and Interpretations
Various explanations have been offered to account for the construction of the magnificent edifices of Baalbec. Some Oriental accounts attribute the creation of the buildings to the genii or the fairies. Other views, such as those held by Ibn Chaukel and Abulfeda, ascribe the construction to the Aadites or the Sabaeans. Abulfeda noted in *Tabula Syria* that there are no buildings in all of Syria more magnificent than those at Baalbec.
