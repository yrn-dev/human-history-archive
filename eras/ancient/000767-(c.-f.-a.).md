# C. FORENSIC, IN PUBLIC CAUSES

## Overview of Public Causes
In the context of public causes, forensic matters are categorized by the nature of the offense or the legal procedure involved. These are primarily divided into several distinct types of legal actions, including those relating to offenses against the state, unconstitutional procedures, claims for withheld money, official scrutinies, military offenses, murder, and impiety.

## Offenses Against the State and Procedure
Offenses directly against the state, referred to as *graphai demosion adikematon*, encompass crimes such as embezzlement of public moneys, malversation in office, and treason. Recorded cases in this category include:
*   For Polystratus (407 B.C.)
*   Defence on a Charge of Taking Bribes (402 B.C.)
*   Against Nicomachus (399 B.C.)
*   Against Ergocles and Against Epicrates (both 389 B.C.)
*   Against the Corndealers (approximately 386 B.C.)

Additionally, causes relating to unconstitutional procedure, known as *graphe paranomon*, include cases such as the one concerning the Property of the Brother of Nicias in 395 B.C.

## Financial Claims and Official Scrutiny
Causes involving claims for money withheld from the state are termed *apographai*. Examples include the case On the Property of Aristophanes (387 B.C.), Against Philocrates (389 B.C.), and a cause For the Soldier (approximately 394 B.C.), though the latter may have been written by an imitator rather than Lysias.

The process of *dokimasia*, or scrutiny, typically involves the Senate's review of Officials Designate. Notable causes in this category include:
*   Defence on a Charge of Seeking to Abolish the Democracy (401 B.C.)
*   For the Invalid (approximately 402 B.C.)
*   For Mantitheus (392 B.C.)
*   Against Evandrus (382 B.C.)
*   Against Philon (occurring between 404 and 395 B.C.)

## Military, Criminal, and Religious Causes
Military offenses, categorized as *graphai lipotaxiou* or *astrateias*, are exemplified by the causes Against Alcibiades I and II in 395 B.C.

Causes relating to murder or the intent to murder (*graphai phonou, traumatos ek pronoias*) include:
*   Against Eratosthenes (403 B.C.) and the subsequent case On the Murder of Eratosthenes (date uncertain).
*   Against Agoratus (399 B.C.)
*   Against Simon (393 B.C.)
*   On Wounding with Intent (date uncertain).

Finally, causes relating to impiety (*graphai asebeias*) include the case On the Sacred Olive (not before 395 B.C.), For Callias (date uncertain), and Against Andocides, which is considered spurious though perhaps contemporary.
