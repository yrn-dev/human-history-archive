# Chronological Intervals and Time-Based Classifications

In the study of history and technical administration, the measurement of time—ranging from single years to multi-year intervals—serves as the primary framework for organizing events, calculating eras, and determining depreciation or agricultural cycles.

## Historical Chronology and Discrepancies
Chronological determination often involves "reckoning upwards" from a known date to an earlier era, then "reckoning downwards" to establish intervals between events. For example, Eratosthenês used the capture of Troy as an era to define several intervals: 80 years to the return of the Herakleids, 60 years from there to the Ionic migration, 159 years to the guardianship of Lykurgus, and 108 years to the first year of the first Olympiad.

However, historical records frequently exhibit irreconcilable discrepancies. Regarding the second Messenian war, Pausanias claimed it lasted from B.C. 685-668, with a 39-year interval between the first and second wars. In contrast, Justin reckoned an interval of 80 years, and Eusebius reckoned 90 years. Some historians argue that a 39-year interval is too short to align with the phrase "the fathers of our fathers," suggesting that an age is typically marked by members between 35 and 55 years of age.

Other discrepancies appear in the calculations of Phanias and Kallimachus, who reduced the dates of Eratosthenês by 56 years. Phanias omitted 55 years between the Return and the registered Olympiads, while Kallimachus omitted 56 years between the Olympiad of Iphitus and the Olympiad won by Corœbus.

## Political Eras and Dynastic Intervals
In the history of the Bacchiads, a series of reigns is recorded with varying lengths: Aletes (38 years), Ixion (38 years), Agelas (37 years), Prymnis (35 years), Bacchis (35 years), Agelas (30 years), Eudêmus (25 years), Aristomêdês (35 years), Agêmôn (16 years), Alexander (25 years), Telestês (12 years), and Automenês (1 year). 

One year after Automenês took power, the Bacchiad family—consisting of 200 people—abolished the royalty to establish a standing oligarchy with an annual Prytanis. This oligarchy lasted for 90 years (though Strabo states it lasted nearly 200 years) until Kypselus subverted it in 657 B.C. Chronologists provide an interval of 447 years between the Return of the Herakleids and the accession of Kypselus, and 357 years between that return and the start of the Bacchiad oligarchy.

## Punic War Intervals
The period from the first Punic war to the ruin of Carthage spanned 118 years, divided into five distinct intervals:
1. The first Punic war: 24 years.
2. Interval between the first and second Punic wars: 24 years.
3. The second Punic war: 17 years.
4. Interval between the second and third Punic wars: 49 years.
5. The third Punic war: 4 years and some months.

## Calendar Reform and Leap Years
The reformation of the calendar occurred in B.C. 46 under Caesar, who made the year 365 days and directed that one day be intercalated every fourth year to align the civil year with the solar year. In B.C. 46, Caesar intercalated two months of 67 days and an additional 23 days, making that specific year 445 days long.

The additional day every fourth year was originally inserted in February between the 24th and 25th day, termed *bis-sexto calendas* (bissextile). In the modern calendar, this intercalary day is the 29th of February. A historical error occurred when pontiffs intercalated every third year instead of every fourth; over 36 years, they intercalated 12 times instead of 9. Augustus corrected this by ordering all years from the 37th to the 48th of the era to be common years.

## Technical and Agricultural Timeframes
Time intervals are also used to categorize agricultural growth and industrial depreciation:

**Agriculture:** In Kent, hops are planted in October or November. Some planters grow potatoes or mangels between rows during the first year, though this is considered a mistake as plants do not bear much until the second year.

**Industrial Deductions:** In certain maritime or machinery contexts, deductions for repairs are categorized by age:
* **Between 3 and 6 years (C.):** Specific deductions are made for repairs to woodwork, sails, and rigging, with one-sixth deducted off machinery and ironwork of masts and spars.
* **Between 6 and 10 years (D.):** One-third is deducted off ironwork of masts and spars, all hawsers, ropes, rigging, and the renewal of all machinery.
* **Between 10 and 15 years (E.):** One-third is deducted off all repairs and renewals, except for cementing, chain cables, and ironwork of the hull (which receive a one-sixth deduction).
