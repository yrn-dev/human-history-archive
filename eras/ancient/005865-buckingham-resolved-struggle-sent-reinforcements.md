# Buckingham and the Siege of St Martin's

## The Siege of St Martin's
Buckingham conducted a military enterprise involving the siege of the fort of St Martin's, located on the Isle of Ré. Due to the hardness of the ground, the operations for the siege were shifted into a blockade. By September 27, the defenders of the fort had announced that they were ready to surrender the following morning. However, during the night, a gale brought a flotilla of French provision boats that successfully broke through the English blockading squadron. As a result, the fort received enough provisions to last for an additional two months.

## Request for Reinforcements
Following the failure of the surrender, Buckingham resolved to continue the struggle and sent a request to England for reinforcements. While King Charles would have been glad to answer this call, the nation no longer possessed the enthusiasm to supply the need, and the exchequer lacked the necessary money, as England had ceased to care for the war.

## Retreat and Aftermath
Before any reinforcements could arrive, the French deployed a superior force on the island. This forced Buckingham to retreat on October 29. The retreat resulted in heavy losses; of the nearly 7,000 troops, only 2,989 returned to England. Despite this failure, Buckingham remained buoyant in spirit, attributing the outcome to ill luck or the misconduct of others. He continued to develop new plans for the war.

## Political Context in England
Following these events, a parliament met on March 17, 1628. This parliament was determined to secure an obligation from the king to stop encroaching upon the liberties of his subjects in the future.
