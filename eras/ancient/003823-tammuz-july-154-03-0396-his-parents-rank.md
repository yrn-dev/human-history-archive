# Bardaisan

## Early Life and Education
Bardaisan was an early teacher of Christianity in Mesopotamia. According to the *Chronicle of Edessa*, which is considered trustworthy, he was born in the city of Edessa on the 11th of Tammuz (July) in A.D. 154. He was born to parents of rank who were likely pagan. In his youth, Barhebraeus records that Bardaisan served as a priest in a heathen temple located at Mabbog. 

Tradition also suggests that Bardaisan shared an education with a royal prince who later became the king of Edessa. This prince may have been Abgar bar Manu, who reigned from 202 to 217. Bardaisan is said to have converted this prince to Christianity and may have played a significant role in the christianization of the city of Edessa.

## Theological Transitions and Works
The nature of Bardaisan's religious beliefs is a subject of differing accounts. Epiphanius and Barhebraeus assert that he was initially an orthodox Christian and later became a follower of Valentinus. Conversely, Eusebius and the Armenian Moses of Chorene state the opposite: that he began with errors and, in his later years, largely—though not completely—purged himself of them.

Bardaisan was a prolific writer of Syriac works, although these have entirely perished, with the possible exception of the *Hymn of the Soul* found in the *Acts of Thomas*. His earliest known works, as cited by Eusebius and others, were polemical dialogues written against Marcionism and various other heresies; these texts were subsequently translated into Greek. Additionally, during a period of persecution under Caracalla, he wrote an apology for the Christian religion. It is noted that he may have retreated into Armenia for a time during the persecution under Caracalla to preach Christianity.

## Contributions to Hymnology
Bardaisan is regarded as the father of Syriac hymnology. According to St Ephrem, Bardaisan composed 150 hymns, which were written in imitation of the Davidic psalter. His original poetic genius is evidenced by the favor his poems enjoyed during the century and a half between his time and that of St Ephrem. If the *Hymn of the Soul* in the apocryphal *Acts of Thomas* is indeed his work, it represents practically the only piece of real Syriac poetry that has survived.

## Legacy and School
Bardaisan founded a school that was soon branded as heretical. Despite this, his influence persisted through his poetic contributions and his early efforts in spreading Christianity in Mesopotamia and potentially Armenia.
