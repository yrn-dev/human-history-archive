# FROM THE FESTIVAL OF OLYMPIAD 90 DOWN TO THE BATTLE OF MANTINEIA

## Diplomatic Maneuvers Following Olympiad 90
Shortly after the events of the Olympic festival of Olympiad 90, the Argeians and their allies dispatched a new embassy to the Corinthians. The goal of this mission was to invite the Corinthians to join them, as the Argeians believed the recent affront delivered to Sparta provided a promising opportunity to persuade the Corinthians to desert the Spartans.

However, Spartan envoys were also present during these proceedings. Although the discussions were protracted, no new resolution was reached. The congress was abruptly terminated by an earthquake, though it is noted that this event may have been simulated for convenience. The Corinthians, who appeared to distrust Argos due to her union with Athens and leaned toward Sparta, remained unwilling to formally declare themselves in favor of one party, as doing so would create an enemy of the other.

## The Nature and Evolution of the Olympic Games
The Olympic games served as a primary means of measuring the chronological sequence of Grecian events for inquirers from the third century B.C. downwards. This system began with Korœbus in 776 B.C., who was the first in a continuous series of victorious runners formally inscribed and preserved by the Eleians.

Originally, the games were humble, consisting only of a match of runners in a measured course known as the Stadium. Over time, the variety and number of matches increased:
* **7th Olympiad:** Daiklês the Messenian became the first to receive only a wreath from the sacred olive-tree as a reward, replacing more substantial presents with an honorary reward.
* **14th Olympiad:** A second race was introduced, the double stadium (or *diaulos*), where runners went up and down the course.
* **15th Olympiad (720 B.C.):** A third race, the long course (or *dolichos*), was added.
* **18th Olympiad:** Wrestling and the pentathlon were introduced. The pentathlon included wrestling, running, the javelin, the quoit, and jumping.
* **23rd Olympiad (688 B.C.):** Boxing matches were added.
* **25th Olympiad (680 B.C.):** The chariot race with four full-grown horses was introduced, bringing a new class of competitors to the games: rich men and women.

## Other Major Grecian Festivals
While the Olympic and Pythian games remained the most venerated, the Nemean and Isthmian games also achieved significant celebrity, though the Olympic prize was considered the highest. Unlike the Olympic and Pythian games, which occurred every four years, the Nemean and Isthmian festivals occurred every two years.

### The Nemean Festival
Celebrated in the valley of Nemea in honor of the Nemean Zeus, this festival was located between Phlius and Kleônæ. It was originally administered by the Kleônæans. While some sources suggest the Argeians seized administration in Olympiad 53, other evidence suggests the Kleônæans continued to administer the festival until approximately Olympiad 80 (460 B.C.). After 460 B.C., the Argeians assumed the administration. Like the Olympic games, the Nemean games employed *Hellanodikæ* to maintain order and distribute prizes.

### The Isthmian Festival
The Isthmian games were celebrated by the Corinthians at their isthmus in honor of Poseidôn. These games were *trietêric*, occurring every alternate year in the second and fourth Olympic years during the spring (around April or early May). For example, the festival was celebrated in April 412 B.C., toward the end of the fourth year of Olympiad 91. Historically, the festival was significant enough that Solon, in or after 594 B.C., conferred a premium upon any Athenian citizen who won a prize there.

## Chronological and Cultural Context
The broader cultural landscape of Greece included the recitation of epic poetry. Works such as the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* (likely dating between 850 B.C. and 776 B.C.) were not typically read by individuals in isolation but were sung or recited to assembled companies at festivals.

In later periods, festivals continued to serve as political and social markers. The 99th Olympiad in 384 B.C. was particularly distinguished because it followed a general war of eight years that ended with the peace of Antalkidas in 387 B.C. Because the festivals of 392 B.C. and 388 B.C. had low attendance due to war, the 99th Olympiad—much like the 90th Olympiad in 420 B.C.—brought together those who had long been separated in religious fraternity. This environment attracted ambitious figures; for instance, Dionysius used the 384 B.C. festival to display his power by sending a solemn legation (*Theôre*) with gold, silver, splendid tents, chariots-and-four, and trained chorists to exhibit his own poetical compositions.
