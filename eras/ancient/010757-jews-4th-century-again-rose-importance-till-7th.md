# Israelites and Jewish History

The history of the Israelites and Jewish populations is marked by periods of displacement, conflict with imperial powers, and settlement in various regions of the ancient world.

## Ancient Settlements and Conflicts
The city of Ascalon, located on the Mediterranean coast 12 miles north of Gaza, was one of the five chief cities of the Philistines. While it was situated within the nominal territory of the tribe of Judah, the Israelites never possessed the city for any significant length of time. The Bible records a single incident involving the city: the spoliation by Samson. In 701 B.C., the city was taken by Sennacherib. Later, Ascalon suffered severe damage during wars involving the Romans and Jews.

## Displacement and Refugees
Following the destruction of Jerusalem by Nebuchadrezzar in 588 B.C., Jewish fugitives sought refuge in Egypt. Among these refugees was Jeremiah, who traveled to Tahpanhes (also known as Daphnae or Taphne), an ancient fortress near the Syrian frontier on the Pelusian arm of the Nile. The site of Daphnae is known as the "Castle of the Jew's Daughter," a name that appears to preserve the tradition of these Jewish refugees.

## Judea and the Roman Period
During the Roman administration of Judea, the region experienced significant hardship. Under the procurators Cuspius Fadus and Tiberius Alexander, a great famine occurred in Judea. This famine lasted for many years, continuing through the fifth, sixth, and seventh years of the reign of Claudius. During this period of crisis, the Jerusalem Jews received benefactions and supplies from Queen Helena.

## Later Settlements
In southern Russia, the town of Lutsk (also known as Luck), located in the government of Volhynia on the Styr, became a place of residence for Jewish populations. The inhabitants of Lutsk, many of whom were Jews, earned their living primarily through the shipping of goods on the Styr.
