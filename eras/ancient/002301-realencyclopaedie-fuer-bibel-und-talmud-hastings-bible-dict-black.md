## Application in Biblical Analysis
These reference works are frequently utilized by scholars to analyze specific biblical figures and narratives. For example, in the study of the Balaam narratives, these encyclopedias provide foundational data for discussing the recognition of non-Hebrew prophets. The narratives suggest that Yahweh was the God of Balaam as well as Moses, potentially reflecting a period when Yahweh was recognized as a deity by a circle of connected tribes that included the Israelites.

In the analysis of the book of Daniel, specifically the narrative of "Bel and the Dragon," scholars such as Marshall in *Hastings' Bible Dictionary* have contributed theories regarding the original language of the text. While some maintain a Greek original, Marshall argues for an Aramaic origin, viewing certain Aramaic texts as having primary value. Additionally, Marshall suggests that the Greek term for "pitch" in the dragon narrative may have derived from the Aramaic term *zafa*, meaning "storm-wind."
