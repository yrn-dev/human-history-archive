# Ciceron et ses amis

## Overview
*Ciceron et ses amis* is a work of erudition written by the French classical scholar Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier. First published in 1865, the work achieved a level of success rarely granted to scholarly texts of its nature. An English translation of the work, titled *Cicero and his Friends*, was produced by A.D. Jones in 1897.

## Content and Methodology
In *Ciceron et ses amis*, Boissier focuses on the study of the manners of ancient Rome. The author is noted for his ability to reproduce the characteristics of Roman society and re-create its environment with exquisite vivacity. Due to its scholarly value, the work is cited as a general reference for those studying the Mithradatic wars.

## About the Author
Gaston Boissier (1823–1908) was a secretary of the French Academy and a distinguished classical scholar born in Nimes on August 15, 1823. His interest in ancient history was sparked early in life by the Roman monuments located in his native town. 

Boissier's academic career began with a focus on epigraphy. At age twenty-three, he served as a professor of rhetoric in Angouleme for ten years. After being heard by a university travelling inspector, he was appointed as a professor at the Lycee Charlemagne in Paris. His early literary contributions included a study on M. Terentius Varro (1861) and a thesis on the poet Attius (1857). 

In 1861, Boissier became a professor of Latin oratory at the College de France and contributed actively to the *Revue des deux mondes*. Following the success of *Ciceron et ses amis*, he published other significant works, including *L'Opposition sous les Cesars* (1875), which examined the political decadence of Rome under the early successors of Augustus, and *La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins* (1874), a two-volume analysis of the religious movement in antiquity that preceded the acceptance of Christianity.

Boissier was elected to the French Academy in 1876 following the death of H.J.G. Patin. He later served as the academy's perpetual secretary, a position he was appointed to in 1895.
