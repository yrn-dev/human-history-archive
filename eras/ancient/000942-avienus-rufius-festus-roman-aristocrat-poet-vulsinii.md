# AVIENUS, RUFIUS FESTUS

Rufius Festus Avienus was a Roman poet and aristocrat from Vulsinii in Etruria. He flourished during the second half of the 4th century A.D. and was a staunch supporter of the old religion and a pagan. In his administrative career, he likely served as the proconsul of Achaia in 372 and the proconsul of Africa in 366.

## Literary Works and Translations

Avienus produced several works in different poetic meters, focusing heavily on geography and the translation of Greek texts. Writing in hexameters, he translated the *Phainomena* of Aratus and created a paraphrase of the *Periegesis* of Dionysius, which he titled *Descriptio Orbis Terrarum*. Some verses found in the Latin version of the *Periegesis* are not present in the Greek original of Dionysius; these are attributed by some to the fancy of Avienus rather than to the manuscripts.

Additionally, Avienus wrote a description of the coasts of the Black, Caspian, and Mediterranean Seas. This work was composed in iambic trimeters and spanned several books, though only a fragment of the first book survives. He also translated Arrian's *Periplus Ponti Euxeni* into Latin.

Other works attributed to Avienus include epitomes of Virgil's *Aeneid* and the works of Livy, both written in iambic trimeters, though these are now lost. He is also credited with some minor poems found in anthologies, including a humorous piece addressed to a man named Favianus requesting pomegranates for medicinal use.
