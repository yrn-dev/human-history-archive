# Partitiones (1819), Aristaenetus, Epistolae (1822), and Eunapius, Vitae

The works *Partitiones* (1819), *Epistolae* of Aristaenetus (1822), and *Vitae* of Eunapius (1822) are scholarly editions produced by the French classical scholar Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie.

## Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie
Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie (1774–1857) was a French classical scholar born in Paris on August 12, 1774. His early career began in 1792 in the public service under General Dumouriez. Although driven from this position in 1795, he was later restored by Lucien Bonaparte, serving as the secretary to the prefecture of the Upper Marne. Following this period, he resigned from public employment to dedicate himself to the study of the Greek language.

Boissonade's academic career progressed through several prestigious appointments. In 1809, he was named deputy professor of Greek at the faculty of letters in Paris, becoming a titular professor in 1813 after the death of P.H. Larcher. By 1828, he had succeeded J.B. Gail as the chair of Greek at the College de France. In addition to these teaching roles, he served as the perpetual secretary of the Academie des Inscriptions and as the librarian of the Bibliotheque du Roi. He died on September 8, 1857.

## Scholarly Contributions to Greek Literature
Boissonade focused his scholarly attention primarily on later Greek literature. His bibliography includes a wide array of edited texts and collections:

*   **Works from 1806–1815:** He produced editions of Philostratus' *Heroica* (1806), Marinus' *Vita procli* (1814), and Tiberius Rhetor's *De Figuris* (1815).
*   **Works from 1819–1822:** During this period, he published *Partitiones* by Herodian (1819), *Drosilla et Charicles* by Nicetas Eugenianus (1819), the *Epistolae* of Aristaenetus (1822), and the *Vitae Sophistarum* by Eunapius (1822).
*   **Later Works:** His subsequent publications included Philostratus' *Epistolae* (1842), Babrius' *Fables* (1844), and Tzetzes' *Allegoriae Iliados* (1851). He also compiled a *Collection of Greek Poets* spanning 24 volumes.

Beyond these specific titles, Boissonade authored the *Anecdota Nova* (1844) and the *Anecdota Graeca* (1829–1833), both of which are noted for their importance to the study of Greek grammarians and Byzantine history.
