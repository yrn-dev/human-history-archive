## Events of 1572–1573

The years 1572 and 1573 are marked by significant military and political activity across different regions. In Japan, Ieyasu and Takeda Shingen formed a compact against the Imagawa in 1572, leading to a successful campaign by Ieyasu against Ujizane, the son of Imagawa Yoshimoto. However, this alliance was brief; by November 1572, Shingen led a force to seize two Tokugawa castles and threatened the important castle at Hamamatsu. In January 1573, a series of bloody engagements known as the War of Mikata-ga-hara occurred outside Hamamatsu. Ieyasu narrowly escaped into the castle by leaving the gates open to suggest an ambush. The conflict ended when Takeda Shingen died in May 1573.

In Europe, 1572 saw the erection of a fort in Maassluis, Holland, by Philip of Marnix, lord of St Aldegonde; this fort was subsequently captured by the Spanish in 1573. In England, Reginald, the grandson of Henry (who had not assumed the title due to poverty), was recognized as the earl of Kent in 1572; Reginald died in 1573. Additionally, the English admiral Sir Francis Drake set sail in 1572 with two ships, the "Swan" and the "Pasha," to attack Spaniards in the West Indies. Drake plundered the town of Nombre de Dios and traveled across the isthmus of Panama, where he first viewed the Pacific. He returned to Plymouth on August 9, 1573.

Other notable occurrences in 1573 include the birth of the English poet and divine John Donne in the London parish of St Nicholas Olave. In the field of literature, Hieronymus Mercurialis published *De arte Gymnastica* in Amsterdam in 1572.

## English Biographical and Administrative Records

The archive contains records of various English figures and administrative roles. Richard de Lucy, known as "the loyal," served as the chief justiciar of England under Henry II. After serving as sheriff and justiciar of Essex during the reign of Stephen, he held the office of chief justiciar for twelve years following the death of Robert de Beaumont in 1168. Lucy was involved in suppressing the rebellion of the English barons in 1173 and commanded royalists at the battle of Fornham. He resigned his post in 1179 and died at Lesues Abbey in Kent. His son, Godfrey de Lucy, served as bishop of Winchester from 1189 until September 1204.

In the arts, Joseph Maas (1847–1886) was an English tenor born in Dartford and a former chorister at Rochester Cathedral. After studying in Milan in 1869, he achieved success in London in 1871 and later became the principal tenor for Carl Rosa's company in 1878.
