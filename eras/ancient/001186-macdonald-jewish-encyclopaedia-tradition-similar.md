## Analysis of the Tower of Babel
D. B. Macdonald is cited in the *Jewish Encyclopaedia* regarding the tradition of the Tower of Babel. This narrative, as it appears in Genesis, is described as being polytheistic and anthropomorphic. While the origin of the story has not been located in Babylonia, it is believed that the tower was likely inspired by the temple towers of Babylon, such as E-Saggila, the great temple of Merodach (Marduk).

The dispersion of mankind and the variety of languages were viewed as a curse. Professor Cheyne suggests that an ancient North Semitic myth existed to explain this phenomenon, and the event was eventually localized in Babylon. Various accounts describe the tower's end: Cornelius Alexander and Abydenus state it was overthrown by winds, while Yaqut and the *Lisan el-'Arab* record that mankind was swept into the plain of "Babil" by winds and subsequently scattered in the same manner.
