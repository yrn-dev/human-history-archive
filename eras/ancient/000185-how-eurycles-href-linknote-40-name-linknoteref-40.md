# Eurycles

Eurycles was a Lacedemonian (Spartan) who became a prominent figure in the court of King Herod. Described as a man of a perverse mind and corrupt character, he utilized flattery, cunning, and the guise of friendship to infiltrate the inner circle of the royal family for personal financial gain.

## Arrival and Infiltration
Eurycles traveled to Judea and sought the patronage of King Herod, as the resources of Greece were insufficient to support his luxury. He initially gained the king's favor by presenting him with splendid gifts, a strategy he used as a bait to ensure he would eventually receive far greater presents in return. Due to his Spartan origin, he was held in high regard by Herod and the king's associates.

Through subtle speech and lying encomiums, Eurycles identified Herod's "blind side" and tailored his behavior to please the king, eventually becoming one of his most intimate friends. Simultaneously, Eurycles cultivated relationships with other members of the royal household. He lodged in the house of Antipater and established a rapport with Alexander, pretending to have the favor of Archelaus, the king of Cappadocia, and showing respect to Glaphyra.

## Manipulation of the Royal Family
Eurycles adopted a persona of a particular friend to everyone he encountered, leading others to believe his presence was for their own advantage. He specifically targeted the young Alexander, persuading him that Eurycles was the only person to whom he could safely open his grievances. Alexander confided in him regarding his feelings of alienation from his father, as well as his distress over the loss of dignity suffered by his mother and Antipater.

Rather than offering support, Eurycles used these private disclosures to create calumnies. He carried Alexander's complaints to Antipater, claiming he was motivated by kindness. Eurycles further manipulated the family dynamics by delivering a "portentous speech" to Herod, in which he commended Antipater as the only child who truly loved his father and claimed Antipater was an impediment to a plot being hatched by the other sons.

## Accusations and Consequences
The actions of Eurycles led to the false accusation of Herod's sons, specifically Alexander and Aristobulus. These calumnies caused Herod to become exasperated to an "incurable degree." Although Herod eventually found the proofs of the conspiracy to be too weak, he ordered his sons to be kept in custody.

During this period, Euaratus of Cos, another intimate friend of Alexander, testified under oath that he had never heard the accusations against the young men. However, this testimony had no effect, as Herod was only disposed to listen to evidence that supported the guilt of his sons.

## Reward and Banishment
Despite the weakness of the evidence, Herod referred to Eurycles as his "savior and benefactor" and rewarded him with fifty talents. Following this payment, Eurycles immediately departed for Cappadocia to avoid any accurate accounts of his actions being reported. In Cappadocia, he obtained further money from Archelaus by impudently claiming that he had reconciled Herod with Alexander.

Eurycles eventually returned to Greece, where he used his ill-gotten wealth for further wicked purposes. He was later accused twice before Caesar for plundering cities and filling Achaia with sedition, which resulted in his banishment.
