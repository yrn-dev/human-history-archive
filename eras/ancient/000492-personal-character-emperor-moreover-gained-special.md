# The Personal Character of the Emperor

In various historical contexts, the personal character and individual habits of an emperor have significantly influenced the administration of the state, the nature of religious authority, and the dynamics of political power.

## Administrative Influence under Emperor Francis
In the empire governed by Francis, the personal character of the emperor acquired special importance due to modifications in the administrative system. Originally, the empire was organized into several departments, including the Aulic council of war, a general Aulic chamber for trade, mines, domains, post, and finance, a general directory of accounts, and Aulic chanceries for Transylvania, Hungary, and Austria, as well as a chancery of the state, court, and household. The heads of these departments held the rank of secretaries of state and met in council under the royal presidency.

As this body became too unwieldy to function as an effective cabinet, Maria Theresa established the council of state. During the early years of Francis's reign, he utilized a cabinet minister to maintain contact with the departments. However, Francis possessed a passion for detail, and after 1805, he personally undertook the task of keeping the administration together. 

This personal approach created a rigid system:
* **Lack of Contact:** Francis had no personal contact with his ministers; communication was conducted solely in writing, and ministers often went months without meeting to discuss business.
* **Centralization of Decision-Making:** As the council of state was subdivided and enlarged, the emperor became the only synthesis for the various administrative departments.
* **Strict Jurisdiction:** Department heads had strictly defined jurisdictions. Any matter falling outside established precedent, regardless of how insignificant it was, had to be referred to the throne for an imperial decision.

This inelastic system deadened initiative. Officials at all levels, subject to elaborate checks, eventually refused to take responsibility. Consequently, the smallest administrative questions were passed up the bureaucratic hierarchy only to be shelved and forgotten in the imperial cabinet, as Francis could not personally deal with every detail of his vast empire.

## Religious and Quasi-Clerical Character
In the middle ages, the personal character of the emperor was viewed through a religious lens due to the anointing and crowning services derived from the Bible. The act of anointing was considered the most essential feature of this service. Because of this unction, the sovereign was regarded as a *mixta persona*—part layman and part priest.

This theory gained wide acceptance, and the anointed emperor was seen as possessing a priestly character. This status granted the emperor specific ecclesiastical positions, making him a canon of St Peter at Rome, St John Lateran, and the collegiate church of Aachen. Records indicate that during the imperial coronation at Rome, the roles were divided: the pope sang the mass, the emperor read the gospel, and the king of France (or the king of Sicily) read the epistle.

## Political Maneuvering and Imperial Favor
The personal character of those surrounding an emperor often determined their rise to power. For example, Otho, while serving as the commander of Further Lusitania, demonstrated a rare capacity for business and fidelity to the emperor Galba. Otho secured his position in Galba's favor through strategic personal conduct:
* **Courtship of Influentials:** While traveling with Galba, Otho paid special court to Vinius through conversation and presents, ensuring his place as second in the emperor's favor by always yielding the first place to Vinius.
* **Public Affability:** Otho avoided unpopularity by being easy of access and assisting petitioners without taking bribes.
* **Military Influence:** He won the affection of the army by taking a special interest in common soldiers and obtaining promotions for them via the emperor, Vinius, or powerful court freedmen such as Asiaticus and Icelus. Otho further corrupted the army by presenting gold pieces to each soldier of the guard whenever he entertained Galba, appearing to honor the emperor while securing the soldiers' loyalty for himself.
