# Paul de Wit

Paul de Wit was the curator or owner of a music-historical museum whose collection and accompanying catalog served as a reference for the study of early musical instruments.

## The Museum Catalog
De Wit authored the *Katalog des Musikhistorischen Museums von Paul de Wit*, published in Leipzig. The archive contains references to editions of this work from 1903 and 1904. This catalog provided detailed classifications and descriptions of various instruments, which have been used by historians to identify and compare early musical forms.

## Instrumental References
The catalog is cited as a primary source for the identification of early bassoon-type instruments. Specifically, the 1903 edition (p. 127, No. 380, with an illustration on p. 121) describes a dolcian or single curtail created by J. C. Denner. This instrument is compared in musicological studies to the "double curtail" described by Randle Holme and the dolcian in C found in the catalogue of Capt. C. R. Day. These forms of early bassoons are also described and figured by Michael Praetorius in *Syntagma Musicum* (1618) and by Mersenne.

Additionally, the 1904 edition of the catalog (p. 142, No. 564) is used to classify a specific instrument as a *Jagertrompete* (hunting trumpet) based on the works of Praetorius, noting that the instrument possesses a trumpet mouthpiece.

## Collection Status
The collection associated with the *Musikhistorischen Museums von Paul de Wit* was subsequently transferred to Cologne.
