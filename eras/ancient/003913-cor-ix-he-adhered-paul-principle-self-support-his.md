# Barnabas and the Principle of Self-Support

## Overview of Barnabas
Barnabas was a figure in early Christian mission work who, after sailing with Mark to resume his labors in Cyprus, largely disappears from the historical record. The available evidence regarding his later activities consists of occasional and increasingly doubtful glimpses. However, certain records indicate that he was well known and respected at Colossae around A.D. 60.

## Mission Work and Principles
According to 1 Cor. ix. 6, Barnabas adhered to the principle of self-support in his mission work, a practice also utilized by Paul. 

## Traditions and Attributions
Various traditions attempt to account for the later life and travels of Barnabas:
* **Alexandria:** Some early traditions, including those from Clement of Alexandria, regard him as one of the seventy. He is plausibly linked to Alexandria, and the *Epistle of Barnabas* (an Alexandrine text) is ascribed to him.
* **Rome and Milan:** There is stronger, more varied evidence suggesting he visited Rome, cited in the *Encomium* (a late Cypriot source), the early *Actus Petri Vercellenses*, and Clement's *Recognitions* and *Homilies*. Later tradition also suggests a visit to Milan.
* **Epistle to the Hebrews:** Some Western traditions, beginning with Tertullian, ascribe the epistle to the Hebrews to Barnabas. This may be an inference based on the text's description of itself as a "word of exhortation" addressed to the "son of exhortation," a title associated with Barnabas in Acts iv. 36.

## Later Life and Death
Legends claiming that Barnabas performed missionary labors in Cyprus and suffered martyrdom at Salamis are considered late and untrustworthy. While the exact date of his death remains uncertain, it is probable that he was no longer living at the time the Book of Acts was written, approximately A.D. 75-80.
