# Backgammon

Backgammon is a board game in which players move men from point to point based on the throws of dice. The movement of the men is directional: white moves from the inner table of black to black's outer table, then to white's outer table, and finally to white's inner table. Black moves in the opposite direction.

## Movement and Dice
Players move their men alternately. A player may move a man a number of points corresponding to the dice thrown, provided the destination point is not "blocked" by two or more of the adversary's men. A player may use the whole throw for one man or divide it between two men. 

If doublets are thrown (for example, two sixes), the player makes four moves of that number. Some rules state that a player who throws doublets must also play the number corresponding to the opposite side of the dice; for instance, throwing sixes requires moving sixes and then aces. In such cases, the player also receives another throw. If doublets are thrown a second time, the player moves and throws again. A player must be able to complete the doublets thrown to take the corresponding doublets and receive another throw.

It is compulsory to move the whole throw unless blocked. If a player can move the entire throw by changing the order of the dice (e.g., moving a five first to enable a six), they must do so.

## Points, Blots, and Hitting
When a player places two men on the same point, they are said to "make a point." A single man on a point is called a "blot." If a blot is left, it may be "hit" (taken up) by the adversary if they throw a number that allows them to place a man on that point.

A man who is hit is placed on the bar. This player must enter the adversary's home table again at the next throw, provided the throw results in a number corresponding to an unblocked point. The points in the home tables are counted from the ace-point as 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. A player cannot move any other man while they have one on the bar that must enter. Consequently, making all the points in one's own board is an advantage, as it can prevent an adversary from entering after being hit, allowing the player to continue throwing until a point opens.

## Strategy and Probability
Effective play requires knowledge of the thirty-six different possible throws of two dice. Players use these combinations to determine where blots may be left with the lowest probability of being hit. For example, if a blot can only be taken up by an ace, there are eleven ways to throw an ace, making the odds 25 to 11 against being hit.

General principles of movement include:
1. Making points that best obstruct the opponent.
2. Choosing positions for blots where they are least likely to be hit.

The most desirable points to secure are the five-point in both one's own and the adversary's inner table, followed by one's own bar-point and the four-point in one's own inner table. When entering a man that the adversary is likely to hit, it is preferable to leave the blot on the lowest possible point (e.g., the ace-point over the deuce-point).

## Bearing Off and Winning
The game proceeds until a player moves all their men into their inner table, or "home." The player then begins to "bear" (remove) men from the board by throwing a number that corresponds to the point the man is on. If the corresponding point is unoccupied, a move must be made if room exists; however, if the six-point is empty and a six is thrown, a man may be borne from the five-point, and so on.

If a player is hit on a blot after they have started bearing off, they must enter the adversary's inner table and bring that man back into their own inner table before they can resume bearing.

The winner is the first to take off all their men. The type of victory depends on the adversary's progress:
* **A single game (a "hit"):** Won if the adversary has already begun bearing.
* **A double game (a "gammon"):** Won if the adversary has not borne a single man.
* **A triple game (a "backgammon"):** Won if, when the winner bears their last man, the adversary has not borne a man and either has a man up or has one man in the winner's inner table.

In a series of games, the winner of a "hit" takes the first throw in the next game. If a "gammon" is won, each player throws a single die to determine the first move.

## Laws of the Game
According to the laws provided by Hoyle:
* A man touched by the caster must be played if possible.
* A man is not considered played until it is placed on a point and quitted.
* There is no penalty for omitting a man from the board.
* Men borne before a "taken up" man has entered must be entered again.
* If a player mistakes their throw and the adversary has already thrown, the move cannot be altered unless both players agree.
* If dice are "cocked" (do not lie squarely), a fresh throw is required.
