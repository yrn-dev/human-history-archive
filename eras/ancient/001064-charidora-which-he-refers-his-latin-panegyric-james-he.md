# Diophantus and Charidora

*Diophantus and Charidora* is the first English poem written by the Scottish poet Sir Robert Aytoun.

## Author and Literary Context
Sir Robert Aytoun (1570–1638) was the son of Andrew Aytoun of Kinaldie, Fifeshire. Educated at the university of St Andrews, Aytoun was incorporated as a student of St Leonard's College in 1584 and earned his M.A. in 1588. He spent several years living in France. 

Aytoun is noted as one of the earliest Scottish writers to utilize the southern standard as a literary medium for his English works. In addition to *Diophantus and Charidora*, his English output included a variety of sonnets, songs, and other pieces focused on amatory subjects.

## Relation to Other Works
Aytoun's body of work also includes several Latin poems. These include *Basia, sive Strena ad Jacobum Hayum*, *Lessus in funere Raphaelis Thorei*, *Carina Caro*, and an *Epicedium in obitum Thoma Rhodi*, as well as various epitaphic and occasional minor pieces. 

The poem *Diophantus and Charidora* is specifically mentioned by Aytoun within another of his works: a Latin panegyric written for James VI upon his accession to the English throne. This panegyric was composed while Aytoun was in Paris and resulted in him gaining immediate favor at court.

## Professional Life of the Author
Following his literary success and favor at court, Aytoun was knighted in 1612. He served as the private secretary to the queens of Charles I and James I and held various lucrative offices. He died in London and was buried in Westminster Abbey on February 28, 1638. While he maintained a high reputation among his contemporaries as both a person and a writer, he did not possess an ambition to be known as a writer.
