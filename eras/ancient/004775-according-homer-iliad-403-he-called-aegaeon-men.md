# Briareus

Briareus, also known as Aegaeon, is a figure in Greek mythology identified as one of the Hecatoncheires. He is characterized as having fifty heads and three hundred arms. He was the brother of Gyges (or Gyes) and Cottus, and is described as the son of Gaea and either Uranus or Poseidon.

## Mythological Roles and Traditions
The legends surrounding Briareus and his brothers are varied and sometimes contradictory. In the most widely recognized myth, Zeus summoned Briareus and his brothers to assist him during the war between the Olympian gods and the Titans. Following the defeat of the Titans, the three brothers were stationed at the gates of Tartarus, where the defeated enemies were consigned.

Other traditions present a different role for Briareus. One account describes him as one of the assailants of Olympus who was buried under Mount Aetna after his defeat. In the *Iliad* (i. 398), Homer mentions Briareus as an assistant to Zeus during a period when other Olympian deities were plotting against the king of gods and men. Furthermore, a separate tradition depicts him as a giant of the sea and the inventor of warships, ruling over the fabulous Aegaea in Euboea and acting as an enemy of Poseidon.

## Nomenclature and Symbolism
According to Homer in the *Iliad* (i. 403), the figure was called Aegaeon by men, while the gods referred to him as Briareus. 

Scholars have attempted to determine what natural phenomena the Hecatoncheires symbolize. It has been suggested that they may represent the multitudinous motion of sea waves or the gigantic forces of nature associated with convulsions and earthquakes.
