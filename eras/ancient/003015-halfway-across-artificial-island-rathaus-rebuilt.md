# Bamberg

## Geography and Urban Layout
Bamberg is a town and archiepiscopal see located in the kingdom of Bavaria, Germany. It is situated on an open plain along the river Regnitz, approximately two miles above the point where the river joins the Main. By railway, it is located 39 miles north of Nuremberg. 

The town is divided into two primary sections. The upper town is constructed across seven hills, with each hill crowned by a church. The lower town is divided into three districts by the Ludwigskanal and the river; this area remains partially enclosed by ditches and walls. The different sections of the lower town are connected by bridges, the most notable of which is the *Obere Bruecke*, completed in 1455. Located halfway across this bridge on an artificial island is the Rathaus, which was rebuilt between 1744 and 1756.

## Religious Architecture
The town's cathedral is a late Romanesque structure featuring four imposing towers. Founded in 1004 by Emperor Henry II and finished in 1012, the building was partially burnt and subsequently rebuilt during the 13th century. The cathedral houses several significant works of art, including an equestrian statue of Emperor Conrad III and a marble tomb for the empress Cunigunde and the founder, Henry II, which was carved by Tilman Riemenschneider between 1499 and 1513.

Other significant churches in Bamberg include:
* **Jakobskirche**: A Romanesque basilica dating from the 11th century.
* **Marienkirche (or Obere Pfarrkirche)**: Built between 1320 and 1387 and restored to its original pure Gothic style.
* **St Martinskirche**.
* **Michaelskirche**: A 12th-century Romanesque church located on the Michaelsberg. It previously served as the church for a Benedictine monastery that was secularized in 1803.

## Civic Buildings and Institutions
The former Benedictine monastery on the Michaelsberg now houses the museum, municipal art collections, and the Buergerspital (alms-house). The town's royal lyceum, which previously served as a Jesuit college, contains the royal library—consisting of over 300,000 volumes—and other notable collections.

Bamberg features two palaces:
* **The Old Palace (*Alte Residenz*)**: A picturesque building constructed in 1591 on the site where the counts of Babenberg once had a residence.
* **The New Palace**: Built between 1698 and 1704, this building was formerly used by prince-bishops. Between 1864 and 1867, it was occupied by the deposed King Otto of Greece.

Additionally, the town features the Maximilian fountain (1880), which includes statues of St Otto (bishop of Bamberg), Conrad III, Emperor Henry II and his wife, and Maximilian I of Bavaria. A castle known as the Altenburg, standing at 1266 feet, is located a short distance from the town.

## Population
The population of Bamberg grew from 31,521 in 1885 to 45,308 by 1905.
