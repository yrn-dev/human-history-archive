# Heber and Malchiel

The names Heber and Malchiel appear in historical and genealogical records within the archive, associated with different groups and lineages, including the Israelites and the Kenites.

## Associations with the Tribe of Asher
In the context of the district west of Galilee, known to the Egyptians as Aser(u) during the time of Rameses II and Seti I (the latter half of the 14th century B.C.), the archive notes discussions regarding the nature of the Asher population. Some theories suggest Asher was an Israelite tribe that migrated north, or a district regarded as part of the national kingdom and categorized as a "son" of Jacob through genealogical devices.

Within this context, two specific clans of Asher, Heber and Malchiel, have been associated with the Habiri (a people) and Milk-ili (a hostile chief). These names appear in the Amarna Tablets. ## Heber in Genealogical Traditions
The name Heber appears prominently in the genealogical records of the sons of Shem. According to these accounts, Shem, the third son of Noah, had five sons. One of these was Arphaxad, who fathered Sala. Sala, in turn, was the father of Heber.

Heber is identified as the progenitor from whom the Israelites were originally called Hebrews. Heber begat two sons: Phaleg and Joetan. Phaleg received his name because he was born during the dispersion of nations to their respective countries; in the Hebrew language, Phaleg signifies "division."

Joetan, the other son of Heber, fathered twelve sons:
*   Elmodad
*   Saleph
*   Asermoth
*   Jera
*   Adoram
*   Aizel
*   Decla
*   Ebal
*   Abimael
*   Sabeus
*   Ophir
*   Euilat
*   Jobab

These descendants inhabited areas extending from Asia to Cophen, which is described as an Indian river.

## Heber the Kenite
Separate from the aforementioned lineages, the archive records a Heber who belonged to the Kenites. The Kenites were a clan or tribe located in the south of Palestine. They were closely associated with the Amalekites, though they did not share the Amalekites' hostility toward Israel. Because of this, they were spared by Saul when he was commanded by Yahweh to destroy the Amalekites, and they maintained friendly terms with David while he lived in Judah.

Heber the Kenite lived near Kadesh in Naphtali. His wife was Jael, who is noted as the slayer of Sisera. The presence of Heber's clan in the Naphtali region is attributed either to the nomadic habits of the Kenite tribe or to a general northward movement of clans.
