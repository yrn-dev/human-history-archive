# FROM THE 29TH TO THE 35TH SOVEREIGN

The period spanning the 29th to the 35th sovereigns of Japan encompasses a succession of rulers from the mid-6th century to the mid-7th century. This era is characterized by the reigns of several monarchs and the influence of prominent hereditary clans known as *uji*.

## Chronology of Sovereigns
The succession of sovereigns during this period is recorded as follows:
*   **29th Sovereign, Kimmei**: A.D. 540–571
*   **30th Sovereign, Bidatsu**: A.D. 572–585
*   **31st Sovereign, Yomei**: A.D. 586–587
*   **32nd Sovereign, Sushun**: A.D. 588–592
*   **33rd Sovereign, Suiko**: A.D. 593–628
*   **34th Sovereign, Jomei**: A.D. 629–641
*   **35th Sovereign, Kogyoku**: A.D. 642–645

Regarding the 35th sovereign, Kogyoku, records indicate that she abdicated and became Empress Dowager before later becoming Empress again (referenced under Saimei).

## The Role of the Uji
During the broader historical span from the time of Emperor Jimmu to that of Empress Suiko (A.D. 593–628), the administration of the state relied on nobles serving in ministerial capacities. Out of forty such ministers, the distribution of power was heavily concentrated in specific *uji*:
*   **Mononobe**: 16 ministers
*   **O-omi (Kwobetsu)**: 9 ministers
*   **Otomo**: 6 ministers
*   **Nakatomi**: 6 ministers
*   **Oga**: 2 ministers
*   **Imibe**: 1 minister

The military *uji*, specifically the Mononobe and Otomo, provided twenty-two of the forty ministers over a period of approximately twelve centuries.

## Functions of the Great Uji
Several *uji* performed specialized roles essential to the operation of the Court and the spiritual life of the nation:

*   **Nakatomi**: This clan acted as intermediaries between the Throne and the deities, discharging duties related to divination and religious supplication.
*   **Imibe**: This clan was responsible for all matters pertaining to religious festivals. Their office required them to practice *imi suru*, which meant abstaining from all things considered unclean.
*   **Kume**: Descended from Amatsu Kume no Mikoto, the members of this *uji* served as guards of the Court and as chamberlains.
*   **Oga-uji**: This clan, descended from Okuninushi, is recognized as the eighth of the great *uji*.

## Related Historical Records
Information regarding this era and the early history of the Japanese people is preserved in the *Ko-jiki* (Records of Ancient Things), which contains the *Kuji-hongi* and extends to A.D. 628.
