# Justin

The name Justin appears in the archive's records across several distinct historical contexts, referring to an early Christian apologist, a source for Greek and Macedonian history, and a figure in later Roman imperial history.

## Justin Martyr and Early Christian Rites
Justin, writing around A.D. 140 in his first *Apology*, provided a detailed account of the process by which individuals dedicated themselves to God after being "made new through Christ." According to Justin, those who believed the teachings were true and committed to living accordingly were instructed to pray and entreat God for the remission of past sins through fasting, a practice joined by those performing the instruction.

The rite of regeneration involved bringing the candidates to water for a washing. This was performed in the name of the "Father and Lord of the universe," the "Saviour Jesus Christ," and the "Holy Spirit." In related instructions, it is noted that if neither the standard requirements were met, water could be poured upon the head three times in the name of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. The process required the person baptizing and the person being baptized to fast, with the latter specifically bidden to fast for one or two days prior to the rite.

Additionally, Justin is linked to a chain of tradition extending to Jerome in the *Morale des Peres*. He is also cited alongside other authors regarding views on the desire to procreate.

## Justin as a Historian of Greece and Macedonia
Justin serves as a significant, though sometimes contested, source for the history of ancient Greece and Macedonia. His accounts frequently overlap with or contradict other ancient historians such as Diodorus, Herodotus, and Plutarch.

### The Persian Invasions and Athens
Regarding the Persian invasions of Greece, Justin provides specific figures that differ from other sources. He states that ten thousand Athenians and one thousand Platæans were involved, whereas other authors list ten thousand as the total for both groups. Justin further claims the total Persian army numbered six hundred thousand, with two hundred thousand perishing. These figures contrast with other records that suggest totals of five hundred thousand, three hundred thousand, or one hundred and ten thousand men. While some suggest Justin follows Herodotus on these matters, he is noted to include details absent from Herodotus and to vary from him on several unimportant particulars.

### Macedonian Dynasties and Figures
Justin provides information on the lineage and political struggles of Macedonia:
* **Lineage:** He identifies Menelaus as the father of Amyntas and the grandfather of Philip. He describes Menelaus as an illegitimate son.
* **Philip and Amyntas:** Justin records that Philip initially governed as the guardian of his young nephew, Amyntas (son of Perdikkas), before being constrained by the Macedonians to take the crown. Justin also notes that Amyntas survived Philip's reign but was later executed by Alexander on charges of conspiracy.
* **Military Strength:** In accounts of Philip's army, Justin's statements regarding the size of the force contradict those of Diodorus, who claimed Philip's army was superior in number.

### Other Greek Accounts
Justin is cited in relation to the treatment of a young woman described by Thucydidês as a deliberate insult, providing more precise statements regarding the use of torture. He is also quoted for his "just and discriminating" language regarding Nicias, noting that Nicias contended to remain whether due to the shame of a poorly handled matter, fear of the citizens' lost hope, or the impulse of fate.
