# Baena

Baena is a town located in the province of Cordova in southern Spain. It is situated 32 miles by road to the south-east of the city of Cordova. The town is positioned picturesquely on the slope of a hill near the river Marbella; the summit of this hill is crowned with a castle that was formerly the property of the famous captain Gonzalo de Cordova.

## Demographics and Economy
As of 1900, the population of Baena was 14,539. The local economy is driven by several chief industries, including the manufacture of olive-oil, linen-weaving, horse-breeding, and farming.

## History and Antiquities
The town has roots dating back to the Roman period. The site of the Roman town, known as Biniana or Baniana, can still be traced today, and various Roman antiquities have been disinterred from the area.

In the medieval period, Baena was the site of a conflict in 1292 when the Moors, led by Mahommed II of Granada, besieged the town. The siege was unsuccessful, as the town was held for Sancho IV of Castile. This successful defense is commemorated in the town's coat-of-arms, which features five Moorish heads.

## Infrastructure and Geography
Baena is located within the province of Cordova, an inland region of southern Spain. The province is divided by the river Guadalquivir into two distinct areas: the mountainous Sierra Morena to the right bank and the great plains of La Campina and undulating country to the left bank. 

The nearest railway station to Baena is Luque, which had a population of 4,972 in 1900. Luque is located 4 miles south-east of Baena on the Jaen-Lucena line. Additionally, the area near Luque is noted for the production of iron ore.
