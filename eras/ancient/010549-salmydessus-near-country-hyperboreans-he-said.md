# Hyperboreans and Salmydessus

The Hyperboreans are a people of Greek mythology and legend, described as dwelling in a remote northern region. Their homeland is characterized in various accounts as a "delicious country" and an "elysium" located beyond the Rhipaean mountains. According to the poet Pindar, this land is inaccessible by either land or sea, reachable only through the "wings of the poet."

## Geography and Neighbors
The location of the Hyperboreans is placed at the furthest reaches of the known world. Aristeas, the son of Caÿstrobios of Proconnesos, composed verses stating that beyond the land of the Issedonians dwelt the Arimaspians—a one-eyed race—and beyond them were the gold-guarding griffins. The Hyperboreans dwelt beyond these, extending as far as the sea.

While Aristeas described these regions, other sources provide conflicting reports. The Scythians, for instance, report nothing about a Hyperborean people, though the Issedonians are said to do so. Herodotus notes that the Northern parts of the continent are largely uninhabitable due to continual snowfall, which the Scythians symbolically refer to as "feathers" that fill the air, preventing passage or sight into further parts of the continent.

## Mythological Associations
The Hyperboreans are closely linked to the god Apollo (Phoebus). Aristeas is said to have undertaken a pilgrimage under Apollo's inspiration to visit the Hyperboreans, who were special votaries of the god. Additionally, legend states that Leto was conveyed from the Hyperboreans to Delos in the form of a she-wolf over a period of twelve days to escape Hera.

There are also ritual connections between the Hyperboreans and the island of Delos. When Hyperborean maidens died there, the boys and girls of the Delians would cut off their hair as a tribute. Unmarried girls would wind a lock of hair around a spindle and place it on the tomb, which is located to the left of the entrance to the temple of Artemis under an olive tree; boys would wind their hair around a green shoot of a tree and place it upon the same tomb.

## Salmydessus and Boreas
Salmydessus is a location in Thrace, situated near the country of the Hyperboreans. It is cited as one of the dwelling-places of Boreas, the personification of the north wind and son of Astraeus and Eos. 

Boreas is known for carrying off Oreithyia, the daughter of King Erechtheus of Athens, to Mount Haemus, where they lived as king and queen of the winds. Their children included two daughters, Chione and Cleopatra, and two sons, Zetes and Calais (the Boreadae). 

The connection between Salmydessus and the Boreadae is highlighted in the accounts of the Argonauts. Upon arriving at Salmydessus, the winged twins Zetes and Calais liberated their sister, Cleopatra, and her two sons from a prison. They had been imprisoned by Cleopatra's husband, Phineus, who was the king of that country. In an alternative account, the twins are said to have delivered Phineus from the Harpies.

## Historical and Legendary References
The concept of the Hyperboreans occasionally appeared in reports of historical events, often as a result of "strange legends." For example, Herakleides of Pontus reported a rumor that an army from the Hyperboreans had sacked a Greek colony called Rome near the great ocean. This is interpreted as a legendary adornment of the actual capture of Rome by the Gauls.

Other legends mention Abaris, a reported Hyperborean who traveled the earth carrying an arrow and eating no food. In the context of ancient migrations, the mystic Aristeas suggested that the movement of tribes—such as the Cimmerians being expelled from the Euxine sea—began with the griffins in the extreme north, as the sacred nature of the Hyperboreans made them incompatible with bloodshed or aggression.
