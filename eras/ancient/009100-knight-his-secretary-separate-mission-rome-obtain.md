# Missions to Rome and Diplomatic Envoys

The historical archive contains various accounts of diplomatic missions and the roles of secretaries and knights in state affairs. ## Diplomatic Missions to Rome

Michel de Castelnau, Sieur de la Mauvissiere, a French soldier and diplomatist born around 1520, conducted a mission to Rome to determine the views of Pope Paul IV regarding France. Upon his arrival, he discovered that Paul had died; consequently, Castelnau utilized his influence to support the election of Pius IV. This mission followed other assignments for Castelnau, including roles as ambassador to Queen Elizabeth and envoy to the princes of Germany.

Other figures mentioned in the archive conducted missions to Italy or the Mediterranean, though not all were specifically directed to Rome. George Macartney, Earl Macartney, returned to England in 1795 following a confidential mission to Italy.

## The Role of Secretaries in State Affairs

Secretaries have historically played critical roles in the execution of diplomatic and administrative tasks. In the Persian administration under King Dareios, the archive notes that all provincial governors employed Royal Secretaries. An example of the power associated with this office is seen in the actions of Bagaios, the son of Artontes. Bagaios used the Royal Secretary in Sardis to read sealed papers to the guard spearmen. By presenting a final paper read by the secretary, Bagaios conveyed a command from King Dareios to slay Oroites, which the guards executed immediately.

In English diplomacy, secretaries often held positions of significant influence. Dudley Carleton, Viscount Dorchester, served as secretary to the earl of Northumberland, a connection that initially associated his name with the Gunpowder Plot. In other contexts, secretaries accompanied high-ranking envoys; for instance, Captain (later Sir Henry) Yule served as secretary to Major (later Sir Arthur) Phayre during a mission to the Burmese court in the summer of 1855.

## Knights and Orders of Merit

The archive details several instances of individuals being knighted as a result of their diplomatic or public service. George Macartney was knighted upon resigning his office as chief secretary for Ireland. Sir Henry Killigrew, an English diplomatist who conducted negotiations in Scotland and Germany, was knighted in 1591. Dudley Carleton was knighted in 1610 before being sent as ambassador to Venice.

The archive also describes formal orders of knighthood. The Royal Victorian Order, instituted by Queen Victoria on April 25, 1896, includes a secretary and various classes of knights, such as knights commanders and knights grand commanders. Similarly, the Order of St Michael and St George, founded in 1818, includes a secretary among its officers. This order was originally for natives of Malta and the Ionian Islands, but was later extended to British subjects who held high and confidential offices in colonial possessions or rendered services related to the foreign affairs of the Empire.

## Other Diplomatic Activities

Beyond Rome and Italy, the archive records various other international missions. Dudley Carleton served as ambassador to Holland, where he worked to improve relations despite commercial disputes and the massacre of Amboyna. In 1855, King Mindôn of Burma sent a mission of compliment to Lord Dalhousie, the governor-general. While Major Phayre's subsequent mission to the Burmese court succeeded in increasing knowledge of the country, it failed in its primary objective of obtaining a treaty.
