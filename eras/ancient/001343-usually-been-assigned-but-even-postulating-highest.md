# Chronology of Early Babylonian Civilization

## Estimates of Antiquity
The dating of the earlier dynasties of Babylonia has undergone considerable reductions, which in turn affects the overall estimate of the age of Babylonian civilization. In previous scholarship, many writers assigned dates as high as 5000 or 6000 B.C. to the earliest remains of the Babylonian Semites and the Sumerians. These high estimates were largely dependent on a statement made by Nabonidus, who claimed that a period of 3200 years separated his own era from that of Nar-am-Sin, the son of Sargon of Agade. Based solely on this statement, a date of 3800 B.C. was usually assigned to Sargon.

## Challenges to the Nabonidus Chronology
The accuracy of the figure provided by Nabonidus has not been universally accepted. Some scholars, such as Lehmann-Haupt, have suggested an emendation of the text to reduce the number by one thousand years, while Winckler has characterized the statement as an uncritical exaggeration. 

There are several theories regarding why the scribes of Nabonidus produced such high figures. One possibility is that the scribes were not interested in diminishing the antiquity of the foundation-inscription of Nar-am-Sin, which Nabonidus had unearthed. Additionally, recent discoveries suggest that the scribes may have acted in good faith but mistakenly reckoned a number of early dynasties as consecutive when they were actually contemporaneous.

## Gaps in Chronological Schemes
Even when the highest possible dates for the Dynasties of Ur and Babylon are postulated, the resulting chronological scheme contains enormous gaps. These gaps are not represented by any record or royal name. In an attempt to fill these voids, Radau invented dynasties and kings; however, the existence of these invented figures has since been definitely disproved.

## Current Archaeological and Epigraphic Evidence
At present, it is not possible to fix a definite date for the early kings of Agade. However, both epigraphic and archaeological evidence indicates that the empire of the Semitic kings of Agade was not separated by a very long interval from the empire of the kings of Sumer and Akkad. The rule of the latter was inaugurated by the founding of the Dynasty of Ur.
