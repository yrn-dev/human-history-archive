# Cyrus and the Rise of the Persian Empire

## Accession and Early Conquests
Cyrus, known as the "king of Anshan" in Elam, began his rise to power by revolting against his suzerain, Astyages, the king of the "Manda" or Scythians, at Ecbatana. This revolt occurred in either 553 B.C. or 549 B.C. (the sixth year of the Babylonian king Nabonidus). During this conflict, the army of Astyages betrayed their king to Cyrus. Consequently, Cyrus established himself at Ecbatana, ending the empire of the Scythians—an empire Greek writers referred to as that of the Medes.

By 550 B.C., Cyrus had acceded to the throne of Cyaxares. At this time, he ruled an empire extending from Persia to the boundaries of Lydia, and potentially as far as India. His authority was further solidified in the east; although the province of Baktria initially resisted, it acknowledged his authority after discovering that Cyrus had become the master of Astyages and his son-in-law.

## The Conquest of Babylonia
Three years after becoming king of Persia, Cyrus engaged in a campaign in northern Mesopotamia. At this time, the Babylonian king Nabonidus had established a camp at Sippara near the northern frontier, with his son—likely Belshazzar—in command of the army. 

In 538 B.C., Cyrus invaded Babylonia. In June of that year, a battle took place at Opis, resulting in a Babylonian defeat. Following this, Sippara surrendered. Nabonidus fled to Babylon, where he was pursued by Gobryas, the governor of Kurdistan. On the 16th of Tammuz, two days after the fall of Sippara, the soldiers of Cyrus entered Babylon without fighting. Nabonidus was captured in a hiding place, and Kurdish guards were stationed at the gates of the temple of Bel to ensure services continued without interruption.

## Differing Historical Accounts of the Fall of Babylon
Archive sources provide varying accounts of the transition of power in Babylon. According to the writings of Josephus, the city was taken by Cyrus, the king of Persia, after Baltasar had reigned for seventeen years. In this account, the prophet Daniel interpreted a writing on the wall for Baltasar, foretelling misfortunes. After the city fell, Darius and his kinsman Cyrus ended Babylonian dominion. This version states that Darius was sixty-two years old, the son of Astyages, and appointed Daniel as one of three presidents over 360 provinces.

However, other archive records characterize "Darius the Mede" as a mythical figure or a historical interpolation. These sources argue that there is no historical evidence of a Median ruler between the last Semitic king of Babylon and Cyrus. They suggest the confusion may have arisen from the later king, Darius Hystaspis, or from the Median destruction of Nineveh sixty-eight years prior to the fall of Babylon.

## Relations with Lydia and Greece
The growth of Persian power under Cyrus was noted by other contemporary monarchs, specifically Croesus, the king of Lydia. After a period of mourning for his son, Croesus sought to cut short the power of the Persians while it was still growing.

In later periods, the legacy of Cyrus continued to influence Persian politics. The descendants of Cyrus, such as Atossa, played roles in the succession of later kings. For example, Xerxes claimed a right to the throne based on being the son of Atossa, arguing that Cyrus was the one who had won freedom for the Persians.

## Later Conflicts and Legacy
The archive contains records of later figures named Cyrus. One such Cyrus served as a prince and enemy of Athens, utilizing the treasures and forces of the Persian king to wage war. This Cyrus was influenced by Lysander and maintained strong anti-Athenian dispositions, refusing to mediate with Athenian envoys or accept the views of Tissaphernes regarding the prolongation of the Grecian wars.

Another account describes a conflict involving a Cyrus who was pursued by the Greeks. During a retreat, Cyrus was confronted by Artagerses, the leader of the Kadousians. Artagerses hurled a javelin at Cyrus, which was deflected by a breastplate. Cyrus responded by killing Artagerses with a javelin to the throat.
