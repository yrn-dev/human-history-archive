# Babylonian and Assyrian Proper Names

The study of proper names used by the Babylonians and Assyrians has historically presented significant challenges to scholars. During the early stages of deciphering cuneiform inscriptions, the reading of these names caused great difficulty. While most of these obstacles have been overcome and a general consensus exists regarding the principles of pronunciation and formation for the thousands of names found in literary productions, business documents, votive inscriptions, and historical records, some minor differences in scholarly opinion remain.

## Linguistic and Cultural Context
The Assyrian language is described as being practically identical to the Babylonian language. Similarly, the Assyrians are considered to be the same people as the Babylonians, though they possess some foreign admixtures. 

## Formation of Names
Proper names in these cultures can vary in complexity and structure. Some full names are composed of three or more elements. However, an early trend toward reduction is evident in the records, where these elements were reduced to two. This reduction occurred either through the omission of the name of a deity or by combining a verbal form with the name of a deity.

Further simplification led to names consisting of only one element. A characteristic feature of these single-element names is the frequent use of "hypocoristic affixes," which function similarly to modern pet-names. Examples of these endings include:
* *-tum* (feminine)
* *[=a]n*
* *[=a]*
* *um*
* *atum*
* *atija*
* *sha*

Additionally, in many instances, the divine element of a name was lopped off, although it had originally been present.

## Scholarly Research and Documentation
Because different scholars continue to employ varying spellings and transliterations of Babylonian and Assyrian names, absolute uniformity in the field has not yet been achieved. To address the problems and difficulties involved—particularly regarding Sumerian names—scholars refer to several key works categorized by historical period:
* **Sumerian period:** Huber, *Die Personennamen in den Keilschrifturkunden aus der Zeit der Koenige von Ur und Nisin* (1907).
* **Old Babylonian period:** Ranke, *Early Babylonian Proper Names* (1905).
* **Neo-Babylonian period:** Tallqvist, *Neu-Babylonisches Namenbuch* (1905).
