# Authorities for Obscure and Difficult History

The reconstruction of history often relies on authorities and sources that are fragmented, contradictory, or obscure. Across various eras and regions, historians must navigate conflicting accounts, linguistic corruption, and the absence of definitive records to establish a probable narrative of the past.

## Ancient Empires and Early Civilizations
The history of early empires is frequently characterized by a lack of certainty. In the case of the Assyrian Empire, the duration of its power is a subject of significant debate among authorities. Ctesias, whose view is shared by Justin, asserts the empire lasted thirteen hundred years, while Herodotus reduces this figure to five hundred and twenty. This discrepancy may be attributed to interruptions or diminutions of power within the empire. Because monuments are often contrary to one another and modern systems of interpretation differ, establishing an incontestable opinion is difficult. One method of establishing probability is to equate the antiquity of the Assyrian empire with that of its capital, Babylon, which holy Scripture records was built by the great conqueror Nimrod.

Similarly, the history of Britain following the withdrawal of Roman troops is considered extremely obscure. Two primary accounts exist regarding the arrival of the Saxons. Gildas records that the Britons called in the Saxons for protection against raids by the Scots and Picts, though the Saxons eventually turned against the Britons due to insufficient payment. Conversely, the *Historia Brittonum* suggests that the first Saxon leaders, Horsa and Hengest, arrived as exiles seeking protection from King Vortigern. According to this tradition, they expelled northern invaders before overcoming the Britons through treachery. Despite these differing narratives, most Welsh, Gaulish, and English authorities agree that the first invasion occurred between 428 and 455.

## Linguistic and Classical Sources
In some instances, history is hidden within language itself. In *Geschichte der deutschen Sprache*, the history of the Teutonic tribes is determined through the study of the German language. This process involves collecting scattered allusions and words from classical writers to determine the relationship between German and the languages of the Scythians, Thracians, and Getae. However, these languages are often known only through corrupted remains preserved by Latin and Greek authors, leaving many questions permanently obscure.

The use of classical authors also presents challenges regarding the nature of the source material. In Grecian history, some learned German manuals and the work of K. F. Hermann have made sweeping assertions against Athenian democratic institutions based on passages from Aristophanês. However, it has been noted that many "matter-of-fact" historical transcriptions are actually Aristophanic jests. This comic point of view is also evident in Xenophon's description of the jester Philippus in the *Symposion*, and the purpose of Plato's *Symposion* is described as very difficult to follow.

## Political and Social Transitions
The decline of Mongol energy during the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries is noted as being very difficult to explain. After a period of world ascendancy under Timurlane and Jengis, Central Asia fell into extreme political impotence. Authorities suggest several possible causes for this recession, including infections of a malarial type, unrecorded pestilences, and changes in climate. Some authorities further propose that the spread of Buddhist teaching from China exerted a pacifying influence. By the sixteenth century, these peoples were being pushed back and subjugated by China in the east and Christian Russia in the west.

In more modern contexts, the "obscure" nature of history refers to the difficulty of gauging diplomatic significance in real-time. During the months of July, August, and September preceding a potential war between Great Britain, France, and Germany, the situation remained oppressive and obscure. It was extremely difficult for British authorities to gauge the exact significance of the points at issue or the precise character of German diplomacy.

## Specialized Historical Transactions
For specific, obscure transactions, historians refer to specialized academic reviews. The authorities for certain difficult transactions are discussed by E.A. Freeman in the *English Historical Review* (July 1887) and are compared with the work of Gibbon in *Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire* (vol. iii, pp. 505-506).

In the realm of literary history, determining the specific contributions of collaborators can be difficult. For example, the popular novels of Elizabeth Bekker (also known as Wolff), such as *Abraham Blankaart* and *Historie van Sara Burgerhart*, were written in conjunction with Agatha Deken, making it difficult to determine the exact qualities contributed by each author.
