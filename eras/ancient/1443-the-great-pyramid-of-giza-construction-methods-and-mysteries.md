# The Great Pyramid of Giza: Engineering the Eternal

## Overview
The Great Pyramid of Giza, constructed for the Pharaoh Khufu during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, remains the preeminent symbol of ancient Egyptian ingenuity. Standing as the only surviving wonder of the ancient world, it was the tallest man-made structure on Earth for over 3,800 years. 

The primary mystery surrounding the pyramid is not *why* it was built—as a monumental tomb and a vehicle for the king's ascent to the afterlife—but *how* it was achieved. The structure consists of approximately 2.3 million stone blocks, some weighing up to 80 tons. While the Egyptians left no blueprints, archaeological evidence suggests a highly organized state bureaucracy capable of mobilizing thousands of skilled laborers and seasonal farmers.

Historians and engineers continue to debate the specific methods of elevation. The "Straight Ramp" theory suggests a long incline, though this would have required as much material as the pyramid itself. The "Spiral Ramp" theory proposes a path winding around the exterior. More recently, some scholars have proposed internal ramps or the use of water shafts to lubricate the movement of sledges.

## Key Facts
*   **Composition:** The core is primarily local limestone, while the interior chambers (like the King's Chamber) are made of massive Aswan granite.
*   **Precision:** The pyramid is aligned to true north with an accuracy of within three-sixtieths of a degree.
*   **The Casing:** Originally, the pyramid was covered in polished Tura limestone, which would have reflected the sun, making the structure shine brilliantly white.
*   **Labor Force:** Contrary to the Greek historian Herodotus's claim that slaves built the pyramids, modern archaeology (specifically the discovery of worker tombs) proves the laborers were paid workers who were well-fed and respected.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Mortar Mystery:** The mortar used to bind the stones is stronger than the stones themselves, and while its exact chemical composition is known, scientists cannot perfectly replicate its durability today.
2. **Eight Sides:** The Great Pyramid is not actually four-sided; the faces are slightly concave, creating an eight-sided effect that is only visible from the air during the equinoxes.
3. **The Void:** In 2017, the "ScanPyramids" project used muon tomography (cosmic-ray imaging) to detect a massive, previously unknown void above the Grand Gallery.
