# Sippara

Sippara is an ancient city located in Babylonia. Archaeological evidence indicates the existence of two distinct sites associated with the name: one dedicated to the sun-god and another dedicated to the goddess Anunit.

## Geography and Location
The site of the temple of the sun-god of Sippara was discovered by Hormuzd Rassam between 1880 and 1881 at Abu-Habba. This location is situated south-west of Bagdad, positioned midway between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers on the south side of a canal. This canal may have once served as the main stream of the Euphrates. The Sippara associated with the goddess Anunit, known today as Der, is located on the opposite bank.

## Historical Significance and Administration
Sippara's history is closely linked to the region of Akkad. The capital of the elder Sargon was situated close to Sippara, and some sources suggest it may have been a quarter of the city itself. The rise of Sargon's empire contributed to the extension of the name "Akkad" to denote northern Babylonia.

During the reign of Nebuchadrezzar, a line of fortification was constructed between Sippara and Opis to protect the kingdom from attacks originating from the north. This fortification is identified as the "Wall of Semiramis" mentioned by Strabo.

In the later Babylonian period, the city remained a center of military and religious activity. Belshazzar, the son of Nabonidos, took command of the army and lived with it in a camp near Sippara. The Babylonian Chronicle notes that from the 7th year of Nabonidos (548 B.C.), "the son of the king" remained with the army in Akkad, in the close vicinity of Sippara.

## Religious and Economic Life
The temple of the sun-god at Sippara was a significant recipient of royal patronage. Belshazzar bestowed numerous gifts upon the temple. Records show that in the 17th year of Nabonidos, approximately three weeks after the forces of Cyrus entered Babylonia, Belshazzar paid 47 shekels of silver to the temple as a "tithe" on behalf of his sister.

The city also appears in administrative and legal records. A contract discovered at Sippara is dated to the fourth year of the Assyrian king Assur-etil-ilani. This period was marked by instability, as it is possible that Assur-etil-ilani's rule in Babylonia was disputed by his vizier (Rab-shakeh), Assur-sum-lisir.

## Archaeological Discoveries
Modern exploration of Sippara has been conducted by various international efforts. In 1880-1881, Hormuzd Rassam fixed the position of the two Sipparas (or Sepharvaim) through his discovery of the sun-god's temple at Abu-Habba. Additionally, in 1897, Dr. V. Scheil, working for the Turkish government, discovered a collection of tablets on the site of Sippara, which were subsequently housed in the Museum at Constantinople.
