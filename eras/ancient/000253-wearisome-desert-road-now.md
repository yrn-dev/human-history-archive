# OF THE WEARISOME AND DESERT ROAD THAT HAS NOW TO BE TRAVELLED

The route departing from the city of Kerman is characterized by extreme hardship and a lack of resources, spanning a journey that is described as most wearisome for the first seven days.

## The Initial Three-Day Trek
The first three days of the journey from Kerman are marked by a near-total absence of water. Any water encountered during this period is described as a bitter green substance, so heavily salted that it is undrinkable for humans. Consumption of even a single drop of this water is said to cause excessive purging, a condition that also applies to the salt produced from these streams, rendering it unusable.

Because of these conditions, travelers must carry sufficient water to sustain people for the full three days. Cattle, however, are forced to drink the salt water due to their intense thirst. This often results in severe scouring, which in some instances leads to the death of the animals. During this three-day stretch, there is no human habitation, and the region is a desert defined by extreme drought. The environment is so barren that no wild beasts exist there, as there is nothing available for them to eat.

## The Underground Stream and Subsequent Desert
Following the initial three days of drought, travelers reach a stream of fresh water that runs underground. This water is accessible through holes broken in the ground, possibly caused by the stream undermining the surface. This location provides an abundant supply of water, allowing travelers and their beasts to rest and refresh themselves after the hardships of the desert.

After this respite, the journey enters another desert that extends for four days. While similar to the first desert, this section is distinguished by the presence of wild asses. At the conclusion of these four days, the kingdom of Kerman ends, and travelers arrive at the city of Cobinan.

## Geographical and Route Analysis
Historical analysis of this route suggests several possibilities regarding the path taken. While the direct road from Kerman to Kúbenán (via Zerend and Sár i Benán) totals approximately 95 miles, it is noted that a seven-day journey suggests a more circuitous route. One theory proposes a path to the east of the mountains via Kúhpáyeh and the desert north of Khabis.

Another analysis identifies this region as the Desert of Lút. In this desert, the only available water is found in a rivulet called Shor-Rúd (the Salt River), which is described as dirty, salt, bitter, and green, aligning with the descriptions of the water encountered on the road. Some scholars suggest the route ran nearly due north from Kerman toward Tún or Tabbas, though it would still pass the Shor-Rúd.

## Wildlife and Environmental Observations
The presence of wild asses on the road has been a point of historical record. These animals are identified as the *Asinus Onager*, known as *Gor Khar* in Persia or *Kulan* by the Tartars. Reports indicate that these animals, along with other game, can be found in the wooded spots along the road. Specifically, the area around Báfk is noted for its groves of date-trees, thickets of the Ghez tree, and abundant wild tufts and bushes.
