# F. E. Peiser: Babylonische Vertrage and Keilinschriftliche Actenstuecke

## Overview of Legal Works
F. E. Peiser was a scholar who produced several significant works regarding the legal and contractual systems of ancient Babylon. Among his primary contributions are the publications *Babylonische Vertrage*, published in Berlin in 1890, and *Keilinschriftliche Actenstuecke*, published in Berlin in 1889. These texts serve as resources for the study of contracts and legal documents from the region.

## Contributions to Babylonian Law
Peiser's work on Babylonian legal life extended beyond these two volumes. He collaborated with J. Kohler to produce *Aus dem babylonischen Rechtsleben*, published in Leipzig starting in 1890. Additionally, Peiser contributed to the *Keilinschriftliche Bibliothek* edited by Schrader, specifically authoring the fourth volume titled "Texte juristischen und geschaeftlichen Inhalts," which was published in Berlin in 1896.

Further expanding his research into the legal frameworks of the era, Peiser and J. Kohler also published *Hammurabis Gesetz* in Leipzig in 1904, focusing on the laws of Hammurabi.

## Influence on Chronological Systems
The research and emendations provided by Peiser had an impact on the historical dating of Babylonian dynasties. Specifically, the scholar Hommel utilized an emendation by Peiser regarding the length of Dynasty III. This adoption, combined with Hommel's own elimination of Dynasty II, led Hommel to propose extremely low dates in 1898. However, by 1901, Hommel abandoned Peiser's emendation in favor of alternative chronological schemes.
