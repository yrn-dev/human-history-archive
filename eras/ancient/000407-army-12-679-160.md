## Financial Expenditures in the Dual Monarchy
In the budgetary records of the Dual Monarchy, the Ministry of War oversaw significant expenditures for both the army and the navy. In one recorded period, the specific expenditure for the Army was L12,679,160, while the Navy accounted for L2,306,100. Additional military costs included Extraordinary Military Expenditure totaling L4,785,500 and Extraordinary Military Expenditure in Bosnia amounting to L315,920.

Joint budget data for the Ministry of War (covering both Army and Navy) shows a steady increase in expenditure from 1875 to 1905. In 1875, the expenditure was 9,005.4 thousand sterling, rising to 10,085 thousand in 1885, 12,539 thousand in 1895, 13,887.5 thousand in 1900, and reaching 18,087.7 thousand by 1905.

## Military Mobilization in Japan
In 15th-century Japan, armies were collected in Kyoto by two primary protagonists, Katsumoto and Sozen. By the close of 1466, these forces were estimated at 160,000 men for Katsumoto and 110,000 men for Sozen. 

The shogun attempted to prevent widespread disturbance by ordering rival inheritors to fight their own battles and declaring that anyone striking the first blow on their behalf would be labeled a rebel. Despite these orders, Sozen's followers attacked Hatakeyama Masanaga, a former kwanryo, in February 1467 and drove him from the capital. During this period, the shogun was kept under the guard of Hosokawa troops. These troops, encamped to the north and east of Muromachi, were designated as the Eastern Army, while the Yamana forces, massed to the south and west, were known as the Western Army.

## Ancient Greek Military Affairs
Historical records regarding ancient Greece detail various military preparations and conflicts. Demosthenes noted instances where matters concerning war were disordered, undefined, and uncorrected, mentioning the appointment of trierarchs and the search for financial resources. He further argued for the necessity of continuous preparation and power rather than relying on assistance to wage war.

In the conflict between Sparta and her enemies, warfare extended to the coast of Asia Minor and the islands. Notable events include the defeat near Knidus in 394 B.C. and the maritime force of Konon and Pharnabazus at the Isthmus of Corinth in 393 B.C. The subsequent restoration of the fortified port and Athenian Long Walls, along with Konon's fleet activity, led the Spartans to fear the emergence of a second Athenian maritime empire.

## Medieval and Other Military References
In medieval Europe, the kings of France served as advocates and vassals of the monastery of St. Denys. From the 12th to the 15th century, the French armies were led by the *oriflamme*, a square, red or "flaming" banner received from the abbot.

Other records mention the movement of armies in the East, such as Conrad, who advanced with part of his army along a central road between the coast and the route to Iconium. His army was destroyed without a battle after being betrayed by the Greeks. Later, Conrad and Louis advanced to Ephesus before returning to Constantinople at the invitation of Manuel; Louis is noted for engaging in a "glorious action" at the passage of the Mæander.
