# Cuthbert Hamilton Turner

Cuthbert Hamilton Turner, M.A., is a scholar and academic who has held several distinguished positions at the University of Oxford. He served as a Fellow of Magdalen College, Oxford, and is a Fellow of the British Academy. Between 1906 and 1909, Turner held the position of Speaker's Lecturer in Biblical Studies at the University of Oxford. Additionally, from 1899 to 1902, he served as the first Editor of the *Journal of Theological Studies*.

## Published Works

Turner has contributed to biblical scholarship through various writings. He is the author of "Chronology of the New Testament" and "Greek Patristic Commentaries on the Pauline Epistles," both of which appear in Hastings' *Dictionary of the Bible*. He is also associated with the work *New Testament Chronology*.

## New Testament Chronology

The study of New Testament chronology is divided into two primary sections: the chronology of the apostolic age (the chronology of the Acts) and the chronology of the life of Christ (the chronology of the Gospels).

### The Chronology of the Gospels
The chronology concerning the life of Christ is organized around three definite points and the intervals between them: the Nativity, the Baptism, and the Crucifixion. The duration of Christ's public ministry connects the Baptism to the Crucifixion, while the age of Christ at the time of his Baptism connects the Nativity to the Baptism. These different data points are used to mutually test, confirm, or correct one another.

Regarding the date of the Nativity, the common computation of Anni Domini, first proposed by Dionysius Exiguus in Rome during the early 6th century, has long been viewed as too late. Primitive church fathers, including Hippolytus, Clement of Alexandria, Irenaeus, and Tertullian, suggested dates of 2 or 3 B.C. While the received chronology of the last two centuries has moved this date to 4 B.C., evidence suggests the true date may be even earlier.

### The Chronology of the Acts
The chronology of the New Testament outside of the Gospels covers the period from the Crucifixion in A.D. 29 (30) to the fall of Jerusalem in A.D. 70 and the persecution of Nero in A.D. 64. Because many events in Christian history during this window cannot be dated with exactness, scholars rely on the book of Acts as the only continuous authority. 

The construction of a chronology for at least the latter half of the apostolic age is made possible by two synchronisms with secular history found in Acts: the replacement of Felix by Festus (xxiv. 27) and the death of Herod Agrippa I (xii. 23).
