# Montenegro

## Geography and Regional Context
Montenegro is a mountainous region located within the Balkan Peninsula, the most easterly of the three large peninsulas at the southern extremity of the European continent. Geographically, it is situated among the lofty mountains of the west, alongside Albania, and is part of a complex system of ranges that run generally from the north-west to the south-east.

In a broader historical and regional context, Montenegro is included in the area known as Illyria. This region extends along the eastern shore of the Adriatic from Fiume to Durazzo and reaches inland as far as the Servian Morava and the Danube. Illyria comprises several modern states and provinces, including Montenegro, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Dalmatia, the southern half of Croatia-Slavonia, the sanjak of Novibazar, part of western Servia, and the extreme north of Albania.

## Early Inhabitants and Migrations
In antiquity, the northerly mountains of Montenegro were inhabited by tribes bearing the general name of Illyrians. These tribes were bounded to the west by the Adriatic and to the east by the mountain-range of Skardus.

The ethnographic composition of the region changed significantly during a great Slavonic immigration that began in the 3rd century A.D. and continued for the following four centuries. In the 7th century, Serbo-Croats invaded the north-western regions of the peninsula, which included Montenegro, Servia, Bosnia, Herzegovina, Croatia, and northern Albania. During this period, the immigrant Serbo-Croats either assimilated or expelled the existing Illyrian population.

## Ethnic and Religious Development
The population of Montenegro is identified as part of the Slavonic group, specifically the Serbs. The Serbo-Croats originally settled in the Balkan Peninsula in the 7th century after leaving their homes among the Carpathians. While a distinction between the Croats of the north-west and the Serbs of the central region was established by the 12th century—a distinction drawn by early Byzantine chroniclers—this division did not stem from racial or linguistic differences.

Instead, a strong religious difference developed over time. While those along the Dalmatian and Croatian coasts were influenced by Latin civilization and converted to the Roman Catholic Church, the Orthodox creed prevailed in the wild and mountainous interior. Consequently, the Orthodox Serbs inhabit Montenegro, along with Herzegovina, the kingdom of Servia, parts of Bosnia, and Old Servia (including north-western Macedonia and Novibazar).

## Language and Culture
The inhabitants of Montenegro speak a Servian dialect of the Slavonic language. During the period of Turkish rule in the Balkans, the official language was Turkish, and Greek was often the language of the Christian upper classes due to the privileges of the Greek church and its literary superiority. However, the Slavonic masses preserved their own languages, which prevented the extinction of their nationalities.

Montenegro is noted for its early contributions to Slavonic literacy. In "free Montenegro," the first Slavonic printing-press was established in 1493. While the Servian, Bulgarian, and Rumanian languages adopted many words from Greek and borrowed largely from Turkish vocabularies, they maintained their own structural forms. In later Servian literature, the popular idiom eventually superseded ecclesiastical Russian-Slavonic under the influence of Vuk Karajich and Obradovich.
