# The Reports of the Magicians and Astrologers of Nineveh and Babylon

The *Reports of the Magicians and Astrologers of Nineveh and Babylon* is a two-volume work published in London in 1900 by R. C. Thompson. It is cited in historical and bibliographic records as a significant source for the study of astrology and its relationship to astronomy, as well as its connection to other forms of popular belief.

## Context within Ancient Mesopotamian Studies
The work is situated among a body of literature concerning the religious and scientific practices of the ancient Near East. It is frequently listed alongside other monographs and texts focusing on the religion, magic, and divination of the Assyrians and Babylonians. These include studies on Babylonian magic and sorcery, the "Devils and Evil Spirits of Babylonia" (also by R. C. Thompson), and various transliterations and translations of cuneiform texts.

The subject matter of the *Reports* aligns with the broader academic exploration of the "Chaldæans" at Babylon. The Chaldæans were an hereditary order of priests who served as masters of religious ceremonies, literature, and science. They were specifically devoted to astronomical observation, a practice favored by the brilliant skies of the region.

## Relationship to Astrology and Astronomy
In the archive's bibliographies, Thompson's work is categorized as part of the history of astrology. It is grouped with texts that examine the "Chaldean astrology" and the "astrological-astronomical texts" of the era. The study of these reports contributes to the understanding of how ancient practitioners in Nineveh and Babylon integrated the observation of stars with the practice of astrology.

This field of study often intersects with the history of divination. The *Reports* are listed alongside other works on Assyro-Babylonian divination and the "astronomical epocha" at Babylon, such as the Æra of Nabonassar (beginning in 747 B.C.), which provides a chronological framework for the list of Babylonian kings.

## Geographic and Political Setting
The practitioners described in the *Reports* operated within the two primary centers of power in Mesopotamia: Nineveh and Babylon. 

*   **Nineveh:** Located on the Tigris, Nineveh was a "stone city" and served as the capital for the Assyrians. It is described as a city of immense magnitude; some sources, citing Ktêsias, suggest a circuit of nearly sixty English miles.
*   **Babylon:** Located on the Euphrates, Babylon was a "brick city." While it was often in some form of dependence on the sovereigns of Nineveh, it was governed by its own chiefs and kings. It is noted as having been the first city in Western Asia, characterized by its dimensions, walls, and the laborious cultivation of its fertile lands.

For centuries, power fluctuated between these two cities, with rulers from either Nineveh or Babylon claiming the title of "king of the world." The intellectual and religious environment of these cities—specifically the influence of the Chaldæan priests—provided the foundation for the astrological and magical reports documented by Thompson.
