# Mithradates I. and Phraates II.

## Expansion of the Arsacid Empire
The Arsacid empire rose in the west during a period characterized by the sudden and complete overthrow of the Graeco-Bactrian kingdoms, which demonstrated the inherent weakness of those Greek states. During this era of expansion, Mithradates I. and Phraates II. began to conquer several of the western districts of the Graeco-Bactrian territories. Their conquests were particularly notable in Areia, which is identified as Herat.

## Regional Geopolitics and the Arsacids
The territorial gains of the Arsacids occurred alongside significant shifts in power across eastern Iran and Central Asia. While the Arsacids secured western regions—including Seistan, Areia (Herat), and portions of the Helmund valley—other forces emerged to the north. Mongolian tribes, referred to by the Greeks as Scythians, entered the region, with the Tochari (identified by the Chinese as the Yue-chi) being the most prominent among them. 

According to Chinese sources, these tribes entered Sogdiana in 159 B.C. and conquered Bactria in 139 B.C. Over the following generation, they ended Greek rule in eastern Iran. While Greek conquerors such as Apollodotus and Menander maintained their presence in India for a longer period, by the middle of the 1st century B.C., the "Indo-Scythian" empire, ruled by the Kushan (or Kushana) dynasty, controlled western India and the whole of eastern Iran.

## Later Transitions in the Region
The Kushan dynasty, which established its principal seat in Bactria and maintained control over Baluchistan and eastern Afghanistan, eventually began to decay in the 3rd century. By approximately A.D. 320, the Gupta empire was founded in India, reducing the Kushanas to eastern Iran, where they engaged in conflict with the Sassanids.

The region saw further successive waves of conquest. In the 5th century (around 450), the Ephthalites, also known as "white Huns," subjected Bactria. They were subsequently followed by the Turks, who appeared in history around A.D. 560 and subjugated the lands north of the Oxus, bringing most of the Kushan or Tochari small principalities under their control. This cycle of rule ended when the Arabs overthrew the Sassanian empire and advanced eastwards, bringing Bactria and the entirety of Iran up to the banks of the Jaxartes under the rule of Islam and the caliph.
