# Seventh and Eighth Years of the War—End of Corcyraean Revolution—Peace

## The Conflict Between Corinth and Corcyra
Following a naval engagement, the Corcyraeans established a trophy on the headland of Leukimme. While they slew most of their captives, they retained the Corinthians as prisoners of war. This victory left the Corcyraeans as the masters of the sea in those regions. They utilized this dominance to ravage the territory of Leucas, a colony of Corinth, and burnt Cyllene, the harbor of the Eleans, as punishment for the Eleans providing money and ships to Corinth.

In response to the suffering of its allies, Corinth spent two consecutive years building ships and utilizing large bounties to recruit rowers from the Peloponnese and other parts of Hellas to create an efficient fleet. Alarmed by these preparations and lacking any allies in Hellas—having not joined either the Lacedaemonian or Athenian confederacies—the Corcyraeans sought an alliance with Athens. Corinth, attempting to prevent the joining of the Athenian and Corcyraean navies, sent its own embassy to Athens to oppose this union.

By the end of the summer, Corinth established encampments at Chimerium in Thesprotis and Actium to protect friendly cities and Leucas. The Corcyraeans countered by forming a station on Leukimme. The two forces remained in confrontation until winter approached.

## The Situation at Pylos
The Athenians held prisoners whom they determined to keep until a peace was reached, with the threat of execution should the Peloponnesians invade their country in the interim. Meanwhile, the defense of Pylos continued. Messenians from Naupactus, who shared a common dialect with those in their old country, conducted destructive incursions into Laconia. 

The Lacedaemonians, inexperienced in this type of warfare, became seriously uneasy as Helots began deserting and the threat of revolution grew within their own country. Despite a reluctance to reveal these vulnerabilities to Athens, the Lacedaemonians sent multiple envoys to Athens to recover the prisoners and Pylos. The Athenians rejected these envoys, continuing to seek further advantages.

## Developments in the Seventh Year
During the first half of the seventh year of the Peloponnesian war, operations in Sicily remained indecisive. While the Athenian fleet under Eurymedon joined Pythodôrus, and a large fleet remained at Rhegium, the Athenians did not undertake any considerable actions during the autumnal half of the year.

However, the capture of Sphakteria and the subsequent humiliation of Sparta created a shift in momentum. The Sicilian Dorians, intimidated by the changing state of affairs, sought pacification. The Dorian city of Kamarina, which had previously aligned with Chalkidic or Ionic cities, first made a separate accommodation with the neighboring city of Gela. Subsequently, deputies from all cities in the island were invited to Gela to conclude a peace.

## The Eighth Year and the Shift in Fortune
The eighth year began with high hopes for Athens and dark prospects for Sparta. Athens held Pylos, Kythêra, and Spartan prisoners, leading to the belief that a rising among the Helots was probable. 

During this year, Athens launched two major enterprises: one against Bœotia and one against Megara. The plan against Megara was partially successful, but the campaign against Bœotia resulted in a ruinous defeat. Furthermore, Athenian power was further diminished by losses in Thrace following the defeat at Delium and the rising expectations surrounding the career of Brasidas. Consequently, the year ended in painful humiliations for Athens, contrasting sharply with the sanguine hopes that had characterized its beginning.
