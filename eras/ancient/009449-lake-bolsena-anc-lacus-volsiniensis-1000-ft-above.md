# The Lake of Bolsena

The Lake of Bolsena, known in ancient times as *Lacus Volsiniensis*, is a body of water located in Italy at an elevation of 1,000 feet above sea level. It is almost circular in shape, covering an area of 71 square miles and reaching a depth of 480 feet. While it served as the central point of a large volcanic district, it is likely not an extinct crater itself, though basaltic formations are visible in places along its sides. The lake is noted for being abundant in fish, but its banks are somewhat deserted and are not free from malaria.

## Islands and Landmarks
The lake contains two islands: Martana and Bisentina. The island of Bisentina features a church constructed by Vignola. On the island of Martana, there are remains of a castle where Amalasuntha, the daughter of Theodoric, was imprisoned and strangled.

## The Town of Bolsena
Situated on the north-east bank of the lake is the town of Bolsena (ancient *Volsinii*), located in the province of Rome, Italy. As of 1901, the town had a population of 3,286. It is positioned 12 miles west-south-west of Orvieto by road and is dominated by a picturesque medieval castle.

The town contains the church of S. Christina, which dates from the 11th century and features frescoes that may belong to the school of Giotto. The church possesses a fine Renaissance facade constructed around 1500 by Cardinal Giovanni de' Medici (who later became Pope Leo X), as well as terra cottas by the Della Robbia. Beneath the church are catacombs containing the tomb of S. Christina, which was discovered in 1880. According to legend, S. Christina was martyred in 278 by being drowned in the lake.

## The Miracle of Bolsena
A significant event known as the miracle of Bolsena occurred in 1263 at one of the altars in the crypt of the church of S. Christina. A Bohemian priest, who was sceptical of the doctrine of transubstantiation, became convinced of its truth when drops of blood appeared on the host he was consecrating. In commemoration of this event, Pope Urban IV ordered the erection of the cathedral of Orvieto and instituted the festival of Corpus Christi. This miracle is also the subject of a celebrated fresco by Raphael located in the Vatican.

## Historical Geography
According to a generally adopted theory, the Roman Volsinii was transferred to Bolaena, while the Etruscan Volsinii occupied the site of Orvieto. Because of this, Orvieto was referred to as *Urbs vetus* during medieval and late classical times.
