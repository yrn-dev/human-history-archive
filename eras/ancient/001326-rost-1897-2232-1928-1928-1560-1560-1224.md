# Rost (1897)

The data attributed to Rost (1897) pertains to the chronological dating of the first three dynasties of Babylon. These dates are presented as part of a comparative analysis of various historical and archaeological timelines proposed by different scholars.

## Chronology of Babylonian Dynasties
According to the records from 1897, Rost proposed a specific timeline for the durations of the early Babylonian dynasties. The dates provided for these periods are as follows:

*   **Dynasty I:** 2232–1928 B.C.
*   **Dynasty II:** 1928–1560 B.C.
*   **Dynasty III:** 1560–1224 B.C.

## Academic Context and Sources
The work by Rost from 1897 was published in *Mitteil. der vorderas. Gesellschaft*, volume ii. This research exists alongside other chronological theories of the era. For instance, Rost later provided a different set of dates in 1900, published in *Orient. Lit.-Zeit.*, iii, No. 6, which listed the dynasties as 2231–1941 B.C., 1940–1573 B.C., and 1572–1179 B.C.

The 1897 figures are contrasted in the archive with several other scholars' estimations:
*   **Oppert (1888):** Dated the first three dynasties from 2506–2202 B.C., 2202–1834 B.C., and 1834–1257 B.C.
*   **Sayce (1899 and 1902):** Proposed dates of 2478 (or 2460)–2174 B.C., 2174–1806 B.C., and 1806–1229 B.C.
*   **Winckler:** Offered various ranges between 1892 and 1905, with his 1905 estimate placing the dynasties approximately at 2400–2100 B.C., 2100–1700 B.C., and 1700–1150 B.C.
*   **Maspero (1897):** Suggested 2416–2082 B.C., 2082–1714 B.C., and 1714–1137 B.C.
*   **Peiser (1891):** Proposed a significantly later timeline starting at 2051–1947 B.C., followed by 1947–1579 B.C., and 1579–1180 B.C.
