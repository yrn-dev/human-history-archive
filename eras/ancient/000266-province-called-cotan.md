# COTAN

## Geography and Administration
Cotan is a province situated between the north-east and east. The territory is described as being eight days' journey in length. The province is subject to the Great Kaan, though some historical records note that the region was destroyed by the Mongols under Jenghiz Khan in 1220. The capital of the province, which gives the kingdom its name, is also called Cotan.

## Economy and Society
The capital city is described as the most noble of the towns and villages found throughout the country. Cotan is characterized by a great abundance of resources, specifically cotton, hemp, flax, wheat, and wine. The inhabitants maintain estates, gardens, and vineyards. The economy of the province is based on manufactures and commerce; the people are noted for not being soldiers.

## Religious History
The population of Cotan is described as worshippers of Mahommet. However, the region's religious history has seen various shifts:
* **Buddhism:** In the early centuries of the Christian era, the area was a flourishing center for the cult of Buddha, featuring temples decorated with precious metals and paintings. The Buddhist government of Khotan was destroyed by Boghra Khân around 980–990, though it was temporarily restored in 1209 by Kutchluk Khân, the chief of the Naïmans.
* **Islam:** In the 8th century, the Arab chieftain Kotaiba ibn Moslim conquered the region after a 25-year struggle and imposed Islam upon the people.
* **Other Influences:** A bronze cross, the only Christian monument discovered in Khotan, was brought back by Grenard.

## Nomenclature and Historical Records
The province is known by several names across different languages and chronicles. In Sanskrit, it is referred to as Kustana, and in Chinese as Yu-than, Yu-tien, Kiu-sa-tan-na, and Khio-tan. Chinese chronicles mention the location as early as the 2nd century B.C., and the Chinese conquered the area in A.D. 73, after which it generally remained dependent upon the Chinese empire.

## Later History
Following the period described by Marco Polo, who passed through the town in 1274, the region faced further instability. It suffered severe damage during the Dungan revolt against China between 1864 and 1875. Shortly thereafter, the region was again impacted when Yakub Beg of Kashgar established himself as the master of East Turkestan.
