# ASCLEPIODOTUS

## Overview
Asclepiodotus was a Greek military writer who flourished during the 1st century B.C. Historical records regarding his life are extremely sparse; the only known biographical detail is that he was a pupil of the Stoic philosopher Poseidonius, who died in 51 B.C.

## Literary Contributions
Asclepiodotus is the supposed author of a treatise concerning Graeco-Macedonian tactics, titled *Taktika Kephalaia*. However, scholars suggest that this work is likely not his own original composition. Instead, it is believed to be a skeleton outline of lectures delivered by his master, Poseidonius, who is known to have written on the subject of tactics.

## Influence of Poseidonius
As the teacher of Asclepiodotus, Poseidonius was a prominent figure of his era, described as the chief Stoic of his time. A native of Rhodes, Poseidonius was a teacher to Cicero and was known for his ability to flatter his former pupil's vanity. 

Poseidonius's intellectual contributions spanned several fields:
* **Astronomy:** Cicero mentions a "Sphere of Poseidonius" used to represent the motions of the moon, the sun, and the five planets (stars).
* **Roman Nomenclature:** Poseidonius argued against the theory that the third name (cognomen) of Romans was the proper individual name. He used the examples of Lucius Mummius (given the name Achaicus) and Scipio (given the name Africanus) to argue that if the third name were the individual name, those with only two names would effectively be without a name. He proposed that the first name marked the individual, the second was common to the kin (such as the Cornelii or Pompeii), and the third was an appellation based on a person's actions, disposition, or physical peculiarity (such as Sulla or Torquatus).
* **History:** In his analysis of Roman military history, Poseidonius characterized the Roman leaders Fabius Maximus and Claudius Marcellus as the "shield" and "sword" of Rome, respectively. He viewed the steadiness of Fabius and the warlike ardour of Marcellus as the combined saving of the state during the conflict with Hannibal.
* **Natural Phenomena:** Poseidonius provided accounts of the Nymphæum in the territory of Apollonia, describing it as a rock that emits fire with springs of warm asphaltus at its base. He noted that excavations in the asphaltic earth are filled back in over time as the earth changes into asphaltus.
