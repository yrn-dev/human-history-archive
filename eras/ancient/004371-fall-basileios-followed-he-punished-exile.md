# The Fall and Punishment of Basileios

## The Administration of Basileios
In the Eastern Roman Empire, during the early reign of Basil II (c. 958–1025), the administration was managed by a eunuch named Basileios. An illegitimate son of Romanus I and the president of the senate, Basileios was described as a gifted and wily individual. He initially hoped that the young emperors, Basil II and his brother Constantine, would serve as his puppets. While Basileios held power, Basil II refrained from interfering, instead focusing his efforts on learning military science and the details of administrative business.

## Conflict and Rebellion
The stability of the throne was threatened by the landed aristocracy of Asia Minor, whose ambitions were a constant menace. One such figure was the general Bardas Sclerus, who was deposed from his position in the East by Basileios. Sclerus subsequently seized control of the Asiatic provinces and threatened Constantinople. The revolt was eventually suppressed in 979 after Bardas Phocas, a general recalled from a monastery, defeated Sclerus in a third battle. However, Phocas later rebelled himself in 987 and was proclaimed emperor by his troops. Evidence suggests that the minister Basileios was privy to this rebellion, which was fueled by dissatisfaction regarding the energy displayed by the emperor.

## The Fall of Basileios
Following these events, Basileios suffered a political fall. His punishment consisted of exile and the confiscation of his enormous property. 

## Social and Economic Policies
Basil II engaged in a ruthless war against the system of immense estates that had developed in Asia Minor. This system had previously been the target of efforts to check it by his predecessor, Romanus I. Through these actions, Basil II sought to provide protection for the middle and lower classes.
