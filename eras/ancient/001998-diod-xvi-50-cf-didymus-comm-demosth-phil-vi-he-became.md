# Bagoas

Bagoas was a Persian official and eunuch who rose to become a dominant political figure during the reigns of several Persian kings. The name Bagoas (_Bagoi_) is a shortened version of names such as Bagadata, meaning "given by God," and was frequently used for eunuchs.

## Rise to Power and Administration
Bagoas served as the confidential minister to Artaxerxes III. In an effort to bring Egypt back under the control of the Persian Empire, Bagoas allied himself with Mentor, a Rhodian condottiere. This partnership succeeded, likely around 342 B.C. Following this victory, Mentor was appointed general of the maritime provinces to suppress rebellions and provide Greek mercenaries to the king. Meanwhile, Bagoas was tasked with the administration of the upper satrapies. Through this role, Bagoas accumulated significant power, eventually becoming the real master of the kingdom.

## Wealth and Religious Conflict
Bagoas acquired great wealth through the exploitation of Egyptian religious institutions. He confiscated sacred writings from Egyptian temples and subsequently sold them back to the priests in exchange for large bribes.

His influence extended to the religious affairs of Jerusalem. When Jesus, the high priest of Jerusalem, murdered his brother Johannes within the temple, Bagoas—who had been a supporter of Johannes—responded by imposing a new tax on the Israelites. Bagoas entered the temple and asserted that he was purer than the murderer who was currently performing the priestly office.

## Political Maneuvers and Regicide
Bagoas exercised lethal control over the Persian throne. In 338 B.C., he murdered King Artaxerxes III and all of the king's sons, with the exception of the youngest, Arses. Bagoas raised Arses to the throne, but two years later, he murdered Arses as well. He then installed Darius III as king.

The relationship between the new king and his vizier (known in Greek as the _chiliarchos_) eventually deteriorated. When Darius III attempted to establish his independence from Bagoas, Bagoas tried to poison the king. However, Darius was warned of the plot and forced Bagoas to drink the poison himself.

## Legacy and Other Figures
Following the conquests of Alexander the Great, Bagoas's former possessions were redistributed. Alexander presented Bagoas's house in Susa, which contained rich treasures, to Parmenio. Additionally, Bagoas is noted for having gardens in Babylon that contained the best species of palms.

Historical records mention another individual named Bagoas, also a eunuch, who was a favorite of Alexander the Great.

A separate account suggesting that Bagoas was an Egyptian who killed Artaxerxes III in retaliation for the killing of the sacred Apis is regarded as lacking historical value.
