# The Noble Truths and the Eightfold Path

The Noble Truths constitute the core principles of the gospel delivered by the Buddha (the Blessed One). These truths were presented as a means to achieve deliverance from life and its inevitable suffering, providing a way to end the "weary path of transmigration" that the Buddha and his brethren had long traversed.

## The Nature of Suffering and Its Origin
The first Noble Truth concerns the reality of suffering. According to the teachings, birth, decay, disease, and death are all attended with pain. Suffering is further defined as the union with the unpleasant, the separation from the pleasant, and any craving that remains unsatisfied. In summary, the conditions of individuality—referred to as the five aggregates of clinging—are painful.

The second Noble Truth identifies the origin of this suffering as "craving thirst." This craving causes the renewal of becomings and is characterized by a search for satisfaction in various forms: the gratification of the senses, the desire for prosperity, or the craving for a future life. This thirst is accompanied by sensual delights.

## The Cessation of Pain
The third Noble Truth is the passing away of pain. This state is achieved when no passion remains and when one is emancipated from, gets rid of, and no longer harbours the craving thirst. 

The Buddha taught that once the four Noble Truths are grasped, the craving for existence is rooted out and that which leads to renewed existence is destroyed. Consequently, there is no more birth. For the elect disciple who possesses this way of Truth, rebirth as a ghost, an animal, or in any place of woe is destroyed, and they are no longer liable to be reborn in a state of suffering, thereby ensuring final salvation.

## The Noble Eightfold Path
The fourth Noble Truth is the way that leads to the cessation of pain, known as the Noble Eightfold Path. This path is described as a "Middle Path" discovered by the Tathagata, which avoids two extremes: the low and pagan ideal of devotion to attractions based on passions (fit for the worldly-minded and ignoble), and the practice of painful, unprofitable asceticism.

The Noble Eightfold Path is designed to bestow understanding, open the eyes, and lead to insight, higher wisdom, peace, and Nirvana. It consists of the following eight elements:
*   Right Views
*   Right Aspirations
*   Right Speech
*   Right Conduct
*   Right Mode of Livelihood
*   Right Effort
*   Right Mindfulness
*   Right Rapture

## Historical Context of the Teaching
The Buddha shared these principles with five ascetics in the Deer-forest near Benares. These ascetics had initially been reluctant to recognize him as a teacher or "master" because he was a Kshatriya who had broken his vows and ceased to keep his body under. They questioned how his mind could have won victory while he yielded to his body. In response, the Buddha explained the principles of his new gospel through the Noble Truths and the Noble Eightfold Path, claiming he had found the "ambrosia of truth."
