# Istria

Istria is a region located on the Adriatic coast, historically associated with the crown-lands of the Austrian Empire and the broader geopolitical entity of Illyria. It is bordered to the west by the Adriatic Sea, where it is separated from the Brionian Islands by the narrow Canale di Fasana.

## Early History and Greek Settlement
Istria was founded by the Milesians, likely between 600 and 560 B.C., near the southern embouchure of the Danube. It was established during the same period as Apollonia and Odêssus on the same coast. Despite these early settlements, the region and its neighboring towns, such as Tomi—the site of Ovid's banishment—acquired little importance. This lack of growth is attributed to the uninviting nature of the location and the constant threat from the neighboring murderous Getæ.

## Religious Architecture and Early Church
The region contains significant early Christian architecture, most notably the basilica of Parenzo, dating to approximately A.D. 542. This structure is recognized for having sustained very little alteration over time. The basilica measures 121 feet by 32 feet and features a cloistered atrium and a baptistery that is square on the outside and octagonal within. Its interior includes a nave divided from the aisles by nine pillars, some of which were borrowed from earlier buildings, and capitals of Byzantine style. The apse is inlaid with mother-of-pearl and rich marble, and it retains the bench for the presbyters and the bishop's throne. Other features of the site include a belfry, a chapel of St Andrew, and a choir occupying the three easternmost bays.

In the ecclesiastical history of the region, Pope Gregory made strenuous efforts to eliminate the heresy of the Three Chapters in northern Italy and Istria.

## Political Administration and the Austrian Empire
Istria was part of the Austrian crown-lands and was grouped with Trieste and Görz and Gradisca under the common name *Küstenland* (coast-land or littoral). These three crown-lands were united for specific administrative purposes under the governor of Trieste, whose financial and legal authorities held jurisdiction over the entire littoral. As of 1900, the combined population of these three regions was 755,183, with a total area of 3,084 square miles.

In the 19th century, Istria was subject to various political reorganizations:
* **1809:** The name "Illyrian Provinces" was revived, including Istria, Carniola, Dalmatia, Fiume, Trieste, Görz and Gradisca, and parts of Croatia and Carinthia. These territories were ceded by Austria to Italy at the peace of Schönbrunn on October 14, 1809, and were occupied by French troops under Napoleon.
* **1813–1816:** French occupation ceased to be effective by the end of 1813, and the provinces reverted to Austria. In 1816, the kingdom of Illyria was constituted, comprising Istria, Carniola, Carinthia, Trieste, and Görz and Gradisca. This remained a kingdom of the Austrian crown until 1849.

## Nationalism and the Irredentist Movement
During the period of Austrian rule, the Italians of Istria and Trieste were the only people in the empire who truly desired separation from Austria. A group known as the *Italianissimi* sought annexation to Italy. This sentiment was mirrored by a larger party in Italy called the "Irredentists," who demanded that Italian-speaking districts under Austrian rule, including Istria, Trieste, and South Tirol, be joined to Italy. This movement led to public riots and diplomatic tension, including the tearing down of the Austrian flag from the consulate in Venice and insults directed at the embassy in Rome.

In the allotment of constituencies, the Austrian government used registers based on race to ensure that different ethnic groups voted for candidates of their own race. This system prevented, for example, Slovenes from achieving victory over Italians.

## Geography and Notable Figures
Off the west coast of Istria lie the Brionian Islands, a group of twelve islands belonging to Austria. These islands were historically noted for their quarries, which provided material for the bridges and palaces of Venice and the Adriatic coast, as well as for Berlin and Vienna. Because they command the entrance to the naval harbor of Pola, the largest island (Brioni) hosts a strong fortress called "Fort Tegetthoff." The islands are inhabited by approximately 100 Italian quarrymen.

The region is also associated with Benedetto Marcello (1686–1739), an Italian musical composer and noble. In 1730, Marcello went to Pola to serve as Provveditore. He remained there for eight years, though the climate of Istria eventually impaired his health, leading to his retirement to Brescia.
