# The Maccabean Dynasty and the 2nd Century B.C.

## Political Expansion and Territorial Dominion
During the early decades of the 2nd century B.C., the Maccabean dynasty experienced a period of prosperity. During these "palmy days," the Maccabees extended their dominion in several directions: northwards over Galilee and Samaria, and eastwards beyond the Jordan. 

## National Identity and the Twelve Tribes
The territorial expansion of the Maccabees led to a shift in the prevailing beliefs regarding the composition of the nation. It was supposed that during this time, the Twelve tribes were present in Palestine. This belief—that the kingdom once again embraced the entire nation—displaced an older view which held that nine and a half tribes remained in captivity. Following the downfall of the Maccabean dynasty, the older perspective regarding the tribes in captivity revived during the 1st century A.D.

## Religious Influence and Proselytizing
The period of the Maccabeans and Herods in Judea was characterized by significant religious activity. There was a great proselytizing of Arabs during this era. This religious movement contributed to a landscape where the Yemen was ruled in succession by Zoroastrians, Christians, and Jews (specifically Arab proselytes to Judaism).

## Chronological Debates in Literature
The dating of certain historical and religious texts is often linked to the Maccabean period. For example, some scholars, including Fritzsche, Schrade, Keil, and Toy, assign the dates of certain book constituents (specifically i.-iii. 8) to the time of the Maccabees. Fritzsche specifically maintains that these dates align with the early decades of the 2nd century B.C.
