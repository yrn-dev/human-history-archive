# Balbus

The name Balbus, which literally means "stammerer," was borne by several Roman families. Among these were the Acilii Balbi, including Manius Acilius Balbus, who served as consul in 150 B.C. and 114 B.C. Other individuals of this name include Q. Antonius Balbus, who was praetor in Sicily in 82 B.C.; T. Ampius Balbus, a supporter of Pompey who was later pardoned by Julius Caesar; and Marcus Atius Balbus, the husband of Julia (sister of Caesar) and father of Atia, the mother of Augustus.

The most prominent figures with this name were the two Cornelii Balbi, who were natives of Gades (Cadiz).

## Lucius Cornelius Balbus Major

Lucius Cornelius Balbus Major was born early in the last century B.C. and is generally considered to be of Phoenician origin. He was a noble Spaniard and the adopted son of the Greek historian Theophanes. For his services in Spain against Sertorius, Pompey conferred Roman citizenship upon him and his family. This grant was confirmed by a lex passed in 72 B.C. during the consulship of Cn. Cornelius Lentulus; it is believed that Balbus assumed the name Cornelius to show gratitude to the consul.

Balbus became a chief financier in Rome and was involved in the formation of the First Triumvirate. He maintained a close relationship with Julius Caesar, serving as his chief engineer (*praefectus fabrum*) when Caesar was propraetor to Spain in 61 B.C. and in Gaul in 58 B.C. Due to his wealth, influence, and status as a naturalized foreigner, Balbus faced opposition. In 56 B.C., a native of Gades prosecuted him for illegally assuming the rights of a Roman citizen—a charge that also targeted the triumvirs. Balbus was acquitted after Cicero, Pompey, and Crassus spoke on his behalf.

During the civil war, Balbus rendered important secret services to Caesar and served as his private secretary. He attempted to have Cicero mediate between Caesar and Pompey to prevent Cicero from siding with the latter. Following Caesar's murder, Balbus attached himself to Caesar Octavianus. He served as praetor in 43 or 42 B.C. and became consul in 40 B.C., marking the first time an alien was granted this honor. He was also admitted into the pontificate.

Balbus kept a journal documenting the events of his own life and that of Caesar. Additionally, he urged his friend Hirtius (Pansa) to write the Eighth Book of the Gallic War, which was subsequently dedicated to Balbus.

## Lucius Cornelius Balbus Minor

Lucius Cornelius Balbus Minor was the nephew of Balbus Major and received Roman citizenship at the same time as his uncle. He served under Caesar during the civil war, participating in the Spanish and Alexandrian wars and undertaking several important missions. For these services, he was admitted into the college of pontiffs.

In 43 B.C., he served as quaestor in Further Spain, where he amassed a large fortune by plundering the local inhabitants. In the same year, he visited Bogud, the king of Mauretania. He does not reappear in the record until 21 B.C., when he served as proconsul of Africa. It has been suggested by Mommsen that his long absence from high office was due to Augustus's displeasure regarding his conduct as praetor, and that his eventual appointment in Africa was based on his exceptional fitness for the role.

In 19 B.C., Balbus defeated the Garamantes. On March 27 of that year, he was granted a triumph. This was a historic occurrence, as it was the first time a triumph was granted to someone who was not a Roman citizen by birth, and the last time such an honor was given to a private individual.

## Literary and Civic Contributions

Balbus Minor contributed to the capital's infrastructure by building a theatre, which was dedicated in 13 B.C. upon the return of Augustus from Gaul. 

He also showed an interest in literature. He authored a play concerning his visit to Lentulus at the camp of Pompey at Dyrrhachium. Furthermore, according to Macrobius, he wrote a work titled *Exegetika*, which focused on the worship and nature of the gods.
