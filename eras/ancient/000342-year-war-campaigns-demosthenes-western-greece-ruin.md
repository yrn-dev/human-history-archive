# Campaigns of Demosthenes and the Macedonian Conflict

## Political Rise and the Opposition to Philip
During the eight years following the peace of Philocrates and leading up to the battle of Chaeronea, the authority of the orator Demosthenes grew until it became paramount. His influence was bolstered by the "logic of facts," as he argued that Philip of Macedon was an enemy of Greece whose actions exclusively benefited himself while harming others. In 344 B.C., Demosthenes visited the Peloponnesus to counteract Macedonian intrigue, advising the cities that mistrust is the necessary safeguard for free communities against tyrants.

Demosthenes' position was further strengthened by the prosecution of Aeschines for malversation on an embassy. In 341 B.C., Demosthenes delivered the Third Philippic and a speech "On the Affairs of the Chersonese." In the latter, he questioned the nature of the peace with Philip, arguing that if Philip could seize Athenian possessions with impunity while Athenians could not touch Philip's holdings, the necessity for war had arrived.

## Military Initiatives and the Euboean Conflict
As war eventually broke out, Demosthenes urged the Athenians to recover Euboea, which local rulers had handed over to Philip. Under a decree bearing Demosthenes' name, Athenian forces crossed into the island and expelled the Macedonians. Demosthenes also persuaded Athens to provide reinforcements to Byzantium and Perinthus, urging his countrymen to forget previous wrongs from the Social War to save those cities from Philip's siege.

Earlier, in 350 B.C., an Athenian force under Phocion had been sent to Euboea to support the tyrant Plutarchus of Eretria against the faction of Cleitarchus. Demosthenes had protested this action, arguing that strength needed for greater objectives should not be wasted on the local quarrels of a despot. This "inglorious and costly war" resulted in the destruction of Athenian influence throughout Euboea and cost over £12,000 in ransoms for captives.

## The Olynthian League and the Macedonian Advance
In 352 and 350 B.C., embassies from Olynthus sought Athenian help. When Philip opened war against the Chalcidic towns of the Olynthian League in 349 B.C., Demosthenes delivered the First and Second Olynthiacs to support the dispatch of a force to defend Olynthus and attack Philip. In 348 B.C., he delivered the Third Olynthiac, which focused on practical details, such as the use of the festival-fund for the war and the requirement for citizens to serve in person. Despite these efforts, Olynthus and thirty-two towns of the confederacy were destroyed. Demosthenes later remarked that seven years after the event, men could walk over the sites without knowing cities had once existed.

## Diplomatic Efforts and the Peace of Philocrates
In February 346 B.C., Demosthenes was one of eleven envoys, including Aeschines and Philocrates, sent to Philip. A peace was concluded in April 346 B.C., stipulating that both parties keep what they held at the time of ratification. A critical dispute arose regarding the people of Halus in Thessaly and the Phocians. Demosthenes insisted they be included in the peace to prevent Philip from intervening in those regions; however, Philip insisted they be excluded. They were not included, allowing Philip to intervene in the Phocian War, crush Phocis, and take its place in the Amphictyonic Council.

## The Hellenic Confederacy and Later Years
Demosthenes traveled through Greece to incite resistance, successfully forming a confederacy of nearly all Greek cities against Philip. He organized an army consisting of two thousand cavalry and fifteen thousand infantry, in addition to local forces. He faced significant difficulty in winning over the Thebans, who were the most warlike state in Greece and had recently received assistance from Philip in their war against the Phokians.

Following the death of Alexander in 323 B.C., Demosthenes again called Greece to arms. During the Lamian War, he joined an Athenian embassy to induce Greeks to drive the Macedonians out of Greece. In Arcadia, he engaged in a public debate with Pytheas, who advocated for Macedonia. When Pytheas suggested that a city receiving an Athenian embassy must be in a bad way, Demosthenes replied that while asses' milk is brought into houses to cure the sick, Athenians come to cities to save them from ruin.

## Ruin and Death
The Lamian War ended in August 322 B.C. at the battle of Crannon. To avoid the siege of Athens, Antipater demanded the surrender of leading patriots. Consequently, the Assembly passed a decree condemning Demosthenes, Hypereides, and others to death as traitors. This occurred on the 20th of Boedromion (September 16), 322 B.C., the same day the Initiated returned from Eleusis to Athens for the Great Mysteries and the anniversary of Alexander's annihilation of Thebes thirteen years prior. Demosthenes subsequently fled to Calauria, where he died.
