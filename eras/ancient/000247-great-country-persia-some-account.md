# THE GREAT COUNTRY OF PERSIA

Persia is described as a very great country that was illustrious and powerful in ancient times, though it later suffered wasting and destruction at the hands of the Tartars. Historically, it was part of the Macedonian Empire founded by Alexander the Great, which extended from Greece and European Turkey through Asia Minor, Egypt, Syria, and Persia, reaching as far east as northern India.

## Geography and the Eight Kingdoms
Persia contains eight kingdoms. Starting from the beginning of Persia, the first is Casvin. Moving in a southerly direction, the second is Curdistan, the third is Lor, the fourth is Suolstan, the fifth is Istanit, the sixth is Serazy, and the seventh is Soncara. The eighth kingdom, Tunocain, is located at the further extremity of Persia; unlike the others, it lies toward the east and borders the country of Arbre Sol.

The city of Balc serves as the limit of Persia in the direction between the north-east and east. Once a noble and great city featuring marble buildings and fine palaces, it was greatly ravaged by the Tartars and other nations. Local accounts state that Alexander took the daughter of Darius as his wife in this city. Beyond Balc lies a region called Dogana; the journey from Balc toward the north-east and east takes approximately 12 days through an area where human habitation is scarce due to the presence of armies and banditti. This region is characterized by an abundance of game, lions, and water, though travelers must carry their own provisions.

## Major Cities and Regions
Tabriz is the chief city of Azerbaijan and served as the capital for the Mongol Ilkhan of Persia. Following the destruction of Baghdad by Hulaku, Tabriz became a primary political and commercial center of Asia, diverting the route of Indian products toward the Euxine. Other significant locations include:

*   **Yasdi (Yezd):** A great city located on the route to Kerman.
*   **Kerman:** A kingdom and province known for the manufacture of various goods, the presence of falcons, and the production of turquoises and "Ondanique" (Indian steel).
*   **Camadi:** A city of ruins associated with the Nigudarian bands and Karauna robbers. The surrounding warmer plains produce humped oxen and fat-tailed sheep.
*   **Hormos:** A city reached by descent from the interior.
*   **Cremesor (Garmsir):** A term referring to a hot region, specifically the coast-country on the north-east side of the Persian Gulf, including the ports in that quarter and Hormuz.

## Economy and Trade
Persia is noted for a great supply of fine horses, which are highly valued and exported to India. Depending on quality, a single horse can be worth 200 livres Tournois. The country also produces the finest asses in the world, described as large, fast, and possessing a capital amble, with some valued at 30 marks of silver. Dealers transport these animals to the cities of Curmosa and Kisi on the shores of the Sea of India to meet with merchants for further sale.

## Historical Figures and Legends
The history of Persia includes several prominent rulers and figures:
*   **Cyrus:** Accounts of his death vary; some state he perished near the Caspian Sea while making war with the Massagets and Scythians, while others claim he died in peace in Persia and was buried at Pasargadae, near Persepolis.
*   **Cambyses:** The successor to Cyrus who conquered Egypt.
*   **Sapor:** A king of Persia who, during a thirty-year war, assassinated a Roman leader and advanced into Armenia. Under Sapor's rule, Armenia became a reluctant province of Persia for over twenty-seven years. Sapor also forced the surrender of the garrisons at Nisibis and Carrhæ.
*   **The Three Kings:** Associated with various legends and names. While the Roman and Armenian churches use the names Gaspar, Melchior, and Balthasar, other traditions offer different names. Syriac sources mention Aruphon, Hurmon, and Tachshesh, while Persian sources call them Amad, Zad-Amad, and Drust-Amad (meaning "Venit, Cito Venit, Sincerus Venit").
