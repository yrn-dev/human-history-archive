# BUCEPHALUS

Bucephalus (Greek: *boukephalos*) was the favorite Thracian horse of Alexander the Great. He is noted in historical records for his association with Alexander's campaigns and the subsequent commemoration of his death.

## Death and Commemoration
Bucephalus died in 326 B.C. Historical accounts suggest two possible causes for his death: either he succumbed to old age or he died from wounds sustained during the battle on the Hydaspes. To honor the horse, Alexander founded a city named Bucephala (or *Boukephala*). The location of this city is almost certainly identified as a mound situated on the bank of the river opposite the modern Jhelum.

## The Breed of Bucephalus
According to accounts provided by Marco Polo, a breed of horses descending from the strain of Bucephalus existed in a rich, cold mountainous country known for producing horses of remarkable speed. These horses were distinguished from birth by a particular mark on the forehead. Unlike other horses, they were not shod, despite being used on bad roads and in mountainous terrain, and were capable of maintaining a great pace even during steep descents.

This specific breed was held entirely by an uncle of the local king. When the uncle refused to allow the king to have any of the horses, the king put him to death. Following this event, the uncle's widow destroyed the entire breed out of spite, leading to its extinction.
