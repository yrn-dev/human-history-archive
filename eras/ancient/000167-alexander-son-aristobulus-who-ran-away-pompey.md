# Alexander, The Son Of Aristobulus

Alexander was a son of Aristobulus and a figure of conflict during the period when the Romans became the dominant power in Judea. His life was marked by attempts to reclaim authority and military struggles against Roman generals and rival claimants to power.

## Early Conflict and Flight from Pompey
The instability in Jerusalem was precipitated by a sedition between Hyrcanus and Aristobulus. This conflict led to the loss of liberty for the region, as the people became subject to the Romans and were forced to restore territory previously gained from the Syrians. During this period, the Romans exacted over ten thousand talents.

When Pompey arrived, he took Aristobulus and his children bound to Rome. While Aristobulus's younger son, Antigonus, and his daughters were carried to Rome, Alexander ran away from Pompey.

## Military Campaign Against Hyrcanus
Following his flight, Alexander gathered a considerable band of men and launched an expedition against Hyrcanus. He overran Judea and advanced to Jerusalem, where he attempted to rebuild the city wall that had been thrown down by Pompey. 

To prepare for the inevitable Roman response, Alexander assembled a large army consisting of fifteen hundred horsemen and ten thousand armed footmen. He also established fortifications in strategic locations, specifically building walls at Hyrcanium, Alexandrium, and Machaerus, the latter of which was situated on the mountains of Arabia.

## Defeat by Gabinius
The Roman general Gabinius, who succeeded Scaurus in Syria, led an expedition to stop Alexander. Gabinius sent Marcus Antonius ahead of the main army. This force was joined by a select body of soldiers under Antipater, as well as other Jews commanded by Pitholaus and Malichus.

Unable to withstand the combined charge of the Roman forces, Alexander retired. However, upon reaching Jerusalem, he was forced into a battle in which he lost six thousand men; three thousand were killed and three thousand were taken alive. Following this defeat, Alexander fled to the fortress of Alexandrium.

## Death at Antioch
The political landscape shifted when Caesar took power in Rome after the flight of the senate and Pompey beyond the Ionian Sea. Caesar released Aristobulus from his bonds and provided him with two legions to conquer Syria and areas adjoining Judea. However, Aristobulus was killed by poison administered by those of Pompey's party.

Alexander met a different end. By the command of Pompey, and following an accusation brought before a tribunal regarding the mischiefs he had committed against the Romans, Alexander was beheaded by Scipio at Antioch.

## Family and Aftermath
Following the deaths of Aristobulus and Alexander, the remaining children of Aristobulus were cared for by Ptolemy, the son of Menneus and ruler of Chalcis. Ptolemy sent his son, Philippion, to Ascalon to bring Antigonus and his sisters to him. Philippion married one of the daughters, Alexandra, though he was later slain by his father, Ptolemy, who then married Alexandra himself. Ptolemy continued to provide care for Alexandra's brother and sister.
