# The Stature of Og and the Region of Bashan

## The Stature of Og
The great stature of Og is addressed in a passage of the book of Deuteronomy. According to the archive's records, this physical characteristic is explained by the fact that Og was part of the remnant of the aboriginal Rephaim. The Rephaim were a race specifically distinguished by their lofty stature.

## The Rephaim and Bashan
The Rephaim were established in Ashteroth-Karnaim, a location likely identical to Ashtaroth. This city was an important center within the region of Bashan. Following the conquest and partition of the land, the territory of Bashan was allotted to the eastern division of the tribe of Manasseh. Within this region was located Golan, which served as one of the cities of refuge.

## Administration and History of Bashan
Under the rule of Solomon, the region of Argob in Bashan was placed under the administrative control of one of his lieutenants, Ben-Geber. This area was noted for containing sixty great cities equipped with walls and brazen bars. The last historical event concerning Bashan recorded in the Old Testament occurred during the days of Jehu, when the country was taken from Israel by Hazael, the king of Syria.

## Natural Characteristics of the Region
Bashan is described in poetical and prophetic texts in relation to its natural resources and wildlife:
*   **Wildlife:** The "Blessing of Moses" suggests the area was inhabited by lions.
*   **Livestock:** The region was noted for its cattle, which were proverbial for their ferocity. In the book of Amos, the wealthy women of Samaria who oppressed the poor are referred to as "kine of Bashan."
*   **Flora and Geography:** The region is noted for its mountain and specifically for its oaks. These oaks were coupled with the cedars of Lebanon and were used in the construction of oars.

## The Book of Deuteronomy
The discourses of Deuteronomy purport to be addresses delivered by Moses to the assembled people in the land of Moab, opposite Jericho, shortly before his death. These texts emphasize the sole godhead and spirituality of Yahweh, who is presented as the only god of the Israelites. 

The text teaches that Yahweh chose Israel and demonstrated love and faithfulness by redeeming the people from slavery in Egypt and planting them in a free and fertile land. From these acts, the text deduces several practical duties for the Israelites:
*   Loyal and loving devotion to Yahweh.
*   The uncompromising repudiation of all false gods and the rejection of heathen practices.
*   Cheerful and ready obedience to His will.
*   A warm-hearted and generous attitude toward other humans.

The book presents the love of God as the primary spring of human duty and envisions a community where every member is filled with reverence for God and sympathy for their fellow men.
