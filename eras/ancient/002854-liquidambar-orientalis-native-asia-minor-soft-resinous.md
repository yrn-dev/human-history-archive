# Liquidambar orientalis

*Liquidambar orientalis* is a tree native to Asia Minor. It is the biological source of a balsam known as liquid storax, also referred to as *styrax preparatus*.

## Characteristics and Properties
The substance produced by *Liquidambar orientalis* is described as a soft resinous substance. It possesses a pleasing balsamic odour, a quality that becomes especially prominent after the substance has been kept for some time.

## Classification and Composition
In a technical sense, the term "balsam" is properly limited to oleo-resins or resins that contain cinnamic acid, benzoic acid, or both. Liquid storax is categorized as one of the balsams found in commerce, alongside the balsam of Tolu, the balsam of Peru, and liquidambar. These substances form a distinct class allied by their properties, composition, and uses.

## Applications
Liquid storax is utilized in both internal and external medical applications:
* **External use:** It is applied to certain parasitic skin diseases.
* **Internal use:** It is used as an expectorant.

## Related Species and Substances
There are other species of *Liquidambar* that produce similar resinous substances:
* ***Liquidambar Altingia***: Native to the Indian Archipelago (Java), this species yields an analogous substance. This resinous exudation is known as liquid storax or *rasamala* (which became corrupted in some contexts as "rose-malloes") and is commonly used as incense in the East.
* ***Liquidambar styraciflua***: Found in Mexico and the United States, this tree produces *Liquidambar balsam*. Unlike liquid storax, this specific balsam contains cinnamic acid but does not contain benzoic acid. Additionally, an American species of *Liquidambar* produces a liquid exudation used as incense in America and is noted for being similar to rose-malloes.
