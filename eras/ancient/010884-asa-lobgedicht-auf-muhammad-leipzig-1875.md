# Al Asa's Lobgedicht auf Muhammad

*Al Asa's Lobgedicht auf Muhammad* is a published edition of the eulogy written for Mahomet by the Arabian poet A'sha. The work was edited by H. Thorbecke and published in Leipzig in 1875.

## The Author: A'sha
A'sha, also known as Maimun ibn Qais, was an Arabian poet born in Manfuha, a village located in al-Yemama in the center of Arabia. His life spanned the period before and after the emergence of Mahomet, and he lived long enough to accept the prophet's mission. 

A'sha was a wandering singer who traveled extensively throughout Arabia, journeying from al-Hira in the north to Hadramut in the south. During his travels, he frequently visited the annual fair at Okaz (Ukaz).

## Poetic Style and Themes
The poetry of A'sha is noted for its technical skill and variety. His work is praised for the use of various meters and his proficiency in both satire and praise. Specific subjects of his poetry include:
*   **Nature and Leisure:** He is recognized for his descriptions of the wild ass and his praise of wine.
*   **Romance:** His love poems are dedicated to a black female slave named Huraira.
*   **Religious Eulogy:** His most famous poem is the eulogy written in praise of Mahomet.

## Beliefs and Influences
Even prior to the time of Mahomet, A'sha is said to have been a monotheist who believed in the last judgment and the resurrection. These specific beliefs may have resulted from his interactions with the `Ibadites (Christians) of al-Hira and the bishop of Nejran (Najran).
