# Adoption and the Concept of Adoption

In various historical and legal contexts, adoption has served as a mechanism for ensuring elder care, transferring professional skills, securing political succession, and establishing spiritual relationships.

## Legal and Social Adoption in Roman Law
Adoption was a common practice, particularly for parents who were childless or whose children had grown up and married. The primary purpose was often to secure care for the parents during their old age. These arrangements were formalized through contracts that specified the maintenance expected by the parent and the assets the parent would leave to the child.

### Requirements and Restrictions
The consent of the biological parents was generally required, typically through a deed that transferred the child and severed the child's claims to the biological parents. However, certain groups—including slaves, palace officials, hierodules, and vestals—possessed no rights over their children and could not block an adoption. Foundlings and illegitimate children also lacked parents who could object.

If an adopted child discovered their biological parents and attempted to return to them, the penalty was severe: their tongue or eye was torn out.

### Rights and Inheritance
An adopted child was considered a full heir and could be assigned the position of the eldest son via contract, though they were usually the residuary legatee. In the distribution of a father's estate, legitimate children shared equally, with exceptions for dowers for daughters, bride-prices for unmarried sons, or specific deeds for favorite children. While the eldest son had no specific birthright, he typically served as the executor to equalize shares.

In cases where a man adopted a son but later married and had biological children, he could dissolve the adoption contract. In such an event, the adopted child was entitled to one-third of a child's share in goods, but was barred from receiving real estate, which remained within the biological family.

### Specialized Forms of Adoption
Adoption was also utilized for professional and religious purposes:
*   **Craftsmanship:** Craftsmen often adopted sons to learn a trade, profiting from the son's labor. This functioned as a form of apprenticeship; if the father failed to teach the craft, the son could prosecute him to annul the contract.
*   **Vestals:** Vestal virgins frequently adopted other vestals to ensure they were cared for in their old age.
*   **Slavery:** Slaves were sometimes adopted, though they could be reduced back to slavery if they proved "unfilial." If an adopted child failed their filial duties, the contract could be annulled in law courts.

## Political Adoption in the Roman Empire
Adoption was used as a tool for imperial succession. For example, Marcus Aurelius Antoninus was adopted by his grandfather after his father, Annius Verus, died when Marcus was three months old. Later, the Emperor Hadrian adopted Titus Antoninus Pius as his successor on the condition that Antoninus Pius, in turn, adopt both Marcus (at age seventeen) and Lucius Ceionius Commodus.

## Spiritual and Symbolic Adoption
The concept of adoption also transitioned into religious contexts. The idea of being adopted as a son and heir of God in the ceremony of baptism is believed to have been taken by Paul from Roman law. This spiritual adoption mirrored the legal ceremony of *emancipatio*, which extinguished the *patria potestas* (the father's power of life and death over a son). Just as legal manumission dissolved previous agnatic relationships, baptism was seen as freeing the believer from Satan and incorporating them into a society of brethren based on spiritual rather than physical bonds.

## Other Uses of the Term
Beyond familial and legal structures, the term "adopted" has been used to describe the acceptance of ideas, names, or customs:
*   **Ideology and Names:** Jacques Nicolas Billaud-Varenne adopted the principles of the French Revolution with enthusiasm. Similarly, the "Company of Jesus" adopted its name to reflect the military ideal of the society's duties.
*   **Customs:** The Scythians were noted for being averse to adopting strange customs, particularly those of the Hellenes. This is illustrated by the case of Anacharsis, who was killed by King Saulios after adopting foreign customs and performing ceremonies in honor of the Mother of the gods.
*   **Political Policy:** In the 19th century, Lord Dufferin opposed the policy of Home Rule when it was adopted by Gladstone.
*   **Historiography:** Scholars such as Rubruquis and Timkowski have been noted for adopting the story that Chinghiz Kaan was a blacksmith.
