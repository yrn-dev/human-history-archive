# The Malice of Antipater and Doris

The domestic history of King Herod was marked by severe instability and internal conflict, primarily driven by the rivalry between his children and the influence of his wife, Doris. While Herod achieved significant external successes, his family suffered from "wild disorders" resulting from the tension between the sons of his different wives.

## Family Structure and Rivalries
Herod had a large family, consisting of nine wives and children by seven of them. Among these were Doris, a woman from a lower family of his own nation whom he had married when he was a private person; Mariamne, the daughter of Alexander (son of Aristobulus); Malthace the Samaritan; Cleopatra of Jerusalem; and Pallas. 

From Doris, Herod had his eldest son, Antipater. From Mariamne, he had two daughters and two sons, Alexander and Aristobulus. Other children included Herod Philip and Antipas (by Malthace), as well as Herod and Philip (by Cleopatra), and Phasaelus (by Pallas). He also had daughters named Roxana and Salome.

Conflict arose when Herod preferred Antipater over his other sons. Alexander and Aristobulus were deeply grieved that the privilege of the first-born was confirmed to Antipater, while Antipater was angry that his brothers were intended to succeed him.

## The Influence of Doris and Antipater
Antipater is described as having a "mutable and politic" disposition, using cunning to conceal his hatred for his brothers. He established a system of surveillance, employing friends to spy on Alexander and reporting his words to Herod with additions and calumnies. Antipater frequently provoked Alexander to speak so that forged imputations could be given a foundation of truth. He further corrupted Alexander's acquaintances through bribes and flattery to betray their master.

This malice was supported by Antipater's mother, Doris. She is described as being "more harsh than a step-mother" and hating the sons of the queen more than is typical for a relationship with sons-in-law. Together, Antipater and Doris became "all in all" to the king. Herod, influenced by them, commanded his intimate friends and children to alienate themselves from Alexander and his friends.

## The Unease of Alexander
Alexander lived in a state of great uneasiness, as he and Aristobulus were unaware of the specific calumnies being raised against them. They only perceived the shift in their father's affection through his coldness and the distress he showed. 

This tension was further augmented by Alexander's wife, Glaphyra. Claiming nobility from Temenus on her father's side and Darius, son of Hystaspes, on her mother's side, she asserted she was a lady superior to all others in the kingdom, which increased the hatred against the brothers.

Antipater also worked to isolate the brothers from other family members, irritating their aunt Salome and making their uncle Pheroras an enemy.

## Plots and Political Maneuvers
Antipater sought to remove any potential obstacles to his own hopes. He forged letters in the name of friends in Rome against his brothers Archelaus and Philip (the king's sons educated in Rome), claiming they reproached their father and were uneasy about being recalled. 

In a direct appeal to Herod, Antipater argued that the king should not have the "bare name of a king" while power resided elsewhere. He specifically urged Herod to change intended marriages and requested that Alexander's son not have Pheroras and Archelaus as curators, arguing that the royal family was too numerous to sustain such arrangements.

## The Downfall of Antipater
The malice of Antipater eventually led to his own punishment, triggered by the death of Pheroras. It was discovered that Pheroras had been killed by a poisonous draught. Investigations revealed that Antipater's mother and sister had brought a woman from Arabia skilled in mixing drugs under the pretense of preparing a love potion for Pheroras; instead, she provided deadly poison.

Further examinations of Bathyllus, a freedman of Antipater, uncovered additional designs. Bathyllus had brought a second "deadly potion" consisting of the poison of asps and other serpents, intended for Pheroras and his wife to use to destroy the king should the first potion fail.

## Herod's Reactions and Purges
As Antipater influenced Herod's "mad and licentious" way of acting, he encouraged the king to remove all opposition. Herod began examining those faithful to Alexander under torture to discover suspected attempts against him. One tortured individual claimed that Alexander, envious of his father's ambition, had conspired with Aristobulus to lie in wait during a hunt and kill Herod before fleeing to Rome to claim the kingdom.

Following these discoveries, Herod cast Doris and her accomplices, as well as Mariamne, out of the palace. He also blotted his son Herod out of his testament.
