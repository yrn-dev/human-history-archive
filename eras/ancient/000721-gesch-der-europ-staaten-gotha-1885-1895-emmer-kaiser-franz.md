# History of the Austrian Empire and Related European States

The historical record of the Austrian Empire and various European states is documented through a vast array of general histories, specialized monographs, and official records. These sources track the development of the House of Austria, the evolution of the Austrian state, and the political shifts within the broader European landscape.

## General Histories of Austria
The English language possesses one primary general history of Austria: Archdeacon William Coxe's *History of the House of Austria, 1218-1792*, published in three volumes in 1817. This work was later continued by W. Kelly in 1853, with a new edition appearing in 1873. While this remains the only general history in English, it has been superseded by more recent research.

Other significant general works include J. Majlath's *Geschichte des oesterreichischen Kaiserstaates* (1834-1850) and Franz X. Krones (Ritter von Marchland), who authored the five-volume *Handbuch der Gesch. Oesterreichs* (1876-1879) and *Gesch. der Neuzeit Oesterreichs vom 18ten Jahrhundert bis auf die Gegenwart* (1879), the latter written from a German-liberal perspective.

## The 1848 Revolutions and State Ideology
The mid-19th century was a period of significant upheaval. Count F. von Hartig's *Genesis der Revolution in Oesterreich im Jahre 1848* (1851) is noted as a work of much value that created a great sensation upon its release. W. H. Stiles provided an eyewitness account of these events in *Austria in 1848-1849* (1852). Further detailed accounts of the period following the October uprising of 1848 are found in the four volumes of J. A. Freiherr von Helfert's *Gesch. Oesterreichs vom Ausgange des Oktoberaufstandes* (1869-1889).

The conceptual development of the state is explored in H. Bidermann's *Gesch. der oesterreichischen Gesamtstaatsidee, 1526-1804*, with parts 1 and 2 covering the period up to 1740.

## The House of Habsburg and Regional Territories
The original archduchy, which encompasses Upper Austria, serves as the oldest possession of the House of Habsburg in its current dominions and is the nucleus of the Austrian empire. Documentation on this region includes F. Umlauft's *Das Erzherzogtum Oesterreich unter der Enns* and M. Vansca's *Gesch. Nieder- u. Ober-Oesterreichs*.

The reign of Maria Theresia is extensively documented, most notably in A. von Arneth's ten-volume *Geschichte Maria Theresias* (1863-1879) and his work *Maria Teresias ersten Regierungsjahre* (1863). Official Austrian records also include *Kriege der Kaiserin Maria Theresia* and *Gesch. des oesterr. Erbfolgekrieges*.

## Other European States and Figures
Historical records extend to other European entities and leaders:

*   **Baden:** The Grand Duchy of Baden is documented in works such as *Das Grossherzogtum Baden in geographischer ... Hinsicht dargestellt* (1885) and F. von Weech's *Badische Geschichte* (1890). Records note that Frederick assumed the title of grand-duke on September 5, 1856.
*   **Ludwig IV (the Bavarian):** Modern authorities on Kaiser Ludwig include F. von Weech, who wrote on Ludwig and King Johann of Bohemia (1860), and R. Döbner, who detailed the conflict between Ludwig IV and Friedrich the Handsome of Austria (1875). Other works focus on Ludwig's struggle with the Roman Curia and his political relations with France.
*   **Frederick the Great:** Research into Frederick's life and reign includes works by Carlyle, Preuss, and v. Taysen, as well as the Prussian official publication *Die I. und II. schlesischen Kriege* (1890-1895).
