# Jesus, He Who is Illuminated is Washed

## The Concept of Illumination and Baptism
In early Christian practice, the rite of baptism was frequently referred to as "Illumination" (from the Greek *photismos*). This terminology stems from the belief that those who learn the tenets of the faith are illuminated in their understandings. The process of illumination involves a specific sequence of spiritual and physical acts. For those who choose to be born again and have repented of their sins, the name of God the Father and Lord of the universe is pronounced over them. This figure leads the person to the laver to be washed.

The act of washing is performed in the name of Jesus Christ—who was crucified under Pontius Pilate—and in the name of the Holy Ghost, who foretold all things about Jesus through the prophets. According to Justin, this specific baptism is the "water of life" and is the only means to purify those who have repented. He contrasts this with rites of ablution that cleanse only the flesh and body, arguing instead for a baptism that cleanses the soul from hatred, envy, and wrath. Those who approach God through Jesus Christ are said to receive a spiritual circumcision, rather than a carnal one, similar to that of Enoch.

## Rituals and Procedures of the Illuminated
The process of becoming illuminated involved rigorous preparation and a series of symbolic physical actions. Candidates for baptism prepared themselves through vigils, fasting, and prayer. Before entering the font, which was done naked by both sexes, neophytes were required to renounce the angels and pomps of the devil. These baptisms were typically conducted by bishops, or by priests and deacons commissioned by a bishop, during the seasons of Pentecost and Easter.

The physical rites served as symbols for spiritual transformations:
*   **Washing of the flesh:** Performed so that the soul may be freed from stain.
*   **Anointing of the flesh:** Performed so that the soul may be consecrated.
*   **Sealing with the cross:** Performed so that the soul may be protected.
*   **Imposition of hands:** Performed so that the soul may be illuminated by the Spirit.
*   **Feeding on the body and blood of Christ:** Performed so that the soul may be sated with God and filled.

Following these rites, neophytes were given a draught of honey and milk.

## Variations in Early Practice
Historical records indicate various approaches to baptism and the reception of the Spirit. While many repentant individuals were baptized in the name of Christ Jesus to wash away sins before the laying on of hands for the reception of the Spirit, other instances occurred. For example, twelve disciples who had been baptized into the baptism of John were re-baptized into the name of the Lord Jesus at the instance of Paul, after which Paul laid hands on them and the Holy Ghost came upon them, leading them to prophecy and speak with tongues.

Some second-century sects rejected water baptism entirely. Their reasoning was that the external washing of a perishable body could not contribute to the illumination of the inner man, as knowledge of the truth itself provides freedom. Additionally, the rite of laying on of hands was sometimes used as a substantive rite separate from baptism, such as in the ordination of the seven deacons, the healing of the sick (often accompanied by anointing with oil), or the recovery of sight and filling with the Holy Ghost, as seen in the case of Paul.

## Jesus and the Critique of Ritual Washing
In his recorded utterances, Jesus expressed little patience for the "bargaining righteousness" of formal religion and the meticulous observance of pious rules. He specifically challenged the traditions of the Pharisees and scribes regarding ritual cleanliness. When questioned why his disciples ate bread with "unwashen hands"—violating the tradition of the elders which required the washing of hands, cups, pots, tables, and brazen vessels—Jesus labeled the critics hypocrites. 

He cited Isaiah to argue that such people honor God with their lips while their hearts remain far from Him. Jesus asserted that they were teaching the commandments of men as doctrines and rejecting the commandment of God in favor of their own traditions, such as the washing of pots and cups.

## The Epiphany and the Illumination of Jesus
The feast of Epiphany, meaning the apparition of a divine being, is closely linked to the baptism of Christ. In the East, the commemoration of this baptism was called the "Day of Lights" or the "Theophany," signifying the illumination of Jesus or the light that shone in the Jordan. 

Greek texts from around the year 795 recite that Christ hallowed the waters of the Jordan by His presence. Hymns associated with this feast describe the "Increate" being ordained by His own creature and the "Unbending" bending His neck to His servant to free humanity from servitude. These texts claim that through this event, believers were liberated from darkness and illumined by the light of divine knowledge.
