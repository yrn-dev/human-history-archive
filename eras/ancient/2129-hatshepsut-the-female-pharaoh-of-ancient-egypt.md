# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview
Hatshepsut remains one of the most intriguing figures of the 18th Dynasty during the New Kingdom of Egypt. While she began her tenure as regent for her stepson, Thutmose III, she eventually took the unprecedented step of declaring herself Pharaoh. Unlike many of her successors who focused on military conquest, Hatshepsut’s reign is characterized by economic prosperity, architectural innovation, and the restoration of trade networks.

Her ascent to power was a masterclass in political legitimacy. To justify her rule in a society where the pharaoh was traditionally male, she claimed divine birth, asserting that the god Amun-Ra had fathered her. In official iconography, she is often depicted with the traditional false beard and musculature of a king, signaling her role not as a "queen acting as king," but as a legitimate pharaoh in her own right.

## Key Facts
*   **The Punt Expedition:** One of her greatest achievements was a massive trading expedition to the Land of Punt (likely modern-day Somalia or Eritrea). This mission brought back luxury goods, including gold, ebony, ivory, and live myrrh trees, which were planted at her temple.
*   **Architectural Legacy:** She commissioned the Djeser-Djeseru ("Holy of Holies"), her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari. This structure is celebrated by historians for its symmetry and integration into the limestone cliffs.
*   **The "Damnatio Memoriae":** Years after her death, Thutmose III (or his successor) ordered the systematic destruction of her images and the chiseling away of her name from monuments. Historians debate the motive: some suggest it was personal vendetta, while others argue it was a political move to ensure a clean line of succession for the male heirs.

## Did You Know?
1. **Divine Lineage:** Hatshepsut utilized "theology" as a political tool, commissioning reliefs that depicted the god Amun visiting her mother in the guise of her father.
2. **The False Beard:** She did not wear the pharaonic beard for fashion, but as a symbolic garment of office to align herself with the traditional image of the Egyptian king.
3. **Trade over War:** While she did lead some minor military campaigns, she is primarily remembered for shifting Egypt's focus from expansionism to commercial wealth.
