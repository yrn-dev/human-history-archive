# Eighth and Ninth Years of the War—Invasion of Boeotia—Fall of Amphipolis

## Political Developments in Megara
Following a period of negotiation involving the Athenians, Brasidas departed for Corinth to prepare for an expedition to Thrace, which had been his original destination. During this time, the Athenians also returned to their homes. In Megara, the city most heavily implicated in the negotiations with Athens, those involved in the correspondence were detected and disappeared.

The remaining citizens conferred with friends of the exiles and restored the party at Pegae. This restoration was contingent upon solemn oaths that no vengeance would be taken for past events and that the party would consult only the real interests of the town. However, once in office, this party conducted a review of the heavy infantry. They separated the battalions and identified approximately one hundred enemies, specifically those believed to be most involved in the correspondence with the Athenians. These individuals were brought before the people and, through an open vote, were condemned and executed. This resulted in the establishment of a close oligarchy in the town, a revolution that lasted for a very long time despite being executed by a small number of partisans.

## Military Context and Successes
The events of the eighth and ninth years of the war are associated with the invasion of Boeotia, the fall of Amphipolis, and the brilliant successes achieved by Brasidas.
