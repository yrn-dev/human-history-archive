# Shinar

Shinar is a geographical region in Babylonia associated with early human settlement and the rise of the city of Babylon. In historical and scriptural accounts, it is identified as a province of which Babylon served as the metropolis.

## Geographical Identification
In the context of Babylonia, the region is often discussed in relation to the alluvial plains. While some have supposed that Sumer was the original of the Biblical Shinar, evidence suggests that Shinar represented northern rather than southern Babylonia. It is likely that Shinar corresponds to the Sankhar mentioned in the Tell el-Amarna tablets. 

This northern region was opposed to the southern areas known as Kengi ("the land") or Kengi Sumer ("the land of Sumer"). While the south was characterized as "lowlands," the northern regions—originally referred to as Urra (meaning "clayey soil" or "highlands")—eventually became known as Akkad. This shift occurred following the rise of Sargon's empire, with the imperial title "Sumer and Akkad" later denoting the entirety of Babylonia. Following the Kassite conquest, northern Babylonia was further identified as Kar-Duniyas, meaning "the wall of the god Duniyas."

## Early Settlement and the Tower of Babylon
According to scriptural accounts, after the deluge, mankind traveled from the mountain of the East where the ark had rested and settled in Shinar. The sons of Noah—Shem, Japhet, and Ham—were the first to descend from the mountains into the plains, establishing their habitation in the plain of Shinar. They encouraged others, who were fearful of the lower grounds due to the flood, to join them.

While in Shinar, the people disregarded God's command to send out colonies to populate the earth. Believing their prosperity was a result of their own power rather than divine favor, and fearing that division would make them easier to oppress, they instead attempted to build a city and a tower whose top would reach heaven. This tower was constructed of burnt brick and cemented with bitumen to prevent the admission of water.

The project was halted by a divine miracle: God produced a multitude of diverse languages among the people, causing a confusion of tongues that made mutual understanding impossible. Consequently, the people abandoned the site. The location became known as Babylon, derived from the Hebrew word *Babel*, meaning "confusion."

## Political History and Governance
Following the dispersion of the people, Nimrod is credited as the first to encompass the site of the tower with walls, establishing his empire there and settling his friends and confederates within. Other cities mentioned in scripture were also located within the land of Shinar.

In the era of Abraham, Shinar was governed by Amraphael. This king of Shinar, along with two other princes, followed Chedorlaomer, the king of the Elamites, during a military campaign against five kings of the land of Canaan.
