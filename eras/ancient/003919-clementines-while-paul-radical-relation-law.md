# Clementines

The term "Clementines" appears in historical and religious contexts within the archive, referring to specific literary works, legal records, and political oppositions.

## Religious and Theological Context
In the context of early Christian history, the Clementines are a source in which Barnabas is spoken of with respect. Barnabas is described as having filled a mediating role, occupying a position intermediate between Pauline Christianity and Jewish Christianity, a trait generally characteristic of Christian Hellenists. 

In contrast to the respect shown to Barnabas, the Clementines discountenance Paul. This negative view is attributed to Paul's position as a radical in relation to the Law.

## Legal and Political Records
The Clementines also appear as a source of legal and ecclesiastical record. Specifically, the oath of fidelity that was imposed upon the emperor by the pope is recorded and sanctified within the Clementines (l. ii. tit. ix.). 

Additionally, the term is associated with political and national conflict. Historical records indicate that the English nation exhibited zeal against the Clementines, a sentiment displayed in a declamation or epistle written in the name of Edward III. This opposition was not limited to rhetoric; the bishop of Norwich led a crusade consisting of 60,000 individuals across the sea.
