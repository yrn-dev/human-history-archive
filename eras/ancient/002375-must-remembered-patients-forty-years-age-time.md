# Age and Time in Historical and Clinical Contexts

The concept of age and the passage of time appear across various historical, clinical, and religious records, often serving as critical markers for prognosis, political transitions, and personal milestones.

## Clinical Observations on Alopecia Areata
In the study of alopecia areata—a condition characterized by smooth, rounded, and somewhat depressed patches of hair loss—age is a primary factor in determining the prognosis. The disease is marked by the loss of a large proportion of follicles, with short broken hairs typically visible at the margins of the patches. Clinical evidence suggests the disease may be transmitted, and organisms known as *Bacterium decalvens* are invariably present, sometimes forming a continuous sheath around the hair.

For patients under forty years of age, time alone will generally bring about the desired end, although treatment can hasten the recovery process. However, the prognosis becomes less favorable after the age of forty, with every additional year of age reducing the likelihood of a positive outcome. Management of the condition requires careful attention to the sufferer's general hygiene and mode of life, and the treatment of any weakness. A recommended daily lotion consists of *Acidi lactici* (one drachm to one ounce), *Ol. ricini* (two drachms), and *Spt. vini* (up to four ounces).

## Political and Imperial Transitions
Age frequently defines the timeline of leadership and the duration of reigns in imperial histories. Cublay Kaan, a descendant of Chinghis Kaan and the sixth Lord in that succession, ascended the throne in 1256. By the year 1298, he had reigned for two-and-forty years. At that time, his age was approximately eighty-five, meaning he was about forty-three years old when he first became sovereign. Prior to his accession, he had served as an excellent captain and gallant soldier in various wars.

In the history of the Persian throne, Cabades (or Kobad) died on September 13, A.D. 531, at the age of eighty-two, following a reign of forty-three years and two months. His successor, Nushirvan, is recorded in the annals of Eutychius as having reigned for forty-seven years and six months, placing his death in March, A.D. 579.

In early Japanese history, the Chronicles assign Prince Iware an age of forty-five at the time of an expedition's departure from Kyushu in 667 B.C. He was fifty-two when he assumed imperial dignity on the first day of the first month of 660 B.C. at Kashiwa-bara, and is recorded to have lived to the age of 127.

## Personal Milestones and Biographies
The records of influential figures often highlight the intersection of age and achievement. Lord Durham was regarded as a great popular leader and the chief author of the first Reform Act and the *Report* on colonial policy. Despite his abilities, his career was marred by personal defects, including vanity, impatience, and a tendency to be hypersensitive to criticism. His career terminated at the age of forty-eight.

In ancient Greece, the birth of Plato is subject to fluctuating accounts, with dates ranging between 429 and 427 B.C. Plato himself stated that he was about forty years of age during his first visit to Sicily. If his birth is accepted as 428 B.C., he would have been forty in 388 B.C. However, some interpretations suggest he may have been forty-one or forty-two during his interviews with Dionysius and his conversations with Dion at Syracuse, particularly if these events occurred after the capture of Rhegium in 387 B.C.

## Asceticism and Penance
The Syrian Anachoret, Simeon Stylites, began his religious life at the age of thirteen by entering an austere monastery. He eventually established a residence on a column located thirty or forty miles east of Antioch. He remained in this station for thirty summers and thirty winters, resisting extreme heat and cold. His devotion included praying in the figure of a cross or bending his skeleton from forehead to feet; one spectator recorded twelve hundred and forty-four such repetitions. Simeon eventually expired on his column due to the progress of an ulcer in his thigh.

## Teachings on Patience and Remembrance
The sacred texts of the Qur'an present a vision of life centered on submission to Allah, patience, and remembrance. Believers are instructed to seek assistance through prayer and patient perseverance (or steadfastness), as Allah is with those who are patient. Remembrance of Allah is emphasized as a means of success; those who remember Allah much are promised forgiveness and a vast reward. This reward is extended to both men and women who submit, believe, obey, speak the truth, persevere in righteousness, remain humble, give alms, fast, and guard their modesty.

The texts further command believers to obey Allah and His Messenger and to avoid disputes, as quarreling leads to a loss of heart and power. It is taught that when a matter has been decided by Allah and His Messenger, it is not fitting for a believer to seek another option, as disobedience leads to a clearly wrong path.
