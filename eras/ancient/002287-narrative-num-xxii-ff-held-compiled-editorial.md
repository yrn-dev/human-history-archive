# The Narrative of Balaam in Numbers xxii ff.

The biblical account of Balaam, found primarily in Numbers xxii-xxv, describes the interactions between a Gentile seer and the Israelites during their sojourn in the plains of Moab. This narrative occurs east of the Jordan River at the end of the Forty Years' wandering, shortly before the crossing of the Jordan and the death of Moses.

## The Story of Balaam
Balaam, the son of Beor, was a prophet and seer. The narrative begins when Balak, the king of Moab, becomes alarmed after the Israelites conquer two kings of eastern Palestine: Og, king of Bashan, and Sihon, king of the Amorites. In response to this threat, Balak sends for Balaam to curse the Israelites.

Although Balaam hesitates, he eventually comes. However, when he attempts to curse the Israelites, Yahweh compels him to bless them instead. The structure of the passage in Numbers xxii-xxv consists of a narrative framework that houses seven oracular poems. The first four of these poems are of some length, while the final three are very brief.

## Editorial Composition and Sources
The narrative in Numbers xxii ff. is not considered a single continuous story but is held to be compiled with editorial additions from two ancient documents, dated between 900 and 700 B.C. These sources are commonly denoted by the symbols "J" and "E." 

The writer referred to as "E" is characterized as having lived apparently in the northern kingdom and preferred the name *Elohim* ("God") over *Jehovah*. "E" recorded traditions current in northern Israel in a style that, while similar to "J," was less bright and vivid and contained small differences in representation. Most of the history of Balaam in Numbers xxii-xxiv is attributed to this "E" source.

The compilers combined these two older accounts, using them as a framework for the oracular poems. In this process, the compilers altered, added, and omitted material. When the two documents provided nearly identical statements, one was often omitted, meaning neither original account is necessarily presented in full within the composite passage.

## Reconstruction of the Source Material
Because the distribution of material between the two documents is uncertain, scholars have proposed probable schemes for the original accounts. One such reconstruction suggests that the "J" source included the following sequence:

Balak, king of Moab, alarmed by the conquests of the Israelites, sends the elders of Moab and Midian to Balaam, son of Beor. This request is sent to the land of Ammon (a reading accepted by many modern scholars and found in the Vulgate, Syriac, and Samaritan Pentateuch versions, though the current Hebrew text reads "his people") to induce Balaam to curse Israel. Balaam responds that he can only do what Yahweh commands.

## Historical Context and Traditions
The story of Balaam is believed to be based on ancient traditions that existed in various forms, though the references within the Old Testament are not wholly consistent. This narrative is part of the broader Pentateuch, which some analysis suggests was written after the time of Moses. For instance, Deuteronomy xxxiv. 6 notes that no man knew the location of Moses' sepulchre "to this day," implying the words were written after his interment.
