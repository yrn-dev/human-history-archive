# Lehmann-Haupt (1898)

Lehmann-Haupt was a scholar who proposed a chronological system for the early dynasties of Babylon. His first system, published in 1898 in the work *Zwei Hauptprobleme*, focused on establishing the dates for the first three dynasties of Babylon.

## Chronological System of 1898
In his 1898 system, Lehmann-Haupt accepted the figures provided by the Kings' List without attempting to emend them. This approach aligned him with other scholars of the period, including Maspero, Delitzsch, Winckler, Rogers, Sayce, and Oppert. 

Despite accepting the Kings' List figures, Lehmann-Haupt arrived at a "low date" for the conclusion of Dynasty III. He achieved this by emending the figure found in the Bavian inscription of Sennacherib. Specifically, he reduced Sennacherib's figure by one hundred years. This method differed from that of Rost, who had suggested increasing the figure by sixty years.

According to the data associated with Lehmann-Haupt's 1898 system, the dates for the three dynasties were as follows:
*   **Dynasty I:** 2360–2057 B.C.
*   **Dynasty II:** 2056–1689 B.C.
*   **Dynasty III:** 1688–1113 B.C.

## Influence and Later Revisions
The system proposed by Lehmann-Haupt in 1898 influenced the work of Marquart, whose own system was published the following year. Lehmann-Haupt's slightly reduced figure for the start of Dynasty I was developed by incorporating information from the first published date-list.

Lehmann-Haupt later revised his scheme of chronology. A subsequent version was published in 1903 in *Beitraege zur alten Geschichte (Klio)*, Bd. iii. Heft 1. The dates provided in this 1903 revision were:
*   **Dynasty I:** 2296–2009/8 B.C.
*   **Dynasty II:** 2008/7–1691 B.C.
*   **Dynasty III:** 1690–1115 B.C.

## Context of Babylonian Chronology
Lehmann-Haupt belonged to a group of writers—which also included Rost, Peiser, and Marquart—who attempted to resolve discrepancies in chronological data by emending figures found in inscriptions. This was in contrast to other methodologies; for example, Peiser had previously suggested that the length of Dynasty III in the Kings' List (recorded as "9 soss and 36 years," totaling 576 years) was a scribal error for "6 soss and 39 years," a change that would have reduced the dynasty's length by 177 years.
