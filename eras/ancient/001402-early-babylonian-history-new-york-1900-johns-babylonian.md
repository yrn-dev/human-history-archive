# Early Babylonian History

## Overview and Cultural Relations
Early Babylonian history is characterized by a close relationship with Assyria. The Assyrian language is considered practically identical to the Babylonian language. Furthermore, the Assyrians and Babylonians are regarded as the same people, though the Assyrians possess some foreign admixtures. In the study of names from this period, it is noted that the divine element was originally present in many names, even if it was later lopped off.

## Political History and the First Empire
A significant era in early Babylonian history was the flourishing of the first Babylonian Empire, which was founded by Hammurabi. While the exact correspondence of dates between early Babylonia and early Egypt remains doubtful, it is believed that this empire was flourishing during the period when nomadic Semites, known as the Hyksos, founded a "shepherd" dynasty in Egypt. Following the Egyptian war of liberation around 1600 B.C., the subsequent "New Empire" of Egypt eventually pushed expeditions as far as the Euphrates, initiating a long-term struggle between the Babylonian-Assyrian power and the Egyptian state.

## Legal Systems and Governance
The legal framework of early Babylonia is exemplified by the Code of Hammurabi. Various scholarly interpretations and editions of this code date the reign of King Hammurabi to approximately 2250 B.C. The study of Babylonian law and governance is supported by a wide array of documents, including contracts, letters, and inscriptions. These records include "Assyrian Deeds and Documents relating to the Transfer of Property" and other texts concerning Babylonian and Assyrian laws and contracts.

## Religion and Astral Theology
The religion of the Babylonians and Assyrians exerted a profound influence on other Semitic peoples. A central component of this belief system was astral theology, which affected the ancient world generally, including the Romans and the Greeks. 

The civilization of the Euphrates valley provided a significant impetus for the purification of old Semite religions. Traces of direct borrowing from Babylonia are discernible in the traditions, myths, and legends of the Old Testament. Indirect influences are also noted in the Psalms, the prophetical books, and "Wisdom Literature." These Babylonian-Assyrian influences extended even into the New Testament period; for example, Babylonian elements are present in early Christian gnosticism, and the development of apocalyptic literature is attributed by many scholars to the astral theology of Babylonian and Assyrian priests.

## Scholarly Sources and Documentation
The reconstruction of early Babylonian history relies on various archaeological and textual authorities. Key sources include:
*   **Cuneiform Texts:** Tablets from the British Museum and the "Keilinschriftliche Bibliothek."
*   **Expeditions:** The Babylonian Expedition of the University of Pennsylvania and discoveries in Chaldea.
*   **Chronicles:** The "Babylonian Chronicle" and other chronicles concerning early Babylonian kings.
*   **Specific Records:** The "Letters and Inscriptions of Khammurabi" and "Old Babylonian Inscriptions."
