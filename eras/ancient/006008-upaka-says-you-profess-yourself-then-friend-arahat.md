# The Encounter Between the Buddha and Upaka

## The Mission to Benares
At the age of 35, the teacher intended to proclaim his new gospel. His initial plan was to share this message with his former teachers, Udraka and Alara; however, upon discovering that they had passed away, he decided to seek out his former five disciples. These disciples were residing in the Deer-forest near Benares, and the teacher set out for that location.

## The Meeting with Upaka
While traveling toward the city of Benares, the Buddha encountered an acquaintance named Upaka, who was a wandering sophist. Upaka was struck by the Buddha's expression and inquired about the religion that allowed him to appear simultaneously glad and calm. 

The Buddha responded by stating that he was on his way to Benares to "beat the drum of the Ambrosia," which is described as setting up the light of the doctrine of Nirvana within the darkness of the world. During this exchange, the Buddha proclaimed himself to be the Buddha who knows and knows no teacher.

## The Concept of the Conqueror
Upon hearing this proclamation, Upaka asked if the Buddha professed himself to be a conqueror and an Arahat. The Buddha explained the nature of being a conqueror, stating that true conquerors are those who have conquered "the intoxications." These intoxications are defined as the mental intoxication that arises from craving after future life, sensuality, or ignorance. The Buddha asserted his status as a conqueror because evil dispositions had ceased within him.

Following this explanation, Upaka addressed him as "venerable Gotama" and informed him that his way lay in a certain direction. Upaka then shook his head and turned to travel in the opposite direction.
