## Mathematical Applications and Fourier Series
In the study of functions, the variable $c$ is utilized in the determination of coefficients $a_n$ and $b_n$ for series representing a given function $f(x)$ within a "periodic interval" between $-c$ and $c$. According to the rules provided by Fourier, these coefficients are determined as follows:
*   $a_0 = \frac{1}{c} \int_{-c}^{c} f(x) dx$
*   $a_n = \frac{1}{c} \int_{-c}^{c} f(x) \cos \frac{n\pi x}{c} dx$
*   $b_n = \frac{1}{c} \int_{-c}^{c} f(x) \sin \frac{n\pi x}{c} dx$

Fourier's theorem posits that if this periodic interval is divided into a finite number of partial intervals where the function is ordinary, the series represents the function within those intervals.

## Roman Political Figures
Historical records identify C. Claudius Marcellus as a consul for the year B.C. 49, the year in which war broke out. He was the cousin of another consul Marcellus who served in B.C. 50. The latter Marcellus is noted for presenting a sword to Pompeius when commissioning him to fight against Cæsar. While Plutarch appears to confound these two individuals in his writings, Dion Cassius and Appianus distinguish between the Marcellus of B.C. 49 and the Marcellus of B.C. 50.

## Forensic and Public Causes
In the classification of forensic causes in public cases, several categories of legal actions are identified:
*   **Offences against the State** (*graphai demosion adikematon*): These include treason, embezzlement of public moneys, and malversation in office. Examples include the case for Polystratus (407 B.C.) and the Defence on a Charge of Taking Bribes (402 B.C.).
*   **Unconstitutional Procedure** (*graphe paranomon*): Such as the case regarding the Property of the Brother of Nicias (395 B.C.).
*   **Claims for Money withheld from the State** (*apographai*): Including the case on the Property of Aristophanes (387 B.C.).
*   **Scrutiny** (*dokimasia*): Specifically the Senate's scrutiny of Officials Designate, such as the case against Evandrus (382 B.C.).
*   **Military Offences** (*graphai lipotaxiou, astrateias*): Including cases against Alcibiades (395 B.C.).
*   **Murder or Intent to Murder** (*graphai phonou, traumatos ek pronoias*): Such as the case against Eratosthenes (403 B.C.).
*   **Impiety** (*graphai asebeias*): Including the case on the Sacred Olive (not before 395 B.C.).
