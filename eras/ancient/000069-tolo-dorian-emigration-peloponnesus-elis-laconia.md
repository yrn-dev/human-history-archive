# ÆTOLO-DORIAN EMIGRATION INTO PELOPONNESUS

The settlement of the Peloponnesus by Dorian and Ætolian emigrants represents a foundational shift in the peninsula's historical and ethnic composition. According to the beliefs of the Grecian world and the writings of Herodotus, the great Dorian states that became the capital powers of the peninsula were originally emigrants. This movement included not only the Dorians but also the Ætolians of Elis, the Triphylians, and the Dryopes at Asinê and Hermionê.

## The Dorian Emigration and Legendary Accounts
The legendary narrative of the Dorian arrival is known as the "Return of the Children of Hêraklês." In this account, a single expedition—directed by the Delphian god and led by three brothers who were descendants of Achæo-Dorian heroes through Hyllus—vanquished and expelled the pre-existing population. This mythical event explains the partition of the peninsula and the establishment of Dorian rule.

Historically, the Dorians are associated with the occupation of Sparta, Messênia, and Argos. In the case of Argos and Corinth, settlers arrived by sea, with those in Argos establishing themselves quite distinctly from the settlements in Sparta and Messênia. Early Dorian presence is also noted in Krete. The legendary alliance of the Dorians with the Herakleids is depicted as a powerful force; specifically, Têmenus, Kresphontês, and Aristodêmus are said to have invaded across the gulf of Corinth, guided by Oxylus.

## Settlement of Laconia and Messenia
The Dorian settlement at Sparta was achieved by marching along the valleys of the Eurotas and Alpheus. Initially, these settlements were confined to Sparta and the region of Stenyklêrus. In Laconia, the invading Dorians and Herakleidæ are said to have driven out the Achæans. By the fifth century B.C., the Dorians and Achæans in this region had merged into a single political and ethnical aggregate known as Laconian or Lacedæmonian, which included both the Spartans and the Periœki.

In Messênia, the Dorian establishment displaced the Pylians and the heroic family of Nêleus and Nestôr. The kings of Stenyklêrus did not possess the entirety of Messenia. Following these establishments, the Pylians retired to Athens under their leader Melanthus, while others joined the Ionic emigration.

## The Ætolian Settlement in Elis
The territory of Elis, located in the westernmost part of the Peloponnesus south of Achaia and west of Olenus and Mount Pholoê, was occupied by a population of Ætolian origin. This racial connection between the historical Eleians and Ætolians was recognized by both parties. The Ætolians, led by Oxylus, settled in the region known as the "Hollow Elis," which was largely fertile, though the coastal areas remained sandy and barren.

The Eleians eventually conquered their neighbors, the Pisatans (of the Pisatid) and the Triphylians. The Triphylians, who lived in small townships such as Lepreon, were believed to be of the same race as the Minyæ of ante-Bœotian Orchomenos. While the Eleians maintained a unified government over this expanded territory, the Pisatans and Triphylians continued to resist their ascendency.

## The Olympic Festival and Regional Union
The Olympic festival served as an early point of union for the Spartans, Messenians, and Eleians. While the festival was originally a privilege of the Pisatans, it gained importance through the Ætolo-Eleian settlement and its combination with the Dorian powers of Laconia and Messenia. Tradition suggests that Iphitus of Elis and Lykurgus of Sparta collaborated to ensure the inviolability of Eleian territory and the sanctity of the Olympic truce.

The early victors of the games reflect this western Peloponnesian dominance; of the first twelve proclaimed victors (from 776 B.C. onwards), seven were Messenians and three were Eleians. It was only after the 12th Olympiad that competitors from Megara, Corinth, and Epidaurus began to appear, with extra-Peloponnesian victors following later.

## Displacement and Dialect
The Dorian establishments triggered several migrations of indigenous peoples:
1. The Epeians of Elis were either merged with the newcomers under Oxylus or expelled.
2. The Pylians moved to Athens or joined the Ionic emigration.
3. A portion of the Achæans, led by descendants of Orestês, formed the Æolic emigration to the Trôad, Lesbos, and the Gulf of Adramyttium.
4. Other Achæans expelled the Ionians from Achaia in the north, forcing them to retire to Attica.

Regarding language, the Doric dialect was widespread, used not only by the Dorians but also by the Ætolians, Phokians, Delphians, Lokrians, and the Achæans of Phthiôtis. Within the Peloponnesus, Doric was spoken by the Achæans in the north and the Dryopes of Hermionê. While Thucydidês characterized the Corinthians as Æolians and Strabo suggested the Æolic dialect was originally preponderant in the Peloponnesus, there is little evidence to determine the exact speech of the population present before the Dorian invasion.
