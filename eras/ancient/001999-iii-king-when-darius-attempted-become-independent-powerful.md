# Darius III and the Influence of Bagoas

## Accession of Darius III
Darius III, also known as Codomannus, was raised to the Persian throne by the eunuch Bagoas. Bagoas had previously established himself as the real master of the kingdom after serving as the confidential minister to Artaxerxes III. Bagoas had gained immense power and wealth, the latter partly achieved by confiscating sacred writings from Egyptian temples and selling them back to the priests for large bribes. 

Before placing Darius III on the throne, Bagoas had murdered Artaxerxes III in 338 and subsequently killed his son, Arses, in 336. Darius III was a distant relative of the royal house. According to some sources, his father was Arsames, son of Ostanes, and a brother of Artaxerxes. Prior to his ascension, Codomannus had distinguished himself in a war against the Cadusians.

## Conflict with Bagoas
Upon becoming king and adopting the name Darius, the new monarch took note of the fate of his predecessors. He sought to become independent of Bagoas, who served as the powerful vizier (known in Greek as the *chiliarchos*). In response to the king's attempt to assert his independence, Bagoas attempted to poison Darius III. However, Darius was warned of the plot and successfully forced Bagoas to drink the poison himself.

## Military Campaigns and Fall
The reign of Darius III was marked by the rise of Macedon. Philip II of Macedon had already sent an army into Asia Minor by 336, and the campaigns of Alexander began in the spring of 334. Darius III personally took the field against Alexander the following year. He suffered defeats at Issus and later at Arbela in 331. While fleeing to the east, Darius III was deposed and killed by Bessus in July 330.

## Other Figures Named Darius
The name Darius was common among Persian royalty and later dynasts. Other notable figures include:

*   **Darius (son of Hystaspes):** A member of a younger branch of the Achaemenidae who regained the kingdom in 521 after killing the usurper Gaumata (who ruled as Bardiya). He suppressed various rebellions in provinces such as Babylon, Media, and Susiana, and used stratagems to eliminate independent satraps like Orœtês of Phrygia, Lydia, and Ionia.
*   **Darius II (Ochus):** A bastard brother of Xerxes II who took the throne after killing his brother Secydianus. His reign was characterized by a heavy dependence on his wife, Parysatis, and conflicts with Athens, leading him to ally with Sparta.
*   **Darius the Mede:** A monarch mentioned in the Book of Daniel as receiving the kingdom of Babylon after the fall of the native Babylonian house. However, historical records and other scriptural passages indicate this figure is likely a historical inaccuracy or a confusion of history.
