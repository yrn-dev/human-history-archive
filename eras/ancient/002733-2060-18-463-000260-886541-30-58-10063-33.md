## Classical Greek History
Several entries detail events in ancient Greece, often citing Diodorus, Herodotus, and Thucydides.

### Spartan and Peloponnesian Events
Historical records describe an incident involving the Spartan Ischolaus in the district of Skiritis. During an invasion where the army was split into four portions—the Eleians (westernmost), the Argeians (easternmost), the Thebans, and the Arcadians—the Arcadians encountered Ischolaus at Ium or Oeum. Through superior numbers and vehemence of assault, the Arcadians overpowered him, leading to the death of Ischolaus and nearly all his division. Following this, the four divisions united at Sellasia, which was subsequently burnt and destroyed before the invaders encamped in the sacred grove of Apollo and reached the bridge over the Eurotas river leading to Sparta.

Other Spartan records detail a conspiracy involving Kinadon. Kinadon was seized on the road to Aulon by guards sent by the ephors. After being tortured, he revealed the names of his accomplices, including the prophet Tisamenus. Kinadon stated his motivation for the conspiracy was a desire to be "inferior to no man at Sparta." He and his accomplices were punished by being conducted around the city manacled with clogs around their necks while being scourged and pricked, before eventually being put to death.

### Other Greek Incidents
Records mention Mæandrius, who sought aid from Sparta to re-establish himself at Samos, following the example of Samian exiles under Polykratês. However, King Kleomenês and the ephors refused to assist him. Additionally, there is mention of Sylosôn being installed as a despot on an island populated primarily by women and children, an event following a "deed of blood" that later prompted Otanês to take measures to repeople the island.
