# Baeza and its Fortifications

Baeza (anciently known as _Beatia_) is a town located in southern Spain within the province of Jaen. It is situated in the Loma de Ubeda, a mountain range positioned between the river Guadalimar to the north and the river Guadalquiver to the south. As of 1900, the town had a population of 14,379.

## Urban Infrastructure and Transport
Baeza is served by a railway station located 3 miles to the south-west on the Linares-Almeria line. Historically, the town was known for the production of a celebrated red dye derived from the native cochineal. In terms of commerce, the town currently has little trade outside of farm-produce.

## Historical Development
During the middle ages, Baeza was a flourishing Moorish city, with reports suggesting it contained 50,000 inhabitants. This period of prosperity ended in 1239 when the city was sacked by Ferdinand III of Castile. Following this event, the bishopric of the town was transferred to Jaen in 1248. The town is also noted as the birthplace of the painter and sculptor Caspar Becarra.

## Architecture and Fortifications
Baeza possesses several significant buildings, including a cathedral, a Franciscan monastery, and the buildings of the university. The university was founded in 1533 and was later replaced by a theological seminary.

The town was once protected by fortifications of great strength. Among the remaining vestiges of these old defenses are the arch of Baeza and the Ubeda and Cordova gates.
