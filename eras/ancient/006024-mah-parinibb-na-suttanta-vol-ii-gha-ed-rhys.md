# Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta

The *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* is a significant Buddhist text that provides the account of the final journey and the last days of the Buddha. It is preserved within the Pāli canon as part of the *Dīgha Nikāya*.

## Classification and Structure
The *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* is located in volume ii of the *Dīgha Nikāya*. The *Dīgha Nikāya* is known as the "Collection of Long Ones," as it consists of the longest *suttantas*. In the context of early Buddhist literature, a *sutta* (Pāli) is a short memorial sentence or verse, typically occupying one or two pages and containing a single proposition. When several such *suttas* with similar propositions are collected within the framework of a dialogue, the resulting work is called a *suttanta*. While a typical *suttanta* is approximately a dozen pages in length, the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* is one of the few that is significantly longer.

The style of these texts is designed for memorization rather than reading, consisting of strings of passages intended to be learned by heart. Despite this, the dialogues are noted for a dignified style that occasionally reaches eloquence and employs a method of Socratic questioning.

## Content and Significance
The *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* serves as the primary canonical authority for the "third long episode" of the Buddha's life, which details his final days. The text spans pages 72 to 168 of volume ii of the *Dīgha Nikāya*. It functions as a narrative account of the Buddha's last journey, providing a detailed record of the events leading up to his death.

## Editions and Translations
The text of the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* was edited by Rhys Davids and Carpenter and published by the Pāli Text Society between 1890 and 1893. A translation of this work can be found in the volume titled *Buddhist Suttas*, published in Oxford in 1881, where the translation occupies pages 1 through 136.
