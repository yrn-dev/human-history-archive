# Buddhaghosa

Buddhaghosa was a celebrated Buddhist writer born in north India approximately A.D. 390 near the great Bodhi tree at Budh Gaya. A Brahmin by birth, he was the son of a man named Kesi. 

## Journey to Ceylon and Literary Work
Buddhaghosa was induced by his teacher, Revata, to travel to Ceylon. The purpose of this journey was to translate commentaries on the scriptures into Pali, as these commentaries had been preserved in the Sinhalese language. Upon arriving in Anuradhapura, Buddhaghosa studied under Sanghapala. 

To demonstrate his ability to the fraternity there, he wrote a work titled *Visuddhi Magga* (the Path of Purity), which serves as a summary of Buddhist doctrine. After receiving the consent of the fraternity, he spent many years laboring on the translation of the commentaries. Following the completion of these works, he returned to the region of the Bodhi tree in north India.

## Early and Subsequent Writings
Prior to his arrival in Ceylon, Buddhaghosa had already authored a book entitled *Nanodaya* (the Rise of Knowledge). He had also begun a commentary on the principal psychological manual found in the *Pitakas*; however, he later rewrote this specific work while in Ceylon.

Among his other known contributions is the *Sumangala Vilasin*, a portion of the aforementioned commentaries, of which one volume has been edited. Additionally, extracts exist from his commentary on the Buddhist canon law. While a Pali edition of this last work is based on a comparison with a nearly contemporaneous Chinese translation, it was discovered in that translation.

## Scope and Influence of His Works
The works specifically mentioned represent only a small fraction of Buddhaghosa's total output, which is described as prodigious. It is known that he wrote books that would fill approximately 20 octavo volumes of about 400 pages each. While other writings are ascribed to him, it remains uncertain if they are truly his work.

Because the date of Buddhaghosa's life is approximately certain, his voluminous writings provide a standard by which the age of other writings can be tested. Consequently, his work is considered of the greatest value for the history of Indian ideas and Indian literature.

## Context of Buddhist Origins
Regarding the origins of the texts associated with this tradition, the archive notes that the division of Buddhism into "northern" and "southern" is misleading. While some manuscripts came from Ceylon and others from Nepal, the original works were all composed in the same region: the valley of the Ganges, specifically the Majjhima Desa (the middle country). 

The differences found in these manuscripts are not a result of their geographical location, but rather the difference in time at which they were originally composed. Therefore, the distinction between these works is chronological rather than geographical or linguistic.
