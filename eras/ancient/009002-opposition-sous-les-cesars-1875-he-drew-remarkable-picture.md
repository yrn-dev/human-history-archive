# L'Opposition sous les Cesars

*L'Opposition sous les Cesars* is a historical work published in 1875 by the French classical scholar Marie Louis Antoine Gaston Boissier. The text provides a detailed examination of the political state of Rome, specifically focusing on the period of political decadence that occurred under the early successors of Augustus.

## Authorship and Context
The author, Gaston Boissier (1823–1908), was a secretary of the French Academy and a professor of Latin oratory at the College de France. Born in Nimes, Boissier was drawn to the study of ancient history from an early age by the Roman monuments located in his native town. His academic foundation was built upon epigraphy, and his early career included a ten-year tenure as a professor of rhetoric at Angouleme before he was called to Paris to teach at the Lycee Charlemagne.

Boissier's literary contributions began with a thesis on the poet Attius in 1857 and a study of M. Terentius Varro in 1861. He gained significant recognition for his ability to reproduce the characteristics of ancient Roman society with "exquisite vivacity." Prior to the publication of *L'Opposition sous les Cesars*, Boissier published *Ciceron et ses amis* in 1865 and a two-volume work in 1874 titled *La Religion romaine d'Auguste aux Antonins*, which analyzed the religious movements of antiquity that preceded the acceptance of Christianity.

## Content and Themes
In *L'Opposition sous les Cesars*, Boissier presents a "remarkable picture" of the political decline of the Roman state. The work focuses on the era of the early successors of Augustus, analyzing the nature of political opposition and the subsequent decadence of the Roman political system during this transition.

## Academic Reception and Legacy
By the time of the work's publication in 1875, Boissier had earned the universal respect of men of letters and scholars. This professional standing contributed to his election as a member of the French Academy in 1876, following the death of H.J.G. Patin. Boissier's career culminated in his appointment as the perpetual secretary of the French Academy in 1895.
