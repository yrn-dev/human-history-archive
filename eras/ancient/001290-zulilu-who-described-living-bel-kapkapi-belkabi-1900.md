# Zulilu

## Overview and Role in Assyrian History
Zulilu is identified in historical records as the "founder of the monarchy" in Assyria. His placement in the chronological sequence of Assyrian leadership puts him after Bel-kapkapi (also referred to as Belkabi), who is dated to 1900 B.C. Zulilu is further noted as being the ancestor of Shalmaneser I.

## The Early Assyrian Monarchy
The rise of Assyria occurred during a period when the region's political status was fluid. Under the rule of Khammurabi, a figure named Samsi-Hadad (or Samsi-Raman) appears to have served as a vassal-prince at Assur. Following this period, the monarchy established by Zulilu began to develop. 

The lineage following the foundation of the monarchy included several successors, such as Assur-rabi and his son Assur-nirari, followed by Assur-rim-nisesu. The line continued through Erba-Hadad and his son Assur-nadin-akhi I, leading eventually to Assur-yuballidh I.

## Expansion and Political Relations
As Assyria grew in power, it did so at the expense of Babylonia. This shift in power was evidenced by the diplomatic and familial ties between the two regions. For example, the Kassite king of Babylonia married the daughter of the Assyrian ruler Assur-yuballidh. However, this union resulted in disaster when a Kassite faction at the Babylonian court murdered the king and installed a pretender. In response, Assur-yuballidh marched into Babylonia to avenge his son-in-law, placing Burna-buryas of the royal line on the throne. Burna-buryas reigned for 22 years and maintained correspondence with Amenophis IV (Amon-hotep) of Egypt.

## Transition to Supremacy
While the Assyrians remained nominal vassals of Babylonia for a time, they eventually abandoned this status. Shalmaneser I, the great-great-grandson of Assur-yuballidh and a descendant of Zulilu, openly claimed supremacy in western Asia around 1300 B.C. Shalmaneser I founded the city of Calah and conducted extensive campaigns against wild tribes in the north-west and the Hittites, eventually settling Assyrian colonists in Cappadocia.

The transition of power from Babylonia to Assyria was completed by Shalmaneser's son, Tukulti-In-aristi. He conquered Babylon and put its king, Bitilyasu, to death, effectively making Assyria the mistress of the oriental world.
