# ASCOLI PICENO

Ascoli Piceno, known in ancient times as *Ausculum*, is a town and episcopal see located in the Marches region of Italy. It serves as the capital of the province of Ascoli Piceno. The town is situated 500 feet above sea level on the southern bank of the Tronto (ancient *Truentus*) river at the point where it meets the Castellano. It is surrounded by lofty mountains and is located 53 miles direct from Ancona, and 17 miles west of Porto d' Ascoli, a station on the coast railway. As of 1901, the population of the town was 12,256, while the commune had a population of 28,608.

## Ancient History
The ancient city of Asculum served as the capital of Picenum and was positioned in a strong location within difficult terrain. The Romans captured the city in 268 B.C., a period during which the Via Salaria was likely extended to the town, placing it 120 miles from Rome. 

The city played a prominent role in the Social War against Rome; in 90 B.C., the inhabitants massacred the proconsul Q. Servilius and all Roman citizens residing within the city walls. In 89 B.C., Pompeius Strabo captured the city after a long siege. Following this victory, the leader Judacilius committed suicide, the principal citizens were executed, and the remaining population was exiled. Pompeius Strabo celebrated his triumph on December 25 of that year. 

The city was later occupied by Caesar as a strong position after he crossed the Rubicon. It eventually received a Roman colony, possibly under the triumvirs, and grew in importance. By A.D. 301, it became the capital of Picenum Suburbicarium. In 545, the city was taken by Totila, though Paulus Diaconus later described it as the chief city of Picenum.

## Medieval and Modern Governance
From the era of Charlemagne, Ascoli Piceno was ruled by its bishops, who held the title of prince and possessed the right to coin money. This arrangement continued until 1185, when the town became a free republic. The city engaged in numerous struggles with Fermo before coming more directly under papal authority during the 15th century.

## Architecture and Landmarks
The town features a variety of Roman, medieval, and Renaissance structures.

### Roman Remains
The Porta Romana is a double-arched gate from the Roman period. Nearby are the remains of massive ancient city walls constructed from rectangular stone blocks 2 feet in height; evidence of even earlier fortifications has been discovered at this site. Other Roman vestiges include the site of the Roman theatre and the church and convent of the Annunziata, which are built upon large concrete Roman substructures that likely supported a significant building. Additionally, the church of S. Gregorio was built into a Roman tetrastyle Corinthian temple, of which the *cella* and two columns are still preserved.

### Religious Buildings
The cathedral is Romanesque in origin, though it has undergone significant alterations. It was restored in 1888 by Count Giuseppe Sacconi, and its dome contains frescoes from the same period painted by Cesare Mariani. The cathedral treasury once held a cope presented by Pope Nicholas IV, which was stolen in 1904 and sold to J. Pierpont Morgan; Morgan later returned the item to the Italian government, and it was moved to the Galleria Corsini in Rome for safety. Other notable Romanesque buildings include the baptistery, which retains its ancient character, and the churches of SS. Vincenzo ed Anastasio and S. Vittore.

The Gothic church of S. Francesco is located in the picturesque colonnaded market-place.

### Civic and Military Structures
The original Palazzo del Comune, which now serves as the prefecture, is a Gothic building with Renaissance additions located in the market-place. The town's castle, situated higher up, offers a view of the mountains and town, though it shows no traces of fortifications older than the medieval period. Additionally, the town contains the fortress of the Malatesta, which was constructed in 1349.

## Regional Context
Ascoli Piceno is part of the Marches (*Le Marche*), a territorial division of Italy that includes the provinces of Ancona, Macerata, Pesaro and Urbino, and Ascoli Piceno. The region is bounded by the Adriatic to the east, the Emilia to the north, the Abruzzi to the south, and Tuscany and Umbria to the west. The territory of Ascoli Piceno, along with Macerata and Ancona, belonged to ancient Picenum. While a railway runs along the coast from Bologna to Brindisi—with a branch from Porto d'Ascoli leading to the town—the mountainous nature of the country means that north-south communication is otherwise limited to devious roads.
