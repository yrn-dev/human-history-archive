# Bauli and the District of Baiae

## Overview and Identification
Bauli was a villa resort located near Baiae. Its name is derived from the Greek *Boaulia*, meaning "stalls," referring to the place where Hercules concealed the oxen of Geryon. There are differing scholarly views regarding its exact location. Some identify Bauli with the modern village of Bacoli due to a presumed similarity between the ancient and modern names; this location is 2 miles south-southeast of Baiae. Other researchers, such as G. B. de Rossi in *Notizie degli scavi* (1888), identify it with the Punta dell' Epitaffio, situated 1 mile northeast of Baiae.

## Notable Residents and Events
The resort was home to several prominent figures. Pompey possessed a villa on the hills of Bauli, while Hortensius owned a villa on the shores of the Lacus Lucrinus. The villa of Hortensius was noted for its tame lampreys and served as the setting for the dialogue in the second book of Cicero's *Academica Priora*. 

Later, the villa of Hortensius became imperial property and was the site where Nero murdered Agrippina. Additionally, the emperor Caligula constructed a bridge of boats extending from Bauli to Puteoli.

## Archaeological Remains of the District
The district of Baiae and its surrounding areas were once characterized by splendid baths and villas, some of whose foundations were built far out into the sea. While fragmentary remains exist, it is generally difficult to identify specific buildings, and many names assigned to the ruins lack authentication.

At Baiae itself, there are three large, lofty domed buildings—one circular and two octagonal—constructed of brick and *opus reticulatum*. Although popularly referred to as temples, these are actually the remains of *nymphaea* or baths. The Punta dell' Epitaffio is also covered in ruins.

## Ruins at Bacoli
The village of Bacoli contains several significant archaeological sites:
* **The "Tomb of Agrippina"**: A small theatre located to the north of the village.
* **Villa Remains**: The remains of a large villa are located under the village.
* **Water Reservoirs**: To the east lies a large water reservoir known as the Cento Camerelle. To the south is another reservoir with a vaulted ceiling called the *piscina mirabilis*, which measures 85 by 230 feet.

## The Villa of Marius
The villa of Marius was purchased by Lucullus before eventually passing into the possession of the imperial house. This site was the location of the death of Tiberius. The villa is referred to in different sources as *Baiana* or *Misenensis*. Th. Mommsen (in *Corp. Inscrip. Latin*., 1883) suggests the villa may be located at Bacoli, though Beloch argues it is situated on the promontory south of Misenum, a placement that may align more closely with the descriptions provided by Phaedrus.
