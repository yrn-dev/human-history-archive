# Maritza and the Basin of Thessaly

The Balkan Peninsula, an area of 184,779 square miles, is characterized by a predominantly mountainous surface. Within this region, the only extensive plains are those formed by the valleys of the Danube and Maritza, as well as the basin of Thessaly.

## The Maritza Valley
The Maritza valley is one of the primary plain regions of the Balkan Peninsula. A notable location within this valley is the town of Demotica (also known as Dimotica or Demotica), situated in the vilayet of Adrianople in European Turkey. The town is located on the left bank of the river Kizildeli, near where it joins the Maritza, and sits at the foot of a conical hill.

Historically known in the middle ages as Didymotichos, the town served as one of the principal marts of Thrace. In modern times, it has maintained commercial importance, exporting grain, silk, linen, and pottery, which are transported to Dédéagatch for shipment. Demotica was the birthplace of the Turkish sultan Bayezid I in 1347. Additionally, Charles XII of Sweden resided in the town from February 1713 to October 1714 following the battle of Poltava. The town's architecture includes an ancient citadel and palace on its hill, as well as mosques, public baths, and several Greek churches; it formerly served as the seat of a Greek archbishop.

## The Basin of Thessaly
The basin of Thessaly, also referred to as the central plain, is drained by the Salambria, known in ancient times as the Peneius. It is described as the most spacious of four distinct enclosed plains located on the east side of the Skardus and Pindus mountain ranges. These four plains—Thessaly, Bitolia, Tettovo, and the basin of Greveno and Anaselitzas—are generally bounded by precipitous alpine mountains and each possesses only one cleft for drainage via a single river.

The basin of Thessaly is noted for its distinguished fertility and is considered the amplest space of continuously productive land in Hellas. The Peneius river receives streams from Othrys, Pindus, and Olympus. Local tradition suggests the region was once a lake until Poseidôn created an efflux by cutting open the defile of Tempê.

## Geography and Settlement of Thessaly
The fertile region of Thessaly begins at Thermopylæ, marked by the ancient fortress and steep rock of Thaumaki. From this point, the land consists of low declivities and plains reaching northward toward Olympus. While the territory included a narrow strip of coast in the interior of the gulf of Pagasæ—containing the towns of Pagasæ and Amphanæum—its primary expansion was inland.

Major cities situated within this inland territory included:
* Larissa
* Pheræ
* Pharsalus
* Trikka
* Krannôn
* Metropolis
* Pelinna
* Atrax
* Skotussa
* Pharkadôn

The Thessalians, who occupied this central basin, held a state of irregular dependence over several other tribes, including the Dolopes, Malians, Magnetes, Perrhæbians, and Achæans of Phthiôtis, as well as certain Macedonian and Epirotic tribes located beyond Olympus and Pindus.
