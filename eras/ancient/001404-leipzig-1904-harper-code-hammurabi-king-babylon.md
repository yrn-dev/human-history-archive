# The Code of Hammurabi

The Code of Hammurabi is recognized as the earliest known code of law in history. It was promulgated by Hammurabi, the king of Babylon and founder of the first Babylonian Empire.

## Historical Context
The first Babylonian Empire was established by the Amorites, a Semitic people who settled in the up-river town of Babylon after a period of warfare following the decay of the Sumerian Akkadian Empire. Hammurabi consolidated this empire around 2100 B.C. (though other records suggest dates around 2200 B.C. or 2250 B.C.). 

During the early stages of Sumeria and Akkadia, city-kings functioned more as medicine-men and priests than as traditional monarchs. In these societies, the god of the priests was considered the real overlord and universal landlord, possessing wealth and authority that surpassed that of the king, particularly within city walls. Hammurabi is noted as one of the earlier monarchs to take a firm grip on the affairs of the community, though he did so with politeness toward the gods. In one inscription regarding his irrigation work in Akkadia and Sumeria, Hammurabi states, “When Anu and Bel entrusted me with the rule of Sumer and Akkad----.”

## The Code and its Promulgation
The Code of Hammurabi allowed for a more systematic study of Babylonian law than was previously possible through the analysis of thousands of individual contracts, deeds, bonds, receipts, and legal decisions. While other materials—such as royal charters, private letters, and "Sumerian Family Laws"—provide supplementary information, the Code remains the primary systematic source.

The law is presented as having a divine origin. At the head of the code, an image depicts Hammurabi receiving the law from the god Shamash, who is the nominal promulgator of the text.

## Related Archaeological Evidence
Other artifacts associated with Hammurabi include a brick inscribed with cuneiform characters. This inscription records the construction of a temple dedicated to a Sun God.

## Scholarly Bibliography
The study of the Code and Babylonian law has been documented by various scholars in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Notable works include:

* **J. Kohler and F. E. Peiser**: *Hammurabis Gesetz* (Leipzig, 1904).
* **S. A. Cook**: *The Laws of Moses and the Code of Hammurabi* (London, 1903).
* **V. Scheil**: The *Editio princeps* of the Code, published in *Textes Elamites-Semitiques* (Paris, 1902).
* **D. H. Mueller**: *Die Gesetze Hammurabis* (Vienna, 1903).
* **H. Winckler**: "Die Gesetze Hammurabis Koenigs von Babylon um 2250 v. Chr." in *Der alte Orient*.
