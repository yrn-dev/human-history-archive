# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview
Hatshepsut remains one of the most intriguing figures of the Eighteenth Dynasty of Egypt. Initially serving as regent for her stepson, Thutmose III, she took the unprecedented step of declaring herself Pharaoh, ruling as a full monarch for approximately two decades. Unlike many of her predecessors and successors, Hatshepsut’s reign was characterized less by military conquest and more by the restoration of trade networks and an ambitious building program.

Her rise to power was a masterclass in political legitimacy. To justify her rule in a society where the pharaoh was traditionally male, she claimed divine birth, asserting that the god Amun-Ra had fathered her. Over time, her iconography shifted; she was often depicted wearing the traditional pharaonic false beard and kilt, blending female identity with the masculine symbols of sovereign power.

## Key Facts
*   **The Expedition to Punt:** One of Hatshepsut's greatest achievements was a massive trading expedition to the Land of Punt (likely modern-day Somalia or Eritrea). This mission brought back luxury goods, including gold, ebony, and live myrrh trees, which were planted at her temple.
*   **Architectural Legacy:** She commissioned the Djeser-Djeseru ("Holy of Holies"), her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari. This structure is celebrated by historians for its symmetry and integration into the limestone cliffs.
*   **The "Damnatio Memoriae":** Years after her death, Thutmose III (or his successor) ordered the destruction of her images and the chiseling away of her name from monuments. Historians debate the motive: some argue it was personal spite, while others suggest it was a political move to ensure a clean line of succession for Thutmose III's own heirs.

## Did You Know?
1. **Divine Lineage:** Hatshepsut utilized "theology" as a political tool, commissioning reliefs that depicted the god Amun visiting her mother in the guise of her father.
2. **The False Beard:** While she began her reign in female dress, she eventually adopted the ceremonial false beard to signify her role as the "Living Horus."
3. **Rediscovery:** Much of what we know about her was recovered in the 19th century when archaeologists began piecing together the shattered fragments of her statues and stelae.
