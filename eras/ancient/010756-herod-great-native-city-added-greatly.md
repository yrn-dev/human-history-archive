# Herod the Great

Herod the Great was a native of Ascalon, a well-fortified Mediterranean coastal city and one of the five chief cities of the Philistines. During his time, he added greatly to the beauty of his native city.

## Military Career and Rise to Power
Herod demonstrated significant military courage and strategic ambition in his dealings with Mark Antony. In one instance, Herod sought to accuse Machaerus of maladministration. Although Machaerus eventually begged for reconciliation, Herod continued his journey to Antony. Upon learning that Antony was besieging Samosata—a strong city located near the Euphrates—Herod hastened to join the effort to show his courage and oblige Antony. Herod successfully ended the siege, slaying a large number of barbarians and taking a large prey. As a result, Antony admired Herod's courage further, heaped more honors upon him, and provided more assured hopes that Herod would gain his kingdom. This period of conflict eventually forced King Antiochus to deliver up Samosata.

## Architectural Achievements and Public Works
Herod was known for building and adorning many places in a magnificent manner. He established several new-built cities and fortresses, which he showcased to Marcus Agrippa. These included:
* **Cæsarea**: A city where Herod built a port and celebrated games that returned every fifth year.
* **Sebaste**: One of the cities where he entertained guests with costly dainties.
* **Fortresses**: He erected several fortresses at great expense, specifically Alexandrium, Herodium, and Hyrcania.

Herod also maintained a presence in Jerusalem, where the people received Marcus Agrippa in festival garments with acclamations.

## Family Conflicts and Personal Life
Despite his outward success and the "admired grandeur of a kingdom," Herod's private life was marked by severe family disorders and confusion of mind. He suffered from seditions within his family, which increased during his absences abroad. 

Herod's relationship with his children was particularly volatile. He bound his sons after being falsely accused by Eurycles and later, with permission from Cæsar, accused his sons before an assembly of judges at Berytus. He also dealt with threats from young men who sought to revenge their mother's death upon him, with some hoping to use Archelaus, the king of Cappadocia, to bring accusations before Cæsar. 

Herod's family history also includes the death of his brother, Joseph. After Joseph was murdered, Herod cut off the head of the murderer, Pappus, and sent it to his other brother, Pheroras. In a separate period of his life, Herod besieged Jerusalem and married Mariamne.

## Political Alliances and Adversaries
Herod navigated complex political landscapes involving the Romans and the Parthians. He maintained a close friendship with Marcus Agrippa, beseeching him to visit Judea and providing him with the best dainties and hospitality. He also dealt with the Roman leader Cæsar, though at times he faced accusations from individuals such as Sylleus, which led Herod to send Nicolaus to Rome.

During a conflict involving the Parthians, Herod's associates Hyrcanus and Phasaelus were seized and imprisoned. Herod was nearly captured himself when a butler was sent to seize him; however, he was warned of the Parthians' perfidiousness by Phasaelus and the daughter of Hyrcanus (whom Herod had espoused). Herod credited the daughter of Hyrcanus with very great wisdom for her role as his monitor in this matter.
