# Abellinates cognomine Protropi and the Abellinates

## Overview of Abellinum
Abellinum was an ancient settlement located on the boundary between the territory of the Hirpini and Campania. Its ruins are situated approximately 2.5 miles north-east of the modern city of Avellino, close to the village of Atripalda. The site contains an amphitheatre and city walls constructed in *opus reticulatum*, indicating that these structures date to the early imperial period. Geographically, Abellinum was positioned at the junction of roads leading to Beneventum from Salernum and Nola, and potentially from Suessula.

## The Abellinates
During the early imperial period, Abellinum served as the primary location for a specific tribe. Associated with this tribe were the Abellinates, who existed as independent communities. These groups were divided by their cognomens and their locations among other peoples:

*   **Abellinates cognomine Protropi**: This group was situated among the Hirpini.
*   **Abellinates cognominati Marsi**: This group was located among the Apulians.

## Regional Context: The Hirpini
The Hirpini were one of the peoples of Italy. According to Italian legend, the Hirpini, along with the Piceni, were led to their places of settlement by the woodpecker and the wolf, both of which were sacred animals of the god Mars. The cult of Mars was prominent among the Latins and kindred peoples during their struggles to establish settlements amidst the forests and mountains of Italy. This period was characterized by the clearing of primeval woodlands and the dangers posed by wild beasts, disease, and enemy raids.

## Modern Avellino
The modern city of Avellino, from which the ancient Abellinum derives its name, is an episcopal see and the capital of the province of Avellino in Campania, Italy. It is located at the foot of Monte Vergine, 1,150 feet above sea level, and is positioned 28 miles (direct) or 59 miles (by rail) east-north-east of Naples. As of 1901, the population was 23,760. The city serves as a railway junction for lines connecting to Rocchetta S. Antonio and Benevento.

Historical landmarks in the city include the ruins of a castle built in the 10th or 9th century. This castle is noted as the site where Count Roger II was crowned king of Apulia and Sicily by the antipope Anacletus II.
