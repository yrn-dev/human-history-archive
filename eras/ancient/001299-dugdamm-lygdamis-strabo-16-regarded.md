# Dugdamme

Dugdamme, also identified as Lygdamis in the writings of Strabo (i. 3. 16), was a leader of the Cimmerians. During a period when the Assyrian Empire was maimed and exhausted by long struggles—specifically contests with Elam and the devastation of Susa (or Shushan)—the empire became vulnerable to external threats. While the Babylonians referred to the Scythian hordes as Manda, the Cimmerian tribe specifically infested Asia Minor.

## Relation to Assyria
The Assyrian ruler Assur-bani-pal viewed Dugdamme with extreme hostility, referring to him as "a limb of Satan." Dugdamme's military activities included the sacking of Sardis. His eventual death in Cilicia was regarded by Assur-bani-pal as a special mark of divine favour. Despite the death of Dugdamme, other Scythian invaders continued to enter the region to take his place, contributing to the decay of the Assyrian Empire before the death of Assur-bani-pal around 626 B.C.

## Context of Assyrian Decline
The era of Dugdamme's incursions coincided with a broader systemic collapse of Assyrian power. The empire had been drained of wealth and its fighting population. Because the provinces of Babylonia and Elam had been devastated, they could not provide the necessary funds for the imperial exchequer, making it difficult for Assyria to garrison conquered populations or defend its frontiers against the Scythians and Cimmerians.
