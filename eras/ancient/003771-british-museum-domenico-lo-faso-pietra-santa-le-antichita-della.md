# British Museum

The British Museum serves as a repository for a diverse array of historical artifacts, ranging from ancient sculptural casts and illuminated manuscripts to pivotal editions of religious and linguistic texts.

## Sculptural Casts and Antiquities
The museum's collections include casts of significant historical works. One such item is a cast of a sarcophagus originally located in the cathedral of Girgenti in Sicily. This specific piece is documented in the work *Lo Faso Pietra-Santa, le antichita della Sicilia* (Palermo, 1834), specifically in volume 3, plate 45 (2) and text page 89. The cast is preserved within the sepulchral basement of the British Museum.

## Manuscripts and Musical Illustrations
The British Museum houses various manuscripts that provide insight into historical artistic and musical practices. One notable example is Add. MS. 18,851, a fine Spanish manuscript dating from the end of the 15th century. This volume was illustrated by Flemish artists for presentation to Queen Isabella. It is characterized by a profusion of musical instruments depicted in numerous concert scenes. Specifically, bag-pipes are illustrated on folios 13, 412^b, and 419; one of these instruments is depicted with a curved chaunter and two drones—one cylindrical and one conical—which are bound together.

Additionally, the museum holds the "London-Lumley" manuscript (MSS. Reg. 13 A xiv.), which dates to the late 13th century.

## Biblical and Religious Texts
The archive indicates that the British Museum possesses a significant collection of Bibles and religious codices spanning several centuries:

*   **Ancient Codices:** The museum holds the *Codex Alexandrinus*, a probable Northumbrian copy of the *Codex Amiatinus*, and a 9th-century Hebrew *Pentateuch*.
*   **Medieval and Early Modern Texts:** The collection includes a Vulgate from a manuscript written in A.D. 1148 for the monastery of Ste Marie de Parco in Louvain, and a 13th-century Latin Bible that previously belonged to Robert de Bello, the abbot of St Augustine's, Canterbury.
*   **English Versions:** The museum preserves an early Wycliffite version from a copy belonging to Thomas of Woodstock, duke of Gloucester, written toward the end of the 14th century. It also holds the first printed English Bible from 1535 and the first edition of the Authorized Version from 1611.
*   **Printed Fragments:** The museum possesses the only remaining fragment of Tyndale's Quarto Edition of the New Testament, printed in Cologne in 1525, as well as the 42-Line Bible printed at Mainz between 1452 and 1456.
