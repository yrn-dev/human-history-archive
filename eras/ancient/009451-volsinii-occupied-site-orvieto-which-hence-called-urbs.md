# Volsinii and the Site of Orvieto

## Historical Transition and Nomenclature
According to a generally adopted theory, the Etruscan city of Volsinii originally occupied the site where Orvieto now stands. Due to this historical occupation, the site was referred to as *Urbs vetus* during medieval and late classical periods. Following this period, the Roman Volsinii was transferred to Bolaena.

## Geography and the Lake of Bolsena
The town of Bolsena, known in ancient times as Volsinii, is located in the province of Rome, Italy. It is situated on the north-east bank of the Lake of Bolsena, approximately 12 miles west-south-west of Orvieto by road. 

The Lake of Bolsena (anciently *Lacus Volsiniensis*) is an almost circular body of water with an area of 71 square miles and a depth of 480 feet, sitting 1,000 feet above sea level. While it is the central point of a large volcanic district, it is likely not an extinct crater itself. The lake's sides feature basaltic formations in certain areas. The lake contains two islands: Martana, which holds the remains of the castle where Theodoric's daughter, Amalasuntha, was imprisoned and strangled, and Bisentina, which contains a church built by Vignola. Although the lake abounds in fish, its banks are somewhat deserted and are not free from malaria.

## The Town of Bolsena
As of 1901, the population of Bolsena was 3,286. The town is dominated by a picturesque medieval castle and is home to the church of S. Christina. This church dates from the 11th century and contains frescoes that may belong to the school of Giotto. The building features a Renaissance facade constructed around 1500 by Cardinal Giovanni de' Medici (later Pope Leo X) and terra cottas created by the Della Robbia.

Beneath the church are catacombs containing the tomb of Saint Christina, which was discovered in 1880. Legend states that Christina was martyred by drowning in the lake in 278.

## The Miracle of Bolsena and the Cathedral of Orvieto
In 1263, a miracle occurred at one of the altars in the crypt of the church of S. Christina. A Bohemian priest, who was skeptical of the doctrine of transubstantiation, became convinced of its truth when drops of blood appeared on the host he was consecrating. In response to this event, Pope Urban IV instituted the festival of Corpus Christi and ordered that the cathedral of Orvieto be erected. This miracle was later depicted in a celebrated fresco by Raphael in the Vatican.

## Infrastructure and Connectivity
Volsinii was a point of passage on the Via Cassia, an ancient high-road in Italy that led from Rome through Etruria to Florentia (Florence). The Via Cassia ran east of the Lacus Sabatinus, passed through Sette Vene, and continued through Sutrium, Forum Cassii, Volsinii, Clusium, and Arretium. The modern highroad from Rome to Florence closely follows this ancient line.

The exact date of the Via Cassia's construction is uncertain, though it cannot predate 187 B.C., the year consul C. Flaminius built a road from Bononia to Arretium. Cicero mentioned the Cassia as one of three roads from Rome to Mutina in 45 B.C. Additionally, a milestone from A.D. 124 records that Hadrian performed repairs on the road from the boundary of the territory of Clusium to Florence, covering a distance of 86 miles.
