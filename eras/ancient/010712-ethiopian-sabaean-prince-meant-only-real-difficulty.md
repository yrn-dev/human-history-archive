# Ethiopian and Sabaean Princes

The historical and geographical records regarding Ethiopian and Sabaean figures vary across different eras, ranging from biblical narratives and classical Greek accounts to later inscriptions from the kingdom of Axum.

## Biblical and Regional Identifications
In certain narratives, the term "Cushite" is used to designate individuals who may be Ethiopian or, more likely, Arabian, as Cush is identified as the "father" of the Sabaeans. In the context of biblical narratives involving Zerah, some scholars suggest that if Zerah is interpreted as a Sabaean or Ethiopian prince, certain difficulties in the narrative are removed. This interpretation is proposed because there is no known king named Zerah of Ethiopia during that specific period, and the records do not seem to provide room for such a person.

## Classical Greek Accounts of Ethiopians
According to Herodotus, the Ethiopians were divided into two distinct bodies. One group was located above Egypt, and the other was situated toward the direction of the sunrising (the East). These Eastern Ethiopians were commanded by Arsames and served alongside the Indians. While they were similar to other Ethiopians, they differed in their language and the nature of their hair; the Eastern Ethiopians possessed straight hair, whereas the Ethiopians of Libya had hair that was woolly and thicker than that of any other men.

The Eastern Ethiopians were armed similarly to the Indians. Their distinctive attire included the flayed skin of a horse's forehead—complete with the mane and ears—worn upon the head. The mane functioned as a crest, and the ears were set up stiff and straight. For defense, they utilized shields made from the skins of cranes. In contrast, the Libyans, commanded by Massages (the son of Oarizos), used leather equipment and javelins with burnt points.

## The Kingdom of Axum and the Princes of Ethiopia
Later historical records, including those cited by Athanasius, refer to the kings of Axum. Inscriptions discovered in the ruins of Axum by Mr. Salt in 1806 detail the reign of a prince named Aeizanas. Aeizanas is styled as the king of several groups and regions, including:
* The Axumites
* The Homerites (the inhabitants of Yemen)
* The Ethiopians
* The Sabsuites
* Raeidan, Silea, Tiamo, the Bougaites, and Kaei.

Aeizanas is credited with a victory over the Bougaitæ (identified by some as the Blemmyes or Bedjah/Bodjah). At the time of his rule, the king of the Ethiopians exercised authority over the Homerites of Yemen. Aeizanas did not identify as a Christian, instead calling himself the son of the invincible Mars. While a brother named Saiazanas and another named Adephas are mentioned, Aeizanas appears to have been the sole king.

## Ethiopian Influence in Egypt
Other historical accounts mention an Ethiopian prince named So (also identified as Sabacon or Sevechus) who reigned over Egypt. This ruler is described as being more interested in the functions of a priest than those of a king, eventually causing himself to be consecrated as the high-priest of Vulcan. Due to his devotion to superstition, he neglected the military defense of his kingdom and deprived military men of their revenues and privileges, believing that he would never require their assistance.
