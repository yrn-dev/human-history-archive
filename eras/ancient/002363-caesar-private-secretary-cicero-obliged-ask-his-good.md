# Lucius Cornelius Balbus Major

Lucius Cornelius Balbus, known as Balbus Major to distinguish him from his nephew, was a prominent figure in Rome during the first century B.C. Generally considered to be of Phoenician origin, he was a naturalized foreigner who rose to significant political and financial influence.

## Early Career and Citizenship
Balbus earned Roman citizenship for himself and his family through the intervention of Pompey, as a reward for services rendered against Sertorius in Spain. He established himself as one of the chief financiers in Rome and maintained friendly relations with various political parties. This diplomatic approach led to his involvement in the formation of the First Triumvirate.

Balbus carefully cultivated a relationship with Julius Caesar. He accompanied Caesar during his tenure as propraetor in Spain in 61 B.C. and later served as the chief engineer (*praefectus fabrum*) during Caesar's campaigns in Gaul starting in 58 B.C.

## Legal Challenges and Political Influence
Due to his wealth, his influence, and his status as a naturalized foreigner, Balbus acquired many enemies. In 56 B.C., a native of Gades prosecuted him on the charge of illegally assuming the rights of a Roman citizen. This legal action was also directed at the triumvirs. Balbus was acquitted after Crassus, Pompey, and Cicero all spoke on his behalf.

During the subsequent civil war, Balbus attempted to persuade Cicero to act as a mediator between Pompey and Caesar. His objective was to prevent Cicero from definitively siding with Pompey. Cicero later admitted that he allowed himself to be dissuaded from mediating, despite his own better judgment.

## Role as Private Secretary and Later Career
Following these events, Balbus became the private secretary to Julius Caesar. His influence grew to the extent that Cicero was obliged to seek Balbus's good offices with Caesar. Balbus also maintained a diary documenting the primary events of both his own life and the life of Caesar. Additionally, the eighth book of the *Bell. Gall.*, likely written by Hirtius at Balbus's instigation, was dedicated to him.

After the assassination of Caesar, Balbus attached himself to Octavian. He continued to rise in the Roman political hierarchy, serving as praetor in either 42 or 43 B.C. In 40 B.C., he attained the position of consul, marking the first time such an honor was conferred upon an alien. The exact year of his death is not recorded in the archive.
