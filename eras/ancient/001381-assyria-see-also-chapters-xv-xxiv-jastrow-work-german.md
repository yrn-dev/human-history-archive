# Assyria

## Historical Relationship with Babylonia
Assyria and Babylon were two great empires that developed on the banks of the Euphrates and Tigris rivers. Historically and geographically, these two entities were closely intertwined, with the power of one often serving as a measure of the weakness of the other. While classical accounts frequently prioritize Assyria over Babylonia, the decipherment of inscriptions has revealed that Babylonia was actually the mother country. For the majority of their history, with the exception of some seven or eight centuries, Assyria can be described as a dependency or province of Babylon.

Due to this relationship, the Assyrians derived their culture, religion, literature, arts, and sciences from their southern neighbors. The two groups were similar in both language and race.

## Geography and Defense
The city of Nineveh was located in a part of Assyria that bordered Media. In ancient times, the region employed large-scale fortifications for protection against foreign invasion. Ammianus Marcellinus recorded seeing the ruins of a wall near Macepracta on the left bank of the Euphrates, which had once stretched a great distance to defend Assyria. This structure is presumed by some to be the Wall of Media, which may have reached from the Tigris to the Euphrates or to a canal cut from the Euphrates.

## Religion and Nomenclature
In the study of the region's spiritual life, a distinction is made in spelling between the deity and the land: the god is typically spelled *Asur*, while the country is spelled *Assur*. The name Assur is noted as being distinct from the Asshur mentioned in 1 Chronicles ii. 24 and ii. 45. 

The religious landscape of the region involved a complex system of priesthoods, including the use of specific philological methods. Various aspects of their spiritual and occult practices have been documented in historical texts, including:
*   **Divination and Astrology:** Records include reports from the magicians and astrologers of Nineveh and Babylon, as well as documents relating to presages and Chaldean astrology.
*   **Magic and Ritual:** Sources describe Assyrian magic, the use of conjuration series such as *Maqlu*, and the study of devils and evil spirits.
*   **Worship:** Evidence exists of prayers directed toward the sun god.

## External Accounts and Oracles
Assyria appears in various ancient oracles and historical narratives. One such account describes the destruction of the Kenite state by Assyria; however, the identity of "Assyria" in this context is debated, with some identifying it as the ancient realm of Nineveh and others as the Seleucid kingdom of Syria. Another oracle speaks of ships arriving from the West to attack "Eber" and Assur, an event that may refer to Alexander the Great's conquest of Persia.
