# Archaeologia

*Archaeologia* is a publication that has served as a venue for various archaeological and antiquarian studies, including research on masonry, burial mounds, and ancient art.

## Masons' Marks in England
The study of "masons' marks in various countries" was brought to prominence by George Godwin, F.R.S., F.S.A., &c. In 1841, Godwin communicated his findings to the Society of Antiquaries, and he later contributed to *Archaeologia*, vol. xxx, p. 113. Godwin is credited with being the first to draw attention to these signs within England. 

These marks are often overlooked by archaeologists, even in frequently visited buildings, until a few are identified, after which they appear in astonishing numbers. In the *Sessional Papers* of the Royal Institute of British Architects (No. 9, 1868-1869), numerous samples of these marks from across Europe were provided to illustrate Godwin's paper. This system is considered a modern method for connecting a stone worker to their specific work. In some instances, a second mark on a block may indicate an overseer, and there are cases where three or more marks are present.

## Studies of Burial Mounds and Cairns
*Archaeologia* has published significant research regarding ancient burial sites. Dr. J. Thurnam contributed a work titled "On Ancient British Barrows" in volumes 42 and 43 (1869). Additionally, W. Gowland published "The Dolmens and Burial Mounds in Japan" in volume 55 (1897).

## Celtic Art and Other Contributions
The publication has also been used to document ancient art. Arthur J. Evans contributed work on late Celtic art in volumes lii. through lv. Other references to the publication include volume xvii, pp. 176-179 (London, 1814).
