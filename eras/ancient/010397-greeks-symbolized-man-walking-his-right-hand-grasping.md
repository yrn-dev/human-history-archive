# Bootes

## Overview and Symbolism
Bootes (from the Greek *bootaes*, meaning a ploughman, and *bous*, meaning an ox) is a constellation located in the northern hemisphere. In ancient Greek symbolism, the constellation was represented as a man walking. In this depiction, the man is grasping a club with his right hand, while his left hand is extended upwards, holding the leash of two dogs. These dogs appear to be barking at the Great Bear.

## Historical and Literary References
The constellation is mentioned in the works of Aratus (3rd century B.C.) and Eudoxus (4th century B.C.). It is also cited as being perhaps alluded to in the book of Job, as well as appearing in the works of Hesiod and Homer.

## Stellar Composition
Various astronomers have catalogued the stars within Bootes:
*   Ptolemy recorded twenty-three stars.
*   Tycho Brahe recorded twenty-eight stars.
*   Hevelius recorded fifty-two stars.

The brightest star in the group is Arcturus. Other notable stars include:
*   *$\epsilon$ Bootis*: A double star consisting of a blue star of magnitude 6-1/2 and a yellow star of magnitude 3.
*   *$\xi$ Bootis*: A double star consisting of a purple star of magnitude 6-1/2 and a yellow star of magnitude 4-1/2.
*   *W. Bootis*: A star characterized as irregularly variable.

## Alternative Nomenclature
Throughout history, Bootes has been known by numerous other names. These include Arcas, Bubulus, Bubuleus, Lycaon, Icarus, Philometus, Arctophylax, Arcturus minor, Canis latrans, Clamator, Plaustri custos, Plorans, Thegnis, and Vociferator. 

Different cultures and individuals have applied their own labels to the constellation:
*   **Arabs**: Termed it Archamech or Aramech.
*   **Hesychius**: Named it Orion.
*   **Jules Schiller**: Referred to it as St Sylvester.
*   **Schickard**: Called it Nimrod.
*   **Weigelius**: Referred to it as the Three Swedish Crowns.
