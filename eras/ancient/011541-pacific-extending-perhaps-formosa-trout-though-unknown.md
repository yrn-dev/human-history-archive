# Formosa and the Pacific Region

## Geography and Regional Classification
Formosa is regarded as an unquestionable outlier of Asia, situated among several important island groups on the east and south-east of the continent. These groups include the Philippines, Luchu, the Japanese group, and the Kurile islands. Some of the more southern islands in these regions serve as links between the Asian continent, Australia, and the islands of the Pacific.

In the waters surrounding these regions, the Kuro-shio, or "Black Tide," is a strong current that sweeps northward from the Philippines and Formosa. This current is characterized by a deep indigo color. Upon reaching the vicinity of Kyushu, the tide is deflected eastward, passing the Kii promontory and the southern coast of Kyushu before entering the Pacific.

## Climate and Meteorological Phenomena
The hotter seas of Asia are subject to revolving storms known as cyclones. In the China Sea, these storms are referred to as typhoons. These cyclones occur during the hot months of the year and typically advance from the north-east to the south-west, though they occasionally move from east to west. These storms originate near the island of Formosa and extend to approximately the 10th degree of North latitude. Like the cyclones of the Indian Sea, typhoons in the China Sea appear to advance toward areas of greatest heat, with winds circulating from north by west through south to east.

## Marine and Freshwater Fauna
The Pacific and surrounding Indo-Pacific regions are characterized by a rich variety of fish fauna, which is more diverse in species and genera than that of the Atlantic. The tropical zones, particularly around coral reefs, exhibit the greatest variety of gorgeous coloration and bizarre forms.

Specific fish distributions in and around the Pacific and Asian waters include:
*   **Salmonidae:** While entirely absent from southern Asia, these fish exist in rivers flowing into the northern Pacific and the Arctic Ocean, extending possibly as far as Formosa.
*   **Pomfrets (Stromateidae):** These fish extend to the Pacific and China.
*   **Polynemidae:** This group ranges from the Atlantic through the Indian Ocean to the Pacific.
*   **Pearly Nautilus:** An isolated genus closely related to the extinct Ammonites, the Pearly Nautilus is found in the warm waters of the Pacific and Indian oceans.

In contrast, trout are unknown in Indian rivers, though they are found in streams flowing into the Caspian beyond the Indus watershed.

## Political History and Territorial Disputes
The status of Formosa and the nearby Luchu archipelago was a point of contention between the Japanese and Chinese governments. In 1879, Japan converted Luchu into a prefecture named Okinawa (meaning "extended rope"), dethroning its prince. While China protested, a conference in Peking resulted in an agreement to divide the archipelago equally; however, China refused to ratify this compromise, and Japan continued its administration of the islands.

By 1895, Formosa came into Japanese possession, which ended disputes over the title to the chain of islands. However, other records indicate that a Japanese campaign in Formosa was a limited affair, involving the shooting-down of a few semi-savages without penetrating the island's interior. This led to diplomatic tension, as the "Middle Kingdom" (China) claimed Japan had invaded Chinese territory. This was eventually resolved through a peaceful solution involving the recognition of Formosa as part of the Middle Kingdom and the payment of a small indemnity by China amounting to 500,000 Mexican dollars.

## Etymological Notes
There is historical discussion regarding the name "Formosa." Some sources suggest the name may have resulted from a misunderstanding by Rusticiano of the word *Harmuza*, potentially influenced by Marco Polo's descriptions of a beautiful plain. This is compared to linguistic shifts such as the Spanish *hermosa* for *formosa* or the change of *Harmuza* to *Mafomet* for Mahomet. Additionally, the city of Hormuz is noted to have been founded by Shah Mahomed Dirhem-Ko (Xa Mahamed Dranku) in a plain of the same name.
