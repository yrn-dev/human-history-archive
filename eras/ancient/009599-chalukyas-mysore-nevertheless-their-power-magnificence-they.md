# Chalukyas

The Chalukyas were a prominent dynasty that exercised significant power in western and southern India between the 5th and 8th centuries. Their influence extended across various regions, including the southern Deccan and the islands that would later comprise Bombay.

## Territorial Expansion and Mastery
During the 7th century, the Chalukyas established a dominant empire in the southern Deccan. They achieved this by overcoming rivals, including the Rashtrakutas and other smaller kingdoms, which they subsequently absorbed into their empire. The southern boundary of the empire of Harsha (who died in 647 or 648) was the Nerbudda; the Chalukyas operated south of this line.

The reach of the Chalukyas extended to the western coast. During the period between 450 and 750, the city of Puri on Elephanta Island served as a principal location in Bombay harbour under Chalukya and Maurya rule.

## Conflicts and Rivalries
The Chalukyas were engaged in prolonged struggles for mastery in the southern Deccan, primarily against the Rashtrakutas. Additionally, they fought against the Pallavas, a conflict that saw the Chalukya leader Vikramaditya II capture the Pallava capital around 740.

Despite their magnificence and power, the Chalukyas eventually succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Rashtrakutas. The Rashtrakutas succeeded to the majority of the Chalukya dominions, which included Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line.

## External Relations and Decline
In 710-711 (92 A.H.), Arab forces invaded India and established themselves in Sind by 712. While the Arabs conducted various raids, they did not launch any serious attacks on the Chalukya or Gurjara empires.

The decline of Chalukya authority also manifested in the loss of allegiance from subordinate princes. For example, Bhillama I, a prince who renounced his allegiance to the Chalukyas around A.D. 1187, founded the Yadava dynasty in the west and established the city of Deogiri.

## Regional Transitions
In other parts of India, such as the northern Circars, the Chalukyas were eventually replaced by the Cholas. The Cholas were later ousted by the Reddi kings during the 14th century.
