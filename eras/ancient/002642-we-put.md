## Spiritual Trust and Reliance

Messengers describe themselves as mortals and humans like those they address, noting that Allah bestows favors and grace upon His servants as He pleases. ## Judicial and Penal Actions
Historical accounts frequently document the act of putting individuals into bonds, prison, or to death as a means of political or social control.

### Ancient Greece
In Lacedemonia, the Lacedemonians resolved to put the Minyai to death after the Minyai became insolent and sought a share of royal power. The Lacedemonians practiced a custom of putting people to death only by night. However, the Minyai escaped by putting on the clothes of their wives, who were native Spartans, and exiting the prison disguised as women.

In Athens, during the period of the mutilators of the Hermæ, there is a historical dispute regarding executions. While Andokidês affirms that some accused persons were put to death upon his deposition, Thucydides suggests that only those against whom Andokidês himself informed were put to death. Thucydides notes that the Athenians detained many persons on suspicion, but argues that putting these individuals to death would have prevented the full disclosure of the plot and the identification of internal enemies.

### Judean and Syrian Conflicts
In the government of Aristobulus, who was the first to put a diadem upon his head (occurring four hundred seventy-one years and three months after the people were set free from Babylonian slavery), the ruler put his brethren and his mother in bonds. His mother was subsequently pined to death in prison.

During wider conflicts in Syria, various cities took action against the inhabitants of the region. In Askelon, two thousand five hundred were slain, and in Ptolemais, two thousand were killed and others put into bonds. Tyre put a great number to death and kept many in prison. Similarly, Hippos and Gadara put the boldest to death while keeping others in custody. Conversely, the Antiochtans, Sidontans, and Apamians refused to kill or put these people in bonds, potentially due to the large number of inhabitants or commiseration for those who made no innovations.

## Military and Naval Operations
The term is used in the context of deploying forces and initiating naval engagements. In the Peloponnesian War, the Athenians were compelled to put to sea in haste with untrained crews to relieve Euboea. After being surprised by Agesandridas, the Athenians were put to flight and chased to the shore.

In more modern naval correspondence, W. S. C. expressed a desire to see the Royal Naval Volunteer Reserve mobilised and put afloat on First Fleet ships for a week as additional complements, requesting definite proposals and estimates for this policy.

### Dietary Practices
Marco Polo records a method of food preservation used by certain peoples who dry milk into a paste. When food is needed, they put this paste in water and beat it until it dissolves. Travelers would take approximately ten pounds of this dried milk; in the morning, a man would put a half pound of it into a leather bottle with water, which would churn into a pap as he rode.
