# A. Holder

A. Holder was a scholar and editor whose work spanned several disciplines, including the study of ancient Celtic languages, classical philology, and the works of the Roman poet Horace.

## Contributions to Celtic Studies
Holder is the author of *Altkeltische Sprachschatz* (published between 1896 and 1908), a work that serves as an alphabetical collection of the chief passages regarding early Britain found in classical writers. These classical references include the writings of Tacitus, Ptolemy, Julius Caesar, the elder Pliny, Strabo, Cassius Dio, and Diodorus. Additionally, the work draws upon lists from the *Notitia Dignitatum* (circa A.D. 400), the Ravennas (a 7th-century *rechauffé*), and the Antonine Itinerary (likely from A.D. 210-230).

Holder's research in this field is also cited as a reference for the study of the Boii, a people belonging almost certainly to the Early Iron Age. The Boii are characterized as a race of large men who likely utilized long iron swords to deliver cutting blows.

## Classical Philology and the Works of Horace
In the field of classical literature, Holder is recognized for his critical editions and scholarly commentaries on the works of Horace. Together with O. Keller, Holder produced a recension of Horace's works. This edition is noted for providing the largest collection of *testimonia* from ancient writers, as well as an extensive collection of emendations and variants to the text. The *apparatus criticus* for this work is described in detail in the preface to the second edition's first volume, published in 1899.

Holder also focused on the ancient scholia—marginal notes or commentaries—found in many manuscripts of Horace. He specifically collected and edited the Porphyrio scholia in 1902. These scholia were originally derived, either through copying or abridgment, from the commentaries of Porphyrio, who lived around A.D. 200.
