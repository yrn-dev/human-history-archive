## English Literature and Drama
The year 1580 serves as a chronological boundary in the study of English literature. It marks the end of a period in prose, verse, and drama that left few memorials of form. Following 1580, the English drama entered a more individualistic phase, characterized by the emergence of the "University wits" between 1580 and 1595. 

Among these figures was John Lyly, who began writing comedies—including *Campaspe*, *Sapho and Phao*, and *Midas*—mostly between 1580 and 1591. Lyly is noted as the first practitioner in prose to utilize pointed talk and shapely comic plots, reflecting the artificial style of courtly masquers.

During this incursion, a fortress was erected, though it was subsequently destroyed by the English in 1580.

A prominent event under his leadership occurred in November 1580, when 600 Spaniards and Italians were massacred at Smerwick, an action for which Grey was held responsible.

## Religious and Academic Events
In Japan, the year 1580 saw a significant shift in religious practice in the city of Kuchinotsu. Following the arrival of a "great ship from Macao" in 1578 and the influence of the Jesuit vice-general Valegnani, the city became Christian in 1580. This transition involved the people burning their idols and destroying forty temples, while saving some materials for the construction of churches.

In England, 1580 marks the death of the recusant Dr. Thomas Young (1514–1580). Following this, the Mastership of Pembroke Hall was filled.
