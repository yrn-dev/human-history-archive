# Darius Restored

## Political Restoration and Chronology
In the historical record of the kings of Babylon and Persia, the period surrounding 513 B.C. is marked by the restoration of Darius, son of Hystaspes. According to chronological records, Darius had previously ascended to power in 520 B.C. However, in 514 B.C., a rebel king known as Nebuchadrezzar IV emerged. This period of instability concluded in 513 B.C. when Darius was restored to his position.

## Military Campaigns and Geographic Knowledge
The year 513 B.C. is also associated with a military campaign led by Darius into Scythia. The results of this campaign, along with the voyage of Scylax of Caryanda from the Red Sea to the Indus, provided critical geographic data for early cartographers. Hecataeus of Miletus (549-472 B.C.), author of the *Periodos* (a description of the earth), utilized information from Darius' campaign in Scythia to inform his world map. Despite this data, Hecataeus maintained a circular shape for the earth and a circumfluent ocean.

## Relations with the Bani Israel
During the reign of Darius, the Israelites (Bani Israel) experienced a period of restoration under the protection of the Persian prince. Under the leadership of Zorobabel, the people of God returned from their captivity in Babylon to Jerusalem. Zorobabel obtained grants from Darius, for which he offered thanks to God, and subsequently brought the news to his countrymen. 

The Israelites celebrated the rebuilding and restoration of their country with a seven-day festival of eating, drinking, and music, including the use of pipes and cymbals. Following this, rulers were chosen from the tribes of their forefathers to travel to Jerusalem with their families and cattle, accompanied by guides sent by Darius. Under the protection of Darius and animated by the exhortations of the prophets Zechariah and Haggai, the Israelites eventually completed the construction of their temple, which had been delayed for many years due to the cabals of enemies. Additionally, some historical opinions place the events of the history of Esther within the reign of Darius.

## Interactions with the Greek World
Darius' influence extended into the Aegean, where the Pelasgians of Lemnos and Imbros resisted his forces. The Pelasgians, described as having a cruel and piratical character, held out bravely in their cities of Myrina and Hephæstias against Otanês. They only submitted after enduring severe hardship. The fear of Darius was such that their king, Hermon, is reported to have emigrated voluntarily.

Darius also had interactions with Greek professionals. On one occasion, after spraining his foot during a chase, Darius found that his habitual Egyptian surgeons were unable to cure him, leaving him in violent pain and without sleep for seven days. He was eventually treated by a Greek surgeon named Dêmokêdês, who had been a slave of Orœtês. After Dêmokêdês successfully restored the king's foot to a sound state, Darius rewarded him with gold chains, a splendid house, and the status of a companion at his table.

## Later Reflections and Legacy
In later accounts, a Darius is depicted in a state of misfortune, having been conquered by Alexander. Upon learning of the death of his wife, Statira, and the magnificent funeral granted to her by Alexander, Darius expressed grief over the fortune of Persia. He questioned whether the honorable treatment of his wife and sister by the conqueror was a sign of genuine magnanimity or a mask for dishonor. 

Despite his suspicions, Darius eventually prayed to the parent gods who watch over the Persian throne, requesting that he might restore the fortune of Persia to its former state to repay Alexander's kindness. He stated that if the Persian empire were doomed to perish, he hoped no conqueror other than Alexander would mount the throne of Cyrus.
