# Greek and Roman Antiquities at the British Museum: Etruscan Bronzes

The department of Greek and Roman antiquities at the British Museum houses several significant artifacts from the Etruscan civilization, ranging from musical instruments to figures that provide insight into ancient costume and social traditions.

## Etruscan Musical Instruments
Among the notable holdings of the British Museum are two bronze Etruscan *cornua* (identified as No. 2734). These instruments are semicircular in shape and bear a resemblance to hunting horns from the middle ages. They are characterized by well-preserved, cup-shaped mouthpieces. The two horns differ slightly in size, measuring 2 ft. 1 in. and 2 ft. 2 in. from end to end.

## Etruscan Costume and Representation
The museum's collection includes a small bronze figure that serves as a primary example of specific Etruscan attire. This figure depicts two articles of costume that were peculiar to the Etruscans: the *tutulus*, which is a high conical hat, and *calcei repandi*, which are shoes featuring turned-up points. These specific items of dress have analogies in oriental styles, supporting traditions that the Etruscan people originated from Asia.

In contrast to these unique items, other evidence suggests that Etruscan female dress generally did not differ significantly from Greek dress, typically consisting of the *himation* and *chiton*. While the *himation* was often worn as a shawl in earlier times, it differed from the Greek *peplos*.

## Related Artifacts and Materials
Beyond the bronze works, the British Museum possesses a celebrated terra-cotta sarcophagus of a later date. The female figure reclining on the lid of this sarcophagus is depicted wearing a Greek *chiton* made of thin white material with short sleeves fastened by loops and buttons on the outside of the arm. Her attire includes a dark purple thick-stuff *himation* wrapped around her legs and hips, and sandals made of leather soles with leather straps. Under these straps, she wears thin socks that leave the toes uncovered. Her adornments include a necklace with heavy pendants, pierced ears for earrings, and a flat diadem bound around her head. Her hair is styled with long tresses and a ribbon at the roots behind.

Additionally, the department of Greek and Roman antiquities holds a slightly broken terra-cotta model of a small spiral instrument, similar to a medieval post-horn or hunting-horn. This object was excavated at Ventoux in France and was acquired from the collection of M. Morel.

## Composition of Classical Bronzes
The bronze materials used in the antiquities of this era, including those found in classical antiquity, were primarily composed of copper alloyed with silver, lead, tin, or zinc. The proportions of these metals varied based on the time period or the intended purpose of the object. In such bronze remains, the copper content typically varies between 67% and 95%.
