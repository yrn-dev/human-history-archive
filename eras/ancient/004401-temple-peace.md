# Temple of Peace

The Temple of Peace was a significant architectural and cultural monument in ancient Rome, commissioned by the Emperor Vespasian. It served as both a symbol of imperial glory and a centralized repository for the world's most esteemed artistic and historical treasures.

## Construction and Purpose
The Emperor Vespasian resolved to build the Temple of Peace after the Roman affairs had been established on the surest foundations and following the conclusion of his triumphs. The construction was completed in a manner and timeframe that exceeded all human opinion and expectation. Vespasian utilized a vast quantity of wealth, acquired through Providence and his previous exploits, to adorn the temple with an extensive collection of statues and pictures.

The temple was designed to be a global gallery; it housed rarities that, in previous eras, would have required travelers to wander across the entire habitable world to see individually. Because of this concentration of art and antiquity, the temple became a daily gathering place for the learned and the artists of Rome.

## Collections and Artifacts
The Temple of Peace functioned as a museum for works of art that had survived civil tumults. Specific works mentioned in historical records as being located within the temple include celebrated pictures by Protogenes and Timanthes.

In addition to classical art, Vespasian used the temple to display the spoils of his military victories. He deposited golden instruments and vessels taken from the Jewish temple within the Temple of Peace as ensigns of his own glory. However, certain items were excluded from this collection; the purple veils of the holy place and the Law were ordered to be kept within the royal palace instead.

## Archaeological Significance
The site of the Temple of Peace has remained a point of interest for historians and archaeologists. A multitude of antiques have been unearthed from the site where the temple once stood.
