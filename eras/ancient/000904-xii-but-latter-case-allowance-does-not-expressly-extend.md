# General Average and Cargo Damage under Rule XII

## Overview of General Average Rules
In maritime law, Rule VIII allows for General Average (G.A.) in instances where cargo sustains damage during discharge, such as when it is lightered to facilitate the removal of a ship from a strand. Similarly, Rule XII provides for the allowance of corresponding damage when cargo is discharged at a port of refuge to enable necessary repairs to be performed on the ship.

## Limitations of Rule XII
While Rule XII allows for damage sustained during the discharge of cargo at a port of refuge, the allowance does not expressly extend to damage that occurs while the goods are stored on land. There is a lack of clarity regarding whether the law requires contribution for losses sustained while landed for repairs, such as loss resulting from fire or theft.

## Special Risks and Compensation
In cases where the landing of cargo was necessitated by a General Average act—for example, the cutting away of masts—it appears that the resulting loss should be made good. This is because the goods were exposed to special risks due to the act. When estimating the amount of damage to be compensated, the risks the goods would have encountered had they remained on board throughout the voyage are taken into account.

## Case Study: Livestock in Brazil
The application of General Average was demonstrated in the case of *Anglo-Argentine &c. Agency v. Temperley Shipping Co.* (1899, 2 Q.B. 403). In this instance, cattle were taken into a port of refuge in Brazil following accidental damage to the ship. Because of this, the cattle could not be legally landed at their original destination of Deptford and had to be transported to another port, Antwerp. Upon arrival in Antwerp, the cattle were of significantly less value; this loss of value was allowed in General Average.
