# From the Peace of Nikias to the Olympic Festival of Olympiad 90

## The Peace of Nikias and Initial Conditions
The Peace of Nikias was concluded in March 421 B.C. between Athens and the Spartan confederacy, intended to last for fifty years. A primary and leading clause of this peace, as well as the preceding one-year truce, expressly stipulated that all parties had the liberty to attend common festivals and temples. This general provision embraced both Delphi and Olympia. The importance of these stipulations is evidenced by their placement at the front of the treaty, as Athens and its allies had been excluded from sending a solemn legation (theôry) or attending as spectators at the Olympic games for the previous eleven years, including two Olympic festivals.

## Diplomatic Tensions and Alliances
While the majority of the Peloponnesian alliance accepted the peace at Sparta, several powerful members refused, including the Corinthians, Megarians, Bœotians, and Eleians. The Lacedæmonians expressed great anxiety for peace and took steps to execute it, though Amphipolis was not restored to Athens. A separate alliance for mutual defense was concluded between Sparta and Athens, under which Athens restored Spartan captives. However, the Athenian peace party, led by Nikias, was accused of mismanaging political interests; the terms of the alliance resulted in Athens renouncing positional advantages without gaining the calculated concessions, while the Lacedæmonians gained materially.

This led to Athenian discontent and repentance over the release of captives. Simultaneously, new combinations formed in the Peloponnesus. The Corinthians persuaded Argos to lead a new Peloponnesian alliance. A congress of recusant allies met at Corinth, where the Mantineians joined Argos. While the Bœotians and Megarians refused to break with Sparta, the Eleians and Corinthians eventually allied with Argos. Tegea, however, refused to separate from Sparta.

## The Olympic Festival of 420 B.C.
The Olympic festival of the 90th Olympiad occurred in 420 B.C., around the beginning of July. This festival was particularly memorable as the first celebrated since the conclusion of the peace. The return of the Eleian heralds to Attica to announce the games and the subsequent Athenian visit were viewed as novelties. There were rumors that the war had impoverished Athens to the point that it could not appear with appropriate magnificence before Olympic Zeus.

During this period, Alkibiadês contended in the festival with seven chariots. While some historians, such as Dr. Thirlwall, suggest there was only one chariot race and that Lichas won the prize—implying Alkibiadês did not—these propositions are viewed by others as improbable and unfounded.

## The Administration of Olympia
The superintendence of the Olympic games remained a point of contention. The Pisatans, inhabitants of the district surrounding Olympia, petitioned the Lacedæmonians during a Spartan invasion of Elis to be restored as the administrators of the temple of Zeus. They claimed the Eleians were usurpers of a privilege that had belonged to the Pisatans in early days. The Lacedæmonians dismissed these claims, reasoning that the Pisatans were mere villagers without a considerable city and were incompetent to maintain the dignity of a festival that had acquired such Pan-Hellenic importance.

## The Alliance of the Four Cities
Shortly before the 420 B.C. festival, possibly in May, an alliance was concluded between Athens, Argos, Elis, and Mantineia. The terms required Athens to swear oaths on behalf of herself and her allies, while Argos, Elis, and Mantineia swore by their separate cities. These oaths were to be renewed every four years: Athens would swear at the other three cities within thirty days before each Olympic festival, and the three cities would swear at Athens ten days before the festival of the greater Panathenæa. 

To formalize the agreement, the words of the treaty and the oaths were to be engraved on stone columns in the temples of the four cities and on a brazen column at Olympia, funded jointly. The treaty allowed for changes to its provisions provided there was joint consent among the four cities.

## Related Religious Observances
Prior to these events, the Athenians had been induced by an oracle to purify the sacred island of Delos to propitiate Apollo, believing a terrible epidemic was caused by his wrath. This purification involved exhuming all dead bodies from the island and reinterring them on the neighboring island of Rheneia, with a decree that no future births or deaths occur on Delos. The Athenians also renewed the ancient Delian festival, which had once been a meeting point for the Ionic race. They celebrated it with matches and chariot races of unprecedented splendor, appointing the festival to be held every fourth year. This revival was particularly gratifying as the Athenians had been excluded from the Pythian and Olympic games at that time.
