### Byelostok
Also known as Bialystok, this town is located in West Russia, 53 miles by rail southwest of Grodno. It sits on the main railway line from Moscow to Warsaw and the Kiev-Grayevo line. Founded in 1320, it was part of Prussia after the third partition of Poland before being annexed to Russia in 1807 following the peace of Tilsit. The town's development accelerated after 1845 with the construction of woollen-mills. Its population grew from 13,787 in 1857 to 56,629 in 1889, and finally to 65,781 in 1901, with three-fourths of the population being Jews. The local economy is supported by factories producing felt hats, silk, and woollen goods.

### Byezhetsk
Located in the government of Tver, Byezhetsk is 70 miles north-northeast of the city of Tver on the right bank of the Mologa (57° 46' N. lat. and 36° 43' E. long.). Mentioned in chronicles from 1137, it was incorporated into the grand-duchy of Moscow in 1479 after the fall of Novgorod. The town is noted for producing shearing hooks and scythes, as well as nails, axes, and other hardware. It also engages in the trade of flax, hemp, linen, and grain. Its population was 5,423 in 1860 and 9,090 in 1897.
