# AUXENTIUS

Auxentius was a theologian of Cappadocia who flourished around 370 A.D. He was an Arian of significant eminence and became a central figure in the theological conflicts of the West during the 4th century.

## Episcopal Career in Milan
Auxentius rose to prominence when Constantine deposed orthodox bishops who had resisted his authority. Following these depositions, Auxentius was installed into the seat of Dionysius, the bishop of Milan. In this capacity, he came to be viewed as the primary opponent of the Nicene doctrine within the Western region.

His influence and prominence were such that he was specifically named in a condemnatory decree issued by a synod in A.D. 369. This synod had been convened by Damasus, the bishop of Rome, who was urged by Athanasius to hold the assembly in defense of the Nicene doctrine.

## Theological Opposition and Tenure
Despite the opposition from the Nicene party, Auxentius maintained his position for a considerable period. When the orthodox emperor Valentinian ascended the throne, Auxentius was not removed from his diocese and was left undisturbed. However, his theological doctrines were subjected to public attacks by Hilary of Poitiers. The primary historical source regarding these disputes is the *Liber contra Auxentium*, found in the Benedictine edition of the works of Hilary.

Later efforts to eliminate the champions of the council of Rimini from Italy and Illyria were led by Bishop Damasus. Despite a declaration made at a council convened by Damasus in 372, the bishop was unsuccessful in his attempts to evict Auxentius from Milan.

## Death and Succession
Auxentius died shortly after the failed attempts by Damasus to remove him from his see. Following his death, he was succeeded by Ambrose. Ambrose subsequently undertook the task of bringing the previously abortive efforts to remove Arian influence to a successful conclusion, working to complete the return of Illyria to the confessions of Nicaea.
