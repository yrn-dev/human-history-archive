# The Pompeii Diptychs of 1875

## Discovery and Physical Composition
In 1875, several diptychs were discovered at Pompeii. These objects consisted of wooden leaves that had been hollowed out on their inner sides and filled with blackened wax. To allow the leaves to open and close, they were hinged together at the back using leather thongs.

## Use and Significance
The diptychs served as writing surfaces where text was scratched directly into the wax. One specific example found among these artifacts contains a record of a payment made to Umbricia Januaria in A.D. 55. Because of this, the Pompeii diptychs are recognized as the earliest known Latin manuscripts. In the evolution of written records, these diptychs are considered the prototypes of the modern book.

## Evolution of the Diptych
Following the early wooden forms, the diptych evolved into a more ornamental object. From approximately the 1st century to the 6th century, diptychs were crafted from carved ivory. These luxury versions were used by Roman consuls as gifts for great personages. Later, the term was also applied to ecclesiastical records; for instance, a church diptych served as a record of bishops who had passed to heaven, functioning as an equivalent to the book of life.

## Context of Writing Materials
The diptychs represent one stage in the history of protective covers for written matter, which generally followed the shape of the material being protected. Other early methods of recording and preserving information included:
* **Assyrian Tablets:** Dating to about the 8th century B.C., these cuneiform tablets recording loans, slave sales, and general matters were often enclosed in outer shells of the same shape with short titles.
* **Egyptian Papyrus:** These rolls were typically bound with papyrus tape, often sealed with Nile mud, and stored in rectangular hollows carved from wood.
* **Tree Bark:** Used by uncultured nations for medical receipts or magical formulae, bark books were generally rolls folded backwards and forwards, similar to a concertina.

## Related Architectural Findings at Pompeii
The concept of hinged leaves found in the diptychs is mirrored in the architectural remains of Pompeii. Evidence of hinges connecting the folding leaves of doors has been found in the city. Additionally, a painting in the portico of Eumachia depicts a door with three leaves, where the two outer leaves were likely hung and the inner leaf folded onto one of the others.
