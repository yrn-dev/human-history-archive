# Numismatic and Antiquarian Collections

The study and organization of coins, inscriptions, and ancient relics have been central to the work of various antiquarians, historians, and institutions across Europe and Asia. These efforts range from the cataloging of imperial collections in Rome and Athens to the discovery of regional coinage in the Volga region.

## Antiquarian Work in Rome
Bartolommeo Borghesi (1781–1860) was an Italian antiquarian who studied in Rome and Bologna. After weakening his eyesight through the study of medieval documents, he shifted his focus to numismatics and epigraphy. While in Rome, Borghesi was commissioned by Pius VII to arrange and catalogue several coin collections, including those of the Vatican. His scholarly contributions included the work *Nuovi Frammenti dei Fasti Consolari Capitolini* (1818–1820), which provided positive bases for the chronology of Roman history. Before his death, Borghesi planned a collection of all Latin inscriptions from the Roman world; this design was later realized by the Academy of Berlin under Mommsen as the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*.

## Museums and Collections in Greece
The museums of Athens have expanded through continuous excavation and are noted for their arrangement and preservation of ancient art. The National Museum, established in 1866, contains a variety of items, including prehistoric antiquities found by Schliemann at Mycenae and Tiryns, as well as bronzes from Delphi and Olympia. The Academy in Athens houses a valuable collection of coins, which is superintended by Svoronos. Additionally, the Epigraphical Museum maintains a large number of inscriptions, which were arranged by A. Wilhelm and H.G. Lolling of the Austrian Institute.

## European Private and Institutional Collections
Various other collections of coins and historical relics exist across Europe:
* **Austria:** In the first part of the 19th century, the Austrian statesman Metternich created a museum at his castle near Königswart. This museum contains a collection of rare coins, alongside armour, portraits, minerals, and a library.
* **Denmark:** Peter Oluf Bröndsted (1780–1842), a Danish archaeologist, served as the director of the collection of coins and medals as well as the museum of antiquities.
* **England:** The Bodleian Library in Oxford preserves the autograph note-book of Bale in the Selden Collection, which contains materials for two published catalogues.

## Regional Finds and Specialized Catalogues
Numismatic evidence has also been used to identify historical sites and figures. In the town of Ukek (or Ucaca) on the right bank of the Volga, coins with Mongol-Arab inscriptions struck by Tuktugai Khan in 1306 have been described by Erdmann and Fraehn. In the 16th century, Christopher Burrough noted the presence of old tombs with inscriptions and sculptures in the area, though these have since vanished.

In the study of Roman history, specialized catalogues have been used to verify the existence of historical figures. For example, Captain Smyth's "Catalogue of Medals" provides a list of nineteen figures, categorizing them by whether their coins are undoubtedly true, suspected, or unknown. This includes figures such as Posthumus, Tetricus, and Victorinus.
