# Crito

The term "Crito" appears in the archive's records in three distinct contexts: as a dialogue attributed to Plato, as a pseudonym used in theological scholarship, and as a reference to a specific individual in the context of Cambridge.

## The Dialogue of Plato
In the dialogue *Crito*, Socrates presents an argument regarding his escape from the city. He represents the laws of the city as pleading with him, asserting that because he was educated, nurtured, and begotten under them, he and his ancestors are the offspring and servants of the state. This political theory posits that the state antedates the individual, who is born into it as a part and parcel of the whole; consequently, the individual must live and die for the state or its legitimate authority. Loyalty is presented as the ethical outcome of this theory.

The authenticity of *Crito* has been a subject of critical inquiry. The German philosopher and philologist Georg Anton Friedrich Ast (1778–1841), who conducted extensive research on Plato's dialogues, denounced *Crito* as spurious. Ast distrusted tradition and used internal evidence from a few selected dialogues as his standard to determine authenticity.
