## Ancient Governance and Military History

In the study of early Athenian government, the period following the discontinuance of the king and the adoption of annual archons is described as an imperfectly known period. During this time, the primary political authorities were the nine annual archons—selected from the eupatridæ—and the senate of areopagus. The senate of areopagus is considered a primordial institution of immemorial antiquity, though its specific functions and constitution changed over time. It is believed to represent the ancient Homeric council of old men.

Similarly, military history often suffers from gaps in the record. In Britain, the military history between A.D. 85 and 115 is characterized as a "blank." While it is known that the northern Britons rose in revolt around 115 or 120 and destroyed the Ninth Legion at York, the specific frontier adopted after the departure of Agricola remains unknown. Later, in 122, Emperor Hadrian introduced a new frontier policy, constructing a continuous rampart from Wallsend to Bowness (over 70 miles from Tyne to Solway). Although inscriptions and literature prove the wall's existence, the specific details of Hadrian's work are imperfectly known because it is difficult to distinguish his original remains from later additions.

In Greek history, the battle of Mantinea is another example of a poorly documented event. The historian Polybius did not provide his own description of the battle, leaving its details imperfectly known. Modern readers must rely on accounts from Diodorus, which may have been borrowed from the narratives of Ephorus.

## Archaeological and Geological Discoveries

The field of Assyrian art was significantly expanded through the work of Mr. Layard, whose publications "Nineveh and its Remains" and "The Monuments of Nineveh" brought sculptures and monuments to the British Museum. Similar relics were deposited in the Louvre by M. Botta. Despite these acquisitions, the ground from which these items were recovered has been most imperfectly examined, suggesting that further investigation could yield a more ample harvest of information. These sculptures, which display a style different from early Egypt, are dated to the eighth or seventh century B.C. at the latest.

In geology, the Miocene formation in Greenland is a well-known feature, particularly on Disco Island and parts of the Noursoak Peninsula. This formation consists of coal, shale, and sandstone containing plant and insect remains. However, these Miocene beds are as yet only imperfectly distinguished from the Cretaceous series.

## Biological and Anatomical Studies

The archive's records on anatomy include the thymus gland, a light pink gland located in the thorax. While its general structure—consisting of two unequal lobes divided into lobules—is understood, the cortex is noted to be imperfectly divided into a number of follicles. The gland typically grows until the second year of life, remains stationary until puberty, and then degenerates, though rare cases exist where it remains well developed in adults between 40 and 50.

In the field of ichthyology, the knowledge of fish fauna has evolved unevenly. For example, the fishes of North America were imperfectly known in 1880. This gap was later addressed by D. S. Jordan and B. W. Evermann through a four-volume "Descriptive Catalogue," which replaced a 1882 synopsis.

## Textual and Critical Analysis

The collation of ancient manuscripts also reveals varying levels of completeness. While Hebrew manuscripts were collated with thoroughness by de Rossi and Kennicott at the end of the 18th century, the Samaritan manuscripts remain very imperfectly collated. A similar lack of complete collation exists for Syriac and other versions, with the exception of the Septuagint, where significant though incomplete work has been performed.
