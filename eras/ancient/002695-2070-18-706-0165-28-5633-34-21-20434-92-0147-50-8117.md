## Baconian Philosophy
The archive documents the philosophy of Francis Bacon, focusing on the relationship between human power and human knowledge. According to these texts, the aim of human power is to generate or superinduce new natures on a given body. Conversely, the aim of human knowledge is to discover the "nature-engendering nature" (_natura naturans_), the source of emanation, or the true specific difference and form of a given nature.

Bacon describes a "form" as a mechanism where, if the form is provided, the nature infallibly follows; if the form is removed, the nature infallibly vanishes. Furthermore, a true form deduces a given nature from a source of being that is inherent in more natures and is better known in the natural order than the form itself.

## Ancient Greek History and Philosophy
The records include the activities of Xenophanês of Kolophon, a philosopher active approximately between 570 and 480 B.C. Xenophanês migrated to Elea in Italy and Zanklê and Katana in Sicily after Ionia became subject to the Persians between 540 and 530 B.C. He founded the Eleatic school of philosophers, whose speculations were later developed by Melissus, Zeno, and Parmenidês. Xenophanês authored poems on the foundations of Elea and Kolophon, as well as a poem on Nature. He was known for a controversial manner and for denouncing the descriptions of gods provided by Hesiod and Homer.

Despite a sudden sally by the garrison from the acropolis of Mendê into Skiônê, Nikias successfully enclosed the town.

## The History of Baracoa
Baracoa is the oldest town in Cuba, settled in 1512 by Diego Velazquez. It served as the island's capital from 1512 to 1514 and its first diocese's cathedral seat from 1518 to 1522, before these honors were transferred to Santiago de Cuba. For the following two centuries, Baracoa was an obscure village with limited commerce, frequently plundered by pirates. To survive, the town eventually provided harbor to pirates and engaged in illicit trade.

By the middle of the 18th century, Baracoa remained largely disconnected from Santiago and Havana. Toward the end of the century, it served as a deposit for Spanish and French corsairs and saw an increase in industrial importance due to the arrival of approximately 100 immigrant families from Santo Domingo. In 1807, an English force launched an unsuccessful attack on the city, and the port was eventually opened to foreign commerce in 1826. As of 1907, the population was 5633. The city is a trading center for cocoanut and banana exports and is dominated by the "Anvil of Baracoa," a regular hill formation reaching about 3000 feet.
