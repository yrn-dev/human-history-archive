# Decline of Buddhism in India

## Causes of Decline
It was previously supposed, based on late priestly texts containing boasts of persecution, that the decline of Buddhism in India was caused by Brahmin persecution. However, older authorities—with one doubtful exception—do not mention such persecution. 

Evidence from a comparison between the canonical books of older Buddhism and later texts from subsequent centuries reveals a steady decline from the original standpoint. Buddhist views began to approximate the religions and philosophies of other Indian traditions. This process of approximation led to a "day of compromise," where the margin of difference between Buddhists and their opponents gradually faded.

## Impact of Imperial Support
The conversion and strenuous support of Asoka in the 3rd century B.C., the most powerful ruler India had known, appeared to the world as proof of the movement's success. In reality, this event hastened its decline. The movement for reform was weakened by the adhesion of large numbers of nominal converts, particularly those from less advanced and newly incorporated provinces. These half-converted followers welcomed and supported any relaxation of the old, thoroughgoing Buddhist positions.

## Philosophical and Ritual Shifts
As the movement declined, several core tenets and ideals were abandoned or altered:
*   **Soul Theory:** The soul theory gradually regained the upper hand.
*   **Popular Beliefs:** Buddhists began to favor popular superstitions and popular gods once again.
*   **Ethics:** New speculations began to overshadow the philosophical basis of the old ethics.
*   **The Ideal of Life:** The old ideal—the salvation of the Arahat achieved solely in this world through self-mastery and self-culture—was either forgotten or mentioned only to be condemned.

## Final Dissolution
Because the distinctiveness of the faith faded, the need for a separate organization became increasingly less apparent. While the ceremonies, sacrifices, and the entire pantheon of Vedic gods did pass away, the party of reform known as ancient Buddhism was also overwhelmed. Modern Hinduism subsequently arose from the ruins of both.
