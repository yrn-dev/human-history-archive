# Mon Cousin de la main gauche

*Charles Pointel, ou Mon Cousin de la main gauche* is one of the early literary productions of the author Balzac. It is categorized as part of the *incunabula* of his work and is included under the title *Oeuvres de jeunesse*.

## Composition and Publication
The work emerged during a period of Balzac's life following a "garret-period" that lasted from 1820 to 1822. This was succeeded by another period of equal length spent at home. In 1821, before this second period had concluded, Balzac first found his way into print. *Mon Cousin de la main gauche* was produced during this time alongside another early work titled *Les Deux Hector, ou Les Deux Families bretonnes*.

In the year following the publication of these initial works, Balzac produced six additional titles:
* *L'Heritiere de Birague*
* *Jean Louis, ou La Fille trouvee*
* *Clotilde de Lusignan, ou Le Beau Juif*
* *Le Centenaire, ou Les Deux Beringheld*
* *Le Vicaire des Ardennes*
* *Le Tartare, ou Le Retour de l'exile*

These were further followed in 1823 by three more works: *La Derniere Fee, ou La Nouvelle Lampe merveilleuse*; *Michel et Christine et la suite*; and *L'Anonyme, ou Ni pere ni mere*.

## Publication Details and Style
The early novels of Balzac, including *Mon Cousin de la main gauche*, originally filled approximately two score volumes. They were not published under the author's own name but were released under various pseudonyms, such as "Horace de Saint Aubin" and "Lord R'hoone" (the latter being an anagram of "Honore"). Some of these works were created in actual collaboration with two or three other writers.

Critically, these early works are described as containing only the "faintest dawn" of the true Balzac. None of the novels are considered good as a whole, and very few individual parts are deemed deserving of that word, even with significant qualification. Despite this, they are noted as being essential for study, as it is difficult to apprehend the true Balzac without having studied these early efforts.

## Cessation of Early Writing
The production of these types of novels eventually ceased for a time. This was not due to the author's own conviction regarding their poor quality—although he did not hold any serious delusions about the subject—nor was it because the works failed to generate a certain amount of money. Instead, the cessation occurred because Balzac entered into business ventures. These ventures, described as the most prolonged and disastrous of his "dabblings," involved him acting as a printer, a type-founder, and to some extent, a publisher. These experiences resulted in a general failure and left him burdened with a load of debt.
