# The Babil Mound

The Babil mound is one of the three vast mounds located on the eastern bank of the Euphrates river at the site of the ancient city of Babylon. Situated at the northern extremity of the city walls, the mound is enclosed on its northern and eastern sides by one of three lines of rampart.

## Architectural Significance and the Palace of Nebuchadrezzar
The Babil mound probably represents the site of a palace constructed by Nebuchadrezzar. This structure was attached to a defensive outwork measuring 60 cubits in length. 

The broader topography of the area is primarily that of the Babylon of Nebuchadrezzar, as the older version of the city was destroyed by Sennacherib and has left few or no traces. In addition to the Babil mound, the site features the Qasr (or Mujelliba) mound in the center—which contains the old palace of Nabopolassar and a new palace built by Nebuchadrezzar—and the Ish[=a]n 'Amran ibn 'Ali with the Jumjuma spur to the south.

## The Hanging Gardens
Due to the discovery of remains of irrigation works by H. Rassam, it is suggested that the Babil mound may be the site of the Hanging Gardens. According to available accounts, these gardens consisted of trees and flowers planted atop a series of arches reaching approximately 75 feet in height. The gardens were square in shape, with each side measuring 400 Greek feet. To maintain the vegetation, water was raised from the Euphrates river using a screw.

## City Walls and Decorations
The city of Babylon was characterized by its great walls, towers, and palaces, constructed from bricks. The Babylonians were highly proficient in brickmaking, utilizing well-burnt bricks more than 6,000 years ago and employing colored glaze or enamel for wall decorations.

Nebuchadrezzar specifically recorded that he covered the walls of some of the city and E-Saggila gates with blue enamelled tiles featuring portrayals of dragons and bulls. Furthermore, he placed large serpents and bulls made of bronze on the thresholds of these gates.

## Topographical Context
The understanding of the site's topography is derived from the inscriptions of Nebuchadrezzar, classical writers, and excavations conducted by the *Deutsche Orientgesellschaft* starting in 1899. Other notable features of the city include:
* **The Gate of Ishtar:** Located on the east side of the Qasr mound.
* **A-ibur-sabu:** A raised road for solemn processions paved with stone and lined with enamelled tiles depicting a procession of lions, leading from the Gate of Ishtar to E-Saggila.
* **Waterways:** The Arakhtu (the "river of Babylon") with brick quays built by Nabopolassar lay to the south of the Qasr mound, while a canal, likely the Libilkhegal, was located to the north.
