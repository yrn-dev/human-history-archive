# Southern Italy

## Cultural and Linguistic Characteristics
Southern Italy is characterized as one of the "happier climes," sharing a cultural tone and a "simple delight of living" with similar regions such as France and Greece. This is evidenced by popular verse and snatches of poetry that focus on themes of nature, specifically apple blossom, rose trees, and the nightingale singing for loverless maidens.

Linguistically, the early population of southern Italy reflects a complex history of migration and interaction. Comparison between the Greek, Latin, and Oscan languages suggests they are sisters within the same Indo-European family. There is a recognized "Pelasgic" affinity connecting the Latins, Œnotrians, and Sikels. Evidence suggests that the Sikels were once spread across a larger portion of southern Italy before migrating into Sicily due to invasions by the Oscan or Ausonian tribes, who are viewed as intruders from the central mountains.

## Geography and Climate
The climate of southern Europe, which includes southern Italy, is described as having hot summers and winters that are cold everywhere. This climate is noted as being analogous to that of China. Furthermore, the region's "happier climate" produced rich fruits and delicious wines, which were highly envied by outside groups, such as the Germans.

## Ancient Legends and Early History
The southern region of Italy is central to the legends of Æneas. According to various irreconcilable tales, Æneas visited the Salentine peninsula and several other locations in southern Italy during his wanderings from Troy to Latium. These visits were often certified by local legends, permanent ceremonies, and temples dedicated to his mother, Aphroditê, as well as various tombs attributed to Æneas himself.

In later periods, the region was subject to the ambitions of various leaders. Dionysius attained significant military celebrity, becoming the master of much of southern Italy and most of Sicily. He maintained the most powerful standing army in Greece and was known as an unscrupulous plunderer of holy temples, though he was also a recognized tragic poet whose work gained prizes at the Lenæan festival in Athens between 368-367 B.C.

## Roman Expansion and Conflict
The "peace of Italy" following certain conflicts was marked by instability. The Gauls rose and marched south, though they were eventually defeated with 40,000 killed at Telamon. During this period of threat, Roman society experienced moral and intellectual degradation, leading to the proposal and execution of human sacrifices.

To secure the region, Rome planted colonies in the valley of the Po and began the construction of the Via Flaminia, a great northward artery. This expansion indicated that Italy was considered incomplete until it reached the Alps. Additionally, Roman expeditions to annex Sardinia and Corsica—Carthaginian provinces in revolt—served as a precursor to the Second Punic War.

## Religious Assimilation
In the era of great Greek-speaking empires under autocratic monarchs, there was a trend toward the assimilation of deities. As men required gods with an outlook as wide as their empires, they began to identify various local and tribal deities as different names for a single god. Under this process of identification, the Roman Jupiter was seen as similar to the Greek Zeus, the Babylonian Bel-Marduk, and the Egyptian Ammon.
