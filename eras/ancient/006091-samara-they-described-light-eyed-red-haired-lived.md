# Samara

The region of Samara is associated in historical records with several distinct groups of people, including the ancient Budini and the Bashkirs.

## The Budini
The Budini were an ancient nation located in the northeast of the Scythia described by Herodotus. They were likely situated on the middle course of the Volga River in the vicinity of Samara. Physical descriptions of the Budini characterize them as being red-haired and light-eyed. Their lifestyle was centered on hunting within the thick forests of their territory.

It is suggested that the Budini were Finns, specifically of the branch that is now represented by the Permiaks and Votiaks, who were pushed northwards by later immigrants. Within the land of the Budini existed a wooden city. This city was inhabited by a separate race known as the Geloni, who appear to have spoken an Indo-European tongue.

Regarding the natural history of the Budini's forest lands, later writers such as Aelian (citing Aristotle) mention the tarandus. This animal, which lived in the woods of the Budini, is believed to have been the reindeer.

## The Bashkirs
The Bashkirs are a people who inhabit several Russian governments, including Samara, Perm, Orenburg, and Ufa, as well as parts of Vyatka. Their territory is primarily located in the neighbouring plains and on the confines and slopes of the Ural.

The Bashkirs speak a Tatar language. However, some authorities propose that they are ethnically a Finnish tribe that was transformed through Tatar influence. The name "Bashkir" or "Bash-kurt" first appears in the writings of Ibn-Foslan at the beginning of the 10th century. In his accounts of travels among the Volga-Bulgarians, Ibn-Foslan describes the Bashkirs as an idolatrous and warlike race. It is noted that during the 10th century, the people did not use the name Bashkir themselves; rather, it served as a nickname.
