# Gotama (The Buddha)

## Early Life and Family
Gotama, known individually as Siddhattha, was born in a garden or grove called Lumbini, located in the Sakiya land. According to ancient accounts, his mother was traveling from Kapilavastu, the capital of the Sakiyas, to her own mother's home in Devadaha, the capital of the Koliyas, when she gave birth to him in the grove. 

At the age of nineteen, Siddhattha married his cousin Yasodhara, who was the daughter of a Koliyan chief. Following his marriage, he devoted himself to a life of luxury. He eventually had one child, a son, whose birth was celebrated by the people of Kapilavastu, as he was the only grandson of the raja.

## Spiritual Awakening and Renunciation
In his twenty-ninth year, Siddhattha experienced a series of visions while driving to his pleasure-grounds. He encountered a man broken down by age, a man suffering from a loathsome disease, and a decomposing corpse. His charioteer, Channa, informed him that these sights represented the fate of all living beings. Subsequently, he saw an ascetic walking in a dignified manner and learned from Channa about the "Wanderers," who were traveling teachers.

These experiences stirred Siddhattha's mind, leading him to view earthly gains as vanity and to long for a life of meditation and abstinence. One evening, after witnessing dancing-girls perform Natch dances, he was filled with an overpowering loathing. At midnight, he awoke with a resolved mind, instructed his charioteer Channa to saddle his horse, and left his home.

## Enlightenment and Early Ministry
Following a great struggle under the Bo tree, Gotama entered a new phase of his career. He traveled to Benares and visited the Deer-forest, where five ascetics resided. Although the ascetics initially refused to recognize him as a superior because he had broken his vows, Gotama informed them that he had found the "ambrosia of truth." He explained the principles of his gospel to them through the Noble Eightfold Path and the noble truths.

## Mission and Itinerant Preaching
For forty-five years, Gotama traveled as an itinerant preacher in the valley of the Ganges, generally remaining within 250 meters of Benares. He established a pattern of spending the rainy months at a single location, typically in homes or *viharas* given to his society. After each rainy season, he would gather his disciples and instruct them to wander out of compassion for the world to preach the doctrine and proclaim a "pure and perfect life." He commanded that no two disciples travel together.

## Disciples and Opposition
In the twentieth year of his mission, Gotama's cousin Ananda became a mendicant and served as a devoted attendant to the Buddha. Another cousin, Devadatta, the son of the raja of Koli, also joined the society. However, Devadatta became envious of Gotama.

Devadatta stirred up Ajatasattu, the king of Rajagaha who had ascended to the throne after killing his father, Bimbisara. Ajatasattu sought to persecute Gotama. While the specific accounts of how the Buddha overcame the devices of Devadatta and Ajatasattu are considered legendary, the archive notes the general fact of Ajatasattu's initial opposition and his subsequent conversion.
