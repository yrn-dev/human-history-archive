# Brigantia and the Brigantes

## The Brigantes People
The Brigantes were a people of northern Britain. The name "Brigantes" is Celtic, meaning either "privileged, free" or "mountaineers." Their territory spanned a significant portion of northern Britain, extending from the mouth of the Abus (Humber) in the east to the Belisama (identified as either the Ribble or the Mersey) in the west. To the north, their lands reached as far as the Wall of Antoninus. This region encompassed the whole of Lancashire, Durham, Westmorland, and Cumberland, as well as most of Yorkshire and a portion of Northumberland. The chief town of the Brigantes was Eburacum, also known as York.

## Roman Contact and Conquest
The Brigantes first encountered the Romans during the reign of Claudius, at which time they were defeated by Publius Ostorius Scapula. Later, during the reign of Vespasian, the people submitted to Petillius Cerealis. However, they were not finally subdued until the time of Antoninus Pius.

## Religious Figures and Inscriptions
The Brigantes had an eponymous goddess named Brigantia, whose name has been recorded on various inscriptions. Additionally, records mention a god named Brigans (or Bergans).

## Expansion and Related Terms
A branch of the Brigantes settled in the south-east corner of Ireland, specifically near the river Birgus (Barrow). Separately, the French word "brigandine" refers to armor used by light-armed foot soldiers known as *brigantes* or *brigandi* during the middle ages. This armor consisted of a padded tunic made of leather or canvas, lined with rings of iron or closely sewn scales.
