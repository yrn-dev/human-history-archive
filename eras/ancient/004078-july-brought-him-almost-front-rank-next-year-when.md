# Military Formations and Command Structures in Classical and Modern History

The organization of troops, the arrangement of ranks, and the hierarchy of command have been central to military operations across various eras, from the ancient conflicts of Greece and Rome to the revolutionary and colonial wars of France.

## Ancient Greek and Persian Formations

In the Peloponnesian War, the Lacedaemonians maintained a strict legal hierarchy of command when in the field. All orders originated from the king, who issued commands to the Polemarchs. These were then passed to the Lochages, then to the Pentecostyes, then to the Enomotarchs, and finally to the Enomoties. This structure ensured that orders reached the troops quickly, as nearly the entire army—except for a small portion—consisted of officers under officers.

During the battle at Plataia, Mardonios organized the Barbarian forces against the Hellenes with specific strategic placements. The Persians, being superior in number, were arrayed in deeper ranks than the Lacedaemonians; Mardonios placed the strongest part of the Persian body opposite the Lacedaemonians and the weaker part opposite the Tegeans. Following the Persians, he ranged the Medes (opposite the Corinthians, Potidaians, Orchomenians, and Sikyonians), the Bactrians (opposite the Epidaurians, Troizenians, Lepreates, Tirynthians, Mykenians, and Phliasians), and the Indians (opposite the Hermionians, Eretrians, Styrians, and Chalkidians). The Sacans were placed in front of the men of Amprakia, the Anactorians, Leucadians, Palians, and Eginetans. Finally, the Boeotians, Locrians, Malians, Thessalians, and a thousand Phokians were ranged opposite the Athenians, Plataians, and Megarians, while the Macedonians and those from the borders of Thessaly faced the Athenians.

In other Athenian operations, such as those led by Nicias and Demosthenes, the army marched in a hollow square. The division under Nicias led, followed by Demosthenes' division. The heavy infantry occupied the outside of the square, while the bulk of the army and baggage-carriers were positioned in the middle.

## Roman and Carthaginian Tactics

In Roman military history, the role of the "front rank" was often a point of critical engagement. During a battle involving Hannibal, the Carthaginian leader ordered his elephants into the front rank to attack Roman lines. This caused significant disturbance until a tribune named Flavius used a spiked staff to force the elephants back. This allowed the Roman cavalry, under the orders of Marcellus, to rout the Carthaginians, resulting in over eight thousand enemy deaths.

Individual bravery within the ranks was also noted in Athenian service. During the campaign of Potidaea, Sokrates and Alkibiades served together, with Alkibiades taking his place next to Sokrates in the ranks. During an engagement, Sokrates stood in front of the wounded Alkibiades to defend him and save his life and arms.

## Roman Political and Military Ascent

The career of Gaius Marius (155-86 B.C.) illustrates the transition from the ranks to high command. Born the son of a small farmer of plebeian descent, Marius first served in Spain under Scipio Africanus, rising from the ranks to become an officer. His military energy and popularity eventually led to his election as praetor in 115, where he subjugated Further Spain. In the war with Jugurtha (109-106), he first gained prominence as a lieutenant to the consul Quintus Caecilius Metellus Numidicus. By 107, he was elected consul for the first time, a rare honor for a "new man." He later annexed and subdued the province of Numidia.

## French Revolutionary and Imperial Command

The French Republic and Empire saw various shifts in command and political control. In 1795, Barras was appointed to command troops defending the Convention against the National Guards of Paris. He nominated Bonaparte as a subaltern, leading to the dispersion of royalists near the Tuileries on 13 Vendemiaire. Barras later became one of the five Directors of the French republic and facilitated the marriage of Josephine de Beauharnais to Bonaparte. Eventually, Bonaparte overthrew the Directory via the *coup d'etat* of 18-19 Brumaire, ending Barras' political career.

Other French figures rose through the ranks of the army. Jean Baptiste Drouet, Count d'Erlon (1765-1844), entered the army as a private in 1782 and rose to become an officer. He served as a general of brigade in 1799 and later as a general of division in Napoleon's 1805 and 1806 campaigns. He later commanded an army corps in the Peninsular War and the I. army corps in the Army of the North during the Waterloo campaign.

In Algeria, Bugeaud initiated a "war of flying columns," winning his first victory on July 7, 1836. He eventually became governor-general of Algeria in 1840 and a marshal of France in 1843. His tenure was marked by the construction of roads to secure French positions and an increase in French colonists from 17,000 to 100,000.
