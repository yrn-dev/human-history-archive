# Ahab of Israel

Ahab was a king of Israel and a member of the dynasty founded by Omri. His reign was characterized by ongoing hostilities with Syria, strategic alliances, and eventual conflict with the Assyrian Empire.

## Relations with Syria and Assyria
Hostilities between Israel and Syria persisted into the days of Ahab. Previously, the king of Syria had taken cities from Omri and secured the right to establish a merchant quarter in Samaria. Ben-Hadad, the son of the Syrian king, launched an unsuccessful attack on Israel at Aphek; Ahab subsequently allowed him to depart upon the reversal of those terms, an action that led to a prophetic denunciation.

According to Assyrian records, Ahab acted as an ally to Ben-Hadad at the battle of Karkar in 854 B.C., fighting against Shalmaneser II. This suggests a period of intermediate vassalage for Ahab. In 853 B.C., a confederacy of Syrian princes, led by Ben-Hadad of Damascus and including Ahab, was shattered by the forces of Shalmaneser II. It is inferred that Ahab's death in battle with the Syrians occurred in 853 B.C. during an attempt to throw off this Assyrian vassalage.

## Military Campaign at Ramoth
Ahab joined forces with Jehoshaphat, the king of Jerusalem, for an expedition to Ramoth, a city of Gilead. Upon learning of this, the king of Syria deployed his army to oppose them. To circumvent a prediction made by Micaiah, Ahab and Jehoshaphat employed a stratagem: Ahab set aside his royal robes, and Jehoshaphat wore them instead to stand before the army.

Despite this artifice, Ben-Hadad had instructed his commanders to target only the king of Israel. When the Syrians encountered Jehoshaphat, they initially attacked him believing he was Ahab, but retreated once they realized his true identity. During the battle, which lasted from morning until evening, a nobleman under Ben-Hadad named Naaman shot an arrow that pierced Ahab's breastplate and wounded him in the lungs. To prevent his army from fleeing, Ahab concealed his injury and remained in his chariot until sunset, at which point he fainted and died.

## Domestic Life and Dynasty
Ahab's life is associated with the possession of an ivory house and various cities. He entered into a Phoenician alliance, which resulted in a stern religious judgment. Ahab was married to Jezebel; their daughter, Athaliah, later married Jehoram, the son of Jehoshaphat of Judah.

Ahab's dynasty was eventually overthrown by the usurper Jehu. Ahab was succeeded by his son Ahaziah, followed by his other son, Jehoram (also referred to as Joram). Jehoram's reign involved further conflicts with the Syrians, during which the Syrian general Naaman was cleansed of leprosy by the prophet Elisha.

## Religious and Moral Record
The records of Ahab's reign include narratives involving the prophets Elijah and Elisha. Ahab is described as having slain, or permitted his wife Jezebel to slay, Naboth and the prophets of the Lord. These actions are cited in the context of the later judgment and overthrow of his house.
