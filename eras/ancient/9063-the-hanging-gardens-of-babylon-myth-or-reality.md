# The Hanging Gardens of Babylon: Myth or Reality?

Among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, the Hanging Gardens of Babylon remain the most enigmatic. While the Pyramids of Giza still stand, the Gardens have left no definitive archaeological footprint in Babylon (modern-day Iraq), leading historians to question whether they were a physical masterpiece or a poetic invention.

### Overview
According to classical Greek and Roman historians, such as Berossus and Diodorus Siculus, the gardens were built by King Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BCE) for his wife, Amytis of Media. Amytis reportedly missed the lush, mountainous greenery of her homeland, prompting the King to construct a series of tiered terraces supporting a vast array of exotic flora, irrigated by a sophisticated system of pumps drawing water from the Euphrates.

However, a significant historical problem persists: there are no mentions of these gardens in the extensive cuneiform records of Nebuchadnezzar’s own reign. This silence has led many scholars to conclude that the gardens were a literary trope used by later writers to romanticize the East.

An alternative theory, proposed by Dr. Stephanie Dalley of Oxford University, suggests the gardens existed but were located 300 miles north in Nineveh. Dalley argues that Sennacherib, the Assyrian king, built a similar marvel. This theory is supported by Assyrian reliefs depicting lush gardens and advanced aqueducts, suggesting the "Babylonian" gardens were actually a misattribution by later historians.

### Key Facts
*   **Engineering:** If they existed, the gardens would have required advanced hydraulic engineering to lift water to high terraces, possibly using an early version of the Archimedes screw.
*   **Architectural Style:** They are described as "hanging" not because they suspended from wires, but because of the tiered, terrace-like structure (*zikkurat* style) that created a vertical forest.
*   **The Debate:** The central conflict in historiography is between the **Classical Tradition** (Greek accounts) and the **Archaeological Record** (lack of Babylonian evidence).

### Did You Know?
*   **The Missing Records:** Despite thousands of tablets detailing Nebuchadnezzar's building projects, not one mentions a "hanging garden."
*   **Botanical Feat:** The gardens were said to feature trees and shrubs from across the known world, requiring a complex soil layering system to prevent water from rotting the stone structures below.
*   **Nineveh's Evidence:** Excavations at Nineveh have revealed a massive canal system that could have supported the scale of gardens described in the myths.
