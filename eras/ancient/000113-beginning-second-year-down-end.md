# FROM THE BEGINNING OF THE SECOND YEAR DOWN TO THE END OF THE THIRD YEAR OF THE PELOPONNESIAN WAR

## Overview of the Conflict
At the conclusion of one year following the Thebans' attempted surprise of Platæa, the relative strength of the belligerent parties in Greece remained unchanged. Despite mutual damage—which was most significant in Attica—neither side had achieved a decisive victory. Operations involving the invasion of Attica and flying descents around the Peloponnesus coast failed to advance the objectives that had originally led the Peloponnesians to war.

## Strategic Stagnation and the Blockade of Potidæa
The Peloponnesians' most urgent desire was the relief of Potidæa; however, this goal was not advanced because the Athenians maintained their blockade of the city. The results of the first year of operations served to justify the anticipations of Archidamus and Periklês, while simultaneously disappointing the Corinthians and other ardent instigators of the war.

## Military Expeditions and Chronology
In the subsequent period, Kleon led an expedition to Thrace. This occurred after the Pythian holy truce, beginning in August. Between August and the end of September, the battle of Amphipolis and the capture of Torônê took place. 

Regarding the timing of these events, Dr. Arnold posits that the truce for a year had lasted until the Pythian games and then ended, rather than expiring on the 14th of Elaphebolion. According to this view, the truce was tacitly continued for nearly four months until after midsummer, and Kleon was not sent to recover Amphipolis until the middle of Hekatombæon.
