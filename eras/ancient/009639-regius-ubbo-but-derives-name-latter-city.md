# Hippo Regius and Bona

## Overview and Etymology
Hippo Regius, also known as Ubbo, was a maritime colony and seaport located approximately two hundred miles westward of Carthage. The Romans bestowed the epithet *Regius* (meaning "Royal") upon the city because it served as a favorite residence for the Numidian kings. In modern times, the location is associated with the city of Bona. While Bona is identified with the ancient *Aphrodisium*, the seaport of Hippo Regius or Ubbo, the name Bona derives from the latter city. The Arabic name for Bona, *annaba*, translates to "the city of jujube trees."

## Roman Era and St. Augustine
During the first three centuries of the Christian era, Hippo was among the wealthiest cities in Roman Africa. Its primary historical significance is tied to St. Augustine, who served as the city's priest and bishop for thirty-five years. Augustine is described as a pillar of the Catholic church who maintained a pure and austere life from the moment of his conversion until his death. He was known for his argumentative mind and his perpetual controversies against the Pelagians, the Donatists, and the Manichæans, as he waged a zeal against heretics of every denomination.

Augustine's extensive theological works were preserved in a library that included a complete exposition of the gospel and the psalter, a large collection of homilies and epistles, and two hundred and thirty-two separate treatises on theological subjects.

## Vandal Conquest and Later History
In 431, Hippo was captured by the Vandals under the leadership of Genseric. The city fell after a siege lasting fourteen months, during which time St. Augustine died. While the city was burnt by the Vandals, the cathedral and Augustine's library and manuscripts escaped general destruction. 

The town was later partially restored by Belisarius, but it was sacked again in the 7th century by the Arabs. The Arabs subsequently built the city of Bona, which for several centuries remained a possession of the rulers of Tunis; these rulers constructed the Kasbah in 1300.

## European Influence and Modern Development
From the start of the 14th century to the middle of the 15th century, Bona was frequented by Spaniards and Italians. In the 16th century, Charles V held the city for a period and strengthened its citadel. Following this, the city was held in succession by Tunisians, Genoese, and Algerines. Between the era of Louis XIV and the Revolution, the French *Compagnie d'Afrique* maintained active trade through the port.

The French occupied the town for a few months in 1830 and reoccupied it in 1832. During this second occupation, Captains Yusuf and Armandy, leading a small force of marines, seized and held the Kasbah until reinforcements arrived. Since that period, Bona has experienced industrial development, which was further stimulated in 1883 following the discovery of phosphate beds at Tebessa.

## Archaeological Remains
The ruins of Hippo, located about a mile south of the modern town, consist of fragments of walls and large cisterns that have since been restored. On the hill where Hippo once stood, a large basilica dedicated to St. Augustine—featuring a chancel facing west—was opened in 1900. An altar topped with a bronze statue of the saint has also been erected among the ruins.
