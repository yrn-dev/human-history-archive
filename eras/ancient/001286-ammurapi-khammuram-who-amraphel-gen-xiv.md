# Khammurabi

## Identification and Nomenclature
Khammurabi was a ruler of Babylonia whose name is also recorded in historical sources as Ammurapi and Khammuram. He is identified as the figure referred to as Amraphel in Genesis xiv. 1.

## Rise to Power and the Elamite Conflict
Khammurabi was the son and successor of Sin-muballidh. During the period preceding his decisive successes, Babylonia had been under foreign influence. The Elamites, led by their king Kudur-Lagamar (also known as Chedor-laomer), appear to have captured Babylon and destroyed the temple of Bel-Merodach. 

Khammurabi eventually retrieved his fortunes. In the thirtieth year of his reign, dated to 2340 B.C., he defeated the Elamite forces in a decisive battle and expelled them from Babylonia.

## Unification of Babylonia
Following the defeat of the Elamites, Khammurabi spent the subsequent two years expanding his dominion by adding Yamutbal and Larsa to his territory. Through these conquests, he formed Babylonia into a single monarchy with Babylon serving as its capital. 

Under his rule, Babylonian independence was recovered, leading to a great literary revival. The authority of Babylon extended significantly, with its rule being obeyed as far as the shores of the Mediterranean.

## Administration and Foreign Relations
The reign of Khammurabi is well-documented through the discovery of autograph letters and vast numbers of contract tablets. These records provide evidence of the geopolitical reach of his administration. For instance, one letter from Khammurabi ordered the dispatch of 240 soldiers from Situllum and Assyria, indicating that Assyria was a dependency of Babylonia at that time. Furthermore, Babylonia maintained constant intercourse with the west, with troops and officials passing into Canaan and Syria.

## Historical Context of the Dynasty
Khammurabi's ascent followed a period of instability and shifting power. Before his reign, the fortress of Babylon was built during the fifth year of the reign of Sumu-la-ilu, the son of Sumuabi. Sumuabi, who may have come from Canaan or southern Arabia, had established himself as the master of northern Babylonia while Elamites occupied the south. 

During the reign of Sin-muballidh (Sumu-la-ilu's great-grandson), the Elamites had placed the country under tribute and appointed Eri-Aku (also called Arioch or Rim-Sin) as the king of Larsa. Eri-Aku was the son of Kudur-Mabug, who served as the governor of Syria and prince of Yamutbal.
