# Early History and Settlement of Bohemia

## Ancient Inhabitants and the Boii
The country of Bohemia derives its name from the Boii, a Celtic tribe. According to most authorities, the Boii had a settlement on the Danube in part of modern Bohemia from very early times; this area was known as *Boiohemum*, or the "land of the Boii," and the tribe established a capital by the same name. The Boii were eventually weakened by constant warfare with neighboring tribes and were subdued around 12 B.C. by the Marcomanni, a Teutonic tribe.

## Slavic Conquest and the Cechs
Following the Marcomanni, other Teutonic tribes expelled them from the region. Eventually, Slavic tribes conquered Bohemia, with the Cechs being the most important among them. While the exact date of the arrival of the Cechs in Bohemia is very uncertain—and references in Byzantine and classical writers are often misleading—recent archaeological research indicates that Slavic inhabitants existed in Bohemia as far back as the beginning of the Christian era. The Cechs are believed to have become the masters of the country during the 5th century.

## Early Rulers and Legends
The first ruler of the Cechs mentioned in historical records is Samo. He is credited with defeating the Franks in a great battle at Wogatisburg in 630 (likely near the present town of Eger) and defeating the Avars, a Turanian tribe that had briefly held overlordship over Bohemia. 

Following the death of Samo, the history of the region becomes obscure for approximately 130 years. Later records from early chroniclers, including Cosmas, describe the foundation of a Bohemian principality by Krok (or Crocus) and his daughter Libussa. According to legend, Libussa married a peasant named Premysl who was found ploughing his field. Ancient chroniclers used this semi-mythic figure to construct a continuous list of Premyslide princes, though the dates of their reigns and their attributed deeds are not considered historical.

## Later Political Transitions
In later centuries, Bohemia experienced significant political upheaval. In 1608, a conflict arose between Rudolph and his brother Matthias. While Matthias invaded Bohemia and met with the estates at Caslav, the Bohemians initially declined his overtures. By June 25, 1608, an understanding was reached at Liben, where Rudolph ceded the government of Upper and Lower Austria, Moravia, and Hungary to Matthias while retaining his own rights as king of Bohemia.

By 1619, the Bohemian crown became vacant upon the death of Matthias. On July 31, the Bohemian estates formally deposed Ferdinand, and on August 26, they elected Frederick, elector palatine, as king. Frederick and his queen, Elizabeth of England, arrived in October and were crowned at St Vitus's cathedral in Prague. However, this period was marked by warfare with Austria. In September 1620, a large imperialist army under the command of Bouquoi, Tilly, and the duke of Bavaria entered Bohemia. On November 8, 1620, the imperial forces defeated the Bohemian army at the "Bila Hora" (White Hill). Following this defeat, the imperialists occupied the port of Prague, and King Frederick fled the city the next morning.
