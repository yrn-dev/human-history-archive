# THE CITY OF CAMPICHU

## Overview and Administration
Campichu is described as a great and noble city located within the province of Tangut. It serves as the capital and the primary place of government for the entire province of Tangut.

## Religious Life and Architecture
The population of Campichu is diverse in faith, consisting of Christians, Saracens, and Idolaters. The Christian community maintains three fine churches within the city. The Idolaters possess numerous abbeys and minsters constructed according to their own traditions.

These Idolater sites house a vast number of idols of varying sizes. Some of the largest idols reach ten paces in stature and are positioned lying at length. These figures are crafted from stone, clay, or wood, and are characterized by a high polish and a covering of gold. Surrounding these primary idols are other figures of considerable size, depicted as if they are paying homage to and adoring the larger images.

## Regional Geography and Travel
Campichu serves as a starting point for travel to several other regions within and beyond the Province of Tangut:

**The Route to Erguiul**
Traveling east from Campichu involves a journey of five days across a tract of land where spirits are heard speaking during the night. This path leads to the kingdom of Erguiul, which is one of several kingdoms comprising the Province of Tangut and belongs to the Great Kaan. The inhabitants of Erguiul include worshippers of Mahommet, Idolaters, and Nestorian Christians.

**The Route to Etzina**
Traveling north from Campichu requires a twelve-day ride to reach the city of Etzina. Etzina is situated on the verge of the Sandy Desert and also belongs to the Province of Tangut. The people of Etzina are Idolaters who rely on cattle and cultivation for their livelihood, as they possess no trade. The region is known for its abundance of cattle, camels, and high-quality falcons, specifically Lanners and Sakers.

## The Sandy Desert
Etzina serves as a critical supply point for those traveling further north. Travelers must secure victuals for forty days at Etzina because the subsequent journey across the desert extends for forty days without any baiting-places or habitations. While some people may be encountered in the desert during the summer, the extreme cold makes this impossible in the winter. The desert landscape includes occasional small pine-woods, where wild beasts and numbers of wild asses are found.
