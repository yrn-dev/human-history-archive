# Astrological-Astronomical Texts (Leipzig, 1892) and Martin, Textes

The study of ancient astrological and astronomical records is supported by a variety of specialized monographs and collections of texts, particularly those focusing on the civilizations of Mesopotamia and the Greco-Roman world. Among these scholarly contributions are the work *Astrological-Astronomical Texts*, published in Leipzig in 1892 by Craig, and the *Textes religieux assyriens et babyloniens*, published in Paris in 1900 and 1903 by Martin.

## Mesopotamian Astrological and Astronomical Works
The archive identifies several key texts and authors dedicated to the study of astrology and astronomy in Nineveh and Babylon. R. C. Thompson authored *The Reports of the Magicians and Astrologers of Nineveh and Babylon* (London, 1900), a work cited for its relevance to the history of astrology and its affinities with astronomy and other popular beliefs. 

Further contributions to the understanding of Chaldean astrology include the work of Ch. Virolleaud, specifically *L'Astrologie chaldeenne* (Paris, 1905), which provides translations and transliterations of cuneiform texts. Additionally, F. X. Kugler's *Sternkunde und Sterndienst in Babel* (Freiburg, 1907) serves as a significant resource on the subject.

## Related Religious and Divinatory Texts
The study of these astronomical texts often intersects with broader research into Assyrian and Babylonian religion and divination. Relevant works include:
* **A. Boissier**: *Documents assyriens relatifs aux presages* (Paris, 1894-1897) and *Choix de textes relatifs a la divination assyro-babylonienne* (Geneva, 1905-1906).
* **J. A. Craig**: In addition to the 1892 astrological work, Craig produced *Assyrian and Babylonian Religious Texts* (Leipzig, 1895-1897).
* **L. W. King**: Author of *Babylonian Religion and Mythology* (London, 1899) and *Babylonian Magic and Sorcery* (London, 1896).
* **Morris Jastrow, jun.**: *Religion Babyloniens und Assyriens* (Giessen, 1904).

## Greek Astrology and Astronomy
The archive also notes the development of these sciences in the Greek tradition. A. Bouche-Leclercq's *L'Astrologie grecque* (Paris, 1899) is recommended for its comprehensive bibliography on the history of astrology. Other notable works include Franz Boll's *Sphaera* (Leipzig, 1903) and Franz Cumont's *Catalogus Codicum Astrologorum Graecorum* (Brussels, 1898).

In the realm of mathematics and astronomy, Autolycus of Pitane, who flourished in the second half of the 4th century B.C., wrote two treatises. One contains propositions on the motion of the sphere, and the other, in two books, discusses the rising and setting of fixed stars.
