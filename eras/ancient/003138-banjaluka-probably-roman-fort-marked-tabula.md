# Banjaluka

Banjaluka is a city in Bosnia and the capital of a district of the same name. Geographically, it is situated at the head of a narrow defile, enclosed by steep hills to the east and west, while expanding to the north to meet the valley of the Save. The city lies on the river Vrbas, and a small stream known as the Crkvina enters the Vrbas from the north-east.

## Roman Origins and the Tabula Peutingeriana
Banjaluka is likely the site of a Roman fort identified as *Castra* in the *Tabula Peutingeriana*. This map, a reduced copy of a 3rd-century road map of the world, was copied in 1265 by a monk at Colmar and later acquired by Conrad Peutinger of Augsburg. The original is held in the imperial library of Vienna. According to this source, the fort was located on the river Urbanus, positioned on the road leading from Servitium in Pannonia to Salona on the Adriatic.

The city retains evidence of its Roman history through its celebrated baths. While most are in ruins, one massive domed building dating from the 6th century remains in use. Additionally, modern baths have been opened to utilize the area's hot springs.

## Historical Development
The origin of the name Banjaluka, which means "Baths of St Luke," is uncertain. The city's rise to prominence was accelerated in the 15th century following the fall of Jajce, a rival stronghold located 22 miles to the south. Subsequently, Banjaluka became a frequent site of conflict between the Turks and Austrians, with notable encounters occurring in 1527, 1688, and 1737. By the last half of the 18th century, no other Bosnian city possessed greater importance or prosperity.

In 1831, the city served as the starting point for Hussein Aga Borberli, known as *Zmaj Bosanski* ("the Dragon of Bosnia"), who set forth from Banjaluka to wage a holy war against Sultan Mahmud II.

## Urban Features and Infrastructure
As of 1895, the population of Banjaluka was 13,666, including approximately 7,000 Moslems. The city serves as the seat for an Austrian garrison, a district court, and both Orthodox and Roman Catholic bishops. 

The city's architecture and landmarks include:
* **Religious Sites:** More than 40 mosques, the largest and most beautiful of which is the 16th-century Ferhadiya Jamia. The city also houses Trappist and Franciscan monasteries.
* **Educational and Commercial Buildings:** A Turkish bazaar, a real-school, and a girls' school operated by the Sisterhood of the Sacred Blood of Nazareth.
* **Military and Civic Structures:** A citadel and barracks located in the angle formed by the Vrbas river and the Crkvina stream.
* **Transport:** The city is the terminus of a military railway that connects to the Hungarian state line at Jasenovac, located 30 miles north-north-west.

## Economy and Natural Resources
Banjaluka utilizes the power of its two rapid rivers to operate many corn-mills. The city's industrial base includes a brewery, cloth-mills, gunpowder-mills, a government tobacco factory, and a model farm. The adjoining hills are a source of various minerals, specifically silver, iron, and coal.
