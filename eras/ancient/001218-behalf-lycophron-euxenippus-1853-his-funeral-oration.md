# On Behalf of Lycophron and Euxenippus and the Funeral Oration

The works *On Behalf of Lycophron and Euxenippus* (1853) and the *Funeral Oration* (1858) are speeches by Hypereides. These texts were brought to light through the scholarly work of Dr. Babington, who published the *editio princeps* of these speeches, as well as Hypereides' *Against Demosthenes* (1850).

## Discovery and Publication
The publication of these speeches was made possible by the discovery of papyri at Thebes, Egypt, in 1847 and 1856. Dr. Babington's editions of these specific texts established his reputation and fame as a Greek scholar.

## About the Scholar
Dr. Babington was a versatile individual with a wide range of interests. His early experience with country life led to a passion for natural history, specifically ornithology and botany, and he was considered an authority on conchology. His scientific contributions include the *Flora of Suffolk* (1889), written with W. M. Hind, and the *Catalogue of Birds of Suffolk* (1884-1886). He also authored appendices on ornithology and botany for Potter's *History and Antiquities of Charnwood Forest* in 1842.

Beyond his work with Greek papyri, Babington was active in various other academic fields:
* **Classical Studies:** He catalogued Greek and English coins for the Fitzwilliam museum and classical manuscripts for the University Library. He also published inscriptions found in Crete by the explorer T. A. B. Spratt in 1855 and 1865.
* **History and Archaeology:** He wrote an *Introductory Lecture on Archaeology* (1865) and documented *Roman Antiquities found at Rougham* in 1872.
* **Editing and Defense:** He edited the first two volumes of Higden's *Polychronicon* (1858) and Bishop Pecock's *Repressor of Overmuch Blaming of the Clergy* (1860). In 1849, he wrote *Mr Macaulay's Character of the Clergy ... considered*, a work defending 17th-century clergy against the criticisms of Macaulay, which earned the approval of Mr. Gladstone.
* **Reformation Texts:** In 1855, he published a facsimile of the 1543 Venice *editio princeps* of *Benefizio della Morte di Cristo*, a Reformation-era book attributed to Paleario. This work, which had seen nearly all its copies destroyed by the Inquisition, was published by Babington with an introduction and versions in English and French.
