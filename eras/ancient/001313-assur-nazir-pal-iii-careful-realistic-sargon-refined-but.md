# Assur-nazir-pal III

Assur-nazir-pal III was an Assyrian monarch who reigned from 883 to 858 B.C. His reign marks a pivotal point in the historical record, as it is with Assur-nazir-pal III that knowledge of Assyrian history begins once more to be fairly full. He was the son of Tukulti-In-aristi.

## Imperial Expansion and Governance
Under the rule of Assur-nazir-pal III, the Assyrian Empire experienced a period of significant growth, extending its reach in all directions. This expansion was accompanied by a considerable development of wealth and art, as evidenced by the temples, palaces, and other buildings he commissioned. 

During this era, Calah (known as *Kalah* in Assyrian inscriptions) became the favorite residence of the king. Assur-nazir-pal III is distinguished among the conquerors of Assyria for his revolting cruelties.

## Architecture and Art
The architectural legacy of Assur-nazir-pal III is characterized by a transition in materials and style. While early Assyrian construction followed the Babylonian tradition of using brick—even in the north where stone was naturally available—later architects began to employ stone. The palaces of the Assyrian kings featured walls lined with colored and sculptured stone slabs rather than the painted walls common in Chaldaea.

In the evolution of Assyrian bas-reliefs, the period under Assur-nazir-pal III is identified as the first of three distinct stages; the art of this era is described as vigorous but simple. This contrasts with the later periods of Sargon, which were careful and realistic, and those of Assur-bani-pal, which were refined but lacked boldness.

## Archaeological Discoveries
Excavations conducted by Hormuzd Rassam and other scholars have uncovered several significant structures attributed to Assur-nazir-pal III:

*   **The Temple at Balawat:** Located at the mounds of Balawat (called Imgur-Bel by the Assyrians), approximately 15 miles east of Mosul, Rassam discovered a small temple dedicated to the god of dreams. This temple contained a stone ark or coffer holding two rectangular alabaster tables with inscriptions.
*   **The Palace at Nimrud:** Excavations at Nimrud (Calah) revealed the remains of a palace belonging to Assur-nazir-pal III, where hundreds of enamelled tiles were discovered.

## Chronology and Succession
The dating of Assur-nazir-pal III's reign is supported by the exact chronological computation methods of the Assyrians, who appointed a special officer each year to give their name to that year. These "canons" or lists, along with full annals, allow for precise dating. Assur-nazir-pal III's reign is recorded as 883–858 B.C. He was succeeded by his son, Shalmaneser II, who reigned for 35 years.
