# Henry Perkins

## Overview and Collection
Henry Perkins (d. 1855) was a book collector noted for his application of the "cabinet theory" of collecting. His personal library consisted of 865 fine manuscripts and specimens of early printing. Following his death, this collection was sold in 1870, where it realized a sum of nearly £26,000.

## The Cabinet Theory of Collecting
Perkins is credited with carrying out the modern cabinet theory of book-collecting with conspicuous skill. This approach emerged as an alternative to the traditional miscellaneous library, where collectors sought to represent every class of book. Such large-scale, miscellaneous buying became increasingly discouraged due to the growing cost of books and difficulties regarding available house-room.

In contrast, the cabinet theory emphasizes unity and compactness. Rather than requiring a dedicated gallery or special room, the goal of this method is to create small collections that epitomize the owner's specific tastes. These collections are designed to be housed in nothing more substantial than a neat bookcase or cabinet.

## Influence and Popularity
The method championed by Perkins became increasingly popular among book buyers in the United States of America, England, and France. In France, this trend was embraced by a group of book-loving amateurs who prided themselves on the compactness of their treasures. These collectors distinguished themselves from their predecessors, whom they referred to as the *bibliophile de la vieille roche*. French bibliophiles successfully applied this theory to the collection of dainty illustrated French books from the 18th century.
