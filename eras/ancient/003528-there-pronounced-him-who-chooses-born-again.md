# Christian Baptism and Illumination

## The Rite of Baptism
In the early Christian tradition, the process of baptism is associated with the choice to be born again and the repentance of sins. According to the records, the person seeking to be washed is led to the laver by one who pronounces the name of God the Father and Lord of the universe. This name is used alone, as the name of the ineffable God cannot be uttered by anyone. 

The rite involves washing in the name of the Holy Ghost, who foretold all things about Jesus through the prophets, and in the name of Jesus Christ, who was crucified under Pontius Pilate. This process of washing is referred to as "Illumination" (from the Greek *photismos*), based on the concept that those who learn these teachings are illuminated in their understandings.

## Spiritual Purification
Justin, in his dialogue with Trypho, argues that Christian baptism is the only means of purifying those who have repented, contrasting it with the rites of ablution practiced by the Israelites (Bani Israel). He describes Christian baptism as the "water of life," while characterizing the cisterns dug by others as broken and profitless. 

Justin posits that baptism which cleanses only the flesh and body is of little use; instead, he advocates for the baptism of the soul from hatred, envy, and wrath, asserting that this is how the body becomes pure. Furthermore, he notes that those who have approached God through Jesus Christ have received a spiritual circumcision, rather than a carnal one, similar to the manner of Enoch.

## Historical Context and Terminology
By the late 2nd century, the term "illumination" was regularly used to describe the rite of baptism. This is documented in the writings of Tertullian, specifically in his treatise *On the Resurrection of the Flesh*.
