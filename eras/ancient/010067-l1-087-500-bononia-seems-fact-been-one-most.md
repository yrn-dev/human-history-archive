# Bononia

Bononia, known in the modern era as Bologna, was a primary city of ancient Italy and served as the chief town of ancient Aemilia. Its historical significance in antiquity is mirrored by the importance of Bologna in modern Italy.

## Origins and Early History
Classical writers attributed the origin of Bononia to the Etruscans, stating it was founded from Perusia by Ocnus or Aucnus under the name Felsina. However, archaeological excavations have revealed a more complex early history. Discoveries include approximately 600 ancient Italic (possibly Ligurian) huts and cemeteries from the Umbrian period (dating between 800 and 600 B.C.), which immediately preceded the Etruscan civilization (c. 600-400 B.C.). Evidence of an extensive Etruscan necropolis has been found on the site of the modern cemetery, as well as on the Arnoaldi Veli property and in the public garden.

By 196 B.C., when the town first appears in historical records, it was in the possession of the Boii and had likely already changed its name from Felsina. It subsequently became a Roman colony in 189 B.C.

## Roman Infrastructure and Administration
The importance of Bononia was solidified by its strategic position within the road system of north Italy. In 187 B.C., the Via Aemilia connected the city with Placentia and Ariminum. In the same year, a road was constructed to Arretium, and another was likely built to Aquilelia around 175 B.C.

The city acquired Roman citizenship in 90 B.C. During the political conflicts of 43 B.C., Mark Antony utilized Bononia as his base of operations against Decius Brutus and settled colonists there. Later, Augustus added further colonists and constructed a new aqueduct from the Letta, a tributary of the Rhenus; this aqueduct was restored to use in 1881. In A.D. 53, following a fire, the emperor Claudius provided a subvention of 10 million sesterces (L1,087,500).

## Cultural Contributions
Bononia contributed to the development of Roman theater. During the time of Sulla, Pomponius of Bononia, along with Novius, helped transform the *Atellanae Fabulae* (Atellan fables)—which were originally improvised, coarse sketches of country and town life—into a regular literary form of written comedies.

## Late Antiquity and Transition
Bononia demonstrated resilience during the general ruin of the empire, successfully resisting Alaric in 410. Following this period, the city belonged to the Greek exarchate of Ravenna. While no Roman remains exist above ground today, various discoveries have been made within the city walls.

## Modern Bologna
In the modern era, Bologna continues to be an important center, particularly regarding transportation and gastronomy. It serves as a major railway hub where the main line from Milan divides: one branch follows the path of the ancient Via Aemilia toward Brindisi, Ancona, and Rimini, while another passes through the Apennines toward Rome and Florence. Other lines connect the city to Padua, Ferrara, and Portomaggiore, with steam tramways serving Malalbergo, Pieve di Cento, and Vignola. The city is also noted for its specialties, including liqueurs, *tortellini* (a type of macaroni), and *salami* or *mortadella* (Bologna sausage).
