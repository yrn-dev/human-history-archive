# When Herod Is Rejected In Arabia, He Makes Haste To Rome

## Flight from Arabia
Following the capture of Hyrcanus and Phasaelus by the Parthians, Herod sought to secure the release of his brother, Phasaelus. He traveled to Arabia with the intention of obtaining money from the king to redeem him, reasoning that if the king would not provide a free gift based on his father Antipater's friendship, he would borrow the funds. To ensure this, Herod brought along his brother's seven-year-old son as a pledge and was prepared to offer three hundred talents.

However, Herod found no lasting friendship among the Arabians. King Malichus commanded Herod to leave his country, using the pretext that Parthian ambassadors had denounced him. In reality, Malichus and other potent men in his kingdom wished to avoid repaying the debts and gifts that Antipater had deposited with them. Upon discovering that the Arabians were his enemies, Herod departed for Egypt.

## Journey Through Egypt
During his journey, while traveling toward Rhinocurura, Herod received news that his brother Phasaelus had died. Reports regarding Phasaelus's death varied: one account states he committed suicide by dashing his head against a stone to avoid abuse; another suggests a surgeon sent by Antigonus killed him using poisonous ingredients in the wound. Before he died, Phasaelus was reportedly informed by a poor woman that Herod had escaped, leading him to die with the comfort that someone remained alive to avenge him.

After lamenting his brother, Herod proceeded to Pelusium. Although he initially struggled to find passage from the fleet, the captains, out of reverence for his fame and dignity, conducted him to Alexandria. There, he was received with great splendor by Cleopatra, who attempted to persuade him to lead her forces in an upcoming expedition. Herod rejected these solicitations.

## Arrival in Rome and Accession to the Kingdom
Despite the tumults occurring in Italy and a severe storm, Herod sailed for Rome. Upon his arrival, he sought the support of Antony and Caesar. By promising a significant amount of money to Antony, Herod successfully obtained the support of both the Senate and Caesar. Consequently, he was made King of the Jews.

Following this appointment, Herod sailed from Italy back to Judea, where he fought against Antigonus. With the assistance of Sosius, he took Jerusalem by force and ended the government of the Asamoneans.

## Later Relations with Arabia
Herod's relationship with Arabia remained volatile over time. In one instance, he made war with the King of Arabia, eventually conquering him and being chosen by the Arabs to serve as the governor of that nation. 

In a separate conflict, Herod the Tetrarch quarreled with Aretas, the King of Arabia. This enmity began when Herod's brother (the son of the high priest Sireoh's daughter) entered into an agreement to marry Herodias, the wife of Herod the Tetrarch, on the condition that the Tetrarch divorce the daughter of Aretas. When Aretas's daughter discovered the plot, she fled to her father in Arabia. This, combined with a dispute over limits in the country of Gamalitis, led to war. Herod's army was eventually destroyed due to the treachery of fugitives from the tetrarchy of Philip who joined Aretas.

## Legal Disputes and Roman Intervention
At another point, Herod was involved in a dispute involving a man named Sylleus and the Arabians. An ambassador named Nicolaus sought to reconcile Caesar and Herod by accusing Sylleus of various crimes, including the slaughter of the friends of King Obodas and adultery with Arabian and Reinan women. Nicolaus argued that Sylleus had intentionally alienated Caesar from Herod through falsehoods. During these proceedings, Caesar questioned whether Herod had led an army into Arabia, slain two thousand five hundred men, or pillaged the country.
