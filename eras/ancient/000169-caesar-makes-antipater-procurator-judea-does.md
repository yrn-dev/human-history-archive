# Caesar Makes Antipater Procurator Of Judea

## Rise of Antipater and Roman Relations
Antipater established a strong relationship with Caesar by undertaking hazardous enterprises and exposing himself to many dangers. His valor was evidenced by numerous wounds across his body. Following the settlement of affairs in Egypt and Caesar's return to Syria, Antipater was granted the privilege of Roman citizenship and freedom from taxes. Caesar further bestowed upon him honors and marks of friendship, and confirmed Hyrcanus in the high priesthood.

Antipater's standing was later challenged by Antigonus, whom Antipater described as the son of a fugitive and an enemy to the Romans. Antipater argued that Antigonus sought to govern public affairs not out of need, but to stir up sedition among the Jews and work against the Romans. To prove his own loyalty, Antipater displayed his wounds to the multitude, stating that his body spoke of his goodwill to Caesar. Consequently, Caesar declared Hyrcanus the most worthy of the high priesthood and allowed Antipater to choose his own authority. Antipater was constituted procurator of all Judea and received permission to rebuild the walls of his country that had been thrown down. Caesar ordered these grants to be engraved in the Capitol as indications of his justice and Antipater's virtue.

## Governance of Judea and Appointments
Upon returning to Judea after conducting Caesar out of Syria, Antipater first rebuilt the wall of Jerusalem that had been overthrown by Pompey. He then traveled through the country to quiet tumults. He advised the people that they would live peaceably if they submitted to Hyrcanus, but warned that those who raised new troubles would find him as their lord instead of their procurator, Hyrcanus as a tyrant instead of a king, and the Romans and Caesar as enemies.

Because Antipater viewed Hyrcanus as inactive and unfit to manage the kingdom's affairs, he settled the country's affairs himself. He appointed his eldest son, Phasaelus, as governor of Jerusalem and its surrounding areas. His next son, Herod, who was very young, was sent with equal authority to serve as governor of Galilee.

## Civil War and the Succession of Procurators
During a period of Roman disturbance regarding Apamia and a civil war triggered by the treacherous slaughter of Sextus Caesar by Cecilius Bassus, Antipater provided assistance to Caesar's commanders. He sent his sons to aid them, acting out of friendship for both the murdered Sextus and the Caesar who remained alive. Following this conflict, Marcus succeeded Sextus.

Later, during the war between Cassius and Brutus against Antony and the younger Caesar (Augustus), Cassius and Marcus raised an army in Syria. Because Herod was expected to provide significant necessaries, they appointed him procurator of all Syria and provided him with an army of horse and foot. Cassius further promised Herod that he would be made king of Judea after the war.

## The Fall of Antipater
The rise of Herod's power and hopes caused fear in Malichus. Malichus attempted to lay snares for Antipater to preserve the government of Hyrcanus. Although Antipater initially perceived this and retreated beyond Jordan to gather an army of countrymen and Arabs, he eventually reconciled with Malichus after the latter denied the plots under oath. Marcus, the president of Syria, had nearly killed Malichus for causing disturbances in Judea, but saved him due to Antipater's intercession.

Ultimately, Malichus succeeded in his plot by corrupting one of the king's cup-bearers with money. Antipater was given a poisoned potion at a feast and died. He is remembered as a man active in the management of affairs who recovered and preserved the government for Hyrcanus.

## Later Political Developments
In subsequent years, the region saw further Roman administrative changes. Cyrenius was sent by Caesar to conduct a taxation of Syria and Judea, and Coponius was sent to serve as the procurator of Judea. Additionally, Herod and Philip built several cities in honor of Caesar.

In separate internal conflicts, Herod once ordered Antipater (the son) to have no intercourse with Pheroras or Pheroras's wife. This Antipater eventually moved to Rome via Italian friends, carrying Herod's testament. In this document, the kingdom was bequeathed to Antipater, with Herod (the son of Mariamne) named as Antipater's successor.
