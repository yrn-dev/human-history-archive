# ASCLEPIADES (OF SAMOS) AND ASSUMPSIT

## Asclepiades of Samos
Asclepiades of Samos was a lyric poet and epigrammatist who flourished around 270 B.C. He is recognized as the most important and earliest of the erotic and convivial epigrammatists. While some of his compositions are actual inscriptions, the majority of his work consists of love-songs, and he also wrote pieces praising poets he specifically admired. 

In the Greek Anthology, there are approximately 40 epigrams that bear his name, though it is considered doubtful whether he is the author of all of them. Additionally, it is possible that the Asclepiadean metre was named after him. He was a friend of Theocritus.

## Assumpsit
*Assumpsit* is a term derived from the Latin *assumere*, meaning "he has undertaken." In a legal context, it refers to an action brought to recover damages resulting from the non-performance or breach of a simple contract. Such contracts could be express or implied, and could have been made in writing or orally.

The term originates from the word used by plaintiffs in pleadings to describe the promise or undertaking of the defendant. Actions of *assumpsit* were generally categorized into two types:
* **Common or *indebitatus assumpsit***: These were typically brought based on an implied promise.
* **Special *assumpsit***: These were founded upon an express promise.

As a form of action, *assumpsit* became obsolete following the passage of the Judicature Acts of 1873 and 1875.
