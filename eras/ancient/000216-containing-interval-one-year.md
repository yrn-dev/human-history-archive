## Mathematical Intervals
In the study of functions within a restricted domain, the domain of the argument may contain an interval. Within such an interval, a function may possess an inferior limit or a superior limit that is attained; these are defined as the minimum value or the maximum value of the function, respectively.

Additionally, in the context of power series, a series converges uniformly in any interval contained within its domain of convergence, provided the end-points are excluded. If a series converges uniformly in the interval between a and b, it may be integrated term by term. Similarly, if a series of derived functions converges uniformly in an interval, the original series converges in that interval and represents a continuous differentiable function, allowing it to be differentiated term by term. Furthermore, a series may converge uniformly in an interval even if its terms represent functions that are not continuous throughout that interval.

## Legal Reporting and Intervals
The Council of Law Reporting, established in 1863, provides legal reports and information at various intervals. Subscribers pay a subscription of four guineas a year to receive reports from the House of Lords and all superior courts. The judicial committee of the privy council issues a king's printer's copy of the statutes in monthly parts, while weekly notes are provided containing announcements of new rules under other acts of parliament and the Judicature Acts, as well as current decisions.

The Council also publishes Digests of decided cases. One such Digest published in 1892 covered a twenty-five-year interval from 1865 to 1890, which was later supplemented by a Digest for the following ten-year interval from 1891 to 1900. Current indexing is now performed continuously through monthly and annual publications, which are consolidated into a digest at stated intervals, such as every five years.
