# History of the Graeco-Bactrian and Indo-Scythian Kingdoms

The study of the Graeco-Bactrian and Indo-Scythian kingdoms relies upon a diverse array of historical, numismatic, and literary sources. These sources provide insight into the period following the conquests of Alexander the Great, extending through the era of the Indo-Scythians and the subsequent decline of the Arsacids.

## Bibliographic and Numismatic Sources

Research into the Graeco-Bactrian and Indo-Scythian periods is supported by several key scholarly works. H. H. Wilson's *Ariana Antiqua* (1841) and the writings of Cunningham, specifically "The Greeks of Bactriana, Ariana and India" in the *Numismatic Chronicle* (N. Ser. viii.-xii.), serve as foundational texts. Further analysis of the successors of Alexander the Great in India and Bactria is provided by A. von Sallet in *Die Nachfolger Alexanders des Grossen in Baktrien und Indien* (1879).

Numismatic evidence is particularly vital for this period. P. Gardner's *The Coins of the Greek and Scythic Kings of India* (1886), a catalogue of Greek coins in the British Museum, offers detailed data on the coinage of these regions. Additionally, A. Stein contributed to the understanding of religious iconography in the region through the work "Zoroastrian Deities on Indo-Scythian Coins," published in the *Babylonian and Oriental Record* in 1887.

## Regional and Dynastic Histories

The broader historical context of Iran and its neighboring territories, from the time of Alexander the Great until the fall of the Arsacids, is documented by A. von Gutschmid in *Geschichte Irans und seiner Nachbarlaender von Alexander dem Grossen bis zum Untergang der Arsaciden* (1888). 

Regarding the Kushan or Indo-Scythian period of Indian history, the works of Vincent A. Smith are primary references, including his 1903 article in the *Journal of the R. Asiatic Soc.* and his *Early History of India* (2nd ed. 1908). W. W. Tarn also explored the cultural intersections of the region in "Notes on Hellenism in Bactria and India," published in the *Journ. of Hellenic Studies* in 1902.

## Literary and External Records

The archive indicates that very few notices from Greek authors have been preserved, with the most notable exceptions being the writings of Strabo and Justin (including the prologues of Trogus). For later periods, historians rely on a combination of Byzantine authors and sources from Armenia and Persia. One such source is the translation of Tabari by Th. Noeldeke, titled *Geschichte der Perser und Araber zur Zeit der Sasaniden* (1890), and the work *Er[=a]n[vs]ahr* by J. Marquart (1901).

Chinese sources also provide critical data on the Greek kings of Bactria. These have been analyzed and presented by several scholars:
* Deguignes in *Recherches sur quelques evenements qui concernent l'histoire des rois grecs de la Bactriane*.
* E. Specht in *Etudes sur l'Asie centrale d'apres les historiens chinois* (published in *Journal asiatique* in 1857 and 1883).
* Sylvain Levi in "Notes sur les Indo-scythiens," published in *Journal asiatique*.

## Linguistic and Palaeographic Context

The study of these kingdoms is further aided by the analysis of the Indian alphabet. J. Buhler's *Indische Palaeographie*, found in *Grundriss der indo-arischen Philologie* (Bd. i.), provides the necessary framework for understanding the character and history of the scripts used in the region.
