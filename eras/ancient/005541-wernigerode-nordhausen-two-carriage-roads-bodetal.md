# Brocken and the Harz Region

The Brocken is a mountain located in Prussian Saxony, Germany, and serves as the highest point of the Harz range, reaching an elevation of 3,733 feet. It is characterized as a huge, dome-shaped mass of granite-strewn, bare terrain. Due to its position as the greatest elevation in north Germany, the summit offers magnificent views; in clear weather, observers can distinctly see the Elbe, Magdeburg, the Thuringian forest, and the towers of Leipzig.

## Access and Transportation
The summit of the Brocken is accessible via three primary routes. A mountain railway extending 12 miles runs from Dreiannen-Hohne, which is a station located on the normal gauge railway line connecting Wernigerode and Nordhausen. Additionally, two carriage roads provide access to the summit, originating from Ilsenburg and the Bodetal.

## Folklore and Cultural Significance
The Brocken holds a significant place in the folklore of north Germany and is associated with numerous legends. Despite the penetration of Christianity into the region, the mountain remained a site of heathen worship for a long period. Every year on Walpurgis night (May 1st), curious rites were performed there. These practices were condemned by Christian priests, leading to a widespread belief that witches and the devil held orgies on the mountain. This superstition persists among many local country people, who continue to believe the mountain is haunted on that specific night. In literature, the mountain is featured in the "Brocken scene" of Goethe's *Faust*.

## Surrounding Localities

### Ilsenburg
Located in Prussian Saxony under the north foot of the Harz Mountains at the entrance to the Ilsethal, Ilsenburg is a village and health resort. It is situated 6 miles northwest of Wernigerode on the railway line to Goslar. As of 1900, its population was 3,868. The village features an Evangelical church, a high grade school, and a modern château belonging to the princes of Stolberg. Its local industry includes the manufacture of machines, metal wares, and iron bolts and screws.

Ilsenburg is a prominent tourist destination in the Harz Mountains, attracting approximately 6,000 visitors annually. The village is home to the old castle, Schloss Ilsenburg, which sits on a high crag. Originally an imperial stronghold likely built by the German king Henry I, it was resided in by Emperor Otto III in 995. In 1003, Henry II gave the castle to the bishop of Halberstadt, who established a Benedictine monastery there; the associated school was highly regarded by the end of the 11th century. Following the Reformation, the counts of Wernigerode used the castle as their residence until 1710. Above the town on the plateau edge is the Ilsenstein, a granite peak rising about 500 feet above the valley. It is topped with an iron cross erected by Count Anton von Stolberg-Wernigerode to honor friends who died in the wars of 1813-1815.

### Ilfeld
Ilfeld is a town in the Prussian province of Hanover, situated at the south foot of the Harz at the entrance to the Bährethal. It is located 8 miles north of Nordhausen via the railway to Wernigerode and has a population of 1,600. The town's economy includes brewing and the manufacture of plaster of Paris, paper, and parquet-flooring. It is also recognized as a health resort.

The town developed in the 14th century around a Benedictine monastery founded circa 1190. This monastery was reformed in 1545 and converted into a gymnasium (school) in 1546. Under the rectorship of Michael Neander (1525-1595), the school gained a reputation for scholarship that it has maintained. Ilfeld also contains an Evangelical church and a fine library.
