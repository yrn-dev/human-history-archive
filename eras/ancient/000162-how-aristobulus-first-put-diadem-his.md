# Aristobulus and the Establishment of the Kingdom

## Transition of Government
Following the death of Hyrcanus, who had administered the government for either thirty-one or thirty-three years, his eldest son, Aristobulus, took control of the public affairs. Hyrcanus had been esteemed by God as worthy of three great privileges: the high priesthood, the government of his nation, and the gift of prophecy. Through this gift, Hyrcanus had foretold that his two eldest sons would not long continue in the government of public affairs.

Upon taking power, Aristobulus resolved to change the form of government into a kingdom. He became the first to put a diadem upon his head. According to the archive's records, this event occurred either four hundred seventy-one years and three months, or four hundred eighty-one years and three months, after the people were delivered from Babylonian slavery and returned to their country.

## Treatment of Family
Aristobulus's reign was marked by extreme cruelty toward his own kin. Because his mother had been left by Hyrcanus to be the governess or mistress of public affairs, she contested the government with Aristobulus. In response, he put her in bonds and cast her into prison, where he proceeded to the degree of barbarity of causing her to be pined to death by hunger.

Regarding his brothers, Aristobulus initially showed affection for Antigonus, who was next in line after him, treating him as an equal and a partner in the kingdom. However, he bound his other brothers and placed them in prison.

## Conflict with Antigonus
The relationship between Aristobulus and Antigonus eventually deteriorated due to the influence of wicked persons who sought to create a difference between the brothers. During a time when Aristobulus was sick, Antigonus visited the temple to celebrate the feast of tabernacles and offer prayers for his brother's recovery. Antigonus appeared splendidly adorned and was accompanied by soldiers in armor.

Calumniators used this pompous appearance to convince Aristobulus that Antigonus was affecting royal authority and intended to kill him to reign for himself. Although Aristobulus had previously dismissed such reproaches as the result of envy, he eventually believed these claims. This led to the catastrophe where Aristobulus slew Antigonus.

## Political Opposition and Roman Intervention
Aristobulus's unexpected seizure of government caused fear among other people, particularly Antipater. Antipater, an Idumean of great riches and authority, hated Aristobulus and persuaded Hyrcanus to flee to Aretas, the king of Arabia. Antipater argued that Hyrcanus had been unjustly deprived of the dominion that belonged to him by the prerogative of his birth.

Aretas provided Hyrcanus with an army of fifty thousand horsemen and footmen to restore him to the kingdom. Aristobulus was unable to resist this force and was driven back to Jerusalem. He would have been taken by force if not for the timely intervention of Scaurus, a Roman general sent from Armenia by Pompey the Great, who raised the siege.

## The High Priesthood and Later Events
In the history of the high priesthood, which was traditionally reserved for those of the blood of Aaron, Aristobulus is noted as the second person to unlawfully deprive another of that dignity, having taken the office from his brother.

The archive records that after the conflict with Antigonus and the subsequent political turmoil, Aristobulus himself died. In one account, his death followed the slaying of Antigonus; in another, it is noted that he reigned for no more than a year. Later historical records indicate that after fleeing to Rome, he was caught by Gabinius and sent back to Rome again.
