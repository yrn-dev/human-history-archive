# Aveiro

Aveiro is a seaport, episcopal see, and the capital of an administrative district in Portugal. It is situated on the river Vouga and is located on the Lisbon-Oporto railway. The city is built on the southern shore of a marshy lagoon that measures approximately 15 miles from north to south with an average breadth of about 1 mile, containing many small islands. To provide access to the Atlantic Ocean, an artificial canal known as the Barra Nova, which is about 33 feet deep, was constructed between 1801 and 1808.

## History and Governance
Aveiro is probably the site of the Roman Talabriga. During the 16th century, the city's cathedral was constructed, and the city was established as a duchy. The title of "duke of Aveiro" eventually became extinct in 1759 following the execution of its last holder, Dom Jose Mascarenhas e Lancaster, who was burned alive for high treason.

## Economy and Industry
The city has a history of maritime exploration and trade. In the 16th century, Aveiro was the birthplace of Joao Affonso, who was among the first navigators to visit the fishing-grounds of Newfoundland. This led to the city becoming famous for a fleet of more than sixty vessels that sailed to that region annually, returning with cargoes of dried codfish.

Modern local industries include the gathering of aquatic plants (*molico*), the preparation of sea-salt, and the catching and curing of fish, with a particular emphasis on oysters and sardines. The city also engages in a brisk trade in fruit, oil, and wine.

## Geography and Administration
The administrative district of Aveiro coincides with the north-western portion of the province of Beira. As of 1900, the district had an area of 1,065 square miles and a population of 303,169. The city of Aveiro itself had a population of 9,979 in 1900.

The broader province of Beira is an ancient principality in central and northern Portugal, bounded by the Atlantic Ocean to the west and the Spanish provinces of Estremadura and Leon to the east. Beira is divided into several districts, including Coimbra, Guarda, Vizeu, Castello Branco, and Aveiro. Popularly, the province is viewed in three sections: Beira Alta (Upper Beira), Beira Baixa (Lower Beira), and Beira Mar (Maritime Beira). The latter coincides with the former coastal province of Douro and includes the districts of Coimbra and Aveiro.

The region's geography is characterized by a flat coastline of approximately 72 miles, consisting of marshland, heath, and sandy pine forests. Notable features of this coastline include Cape Mondego and the lagoon of Aveiro. While the coast is flat, the interior of the province is largely mountainous, reaching its highest point at 6,532 feet in the Serra da Estrella. The district of Aveiro contains good pasture-land as well as mines for lead and copper.

## Nearby Locations
The seaport of Ilhavo is located 3 miles southwest of Aveiro on the Aveiro lagoon. As of 1900, Ilhavo had a population of 12,617 and was inhabited primarily by fishermen. The town is known for the export of salt and the Vista-Alegre, a celebrated manufactory of porcelain and glass recognized for a high degree of perfection in the art of glass-cutting. While tradition suggests Ilhavo was founded by Greek colonists around 400 B.C., the validity of this tradition is considered doubtful.
