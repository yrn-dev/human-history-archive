# Vespasian Is Sent Into Syria By Nero In Order To Make War

## Appointment and Rationale
Following the defeat of Cestius and the subsequent ill success of Roman forces in Judea, the Emperor Nero experienced a concealed state of terror and consternation. Although Nero publicly maintained a facade of superiority, claiming that the misfortunes were due to the negligence of the commander rather than the valor of the enemy, he was privately solicitous regarding the recovery of Roman affairs.

To address the crisis, Nero appointed Vespasian as the general to lead the war. Nero viewed several circumstances as favorable omens for this choice: Vespasian's age provided him with great skill and sure experience, and his sons served as hostages for his fidelity to the emperor. Nero believed the flourishing age of the sons would make them fit instruments under their father's prudence. To ensure Vespasian's complaisance, Nero utilized flattering compellations and great encomiums during the appointment.

## Mobilization of Forces
Upon receiving his command, Vespasian coordinated the gathering of Roman military strength. He sent his son, Titus, from Achaia—where Titus had been with Nero—to Alexandria. Titus was tasked with bringing back the tenth and fifth legions. Simultaneously, Vespasian crossed the Hellespont and traveled by land into Syria. There, he assembled the Roman forces along with a considerable number of auxiliaries provided by neighboring kings.

## Military Campaign in Judea
Vespasian, accompanied by his elder son, launched an expedition into the country of Judea. His campaign involved the capture of various cities in Galilee; some were taken entirely by force, while others were secured through treaties and specific terms. During these operations, a number of his auxiliaries were cut off in Galilee.

## Transition of Power and Political Uncertainty
While Vespasian was in Cesarea preparing his army to march directly toward Jerusalem, he received news that Nero had died after a reign of thirteen years and eight days. The accounts of Nero's end include his abuse of power, the influence of his unworthy freed-men Nymphidius and Tigellinus, and his eventual suicide in the suburbs of Rome after being deserted by his guards.

This political upheaval caused Vespasian to postpone his expedition against Jerusalem. He remained in a state of waiting to see where the empire would be transferred. When Galba was declared emperor, Vespasian waited for directions regarding the war and sent Titus, accompanied by King Agrippa, to salute Galba and receive commands concerning the Jews. However, while sailing along the coasts of Achaia during the winter, Titus and Agrippa learned that Galba had been slain after a reign of seven months and seven days.

## Later Advancement to Emperor
During the conflict, Josephus was taken captive by the Romans. In a secret conference with Vespasian and Titus, Josephus prophesied that Vespasian and his son would become Caesar and emperor, asserting that he had been sent by God to deliver this news. While Vespasian initially dismissed this as a cunning trick for self-preservation, he later became convinced of the truth of the prediction through other signs and the confirmation of captives who verified Josephus's previous predictions regarding the fall of Jotapata.

Vespasian eventually took up arms against Vitellius, whose perceived incapacity tempted him to do so. Following the overthrow of Vitellius, Vespasian ascended to the empire. In his subsequent role as emperor, he recalled Gaius Cassius Longinus, a Roman jurist who had been banished to Sardinia by Nero.
