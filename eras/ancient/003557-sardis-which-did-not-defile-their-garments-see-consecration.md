# Sardis

Sardis is an ancient city associated with the Lydians and later the Persians. Its history is marked by strategic military conquests, political instability, and the influence of various ruling powers.

## Conquest and Fall of the City
The taking of Sardis occurred during a siege led by Cyrus. After an initial failed attempt by the army to scale the walls, a Mardian named Hyroiades discovered a vulnerability in the citadel. While the Lydians believed the citadel was unassailable due to its precipitous nature, Hyroiades had observed a Lydian descend that side of the wall to recover a helmet. 

This specific area of the wall, turned towards Tmolos, had remained unprotected by a ritual safeguard. Meles, a former king of Sardis, had previously carried a lion—borne to him by his concubine—around the walls of the city based on a decision by the Telmessians that such an act would keep Sardis safe from capture. However, Meles had omitted the precipitous part of the citadel from this circuit. Hyroiades and other Persians ascended this section, leading to the eventual capture of Sardis and the plundering of the entire city.

## Persian Administration and Political Unrest
Following its capture, Sardis remained a center of Persian administrative activity. The city was governed by provincial governors who employed Royal Secretaries to manage official correspondence. During the reign of Dareios, the governor Oroites was assassinated in Sardis through a deception orchestrated by Bagaios, the son of Artontes. 

Bagaios gained the trust of the guard by presenting several papers bearing the seal of Dareios. After testing the guards' obedience with various documents, he presented a final paper stating that King Dareios commanded the Persians in Sardis to slay Oroites. The spearmen of the guard obeyed the written command and killed the governor.

## Lydian Relations and Social Engineering
The relationship between the Persians and the Lydians was characterized by recurring revolts. Cyrus once expressed frustration to Croesus regarding the Lydians' tendency to cause trouble, suggesting that it might be best to sell them all as slaves. Croesus, fearing the destruction of Sardis, intervened, arguing that the city itself was guiltless and that the blame for recent unrest lay with Pactyas, the man entrusted with the charge of Sardis.

To prevent further revolts and neutralize the danger posed by the Lydians, Croesus suggested a strategy of social softening. He proposed that the Lydians be forbidden from possessing weapons of war and instead be commanded to wear tunics under their outer garments and buskins. Furthermore, he suggested they be encouraged to train their sons as retail-dealers and to play the harp and lyre, theorizing that this would make them "women instead of men" and remove the fear of future rebellion.

## Other Historical Associations
Sardis also served as a location for the harem of Cyrus. Following a conflict in which the head and right hand of a deceased prince were cut off by order of Artaxerxes, the camp of the Cyreians was pillaged. This included the harem at Sardis, which contained two Grecian women of free condition and good family—one from Miletus and one from Phokæa. The Phokæan woman, named Milto, was taken prisoner and transferred to the harem of Artaxerxes, while the younger woman from Miletus managed to escape.

## Religious and Textual References
In the New Testament, specifically in Revelation iii. 4, there is a reference to "a few names" in Sardis who "did not defile their garments." In this context, the term "names" is interpreted as referring to "persons."
