# Praefectus Fabrum

The term *praefectus fabrum* refers to a specific official role within the Roman administrative and military structure, serving as a chief engineer.

## Lucius Cornelius Balbus Major
Lucius Cornelius Balbus, known as *Major* to distinguish him from his nephew, held the position of *praefectus fabrum* while accompanying Julius Caesar. He served in this capacity during Caesar's time as propraetor in Spain in 61 B.C. and subsequently in Gaul in 58 B.C.

## Background and Naturalization
Balbus was generally considered to be of Phoenician origin. He was granted Roman citizenship, along with his family, by Pompey in recognition of services rendered against Sertorius in Spain. Despite this naturalization, his status as a foreigner, combined with his wealth and influence, led to significant enmity. 

In 56 B.C., a native of Gades prosecuted Balbus on the grounds that he had illegally assumed the rights of a Roman citizen. This legal challenge was directed not only at Balbus but also at the triumvirs. Balbus was ultimately acquitted after Pompey, Crassus, and Cicero spoke on his behalf.

## Political Career and Influence
Balbus was a chief financier in Rome and played a significant role in the formation of the First Triumvirate. He maintained strategic friendships across various political parties and ingratiated himself with Caesar. Following his time as chief engineer, Balbus served as Caesar's private secretary. His influence was such that Cicero felt compelled to seek Balbus's good offices with Caesar.

During the civil war, Balbus attempted to use Cicero as a mediator between Pompey and Caesar to prevent Caesar from siding definitively with Pompey, though Cicero admits he was dissuaded from this action against his own better judgment.

After the assassination of Caesar, Balbus aligned himself with Octavian. He achieved high office, serving as praetor in 43 or 42 B.C. In 40 B.C., he became consul, marking the first time this honor was conferred upon an alien.

## Literary Contributions
Balbus maintained a diary documenting the primary events of his own life and the life of Julius Caesar, as noted by Suetonius. Additionally, the 8th book of *Bell. Gall.* was dedicated to him; this work was likely written by his friend Hirtius at Balbus's instigation.
