# Hatshepsut: The Female Pharaoh of Ancient Egypt

## Overview
Hatshepsut (r. c. 1479–1458 BCE) remains one of the most intriguing figures of the 18th Dynasty. While several women held power in Ancient Egypt, Hatshepsut is distinguished by her transition from regent for her stepson, Thutmose III, to a fully crowned Pharaoh. 

Her reign was characterized by economic prosperity, the restoration of trade networks—most notably the famous expedition to the Land of Punt—and an ambitious building program. To legitimize her rule in a society where the kingship was traditionally male, Hatshepsut utilized a sophisticated blend of religious propaganda and artistic evolution. She claimed divine birth, asserting that the god Amun-Ra had fathered her, and gradually shifted her iconography from feminine forms to the traditional masculine regalia of the Pharaoh, including the false beard and kilt.

Historians have long debated the motives behind the later attempt to erase her name and images from monuments. For decades, it was believed that Thutmose III sought to destroy her memory out of spite. However, modern scholarship suggests a more pragmatic political motive: the removal of her image may have been an effort to ensure a clean line of succession for Thutmose III's own heirs, removing the "anomaly" of a female king from the official royal annals.

## Key Facts
*   **Architectural Legacy:** Her most famous achievement is her mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari, a masterpiece of classical symmetry that blends into the limestone cliffs of the Theban hills.
*   **Trade and Diplomacy:** Unlike many of her successors, Hatshepsut focused on trade over conquest. The expedition to Punt brought back luxury goods, including myrrh trees, gold, and exotic animals.
*   **Religious Legitimacy:** She heavily promoted the cult of Amun, positioning herself as his daughter to solidify her right to the throne.
*   **Gender Fluidity in Art:** Her statues evolve from showing her as a traditional queen to a figure with a masculine physique and royal beard, symbolizing the *office* of the Pharaoh rather than her biological sex.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Myrrh Trees:** Hatshepsut is credited with one of the first recorded attempts to transplant foreign trees, bringing live myrrh trees from Punt to be planted at her temple.
2. **The Obelisks:** She commissioned massive granite obelisks at the Temple of Karnak, which were among the tallest in the world at the time.
3. **The "Hidden" Pharaoh:** Because her name was chiseled away from many monuments, she was largely forgotten by history until the 19th century when Egyptologists began deciphering hieroglyphics.
