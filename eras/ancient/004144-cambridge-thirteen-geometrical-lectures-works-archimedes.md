# Archimedes and the Geometrical Works of Barrow

## The Works and Life of Archimedes
Archimedes was a relative and friend of Hiero and is remembered for his immense philosophic speculations and a "more than human intellect." He is noted for his contributions to the science of mechanics, asserting that with a given power he could move any given weight. To demonstrate this theory to King Hiero, Archimedes used a system of pulleys to pull a fully loaded three-masted transport ship from the king's navy across land with a smooth motion, as if it were moving over the sea.

Following this demonstration, Hiero requested that Archimedes construct machines for the defense and attack of a city. These engines were deployed in Syracuse, where Archimedes directed their operation. During the Roman assaults led by Marcellus, Archimedes' weapons were the sole trust of the city for its safety. The Roman soldiers became so intimidated that the sight of a small beam or rope over the wall caused them to flee, fearing Archimedes was deploying another engine. Marcellus sarcastically referred to him as a "Briareus of a geometrician" for his ability to hurl many weapons at once.

Despite his success in mechanics, Archimedes regarded the useful arts and mechanics as "base and vulgar." Consequently, he did not leave behind any writings on the subject. He reserved his study and delight for speculations of absolute beauty and excellence, where accuracy of reasoning and wondrous power could be attained. His written works in geometry are cited as having the most difficult hypotheses explained on the clearest and simplest principles.

## Barrow's Geometrical Contributions
Barrow was a clear-sighted and able mathematician who was considered by his English contemporaries to be second only to Newton, though continental writers did not rank him as highly. He was skilled in the severe geometrical method. In his work *Method of Tangents*, he utilized a course of reasoning that approximated the logic Newton later used to develop the doctrine of ultimate ratios.

Barrow produced numerous scientific works, most of which were written in Latin. Among his most important contributions are:
*   *Euclid's Elements*
*   *Euclid's Data*
*   *Optical Lectures* (read in the public school of Cambridge)
*   *Mathematical Lectures* (read in the public schools of the university of Cambridge)
*   *Thirteen Geometrical Lectures; The Works of Archimedes, the Four Books of Apollonius's Conic Sections, and Theodosius's Spherics, explained in a New Method*
*   A *Lecture* investigating and briefly demonstrating Archimedes' Theorems of the Sphere and Cylinder.

While his scientific output was vast, it is noted that his substantial contributions to science were not of great importance, and his lectures on elementary principles provided little insight into the difficulties between philosophy and mathematics. In contrast, his *Sermons* were highly regarded as weighty and elaborate pieces of reasoning, despite a ponderous style.

## The Development of Mechanics and Geometry
The science of mechanics was initiated by Archytas and Eudoxus, who used solid instruments called mesographs—constructed from various sections and curves—to solve geometrical questions, such as finding a mean proportional. This approach was opposed by Plato, who believed that introducing the senses and "base servile labour" into geometry destroyed its real excellence, which he believed belonged to the region of pure intellect. This conflict led to the separation of mechanics from geometry; for a long period, mechanics was viewed with contempt by philosophy and categorized among the military arts.
