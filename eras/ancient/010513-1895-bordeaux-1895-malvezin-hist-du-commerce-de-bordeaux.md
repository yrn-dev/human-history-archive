# Bordeaux

Bordeaux (originally *Burdigala*) is a major city in south-western France and the capital of the department of Gironde. It is situated on the left bank of the Garonne river, approximately 60 miles from the sea, within a plain that includes the Medoc wine-growing district. The city is positioned where the Garonne forms a semicircle, separating the main city from the suburb of La Bastide on the right bank.

## History
Originally the chief town of the Bituriges Vivisci, Bordeaux became a flourishing commercial center under the Roman Empire and served as the capital of Aquitania Secunda in the 4th century. The writer Ausonius, a native of the city, described it as a walled, four-square city with lofty towers and one of Gaul's greatest educational centers. Following the disintegration of the Roman Empire, the city suffered a period of decline, not recovering its prosperity until the start of the 10th century.

From 1154 to 1453, Bordeaux and Guienne belonged to the English kings. During this time, the city hosted the court of Edward the Black Prince, and his son Richard was born there. English administration fostered the city's trade and liberties; in 1235, Bordeaux gained the right to elect its mayors, who worked with a municipal council known as a "jurade." The city's regional influence grew as it led a federation that included towns such as Libourne and St Emilion. Extensive trade was established with English ports, including Bristol, London, Exeter, Hull, Chester, and Dartmouth.

The English period ended in 1453 following the battle of Castillon and a three-month siege, leading to the city's submission to Charles VII. While privileges were initially curtailed, Louis XI partially restored them and established the parlement of Guienne in the city. In 1548, the inhabitants were severely punished by the constable Anne de Montmorency after resisting a salt-tax by force of arms.

In later centuries, Bordeaux saw religious and political volatility. The reformed religion gained many followers, though nearly three hundred inhabitants died during the massacre of St Bartholomew. The 17th century was marked by disturbance; the city supported the Fronde, and royal troops were sent to repress insurrections in 1653 and 1675. During the mid-18th century, the marquis de Tourny, the *intendant* of Guienne, improved the city's layout by creating public squares and widening streets.

During the French Revolution, Bordeaux served as the headquarters for the Girondists and suffered severely during the Reign of Terror. Its commerce declined under Napoleon I. In 1814, the city declared for the house of Bourbon, and Louis XVIII later granted the title of duc de Bordeaux to his grand-nephew, the comte de Chambord. In 1870, the French government was moved from Tours to Bordeaux as German forces approached.

## Geography and Urban Layout
Bordeaux is accessible via the Orleans railway (359 miles south-southwest of Paris) and the Southern railway (159 miles northwest of Toulouse). The city is characterized by low white houses rather than the high tenements found in other large French towns. It is surrounded by a semicircle of boulevards and suburbs, including Begles, Talence, Merignac, Cauderan, and Le Bouscat.

The city's infrastructure includes the Pont de Bordeaux, a stone bridge from the early 19th century measuring 1,534 feet, and a railway bridge connecting the two main railway stations. The center of the city is characterized by narrow, winding streets and a crescent of busy quays featuring factories, mansions, and warehouses. Key landmarks include:
* **Place des Quinconces:** A central square with plane trees, rostral columns serving as lighthouses, and statues of Montesquieu and Montaigne. It also features a monument to the Girondin deputies proscribed in 1793.
* **Place de la Comedie:** Located at the south-west corner of the Place des Quinconces, containing the 18th-century Grand Theatre.
* **Public Spaces:** The allees de Tourny and the public garden.

## Administration and Education
Bordeaux serves as a significant administrative and religious hub. It is the seat of an archbishop, a court of appeal, and the headquarters of the XVIII. army corps. Its legal and commercial infrastructure includes a court of assizes, tribunals of commerce and first instance, a chamber of commerce, a council of trade-arbitrators, and a branch of the Bank of France.

The city is a major educational center, serving as the center of an *academie*. Its institutions include:
* Faculties of medicine and pharmacy, science, letters, law, and Catholic theology.
* A higher school of commerce, a naval school of medicine, and a chair of agriculture.
* Lycees, training colleges, and a school of fine art.
* A library containing over 200,000 volumes, several museums (including one for sculptures and pictures), and various learned societies.
