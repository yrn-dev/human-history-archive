# The Kassite Dynasty of Babylonia

The Kassite dynasty was established in Babylonia around 1780 B.C. by a founder known as Gandis, Kandis, or Gaddas. The Kassites, also referred to as Kossaeans, originated from the mountains of Elam. Prior to the establishment of this dynasty, the region had been ruled by the "First Dynasty of Babylon," which concluded with the reign of Samsu-ditana, son of Ammi-zadok. Following the First Dynasty, a series of 11 Sumerian kings reigned for a period of 368 years—a figure noted by historians as likely being exaggerated—with only one of these kings appearing in contemporaneous documentation.

## Duration and Governance
The Kassite dynasty lasted for 576-3/4 years (recorded in some archive lists as 576 years and 9 months) and consisted of 36 kings. This period of foreign dominion is noted for its striking analogy to the contemporary rule of the Hyksos in Egypt. 

Under Kassite rule, the nature of the Babylonian monarchy shifted. The Semitic kings of Babylonia had previously been invested with divine attributes; however, these disappeared under the Kassites. The title of "god" was never bestowed upon a Kassite sovereign. Despite this change in royal status, Babylon remained the capital of the kingdom and served as the holy city of western Asia. In this city, the priests held all-powerful status, and it was the only place where the right to inherit the old Babylonian empire could be conferred.

## Territorial and Political Impact
The ascension of the Kassites led to a decline in Babylonian territorial control. Babylonia lost its empire over western Asia, resulting in the independence of Palestine and Syria. Simultaneously, the high-priests of Assur established themselves as kings of Assyria.

The archive lists several sovereigns of the dynasty and their respective reign lengths, including:
*   **Gandis**: 16 years
*   **Agum-sipak**: 22 years
*   **Bitilyasu I**: 22 years
*   **Ussi (?)**: 9 years

Other listed rulers include Adu-metas, Tazzi-gurumas, Agum-kakrime, and Kara-indas. Kadasman-Bel, the son of Kara-indas, maintained correspondence with Amon-hotep (Amenophis) III of Egypt around 1400 B.C. Later rulers included Kuri-galzu II, Burna-buryas (22 years), Kuri-galzu III (26 years), Nazi-Maruttas (17 years), Kadasman-Turgu (13 years), Kudur-bel (6 years), and Sagarakti-suryas (13 years).

## Later Period and Transition
The dynasty experienced periods of external influence, such as the seven-year rule of Tukulti-In-aristi of Assyria in 1272 B.C., during which native vassal kings served. These included Bel-sum-iddin (1-1/2 years), Kadasman-Bel II (1-1/2 years), Hadad-sum-iddin (6 years), Hadad-sum-uzur (30 years), Meli-sipak (15 years), Merodach-baladan I (13 years), Zamama-sum-iddin (1 year), and Bel-sum-iddin (3 years).

The Kassite era eventually gave way to other successions:
*   **Dynasty of Isin**: Comprised of 11 kings who reigned for 132-1/2 years, beginning around 1203 B.C. Notable figures included Nebuchadrezzar I, Bel-nadin-pal, Merodach-nadin-akhi (22 years), and the usurper Hadad-baladan.
*   **Dynasty of the Sea-coast**: Beginning around 1070 B.C., including Simbar-sipak (18 years), Ea-mukin-zeri (5 months), and Kassu-nadin-akhi (3 years).
*   **Dynasty of Bit-Bazi**: Beginning around 1050 B.C., including [E]-Ulmas-sakin-sumi (17 years), Ninip-kudur-uzur I (3 years), and Silanim-Suqamuna (3 months).
*   **Dynasty of Elam**: An Elamite ruled for 6 years starting around 1030 B.C.

This sequence of dynasties concluded with the start of the Second Dynasty of Babylon in 1025 B.C.
