# LUCIUS CORNELIUS BALBUS (MAJOR)

Lucius Cornelius Balbus, referred to as "Major" to distinguish him from his nephew, was a prominent figure in Rome born early in the last century B.C. He is generally considered to have been of Phoenician origin and was a native of Gades.

## Citizenship and Early Career
Balbus obtained Roman citizenship for himself and his family through the favor of Pompey, as a reward for his services in Spain against Sertorius. This citizenship was confirmed by a lex passed in 72 B.C. during the consulship of Cn. Cornelius Lentulus; it is suggested that Balbus assumed the Roman name Cornelius to show gratitude to the consul. 

In his early career, Balbus established himself as one of Rome's chief financiers and maintained friendly relations with various political parties. He played a significant role in the formation of the First Triumvirate. He also sought to ingratiate himself with Julius Caesar, accompanying him as chief engineer (*praefectus fabrum*) during Caesar's time as propraetor in Spain in 61 B.C. and later in Gaul in 58 B.C.

## Legal Challenges and Political Influence
Due to his wealth, influence, and status as a naturalized foreigner, Balbus acquired many enemies. In 56 B.C., a native of Gades prosecuted him on charges of illegally assuming the rights of a Roman citizen. This legal action was directed at Balbus as well as the triumvirs. Balbus was acquitted after Cicero, Pompey, and Crassus spoke on his behalf.

During the subsequent civil war, Balbus attempted to use Cicero as a mediator between Caesar and Pompey to prevent Cicero from siding definitively with Pompey. While Cicero admitted he was dissuaded from this mediation against his better judgment, Balbus eventually became Caesar's private secretary. His influence grew to the extent that Cicero found it necessary to ask Balbus for his good offices with Caesar.

## Later Career and Honors
Following the murder of Caesar, Balbus attached himself to Octavian. He served as praetor in 43 or 42 B.C. and reached the consulship in 40 B.C. This appointment was notable as it was the first time the honor of consul was conferred upon an alien. Additionally, he was raised to the pontificate, an honor previously unknown for a stranger.

## Literary Contributions and Records
Balbus maintained a diary documenting the primary events of his own life and the life of Julius Caesar. He is also credited with instigating his friend Hirtius to write the eighth book of the *Bell. Gall.* (Gallic War), which was subsequently dedicated to him. Furthermore, Caesar wrote letters to Balbus and C. Oppius using a kind of cipher; two of these letters are preserved in the collection of Cicero's letters (*Ad Atticum*).
