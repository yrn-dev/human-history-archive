# Baluchistan Agency and Related Administrative Districts

## The Baluchistan Agency
The Baluchistan Agency is an administrative entity extending from the Persian frontier and the Gomal to the Arabian Sea. Within this agency, the political status of the province as a whole is nearly identical to that of the native states found in the Indian peninsula. Supreme control is practically exercised by the agent to the governor-general of India, who is supported by a staff of political assistants.

The agency contains districts that maintain a level of independence comparable to any found in Afghanistan. However, the independence of Baluchistan as a whole requires qualification; while some areas exist in various degrees of independence, they are all subject to British control. Consequently, Baluchistan is no longer viewed as a distinct entity among Asiatic nations in the same way that Afghanistan is.

## British Baluchistan
British Baluchistan is an official province within the Baluchistan Agency. It officially comprises the districts of Sibi, Peshin, and Thal-Chotiali. These specific districts were originally Afghan territories. Although they had been nominally handed over to Kalat forty years prior to 1879, they were formally transferred to British authority via the treaty of Gandamak in 1879.

## Geography and Environment of the Baluchistan Highlands
The Baluch highlands have experienced widespread desiccation of large areas. Evidence of previous agricultural prosperity remains in the form of cultivation and Arab irrigation works. The disappearance of the Arabs from agriculture and trade is attributed to climatic changes rather than tribal disturbances. 

These climatic shifts may have been influenced by the wanton destruction of forests and ordinary processes of erosion and denudation acting on recent deposits over older, harder beds. The region has also seen alterations in level during recent geologic periods, including compressions that caused the ground to rise over the crests of anticlinal folds. A notable instance of this occurred on December 20, 1892, when a severe earthquake along an old line of fault west of the Kawaja Amran range resulted in a shortening of the railway line by approximately 2-1/2 feet. Additionally, the coast exhibits active volcanic agency through the presence of mud volcanoes.

## Comparative District Administration in British India
Other regions of British India utilized similar administrative structures involving districts and agencies. The Bombay Presidency, for example, was a province consisting of native states and British districts, bounded by the Arabian Sea to the west and Baluchistan, Rajputana, and the Punjab to the north. 

The Bombay Presidency was divided into twenty-six districts across four commissionerships: Sind, the southern or Carnatic, the central or Deccan, and the northern or Gujarat. Among these was the district of Bijapur (formerly Kaladgi), a barren plain of 5,669 sq. m. that experienced significant population fluctuations due to severe famines in 1899-1000 and 1876-1878.

Furthermore, the presidency included 353 separate native state units. These were administered by collectors of the districts where smaller states were located or by political agents. These states were organized into groups, such as the Palanpur, Mahi Kantha, and Rewa Kantha agencies in North Gujarat, and the Khandesh political agency in North Konkan.
