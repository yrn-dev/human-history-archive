# Dīgha Nikāya

The *Dīgha Nikāya* is the first of the four Nikāyas within the Pāli canon. Its name translates to "the Collection of Long Ones," referring to the *suttantas* it contains. It serves as a primary repository of the dialogues and teachings attributed to the Buddha and, in some instances, his leading disciples.

## Nature and Structure of the Texts
The materials within the *Dīgha Nikāya* are not books in the modern sense, but are memorial sentences or verses designed to be learned by heart. The arrangement and style of these texts are subordinated to the necessity of memorization. 

The archive distinguishes between different lengths of Buddhist records:
*   **Suttas:** Very short passages, typically occupying one or two pages, containing a single proposition.
*   **Suttantas:** Collections of suttas containing similar propositions woven into the framework of a single dialogue. These usually span about a dozen pages.

The *Dīgha Nikāya* specifically collects the longest of these *suttantas*. It is distinguished from the *Majjhima Nikāya* (the "Collection of the suttantas of Medium Length"), as the *suttantas* in the *Dīgha* are longer than those in the *Majjhima*, which are in turn longer than the ordinary *suttas* found in the subsequent two collections.

## Content and Style
The *Dīgha Nikāya* consists of dialogues in which the Buddha or his disciples engage in conversations regarding ethical, philosophic, or religious points. These dialogues are characterized by an earnest, elevated tone and frequently employ a method of Socratic questioning. While they reflect the cultured thought of their era and possess a dignified style that occasionally reaches eloquence, they differ from Western writings because they were intended for oral transmission rather than reading.

The collection is not organized by subject, but rather by the length of the dialogues. This arrangement can make it difficult to locate specific statements of doctrine on a particular topic.

## Evolution of the Records
The records in the *Dīgha Nikāya* are believed to have been built up gradually. The process began with short *suttas* embodying doctrine in set phrases. These were later placed within narrative frameworks—introductory stories explaining when, how, and to whom a *sutta* was enunciated. Multiple *suttas* and episodes were then woven together to form *suttantas*, which were eventually grouped into the Nikāyas.

Parallel to these prose developments was the use of verse. Verses were utilized to aid memory and to express feelings of ecstasy and enthusiasm inspired by the reforming doctrines. These lyrics were often accompanied by prose comments to explain their meaning and origin.

## Notable Passages and Episodes
The *Dīgha Nikāya* contains several significant accounts and references:
*   **The Life of the Buddha:** The collection includes a prose account of the Buddha's "going forth" (found in vol. i, p. 115). Most notably, it contains the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta* (vol. ii, pp. 72-168), which provides the text for the account of the Buddha's last days and his final journey.
*   **Commentaries:** The canon preserves an ancient commentary on the Path within the *Dīgha* (ii. 305-307 and 311-313).
