# The Gospel of the Buddha

## Core Teachings on Suffering
According to the ancient scriptures, the gospel delivered by the sage in his sermon at Benares teaches that suffering is an inseparable part of birth and old age. The archive identifies several forms of suffering, including sickness, death, the experience of being united with the unloved, and the pain of separation from those who are loved. Furthermore, suffering arises when one does not obtain what is desired. The sage describes the "entire fivefold clinging to the earthly" as suffering.

The origin of this state is attributed to a "thirst for being," which includes the thirst for power, pleasures, and being itself. This thirst, accompanied by desire and lust, leads an individual from birth to birth.

## The Path to Extinction
To end suffering, this thirst must be extinguished through the complete annihilation of desire. This process involves letting go of desire, expelling it, and separating oneself from it entirely. The scriptures outline eight specific ways to achieve this extinction:
* Rectitude of faith
* Resolve
* Speech
* Action
* Living
* Effort
* Thought
* Self-concentration

## Relationship with the Outer World
The gospel teaches that one must be "done with the outer world." Participation in the outer world is viewed as something that is not the self; instead, such participation is the means by which a being experiences birth and death, emotions, longings, appetites, pleasure, pain, change, and suffering.

The practical application of this philosophy is seen in the life of the monk, who separates himself from the world. Those seeking the higher life of an ascetic or saint must observe five specific commandments:
1. To kill no living thing.
2. Not to lay hands on the property of another.
3. Not to touch the wife of another.
4. Not to speak what is untrue.
5. Not to drink intoxicating drinks.

## Nirvana and the State of the Saint
The ultimate goal of this path is the "heaven of extinction," known as Nirvana, which is defined in the ancient scriptures as supreme happiness. Nirvana is attained by those who have put off all fear, hope, lust, desire, and the will to exist as a sentient, sinful being. 

A person who has achieved this state may continue to tread the earth, but they are considered a Brahman or a saint. Such an individual is described as being in heaven, having quitted the transient to enjoy eternity.

## Later Interpretations
Following the time of Gautama, some of his less intelligent disciples sought to impress the outer world by attributing "vulgar marvels" to him. For example, one devout follower believed that the master's moment of mental irradiation was marked by an epileptic fit of the elements.
