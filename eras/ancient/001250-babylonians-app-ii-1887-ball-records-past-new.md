# Babylonians

The Babylonians were an ancient civilization whose influence extended across the Near East, leaving behind a significant record of administrative, linguistic, and religious development. Their history is reconstructed through archaeological excavations, cuneiform tablets, and the study of their interactions with neighboring powers such as Egypt.

## Administrative Records and Book-keeping
Evidence indicates that the Babylonians employed sophisticated methods of accounting and record-keeping from the remotest times. Babylonian records dating as far back as 2600 B.C. have been discovered, consisting of small slabs of clay inscribed with a stylus. These tablets frequently feature nail marks and impressions from cylinder seals. These markings served as a personal seal for the individual, representing an ancient counterpart to the modern practice of identifying individuals by fingerprints.

## Linguistic and Regional Influence
The Babylonian language served as a medium for international diplomacy and administration in the ancient world. Correspondence discovered at Tel el-Amarna in 1887 reveals that the Babylonian language was used for communication between Egyptian kings of the 18th dynasty—specifically Amen-hotep III and IV—and governors established in various parts of Palestine. This evidence suggests that Palestine had been under Babylonian influence for a considerable period before it became an Egyptian province.

Among the figures mentioned in these diplomatic records is Burnaburiash (Burna-buryas), the king of Babylon. Egyptologists and Assyriologists generally agree that the date of these monarchs was approximately 1400 B.C.

## Religion and Mythology
The study of Babylonian religion and mythology is supported by a vast array of specialized texts and monographs. Key areas of study include:
*   **Cosmology and Creation:** The "Seven Tablets of Creation" provide insight into Babylonian mythological origins.
*   **Divination and Magic:** Records exist regarding the reports of magicians and astrologers in Babylon, as well as texts concerning Babylonian magic, sorcery, and the nature of devils and evil spirits. There are also specific documents relating to presages and the "Maqlu" incantation series.
*   **Worship:** Research has focused on the "Hoofdtempel van Babel" (the main temple of Babylon) and the specific prayers offered to the sun god.

## Archaeological and Historical Authorities
The history of Babylonia has been documented by numerous researchers and expeditions. Significant contributions include the work of A. H. Layard in *Nineveh and Babylon* (1853) and the findings of the Babylonian Expedition of the University of Pennsylvania. Other key authorities include L. W. King, who published the *Letters of Khammurabi* and works on Babylonian religion, and H. V. Hilprecht. 

Further insights into the city's physical layout and geography have been provided by F. H. Weissbach in *Das Stadtbild von Babylon* (1904) and F. Hommel in his work on the geography and history of the ancient Orient. The "Babylonian Chronicle," as analyzed by Th. G. Pinches, also serves as a primary source for understanding the chronological events of the region.
