# Bona

Bona (also known as Bone) is a seaport located in Algeria at 36 deg. 53' N., 7 deg. 46' E. It is situated on a bay of the Mediterranean at the foot of the wooded heights of Edugh and serves as the chief town of an arrondissement in the department of Constantine. It is located 136 miles northeast of Constantine and 220 miles by rail west of Tunis.

## Ancient History and Hippo Regius
Bona is identified with the ancient *Aphrodisium*, which served as the seaport for Hippo Regius (also referred to as *Ubbo*). The Romans named the site Hippo Regius ("Royal") because it was a favorite residence of the Numidian kings. During the first three centuries of the Christian era, Hippo was among the richest cities in Roman Africa.

The city is most noted for its connection to St. Augustine, who served as its priest and bishop for thirty-five years. During the 5th century, the city was besieged for fourteen months by the Vandals under Genseric, who captured it in 431. St. Augustine died during this siege. While the city suffered general destruction, Augustine's library and manuscripts, as well as the cathedral, were saved. The library contained 232 separate treatises on theological subjects, a complete exposition of the gospel and psalter, and a collection of homilies and epistles.

In later periods, the town was partially restored by Belisarius, only to be sacked again by the Arabs in the 7th century. In 1900, a large basilica dedicated to St. Augustine, featuring a chancel facing west, was opened on the hill where Hippo once stood. The ruins of the ancient city, located about a mile south of the modern town, consist of fragments of walls and large cisterns that have since been restored.

## Development of the Modern City
The modern town of Bona was built by the Arabs and derives its name from the Arabic *annaba*, meaning "the city of jujube trees." For several centuries, it was a possession of the rulers of Tunis, who constructed the Kasbah in 1300. Between the beginning of the 14th century and the middle of the 15th century, the city was frequented by Spaniards and Italians. In the 16th century, Charles V held the city for a period and strengthened its citadel. Subsequent control of the city passed between Algerines, Tunisians, and the Genoese.

From the era of Louis XIV until the Revolution, the French *Compagnie d'Afrique* conducted active trade through the port. The French occupied the town for a few months in 1830 and reoccupied it in 1832 after Captains Yusuf and Armandy, leading a small force of marines, seized the Kasbah. Since that time, Bona has experienced significant industrial development, particularly after 1883 following the discovery of phosphate beds at Tebessa.

## Urban Layout and Architecture
Bona is characterized as a flourishing French city with wide, well-laid-out streets, some of which are very steep. The town is surrounded by a modern rampart built outside the original Arab wall, which had become too small for the city's growth. A broad, tree-lined promenade called the Cours Jerome-Bertagna (formerly the Cours National) runs through the center of the town and houses hotels, banks, and a theatre.

Key landmarks include:
* **The Cathedral of St. Augustine:** A large church built in a quasi-Byzantine style located at the northern end of the Cours Jerome-Bertagna. It houses a relic believed to be the right arm of St. Augustine, which arrived from Pavia in 1842.
* **The Grand Mosque:** Located on one side of the Place d'Armes, this structure was built using ruins from ancient Hippo.
* **The Kasbah:** A citadel situated on a hill to the northeast of the town.
* **Public Facilities:** The city contains civil and military hospitals, as well as barracks capable of accommodating 3,000 men.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Bona is a major center of commerce, ranking third in Algeria after Oran and Algiers. It maintains direct steamship communication with Marseilles. The city's inner harbour covers 25 acres with quays accommodating vessels drawing 22 feet; a spacious outer harbour was constructed between 1857 and 1868 and later enlarged between 1905 and 1907.

The city's trade includes the import of manufactures and general merchandise. Exports include wool, sheep, barley, zinc, iron, phosphates, esparto, and cork. Local industry includes the manufacture of leather, tapestry, and native garments. The town's water supply is sourced from the Edugh hills. To improve sanitary conditions, a system of canals was used to drain the marshes at the mouths of the Bujema and Seybuse rivers, which enter the sea to the south of the town. The surrounding region also contains marble quarries and cork woods.
