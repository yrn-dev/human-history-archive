# Seventeenth and Eighteenth Years of the War—Inaction of the Athenian Army

## Naval Movements and Objectives in Sicily
During the seventeenth and eighteenth years of the war, the Athenian generals stationed in Sicily divided their armament into two separate parts. Each general took a portion by lot, and the combined force sailed toward Egesta and Selinus. The primary objectives of this expedition were to determine if the Egestaeans would provide money and to investigate the status of the quarrel existing between Selinus and Egesta.

As they coasted along Sicily with the shore on their left toward the Tyrrhene Gulf, the Athenian forces attempted to touch at Himera, which was the only Hellenic city in that region of the island. However, they were refused admission and were forced to resume their voyage.

## Military Actions and the Capture of Hyccara
During their journey, the Athenian forces encountered Hyccara, a small Sicanian seaport that was at war with Egesta. The Athenians captured the town and made the inhabitants slaves. This town was subsequently handed over to the Egestaeans, some of whose cavalry had joined the Athenian forces.

Following the capture of Hyccara, the army traveled through Sicel territory to reach Catana. Meanwhile, the fleet continued along the coast, transporting the captured slaves on board.

## The Role of Nicias and Financial Transactions
While the main force proceeded, Nicias sailed directly from Hyccara along the coast to Egesta. After conducting his business and receiving thirty talents, Nicias rejoined the rest of the forces. The Athenians then sold the slaves they had captured for a total sum of one hundred and twenty talents.

## Efforts to Secure Allies and the Siege of Hybla
The Athenian forces sailed to their Sicel allies to urge them to provide troops. During this period, half of the Athenian force was deployed to Hybla, a hostile town located in the territory of Gela. Despite these efforts, the Athenians did not succeed in taking the town.
