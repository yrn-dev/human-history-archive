# Reconciliation of Berossus and the Kings' List

The effort to reconcile the chronological data provided by the Chaldaean historian Berossus with the figures found in the Kings' List has been a central point of contention among historians of ancient chronology. Because the native history of Berossus did not survive in its entirety, scholars rely on quotations from Syncellus, Eusebius, Ptolemy, and Josephus. While some of Berossus's records—such as the list of ten antediluvian kings who reigned for 432,000 years—have been partially confirmed by inscriptions, his eight postdiluvian dynasties are considered difficult to reconcile with monuments, and their associated numbers are viewed as probably corrupt.

## Systems of Chronological Reconciliation

Various scholars have proposed different systems to resolve the discrepancies between the Kings' List and the data from Berossus. These approaches generally fall into several groups based on their methodology.

### Acceptance of the Kings' List
Some historians have prioritized the Kings' List over other sources. Oppert's system represents the earliest suggested dates; he accepted the figures of the Kings' List and claimed to have reconciled them with those of Berossus, though he ignored later chronological notices. His system depended on a "cyclic date" of 2517 B.C., for which there is no evidence.

A larger group of scholars, including Sayce, Rogers, Maspero, Delitzsch, and Winckler, employed a similar principle. They fixed the date for the close of Dynasty III and used the figures of the Kings' List for earlier periods without emending them, making no attempt to reconcile their results with conflicting data.

### Emendation of Figures
Another group of writers, including Peiser, Rost, Lehmann-Haupt, and Marquart, attempted to resolve discrepancies by emending figures found in inscriptions. In 1891, Peiser sought to make the total duration of the dynasties agree with the system of Berossus and the statement of Nabonidus regarding the date of Khammurabi. He proposed that the Kings' List figure for Dynasty III ("9 soss and 36 years," or 576 years) was a scribal error for "6 soss and 39 years," thereby reducing the length of the dynasty by 177 years. Rost later reduced this figure further, though he attempted to offset the reduction by emending Sennacherib's date for Marduk-nadin-akh[e]'s defeat of Tiglath-pileser I from "418" to "478."

### Structural Revisions
A third group, led by Hommel and Niebuhr, focused on the treatment of Dynasty II to reduce the dates assigned to Dynasty I. Hommel initially reversed the order of the first two dynasties in 1886. In later systems (1895, 1898, and 1901), he abandoned this, suggesting instead that Dynasty III followed Dynasty I immediately, while Dynasty II was either apocryphal or synchronized with Dynasty I. Niebuhr modified this by reducing the independent existence of Dynasty II to 143 years, allowing it to overlap Dynasty I by 225 years.

## Hommel's 1901 Reconciliation
In 1901, Hommel proposed two alternative schemes. In one of these specific schemes, he attempted to reconcile Berossus with the Kings' List by assigning Dynasty II an independent existence of approximately 171 years. As a possible alternative, he presented a theory practically identical to his 1895 proposal.

## Impact of Conflicting Data
The difficulty in reconciling these sources has led to significant variance in historical dating. While the difference between the earliest and latest suggested dates for the close of Dynasty III is only 144 years, the discrepancy for the beginning of Dynasty I is as great as 622 years. This is attributed to the fact that some writers ignore inconsistencies between the Kings' List, the system of Berossus, and the chronology of Nabonidus, while others use "ingenious devices" and emendations to force the data into agreement.
