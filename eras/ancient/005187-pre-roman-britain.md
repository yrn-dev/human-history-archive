# PRE-ROMAN BRITAIN

## Nomenclature and Origins
The name Britain is the anglicized version of the classical name for Scotland, Wales, and England, and is sometimes used to refer to the British Isles as a whole (*Britannicae Insulae*). The Latin term *Britannia* and the Greek *Pretanikai nêsoi* or *Brettania* are considered attempts to reproduce an original Celtic name, though the exact form of that original remains a subject of dispute.

## Social Structure and Culture
Prior to the arrival of Caesar, the inhabitants of Britain had progressed beyond the use of beast skins for clothing. While the "wilder and poorer sort" residing in the fens and the north differed in appearance, the general population wore hats, cloaks, and sleeved coats with skirts cut above the knee, as well as loose trousers similar to the fashion found in Gaul. 

The Britons were skilled weavers and dyers. Their coats and trousers were often bright-colored, striped, or checkered, with red being the preferred hue. Cloaks, which resembled a Highland plaid in their square shape, were typically black or blue and featured a rough texture on one side. Specialized roles within the society were denoted by specific colors of dress: leeches and star-gazers wore green, while Druid bards wore blue. Women wore gowns that reached the ankle, with shorter tunics worn above them.

## Material Culture and Ornamentation
British chiefs utilized gold for ornamentation, wearing arm-rings and torques around their necks. They also used pins and brooches made of ivory or metal. Beads were common accessories, crafted from glass purchased from Southern merchants, or from jet and amber sourced from their own coasts, as well as from brass.

In terms of currency, the pre-Roman period saw the production of crude imitations of Roman currency. These imitations are noted for their level of incompetence, comparable to the deterioration of coinage seen in Bactria during its transition into Afghanistan.

## Warfare and Fortifications
The Britons were not an armored race. In combat, they frequently fought naked from the waist up, utilizing woad staining and tattooing to create a dreadful appearance. However, Pliny noted that their close-woven felts were nearly sword-proof.

The landscape of pre-Roman Britain included prehistoric earthworks referred to as "castles" (*castella*), such as Maiden Castle and Hollingbury Castle. While some have suggested that the *vallum* (a work later associated with Hadrian's Wall) might have been assigned to pre-Roman natives, clear evidence indicates it is a Roman work.

## Transition to Roman Influence
Before the Roman Empire's expansion, Britain was a region that lacked pre-existing great cities, temples, and cultures. The subsequent Roman occupation "Latinized" the region, introducing Roman customs, fashions, and gods, and establishing cities and towns where Latin became the dominant speech.
