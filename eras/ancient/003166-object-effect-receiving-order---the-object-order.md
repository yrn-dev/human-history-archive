# Object and Effect of Receiving Order

In legal and financial contexts, a receiving order is a judicial mechanism used to manage the affairs of a debtor. The primary object of such an order is to bring the debtor and their affairs under the jurisdiction of the court and to protect the debtor's property until the first meeting of creditors takes place.

## Legal Effects and Administration
The immediate effect of a receiving order is to stay all separate actions against the debtor. While the legal title to the property remains with the debtor, the order constitutes the official receiver attached to the court as the receiver of that property. 

In instances where a business or estate requires ongoing management, the official receiver is permitted to appoint a special manager. The remuneration for this manager is determined by the creditors or, if the creditors fail to do so, by the Board of Trade.

## Obligations of the Debtor
A receiving order imposes several strict obligations upon the debtor to ensure a full investigation of their affairs:

*   **Statement of Affairs:** Within a prescribed period, the debtor must submit a statement of affairs to the official receiver. This document must include the nature and value of assets, an accounting for any deficiency, and the names and addresses of creditors, including the amount of their claims and any securities they hold. Any false statement or material omission regarding expenses or losses is considered a misdemeanour under the Debtors Act, unless the debtor can prove there was no intention to defraud. This statement is available for inspection by creditors.
*   **Examination:** The debtor must submit to a public examination in court. This process may involve the trustee, the official receiver, and any creditor who has proved their debt. Evidence provided during this examination may be used against the debtor. Additionally, the court may specially examine the debtor at any time regarding their property or dealings.
*   **Cooperation:** The debtor is required to attend the first meeting of creditors and must wait upon the trustee, special manager, and official receiver to provide all necessary information.

## Enforcement and Restrictions
The court possesses several powers to ensure compliance with a receiving order. If there is reasonable ground to believe the debtor intends to remove goods, destroy papers, or abscond, the debtor may be arrested. Arrest may also occur if the debtor fails to attend a court-ordered examination without good cause. Furthermore, the court may order the post-office to re-address the debtor's letters to the trustee or official receiver for a period of three months.

## Impact on Trustees
A receiving order also has specific implications for those serving as trustees. If a receiving order is made against a trustee, that individual thereby vacates their office.
