# The Holy Ark

The Holy Ark, also referred to as the Ark of the Covenant of God or simply the Ark, was a sacred object of the Israelites. In the Hebrew language, it was called *Eron*.

## Construction and Appearance
The Ark was constructed from a type of wood that was naturally strong and resistant to corruption. Its dimensions were five spans in length, with a breadth and height of three spans each. The entire structure was covered in gold, both internally and externally, ensuring the wooden core remained hidden.

The Ark featured a cover attached by golden hinges, designed to fit evenly without any eminences. Upon this cover were two images known as Cherubims—flying creatures whose forms did not resemble any creatures seen by men, though Moses reported seeing such beings near the throne of God. For transport, the Ark was equipped with two golden rings on each of its longer boards, through which gilt bars passed. This allowed the Ark to be borne on the shoulders of priests, as it was not to be drawn by beasts of burden in a cart.

## Contents and Purpose
The Ark served as the repository for the two tables upon which the ten commandments were written. These were divided with five commandments on each table and two and a half on each side. Moses placed the Ark in the most holy place. Later, during the reign of King Solomon, the Ark was moved into the newly built temple in Jerusalem during the seventh month (known as Thisri by some and Hyperberetoets by the Macedonians). During this transfer, the Ark was placed in the most secret place of the temple between two cherubims, whose wings were framed to embrace and cover it.

## Military Conflicts and the Philistines
The Ark was viewed by the Israelites as a symbol of divine presence and strength. During a conflict with the Philistines, after an initial loss of 4,000 men, the Israelites brought the Ark into their camp. This caused great fear among the Philistines, who believed "God is come into the camp" and recalled the plagues that smote the Egyptians. However, the Israelites' trust in the Ark did not lead to victory; they were defeated in a pitched battle, losing approximately 30,000 footmen. During this defeat, the sons of the high priest, Hophni and Phinehas, were slain, and the Ark was captured and carried away by the Philistines.

## Recovery and Restoration
Following a period of four months with the Philistines, the Ark was returned to the Hebrews. The Philistines utilized a yoke of kine (oxen) to draw the cart carrying the Ark. Upon its return, the Ark arrived at the village of Bethshemesh. There, the wrath of God struck seventy persons dead because they were not priests and were therefore unworthy to touch the Ark.

Acknowledging their unworthiness, the people of Bethshemesh sent word to the public senate of the Israelites. The Ark was subsequently moved to Kirjathjearim and placed in the house of Abinadab, a Levite commended for his religious and righteous life. Abinadab's sons served as the principal curators of the Ark, ministering to the Divine service for twenty years while it remained in Kirjathjearim.

## Textual Corrections and Literary Parallels
In certain historical and rabbinical texts, the Ark appears in descriptions of the destruction of Jerusalem. Analysis of these parallels, specifically within the *Pesikta Rabbati*, has allowed scholars to correct corrupt readings in certain texts. For example, a reference in section vi. 7 that read "the holy Ephod" (*'PWD HQWDSH*) has been corrected to "the holy Ark" (*'RWN HQWDSH*).
