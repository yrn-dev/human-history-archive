## Taxation in Australia
Income tax structures in Queensland and Victoria are detailed as follows:

**Queensland:**
Income tax is levied on personal exertion and property (such as royalties, interest, rents, and income from companies). 
- Property income: Incomes up to £100 are exempt; £100 to £120 pay £1 tax; £120 to £300 have £100 exempt and pay 1s. per pound over £100; incomes over £300 pay 1s. per pound.
- Personal exertion: 10s. for incomes between £100 and £125; £1 between £126 and £150; between £151 and £300, £100 is exempt and 6d. is paid per pound over £100; between £301 and £500, 6d. per pound; between £501 and £1,000, 6d. on the first £500 and 7d. on the remainder; between £1,001 and £1,500, 7d. on the first £1,000 and 8d. on the remainder; incomes over £1,500 pay 8d. per pound.
- Companies and absentees are charged 1s. per pound.

**Victoria:**
The rate for income derived from property is double that of personal exertion. For 1905, personal exertion rates were:
- First £500: 3d. in the pound.
- Second £500: 4d. in the pound.
- Third £500: 5d. in the pound.
- Incomes exceeding £1,500: 6d. in the pound.
- Companies (excluding life insurance) were charged 7d. in the pound, while life insurance companies were charged 8d. in the pound.
