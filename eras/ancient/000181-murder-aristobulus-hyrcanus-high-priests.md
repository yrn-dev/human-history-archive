# The Murder of Aristobulus and Hyrcanus, the High Priests

The deaths of the high priests Aristobulus and Hyrcanus occurred during the reign of King Herod, a period marked by political instability and the consolidation of Herod's power in Judea. These events were part of a broader pattern of executions carried out by Herod against members of the high priesthood and his own inner circle.

## The High Priesthood and its Traditions
According to historical records, the high priesthood began with Aaron, the brother of Moses, and was passed down through his descendants. Custom dictated that only those of the blood of Aaron could hold this dignity; even a king from another stock was barred from the office. From Aaron until Phanas, there were eighty-three high priests. The first thirteen officiated in the wilderness during the time of the tabernacle, while the subsequent eighteen served in Jerusalem from the time of King Solomon until the Babylonian expedition by Nebuchadnezzar, who took the high priest Josadek captive.

## Conflict Between Hyrcanus and Aristobulus
The instability of the priesthood was exacerbated by the rivalry between two brothers, Hyrcanus and Aristobulus. Hyrcanus, described as a pliable weakling, was appointed high priest by his mother, Salome Alexandra. His younger brother, Aristobulus, who was energetic and chafed at his exclusion from office, seized twenty strongholds and led an army toward Jerusalem. Following the death of Alexandra, Hyrcanus agreed to retire in favor of Aristobulus.

The brothers engaged in a battle near Jericho to decide the dispute over the kingdom. During this conflict, many of Hyrcanus's soldiers deserted him for Aristobulus. Hyrcanus fled to a citadel (referred to as Antonia in some accounts), where he held Aristobulus's wife and children as hostages. Eventually, the brothers reached an agreement in the temple, confirmed by oaths and embraces before the multitude. They agreed that Aristobulus would be king, while Hyrcanus would live as a private man, enjoying his estate without interfering in public affairs. They subsequently exchanged houses, with Aristobulus moving into the royal palace.

## Roman Intervention and the Rise of Herod
The discord between the brothers led to the interference of the Romans. Pompey besieged Jerusalem and subsequently appointed Hyrcanus as high priest and ethnarch, though he was denied the title of king. During this period, Pompey entered the temple—a place where only the high priest was permitted—and observed the gold candlestick, table, pouring vessels, censers, and two thousand talents of sacred money. 

Aristobulus and his children were taken to Rome as captives. Later, the Parthians set up Antigonus, the surviving son of Aristobulus, as king. This prompted Herod, an Idumaean, to seek the help of the Romans to seat himself on the throne of Judea in 37 B.C.

## The Executions Under King Herod
Upon establishing his rule, Herod manipulated the high priesthood to secure his position and quiet family troubles. He removed Ananelus from the high priesthood. Ananelus was a friend of Herod and a descendant of the high priests who had been carried captive to Babylonia. Herod's action was considered unlawful, as it was rare for someone to be deprived of the dignity once they had attained it; previous instances included Antiochus Epiphanes (who deprived Jesus in favor of Onias) and Aristobulus (who took the dignity from his brother).

Herod appointed the young Aristobulus as high priest in place of Ananelus. However, this appointment was a precursor to his death; Herod took care that Aristobulus be murdered shortly after his appointment. Following this, Herod slew Hyrcanus. After the execution of Hyrcanus, Herod hastened to Caesar to obtain the kingdom from him.

Herod's campaign of violence extended beyond the high priests. He also executed Sohemus, Mariamne the Queen, Alexandra, Costobarus, his most intimate friends, and the sons of Babbas.
