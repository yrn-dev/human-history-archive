# Sin-na-di-in-shu-mi and Bel-na-di-in-shu-mi

## Etymology and Meaning
Sin-na-di-in-shu-mi and Bel-na-di-in-shu-mi are proper names found within Babylonian-Assyrian business documents. These names serve as linguistic models for a specific class of names where a deity is placed as the first element, followed by the term MU-MU. 

The meaning of these specific names is translated as "Sin is the giver of a name" and "Bel is the giver of a name," respectively. In this context, the phrase "giver of a name" refers to the granting of offspring.

## Linguistic Significance in the Khammurabi Period
These names are of particular value to historians and linguists studying the Khammurabi period, a time when phonetic writing was the prevailing fashion. Because business documents from this era often list interested parties, scribes, or witnesses, they provide a large volume of proper names. 

The archive's sources indicate that these names often appear in variant readings. A single name might be written phonetically in one instance and ideographically in another. By comparing these phonetic and ideographic versions, researchers can draw reliable conclusions about the correct reading of other names belonging to the same group.

## Related Deities
The names Sin-na-di-in-shu-mi and Bel-na-di-in-shu-mi reference deities who were part of a broader religious structure. Sin is identified as the moon-god and was associated with Ningal of Ur. Bel is another deity whose traits were eventually absorbed into the eclectic power of Marduk. 

Marduk, originally a solar deity, became a composite figure by taking on the attributes of several chief gods from older centers, including Sin, Bel, Adad (or Ramman), Nergal, Shamash, and Ea. Other deities associated with these centers included Damkina of Eridu, Tashmit of Borsippa, Allatu of Kutha, and Shala.

## Comparison with Other Historical Names
The process of using variant readings to decipher names is also applied to other historical figures. For example, the name of King Nebuchadrezzar appears in several different forms:

* AK-DU-u-[s.]u-ur
* AK-ku-dur-ri-SHES
* PA-GAR-DU-SHES

Through these variations, it is determined that PA or AK (with the deity determinative AN) represents Na-bi-um (or Nebo), GAR-DU or DU represents kudurri (meaning "boundary" or "territory"), and SHES represents u[s.][s.]ur (the imperative of nasaru, meaning "protect"). Consequently, the name Nebuchadrezzar signifies, "O, Nebo! protect my boundary" (or "my territory").
