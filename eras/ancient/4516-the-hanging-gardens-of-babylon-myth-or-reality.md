# The Hanging Gardens of Babylon: Myth or Reality?

Among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, the Hanging Gardens of Babylon remain the most enigmatic. While the Pyramids of Giza still stand and the Lighthouse of Alexandria is documented through archaeological fragments, the Gardens are the only wonder whose physical location has never been definitively established. This has led to a centuries-long debate among historians: were they a magnificent engineering feat of the Neo-Babylonian Empire, or a poetic fabrication?

### Overview
According to classical accounts—primarily those of Berossus, Diodorus Siculus, and Strabo—the gardens were built by King Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BCE) for his wife, Amytis of Media, who longed for the green mountains of her homeland. The descriptions depict a series of tiered terraces supporting a lush variety of flora, irrigated by a sophisticated system of pumps and screws that lifted water from the Euphrates River.

However, a significant historical problem exists: there is no mention of such gardens in the extensive cuneiform records of Nebuchadnezzar’s own administration. While the king boasted of his walls and temples, he remained silent on the gardens. This discrepancy has led some scholars to conclude that the gardens were a "literary topoi"—a romanticized ideal of an Eastern paradise created by Greek writers.

In recent decades, a provocative alternative theory has emerged. Dr. Stephanie Dalley of Oxford University proposes that the gardens were not in Babylon at all, but in Nineveh, built by the Assyrian King Sennacherib (r. 704–681 BCE). Dalley argues that ancient texts and reliefs from Nineveh describe a palace with advanced irrigation and "hanging" greenery that matches the classical descriptions far more accurately than the ruins of Babylon.

### Key Facts
*   **The Irrigation Puzzle:** If the gardens existed, they required an advanced hydraulic system to fight gravity, potentially utilizing the "Archimedes screw" centuries before Archimedes.
*   **The Location Debate:** Historians are split between the "Babylon Theory" (traditional), the "Nineveh Theory" (modern archaeological), and the "Myth Theory" (literary).
*   **The Flora:** Descriptions suggest a multi-layered forest of exotic trees and shrubs, creating a mountain-like silhouette in the flat Mesopotamian plain.

### Did You Know?
1. **Missing Records:** Despite thousands of clay tablets recovered from Babylon, not a single contemporary Babylonian text mentions the "Hanging Gardens."
2. **The "Hanging" Misnomer:** The gardens weren't actually hanging by ropes; the Greek word *kremastos* refers to plants overhanging a terrace or balcony.
3. **The Nineveh Evidence:** Archaeological excavations at Nineveh have revealed a massive system of canals and aqueducts that could have supported such a garden.
