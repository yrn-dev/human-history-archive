# Hippo and Bona

## Overview and Identification
Bona, also known as Annaba (meaning "the city of jujube trees"), is a seaport in Algeria located at 36 deg. 53' N., 7 deg. 46' E. It is situated on a bay of the Mediterranean at the foot of the wooded heights of Edugh. Bona is identified as the ancient *Aphrodisium*, which served as the seaport for the city of Hippo Regius (also known as *Ubbo*). The modern town derives its name from Hippo.

## Ancient Hippo
Hippo Regius was named "Royal" by the Romans because it served as a favorite residence for the Numidian kings. During the first three centuries of the Christian era, it was among the richest cities in Roman Africa. The city is most noted for its connection to St Augustine, who served as its bishop and priest for thirty-five years.

In 431, the city was captured by the Vandals under Genseric following a siege that lasted fourteen months; St Augustine died during this period. While general destruction occurred, Augustine's library, manuscripts, and the cathedral were spared. The town was later partially restored by Belisarius, though it was sacked again by the Arabs in the 7th century. Today, the ruins of Hippo—which include fragments of walls and restored large cisterns—lie approximately one mile south of the modern town. A large basilica dedicated to St Augustine, featuring a chancel facing west, was opened in 1900 on the hill where Hippo once stood, and a bronze statue of the saint stands upon an altar among the ruins.

## Development of Bona
The city of Bona was built by the Arabs and remained for centuries under the control of the rulers of Tunis, who constructed the Kasbah (citadel) in 1300. Between the beginning of the 14th century and the middle of the 15th century, the city was frequented by Spaniards and Italians. In the 16th century, Charles V held the city and strengthened the citadel. Subsequent control passed between Algerines, Tunisians, and the Genoese.

French influence grew through the *Compagnie d'Afrique*, which maintained active trade from the time of Louis XIV until the Revolution. The French occupied the town for a few months in 1830 and reoccupied it in 1832 after Captains Yusuf and Armandy seized the Kasbah with a small force of marines. Since then, Bona has experienced industrial development, particularly after the discovery of phosphate beds at Tebessa in 1883.

## Urban Layout and Architecture
Bona has the character of a flourishing French city with wide, well-laid-out streets, some of which are very steep. The town is enclosed by a modern rampart built outside the original Arab wall, which had become too small for the city's growth. A broad, tree-lined promenade called the Cours Jerome-Bertagna (formerly the Cours National) runs through the center, housing hotels, banks, and a theatre. 

Key landmarks include:
* **The Cathedral of St Augustine:** A large quasi-Byzantine church at the northern end of the promenade. It houses a relic believed to be the right arm of St Augustine, brought from Pavia in 1842.
* **The Grand Mosque:** Located on one side of the Place d'Armes (the chief square), this structure was built using ruins from ancient Hippo.
* **The Kasbah:** The citadel situated on a hill to the north-east.
* **Public Facilities:** The town contains civil and military hospitals and barracks capable of accommodating 3,000 men.

## Economy and Infrastructure
Bona is a major center of commerce, ranking third in Algeria after Oran and Algiers. It maintains direct steamship communication with Marseilles. The city exports wool, sheep, phosphates, cork, barley, zinc, iron, and esparto, while importing manufactures and general merchandise. Local industry includes the production of tapestry, leather, and native garments.

The town's harbor consists of an inner harbor covering 25 acres with quays for vessels drawing 22 ft., and a spacious outer harbor built between 1857 and 1868 and enlarged between 1905 and 1907. To improve sanitary conditions, a system of canals was used to drain the marshes at the mouths of the Bujema and Seybuse rivers. The town's water supply is sourced from the Edugh hills, and the surrounding vicinity contains marble quarries and cork woods.
