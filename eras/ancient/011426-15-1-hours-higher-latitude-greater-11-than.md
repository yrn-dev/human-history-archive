# Solar Heating and Day Length in Higher Latitudes

## Solar Heating and Latitude
The temperature of areas located north of the tropic of Cancer is influenced significantly by the length of the day. While the mid-day altitude of the sun is smaller in higher latitudes—resulting in a loss of heat—this is more than compensated for by the increased length of the day. 

For example, on June 21st, the difference in the heating power of the sun's rays at noon between latitude 20 deg. and latitude 45 deg. is only approximately 2%. However, the accumulated heat received throughout the day is significantly different. In the higher latitude, the day is lengthened to 15-1/2 hours, making the accumulated heat about 11% greater than in the lower latitude, where the day consists of only 13-1/4 hours.

## Temperature Variations in Asia
Asia exhibits extreme temperature ranges, containing some of the highest and lowest temperatures on the globe. In southern Asia, the highest mean temperature attained is not much above 82 deg. Fahr. In contrast, the north coast of eastern Siberia has a mean temperature only a few degrees above 0 deg. Fahr.

The isothermals of mean annual temperature across northern Asia generally follow regular curves. In these regions, a reduction of 1 deg. of latitude corresponds to a rise of approximately 1/2 deg. Fahr., provided modifications due to elevation are excluded. This pattern holds up to approximately 30 deg. N, where the mean temperature is about 75 deg. Fahr.; south of this point, the increase in temperature occurs more slowly.

## Modifying Factors and Atmospheric Effects
While day length and latitude provide a main outline for temperature observations, other factors influence the climate. In the tropical region, periodical summer rains act as a very important modifying cause, as they tend to greatly arrest the increase of summer heat.

Atmospheric events can also distribute materials across various latitudes. For instance, following a volcanic eruption, fine dust was carried by aerial currents. Within the tropics, these particles moved from east to west at an estimated rate of about 73 m. an hour. Within six weeks, this dust diffused over nearly the entire space between latitude 30° N. and 45° S., eventually spreading to Europe, Asia, North and South America, Australasia, and from the Cape of Good Hope to the north of Scandinavia.

## Meteorological Laws and Geography
In the Northern Hemisphere, Buys Ballot's Law is approximately true in the higher latitudes. This meteorological rule states that if one stands with their back to the wind, the low-pressure area will be on the left-hand side. This is a consequence of Ferrel's Law. In low latitudes, the angle between the wind and the barometric gradient is not a right angle, and in the Southern Hemisphere, the law is reversed.
