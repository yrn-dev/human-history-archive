# The Epistle of the Alexandrine Teacher

## Overview and Authorship
The epistle is identified as the earliest of the Apostolic Fathers. It was addressed to a group of Churches, or a single Church, that the writer had recently visited. The author does not present himself as an authoritative "teacher" in his tone, preferring to write as a friend who has come to love the recipients. However, the writer does belong to the class of "teachers" possessing a recognized spiritual gift, or *charisma*, similar to those mentioned in the *Didache*. Because of this status, the author feels entitled to present his *gnosis* with a claim to a deferential hearing.

## Origin and Classification
While the epistle lacks a specific address, it is styled as a "general epistle" by Origen. Evidence suggests it was not written to Alexandria itself, but was instead written by a teacher of the Alexandrine Church to a body of Christians located in Lower Egypt whom the author had recently visited.

## Dating and Debate
The date of the epistle has been a subject of significant debate. One primary view, supported by Lightfoot's reading of the apocalyptic passage in chapter iv (with a slight modification proposed by Sir W. M. Ramsay), concludes that the text dates to the reign of Vespasian, between A.D. 70 and 79. 

A main counter-view argues for a date around A.D. 130. However, proponents of the earlier date argue that the A.D. 130 position cannot provide a natural account of the apocalyptic passage in chapter iv and misconstrues the reference in chapter xvi regarding the building of the spiritual temple, which refers to the Christian Church.

## Central Themes
The central problem addressed in the epistle is the relationship between Christianity and Judaism. Specifically, it examines the Old and New forms of a Covenant which, being Divine, must in some sense remain the same. The early Church found this issue troublesome and was unable to solve it satisfactorily, partly due to the absence of a theory regarding the gradual development of the race's education.
