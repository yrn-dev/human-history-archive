# Studia Biblica

*Studia Biblica* is a scholarly publication cited across various academic disciplines, including Christian archaeology, biblical textual criticism, and the study of ancient versions of sacred texts.

## Contributions to Christian Archaeology and Ritual
The archive's records indicate that *Studia Biblica* has served as a venue for research into early Christian practices. Specifically, volume v contains an essay by Rogers focusing on Baptism and Christian Archaeology. This research intersects with historical accounts of the status of the baptizer in the early church. While official church standards generally restricted the right to confer baptism to bishops, priests, or presbyters—and reserved confirmation or the seal for bishops alone—exceptions existed. 

In the Montanist churches, women performed baptisms. Historical traces of this practice are also found in the earliest church and the Caucasus, exemplified by St. Thekla, who baptized herself in her own blood, and St. Nino, the female evangelist of Georgia, who baptized King Mirian. Additionally, the archive notes that in instances where death was imminent, or in the case of new-born children, a woman or a layman could perform the baptism.

## Textual Criticism and Biblical Versions
*Studia Biblica* has been utilized in the critical analysis of various biblical manuscripts and versions. 

### The Peshito and Syriac Texts
The publication has featured research on the Peshito, a Syriac version of the New Testament. While G.H. Gwilliam provided a text of the Peshito in 1901, an article by F.H. Woods in *Studia Biblica* (volume iii, pp. 105-138) on the same subject was later rendered out of date by the work of F.C. Burkitt regarding St. Ephraim's quotations from the Gospel. Furthermore, Gwilliam contributed to the understanding of the Karkaphensian—a Syriac "Massorah" or collection of notes on New Testament texts likely originating from the monastery of the Skull—through materials published in *Studia Biblica* (specifically pp. 60-63).

### Old Latin Manuscripts
The journal has also contributed to the study of Old Latin manuscripts. Specifically, Wordsworth and Sanday published work regarding the "Corbey S. James" in the first volume of *Studia Biblica* in 1885. This contributes to the broader history of Old Latin biblical texts and the general field of textual criticism.
