# London (1885-1893)

The period between 1885 and 1893 in London was marked by significant activity in the fields of historical scholarship, academic appointments, linguistics, and political candidacy. ## Academic and Professional Appointments
London's educational institutions provided platforms for various scholars. Edward Spencer Beesly, an English historian and positivist, held positions as a professor of history at University College, London (appointed 1859) and as a professor of Latin at Bedford College, London (appointed 1860). Beesly eventually resigned these appointments in 1893 and 1889, respectively. In 1893, he became the editor of the *Positivist Review*.

In the realm of science and botany, Sir William Turner Thiselton-Dyer served as the Director of the Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew, from 1885 to 1905.

## Political Activity
The mid-1880s saw political efforts by academic figures in the London area. Professor Edward Spencer Beesly stood as a Liberal candidate for two different constituencies: he ran for Westminster in 1885 and for Marylebone in 1886, though both attempts were unsuccessful.
