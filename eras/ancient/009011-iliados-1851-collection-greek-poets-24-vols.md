# Allegoriae Iliados (1851) and Collection of Greek Poets

The *Allegoriae Iliados* (1851) and the *Collection of Greek Poets* are scholarly works produced by the French classical scholar Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie. These publications are part of a broader body of work dedicated to the study of Greek literature and the preservation of classical and Byzantine texts.

## Author Biography
Jean Francois Boissonade de Fontarabie (1774–1857) was born in Paris on August 12, 1774. His early career began in 1792 within the public service under the administration of General Dumouriez. Although he was driven from this service in 1795, he was later restored by Lucien Bonaparte, serving as the secretary to the prefecture of the Upper Marne. 

Following a definitive resignation from public employment, Boissonade devoted himself to the study of the Greek language. This academic focus led to several prestigious appointments:
* **1809:** Deputy professor of Greek at the faculty of letters in Paris.
* **1813:** Titular professor, succeeding P.H. Larcher.
* **1828:** Successor to J.B. Gail in the chair of Greek at the College de France.

In addition to his teaching roles, he served as the perpetual secretary of the Academie des Inscriptions and held the position of librarian of the Bibliotheque du Roi. He died on September 8, 1857.

## Literary Contributions and Greek Scholarship
Boissonade's scholarly attention was primarily directed toward later Greek literature. His extensive bibliography includes works on various authors and texts, such as:
* **Philostratus:** *Heroica* (1806) and *Epistolae* (1842).
* **Marinus:** *Vita procli* (1814).
* **Tiberius Rhetor:** *De Figuris* (1815).
* **Nicetas Eugenianus:** *Drosilla et Charicles* (1819).
* **Herodian:** *Partitiones* (1819).
* **Aristaenetus:** *Epistolae* (1822).
* **Eunapius:** *Vitae Sophistarum* (1822).
* **Babrius:** *Fables* (1844).

Among these contributions are the *Allegoriae Iliados*, published in 1851, and a comprehensive *Collection of Greek Poets* spanning 24 volumes. 

Beyond these specific literary studies, Boissonade produced the *Anecdota Nova* (1844) and the *Anecdota Graeca* (1829–1833). These two works are noted for their importance to the study of Greek grammarians and Byzantine history.
