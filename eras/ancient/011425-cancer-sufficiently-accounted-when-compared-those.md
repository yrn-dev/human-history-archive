# The Tropic of Cancer and Related Observations

## Thermal Characteristics of the Tropic of Cancer
The area located north of the tropic of Cancer experiences very high summer temperatures. When compared with the temperatures observed south of the tropic, these are sufficiently accounted for by the increased length of the day in the higher latitude. This increased duration more than compensates for the loss of heat resulting from the smaller mid-day altitude of the sun.

For example, on the 21st of June, the difference in the heating power of the sun's rays at noon between latitude 20 deg. and latitude 45 deg. is only approximately 2%. However, the accumulated heat received during the day is about 11% greater in the higher latitude, where the day is lengthened to 15-1/2 hours, compared to the lower latitude, where the day consists of only 13-1/4 hours.

In the tropical region, the periodical summer rains serve as a very important modifying cause. These rains tend to greatly arrest the increase of summer heat in the areas where they prevail, giving the heat altogether peculiar characteristics.

## Geographical and Astronomical Observations
Historical records indicate an extension of geographical and astronomical observations over the four centuries between Herodotus and Diodorus. Diodorus (iii, 40) utilized correct language regarding the direction of shadows when located southward of the tropic of Cancer, a point of comparison also noted by Pliny (H. N. vi, 29).

## Medical Contexts of Cancer
The term "cancer" appears in various medical and historical contexts within the archive:

### Clinical Manifestations and Causes
Cancer is identified as a cause of ascites, which is the effusion of non-inflammatory fluid within the peritoneum. Specifically, portal obstruction—the commonest cause of well-marked ascites—can be produced by diseases within the liver, such as cancer, or diseases outside the liver, such as cancer of the pancreas, duodenum, or stomach, which cause pressure on the portal vein. Additionally, in aged patients, cancer can cause chronic peritonitis, which may lead to ascites.

### Historical Medical Cases
In May 1887, the crown prince of Germany (later emperor Frederick III) was attended to by several physicians. On May 18th, physicians including Tobold, Karl Gerhardt, and E. von Bergmann diagnosed the prince's throat ailment as cancer of the throat. However, Sir Morell Mackenzie, a specialist in diseases of the throat, disagreed with this diagnosis. Based on a microscopical examination of tissue performed by R. Virchow, Mackenzie insisted the disease was not demonstrably cancerous and suggested the growth might be benign and curable. This distinction was of political importance because, under the family law of the Hohenzollerns, it was doubted whether an individual suffering from an incapacitating disease like cancer could occupy the German throne.
