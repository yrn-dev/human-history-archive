# The Assyrian Invasion of Egypt and the Defeat of Tirhaka

## The Campaign of Esar-haddon
In February 674 B.C., the Assyrians began an invasion of Egypt. This campaign escalated in Nisan (or March) 670 B.C., when an expedition of an unusually large scale departed from Nineveh. The Assyrian forces crossed the Egyptian frontier on the 3rd of Tammuz (June).

## The Defeat of Tirhaka
Tirhaka led the Egyptian forces against the invading Assyrian army. Over a period of fifteen days of continuous fighting, the Egyptians were defeated three times, suffering heavy losses. During these engagements, Tirhaka himself was wounded. As a result of these defeats, Tirhaka was driven back to Memphis.

## The Fall of Memphis and Aftermath
The victorious Assyrian army entered Memphis on the 22nd of the month, forcing Tirhaka to flee to the south. To commemorate this victory, a stele was erected at Sinjirli, north of the Gulf of Antioch; this monument represents Tirhaka with the features of a negro and is currently housed in the Berlin Museum.

## Subsequent Revolts and Political Shifts
Egypt revolted two years later, in 668 B.C. While marching to suppress this rebellion, the Assyrian king Esar-haddon fell ill and died on the 10th of Marchesvan (October). He was succeeded by Assur-bani-pal, while his brother, Samas-sum-yukin, was appointed as the viceroy of Babylonia. This administrative arrangement was intended to provide the Babylonians with a semblance of independence to ensure their contentment. However, the plan failed as Samas-sum-yukin eventually led a revolt that shook the Assyrian empire. During the ensuing years of struggle, Egypt succeeded in recovering its independence.

## Other Assyrian Interactions with Egypt
Separate records indicate that the Assyrian king Sennacherib also attempted to campaign against Egypt. Sennacherib spent a significant amount of time besieging Pelusium and had constructed banks of great height against the city walls. However, he abandoned the siege and returned home without success upon receiving news that Tirhaka, the king of the Ethiopians, was marching through the desert with great forces to aid the Egyptians.
