# The Roman As

The *as* was a Roman coin that experienced significant fluctuations in weight and value across different historical periods. Its development reflects the evolution of the Roman economy, moving from primitive exchange mediums to a standardized coinage system.

## Origins and Early Form
The introduction of coined money in Rome is attributed to Servius Tullius. The earliest form of the *as* was known as *as libralis*, composed of *aes*, a mixed metal alloy consisting of lead, tin, and copper. Nominally, the *as libralis* weighed 12 ounces (1 lb.), although its actual weight was 10 ounces. 

In its primitive stage, the coin took the shape of an irregular oblong bar. These bars were stamped with figures of a sow, ox, or sheep. The use of these animal figures, combined with the word for money, *pecunia* (derived from *pecus*, meaning cattle), indicates that cattle served as the earliest medium of exchange in Italy. During this period, the value of the coin was indicated by other marks or little globules and points. This original oblong coinage is referred to in the archive as *aes rude*.

## Evolution of Design and Weight
Once the round shape for the coin was introduced, the design became standardized: one side featured the double head of Janus, while the other side depicted a ship's prow. Subdivisions of the *as* followed a similar pattern, featuring the ship's prow on one side and the head of a deity on the other.

The weight of the *as* was reduced several times due to economic pressures:
* **First Punic War:** The treasury was exhausted, leading to a reduction of the *as* to 2 oz.
* **Second Punic War:** The weight was halved again to 1 oz.
* **Papirian Law (89 B.C.):** The coin was further reduced to a diminutive weight of half an ounce.
* **Era of Octavian, Lepidus, and Antony:** The weight was reduced further to 1/3 of an ounce.
* **Emperor Severus:** The weight was lowered again to approximately 5/24 of an ounce.

Throughout the empire and the commonwealth, the term *aes grave* was used to distinguish the old, heavier *as* from the existing depreciated coins.

## Value and Depreciation
The value of the *as* declined sharply over time. Before the introduction of silver coinage in 269 B.C., the value of the *as* was approximately 6d. By the time of Cicero, the value had plummeted to less than a halfpenny.
