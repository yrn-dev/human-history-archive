# Uru-Malik

Uru-Malik was a historical figure who served as a governor during the period of the Babylonian empire. According to archival documents, specifically those relating to a cadastral survey, he held the position of governor of the land of the Amorites. 

## Regional Administration and Geography
The land of the Amorites, over which Uru-Malik presided, was the term used by the Babylonians to refer to the regions of Palestine and Syria. His administration occurred during the era of the Akkadian empire, specifically under the reign of Naram-Sin, the son and successor of Sargon. 

## Origins and Nomenclature
The name Uru-Malik is noted in historical records as appearing to indicate that he was of Canaanitish origin.

## Historical Context
Uru-Malik served during a period of significant imperial expansion and administrative development. Naram-Sin, the ruler of the time, expanded his influence by marching into Magan and taking its king captive. Naram-Sin adopted the imperial title "king of the four zones" and was addressed as a god, specifically "the god of Agad" (Akkad). 

The Babylonian state during this era was characterized by several advanced systems:
* **Infrastructure:** The empire was integrated through a network of roads that supported a regular postal service.
* **Administration:** The use of clay seals, which functioned as stamps, was common; examples bearing the names of Sargon and Naram-Sin are preserved in the Louvre.
* **Land Management:** The mention of Uru-Malik is found within documents pertaining to a cadastral survey.
* **Intellectual Pursuits:** It is probable that Sargon established a library for which the first collection of terrestrial omens and astronomical observations was compiled.
