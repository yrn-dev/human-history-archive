# Military Command and Army Organization in History

The history of military engagement is characterized by the assembly of diverse forces, the strategic appointment of commanders, and the implementation of specific tactical equipment to counter enemy strengths. Across various eras and regions, the effectiveness of an army has often depended on its numerical strength, the quality of its appointments, and the leadership of its generals.

## Republican Forces in Colombia
In the early 19th century, Simón Bolívar led the republican forces during the struggle for independence in South America. Following the enactment of the fundamental law on December 17, 1819, which united the republics of New Granada and Venezuela into the Republic of Colombia under his presidency, Bolívar returned to the field. At this time, he commanded the most numerous and best appointed army the republicans had assembled to date.

Using this force, Bolívar gained significant advantages over the Spanish forces led by Morillo. Although a six-month armistice was concluded at Truxillo on November 25, 1820, in hopes of sparing further bloodshed, the agreement expired after Morillo was replaced by General Torre. Bolívar subsequently delivered a decisive blow at the battle of Carabobo, routing the Spaniards and forcing the remnants of their army to take refuge in Puerto Cabello, where they eventually surrendered to Paez two years later. This victory effectively ended the war in Venezuela, allowing Bolívar to enter Caracas on June 29, 1821.

## Ancient Greek and Persian Engagements
Military history in Greece and Asia Minor highlights the role of mercenary forces and the strategic use of terrain. Pelopidas, marching into Thessaly against the despot Alexander, encountered an enemy force that was a well-appointed mercenary army, superior in number. The battle centered on the hills known as Kynos Kephalæ (the Dog’s Heads). While Pelopidas' cavalry initially succeeded, his infantry was repulsed twice. It was only after Pelopidas personally led a third attack that his discouraged troops drove the enemy from the summit, causing Alexander's army to retire in disorder.

In other conflicts, such as those involving Derkyllidas, the coordination of land and sea forces was paramount. Derkyllidas marched into Karia to attack Tissaphernes, cooperating with a Lacedæmonian fleet under Pharax. Tissaphernes and Pharnabazus, acting as satraps, commanded a powerful force consisting of Grecian and Karian troops supported by numerous Persian cavalry. During a surprise encounter, Derkyllidas organized his hoplites into a battle array eight deep, with peltasts and horsemen on the flanks, though the surprise caused alarm among the Ionian and insular hoplites.

## Persian Army Structure and Equipment
The Persian military utilized a highly structured hierarchy and specialized equipment for different national contingents. Foot-soldiers were organized by divisions and numbered, with a clear chain of command: commanders of tens of thousands appointed commanders of thousands, who in turn appointed commanders of hundreds and tens.

Equipment varied by the origin of the troops:
* **The Mares:** Wore native helmets of plaited work, small hide shields, and carried javelins.
* **The Colchians:** Utilized wooden helmets, short spears, knives, and small shields made of raw ox-hide.
* **Island Tribes:** Those from the Erythraian Sea, known as the "Removed," used arms and clothing similar to those of the Medes.

## Roman and Israelite Military Leadership
In Rome, during a pressing crisis, the Senate and people appointed Camillus as dictator for a fifth time. Despite being nearly eighty years old, Camillus levied an army and implemented specific defensive innovations to counter the Gauls. Recognizing that the Gauls relied on heavy sword blows to the head and shoulders, Camillus equipped his soldiers with smooth iron helmets to make swords glance off and fitted brass rims to their shields to prevent the wood from breaking. He also trained his troops to use long pikes to intercept enemy sword-cuts.

In the history of the Israelites, the general Joab commanded a "mighty and strong army" during the pursuit of Sheba, the son of Bichri. During the siege of a metropolis of the Israelites, a wise woman convinced the citizens to surrender Sheba to avoid the depopulation of their city. After the head of Sheba was thrown into Joab's army, the siege was raised. Upon returning to Jerusalem, Joab was again appointed general of all the people.
