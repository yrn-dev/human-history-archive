# Bibliorum Sacrorum Latinae Versiones Antiquae

The *Bibliorum Sacrorum Latinae Versiones Antiquae* is a work by Sabatier that focuses on the ancient Latin versions of sacred texts. A primary component of this work is the presentation of the Latin versions of the Book of Baruch.

## The Latin Versions of the Book of Baruch
The archive identifies two distinct Latin versions of the Book of Baruch. The first is the version found in the Vulgate, which dates to a period prior to Jerome and is characterized as being tolerably literal. 

The second Latin version was first published in 1688 by Jos. Maria Caro. This version was subsequently reprinted by Sabatier in the *Bibliorum Sacrorum Latinae Versiones Antiquae*, where it was placed side by side with the ante-Hieronymian version. In comparison to the earlier Vulgate version, this later version is founded upon the preceding one and is less literal.

## Context of the Book of Baruch
The canonicity of the Book of Baruch has varied across different traditions and eras. It was never accepted as canonical by the Palestinian Jews, as noted in Baba Batra 14^b. While the *Apostolic Constitutions* (v. 10) claim the book was read in public worship on the 10th day of the month Gorpiaeus, this statement is regarded as hardly correct. 

Within the church, the book was in general use until its canonicity was rejected by the Protestant churches. Conversely, it was accepted by the Roman church at the council of Trent.

## Other Versions of the Text
Beyond the Latin translations, other versions of the Book of Baruch exist:
* **Syriac and Arabic:** Versions of the text printed in the London Polyglot are described as literal.
* **Hexaplar-Syriac:** A version produced by Paul, the bishop of Tella, at the beginning of the 7th century, which has been published by Ceriani.
