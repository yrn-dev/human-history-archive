# Herod's Accusations Against His Sons at Berytus

## Background of Familial Conflict
The tension between Herod and the sons of Mariamne stemmed from a legacy of hatred inherited from their mother. The sons viewed Herod as an enemy, a sentiment that intensified during their education in Rome and upon their return to Judea. As they reached marriageable age, one son married the daughter of Salome—who had been the accuser of their mother—while the other married the daughter of Archelaus, the king of Cappadocia.

The sons became bold in their speech and harbored hatred in their minds. This boldness led others to calumniate them, informing Herod that both sons were engaged in treacherous designs against him. Specifically, the son-in-law of Archelaus was accused of preparing to flee to Caesar to accuse Herod. In response to these reports, Herod restored Antipater (his son by Doris) to favor to serve as a defense against his other sons.

## The Role of Eurycles
Herod eventually ordered his sons to be kept in custody, despite finding the proofs against them to be too weak. This action was heavily influenced by Eurycles, whom the archive describes as a "pest" of the family and the "forger of all this vile accusation." For his role in these events, Herod rewarded Eurycles with fifty talents. 

Following this reward, Eurycles traveled to Cappadocia to obtain money from Archelaus, falsely claiming he had reconciled Herod to Alexander. He later traveled to Greece, where he was twice accused before Caesar of plundering cities and filling Achaia with sedition, resulting in his banishment.

## The Trial at Berytus
Acting on directions from Caesar, Herod traveled to Berytus to assemble a court. The judicature was presided over by Saturninus and Pedanius, as appointed by Caesar's letters, along with their lieutenants and the procurator Volumnius. Other attendees included Herod's friends and kinsmen, including Pheroras and Salome, as well as the principal men of Syria. Herod excluded Archelaus from the proceedings due to a suspicion arising from Archelaus's status as Alexander's father-in-law.

Herod employed a cunning strategy by not producing his sons in open court. He believed that if they appeared, they would be pitied, and if they were allowed to speak, Alexander would easily answer the accusations. Instead, the sons remained in custody at Platane, a village of the Sidontans.

## Testimony and Condemnation
During the proceedings, Euaratus of Cos, an intimate friend of Alexander, attempted to defend the accused. Euaratus testified under oath that he had never heard the things Alexander was accused of from the young men. However, this testimony failed to clear them. Herod was only disposed to listen to evidence that supported the accusations, and those who expressed indignation toward the sons and believed them guilty were most agreeable to him.

Despite not being produced before the courts, the sons were condemned. Shortly thereafter, they were sent to Sebaste, where they were strangled.
