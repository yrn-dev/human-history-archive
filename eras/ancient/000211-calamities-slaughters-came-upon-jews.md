# The Calamities And Slaughters That Came Upon The Israelites

The history of the Israelites is marked by various periods of intense violence, massacres, and natural disasters occurring across different regions, including Judea, Mesopotamia, Egypt, and Syria. These events often stemmed from ethnic tensions, political instability, or the actions of provincial governors and foreign armies.

## Massacres in Cesarea and Syria
In Cesarea, a sudden and coordinated slaughter occurred in which the people of the city killed the Israelites living among them. This event took place within a single hour, resulting in the deaths of over twenty thousand Israelites. Following this, the city was emptied of its Israelite inhabitants, as Florus captured those attempting to flee and sent them in bonds to the galleys.

This event provoked a violent reaction from the nation, which divided into several parties to lay waste to neighboring cities and villages. The Israelites attacked and plundered various locations, including Philadelphia, Sebonitis, Gerasa, Pella, Scythopolis, Gadara, and Hippos. They further destroyed or set fire to cities in Gaulonitis and attacked Kedasa, Ptolemais, Gaba, and Cesarea. Sebaste and Askelon were attacked with violence and burnt to the ground, while Anthedon and Gaza were entirely demolished. These campaigns were accompanied by an immense slaughter of the men caught in the surrounding villages.

## Conflict in Mesopotamia and Babylonia
In Babylonia, the Israelites faced significant hardship due to a deep-seated hatred from the Babylonians, fueled by the contrariety of their laws. After the ruin of a party led by Anileus, the Babylonians attacked the Israelites. Unable to fight or coexist with them, the Israelites fled to Seleucia, the principal city of the region. While they lived there for five years without misfortune, a pestilence struck Babylon in the sixth year, leading to further removals of inhabitants and a heavier calamity for the Israelites.

A particularly severe disaster befell the Israelites in Mesopotamia and Babylonia, described as greater than any previous record. This crisis was linked to the cities of Neerda and Nisibis, which the Israelites used as treasuries to deposit the half shekel offered to God and other devoted items before they were transmitted to Jerusalem. The security of these donations was threatened by the ravages of the Parthians, to whom the Babylonians were then subject.

## Persecutions in Alexandria and Damascus
In Alexandria, a violent crackdown occurred when two Roman legions and five thousand soldiers from Libya were sent to suppress those seeking innovations. The soldiers entered the district called Delta, where the Israelite population lived. Despite a period of resistance by the best-armed Israelites, the Roman forces eventually destroyed them unmercifully. The slaughter included infants and the aged, leaving fifty thousand dead in heaps and the area overflowed with blood. The killing only ceased when Alexander commiserated with their condition and ordered the Romans to retire, though the local populace of Alexandria continued to exhibit great hatred toward the Israelites.

Similarly, in Damascus, the local population took advantage of the destruction of the Romans to slaughter the Israelites residing there. The Israelites had already been cooped up in a place of public exercises due to suspicion. To prevent their own wives—many of whom were addicted to the Israelite religion—from interfering, the attackers concealed their plans. In one hour, ten thousand unarmed Israelites were killed by having their throats cut.

## Military Defeats and Natural Disasters
Under the reign of Herod, the Israelites faced military setbacks during conflicts with the Arabians. Although Herod initially conquered an army near Diespous, a subsequent engagement at Kanatha in Celesyria turned disastrous. Despite Herod's orders to build a wall around the camp, the multitude attacked the Arabians prematurely. This led to a counter-attack by the Arabians and the forces of Athenio, one of Cleopatra's generals. Herod's men were routed, and a great slaughter ensued. Those who escaped to Ormiza were surrounded and captured along with their camp.

Additionally, a providential calamity struck in the seventh and twenty-ninth year of Herod's reign. During the spring, an earthquake occurred that destroyed thirty thousand men and an immense number of cattle, though the army escaped harm as it was stationed in the open air.

## Conflicts in Jerusalem
During the presidency of Cestius Gallus in Syria, approximately three million Israelites gathered to beseech him to address the calamities of their nation and the actions of Florus. While Cestius promised to ensure Florus treated them more gently, Florus sought to provoke a revolt to conceal his own enormities through a greater misery.

In subsequent clashes at the temple in Jerusalem, Israelites attempted to break through the east gate to attack the guards of the outward court. Caesar supported the guards with chosen horsemen, leading to the slaughter of those in the forefront of the Israelite onset. After a series of retreats and advances, the Israelites were eventually overborne and forced to shut themselves within the inner court of the temple.
