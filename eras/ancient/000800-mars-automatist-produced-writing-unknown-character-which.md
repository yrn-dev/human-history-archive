# Mars

The term "Mars" appears in the archive's records in two primary contexts: as a central deity in Roman and Italian religious history and as a subject in studies of psychical research and literature.

## The Roman Deity Mars
Mars (also known as Mavors, Marmar, Marspiter, or Maspiter) was the most important deity of the Roman state after Jupiter. Unlike many other Roman gods, Mars maintained an essentially Roman and Italian character, resisting foreign influences. Evidence of his worship is found across Latium, Umbria, Picenum, Samnium, and certain Etruscan cities. In some communities, as noted by Ovid, he gave his name to a month; in Rome, he was the namesake of the first month of the old Roman year.

### Functions and Characteristics
In historical times, the primary function of Mars in Rome was the protection of the state during war. He became so closely identified with this role that his name served as a synonym for *bellum* (war). Some accounts suggest he was from the beginning a god of war only. However, other interpretations of his cult describe him as a sun-god, a vegetation-spirit, or a god of wind and storm.

The cult of Mars is deeply linked to the experiences of the Latins and kindred peoples during their struggles to establish settlements in the forests and mountains of Italy. This period of danger is reflected in the "fierce character" of the god, referred to as *fere Mars* in the hymn of the Fratres Arvales. His associations include:
*   **Agriculture:** Mars is linked to the clearing of primeval woodlands and the protection of agriculture from wild beasts, disease, and enemy raids. In the prayer of Cato's farmer, he is called "Father Mars" and identified as Silvanus, the dweller in the woodland.
*   **Sacred Animals:** The woodpecker and the wolf, both forest dwellers, were his sacred animals. Italian legend credits these animals with leading the Hirpini and the Piceni to their settlement sites.
*   **Foundation Legends:** Mars is associated with the early foundation legends of Italy and Rome. He was the recipient of the *ver sacrum*, a dedication of the entire produce of a spring, which included children born during that time who were eventually sent forth to create new settlements.

## Mars in Psychical Research and Literature
The name Mars also appears in the context of automatic writing—writing performed without the volition of the agent. In a well-known case recorded by Th. Flournoy in *Des Indes a la planete Mars*, an automatist produced writing in an unknown character. This writing purported to be the Martian language. 

While the handwriting of an automatist generally resembles their ordinary script, it can sometimes differ markedly, and a single agent may use two or three distinct handwritings. In other instances of automatic writing, agents may produce imitations of the handwriting of other people, whether living or dead, or produce "mirror writing" (*Spiegelschrift*) that is only legible in a looking-glass.

Additionally, Mars appears in the works of Du Maurier. One of his stories features a character named Barty Josselin and his guardian angel from Mars.
