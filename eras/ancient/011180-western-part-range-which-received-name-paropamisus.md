# Paropamisus

The Paropamisus, referred to by the ancients as Paropamisus Mons, is the western portion of a vast mountain range that extends across Asia. This range begins at the intersection of the 36th parallel of latitude and the 71st meridian, stretching in one direction toward the north east to the northern slope of the Tibetan plateau and in the other nearly due west toward the Caspian.

## Geography and Topography
The range serves as a significant geographical divide. Its north eastern section is characterized by great altitude and separates the headwaters of the Indus and its tributary, the Kabul—which flow south through Peshawar into the Arabian Sea—from the headwaters of the Oxus, which flow toward the Aral Sea. 

As the range extends westward, it diminishes in height once it passes west of the 65th meridian. This highland system is linked to other regional formations; specifically, the northern highland system of Khorasan, which includes the Elburz and its continuation, extends to the Paropamisus.

## Relation to Bactria
The Paropamisus forms the western boundary of the region known as Bactria (or *Bactriana*). Bactria is the ancient name for the country situated between the Oxus (Amu Darya) and the range of the Hindu Kush, which is identified as the Paropamisus. 

Bactria is described as a mountainous country with a moderate climate, characterized by abundant water and very fertile land. This fertile soil is surrounded and limited by the Turanian desert. The capital of this region was Bactra, known in modern times as Balkh.

## Historical and Cultural Significance
Bactria was the home of one of the Iranian tribes. It is believed that the prophet Zoroaster preached and gained his first adherents in these regions, where the fertile mountainous land meets the desert. From this area, his religion spread to the western parts of Iran. 

Because of this association, the sacred language of the Avesta—the holy book of Zoroastrianism—was often called "old Bactrian." However, scholars have since abandoned this term. Additionally, the archive notes that the once-common belief that Bactria was the cradle of the Indo-European race is now considered wrong by linguists. Current understanding suggests that Aryans probably originated in Europe and lived as a single people in the eastern part of Iran before separating into Iranians and Indians.
