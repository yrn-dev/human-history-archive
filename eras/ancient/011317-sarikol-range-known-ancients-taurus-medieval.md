# Sarikol

The Sarikol is a meridional mountain range located on the western edge of the Kashgar plains. It serves as the political boundary between China and Russia.

## Geography and Structure
The Sarikol range is characterized by a structure consisting of two parallel chains. The western chain functions as the water-divide of the Pamirs. The eastern chain, which has been referred to as the Kandar or Kashgar range, is split at intervals by lateral gorges. These gorges allow for the passage of the main drainage originating from the eastern slopes of the Pamir.

In the broader context of Asian geography, the Sarikol forms the eastern face of the great elevation of the Pamir. To the west of Kashgar, the range, along with the Pamir, limits a central depression. This depression, which is believed to have once been a vast inland sea, extends westwards through Mongolia from the east of the Gobi desert. The level of this depression between the Sarikol range and the mountains enclosing the sources of the Hwang-ho likely does not exceed 2,000 feet above sea level.

## Historical Nomenclature
The range has been known by different names across various eras. In medieval times, it was known as Bolor, and it was referred to as Taurus by the ancients.

## Political Significance
The Sarikol range intervenes as a rugged line between Chinese Turkestan and the Russian Pamirs. While it defines the boundary between Russia and China, the actual dividing line remains indefinite. The range is part of a larger system of mountain barriers and plains that have been examined to assess their value as bases of aggression and their impact on practical politics, particularly concerning the demarcation of boundaries between Russia, Afghanistan, and China.
