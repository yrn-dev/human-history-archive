# Province of Charchan

The Province of Charchan is a territory of Great Turkey situated between the north-east and the east. The region is characterized by a sandy landscape, and the roads leading to it from Pein are similarly sandy. While much of the water found within the province is bitter and bad, there are specific locations where fresh and sweet water is available.

## Geography and Natural Resources
The province contains rivers that bring down Chalcedony and Jasper. These gemstones are transported for sale into Cathay, where they command great prices. 

The terrain is notably arid. When an army passes through the land, the local population—including their wives, children, and cattle—escapes into the sandy waste, traveling a distance of two or three days' journey. Because the inhabitants know the specific spots where water can be found, they are able to sustain themselves and their livestock in areas where they are impossible to discover, as the wind quickly covers their tracks with sand.

## Population and Urban Centers
The people of Charchan worship Mahommet. The province is dotted with numerous villages and towns, the most significant of which is the chief city of the kingdom, which also bears the name of Charchan.

Archaeological observations by Grenard indicate the existence of an old town of Charchan featuring ruins of dwellings deep in the sand and traces of an ancient canal. These ruins include walls constructed from large, solid-baked bricks that remain well preserved. Other similar ruins are located in the environs, specifically at Tatrang (one day's march to the north) and Yantak Koudouk.

## Travel and Connectivity
Traveling from Charchan toward the province of Lop requires a journey of approximately five days through the sands. During this transit, travelers encounter only bad and bitter water until they reach a place where the water is sweet. This journey concludes at the city of Lop, which serves as the entrance to the Great Desert and a place of repose for travelers before they enter the desert.

Regarding the distance to Charchan, various itineraries provide differing estimates. One account suggests the journey from Kiria to Charchan is 9 marches, estimated at 154 miles. Another source, Mr. Shaw, estimates the distance from Ilchi to Charchan as 22 marches, or 293 miles. Both sources agree that the distance from Charchan to Lop is approximately 6 marches, which aligns with the five-day estimate provided by Marco Polo.

## Historical and Linguistic Notes
Some scholarly analysis suggests that the old name of Charchan, *Tché-mo-to-na*, may have Tibetan origins. In this context, the last two syllables might represent *grong* (town) or *kr’om* (bazaar), while the prefix relates to the Tibetan word for sand (*byé-ma*), effectively meaning "town of sand." This etymology is supported by the fact that Charchan, along with Kiria and Pialma, is surrounded by remarkably deep sand.
