# William Booth

## Early Life and Religious Development
William Booth was born in Nottingham on April 10, 1829. At fifteen years of age, he experienced a strong religious turn influenced by the Wesleyan Methodists, leading him to become a local preacher within that body. In 1849, Booth moved to London. According to his own account, his passion for preaching in the open air resulted in his severance from the Wesleyans. He subsequently joined the Methodist New Connexion and was ordained as a minister. However, he left this organization in 1861 because he was not employed in "travelling evangelization" as he had desired.

## Family and Early Ministry
In 1855, William Booth married Catherine Mumford. The couple had a family consisting of four children. Both Booth and his wife were active in preaching, conducting their work first in Cornwall, and later in Walsall and Cardiff. While in Walsall, a "Hallelujah band" was organized, consisting of other individuals and converted criminals who provided public testimony regarding their conversion.

## Foundation of the Salvation Army
Booth returned to London in 1864, where he continued his services in the open air and in tents. He founded an organization that underwent several name changes, being known successively as the East London Christian Mission, the East London Revival Society, and the Christian Mission. In 1878, the organization became the Salvation Army.

The Salvation Army is a quasi-military organization, and Booth based its "Orders and Regulations" on the model of the British army. The Army employs several specific methods of operation:
* Holding processions and outdoor meetings.
* Visiting prisons, private houses, and public-houses.
* Conducting meetings in factories, theatres, and other unusual buildings.
* Utilizing the language of everyday life and popular song-tunes.
* Encouraging every convert to serve as a daily witness for Christ in both private and public settings.

## Opposition and Global Expansion
The early campaigns of the Salvation Army met with violent opposition. A group known as the "Skeleton Army" was formed specifically to break up the Army's meetings. For many years, followers of Booth faced imprisonment and fines for acting as breakers of the peace, though these disorders had largely ceased by 1889.

As an indefatigable speaker, organizer, and traveller, "General" Booth expanded the operations of the army internationally. The organization extended its reach to the United States in 1880 and to Australia in 1881. It further spread to India, Ceylon, the European continent, and other regions.
