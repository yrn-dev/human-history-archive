# Azariah and the Captains of Hundreds

## The Role of Azariah in the Priesthood and Government
The name Azariah is associated with several distinct individuals within the historical records of the Old Testament. Among these were figures of significant administrative and religious standing. One Azariah served as one of the "princes" under King Solomon and was the son of Zadok the priest; he is listed among several Azariahs who were descendants of Levi. Another individual by the same name, the son of Nathan, held the position of a high official during the reign of King Solomon.

In the religious and prophetic sphere, an Azariah served as a prophet during the reign of King Asa of Judah. Additionally, the records mention an Azariah who was the son of Hilkiah and the grandfather of Ezra the Scribe.

## Azariah and the Captains of Hundreds
In a specific administrative appointment, Jehoiada the priest designated two individuals as "captains of hundreds." These two men were the son of Jeroham and the son of Obed, both of whom bore the name Azariah.

## Other Figures Named Azariah
The archives list several other persons named Azariah across different eras and roles:
*   **Royalty:** One Azariah, also known as Uzziah, was the son of Amaziah and Jecholiah and served as King of Judah. Another King of Judah, also referred to as Jehoahaz and Ahaziah, was the son of Jehoram.
*   **Lineage of Judah:** The records identify an Azariah who was the son of Ethan and a great-grandson of Judah, as well as another who was the son of Jehu from the posterity of Judah.
*   **Other Officials and Figures:** Azariah, the son of Maaseiah, was commissioned by Artaxerxes to help restore the wall of Jerusalem. In contrast, the son of Hoshaiah, also named Azariah, is recorded as an opponent of the prophet Jeremiah.
*   **Captivity in Babylon:** An Azariah was among the companions of the prophet Daniel. During the reign of Nebuchadrezzar, he was given the name Abednego. He and two other companions were cast into a "burning fiery furnace" because they refused to worship a golden image established by the monarch.

## Related Lineages: Obed and David
The name Obed appears in the genealogy leading to the monarchy of the Israelites. Booz married Ruth, and they had a son whom Naomi helped nurse. By the advice of other women, the child was named Obed, a name which in the Hebrew dialect signifies a servant, reflecting the hope that he would be subservient to Naomi in her old age. 

The son of Obed was Jesse, and the son of Jesse was David. David eventually became king and passed his dominions to his sons for twenty-one generations.

## Military and Administrative Organization
The records describe various systems of military leadership among the Israelites and other nations. Under King David, the army was divided into twelve parts, each consisting of twenty-four thousand men. These divisions were managed by commanders, leaders, and "captains of hundreds." These forces were ordered to wait on Solomon in thirty-day rotations.

When the principal men of the Hebrew people first came to David at Hebron, they included heads of thousands and other rulers. This group included various tribal contingents, such as four thousand seven hundred from the tribe of Levi led by Jehoiada, and twenty-two captains of the kindred of Zadok the high priest.
