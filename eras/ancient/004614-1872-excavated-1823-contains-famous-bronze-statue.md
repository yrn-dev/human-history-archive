# Archaeological and Artistic Bronze Works

The archive's records detail various bronze works across different eras and regions, ranging from ancient statues discovered during excavations to Renaissance masterpieces and modern commemorative monuments.

## Ancient Bronze Statuary and Excavations

In the city of Brescia, Lombardy, excavations conducted in 1823 revealed a Corinthian temple on the site of the former forum. This building, which some attribute to Hercules but is more likely the city's Capitolium erected by Vespasian in A.D. 73, yielded a famous bronze statue of Victory, which was found in 1826.

In Athens, the National Museum houses bronzes sourced from Delphi, Olympia, and other locations. The city's history also records the existence of colossal bronze works that have since been lost or are yet to be found. A 30-foot-high bronze statue of Athena, known as Athena Promachos and created by Pheidias, was positioned facing the entrance of the Propylaea; it was likely set up by Cimon to commemorate the defeat of the Persians. Additionally, records indicate a great bronze Athena once stood in the middle of the agora, though there is no hope of recovering it. On the Acropolis, Attalus I of the Pergamum dynasty contributed to the city's adornment by erecting a number of bronze statues.

## Renaissance and Early Modern Bronze Art

The 15th century saw significant advancements in bronze casting, particularly in the work of Donatello. After assisting Brunelleschi with the bronze doors of the sacristy of S. Lorenzo, Donatello moved to Padua in 1443. His first major work there was a bronze crucifix for the high altar. 

Between 1443 and 1453, Donatello worked on an equestrian statue of the Condottiere Erasmo de' Narni, known as Gattamelata. Completed in 1453, the statue is characterized by a powerful, majestic repose and a head modeled with searching realism. To achieve these works, Donatello maintained a workshop in Padua consisting of a "small army" of assistants, including bronze-casters and metal-workers.

## Commemorative Bronze Monuments

More recent history includes the use of bronze for public monuments in Hungary. In Kolozsvár, Transylvania, a bronze equestrian statue of King Matthias Corvinus, created by the sculptor Fadrusz in 1902, stands in the principal square. Additionally, a bronze statue was erected by public subscription in the Kerepes cemetery to commemorate the orator and patriot Louis Kossuth, who died in 1894.

## Other Archaeological Findings

Beyond statuary, bronze was a primary material for ancient industry. Excavations at Bibracte, an ancient Gaulish town and former capital of the Aedui, revealed the presence of workshops belonging to bronze workers, as well as iron workers and enamellers.
