## Legal and Political Episodes

In the history of English law, a significant and painful episode concerns the condemnation of Bacon. This period is characterized by severe condemnation and presents difficulties that may be insuperable. Bacon's own account, which serves as the primary source of knowledge on the matter, acknowledges that his actions amounted to corruption and were worthy of condemnation. He identified three distinct ways in which bribes could be given. 

While Bacon explicitly denied the deliberate sale of justice—stating he never had a reward or bribe in mind when pronouncing an order or sentence—he confessed to receiving bribes from suitors *pendente lite* in four specific cases and others by implication. Many charges against him were of little weight, including a charge of negligence regarding his servants, which is viewed as implying no moral blame. A majority of the other charges involved gratuities given after a decision had been reached. Despite his actions, records suggest his decisions were thoroughly just, often given with other officials; in some instances, he ruled against the party providing the bribe. In the case of Lady Wharton, it appears he accepted a present knowing his opinion would be adverse to her cause. Bacon eventually expressed a desire that his fall would help purify the courts of such corrupt practices.

## Medical Conditions and Physical Pain

The archives record several conditions characterized by severe or painful episodes:

**Lumbago**
Lumbago is a painful ailment of the lower back muscles, generally considered to be of rheumatic origin. It often begins with a sudden seizure of stabbing or cutting pain on one or both sides of the small of the back. This pain is greatly aggravated by coughing, sneezing, drawing a deep breath, or attempting to rise from a recumbent posture. The intensity of the suffering can sometimes be mistaken for inflammation of internal organs, such as the bowels or kidneys. It is often brought on by muscle strain or exposure to damp and cold.

**Parasitic Infections**
Certain parasites cause symptoms that vary from slight discomfort to the most severe, resulting in death. These symptoms include pain in the perineal region, haematuria, and anaemia due to blood loss. If the infection continues, it may lead to cystitis or, if the rectum is affected, a discharge of mucus and prolapsus ani.

**Burns and Scalds**
Burns (caused by dry heat) and scalds (caused by moist heat) are classified by the severity of the lesion:
* **First Degree:** Characterized by redness, swelling, and severe pain.
* **Second Degree:** Involves vesicles containing yellowish fluid and exposed sensitive papillae.
* **Third Degree:** Involves partial destruction of the true skin and yellowish or black sloughs. Pain is intense initially, disappears on the second day, and returns after a week.
* **Fourth Degree:** Total destruction of the true skin. Pain is less severe because nerve endings are destroyed, but it results in deep puckered scars and potential loss of function.
* **Fifth and Sixth Degrees:** These involve the destruction of underlying muscles and the charring of bones, often seen in epileptics who fall into fires.
