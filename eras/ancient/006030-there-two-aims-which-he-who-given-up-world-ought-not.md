# The Middle Path and the Nature of Suffering

## The Middle Path and the Noble Eightfold Path
For those who have given up the world, there are two specific aims that should not be followed. The first is a devotion to things whose attractions depend upon the passions; this is described as a low, unprofitable, and ignoble pagan ideal suited only for the worldly-minded. The second is the practice of asceticism, which is likewise characterized as unprofitable, ignoble, and painful.

In place of these extremes, the Tathagata discovered a Middle Path. This path is designed to bestow understanding, open the eyes, and lead the practitioner toward insight, peace, higher wisdom, and Nirvana. This is identified as the Noble Eightfold Path, which consists of the following components:
* Right Views
* Right Aspirations
* Right Speech
* Right Conduct
* Right Mode of Livelihood
* Right Effort
* Right Mindfulness
* Right Rapture

## The Noble Truths of Suffering
The Noble Truth regarding suffering establishes that pain is inherent in several aspects of existence. Specifically, birth, decay, disease, and death are all attended with pain. Furthermore, pain is found in the union with the unpleasant, the separation from the pleasant, and any craving that remains unsatisfied. In summary, the five aggregates of clinging, which are the conditions of individuality, are painful.

The origin of this suffering is identified as "craving thirst." This craving causes the renewal of becomings and is accompanied by sensual delights. It seeks satisfaction in various forms, including the craving for prosperity, the craving for a future life, or the craving for the gratification of the senses.

## Worldly Desires and Spiritual Rewards
Contrasting perspectives on worldly aims are presented in the accounts of Qarun (also referred to as Korah). Qarun appeared before his people in finery, pomp, and worldly glitter. Those who desired the life of the world expressed a wish to possess the kind of mighty good fortune given to him.

However, those granted true knowledge responded that the reward of Allah in the Hereafter is superior for those who believe and do good. According to these sources, this reward is attainable only for those who are patient and steadfastly persevere in good. Similarly, some individuals pray to their Lord to grant them good in both this world and the Hereafter, while asking for protection from the doom or chastisement of the Fire. Those who make such requests will be allotted what they have earned, as Allah is swift in reckoning.
