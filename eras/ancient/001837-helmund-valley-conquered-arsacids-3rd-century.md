# Helmund Valley

The Helmund valley is a geographical region located in the eastern portion of the Iranian plateau and modern Afghanistan. It is characterized by its relationship to the Helmund river and the surrounding basin and desert landscapes.

## Geography and Hydrography
The Helmund valley is situated within a central section of the continent defined by a depression where drainage is typically lost in swamps or *hamuns*. The average level of this section is estimated not to exceed 2,000 feet above sea level. 

The valley is separated from the Helmund *hamun* basin by a divide with an altitude of approximately 4,000 feet above sea level. This divide represents the only orographic obstacle between the valley of the Hari Rud (west of Herat) and the Helmund basin; consequently, it is the only direct connection between the northern and southern sections of the continent that does not require the crossing of mountain passes or formidable altitude barriers. This geographical feature is noted for its significance regarding the development of Asiatic railways.

## Political Boundaries and Infrastructure
The boundary between Afghanistan and Baluchistan is positioned 50 miles south of the Helmund. This placement entirely shuts off the Helmund valley and the approach to Seistan—which lies between the Helmund and the Gaud-i-Zirreh—from Baluchistan. The Gaud-i-Zirreh serves as the only approach from the east during seasons of flood.

Despite this separation, a connected desert route exists between Nushki and Seistan to the south, which remains open during ordinary seasons. This route has undergone significant development, including the digging of wells and the establishment of *serais* (posts) at intervals. This "new Helmund desert route" has facilitated *khafila* traffic and secured trade between Quetta and Seistan, particularly following the acquisition of administration rights for the Nushki district by Great Britain.

## Historical Governance
The Helmund valley, along with Seistan and Areia (Herat), was historically part of the western regions of eastern Iran. During the period of the "Indo-Scythian" empire—ruled by the Kushan (Kushana) dynasty—the Kushans maintained control over Baluchistan and eastern Afghanistan. However, the western regions, specifically the Helmund valley, Seistan, and Areia, were conquered by the Arsacids.

The broader region of eastern Iran and the surrounding territories saw various successive powers. Following the decay of the Kushan dynasty in the 3rd century, the Kushanas were reduced to eastern Iran, where they fought against the Sassanids. Later, in the 5th century, the Ephthalites (or "white Huns") subjected Bactria around 450, followed by the Turks around A.D. 560. Eventually, after the overthrow of the Sassanian empire by the Arabs, the region submitted to the rule of Islam and the caliph.
