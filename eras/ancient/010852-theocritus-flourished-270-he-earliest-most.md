# Theocritus

Theocritus was a Syracusan poet of the 3rd century B.C. who is recognized as a primary figure in the development of the pastoral idea. He is associated with the Alexandrian school, alongside other poets such as Moschus and Bion.

## Literary Contributions and Style
Theocritus is most celebrated for his collection of bucolics, of which approximately thirty poems have been preserved. These works are written primarily in hexameter verse and utilize the Doric dialect. His influence was significant enough that the Roman poet Virgil is believed to have originally named his own pastoral poems "bucolics" specifically to challenge a comparison with the writings of Theocritus.

Among his preserved works are ten pieces known as "Idyls" (from the Greek *eidyllion*, meaning "a little piece in the style of"). While these ten pieces are diverse and serve as the type from which the popular modern conception of the idyl is derived, there is no evidence in ancient literature that the poet intended them to be a recognized or typical form of verse. 

Theocritus's work is characterized by a vigour and truthfulness to nature that distinguishes it from later developments in pastoral poetry, such as the works of Bion, which are noted for exaggerated sentimentality. His poetry often graphically depicts the feelings and life of common Syracusans, as seen in the *Adoniazusae* (the "Festival of Adonis").

## Notable Works
The archive identifies several specific compositions by Theocritus:

*   **The "Festival of Adonis" (*Adoniazusae*):** A realistic mime concerning the second day of the festival of Adonis, which celebrates the reunion of Adonis and Aphrodite.
*   **"Dirge for Daphnis":** The first of his poems, which has become the prototype for the modern elegy rather than the modern idyl.
*   **Epical Romances:** Six small epical romances that helped start the conception of the idyl.

## Professional and Personal Associations
Theocritus was a friend of Asclepiades of Samos, an epigrammatist and lyric poet who flourished about 270 B.C. and is regarded as the most important of the erotic and convivial epigrammatists. 

While some older accounts suggested that the poet Bion was a contemporary and imitator of Theocritus living around 280 B.C., this view is now generally regarded as incorrect. Modern scholarship, specifically the work of W. Stein, places Bion in the first half of the 1st century B.C.
