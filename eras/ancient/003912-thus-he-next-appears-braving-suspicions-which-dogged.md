# Barnabas

## Identity and Origins
Barnabas is a figure appearing in the New Testament. According to Acts iv. 36, the name Barnabas was a surname given to him by the apostles. He is described as a Levite who was a man of Cyprus by birth. While he was not one of the Twelve, he attained the rank of an apostle, as noted in 1 Cor. ix. 6 and Acts xiv. 4 and 14.

## Etymology of the Name
The name Barnabas is Semitic, and its Greek rendering is *huios parakleseos*. This has been translated in different ways: the Authorized Version translates it as "son of consolation," while the Revised Version uses "son of exhortation." 

There is an ongoing philological difficulty regarding the Greek rendering, as no satisfactory etymology for Bar-nabas in this sense has been suggested. One theory based on philological analogy suggests the form Bar-Nebo; however, this is considered to lack intrinsic fitness for a Levite and does not align with the statements found in Acts. Consequently, it is generally assumed that the name stems from an unknown Aramaic form equivalent to *paraklesis*. In the context of the usage in Acts, this is best understood as comfort or encouragement—specifically the kind of comfort provided by encouraging words rather than rousing eloquence.

## Character and Role
The accounts of Barnabas emphasize his goodness of heart, with Acts xi. 24 explicitly describing him as "a good man." This quality is linked to his fineness of perception and a large insight into essentials. His gift as a "prophet" likely manifested as a practically helpful and encouraging form of support.

His first appearance in the records is characterized by a generously helpful nature. He later played a prominent role by braving the suspicions that followed Saul (Paul), who had been a persecutor. Barnabas, who may have been an old acquaintance of Saul in Hellenist circles at Jerusalem, introduced him to the older apostles. This action further demonstrates his reputation as a man of authority and insight.
