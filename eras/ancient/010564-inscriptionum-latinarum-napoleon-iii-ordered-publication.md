# Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum

The *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* is a comprehensive collection of Latin inscriptions from the Roman world. The design for the publication of such a collection was conceived by the Italian antiquarian Bartolommeo Borghesi before his death on April 16, 1860. Following his vision, the work was undertaken by the Academy of Berlin under the auspices of Mommsen.

## Scope and Contents
The *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* serves as a vast repertory of epigraphic evidence across various regions and subjects. Its volumes document a wide array of Roman remains and historical records, including:

*   **British Inscriptions:** Volume VII, edited by Hubner, contains British inscriptions and is considered a valuable repertory for early Roman epitaphs in Britain. These include the earliest existing British epitaphs, which were written in Latin following the Roman form and were primarily commemorative of soldiers from the legions of occupation.
*   **Roman Ruins:** The results of explorations of ruins, such as those at Carnuntum by the *Carnuntumverein* society, are recorded in volume III, part I.
*   **Ecclesiastical and Public Records:** The collection includes epitaphs of later figures, such as that of Magnus Felix Ennodius (died A.D. 521), the bishop of Pavia, found in volume V, part II.
*   **Military and Political Memorials:** The work records inscriptions from memorial columns, such as the *columna rostrata* set up for the Roman general Gaius Duilius following his victory over the Carthaginian fleet in 260 B.C. (recorded in volume I).
*   **Religious and Ritual Texts:** The collection includes records related to the Arvals (or Arvels/Arthels), specifically in volume VI.

## Contributions and Cartography
The *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum* has benefited from the contributions of various scholars. Notably, the German geographer Heinrich Kiepert, a professor at the university of Berlin, produced valuable maps that were included in the work.

## Related Works of Bartolommeo Borghesi
Bartolommeo Borghesi, whose design led to the creation of the *Corpus Inscriptionum Latinarum*, was a student of Rome and Bologna who specialized in numismatics and epigraphy. He is known for his monumental work, *Nuovi Frammenti dei Fasti Consolari Capitolini* (1818-1820), which provided positive bases for the chronology of Roman history. 

Napoleon III ordered the publication of a complete edition of Borghesi's works. This edition consisted of ten volumes; the first was published in 1862, and the series was completed in 1897.
