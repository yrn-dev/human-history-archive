## Literary and Historical Analysis
In the study of the *Iliad*, M. G. provides analysis regarding the nature of the "Heroic Age," a period characterized by the breakdown of old civilizations by nomads or barbarians. According to M. G., this era was defined by the rise of chiefs who established kingdoms through conquest and plunder. This societal shift resulted in the fragmentation of the tribe, replaced by the *comitatus*—groups of casual men who attached themselves to specific leaders, such as Patroclus or Phœnix attaching themselves to Achilles.

M. G. further notes that during this period, religion became localized and was subsequently broken up, leading to a scarcity of religion and magic within the *Iliad* or the *Nibelungenlied*. Additionally, M. G. observes that the *Iliad* utilizes visual adjectives in its descriptions. In contrasting the heroic tradition with the comic tradition, M. G. points out that while centaurs, barbarians, and figures in the comic tradition may get drunk, Greek heroes in Homer or the heroic tradition never do.

G. questions the historical narrative of Troy being destroyed six separate times. M.
