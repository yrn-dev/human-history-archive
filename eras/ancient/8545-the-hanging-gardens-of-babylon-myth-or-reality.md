# The Hanging Gardens of Babylon: Myth or Reality?

Among the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, the Hanging Gardens of Babylon remain the most enigmatic. While the Colossus of Rhodes and the Lighthouse of Alexandria left behind archaeological footprints or clear historical records of their destruction, the Gardens are unique: no contemporary Babylonian text mentions them, and no physical ruins have ever been found in Babylon.

### Overview
According to Greek and Roman historians—such as Berossus and Diodorus Siculus—the gardens were built by King Nebuchadnezzar II (r. 605–562 BCE) for his Median wife, Amytis, who longed for the green mountains of her homeland. The descriptions depict a series of tiered terraces supported by stone columns, featuring a sophisticated irrigation system that pumped water from the Euphrates River to sustain a lush canopy of exotic flora.

However, modern historians are divided. The lack of evidence in the extensive cuneiform archives of Nebuchadnezzar’s reign suggests the gardens may have been a poetic exaggeration or a "literary trope" used by Greeks to describe the opulence of the East. 

A compelling alternative theory was proposed by Dr. Stephanie Dalley of Oxford University. She suggests the gardens actually existed, but were located 300 miles north in **Nineveh**, the capital of the Assyrian Empire. Dalley argues that King Sennacherib (r. 704–681 BCE) constructed a magnificent garden with an advanced aqueduct system, and that later historians simply confused the Assyrian city of Nineveh with the Babylonian city.

### Key Facts
*   **The Engineering Puzzle:** If they existed in Babylon, the gardens would have required an early version of the Archimedes screw to lift water to the upper terraces.
*   **The "Hanging" Misnomer:** The word "hanging" is a mistranslation of the Greek *kremastos*, which more accurately means "overhanging" or "tiered," describing the terrace structure.
*   **The Absence of Evidence:** Despite extensive excavations of Babylon by German and Iraqi archaeologists, no irrigation pipes or garden foundations have been identified.

### Did You Know?
1. **The Wife's Longing:** The legend claims the gardens were a romantic gesture to cure Amytis's homesickness for the hills of Media.
2. **Assyrian Reliefs:** Bas-reliefs found in Nineveh depict a lush garden with a sophisticated water system, supporting the theory that the "Babylonian" gardens were actually Assyrian.
3. **The Last Witness:** The gardens disappeared from historical records after the 1st century CE, likely destroyed by earthquakes or neglect.
