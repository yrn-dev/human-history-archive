# AUTHORITIES AND SCHOLARLY INVESTIGATION

The process of establishing historical and literary authority involves the publication of ancient texts, the application of critical methods to existing versions, and the ongoing analysis of data by scholars. Across various fields of study, the transition from relying on traditional texts to utilizing modern critical resources and archaeological evidence has significantly altered the understanding of the past.

## Classical and Ancient Textual Studies
In the study of ancient authorities, scholars have prioritized the necessary first step of publishing ancient texts. However, the subsequent work of analyzing and classifying the data contained within these authorities has remained imperfect. Key published works in this domain include the 57 volumes of the *Pali Text Society*, the *Jataka* (7 vols., 1877-1897), the *Vinaya* (5 vols., 1879-1883), the *Dhammapada* (2nd ed., 1900), the *Divyavadana* (1882), the *Mahavastu* (3 vols., 1882-1897), the *Buddha Carita* (1892), and the *Milinda-pañho* (1880).

In the realm of Roman rhetoric, the authorship of the *Rhetorica* (known as *Auctor ad Herennium*) has been a subject of scholarly debate. While Jerome and Priscian attributed the work to Cicero, modern scholars have supported claims by Ateius Praetextatus, M. Antonius Gnipho, and L. Aelius Stilo. Some identify the author as Cornificius, based on passages quoted by Quintilian. Internal evidence suggests the work was composed between 86-82 B.C. during the period of Marian domination in Rome.

## Biblical Revision and Critical Methods
The development of the New Testament's "Revised Version" (1881) highlights the evolution of critical scholarship. Scholars in 1611 possessed meagre critical resources and failed to give sufficient attention to early manuscripts, resulting in an "Authorized Version" based on the "Received Text" that competent scholars eventually deemed incomplete.

A committee appointed in 1870 sought to reconstruct the Greek text as a foundation for revision. This committee was influenced by Westcott and Hort, who provided installments of their text and exercised a predominating influence on the question of various readings. Unlike the 1611 translators, the revisers adopted a method of scrupulous faithfulness, consistently using the same English word for the same Greek word.

## Historical Authorities and Compilations
Certain works have achieved a status of undisputed authority. Gibbon's *The Decline and Fall of the Roman Empire* is described as indispensable to the student of history, serving as the sole undisputed authority for the vast period it covers. While some specific subjects within the work may have been more completely investigated by others, the work is noted for its luminous arrangement, general accuracy, and vigorous style. Later editions, such as one from June 1845, incorporated new classical fragments and an increase in Oriental literature to further the pursuit of historic truth.

In the field of classical scholarship, individuals such as Thomas Kidd (1770-1850) contributed by editing the works of other scholars, including Richard Porson, Richard Dawes, and David Ruhnken. Similarly, Edmund Henry Barker (1788-1839) was a prolific writer and lexicographer who edited portions of classical authors for schools and was one of the first to write commentary notes in English rather than Latin.

## Philology and Linguistics
The study of Sanskrit and Semitic philology has been advanced by scholars such as Theodor Benfey (1809-1881). Benfey produced a *Sanskrit-English Dictionary* (1866), a *Manual of Sanskrit* (1852-1854), and an edition of the *Sama-veda* (1848). His work also included a study on the names of the Hebrew months, which he proved were derived from Persian.

In Japan, high literature provides a wealth of records. The Nara epoch produced the *Manyo-shu* (Myriad Leaves), and the Engi era produced the *Kokin-shu*, an anthology of over eleven hundred poems. The Heian epoch is noted for the full development of the Japanese language, utilizing a rich system of particles and terminations with a vocabulary drawn almost exclusively from native sources.

## Archaeological Evidence and Topography
In Athens, recent investigations have provided new light on topography, monuments, and art. The Greek government and various scientific societies have conducted costly excavations, while museums have been scientifically arranged to allow for systematic study. This shift has allowed observers to test theories—previously based primarily on literary evidence—through on-the-spot scrutiny. Consequently, many previously accepted theories, including some held by Leake, have been challenged or confirmed. Athens has thus evolved into a center of research and a meeting-place for scholars of the Greek world.
