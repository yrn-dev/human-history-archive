# The Term "Aryan" in Earliest Literature

The term "Aryan" (and its linguistic variants) appears in the earliest literature of several regions, serving primarily as a national designation. Its usage and etymology have been the subject of extensive study in comparative philology, most notably by Max Muller, whose works have made this specific use of the word familiar to the general public.

## Geographic and Linguistic Distribution
Based on the earliest occurrences of the word, it is evident that the term was employed as a national name across a broad geographic range, including India, Persia, and Bactria. The term appears in various ancient languages as follows:
*   **Sanskrit:** *arya-* and *arya*
*   **Zend:** *airya-*
*   **Old Persian:** *ariya-*

## Etymological Debates
The precise origin of the word is a matter of scholarly contention. Max Muller has asserted a connection between the term and *ira*, a Sanskrit word for earth, though this remains far from certain. Spiegel has noted that while the word can be linked to a root *ar-*, there are multiple roots of that form with differing meanings, and no certain criterion exists to determine which root is the correct relation.

Other proposed connections have been dismissed or remain uncertain:
*   **Ireland:** An attempt by Pictet to establish a connection with *Erin* (Ireland) is considered impossible.
*   **Celtic and Germanic names:** The word may share an origin with the *Ario-* found in names such as *Ariovistus*, which appears in both Germanic and Celtic words.
*   **Armenia:** The name of Armenia (Old Persian *Armina-*) has been connected to the term, but its origin is regarded as uncertain.

## Distinctions within Sanskrit
Within the Sanskrit language, it is probable that two distinct words must be identified:
1.  *Arya*: The origin of the term "Aryan," from which the common term *arya* is derived.
2.  *Arya*: A term that frequently appears in the *Rig Veda* as an epithet used for deities.

Additionally, in various passages, the form *aryas* may be interpreted as the genitive of *ari*, which is explained as meaning "pious, devoted, active."
