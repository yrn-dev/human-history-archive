# Baiae and the District of Bacoli

The district of Baiae and its surrounding areas are characterized by the fragmentary remains of once splendid baths and villas. Due to the nature of these ruins, it is generally not possible to identify specific buildings, and the names currently applied to various ruins lack authentication.

## Architectural Remains at Baiae
Within Baiae itself, there are three large, lofty domed buildings. While these are popularly referred to as temples, they are actually the remains of *nymphaea* or baths. These structures are constructed of brick and *opus reticulatum*; two of the buildings are octagonal and one is circular, though all three feature circular interiors.

## Ruins of Bacoli and Punta dell' Epitaffio
The Punta dell' Epitaffio is covered with remains. Similarly, several ruins are located at Bacoli:
* **North of the village:** A small theatre, which is called the tomb of Agrippina.
* **Under the village:** The remains of a large villa.
* **To the East:** The remains of a large water reservoir known as the Cento Camerelle.
* **To the South:** Another water reservoir with a vaulted ceiling, known as the *piscina mirabilis*, which measures 85 by 230 ft.

## The Villa of Marius and Bauli
The villa of Marius was purchased by Lucullus before eventually becoming the property of the imperial house. This site was the location of the death of Tiberius. The villa is referred to variously as *Misenensis* or *Baiana*. While Beloch suggests it is located on the promontory south of Misenum to align with descriptions by Phaedrus, Th. Mommsen suggests it may be sought at Bacoli.

Near Baiae lay the villa resort of Bauli, named after the Greek *Boaulia* (meaning stalls), where Hercules concealed the oxen of Geryon. There is scholarly disagreement regarding its location; some identify it with the modern village of Bacoli (located 2 m. S.S.E. of Baiae), while others identify it with the Punta dell' Epitaffio (located 1 m. N.E. of Baiae).

Bauli was home to villas owned by Hortensius and Pompey. Pompey's villa was situated on the hills. The villa of Hortensius, located on the shores of the Lacus Lucrinus, was noted for its tame lampreys and served as the setting for the dialogue in the second book of Cicero's *Academica Priora*. This property later became imperial property and was the site where Nero murdered Agrippina. Additionally, Caligula constructed a bridge of boats extending from Bauli to Puteoli.
