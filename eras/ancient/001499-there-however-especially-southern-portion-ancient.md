# Bachian

## Geography and Geology
Bachian (also known as Batjan) is one of the Molucca Islands located in the Molucca Sea within the residency of Ternate in the Dutch East Indies. Its geographical coordinates are 0deg13'-0deg55' S. and 127deg22'-128deg E. The island, which has an area of 914 sq. m., is irregular in form and consists of two distinct mountainous regions connected by a low isthmus. 

The island's geological composition is partly volcanic, a fact indicated by the presence of hot springs. However, the southern portion of the island contains ancient and non-volcanic rocks. The highest point of elevation is found in the south at the mountain of Labua, which reaches 6950 ft.

## Natural Resources and Wildlife
Bachian is richly wooded across a large portion of its territory. It abundantly produces cocoa-nuts, sago, and cloves, the latter of which are indigenous to the island. Mineral discoveries on the island include coal.

The island is noted for its variety of birds and insects. It is particularly remarkable for being the most eastern point on the globe inhabited by the *Quadrumana*, a black ape that also occurs in Celebes.

## Population and Settlement
The interior of Bachian is uninhabited. The coastal dwellers are not indigenous and comprise a diverse population of approximately 13,000 people. This population includes:
*   The Sirani (Christian descendants of the Portuguese)
*   Malays with a Papuan element
*   Immigrants from Celebes
*   Galela men from the north of Halmahera
*   Arabs and Chinese

The chief village is situated on the west side of the isthmus; it is called Bachian, though the inhabitants refer to it as Amasing.

## Governance and Economy
Bachian is the most important island in a group that was formerly under the governance of a sultan. Since 1889, it has been governed by a committee of chiefs who are under the control of a Dutch *controleur*.

Economic efforts to exploit the island were attempted by a Batjan company starting in 1882. These attempts were unsuccessful due to a lack of labourers and a deficient knowledge of the capabilities and soil of the island.
