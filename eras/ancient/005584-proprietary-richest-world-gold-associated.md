# Global Deposits and Concentrations of Gold and Wealth

## Mining and Mineral Deposits
Across various regions of the world, specific sites have been identified for their exceptional mineral wealth, particularly gold and silver. In New South Wales, Australia, the town of Broken Hill serves as a silver-mining center. One of its neighboring mines, the Proprietary, is described as the richest in the world. In this location, gold is associated with the silver, and the area also yields zinc, tin, good copper lodes, and large quantities of lead.

In Victoria, Australia, the city of Ballarat and the town of Ballarat East developed following the discovery of gold-fields in 1851. The alluvial gold-fields in this region were the richest ever opened up. As these deposits were exhausted, mining shifted to quartz reefs at deep levels, with some mines reaching depths exceeding 2,000 feet.

In north-western Canada's Yukon Territory, the Klondike district is noted for its gold-bearing gravels found along creeks tributary to the Klondike and Indian rivers. The most productive of these creeks include Bonanza, Hunker, Dominion, Gold Run, and its tributary Eldorado. Of these, Eldorado was much the richest for its two or three gold-bearing miles, likely surpassing any other known placer deposit. Following a rush in 1896, the output of gold reached a climax of $22,000,000 in 1900, though production declined to $5,600,000 by 1906. Due to the frozen ground, miners utilized thawing processes involving steam and fires, later employing hydraulic mining and dredges.

## Historical Wealth and Trade
Ancient Lydia, under the rule of Alyattes (grandson of Gyges), became a considerable power and a center of trade between Europe and Asia. The country was rich in gold and productive, leading the Lydian monarch to be reputed as the richest in Asia. Lydia is also credited as the first country in the world to provide coined money and inns for traders.

In the accounts of the kings of Norway, Harald is noted for spending many years in Africa. There, he gathered extraordinary treasure consisting of jewels, gold, and other precious things. The records suggest this was possible because he plundered a part of the world that was the richest in gold and valuable things. Harald sent the wealth he did not need for expenses north to Novgorod for the keeping of King Jarisleif.

## Cultural and Religious Perspectives on Wealth
The Qur'an presents a vision of wealth and its relation to the afterlife. It mentions that if all people had not been a single nation, those who disbelieve in the Beneficent Allah would have been assigned roofs and stairs of silver, as well as silver doors and couches to recline upon. Additionally, they would have been given adornments or embellishments of gold. However, the text teaches that such things are merely provisions or conveniences of the present world's life, while the Hereafter is reserved for the Righteous or those who guard against evil.
