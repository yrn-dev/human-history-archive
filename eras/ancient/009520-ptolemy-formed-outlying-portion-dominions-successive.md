# Ptolemy

The name Ptolemy appears in historical records across several distinct contexts, most notably as a dynasty of Egyptian monarchs, a conspirator in Macedonia, and a geographer whose work influenced early modern cartography.

## The Ptolemaic Dynasty of Egypt
Following the death of Alexander the Great, his empire was divided among his subordinate generals. One of these generals, Ptolemy, seized Egypt and established a kingdom. This dynasty, also known as the Lagides—named after Lagus, the father of the first Ptolemy to reign in Egypt—consisted of fourteen monarchs, including Cleopatra. After Cleopatra's death, the Egyptian dominions became a province of the Roman Empire.

While all these princes shared the common name of Ptolemy, they were distinguished by specific surnames. Six of these kings and the durations of their reigns include:
*   **Ptolemy Soter**: Reigned for thirty-eight years and some months, beginning immediately after the death of Alexander the Great.
*   **Ptolemy Philadelphus**: Reigned for forty years (including two years spent reigning during his father's lifetime).
*   **Ptolemy Euergetes**: Reigned for twenty-five years.
*   **Ptolemy Philopator**: Reigned for seventeen years.
*   **Ptolemy Epiphanes**: Reigned for twenty-four years.
*   **Ptolemy Philometor**: Reigned for thirty-four years.

The Ptolemies employed a bureaucratic system of government, though they allowed some local independence in outlying possessions; for example, the internal government of Jerusalem was left to the high priests. In some instances, the dynasty practiced joint-kingship to secure succession if a supreme king died while his heir was an infant, as seen with Ptolemy Eupator, the son of Ptolemy Philometor.

## Military Conflicts and the Successors
As one of the successors of Alexander, Ptolemy was frequently involved in wars with other generals. During a conflict with Antigonus, Ptolemy landed in Syria and ravaged the country. Antigonus sent his twenty-two-year-old son, Demetrius, to oppose him. Ptolemy, a veteran trained under Alexander, defeated Demetrius near the city of Gaza, resulting in fifteen thousand deaths and eight thousand prisoners. Despite the victory, Ptolemy returned Demetrius's property, tent, and captured friends, sending a message that they should fight for empire and honour rather than as mortal foes.

## Ptolemy of Alôrus
Separate from the Egyptian dynasty, a Ptolemæus (or Ptolemy) of Alôrus is recorded as the leader of a conspiracy against Alexander of Macedon. Alexander, who had reigned for scarcely two years and was harassed by internal dissensions, was assassinated in 368 B.C. by conspirators from the cities of Pydna and Alôrus. Following this event, Ptolemy served as regent, administering the affairs of Eurydikê (the widow of the prince Amyntas) and her minor children, Perdikkas and Philip, in a struggle against a royal pretender named Pausanias.

## Geographical and Cartographic Influence
The works of Ptolemy also had a significant impact on the mapping of the world. In the context of Indian geography, the "Heptanesia" of Ptolemy refers to the seven islands that originally constituted Bombay. 

During the 16th century, cartographers attempted to combine the new discoveries of the Portuguese and the ideas of Columbus with older knowledge. This often involved adding a "sprinkling" of Ptolemy's nomenclature to the nomenclature of Marco Polo. These attempts resulted in an inconsistent "hotch-potch" that failed to provide a consistent representation of geographical facts.
