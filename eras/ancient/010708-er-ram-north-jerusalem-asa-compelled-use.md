# Asa of Judah

Asa was a king of Jerusalem and the two tribes of Judah, succeeding Abijah, who was the son of Rehoboam. Sources describe Asa as being either the son or the brother of Abijah. He is characterized as a man of excellent character and piety who had a regard for God and sought to observe the laws. During his reign, he implemented a religious reformation, purifying his kingdom by removing everything wicked.

## Reign and Chronology
Asa reigned in Jerusalem for forty-one years. According to the chronological frameworks found in the Books of Kings, Asa began his reign in the twentieth year of Jeroboam, king of Israel. His reign was contemporary with several monarchs of Israel, including Baasha, Zimri, and Omri. In the third year of Asa's reign, Baasha, the son of Ahijah, began to reign over Israel in Tirzah.

## Military Conflicts
Asa's reign was marked by significant military engagements, most notably against Zerah, the king of Ethiopia (and possibly Egypt). After Asa had reigned for ten years, Zerah launched an expedition with a massive army consisting of one million men (nine hundred thousand footmen and one hundred thousand horsemen) and three hundred chariots. The Ethiopian forces advanced as far as Mareshah, a city belonging to the tribe of Judah.

Asa met Zerah in the valley of Zephathah. Despite being outnumbered, Asa relied on the assistance of God, praying that the weaker might be made superior to the more numerous. This faith was answered, and the Ethiopian army was struck with terror and irrevocably defeated, being destroyed before the Lord and his host.

## Conflict with Baasha
Asa also faced conflict with Baasha of Israel. Baasha fortified Ramah (_er-Ram_), located five miles north of Jerusalem. To counter this threat, Asa used the remaining funds from the temple to bribe the king of Damascus to break his league with Baasha and attack Israel. This strategy was successful; the king of Damascus invaded Galilee, forcing Baasha to withdraw from Ramah. Asa subsequently used the building materials Baasha had collected at Ramah to fortify Mizpah, located immediately north of Jerusalem, as well as Geba.

## Legacy and Succession
The Book of Chronicles records a great religious revival involving both Judah and Israel during Asa's time. Because of his righteousness and piety, Asa is described as having attained a long and blessed old age through God's blessing. Upon his death, he was succeeded in the government by his son, Jehoshaphat, who was born to Asa's wife, Azubah. Jehoshaphat was noted for following the courage and piety of his forefather David.
