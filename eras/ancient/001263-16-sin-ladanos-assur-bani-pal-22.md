# Assur-bani-pal

Assur-bani-pal (whose name means "Assur creates a son") was the king of Assyria and its empire, reigning from 668 to 626 B.C. He is described as the prototype of the Greek Sardanapalus and may appear as "Asnapper" in the corrupted form found in Ezra iv. 10. His reign represents the height of Assyrian power, though it also preceded the rapid decay and eventual collapse of the empire.

## Accession and Early Campaigns
Assur-bani-pal was publicly nominated as king of Assyria by his father, Esar-haddon, on the 12th of Iyyar, prior to Esar-haddon's death in 668 B.C. To satisfy Babylonian national sentiment, Esar-haddon assigned Babylonia to Assur-bani-pal's twin brother, Samas-sum-yukin.

Upon ascending the throne, Assur-bani-pal's immediate priority was the completion of the Egyptian campaign. He pursued Tirhakah, who had reoccupied Egypt; the Assyrian army ascended the Nile from Memphis to Thebes over a period of forty days. Following the death of Tirhakah in 667 B.C., a general revolt led by Thebes broke out. Although Memphis was taken by assault and Assyrian troops were initially driven out, Assur-bani-pal deployed fresh forces, plundered Thebes, and destroyed its temples. As trophies of this victory, two obelisks were transported to Nineveh.

## Imperial Expansion and Diplomacy
At the peak of his authority, Assur-bani-pal extended Assyrian influence across several regions:
*   **Tyre:** The city was forced to surrender after a close siege that cut off its water supply.
*   **Manna (Minni):** Located south-east of Ararat, this land was wasted, its capital captured, and its king reduced to a vassal.
*   **Elam:** A war against Teumman of Elam resulted in the defeat of the Elamite army. Teumman's head was sent to Nineveh, and the Assyrians appointed Umman-igas as king. Later, despite efforts to avoid further conflict, a prolonged struggle with Elam occurred. Aided by an Elamite civil war, the Assyrians wasted the country with sword and fire and leveled the capital, Susa (or Shushan).
*   **Diplomatic Relations:** The kings of Tabal and Cilicia offered their daughters to Assur-bani-pal's harem. Embassies were sent from Ararat, and Gyges of Lydia dispatched envoys to the "great king" seeking assistance against the Cimmerians.

## Internal Conflict and Decline
The arrangement regarding Babylonia eventually failed. Samas-sum-yukin identified strongly with his subjects, revived the Sumerian language as the official tongue, and claimed succession to ancient monarchs. This led to a revolt that severely shook the Assyrian empire. After years of struggle—during which Egypt regained its independence in 660 B.C. with help from mercenaries sent by Gyges of Lydia—Babylon was starved into surrender. Samas-sum-yukin and his supporters were subsequently put to death.

The long wars in Babylonia and Elam left Assyria maimed and exhausted, draining its wealth and fighting population. This exhaustion made the empire vulnerable to the Scythians (called Manda by the Babylonians) and Cimmerians. Assur-bani-pal regarded the death of the Scythian leader Dugdamme (Lygdamis) as a sign of divine favor, though he referred to Dugdamme as "a limb of Satan."

## Cultural Contributions and Personal Life
Assur-bani-pal is characterized as a magnificent patron of art and literature, though he is also described as superstitious, cruel, indolent, and luxurious. He is noted for entrusting army commands to others and then appropriating their successes.

His most significant contribution was the creation of the great library of Nineveh, located in a palace he built on the northern side of the mound of Kuyunjik. The palace was lined with sculptured slabs representing the best of Assyrian art. Assur-bani-pal employed scribes to copy older Babylonian tablets, though his personal interests leaned toward astrology and omens.

In his private life, he is depicted reclining on a couch during meals, with his wife seated on a chair beside him. There remains historical doubt as to whether Kandalanu (Kinela-danos), who served as viceroy of Babylonia after the revolt was suppressed, was actually Assur-bani-pal under a different name.

## Death and Aftermath
Assur-bani-pal died in 626 B.C. At the time of his death, the empire was already in decay. His successor, Assur-etil-ilani, saw the Scythians penetrate as far as the borders of Egypt and burn the city of Calah. The subsequent decline continued through the reigns of Assur-sum-lisir and Sin-sarra-uzur (Sarakos) until Nineveh was captured and destroyed by a Scythian army allied with the Babylonians under Nabopolassar, ending the Assyrian empire.
