# Straits

Straits are narrow bodies of water that separate landmasses, often serving as critical boundaries for administrative provinces, geographical regions, or as conduits for ancient commerce and military movement.

## The Strait of Gibraltar and the Pillars of Hêraklês
The Strait of Gibraltar, also referred to as the Pillars of Hêraklês, serves as a primary maritime boundary. In Spain, it forms the southern border of the maritime province of Cadiz, which was established in 1833 from districts of the province of Seville. The southernmost promontory of the European mainland, Punta Marroqui, is located on these straits. Nearby, the river Barbate flows into the straits after receiving several small tributaries that form the Laguna de la Janda, a broad and marshy area near the river's mouth. The east coast of this region is characterized by Algeciras Bay, which is overlooked by the fortress and rock of Gibraltar.

Historically, the strait was a focal point for Phenician commerce. While few Phenician settlements existed between the western portion of the French province of Constantine and the Pillars of Hêraklês, the colony of Gadês was established outside the strait. Gadês became the center of an extensive trade network reaching the Scilly Islands and Britain to the north, and extending south along the western coast of Africa for at least thirty days' sail. Additionally, the island of Kernê, located twelve days' sail from the strait of Gibraltar, served as a dépôt for Phenician merchandise traded with the interior.

## Arctic Straits and Waterways
In the northern regions, particularly around Baffin Land in Canada's Franklin district, several straits and channels define the geography of the barren insular tract. Baffin Land is separated from Greenland by the Davis Strait and Baffin Bay. It is further separated from Ungava by the Hudson Strait, and from the Melville Peninsula and Keewatin by the Fury-and-Hecla Strait and Fox Channel. 

In eastern Asia, the Bering Strait marks the northeastern limit of an immense plateau that extends from the Tian-shan Mountains to the Khingan Mountains and from the Himalaya to the strait.

## Other Global Straits and Passages
In the Pacific, the Torres Straits separate the north point of Australia from New Guinea. New Guinea's interior is largely unknown, though its mountains are said to reach 20,000 ft. and appear permanently covered in snow.

In historical military contexts, straits have served as strategic crossing points for armies. During the siege of Lilybæum, after Rome received news of the loss of part of its forces, an army of ten thousand men was raised and marched by land to join the besiegers after crossing the strait.
