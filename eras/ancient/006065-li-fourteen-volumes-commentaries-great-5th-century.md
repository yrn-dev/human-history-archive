# Pāli Commentaries of the 5th Century

The Pāli commentaries of the 5th century consist of fourteen volumes of works authored by great scholars based in Ceylon and south India. These texts are significant not only for their religious content but also as sources of important historical data regarding the social and religious life of India during the periods they describe.

## Authorship and Origins
The majority of these fourteen volumes are attributed to two primary scholars: Buddhaghosa of Budh Gaya and Dhammapāla of Kancipura (the ancient name for Conjeeveram). 

Buddhaghosa was a celebrated Buddhist writer and a Brahmin by birth, born near the great Bodhi tree at Budh Gaya in north India around A.D. 390. He was the son of Kesī. Under the influence of his teacher, Revata, Buddhaghosa traveled to Ceylon. This journey was undertaken because the commentaries on the scriptures had been preserved there in the Sinhalese language, and Buddhaghosa sought to translate them into Pāli.

## The Work of Buddhaghosa
Upon arriving in Anuradhapura, Buddhaghosa studied under Sanghapāla. Before beginning his translations, he sought and received the consent of the fraternity there. To demonstrate his ability, he first wrote the *Visuddhi Magga* (the Path of Purity), which serves as a summary of Buddhist doctrine.

Buddhaghosa's literary output was prodigious. It is known that he wrote books that would fill approximately 20 octavo volumes of about 400 pages each. His specific works include:
*   **Nānodaya (the Rise of Knowledge):** A book written before his arrival in Ceylon.
*   **Commentary on the Pitakas:** He began a commentary on the principal psychological manual contained in the *Pitakas* in north India and later rewrote it while in Ceylon.
*   **Sumangala Vilāsinī:** A portion of the translated commentaries; one volume of this work has been edited.
*   **Commentary on Buddhist Canon Law:** Extracts of this work have been discovered in a nearly contemporaneous Chinese translation, which served as the basis for a Pāli edition.

After completing many years of labor in Ceylon, Buddhaghosa returned to the neighborhood of the Bodhi tree in north India.

## Preservation and Publication
The record of early Buddhism in the north of India—specifically north or a few miles south of the Ganges—contains many gaps. However, the development of the religion through its first eight or nine centuries can be traced. 

Current efforts to make these texts accessible include the work of the Pāli Text Society, which continues to publish two volumes per year. The Pāli Text Society has published the rewritten text of Buddhaghosa's commentary on the psychological manual of the *Pitakas*. Additionally, the Russian Academy has started a series to publish important Sanskrit works that remain in manuscript form.

The voluminous writings of authors like Buddhaghosa, whose dates are approximately certain, provide a vital standard for testing the age of other writings in the history of Indian ideas and literature, where definite dates are often lacking.
