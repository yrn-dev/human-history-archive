# Paleario

## Overview of the Author and Work
Paleario is the attributed author of *Benefizio della Morte di Cristo*, a book described as remarkable and originating from the period of the Reformation. The text is historically significant due to the efforts made to suppress it; nearly all existing copies of the work were destroyed by the Inquisition.

## The 1855 Edition
In 1855, Dr. Babington published an edition of *Benefizio della Morte di Cristo*. This publication served as a facsimile of the *editio princeps*, which had been originally published in Venice in 1543. To make the work accessible to a wider audience, Babington's edition included an introduction as well as versions of the text in English and French.

## About Dr. Babington
The recovery and publication of Paleario's work was part of the diverse scholarly output of Dr. Babington. A many-sided individual, Babington was recognized as a Greek scholar, gaining fame for his editions of the speeches of Hypereides—specifically *Against Demosthenes* (1850), *On Behalf of Lycophron and Euxenippus* (1853), and the *Funeral Oration* (1858)—which he produced from papyri discovered in Egypt at Thebes in 1856 and 1847.

Beyond his work on the Reformation and classical Greek texts, Babington's interests spanned several other disciplines:
*   **Natural History:** He possessed a taste for ornithology and botany, influenced by his familiarity with country life. He authored the *Flora of Suffolk* (1889) with W. M. Hind and the *Catalogue of Birds of Suffolk* (1884-1886). He also contributed appendices on these subjects to Potter's *History and Antiquities of Charnwood Forest* (1842) and was an authority on conchology.
*   **History and Archaeology:** He published *Roman Antiquities found at Rougham* (1872) and an *Introductory Lecture on Archaeology* (1865). He also edited Bishop Pecock's *Repressor of Overmuch Blaming of the Clergy* (1860) and the first two volumes of Higden's *Polychronicon* (1858).
*   **Classical Studies:** He catalogued Greek and English coins for the Fitzwilliam museum and classical manuscripts for the University Library. Additionally, he published inscriptions found in Crete by the explorer T. A. B. Spratt in 1855 and 1865.
*   **Ecclesiastical Defense:** In 1849, he wrote *Mr Macaulay's Character of the Clergy ... considered*, a work defending 17th-century clergy against the strictures of Macaulay, which earned the approval of Mr. Gladstone.
