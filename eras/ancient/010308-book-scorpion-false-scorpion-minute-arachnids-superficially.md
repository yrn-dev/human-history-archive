# BOOK-SCORPION

The book-scorpion, also referred to as the false scorpion, is a minute arachnid belonging to the class Arachnida and the order Pseudoscorpiones. These creatures superficially resemble scorpions that lack tails.

## Distribution and Habitat
Book-scorpions are found in all tropical and temperate countries. Their habitats vary significantly depending on the species:
* **General Habitats:** Most live beneath the bark of trees, under stones, or within vegetable detritus.
* **Human Dwellings:** Certain common British forms, specifically *Chiridium museorum* and *Chelifer cancroides*, are frequently found in furniture, old chests, and books.
* **Coastal and Cave Environments:** Species such as *Ganypus littoralis* and its allies may be located between tide-marks under pieces of coral or stones. Other species, which are mostly blind, reside permanently in dark caves.

## Diet and Behavior
The diet of the book-scorpion consists of mites or minute insects. They exhibit specific behaviors related to their feeding habits, such as lodging themselves beneath the wing-cases of large beetles in tropical regions to feed on parasitic mites. Similarly, they are known to seize the legs of other two-winged insects, such as horse-flies, for the same presumed purpose.

To ensure safety during the processes of moulting and hibernation, book-scorpions spin a small spherical cocoon.

## Reproduction and Life Cycle
Book-scorpions are oviparous. After the eggs are laid, the mother carries them attached to the lower surface of her body. The young remain with the parent until they have acquired their definite form and are capable of shifting for themselves.

## Evolutionary Context and Related Forms
In the broader history of animal life, creatures related to sea scorpions and ancient king crabs are identified as the earliest land scorpions and spiders. These forms emerged as animals began to migrate from the water to the land, following the emigration of plants.

The transition from water to land involved significant biological modifications. For the land scorpion, the gill-plates characteristic of the primitive sea scorpion became sunken into the body, creating lung-books that protect the organism from rapid evaporation.

## The Sea Scorpion
The sea scorpion is a distinct, more powerful and mobile creature that ruled during the Lower Palæozoic age. These ancient organisms are found in the Record of the Rocks, where they are described as crawling among seaweeds in the primordial seas. One specific specimen of a sea scorpion is identified as *Pterichthys milleri*, which is noted for its body armour.
