# The Teachings of Jesus

The personal teachings of Jesus of Nazareth are recorded in the four gospels and are noted for marking a new phase in the moral and spiritual life of the human race. While the gospels contain the personality and teachings of Jesus, they contain very little of the dogmas later established by the Christian church.

## Core Moral and Spiritual Concepts
The teachings of Jesus emphasize the universal Fatherhood of God and the implicit brotherhood of all men. Central to this vision is the insistence upon the sacredness of every human personality, viewing each individual as a living temple of God. This perspective introduced a new respect for man as man into the world.

The spirit of these teachings, as preserved in the gospels, was opposed to the subjugation of man by man. This stance was further reflected in Christianity's opposition to outrages upon human dignity, such as the gladiatorial combats held in the arena.

## The Kingdom of Heaven and Later Doctrines
The original teaching of Jesus focused on the Kingdom of Heaven. Later developments in Christian doctrine were largely the work of immediate followers, most notably St. Paul. Although Paul had never heard Jesus preach or seen him, he laid down the broad lines of Christian belief in his epistles. 

While Paul did little to enlarge the original teaching of the Kingdom of Heaven, he expanded the understanding of Jesus' identity and purpose. Paul taught that Jesus was the promised leader of the Jews (the promised Christ) and that his death served as a sacrifice for the redemption of mankind, similar to the sacrificial victims of primordial civilizations.

## Critical and Exegetical Study
The study of the New Testament and the words of Jesus has been a subject of extensive critical and exegetical investigation by various scholars.

### Scholarly Contributions
Several works have focused on the linguistic and historical analysis of these teachings:
* **G. Dalman** authored *Die Worte Jesu* (1898), translated as *The Words of Jesus* (1905), focusing on Semitic elements in the New Testament.
* **Johannes Weiss** wrote *Die Predigt Jesu vom Reiche Gottes* (1st ed. 1892, 2nd ed. 1900).
* **Bengel** is recognized for his edition of the Greek New Testament and his *Gnomon*, an exegetical commentary.
* **C.A. Briggs**, an American scholar, contributed to the *International Critical Commentary* and the *Hebrew Lexicon*.

### Textual History
The way the gospels were read evolved over time. In the 2nd century, Tatian, a pupil of Justin Martyr, created the *Diatessaron*, a harmony of the four gospels. This format remained the usual way the gospels were read until the beginning of the 5th century, when it was replaced by the Peshito and copies of the *Diatessaron* were systematically destroyed.
