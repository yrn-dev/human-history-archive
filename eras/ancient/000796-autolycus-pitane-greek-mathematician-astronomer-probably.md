# AUTOLYCUS OF PITANE

Autolycus of Pitane was a Greek astronomer and mathematician who is believed to have flourished during the second half of the 4th century B.C. This dating is based on the account that he provided instruction to Arcesilaus.

## Mathematical and Astronomical Works
The extant body of work attributed to Autolycus consists of two treatises. The first, *Peri kinoumenes sphairas*, presents a series of simple propositions concerning the motion of the sphere. This work is noted for its historical significance, as it provides insight into the state of spherical geometry prior to the work of Euclid and Autolycus himself. 

The second work, *Peri epitolon kai duseon*, is composed of two books and focuses on the rising and setting of the fixed stars.

## Textual History and Editions
The works of Autolycus have been preserved and disseminated through several translations and editions. There are various versions of his texts in Latin, as well as a French translation produced by Forcadel in 1572. In 1885, F. Hultsch published an edition in Leipzig that included the Greek text accompanied by a Latin translation.

## Geography of Pitane
Pitane, the town associated with Autolycus, was an ancient Greek town of Æolis. It was located on the coast at the mouth of the Evenus, positioned opposite the island of Lesbos (Mytilene). Pitane was also mentioned by the Greek geographer Strabo and Pliny as the place where "floating bricks" were manufactured. These bricks, which were later rediscovered in 1791 by an Italian named Fabroni, are characterized by their light weight, fair strength, and poor heat conductivity.

## Distinctions from Mythology
It is necessary to distinguish the mathematician Autolycus of Pitane from the figure of Autolycus found in Greek mythology. The mythological Autolycus was the son of Hermes and the father of Anticleia (the mother of Odysseus). He resided at the foot of Mount Parnassus and was renowned as a swindler and thief, possessing the supernatural ability to change anything his hands touched. According to myth, he took part in the Argonautic expedition and taught Heracles the art of wrestling.
