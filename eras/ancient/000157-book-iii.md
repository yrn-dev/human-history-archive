## Religious and Apocryphal Texts
In the context of the Books of Maccabees, which are Apocryphal books of the Old Testament, Book III is specifically noted for its inclusion in the Septuagint. It is distinguished from the two books of Maccabees found in the Vulgate, which were declared canonical by the council of Trent in 1546. While Book III is absent from the Vulgate, it is found in a Syriac recension.

In other religious literature, the Book of Baruch contains a section (iii. 9-iv. 29) that is believed by some to preserve, either in whole or in part, a lost epistle written by Baruch to the two and a half tribes located at Babylon.
