# Tiles and Tile-Work

Tiles have been utilized across various cultures and eras for architectural roofing, decorative flooring, and functional infrastructure. Their application ranges from the construction of imperial palaces and religious temples to the development of modern drainage systems.

## Architectural and Decorative Uses

In historical Japanese architecture, tiles were used almost exclusively for temple roofs during earlier periods. In the city of Kyoto, the Imperial citadel featured a fence roofed with tiles and a palace roofed with green tiles of Chinese manufacture. While most private dwellings were shingled, some featured slate-coloured tiles. During the Nara epoch, the roofs of official buildings were also tiled.

In India, the Chand Minar—a 210-foot high tower in Daulatabad erected in 1445 by Ala-ud-din Bahmani—was originally covered with beautiful Persian glazed tiles. This tower is regarded as one of the most remarkable specimens of Mahommedan architecture in southern India.

In England, the church of All Saints in Bakewell contains admirable specimens of encaustic tiles. Additionally, the potter William Frend De Morgan (born 1839) became known in artistic circles for "De Morgan" tiles, which were noted for his rediscovery of certain beautiful glazes and colours.

## Ancient Roofing Techniques

In ancient architecture, the term "imbrex" refers to a covering tile used on roofs. In this system, a plain tile is turned up on each side, and the imbrex covers the joint. While the simpler version of the imbrex is semicircular, some Greek temples utilized an imbrex with an angular top and vertical sides. In the temple of Apollo at Bassae, the tiles and the imbrex were crafted from Parian marble and worked as a single piece from solid marble.

## Archaeological Evidence

Tiles often serve as indicators of ancient urban settlements. At Silchester, a Roman town that has been systematically uncovered, the soil remains thick with potsherd and tile. In hot summers, the growth of corn reveals the remains of streets beneath the surface.

## Functional Applications

Beyond decoration and roofing, tiles are employed in agricultural and civil engineering for drainage. Tile-drainage involves laying pipes in trenches; the cost of this process varies based on soil tenacity, wages, and pipe costs, ranging from approximately £2 10s. per acre on loose soils to £10 per acre on tenacious soils. To facilitate the installation of these pipes, workmen use a tile-hook, an implement used to lower pipes from the edge of a trench to the bottom.
