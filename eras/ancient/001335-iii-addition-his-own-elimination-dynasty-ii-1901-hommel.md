# Chronological Systems of Hommel and Niebuhr

The determination of dates for the early dynasties of Babylon has been the subject of various scholarly systems, notably those proposed by Hommel and Niebuhr. These systems are characterized by their efforts to reduce the dates assigned to Dynasty I, primarily through the re-evaluation or elimination of Dynasty II.

## The Systems of Hommel

Hommel proposed several chronological schemes between 1886 and 1901, often adjusting his theories to reconcile different historical sources or to reduce the date of Khammurabi.

In his first system, published in 1886, Hommel reversed the order of the first two dynasties found in the Kings' List, placing Dynasty II before Dynasty I. By 1895 and 1898, he abandoned this reversal and instead adopted a suggestion from Halevy. This new approach proposed that Dynasty III followed Dynasty I immediately. Under this theory, Hommel suggested that Dynasty II was either mainly apocryphal (described as *eine spaetere Geschichtskonstruction*) or had synchronized with Dynasty I.

In 1898, Hommel proposed extremely low dates. These were the result of two factors: his elimination of Dynasty II and his adoption of an emendation by Peiser regarding the length of Dynasty III. Peiser had suggested that the Kings' List figure of "9 soss and 36 years" (576 years) for Dynasty III was a scribal error for "6 soss and 39 years," thereby reducing the dynasty's length by 177 years.

By 1901, Hommel abandoned Peiser's emendation and offered two alternative schemes. In one, he attempted to reconcile the Kings' List with Berossus by granting Dynasty II an independent existence of approximately 171 years. As a second alternative, he returned to a theory practically identical to his 1895 proposal.

## The System of Niebuhr

Niebuhr developed a system that served as a modification of Hommel's second theory. Unlike Hommel's later attempts to ignore or eliminate Dynasty II entirely, Niebuhr allowed for its independent existence but reduced it to 143 years. In Niebuhr's chronology, Dynasty II overlapped with Dynasty I by 225 years.

## Comparative Chronologies

The variations in these systems are reflected in the assigned dates for the three dynasties (the First Dynasty of Babylon, the Dynasty of Sisku or Uruku, and the Kassite Dynasty).

## Broader Chronological Context

The efforts of Hommel and Niebuhr exist alongside other scholarly attempts to resolve discrepancies in Babylonian data. A second group of researchers—including Peiser, Rost, Lehmann-Haupt, and Marquart—focused on emending figures from inscriptions. For example, Peiser's reduction of Dynasty III was intended to align the total duration of the dynasties with the system of Berossus and the statements of Nabonidus regarding Khammurabi.

More recent information suggesting the elimination of Dynasty II from the throne of Babylon points to a rise of Dynasty I around 2000 or 2050 B.C. This adjustment harmonizes with the chronological notices of Shalmaneser I and suggests that Nabonidus's estimate of the period of Khammurabi was exaggerated. Furthermore, this perspective suggests that the historical period of Berossus should not be synchronized with Dynasty I of the Kings' List, but perhaps with an earlier dynasty that may have had its capital in another great city of Babylonia, such as the Dynasty of Isin.
