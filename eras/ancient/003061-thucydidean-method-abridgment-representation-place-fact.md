## General Concepts of Abridgment and Epitome
The archive provides a general definition of an "epitome" (from the Greek *epitemnein*, meaning "to cut short"). An epitome is defined as an abridgment, summary, or abstract that provides the salient points of a law case, a book, or a concise account of a particular event. By transference, the term is also used to describe the representation of a larger concrete or abstract thing reproduced in miniature. An example of this is the description of St Mark's as the "epitome of Venice" because it contains architectural examples from the 10th through the 19th centuries.

Other examples of abridgment in the archive include the works of Dupuis. In 1798, Dupuis published a one-volume abridgment of his work *Origine de tous les cultes, ou la religion universelle*. A second abridgment of the same work was later produced by M. de Tracy, which was executed according to a more methodical plan. Additionally, a Latin abridgment of philosophy from 1784 is cited to show that Descartes' innate ideas were universally abandoned by that time.
