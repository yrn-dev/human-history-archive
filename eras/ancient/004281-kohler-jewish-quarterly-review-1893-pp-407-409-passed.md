# Kohler and the Rest of the Words of Baruch

## Analysis of the Text
In the *Jewish Quarterly Review* (1893), specifically on pages 407-409, Kohler provided an analysis of the book titled *The Rest of the Words of Baruch*. According to Kohler's findings, this work was undoubtedly written originally by a Jew, though it was subsequently revised by a Christian.

## Nomenclature and Preservation
The text is known by different names depending on the tradition and language. In the Abyssinian Church, it passed under a double name, referred to as both "the Rest of the Words of Baruch" and "the Rest of the Words of Jeremiah." Its Greek name is the latter, *ta paraleipomena Hieremiou prophetou*. The work has been preserved in several languages, including Slavonic, Armenian, Ethiopic, and Greek.

## Publication History
The Greek version of the text was first printed in Venice in 1609. Subsequent printings include:
* Ceriani in 1868, within *Mon. Sacra*, v. 11-18.
* Harris in 1889, titled *The Rest of the Words of Baruch*.
* Bassiliev in 1893, within *Anec. Graeco-Byzantina*, i. 308 sqq.

## Narrative Content
The book begins with an account regarding the removal of the sacred vessels of the Temple prior to its capture by the Chaldees, a beginning that mirrors the Syriac Apocalypse of Baruch. The narrative describes Baruch remaining in Jerusalem while Jeremiah accompanies the Exiles to Babylon. 

Following 66 years of exile, Jeremiah brings the sons of Israel back to Jerusalem. However, he refuses to admit those who had brought heathen wives with them. The text concludes with a vision of Jeremiah, which is described as Christian.
