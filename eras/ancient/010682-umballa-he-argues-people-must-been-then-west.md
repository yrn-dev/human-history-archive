# Umballa

Umballa is a location associated with the early history of the Indo-Aryan people and the geography of the Indian peninsula.

## Historical and Literary Significance
According to Professor E.W. Hopkins in *India Old and New* (1901), the *Rig Veda* was composed in the district surrounding Umballa. Based on this, Hopkins argues that the people of that time must have been situated to the west of the great rivers. He supports this conclusion by citing the text of the *Rig Veda*, in which the dawn is addressed as one who "comes gleaming over the waters, making good paths" before the wind arises and in shining light.

## Regional Geography and Soil
In a geographical context, Umballa lies to the east of the District of Ludhiana. The eastern edge of the Ludhiana district, which borders Umballa, is characterized by a bed of rich mould covering the clay soil. This specific soil composition is noted as being suitable for the cultivation of sugar-cane and cotton.

## Context of the Indo-Aryan Migration
The area is part of a broader history involving the Eastern branch of the Indo-Europeans, known as the Indo-Aryans. This group settled in India, distinguishing them from the Iranians who remained in Persia and Bactria. While the Hindus have no tradition regarding their invasion of India, it is established that they are not indigenous to the region. Evidence suggests they entered India from the other side of the Hindu Kush. 

By the period of their earliest literature, roughly 1000 B.C., these people were settled in the valley of the Indus. It is believed that the Eastern portion of the stock reached the open country of the Indus by pushing their way along the Kabul valley, shortly after the separation from the Iranian branch.
