# THE WAR OF THE DYNASTIES

The concept of the dynasty—a succession of rulers from a single family—is a recurring theme across the history of various global civilizations. In many regions, the transition between these dynasties was marked by civil war, rebellion, and foreign conquest, leading to cycles of unification and fragmentation.

## Dynastic Cycles in China
Chinese history is characterized by a long series of dynasties, beginning with five exemplary emperors who reigned between 2700 and 2400 B.C. The feudal period was dominated by two great dynasties: the Shang (1750 to 1125 B.C.) and the Chow (1125 to 250 B.C.). The transition from the Chow to the Ts’in Dynasty occurred when the kings of Ts’in ousted the last Chow rulers and seized the sacred sacrificial bronze tripods, allowing them to assume the imperial duty of offering sacrifices to Heaven.

The Ts’in Dynasty, led by Shi-Hwang-ti (the "first universal emperor"), ended the era of divided and feudal China. Shi-Hwang-ti organized the construction of the Great Wall to defend against the Huns. However, his reign was followed closely by a civil war, which resulted in the establishment of the Han Dynasty. Under the Han, the empire expanded beyond its original two river valleys and restrained the Huns.

The decline of the Han Dynasty at the close of the second century A.D. was precipitated by a great pestilence. This led to a period of division known as the "Three Kingdom Period," characterized by warring states and the eruption of barbaric rulers. In the fourth century, a dynasty of civilized Huns established rule in the province of Shen-si, extending their influence into Siberia. From a fusion of these Siberian and native north Chinese elements, the Suy dynasty emerged and conquered the south, initiating a Chinese renascence. This was followed by the Tang dynasty, which endured for three centuries (618–907 A.D.). Following the Tang, periods of civil war interrupted the "pleasures of peace and prosperity" before the rise of the Tsung Dynasty (960 to 1119 A.D.).

## Dynasties of Egypt and the Near East
In Egypt, history is divided into a succession of dynasties spanning over 4,000 years. The "old kingdom" phase culminated in the IVth Dynasty, a period of immense wealth and splendor. The monarchs of this era—Cheops, Chephren, and Mycerinus—constructed the three great pyramids at Gizeh. These massive sepulchral piles exhausted Egypt's resources over three reigns, leaving the nation wasted as if by a war. The dynastic sequence ended with Dynasty XXXI when Egypt fell to Alexander the Great in 332 B.C., following a prior conquest by the Persians.

## Dynastic Transitions in Burma and Japan
In Burma, the royal lineage claims descent from ancient Buddhist monarchs of India. Between the 11th and 13th centuries, the old Burman empire reached its peak, but the dynasty and its capital, Pagan, were destroyed by a Mongol invasion in 1284 A.D. under Kublai Khan. Subsequently, Central Burma was often controlled by Shan dynasties. In the early 16th century, the princes of Toungoo established a dynasty that held Pegu, Ava, and Arakan, though their wars eventually exhausted the country. A subsequent dynasty in Ava maintained supremacy until the 18th century, when the Peguans (Talaings) revolted and captured Ava. In 1753, Alompra reclaimed the capital from the Peguans.

Conversely, Japanese history is noted for being faithful to one uninterrupted line of sovereigns, contrasting with the Chinese experience where dynasties were frequently founded upon rebellion and parricide. However, the introduction of Confucianism in Japan is linked to a civil war that broke out in A.D. 671 following the death of Tenji Tenno, ending in 672 with the suicide of the Emperor's son.

## European Dynastic Shifts
In the history of the Franks, the Merovingian dynasty began with Clodion, who established his realm in Tournai. His descendant, King Clodovech, expanded the realm to include Northern France after wars against the West-goths and Burgundians in 506, moving the capital to Paris. By the seventh century, the Merovingian kings had become weaklings dominated by mayors of the palace. In 751, a mayor of the palace named Peppin founded a new royal dynasty: the Carolingians.

## Chronological Uncertainties
The recording of dynasties is not always precise. In Grecian history, there is significant uncertainty regarding chronology before the Olympiads. While some scholars suggest the existence of authentic dynasties reaching back to the war of Troy, others argue that such claims lack sufficient evidence and rely on conjecture rather than proof.
