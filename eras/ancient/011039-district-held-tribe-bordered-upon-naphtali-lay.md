# Asher

## Origins and Etymology
Asher was a tribe of Israel named after the son of Zilpah (the maid of Leah) and Jacob. According to the narrator of Genesis xxx. 12 seq. (J), the name Asher is intended to mean propitious or happy. This naming may be an allusion to the fertility of the territory held by the tribe, a connection further supported by Deuteronomy xxxiii. 24 and Genesis xlix. 20. Alternatively, the name may have originally served as a divine title, similar to the name Gad.

## Geography and Territory
The district held by the tribe of Asher was situated in a region where its boundaries were not definite and references to its territory remain obscure. Geographically, the district bordered upon Naphtali and lay to the south of Dan. To its south were the territories of Zebulun and Issachar. 

Because the tribe was located in the closest proximity to the Aramaeans and Phoenicians, the population of the district was exceptionally mixed. Descriptions of the occupation of Palestine in Judges i. 31 seq. indicate that the territory contained a strong Canaanite element. Due to its position, the tribe was bidden in the Blessing of Moses to defend itself, which was evidently a warning against invasion (Deut. xxxiii. 25).

## Historical Accounts
The tribe of Asher is mentioned in several historical narratives with varying roles:

*   **Conflict with Sisera:** The tribe is blamed in Judges v. 17 for failing to take part in the fight against Sisera.
*   **Defeat of the Midianites:** Asher is listed alongside Naphtali and Zebulun as participants in Gideon's defeat of the Midianites (Judg. vi. 35, vii. 23). However, this specific account is not considered the older of the two versions of the event, and the inclusion of Asher's name is likely the result of a late redactor.
