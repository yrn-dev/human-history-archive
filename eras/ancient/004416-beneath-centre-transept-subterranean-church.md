# Subterranean Church of the Nativity

The subterranean church of the Nativity is located beneath the centre of the transept of the church of the Nativity at Bethlehem.

## Architectural Context
The church of the Nativity at Bethlehem serves as a standing example of an oriental basilica from the same date as the original churches of the Apostles and St Sophia at Constantinople. While those two buildings have entirely passed away, the church at Bethlehem remains, having been rebuilt by Justinian during the 6th century.

The structure follows a basilican plan, which was originally the rule in both the West and the Eastern church before the erection of St Sophia at Constantinople introduced a new type that almost entirely superseded it. This basilican form was also termed *dromical*, a name derived from its shape, which resembled a race-course (*dromos*).

The church of the Nativity features several specific architectural elements:
* An oblong atrium.
* A narthex, also referred to as a vestibule.
* Double aisles characterized by Corinthian columns.
* A transept, with each end terminating in an apse, in addition to the apse located in the usual position.

## Related Subterranean Structures in Early Churches
The presence of a subterranean space beneath the main structure of a church is a recurring feature in early ecclesiastical architecture. In many basilicas, a subterranean chapel known as the *confessio* was located beneath the altar. This space typically contained the body of the patron saint and the relics of other holy persons. The *confessio* often reproduced the original grave or catacomb-chapel where the patron saint was interred, serving as the sacred nucleus around which the rest of the church was constructed.

Examples of this arrangement can be found in the original cathedral of Canterbury, as well as in S. Apollinare in Classe at Ravenna and the Roman basilicas of St Paul and St Peter. In the case of Old St Peter's, the subterranean chapel was believed to contain the remains of St Peter and was the center of devotion for a large portion of the Christian world; a vaulted crypt ran around this area.

Other instances of subterranean architectural features include:
* **S. Clemente:** An earlier church exists below the current building. This older structure was abandoned and filled with earth—likely due to the ruins caused by Robert Guiscard in 1084—and served as the foundation for the new edifice.
* **St Nicholas (Carrickfergus):** This antiquated cruciform structure contains a visible entrance to a subterranean passage located under the communion-table, which connects the church to a former Franciscan monastery.
