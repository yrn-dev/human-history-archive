# Philologie

Philologie (Philology) is a field of scholarly study encompassing the analysis of language, grammar, and the critical commentary of classical and historical texts. Its application ranges from the study of ancient Greek and Latin works to the historical development of the English language.

## Classical Philology in Antiquity
The development of grammatical analysis among the Greeks was a slow process, though certain sophists are noted for being decidedly in advance of their age regarding these studies. In the ancient world, the role of the commentator and critic was central to the preservation and understanding of literature. Aemilius Asper, a Latin grammarian who possibly lived in the 2nd century A.D., served as a critic and commentator on the works of Virgil, Sallust, and Terence. Fragments of his work on Virgil indicate that he possessed taste and good judgment. It should be noted that two short grammatical treatises attributed to Asper are of very little value and belong to a later period, specifically the 6th century during the time of Priscian.

## German Classical Scholarship
In the modern era, German scholars have contributed significantly to classical philology through the editing of texts and the teaching of linguistic structures. Franz Bücheler (1837–1908), a German classical scholar educated at Bonn, held professorships at Freiburg, Greifswald, and Bonn. He was highly successful as both a commentator and a teacher. Bücheler served as the joint-editor of the *Rheinisches Museum für Philologie* starting in 1878. 

His scholarly contributions include several editions, such as *Frontini de aquis urbis Romae* (1858), *Pervigilium Veneris* (1859), *Petronii satirarum reliquiae* (1862; 3rd ed. 1882), *Hymnus Cereris Homericus* (1869), *Q. Ciceronis reliquiae* (1869), and *Herondae mimiambi* (1892). Additionally, he wrote *Grundriss der lateinischen Deklination* (1866) and *Das Recht von Gortyn* (1885, with Zitelmann), and he supervised the 1893 third edition of O. Jahn's *Persii, Juvenalis, Sulpiciae saturae*.

## Germanic and English Philology
Philology also extends to the historical study of the English language and its Germanic roots. This includes the study of Old English, Middle English, and the transition to the Renaissance. Key works in this field include H. Paul's *Grundriss der germanischen Philologie*, which contains contributions by A. Brandl on both Old English ("Altenglische Litteratur") and Middle English ("Mittelenglische Litteratur").

Other significant contributions to English philology include:
* **Grammar and Syntax:** M. Kaluza's *Historische Grammatik der englischen Sprache* (1890), L. Kellner's *Historical Outlines of English Syntax* (1892), and Lorenz Morsbach's *Mittelenglische Grammatik* (1896).
* **Etymology and Language History:** Professor W.W. Skeat's *Principles of English Etymology* (1887–1891), Johan Storm's *Englische Philologie* (1892–1896), and O.F. Emerson's *History of the English Language* (1894).
* **Old English:** Eduard Sievers' *Angelsachsische Grammatik* (1898) and K.D. Bulbring's *Altenglisches Elementarbuch* (1902).

Further scholarly discourse is maintained through publications such as *Englische Studien* (edited by Kolbing and later Hoops), *Anglia* (edited by Wulker and Flugel), and the publications of the Modern Language Association of America.

## Interdisciplinary Applications
Philological methods are often employed in the study of ancient legal procedures and history. For example, the study of ancient and obscure criminal procedures in Athens—specifically regarding the judges *ἐν Φρεαττοῖ* and the courts of the *ephetæ*—has been addressed in works such as Matthiæ's *De Judiciis Atheniensium*, published in *Miscellan. Philologie*.
