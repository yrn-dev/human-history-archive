# AS

The *as* was a Roman unit of weight and measure. In a general sense, the term *as* denoted any integer or whole. This conceptual use of the word as a representation of a whole is the origin of the English word "ace."

## Divisions and Fractions
The *as* was divided into 12 *unciae*. This division is the etymological source of both the English words "inch" and "ounce." The system utilized a variety of specific names for its fractions:

*   **Deunx**: 11/12
*   **Dextrans**: 5/6
*   **Dodrans**: 3/4
*   **Bes**: 2/3
*   **Septunx**: 7/12
*   **Semis**: 1/2
*   **Quincunx**: 5/12
*   **Triens**: 1/3
*   **Quadrans**: 1/4
*   **Sextans**: 1/6
*   **Sescuncia**: 1/8
*   **Uncia**: 1/12

## Applications in Weight, Length, and Measure
Depending on the context of the measurement, the *as* represented different standard units:

*   **Weight**: The unit of weight was the *libra* (pound), which is approximately 11-4/5 oz. avoirdupois.
*   **Length**: The unit of length was the *pes* (foot), measuring approximately 11-3/5 inches.
*   **Surface**: The unit of surface was the *jugerum*, which is approximately 2/3 of an acre.
*   **Liquid Measure**: The unit was the *amphora*, approximately 5-3/5 gallons.
*   **Dry Measure**: The unit was the *modius*, approximately 9/10 of a peck.

## Legal and Financial Usage
Beyond physical measurements, the term *as* was applied to the concept of a whole inheritance or estate. In legal terminology, a *heres ex asse* was an heir to the whole estate, while a *heres ex semisse* was an heir to half of the estate. Additionally, the *as* was employed in the calculation of rates of interest.
