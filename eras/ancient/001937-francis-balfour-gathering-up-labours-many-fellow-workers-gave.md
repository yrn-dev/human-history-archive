# Francis Maitland Balfour

## Early Life and Education
Francis Maitland Balfour (1851–1882) was a British biologist born in Edinburgh on November 10, 1851. He was the younger brother of Arthur James Balfour. During his time at Harrow school, Balfour showed little interest in ordinary routines; however, he was encouraged in the pursuit of natural science by one of his masters, Mr. George Griffith. His interest in science, and specifically geology, had been cultivated by his mother from an early age.

In 1870, Balfour entered Trinity College, Cambridge, where he was elected a natural science scholar in 1871. Despite a reading regimen that did not follow the usual lines for the Schools, he achieved second place in the Natural Science Tripos in December 1873.

## Academic Development and Research
Balfour's focus shifted toward animal morphology after attending a course of lectures on embryology delivered by Sir Michael Foster in 1871. Following his tripos, he was selected for one of the two seats allocated to the University of Cambridge at the Naples zoological station. 

His research conducted at the Naples station led to his election as a fellow of Trinity in 1874. This work also provided the material for a series of papers, later published as a monograph in 1878, concerning Elasmobranch fishes. This research provided new insights into the development of several organs in Vertebrates, specifically the nervous and uro-genital systems.
