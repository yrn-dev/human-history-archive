# George Boole and the Laws of Thought

## Logical Inference and Symbolic Method
George Boole developed a system of logical inference that was considered both original and remarkable. This system, which is fully detailed in his work *Laws of Thought*, established a general symbolic method. Boole demonstrated that by using a purely symbolic treatment of premises, it is possible to derive any conclusion that is logically contained within those premises, regardless of the number of terms involved in the propositions.

## Probabilities and Mathematical Works
In the second part of the *Laws of Thought*, Boole attempted to discover a general method regarding probabilities. His goal was to create a system that would allow for the determination of the consequent probability of any event logically connected to a given system of events, based on the probabilities already known. While Boole published very little outside of his logical and mathematical contributions, his intellectual interests were extensive.

## Literary and Philosophical Interests
Boole possessed a wide and deep acquaintance with general literature. His favorite poet was Dante, and among Dante's works, he preferred the *Paradiso* over the *Inferno*. His studies frequently included the ethics of Spinoza, the metaphysics of Aristotle, and the philosophical writings of Cicero, along with other kindred works.

## Reflections on Science and Society
Boole's thoughts on religious, philosophical, and scientific matters were expressed through four specific addresses. These printed works are titled:
* *The Genius of Sir Isaac Newton*
* *The Right Use of Leisure*
* *The Claims of Science*
* *The Social Aspect of Intellectual Culture*
