# Cozeba

The term Cozeba is associated with the historical and religious interpretations of the identity of Simeon, the leader of the revolt against Rome during the reign of Hadrian (A.D. 132-135). In the context of this figure, Cozeba is linked to the title "man of Cozeba," with a reference found in Chron. iv. 22.

## The Identity of Simeon
Simeon was the leader of a revolt against Roman authority. In Christian sources, he is referred to as Barcochebas, Bar-Cochab, or Bar Kokba, a name meaning "son of a star." This specific designation does not appear in the writings of Roman historians. During the insurgency, the rebels were able to strike coins that identified Simeon as the "prince of Israel," alongside Eleazar the priest.

## Rabbinic Interpretations and Nomenclature
The naming of Simeon varies significantly between different traditions. While Christian sources use the "son of a star" designation, Rabbinic sources refer to him as Bar (Ben) Coziba, which translates to "son of deceit." This name is derived from the Hebrew root KZB, meaning "to be false," and is thought to reflect a later verdict of condemnation following the failure of the revolt.

The distinction between "Cochab" and "Coziba" is further explained through the perspective of Rabbi 'Aqiba. Rabbi 'Aqiba recognized Simeon as the Messiah and applied the text of Num. xxiv. 17 to him. In doing so, he read the passage not as *Cochab* ("a star"), but as *Cosiba* ("goes forth from Jacob"). Consequently, the title Bar-cochab is viewed as a Messianic title for the "man of Cozeba." It is noted that later Rabbis recalled his original name with sinister intention.

## The Roman-Jewish Conflict
Initially, the Roman Empire paid little attention to the insurgents. During this period, the rebels not only minted their own currency but also persecuted Christians who refused to participate in the revolt. Eventually, however, the Romans collected troops and systematically reduced the various fortresses occupied by the Israelites. The conflict reached its conclusion with the fall of Beth-thar (Bethar).

## Legacy and Accounts
Historical records include extraordinary stories regarding the prowess of Barcochebas. These accounts specifically highlight the rigorous and severe ordeals to which he subjected his soldiers as a method of training.
