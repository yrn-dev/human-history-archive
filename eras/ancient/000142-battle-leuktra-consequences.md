# BATTLE OF LEUKTRA AND ITS CONSEQUENCES

The Battle of Leuktra was a pivotal military engagement between the forces of Sparta and Thebes. The conflict arose from a long-standing dispute over presidential rights in Bœotia, a matter previously decided in favor of Sparta during the peace of Antalkidas. However, in the sixteen years preceding the battle, the balance of power shifted, allowing the Thebans to reconquer these rights. This led to a renewed confrontation characterized by diminished Spartan power and increased Theban force and confidence, led by a new leader of inestimable worth.

## Prelude and Omens
Prior to the engagement, the Thebans took up a position at Leuktra opposite the Spartan army. According to Plutarch, Pelopidas experienced a disturbing vision during this time. The plain of Leuktra contained the tombs of the Leuktridæ, the daughters of Skedasus, who had been violated by Spartan strangers. Their father, unable to obtain satisfaction from the Lacedæmonians, cursed the Spartan race before committing suicide at the tombs. Legends and oracles had long warned Spartans to beware of the vengeance of Leuktra, though many did not understand the specific location of the threat.

## The Engagement
The Spartan campaign was led by King Kleombrotus, who marched into Bœotia and encamped at Leuktra. The Thebans, under the leadership of Epaminondas, adopted a new order of battle. Despite the confidence of Kleombrotus and the Spartans, the result was a complete Spartan defeat. King Kleombrotus was slain during the battle.

There is a discrepancy in historical accounts regarding the timing of reinforcements. Diodorus states that Jason of Pheræ arrived in Bœotia with a considerable force and that Archidamus marched from Sparta with a second army before the battle. In this version, Kleombrotus had initially agreed to a truce and evacuation but broke it upon being reinforced by Archidamus. In this account, Kleombrotus commanded the right wing and Archidamus the left. However, other sources indicate that Jason arrived at Leuktra after the battle had already occurred.

## Immediate Aftermath in Sparta
The defeat produced a great surprise and an immense alteration of feeling throughout Greece. In Sparta, the return of defeated soldiers presented a grave social crisis. Spartan custom dictated that any citizen surviving a defeat faced extreme scorn and ignominy; such individuals were treated as outcasts, shunned by their families and fellow citizens, and subjected to physical abuse if they appeared cheerful.

Because the losses at Leuktra were severe and the number of fully qualified Spartan citizens was already declining, the ephors feared the danger of creating numerous malcontents. They referred the matter to Agesilaus, who proposed that the customary penalties be suspended for this specific case. This allowed the survivors to reintegrate into Spartan society without dishonor.

## Consequences in Bœotia and Greece
The victory significantly extended the power of Thebes and lowered the estimation of Sparta's military superiority in Greece. In Bœotia, the Thebans took punitive measures against cities; the walls of several Bœotian cities were destroyed. The Thespians, who were present in Bœotia during the battle, were expelled shortly afterward and their town was destroyed.

The victory was not well received in Athens, though the Athenians had remained neutral during the dispute. Following the battle, the Spartan army eventually retired from Bœotia under a capitulation.

## Peloponnesian and Regional Shifts
The defeat at Leuktra triggered a turbulent reaction against philo-Spartan oligarchies across the region. In the Peloponnesus, Spartan harmosts and dekarchies were expelled. In Arcadia, the city of Mantinea was re-established by its own citizens, and the Mantineans delivered a humiliating refusal to Agesilaus. This period saw a movement toward a Pan-Arcadian union, which was eventually formed after a revolution in Tegea expelled the philo-Spartan party.

Other regional developments included:
* **Athens:** The city placed itself at the head of a new Peloponnesian land-confederacy.
* **Amphyctionic Assembly:** Thebes brought an accusation against Sparta, resulting in the Spartans being condemned to a fine.
* **Jason of Pheræ:** Jason utilized his power and ambition to pursue his own plans, including involvement with the Pythian festival, before his eventual assassination at Pheræ. His death provided relief to Thebes and satisfaction to Greece.
