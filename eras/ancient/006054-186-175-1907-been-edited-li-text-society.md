# Pāli Texts and the Nikāyas

The Pāli books are not books in the modern sense, but are instead memorial verses or sentences designed to be learned by heart. Because of this primary necessity, their arrangement and style are entirely subordinated to the requirements of memory.

## Structure of Pāli Literature
The basic unit of these texts is the *sutra* (Pāli: *sutta*), which is typically very short—usually spanning one or two pages—and contains a single proposition. When several suttas containing similar propositions are collected within the framework of a single dialogue, the resulting work is called a *suttanta*. While most suttantas are approximately a dozen pages in length, a few are longer. A collection of these suttantas may be referred to as a book, though such a collection is a string of passages rather than an essay or a narrative.

## The Nikāyas
The Nikāyas are collections of these suttantas. The first of the four Nikāyas is the *Dīgha Nikāya*, which translates to "the Collection of Long Ones," as it contains the longest suttantas. The *Majjhima Nikāya* is the "Collection of the suttantas of Medium Length," containing works shorter than those in the Dīgha Nikāya but longer than those found in the subsequent two collections.

Together, the Dīgha and Majjhima Nikāyas contain 186 dialogues. In these texts, the Buddha, or occasionally one of his leading disciples, is depicted in conversation regarding ethical, philosophic, or religious points of the system known as Buddhism. These dialogues are characterized by an earnest and elevated tone, the use of Socratic questioning, and a depth of philosophic insight that reflects the cultured thought of the era. While they are dignified and occasionally eloquent, their style differs from Western writings because they were intended for memorization rather than reading.

## Canonical Authorities on the Life of the Buddha
Canonical Pāli texts, which reached their current form before the 4th century B.C., provide episodic accounts of the life of the Buddha. These include:

*   **Birth:** Accounts are found in the *Majjhima Nikāya* (edited by Trenckner and Chalmers, Pāli Text Society, 1888-1899) and the *Anguttara Nikāya* (edited by Morris and Hardy, Pāli Text Society, 1888-1900).
*   **Adoration of the babe:** This old ballad is found in the *Sutta Nipāta* (edited by Fausböll, Pāli Text Society, 1884).
*   **Youth at home:** This is recorded in the *Anguttara Nikāya* (i. 145).
*   **The going forth:** This is presented as an old ballad in the *Sutta Nipāta* and as a prose account in the *Dīgha Nikāya* (edited by Rhys Davids and Carpenter, Pāli Text Society, 1890-1893).
*   **Attainment and Conversion:** The first long episode, covering the going forth, years of penance, the attainment of Buddhahood and Nirvāna, and the conversion of the first five converts, is found in the *Majjhima Nikāya* and the *Vinaya* (edited by Oldenberg, 1879-1883).
*   **Early Teaching:** The second long episode, covering the period from the conversation of the five until the end of the first year of teaching, is found in the *Vinaya*.
*   **Visit to Kapilavastu:** This account is located in the *Vinaya*.
*   **The Last Days:** The third long episode, known as the *Mahāparinibbāna Suttanta*, is found in the *Dīgha Nikāya*.
