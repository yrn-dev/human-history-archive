## Ancient African Geography and Economy
In the context of ancient history, specifically regarding the Carthaginian dominions, the region of Emporium (also referred to as Emporia) is identified as a country of Africa located on the Lesser Syrtis. This area, where Leptis was situated, was noted as being more fruitful than any other part of the Carthaginian territories. The revenue generated from Emporia was so significant that the Carthaginians based nearly all of their hopes upon it. Due to the value of this region, the Carthaginians maintained a state of jealousy and care to prevent the Romans from sailing beyond the Fair Promontory, fearing that the Romans would discover this country and attempt to conquer it.

## Roman Observations of Africa
Cato the Elder, upon returning from Africa, provided a report to the Roman senate that contradicted prevailing Roman beliefs. While some in Rome supposed that Carthage was in a weak, humble state or exhausted of money and men, Cato declared that he found the city crowded with vigorous young men. He reported that Carthage possessed prodigious magazines of arms and warlike stores, as well as immense quantities of silver and gold. He described the inhabitants as haughty and confident, with boundless ambition and hopes. To demonstrate the proximity of the enemy to Rome, Cato presented the senate with African figs, noting that they had been gathered only three days prior.
