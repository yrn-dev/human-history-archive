# Descriptio Orbis Terrarum

The *Descriptio Orbis Terrarum* is a work produced by the Roman aristocrat and poet Rufius Festus Avienus. Avienus, who was from Vulsinii in Etruria and flourished during the second half of the 4th century A.D., was a pagan and a staunch supporter of the old religion. He is believed to have served as the proconsul of Achaia in 372 and the proconsul of Africa in 366.

## Composition and Content
The *Descriptio Orbis Terrarum* is a paraphrase of the *Periegesis* of Dionysius. Avienus composed this work using hexameters. This literary effort was part of a broader body of work by Avienus, which also included a translation of the *Phainomena* of Aratus, also written in hexameters.

## Related Works of Avienus
In addition to the *Descriptio Orbis Terrarum*, Avienus authored several other works:
* A description of the coasts of the Black, Caspian, and Mediterranean Seas, written in iambic trimeters across several books (though only a fragment of the first book remains).
* Epitomes of Virgil's *Aeneid* and the works of Livy, written in the same iambic trimeter, both of which are now lost.
* Various minor poems found in anthologies, including a humorous piece addressed to a man named Favianus requesting pomegranates for medicinal use.

## The Original Source
The *Descriptio Orbis Terrarum* is based on the *Periegesis* of Dionysius.
