# Varus Composes The Tumults In Judea

## Background of the Sedition
Following the departure of Archelaus, the nation of the Jews entered a state of tumult. Varus, the president of Syria, had previously informed Cæsar of this revolt via letters. In an attempt to maintain order, Varus traveled to Jerusalem to restrain those promoting the sedition, as it was evident the nation would not remain at rest. Before returning to Antioch, Varus left one of his legions in the city to keep the Jews quiet, who were described as being fond of innovation.

However, the presence of the legion did not end the sedition. Sabinus, the procurator of Syria, remained behind and caused great distress to the Jews. Driven by extraordinary covetousness and a love of gain, Sabinus used force to seize citadels and conducted a bitter search for the king's money. He armed a multitude of his own servants to act as instruments for these pursuits, which further oppressed the Jews and led them to rebel.

## The Outbreak of Revolt
The situation escalated during the festival of Pentecost (the 50th day after the passover). An immense multitude gathered, not for divine worship, but out of indignation. This group included people from Jericho, Idumea, Galilee, and Perea (beyond Jordan), though those from Judea were the most numerous and active. The rebels divided themselves into three parts and besieged the Romans by pitching camps at the palace on the west, the north side of the temple, and the south side by the Hippodrome.

## The Military Campaign of Varus
Upon being informed of the state of Judea through the writings of Sabinus, Varus feared for the legion he had left behind. He mobilized a significant force consisting of two additional legions (bringing the total Syrian legions to three), four troops of horsemen, and various auxiliary forces provided by tetrarchs and kings. The citizens of Berytus contributed fifteen hundred auxiliaries. Additionally, Aretas, the king of Arabia Petrea, provided footmen and horsemen to purchase Roman favor and express his hatred for Herod.

Varus dispatched a portion of his forces into Galilee, where they attacked the enemy, took the city of Sepphoris, burnt it, and enslaved its inhabitants. Varus himself marched toward Samaria. He did not interfere with the city of Samaria, as it had not joined the seditious, but his army caused widespread destruction in the surrounding area. The Arabians plundered and burnt the fortified village of Sampho and a village belonging to Ptolemy named Arus. Varus also ordered the burning of Emmaus, which had been deserted by its inhabitants, to avenge those who had been destroyed there.

## The Resolution in Jerusalem
As Varus approached Jerusalem, the Jews who had been besieging the Roman legion dispersed and fled throughout the country. The citizens of Jerusalem received Varus and denied any involvement in the revolt. They claimed they had not raised commotions but were forced to admit the multitude due to the festival, asserting they had been besieged alongside the Romans rather than assisting the rebels.

Upon his arrival, Varus was met by Rufus (who led the king's army and those of Sebaste), Gratus, and Joseph, the first cousin of Archelaus. The Roman legion also met him in their accustomed arms. Sabinus, however, avoided Varus's sight and had already fled the city for the sea-side.

## Punishments and Aftermath
Varus deployed a part of his army into the country to capture the authors of the commotion. Those who appeared to be the least concerned in the tumults were placed in custody. However, those judged to be the most guilty were crucified; the number of those executed in this manner was about two thousand.

Following these actions, Varus disbanded his army, as he found them useless for his purposes; the soldiers had behaved disorderly and disobeyed orders in pursuit of gain made through the mischief they caused. When Varus learned that ten thousand Jews had gathered, he attempted to capture them. On the advice of Achiabus, the multitude delivered themselves up to him. Varus forgave the crime of revolting for the general multitude but sent their commanders to Cæsar, many of whom were subsequently dismissed. The only individuals Varus punished without regard to justice were the relations of Herod who had fought against their own kindred.
