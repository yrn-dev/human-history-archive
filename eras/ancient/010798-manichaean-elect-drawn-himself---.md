# The Manichaean Elect

The Manichaeans maintained a distinct class of followers known as the "perfect" or "elect." The lifestyle and convictions of these individuals were rooted in a specific dualistic view of matter and the soul, which dictated their daily conduct, dietary restrictions, and social renunciations.

## Beliefs on the Soul and Matter
The Manichaean worldview held that every act of begetting—whether human or otherwise—resulted in a soul being condemned to a cycle of misery through its imprisonment in flesh. This perspective led the elect to view material existence with hostility. Because of this dualism, the Manichaeans rejected the belief that God was actually made flesh or that He truly died on a cross. 

This theological stance extended to their views on art. The Manichaeans were "arch-enemies" of relics, cross-worship, and material portraiture of Christ, particularly depictions of His sufferings on the cross.

## Dietary Restrictions and the Prohibition of Killing
Influenced by notions from the Farther East and India, the elect scrupulously abstained from eating flesh. This fasting was a direct result of a prohibition against taking life. The commitment to non-violence was so stringent that a fully initiated Manichaean would not perform the act of cutting his own salad. Instead, he employed a catechumen to perform this task on his behalf. Because the act of cutting the plant was viewed as a form of murder, the elect would subsequently shrive the catechumen for having committed the act.

## The Life of the Elect
The personal conduct of the Manichaean elect was characterized by total renunciation of worldly ties and material wealth. A portrait of an elect, preserved by Augustine in *contra Faustum*, describes a life of extreme asceticism and adherence to the "preaching and precept of Christ," which the elect identified as the gospel.

According to this account, the elect gave up the following:
*   **Family and Relationships:** They abandoned father, mother, wife, and children.
*   **Material Wealth:** They cast away gold and silver, ceasing to carry even copper in their belts.
*   **Physical Pleasures:** They renounced delights, pleasures, eating, and drinking.

The elect lived content with their daily bread, expressing no anxiety regarding how their bodies would be clothed or their bellies filled, nor caring for the morrow. The elect viewed themselves as embodying the beatitudes of Christ, describing themselves as pure of heart, gentle, mourners, and peacemakers who hungered, thirsted, and bore hatreds and persecutions for the sake of righteousness.

## Literary and Historical Context
The Manichaeans possessed a "heterodox library" consisting of codices that were described by St. Austin as being numerous, large, and precious. However, these texts were subject to destruction; Austin advised that all such membranes be burned, a recommendation that was rigorously followed. 

Historically, Manichaean beliefs formed enclaves in the Balkans through the transportation of Paulicians and Armenians. These groups, along with others who shared similar views on the divine nature of Christ, influenced the religious instincts of the masses in regions such as Asia Minor, Armenia, western Syria, and Egypt. This environment contributed to a preference for geometrical art—featuring peacocks, animals, foliage, and decorative crosses—over the delineation of human figures.
