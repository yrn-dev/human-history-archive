# Eine Mithrasliturgie

## Overview
*Eine Mithrasliturgie* is a work edited by Albr. Dieterich and published in Leipzig in 1903. The text is based on a Paris papyrus and contains the descriptions of an ancient mystic regarding his spiritual re-birth.

## Concepts of Re-birth and Spirit
The text presents a vision of spiritual restoration through the language of a prayer. The mystic addresses a being described as the "first spirit of the spirit in me," the "first beginning (or principle) of my beginning," and the "First birth of my birth." 

The central longing of the mystic is to be restored to a "deathless birth" (*genesis*). The text acknowledges that the individual is "let and hindered by his underlying nature," yet is driven by a "pressing need and spur of his longing." The goal of this process is to allow the mystic to gaze upon the "deathless principle" using a "deathless spirit."

## Vision of the Divine and the Cosmos
The mystic describes a journey of perception and consecration involving several elemental and spiritual stages. The text specifies that the gaze upon the deathless principle occurs through:
* The deathless water
* The air
* The solid

The mystic seeks to be re-born through "idea" (or reason) and to be consecrated so that the "holy spirit breathe in him." This state of being allows the mystic to admire "holy fire" and behold the "dread water" and the "abyss of the Orient." Furthermore, the mystic aspires to be heard by the "circumambient ether" and the "quickening" ether.

## Human Nature and Divine Power
The text contrasts the mortal origin of the mystic with the divine power that enables his transformation. The mystic describes himself as a "mortal born of mortal womb." However, he asserts that he has been "enhanced in excellence" through the "right hand of the Deathless one" and the "might of the All-powerful." This divine intervention allows him to gaze upon the "revealed reality" with "deathless eyes."

## Scholarly Context
In broader academic literature, *Eine Mithrasliturgie* is cited as a source for the study of ancient rites. It is listed alongside works such as J. Anrich's *Das antike Mysterienwesen* (1894), which details the corresponding rites of the Greek mysteries.
