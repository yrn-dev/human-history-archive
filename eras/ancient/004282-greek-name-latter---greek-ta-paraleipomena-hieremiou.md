# The Rest of the Words of Baruch

## Overview and Nomenclature
The book known as *The Rest of the Words of Baruch* is a text that was originally written by a Jew, though it was subsequently revised by a Christian, as noted by Kohler in the *Jewish Quarterly Review* (1893). The work is characterized by a double name within the Abyssinian Church, where it is referred to as both "the Rest of the Words of Baruch" and "the Rest of the Words of Jeremiah." In Greek, the text is known by the latter title: *ta paraleipomena Hieremiou prophetou*.

## Preservation and Publication
The text has been preserved in several languages, including Slavonic, Armenian, Ethiopic, and Greek. The Greek version saw its first printing in Venice in 1609. Subsequent editions were produced by Ceriani in 1868 within his *Mon. Sacra* (v. 11-18), by Harris in 1889 under the title *The Rest of the Words of Baruch*, and by Bassiliev in 1893 in *Anec. Graeco-Byzantina* (i. 308 sqq).

## Narrative and Content
The book opens with an account similar to that found in the Syriac Apocalypse of Baruch, detailing the removal of the sacred vessels of the Temple prior to its capture by the Chaldees. The narrative describes a separation of figures: Baruch remains in Jerusalem, while Jeremiah accompanies the exiles to Babylon.

Following 66 years of exile, Jeremiah leads the sons of Israel back to Jerusalem. However, he refuses admission to those who had brought heathen wives with them. The text concludes with a vision of Jeremiah, which is described as being Christian in nature.
