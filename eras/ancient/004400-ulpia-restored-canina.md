# Basilica Ulpia

The Basilica Ulpia was a major public building in ancient Rome, constructed by the Emperor Trajan around A.D. 112. It was built in connection with his forum and stood as one of the most extensive basilicas in the city.

## Architectural Design and Layout
The Basilica Ulpia followed a normal architectural scheme for its type, featuring a central space that was surrounded by galleries. These galleries were supported by either piers or columns. 

The layout of the building is documented in the Capitoline plan of Rome. While the building featured a semicircular projection at one end, this element was distinct from the basilica itself and was not a vaulted apse. An attempted restoration of the interior of the Basilica Ulpia was produced by Canina.

## Function and Use
The Basilica Ulpia served as a venue for legal proceedings. Specifically, it was regularly used for the centumviral court.

## Context within Roman Architecture
The Basilica Ulpia was part of a broader trend of basilica construction in Rome. By the age of Augustus, there were at least five basilicas in the immediate vicinity of the forum, including the Basilica Julia (which ran parallel to the forum's southern side) and the Basilica Fulvia-Aemilia. In total, approximately twenty public basilicas existed in Rome, alongside various private basilicas used for recreation or business, such as the one located in the palace of Domitian on the Palatine.

Beyond the capital, basilicas were nearly universal throughout the empire and in provincial Italian cities. Evidence of this prevalence exists as early as the death of Augustus; Suetonius records that when the emperor's body was transported from Nola in Campania to Rome, it rested in the basilica of every town along the way.

## Comparison with Other Basilicas
The form of the Basilica Ulpia differed from other Roman structures:
* **Internal Divisions:** Unlike the basilicas at Trier or Timgad, which had no interior divisions, the Ulpia utilized galleries.
* **Colonnades:** While the Ulpia had galleries surrounding the central space, other examples—such as those in North Africa, the palace basilica of Domitian, and the basilica at Silchester—featured colonnades only along the long sides, lacking them across the ends.
* **Structure:** The Ulpia differed from the Basilica of Maxentius or Constantine, which was an entirely vaulted structure resembling the great halls of the Roman Thermae.
* **Tribunals:** While basilicas at Silchester, Timgad, and Pompeii featured fixed tribunals, the Ulpia's specific end projection was not a vaulted apse.
