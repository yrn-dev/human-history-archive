# Avignon and the Cavares

## Roman Gallia and the Cavares
Avignon (known as *Avenio*) was an important town belonging to the Cavares, a Gallic tribe. During the Roman period, the city established itself as one of the leading cities of Gallia Narbonensis. This region of Gallia also included other Celtic tribes, such as the Allobroges, who resided on the Rhone and whose chief town was Vienna (modern Vienne).

## Medieval Transitions and Conflict
Following the Roman era, Avignon faced severe harassment from the Saracens and during the barbarian invasions. Over time, the city was attached to several different powers, including the domains of the counts of Toulouse, Forcalquier, and Provence, as well as the kingdoms of Arles and Burgundy. 

By the end of the 12th century, Avignon had become a republic. However, in 1226, Louis VIII dismantled the city and took it as punishment for its support of the Albigenses. The city was later forced to submit to the counts of Provence and Toulouse in 1251.

## The Papal Seat
In 1309, Clement V chose Avignon as his residence, and the city served as the papal seat until 1377. During this period, in 1348, the countess of Provence, Joanna, sold the city to Clement VI. After Gregory XI migrated back to Rome, Avignon became the residence of two antipopes: Benedict XIII and Clement VII. Benedict XIII was eventually expelled from the city in 1408.

The popes continued to possess the town, governing it through legates. While several kings of France attempted to unite Avignon with their own dominions, it remained under papal possession until 1791, when it was annexed by the National Assembly.

## Modern History and Religious Administration
The annexation in 1791 was marked by bloodshed resulting from conflicts between Republicans and adherents of the Papacy. Later, in 1815, the town was the site of the assassination of Marshal Brune, who was killed by adherents of the royalist party.

In terms of ecclesiastical history, a bishopric was founded in the city during the 3rd century. This see was later elevated to an archbishopric in 1475.
