# Draw- or Two-handed Bridge

The term "draw- or two-handed bridge" refers to two distinct concepts within the archive's records: a specific variation of the card game bridge designed for two players and a type of movable physical bridge known as a bascule bridge.

## Card Game Variation
Draw- or Two-handed Bridge is described as the best form of bridge for two players. In this version, each player is provided with a dummy placed opposite to them. To ensure the integrity of the game, a special stand is required so that the cards are arranged in a manner that prevents the opponent from seeing the other player's dummy.

The rules for making a declaration follow the same procedures used in dummy bridge or three-handed bridge; the dealer may either make the declaration themselves or pass it to their dummy. A noted objection to this format is that the opponent cannot see the dealer's dummy, which removes the opportunity to check for an erroneous declaration. This specific issue could be mitigated by removing the dealer's option to pass the declaration.

## Engineering and Physical Structures
In the context of civil engineering, draw bridges are also referred to as bascule bridges. The original type of this structure is the fortress draw-bridge, which utilizes a single leaf, or bascule, that turns around a horizontal hinge located at one abutment. These bridges are supported by abutments at each end when closed and are raised using counterweights and chains.

A more common engineering variation involves a bridge with two leaves or bascules, with one hinged at each abutment. In this design, the bascules are locked at the center when the bridge is closed. To achieve balance at the hinge, each bascule is prolonged backwards beyond the hinge point; these prolongations sink into the piers as the bridge opens.
