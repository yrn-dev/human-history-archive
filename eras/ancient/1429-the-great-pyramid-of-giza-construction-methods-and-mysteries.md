# The Great Pyramid of Giza: Engineering the Eternal

## Overview
The Great Pyramid of Giza, commissioned by the Pharaoh Khufu during the Fourth Dynasty of the Old Kingdom, stands as the sole survivor of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. Completed around 2560 BCE, it remained the tallest man-made structure on Earth for over 3,800 years. While its purpose as a royal tomb is widely accepted by Egyptologists, the sheer scale of its construction—consisting of roughly 2.3 million stone blocks—continues to spark intense scholarly debate.

The primary mystery lies in the logistics of construction. Historians and engineers debate whether the Egyptians used a single long external ramp, a series of internal spiral ramps, or a combination of both. While the "external ramp" theory is traditional, some modern researchers, such as architect Jean-Pierre Houdin, propose an internal ramp system to explain how the massive stones reached the pyramid's peak. Furthermore, the precision of the pyramid's alignment to true north is a testament to the Egyptians' advanced understanding of stellar observation and geometry.

## Key Facts
*   **Composition:** The core consists primarily of local yellow limestone, while the interior chambers (like the King's Chamber) utilize massive granite slabs transported from Aswan, over 500 miles away.
*   **Workforce:** Contrary to the Greek historian Herodotus's claim that 100,000 slaves built the pyramid, archaeological evidence from the "Workers' Village" suggests a professional workforce of skilled laborers and conscripted peasants who were well-fed and housed.
*   **Precision:** The base of the pyramid is level to within 15 millimeters, and the sides are aligned almost perfectly with the cardinal points of the compass.
*   **Internal Structure:** It is the only pyramid known to contain a complex system of ascending and descending passages, including the "Grand Gallery."

## Did You Know?
1. **The Casing Stones:** The pyramid was originally covered in highly polished white Tura limestone, which would have made the structure shine brilliantly under the Egyptian sun.
2. **The Weight:** The total mass of the Great Pyramid is estimated at approximately 6 million tonnes.
3. **The Air Shafts:** Small shafts lead from the King and Queen's chambers toward the exterior; many historians believe these were symbolic "star shafts" intended to allow the Pharaoh's soul to ascend to the heavens.
