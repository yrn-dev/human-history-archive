# Cambrian, Ordovician, Devonian and Carboniferous Ages in Southern Regions

The geological record of the Palaeozoic era, specifically the Cambrian, Ordovician, Devonian, and Carboniferous ages, is evident across various southern regions of the world, including South America, Southern Europe, and parts of Asia and the British Isles. These strata often reveal the historical presence of ancient seas, mountain-building events, and the distribution of early land masses.

## South America and Bolivia
In Bolivia, the eastern ranges of the Andes are composed of Palaeozoic rocks characterized by granitic and other intrusions. The distribution of these rocks varies by latitude: in southern Bolivia, the Cambrian and Ordovician beds constitute the greater part of the eastern Andes. Conversely, in the northern regions, particularly the north-eastern ranges, the Devonian and Carboniferous systems are more extensively developed. These Palaeozoic beds have yielded fossils from all four of these ages. 

The Palaeozoic strata in this region are directly overlaid by gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, a series similar to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Brazil and Argentina. At the base of this overlying series, tuff or conglomerate of porphyritic rocks is frequently present.

## Southern Europe and the British Isles
In Belgium, the "Hercynian chain" is composed entirely of Palaeozoic rocks. In the Ardennes, the rocks of this ancient mountain chain are primarily of the Devonian System. However, Cambrian beds rise through these Devonian strata, forming masses such as Stavelot and Rocroi, which appear to have been islands within the Devonian sea. In this specific area, the Ordovician and Silurian systems are absent, leaving the Devonian to rest unconformably upon the Cambrian. Carboniferous beds are found in the north of the Palaeozoic area; near Dinant, they are folded among Devonian beds, while a significant band containing the coalfields of Charleroi, Mons, and Liege runs along the northern border of the Ardennes.

In Cornwall, the sedimentary rocks are largely slaty "Killas." While much was previously classified as Devonian, further study has identified a small tract of Ordovician age near Gorran and Veryan. In the southern portion, the Menaccan series (the lowest Devonian beds) rests unconformably upon Ordovician beds, including the Mylor, Falmouth, and Portscatho series, as well as the Veryan series containing Caradoc fossils. To the north, the Culm measures consist of Carboniferous limestones, grits, and slates.

## Asia and the Middle East
The early geological history of Asia indicates that a large portion of the continent was submerged under the sea during the Ordovician and Cambrian periods. In Siberia, Cambrian and Ordovician beds lie mostly flat and undisturbed, overlaid by basic lava outflows and freshwater deposits of the Angara series.

In Asia Minor, Devonian and Carboniferous fossils have been identified in several locations. These include findings in the Anti-Taurus mountains, near the Bosporus (Devonian), and at Balia Maden in Mysia (Carboniferous). Additionally, Carboniferous rocks are present at Eregli (Heraclea Pontica), where they have been utilized for coal.

Regarding larger land masses, the peninsula of India is noted to have remained above the sea since at least the Carboniferous period. This region is part of a southern land mass known as Gondwanaland, which was separated from the northern land mass, Angaraland, by the Tethys (or central Mediterranean) sea.

## Geological Characteristics and Transitions
The transition between these ages often involves unconformities and intense tectonic activity. In the Ardennes, the absence of Ordovician and Silurian strata creates a direct contact between the Cambrian and Devonian. In Cornwall, severe thrusting from the south has resulted in the contortion and folding of these Palaeozoic rocks. Similarly, in Belgium, the Coal Measures (Carboniferous) represent the latest beds involved in the folds of the Hercynian chain, with the final elevation occurring toward the close of the Carboniferous period.
