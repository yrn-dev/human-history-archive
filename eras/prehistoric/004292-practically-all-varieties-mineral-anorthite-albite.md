# Plagioclase Felspar

Plagioclase felspar is a rock-forming mineral series that includes various species ranging from anorthite to albite. In its fresh state, the mineral is transparent and appears simple under ordinary light; however, when polarized, it reveals a complex twinned structure that breaks up into a series of bars of different colors.

## Occurrence in Basaltic Rocks
Plagioclase felspar is a constituent of basaltic rocks, where the minerals generally maintain a uniform character throughout the group. While practically all varieties of the mineral from anorthite to albite are known to occur in basalt, the most common species found in these rocks are labradorite and bytownite. When these minerals appear as phenocrysts, they may exhibit very perfect crystalline outlines.

The limpid character of fresh plagioclase is destroyed by weathering. This process produces turbid pseudomorphs that contain minerals such as white micas, calcite, kaolin, and epidote.

## Bytownite
Bytownite is a member of the plagioclase series of felspars. The name was first introduced in 1835 by T. Thomson to describe a greenish-white felspathic mineral discovered in a boulder near Bytown (the current city of Ottawa) in Ontario, though later microscopic examination revealed that specific material to be a mixture. The name was subsequently applied by G. Tschermak to plagioclase felspars situated between anorthite and labradorite, a definition generally adopted by petrologists.

In terms of physical characters, optical properties, and chemical composition, bytownite is much closer to the anorthite end of the series than to albite. It is a common constituent of basic igneous rocks, including basalt and gabbro. Notably, isolated crystals of bytownite with well-defined faces are unknown.

## Presence in Other Rock Types
Beyond basaltic rocks, various members of the plagioclase series appear in other geological formations:

*   **Marbles:** Purest marbles often contain accessory minerals, which can make up a considerable proportion of the mass. Among the feldspars present in these rocks, plagioclase varieties such as anorthite, labradorite, and albite occur more frequently than orthoclase.
*   **Epidiorite:** These metamorphic rocks, which are synonymous with hornblende-schist or amphibolite, consist essentially of felspar and hornblende. In these rocks, the felspar may be recrystallized and clear, or it may be converted into a turbid aggregate consisting of albite, zoisite, quartz, sericite, and epidote.
*   **Associated Minerals:** Albite has been found in association with quartz and prehnite in a zone of chlorite-schists and amphibolite at Le Bourg d'Oisans in Dauphine.
