# Sandstone in the Upper Ludlow Series

The Upper Ludlow series, part of the Ludlow group (or Ludlovian), represents the uppermost subdivision of the Silurian rocks in Great Britain. While the Ludlow group is essentially shaly in character, the strata become increasingly sandy toward the top, eventually passing gradually into the base of the Old Red Sandstone.

## The Downton Castle Sandstone
Within the Ludlow group, the Downton Castle sandstones form a significant formation, measuring 90 ft. in thickness. These are categorized as grey, yellow, or red micaceous, shaly sandstones. Along with the Tilestones and Ledbury shales, the Downton Castle sandstones are occasionally grouped under the term "Downtonian." These formations serve as passage beds between the Silurian and the Old Red Sandstone. Fossils found within these sandstones include *Platyschisma helicites*, *Lingula cornea*, and numerous ostracods and phyllocarids.

## Upper Ludlow Rocks and Sandy Beds
The Upper Ludlow rocks themselves consist primarily of shales and soft mudstones, though they contain several harder sandy beds. These specific beds are notable for being capable of being worked as building-stones and are frequently covered with annelid tracks and ripple-marks. One such uppermost sandy layer is designated as the "Fucoid bed" due to its abundance of seaweed-like impressions.

## The Ludlow Bone Bed
A prominent feature found at the base of the Downton Sandstone in the Upper Ludlow series is the Ludlow Bone Bed. In a restricted geological sense, this is a thin layer of bony fragments occurring on a well-defined geological horizon. Near Ludlow, this bed appears as a brown layer ranging in thickness from a quarter of an inch to 4 inches. At the site of Ludlow itself, two such beds are known, separated by approximately 14 ft. of strata.

The Ludlow Bone Bed is composed almost entirely of scales, teeth, and fragments of spines from ganoid fish. Other associated remains include mollusca and *Pterygotus*. This layer has been traced from Ludlow into Gloucestershire for a distance of 45 m.

## Associated Fossils of the Upper Ludlow
The geological horizon of the Upper Ludlow rocks contains a variety of fossils. Common findings include:
*   **Plants:** *Chondrites* and *Actinophyllum*.
*   **Fish:** *Cyathaspis*, *Cephalaspis*, and *Auchenaspis*.
*   **Invertebrates:** Cephalopods (specifically *Orthoceras bullatum*), gasteropods, pelecypods, trilobites, eurypterids, phyllocarids, and ostracods.
*   **Brachiopods:** *Chonetes striatella* and *Lingula minima*.

## Regional Variations
The Ludlow rocks exhibit different characteristics across various regions:
*   **Southern Scotland:** In the Kirkcudbright Shore and Riccarton district, these are represented by the Balmae Grits (500-750 ft.) and Raeberry Castle beds. In the northern belt of the Pentland Hills and Lanarkshire, the upper (Downtonian) part consists principally of conglomerates and thick yellow and red sandstones with green mudstones.
*   **Wales:** In Merionethshire and Denbighshire, the upper portion of the Denbighshire Grits belongs to this horizon, including the Upper Grit beds, the Dinas Bran beds, and the *Monograptus leintwardinensis* beds.
*   **Lake District:** The Coniston Grits (4000 ft.) succeed the Coldwell beds in this area.
*   **Ireland:** The Ludlow rocks include the "Croagmarhin beds" of the Dingle promontory and the "Salrock beds" of County Galway.
