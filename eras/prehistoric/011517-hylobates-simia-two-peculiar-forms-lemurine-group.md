# Hylobates and Simia

## Classification and Distribution
*Hylobates* and *Simia* are identified as peculiar genera within the order Quadrumana. These animals are found in the Indian region of Asia, a geographical area where the mammal fauna is considered to be much more highly developed than the fauna of the Palaearctic region.

## Regional Context
The Indian region is characterized by several distinct mammalian groups. In addition to the Quadrumana represented by *Hylobates*, *Simia*, and *Semnopithecus*, the region is home to two peculiar forms of the Lemurine group. The fauna of this region also includes:

*   **Bats:** Both frugivorous and insectivorous divisions are well represented.
*   **Insectivora:** This group includes very peculiar forms such as *Tupaia* and *Gymnura*.
*   **Carnivora:** This region is regarded as the true home of the tiger, although the animal has traveled far north into the Palaearctic division of Asia. Other characteristic members include various ichneumons, civets, and the benturong (*Arctictis*), as well as two species of bears restricted to the Indian region.
*   **Rodents:** Porcupines of two genera and numerous squirrels are present.
*   **Proboscidea:** The Indian elephant is one of only two remaining representatives of this order.
*   **Ungulates:** This group is represented by one species of tapir, four species of rhinoceros, and several peculiar forms of the swine family. The Bovidae (hollow-horned ruminants) include several genera of antelopes and species of true *Bos*, specifically *B. bubalus*, *B. frontalis*, and *B. sondaicus*. Deer are numerous, with the chevrotains (*Tragulus*) serving as a characteristic group for the region.
*   **Edentata:** This order is represented by several species of scaly ant-eater, known as *Manis*.

## Comparison with Palaearctic Fauna
The distribution of the Quadrumana (monkeys) differs significantly between the Indian region and northern Asia. While *Hylobates* and *Simia* are part of the developed fauna of the Indian region, the Quadrumana are nearly unrepresented in northern Asia. In that region, only one species occurs in Japan, and one or two others are found in Tibet and northern China.
