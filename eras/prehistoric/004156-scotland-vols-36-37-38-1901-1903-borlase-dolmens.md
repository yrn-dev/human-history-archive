# Scotland

## Historical Documentation and Records
The history of Scotland is documented through various collections of records and scholarly works. Key archival sources include the *Documents and Records illustrating the History of Scotland*, edited by F. T. Palgrave (1837), and the *Documents illustrative of the History of Scotland, 1286-1306*, edited by J. Stevenson (1870). Further primary materials are found in the *Calendar of Documents relating to Scotland*, edited by J. Bain (1881-1888). 

Comprehensive historical narratives have been provided by J. H. Burton in *History of Scotland*, vol. ii. (1905) and A. Lang in *History of Scotland*, vol. i. (1904). Specific figures of the Scottish past have also been the subject of detailed study, such as Sir H. Maxwell's work on *Robert the Bruce* (1897). Additionally, the correspondence regarding Charles II and Scotland in 1650 is preserved in the seventeenth volume of the Scottish History Society (1894).

## Prehistoric and Roman Scotland
Archaeological research into Scotland's early periods includes the study of pagan times by J. Anderson (1886). Exploration of the cairns of Arran and Bute was recorded by Dr. T. H. Bryce in the *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland* (vols. 36, 37, 38, 1901-1903). 

The Roman occupation of Scotland has been analyzed through various reports and studies. These include the *Antonine Wall Report* (1899) by Haverfield and J. Macdonald's *Roman Stones in Hunterian Mus.* (1897). Older perspectives on the region are found in Stuart's *Caledonia Romana* (1852). General Scottish excavations are documented in volumes xx. through xl. of the *Proceedings of the Society of Antiquaries of Scotland*, with specific focus given to *Bar Hill* by J. Macdonald (1906).

## Literary Figures and Works
Scottish literature features a variety of writers and poets across different eras. John Bellenden (also known as Ballantyne or Bannatyne), a writer born near the end of the 15th century in south-east Scotland (possibly East Lothian), is a notable figure. Educated at the universities of St Andrews and Paris, Bellenden served as clerk of accounts for James V. 

At the king's request, Bellenden translated the first five books of Livy and Boece's *Historia Scotorum*. The latter, published in 1536 as *The History and Chronicles of Scotland*, is regarded as a vigorous specimen of Scottish prose. For these translations, Bellenden was appointed a canon of Ross and archdeacon of Moray. A proponent against the Reformation, he was forced into exile; records of his death vary between 1550 in Rome and 1587. His translations of Livy were later edited for the Scottish Text Society by W. A. Craigie (1901, 1903), including a reprint of a holograph first draft discovered in the British Museum in 1902.

Other literary contributions include the *Popular Rhymes of Scotland*, published by Robert Chambers, and the work of Robert Buchanan. Buchanan's *The Land of Lorne* (1871) provides a vivid account of yachting experiences along the west coast of Scotland.
