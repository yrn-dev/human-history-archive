# The Silent Sentinels: Megalithic Cultures of Prehistoric Europe

## Overview
Megalithic architecture—derived from the Greek *megas* (great) and *lithos* (stone)—represents one of the most ambitious engineering feats of the prehistoric world. Emerging primarily during the Neolithic and Early Bronze Age, these monuments are found across Western Europe, from the Atlantic coast of Portugal to the plains of Scandinavia.

While Stonehenge in England is the most iconic example, it belongs to a broader tradition of communal labor and spiritual expression. These structures generally fall into three categories: **menhirs** (single standing stones), **dolmens** (portal tombs), and **cromlechs** (stone circles). Historians and archaeologists believe these sites served multiple functions, acting as burial chambers, territorial markers, and sophisticated astronomical observatories. However, because these cultures were non-literate, the exact ideological motivations remain a subject of intense scholarly debate.

## Key Facts

### The Engineering of Stonehenge
Stonehenge was not built in a single event but evolved over 1,500 years. The initial phase (c. 3000 BCE) was a simple earthwork enclosure. The iconic sarsen stones and smaller bluestones were added later. A point of significant historical interest is the origin of the bluestones; geological analysis confirms they originated from the Preseli Hills in Wales, over 140 miles away, raising questions about whether the stones were transported by land, sea, or if the people migrated with them.

### Astronomical Alignment
Many megaliths exhibit precise alignments with solar and lunar events. Stonehenge is famously aligned with the summer solstice sunrise and winter solstice sunset. Similarly, Newgrange in Ireland is engineered so that the winter solstice sun illuminates the inner chamber. While some scholars argue this proves a "prehistoric science," others caution against over-interpreting these alignments, suggesting they may have been symbolic rather than purely mathematical.

### Social Organization
The sheer scale of these monuments implies a highly organized social structure. Moving stones weighing up to 25 tons required coordinated leadership and a surplus of food to support a non-farming labor force. This suggests that Neolithic societies were more hierarchical and socially complex than previously assumed.

## Did You Know?
* **Not Just England:** France possesses some of the oldest megaliths in the world, specifically the Carnac stones in Brittany, which consist of thousands of aligned stones.
* **Acoustic Design:** Recent archaeological studies suggest that some stone circles were designed for acoustic effects, amplifying sound within the center to create a "sacred" auditory experience.
* **The "Henge" Definition:** A "henge" is not actually the stone circle itself, but the circular earthwork (a ditch and bank) that often surrounds the stones.
