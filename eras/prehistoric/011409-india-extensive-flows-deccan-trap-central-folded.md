# The Deccan Trap

The Deccan Trap refers to a series of extensive basaltic lava flows located in India. These formations are part of a broader pattern of volcanic activity that occurred during or since the Cretaceous period across various regions of the continent.

## Geological Composition and Distribution
The Deccan Trap consists of great basaltic lava fields. In the region south of Gujarat, nearly the entire area of Bombay is covered by these horizontal lava flows. This series extends across the greater part of the Kathiawar peninsula and reaches into Cutch. In the areas of Cutch and Kathiawar, the trap flows are underlaid by Neocomian beds—which include shales and sandstones containing plant remains identical to those in the upper portion of the Gondwana system—and marine Jurassic beds containing numerous Ammonites. In Cutch, the Deccan Trap is overlain by Tertiary shales, sandstones, and limestones.

Between the great sheets of the Deccan traps, a clay-like substance known as bole occurs. Bole is a hydrous aluminium silicate of red, brown, or yellow color that typically drops to pieces in water rather than being plastic.

## Topography and the Western Ghats
The trap formation is a defining feature of the Western Ghats (also known as the Sahyadri), a range of hills running down the coast. This range stretches southwards for more than 500 meters with a breadth of 10 to 20 meters. The Western Ghats generally run parallel to the ocean at a distance not exceeding 50 meters, with an average elevation of approximately 1,800 feet, though some individual peaks are more than double that height.

The landscape of the Western Ghats is characterized by the typical behavior of trap formations, descending to the plains in terraces with abrupt fronts. While the western declivity is abrupt, the landward slope is often very gentle, with the crest of the range sometimes only slightly raised above the Deccan plateau. A notable elevation in this region is Mahabaleshwar, a plateau 4,500 feet high located 37 meters from Poona. Towards the northern extremity of the Sahyadri hills, the country is broken and rugged, featuring spurs, masses of rock, and isolated peaks that serve as watersheds for the great rivers of the Deccan.

## Regional Volcanic Context
The volcanic activity associated with the Deccan Trap is linked to wider geological trends. In north-western Baluchistan, there is an extensive development of conglomerates and volcanic tuffs in the lower Tertiary and upper Cretaceous periods, which are likely contemporaneous with the Deccan Traps of India. 

On a continental scale, the Deccan Trap is situated in the southern region of unfolded beds. This contrasts with the central folded belt, which contains the now mostly extinct volcanoes of Persia, Armenia, Baluchistan, and Asia Minor. While the great volcanoes of the present day are located in the islands off the south eastern and eastern coasts, the eruptions in the Deccan and other mainland regions have almost ceased.
