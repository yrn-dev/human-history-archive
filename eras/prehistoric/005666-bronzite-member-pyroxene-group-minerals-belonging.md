# BRONZITE

Bronzite is a mineral belonging to the pyroxene group. Within this group, it is part of the orthorhombic series, which also includes hypersthene and enstatite. Rather than being classified as a distinct species, bronzite is a ferriferous variety of enstatite.

## Chemical Composition and Classification
Enstatite is a magnesium metasilicate with the chemical formula $\text{MgSiO}_3$, where small amounts of ferrous oxide (up to approximately 5%) may replace the magnesia. Bronzite, expressed as $(\text{Mg,Fe})\text{SiO}_3$, contains a higher concentration of ferrous oxide, ranging from about 5% to 14%. As the iron content increases beyond this range, there is a transition to hypersthene.

## Physical Properties
Bronzite typically appears in green or brown colors. Its specific gravity is approximately 3.2–3.3, though this value varies depending on the amount of iron present in the mineral.

A defining characteristic of bronzite is its bronze-like sub-metallic lustre found on its cleavage surfaces. This is the result of partial alteration known as "schillerization." During this process, iron separates into very fine films of hydroxides and oxide along the mineral's cleavage cracks, creating a metallic sheen or "schiller." While this effect is present in bronzite, it is even more pronounced in hypersthene.

## Geological Occurrence
Bronzite is found in several types of geological formations:
* **Igneous Rocks:** It is a constituent of many basic igneous rocks, specifically peridotites, gabbros, and norites.
* **Serpentines:** It occurs in serpentines that have been derived from the aforementioned basic igneous rocks.
* **Metamorphic Rocks:** It is found in some crystalline schists.

## Ornamental Use and Related Varieties
Bronzite is occasionally cut and polished, typically in convex forms, to create small ornamental objects, though it is used less extensively for this purpose than hypersthene. In instances where the mineral possesses a pronounced fibrous structure, the metallic sheen resembles that of a cat's-eye. Large masses suitable for cutting have been located in the serpentine of Kraubat near Leoben in Styria, as well as in the norite of the Kupferberg in the Fichtelgebirge.

A related altered form of bronzite or enstatite is known as schiller-spar or bastite. In bastite, the original enstatite has undergone hydration—resulting in a composition approximately that of serpentine—in addition to schillerization. Bastite maintains the same metallic sheen as bronzite and appears in brown or green. A typical locality for this mineral is Baste in the Radauthal, Harz, where pale greyish-green patches of bastite are found embedded in darker serpentine. While it can be used as a decorative stone when cut and polished, it is seldom used for that purpose.
