# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition of Stone Age tool-making represents one of the most significant cognitive leaps in human evolution. Rather than mere survival instincts, the progression from the Oldowan to the Mousterian industry demonstrates an increasing capacity for abstract thought, foresight, and manual dexterity. This evolution reflects the biological and behavioral shifts from early *Homo habilis* to the sophisticated *Homo neanderthalensis* and early *Homo sapiens*.

The process began with "mode 1" technology—simple flakes—and evolved into "mode 3" technology, where tools were intentionally shaped from a prepared core. This shift allowed early humans to move from opportunistic scavenging to active hunting and processing of complex materials.

## Timeline of Lithic Industries

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Tool-Maker | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Tool Use
The Oldowan industry, named after Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania, is characterized by "choppers." These were created by striking a river cobble with a hammerstone to create a sharp edge. While primitive, these tools allowed early hominids to butcher carcasses and crack bones for marrow, providing the high-calorie fats necessary for brain growth.

### The Acheulean: Symmetry and Planning
The Acheulean represents a massive leap in cognitive complexity. Unlike the Oldowan, where the tool's shape was accidental, the Acheulean hand-axe was bifacial—worked on both sides to create a symmetrical point. This suggests the maker had a "mental template" of the finished product before they began striking the stone.

### The Mousterian: The Levallois Revolution
The Mousterian industry is defined by the **Levallois technique**. Instead of shaping a stone into a tool, the knapper carefully prepared a core to strike off a single, predictable, and razor-sharp flake. This allowed for specialized tools, such as scrapers for hides and spear points for hunting. Historians and archaeologists often debate the "cognitive gap" between Acheulean and Mousterian tools, questioning whether the shift was a sudden mutation in intelligence or a gradual cultural adaptation to colder climates.

## Did You Know?
* **Material Selectivity:** Early humans didn't use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates (like flint, chert, and obsidian) because they fracture predictably (conchoidal fracture).
* **The "Hand-Axe" Mystery:** Some archaeologists argue that Acheulean hand-axes were not just tools, but "social signals" used to demonstrate fitness and skill to potential mates.
* **Global Variation:** While the Acheulean was widespread across Africa and Eurasia, it is notably absent from parts of East Asia, leading to the "Movius Line" theory regarding cultural divergence.
