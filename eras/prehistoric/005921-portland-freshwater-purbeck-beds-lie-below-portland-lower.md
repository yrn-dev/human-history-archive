# Portland

The term Portland refers to various geological formations, specific geographic locations in England, and a social organization in London.

## Geology of the Portland Beds
The Portland beds are a geological formation characterized by a composition that is sandy in its lower sections and calcareous in its upper sections. In certain regions, such as Buckinghamshire, the outcrop of these beds follows a south-west to north-east direction, extending from Thame through Aylesbury. In this area, the Lower Portland contains the Hartwell Clay. The Portland beds are noted for being abundant in fossils and are quarried in several locations for building stone.

In Dorsetshire, the Portland beds consist of Portland Stone and Portland Sand. These beds are situated beneath an elevated plateau of Purbeck Beds. In the Isle of Portland, the Portland formation is well exposed and yields well-known freestones. Beneath the Portland beds in this region, the Kimmeridge Clay crops out, featuring "Coal" bands that form a lower platform near the village of Kimmeridge.

## The Isle of Portland
The Isle of Portland is a peninsula in Dorsetshire, located south of Poole Harbour. It is characterized by the exposure of both the Portland and Purbeck formations. The island is home to significant quarries that supply a white freestone used in many of the finest buildings in London and other locations. Additionally, the Isle of Portland serves as an important fortified naval station, featuring a harbour of refuge under the administration of the Admiralty.

## Related Geological Formations
The Portland beds are closely associated with the Purbeck and Lower Greensand beds. Freshwater Purbeck beds are situated below both the Portland and Lower Greensand beds; in some areas, such as the ridge between Whitchurch and Oving, these freshwater beds form a cap. 

In the Isle of Purbeck, the Lower Purbeck contains the "Dirt bed," which consists of the remains of a Jurassic forest. This feature is visible both near Mupe Bay and on the Isle of Portland. Other related materials in the region include the Middle Purbeck building stone and the Upper Purbeck *Paludina* marble. The latter is a shelly limestone composed of the shells of the freshwater snail *Paludina carinifera* embedded in greenish, grey, or blue limestone. This marble was frequently used by medieval architects for sepulchral monuments and slender clustered columns in structures such as the Temple Church, Westminster Abbey, and the cathedrals of Lincoln, Worcester, Winchester, and Salisbury.

## The Portland Club
The Portland Club is a London-based organization that, in 1895, drew up a code of laws. This code was adopted in July 1895 by a joint committee consisting of the Portland and Turf Clubs. A revised version of this code was implemented in January 1905.
