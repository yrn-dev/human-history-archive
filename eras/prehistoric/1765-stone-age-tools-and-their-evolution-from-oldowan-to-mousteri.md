# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition from the Oldowan to the Mousterian tool industry represents more than just a change in stone-shaping techniques; it mirrors the cognitive and behavioral evolution of early hominins. For millions of years, the ability to manipulate stone allowed ancestors of *Homo sapiens* to access high-protein food sources, migrate across diverse environments, and eventually dominate the Pleistocene landscape. This progression is characterized by a shift from opportunistic "choppers" to highly standardized, multi-purpose tools.

## Timeline of Lithic Industries

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Hominin | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oldowan** | 2.6 – 1.7 Million Years Ago | *Homo habilis* | Simple flakes and choppers |
| **Acheulean** | 1.7 Million – 200,000 Years Ago | *Homo erectus* | Symmetrical hand-axes |
| **Mousterian** | 160,000 – 40,000 Years Ago | *Homo neanderthalensis* | Levallois (prepared core) technique |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Tool Use
The Oldowan industry, discovered at Olduvai Gorge, is the earliest widespread stone tool tradition. These tools were "mode 1" technology—simple river cobbles struck with a hammerstone to create a sharp edge. While rudimentary, these tools allowed hominins to butcher carcasses and crack bones for marrow, providing the caloric density necessary for brain growth.

### The Acheulean: The First "Design"
The transition to the Acheulean marked a cognitive leap. Unlike the Oldowan, Acheulean tools (mode 2) were bifacial, meaning they were worked on both sides to create a symmetrical teardrop shape. The "hand-axe" became the Swiss Army knife of the Paleolithic. Historians and anthropologists debate whether this symmetry was purely functional or if it suggests an emerging aesthetic sense or social signaling among *Homo erectus*.

### The Mousterian: Precision and Planning
The Mousterian industry is most closely associated with Neanderthals. It utilized the **Levallois technique**, a sophisticated method where the knapper carefully prepared a stone core to strike off a single, predictable, and sharp flake. This allowed for the creation of specialized scrapers and spear points. This shift indicates a higher capacity for abstract thought, as the toolmaker had to visualize the final product within the raw stone before the first strike.

## Did You Know?
* **Material Matters:** Early hominins didn't use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates like chert, flint, and obsidian because they fracture predictably (conchoidal fracture).
* **The "Hand-axe" Mystery:** Some archaeologists argue that Acheulean hand-axes were not tools at all, but rather "social lures" used to attract mates by demonstrating manual dexterity and intelligence.
* **Global Variation:** While these industries are the "standard" model, recent finds in Asia (such as the Movius Line theory) suggest some populations relied more heavily on bamboo or perishable materials rather than stone.
