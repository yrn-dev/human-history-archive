# Plateau Gravels and Geological Formations

The presence of gravel deposits and plateau formations varies significantly across different global regions, appearing in contexts ranging from prehistoric archaeological sites to diverse geological landscapes.

## Prehistoric Deposits in Sussex
In Sussex, gravel deposits have provided critical evidence regarding early human evolution. Excavations for road gravel led to the discovery of skull fragments from a creature named *Eoanthropus*, or "dawn man." These remains were found in a gravel deposit attributed to the Third Interglacial period, which is estimated to have begun 100,000 years ago and lasted for 50,000 years. However, it is noted that this gravel may have resulted from the washing out of even earlier strata, meaning the skull fragments could potentially date back to the First Glacial period. 

The skull of *Eoanthropus* is characterized as being thicker than that of any living race of men, with a brain capacity that falls between that of man and *Pithecanthropus*. In addition to these human remains, the gravel pits yielded the leg-bone of a deer featuring marks that may be cuts, a bat-shaped instrument made of elephant bone, and the teeth of hippopotamus and rhinoceros.

## Geological Formations of British East Africa
In British East Africa, the landscape is divided into four distinct physiographical regions. One of these is the ancient plateau, which begins at Taru and extends to the borders of Kikuyu. This region consists of ancient crystalline rocks covered by immense quantities of volcanic rocks from the post-Jurassic to Recent periods, creating the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa.

The sedimentary formations in this region include several types of deposits. These range from Recent alluvium and superficial sands to Pleistocene deposits, which include raised coral rock and conglomerates of Mombasa Island, as well as gravels containing flint implements. Other formations include Jurassic glacial beds, Karroo shales and limestones of Changamwe, and Archaean gneisses, schists, and granites.

## Other Plateau Regions and Deposits
Plateaus and associated deposits are found in various other global contexts:

*   **Asia and Persia:** The lakes of Asia include highland lochs and the swamps of Persia. In south-west Baluchistan and Persia, plateau *hamuns* change their extent and form seasonally and are often impregnated with saline deposits from the underlying strata. In the Persian desert, *kavirs* (salt depressions) consist of widespread deposits of salt and mud.
*   **Siberia:** The northern part of the region between the Arctic Ocean and the Seas of Bering and Okhotsk is a barren plateau, 1,000 to 2,000 feet high. This area is deeply indented by long fiords and the rivers of the Anadyr basin.
*   **Russian Poland:** The government of Kielce is an elevated plateau with an altitude of 800 to 1,000 feet. Its geology includes Silurian and Devonian quartzites, Carboniferous coal seams, and Triassic deposits containing lead and zinc ores of considerable thickness.
*   **India:** In Bengal, the plateau of Chota Nagpur is composed primarily of gneissic rocks. The region is also characterized by the great thickness of Gangetic alluvium; a borehole in Calcutta reached a depth of approximately 460 feet below sea level without encountering marine deposits. In Lucknow, the residency proper is situated on the summit of a plateau.

## Industrial and Technical Applications of Gravel
Gravel is utilized in various industrial and construction capacities. In shipbuilding and metallurgy, the term "dump" refers to the depositing of large quantities of material. Specifically, in hydraulic gold mining, "dumping" is the method used to dispose of masses of gravel that have been disintegrated by water.

In construction, gravel is categorized by its strength (strong or weak) when used as a foundation. Because the power of resisting pressure differs between a wall and the ground, "footings" are applied to walls to expand the bearing area. If a building is constructed on gravel, sand, or clay, the footing must be expanded based on the resistance of the structure. In cases of loose or imperfectly resisting soil, a solid platform, often a layer of concrete, is used under the footing to prevent the structure from sinking.
