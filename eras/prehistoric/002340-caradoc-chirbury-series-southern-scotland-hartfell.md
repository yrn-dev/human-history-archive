# Caradoc and Chirbury Series

The Caradoc and Chirbury Series are geological formations that represent specific strata within the Ordovician System. In the region of Shropshire, these series serve as the representatives of the Bala Series.

## Relationship to the Bala Series
The Bala Series is characterized geologically as a sequence of sandstones and dark slates containing beds of limestone. It was initially described by A. Sedgwick, who categorized it as the upper portion of the Cambrian System; however, it is currently positioned at the top of the Ordovician System, situated above the Llandeilo beds. 

The series is identified by the Bala limestone, which ranges from 20 to 40 feet in thickness and is recognizable across most of North Wales, serving as the equivalent to the Coniston limestone found in the Lake District. In its type area, the series includes the Hirnant limestone—a thin and inconstant bed—which is separated from the Bala limestone by 1,400 feet of slates. Below these are further slates and volcanic rocks, including large contemporaneous deposits of felsitic lava and tuff, which reach several thousand feet in thickness in the Snowdon District.

## Regional Representations
While the Bala Series is defined by the aforementioned characteristics in North Wales, it is represented by different series in other regions:
*   **Shropshire:** Represented by the Caradoc and Chirbury Series.
*   **Southern Scotland:** Represented by the Hartfell and Ardmillan Series.
*   **Ireland:** Represented by similar rocks.

## Geological Context in Southern Scotland
In southern Scotland, specifically within the county of Dumfries, the geology is dominated by the Silurian tableland. This area contains representatives of all divisions of that system, from the Ludlow rocks to the Arenig. 

A significant portion of this county is occupied by strata of Llandovery and Tarannon age, consisting of greywackes, flags, shales, and massive grits that are sometimes conglomeratic. Within this belt, there are lenticular bands of older strata from the Caradoc, Llandeilo, Arenig, and Llandovery ages. These older strata are composed of flags, white clays, black and grey shales, and cherts. These bands emerge along anticlinal folds and are noted for yielding abundant graptolites characteristic of these divisions.

## The Hartfell Shales
Within the basin of the river Annan, Professor Lapworth identified three typical sections to illustrate three great groups of these strata. The Hartfell shales represent the Caradoc division. These shales are located on Hartfell, positioned between the Glenkill shales (Upper Llandeilo) found in the Glenkill Burn north of Kirkmichael, and the Birkhill shales (Lower Llandovery) located in Dobbs Linn near St Mary's Loch.

Additionally, in the extreme northwest of Dumfries county—between Dalveen Pass and Drumlanrig Castle in the south, and the Kello and Spango Waters in the north—there is a broad development of Caradoc, Llandeilo, and Arenig strata. These are represented by black shales, conglomerates, grits, Radiolarian cherts, greywackes, and shales.
