# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The progression of Stone Age tool-making represents one of the most significant cognitive leaps in human evolution. Rather than a linear path, the evolution from the Oldowan to the Mousterian industry reflects an increasing mastery of raw materials, a growing capacity for foresight, and the development of complex social learning. By analyzing these "lithic assemblages," historians and archaeologists can trace the transition from opportunistic scavenging to strategic hunting and organized social structures.

## Timeline of Lithic Industries

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Tool Maker | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oldowan** | 2.6 – 1.7 Million Years Ago | *Homo habilis* / *Australopithecus* | Simple flakes and choppers |
| **Acheulean** | 1.7 Million – 200,000 Years Ago | *Homo erectus* / *Homo heidelbergensis* | Symmetrical hand-axes |
| **Mousterian** | 160,000 – 40,000 Years Ago | *Homo neanderthalensis* / Early *H. sapiens* | Levallois (prepared core) technique |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Tool Use
The Oldowan industry, discovered at Olduvai Gorge, is characterized by "mode 1" technology. These tools were produced by striking a hammerstone against a core to create sharp-edged flakes. These were not designed for a specific shape but were used opportunistically for butchering carcasses and cracking bones for marrow.

### The Acheulean: The First Standardized Tool
The transition to the Acheulean marked a cognitive shift. Unlike the Oldowan, Acheulean tools—most notably the pear-shaped hand-axe—were "mode 2" technology. This required a mental template; the maker had to envision the finished tool within the raw stone. Historians debate whether the symmetry of these axes was purely functional or if it represented an early aesthetic sense or a social signal of fitness.

### The Mousterian: Precision and Specialization
The Mousterian industry represents "mode 3" technology and is closely associated with Neanderthals. The breakthrough here was the **Levallois technique**, a sophisticated method of preparing a stone core so that a single, precise strike would detach a flake of a predetermined size and shape. This allowed for the creation of specialized tools, such as scrapers for hide working and pointed spears for hunting.

## Did You Know?
* **Material Preference:** Early humans didn't use just any stone; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline rocks like flint, chert, and obsidian because they break with a predictable "conchoidal" fracture.
* **The "Hand-axe" Mystery:** Some Acheulean hand-axes have been found in such perfect symmetry that they appear unused, leading some researchers to suggest they were used as social currency or symbols.
* **Composite Tools:** By the Mousterian era, humans began "hafting"—attaching stone points to wooden shafts using resins or fibers—marking the birth of composite weaponry.
