# Mount Robson

## Overview and Geography
Mount Robson is a prominent peak located within the Canadian Rockies. It reaches an altitude of 13,700 ft., making it the highest peak in the Canadian Rockies. While it is the highest point in that specific range, it is not the highest peak in British Columbia; that distinction belongs to Mount Fairweather, which is situated on the International Boundary and rises to 15,287 ft.

## Regional Context
Mount Robson is part of a broader mountain system where the range culminates between the latitudes of 51° and 53°. Other significant high peaks within the Rocky Mountains of Canada include:
* Columbia (12,740 ft.)
* Forbes (12,075 ft.)
* Assiniboine (11,860 ft.)
* Bryce (11,686 ft.)
* Temple (11,626 ft.)
* Lyell (11,463 ft.)

Along the boundary of the United States, the average height of the Rocky Mountain range is 8,000 ft.

## Geological Composition
The Rocky Mountains are primarily composed of palaeozoic sediments. These sediments range in age from the Carboniferous to the Cambrian. Additionally, the range contains subordinate infolded areas of Cretaceous origin, which hold coal.

## Mountain Passes
The region surrounding the Rocky Mountains is characterized by several passes. Moving from the south, these include:
* **South Kootenay (or Boundary Pass):** 7,100 ft.
* **Crow's Nest Pass:** 5,500 ft., which crosses great coal fields and is traversed by the southern branch of the Canadian Pacific railway.
* **Kicking Horse (or Wapta Pass):** 5,300 ft., traversed by the main line of the Canadian Pacific railway.
* **Athabasca Pass:** 6,025 ft.
* **Yellow Head Pass:** 3,733 ft., which is likely to be used by the Grand Trunk Pacific railway.
* **Pine River Pass:** 2,850 ft.
* **Peace River Pass:** 2,000 ft., the route through which the Peace river flows.
