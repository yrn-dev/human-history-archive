# Ricciocarpus and Riccia natans

*Ricciocarpus* and *Riccia natans* are plants belonging to the Ricciaceae family within the order Marchantiales. While most members of the Ricciaceae grow on soil, these two specific genera occur floating on still water.

## Botanical Classification and Structure
As members of the Marchantiales, these plants are thalloid forms. The group is characterized by a dorsiventral thallus, which is constructed on a consistent plan throughout the order. This structure consists of two primary regions:
*   **Upper Stratum:** A specialized region designed for transpiration and assimilation. It contains a series of air-chambers formed by superficial cells that grow upward from the surface.
*   **Lower Region:** A region composed of cells containing little chlorophyll. This area usually forms a midrib consisting of parenchymatous cells. Some of these cells may be differentiated as sclerenchyma fibres, mucilage cells, or contain oil-bodies.

Behind the apex, which contains several initial cells, the plant forms ventral scales known as amphigastria. These scales consist of a single layer of cells with terminal appendages that often fold over to protect the apex. In the genus *Riccia*, these scales are present in only a single median row, whereas other members of the group usually have two rows or occasionally accessory rows.

## Water Absorption and Regulation
The Marchantiales obtain their chief water supply from the soil via rhizoids, and they possess mechanisms to regulate the loss of water through transpiration. The thallus of these plants bears two types of rhizoids:
1.  **Smooth-walled rhizoids:** Wider structures that grow directly down into the soil.
2.  **Peg-rhizoids:** Longer, narrower rhizoids featuring peg-like thickenings of the wall that project into the cell-cavity. 

Peg-rhizoids are peculiar to this group. They converge toward the midrib under the shelter of the amphigastria, forming a wick-like strand. Water is conducted through this strand via cell cavities and capillarity. The upper stratum of the thallus is specifically constructed to regulate the release of the water absorbed through this system.

## Life History and Development
The Marchantiales represent a series of thalloid forms with structures specialized for survival in exposed situations. Within this series, *Riccia* is considered among the lowest members and possesses the simplest known sporogonia. 

In *Riccia*, the sporogonium consists of a wall made of a single layer of cells that encloses the spores. During development, the entire mass of cells derived from the ovum forms a spherical capsule. The only sterile tissue present in this structure is the single layer of peripheral cells that forms the wall. This contrasts with higher forms of Marchantiales, which may possess sterile cells, elaters, a seta, or a sterile foot.

## Environmental Context

Most liverworts are dorsiventral, with one side facing the substratum and the other exposed to light. In thalloid forms, a thicker midrib serves for conduction and storage, while a thinner marginal expansion or wing increases the surface area exposed to light. 

While *Ricciocarpus* and *Riccia natans* are adapted to floating on still water, other members of the Marchantiales exhibit various adaptations to their environments. For example, *Targionia* is found on exposed rocks, *Dumortiera* is a hygrophyte limited to shady and damp areas, and *Conocephalus* possesses a large thallus that covers stones by the waterside. Some xerophytic Marchantiales can survive drought by rolling up the thallus or forming subterranean tubers that persist after the rest of the plant has been killed.
