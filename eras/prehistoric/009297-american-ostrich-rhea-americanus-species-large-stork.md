# American Ostrich and the Jaburu Stork

The American ostrich (*Rhea americanus*) and a specific species of large stork known as the *bata* or *jaburu* (*Mycteria americana*) are birds that inhabit the tropical valleys and plains.

## The American Ostrich (Rhea americanus)

The American ostrich belongs to the order Rheae, a group of terrestrial, flightless birds characterized by a long ischiadic symphysis and the possession of three toes. As members of the broader division Ratitae, these birds lack a sternal keel and a pygostyle, and they possess a compound rhamphotheca. The Ratitae are a heterogeneous, polyphyletic group that was originally cosmopolitan and has existed with certainty since the Miocene.

Within the Ratitae, the genera *Struthio* (ostriches of Africa and South America) and *Rhea* comprise at most five species. While most existing Ratitae are found exclusively in Austrogaea, the ostriches of South America and Africa are exceptions to this regional singularity.

Anatomically, *Rhea* differs from most other Ratitae in the structure of its copulatory organ. In most Ratitae, the penis consists mainly of a right and left united half (*corpora fibrosa*) with a deep longitudinal furrow on the dorsal side, resembling the organ found in tortoises and crocodiles. However, in *Rhea* (as well as in *Anseriformes*), the organ is greatly specialized; it is partly reversible and spirally twisted. This is due to the development of an unpaired, median portion analogous to the mammalian *corpus spongiosum*, and the muscles of this organ are derived solely from the anal sphincter.

## The Jaburu Stork (Mycteria americana)

The *bata* or *jaburu* is a species of large stork found in the tropical plains and valleys. It is a bird of significant size, reaching a maximum height of 8 feet and a wing spread of 8 feet 6 inches.

In terms of anatomy, storks (along with tinamous) possess a penis that is much smaller and simpler than those found in the Ratitae or *Anseriformes*. In these birds, the organ has the appearance of a degenerated organ, contrasting with the majority of birds in which the organ has disappeared completely, necessitating the primitive method of everting the cloaca.
