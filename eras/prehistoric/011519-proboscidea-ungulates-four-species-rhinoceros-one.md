# Proboscidea

The order Proboscidea consists of mammals of which there are currently only two sole remaining representatives: the Indian elephant and the African elephant. These animals are distributed across various regions, including Asia and Africa.

## Distribution and Regional Characteristics

In the Indian region of Asia, the Indian elephant is a characteristic member of the fauna. In the province of Assam, wild elephants are found in abundance. They are known to enter villages in large herds, where they consume everything suitable to their tastes, often causing many depredations. In this region, elephants are caught using tamed female elephants, which are trained to decoy males into snares for the purpose of captivity. A significant number of these animals are tamed and exported from Assam annually.

In Africa, the African elephant is described as being fairly common throughout certain territories. In other regions, however, the elephant is becoming increasingly restricted to unfrequented districts.

## Human Interaction and Utility

Elephants are hunted in the forests of Assam for the ivory they furnish. The government *keddah* establishment from Dacca is responsible for capturing large numbers of elephants in the province. Additionally, the right to hunt elephants is sold via auction to private bidders, with the annual catch of these bidders averaging approximately two hundred animals.

## Evolutionary History and Extinct Species

The history of the Proboscidea and related forms is documented in the fossil record of the Cainozoic period. During the middle Cainozoic, as the world transitioned toward an ice age, the mammoth appeared. The mammoth is described as a big woolly cousin of the elephants, adapted to the cold climates of the Ice Age.

Earlier in the Cainozoic, the record shows a steady universal increase in brain capacity across mammalian tribes. While not members of the Proboscidea, certain "elephantine creatures" existed during the Eocene period in western America. These included forms such as *Loxophodon*, *Uintatherium*, and *Tinoceras*. These creatures, along with the *Dinoceratidae* (including *Dinoceras*, which possessed a massive skull with three pairs of horns or protuberances and huge downward-projecting canine teeth), died out before the close of the Eocene.

## Related Fossil Findings

Fossil evidence of the order has been discovered in various locations. In 1821, Dr. Buckland documented an assemblage of fossil teeth and bones of the elephant, along with the rhinoceros and hippopotamus, discovered in a cave at Kirkdale in Yorkshire.
