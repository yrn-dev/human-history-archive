# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition from the Oldowan to the Mousterian tool industry represents more than just a change in shape; it marks the cognitive evolution of early hominins. For millions of years, the ability to manipulate stone allowed ancestors of *Homo sapiens* to access high-protein food sources, migrate across diverse environments, and eventually dominate the Pleistocene landscape. This progression is characterized by a shift from opportunistic "choppers" to highly standardized, multi-purpose tools created through complex mental planning.

## Timeline of Tool Industries

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Hominin | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oldowan** | 2.6 – 1.7 Million Years Ago | *Homo habilis* | Simple flakes and choppers |
| **Acheulean** | 1.7 Mya – 200,000 Years Ago | *Homo erectus* | Symmetrical hand-axes |
| **Mousterian** | 160,000 – 40,000 Years Ago | *Homo neanderthalensis* | Levallois (prepared core) technique |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Technology
The Oldowan industry, discovered at Olduvai Gorge, is the earliest widespread stone tool tradition. These tools were "mode 1" technology—essentially river cobbles struck with another stone to create a sharp edge. While primitive, these tools allowed hominins to butcher carcasses and crack bones for marrow, providing the caloric surge necessary for brain growth.

### The Acheulean: Symmetry and Planning
The emergence of the Acheulean industry saw the introduction of the "biface" or hand-axe. Unlike Oldowan tools, which were opportunistic, Acheulean tools were shaped according to a mental template. The symmetry of these tools suggests a leap in cognitive ability and foresight. Historians and anthropologists continue to debate whether the extreme symmetry of some hand-axes served a purely functional purpose or represented an early form of aesthetic preference or social signaling.

### The Mousterian: The Prepared Core
The Mousterian industry is most closely associated with Neanderthals. The defining innovation here was the **Levallois technique**. Instead of simply chipping away at a rock, the toolmaker carefully prepared a stone core to strike off a single, predictable, and razor-sharp flake. This allowed for the creation of specialized scrapers, points, and knives, indicating a sophisticated understanding of geometry and material science.

## Did You Know?
* **The "Hand-Axe" Mystery:** Some Acheulean hand-axes are so perfectly symmetrical that some researchers suggest they were used as "Swiss Army knives" of the Paleolithic, serving as hammers, slicers, and digging tools.
* **Material Selectivity:** Early hominins didn't use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates (like flint, chert, or obsidian) because these materials fracture predictably (conchoidal fracture).
* **Global Reach:** While the Oldowan began in Africa, the Acheulean represents one of the first great migrations of technology, spreading into Europe and Asia as *Homo erectus* expanded.
