# Jurassic and Cretaceous Geological Formations and Fossils

The Jurassic and Cretaceous periods are represented by diverse geological strata and fossil remains across several continents, including Asia, Europe, North America, and South America. These formations range from marine deposits containing complex fauna to terrestrial beds consisting of freshwater deposits and volcanic activity.

## Regional Geological Occurrences

### Asia and Siberia
In the northern land mass of Siberia, specifically the area between the Lena and Yenisei, the Angara series includes deposits that are probably Jurassic. While much of this region shows no evidence of being beneath the sea since the early Palaeozoic era, marine Jurassic and Cretaceous fossils have been discovered in the extreme north. 

In Asia Minor, the geological structure is characterized by a complex system of folded beds. Cretaceous limestones and serpentine are prominent in the mountain system running parallel to the Black Sea coast from Sinope to Batum, and Cretaceous beds also form a long strip west of Sinope. Additionally, a band of Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous beds exists south of the Sea of Marmora.

### South America
In Bolivia, the Western Cordillera is primarily composed of Jurassic and Cretaceous beds, along with volcanic ashes and lavas. The intervening plateau contains red sandstones and gypsiferous marls that are likely, at least in part, Cretaceous. Marine fossils discovered by Gustav Steinmann in the middle of this series indicate an age no earlier than the Jurassic, with Steinmann specifically attributing them to the Lower Cretaceous.

### Europe and Greenland
In East Greenland, Jurassic rocks have been identified on Kuhn Island, where marls and sandstones contain genera such as *Aucella*, *Olcostephanus Payeri*, *O. striolaris*, *Belemnites Panderianus*, *B. volgensis*, and *B. absolutus*. Other fossils on the island suggest a relationship to the Middle Dogger of Yorkshire and the coal-bearing Jurassic rocks of Brora, Scotland. Danish expeditions have also identified Rhaetic-Lias plants at Vardekloft and Cape Stewart, as well as fossils corresponding to the Callovian, Cornbrash, and Inferior oolite of England, along with Portlandian and Upper Kimmeridge beds.

In England, the Cornbrash formation is noted as being very fossiliferous. In Yorkshire, it appears as a marly, rubbly, and frequently ironshot oolitic limestone, whereas in the south of England, it is not oolitic.

## Fossil Fauna

### Marine Reptiles and Fish
The Jurassic and Cretaceous periods saw the presence of various marine reptiles. Ichthyosaurians, such as *Ichthyosaurus communis* and *I. intermedius*, are common in the English Lower Lias. The largest known species, *I. trigonodon* from the Upper Lias of Bavaria, had a head measuring approximately two metres and a total length of at least ten metres. Later Jurassic forms from England and North America, including *Ophthalmosaurus* and *Baptanodon*, were nearly or entirely toothless with flexible broad paddles. Lower Cretaceous Ichthyosaur remains show a wide geographical distribution, found in South America, Australia, New Zealand, the East Indies, and Europe.

The fish-fauna of the Lower Cretaceous is similar to that of the Jurassic. However, the Upper Cretaceous formations, such as the Chalk, exhibit a more modern aspect. This period saw the first appearance of spiny-rayed Teleosteans, which are dominant in modern seas, alongside *Berycidae*, *Scopelidae*, *Halosauridae*, *Albulidae*, and *Elopidae*.

### Terrestrial Reptiles
Nearly complete skeletons of reptiles allied to *Iguanodon* have been found in the Cretaceous and Jurassic rocks of North America. In England, *Iguanodon* remains were discovered in the Lower Greensand of Kent and the Wealden formation of Sussex. Specimens from the Wealden beds at Bernissart, Belgium, include *Iguanodon mantelli* (5 to 6 metres in length) and *I. bernissartensis* (8 to 10 metres in length).

## Mineral and Stratigraphical Observations
Certain materials, such as Fuller's earth, are found across various geological horizons. In England, it occurs in Jurassic age deposits near Midford (Bath) and in Cretaceous formations at Nutfield (Surrey). In the United States, it is found in California in rocks ranging from the Cretaceous to the Pleistocene, while a yellow, gritty Jurassic earth is worked in Custer County, South Dakota.

In the Jura region, the Lower Cretaceous and Jurassic rocks have been the subject of detailed stratigraphical division and fossil description by the French geologist Charles Lory.
