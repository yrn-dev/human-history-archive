# Barton Beds

The Barton beds are a geological series of Upper Eocene age consisting of softish grey and brown clays interspersed with layers of sand. These deposits are found within the Hampshire Tertiary basin, where they are notably well exposed in the Isle of Wight and in the cliffs of Hordwell and Barton.

## Composition and Divisions
The Barton beds are generally divided into two primary components: the Barton clay and the Barton sands (also referred to as the Headon Hill sands). The Barton clay is described as being highly fossiliferous. Above this clay lies a sandy series containing few fossils. While the term "Upper Bagshot Beds" has been applied to these sands, the names Barton Sands or Headon Hill Sands are considered preferable.

In the Isle of Wight, the thickness of these formations is significant; the Barton clay ranges from 162 to 255 feet, while the Barton sands range from 140 to 200 feet.

## Regional Distribution and Basin Differences
The distribution of these beds varies significantly between the Hampshire and London basins:

*   **Hampshire Basin:** The Barton beds are well developed here. In Alum Bay, the Barton clay is sharply demarcated from the underlying Bracklesham beds by a bed of conglomerate composed of flint pebbles.
*   **London Basin:** The Barton beds are unknown in the London basin. The Upper Bagshot Sands found in the London area are likely situated at a lower horizon than the Barton Sands of the Hampshire basin.

## Classification and Equivalents
The classification of these beds sometimes overlaps with other formations. The Bracklesham beds are occasionally classed together with the overlying Barton clay under the designation of "Middle Bagshot." 

In a broader geological context, the term "Bartonien" was introduced by Mayer-Eymar in 1857 to describe continental equivalents of this series. In the Paris Basin, the Bartonien corresponds to the Sands of Beauchamp (*sables moyen*), the Sands of Mortefontaine, and the Limestone of Saint-Ouen. Other international equivalents include the Jacksonian in North America and the Sands of Lede in the Belgian Basin.

## Paleontology and Utility
The Barton clay is characterized by a variety of fossils, including:
*   **Mollusks:** *Fusus longaevus*, *Volutilithes luctatrix*, *Ostrea gigantea*, and *Pectunculus (Glycimeris) deleta*.
*   **Vertebrates:** A crocodile (*Diplocynodon*) and various fishes, such as *Lamna* and *Arius*.

Beyond their scientific value, the materials within the broader Bagshot series are utilized for industry. The sands are noted for being very pure and are employed in the manufacture of glass.
