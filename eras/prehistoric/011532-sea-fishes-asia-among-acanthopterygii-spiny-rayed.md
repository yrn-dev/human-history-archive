# Sea Fishes of Asia: The Acanthopterygii

The Acanthopterygii, known as the spiny-rayed fishes, represent a dominant group of Teleosteans in the modern seas. Within the geographical distribution of Asia, these fishes are widely represented across various marine environments, ranging from the Indian seas to the Pacific and the coasts of China.

## Classification and General Characteristics
The Acanthopterygii are categorized under the subclass Teleostei. According to the classification adopted by Albert Günther, the order Acanthopterygii is divided into numerous divisions, including Perciformes, Beryciformes, Kurtiformes, Polynemiformes, Sciaeniformes, Xiphiiformes, Trichiuriformes, Cotto-Scombriformes, Gobiiformes, Blenniformes, Mugiliformes, Gastrosteiformes, Centrisciformes, Gobiesociformes, Channiformes, Labyrinthibranchii, Lophotiformes, Taeniiformes, and Notacanthiformes. Additionally, there is a division known as Acanthopterygii Pharyngognathi.

Morphologically, these fishes are characterized by their fin structures. While some Teleostomes possess horny unjointed rays, the adult Teleost typically features dermal fin-rays that are originally arranged in pairs. These rays may be branched and jointed, or in some instances, they form unbranched rigid spines.

## Distribution of Asian Spiny-Rayed Fishes
The spiny-rayed fishes of Asia are diverse and occupy various ecological niches:

### Percidae and Allied Genera
The _Percidae_, or perches, are largely represented in Asia. The genus _Serranus_ is particularly numerous in Asia and features very large forms, contrasting with Europe where the genus has only one species. Other allied genera are abundant, extending their range from the Indian seas to eastern Africa.

### Squamipennes and Reef Fishes
The scaly-finned fishes, or Squamipennes, are primarily located in the seas of southern Asia, with a particular prevalence near coral reefs.

### Specialized Families and Species
Several other families of Acanthopterygii are distributed throughout the region:
*   **Mullidae:** The red mullets are well-represented by genera that differ from those found in Europe.
*   **Polynemidae:** These fishes range from the Atlantic through the Indian Ocean to the Pacific. They are used in the preparation of isinglass. A specific member, the mango fish, is considered a great delicacy and inhabits the seas from Siam to the Bay of Bengal.
*   **Sciaenidae:** This family extends from China to the Bay of Bengal, though they are not found further to the westward.
*   **Stromateidae:** Known as pomfrets, these fishes resemble the Mediterranean dory and extend into the Pacific and China.
*   **Other Groups:** The seas of south Asia also host the sword fishes (_Xiphidae_), lancet fishes (_Acanthuridae_), and scabbard fishes (_Trichuridae_). Additionally, mullets, blennies, gobies, and various genera of mackerels are abundant.

## Comparative Habitats and Regional Variations
While the marine environments of Asia are rich in Acanthopterygii, the fresh waters of southern Asia are deficient in typical forms of this group. Fresh-water habitats in the region are instead chiefly inhabited by walking and climbing fishes, siluroids, carp, and simple or spined eels.

In terms of broader marine distribution, the spiny-rayed Teleosteans are among the groups that comprise the majority of marine fishes. This distinguishes them from groups such as the _Crossopterygii_, _Dipneusti_, and _Opisthomi_, which are confined to fresh water.

## Evolutionary Context
The spiny-rayed Teleosteans first appeared during the Upper Cretaceous period. During this time, the fish-fauna shifted from the Jurassic-like forms of the Lower Cretaceous to a more modern aspect. The Teleosteans of the Upper Cretaceous were abundantly represented by families such as _Berycidae_, _Scopelidae_, _Halosauridae_, _Albulidae_, and _Elopidae_, many of which are close allies to the inhabitants of the deep sea today.
