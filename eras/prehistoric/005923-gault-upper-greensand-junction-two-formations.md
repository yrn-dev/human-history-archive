# Gault and Upper Greensand

The Gault and Upper Greensand are geological formations within the Cretaceous System. In the study of geology, the term "Greensand" has been applied to three distinct members of this system: the Upper Greensand, the Lower Greensand, and the Cambridge Greensand (the latter being a local phase of the base of the Chalk). The name was originally introduced by early English geologists to describe sandy rocks that often appeared green due to the presence of minute grains of the mineral glauconite.

## The Gault Formation
The Gault is described as an argillaceous type of the Upper Greensand. It consists of a stiff, greyish calcareous clay—originally referred to by William Smith as "the blue marl"—and is located beneath the white chalk. It is noted for the excellent preservation of its fossils.

In certain regions, the Gault manifests as a broad band of bluish clay. For example, in one county, it extends in a north-easterly direction from Towersey. In other areas, such as the vale of Aylesbury, the Gault lies alongside the Oxford Clay and Kimmeridge Clay. In the eastern part of another region, the Gault (along with the Upper Greensand) is referred to as the Selbornian and crops out from beneath the Chalk as a narrow, irregular band. In these districts, the Gault clay is only distinguishable in the northern and southern areas.

## The Upper Greensand
The Upper Greensand is a formation that rests upon the Gault. In some regions, it forms prominent hills, such as the hill upon which the town of Shaftesbury stands. It also appears as outliers in western areas, creating high ground at Pillesden and Lewesden Pens, Golden Cap, and above Lyme Regis.

## The Junction of the Formations
The junction where the Upper Greensand rests upon the Gault is geologically significant for its hydrological properties. At the meeting point of these two formations, numerous springs arise. This specific geological circumstance has influenced human settlement, as it has determined the site of several villages.

## Economic and Agricultural Use
Materials from the Greensand and Gault formations have been utilized for various industrial and agricultural purposes:
*   **Mining:** Phosphatic nodules have been extracted from the Gault at Towersey.
*   **Industry:** Lime is obtained from both the Greensand and Chalk formations.
*   **Agriculture:** In Kent, the "Mid Kents" variety of hops is derived principally from Greensand soils. Additionally, "Bastard East Kents" hops are produced on soils formed by admixtures of various materials, including clay from the Gault and Greensand formations.

## Distinction from Lower Greensand
Historically, there was significant confusion between the Upper Greensand (Selbornian) and the Lower Greensand until the fossils of these rocks were carefully studied. The Lower Greensand is a separate formation that is usually clearly marked off from the overlying Gault. Unlike the Upper Greensand, the Lower Greensand is typically developed in the Isle of Wight, the Wealden district, and Dorsetshire near Swanage, and is often used for the manufacture of glass.
