# Amphioxus

Amphioxus is a biological organism classified within the subclass Leptocardii, a distinction established through the anatomical researches of Johannes Müller. It serves as a significant point of morphological comparison for other chordates and vertebrates, particularly in the study of respiratory, skeletal, and nervous systems.

## Respiratory System and Gill-Slits
Amphioxus possesses numerous gill-slits, ranging from forty to more than a hundred pairs. Throughout its life, the organism adds new gill-slits via fresh perforation at the posterior end of the pharynx. The borders of these clefts are constituted by branchial bars.

In Amphioxus, these bars are categorized into two types: primary bars and tongue-bars. The primary bars are the essential organs of the gill-slits, exceeding the tongue-bars in bulk, size, vascularity, and development. Furthermore, the tongue-bar in Amphioxus is solid. This organization stands in contrast to that of *Balanoglossus*, where the tongue-bar is the essential organ, exceeds the septal bars in bulk, and contains a large coelomic space. It is suggested that this difference represents an evolutionary gradation where the tongue-bar, originally the primary organ of the cleft, underwent reduction and modification to become a secondary bar in Amphioxus.

## Skeletal and Nervous Systems
The skeletal structure of Amphioxus features a notochord, which is a cylindrical rod composed of highly vacuolated cells. This rod is situated dorsal to the gut and ventral to the central nervous system. While the notochord typically extends from the brain's infundibulum to the tip of the tail in most chordates, in Amphioxus, the condition is different; the notochord's extent may be a secondary result of the degenerative shortening of the central nervous system.

Regarding the nervous system, a sagittal section of Amphioxus strongly suggests the presence of vestiges of a thalamencephalon.

## Alimentary and Vascular Systems
The alimentary canal of Amphioxus includes a liver, which is considered the most ancient of the glandular diverticula of the secretory surface. Originally, the liver functioned mainly as a digestive gland.

While the vascular system of *Balanoglossus* is described as consisting of lacunae and channels lacking endothelium, the archive notes that the vascular systems of *Balanoglossus* and Amphioxus do not readily lend themselves to morphological comparison due to the wide differences between the two animals.
