# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition from the Oldowan to the Mousterian tool industry represents more than just a change in shape; it marks a profound cognitive leap in prehistoric hominins. For millions of years, the ability to manipulate stone allowed early humans to access high-calorie food sources, such as bone marrow, which fueled brain growth. This evolution reflects a shift from opportunistic "smashing" to intentional "shaping," signaling the development of foresight, planning, and complex motor skills.

## Timeline of Tool Evolution

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Hominin | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oldowan** | 2.6 – 1.7 Million Years Ago | *Homo habilis* | Simple flakes and choppers |
| **Acheulean** | 1.7 Million – 200,000 Years Ago | *Homo erectus* | Symmetrical hand-axes |
| **Mousterian** | 160,000 – 40,000 Years Ago | *Homo neanderthalensis* | Levallois (prepared core) technique |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Technology
The Oldowan industry, named after Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania, is characterized by "mode 1" technology. These tools were rudimentary, created by striking a hammerstone against a core to produce sharp-edged flakes. While they look like simple pebbles to the untrained eye, they allowed *Homo habilis* to butcher carcasses and scrape hides, fundamentally changing their dietary niche.

### The Acheulean: The First Standard
Around 1.7 million years ago, *Homo erectus* introduced the Acheulean "mode 2" industry. The hallmark of this era is the biface—a tear-drop shaped hand-axe flaked on both sides. Unlike Oldowan tools, Acheulean axes required a "mental template"; the maker had to envision the finished tool within the raw stone before starting. Historians often debate whether the symmetry of these axes was purely functional or represented an early aesthetic sense.

### The Mousterian: Precision and Specialization
The Mousterian industry is associated primarily with Neanderthals and early *Homo sapiens*. This "mode 3" technology utilized the **Levallois technique**, a sophisticated method of preparing a stone core so that a single, precise strike would detach a flake of a predetermined size and shape. This allowed for the creation of specialized tools, such as scrapers and spear points, which could be hafted (attached) to wooden shafts.

## Did You Know?
* **The "Hand-Axe" Mystery:** Some archaeologists argue that the extreme symmetry of late Acheulean hand-axes suggests they may have been used as social signals or "sexual displays" rather than just tools.
* **Material Selectivity:** Early hominins didn't use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates (like chert, flint, and obsidian) because they fracture predictably.
* **Global Spread:** While the Oldowan began in Africa, the Acheulean represents the first time tool technology spread consistently across Africa, Europe, and Asia.
