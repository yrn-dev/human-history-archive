# Carboniferous Formations

The Carboniferous period is represented by various geological formations across several regions, characterized primarily by limestone, shales, sandstones, and coal measures. These formations appear in diverse geographical contexts, ranging from the British Isles to British East Africa.

## British East Africa
In British East Africa, the presence of Carboniferous strata is indicated by specific findings in the sedimentary regions. Shales found on the Sabaki river are categorized as "Carboniferous?" based on evidence obtained by Dr. Gregory, who discovered fish-scales and specimens of *Palaeanodonta Fischeri* within these deposits. In the broader geological classification of the region, these shales of the Sabaki river are listed as the ninth formation among the sedimentary rocks.

## Ireland
Carboniferous formations are extensive in several Irish counties, often appearing in complex structural arrangements.

### County Kerry
In Kerry, Carboniferous shales and sandstones are found in a considerable area on the north and east. These formations reach the coal-measures, which contain unproductive coals on the Glanruddery Mountains and east of Listowel. A fringe of Carboniferous Limestone borders these beds, though it is cut off by the sea at Castlemaine, Tralee, and Knockaneen Bay. The landscape is characterized by synclinal hollows of Carboniferous Limestone that have become marine inlets between promontories of Old Red Sandstone. Additionally, Lough Leane rests on Carboniferous Limestone.

### County Cork
The geology of Cork is influenced by "Hercynian" folds. Carboniferous Limestone appears in several areas, including a synclinal running from Lismore through Millstreet. In the floor of a larger synclinal where Cork city is located, the limestone has become brecciated, veined, and crystalline; a fine red staining at Little Island gives this limestone value as a marble. Above the limestone, beds as high as the Coal Measures and Millstone Grit remain from the Limerick and Kerry borders to Mallow and Kanturk. Carboniferous Slate occupies much of the country to the south, with occasional appearances of the basal Coomhola Grits. Some synclinal patches of Carboniferous Limestone and Shale are also found on the undulating upland of the center of the county.

## Wales and England
In Wales, Carboniferous strata are prominent in the northern counties.

### Denbighshire
In this region, Carboniferous Limestone extends from its southernmost point at Llanymynach to the Cyrnybrain fault. The formation forks into two divisions terminating at Talargoch and the Great Orme's Head, separated by the denuded shales of the Moel Famma range. In the Vale of Clwyd, the limestone is overlaid by New Red Sandstone. In the eastern division, the limestone is overlaid by the Coal Measures—which reach a breadth of 4½ m. near Wrexham—and the Millstone Grit of Minera and Ruabon. A broad strip of red marly beds, now regarded as belonging to the Coal Measures, succeeds these.

## Other British Regions
Carboniferous formations are also noted in various other parts of the British Isles.

### Ireland (Other Counties)
In the county of Kildare, the greater part of the region is formed of typical grey Carboniferous limestone, which is particularly visible in the flat land around Clane. Similar strata form the natural steps at the Salmon Falls at Leixlip. In the county of Louth, Carboniferous Limestone overlies Silurian shales and fine-grained sandstones in the areas north and east of Dundalk.

### Scotland
In the region of Dailly, north of the river Girvan, Llandovery strata appear as an inlier that is surrounded by Carboniferous formations and Old Red Sandstone.
