# Belemnites and Associated Jurassic Fossils

Belemnites are fossilized cephalopods found in various geological formations, often appearing alongside ammonites. In certain contexts, the large belemnites, specifically *Megateuthis giganteus*, are noted as being worthy of notice.

## Distribution and Characteristics in English Formations

In the English Corallian rocks, belemnites are described as abundant. These rocks also feature a significant presence of corals, which frequently form large calcareous masses known as "doggers," with prominent genera including *Isastrea*, *Thecosmilia*, and *Thamnastrea*. Other common fossils in these beds include:

*   **Gasteropods:** Very common forms such as *Bourgetia*, *Chemnitzia*, and *Nerinea*.
*   **Trigonias:** Very numerous in specific beds, particularly *T. mariani* and *T. perlata*.
*   **Pelecypods:** Abundant specimens of *Lucina aliena* and *Astarte ovata*.
*   **Echinoderms:** *Cidaris florigemma* and *Echinobrissus scutatus* are characteristic of these beds.

In other Jurassic contexts, belemnites are found alongside *T. costata* (a form of *Trigonia*), *Cidaris*, *Pleurotomaria*, corals, crinoids, and *T. perovalis* (terebratulas).

## The Kimeridgian Formation

The Kimeridgian in England is divided into an Upper and Lower Series. The Lower Series consists of dark shales, clays, cement-stones, and calcareous "doggers," reaching a maximum thickness of 400 ft. The Upper Series, ranging from 600-650 ft. in the south, consists of clays, paper shales, and dark bituminous shales.

Characteristic fossils of this formation include *Belemnites blainvillei* and *Belemnites abreviatus*. Other characteristic fossils found here are *Exogyra nana*, *Rhynchonella inconstans*, *Lingula ovalis*, *Thracia depressa*, and *Protocardia striatula*. 

The Kimeridgian is also noted for its abundance of large dinosaurian reptiles, including *Megalosaurus*, *Gigantosaurus*, and *Cetiosaurus*, as well as ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs, and remains of chelonian and crocodilian reptiles. Ammonites peculiar to this formation include *Aspidoceras longispinus*, *Reineckia thurmanni*, and *Reineckia eudoxus*.

## Global Occurrences

Beyond England, Jurassic shales and limestones are observed along the railway near Changamwe. These deposits contain plentiful belemnites and gigantic ammonites. According to Dr. Waagen, the ammonites in this region show a striking analogy to forms found in the Acanthicus zone of East India.

## Economic and Geological Context

Fossils such as ammonites are sometimes found in limestones used for decorative purposes. For example, "Ammonite marble" is a dark brown limestone from the Lower Lias of Somersetshire that is crowded with *A. planicostata*.

In the Kimeridgian formation, the clay has been used to produce flower-pots, tiles, and bricks in locations such as Gillingham, Brill, Ely, Horncastle, and Swindon. Additionally, "Kimeridge coal"—a highly bituminous shale—has been worked as fuel on the cliff at Little Kimeridge, and alum has been obtained from the Kimeridge Clay.
