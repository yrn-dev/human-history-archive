# Echoes in Stone: The Megalithic Cultures of Prehistory

## Overview
Megalithic architecture—derived from the Greek *megas* (great) and *lithos* (stone)—represents one of the most ambitious engineering feats of the prehistoric world. Across Western Europe, from the Atlantic coast of Portugal to the plains of Britain, Neolithic and Bronze Age societies erected massive stone structures that served as burial chambers, ritual centers, and astronomical observatories.

While Stonehenge is the most iconic of these monuments, it belongs to a broader tradition of megalithism. These cultures transitioned from nomadic hunter-gatherer lifestyles to settled agricultural communities, a shift that allowed for the social organization and surplus labor required to move stones weighing dozens of tons. Historians and archaeologists debate whether these monuments were built by a single migrating culture or if similar ideas evolved independently through "cultural diffusion" across the continent.

## Key Facts

### The Engineering of Stonehenge
Stonehenge was not built in a single event but evolved over 1,500 years. It began as a simple earthwork enclosure around 3000 BCE. The famous sarsen stones were brought from the Marlborough Downs (about 20 miles away), while the smaller bluestones were transported from the Preseli Hills in Wales—a distance of roughly 140 miles. The method of transport remains a subject of intense scholarly debate, with theories ranging from water rafts to overland sledges.

### Diverse Forms of Megaliths
Beyond circles, megalithic cultures produced various structures:
*   **Menhirs:** Single standing stones, common in Brittany, France.
*   **Dolmens:** Portal tombs consisting of a large flat capstone supported by uprights.
*   **Cromlechs:** Circular arrangements of stones, of which Stonehenge is the most complex.

### Purpose and Function
Most megaliths are associated with the dead or the divine. Passage graves, such as Newgrange in Ireland, demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of the solar calendar; during the winter solstice, light penetrates the inner chamber perfectly. This suggests that megalithic cultures viewed the alignment of the earth and sky as central to their spiritual and agricultural survival.

## Did You Know?
*   **The "Bluestone" Mystery:** Geochemical analysis has confirmed that the bluestones at Stonehenge originate from Wales, suggesting a profound cultural or ancestral connection to that specific landscape.
*   **Newgrange's Age:** The passage tomb at Newgrange in Ireland is actually older than Stonehenge, dating back to approximately 3200 BCE.
*   **Global Phenomenon:** While most famous in Europe, megalithic structures appear globally, including the Gosebek culture in Anatolia and the monuments of Malta.
