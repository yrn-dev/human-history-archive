# Coprolites or Fossil Faeces in the Lias of Lyme Regis

The study of coprolites, or fossil faeces, is associated with the geological research of Dr. Buckland. In his original contributions to the sciences of Palaeontology and Geology, Buckland published a work titled "On the Discovery of Coprolites or Fossil Faeces in the Lias of Lyme Regis, and in other Formations."

## Geological Context of Lyme Regis
Lyme Regis is located in Dorsetshire, a county in the south-western part of England. The region is characterized by a diverse range of geological sections. The south-western portion of the county is primarily occupied by Middle and Lower Lias beds, which consist of limestones in the lower sections and marls and clays in the upper portions. 

The Lias of Lyme Regis is noted for being highly fossiliferous. In addition to coprolites, this formation has yielded other significant paleontological finds, including a new species of Pterodactyle and various large reptiles. Most notably, the Lower Lias of Lyme Regis has provided specimens of *Ichthyosaurus*, such as *Ichthyosaurus communis*.

## Associated Fossil Fauna
The Lias formations are well known for their marine reptiles. *Ichthyosaurus communis* and *I. intermedius* are the most common species found in the English Lower Lias, typically reaching lengths of three to four metres. Other species from the English Lower Lias, such as *I. platyodon*, appear to have been nearly as large as *I. trigonodon* (found in the Upper Lias of Banz, Bavaria), which could reach a total length of ten metres.

Beyond the Lias, other fossil-bearing rocks in Dorsetshire include:
* **The Wealden beds of the Isle of Purbeck:** Where remains of the *Iguanodon* have been found.
* **Other formations:** The Fuller's Earth, Forest Marble, and Inferior Oolite are also identified as fossil-bearing rocks.

## Geography and Administration of Lyme Regis
Lyme Regis is a municipal borough in Dorsetshire with a population of 2,095 as of 1901. Historically, it served as a port and was granted a charter by Edward I in 1284, making it a free borough with a merchant gild. The town's maritime importance was enhanced by the construction of the Cobb, a structure of rock and timber first mentioned in 1328, which provided the only shelter between the river Exe and Portland Roads.

The town's economy historically included a famous lace industry in the 18th century and foreign trade in cloth and wine with France starting as early as 1284. Politically, Lyme Regis returned two members to parliament from 1295 until 1832, when it was reduced to one, before eventually being disfranchised in 1867.

In terms of modern communications, Lyme Regis serves as the terminus for a light railway originating from Axminster on the South Western line.
