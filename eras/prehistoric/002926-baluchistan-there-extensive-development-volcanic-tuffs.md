# Geology and Geography of Baluchistan

Baluchistan is a region characterized by a complex geological structure and a diverse landscape, ranging from folded mountain ranges to coastal deposits and stony deserts. Its geography has been the subject of extensive scientific mapping and systematic survey since 1880, resulting in detailed professional maps extending from the Indus to the Euphrates and from the Oxus to the Indus.

## Regional Geography and Administration
The territory of Baluchistan is divided into different administrative zones. British Baluchistan is an irregularly shaped block of upland territory leased from Kalat. This province is under the ordinary forms of civil administration in British India and includes the Toba plateau, the basins of the Nari and Bolan as far as Sibi to the south-east, and the upper Lora tributaries. Its boundaries reach south through Shorarud to Nushki and extend east to embrace the Thal Chotiali valley.

Beyond the borders of British Baluchistan lie areas of independent Baluchistan, which remain under British political control. The degree of independence in these districts generally varies based on their distance from Quetta. To the west, the boundary between Baluchistan and Afghanistan begins at Nushki and cuts across the Lora hamun, passing the frontier post of Chagai. This border follows the central mountainous water-divide and straight lines through the desert south of the Gaud-i-Zirreh salt swamps. To the north, the boundary between Baluchistan and Afghanistan is demarcated to the point where it touches the Gomal river.

## Mountain Ranges and Stratigraphy
The mountain ranges of Baluchistan are primarily composed of Tertiary and Cretaceous beds. These beds are thrown into a series of folds that run approximately parallel to the mountain ridges. This system is arranged like a festoon hanging southwards between Mount Ararat and Peshawar, though the outer folds loop up at Sibi to create a subsidiary festoon consisting of the Bugti Hills and the Suliman.

In addition to Cretaceous and Tertiary formations, Jurassic rocks contribute significantly to the formation of the hills in British Baluchistan. Along the south side of the upper Zhob, Triassic beds and *Fusulina* limestone have been identified. While most deposits—excluding the later Tertiary beds—are marine in nature, the beds in the broader depressions between mountain ridges are reported to be only slightly disturbed.

## Volcanic Activity and Formations
Baluchistan is situated within a central folded belt that has been the site of great volcanic eruptions since the Cretaceous period. In north-western Baluchistan, there is an extensive development of conglomerates and volcanic tuffs within the lower Tertiary and upper Cretaceous periods; these are likely contemporaneous with the Deccan Traps of India.

The region's volcanic history includes several distinct features:
*   **Intrusions:** During the Tertiary period, great masses of diorite and syenite were intruded.
*   **Volcanic Cones:** A line of recent volcanic cones extends from eastern Persia into western Baluchistan. These cones are composed mostly of ashes and lavas of an andesitic nature. While these volcanoes appear to be extinct within Baluchistan, the Koh-i-Tafdan beyond the Persian frontier continues to emit vapours frequently.
*   **Mud Volcanoes:** These occur along the Makran coast, though it is considered doubtful if they are connected to true volcanic agencies.

## Coastal and Desert Landscapes
The geological layout of the region extends beyond the folded mountain belts. Outside of these folds are the horizontal deposits of the Makran coast. Conversely, within the folds lies the stony desert of north-western Baluchistan.
