# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition from the Oldowan to the Mousterian tool industry represents one of the most significant cognitive leaps in human evolution. For early hominins, the ability to manipulate stone was not merely a matter of convenience; it was an evolutionary catalyst that allowed for dietary expansion, migration into harsher climates, and the development of complex social structures. This progression is marked by a shift from opportunistic "choppers" to highly standardized, multi-purpose tools created through sophisticated reduction strategies.

## Timeline of Lithic Industries

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Hominin | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oldowan** | 2.6 – 1.7 Million Years Ago | *Homo habilis* | Simple flakes and choppers |
| **Acheulean** | 1.7 Million – 200,000 Years Ago | *Homo erectus* | Symmetrical hand-axes |
| **Mousterian** | 160,000 – 40,000 Years Ago | *Homo neanderthalensis* | Levallois (prepared core) technique |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The Dawn of Tool Use
The Oldowan industry, named after Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania, is characterized by "mode 1" technology. These tools were produced by striking a hammerstone against a core to produce sharp-edged flakes. While they appear primitive, these flakes allowed hominins to butcher meat and crack bones for marrow, providing the high-calorie fats necessary for brain growth.

### The Acheulean: The First Standard
The Acheulean represents a shift to "mode 2." Unlike the Oldowan, where the tool's shape was accidental, the Acheulean hand-axe was intentionally shaped into a teardrop or oval. This required "mental templating"—the ability to envision the finished tool within the raw stone. Historians and archaeologists debate whether the extreme symmetry of some hand-axes served a functional purpose or was a form of social signaling or aesthetic preference.

### The Mousterian: Precision and Specialization
Associated primarily with Neanderthals, the Mousterian industry utilized the **Levallois technique**. Instead of simply chipping away at a stone, the knapper carefully prepared a core to strike off a single, predictable flake of a specific size and shape. This allowed for the creation of specialized scrapers, points, and knives. This era marks the transition toward "mode 3" technology, where the focus shifted from the core tool to the flake tool.

## Did You Know?
* **The "Hand-axe" Mystery:** Some Acheulean hand-axes are so perfectly symmetrical that some researchers suggest they were used as "Swiss Army knives" of the Pleistocene, serving as hammers, scrapers, and butchery tools.
* **Material Selectivity:** Early hominins did not use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates (like chert, flint, or obsidian) because these materials break with a predictable "conchoidal" fracture.
* **Cognitive Mapping:** The shift to the Levallois technique indicates a higher level of abstract thought and foresight, as the toolmaker had to perform several "wasteful" steps to prepare the core before the final tool could be produced.
