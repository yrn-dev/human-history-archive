# Crocodiles and Related Findings

## Paleontology and Fossil Records
In the Barton Clay, several characteristic fossils have been identified. These include the mollusks *Fusus longaevus*, *Volutilithes luctatrix*, *Ostrea gigantea*, and *Pectunculus (Glycimeris) deleta*. Additionally, the Barton Clay contains fossils of fishes, specifically *Lamna* and *Arius*, as well as a crocodile identified as *Diplocynodon*. The sands associated with this area are noted for being very pure and are utilized in the production of glass.

## Biological Characteristics and Behavior
The crocodile is described as an amphibious creature of surprising size and strength, capable of living on both land and water. It possesses a scaly hide on its back that cannot be pierced, strong claws, and teeth that are large and tusky. Its eyes are compared to those of a pig. A distinct physiological trait is that the crocodile does not move its lower jaw; instead, it brings the upper jaw down toward the lower.

The crocodile's life cycle begins with eggs no larger than those of geese, which are produced and hatched on land. Despite this small beginning, the animal can grow to seventeen cubits in length or more. Its habits include spending the majority of the day on dry land and the entire night in the river, as the water is warmer than the dew and open air. During the four most wintry months, the creature does not eat.

In the water, the crocodile is blind, though it possesses very keen sight in the air. Because it lives in the water, its mouth often contains leeches. A symbiotic relationship exists with the trochilus; when the crocodile opens its mouth (typically toward the West Wind), the trochilus enters to swallow the leeches, a service for which the crocodile does not harm the bird.

## Regional Distributions
Crocodiles are found in various global regions:
*   **Africa:** In certain territories, the lakes and rivers are infested with crocodiles, which apparently belong to a single species known as the common Nile crocodile.
*   **Asia:** The river Indus is described as the second river in the world producing crocodiles.

## Cultural and Religious Significance in Egypt
In ancient Egypt, attitudes toward crocodiles varied by region. Those dwelling near Thebes and the lake of Moiris regarded them as most sacred. These populations would select one crocodile to be trained to tameness, decorating its ears with gold and molten stone ornaments and placing anklets on its front feet. These animals were provided with appointed food and sacrifices, and upon death, they were embalmed and buried in sacred tombs. Some believed the crocodile was worshipped because it defended Egypt from wild Arab incursions.

Conversely, those living near the city of Elephantine did not view them as sacred and would even eat them. In this region, they were called "champsai," while the name "crocodile" was provided by the Ionians, who compared the animals to lizards found in the stone walls of their own country.

Other Egyptian religious and cultural intersections include:
*   **The Labyrinth:** Located at the southern extremity of the lake of Moiris near the town of Crocodiles (also known as Arsinoë), this massive structure contained subterraneous buildings used as burying-places for kings and for keeping sacred crocodiles.
*   **Deities:** Horus of Edfu is identified as the enemy of the crocodiles and hippopotami of Set. While the crocodile was worshipped at Ombos south of Edfu, a different Ombos opposite Coptos venerated the hippopotamus sacred to Set.
*   **The Ichneumon:** This animal was adored for preventing the overpopulation of crocodiles. The ichneumon would break crocodile eggs while the crocodile was absent or leap into the open mouth of a sleeping crocodile to gnaw its entrails and pierce its belly.
