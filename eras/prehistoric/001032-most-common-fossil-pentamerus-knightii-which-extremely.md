# Pentamerus Knightii and Associated Silurian Fossils

## Occurrence in Aymestry Limestone
*Pentamerus Knightii* is identified as the most common fossil found within the Aymestry Limestone. In certain locations, this fossil is described as being extremely abundant. The Aymestry Limestone is an inconstant limestone that occurs locally in the Ludlow series of Silurian rocks, situated between the Lower and Upper Ludlow shales. 

The limestone derives its name from Aymestry in Herefordshire, where it is visible on both sides of the river Lugg. It is also well developed in the vicinity of Ludlow—where it is occasionally referred to as the Ludlow limestone—and occupies similar positions in the Ludlow shales at the Malvern Hills, May Hill, the Abberley Hills, and Woolhope.

## Lithological Characteristics
The lithological character of the Aymestry Limestone varies significantly depending on the location. It may appear as a dark grey, somewhat crystalline limestone, or it may transition into a shaly, earthy, or flaggy condition. In some instances, it exists merely as a layer of nodules. When the limestone is well developed, it can reach a thickness of 50 ft., consisting of beds ranging from 1 to 5 ft. Due to its superior hardness compared to the soft shales located above and below it, the limestone often forms a conspicuous feature in the landscape.

## Associated Fossil Fauna
In addition to the abundance of *Pentamerus Knightii*, other fossils are present within the Aymestry Limestone. These include trilobites, corals, and other brachiopods. These specimens are noted to be similar to those found in the Wenlock limestone.

## Related Silurian Formations
The Upper Ludlow rocks, which are primarily soft shales and mudstones with some harder sandy beds, contain a variety of other common fossils. These include:
* **Plants:** *Chondrites* and *Actinophyllum*.
* **Brachiopods:** *Chonetes striatella* and *Lingula minima*.
* **Cephalopods:** *Orthoceras bullatum*.
* **Other Invertebrates:** Gasteropods, pelecypods, phyllocarids, ostracods, and eurypterids. Trilobites are also present, though they are less common than in older groups.
* **Fish:** *Cyathaspis*, *Cephalaspis*, and *Auchenaspis*.

Within these rocks, a brown layer known as the "Ludlow Bone bed" exists near Ludlow. This layer ranges from a quarter of an inch to 4 inches in thickness and contains mollusca and fragmentary fish remains associated with *Pterygotus*. Additionally, one of the uppermost sandy layers is termed the "Fucoid bed" due to its abundance of seaweed-like impressions.

Further passage beds between the Silurian and Old Red Sandstone—occasionally grouped as "Downtonian"—include the Ledbury shales, Downton Castle Sandstone, and the Tilestones. Fossils found in these grey, yellow, or red micaceous shaly sandstones include *Platyschisma helicites*, *Lingula cornea*, and numerous ostracods and phyllocarids.
