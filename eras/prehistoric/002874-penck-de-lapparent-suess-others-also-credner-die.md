# A. Penck, A. de Lapparent, E. Suess and Associated Geologists

The works of A. Penck, A. de Lapparent, and E. Suess represent significant contributions to the fields of geology, cartography, and the study of the Earth's historical landmasses. Their research spans various eras of geological time, from the Palaeozoic to the Cainozoic, and covers diverse geographical regions including Asia, Europe, and Scandinavia.

## E. Suess and Global Geological History
E. Suess has contributed extensively to the understanding of the early geological history of Asia and the global distribution of landmasses. Suess identified and named "Angaraland," a northern land mass that remained land since the Palaeozoic era. In conjunction with Neumayr and others, Suess identified a southern land mass called "Gondwanaland," of which the Indian peninsula is a fragment. According to these findings, the Indian peninsula has not been beneath the sea since at least the Carboniferous period.

Suess also defined the intervening sea between these two ancient land masses as the "Tethys," while Neumayr referred to this area as the central Mediterranean sea. Additionally, Suess's work, specifically *Das Antlitz der Erde* (vol. iii), serves as a reference for the geology of Asia Minor. In the Nan-shan mountains, one of several parallel ranges—including the Mushketov, Semenov, and Alexander III.—bears the name Suess, though the mutual relations of these ranges are not yet definitely settled.

## A. de Lapparent and Stratigraphical Classification
A. de Lapparent is a noted author of the *Traite de geologie*, a work that provides general accounts of geological periods and references to original papers. His work is particularly focused on the classification of stages within the Jurassic and Cainozoic eras.

In the study of the Jurassic period, de Lapparent provided a classification for rocks equivalent to the English Corallian, representing them by the *Sequanien* stage. This stage is further divided into an upper *Astartien* and a lower *Rauracien*. However, de Lapparent places the lower part of the Corallian stage within his *Oxfordien* stage. Regarding the "Kimeridgien" of continental geologists, while it traditionally contained three sub-divisions (Virgulian, Pteroceran, and Astartian), de Lapparent now recognizes only the Pteroceran and Virgulian.

In the context of the Cainozoic era (also termed Cenozoic), de Lapparent has used the term *Néozoique* to restrict the application of the "Neozoic" label specifically to the Tertiary epoch.

## A. Penck and Global Cartography
Professor A. Penck is recognized for his contributions to global mapping. In 1890, Penck proposed the creation of a world map that included the oceans, designed on a scale of 1:1,000,000. This ambitious scheme received the promised support of a committee of representatives from the world's leading powers, who met in London in 1909.
