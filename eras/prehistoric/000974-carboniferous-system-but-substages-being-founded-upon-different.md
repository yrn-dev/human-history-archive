# Carboniferous System

The Carboniferous system is a geological period characterized by the presence of limestone, coal-bearing strata, and specific fossil zones. Its deposits are found globally, including significant occurrences in Great Britain, Ireland, Belgium, Asia Minor, Bulgaria, and Korea.

## Geological Classifications and Stages
In geology, the term "Avonian" was proposed by Dr. A. Vaughan in 1905 to describe the Lower Carboniferous rocks found in the Avon gorge at Bristol. The Avonian stage encompasses the same rocks and fossil zones as the earlier "Dinantien" designation. However, the substages of the Avonian differ from those identified by Belgian and French geologists due to different interpretations of zonal fossils and local conditions.

The Avonian is divided into substages:
*   **Kidwellian (Upper Avonian):** This stage is well developed near Kidwelly in Carmarthenshire and is associated with the *Dibunophyllum* zone.
*   **Clevedonian (Lower Avonian):** This stage is well displayed near Clevedon in Somerset and is associated with the *Cleistopora* zone.

Other associated zones mentioned in relation to these systems include *Seminula* (Viseen), *Syringothyris*, and *Zaphrentis* (Tournaisien).

## Regional Occurrences

### Great Britain and Ireland
In Great Britain, Carboniferous limestone occupies areas in the south and east of certain counties, while the Lower Carboniferous cement stone series of the Calciferous Sandstone group may rest conformably upon Old Red Sandstone. In some regions, intrusive basalt sheets are intercalated between Carboniferous strata, forming hills such as Benarty, Cleish Hills, Bishop Hill, and West Lomond. The Hurlet limestone is specifically worked on the Bishop and Lomond Hills.

In Ireland, certain counties lie within a great Carboniferous Limestone plain. In these areas, basic volcanic rocks contemporaneous with the limestone may be found, such as those forming Croghan Hill.

### Continental Europe and Asia
In Belgium, Carboniferous beds occur in the north of the Palaeozoic area. A significant band along the northern border of the Ardennes contains the coalfields of Mons, Charleroi, and Liege. These Coal Measures are located in a narrow trough separated from older Ardennes rocks by a reversed fault known as the *faille du midi*.

In Bulgaria, the Carboniferous system appears in the western Balkans with a continental *facies* (Kulm), where sandstone and argillite beds contain anthracitiferous coal.

In Asia Minor, Carboniferous fossils have been discovered in the Anti-Taurus mountains and at Balia Maden in Mysia. Additionally, Carboniferous rocks occur at Eregli (Heraclea Pontica), where they have been worked for coal.

In Korea, Carboniferous beds are found in the south-eastern provinces. These consist primarily of conglomerates, sandstones, and slates, containing a few seams of coal.

## Carboniferous Marbles and Limestones
The Carboniferous or "Mountain Limestone" is the primary source of British marbles. These stones are worked in North Wales, the Isle of Man, various parts of Ireland, and the English counties of Yorkshire and Derbyshire.

### Varieties of Marble
*   **Encrinital Marble (Entrochal Marble):** This material is characterized by the presence of numerous stone-lilies (encrinites). The fossils create a pattern of white markings on a grey limestone ground. The joints of the arms and stems of these fossils are referred to as "wheel-stones."
*   **Black Marbles:** These are widely used for ornamental objects, such as vases and chimney-pieces. Their color is derived from bituminous matter of organic origin, which is discharged during calcination. These marbles often emit a fetid odor when struck. Notable black marbles are quarried in Ireland (near Galway and Kilkenny) and in Yorkshire (Frosterley), where the pattern is caused by large corals. The finest black marble is obtained from near Ashford in Derbyshire.
*   **Rosewood Marble:** Found in limited quantities near Ashford, this is a dense brown laminated limestone that resembles the grain of rosewood when polished and is used mainly for inlaid work.
*   **Bird's-eye Marble:** A Derbyshire rock crowded with fragments of minute encrinites.
*   **Petit Granit:** A Belgian black marble containing small sections of crinoid stems.
