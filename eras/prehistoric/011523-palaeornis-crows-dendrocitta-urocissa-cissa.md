# Palaeornis and Crows (Dendrocitta, Urocissa, and Cissa)

## Palaeornis
*Palaeornis* is a genus of parrots. Within the Indian region, it is identified as one of the characteristic genera of the area. Additionally, the species *Palaeornis exsul* inhabited Rodriguez, though it is now considered probably extinct.

## Crows of the Indian Region
The Indian region possesses one of the world's richest and most varied assemblages of birds, surpassed only by tropical America. Among the characteristic genera of crows found in this region are *Dendrocitta*, *Urocissa*, and *Cissa*.

## Crows in History and Culture
Crows appear in various historical, cultural, and religious contexts across different civilizations.

### Religious and Symbolic Roles
In the Qur'an, a crow is sent by Allah to dig up the earth to demonstrate to a man how to cover the dead body of his brother. Upon seeing this, the man expressed regret, questioning if he lacked the strength to act like the crow and cover his brother's body.

In Japanese history, flags emblazoned with a crow—specifically a three-legged crow of the sun—were used during a ceremony in the great hall of the palace in A.D. 710. These designs, along with the moon, an azure dragon, a red bird, and the sun, were of Chinese origin.

### Omens and Interpretations
Crows have frequently been interpreted as omens. In the *Heimskringla*, a king's pilot, a bonde, became distressed and unable to row when two crows flew over the ship, croaking and screeching "hideously" and "dreadfully," which the bonde believed betokened something.

In Roman history, crows were observed fighting on the roof of a house as Tiberius advanced toward the Capitol. During this event, a stone pushed off by one of the crows fell at the feet of Tiberius. While this caused some of his adherents to hesitate, Blossius of Cumæ argued that it would be a disgrace for a defender of the Roman people and descendant of Scipio Africanus and Gracchus to fear a crow.
