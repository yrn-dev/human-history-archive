# Palaearctic Division of Asia

The Palaearctic division of Asia comprises the northern portion of the continent, extending as far south as the Himalaya. Zoologically, this area is not distinct from Europe; together with the strip of Africa located north of the Atlas, these regions constitute the Palaearctic region. In a broader faunistic context, the Palaearctic and Nearctic areas are considered the two subdivisions of a single great unit known as the Holarctic region.

## Mammalian Fauna
The mammals of northern Asia are characterized by a strong resemblance to those of Europe, with nearly all European mammals also occurring in the region. However, the Palaearctic fauna in northern Asia is enriched by numerous additional species, primarily those restricted to temperate and cold regions.

### Carnivora
Carnivora are numerous in the Palaearctic division of Asia, with a particular prevalence of animals that frequent cold climates. Characteristic members of this order include:
*   Bears
*   Wolves
*   Foxes
*   Weasels

While the tiger is considered the true home of the Indian region, it has wandered far north into the Palaearctic division of Asia.

### Other Mammalian Groups
The Palaearctic division is characterized by the relative absence of certain groups. Quadrumana, or monkeys, are nearly unrepresented, with only one species found in Japan and one or two others located in Tibet and northern China. Regarding bats, the insectivorous division is numerous, but the frugivorous division is represented by only a single species in Japan.

Other notable mammals include:
*   **Insectivora:** This group is dominated by numerous forms of hedgehogs, shrews, and moles.
*   **Rodents:** Well represented by mice, squirrels, and hares. Specifically characteristic forms in northern Asia include pikas (tailless hares, *Lagomys*) and marmots (*Arctomys*).
*   **Ungulata:** This order includes various forms of sheep, with ten to twelve wild species of *Ovis* found in Asian mountain chains. Other representatives include the yellow sheep (*Gazella gutturosa*) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*), both of which are peculiar forms of antelope. Deer are also present, particularly those of the typical genus *Cervus*. The musk deer (*Moschus*) is one of the most peculiar types and is restricted to northern Asia.

## Avifauna
The Palaearctic subregion possesses a high number of bird families (67) and genera (323). It shares a significant affinity with the Nearctic subregion, with 128 genera common to both. 

The region is home to one single peculiar family of land birds, the *Panuridae*, which includes the bearded tit-mouse (*Panurus biarmicus*). Other peculiar genera include the sandgrouse of central Asia (*Syrrhaptes*), the spoon-billed sandpiper of Siberia (*Eurynorhynchus*), and *Musicicapa* of Europe.

## Regional Comparisons and Boundaries
The Palaearctic region maintains a much greater affinity to the Nearctic than to any other region. While it shares some genera with the Ethiopian and Indian regions—with 71 genera common to the Indian region without occurring in the Ethiopian—it bears extremely few resemblances to the Neotropical or Australian regions.

Geographically and zoologically, there is no definitive line that can be drawn between the Palaearctic and the Oriental (Indian) subregions, as many Indo-Malayan families extend into the Palaearctic. However, the mammal fauna of the Indian region is described as being much more highly developed than that of the Palaearctic.
