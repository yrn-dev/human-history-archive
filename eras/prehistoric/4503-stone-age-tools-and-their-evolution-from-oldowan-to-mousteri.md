# The Evolution of Lithic Technology: From Oldowan to Mousterian

## Overview
The transition of stone tool technology during the Lower and Middle Paleolithic represents more than just a change in hardware; it marks the cognitive evolution of early hominins. The progression from the rudimentary Oldowan choppers to the sophisticated Mousterian flakes demonstrates an increasing ability to visualize a finished product within a raw piece of stone—a cognitive leap known as "conceptualization."

The journey began with the **Oldowan** industry, characterized by simple percussion. These tools were primarily used for scavenging, allowing hominins to access bone marrow and process meat. As *Homo erectus* emerged, the **Acheulean** industry introduced symmetry and standardization, most notably the handaxe. Finally, the **Mousterian** industry, associated largely with Neanderthals, shifted the focus from the "core" (the main stone) to the "flake" (the shard broken off), allowing for specialized tools like scrapers and spear points.

## Timeline of Tool Evolution

| Industry | Approximate Period | Primary Hominin | Key Characteristic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

## Key Facts

### The Oldowan: The First Step
The Oldowan industry is the earliest widespread stone tool tradition. These tools were created by striking a hammerstone against a core to create a sharp edge. While simple, they provided a massive evolutionary advantage, enabling early humans to occupy new ecological niches.

### The Acheulean: The "Swiss Army Knife"
The Acheulean represents a significant leap in complexity. The hallmark tool, the teardrop-shaped handaxe, required "bifacial" flaking (working both sides). Historians and anthropologists debate whether the symmetry of these axes was purely functional for butchery or if it indicates an emerging aesthetic sense or social signaling.

### The Mousterian: Precision and Planning
The Mousterian industry is defined by the **Levallois technique**. Instead of simply shaping a stone, the knapper carefully prepared a core so that a single, precise strike would detach a flake of a predetermined size and shape. This allowed for the creation of diverse tools, including knives and points that could be hafted onto wooden shafts.

## Did You Know?
* **The "Handaxe Mystery":** Some Acheulean handaxes are so perfectly symmetrical that they exceed the requirements for butchery, leading some researchers to suggest they were used as social status symbols.
* **Material Selectivity:** Early hominins didn't use just any rock; they specifically sought out cryptocrystalline silicates (like flint, chert, or obsidian) because they break with a predictable "conchoidal" fracture.
* **Cognitive Mapping:** The transition to the Levallois technique suggests that Neanderthals possessed a "working memory" capable of multi-step planning, a trait once thought unique to *Homo sapiens*.
