# Bracklesham Beds

The Bracklesham beds are a geological formation consisting of sands and clays of shallow-water origin. They are part of the upper Eocene formation found within the Hampshire and London basins in England, and are also well developed in the Isle of Wight.

## Classification and Stratigraphy
In geological classification, the Bracklesham beds are generally accepted as the Middle Bagshot beds. They are sometimes classed together with the overlying Barton clay under the designation of Middle Bagshot. 

The Bracklesham beds are situated between other Bagshot divisions: they overlie the Lower Bagshot beds—which include the Alum Bay, Bournemouth, and possibly the Bovey Tracey beds—and are overlain by the Upper Bagshot beds, which comprise the Barton clay and Barton sand. In the London basin, the Barton beds are unknown.

## Physical Characteristics and Distribution
The thickness and composition of the Bracklesham beds vary by location:
* **Surrey and Berkshire:** The beds range from 20 to 50 feet in thickness.
* **Alum Bay:** The beds reach a thickness of 100 feet. In this area, the lower portion contains beds of lignite. Additionally, the Bracklesham beds are sharply distinguished from the overlying Barton clay by a bed of conglomerate composed of flint pebbles.

## Regional Geological Context
The Bracklesham beds are part of a broader series of deposits in the Hampshire area. This region includes the Bognor Clay (the equivalent of the London Clay), as well as the Woolwich and Reading beds. The London Clay itself is a marine deposit of blue or brown clay reaching 500 feet in thickness. 

In the Isle of Wight, the combined thickness of the Barton clay, Barton sand, and Upper Bagshot beds ranges from 140 to 200 feet.
