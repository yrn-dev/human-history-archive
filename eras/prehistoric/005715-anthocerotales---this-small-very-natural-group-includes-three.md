# Anthocerotales

The Anthocerotales constitute a small and very natural group of plants within the Bryophyta. This group is comprised of three genera: *Anthoceros*, *Dendroceros*, and *Notothylas*. In many respects, the Anthocerotales occupy an isolated position among the Bryophyta.

## Phylogenetic Position
Within the classification of liverworts, the Anthocerotales are regarded as a small and very distinct group. While some researchers, such as Leitgeb, have suggested a relationship between the Anthocerotales and the Jungermanniales, recent research has served to emphasize their isolation rather than confirm such a link. 

The broader relationship between the Anthocerotales, the Hepaticae, the Musci, and the Sphagnales remains an open question, as there are no known connecting forms between these groups. Consequently, it is undetermined whether the Bryophyta are a polyphyletic or monophyletic group.

## Gametophyte Structure
The gametophyte of the Anthocerotales is a thallus. In the genus *Anthoceros*, which has three species occurring in Britain, the thallus is dark green and composed of parenchymatous cells with an ill-defined midrib. Typically, a single large chloroplast is found in each assimilating cell. 

The apical region of the plant is protected by mucilage, which is secreted by mucilage slits—small, pit-like depressions located between the superficial cells of the lower surface. Mucilage may also form within the intercellular spaces of the thallus. These mucilage slits often house colonies of *Nostoc*, which cause the slits to become enlarged.

## Reproductive Organs
The sexual organs of the Anthocerotales are scattered across the upper surface of the thallus. The antheridia are globular and stalked; they are exceptional because they are formed endogenously within special intercellular spaces. The cavity containing them is bounded by a superficial layer of cells that does not break down until the antheridia are nearly mature, though they occasionally develop on the surface of shaded portions of the thallus.

The archegonia have necks that barely project above the general surface of the thallus. While they differ in detail, their structure and development are in agreement with other Hepaticae.

## The Sporogonium
The sporogonium of the Anthocerotales is characterized by its ability for long-continued spore production and growth. It is initially protected by a thick calyptra, which is derived from the thallus tissue surrounding the archegonium. 

The structure of the sporogonium consists of:
* **The Foot:** A large bulbous foot with superficial cells that grow out into processes.
* **The Capsule:** A long capsule that can reach a length of one and a half inches. It continues to grow for months due to the activity of a zone of cells located between the capsule and the foot.

The capsule wall is several layers of cells thick. Because the underlying cells possess chlorophyll and the epidermis contains functional stomata, the capsule is capable of assimilation. The center of the capsule contains a columella, which is a strand of narrow, elongated cells. Spores, mixed with elaters, are formed from the dome-shaped archesporium in the space between the columella and the capsule wall.
