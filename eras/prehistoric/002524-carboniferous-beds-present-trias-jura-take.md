# Geological Formations of the Carboniferous, Triassic, and Jurassic Periods

The geological record of the Earth's crust across various global regions reveals a complex distribution of strata from the Palaeozoic and Mesozoic eras, specifically the Carboniferous, Triassic (Trias), and Jurassic periods. These formations vary significantly by region, appearing as folded mountain chains, sedimentary plateaus, or isolated deposits.

## The Balkan Peninsula and Northern Bulgaria
The Balkan Peninsula is geologically divided into four distinct areas. In the western folded belt, where the strike of the folds runs N.W.-S.E. or N.N.W.-S.S.E., Carboniferous beds are present in the north. In this region, the Trias and the Jura contribute a considerable part to the formation of the mountain chain. Further south in this belt, older beds disappear, and while the chain is chiefly formed of Cretaceous beds, Jurassic rocks are likely present along with Eocene formations.

In Northern Bulgaria, the stratified formation shows a wide variety of systems. The Carboniferous appears in the western Balkans with a continental *facies* (Kulm), where anthracitiferous coal is found within beds of sandstone and argillite. Above these are Mesozoic deposits extending from the Trias to the upper Jurassic, which also occur in the central part of the range.

## Asia Minor and the Near East
In Asia Minor, the geological structure consists of a central plateau of nearly horizontal strata surrounded by intensely folded mountain chains. In the Anti-Taurus mountains, Carboniferous fossils have been discovered in several locations. Additionally, Carboniferous rocks are found at Eregli (Heraclea Pontica), where they have been worked for coal, and Carboniferous fossils have been identified at Balia Maden in Mysia. A band of Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous beds is located south of the Sea of Marmora, which is likely a continuation of the Mesozoic band found on the Black Sea coast.

## British Isles and Regional Formations
In the British Isles, Carboniferous formations are prominent across several counties. In one region, the surface is controlled by "Hercynian" folds. Here, Carboniferous Limestone forms a synclinal running from Millstreet through Lismore. Other areas feature Carboniferous Slate, as well as beds of Coal Measures and Millstone Grit extending from Kanturk and Mallow to the borders of Kerry and Limerick. Passage-beds known as Yellow Sandstone appear near Cork city, marking the transition from the Old Red Sandstone to the Carboniferous.

In Kerry, Carboniferous shales and sandstones reach the coal-measures east of the Glanruddery Mountains and Listowel, where the coals are unproductive. Carboniferous Limestone forms a fringe to these beds and creates the shores of Lough Leane.

In other northern regions, the Lower Carboniferous cement stone series of the Calciferous Sandstone group dips beneath the Old Red volcanic rocks. Carboniferous limestone occupies small areas in the south and east of these counties, with some strata intercalated with intrusive basalt sheets.

## British East Africa
The geological formations of British East Africa are divided into four regions. A foot plateau in this area is composed of sedimentary rocks dating from the Trias to the Jurassic. Additionally, the archive notes the presence of grits and shales of Taru and Masara, which are categorized as potentially Carboniferous.

## Biological Records of the Periods
The fossil record provides insight into the dominant life forms during these eras. During the Carboniferous period, the Arthrodira and Ostracophori disappeared, while the Dipneusti and Crossopterygii remained abundant. The predominant groups were the Chondrostean ganoids (such as *Platysomidae* and *Palaeoniscidae*) and the Selachians, including truesharks and *Pleuracanthus*.

In the Trias, the variety of Dipneusti and Crossopterygii dwindled, and the *Ceratodontidae* appeared. Chondrostean and Holostean ganoids were represented roughly equally. By the Jurassic period, the Holostean ganoids became predominant. This era also saw the appearance of the first annectant representatives of the Teleostei (*Leptolepidae* and *Pholidophoridae*), as well as numerous Coelacanthid crossopterygians, Chimaerids, primitive skates (*Rhinobatidae* and *Squatinidae*), and Cestraciont sharks.
