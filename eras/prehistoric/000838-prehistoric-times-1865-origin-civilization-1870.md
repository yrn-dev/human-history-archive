# Prehistoric Times and The Origin of Civilization

*Prehistoric Times* (1865) and *The Origin of Civilization* (1870) are scientific works authored by John Lubbock, 1st Baron Avebury. These publications were produced during a period of Lubbock's career in which he balanced his interests in science with increasing participation in public affairs.

## Author Biography
John Lubbock was born in London on April 30, 1834. He was the son of Sir John William Lubbock, 3rd baronet, who was a highly distinguished man of science. Lubbock attended Eton in 1845 and entered his father's bank three years later, becoming a partner at the age of twenty-two. He succeeded to the baronetcy in 1865.

Throughout his life, Lubbock maintained a deep commitment to science, serving as the president of both the Anthropological Institute and the Entomological Society. His academic contributions were recognized with honorary degrees from several universities, including Dublin, Edinburgh, Wuerzburg, Oxford, and Cambridge, where he served as the Rede lecturer in 1886. Additionally, he was appointed a trustee of the British Museum in 1878.

## Public and Political Career
Parallel to his scientific writing, Lubbock was an influential member of the Unionist party and a politician. He was elected as a member of parliament for Maidstone in 1870 and again in 1874. After losing that seat in 1880, he was elected as the member for London University, an institution where he had served as vice-chancellor since 1872.

In parliament, Lubbock was responsible for numerous enactments. These included the Bank Holidays Act 1871, as well as various bills concerning public libraries, open spaces, the preservation of ancient monuments, shop hours regulations, and absconding debtors. In the realm of finance, he served on commissions regarding financial questions and coinage, and he was elected as the first president of the Institute of Bankers in 1879. He also held leadership roles as the president of the British Association (1881) and the Linnaean Society (1881–1886). Later in his career, he served as the president of the London Chamber of Commerce (1888–1892) and as the chairman of the London County Council from 1890 to 1892.

## Academic Context and Reception
The works *Prehistoric Times* and *The Origin of Civilization* are cited as authorities in the study of prehistoric eras and early human customs. Specifically, *Prehistoric Times* was published in London in 1865, and *The Origin of Civilization* was published in London in 1870 (with another citation noting a 1875 date). These texts are listed alongside other significant anthropological and historical works of the era, such as E.B. Tylor's *Primitive Culture* and J.G. Frazer's *The Golden Bough*.
