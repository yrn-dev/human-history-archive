# Archaeological Discoveries of the Sakiya Republic

## The Discoveries of William Peppé
Recent archaeological research has provided significant confirmation and expansion of knowledge regarding the earliest periods of history in the region bordering English and Nepalese territory. A pre-eminent discovery was made by Mr. William Peppé on the Birdpur estate. Peppé discovered a stupa, also referred to as a cairn, which had been erected by the Sakiya clan. This structure was built over the clan's share of the ashes from the cremation pyre of the Buddha.

## Asoka Pillars and the Lumbini Garden
In the vicinity of the Birdpur estate, two inscribed pillars put up by Asoka have been identified, providing further evidence of the region's historical significance:

*   **The Lumbini Garden Pillar:** Located approximately 12 meters to the north-east of the Sakiya cairn, this pillar records Asoka's visit to the Lumbini Garden, identifying it as the location where the future Buddha was born. Although the inscription was created more than two centuries after the birth it commemorates, it serves as strong evidence for the site of the garden. It is probable that the descendants of the original possessors of the land continued to occupy the area, as the tradition remained uninterrupted.
*   **The Konagamana Pillar:** Located to the north-west, another pillar records Asoka's visit to a cairn erected by the Sakyas. This specific cairn was built over the remains of Konagamana, one of the previous teachers or Buddhas. Gotama the Buddha had claimed to be a follower of Konagamana.

## The Sakiya Republic
These archaeological finds definitively determine the district that was occupied by the Sakiya republic during the 6th and 7th centuries B.C. While the exact boundaries of the republic are not known, the evidence suggests the clan's territory extended 30 meters or more along the lower slopes of the Himalayas and 30 meters or more southwards into the plains.

## Current State of the Region
The district has been characterized as abandoned jungle since the 3rd century A.D., or possibly earlier. Because of this, the numerous ruined sites throughout the area have remained undisturbed. Consequently, researchers confidently expect further discoveries to be made in the region.
