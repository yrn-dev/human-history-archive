# Geological Formations of British East Africa

The geological structures of British East Africa are categorized into four distinct regions, each defined by specific physiographical features. These regions include the coast plain, the foot plateau, the ancient plateau, and the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa.

## Regional Physiography and Composition

The coast plain is characterized as being narrow in the south and rising with some steepness; it is composed of recent rocks. Succeeding this is the foot plateau, which consists of sedimentary rocks dating from the Jurassic to the Trias periods. 

The ancient plateau begins at Taru and extends to the borders of Kikuyu. This region is composed of ancient crystalline rocks. Over these crystalline foundations, immense quantities of volcanic rocks have accumulated from the Post-Jurassic period to the Recent era, creating the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa.

## Sedimentary Formations

The sedimentary formations recognized in the region are divided by geological age:

*   **Recent:** This category includes alluvium and superficial sands, as well as modern lake deposits and living coral rock.
*   **Pleistocene:** Formations from this era include raised coral rock and the conglomerate of Mombasa Island, as well as gravels containing flint implements.
*   **Jurassic:** This period is represented by the glacial beds of Kenya and the limestones and shales of Changamwe.
*   **Karroo:** This includes flags and sandstones.
*   **Carboniferous (Questionable):** The grits and shales of Masara and Taru are attributed to this period.
*   **Archaean:** The oldest sedimentary formations include the shales of the Sabaki river, as well as gneisses, schists, quartzites (specifically of Nandi), and granites.

## Igneous and Volcanic Formations

The igneous and volcanic activity in the region is categorized into two primary timeframes:

*   **Post-Jurassic to Pleistocene:** This era includes the volcanoes of the rift-valley, Kibo, and various plateau eruptions, such as Kimawenzi and Kenya.
*   **Recent:** This category consists of volcanoes that are currently active, dormant, or extinct.
