# Geology of Bolivia

The geological structure of Bolivia is characterized by a diverse arrangement of rock formations, varying significantly between the eastern ranges, the Western Cordillera, and the intervening plateau.

## The Andean Ranges and Plateau
The eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes are primarily composed of Palaeozoic rocks, which feature various intrusions, including granitic ones. In contrast, the Western Cordillera consists chiefly of Jurassic and Cretaceous beds, alongside ashes and lavas resulting from great volcanoes. The volcanoes of Bolivia are located almost entirely within this Western Cordillera.

Between these two ranges lies a plateau covered by terrestrial and freshwater deposits. This plateau is punctuated by rising ridges of Palaeozoic rock and a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones. The age of these specific deposits is somewhat uncertain, though they are probably, at least in part, Cretaceous.

## Palaeozoic Formations and Fossils
The Palaeozoic beds in Bolivia have yielded fossils from the Carboniferous, Devonian, Ordovician, and Cambrian ages. The distribution of these beds varies by region:
* **Southern Bolivia:** The eastern Andes are largely formed of Ordovician and Cambrian beds.
* **Northern Bolivia:** Devonian and Carboniferous beds are extensively developed, particularly within the north-eastern ranges.

The great summits of the eastern range, such as Sorata and Illimani, are formed from these Palaeozoic rocks with other and granitic intrusions.

## Sedimentary Series and Marine Deposits
The Palaeozoic beds are directly overlaid by a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, which are similar to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Brazil and Argentina. These layers frequently feature a tuff or conglomerate of porphyritic rocks at the base. 

Marine fossils discovered in the middle of this series by Gustav Steinmann suggest an age no earlier than the Jurassic, with Steinmann specifically attributing them to the Lower Cretaceous. However, it is considered possible that this series represents more than one geological system. Aside from these, no later marine deposits have been identified in the plains of Bolivia or the eastern Andes.

## Recent and Freshwater Deposits
A wide area of the region is occupied by freshwater beds dating from the Tertiary period and later. Recent deposits cover a significant portion of the depression situated between the Western and Eastern Cordillera. These deposits appear to be partly of torrential origin, resembling talus-fans found at the foot of mountain ranges in other dry regions. Additionally, Lakes Pampa Aullaguas (Poopo) and Titicaca were undoubtedly more extensive in the past than they are currently.

## Eastern Plains and the Chiquitos
In eastern Bolivia, the plains are punctuated by hills known as the Chiquitos. These hills are composed of ancient sedimentary rocks of unknown age.
