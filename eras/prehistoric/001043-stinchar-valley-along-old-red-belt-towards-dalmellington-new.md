# Stinchar Valley and the Old Red Belt

The Stinchar valley and the surrounding elevated ground in the south of Ayrshire, Scotland, are characterized by a complex geological structure involving Silurian tablelands and a distinct belt of Old Red Sandstone.

## Regional Geography and Topography
The Stinchar is one of the six most noted rivers in Ayrshire, a south-western Scottish county. The county's surface is generally undulating, featuring a small mountainous tract in the north and a larger mountainous region in the south and south-east. Within this southern region, the topography is influenced by the Silurian tableland of southern Scotland.

## Geological Composition of the Southern Region
The hilly region in the south of the county primarily forms part of the Silurian tableland. Along the northern margin of this region lies a belt of elevated ground consisting mainly of Old Red Sandstone strata. In contrast, the fertile low ground in the area is chiefly occupied by younger Palaeozoic rocks.

The Silurian belt, which extends from the mouth of Loch Ryan to the Merrick range, is composed of shales, greywackes, and grits. This area includes thin leaves of black shales containing graptolites of Upper Llandeilo age, which are repeated across a broad area due to folding. 

## The Stinchar Valley and Associated Formations
Within the Stinchar valley, as well as at Craighead, there are limestones of Upper Llandeilo age. These limestones are associated with conglomerates, such as those found at Kennedy's Pass and Bennan Hill near Straiton, which cover Radiolarian cherts, lavas, and volcanic/plutonic rocks unconformably.

Other notable geological features in the vicinity include:
* **Volcanic Rocks:** In the Ballantrae region, there is a significant development of agglomerates, tuffs, and lavas of Arenig age. These are interleaved with black shales and cherty mudstones.
* **Plutonic Intrusions:** The volcanic materials are pierced by granite, gabbro, and serpentine. Serpentine is typically developed on Balhamie Hill near Colmonell, forming two belts that run inland from Burnfoot and near Bennane Head.
* **Other Strata:** Representatives of Wenlock rocks form a narrow belt near the village of Straiton. Additionally, Llandovery strata appear north of the Girvan at Dailly, where they form an inlier surrounded by Carboniferous formations and the Old Red Sandstone.

## Surrounding Highlands and Peaks
The broader region of south and south-east Ayrshire contains several principal hills. These include:
* **Black Craig:** Reaching 2,298 feet, located 5 miles south-east of New Cumnock.
* **Enoch:** Reaching 1,865 feet, located 5 miles east of Dalmellington.
* **Corsancone:** Reaching 1,547 feet, located 3 miles north-east of New Cumnock.
* **Polmaddie:** Reaching 1,750 feet, located 2 miles south-east of Barr.
