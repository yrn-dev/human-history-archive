# Bryophyta: Liverworts and Mosses

The Bryophyta comprise a diverse group of plants, including liverworts and mosses, characterized by various growth forms and reproductive structures. These plants range from simple thalloid forms to more complex structures featuring stems and leaves.

## Liverworts (Hepaticae)

Liverworts are divided into three primary groups: the Marchantiales, the Jungermanniales, and the Anthocerotales. Each group demonstrates a progression from simple to more highly organized forms.

### Marchantiales
The Marchantiales are thalloid forms with specialized thallus structures that allow them to inhabit exposed environments. In the lowest members, such as *Riccia*, the sporogonia are the simplest known, consisting of a single layer of cells forming a wall around the spores. In higher forms, the sporogonium includes a sterile seta and foot, and sterile cells or elaters are present with the spores. 

Sexual organs are located on the upper surface of the thallus. Antheridia may be scattered or grouped on stalked, disk-shaped branch-systems called antheridiophores, as seen in *Marchantia*. Archegonia are similarly positioned; in some forms, they are sunk in depressions, while in others, they are borne on archegoniophores. In *Marchantia* and *Fimbriaria*, a perianth grows around each fertilized archegonium.

### Jungermanniales
The Jungermanniales begin as thalloid forms but progress toward differentiation into stems and leaves. The Anacrogynous Jungermanniaceae include thalloid members like *Pellia epiphylla*, which features a broad, green, sparingly branched thallus with rhizoids on the lower surface for fixation and nutrient absorption. In the Acrogynous Jungermanniacae, the plant possesses well-marked leaves and stems. The sporogonium in this series typically has a sterile foot, and the origin of elaters from sterile cells can be traced. In some genera, such as *Kantia*, *Calypogeia*, and *Saccogyna*, the sporogonium is protected by a hollow tubular marsupial sac that may penetrate the soil.

### Anthocerotales
This small, distinct group includes the genera *Anthoceros*, *Dendroceros*, and *Notothylas*. The gametophyte is a thallus composed of parenchymatous cells, often containing a single large chloroplast per cell. The thallus features mucilage slits that may house colonies of *Nostoc*. The sporogonium of the Anthocerotales is unique; it consists of a large bulbous foot and a long capsule capable of continued growth and spore production for months. The capsule contains a central strand of narrow elongated cells called the columella, and its epidermis contains functional stomata and chlorophyll.

## Mosses (Bryales)

Mosses generally exhibit more complex structures than liverworts. For example, *Funaria hygrometrica* consists of short leafy shoots with branched rhizoids composed of rows of elongated cells. Its stem contains a central strand of narrow cells that function as water-conducting tissue.

### General Structure and Development
In most mosses, the sporogonium develops from a fertilized ovum and consists of a foot embedded in the stem, a long seta, and a capsule. The seta may widen at the base of the capsule into a region called the apophysis, which often bears functional stomata. 

The internal structure of the capsule typically includes a definite columella extending from the base to the apex. The archesporium is derived from the outermost layer of cells of the endothecium. Spores are often released gradually in dry weather via a peristome—one or two series of teeth—or by the detachment of an operculum.

### Specialized Moss Genera
*   **Archidium**: This moss differs from other Bryales as its sporogonium has a small foot and almost no seta. It lacks a sterile columella and an archesporium; instead, a variable number of cells in the endothecium become spore-mother-cells.
*   **Sphagnum**: Known as bog-mosses, these plants are critical in the formation of peat. The stem features a zone of thick-walled cells surrounding a thin-walled central tissue, with an outer layer of large, dead, empty cells that conduct water via capillarity. The leaves lack a midrib and contain similar empty cells. The mature sporogonium lacks a distinct seta, instead raising the capsule on a leafless outgrowth called a pseudopodium.

## Comparative Morphology of Sporogonia

The development of the sporogonium varies across the groups. In *Pellia*, the embryo's terminal tier forms the capsule wall and spore-producing cells, while lower tiers form the seta and foot. In *Funaria*, the sporogonium grows via a two-sided apical cell. In *Sphagnum*, the lower tiers of the embryo form the foot, while the upper half forms the sterile foot and seta.
