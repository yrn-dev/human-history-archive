# Marchantia and Fimbriaria

## Overview of Marchantiales
The Marchantiales are a group of plants most abundant in warm, sunny localities, typically growing on rocks or soil, often in exposed situations. Within this group, the plants possess a dorsiventral thallus. This structure consists of a lower region containing cells with little chlorophyll and an upper stratum specialized for transpiration and assimilation. The upper stratum is composed of air-chambers formed by superficial cells.

The lower region of the thallus usually forms a midrib consisting of parenchymatous cells, which may be differentiated as sclerenchyma fibres, mucilage cells, or cells containing oil-bodies. Behind the apex, which contains several initial cells, a series of ventral scales (amphigastria) is formed. These single-layer cells often have terminal appendages that fold over and protect the apex.

The thallus utilizes two types of rhizoids: longer, narrower peg-rhizoids with wall thickenings projecting into the cell-cavity, and wider smooth-walled rhizoids that grow directly into the soil. The peg-rhizoids, which are peculiar to this group, converge to the midrib under the amphigastria to form a wick-like strand that conducts water via capillarity.

## Reproductive Organs in Marchantia
In the Marchantiales, sexual organs are always located on the morphologically upper surface of the thallus. In the highest forms, such as *Marchantia*, these organs are borne on specialized structures.

The antheridia are grouped on stalked, disk-shaped branch-systems known as antheridiophores. The individual antheridia are situated within depressions, from which spermatozoids are sometimes forcibly ejected.

The archegonia are borne on archegoniophores, which consist of a stalk supporting a disk-shaped head. In *Marchantia*, the archegoniophore is a repeatedly branched continuation of the thallus. The archegonia arise in relation to growing points that are displaced to the lower surface of the disk. To protect the archegonia, they are either sunk into depressions of the disk or protected by a special two-lipped involucre. The stalk of the *Marchantia* archegoniophore contains two grooves.

## The Perianth in Marchantia and Fimbriaria
In the genera *Marchantia* and *Fimbriaria*, a specific biological structure occurs during the reproductive process. An additional investment, which descriptive works term the perianth, grows up around each archegonium after it has been fertilized.

## Vegetative Propagation
Vegetative propagation is widely spread among these plants. Small fragments of the gametophyte can grow into new individuals when artificially divided. While some species use bi-cellular gemmae that escape from superficial cells, *Marchantia* employs a different method. In *Marchantia*, gemmae are formed within cup-shaped or tubular receptacles. These gemmae are forced out of the receptacles by the swelling of mucilage, which is secreted by special hairs. *Marchantia polymorpha* and *Lunularia* spread largely through these gemmae formed in special gemma-cups on the thallus.
