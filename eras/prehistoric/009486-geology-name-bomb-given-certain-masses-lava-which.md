# Volcanic Bombs

In the field of geology, the term "bomb" refers to specific masses of lava that have been expelled from a volcanic vent through explosive action. These projectiles vary significantly in their physical characteristics, ranging in size from the dimensions of a walnut to massive forms weighing several tons.

## Physical Characteristics and Formation
Volcanic bombs typically manifest in spheroidal, ellipsoidal, or discoidal shapes. Their internal structure can be solid, hollow, or characterized as more or less cavernous. It is generally believed that these shapes are partially the result of the mass rotating during its flight through the air; in certain instances, a gyratory movement causes the bomb to become twisted.

However, some geologists have challenged the classification of all such masses as projectiles. Professor J.D. Dana demonstrated that many objects regarded as bombs in Hawaii are actually lava-balls that were not hurled through the air. Similarly, Dr. H.J. Johnston-Lavis argued that many of the so-called bombs found at Vesuvius are merely globular masses that formed within a stream of lava rather than being ejected as projectiles.

## Specialized Types of Bombs
Certain variations of volcanic bombs are identified by their composition and appearance:

*   **Bread-crust Bombs:** Found among masses of pumice ejected from Vulcano, these are named for a coating of obsidian that has been cracked and bent, resembling the crust of a roll. This formation likely occurs when acid magma is expelled in a highly viscous state, and the resulting cooling crust is burst by steam from occluded water.
*   **Quartz Bombs:** Some bombs ejected during recent eruptions of Etna consist of white granular quartz encased in a black scoriaceous crust. In these cases, the quartz represents altered sandstone.
*   **Olivine Bombs:** Bombs composed of granular olivine have been identified in some of the tuffs located in the Eifel.
