# Pectunculus (Glycimeris) deleta and Fossil Fishes

## Pectunculus (Glycimeris) deleta
*Pectunculus (Glycimeris) deleta* is identified as a characteristic fossil found within the Barton Clay. It is listed alongside other characteristic fossils of this formation, including *Ostrea gigantea*, *Volutilithes luctatrix*, and *Fusus longaevus*. In addition to these specimens, the Barton Clay contains a crocodile (*Diplocynodon*) and various fishes, such as *Arius* and *Lamna*. The sands associated with this area are noted for their high purity, which makes them suitable for use in glass making.

## General Characteristics of Fishes
Ichthyology is the branch of zoology dedicated to the study of the distribution, mode of life, and internal and external structures of fishes. Generally, vertebrate animals are classified as fishes if they possess a specific set of characteristics: they live in water and breathe dissolved air using branchiae or gills; they possess a heart consisting of a single atrium and a single ventricle; their skin is either covered in osseous plates, bucklers, scales, or is naked; and their limbs, when present, are modified into fins, which are supplemented by unpaired median fins. Most fishes are oviparous, though some exceptions exist.

## Palaeontological Research and Classification
The study of fossil fishes has been advanced by several key researchers. Cuvier conducted early research into palaeontology, observing that the fossil genus *Palaeoniscus* shared a close resemblance to the living *Lepidosteus* and *Polypterus* in its scales. He also noted the identity of structure in the upper caudal lobe of *Palaeoniscus* and sturgeons, as well as the presence of "fulcra" on the anterior margin of the dorsal fin in both *Palaeoniscus* and *Lepidosteus*. Based on these observations, Cuvier inferred that the fossil genus was allied to either *Lepidosteus* or sturgeons, placing *Lepidosteus* and the fossil genus in the order *Malacopterygii abdominales*.

L. Agassiz later emphasized the importance of scale structure as a characteristic, leading to the identification of a new subclass of fishes known as *Ganoidei*. Agassiz observed that the peculiar scales found in *Lepidosteus* and *Polypterus* were common to all fossil osseous fishes up to the Chalk. In his work *Recherches sur les poissons fossiles* (1833-1843), he methodically arranged fossil forms with descriptions and illustrations. However, later analysis suggested his classification was artificial because the distinctions between ganoid, placoid, ctenoid, and cycloid scales are vague.

## Fossil Fish Varieties and Anatomy
Fossil records reveal a wide variety of fish forms and anatomical structures:

*   **Tail-Fins:** While many sharks possess heterocercal tails, other types exist. The fossil Pleuracanthids possessed protocercal tails, while the ancient *Cladoselache* had a highly developed, practically homocercal tail.
*   **Fin-Folds:** In some fishes, the praecaudal portion of the dorsal fin-fold disappears in parts, leaving one, two, or three enlarged dorsal fins. Ventrally, this fold may be reduced to a single anal fin, which in *Scombridae* is occasionally followed by a series of finlets.
*   **Specific Fossil Genera:** In the "Ludlow Bone bed"—a brown layer found near Ludlow consisting of fragmentary fish remains associated with mollusca and *Pterygotus*—common fish fossils include *Cyathaspis*, *Cephalaspis*, and *Auchenaspis*. Additionally, the Lower Lias in Somersetshire has yielded the *Ichthyosaurus*, described as a Mesozoic fish-lizard.
