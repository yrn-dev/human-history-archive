# Rods and Rodlets

In the study of cellular forms, rods or rodlets are defined as cells that are slightly or more considerably elongated. These cells may be cylindrical, somewhat fusiform, or biscuit-shaped. The specific classification of these forms often depends on their proportions and shape: cylindrical forms that are short—typically only three or four times as long as they are broad—are referred to as *Bacterium*, while longer cylindrical forms are termed *Bacillus*. Biscuit-shaped cells are identified as *Bacteria* in their early stages of division, and spindle-shaped forms are categorized as *Clostridia*.

## Growth and Division
The growth of an ordinary bacterium is characterized by the uniform elongation of the rodlet. This process continues until the length of the cell has doubled, at which point division occurs via a median septum. Following this division, each resulting daughter cell simultaneously doubles in length, and the cycle of elongation and median division repeats.

When these cells remain connected after division, they form a filament. In such filamentous forms, such as *B. ramosus*, the processes of elongation and division repeat uniformly as long as environmental conditions are maintained. Observations of rodlets fixed in nutrient gelatine hanging drops at constant temperatures have revealed several facts regarding their growth:
* Doubling periods remain constant if conditions are constant, as each cell is equally active along the entire length of the filament.
* Growth is influenced by temperature, with specific optimum, minimum, and maximum temperatures at which growth begins, reaches its peak, or is exhausted.
* Maximum growth and the most rapid cell division do not necessarily coincide with the best conditions for the organism's life.
* Sudden temperature changes can check growth, while a slow rise may accelerate it.

Growth can be brought to a standstill or checked by the exhaustion or dilution of food-media, exposure to light, or the presence of metabolic products and traces of poisons. Furthermore, the injury or death of a single cell within a filamentous series lengthens the doubling period because the potential progeny of that cell are removed. Such destruction can be caused by the attacks of other organisms.

## Classification and Morphology
Within the order Haplobacterinae, the family Bacillaceae consists of vegetative cells that are straight and may be ovoid, ellipsoid, or cylindric (rodlets). In this family, division planes are always perpendicular to the long axis. The sub-family Bacilleae contains sporogenous rodlets that remain cylindric in shape, including non-motile *Bacillus* and motile forms such as *Bactrinium* (monotrichous), *Bactrillum* (lophotrichous), and *Bactridium* (peritrichous). Other sub-families include the Clostridieae, which feature spindle-shaped sporogenous rodlets (*Clostridium*), and the Plectridieae, which feature drumstick-shaped sporogenous rodlets (*Plectridium*).

Additionally, the family Spirillaceae contains vegetative cells that are cylindric but curved spirally. These include the comma-shaped *Vibrio*, the more strongly curved *Spirillum*, and the *Spirochaete*, which is coiled in numerous close turns.

## Spore Formation and Germination
Spores may develop in bacillar segments, and the cell wall may change form to accommodate the spore. In some instances, "Koepfchenbacterien" are observed, which are bacterioid segments with a spore at one end where the mother cell-wall has adapted to the spore's outline. Spores can be formed in either long or short segments; typically, only one spore is produced per cell. These spores are highly refractive, colorless, and extremely minute; for example, those of *Bacillus subtilis* measure 0.0006 mm broad by 0.0012 mm long.

Germination occurs when a spore is placed in a suitable medium at a proper temperature, often completing in a few hours. The spore swells and elongates, and the contents grow into a cell similar to the one that produced it. This germinal rodlet may break through the spore membrane, or the membrane may swell and dissolve. In *Bacillus subtilis*, the axis of growth for the germinal rodlet is at a right angle to the long axis of the spore, whereas in *Clostridium butyricum*, the axis of growth coincides with the long axis of the spore.

## Related Structures
Filaments are composed of elongated cylindrical cells that remain united end to end after division. These filaments may be free or attached at a basal end and can vary in diameter and segmentation. They are categorized as simple filaments (such as *Leptothrix*) or those exhibiting false branching (such as *Cladothrix*). Some filamentous forms, like *Beggiatoa*, exhibit slow, oscillatory movements despite having a firm cell-wall and no observed cilia.

Rodlets may also be found within zoogloea, which are gelatinous masses. For example, mixed zoogloea found as a pellicle on vegetable infusions contain both cocci and rodlets arranged in series.
