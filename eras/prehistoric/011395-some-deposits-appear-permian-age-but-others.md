# Permian Geological Deposits and Formations

The Permian system is represented globally by a variety of geological formations, including magnesian limestones, red sandstones, and conglomerates. These deposits often provide evidence of the environmental conditions present during their formation, such as the existence of deserts or salt lakes.

## Regional Occurrences and Composition

### Europe and the United Kingdom
In the United Kingdom, Permian deposits appear in several distinct forms. In the county of Durham, Permian magnesian limestone succeeds the Coal Measures to the east, extending from the Tees to South Shields and along the coast to Hartlepool. This limestone is used for building, lime-making, and the manufacture of Epsom salts, and it occasionally contains fish remains. In the Fulwell Quarries, this stone exhibits concretionary forms that simulate coral and honeycomb structures. Additionally, sandstones and marls found between the Coal Measures and the magnesian limestone at Newbottle, South Shields, and further south are generally classified as Permian, though some may belong to a lower series. In other parts of the UK, such as the west side of the Nith at Dumfries, brick-red sandstones of Permian age are present.

In Bulgaria, the Permian system is represented by conglomerate and red sandstone, which are particularly evident around the basin of Sofia. In Germany, the Permian formation is typically conformable with the overlying Bunter (the lower stage of Triassic rocks) in Thuringia, Hesse, and the Harz region of northern and central Germany.

### Asia and Eastern Europe
In Siberia, specifically in the northern land mass between the Lena and the Yenisei, there are patches of freshwater deposits consisting mainly of conglomerate and sandstone, including workable seams of coal. These are part of the Angara series; while some are probably Jurassic, others appear to be of Permian age.

In the government of Kielce in south-west Russian Poland, Permian sandstones and limestones are found in the southern region. Similarly, in the Carpathians, Permian beds are found in the inner zone. In the eastern Carpathians, these beds are not heavily folded except near the outer margin of the zone; in the western Carpathians, they are crushed and folded against a core of ancient rocks in the outer portion of the zone, while they remain relatively undisturbed in the inner portion.

## Environmental Indicators and Chemical Processes
Permian deposits often indicate specific prehistoric climates. The magnesian limestone of the English Permian is an example of rocks that indicate the prevalence of salt-lake or desert conditions at the time of deposit. This is linked to the process of dolomitization, where magnesium salts from "bitter lakes" percolate through limestone beds and convert them into dolomite.

## Biological and Volcanic Associations
The Permian period is marked by specific evolutionary developments in marine life. In the Upper Permian, Holostean ganoids, such as *Acanthophorus*, made their appearance. This group eventually became dominant during the Lower Cretaceous and Jurassic periods.

In some regions, Permian deposits are associated with volcanic activity. In the United Kingdom, volcanic necks occur within Permian formations.
