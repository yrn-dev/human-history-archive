# Siberian Palaeozoic Plateau and Related Geological Successions

The Siberian Palaeozoic plateau is a geological feature characterized by specific stratigraphic sequences. While the plateau itself serves as a point of comparison for other regions, its geological characteristics are mirrored and expanded upon in other parts of Asia.

## Comparison with Chinese Provinces
In the Chinese provinces of Shensi and Shansi, the geological succession is similar to that of the Siberian Palaeozoic plateau, though the sequence in these provinces is more complete. The structure in Shansi and Shensi consists of a floor of folded Archean rocks. These are overlaid by strata of Lower Palaeozoic age that are nearly horizontal. 

Following the Lower Palaeozoic strata, the sequence in these Chinese provinces includes marine beds belonging to the Carboniferous period. However, from the Upper Carboniferous period onward, no marine deposits are known. Similar to the findings in Siberia, plant-bearing beds are encountered in this region.

## Surrounding Geological Regions
To the south and east of the Palaeozoic plateau lies an extensive area composed primarily of Archean rocks, which includes a large portion of Mongolia north of the Tian-shan. This region is characterized by the following:
* **Deposits:** Plant-bearing deposits belonging to the Angara series are present, but there are no marine beds of Tertiary or Mesozoic age.
* **Structure:** The folds in this region are of ancient date. The area is marked by a series of depressions created by faults; the strips of land between these depressions, which did not sink to the same extent, now form mountain ranges.

Further south, Southern China possesses a different structure, consisting largely of folded mountain chains. Despite this structural difference, the geological succession is very similar to the aforementioned regions. Except for areas near the Burmese and Tibetan borders, Southern China lacks marine deposits of Tertiary or Mesozoic age.

## Related Siberian Geography
Beyond the Palaeozoic plateau, other regions of Siberia exhibit distinct geographical and biological features. The northern part of the region, situated between the Seas of Okhotsk and Bering and the Arctic Ocean, is a barren plateau reaching heights of 1000 to 2000 feet. This area is deeply indented by long fiords—such as the Gulf of Anadyr, Kolyuchin Bay, and the Bays of Ghizhiga and Penzhina—and the rivers of the Anadyr basin.

This northern plateau is bordered to the north by a mountain chain with summits reaching 8000 feet, such as Makachinga peak. The vegetation in this district is limited to mosses, lichens, and a few dwarf species of Siberian trees. 

To the east, the high plateau of East Siberia is bordered by rugged mountains ranging from 4000 to 7000 feet in height. This area includes a narrow strip, 40 to 60 miles wide, along the Sea of Okhotsk and the basin of the Uda. These mountains and their deep valleys are clothed in thick forests of larch, while the undulating hills of the Uda basin are covered by marshes and forests.
