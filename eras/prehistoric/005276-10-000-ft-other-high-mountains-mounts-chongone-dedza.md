# High Mountains and Plateaus of the World

The Earth's geography is characterized by diverse orographical features, ranging from extensive high plateaus to isolated volcanic peaks and massive mountain ranges. These formations vary significantly in altitude, composition, and ecological zoning.

## African Highlands and Peaks
In certain protectorates of Africa, much of the land consists of a high plateau with an average altitude of 3,500 ft. The highest mountain in this specific region is Mount Mlanje, located in the extreme south-eastern corner. This picturesque mass is an isolated "chunk" of the Archean plateau that experienced a later volcanic outburst of basalt, resulting in a summit and sides that exhibit several craters; its highest peak reaches 9,683 ft. Other significant elevations include Mount Rungwe (exceeding 10,000 ft.) in German territory near Lake Nyasa, and Mounts Chongone and Dedza in Angoniland, both reaching 7,000 ft. Additionally, the Konde Mountains and the Nyika Plateau likely exceed 8,000 ft. In the Shiré Highlands, Mount Zomba reaches 6,900 ft. and Mount Chiradzulu reaches 5,500 ft.

Further east, Mount Kenya is a great volcanic mountain with a highest peak of 17,007 ft. (with a possible error of 30 ft.). Its central core consists of several steep pyramids from a denuded old volcano. The mountain features fifteen glaciers, the largest being the Lewis and Gregory glaciers. High-altitude lakes are also present, such as Lake Höhnel at 14,000 ft. and Lake Michaelson at approximately 12,700 ft. 

Mount Kilimanjaro also exhibits distinct vegetation zones. The forest zone on the south side extends from 6,500 to 9,500 ft. In the alpine zone, flowering plants reach up to 15,700 ft. on the sheltered south-west flank of Mawenzi peak, though elsewhere vegetation generally only grows in dwarfed patches beyond 13,000 ft. Mawenzi peak is characterized by jagged rock and stupendous precipices, falling abruptly some 6,500 ft. to a vast ravine on its east side.

## Asian Mountain Systems
In Asia, a vast plateau covers an area as large as Tibet, encompassing Afghanistan, Baluchistan, Iran (Persia), Armenia, and provinces of Asia Minor. The eastern flank of this table-land consists of hills that generally do not exceed 4,000 or 5,000 ft., though some summits reach 10,000 ft. or more. To the south and southwest, a mountain scarp flanks the Mesopotamian plains and the Tigris, rising in many points to 10,000 ft. and extending through Kurdistan and Armenia.

In the province of Azerb[=a]ij[=a]n (Persia), the mountains of Sahand rise to 12,000 ft. The highest mountain in the province is Mount Savelan, reaching 15,792 ft., while the Talish Mountains reach 9,000 ft. In Central Asia, the river Ili rises on the western slopes of Mount Kash-katur at an altitude of 11,600 ft.

In the North-West Frontier Province of India, the district of Kohistan is separated by a mountain range over 16,000 ft. high. These mountains are rocky wastes and snow-bound from their crests down to 12,000 ft., with forests and grass extending down to 5,000 or 6,000 ft.

## North American Ranges
The Rocky Mountains are primarily composed of palaeozoic sediments from the Cambrian to the Carboniferous periods, with some Cretaceous areas containing coal. While the average height along the United States boundary is 8,000 ft., the range culminates in Canada. The highest peak in British Columbia is Mount Fairweather at 15,287 ft., and the highest peak in the Canadian Rockies is Mount Robson at 13,700 ft. Other notable peaks include Mount Columbia (12,740 ft.), Forbes (12,075 ft.), Assiniboine (11,860 ft.), Bryce (11,686 ft.), Temple (11,626 ft.), and Lyell (11,463 ft.). Various passes traverse the range, such as the South Kootenay or Boundary Pass (7,100 ft.) and the Crow's Nest Pass (5,500 ft.).

## East Asian Highlands
In the Philippine Islands, the highest land reaches 10,250 ft. Formosa is traversed by a high mountain range reaching nearly 13,000 ft. Similarly, the islands of Japan are crossed by a line of volcanic mountains, some of which are active, with the highest point reaching approximately 13,000 ft. above the sea.

## Religious Texts: The Mount
The Qur'an, in Chapter 52 (At-Tur), begins with an oath "By the Mount" (or "By the Mountain"). The text presents a vision of the end of times where the "doom of thy Lord" will occur, a day characterized by the firmament being in "dreadful commotion" and the mountains "flying hither and thither."
