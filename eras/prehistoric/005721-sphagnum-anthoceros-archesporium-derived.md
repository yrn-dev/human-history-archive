# Archesporium Development in Sphagnum and Anthoceros

The archesporium is the tissue from which spores are produced within the sporogonium of certain plants. In the study of bryophytes, the origin of this tissue varies significantly between different groups of mosses and liverworts.

## Development in Sphagnum and Anthoceros

In the genus *Sphagnum* (known as bog-mosses) and in the genus *Anthoceros* (a member of the Anthocerotales), the archesporium is derived from the amphithecium. The amphithecium is the peripheral layer of cells that is separated from the inner group of cells, known as the endothecium, by the first periclinal division in the region that forms the capsule.

This developmental path distinguishes *Sphagnum* and *Anthoceros* from other mosses. In all other mosses, the archesporium is derived from the outermost layer of the endothecium.

## Characteristics of Anthoceros

*Anthoceros* belongs to the Anthocerotales, a small group that also includes *Dendroceros* and *Notothylas*. The plants possess a dark green thallus composed of parenchymatous cells, typically with a single large chloroplast in each assimilating cell. 

The sporogonium of *Anthoceros* is characterized by a large bulbous foot and a long capsule that can reach a length of an inch and a half due to the activity of a zone of cells between the capsule and the foot. The capsule wall is several layers thick, containing chlorophyll and functional stomata, allowing it to perform assimilation. In the center of the capsule is a columella made of narrow elongated cells. The spores, which are mixed with elaters, are formed from a dome-shaped archesporium located between the columella and the capsule wall.

## Characteristics of Sphagnum

*Sphagnum* is a genus of mosses that occupies an isolated position and is frequently found in temperate and arctic climates, as well as high altitudes in the tropics. These plants often grow in large patches of reddish or pale green color and contribute significantly to the formation of peat.

The *Sphagnum* plant features a peculiar stem and leaf structure. The stem consists of a thin-walled central tissue surrounded by thick-walled cells and layers of large, dead, empty cells with spiral thickenings and round pores that conduct water via capillarity. The leaves lack a midrib and contain similar empty cells among narrow chlorophyll-containing cells.

The mature sporogonium of *Sphagnum* consists of a globular capsule and a wide foot, separated by a constriction. Unlike many other mosses, it has no distinct seta; instead, the capsule is raised on a pseudopodium, which is a leafless outgrowth of the branch. The capsule contains a short, wide columella and a dome-shaped spore-sac, with no air-space present between the spore-sac and the wall.

## Comparative Sporogonium Structure

Across most Bryales, the sporogonium generally follows a consistent plan, with the exception of *Archidium*. In most cases, a columella is present, and the cells derived from the archesporium produce spores. While most mosses develop the sporogonium via an apical cell that cuts off two rows of segments, *Sphagnum* is an exception to this specific developmental process.

In *Archidium*, the development differs further: it lacks a sterile columella and a distinct archesporium. Instead, a variable number of cells within the endothecium become spore-mother-cells, while others provide nourishment.
