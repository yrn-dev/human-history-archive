# The Lakes of Asia

The lakes of Asia are described as innumerable, exhibiting a vast range of sizes. These water bodies vary from highland lochs and the indefinitely extended swamps found in Persia to massive inland seas, such as Lake Balkash and Lake Baikal.

## Geological Origins and Characteristics
Many Asian lakes are situated at high elevations; Lake Victoria is cited as probably the most elevated, reaching 13,400 ft. These high-elevation lakes are considered undoubted vestiges of an ancient period of glaciation and generally show signs of a gradual decrease in size.

Other lakes are relics of earlier geological periods. These formed when land areas, recently upheaved from the sea, were spread at low levels and experienced alternating inundations of fresh and salt water. Typical examples of this type include the Helmund *hamuns* and Lop Nor. These lakes, along with all the plateau *hamuns* of Persia and south-west Baluchistan, are characterized by their instability, changing their extent and form from season to season. Many of these water bodies are impregnated with saline deposits originating from the underlying strata. In the Persian desert, the *kavirs* (salt depressions) are more frequently widespread deposits of salt and mud than areas covered by water.

## Historical and Ancient Geography
Ancient records and geological theories provide further context for the region's water systems. According to some historical outlines of the Fourth Ice Age (approximately 50,000 years ago), the geography of the world differed significantly. During this time, a great inland sea is believed to have spread across South Russia, extending from the present Black Sea far into Central Asia.

In ancient geography, specific lakes served as landmarks for regional boundaries. In Asia Minor, the Lydians extended northward at least as far as the Gygaean Lake (also known as Lake Coloe or modern Mermereh). The southern shore of this lake features the plateau of the Bin Bir Tepe, which served as the primary burial-place for the inhabitants of Sardis and contains the 260 ft. high "tomb of Alyattes."

Additionally, ancient geographers identified several lakes near the coast of the Propontis. These include the small lake Daskylitis and two larger lakes located further inland known as Apollonias (modern Abullionte) and Miletopolitis (modern Moniyas).

## Regional Water Systems and Flora
The distribution of aquatic life in Asia is linked to its water systems. The carp (*Cyprinus carpio*) is believed to be a native of temperate Asia (and possibly south-eastern Europe). This species inhabits slow-running rivers, ponds, canals, and lakes with abundant vegetation. Through human agency, the carp was introduced to other regions, including the Malay Archipelago and Indo-China.
