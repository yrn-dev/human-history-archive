# Geology of Baluchistan and the Makran Coast

The geological structure of Baluchistan is characterized by a complex system of mountain ranges and coastal deposits. The region's mountain ranges are primarily composed of Tertiary and Cretaceous beds, which have been thrown into a series of folds. These folds run approximately parallel to the mountain ridges and are part of an extensive system that resembles a festoon hanging southwards between Mount Ararat and Peshawar. This system includes a subsidiary festoon formed by the Bugti Hills and the Suliman Hills, where the outer folds are looped up at Sibi.

## Coastal and Interior Deposits
Outside of these folded mountain systems lie the horizontal deposits of the Makran coast. Conversely, the stony desert of north-western Baluchistan is located within the folds. In the broader depressions found between the mountain ridges, the beds are reported to be only slightly disturbed.

## Rock Composition and Age
While Cretaceous and Tertiary beds are the primary components of the mountain ranges, Jurassic rocks also play a considerable part in the formation of the hills of British Baluchistan. Additionally, *Fusulina* limestone and Triassic beds have been identified along the south side of the upper Zhob.

With the exception of the later Tertiary beds, the deposits in the region are mostly marine. However, in north-western Baluchistan, there is an extensive development of conglomerates and volcanic tuffs within the lower Tertiary and upper Cretaceous. These are likely contemporaneous with the Deccan Traps of India.

## Volcanic Activity and Intrusions
The region has experienced significant volcanic and intrusive activity:
* **Intrusions:** During the Tertiary period, great masses of diorite and syenite were intruded.
* **Volcanic Cones:** A line of recent volcanic cones stretches from eastern Persia into western Baluchistan, located within the curve of the folded belt. These cones are composed mostly of ashes and lavas that are andesitic. While these volcanoes appear extinct in Baluchistan, the Koh-i-Tafdan beyond the Persian frontier continues to emit vapours at frequent intervals.
* **Mud Volcanoes:** Mud "volcanoes" are present on the Makran coast, though it is considered doubtful if these are connected to true volcanic agencies.

## Regional Geological Context
Baluchistan is part of a broad belt of crumpling that extends from west to east in a series of arcs, forming the principal mountain ranges of central Asia. This belt includes Afghanistan, Persia, Asia Minor, the Tian-shan, the Himalayas, and the Burmese ranges. 

This folded belt is situated between two more rigid masses: a northern mass (including much of Siberia, Mongolia, and northern China) and a southern mass (including the peninsula of India, Arabia, and Syria). In the southern mass, the Mesozoic, Upper Palaeozoic, and Tertiary beds lie almost horizontally upon the oldest rocks, as the region is characterized by faulting or quiescence rather than folding. The elevation of the ranges in the folded belt, such as the Himalayas, is attributed to an overthrust from the north, where the high land of central Asia was pushed against the unyielding old rocks of the Indian peninsula.
