# Entstehung der Ostsee and Scandinavian Hydrographic Research

## Overview of Research
The study of the formation of the Baltic Sea (*Die Entstehung der Ostsee*) and the geological changes in Scandinavia has been documented in several specialized works during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Research in this field encompasses the study of level changes, shoreline shifts, and the broader climatic and hydrographic conditions of the region.

## Key Publications and Authors
Several authors have contributed to the understanding of the Baltic region's development. Credner published *Die Entstehung der Ostsee* in Leipzig in 1895. Complementing this work is the research of G. de Geer, who wrote *Om Skandinaviens nivaforandringar under quartarperioden*, published in Stockholm in 1888. 

Further analysis of the region's physical changes is found in the work of R. Sieger, titled *Seeenschwankungen und Strandverschiebungen in Skandinavien*, published in Berlin in 1893. Additionally, O. Pettersson provided a "Review of Swedish Hydrographic Research" in the *Scottish Geographical Magazine* in 1894.

## Geological and Climatic Context
The broader geological and historical context of these changes is addressed in the work of N. Ekholm, *Om klimatets andringar i geologisk och historisk tid*, published in *Ymer* (Stockholm, 1899). The archive also notes that references to these subjects can be found in the standard works of authors such as A. Penck, A. de Lapparent, and E. Suess.

## Institutional Contributions
Ongoing study of the sea is supported by the *Publications of the International Council for the Study of the Sea*, which has been published in Copenhagen since 1902.
