# Geological Region South and East of the Palaeozoic Plateau

The region situated to the south and east of the Palaeozoic plateau is an extensive area characterized primarily by Archean rocks. This area encompasses a significant portion of Mongolia located north of the Tian-shan.

## Geological Composition and Structure
The region is notably devoid of marine beds from the Tertiary or Mesozoic ages. However, plant-bearing deposits associated with the Angara series have been identified here. Structurally, the folds within this region are of an ancient date. The landscape is characterized by a series of depressions created by faults; the strips of land between these depressions, which did not sink to the same extent, currently form mountain ranges.

## Regional Variations in China
In the Chinese provinces of Shensi and Shansi, the geological succession shares similarities with the Siberian Palaeozoic plateau, though the sequence in these provinces is more complete. The structure consists of a floor of folded Archean rocks. These are overlaid by strata of Lower Palaeozoic age that are nearly horizontal. Following these are marine beds from the Carboniferous period. Beyond the Upper Carboniferous, no further marine deposits are known, and plant-bearing beds are present, mirroring the conditions found in Siberia.

Southern China differs significantly in its structure, as it is largely composed of folded mountain chains. Despite this structural difference, the geological succession remains very similar to the aforementioned areas. Except for regions near the Burmese and Tibetan borders, there are no marine deposits of Tertiary or Mesozoic age in Southern China.

## Broader Continental Context
This region is part of a broad area stretching from the Arctic Ocean to the south of China. In this vast expanse, no marine deposits later than the Carboniferous have been discovered, with the exception of the extreme north. While freshwater and terrestrial deposits of Mesozoic age are found in various locations, the evidence suggests that the majority of this area has remained land since the close of the Palaeozoic era.

The limits of this land mass are indicated by several geological markers:
* **To the east:** Marine Mesozoic deposits are found in Japan.
* **To the west:** Marine Mesozoic deposits are found in the western Tian-shan.
* **To the south:** Marine Mesozoic deposits are found in Tibet.
* **To the north:** Triassic deposits of the Verkhoyansk Range indicate that this specific land mass did not extend to the Bering Sea.
