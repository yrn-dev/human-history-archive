# Rhinoceros, Hippopotamus, &c., discovered in a cave at Kirkdale

The discovery of fossilized remains of large mammals in a cave at Kirkdale, Yorkshire, represents a significant contribution to the fields of Geology and Palaeontology. These findings were formally documented by Dr. Buckland in 1821 in a paper titled "Account of an Assemblage of Fossil Teeth and Bones of Elephant, Rhinoceros, Hippopotamus, &c., discovered in a cave at Kirkdale in Yorkshire in the year 1821," published in *Phil. Trans.*

## Scientific Analysis and Publications
Dr. Buckland utilized the remains discovered at Kirkdale as a foundation for further scientific inquiry. In 1823, he published his first major work, *Reliquiae Diluvianae, or Observations on the Organic Remains contained in caves, fissures, and diluvial gravel attesting the Action of a Universal Deluge* (with a second edition following in 1824). In this text, Buckland supplemented his earlier observations regarding the extinct animals found in the Kirkdale cavern to expound upon his views concerning the Biblical account of the Deluge.

## Comparative Palaeontological Findings
The presence of rhinoceros and hippopotamus remains in Kirkdale is mirrored in other prehistoric sites across England and Wales, suggesting a broader distribution of these animals in the past.

### English Sites
In Devon, Kent's Cavern (also known as Kent's Hole) contains red earth strata holding the bones of the rhinoceros, mammoth, lion, and hyaena. Nearby, the Brixham cavern yielded bones of the rhinoceros, elephant, lion, horse, hyena, and bear, along with various small rodents and birds. In Sussex, gravel pits at Piltdown—associated with a deposit ascribed to the Third Interglacial period—contained teeth of the hippopotamus and rhinoceros, as well as an elephant bone instrument.

### Welsh Sites
In the county formed by an act of Henry VIII from various lordships including Denbigh and Ruthin, limestone caves at Cefn in the Elwy Valley have been found to hold prehistoric remains of the rhinoceros, hippopotamus, elephant, lion, hyena, bear, and reindeer.

## Faunal Transitions and Environmental Context
The presence of these animals in the European plain shifted over time due to climatic changes. Initially, the fauna included elephants, rhinoceroses, and hippopotami. However, as the air chilled, warmth-loving creatures, specifically the hippopotamus, ceased to migrate so far north. This transition led to the prevalence of arctic-type animals, such as the woolly rhinoceros, woolly mammoth, bison, aurochs, musk ox, and reindeer.

In contemporary tropical regions, the rhinoceros remains particularly abundant and dangerous in many parts, while the hippopotamus is listed among the numerous large mammals found on drier steppes.

## Other Uses of Rhinoceros and Hippopotamus Materials
Beyond palaeontological study, the hides of these animals have been used for the construction of the kurbash (or kourbash). Derived from the Arabic *qurbash*, this is a whip or strap approximately one yard in length. It has been used as an instrument of torture and punishment in various Mahommedan countries, particularly within the Turkish empire, to collect taxes, maintain authority, or pervert justice. While such use was once common in Egypt, it was abolished by British authorities.
