# Liverworts in Longitudinal Section

The study of liverworts through longitudinal sections of their young sporogonia reveals the internal organization of the embryos and the specific cells responsible for the production of reproductive tissues. These sections highlight the diversity in development across the three primary groups of liverworts: the Marchantiales, the Jungermanniales, and the Anthocerotales.

## Sporogenous Tissue and Archesporial Cells
In longitudinal sections of young liverwort embryos, specific cells are identified as archesporial cells. These are the cells from which both the spores and the elaters will eventually arise. In semi-diagrammatic figures of these sections, these cells are typically shaded to distinguish them from other tissues. The distribution and behavior of these cells vary by species, as seen in examples such as *Riccia*, *Marchantia polymorpha*, *Pellia epiphylla*, *Anthoceros laevis*, *Cephalozia bicuspidata*, and *Radula complanata*.

## Development in the Marchantiales
The Marchantiales consist of thalloid forms with specialized thallus structures. Within this group, the development of the sporogonium varies significantly:

*   ***Riccia***: This genus possesses the simplest known sporogonia. In a longitudinal section, the entire mass of cells derived from the ovum forms a spherical capsule. The only sterile tissue present is a single layer of peripheral cells that constitutes the wall.
*   **Higher Marchantiales**: In more advanced forms, such as *Marchantia polymorpha*, the embryo is separated by a first transverse wall. These higher forms feature a sterile seta and foot, and they produce elaters along with the spores.

## Development in Other Liverwort Groups
The other two major groups exhibit different organizational patterns in their sporogonia:

*   **Anthocerotales**: This distinct group features a gametophyte that is a thallus. Its sporogonium is characterized by a sterile columella and the capacity for continued spore production and growth over a long period.
*   **Jungermanniales**: This group shows a progression from thalloid forms toward plants differentiated into stems and leaves. Even in the simplest forms of this series, the sporogonium possesses a sterile foot. The origin of elaters from sterile cells can be traced within this group.

## Comparative Morphology and Propagation
While the general life history of liverworts resembles that of *Pellia*, the differentiation between the three groups has proceeded along divergent lines. Beyond the sporogonium, liverworts exhibit various methods of vegetative propagation. Some species, such as *Aneura*, produce bi-cellular gemmae from the contents of superficial cells. In *Blasia* and *Marchantia*, gemmae are formed within cup-shaped or tubular receptacles and are expelled by the swelling of mucilage secreted by special hairs.

In contrast to the rhizoids of mosses (such as *Funaria hygrometrica*), which are branched and composed of rows of elongated cells, liverwort rhizoids differ in structure. Additionally, the stem of a moss is more complicated than that of any liverwort, featuring a central strand of narrow cells for water conduction.
