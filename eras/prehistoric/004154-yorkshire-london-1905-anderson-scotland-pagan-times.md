# Yorkshire and Related Historical Figures

## Regional Geography and Industry
Yorkshire is a region in England containing various towns and estates. In the West Riding of Yorkshire, the market town and municipal borough of Barnsley (also known as Black or Bleak Barnsley) is situated on rising ground west of the river Dearne, approximately 15 miles north of Sheffield. As of 1901, Barnsley had a population of 41,086.

The town is a center for several industries, most notably the weaving of other cloth and linen, as well as the manufacture of steel and iron. Other industrial activities include the operation of dyeworks, printfields, bleachfields, sawmills, cornmills, and malt-houses, along with the production of wire, needles, and glass. The area is characterized by large coalfields that extend beneath the town, with coke and coal being exported to Hull and London. Barnsley is connected via the Aire and Calder Navigation system of canals and several railways, including the Great Northern, Hull & Barnsley, Lancashire & Yorkshire, Great Central, and Midland lines.

## Notable Institutions and Landmarks
Barnsley's local institutions include a free library, a public hall, a court house, and several churches. The parish church of St Mary, built in 1821, is the oldest and occupies an early site. Educational facilities include a free grammar school dating from 1665, and a philosophical society established in 1828. In 1905, a monument was erected to honor prominent members of the Yorkshire Miners' Association. The town's park was provided in 1862 by the widow of Joseph Locke, M.P.

Historical sites in the vicinity of Barnsley include:
* **Monk Bretton Priory**: A Cluniac foundation from 1157 that retains fragments of its church, Decorated domestic remains, and a Perpendicular gatehouse.
* **Wentworth Castle**: Built in 1730 by Thomas, earl of Strafford, this site is located in a beautiful park and houses a collection of historical portraits.

## Historical Figures from Yorkshire
Several prominent individuals are associated with Yorkshire estates and locations:

**Thomas Darcy, Baron Darcy (1467-1537)**
A member of a family seated at Templehurst in Yorkshire, Darcy was an English soldier and diplomat. He served as the captain of Berwick and was created Baron Darcy in 1505, at which time he became the warden of the east marches toward Scotland. He held various important offices in the north of England and served on the royal council, splitting his time between London and the north. 

Darcy's later years were marked by conflict with Henry VIII due to his opposition to the separation from Rome and his belief that spiritual power should decide matrimonial cases. After being detained in London, he returned to Yorkshire in late 1535. During the Pilgrimage of Grace, he initially defended Pontefract Castle before surrendering it to the rebels and joining them at Doncaster as a leader. He was eventually seized and taken to London on suspicion of fresh treasons.

**Henry Stewart, Lord Darnley (1545-1567)**
Born at Temple Newsam in Yorkshire on December 7, 1545, Lord Darnley was the eldest son of Matthew Stewart and Lady Margaret Douglas. Educated in England, he was noted for his exceptional skill in military exercises. He later became the second husband of Mary, queen of Scots, marrying her at Holyrood on July 29, 1565.
