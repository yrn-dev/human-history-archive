# Asiatic Buffalo

The Asiatic buffalo, scientifically known as *Bos (Bubalus) bubalis*, is a species primarily restricted in its wild state to Ceylon and India. While these are its primary regions, local races of the species are likely represented by buffaloes found in the Islands and the Malay Peninsula. Beyond its native range, the species has been introduced to other regions, including Italy, Egypt, and Asia Minor.

## Physical Characteristics and Distinctions
The Asiatic buffalo possesses several distinct physical markers that differentiate it from South African species. These include a head that is more narrow and elongated, ears that are less thickly fringed, and horns characterized by their large size and wide separation. Additionally, all Asiatic buffaloes are distinguished from African forms by the direction of the hair on the fore-part of the back, which is directed forwards. This specific trait has led some to suggest that the Asiatic buffaloes should be classified as a separate genus, *Buffelus*, or a distinct subgenus.

Within the region of Assam, two specific local races have been identified:
*   ***B. bubalis macrocercus***: This race is characterized by horns of immense size that are directed mainly outwards, rather than curving upwards in a circular fashion.
*   ***B. bubalis fulvus***: This race is distinguished by the color of its hide and hair, which is tawny instead of black.

## Habitat and Social Behavior
The typical haunts of the Indian buffalo are swamps and grass-jungles. In these environments, the grass can exceed 20 feet in height. Similar to the Indian rhinoceros, buffaloes create covered pathways within this tall grass, allowing them to remain completely concealed. 

They are social animals, often found in herds consisting of fifty or more individuals. They are particularly fond of marshes, where they spend their days wallowing in mud. They are described as not being shy and are known to cause significant damage to crops.

## Life Cycle and Combat
The rutting season for the species occurs during the autumn. During this time, a single male is followed by several females, forming a small temporary herd. The gestation period lasts for ten months, resulting in the birth of one or two calves.

The bull is a powerful animal, said to be capable of overthrowing an elephant. It is generally more than a match for the tiger; tigers typically avoid combat with the buffalo unless driven by hunger. This strength extends to tame herds as well; drivers of tame buffaloes do not fear entering jungles frequented by tigers, as the massive horns of the cattle can quickly dispatch any tiger that crosses their path. Historically, fights between buffaloes, as well as fights between buffaloes and tigers, were recognized.
