# The Invisible Hand: Raw Material Procurement and the Logistics of Lithics

While previous installments in this series have focused on the *morphology* of tools—the transition from the crude Oldowan choppers to the sophisticated Mousterian flakes—we have largely ignored the "where" and "how" of the stone itself. The evolution of Stone Age technology was not merely a cognitive leap in shaping stone; it was a revolution in logistics, geography, and the strategic procurement of raw materials.

The ability to identify, transport, and curate specific types of stone reveals as much about prehistoric intelligence as the tools themselves. The shift from opportunistic scavenging of river cobbles to the intentional mining of high-quality flint or obsidian marks a fundamental change in how early hominins interacted with their environment.

## From Opportunism to Strategy

In the Oldowan tradition (approx. 2.6 to 1.7 million years ago), tool-making was largely opportunistic. Hominins typically utilized whatever water-worn pebbles were available in their immediate vicinity. The "cost" of transport was low, and the tools were often discarded shortly after use. The focus was on immediate utility rather than long-term planning.

As we transition into the Acheulean and eventually the Mousterian periods, we see the emergence of "raw material selectivity." Early humans began to bypass mediocre stones in favor of cryptocrystalline silicates—such as chert, flint, and obsidian—which fracture predictably (conchoidal fracture). This indicates a mental map of the landscape; the tool-maker was no longer looking at the ground beneath their feet, but planning a journey to a known quarry.

## The Logistics of Transport and Curation

The Mousterian industry (associated largely with Neanderthals) demonstrates a sophisticated understanding of resource management. Archaeologists have discovered "curated" toolkits—stones transported over dozens of kilometers from their original source. 

This shift suggests a transition from "expedient" technology to "curated" technology. When a hominin carries a high-quality core over a long distance, they are investing in the future. They are calculating the risk of the journey against the reward of a sharper, more durable edge. There is ongoing scholarly debate regarding the extent to which these materials were traded between different groups, or if the transport was strictly a result of migratory patterns. However, the presence of non-local stone in various sites strongly suggests a complex relationship with geography.

## Key Facts
* **Conchoidal Fracture:** The specific way certain stones (like obsidian) break, allowing for the creation of razor-sharp edges.
* **Lithic Procurement:** The process of searching for and collecting specific raw materials for tool production.
* **Expedient Tools:** Tools made from local materials for immediate use and then discarded.
* **Curated Tools:** High-quality tools designed for longevity and transported across distances.
* **Raw Material Selectivity:** The cognitive ability to reject inferior stone in favor of superior materials found elsewhere.

## Did You Know?
* **Obsidian's Value:** Obsidian is volcanic glass and can be flaked to an edge thinner than a modern steel scalpel.
* **The "Core" Concept:** In the Mousterian Levallois technique, the stone was carefully prepared as a "core" so that a single, perfect flake could be struck off with one blow.
* **Geological Fingerprinting:** Modern historians use "sourcing" (chemical analysis) to trace a stone tool back to the exact quarry where it was mined thousands of years ago.
