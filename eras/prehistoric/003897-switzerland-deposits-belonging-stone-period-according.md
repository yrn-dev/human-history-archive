# Switzerland: Stone Period Deposits

The archaeological record of Switzerland, particularly within its lake dwellings, provides significant evidence regarding the diet and agricultural practices of humans during the Stone Period. These deposits have allowed researchers to identify the specific types of grains cultivated and consumed by early inhabitants.

## Agricultural Evidence and Cereal Grains
Deposits from the Stone Period in Swiss lake dwellings have revealed that barley was a primary aliment. According to Professor Heer, three distinct varieties of barley have been identified in these sites:
*   The common two-rowed variety (*H. distichum*).
*   The large six-rowed variety (*H. hexastichum*, var. *densum*).
*   The small six-rowed variety (*H. hexastichum*, var. *sanctum*).

Of these, the small six-rowed variety is the most commonly found and is considered the most ancient. This specific variety is known as the sacred barley of antiquity, which appears in ancient coins and is frequently depicted as plaited in the hair of the goddess Ceres.

In addition to barley, lake dwellers in Switzerland sowed patches of wheat. This practice is described as following an immemorial human tradition, with the seeds having been brought over generations from a distant center of diffusion located in the eastern Mediterranean region.

## Diet and Food Processing
Beyond the cultivation of wheat and barley, the inhabitants of the Swiss lake dwellings consumed peas and crab-apples. The crab-apple is noted as the only type of apple that existed in the world at that time, as modern apples had not yet been produced through selection and cultivation.

The methods used to process these foods were rudimentary. Evidence indicates that grains were roasted and ground between stones before being stored in pots. The resulting bread was exceedingly heavy and solid, as evidenced by round flat slabs recovered from deposits. There is no evidence that these people used yeast, which suggests they did not produce fermented drinks.

## Geological Context
While the Stone Period deposits provide insight into human activity, the broader geological landscape of Switzerland includes various mineral formations. In the Binnenthal near Brieg, water-clear crystals of dolomite are found within white crystalline dolomite-rock. These crystals also appear in magnesian silicate rocks, such as talc-schist, serpentine, and other deposits. Additionally, a columnar variety of dolomite known as taraspite is found in Tarasp, Switzerland.
