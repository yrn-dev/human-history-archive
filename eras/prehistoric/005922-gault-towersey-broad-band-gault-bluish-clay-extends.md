# Gault

The Gault is a geological formation characterized as a stiff, greyish calcareous clay. It is situated beneath the white chalk and is noted for the excellent preservation of its fossils. Historically, the formation was referred to by the geologist William Smith as "the blue marl."

## Regional Distribution and Characteristics

In certain regions, the Gault appears as a bluish clay. In one instance, a broad band of this bluish clay extends from Towersey across the county in a north-easterly direction. In other areas, such as the eastern part of a county where Eocene beds of the Hampshire Basin are present, the Gault (along with the Upper Greensand) crops out from beneath the Chalk as a narrow, irregular band. In these districts, the Gault clay is only distinguishable in the northern and southern areas.

The Gault also outcrops along the base of the escarpment of the North and South Downs. In the vale of Aylesbury, the Gault is located alongside the Kimmeridge Clay and Oxford Clay.

## Geological Relationships

The Gault is described as an argillaceous type of the Upper Greensand. It typically rests above the Lower Greensand, and the two are usually clearly marked off from one another. The Upper Greensand rests upon the Gault; the junction of these two formations is a site where numerous springs arise.

In areas where the Gault and chalk approach each other, concretions of flints and iron pyrites are found together.

## Economic and Practical Uses

The Gault formation has provided various materials for industrial and agricultural use:

*   **Mineral Extraction:** Phosphatic nodules have been worked from the Gault at Towersey.
*   **Agriculture:** In Kent, soils formed by admixtures of clay from the Gault, along with Greensand, Chalk, loam, and clay-loams, are used to produce "Bastard East Kents" hops.
*   **Construction:** Gault brickwork is utilized in building. In tests conducted between 1896 and 1897 by the Royal Institute of British Architects, Gault brickwork in lime mortar crushed under a pressure of 31.14 tons per sq. ft., while Gault brickwork in cement mortar crushed at 51.34 tons per sq. ft.
