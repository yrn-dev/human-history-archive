# Jungermanniaceae Acrogynae

The Jungermanniaceae Acrogynae are a group of liverworts characterized by a foliose plant structure. They are part of the larger series known as Jungermanniales, which is divided into two main groups based on whether the formation of archegonia utilizes the apex or terminates the growth of the branch. In the Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae, the archegonia occupy the ends of the branches or the main shoot.

## Vegetative Structure and Growth
The plant consists of leafy shoots. In the majority of these liverworts, the stem grows via a pyramidal apical cell that cuts off three rows of segments, with each segment giving rise to a leaf. The leaves are typically arranged in two lateral rows and a ventral row, known as amphigastria; however, the amphigastria are often differently shaped and smaller than the lateral leaves, and in some genera, they are entirely absent.

The insertion of the leaves on the stem varies. In some instances, leaves retain a transverse insertion with two equally developed lobes. More commonly, they are obliquely inserted, meaning the edge of each leaf lies over or under the edge of the leaf preceding it. The development of the two lobes is often unequal:
* In *Radula*, *Poretta*, and the *Lejeuneae*, the lower lobe is the smaller of the two.
* In *Scapania*, the upper lobe is the smaller.

The folding of one lobe against another serves to assist the plant in retaining water. In several genera, particularly epiphytic forms such as *Pleurozia*, *Lepidolaena*, and *Frullania*, pitcher-like structures have developed. In *Trichocolea*, the leaves are finely divided and, combined with hair-like paraphyllia, create a loose weft around the stem. Branches emerge by the side of and below the leaves, while rhizoids originate from the bases of the leaves or the lower surface of the stem.

## Sexual Generation and Reproduction
The sexual organs of the Jungermanniaceae Acrogynae may be located on distinct individuals or on the same individual. The antheridia are typically long-stalked and globular, and they are protected by leaves that are frequently modified in shape.

The archegonia are positioned at the apex of a lateral branch or the main stem. While a single archegonium may arise from the apical cell (as seen in *Lejeunea*), it is more common for multiple archegonia to form from surrounding segments. The primary protection for the archegonia is a tubular perianth—a coherent whorl of leaves that grows independently of fertilization. This perianth also protects the sporogonium during its development.

In certain forms, the end of the stem bearing the sporogonium grows downward to create a hollow tubular sac, or the base of the sporogonium bores into the thickened stem end to form a marsupial sac. This sac usually penetrates the soil and possesses rhizoids on its outer surface; British examples of this include *Saccogyna*, *Calypogeia*, and *Kantia*.

## Spore Development and Germination
At maturity, the seta elongates rapidly, and the capsule opens by splitting into four valves (or halves), which allows the spores and elaters to escape. In the Jubuloideae group, the seta is short, and the elaters remain fixed to the valves at dehiscence, extending from the base to the upper part of the capsule.

The germination of the spore typically results in a short filament. However, in genera such as *Lejeunea* and *Radula*, a flat plate of cells is first formed, growing by a two-sided apical cell. In some tropical forms, the pro-embryonic stage is prolonged, and leafy shoots only arise in connection with the sexual organs. Examples include *Lejeunea Metzgeriopsis*, which grows on the leaves of living plants and forms a flat branched thallus applied to the substratum, and *Protocephalozia*, which grows on bare earth in South America and possesses a filamentous pro-embryo.

## Distribution and Diversity
The general plan of construction is very similar across the majority of existing liverworts in this group. In Britain, there are numerous species distributed across thirty-nine genera.
