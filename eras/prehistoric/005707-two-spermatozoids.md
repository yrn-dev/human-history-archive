# Two Spermatozoids

In the study of botanical structures, specifically within the context of certain plant species such as *Marchantia polymorpha*, the spermatozoid is a component of the sexual reproductive process. In the anatomical illustrations of *Marchantia polymorpha*, the spermatozoids are depicted as distinct entities.

## Reproductive Organs and Function
The sexual organs of these plants are consistently located on the morphologically upper surface of the thallus. In the case of *Marchantia*, the antheridia—the organs associated with the production of spermatozoids—are grouped on special stalked, disk-shaped branch-systems known as antheridiophores. These individual antheridia are situated within depressions. From these depressions, the spermatozoids are, in some instances, forcibly ejected.

To ensure successful reproduction, the plant possesses adaptations to protect its sexual organs and to retain water in the vicinity of the archegonia. This retention of water is essential to enable the spermatozoid to reach the ovum.

## Related Botanical Structures
The reproductive cycle involves several specialized structures beyond the antheridia. In *Marchantia* and *Fimbriaria*, a structure called the perianth grows around each fertilized archegonium. Furthermore, the sporogonium in the Marchantiaceae is raised on a special archegoniophore. Within the sporogonium, elaters serve as lines of conduction for plastic material to developing spores and later assist in the dispersal of those spores.

## Vegetative Propagation
Aside from sexual reproduction involving spermatozoids, vegetative propagation is widely spread in these plants. Small fragments of the gametophyte can grow into new individuals. This can occur through the separation of branches via the decay of older portions or through special gemmae. In *Marchantia* and *Blasia*, gemmae are formed within cup-shaped or tubular receptacles and are forced out by the swelling of mucilage secreted by special hairs. These gemmae may consist of single cells, two cells, or numerous cells.
