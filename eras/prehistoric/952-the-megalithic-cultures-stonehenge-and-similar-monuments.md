# Sentinels of Stone: The Megalithic Traditions of Prehistory

## Overview
Across the landscapes of Western Europe, from the rugged coasts of Brittany to the rolling hills of Salisbury Plain, stand the remnants of a profound prehistoric ambition: the megaliths. Derived from the Greek words *megas* (great) and *lithos* (stone), these monuments represent some of the earliest evidence of complex social organization and spiritual architecture in human history.

While Stonehenge remains the most iconic example, it was part of a broader global phenomenon. These cultures—predominantly Neolithic farmers and early Bronze Age societies—invested immense collective labor to transport massive sarsen and bluestones over dozens of miles. These structures generally fall into three categories: **Menhirs** (single standing stones), **Dolmens** (portal tombs), and **Henges** (circular earthworks often containing stone circles).

The purpose of these sites remains a subject of scholarly debate. However, archaeological evidence suggests they served as multi-functional hubs: celestial observatories for tracking solstices, territorial markers, and sacred necropolises where the living communed with the ancestral dead.

## Timeline of Megalithic Construction

| Period | Approximate Dates | Key Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Neolithic** | 4500 – 3500 BCE | Rise of passage graves and simple dolmens (e.g., Carnac stones). |

| **Late Neolithic/Early Bronze Age** | 2500 – 1500 BCE | Peak of Stonehenge's architectural evolution; transition to individual burials. |

## Key Facts

*   **Engineering Marvels:** The stones at Stonehenge were not moved by magic, but by sophisticated prehistoric engineering. Sarsen stones (weighing up to 30 tons) were likely moved on wooden sledges over lubricated tracks, while the smaller bluestones were transported from the Preseli Hills in Wales—a journey of roughly 140 miles.
*   **Astronomical Alignment:** Many megalithic sites are precision-aligned with solar events. At Stonehenge, the axis of the monument aligns with the sunrise of the summer solstice and the sunset of the winter solstice, suggesting the sites functioned as prehistoric calendars to regulate farming and ritual cycles.
*   **Social Stratification:** The sheer scale of these projects implies a hierarchical society. To quarry, transport, and erect these stones required a centralized authority capable of feeding and organizing hundreds of workers, signaling a shift from nomadic bands to settled, organized chiefdoms.
*   **Diverse Forms:** Beyond Britain, sites like *Newgrange* in Ireland showcase the sophistication of the era; its inner chamber is illuminated only once a year during the winter solstice, proving that the "science" of light and stone was widespread.

## Did You Know?

1. **Acoustic Design:** Recent studies suggest that stone circles may have been designed for acoustics, creating an "echo chamber" effect that amplified the voices of priests or musicians during rituals.
2. **Not Just Stone:** While we call them "megaliths," many of these sites originally featured massive wooden posts and elaborate timber circles that have since rotted away, leaving only the stone skeletons behind.
3. **Global Reach:** While most famous in Europe, megalithic traditions also appeared independently in Asia (Korea and Japan) and Africa, proving that the urge to mark the earth with permanent stone is a universal human impulse.
