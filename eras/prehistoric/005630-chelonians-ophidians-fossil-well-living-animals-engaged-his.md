# Chelonians and Ophidians

In the study of natural history and the classification of animals, the terms Chelonians and Ophidians refer to specific orders of reptiles. The systematic division of reptiles into four distinct orders—Saurians, Batrachians, Chelonians, and Ophidians—is attributed to the work of Alexandre Brongniart.

## Alexandre Brongniart
Alexandre Brongniart (1770–1847) was a French geologist and mineralogist who contributed significantly to the scientific understanding of the natural world. Born in Paris on February 5, 1770, he was the son of the architect who designed other public buildings in Paris as well as the Bourse. Brongniart's early academic background included the study of chemistry under Lavoisier, honors at the École de Médecine, and studies at the École des Mines. 

Before his later scientific achievements, Brongniart served as a *pharmacien* in the army of the Pyrenees. Following a period of imprisonment due to a slight political offence, he was appointed as a professor of natural history at the Collège des Quatre Nations. He eventually succeeded Haüy as the professor of mineralogy at the Museum of Natural History. While he is perhaps most famous for his work as the director of the Sèvres porcelain factory—where he established the foundations of ceramic chemistry and authored the classic *Traité des arts céramiques* (1844)—he maintained a rigorous scientific practice in geology and zoology.

## Classification and Study of Reptiles
Brongniart's contributions to zoology include the organization of reptiles into four orders:
*   Saurians
*   Batrachians
*   Chelonians
*   Ophidians

His scientific inquiry was not limited to living specimens; he engaged his attention with both living and fossil animals. Through his studies of the strata surrounding Paris, Brongniart was also instrumental in the establishment of the Tertiary formations.

## Fossil Evidence of Chelonians
The existence of chelonians is evidenced in the geological record. In England, specifically within the Kimeridgian formation, chelonian remains have been discovered. This formation is characterized by various lithological features, including dark bituminous shales, clays, and paper shales. In the south of England, the Upper Series of the Kimeridgian reaches a thickness of 600-650 feet, while the Lower Series reaches a maximum of 400 feet.

Chelonian remains are found in these beds alongside other prehistoric fauna. These include crocodilian remains and a variety of large dinosaurian reptiles, such as *Megalosaurus*, *Gigantosaurus*, and *Cetiosaurus*, as well as ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs. Other characteristic fossils found in these same formations include various ammonites (such as *Cardioceras alternans* and *Reineckia eudoxus*), *Exogyra virgula*, and *Protocardia striatula*.
