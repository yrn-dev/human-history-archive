# Argentina and Brazil

Argentina and Brazil are two major nations of South America. Their geological, biological, and administrative histories are characterized by shared regional features and distinct national developments.

## Geology and Stratigraphy
The geological records of Argentina and Brazil include several significant formations. Strata from the Eocene period occur in both countries as part of the Patagonia-Megellanian series. Additionally, both nations contain a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones known as the *formacion petrolifera*. In the eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes, Palaeozoic beds are directly overlaid by a series of red sandstones and gypsiferous marls similar to this *formacion petrolifera* found in Argentina and Brazil; these deposits frequently feature a tuff of porphyritic rocks or a conglomerate at their base.

Furthermore, rocks from the Bajocian stage, as recognized by their fossil contents, are present in both Argentina and Brazil, as well as in Chile and Bolivia.

## Fauna and Natural History
The indigenous fauna of the region is closely linked across national borders. The fauna of Bolivia corresponds closely to that of Brazil and Argentina. Shared species across these neighboring districts include the puma, jaguar, wildcat, coati, tapir (also known as *anta*), sloth, ant-bear, paca (*Coelogenys paca*), and capybara. Other widely distributed animals in the region include the armadillo, opossum, ferret, and skunk. In the colder plateau regions and northern Argentina, the chinchilla (*C. laniger*) is found and is valued for its fur.

## Cartography and Geographic Surveys
The approach to scientific mapping has differed between the two nations. In Brazil, the central government has performed little to nothing regarding this effort. However, the states of Mines Gerães and São Paulo have employed *commissãos geographicos e geologicos* to produce topographical maps. Brazil has also benefited from river surveys of the Rio Madeira (1860-1875) and the Amazonas, conducted by W. Chandless (1862-1869) and Azevedo and Pinto (1862-1864). Despite a 1876 proposal by a committee presided over by Marshal H. de Beaurepaire-Rohan to create a map on a scale of 1:200,000, the proposal was never acted upon. Consequently, researchers have relied on general atlases or the *Atlas do imperio do Brazil* by Mendes de Almeida (1868).

Argentina established an official geographical institute in 1879. However, the institute's publications—including H. Hoskold's *Mapa topografica* (1895) and A. Seelstrang's *Atlas* (1886-1892)—as well as various provincial maps, are not based upon scientific surveys.

## Demographics and Regional Interaction
Argentina and Brazil are noted for having white populations that increase in percentage, a trend not mirrored in neighboring Bolivia. 

Regional interactions are evident in border towns such as Bage, a municipality in the Brazilian state of Rio Grande do Sul. Located 25 miles from the Uruguay frontier and 176 miles west-north-west of the city of Rio Grande do Sul, Bage serves as a commercial center for a district dominated by pastoral occupations. Due to its strategic location, Bage has held military importance since colonial times. It was captured in 1827 by the Argentine general Lavalle and played a prominent role in most of the civil wars of Argentina.
