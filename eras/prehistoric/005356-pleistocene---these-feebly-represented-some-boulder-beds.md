# Pleistocene

The Pleistocene is a geological period characterized in various regions by glacial activity, the deposition of boulder beds, and specific fossil remains. Its representation varies significantly across different global geographies, ranging from high mountain slopes to lowland deposits.

## African Geological Evidence
In British East Africa, the Pleistocene is represented by several distinct formations. These include raised coral rock and the conglomerate of Mombasa Island. Additionally, the period is marked by gravels containing flint implements. 

On the higher slopes of Kenya and Kilimanjaro, the Pleistocene is feebly represented by boulder beds. These deposits provide evidence that the glaciers of these two mountains extended much farther down their slopes during the Pleistocene epoch. In terms of volcanic activity, the period is associated with plateau eruptions as well as the volcanoes of the rift-valley, Kenya, and Kimawenzi.

## European and British Deposits
In Denmark, the surface is largely composed of "Boulder Sand" and "Boulder Clay." The latter originated from the action of ice on the mountains of Norway during the Glacial period. These unstratified deposits rest generally on Cretaceous limestone, though on the island of Bornholm, the boulder clay lies directly on primitive rock. Stratified deposits of sand, gravel, and clay—some containing remains of arctic animals—were formed by the action of water on the boulder clay. It is believed that Denmark did not appear above water until approximately the close of the Glacial period.

In England, specific Pleistocene deposits have yielded paleontological data. For example, the geologist Peter Bellinger Brodie obtained fossil shells from a Pleistocene deposit located at Barnwell.

## Other Regional Correlations
In certain districts, the classification of Pleistocene strata remains a subject of geological correlation based on imperfect evidence. In the district of Scotland (not to be confused with the nation), some coral limestones are ascribed partly to the Pliocene and partly to the Pleistocene.
