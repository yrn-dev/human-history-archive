# Palaeozoic Beds and Red Sandstone Overlays in Bolivia

In the geological structure of Bolivia, specifically within the eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes, the landscape is characterized by a complex arrangement of Palaeozoic rocks and subsequent sedimentary deposits. The eastern ranges are formed of these Palaeozoic rocks, which include various granitic and other intrusions.

## Composition and Distribution of Palaeozoic Beds
The Palaeozoic beds in this region have yielded fossils corresponding to the Carboniferous, Devonian, Ordovician, and Cambrian ages. The distribution of these strata varies by geography:
*   **Southern Bolivia:** The eastern Andes are primarily composed of Ordovician and Cambrian beds.
*   **Northern Bolivia:** Devonian and Carboniferous strata are more extensively developed, particularly within the north-eastern ranges.

The great summits of the eastern range, including Sorata and Illimani, are formed from these Palaeozoic rocks along with other intrusions of granite.

## The Red Sandstone Series
The Palaeozoic beds are directly overlaid by a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones. This sequence is noted for its similarity to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Brazil and Argentina. 

The base of this series frequently contains tuff of porphyritic rocks or a conglomerate. These red sandstones and gypsiferous marls also appear as ridges rising through terrestrial and freshwater deposits on the intervening plateau between the Western Cordillera and the eastern ranges.

## Age and Classification
The exact age of the red sandstone and gypsiferous marl series is considered somewhat uncertain, though it is likely that the series represents more than one geological system. Some evidence suggests the following:
*   **Cretaceous Period:** It is probable that at least part of the series is Cretaceous.
*   **Jurassic and Cretaceous Indicators:** Gustav Steinmann identified marine fossils in the middle of the series. These fossils are said to indicate an age no earlier than the Jurassic, and Steinmann specifically refers them to the Lower Cretaceous.

## Regional Geological Context
The surrounding geological features provide a contrast to the Palaeozoic and red sandstone sequences. The Western Cordillera consists mainly of Cretaceous and Jurassic beds, as well as ashes and lavas from great volcanoes. Most of Bolivia's volcanoes are located within this Western Cordillera.

While no later marine deposits have been discovered in the plains of Bolivia or the eastern Andes, freshwater beds from the Tertiary period and later dates cover a wide area. Recent deposits in the depression between the Western and Eastern Cordillera appear to be partly of torrential origin, similar to talus-fans found in other dry regions. Additionally, Lakes Pampa Aullaguas (Poopo) and Titicaca were once more extensive than they are currently.

Other notable features in eastern Bolivia include the Chiquitos hills, which rise from the plains and are composed of ancient sedimentary rocks of an unknown age.
