# Geology of Bolivia

The geological composition of Bolivia is characterized by a distinct division between its mountain ranges and the intervening plateau, featuring a variety of Palaeozoic, Mesozoic, and Tertiary formations.

## The Andean Ranges and Plateau
The eastern ranges of the Bolivian Andes are composed of Palaeozoic rocks, which include various intrusions of granite. In contrast, the Western Cordillera consists primarily of Cretaceous and Jurassic beds, along with ashes and lavas from large volcanoes. Between these two ranges lies a plateau covered by terrestrial and freshwater deposits. This plateau is intersected by ridges of Palaeozoic rock and a series of gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, the age of which is somewhat uncertain, though they are likely Cretaceous in part.

## Palaeozoic Formations
The Palaeozoic beds in Bolivia have yielded fossils from the Carboniferous, Devonian, Ordovician, and Cambrian ages. The distribution of these beds varies by region:
* **Southern Bolivia:** In this region, the eastern Andes are formed for the greater part by Ordovician and Cambrian beds.
* **Northern Bolivia:** The Carboniferous and Devonian beds are more extensively developed here, particularly within the north-eastern ranges.

The great summits of the eastern range, including Sorata and Illimani, are formed from these Palaeozoic rocks and their granitic intrusions.

## Sedimentary Series and Fossils
The Palaeozoic beds are directly overlaid by gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, which are similar to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Brazil and Argentina. These layers frequently feature a tuff or conglomerate of porphyritic rocks at their base. 

Gustav Steinmann identified marine fossils in the middle of this series, which he attributed to the Lower Cretaceous, suggesting an age no earlier than the Jurassic. However, it is considered possible that this series represents more than one geological system.

## Other Geological Features
In eastern Bolivia, the plains are punctuated by hills known as the Chiquitos, which consist of ancient sedimentary rocks of an unknown age. While no later marine deposits have been discovered in the plains or the eastern Andes, a wide area is occupied by freshwater beds dating from the Tertiary period and later.

The depression between the Western and Eastern Cordillera is covered by recent deposits. These appear to be partly of torrential origin, similar to talus-fans found in other dry regions. Additionally, Lakes Pampa Aullaguas (Poopo) and Titicaca were once more extensive than they are currently.

## Volcanic Activity
Volcanic activity in Bolivia is concentrated almost entirely within the Western Cordillera.
