# Glacial Beds of Kenya

The glacial beds of Kenya are geological formations located within the region of British East Africa. These beds are part of a complex geological landscape characterized by four distinct physiographical regions: the coast plain, the foot plateau, the ancient plateau, and the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa.

## Geological Classification and Age
In the geological record of the region, the glacial beds of Kenya are categorized among the sedimentary formations. While some sedimentary layers in the area date from the Archaean to the Karroo periods—including the shales of the Sabaki river and the grits of Masara—the glacial beds of Kenya are specifically associated with the Jurassic period in the archive's sedimentary table.

## Pleistocene Glacial Activity
Evidence of glacial activity is further represented by boulder beds found on the higher slopes of Mount Kenya and Mount Kilimanjaro. These formations date to the Pleistocene epoch. The presence of these boulder beds indicates that during the Pleistocene, the glaciers of both Mount Kenya and Mount Kilimanjaro extended significantly further down the mountain slopes than they do in the present day.

## Regional Geological Context
The glacial beds exist within a broader environment of diverse rock types. The region's geology includes:
*   **Ancient Crystalline Rocks:** Found on the ancient plateau extending to the borders of Kikuyu.
*   **Volcanic Rocks:** Post-Jurassic to Recent accumulations that form the volcanic plateau of Central East Africa, including Kibo, Kenya, Kimawenzi, and the volcanoes of the rift-valley.
*   **Other Sedimentary Layers:** These include Pleistocene raised coral rock and conglomerates of Mombasa Island, as well as Jurassic shales and limestones near Changamwe, which contain belemnites and gigantic ammonites.
