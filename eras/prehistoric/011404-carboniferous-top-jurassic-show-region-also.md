# Geological Formations from the Carboniferous to the Jurassic

The geological record from the Carboniferous period through the end of the Jurassic period reveals significant shifts in global landmasses, the formation of mountain chains, and the evolution of marine and terrestrial life. Evidence from various regions indicates the existence of vast landmasses and specific sedimentary sequences that define this era.

## Continental Landmasses and Marine Deposits
During the period following the Palaeozoic era, large areas of the earth remained land. In Asia, a broad region stretching from the Arctic Ocean to the south of China shows an absence of marine deposits later than the Carboniferous, except in the extreme north. This area, named Angaraland by E. Suess, was largely land since the close of the Palaeozoic. Its limits are suggested by marine Mesozoic deposits found in Tibet to the south, the western Tian-shan to the west, and Japan to the east. Triassic deposits in the Verkhoyansk Range indicate that this landmass did not reach the Bering Sea.

Similarly, the peninsula of India has remained land since the Carboniferous period. This is evidenced by the absence of marine fossils (except near its borders) and the presence of the Gondwana series. These terrestrial and freshwater deposits represent the entire geological scale from the top of the Carboniferous to the top of the Jurassic. This region was part of a larger landmass known as Gondwanaland, which likely extended across the Indian Ocean and was once united with southern Africa. Between Angaraland and Gondwanaland lay a sea referred to as the Tethys by Suess or the central Mediterranean sea by Neumayr.

## Regional Geological Formations
Various regions exhibit distinct sedimentary and structural characteristics from the Carboniferous to the Jurassic:

*   **British East Africa:** The region contains sedimentary rocks dating from the Trias to the Jurassic in the foot plateau. Specifically, the shales and limestones of Changamwe date to the Jurassic and contain belemnites and gigantic ammonites. The grits and shales of Masara and Taru are associated with the Karroo age, indicated by *Glossopteris browniana* var. *indica*. Shales from the Sabaki river are tentatively associated with the Carboniferous, containing fish-scales and *Palaeanodonta Fischeri*.
*   **Belgium and Western Europe:** Belgium is situated on the northern side of the "Hercynian chain," a mountain range composed of Palaeozoic rocks. The final elevation of this chain occurred toward the close of the Carboniferous period, with the latest involved beds belonging to the Coal Measures. The presence of Jurassic beds on the southern margin (and their absence on the northern margin) indicates the chain remained a ridge during Jurassic times.
*   **Asia Minor:** Carboniferous rocks are found at Eregli, where they are worked for coal, and fossils have been discovered at Balia Maden in Mysia. A band of Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous beds exists south of the Sea of Marmora.
*   **The Balkans:** In the western folded belt of the Balkan Peninsula, Carboniferous beds are present in the north, while the Jura and Trias contribute significantly to the formation of the mountain chain.
*   **Bolivia:** Palaeozoic beds in the eastern Andes include Carboniferous age fossils. These are overlaid by red sandstones and gypsiferous marls; marine fossils found in the middle of this series indicate an age no earlier than the Jurassic.

## Biological Evolution
The transition from the Carboniferous to the Jurassic saw significant changes in aquatic fauna:

*   **Carboniferous:** This period was characterized by the predominance of Chondrostean ganoids (*Platysomidae* and *Palaeoniscidae*) and Selachians (truesharks, Acanthodians, and *Pleuracanthus*). Crossopterygii and Dipneusti remained abundant, while Arthrodira and Ostracophori disappeared.
*   **Trias:** The variety of Dipneusti and Crossopterygii dwindled, and *Ceratodontidae* appeared. Holostean and Chondrostean ganoids were represented in roughly equal numbers.
*   **Jurassic:** Holostean ganoids became the predominant group. This period also saw the appearance of the first annectant representatives of the Teleostei (*Leptolepidae* and *Pholidophoridae*). Other common fauna included Coelacanthid crossopterygians, Chimaerids, Cestraciont sharks, and primitive skates (*Rhinobatidae* and *Squatinidae*).
