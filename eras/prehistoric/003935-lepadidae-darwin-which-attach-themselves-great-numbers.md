# Lepadidae

The *Lepadidae* are a division of Crustacea known as stalked barnacles. Within the classification system utilized by C. Darwin, these organisms are distinguished from the sessile barnacles, or *Balanidae*.

## Characteristics and Habitat
*Lepadidae* are characterized by their ability to attach themselves in great numbers to various objects floating in the sea, such as drift-wood. Due to this habit of attachment, they serve as one of the primary agents responsible for the fouling of the bottoms of ships during long voyages.

## Classification and Scientific Understanding
Barnacles belong to the division *Cirripedia* or *Thyrostraca*. For a significant period following the replacement of medieval fabulous tales with the scientific study of zoology, the true affinities of barnacles were not fully appreciated. Because of their external appearance, they were initially classified among the *Mollusca*.

The recognition of barnacles as Crustaceans was established in 1830 by Vaughan Thompson. Thompson demonstrated that these organisms develop from a larva that is typically Crustacean and free-swimming. This biological reality led T. H. Huxley to describe the barnacle as a Crustacean that is fixed by its head and uses its legs to kick food into its mouth.

## Historical Myths
From at least the 12th century, a persistent myth in medieval natural history existed regarding the origin of the barnacle or bernacle goose (*Bernicla branta*). Because the Arctic nesting-places of this winter visitor to Britain were unknown at the time, it was fabled that the bird originated from the shell-like fruit of a tree growing by the sea-shore. Some versions of this story claimed the shell grew like a mushroom on rotting timber in the sea; this description is identified as a reference to barnacles of the genus *Lepas*.
