# Jungermanniales

The Jungermanniales represent a large series of liverworts characterized by a significant variety in the organization of their sexual generation. Within the study of Bryophyta, they are considered a probable natural evolutionary group, although the specific partial lines of progression within the group are not entirely clear.

## Classification and Structure
The Jungermanniales are divided into two primary groups based on whether the formation of archegonia utilizes the apex or terminates the growth of the branch:

*   **Anacrogynous Jungermanniaceae:** This is the more primitive group. In these plants, the branch continues to grow after the formation of archegonia. Consequently, the archegonia and the subsequent sporogonia are located on the dorsal surface of the leafy plant or thallus.
*   **Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae:** In this group, the plant is consistently foliose. The archegonia are positioned at the ends of the branches or the main shoot.

The antheridia of these plants are typically long-stalked and globular, and the capsule is characterized by splitting into four halves upon opening.

## Evolutionary Progression
The Jungermanniales exhibit a series of development leading from simple to more highly organized forms. While the lower members of the series are thalloid, they lack the complicated thallus structure found in the Marchantiales. Evolution in the Jungermanniales has proceeded toward the differentiation of the plant into a leaf and stem. Evidence of this transition is found in the lower group of the Anacrogynous Jungermanniaceae, whereas the Acrogynous Jungermanniaceae possess well-marked leaves and stems.

Regarding the sporogonium, even the simplest forms within the Jungermanniales possess a sterile foot. Within this series, it is possible to trace the origin of elaters from sterile cells.

## Phylogenetic Relations
In the broader context of the Hepaticae, the Jungermanniales and Marchantiales are viewed as natural evolutionary groups. The divergence of these two groups from a common ancestry is illustrated by forms such as *Sphaerocarpus*, which shares features with the lower Marchantiales.

There have been suggestions by Leitgeb regarding a relationship between the Jungermanniales and the Anthocerotales. However, recent research has served to emphasize the isolated position of the Anthocerotales rather than confirming the relationship suggested by Leitgeb.

## Comparison with Other Bryophyta
The Jungermanniales are one of three great groups of liverworts. They differ from the Marchantiales—which are a series of thalloid forms specialized for exposed situations—and the Anthocerotales, a small, distinct group where the gametophyte is a thallus and the sporogonium features a sterile columella capable of continued spore production and growth.

Unlike the mosses, where evolution has proceeded until the protonema is a preliminary stage to plant formation, the liverworts follow different lines of differentiation. There are no known connecting forms between the Musci, Sphagnales, Anthocerotales, and Hepaticae, leaving the question of whether the Bryophyta are a polyphyletic or monophyletic group open.
