# Andreaeales

The Andreaeales is a subdivision of the Musci (mosses) represented by a single genus, *Andreaea*. These plants are characterized as small, dark-coloured mosses that primarily grow in tufts on bare rocks within arctic and alpine regions. In Britain, four species of *Andreaea* are found on alpine rocks.

## Growth and Development
The life cycle of the Andreaeales begins when a spore germinates, producing a small mass of cells. From this mass, one or more short filaments grow, which subsequently broaden into a ribbon-shaped thallus. This thallus is several cells thick and remains closely applied to the rock surface. Gemmae and erect branches may develop from this protonema.

The stem of the plant arises in the usual manner but is distinguished by the absence of a conducting strand. The leaves of the genus may or may not possess midribs. A notable characteristic of the leaf is its growth pattern; it grows via a dome-shaped initial cell, which differs from the typical two-sided initial cell found in other mosses.

## Reproductive Structures and Sporogonium
The sexual organs of the Andreaeales include long-stalked antheridia. The sporogonium is raised on a pseudopodium and lacks a seta, a feature it shares with *Sphagnum*. The calyptra on the sporogonium is formed from the upper portion of the archegonial wall, which is carried upward.

While the development of the sporogonium follows a process similar to that of the Bryales, there are key differences. In the Andreaeales, the dome-shaped archesporium extends over the summit of the columella, and the plant lacks an air-space. Furthermore, the columella does not extend to the upper end of the capsule.

## Capsule and Dehiscence
The capsule of the *Andreaea* genus does not open by an operculum. Instead, it opens through a number of lateral longitudinal slits—typically four or six—which do not reach the apex or the base of the capsule. 

There is one exotic species that exhibits a different mode of dehiscence: in this instance, the splits occur only at the upper part of the capsule, and the terminal cap breaks away. Because of this specific characteristic, this isolated example appears to approach the Bryales in the way it opens.
