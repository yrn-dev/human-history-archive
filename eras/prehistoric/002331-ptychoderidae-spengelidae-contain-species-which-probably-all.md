# Ptychoderidae and Spengelidae

The Ptychoderidae and Spengelidae are two families of Enteropneusta. These families are distinguished from the Balanoglossidae by their distribution, developmental processes, and specific anatomical structures.

## Distribution
The Ptychoderidae and Spengelidae are predominantly found in subtropical and tropical regions. In contrast, the Balanoglossidae are predominantly temperate and arctic in their distribution. One recorded example of the Spengelidae is *Glandiceps abyssicola*, which was dredged by the "Challenger" expedition in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of Africa at a depth of 2500 fathoms.

## Development
The families Ptychoderidae and Spengelidae are characterized by an indirect course of development, which is likely pursued by all species within these two groups. This process culminates in a metamorphosis to reach the adult form. The larva associated with this development is known as the *Tornaria*. 

The *Tornaria* larva is transparent and pelagic. It possesses a characteristic form unlike the adult, featuring a complicated longitudinal ciliated band (a ciliated seam) that is frequently drawn out into lappets and convoluted bays. Due to its external form and the possession of a dorsal water-pore that communicates with the anterior body-cavity, the *Tornaria* larva bears a similarity to the larvae of Echinoderms, such as star-fishes, sea-cucumbers, and sea-urchins. This indirect development is suggested to provide a clue regarding the possible derivation of the Enteropneusta.

## Anatomical Characteristics

### The Stomochord
The stomochord is a resistant supporting structure that originates from the gut-wall (specifically as an outgrowth from the anterior dorsal wall of the collar-gut). While it is an integral part of the gut, it no longer assists in alimentation; its lumen is more or less vestigial and its epithelium undergoes hypertrophy and vacuolar differentiation. 

In some species of the Spengelidae, the stomochord features a long capillary vermiform extension in front.

### Genital Pleurae and Lateral Septa
The Ptychoderidae are especially characterized by the presence of genital pleurae. These are paired longitudinal lateral or pleural folds of the body that are mobile. They can be spread out horizontally or approximated at their free edges to close in the dorsal surface. When closed, they embrace the branchial grooves, the gill-pores, and the median dorsal nerve-tract, creating a medullary tube and a temporary peri-branchial tube that remains open at the rear where the folds end. These folds are termed genital pleurae because they contain the bulk of the gonads.

Associated with these pleurae are the lateral septa, which are a pair of vascular folds of the basement membrane. These proceed from the dorsal angles created by the pleural folds with the body-wall in the pharyngeal region, and from the dorsal wall of the gut in the post-branchial portion of the branchio-genital region. In their most fully developed state, these membranes extend to the free border of the genital pleurae.

### Nervous System
The nervous system of the Enteropneusta is diffuse and essentially epidermal, consisting of a layer of nerve-fibres at the base of the epidermis. In the collar-region, there is a concentration of fibres and cells forming the collar nerve-tube (a medullary tube). 

In the Ptychoderidae, this medullary tube is connected to the epidermis at intermediate points via a variable number of unpaired outgrowths from its dorsal wall. These hollow roots, which generally contain an axial lumen in continuity with the central canal, terminate blindly in the dorsal epidermis of the collar. This structure places the fibres of the nerve-tube in direct connection with the nervous layer of the epidermis.
