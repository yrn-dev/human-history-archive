# Mammals and Fauna of Northern Asia

The fauna of northern Asia is characterized by a strong relationship with the mammals of Europe, with nearly all European mammals also occurring in the region. However, the Palaearctic fauna of northern Asia is enriched by numerous additional species, primarily those forms restricted to temperate and cold regions.

## Mammalian Groups of Northern Asia
The distribution of mammals in northern Asia is heavily influenced by climate. Groups that frequent cold climates are numerous, including wolves, foxes, bears, and weasels. Within the order of Insectivora, hedgehogs, shrews, and various forms of moles prevail. The Rodents are well represented by mice, squirrels, and hares, with pikas (tailless hares, *Lagomys*) and marmots (*Arctomys*) serving as characteristic forms of this order in the region.

Conversely, forms associated with warmer climates are scarce. The Quadrumana, or monkeys, are nearly unrepresented; a single species is found in Japan, while one or two others occur in Tibet and northern China. Similarly, while insectivorous bats are numerous, the frugivorous division of the order is represented by only one species in Japan.

## Ungulates and Deer
The order of Ungulata in northern Asia includes several peculiar forms of antelope, such as the *Gazella gutturosa* (yellow sheep) and the saiga (*Saiga tatarica*). Sheep are also represented by various forms, with as many as twelve wild species of *Ovis* found across the mountain chains of Asia.

Deer are among the characteristic forms of northern Asia, particularly those belonging to the typical genus *Cervus*. One of the most peculiar types restricted to northern Asia is the musk deer (*Moschus*).

## Comparison with Other Regions
The mammal fauna of northern Asia differs significantly from the Indian region of Asia, which is described as being much more highly developed. While northern Asia lacks most tropical forms, the Indian region is home to the Indian elephant (one of two remaining representatives of the order Proboscidea), four species of rhinoceros, and one species of tapir. Deer are also numerous in the Indian region, where the group of chevrotains (*Tragulus*) is characteristic. 

In other global regions, deer populations vary. In Bolivia, deer are not numerous, with only two or three species present, the most common being the large marsh deer of the Chaco. ## Ornithology of Northern Asia
The birds of northern Asia are even more closely allied to those of Europe than the mammals are. Approximately three-fourths of the well-known species of Europe extend through Siberia into the islands of the Japanese empire. Like the mammals, the bird population shows a great development of groups characteristic of temperate and cold regions and an absence of tropical forms.

Among the most peculiar birds restricted to northern Asia in their wild state is the genus *Phasianus*. Additionally, eared pheasants (*Crossoptilon*) and gold pheasants (*Thaumalea*) are confined to specific districts within the mountains of north eastern Asia. The *Passeres* are also well developed, with many species of grosbeaks, linnets, finches, stone chats, and larks.
