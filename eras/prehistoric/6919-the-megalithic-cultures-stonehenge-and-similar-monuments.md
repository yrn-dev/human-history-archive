# The Silent Sentinels: Megalithic Cultures of Prehistoric Europe

## Overview
Megalithic architecture—derived from the Greek *megas* (great) and *lithos* (stone)—represents one of the most ambitious engineering feats of the prehistoric world. These monuments, ranging from solitary menhirs to complex passage graves and stone circles, appeared across Western Europe and North Africa, primarily during the Neolithic and Early Bronze Age.

While Stonehenge in England is the most iconic example, it is part of a broader global phenomenon. These structures were not merely piles of rock; they were sophisticated expressions of social organization, spiritual belief, and astronomical observation. Historians and archaeologists debate the primary purpose of these sites. Some argue they served as territorial markers or ancestral shrines, while others emphasize their role as celestial calendars used to track solstices and equinoxes. The sheer labor required to transport stones—some weighing over 20 tons—suggests a highly organized society with a shared ideological vision.

## Timeline of Megalithic Construction

| Period | Approximate Dates | Key Developments |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Early Neolithic** | 4500 – 3500 BCE | Rise of passage graves and dolmens (e.g., Brittany, France). |
| **Middle Neolithic** | 3500 – 2500 BCE | Construction of complex circles and henges (e.g., Avebury). |
| **Late Neolithic/Bronze Age** | 2500 – 1500 BCE | Final phases of Stonehenge; shift toward individual burials. |

## Key Facts

*   **Stonehenge's Complexity:** Stonehenge was not built in a single event but evolved over 1,500 years. It began as a simple earthwork enclosure (c. 3000 BCE) before the famous sarsen stones and smaller bluestones were added.
*   **The Bluestone Mystery:** One of the most debated aspects of Stonehenge is the origin of the bluestones. Geological analysis confirms they originated from the Preseli Hills in Wales, over 140 miles away, raising questions about whether the stones were transported by land, sea, or if the people migrated.
*   **Diversity of Forms:** Megaliths take three primary forms: **Menhirs** (standing stones), **Dolmens** (portal tombs), and **Cromlechs** (stone circles).
*   **Astronomical Alignment:** Many sites are aligned with solar events. At Stonehenge, the axis is aligned with the sunrise of the summer solstice and the sunset of the winter solstice, suggesting a deep preoccupation with the seasonal cycle.

## Did You Know?
1. **Not Just Europe:** While famous in Europe, megalithic structures also appear in Korea, India, and parts of Africa, suggesting that the impulse to build with giant stones was a convergent human behavior.
2. **The "Henge" Definition:** A "henge" is not actually the stone circle itself, but the circular bank and ditch that surround the site.
3. **Carnac's Scale:** The Carnac stones in France are far more numerous than those at Stonehenge, consisting of thousands of standing stones arranged in alignments stretching for miles.
