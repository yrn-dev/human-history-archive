# Ursus ornatus

The *Ursus ornatus*, commonly known as the spectacled bear, is a species of bear native to the Andes. It is characterized as a black species with a broad skull.

## Physical Characteristics and Classification
The spectacled bear is distinguished from all other bear species by a specific anatomical feature: a perforation, or foramen, located on the inner side of the lower end of the humerus. Within the broader classification of bears, which are animals of considerable bulk and among the largest members of their order, *U. ornatus* is categorized under the genus *Tremarctus*.

## Distribution and Habitat
The spectacled bear is found in the wooded Andean foothills. Specifically, it is noted as a rare species within the indigenous fauna of Bolivia, where the local wildlife closely corresponds to that of Peru, Brazil, and Argentina.

## General Context of the Order
Bears are widely distributed across the globe, though they are absent from Australasia and Africa south of the Sahara. Most species are vegetable feeders or omnivorous. This general dietary pattern is observed even in the polar bear (*Ursus maritimus*), which eats grass during the summer despite subsisting on fish and flesh for most of the year. Other species include the brown bear (*U. arctus*) of Asia and Europe, the North American black bear (*U. americanus*), and the small Malay sun-bear (*U. malayanus*).

## Evolutionary History
The bear group is linked to the *Cynodictis*-type through the *Amphicyonidae*, represented by Middle Tertiary genera such as *Proamphicyon*, *Pseudamphicyon*, and *Amphicyon*. A related type, *Arctotherium*, is found in the South American Pleistocene.
