# Belasitza, Perin, and Rila

The mountain masses of Belasitza, Perin, and Rila form the core of the central region of the Balkan Peninsula. This central region is roughly triangular in shape, with its apex located in Servia and its base resting upon the Aegean Sea. Geologically, this area is distinct from the surrounding belts of folded beds that form the chain of the Balkans to the north and the mountain ranges of the Ionian and Adriatic coasts to the west.

## Geological Composition and Stratigraphy
The core of the central region, including Belasitza, Perin, and Rila, consists largely of crystalline and schistose rocks. A defining characteristic of these specific mountain masses is the absence of Palaeozoic and Mesozoic beds. In this core area, the earliest sedimentary deposits are from the Tertiary period, and these deposits lie flat upon the underlying crystalline rocks.

While the core is devoid of these earlier strata, Cretaceous beds are found upon the margins of the central region. Additionally, the eastern parts of Greece, which are composed almost entirely of Cretaceous beds, are considered part of this central area because the folds affecting them run nearly at right angles to the folds of the western chains.

## Structural Characteristics
The central region of the Balkan Peninsula is characterized primarily by faulting rather than folding. While it is wedged between two belts of folding, the central area remains largely unaffected by those folds, except near its margins. Within this region, sedimentary beds are sometimes located in troughs created by faults. Furthermore, the region experienced extensive volcanic outbursts during the Tertiary period.

## Regional Comparisons
The geological makeup of the Balkan Peninsula's central region contrasts sharply with the western folded belt. In the western belt, the strike of the folds generally runs N.N.W.-S.S.E. or N.W.-S.E. Unlike the core of the central region, the western chain contains Carboniferous beds in the north, and the Jura and Trias contribute significantly to its formation. In the south of the western belt, the chain is formed chiefly of Cretaceous beds, though Jurassic and Eocene rocks are likely present.

Additionally, the Rilska Planina, along with Rhodope and adjacent ranges, is part of an Archean formation composed of crystalline schists and gneiss, which is traversed by eruptive veins. This Archean formation also extends over a large portion of the Eastern Rumelian plain.
