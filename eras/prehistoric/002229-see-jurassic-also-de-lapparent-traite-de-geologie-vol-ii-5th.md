# JURASSIC GEOLOGICAL FORMATIONS

The Jurassic period is characterized by a diverse array of geological formations and fossil records distributed globally, with significant study conducted in Europe, Africa, and the Americas. The classification of these strata often involves complex nomenclature and varying interpretations between British and continental geologists.

## Stratigraphy and Nomenclature
In Europe, the classification of Jurassic rocks has faced challenges due to a lack of clearly-marked palaeontological and stratigraphical boundaries. This has led to involved nomenclature and a trend among continental geologists to omit certain terms, such as "Corallian." 

According to the classification by A. de Lapparent, the English Corallian rocks are represented by the *Sequanien* stage. This stage is further divided into a lower *Rauracien* substage and an upper *Astartien* substage. However, this classification does not encompass the entire Corallian stage; the lower portion of the Corallian is placed by de Lapparent within the *Oxfordien* stage.

Another significant continental division is the "Kimeridgien." Traditionally, this contained three sub-divisions defined by A. Oppel and W. Waagen:
*   **Lower (Astartian):** characterized by *Astarte supracorallina*.
*   **Middle (Pteroceran):** characterized by *Pteroceras oceani*.
*   **Upper (Virgulian):** characterized by *Exogyra virgula*.

While most of the Astartian corresponds to the Corallian, the upper portion of the continental Kimeridgian is equivalent to parts of the British Portlandian. A. de Lapparent has since revised this, recognizing only the Pteroceran and Virgulian within the Kimeridgien.

## Regional Geology and Composition
The composition of Jurassic formations varies by geography:
*   **Northern Europe and Russia:** The Kimeridgien is represented by marls and clays, with occasional sandstones and limestones.
*   **Germany:** In Swabia and other regions, a specific ruiniform marble known as *Felsenkalk* occurs on the Kimeridgien horizon.
*   **Southern Europe and the Alps:** The Kimeridgien is primarily calcareous.
*   **Global Distribution:** Representatives of these formations are found in Algeria, Abyssinia, Madagascar, and Caucasia. In South America, they appear alongside volcanic rocks, and they possibly occur in Alaska, California (specifically the Maripan beds), and King Charles's Land.
*   **British East Africa:** The foot plateau of this region is composed of sedimentary rocks dating from the Trias to the Jurassic.

## Palaeontology
The Jurassic strata are noted for a rich variety of fossils. Abundant forms include corals and crinoids. Other significant fossils include:
*   **Mollusks:** Ammonites, large belemnites (*Megateuthis giganteus*), *Pleurotomaria*, and certain forms of *Trigonia* (*T. costata*).
*   **Brachiopods:** *Terebratulas* (*T. perovalis*).
*   **Echinoderms:** *Cidaris*.

## Notable Researchers
Several geologists and palaeontologists have contributed extensively to the study of Jurassic formations:
*   **Pierre Jean Édouard Desor (1811–1882):** A Swiss geologist who focused on Jurassic Echinoderms and published *Échinologie helvétique*.
*   **Eugène Eudes-Deslongchamps (1830–1889):** A French palaeontologist who authored *Le Jura normand* and conducted studies on the *Terrain callovien*, Jurassic brachiopods, and the lower Jurassic stages of Normandy.
*   **H. B. Woodward:** Author of "The Jurassic Rocks of Britain" (published in volumes including 1892, 1894, and 1895).
*   **A. de Lapparent:** Author of *Traite de geologie*, whose work provides general accounts and classifications of these periods.
