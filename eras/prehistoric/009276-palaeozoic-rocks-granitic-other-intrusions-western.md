# Palaeozoic Rocks with Granitic and Other Intrusions

The presence of Palaeozoic rocks associated with granitic and other intrusive masses is a significant geological feature in several regions across the globe, contributing to the formation of mountain ranges, the creation of metamorphic rocks, and the development of mineral deposits.

## The Bolivian Andes
In Bolivia, the eastern ranges of the Andes are composed of Palaeozoic rocks characterized by granitic and other intrusions. These beds have yielded fossils corresponding to the Cambrian, Ordovician, Devonian, and Carboniferous ages. The distribution of these beds varies by region: in southern Bolivia, the eastern Andes are primarily formed of Ordovician and Cambrian beds, whereas the Devonian and Carboniferous are more extensively developed in the north and north-eastern ranges.

The great summits of the eastern range, including Sorata and Illimani, are specifically formed from these Palaeozoic rocks with granitic and other intrusions. These Palaeozoic beds are directly overlaid by gypsiferous marls and red sandstones, a series similar to the *formacion petrolifera* found in Brazil and Argentina. At the base of this overlying series, tuff or conglomerate of porphyritic rocks is frequently present.

## Cornwall and the British Isles
In Cornwall, the landscape is defined by four large masses of granite—Bodmin Moor, St Austell, Penryn, and St Just and Land's End—which rise above a lower-lying region of slaty rocks known as "Killas." While the granite is not the oldest rock in the region (that distinction belongs to the metamorphic schists, gabbro, and serpentine of the Lizard peninsula), it has significantly altered the surrounding Killas.

The sedimentary rocks of the region include a variety of Palaeozoic strata. While much was formerly classed as Devonian, it is now known that an older set of Palaeozoic rocks exists. The Veryan series contains Caradoc fossils and is followed by the Mylor, Falmouth, and Portscatho series. These Ordovician beds are unconformably rested upon by the Menaccan series (the lowest Devonian beds). Additionally, Upper Silurian fossils have been identified near Veryan, and Carboniferous slates, limestones, and grits (the Culm measures) are found north of a line joining South Petherwin and Boscastle. The granite masses were intruded after the period of Carboniferous times and after the sedimentary strata had undergone severe folding and thrusting from the south.

## Metamorphism and Related Rock Types
The interaction between granitic intrusions and surrounding rocks often leads to metamorphism through heat and pressure.

### Marbles
Many marbles are metamorphosed limestones. When fossil-rich limestones approach great crystalline intrusions of granite, they can be converted into true marbles. This process occurs because the heat from the granite and the pressure from overlying rock masses act on the limestone before the granite cools. Consequently, most marbles are pre-Cambrian or Palaeozoic. These often occur alongside phyllites and mica schists, which originated as beds of clay alternating with limestone.

### Hornfels
Hornfels is a group designation for rocks that have been indurated and baked by the heat of intrusive granitic masses. This process renders the rocks massive, hard, and splintery, effacing the original fissile structure of the parent rocks, which may have been slate, shale, limestone, diabase, or sandstone. Common types include "biotite hornfelses," which are black or dark-brown with shining black mica, and "lime hornfelses," which appear in colors such as white, brown, pale-green, or yellow.

## Other Regional Occurrences
Palaeozoic and granitic associations appear in various other global contexts:
*   **Burma:** In eastern Burma, granite and gneiss occur. Carboniferous fossils have been found near Moulmein and in Tenasserim, while Ordovician fossils have been located in the Upper Shan States.
*   **Corsica:** The western part of the island is composed chiefly of granite, including quartz porphyries and granophyres. Between the Gulfs of Galeria and Porto, there are limestones and schists containing fossils of the Upper Carboniferous age.
*   **Brittany and Spain:** Research by Dr. Charles Barrois has focused on the metamorphic and granitic rocks of Brittany, as well as the Palaeozoic rocks of northern Spain and Brittany.
*   **Norway:** Geologist Waldemar Christofer Brögger conducted observations on the Palaeozoic rocks of Norway and the relationship between basic and granitic rocks.
