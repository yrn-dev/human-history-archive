# The Avonian and Kidwelly

## The Avonian Stage
The Avonian is a geological term proposed in 1905 by Dr. A. Vaughan to describe the rocks of Lower Carboniferous age located in the Avon gorge at Bristol. This stage is considered to embrace the same fossil-zones and rocks as the earlier designation known as the "Dinantien." However, the substages of the Avonian do not correspond exactly with those identified by Belgian and French geologists, as they are based on a different interpretation of zonal fossils and different local conditions.

The Avonian is divided into substages characterized by specific zones. The upper substage is the Kidwellian, which is associated with the zone *Dibunophyllum*. The lower substage is the Clevedonian, which is associated with the zones *Zaphrentis* and *Cleistopora*.

## Geological Distribution
The different substages of the Avonian are well displayed in various regions of Great Britain. The upper Avonian, or Kidwellian, is well developed in the area around Kidwelly in Carmarthenshire. Conversely, the lower substage, the Clevedonian, is well displayed near Clevedon in Somerset.

## Kidwelly
Kidwelly (also known as *Cydweli*) is a municipal borough and decayed market-town located in Carmarthenshire, Wales. Geographically, the town is situated near the shores of Carmarthen Bay at the junction of two streams: the Gwendraeth Fach and the Gwendraeth Fawr. As of 1901, the population of the town was 2,285. The town is served by a station on the Great Western railway.

The town's decline is attributed to the prosperity of nearby Llanelly and the accumulation of sand at the mouth of the river.

## History and Landmarks of Kidwelly
Kidwelly is noted for its stirring history and architectural landmarks. Its chief attraction is a magnificent, well-preserved castle, regarded as one of the finest in South Wales. Dating primarily from the 13th century, the castle is situated on a knoll above the Gwendraeth Fach. The site was originally established when William de Londres, a companion of Fitz Hamon and his conquering knights, first erected a castle there.

The town also contains the parish church of St Mary, which dates from the 14th century and features a spire and a lofty tower.

Historically, the town was the site of significant conflict. In 1135, Gwenllian, the wife of Griffith ap Rhys (prince of South Wales), launched a furious attack on Kidwelly. This resulted in a battle at a location still known as Maes Gwenllian, which ended in the total defeat and execution of the Welsh princess. 

The jurisdiction of the town became exclusive after the lordship of Kidwelly became the property of Henry, earl of Lancaster, through marriage. Kidwelly's first charter of incorporation was granted by Henry VI, while its present charter dates from 1618.
