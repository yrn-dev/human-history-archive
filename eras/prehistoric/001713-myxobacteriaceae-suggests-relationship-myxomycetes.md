# Myxobacteriaceae

The Myxobacteriaceae is a group of bacteria established by Thaxter to include certain allied forms of bacteria. This group represents a third division of bacteria, known as Myxobacterinae, which is distinct from the Haplobacterinae and Trichobacterinae divisions.

## History and Classification
The nature of some members of this group was not initially understood. For example, *Chondromyces crocatus* was described by Berkeley as early as 1857, but it was incorrectly ascribed to the Hyphomycetes. In 1892, Thaxter rediscovered the organism and demonstrated its bacterial nature, subsequently founding the group Myxobacteriaceae. Another form described by Thaxter as *Myxobacter* was later identified as being the same as *Polyangium vitellinum*, which had been described by Link in 1795, though its exact nature had remained in doubt until then.

While some botanists initially questioned Thaxter's observations and conclusions, the position of the group was firmly established through later observations by Thaxter and Baur.

## Characteristics and Biological Relationships
The Myxobacteriaceae are characterized by the formation of plasmodium-like aggregations. They build themselves into sporogenous structures of a definite form that are superficially similar to the cysts of the Mycetozoa. Due to these characteristics, Thaxter's group suggests a relationship with the Myxomycetes.

Within the broader classification of organisms, the Myxobacteriaceae fall under the Schizomycetes (bacteria). Schizomycetes are minute vegetable organisms that lack chlorophyll and an ordinary nucleus, and they multiply through repeated bipartitions. They are generally characterized by an absence of ordinary sexual reproduction. While most typical bacteria are viewed as being derived from the Cyanophyceae (blue-green algae), the Myxobacteriaceae provide a point of comparison to the Myxomycetes. In the general classification of fungi, both the Schizomycetes and Myxomycetes (Mycetozoa) are considered independent groups that do not fall under the category of true fungi.

## Habitat and Cultivation
Members of the Myxobacteriaceae are most commonly found growing on the dung of herbivorous animals. They exist both within the alimentary canal of these animals and free in the air. 

To obtain Myxobacteria, dung that has been exposed to the air for at least eight days is kept in the dark at a temperature of 30-35 degrees C. This high temperature is utilized because it is favorable for the growth of the bacteria while being inimical to the fungi that are common on the same substratum.
