# The Neolithic Revolution: How Agriculture Changed Everything

## Overview
The Neolithic Revolution represents one of the most profound shifts in human history: the transition from nomadic bands of hunter-gatherers to settled agricultural communities. Occurring roughly 12,000 years ago, this transition was not a sudden "event" but a gradual process of domestication of plants (such as wheat, barley, and rice) and animals (such as sheep, goats, and cattle).

By shifting from a strategy of extraction to one of production, humans fundamentally altered their relationship with the environment. The ability to create food surpluses allowed for population growth and the emergence of permanent settlements. This stability paved the way for social stratification, specialized labor (such as pottery and metallurgy), and eventually, the rise of the first urban civilizations in regions like the Fertile Crescent, the Yangtze River valley, and Mesoamerica.

However, historians and anthropologists debate whether this shift was an intentional choice or a forced adaptation to climate change. The "Hilly Flanks" theory suggests humans moved into fertile zones to exploit wild grains, while other scholars argue that the end of the last glacial period forced groups to innovate as traditional game disappeared.

## Key Facts
*   **The Fertile Crescent:** While agriculture emerged independently in several global hubs, the region spanning modern-day Iraq, Syria, and Turkey is widely cited as one of the earliest centers of domestication.
*   **Social Stratification:** Surplus food meant that not everyone had to farm. This led to the creation of hierarchies, where priests, warriors, and rulers managed the distribution of resources.
*   **Health Trade-offs:** Contrary to the idea that farming improved life, bioarchaeological evidence suggests early farmers often suffered from more diseases (due to proximity to livestock) and poorer nutrition (due to a less diverse diet) than hunter-gatherers.
*   **Technological Leap:** The need to store grain led to the invention of pottery, while the need to clear land spurred the development of polished stone tools.

## Did You Know?
1. **The First Cities:** Çatalhöyük in modern-day Turkey is one of the best-preserved Neolithic settlements; its houses were built so closely together that people walked on roofs and entered their homes through holes in the ceiling.
2. **Independent Origins:** Agriculture didn't spread from one single point. It developed independently in China (rice/millet), New Guinea (taro), and the Americas (maize/squash).
3. **Genetic Shifts:** Domestication changed the plants themselves; for example, wild wheat shatters its seeds to spread them, but domesticated wheat evolved a "non-shattering" rachis, making it easier for humans to harvest.
