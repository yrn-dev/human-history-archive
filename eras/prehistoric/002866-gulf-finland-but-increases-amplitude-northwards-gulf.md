# Gulf of Finland

The Gulf of Finland is an arm of the Baltic Sea that extends eastwards for approximately 250 miles, serving as the maritime boundary that separates Finland from Esthonia. It is connected to the larger Baltic basin via the Gotland deep, which extends directly into the gulf.

## Geological History and Elevation
The region has undergone significant transformations due to glacial subsidence and subsequent upheaval. During glacial times, a sea arm extended across Sweden to the Gulf of Bothnia, creating conditions salty enough for oysters to flourish. Following an upheaval, the Baltic became fresher and the oysters disappeared. The area later became a vast freshwater lake known as the *Ancylus* lake, which covered approximately 220,000 square miles, including Lake Ladoga and a large portion of Finland.

A subsequent period of subsidence re-established salt-water communication through the Danish channels. This was followed by a process of elevation that continues today. Research by Dr. R. Sieger using isobasic lines (lines of equal rate of elevation) indicates that while movement is almost nil along the axial lines of the Gulf of Finland and the Baltic, the amplitude of this elevation increases as one moves northwards toward the Gulf of Bothnia and toward the main ridge of the southern Sweden *massif*.

## Physical Characteristics and Hydrography
The Gulf of Finland is part of a broader system of basins and hollows. In the upper layers of water—ranging from 30 to 70 metres in thickness—salinity remains almost uniform from the Gulf of Finland to the Gulf of Bothnia, with vertical circulation maintained by convection currents. Beneath these layers lie stagnant masses of salter water that are deficient in oxygen and surcharged with carbonic acid. These deeper waters originated from outside the Baltic, passing over the plateau between Mecklenburg and Falster, though their horizontal movement is restricted by ridges separating the deep hollows.

## Navigation and Climate
The Gulf of Finland is subject to severe winter conditions. The sea is closed to navigation by ice for approximately 150 days of the year. However, the use of ice-breakers allows navigation to continue throughout the winter months.

## Regional Geography and History
The gulf is bordered by several significant locations and political entities:
*   **Borga (Porvoo):** A seaport in the province of Nyland, Finland, located at the entrance of the river Borga into the Gulf of Finland. It is a seat of a court of appeal and a Lutheran bishopric. In 1809, it served as the meeting place for the estates of Finland, where Emperor Alexander I pledged to maintain the liberties and constitution of the grand duchy.
*   **Reval:** A harbour located within the gulf.
*   **Colonial and Imperial Expansion:** Historically, the Hanse merchants and Teutonic knights extended their colonies along the Baltic coast as far as the Gulf of Finland. From the Gulf of Finland extending to the Eastern Ocean, Russia developed into a powerful and civilized empire.
