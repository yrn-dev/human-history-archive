# Mammals of the Marsupial Order in the Molucca Islands

## Distribution and Regional Boundaries
The presence of mammals of the Marsupial order in the Molucca Islands and Celebes serves as a biological indicator of the margin of the Australian region. This distribution is highlighted by a sharp contrast with adjacent islands located to the west of Wallace's line, specifically Borneo and Java, as well as the broader Indian region, where no marsupials have been found.

## Comparative Fauna of the Archipelago
The Molucca Islands and Celebes are characterized by the absence of several mammal groups that are abundant in the western part of the Archipelago. These absent groups include:
*   Ruminants
*   Insectivora
*   Carnivora
*   Quadrumana

While certain animals are absent, other unique species exist in the region. For example, the singular ruminant *Anoa* and a peculiar form of baboon known as *Cynopithecus* are found in Celebes; these animals appear to be allied to African animals rather than Asiatic ones. Additionally, the babirusa is a member of the fauna found in the Moluccas, though it occurs only on the island of Buru.

## Related Biological Observations
The distribution of mammals in this region mirrors patterns seen in other animal classes. Birds of the Indian region disappear abruptly at Wallace's line, while many Australian bird forms reach the line but do not cross it. Celebes also hosts bird species akin to those found in Africa.

In contrast to the terrestrial mammals, marine orders such as Cetacea and Sirenia show different patterns; the Dugong (*Halicore*) is found exclusively in the Indian Ocean, and the dolphin *Platanista* is peculiar to the Ganges river.

## Anatomical Characteristics of Marsupials
From a comparative anatomy perspective, mammals of the Marsupial order are distinguished by their thymus gland. Marsupials are noted for possessing a well-developed thoracic thymus as well as a cervical thymus.
