# Anthocerotales

The Anthocerotales are a small and very natural group of plants within the Bryophyta. This group is characterized by its distinct biological structure and an isolated phylogenetic position among the liverworts (Hepaticae). It comprises three genera: *Anthoceros*, *Dendroceros*, and *Notothylas*.

## Morphology and Gametophyte Structure
The gametophyte of the Anthocerotales consists of a thallus. In the genus *Anthoceros*, which includes three species found in Britain growing in ditches and on the damp soil of fields, the thallus is dark green and composed of parenchymatous cells with an ill-defined midrib. Typically, a single large chloroplast is present in each assimilating cell.

The apical region of the plant is protected by mucilage, which is secreted by mucilage slits—small pit-like depressions located between the superficial cells of the lower surface. Mucilage may also form within the intercellular spaces of the thallus. These mucilage slits often become enlarged due to the constant presence of *Nostoc* colonies living within them.

## Reproductive Organs
The sexual organs of the Anthocerotales are scattered across the upper surface of the thallus. The antheridia are globular and stalked; they are exceptional because they are formed endogenously within special intercellular spaces. The cavity containing them is bounded by a superficial layer of cells that does not break down until the antheridia are nearly mature, though they occasionally develop on the surface of shaded thallus portions.

The archegonia have necks that barely project above the general surface of the thallus. While they differ in detail, their structure and development are in agreement with other Hepaticae.

## The Sporogonium
The sporogonium of the Anthocerotales is highly distinct, possessing a sterile columella and the capacity for spore production and growth over a long period. A young sporogonium is protected by a thick calyptra, which is derived from the thallus tissue surrounding the archegonium.

The structure of the sporogonium includes:
* **The Foot:** A large bulbous foot with superficial cells that grow out into processes.
* **The Capsule:** A long capsule that can reach a length of one and a half inches. It continues to grow for months, driven by a zone of cells located between the capsule and the foot.
* **The Wall:** The capsule wall is several layers of cells thick. Because the underlying cells possess chlorophyll and the epidermis contains functional stomata, the wall is capable of assimilation.
* **Internal Structure:** A strand of narrow elongated cells forms the columella in the center. Between this columella and the wall, spores and elaters are formed from a dome-shaped archesporium.

## Phylogenetic Position
Within the Bryophyta, the Anthocerotales occupy an isolated position. While some researchers, such as Leitgeb, suggested a relationship with the Jungermanniales, recent research has emphasized their isolation. There are no known connecting forms between the Anthocerotales and other groups such as the Hepaticae, Musci, or Sphagnales. Consequently, the relationship of the Anthocerotales to the main stem of the Bryophyta remains problematical, contributing to the open question of whether the Bryophyta are a polyphyletic or monophyletic group.
