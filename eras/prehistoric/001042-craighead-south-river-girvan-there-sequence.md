# Craighead

Craighead is a location situated within the Stinchar valley in the county of Ayrshire, Scotland. It is noted in geological records for the presence of limestones dating to the Upper Llandeilo age.

## Geological Context
Craighead is part of the broader geological landscape of southern Ayrshire, where the hilly regions primarily form part of the Silurian tableland of southern Scotland. The area is characterized by a complex arrangement of strata and volcanic activity. 

The limestones found at Craighead and in the Stinchar valley cover unconformably a variety of older materials, including Radiolarian cherts, mudstones, lavas, and plutonic rocks such as granite, gabbro, and serpentine. Specifically, the volcanic materials of the Arenig age—comprising agglomerates, tuffs, and lavas—are found in the Ballantrae region, while the Silurian belt extending from the mouth of Loch Ryan to the Merrick range consists of shales, greywackes, and grits.

## Regional Geography and Stratigraphy
Craighead is located in a region where the geological sequence varies significantly. South of the river Girvan, there is a sequence of strata ranging from Llandovery-Tarannon to Llandeilo-Caradoc. Notable sections of these strata are visible in Penwhapple Glen near Girvan and on the shore north of Kennedy's Pass. Additionally, Llandovery strata appear as an inlier north of the Girvan at Dailly, surrounded by Carboniferous formations and Old Red Sandstone.

## The County of Ayrshire
Craighead lies within Ayrshire, a south-western Scottish county bounded by Wigtownshire to the south, Kirkcudbrightshire to the south-east, Dumfriesshire and Lanarkshire to the east, and Renfrewshire to the north. The county is bordered to the west by the Firth of Clyde. 

Historically, the shire was divided into three districts:
* **Cunninghame**: North of the Irvine.
* **Kyle**: Located between the Irvine and the Boon.
* **Carrick**: South of the Doon.

Politically, the county is split into North Ayrshire (comprising Cunninghame) and South Ayrshire (comprising Kyle and Carrick). The landscape is generally undulating, with a larger mountainous tract located in the south and south-east.

## Hydrography and Infrastructure
The region surrounding Craighead is served by several notable rivers, including the Stinchar, the Girvan, the Doon, the Ayr, the Irvine, and the Garnock. The Ayr is the longest of these, flowing approximately 38 miles from Glenbuck to the Firth of Clyde.

Transportation in the county is largely managed by the Glasgow & South-Western railway, which operates a trunk line via Girvan to Stranraer, providing the shortest sea passage to the north of Ireland and Belfast. The Lanarkshire & Ayrshire branch of the Caledonian railway also serves portions of the county.
