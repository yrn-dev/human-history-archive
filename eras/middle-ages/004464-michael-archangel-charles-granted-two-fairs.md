# Fairs and Grants of Charles I

The historical record of English boroughs frequently details the granting of markets and fairs through royal charters. During the 17th century, Charles I issued several such grants and administrative changes to various towns, including Basingstoke and Bridgnorth, while also maintaining a military presence in Daventry.

## Grants to Basingstoke
In the town of Basingstoke, Charles I implemented significant administrative changes in 1641, altering the existing corporation to consist of a mayor, seven burgesses, and seven aldermen. In addition to these governance changes, Charles I granted the town two fairs to be held on Basingstoke Down: one occurring at Easter and another on the 10th and 11th of September. These grants supplemented previous privileges, such as a fair at Whitsun granted by Henry VI and a fair at the feast of St Michael the Archangel confirmed by James I.

## Grants to Bridgnorth
The town of Bridgnorth, which had been a royal borough since 1102 and incorporated by James I in 1546, also received a grant from Charles I. In 1630, the king granted the burgesses of Bridgnorth a licence to hold a fair on the Thursday before the first week in Lent, which lasted for two following days. This was in addition to a yearly fair on the feast of the Translation of St Leonard and three following days that had been granted in 1359.

## Military Presence in Daventry
Beyond the granting of commercial privileges, Charles I is noted in the archives for his military activities during the civil wars. In the summer of 1645, Daventry served as the headquarters for Charles I. This period immediately preceded the battle of Naseby, where the king was defeated.

## Related Michaelmas Fairs
While Charles I provided specific grants to Basingstoke and Bridgnorth, other towns held fairs associated with St Michael the Archangel (Michaelmas). In Barnsley, Henry III granted a fair on the vigil and feast of St Michael and two following days in 1249. In Marazion, Richard king of the Romans provided for three fairs, including two on the feasts of St Michael. Of the fairs in Marazion, only the Michaelmas fair has survived.
