# 13th Century Architecture and Art

The 13th century was a period of significant ecclesiastical construction and artistic development across Europe, characterized by the proliferation of Gothic styles and the refinement of illuminated manuscripts.

## Ecclesiastical Buildings of the 13th Century

Throughout Europe, numerous churches and religious structures were established or expanded during the 13th century. In Buda, the Matthias church was begun by King Bela IV during this period, though it was not completed until the 15th century. Buda also contains a Gothic garrison church dating from the 13th century. In Germany, the city of Lübeck features the Marienkirche, built in the 13th century, which is considered one of the finest examples of early Gothic architecture in the country. Similarly, the town of Bonn possesses a minster that dates from the 11th, 12th, and 13th centuries.

Other regional examples include the Roman Catholic church in Löwenberg, Germany, and a basilica in Bacharach, Germany, both of which date to the 13th century. Bacharach also contains the ruins of a Gothic church from the 13th and 15th centuries. In France, the town of Morteau has a church that preserves remains of a structure from the 13th century, and Montbenoit contains the remains of an Augustine abbey that existed from the 13th to the 16th centuries.

## Other 13th Century Structures and Figures

Beyond churches, the 13th century saw the construction of various fortifications and the activity of notable figures. In Ireland, the town of Kildare contains the remains of a 13th-century castle. In Scotland, a sculptured stone at Embo commemorates a 13th-century battle with the Danes, during which Richard de Moravia was killed. Richard was subsequently buried in the cathedral at Dornoch, which was built by Bishop Gilbert de Moravia (who died in 1245).

In the realm of literature and sacred poetry, Nicephorus Blemmydes was a distinguished author of *Canones* (sacred poems) during the 13th century.

## 13th Century Art and Miniatures

The art of the 13th century, particularly in the Low Countries, France, and England, followed a similar general channel, though distinct national characteristics existed. French art was noted for being clean-cut and exquisitely exact, often utilizing deep ultramarine and a copper tone for gold. English art was characterized by its gracefulness and excellence in freehand illustrative drawing, as seen in the 13th and 14th-century manuscripts in the British Museum, such as Queen Mary's Psalter. Flemish art was distinguished by a heavier outline.

A prominent trend during this century was the use of highly decorated diaper-work. This involved using brilliant colors and brightly burnished gold to create chequered patterns or lozenges, which served as backgrounds for initials and miniatures.

## Later Developments and Restorations

Many structures from the 13th century underwent significant changes or restorations in later eras. The Matthias church in Buda was used as a mosque during the Turkish occupation and was restored between 1890 and 1896. The minster in Bonn was restored in 1875 and later adorned with paintings between 1890 and 1894. In Lübeck, the 13th-century chapel serving as the entrance hall to the Hospital of the Holy Ghost was restored in 1866 and decorated in 1898.

In Buda, other notable ecclesiastical buildings include the Reformed church, which was finished in 1898.
