# Ecclesiastical Schisms and Councils of the Christian Church

The history of the Christian church has been marked by various periods of internal division, known as schisms, often arising from disputes over doctrine, the validity of sacerdotal acts, or the legitimacy of papal elections. These conflicts frequently necessitated the convening of councils to restore unity or resolve theological and political disputes.

## The Donatist Schism
In the early 4th century, a powerful sect known as the Donatists emerged in the Christian church of northern Africa. This schism was rooted in the belief that the validity of sacerdotal acts depended upon the personal character of the agent. A central point of contention was the eligibility for office of the *traditores*—those who had delivered up copies of the Scriptures during the Diocletian persecution.

The conflict intensified following the death of Mensurius, the bishop of Carthage, in 311. Mensurius had held moderate views regarding the *traditores*, leading a fanatical party, led by a widow named Lucilla and supported by Secundus of Tigisis, to oppose him. When the friends of the late bishop elected Caecilian as the archdeacon and secured his consecration via Felix, the bishop of Aptunga, the Numidian bishops were not present. Secundus and seventy Numidian bishops subsequently declared Caecilian's consecration invalid because Felix was a *traditor*. After Caecilian refused to appear before a synod of Africa, he was excommunicated.

## Early Papal Elections and the Greek Schism
From the 3rd to the 12th century, the church experienced frequent schisms. Early papal elections were often characterized by violence, venality, and the influence of civil magistrates. This changed when Alexander the Third abolished the votes of the people and clergy, vesting the right of election solely in the college of cardinals.

Efforts to resolve the schism between the Greek and Latin churches were a recurring theme in later councils. The second Council of Lyons (May 7 to July 17, 1274), presided over by Pope Gregory X, sought to terminate the Greek schism. During this period, Emperor Michael Palaeologus of Constantinople, seeking safety from King Charles of Sicily, acknowledged the supremacy of the Roman pontiff in the spiritual domain.

## The Great Schism and the Council of Constance
The Great Schism began in 1378, with Urban VI in Rome and Clement VII at Avignon. This period of division continued until 1417. Prior to the resolution, Gregory XII (pope from 1406 to 1415) had attempted to end the schism by agreeing to renounce his claim if the antipope Benedict XIII did the same. However, political interference by King Ladislaus of Naples hindered these negotiations. 

The Council of Pisa (1409) attempted to resolve the crisis by deposing both popes and electing Alexander V. This led to further complexity until the Council of Constance (1414–1418) finally ended the Great Schism, resulting in the election of Martin V as pope in 1417.

## The Council of Basel and the Schism of 1439
A new conflict arose during the tenure of Eugenius IV and the Council of Basel. Tensions developed between the pope and the fathers of Basel, particularly regarding the union with the Greek church. Emperor John Palaeologus, pressured by the Turks, desired union with the Catholics. While the fathers of Basel suggested meeting in Savoy, Avignon, or Basel to avoid papal influence, the emperor and Eugenius IV rejected these locations.

Eugenius IV dissolved the council of Basel via a bull on September 18, 1437, and summoned the fathers to Ferrara. This led to a new synod, later transferred to Florence, where a momentary and "more apparent than real" union between the Latin and Greek churches occurred on July 6, 1439.

Despite being abandoned by most members, the council of Basel continued under Cardinal Aleman. On January 24, 1438, the council suspended Eugenius IV, and on June 25, 1439, it pronounced his deposition. This created a fresh schism when the council elected Amadeus VIII, duke of Savoy, as pope on November 4, 1439; he took the name Felix V.
