# Basel

Basel is a Swiss city founded in A.D. 374 by the emperor Valentinian, after whom the town is named. Its early history was defined by the influence of the bishop of Augusta Rauricorum, who moved his see to Basel in the 5th century. For several centuries, the city's development was characterized by the growing spiritual and temporal power of its bishops.

## Political Evolution and Governance
By the 14th century, the secular influence of the bishops began to be supplanted by the rising power of the burghers. The city experienced a significant disaster in 1356 when a great earthquake nearly destroyed it. Following a period of instability where the city swayed between the Swiss Confederation and neighboring Rhine cities, Basel was admitted into the Swiss Confederation in 1501.

Political power eventually shifted into the hands of trade gilds, a trend that became especially prominent by the 18th century. Because these gilds favored the city over rural districts, tensions rose, leading the rural areas to proclaim their independence in 1832. In 1833, these districts were organized into the half canton of Basel Landschaft, while the city itself formed the half canton of Basel Stadt.

## Religion and Education
Basel became a primary center for the Reformation movement in Switzerland. This shift led to the retirement of the bishop to Porrentruy in 1525. The bishop remained there until 1792 and eventually settled in Soleure in 1828, following a total reorganization of the bishopric since 1814.

The city has also been a center for higher learning. The university of Basel hosted professors such as Jakob Burckhardt, who served as a professor of history during three separate periods: 1845–1847, 1849–1855, and 1858–1893. Burckhardt remained faithful to Basel, refusing professorships at Berlin in 1872 and Tübingen in 1867.

## Intellectual and Artistic Contributions
Basel has been a site of significant academic and artistic activity. In 1542, the German physician and botanist Leonhard Fuchs published his important work, *De historia stirpium commentarii insignes*, in Basel. This text featured over five hundred outline illustrations, including species of the genus *Digitalis*, which Fuchs named. Additionally, the artist Albrecht Dürer is noted to have stayed in Basel between 1492 and 1494.

## Historical Bibliography
The history of Basel is documented across various specialized works:
*   **General History:** H. Boos published volume i of *Geschichte von Basel* in 1877, covering the period up to 1501. R. Wackernagel authored a three-volume *Gesch. d. Stadt Basel* (1906 sqq.), and A. Burckhardt wrote *Bilder aus d. Geschichte von Basel* (3 vols., 1869–1882).
*   **Legal and Constitutional:** A. Heusler wrote *Verfassungsgeschichte d. Stadt Basel im Mittelalter* (1860) and *Rechtsquellen von Basel* (1856–1865).
*   **Institutional and Economic:** T. Geering wrote on the city's trade and industry in 1885. The history of the university is covered by W. Vischer (1460–1529) and R. Thommen (1532–1632).
