# Sigismund Korybutovic

Sigismund Korybutovic was a Polish prince who served as a regent in Bohemia during the early 15th century, a period marked by significant political instability and religious conflict.

## Arrival and First Regency
Following a meeting of the diet at Caslav on June 1, 1421, royal authority in Bohemia ceased when Sigismund (the Hungarian king) was deposed. The diet decided that a Polish prince should be chosen as the sovereign. In the interim, a provisional government consisting of twenty men from various parties was established.

In 1422, after Sigismund had invaded Bohemia and suffered a decisive defeat at Nemecky Brod (Deutschbrod) at the hands of Zizka, Sigismund Korybutovic arrived in Bohemia. He was recognized as regent by a large majority of the inhabitants. However, his first tenure was brief; due to the influence of the papal see, the rulers of Poland recalled him after a stay of only a few months.

## Civil War and Return
The departure of Prince Korybutovic led to the first outbreak of civil war between two factions of Hussites: the advanced Taborite party and the moderate Hussites, who were also known as Utraquists or Calixtines. While isolated disturbances had occurred between these groups previously, Korybutovic's absence coincided with the escalation into open conflict.

The Bohemians were eventually reunited by two factors: the return of Prince Korybutovic and the threat of an invasion by Germans. This unity resulted in a decisive victory for the Bohemians over the Germans at Aussig in 1426, a battle in which Korybutovic participated.

## Departure and Legacy
Despite his role in the victory at Aussig, Korybutovic soon incurred the dislike of the extreme Hussites. This opposition obliged him to leave Bohemia. His final departure marked the end of all hopes for the establishment of an independent Slav dynasty within Bohemia.
