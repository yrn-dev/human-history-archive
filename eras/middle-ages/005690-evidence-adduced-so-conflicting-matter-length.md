# Evidence and Conflicting Accounts in Historical and Scientific Inquiry

The process of establishing historical and scientific fact often relies on the presentation of evidence, though the reliability of such evidence varies significantly depending on the source, the nature of the subject, and the presence of contradictory accounts.

## Historical Contradictions and Oratory
In the study of diplomatic transactions between Athens and Philip from 347 to 346 B.C., historians face significant challenges due to the contradictory assertions of two rival orators, Æschines and Demosthenes. In 343-342 B.C., Demosthenes impeached Æschines for the corrupt betrayal of Athenian interests during a second embassy to Philip. While the remaining speeches—including the *De Falsâ Legatione* and Æschines' defense—provide copious details and a vivid sense of the era's sentiment, they are considered "painfully embarrassing" for extracting authentic history. 

The contradictions are found not only between the two rivals but also within the earlier and later discourses of the same orator, particularly Æschines. Due to the spirit of perversion and hostile feeling evident on both sides, allegations against one another are generally viewed with skepticism unless collateral grounds of fact or probability can be adduced for confirmation. However, allegations regarding matters that do not harm the opponent remain valuable, and mutual misrepresentations can sometimes be used to detect a basis of real matter of fact.

## Documentary Evidence and Silence
The absence of records is also a critical point of study. Humboldt noted that too much should not be inferred from the silence of authors, citing three undeniable facts for which no evidence exists where it would be most expected: the triumphal entry of Columbus into Barcelona is not found in the archives of Barcelona, the voyages of Amerigo Vespucci in service to the Portuguese crown are missing from the archives of Portugal, and Marco Polo makes no allusion to the Chinese Wall. Similarly, Polo never mentions the use of tea in China, despite traveling through the Tea districts in Fu Kien. In contrast, the Mohammedan merchant Soleyman, who visited China around the middle of the 9th century, describes a wholesome beverage called *sakh*, an infusion of aromatic, bitter leaves sold in all cities of the empire.

Other historical evidence is found in chronicles and annals. The *Laurentian Chronicle* (A.D. 1229) adduces evidence of Russian Christians and a Christian cemetery in the Great City (Bulgar). This is supported by the Nikon, Tver, and Tatischef annals, which relate the death of a Bulgarian Christian martyr.

## Scientific Evidence and Observation
In the biological sciences, the nature of the organism affects the weight given to evidence. The study of Schizomycetes is complicated by their minuteness, ubiquity, and the few "characters" they possess. Because of the danger of error in manipulation, researchers are cautioned against accepting evidence regarding the continuity of two forms unless there is direct and uninterrupted observation. Strictest rules are required when accepting evidence adduced to make the union of forms probable.

Regarding the theory of "spontaneous generation," a chain of evidence grew stronger through the work of Pasteur, Cohn, Brefeld, Burdon-Sanderson, de Bary, Tyndall, Roberts, and Lister. By 1872, it became clear that putrefaction cannot occur without bacteria or other living organisms. Rigid investigation eventually caused every case adduced as "spontaneous generation" to fall.

## Linguistic and Material Evidence
Evidence of similarity between languages can suggest original identity. Professor S. Kanazawa adduces striking evidence of similarity between the languages of Japan and Korea, though no such affinities exist between Japanese and Chinese. While Japan borrowed extensively from China, the original Japanese language differed significantly from its modern form, possessing little capacity for expansion compared to the monosyllabic, ideographic nature of Chinese.

In architecture, material evidence has driven safety changes. Until approximately 1895, most modern buildings used wood joists. However, evidence adduced by fire brigade experts and the occurrence of serious fires led to the introduction of fire-resisting floors. These modern constructions utilize steel girders and joists filled with concrete or patented brick materials, as mandated by authorities such as the London County Council.

## Bibliographic and Political Disputes
Evidence is often adduced through affidavits and catalogues to trace the origins of texts. In 1877, Henry Stevens noted a statement by Simeon Ruytinck that Jacob van Meteren had produced an English translation of the Bible in Antwerp using a scholar named Miles Conerdale. In 1884, W.J.C. Moens provided further evidence via a 1609 affidavit from Emanuel van Meteren, stating his father caused the first Bible to be "Englisshed" by Mr. Myles Coverdal in Andwarp and printed in Paris and London with Mr. Edward Whytchurch. However, W. Aldis Wright questioned this, suggesting the Bible in question was actually the Great Bible of 1539 or Matthew's of 1537, rather than Coverdale's of 1535.

In political history, the reliability of individuals is often a factor. The Roman officer Sylla was sent to negotiate with King Bocchus to deliver up Jugurtha. Bocchus, described as not priding himself on sincerity, fluctuated in his sentiments and debated whether to deliver Sylla to Jugurtha instead. Ultimately, Bocchus returned to his original design and delivered Jugurtha to Sylla.
