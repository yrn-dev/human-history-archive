# Thomas of Stitny

## Overview and Intellectual Context
Thomas of Stitny (c. 1331–1401) was a significant writer on philosophical and religious subjects in 14th-century Bohemia. His work emerged during a period when Bohemia had attained a high intellectual level, which enabled writers of the era to produce scholarly texts using the national language. 

## Works and Influence
Stitny is recognized as one of the most important writers of his period. His writings are noted for containing many ideas that were similar to those of his contemporary, Wycliffe. Among his surviving works, the most valuable are:
*   *O obecnych vecech Krestanskych* (concerning general Christian matters)
*   *Besedni reci* (which may be roughly translated as "learned entertainments")

Because of his contributions and those of other contemporaries whose Bohemian writings have since perished, Stitny is identified as one of the forerunners of Huss.

## Connection to Jan Huss
The intellectual trajectory established by Stitny and his peers influenced Jan Huss, who also wrote in both Latin and the Bohemian language. Huss contributed significantly to the development of the Bohemian language through several works, including his 1412 exposition of the Lord's Prayer, the ten commandments, and the creed (*Vyklad viry, desatera Boziho prikazani, a patere*). Other notable works by Huss include a collection of sermons known as the *Postilla*, a large collection of letters (including touching accounts written while in prison), and treatises regarding simony (*O svatokupectvi*) and the true road to salvation (*O poznani cesty prave k spaseni*).
