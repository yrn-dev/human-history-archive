# BASOCHE

The Basoche (also referred to as Bazoche, Basoque, Basogue, or Bazouges) was an ancient French gild of clerks. The term is derived from the Latin *basilica*, used in the sense of law courts. The gild served as the body from which legal representatives, known as *procureurs*, were recruited.

## Origins and Evolution
The Basoche is described as being very ancient, predating even the gild of the *procureurs*, with which it frequently experienced variance. It originated during a period when the profession of the *procureur* (which encompasses the roles of advocate, legal representative, or procurator) was "free." During this time, individuals who provided these legal services were not yet considered public or ministerial officers.

To facilitate these services, groups of men skilled in law—or reputed to be so—were established near important juridical centers. These clerks initially filled the roles of advocate or representative indifferently. This was the foundation of the Basoche of the parlement of Paris, which organized itself into a gild similar to other trades and professions of the Middle Ages.

Over time, this organization disintegrated into more specialized bodies. One branch became the advocates, and another became the legal representatives. The profession of the latter was regularized in 1344 and eventually became a saleable charge. The remaining original clerks formed the "new" Basoche, which consisted of those working as clerks for the *procureurs*. While some of the wealthier clerks aspired to become *procureurs* themselves, the group retained elements of its original status. An 18th-century author noted that they were permitted to plead before the *juge auditeur* and M. le lieutenant civil *sur les referes* (a procedure for obtaining provisional judgments in urgent cases). Consequently, the *procureurs* of that era were essentially former Basoche clerks who were granted preference over other clerks to officiate in important cases.

## Structure and Governance
The Basoche preserved several picturesque traditions and titles. It was referred to as the "kingdom of the Basoche." For a significant period, the chief of the gild was elected annually by a general assembly and held the title of "king." However, by order, this title had to be relinquished toward the end of the 16th century.

## Prerogatives and Legal Powers
The Basoche maintained two primary legal prerogatives:

1.  **Qualification of Procureurs**: To be recognized as a qualified *procureur*, an individual was required to complete a "stage" in the Basoche, which involved being entered by name on its register for ten years. Simply serving as a clerk to a *procureur* and being registered at that officer's office was insufficient. This requirement led to frequent conflicts during the 18th and 17th centuries between the *procureurs* and the Basoche. While some decisions favored the *procureurs*, the parlement generally upheld the rights of the Basoche. This support was driven by a dread of *certificats de complaisance* issued by *procureurs*, though such certificates remained valid in regions where no Basoche existed.
2.  **Judiciary Powers**: The law recognized the Basoche's judiciary powers. It held disciplinary jurisdiction over its own members and decided civil law personal actions brought by one clerk against another, or by an outsider against a clerk. If a *maitre des requetes* delivered the judgment, it was authoritative and could only be contested via a civil petition before the ancient council of the Basoche.

## Regional Variations and Other Basoches
While the Basoche of the Palais de Justice was prominent, other versions existed:
*   **The Chatelet of Paris**: This body had its own special basoche, which claimed to be even older than that of the Palais de Justice, leading to disputes over certain rights.
*   **The Cour des Comptes of Paris**: The clerks of the *procureurs* here had a Basoche of great antiquity known as the "empire de Galilee."
*   **Local Basoches**: The Basoche of the Palais de Justice once held the right to create provostships within the jurisdiction of the parlement of Paris, resulting in several local basoches.
*   **Independent Basoches**: Some were independent in origin, including the Basoche of the parlement of Toulouse and the "regency" of Rouen.
