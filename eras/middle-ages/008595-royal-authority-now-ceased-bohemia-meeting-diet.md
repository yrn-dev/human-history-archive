# Royal Authority and Constitutional Evolution in Bohemia

## The Crisis of the 15th Century
Royal authority in Bohemia ceased during the early 15th century, leading to a period of significant political instability. At a meeting of the diet at Caslav on June 1, 1421, Sigismund was deposed. The diet decided to choose a Polish prince as sovereign and established a provisional government consisting of twenty men from various parties. Although Sigismund invaded Bohemia again in 1422, he was decisively defeated by Zizka at Nemecky Brod.

Sigismund Korybutovic, a Polish prince, arrived in Bohemia and was recognized as regent by most inhabitants. However, he was recalled by Polish rulers after a few months due to the influence of the papal see. His departure triggered the first civil war between the advanced Taborite party and the moderate Hussites, known as Utraquists or Calixtines. The threat of German invasion and the return of Prince Korybutovic eventually reunited the Bohemians, who won a decisive victory over the Germans at Aussig in 1426. Korybutovic later left Bohemia after incurring the dislike of extreme Hussites, ending hopes for an independent Slav dynasty. In 1427, German princes launched a new crusade against the Hussites, including 1000 English archers under Henry Beaufort, bishop of Winchester and papal legate. The crusaders fled into Bavaria after being seized by panic at Tachau and Mies. Following this, the Bohemians took the offensive, invading the German states and Hungary.

## The Reign of Vladislav and the Rise of Serfdom
Under King Vladislav, the royal prerogative decreased while the power of the nobility reached an unprecedented height. A decree in 1487 practically established serfdom in Bohemia, a measure of immense importance that deprived future rulers of the independent peasantry that had formed the armies of the Prokops and Zizka. Vladislav's "regulations" further curtailed the rights of townsmen, codifying the rights of knights and nobles while ignoring those of the towns. This led to civil discord and the formation of a confederacy by the towns in 1513 under Prince Bartholomew of Munsterberg.

Vladislav died in 1516, succeeded by his son Louis, who had been crowned at age three. While the Bohemian estates recognized the guardianship of Emperor Maximilian I and Sigismund, king of Poland, they refused to allow these guardians to interfere in Bohemian affairs. During this time, the country was ruled largely without control by great nobles, specifically the supreme burgrave Zdenek Leo, lord of Rozmital. In 1517, the diet of St Wenceslas settled long-standing discords; citizens renounced certain privileges, and the other estates recognized municipal autonomy and the presence of towns at the diet.

## Habsburg Consolidation and Religious Strife
The transition to Habsburg rule brought new tensions. Ferdinand I established the Jesuits in Bohemia to make the nobility amenable to the royal will. Following a revolt, Ferdinand oversaw the "Krvavy'sneem" (bloody diet) on August 22, where four leaders—two citizens of Prague and two knights—were decapitated. In 1562, Ferdinand secured the coronation of his son Maximilian to ensure the succession.

By the early 17th century, conflict erupted between King Rudolph and his brother Matthias. Matthias invaded Bohemia and sought the support of the estates, but the Bohemians declined his overtures in 1608. A settlement was reached on June 25, 1608, where Rudolph retained the Bohemian crown but ceded the government of Moravia, Hungary, and Austria to Matthias.

The diet of January 20, 1609, became a pivotal event. The estates demanded religious liberty, requesting that the *Confessio Bohemica* be recognized as a fundamental law and that Protestants (including Lutherans, Utraquists, and Bohemian Brethren) and Roman Catholics have equal rights to state offices. They also sought to limit the Jesuits' ability to acquire land. Despite these demands, Rudolph stated on March 31 that he would grant no concessions regarding religion.

## The Thirty Years' War and the Loss of Autonomy
The battle of the White Hill marked a turning point. Following the execution of national movement leaders on June 21, 1621, Ferdinand implemented wholesale confiscations of land, driving the ancient nobility into exile. Catholicism was re-established through "terrible cruelty," and those refusing the Roman church were expelled. In 1627, Ferdinand published a decree suppressing Bohemia's ancient free constitution. The new system proclaimed the Bohemian crown hereditary in the house of Habsburg and added the clergy as a primary estate. The diet lost legislative power, and the German language was granted equal right with Bohemian in law-courts and government offices.

Bohemia remained a theater of the Thirty Years' War, facing occupation by Saxons in 1631 and pillaging by Swedish troops, culminating in the Swedish capture of part of Prague in 1648.

## Later Habsburg Rule and Nationalist Movements
In 1720, the estates sanctioned the Pragmatic Sanction, recognizing female succession to the throne for Maria Theresa. Despite this, Charles, elector of Bavaria, invaded in 1741 and occupied Prague. Maria Theresa eventually recovered the territory. Under her and Joseph II, Bohemian autonomy was further limited; separate law-courts were abolished in 1749, and an Austro-Bohemian chancellor was appointed to control administration.

In the 19th century, nationalist movements resurfaced. In 1611, the archduke Leopold of Austria had previously invaded Prague but was defeated by the estates. Later, King Matthias, who had once allied with Protestants, adopted the traditional Habsburg policy favoring the Church of Rome. In 1615, a general diet reaffirmed the use of the national language, decreeing Bohemian as the official language.

By 1868, Czech leaders presented a "declaration" to the diet at Prague, demanding the full restoration of the Bohemian kingdom and asserting that no foreign assembly could impose taxes in Bohemia. They sought a separate settlement similar to that of Hungary. This led to serious riots and the imposition of exceptional laws by the government.
