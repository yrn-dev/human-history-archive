# Hanslape Castle

## Overview and Conflict
Hanslape Castle was a fortification located in Buckinghamshire. During the period of conflict between King John and his barons, the castle was held by William Mauduit, who maintained it against the king. This resistance continued until 1216, at which point the castle was captured and demolished by Falkes de Bréauté.

## Regional Context of Buckinghamshire
The history of Hanslape Castle is situated within the broader historical trajectory of Buckinghamshire. During the Norman invasion, the county was likely part of the earldom of Leofwine, the son of Godwin. Because the region provided support to Leofwine at the battle of Hastings, it was subjected to sweeping confiscations following the Conquest. Due to its proximity to London, Buckinghamshire was frequently involved in major national events over the following centuries.

## Later History of the County
Following the era of King John, Buckinghamshire experienced further significant upheavals. The county was severely impacted by the Black Death, with districts such as Winslow becoming almost entirely depopulated. 

During the English civil war, Buckinghamshire was among the first counties to join an association for mutual defence on the side of the parliament. This association established important garrisons in locations including Brill and Aylesbury. Conversely, royalist troops briefly garrisoned Newport Pagnell, and in 1644, the king established his headquarters at Buckingham.
