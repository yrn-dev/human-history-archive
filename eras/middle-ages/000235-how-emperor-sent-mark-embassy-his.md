# HOW THE EMPEROR SENT MARK ON AN EMBASSY OF HIS

## Appointment and Nature of the Mission
Following the arrival of Messer Nicolo and Messer Maffeo Polo, accompanied by Mark, at the court of the Great Kaan, Mark was sent by the Lord on an embassy. While the records describe this as an "ambassage," it is noted that the term "Commissioner" may more accurately express the nature of his employment than "Ambassador" in the modern sense. This distinction is based on the term *Ilchi*, which was likely the term in Mark's mind. The *Ilchi* were a diverse class of individuals employed on government commissions; for instance, in every city, there were typically between one hundred and two hundred *Ilchis* who were entitled to force citizens to provide them with free quarters.

## Return and Report to the Emperor
Upon the conclusion of his mission, Mark returned from the embassy and presented himself before the Emperor. He provided a formal report regarding the business he had been charged with, confirming that the mission had been successfully accomplished.

Following the official report, Mark provided an account of the "novelties and strange things" he had seen and heard during his travels. He delivered this account in an intelligent and pleasant manner.

## Recognition and Title
The Emperor and all those who heard Mark's story were surprised by his account. They remarked that if the young man continued to live, he would certainly become a person of great ability and worth. As a result of this recognition, he was thereafter entitled "Messer Marco Polo."
