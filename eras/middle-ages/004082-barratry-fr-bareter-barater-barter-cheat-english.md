# BARRATRY

Barratry, derived from the Old French *bareter* or *barater* (meaning to barter or cheat), refers to several distinct legal concepts depending on the jurisdiction and the context of the law being applied.

## English Criminal Law
In English criminal law, the offense known as common barratry consists of the act of constantly stirring up and inciting quarrels in a manner that disturbs the peace. This behavior may occur within courts or in other locations. Barratry is recognized as an offense under both statute and common law, and those found guilty are subject to imprisonment and fines.

The law treats barratry as a cumulative offense, meaning that for a conviction to occur, it must be proven that the act was committed at least three times. Under a statute enacted in 1726, any individual belonging to the legal profession who was found guilty of common barratry was disabled from practicing law in the future.

For nearly two hundred years, there were no records of indictments being preferred for this specific offense. However, a case occurred in 1889 at the Guildford summer assizes, *R. v. Bellgrove*. In this instance, the charge of barratry was not proceeded upon because the defendant was convicted of a different offense.

## Marine Insurance and Maritime Law
In the context of marine insurance, barratry is defined as any fraud committed by the master of a ship against the ship's insurers or owner. Such acts are performed with the intent that the master benefit himself at the expense of the owners or insurers.

Continental jurists apply a broader definition to the term. In their view, barratry encompasses any willful act committed by the crew or the master—regardless of the motive—that results in the charterers or owners being damnified. Because of these risks, it is common practice in bills of lading to except barratry from the liability of the shipowners.

## Scottish Law
In Scotland, the definition of barratry differs significantly from the English interpretation. In the Scottish legal system, barratry is the crime committed when a judge is induced by bribery to pronounce a judgment.
