# Weather and Wind Patterns

The study of global weather and wind patterns reveals a wide variety of climatic conditions, ranging from the extreme cold of polar regions to the stagnant heat of the equator, as well as the volatile nature of stormy weather and its impact on human activity.

## Regional Climates and Temperature Extremes

Climatic conditions vary significantly by geography. In certain temperate regions, humidity increases the extremes of both heat and cold. In such areas, sea-level temperatures typically range from an ordinary January low of 48°F (with a known extreme of 45°F) to an ordinary July high of 82°F (with a known extreme of 86°F).

In contrast, the climate of Greenland is characterized by uncertainty, shifting suddenly between dense fog, heavy snow, icy winds, and bright sunshine. The interior of Greenland acts as a "pole of cold" and is described as an elevated desert of snow. Temperatures in the interior are severe; on Nansen's expedition, nighttime temperatures in early September reached approximately -49°F, and winter minimums in the inland ice may sink to -90°F. These extremes are caused by the radiation of heat from the snow-surface into rarefied air. Daily temperature ranges in this region can be as large as 40°F. On the 64th parallel at an elevation of 6,560 ft., the mean annual temperature is estimated at -13°F (or 5°F when reduced to sea-level).

## Wind Systems and Monsoons

Wind directions often follow seasonal patterns, though local influences can disguise these general laws. In some temperate zones, prevailing winds are from the north-west, west, and south during winter, shifting to the north, north-east, and east during summer.

In the Asiatic seas, the south-west monsoon begins in the Arabian Sea with north-westerly and west winds, which transition to south-west as the year progresses, before returning to north-west and north in the autumn. In the Bay of Bengal, the south-west monsoon is characterized by winds from the south and south-east, followed by north-east winds after October. These winds are further modified by geography; for instance, the south-west monsoon generally does not extend over land as a south-west wind, as the air is diverted toward areas of least pressure and loses force. By the time these winds reach the northern extremity of the great Indian plain, their northward velocity is nearly exhausted, and they hardly penetrate the Indus basin or the Himalaya ranges.

Near the equator, the "Doldrums" represent a region of calms where trade-winds die away. This area is characterized by constant precipitation and weather that is vaporous, hot, close, and dispiriting.

## The Impact of Storms and Weather on Navigation

Stormy weather has historically posed significant dangers to maritime navigation and military expeditions. Winds from the south-west and west are noted for making the navigation of certain coasts very dangerous. 

Historical accounts illustrate the risks and strategic uses of such weather:
* **Expeditions:** Harald, son of Godwin, was driven into the ocean by a contrary wind during an expedition to Bretland, eventually landing in Normandy after a dangerous storm. The continuing stormy weather prevented him from returning to sea until winter.
* **Military Strategy:** During the Roman campaign against Allectus, the præfect Asclepiodatus led a squadron that sailed on a stormy day with a side-wind—a feat of daring courage given the imperfect art of navigation at the time. This force used a thick fog as cover to escape the fleet of Allectus and land safely on the western coast.
* **Tactical Advantage:** In the conflict involving Brasidas, stormy weather and light snow provided a cover of suddenness that helped him drive in a guard and take control of property outside Amphipolis.

Conversely, favorable weather can be a decisive factor. During a final embarkation in the Aegean, British commanders found the weather warm and clement with light moist winds. The eventual evacuation occurred in a "perfect calm" with the sea "still as a mill-pond," allowing transports to move in and out of the bay in dead quiet.

## Weather in Sacred Texts

The Qur'an utilizes the imagery of stormy weather to present a parable regarding those who reject their Lord. The text teaches that the works of those who disbelieve are like ashes upon which the wind blows hard or "blows furiously on a tempestuous day." The vision presented is one of extreme failure and great error, where such individuals have no control over anything they have earned.
