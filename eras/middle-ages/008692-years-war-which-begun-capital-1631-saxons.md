# Thirty Years' War

The Thirty Years' War was a major conflict that had its beginnings in the capital of Bohemia. The war was characterized by extensive hostilities across various regions, including Bohemia, Northern Germany, and Pomerania, and was influenced in part by the divided state of German Protestantism resulting from theological differences.

## Conflict in Bohemia
Bohemia served as a primary theatre of hostilities for a significant portion of the war. In 1631, the Saxons occupied a large part of the region and made attempts to re-establish Protestantism. During the later stages of the conflict, Swedish troops frequently pillaged Bohemia. The war's final event occurred in 1648 with the capture of part of Prague by the Swedish general Konigsmark. Following these events, Swedish envoys attempted to secure a degree of toleration for Bohemian Protestants, but these efforts were unsuccessful due to the inflexibility of the imperial representatives.

## Impact on Northern Germany and Pomerania
The war significantly affected various cities and fortifications in Northern Germany and Pomerania:

*   **Greifswald:** Imperialists converted Greifswald into a fortress during the war. However, they vacated the city in 1631, leaving it in the possession of the Swedes. Greifswald remained under Swedish control following the peace of Westphalia.
*   **Kolberg:** This Pomeranian town was captured by the Swedes in 1631. According to the treaty of Westphalia, it subsequently passed to Frederick William I, the elector of Brandenburg, who worked to strengthen the town's fortifications.
*   **Lüneburg:** The town of Lüneburg suffered heavily during the course of the Thirty Years' War.

## Theological and Diplomatic Context
The disasters of the Thirty Years' War were contributed to by the divisions within German Protestantism. In an effort to unite the two confessions, several conferences were convened, including meetings at Leipzig in 1631, Thorn in 1645, and Cassel in 1661. None of these conferences were successful in achieving union.
