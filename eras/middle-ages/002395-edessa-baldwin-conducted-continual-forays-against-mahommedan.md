# Baldwin II and the County of Edessa

## Early Life and Rise to Power
Baldwin II, originally known as Baldwin de Burg, was the son of Count Hugh of Rethel and the nephew of both Baldwin I and Godfrey of Bouillon. He first appeared during the first crusade at Constantinople as one of the men under Godfrey. In June 1099, he assisted Tancred in the occupation of Bethlehem. Following the capture of Jerusalem, Baldwin de Burg served for a period in Antioch with Bohemund. His transition to leadership occurred when Baldwin of Edessa became the king of Jerusalem; the king summoned Baldwin de Burg and appointed him as the count in Edessa.

## Governance of Edessa
As count of Edessa, Baldwin II operated from the farthest Christian outpost in northern Syria. His tenure was characterized by continual forays against Mahommedan princes. These provocations, along with those of the restless Tancred, led to numerous attacks against his principality, particularly from Mosul and Mardin. 

In 1104, Baldwin conducted a great foray joined by Bohemund, which resulted in his defeat and capture at Balich. During his captivity, Tancred served as the guardian of Edessa and showed little interest in securing Baldwin's release. Baldwin eventually recovered his liberty at the start of 1108, which immediately sparked a struggle with Tancred for the recovery of the county. Baldwin regained his principality in September 1108, though the conflict with Tancred persisted until it was composed in 1109.

For the following decade, Baldwin ruled Edessa with success, though he was noted for his severity. His military activities included repelling an attack by Aksunkur of Mosul in 1114 and helping to defeat Aksunkur at Danith in 1115. He was also besieged in Edessa in 1110, an event from which he was relieved by Baldwin I. Beyond his conflicts with Mahommedan powers, records from Matthew of Edessa suggest that Baldwin acted with avarice toward the Armenians, plundering those of wealth and position.

## Kingship of Jerusalem
In 1118, while traveling to Jerusalem to spend Easter, Baldwin II received news of the death of Baldwin I. Upon his arrival, he was made king, a transition heavily influenced by the patriarch Arnulf. Baldwin II reigned for thirteen years, during which time he extended the kingdom of Jerusalem to its widest limits.

His reign was marked by almost incessant fighting in northern Syria. In 1119, he defeated the ami following the death and defeat of Roger of Antioch. According to the writings of Ibn al-Athir, Latin domination under Baldwin II stretched in a long line from el-Arish on the Red Sea to Mardin in the North, interrupted only by the Mahommedan powers of Damascus, Homs, Hamah, and Aleppo. During this period, the Franks controlled the primary trade routes and collected tolls from traders. His power is regarded as having reached its height around 1130.

## Succession and Legacy
Baldwin II had no male heir to succeed him. In 1128, he sent an embassy to Fulk, count of Anjou, offering his daughter Melisinda in marriage with the right of eventual succession to the kingdom. Fulk accepted and married Melisinda in 1129, receiving Tyre and Acre as her dower. Fulk subsequently became king of Jerusalem in 1131.
