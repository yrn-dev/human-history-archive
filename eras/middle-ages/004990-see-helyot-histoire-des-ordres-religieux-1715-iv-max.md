# Helyot and the History of Religious Orders

The works of Helyot, specifically the *Histoire des ordres religieux* (also referred to in some records as *Hist. des Ordres Monastiques*), serve as a significant bibliographic resource for the study of monasticism and religious orders across various traditions and eras.

## Coverage of Monastic Orders

Helyot's writings provide detailed historical accounts of several major religious institutes. In the study of the Benedictines, Helyot's work is cited as a source for the later history of the order, extending from the early period up to the beginning of the 18th century, specifically within volumes five and six of the 1792 edition of *Hist. des ordres religieux*.

Regarding Basilian Monachism, Helyot's *Hist. des Ordres Religieux* (1714), volume one, is utilized as an authority on the history and spirit of the order. Additionally, Helyot's *Hist. des Ordres Monastiques* (volume one, pages 175-181) is referenced in relation to the external evidence and authenticity of certain Ascetic rules.

## The Carmelites and Other Orders

Helyot's work also touches upon the Carmelites. In *Hist. des Ordres Monastiques* (volume one, pages 282-300), Helyot is cited in the context of "profane criticism" directed at the Carmelites by the Jesuits of Flanders—criticism that was eventually silenced by Rome and the inquisition of Spain.

Other specific references to Helyot's *Histoire des ordres religieux* include volume four, chapter four (1715), and volume three, chapters 24-29 (1714), the latter of which is cited alongside other authorities such as Max Heimbucher and C. F. Palmer in discussions regarding Saint Dominic and the English Dominican province.
