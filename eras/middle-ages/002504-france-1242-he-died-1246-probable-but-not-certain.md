# France and Global Events in 1242

## Military and Political Activity in France
In 1242, Henry III of England traveled to France. He was accompanied by Henry, a member of a cadet branch of the Baliol family. This Henry was the son of Ingelram (or Engelram) and Lora (or Lauretta), the daughter of Philip de Valoines, lord of Panmure. Henry had previously served as chamberlain around 1223 and had sided with English barons against John in 1215. Following his activities in France, Henry died in 1246.

## Mongol Movements in Europe
During 1242, the Mongol hosts, which had been victorious at Liegnitz, ceased their westward drive. The Mongols encountered hilly country and woodlands that were unsuitable for their tactics, leading them to turn southward toward Hungary. In the Hungarian plain, they massacred or assimilated the kindred Magyar. 

However, the Mongol advance was halted by the sudden death of Ogdai. This event caused trouble regarding the succession, which resulted in the undefeated Mongol hosts being recalled. Consequently, they began to pour back toward the east, moving across Roumania and Hungary.

## Political Transitions in Japan
The year 1242 marked significant shifts in the administration of Japan, specifically within the regency (shikken) and the Imperial throne.

### The Hojo Regency
Yasutoki, who had held the regency for eighteen years, died in 1242. Because his two sons had died before him, the position of shikken passed to his grandson, Tsunetoki. Tsunetoki's tenure was brief, as he died in 1246, but he is noted for inducing the shogun Fujiwara Yoritsune to resign in 1244. The stated reason for this resignation was portents in the sky. Yoritsune was succeeded by his son, Yoritsugu.

### The Imperial Court in Kyoto
In Kyoto, the 87th sovereign, Shijo, died in 1242. He was succeeded by the 88th sovereign, Go-Saga, who reigned from 1243 to 1246. The Imperial family became divided into two branches during the days of Go-Saga (1242–1246). One branch descended from his elder son, Go-Fukakusa (the senior branch), and the other from his younger son, Kameyama (the junior branch). The senior branch relied on Kamakura and provided information regarding Kyoto intrigues to the Bakufu, while the junior branch sought to reassert the independence of the throne.
