# Cyrillus and Methodius

Cyrillus and Methodius were Greek monks from Thessalonica (also referred to as Salonica) who served as missionaries and writers during the 9th century. They are generally reverenced as the national apostles of Bulgaria, although the primary scene of their labors took place among the Slavs of Moravia.

## Missionary Work and the Conversion of Bohemia
The efforts of Cyrillus and Methodius were central to the introduction of Christianity into Moravia. Their work in this region provided a successful alternative to earlier attempts to introduce the faith into Bohemia from Germany, which had largely failed due to the Bohemians' innate distrust of their western neighbors. 

The influence of Cyrillus and Methodius extended into Bohemia, where the Christian faith was adopted rapidly. A significant milestone in this process occurred around the year 873, when the Bohemian prince Borivoj was baptized by Methodius.

## Influence on Bulgaria
While the monks labored in Moravia, the Bulgarians were evangelized by the disciples of Cyrillus and Methodius. This occurred during the reign of Boris (852-884), who decided to abandon paganism for political reasons, as he found himself surrounded by Christian states. Boris was baptized in 864 with the emperor Michael III acting as his sponsor. Following this conversion, the Bulgarian nation altered its religion in obedience to the sovereign, a transition that resulted in the deaths of some boyars who resisted the change.

## Literary and Linguistic Contributions
Cyrillus and Methodius, along with their disciples, originated ancient Bulgarian literature. This body of work consisted primarily of theological texts translated from the Greek, including:
* Translations of the epistles and gospels.
* Collections of sermons and exegetic religious works.
* Lives of the saints.
* Translations of Greek chronicles and miscellanies.

The "Slavonic apostles" wrote during the 9th century—Cyrillus died in 869 and Methodius in 885. Although their original manuscripts have not been preserved, 10th-century copies allow for the reconstruction of their original language, known as "Old Bulgarian" or archaic Slavonic. This was a synthetic, inflexional language with few foreign vocabulary elements, though its Christian terminology was mainly Greek. Occasional Latin or German words found in the texts were derived from the regions of Pannonia and Moravia, where the two saints conducted their missionary work.

Over time, this language became the liturgical language for various Slavonic countries, leading to different recensions such as Russian-Slavonic, Croatian-Slavonic, and Servian-Slavonic. In Bulgaria, the spoken language evolved significantly over the following three hundred years, eventually leading to the development of "Middle Bulgarian" between the 12th and 15th centuries, which sat between the modern speech and the archaic model used by Cyrillus and Methodius.
