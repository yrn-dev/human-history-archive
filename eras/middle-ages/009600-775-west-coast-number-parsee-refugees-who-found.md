# Parsee Community of Bombay

## Population and Social Influence
Bombay is characterized as a global meeting-place of the Eastern world, possessing a variety of racial types greater than any other city. Its population is composed of several large sections, including Hindus, Mahommedans, Jains, Christians, and Parsees. Among these groups, the Parsees are one of the smallest in number, totaling some 46,000 people. Despite their small size, they are one of the most influential sections of the population. This influence is manifested in the city's economy and philanthropy, as most of the large charities were founded by Parsees and the majority of the great business houses are owned by Parsee millionaires.

## Cultural and Religious Practices
The Parsees maintain specific funerary customs in Bombay. Located towards the northern end of Malabar hill are the Parsee Towers of Silence. In these structures, the Parsees expose their dead to be devoured by vultures. Once the flesh has been removed, the remaining bones are cast into a well, where they eventually crumble into dust.

## Regional Context of Bombay
Bombay serves as a major port and is situated in a region with a complex political history. The northern Konkan area was historically held by the Mauryas of Puri. The surrounding regions saw various powers struggle for dominance, including the Chalukyas and Rashtrakutas in the southern Deccan, and the Gurjaras in Gujarat. The city is noted for its distinguished water-front and architectural landmarks, such as the Taj Mahal hotel, the Victoria terminus of the Great Indian Peninsula railway, and a line of public offices along the esplanade facing Back Bay, which are built in a Gothic style mixed with Saracenic. A museum was also established in the city, with its foundation-stone laid by the prince of Wales in 1905.
