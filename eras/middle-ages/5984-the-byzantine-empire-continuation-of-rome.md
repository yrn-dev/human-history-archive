# The Byzantine Empire: The Eternal Continuation of Rome

## Overview
The Byzantine Empire is often framed in popular history as a distinct entity that succeeded the Roman Empire. However, from a professional historical perspective, the Byzantine Empire *was* the Roman Empire. The inhabitants of Constantinople did not call themselves "Byzantines"—a term coined by 16th-century historians—but rather *Rhomaioi* (Romans).

The empire's transition from a Mediterranean hegemony to a Greek-speaking medieval power was a gradual process of evolution rather than a sudden break. While the Western Roman Empire collapsed in 476 CE, the East survived for another millennium. This survival was predicated on the strategic location of Constantinople, a sophisticated bureaucracy, and the integration of Christianity into the state apparatus.

Historians continue to debate the "point of divergence" between Rome and Byzantium. Some argue that the empire became "Byzantine" during the reign of Heraclius (610–641 CE), when Greek replaced Latin as the official administrative language. Others contend that the empire remained fundamentally Roman in its legal structures and political ideology until its final fall in 1453.

## Key Facts
*   **Legal Legacy:** The *Corpus Juris Civilis* (Body of Civil Law), compiled under Emperor Justinian I, preserved Roman law and served as the foundation for many modern European legal systems.
*   **The Great Schism:** In 1054, theological and political tensions led to a formal split between the Eastern Orthodox Church in Constantinople and the Roman Catholic Church in Rome.
*   **Strategic Bastion:** For centuries, the empire acted as a "shield" for Western Europe, absorbing the brunt of invasions from the Sassanids, Persians, and various Caliphates.
*   **Cultural Synthesis:** The empire successfully blended Roman political administration, Greek culture and language, and Christian spirituality.

## Did You Know?
1. **The Walls of Theodosius:** Constantinople's land walls were the most advanced fortifications of the Middle Ages, remaining virtually impregnable for over a thousand years until the advent of gunpowder artillery.
2. **Greek Fire:** The Byzantines possessed a secret chemical weapon known as "Greek Fire," a liquid incendiary that could burn on water, giving them a decisive naval advantage.
3. **The Roman Title:** Even in the 15th century, the final emperor, Constantine XI, viewed himself as the legitimate successor to Augustus and Constantine the Great.
