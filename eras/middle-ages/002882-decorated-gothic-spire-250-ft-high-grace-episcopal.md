# Decorated Gothic Architecture and Notable Spires

The Decorated Gothic style is represented in various ecclesiastical and civic structures across different regions, often characterized by ornate detailing and significant vertical elements such as spires and towers.

## Examples in North America
In Baltimore, the First Presbyterian church is noted as being in the decorated Gothic style and features a spire that reaches 250 ft. in height. Other significant religious structures in the city include the Grace Episcopal church, the First Methodist Episcopal church, the Roman Catholic cathedral (which has a dome 125 ft. high), and the synagogues of the Oheb Shalom Congregation and the Baltimore Hebrew Congregation.

## Examples in the United Kingdom
In Scotland, the city of Dundee contains St Paul's Episcopal church, which was designed by Sir Gilbert Scott. This church is built in the Decorated Gothic style and possesses a spire 211 ft. high. The church occupies the site of an old castle and was established due to the zeal of Bishop Forbes (1817-1875), who moved the headquarters of the see of Brechin to Dundee.

In Cornwall, England, the prevalence of the Perpendicular style means that Decorated work is relatively scanty. However, the church of St Columb Major and the church of Sheviock in the south-east contain examples of this work. The church of St Bartholomew in Lostwithiel is particularly notable for having a body that is largely Decorated and a beautiful, rich Decorated spire that surmounts an Early English tower.

## Gothic Architecture in Continental Europe
Various cities in Germany and Belgium exhibit Gothic influences in their civic and religious buildings:

*   **Louvain:** The Hôtel de Ville is described as one of the most ornate examples of pointed Gothic in the country. It was constructed between 1448 and 1463 by master mason Mathieu de Layens. The building features a façade with three storeys and ten pointed windows per storey, a lofty roof with a graceful balustrade, and towers at the angles.
*   **Danzig:** This city preserves a medieval aspect, including a beautiful Gothic town hall that is surmounted by a graceful spire.
*   **Lübeck:** The city's five chief churches are stately Gothic edifices constructed from glazed brick, featuring lofty spires. The Marienkirche, built in the 13th century, is cited as one of the finest specimens of early Gothic in Germany.
*   **Lüneburg:** This town features fine Gothic edifices from the 14th and 15th centuries, including the church of St Nicolas, which has a lofty spire, and the church of St John, which has a spire 375 ft. in height.
