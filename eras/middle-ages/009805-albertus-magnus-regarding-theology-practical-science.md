# Albertus Magnus

Albertus Magnus (1193–1280) was a prominent figure of the 13th century, an age characterized by a wealth of great men. A member of the Dominican order, he was one of the leading intellectual figures of his time, often grouped alongside other celebrated thinkers such as Thomas Aquinas and Bonaventura.

## Theological and Philosophical Contributions
Albertus Magnus sought to reconstruct Catholicism by utilizing a sounder system of reasoning. In the realm of theology, he held a specific view regarding the nature of the discipline, regarding theology as a practical science. This perspective was shared by Bonaventura, who believed that the truths of theology were uniquely suited to influence the affections.

During the 13th century, Albertus Magnus and Thomas Aquinas were the primary figures of the Dominican order in theological discussions, serving as rivals to the Franciscans, who were led by the oracle Alexander of Hales.

## Scientific and Natural Observations
Beyond his theological work, Albertus Magnus engaged in the study of the natural world and various materials. In his observations of animals, he referred to the incombustible fibre of the salamander as *pluma Salamandri*. 

He also wrote on mineralogy and gemstones. Regarding the *Balagius*, Albertus Magnus described it as the female of the Ruby Proper (or Carbuncle), noting that some believed the name derived from the word *Palatium* (palace), suggesting it was the "house" of the Ruby.

## Relationship with Other Scholars
Albertus Magnus is frequently compared to other major thinkers of the scholastic period. He is placed in a similar rank of importance to Roger Bacon, though Bacon is sometimes viewed by modern critics as a more modern thinker who was not fully appreciated by his contemporaries. 

In the study of philosophy, Albertus Magnus copied the work of Avicenna. While Avicenna used rubrics provided by Aristotle, Albertus Magnus observed that Avicenna's grouping of materials was his own, even though the materials themselves were borrowed.
