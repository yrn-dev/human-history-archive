# Identification and Identification Systems

The concept of identification appears across various disciplines in the archive's records, ranging from the geographical and historical localization of ancient sites and texts to the formal administrative systems used for personal and legal verification.

## Historical and Literary Identification

In the study of early literature and genealogy, identification often involves attributing anonymous fragments to specific authors. For example, fragments of a "Troy-book" based on Guido da Colonna's *Historia Destructionis Troiae* were once ascribed to Barbour by Henry Bradshaw, the librarian of Cambridge University. These fragments, totaling 3,713 lines (595 and 3,118 respectively), focus entirely on Trojan matters. However, critical opinion now generally opposes this ascription. Furthermore, there is doubt regarding whether these fragments can be identified as the "Stewartis Oryginale" or the "genealogy" of "Brutus lynagis" mentioned by Wyntoun in his *Chronicle*, as the scale of the Trojan story would be an undue allowance for a Scottish genealogy.

In biblical studies, identification concerns the naming of figures in ancient translations. Dr. Nestle noted that in the Syriac translation of Eusebius' history, the name Tolmai (Bartholomew) replaces Matthias, the apostle appointed to replace Judas. If this identification is accurate, the final list of apostles would include two men bearing the patronymic Bartholomew.

## Geographical and Archaeological Identification

The identification of ancient cities and landmarks often relies on a combination of name similarity and physical remains, though the latter is considered more reliable.

### Cities of Bashan
Several identifications have been proposed for the cities of Bashan:
*   **Edrei**: Identified with *Ed-Dera'a*, a site featuring Greco-Roman remains and an extensive subterranean city of unknown date.
*   **Ashteroth Karnaim**: Also known as Ashtaroth or Be-eshterah, this site has been identified with *Busrah* (Bostra), which contains Herodian ruins. Other proposed sites based on name similarity include *Tell Ashari* and *Tell 'Ashtera*.
*   **Salecah**: Identified with *Salkhat* in the Hauran, a site currently inhabited by Druses.
*   **Kenath**: Identified by Porter as Kanawat.

### Central Asia and Asia Minor
In the travels of Marco Polo, the city of Chaghan-Nor ("White Lake" in Mongol) is identified as *Chaghan Balghasun*, an old Mongol-era city with a ruined rampart located about 30 miles north of the Great Wall at Kalgan and 55 miles from Siuen-hwa. This site is known to the Chinese as Pe-ching-tzu (White City) and is also identified by Ritter and potentially S. Setzen as *Arulun Tsaghan Balghasun*.

In Asia Minor, Professor Koch observes that the Great Zab is the only point east of the Tigris that Xenophon assigns in a manner allowing for distinct local identification. Koch also notes that the parasangs specified by Xenophon are delusive as a measure of distance.

## Administrative and Personal Identification

Formal identification systems are utilized for security, legal proceedings, and government administration.

### Military and Legal Identification
During wartime, identification papers are used for security and access. An account from the German armies in the West describes a sentry demanding identification from a guest at the Crown Prince's quarters at Stenay. The guest provided an *Ausweis* signed by "von Moltke," the Chief of the General Staff, to gain entry. In broader military contexts, such as those recorded in world war events, "Identification Papers" are listed as a category of record.

### Government and Judicial Systems
The U.S. Department of Justice utilizes the Victim Notification System (VNS), which assigns individuals a Victim Identification Number (VIN) and a Personal Identification Number (PIN). These identifiers allow victims to access information regarding investigative status, the filing of criminal charges, and BOP custody status.

In financial and legal investigations, such as those involving the Right to Financial Privacy Act (Public Law 95.630), tax identification numbers are required for payments. Requests for information may be made via various legal instruments, including administrative subpoenas, search warrants, judicial subpoenas, and grand jury subpoenas.

## Scientific Identification

In the field of paleontology, the identification of fossil fungi is noted as being problematic. While the existence of fossil fungi is certain—appearing as hyphae in Carboniferous fossil woods or in amber and siliceous petrifactions—very few identifications regarding specific genera or species are considered reliable.
