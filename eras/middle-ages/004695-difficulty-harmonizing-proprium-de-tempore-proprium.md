# The Difficulty of Harmonizing the Proprium de Tempore and the Proprium Sanctorum

In the structure of the Roman Breviary, the liturgical year is organized into several distinct sections. Among these are the *Proprium de Tempore* and the *Proprium Sanctorum*. The tension between these two systems creates a significant liturgical challenge, primarily because they operate on different chronological principles and compete for the same days in the calendar.

## The Proprium de Tempore
The *Proprium de Tempore* consists of the special office of the seasons of the Christian year, spanning from Advent to Trinity. This system provides a comprehensive service for every weekday and Sunday. Its primary components include proper hymns, responsories, antiphons, and a specific course of daily Scripture readings. 

These readings average approximately twenty verses per day and are arranged by season:
* **Advent:** Isaiah.
* **Epiphany to Septuagesima:** Pauline Epistles.
* **Lent:** Patristic homilies, with Genesis read on Sundays.
* **Passion-tide:** Jeremiah.
* **Easter to Whitsun:** The Apocalypse, Catholic epistles, and Acts.
* **Whitsun to August:** Kings and Samuel.
* **August to Advent:** Prophets, Maccabees, and Wisdom books.

## The Proprium Sanctorum
In contrast to the seasonal cycle, the *Proprium Sanctorum* contains the liturgical formularies, psalms, and lessons for the festivals of saints. This section is dependent on the days of the secular month rather than the liturgical season. Much of its content consists of hagiological biography, which remains largely uncritical, though some portions were revised by Leo XIII following archaeological discoveries.

For the worshipper, the *Proprium Sanctorum* serves as a historical record of the church, mirroring the way Scripture readings provide a record of biblical history.

## The Conflict of Harmonization
The primary difficulty in harmonizing these two sections arises from the sheer volume of saints' festivals. Over the centuries, approximately 90% of the days in the year have been allotted to a saint. Consequently, the *Proprium Sanctorum* has encroached upon the *Proprium de Tempore*. 

This encroachment has practical effects on the prayer life of the church. Because many saints' festivals utilize practically the same special psalms, the result is a constant repetition of about one-third of the Psalter, while the remaining two-thirds are rarely recited. This displacement of the seasonal office by the office of the saints is cited as the chief problem facing those concerned with the revision of the Breviary.

## Tools for Resolution
The church has developed several mechanisms to manage the overlap between the seasonal and sanctoral offices:

1. **General Rubrics:** The Breviary contains thirty-seven chapters of general rubrics intended to address the difficulties of harmonization, though these only partly meet the need.
2. **Ordo Recitandi Divini Officii:** To provide more precise guidance, a "Catholic Churchman's Almanack" is published by various dioceses and countries. This document provides minute, daily directions for the proper reading of the office.
3. **Collects:** At the close of the office, short prayers known as collects are used. These prayers, which evolved from the primitive improvisational practices of bishops (as seen in the letters of Cyprian, Clement of Rome, and the *Didache*), serve to sum up the dominant idea of the specific festival being observed.

## Structure of the Breviary
The Roman Breviary is typically divided into four seasonal volumes (winter, spring, summer, and autumn). Each part comprises the Psalter, the *Proprium de Tempore*, the *Proprium Sanctorum*, the *Commune Sanctorum* (general offices for twelve classes of saints, such as martyrs, virgins, and apostles), and Extra Services. These Extra Services include the Office of the Dead, the Little Office of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and offices specific to individual dioceses.
