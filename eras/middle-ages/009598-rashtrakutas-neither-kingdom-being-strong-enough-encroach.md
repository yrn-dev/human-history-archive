# Rashtrakutas

The Rashtrakutas were a significant dynasty in western and southern India, emerging as a dominant power in the Deccan and surrounding regions. Their history is characterized by a long-term struggle for regional mastery, the eventual absorption of rival empires, and a prolonged period of geopolitical equilibrium with other northern powers.

## Rise to Power and Conflict with the Chalukyas
In the southern Deccan, the Rashtrakutas engaged in a persistent struggle for mastery against the Chalukyas. During the 7th century, the Chalukyas initially held the upper hand, overcoming the Rashtrakutas and other rivals to absorb smaller kingdoms into their empire. This Chalukyan dominance extended to various regions, including the islands of Bombay and the country around Belgaum (where they held power from the middle of the 6th century until approximately 760).

However, the tide shifted as the Chalukyas eventually succumbed to their ancient rivals, the Rashtrakutas. Upon the fall of the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas succeeded to the bulk of their dominions. This expansion included the acquisition of Gujarat, where the Rashtrakutas established a branch line of their dynasty.

## Regional Dominance and Balance of Power
Following their victory over the Chalukyas, the Rashtrakutas became one of the primary powers in western India. Their influence extended over the islands of Bombay, which had previously been under the control of the Satavahanas, Mauryas, and Chalukyas. In the district of Belgaum, the Rashtrakutas succeeded the Chalukyas as the ruling power.

Between approximately 750 and 950, a balance of power existed between the Rashtrakutas and the Gurjaras. During these two centuries, neither the Rashtrakuta kingdom nor the Gurjara kingdom was strong enough to encroach upon the other to any significant extent. Despite their strength, the Rashtrakutas were prevented from pursuing larger schemes of conquest due to internal dissensions with their branch line.

## Decline and Succession
The power of the Rashtrakutas eventually broke up. In certain regions, portions of their power survived through successor states. In the area of Belgaum, the Rattas (875–1250) emerged as a surviving remnant of the Rashtrakuta power. The Rattas eventually established Venugrama as their capital from 1210 onward, though they later succumbed to the Yadavas of Devagiri in 1250.
