# January

January is the first month of the calendar year. In the archive's records, it appears in various contexts ranging from financial crises and political appointments to historical disputes and the calculation of religious dates.

## Financial History and the Bank Rate
In the history of English banking, January 1847 marked the beginning of a period of severe pressure caused by speculation in railway enterprises that had been ongoing since 1845. At the start of the month, on January 1st, the bank rate of discount stood at 3%. Due to unanticipated anxieties, this rate was raised to 3-1/2% on January 14th and further increased to 4% by January 21st. This trend of rising rates continued throughout the year, eventually peaking at 8% on October 25th.

## Political Events and Appointments
The month of January has been a significant period for political transitions and appointments in France and the United Kingdom.

In France, Paul Doumer, a Radical politician and former governor of Indo-China, was elected president of the chamber in January 1905 and was re-elected to the position in January 1906. Shortly thereafter, on January 17, 1906, he stood as a candidate in the presidential election in opposition to M. Fallieres, receiving 371 votes against Fallieres' 449.

In the United Kingdom, the month served as the timing for a general election following the resignation of Mr. Balfour on December 4, 1905. Sir Henry Campbell-Bannerman formed a cabinet specifically with a view toward this January election. The Unionists, entering the polls with divided counsels, suffered a crushing defeat, although tariff reformers saw comparative success and Mr. Chamberlain achieved a personal triumph in Birmingham.

## Historical Records and Disputes
Archive documents highlight the importance of January dates in the verification of historical testimonies. In the case of Mary and Darnley, the reliability of reports made by Thomas Crawford of Jordan Hill was questioned. Crawford swore on December 9, 1568, that he had made notes of secret conversations between Darnley and Mary around January 22, 1567. Additionally, records suggest Crawford may have had an interview with Mary as she entered Glasgow to visit Darnley, likely on January 21, 1567.

In the context of English chivalry, the wardrobe account spanning from September 1347 to January 1349 (during the 21st and 23rd years of Edward III) provides evidence regarding the Order of the Garter, noting the issue of habits with garters and embroidered mottos for St George's Day.
