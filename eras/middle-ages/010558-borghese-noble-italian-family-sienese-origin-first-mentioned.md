# BORGHESE

The Borghese are a noble Italian family of Sienese origin. The family is first mentioned in the historical record in 1238.

## Rise to Roman Nobility
The family's presence in Rome was established by Marcantonio Borghese, who settled in the city. He was the father of Camillo Borghese (1550–1620), who was elected pope in 1605 under the title of Paul V. 

During his papacy, Paul V elevated his nephew's status, creating him prince of Vivero on November 17, 1609. Subsequently, in 1610, Philip III of Spain conferred the title of prince of Sulmona upon him. The family further solidified its position among the higher Roman nobility in 1614 through the marriage of the prince's son, Paolo, to Olimpia, who was the heiress of the Aldobrandini family.

## Later History and Napoleonic Era
In 1803, Camillo Filippo Ludovico, Prince Borghese (born 1775), married Pauline, the sister of Emperor Napoleon and widow of General Leclerc. In 1806, he was granted the title of duke of Guastalla. For a period of several years, he served as the governor of the Genoese and Piedmontese provinces. Following the fall of Napoleon, he moved his residence to Florence, where he remained until his death in 1832.

## The Borghese Palace and Villa
The family's palace in Rome is noted as one of the city's most magnificent buildings. It previously housed a splendid gallery of pictures. Most of these artworks were later transferred to the Villa Borghese, located outside the Porto del Popolo. This site, now known as Villa Umberto I, is the property of the Italian government.
