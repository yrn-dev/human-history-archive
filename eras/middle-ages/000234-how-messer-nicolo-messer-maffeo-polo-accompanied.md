# The Journey of Nicolo, Maffeo, and Marco Polo to the Court of the Great Kaan

The travels of the Polo family represent a significant venture of trade and diplomacy between Venice and the East. The party consisted of Messer Nicolo Polo, his brother Messer Maffeo Polo, and Nicolo's son, Messer Marco.

## Origins and Initial Venture
The Polo family descended from Messer Andrea Polo of San Felice, who had three sons: Marco, Maffio, and Nicolo. After the death of the elder Marco, Nicolo's wife gave birth to a son, also named Marco, who became the author of the account of these travels.

In the year of Christ 1260, during the reign of Baldwin at Constantinople, Nicolo and Maffeo Polo arrived in that city from Venice with merchants' wares. Described as noble, wise, and provident, the two brothers decided to cross the Greater Sea on a venture of trade. They equipped themselves with a store of jewels and traveled from Constantinople to Soldaia.

## Travel to the Court of the Great Kaan
Accompanied by Mark, Nicolo and Maffeo proceeded on a journey that spanned both summer and winter. Their travel to the Great Kaan took three and a half years, a duration attributed to severe cold and bad weather. 

The party eventually arrived at the court of the Great Kaan, who was residing in a rich and great city called Kemenfu. This city, also known as Kaipingfu (meaning "City of Peace") or Chemensu, was founded in 1256. Located some distance north of the Chinese wall, it served as the Kaan's favorite summer residence and was styled "Shangtu" or "Upper Court" from 1264. Upon learning that Nicolo and Maffeo were returning, the Great Kaan sent representatives on a journey of 40 days to meet them, ensuring they were supplied with all requirements and honorably entertained along the way.

## Service under the Great Kaan
After presenting themselves before the Great Kaan, Marco Polo entered the Emperor's employment for approximately seventeen years. During this period, he traveled extensively on missions entrusted to him by the Lord, and occasionally conducted private affairs with the Kaan's authority.

Marco gained the Emperor's love and favor by diligently gathering knowledge about matters that would interest the sovereign and relating these findings in regular order upon his return to court. Because of his discretion and success in executing weighty and distant missions, the Emperor treated him with great distinction and kept him close to his person, a proximity that caused envy among some of the Barons. Consequently, Marco visited or gained knowledge of more countries in the world than any other man.

## Departure and Return
Eventually, Nicolo, Maffeo, and Marco asked the Great Kaan for leave to depart. After taking their leave, they returned to their own country. The total duration of their sojourn in the Orient was twenty-six years.

## Family and Later Records
Records indicate that Maffeo Polo survived for some time after the journey. While the exact date of his death is unknown, Venetian archives suggest it occurred between February 1309 (the date of his last will) and 1318. In his will, Maffeo left 1,000 lire of public debt to a Marco Polo known as Marcolino, who is suspected to have been an illegitimate son.

Other family records mention that Maffeo's brother Nicolo had other siblings from a second marriage: Stephen, John, and Matthew. Matthew had five sons and a daughter, Maria, who inherited the family property in 1417 and married Messer Azzo Trevisano.
