# Pater Innatus and the System of Basilides

The *Pater innatus* (innate father) is the supreme God within the system described by the Fathers of the Church regarding the teachings of Basilides. This system is characterized by a series of emanations and numerical associations that reflect a blend of astronomical and oriental influences.

## The Supreme God and the 365 Heavens
According to the doctrines described by the Church Fathers, the *Pater innatus* is the source from which 365 heavens and their accompanying spirits emanated. This specific number corresponds to the astronomical conception of the heavens and their 365 daily aspects. Consequently, the supreme God is sometimes referred to by the name Abraxas (or Abrasax), a name that possesses a numerical value of 365. It is noted that the name of the Persian god Mithras (Meithras) was also known in antiquity to contain this same numerical value, suggesting that speculations regarding the Perso-Hellenistic Mithras may have been transferred to the Gnostic Abraxas.

## The Hypostases
The *Pater innatus* is surrounded by a series of Hypostases, numbering between five and seven. Irenaeus identifies five of these as Nous, Logos, Phronesis, Sophia, and Dunamis. Clemens suggests that Dikaiosune and Eirene may potentially be added to this series. This arrangement of figures surrounding the supreme deity is reminiscent of the *Ameshas-spentas* that surround Ahura-Mazda.

## World-Creating Powers
In the system of Basilides, the world originates from a group of powers—possibly seven in number—which are regarded as the lowest emanations of the supreme God. The concept of seven world-creating angels is described as a specifically oriental speculation, likely derived from the seven planetary deities of later Babylonian religion. 

The transition of these planetary deities into daemonic or semi-daemonic forces in Gnostic systems indicates the influence of a stronger monotheistic Iranian religion, which gained dominance over the Babylonian religion and consequently degraded its gods to the status of daemons. The development of Gnosticism is viewed as having its nursing-ground in the syncretism of these Persian and Babylonian religions.

## Historical Context and Interpretation
Modern analysis suggests caution when using the descriptions provided by the Fathers of the Church to understand the "true Basilides," as the sharply-defined dualism found in some sources is scarcely present in the system the Fathers describe.
