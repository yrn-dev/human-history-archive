# Struggle for the Crown: William Rufus and Robert of Normandy

## The Siege of Bamburgh
During the struggle for the crown between William Rufus and Robert of Normandy, the town of Bamburgh—which had developed around an ancient castle—became a site of military conflict. William Rufus besieged Bamburgh, but he found that the defenses of the castle were too strong to overcome immediately. In response, William constructed a new castle in front of Bamburgh, which he garrisoned and named "Malveisin," a term meaning "Evil neighbour."

The defense of Bamburgh was under the command of Earl Robert of Northumberland. Following a defeat in a sally, the castle eventually surrendered to William Rufus in November 1095.

## Related Political Unrest and Figures
The period was marked by various rebellions and shifting loyalties among the nobility. Robert of Belesme, the earl of Shrewsbury, was a prominent figure of this era. Described as a typical feudal noble—politic, eloquent, and an able military engineer, yet also greedy, cruel, and a systematic traitor—Robert of Belesme was involved in several conspiracies. He first appeared as a supporter of a rebellion against the Conqueror in 1077 and later acted as an accomplice in an English conspiracy against William Rufus in 1088. Despite this, he later served Rufus in Normandy and succeeded his brother Hugh in the earldom of Shrewsbury in 1098.

Other nobles also faced the consequences of rebellion against William Rufus. Robert Mowbray, the earl of Northumberland, was condemned to perpetual imprisonment for rebelling against the king. This imprisonment occurred only three months after Mowbray had married Maud de Laigle.
