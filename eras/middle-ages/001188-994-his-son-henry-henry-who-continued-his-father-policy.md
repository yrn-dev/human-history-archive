# The Babenberg Family

The Babenbergs were a Franconian family that held the duchy of Austria prior to the rise of the House of Habsburg. The family's earliest known ancestor was Poppo, who served as a count in Grapfeld during the early 9th century.

## Early History and the Babenberg Feud
The family initially enjoyed favor under the emperor Charles the Fat. However, this policy was reversed by the German king Arnulf, who instead favored a rival family known as the Conradines. Poppo, one of the sons of the first Poppo, served as margrave in Thuringia from 880 until 892, when he was deposed by King Arnulf. Another son, Henry, who was called duke and margrave in Franconia, died in 886 while fighting against the Normans.

The leadership of the family subsequently passed to the three sons of Duke Henry. These men took their name from their castle of Babenberg, located on the upper Main, which served as the center of their possessions. A conflict known as the "Babenberg feud" emerged between the Babenbergs and the Conradines as both families sought to expand their authority in the region of the middle Main. This rivalry intensified during the troubled reign of the German king Louis the Child at the start of the 10th century.

During this period, two of the Babenberg brothers were killed. The surviving brother, Adalbert, was summoned to the imperial court by Hatto I, the archbishop of Mainz and a regent who supported the Conradines. Adalbert refused to appear and defended his castle at Theres against the king's forces. He eventually surrendered in 906 and was beheaded, despite having been promised safe-conduct. Following these events, the Babenbergs lost their influence within Franconia.

## Transition to the East Mark and Austria
The family regained prominence in 976 through Leopold, a member of the family and count in the Donnegau. Leopold was described as the margrave of the East Mark, a frontier district of Bavaria approximately 60 miles in breadth. It is probable that Leopold received this mark as a reward for his fidelity to emperor Otto II during a Bavarian rising in 976. Under Leopold's leadership, the area of the East Mark was extended at the expense of the Hungarians, and this district eventually grew into the duchy of Austria.

## Succession of Margraves
Leopold was succeeded in 994 by his son, Henry I, who continued the policies established by his father. Henry I was followed in 1018 by his brother, Adalbert. In 1055, the succession passed to Adalbert's nephew, Ernest. Ernest received many tokens of favor due to his marked loyalty to emperors Henry III and Henry IV. He was succeeded as margrave by Leopold II.
