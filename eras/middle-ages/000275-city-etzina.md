# THE CITY OF ETZINA

## Geography and Location
Etzina is a city situated toward the north on the verge of the Sandy Desert. It is located twelve days' ride from the city of Campichu. According to archival records, the city is positioned on the river *Hei-shui* (known as *Etsina* by the Mongols), which flows east of Suhchau and empties into the lakes Sopo-omo and Soho-omo. Geographically, Etzina sits at the apex of a triangle formed by the bases of Kanchau and Suhchau, positioned on the border of the desert.

Alternative historical identifications suggest the city may be located beyond the Chukur Gobi to the east of Hami, where remains of cities and permanent villages exist; this is supported by the similarity between the city's name and the river Az-sind. Other records place the city north of Kanchau and north-east of Suhchau, though some estimates of the distance from Kanchau (120 leagues) are considered too great. In some Chinese maps of the Mongol era, the city is referred to as *Yetsina* or *Itsinai* and is listed as one of the seven *Lu* (Circuits) of the Province of Kansuh.

## Administration and History
Etzina belongs to the Province of Tangut. The river *Hei-shui* once served as part of the frontier for the kingdom of Tangut. Historical accounts indicate that the city was captured in 1226 by Chinghiz during his final campaign against the Tangut.

## Economy and Natural Resources
The inhabitants of Etzina are described as having no trade, sustaining themselves instead through cattle and cultivation. The population possesses an abundance of cattle and camels. The surrounding region is noted for producing high-quality falcons, specifically Lanners and Sakers. The landscape includes occasional small pine-woods, which are home to wild beasts and large numbers of wild asses.

## Culture and Religion
The people of Etzina are characterized as Idolaters.

## Travel and Logistics
Etzina serves as a critical waypoint for travelers heading north. Because the city is the final habitation before a vast desert that extends for forty days' journey to the north, travelers must secure forty days' worth of victuals before departing. This desert region contains no baiting-places or habitations. While it is possible to encounter people in this desert during the summer, the extreme cold of winter makes such encounters unlikely. After completing the forty-day crossing of the desert, travelers reach a northern province.
