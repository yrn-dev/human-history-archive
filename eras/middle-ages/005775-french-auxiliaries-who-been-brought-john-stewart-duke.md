# French Auxiliaries of John Stewart, Duke of Albany

The French auxiliaries brought over by John Stewart, Duke of Albany, were a military force involved in Scottish and English affairs during the early 16th century. These forces were utilized in strategic military operations, including incursions into England.

## Military Activities and the Inroad into England
The French auxiliaries under the leadership of John Stewart, Duke of Albany, participated in a military expedition characterized as an inroad into England. This specific operation is recorded as having been unsuccessful. 

Among those who joined these auxiliaries was George Buchanan, a Scottish humanist. Buchanan had previously been sent to the university of Paris in 1520 by his uncle, James Heriot. Following the death of his uncle in 1522, Buchanan was unable to continue his studies in Paris and returned to Scotland. After recovering from a severe illness, he joined the French auxiliaries brought over by the Duke of Albany and took part in the aforementioned unsuccessful inroad into England.

## Context of the Stewart Influence
The involvement of John Stewart, Duke of Albany, in bringing French auxiliaries to Scotland reflects the broader historical connections between the Stewart family and France. The archive notes that the Stewart family held the earldom of Buchan for approximately a century and a half. Within this lineage, John Stewart, the son of the Scottish regent Robert, Duke of Albany, held a significant position in France, where he was appointed as the constable of France before being killed at the battle of Verneuil in 1424.

## Aftermath and Subsequent Events
Following his time with the French auxiliaries, George Buchanan pursued further academic studies. In the year following the military inroad, he entered the university of St Andrews, graduating with a B.A. in 1525. His primary motivation for attending St Andrews was to hear the logic lectures of the celebrated John Major. When Major moved to Paris, Buchanan followed him in 1526, eventually earning a B.A. in 1527 and an M.A. in 1528.

Buchanan's subsequent career included an appointment as a professor or regent at the college of Sainte-Barbe, where he taught for more than three years. He also served as the Procurator of the "German Nation" at the university of Paris, being elected to the position in 1529 and re-elected four times in four consecutive months. He eventually returned to Scotland around the beginning of 1537 as the tutor to Gilbert Kennedy, the 3rd earl of Cassilis.
