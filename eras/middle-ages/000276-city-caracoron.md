# CARACORON

Caracoron is a city that served as the first possession of the Tartars after they departed from their own country. It is described as being approximately three miles in compass and is surrounded by a strong earthen rampart, a construction necessitated by the scarcity of stone in the region. Within the city, there is a great citadel containing a fine palace where the Governor resides.

## City Layout and Infrastructure
The city is enclosed by a mud-wall featuring four gates, each serving as a hub for specific trade:
*   **East Gate:** The sale of millet and other corn, though the supply is noted as being scanty.
*   **West Gate:** The sale of goats and rams.
*   **South Gate:** The sale of wagons and oxen.
*   **North Gate:** The sale of horses.

The internal layout consists of two primary streets. One street is occupied by the Cathayans, who are all craftsmen. The other is occupied by the Saracens and contains the marketplace. Additionally, the city contains great palaces used by court secretaries.

## Religious and Cultural Diversity
Caracoron is characterized by a variety of religious institutions. The city contains twelve idol temples belonging to different nations, two Mahummeries where the Law of Mahomet is preached, and a single Christian church located at the extremity of the town.

## The Court of Mangu Kaan
Beside the town rampart lies a great court belonging to Mangu Kaan. This area is enclosed by a brick wall, similar to a priory. The court contains a large palace where the Kaan holds a drinking-bout twice a year. The grounds also include several long buildings resembling granges, which are used to store the Kaan's victuals and treasures.

## Regional Context and Geography
The city is situated in a region where the Tartars originally dwelt on the borders of Chorcha. This ancestral land consisted of extensive plains with great rivers, sheets of water, and excellent pasture-lands, though it lacked towns or villages. Before establishing their own dominion, the Tartars paid tribute to a great prince known in their tongue as Unc Can (also referred to as Prester John). This tribute consisted of a tithe of all their gear and one beast out of every ten.

Beyond Caracoron lies a plain and the Altay, the location where the bodies of the Tartar Sovereigns are buried. Traveling north from Caracoron and the Altay for forty days leads to the Plain of Bargu. This region is inhabited by a wild race called the Mescript, who are subject to the Great Kaan. The Mescript people live on fish and birds—which they catch in lakes and marshes when the birds are moulting and unable to fly—and they ride upon stags. They possess neither wine nor corn.

## Historical Records
The city was first mentioned by name by the Western traveller Plano Carpini in 1246. Carpini visited Ormektua, the location of the Sira Orda, which was half a day's journey from Karákorum and used by Okkodai as a summer residence. In 1253, the traveller Rubruquis visited the city itself, noting that, excluding the Kaan's palace, the city was not as impressive as the Borough of St. Denis.
