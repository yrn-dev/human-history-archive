# Robert Beaumeis and Ashby-de-la-Zouch

## Land Ownership and Succession
During the period of the Domesday survey, the estates of Ashby-de-la-Zouch were part of the holdings of Hugh de Grentmaisnel. Shortly after this time, the estate was held by Robert Beaumeis. Following his tenure, the property passed through female descent to the family of la Zouch.

## Development of Ashby-de-la-Zouch
The family of la Zouch provided the adjunct to the town's name; prior to this association, the location was known as Essebi or Ashby. In 1219, Roger la Zouch secured a grant for a two days' fair held at the feast of St Helen, as well as a weekly market. This grant was obtained in consideration of a fine consisting of one palfrey.

## Later Manor History and Fortifications
In the 15th century, the manor was held by James Butler, the earl of Ormond. Following Butler's attainder, the manor was granted to Lord Hastings in 1461. In 1474, Lord Hastings received a royal licence to build and fortify a castle and to empark 3000 acres of land.

This castle served as a place of detention in 1569 for Mary, queen of Scots, who was kept there under the custody of the earls of Shrewsbury and Huntingdon. During the Civil War, the castle was held and fortified for the king by Colonel Henry Hastings, and it was visited by Charles in 1645. Following the conclusion of the war in 1648, the castle was dismantled by order of parliament.

## Economic and Cultural Significance
Ashby-de-la-Zouch is featured in the work *Ivanhoe* by Sir Walter Scott. By the 18th century, the town had become celebrated as one of the premier markets for horses in England. Additionally, the town hosted prosperous factories producing hats as well as cotton and woollen stockings.
