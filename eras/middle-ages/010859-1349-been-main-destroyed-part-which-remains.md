# Events and Figures of 1349

The year 1349 is associated with several significant political transitions, literary achievements, and architectural developments across Europe, as recorded in the archive's historical entries.

## Political Transitions and Territorial Changes
In the region of Dauphine, 1349 marked the end of the era of the independent Dauphins. Humbert II, who had reigned from 1333 to 1349 and was the grandson of the heiress Anna, was the last independent Dauphin. In 1349, he sold his dominions to Charles of Valois. Upon his accession to the French throne as Charles V, Charles bestowed Dauphine upon his eldest son. This established a tradition where the title of Dauphin was borne by all subsequent eldest sons of the kings of France.

In Bohemia, the town of Krumau (known in Czech as Krumlov) experienced a change in status during this period. Peter I, the son of Henry II of Rosenberg, died in 1349; he is credited with raising Krumau to the rank of a city.

## Literary Contributions
The 14th century saw the work of Ulrich Boner (also known as Bonerius), a German-Swiss writer of fables born in Bern. Boner was descended from an old Bernese family and is believed to have received the "tonsure," allowing him the status of *clerici uxoriati*, which permitted him to return to secular life and marry after divesting himself of clerical garb. 

Records mention Boner between the years 1324 and 1349. Around 1349, he wrote a collection of one hundred fables titled *Der Edelstein* in Middle High German. These fables were based primarily on the *Anonymus* and the works of Avianus from the 4th century. Boner dedicated this work to Johann von Rinkenberg, a poet and Bernese patrician who served as the *advocatus* (Vogt) of Brienz and died circa 1350. The work is noted for its clear, simple style and the use of humor to relieve its didactic tone. *Der Edelstein* was later printed in Bamberg in 1461 and is claimed by some to be the first book printed in the German language.

## Military Architecture
In Italy, the town of Ascoli Piceno—an episcopal see and capital of its province located on the south bank of the Tronto—features the fortress of the Malatesta. This specific fortification was constructed in 1349.
