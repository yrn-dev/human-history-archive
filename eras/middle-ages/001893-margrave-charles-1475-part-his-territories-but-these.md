# Margrave Charles I

Margrave Charles I (d. 1475) was a ruler of Baden during the 15th century. His reign was marked by territorial conflict and subsequent loss, though his successor was able to recover the family's standing.

## Territorial Conflicts and Succession
During the 15th century, Margrave Charles I was engaged in a war with the count palatine of the Rhine. As a result of this conflict, Charles I was deprived of a portion of his territories. Following his death in 1475, he was succeeded by his son, Christopher I. The territorial losses suffered under Charles I were more than repaired by Christopher I.

## The House of Baden
The title of margrave (from the German *Markgraf*, meaning "count of the March") originally designated counts established by Charlemagne and his successors to guard the frontier districts of the empire. While these officials initially held the authority of dukes within their own jurisdiction, they were subordinate to dukes in the empire's feudal army. Over time, the title lost its original frontier significance and became a rank associated with the great German princes.

The history of Baden began in 1112 when Hermann, a son of the margrave of Verona and grandson of Bertold, duke of Carinthia and count of Zaehringen, assumed the title of margrave of Baden. The family's territories underwent various divisions and reunifications over the centuries. By 1391, the margrave Bernard I had united several possessions, including Baden-Hochberg.

Following the era of Charles I and Christopher I, the house of Baden continued to evolve. In 1503, the Baden-Sausenberg family became extinct, allowing Christopher I to unite the whole of Baden. Before his death in 1527, Christopher divided the territory among his three sons. This led to further partitions, including the foundation of the lines of Baden-Baden and Baden-Pforzheim (the latter renamed Baden-Durlach after 1565).

## Later History of the Margraviate
The divisions within the house of Baden eventually led to rivalry and open warfare between the main branches of the family. From 1584 to 1622, Baden-Baden was held by a prince of Baden-Durlach. These tensions were further complicated by the Reformation, as different rulers of Baden adopted different faiths. The house remained divided during the Thirty Years' War, during which both branches of the family faced exile. The *status quo* was eventually restored by the treaty of Westphalia in 1648.

In the later 18th century, the territories were again united under a single ruler in 1771 when Augustus George of Baden-Baden died without sons, leaving his territories to Charles Frederick of Baden-Durlach. Charles Frederick, who began his reign in 1738, is noted as the most notable ruler of Baden. He focused on the administration of justice, education, and the development of commerce and agriculture. Through strategic shifts during the Napoleonic wars—initially fighting for Austria and later for Napoleon—Charles Frederick quadrupled the area of Baden before his death in June 1811.
