# Germany

## Industrial Processes and Coke Production
Germany has seen significant development in the industry of coke manufacture. While the process of coking coal in old beehive ovens or similar apparatus often results in the loss of gas issuing at the mouth of the ovens, Germany has widely adopted the method of destructive distillation of coal in closed apparatus, known as retorts, which are heated from the outside. This industry originated in France but has spread more extensively in Germany, where more than half of the produced coke is manufactured using this method.

This process of distillation increases the output of coke by approximately 15% compared to beehive ovens because the heat required is generated by burning part of the gas rather than burning part of the coal itself. The resulting coke is of a quality for iron-making that is as good as beehive coke, although it differs in appearance. Additionally, the gases produced can yield benzene vapours, tar, and ammonia, the value of which products can sometimes exceed the value of the coke itself, with an excess of gas remaining for other purposes.

## Regional Geography and Mineral Wealth of Baden
The physical configuration of Baden leads to extremes of both cold and heat. The warmest district in Germany is the Rhine valley, where the mean temperature is approximately 50° F. In contrast, the highest elevations of the Black Forest record the greatest degrees of cold in the south, with the high table-land maintaining a mean temperature of 43° F. Within this region, January is the coldest month and July is the hottest.

Baden possesses various mineral resources, although its overall mineral wealth is not considered great. The region produces lead, zinc, coal, and iron of excellent quality. Other materials obtained in small quantities include copper, silver, cobalt, sulphur, and vitriol. Additionally, salt, potters' earth, china-clay, gypsum, and abundant peat are found. Baden is also noted for its numerous celebrated mineral springs, with the most frequented being those of Petersthal, Freiersbach, Griesbach, Antogast, Badenweiler, and Baden-Baden.

## Historical Instruments of Torture
Germany historically utilized a torture instrument known as "Spanish boots," which was similar to the "boot" or "bootikin" used in Scotland. While the Scottish version involved driving wedges with a mallet between the leg and a boot made of iron or wood, the German variation included iron boots that were heated on the victim's foot.
