# Mithraism and Related Historical Studies

## The Mithraic Cult
Mithraism was a significant religious movement that developed from Zoroastrianism, a religion of ancient Aryan origin that can be traced back to the Indo-Iranian people prior to their division into Hindus and Persians. The cult entered the Roman Empire around the time of Pompey the Great and experienced wide expansion during the eras of the Antonines and the Cæsars.

The religion centered on Mithras, who was characterized as a god of light and a "Sun of Righteousness." Like the religion of Isis, Mithraism offered the promise of immortality to its adherents. The followers of this cult were primarily composed of soldiers, slaves, and people in distressed circumstances.

## Rituals and Mysteries
The core of Mithraism involved forgotten mysteries revolving around the sacrifice of a benevolent and sacred bull by Mithras. This central act is represented in Mithraic shrines, which typically contain a figure of Mithras slaying the bull. According to the cult's beliefs, the blood of this bull served as the seed of life, and new life sprang from it.

The initiation process for a Mithraist votary involved a specific ritual where the initiate stood beneath a scaffolding while the bull was killed above. As the bull bled copiously, the blood ran down onto the initiate. Through this act of bathing in the sacrificial blood, the votary was considered to be "born again."

## Relationship with Other Cults
There appears to have been a period of "theocrasia," or blending, between the Mithraic cult, the cult of Serapis-Isis-Horus, and the Christian cult. Mithraism shared certain superficial resemblances with the later ritual developments of Christianity, such as the practice of burning candles before an altar.

Some evidence suggests that Christians may have adopted several elements from these popular diffused cults, including:
* The use of Sunday as the primary day of worship instead of the Jewish Sabbath.
* The extensive use of candles during religious ceremonies.
* The legend regarding the adoration by shepherds.
* Concepts and phrases concerning Christ being a blood sacrifice or the notion of being "washed in the blood."
