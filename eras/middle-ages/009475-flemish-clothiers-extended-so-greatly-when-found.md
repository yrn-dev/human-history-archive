# Flemish Clothiers and the Woollen Trade in Bolton

## The Woollen Industry in Bolton
From its early history, Bolton was recognized for its woollen manufactures. The significance of the town as a center for the woollen cloth trade is evidenced by the appointment of an aulneger during the reign of Richard I; this official was tasked with the stamping and measuring of all bundles of woollen goods. 

The industry experienced a notable impulse in 1337 due to the settlement of a party of Flemish clothiers. This influx caused the trade to extend so greatly that by 1566, an act of parliament was required to appoint deputies to assist the aulnegers, with Bolton specifically named as one of the locations where these deputies were to be employed.

## Transition to Cotton and Other Textiles
In 1558, Leland recorded in his *Itinerary* that Bolton produced "cottons," though these were actually woollen goods. Authentic cotton goods did not appear in Lancashire until 1641. At that time, Bolton was identified as the primary seat for the manufacture of dimities, vermilions, and fustians.

The industry received further stimulation following the revocation of the edict of Nantes, which led to the settlement of French refugees in the area. Subsequent textile innovations in Bolton included the first production of velvets around 1756 by Jeremiah Clarke, followed by cotton quiltings and muslins in 1763.

## Industrialization and Expansion
The cotton trade in Bolton was given an "astonishing impetus" through the inventions of Samuel Crompton (1780) and Sir Richard Arkwright (1770), both of whom were born in the parish. The introduction of machinery led to the creation of spinning factories, with the first in Bolton reportedly established in 1780.

The growth of these factories was rapid; by 1851, Bolton housed 66 cotton mills operating 860,000 throstle spindles. Parallel to the textile trade, the industry of bleaching began in the early 18th century, and large ironworks were developed during the latter half of the 19th century. Infrastructure to support this growth included the construction of a canal from Manchester to Bolton in 1791 and the enclosure of Bolton Moor via an act of parliament in 1792.
