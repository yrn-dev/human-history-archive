# Ludus Anglicorum

The *ludus Anglicorum* is a game described in the Harleian MSS. (1527). It is identified as a game of "tables," a subject also alluded to by Chaucer.

## Game Mechanics and Characteristics
The game of tables, as referenced in the context of *ludus Anglicorum*, involved the use of three dice. During gameplay, "men" were moved from the tables of the opponent. 

This type of game is associated with backgammon, which is played on a special board using draughtsmen and the throw of dice. While the specific mechanics of *ludus Anglicorum* are linked to the "tables" described by Chaucer, backgammon itself is characterized by a system where players are liable to be sent back.

## Historical Context and Related Games
The origins of games similar to *ludus Anglicorum* and backgammon extend back to antiquity. The Romans played a similar game known as *Ludus duodecim scriptorum*, or the "twelve-line game." Additionally, Plato mentions a game in the *Republic* (bk. x.) involving the throwing of dice and the strategic placement of men. 

Regarding the timeline of these games, it has been suggested by Strutt that backgammon was invented around the 10th century. In France, a similar game is known as *trictrac*, a name that imitates the sound of rattling dice.

## Etymology of Related Terms
The terminology surrounding these games has various disputed derivations. The word "backgammon" is likely Saxon, originating from *baec* (back) and *gamen* (game). Other proposed etymologies include:
* **Danish:** *bakke* (tray) and *gammen* (game), as suggested by Wedgwood.
* **Welsh:** *bach* (little) and *cammaun* (battle), as suggested by Henry.
