# French Form in its Imperfect Condition

## Relation to the English Ballade
In the study of medieval English ballades, specifically those written by Chaucer, the absence of an envoi is a notable characteristic. This lack of an envoi indicates a relationship with the earliest French form of the ballade in its imperfect condition, as opposed to the form that was later accepted. 

The envoi serves a specific structural function in the ballade, acting as the section that ties together the preceding parts and completes the poem as a cohesive work of art. Consequently, a ballade lacking this section is considered to be in an imperfect state.

## Evolution of the Ballade in English Literature
The original ballade ceased to be written in English after the 16th century. The form was not re-introduced until the latter part of the 19th century. This revival occurred almost simultaneously through the work of several poets, including:
* Andrew Lang
* Austin Dobson
* Algernon Charles Swinburne
* W. E. Henley
* Edmund Gosse

Among these, Austin Dobson's "The Prodigals," published in 1876, is cited as one of the earliest correct English specimens of the form. In 1880, Andrew Lang published *Ballades in Blue China*, a volume that attracted numerous imitators.

## Modern Standards and Characteristics
Modern English ballades generally follow the guidelines established in the 15th century by Henri de Croi. Within English literature, the ballade is regarded as the noblest of the artificial forms of verse, surpassed only by the sonnet. 

The form is versatile, proving equally effective for the expression of mockery and pathos. When utilized by a competent poet, the ballade achieves a rich melodic effect without appearing artificial or fantastic.

## Other Literary Adaptations
While some works are considered original poems, others are translations that deviate from the form's requirements. For example, D. G. Rossetti produced a popular translation of Villon's "Ballade of Fair Ladies." However, this work is noted for entirely disregarding the metrical rules associated with the ballades.
