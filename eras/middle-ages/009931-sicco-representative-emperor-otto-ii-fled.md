# Count Sicco and the Imperial Representation of Otto II

Count Sicco served as a representative of the emperor Otto II during a period of significant instability and conflict regarding the papacy in Rome. His actions were central to the transition of power between several popes in the late 10th century.

## Intervention in the Papacy
During the 970s, the papacy was marked by volatility. Benedict VI had served as pope from 972 to 974, having been installed under the protection of Otto the Great. Following the death of Otto the Great, the citizens of Rome became turbulent, and Benedict VI was strangled by order of Crescentius, the son of Theodora. Crescentius replaced Benedict VI with a deacon named Franco, who assumed the name Boniface VII.

Count Sicco, acting on behalf of the emperor, intervened to drive out the intruded Franco. Through Sicco's intervention, Benedict VII was elected pope in 974. Following the removal of Franco, Benedict VII governed Rome quietly for nearly nine years, a state of affairs noted as being relatively rare for that era.

## Conflict with Boniface VII
The man known as Franco (Boniface VII) did not remain permanently removed from power. In 974, he had been substituted for the assassinated Benedict VI by the Roman barons and Crescentius. However, he was eventually ejected by Count Sicco. Following this ejection, Franco fled to Constantinople.

The political situation shifted again upon the death of emperor Otto II in 983. Franco returned from Constantinople and seized Pope John XIV, throwing him into prison. Franco then installed himself as pope, reigning as Boniface VII from August 984 until July 985.

## Context of the Imperial Administration
Count Sicco's activities occurred during the reign of Otto II (973–983), who was part of a line of Saxon emperors. This line began with Henry the Fowler, who was elected King of Germany in 919, and continued through Otto I (the Great), who was crowned emperor in 962. 

The administration of Otto II involved managing both ecclesiastical appointments in Rome and territorial disputes in Europe. For example, in 977, Otto II granted the government of Lower Lorraine to Charles I, a younger son of Louis d'Outremer, on the condition that Charles act as his vassal and oppose the efforts of his brother, Lothair. This period was further characterized by conflict with Lothair, the king of France, who attempted to capture Otto II at Aix-la-Chapelle in the spring of 978.
