# DISTRICT OF BULANDSHAHR

The District of Bulandshahr is located in the Meerut division of the United Provinces in British India. It encompasses an area of 1899 sq. m. and is characterized as being thickly populated and very highly cultivated.

## Geography and Climate
The district is situated on a level plain that slopes gently from the northwest to the southeast. There is a gradual, slight elevation located approximately midway between the Jumna and Ganges rivers. These two rivers are the principal waterways of the district; the Ganges is navigable throughout the year, while the Jumna is navigable only during the rainy season. Additionally, the Ganges canal intersects the district, providing facilities for both navigation and irrigation, with its headworks located at Narora.

The climate of the region is subject to extremes, experiencing excessive heat during the summer and very cold temperatures in the winter.

## History
Certain historical records for the district begin in A.D. 1018, when Mahmud of Ghazni arrived at Baran and received the submission of the followers of the Hindu raja to Islam. The city was later captured by Kutb-ud-din in 1193. During the 14th century, the district experienced invasions by Mongol and Rajput clans, who established permanent settlements.

Peace was restored with the establishment of the Mogul empire. Due to the zeal of Aurangzeb, a large proportion of the population became Mussulmans during this era. Following the decline of the Mogul empire, chieftains of the Gujars and Jats carved out petty principalities. During this subsequent period, Baran functioned as a dependency of Koil and was administered under Mahratta domination.

In 1803, the British captured the fort of Aligarh and Koil. Bulandshahr and its surrounding territory were initially incorporated into the district of Aligarh in 1805. During the Mutiny of 1857, the district gained an evil reputation; the Gujar peasantry plundered towns, and Mussulman Rajputs and Gujars were actively hostile to the government, while the Jats supported the government. Sir Alfred Comyn Lyall, an Anglo-Indian civil servant, saw service in the Bulandshahr district during the Mutiny.

## Town of Bulandshahr
The town of Bulandshahr is situated on a height on the right bank of the Kali-Nadi. Because of this location, the names Bulandshahr (meaning "high town") and Unchanagar replaced its earlier name, Baran, though it is still occasionally referred to by that name. In 1901, the town had a population of 18,959.

The town's handsome appearance is attributed to the efforts of several successive collectors, most notably F.S. Growse. Growse encouraged the local gentry to beautify their residences and was active in the construction of public buildings. Notable features of the town include a market-place, a town-hall, a public garden, a bathing-ghat, and a tank for the water supply.

## Economy and Infrastructure
The district's economy includes cotton pressing and cleaning mills as well as several indigo factories, although the indigo industry has suffered significantly due to a recent decline. The chief center for trade in the district is Khurja.

Transportation is supported by two major railway lines that cross the district: the Oudh and Rohilkhand railway and the main line of the East Indian railway.

## Demographics
As of 1901, the population of the district was 1,138,101. This figure represented a 20% increase over the preceding decade.
