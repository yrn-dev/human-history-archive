# AZO

Azo (c. 1150–1230) was an influential Italian jurist and professor of civil law. Born in Bologna around the middle of the 12th century, he is sometimes referred to as Azzo or Azzolenus. He is occasionally called Azo Soldanus, a name derived from his father's surname.

## Academic Lineage and Education
Azo was a student of Joannes Bassianus, a professor at the law school of Bologna. Through Bassianus, Azo was connected to the school of Bulgarus, a celebrated 12th-century jurist known as the "Golden Mouth." Bulgarus's school adhered closely to the letter of the law, in contrast to the school of Martinus Gosia, which focused on the equity of "the purse." Azo, along with Bassianus and Accursius, became an adherent of the school of Bulgarus and subsequently exercised a commanding influence over legal studies in Bologna.

## Career and Contributions
Azo served as a professor of civil law at the university in his native town of Bologna. Beyond his academic pursuits, he was an active participant in the municipal life of Bologna during a period when the city, along with other Lombard republics, had achieved municipal independence.

Azo held a position of great importance among the glossators. His primary contribution to legal scholarship was his *Readings on the Code*. This work provided a methodical exposition of Roman law. The *Readings* were collected by his pupil, Alessandro de Santo Aegidio, and were later completed with additions by Odofredus and Hugolinus. The authority of these writings was so significant within the tribunals that the phrase "Chi non ha Azzo, non vada a palazzo" (He who does not have Azo should not go to the palace) became a common saying.

## Pupils and Legacy
Azo gained a high reputation as a professor and taught several notable students, including Accursius and Jacobus Balduinus. Jacobus Balduinus, a Bolognese jurist of a noble family, later became a professor of civil law at the university of Bologna and was elected *podesta* of Genoa, where he was tasked with reforming the republic's law.

Azo died approximately in 1230.

## Distinctions from Other Jurists
In historical records, Azo is distinguished from two other 13th and 14th-century Italian canonists of the same name who also taught at the university of Bologna:
* **Azo Lambertaccius**: A 13th-century professor of canon law and author of *Questiones in jus canonicum*.
* **Azo de Ramenghis**: A 14th-century professor of canon law and author of *Repetitiones super libro Decretorum*.
