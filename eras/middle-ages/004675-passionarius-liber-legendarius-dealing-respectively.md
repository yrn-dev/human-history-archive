# Passionarius and Legendarius

The *Passionarius* and the *Legendarius* are liturgical books identified as necessary for the correct conduct of the canonical office. These texts are part of a broader collection of ecclesiastical works used in the daily service of the Roman Catholic Church.

## Context within the Canonical Office
The *Passionarius* and *Legendarius* are categorized among the essential books required for the canonical office, as listed by the 12th-century liturgical author Jean Beleth. In the structure of the church's daily service, these books function alongside several other specialized texts, including:
*   The *Antiphonarium*
*   The Old and New Testaments
*   The *Homiliarius*, which contains homilies on the Gospels
*   The *Sermologus*, a collection of sermons
*   The works of the Fathers
*   The *Psalterium* and the *Collectarium*

## Content and Purpose
The two books serve distinct roles in providing the readings and narratives used during the canonical hours. The *Passionarius* (also referred to as *liber*) deals specifically with martyrs, while the *Legendarius* deals with saints.

These books emerged as part of the evolution of the Breviary. In the earliest days of Christian worship, the Bible provided all necessary materials, including the psalms and lessons. Over time, the Psalter was separated into a choir-book. By the 4th century, psalms were grouped together, a practice reinforced by monks who divided the 150 psalms over a week and allotted portions to specific hours of the day. St Benedict further organized this arrangement in the 6th century. As the service evolved, additions such as collects, responses, and antiphons were added to assist those who were not skilled in metrical compositions or improvisation, leading to the necessity of the specialized books listed by Beleth.

## Relation to the Breviary
The *Passionarius* and *Legendarius* contribute to the comprehensive nature of the Breviary, which contains the offices for the canonical hours. The Breviary is characterized by its inclusion of hymns and lessons for every day of the year. However, it is distinct from other liturgical volumes in that it excludes the Eucharistic office (which is found in the Missal) and special offices for ordination, burial, marriage, and baptism (which are located in the Pontifical or the Ritual).
