# The Baliol Family: The Cadet Branch of Ingelram

The Baliol family was a prominent lineage that played a significant role in the history of Scotland and England. While the main line of the family included figures such as John de Baliol, King of Scotland, a cadet branch—an inferior branch of the family—descended from Ingelram (also referred to as Engelram).

## Origins and Early Descent
Ingelram was a son of the younger Bernard de Baliol. He married the daughter and heiress of William de Berkeley, who served as the chamberlain of Scotland and was the lord of Reidcastle in Forfarshire. Through this marriage, Ingelram had a son named Henry.

Henry de Baliol became the chamberlain around the year 1223. He married Lauretta (or Lora), the daughter of Philip de Valoines (Valsques), the lord of Panmure. In 1234, Henry inherited a portion of the rich English fiefs belonging to the Valoines family. Politically, Henry aligned himself with the English barons against King John in 1215 and later accompanied Henry III to France in 1242. Henry died in 1246.

## Alexander de Baliol and the Scottish Chamberlainship
It is probable, though not certain, that Henry's son was Alexander de Baliol. Alexander served as the chamberlain of Scotland and the lord of Cavers in Teviotdale. He became a leading figure in Scottish affairs during the latter part of the 13th century, with his first mention as chamberlain occurring in 1287.

Alexander was involved in the negotiations between Edward I of England and the Scottish nobles, which resulted in the treaty of Brigham in 1290 and the treaty of Salisbury in 1289. Around 1296, Alexander was likely deprived of his office as chamberlain. During this period, he may have been imprisoned alongside his kinsman, King John de Baliol. Following these events, Alexander fought in Scotland on behalf of Edward I and was summoned to several English parliaments.

## Later Generations and Extinction
Alexander de Baliol married Isabella de Chilham, through whom he acquired lands in Kent. He died approximately 1309. He was succeeded by his son, also named Alexander. 

The lineage continued to Thomas, the son of the younger Alexander. In 1368, Thomas sold the estate of Cavers to William, the earl of Douglas. Thomas is recorded as the last member of the Baliol family mentioned in the Scottish records.

## Context of the Wider Baliol Family
The broader Baliol family was founded in England by Guy (or Guido) de Baliol, a Norman baron who held fiefs in Normandy, including Dampierre, Harcourt, Bailleul, and Vinoy. Guy arrived in England with William the Conqueror and received lands in the north from William II. 

The family is closely associated with Barnard Castle in Durham. A fortress there was built by Bernard (or Barnard) de Baliol, son or grandson of Guy. This Bernard fought for King Stephen and was taken prisoner at the battle of Lincoln in 1141. He was likely succeeded by a son, also named Bernard, who took King William the Lion of Scotland prisoner in 1174 and confirmed the privileges of the burgesses of Barnard Castle.

The family's influence peaked with John de Baliol (1249–1315), who became King of Scotland in 1292 after a dispute with Robert Bruce, earl of Annandale, was settled by Edward I of England. However, John de Baliol forfeited his English estates and the lordship of Barnard Castle in 1296.
