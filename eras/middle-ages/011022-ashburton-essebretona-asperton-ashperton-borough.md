# Ashburton

Ashburton (historically referred to as Essebretona, Asperton, or Ashperton) is a market town and borough by prescription located in the Ashburton parliamentary division of Devonshire, England. It is situated in a valley surrounded by hills, a short distance from the river Dart and approximately 24 miles north-west by west of Plymouth. The town is served by a branch of the Great Western railway. As of 1901, the population of the urban district was 2,628.

## Governance and Land Ownership
Ashburton is an ancient stannary town. Historically, its governance was managed by a bailiff and a portreeve, both of whom were elected annually during the court leet held by the lord of the manor. According to Domesday records, the town was held in chief by Osbern, the bishop of Exeter, and rendered geld for six hides. 

In 1552, the bishop sold the two manors of Ashburton Foreign and Ashburton Borough, after which they became property of the crown. Eventually, the manors were acquired in moieties by the Clinton family. The joint lords of the manor are currently Sir Robert Jardine and the present Lord Clinton.

## Parliamentary Representation
The town's representation in parliament fluctuated over several centuries. Ashburton returned two members in 1298 and 1407. From 1407 until 1640, it returned only one member, before returning to two members. This representation was reduced by the Reform Act of 1832, which deprived the borough of one member, and further reduced by the Reform Act of 1885, which deprived it of the second.

## Trade and Economy
The town's commercial activity was expanded through various grants. During the reign of Edward II, Bishop Stapledon obtained a Saturday market and two annual three-day fairs, occurring on November 11 (the feast of St Martin in winter) and August 10 (the feast of St Laurence). In 1672, a Tuesday market for the sale of woollen goods and wool made from English yarn was granted to John Ford. Later, in 1705, Andrew Quicke obtained two annual fairs for the sale of merchandise, corn, and cattle, held on the first Thursdays of June and March.

Modern industry in Ashburton includes the manufacture of serge, as well as saw-mills, paint factories, and breweries. The surrounding area contains a large deposit of umber. While copper and tin mines and slate quarries were formerly valuable, the region remains a center for the serge industry, with the neighboring urban district of Buckfastleigh (population 2,520) serving as another center for this trade.

## Landmarks and Education
The town is home to the church of St. Andrew, a cruciform building with a lofty tower constructed in the early 15th century. The church features a fine old oak roof over its north aisle and a tablet dedicated to the memory of the 1st Baron Ashburton, John Dunning, with an inscription written by Dr. Johnson.

Education in the town is provided by a grammar school, which was founded in 1314 as a chantry.

Nearby landmarks include Buckfast Abbey, located between Ashburton and Buckfastleigh. Said to have been a Benedictine house before the Conquest, it was refounded for Cistercians in 1137. In 1882, a French Benedictine community restored the abbey to use; while other sections were rebuilt on original lines, the Perpendicular abbot's tower remains.

## Notable Figures
Two prominent individuals have held the title of Baron Ashburton:

**John Dunning, 1st Baron Ashburton (1731–1783):** Born in Ashburton to an attorney, Dunning was educated at the local free grammar school. He was called to the bar in 1756 and gained significant reputation through his legal work, including a defense of the British East India Company in 1762 and his counsel for Wilkes in 1763. He served as the recorder of Bristol in 1766 and as solicitor-general from 1767 to 1770. A member of the opposition in parliament, he represented Calne starting in 1780. He opposed pensions and sinecure officers, though he was later appointed to the rich sinecure of chancellor of the duchy of Lancaster in 1782.

**Alexander Baring, 1st Baron Ashburton (1774–1848):** A financier and politician and the second son of Sir Francis Baring, Alexander Baring headed the firm Baring Brothers & Co. after 1810. He served in parliament for several constituencies, including North Essex, Callington, Taunton, and Thetford. He held the posts of master of the mint and president of the board of trade in 1834 and was created Baron Ashburton on April 10, 1835. In 1842, he concluded the Webster-Ashburton treaty in America, which settled the north-east boundary of Maine, arranged for the extradition of criminals, and established agreements to suppress the slave trade. He was also a privy councillor and a trustee of the National Gallery and the British Museum.
