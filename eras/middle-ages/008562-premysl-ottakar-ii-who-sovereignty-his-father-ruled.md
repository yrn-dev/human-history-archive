# Premysl Ottakar II.

Premysl Ottakar II. was a prominent king of Bohemia and a member of the Premyslide dynasty. He is regarded as one of the greatest of Bohemia's kings, known to his contemporaries as "the man of gold" due to his immense wealth and "the man of iron" because of his military power.

## Early Life and Accession
The son of King Wenceslas I., Premysl Ottakar II. ruled Moravia under the sovereignty of his father. During the latter years of Wenceslas I.'s reign, which were marked by internal discord, Ottakar served for a period as the chief leader of the malcontents. However, a reconciliation between the father and son occurred before the death of Wenceslas I. Ottakar ascended to the Bohemian throne in 1253.

## Territorial Expansion
During his father's lifetime, Ottakar had already obtained possession of the archduchies of Austria. This acquisition followed a period of disorder in the duchies after the death of the last of the Babenbergs. In 1251, the estates of Austria met at Truebensee and chose Ottakar as their duke, a move supported by the pope. To strengthen this position, Ottakar married Margaret, a sister of Duke Frederick II., and promised assistance to William of Holland in exchange for his investiture.

Around the time he became king of Bohemia, the nobility of Styria also recognized him as their ruler. While King Bela IV. of Hungary had previously conquered Styria and signed a treaty with Ottakar in 1254 confirming his possession, the Styrians eventually resented Hungarian rule. Ottakar utilized this resentment to interfere in the duchy's affairs. Following a war with Hungary, a treaty signed in March 1261 recognized Ottakar as the duke of Styria.

Ottakar's power continued to grow, and in 1269 he inherited the duchy of Carinthia upon the death of Duke Ulrich III. At the height of his power, his possessions stretched from the Adriatic to the Giant Mountains in Bohemia, encompassing nearly all parts of the territory west of the Leitha that would later comprise the Habsburg empire. He also obtained possession of Istria and portions of northern Italy.

## Governance and Domestic Policy
In the duchies of Austria and Styria, Ottakar worked to restore order and benefit the inhabitants of towns. He also introduced German colonists into the eastern districts. Within his broader dominions, Ottakar encouraged the immigration of Germans for political reasons; he sought to use German townsmen as a counterpoise to the significant power held by the Bohemian nobility.

## Conflict with Hungary and the Empire
The expansion of Ottakar's territories led to repeated wars with Hungary. In 1260, he achieved a decisive victory over King Bela of Hungary at the great battle of Kressenbrunn.

In 1273, Rudolph, count of Habsburg, was elected king of the Romans. Rudolph viewed Ottakar's extensive power as a menace to the German throne. Rudolph questioned Ottakar's titles to the three duchies (Austria, Styria, and Carinthia), seeking to recover imperial lands. After being summoned twice before the diet and facing a ruling against him by the imperial court, Ottakar was placed under the ban in July 1275. The resulting war led to Ottakar's submission to Rudolph in November 1276, at which time he renounced the duchies of Austria, Styria, and Carinthia.

It is noted as probable that the German crown had been offered to Ottakar prior to Rudolph's election, but that Ottakar had refused it, possibly due to his Slavic nationality.

## Succession
Ottakar was succeeded by his son, Wenceslas II., who was only seven years old at the time of his father's death. Because of the young king's age, Otto of Brandenburg, a nephew of Ottakar, initially governed Bohemia as guardian. This period was marked by unpopularity and insurrection, leading to a state of complete anarchy until the intervention of Rudolph of Habsburg.
