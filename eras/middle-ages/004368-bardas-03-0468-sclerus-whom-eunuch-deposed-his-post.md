# Bardas Sclerus

## Overview and Background
Bardas Sclerus was an ambitious general of the Roman Empire in the East. He was a member of the powerful landed aristocracy of Asia Minor, a social class whose pretensions were viewed as a perpetual menace to the imperial throne.

## Conflict with Basileios
During the joint reign of the emperors Basil II and Constantine, the administration of the empire was managed by a eunuch named Basileios. An illegitimate son of Romanus I and the president of the senate, Basileios was described as a wily and gifted man who hoped to use the young emperors as his puppets. It was Basileios who deposed Bardas Sclerus from his official post as general in the East.

## Rebellion and Suppression
Following his removal from power, Bardas Sclerus launched a rebellion, aspiring to achieve a position of power similar to that of John Tzimisces or Nicephorus Phocas. Sclerus successfully established himself as the master of the Asiatic provinces and posed a direct threat to Constantinople.

To counter this threat, the empire recalled Bardas Phocas, another general who had previously revolted during a prior reign and had been interned in a monastery. The conflict between the two generals involved three major battles. Although Bardas Phocas was defeated in the first two engagements, he emerged victorious in the third. This victory resulted in the suppression of the revolt in 979.

## Aftermath
Following the defeat of Bardas Sclerus, Bardas Phocas remained the general in the East. He held this position until 987, at which point Phocas himself rebelled and was proclaimed emperor by his troops. Evidence suggests that the minister Basileios may have been privy to Phocas's rebellion, which was driven by dissatisfaction regarding the energy displayed by the emperor.
