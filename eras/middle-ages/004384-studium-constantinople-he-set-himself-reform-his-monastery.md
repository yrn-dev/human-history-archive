# Studium in Constantinople

The monastery of the Studium in Constantinople served as a significant center for the reform and cultivation of Greek monachism during the early middle ages. It is particularly noted for its role in reviving monastic discipline and its contributions to sacred poetry.

## Reforms of Theodore the Studite
By the second half of the 8th century, Greek monachism was experiencing a period of general decadence, following four centuries of little development beyond cycles of relaxation and revival. Around the year 800, Theodore became the abbot of the Studium monastery. Theodore is regarded as the only other creative name in Greek monachism besides St Basil.

Upon assuming leadership, Theodore sought to restore the spirit of St Basil to its primitive vigour. To ensure the permanence of this reformation, he determined that a more practical code of laws was necessary to regulate the specific details of daily monastic life, serving as a supplement to St Basil's Rules. Theodore drew up constitutions that were subsequently codified. These constitutions became the standard for life at the Studium monastery and eventually spread to other monasteries throughout the Greek empire. To this day, the Constitutions of Theodore the Studite, the Rules of Basil, and the canons of the Councils form the primary components of Russian and Greek monastic law.

## Sacred Poetry at the Studium
The monastery of the Studium was also a center for the cultivation of sacred poetry. This occurred during a period when church poetry had entered a new stage characterized by an increase in artistic finish, though a decline in poetical vigour. This era saw the rise of the *Canones*—songs constructed from eight or nine lyrics of differing constructions. While the *Canones* were invented by Andreas, the archbishop of Crete, and further developed by writers such as John of Damascus and Cosmas of Jerusalem in the first half of the 8th century, the Studium monastery later became a site where this form of sacred poetry was specifically cultivated by Abbot Theodorus and others.

## Historical Context and Transition
The Studium monastery operated within a broader landscape of Greek monachism that eventually saw a shift in its center of gravity. During the middle ages, the center of Greek monachism moved from Constantinople to Mount Athos. This transition is marked by the founding of the monastery of St Athanasius on Mount Athos around 960, followed by the establishment of numerous other Latin, Slavonic, and Greek monasteries over the next three to four centuries.
