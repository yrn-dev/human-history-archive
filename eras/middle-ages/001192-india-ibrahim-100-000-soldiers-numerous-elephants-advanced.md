# India

## Demographics and Language
As of the 1901 census, the native population of India was recorded at 294,191,379. This population is characterized as mainly high-type and brown. The white population within India at that time was 169,677.

The population of India is categorized into 118 different groups based on language. These are further organized into several principal groups:
* Indo-European (specifically the Indo-Aryan sub-family)
* Dravido-Mu[n.][d.][a] (including Dravidian and Mu[n.][d.][a] or Kolarian)
* Indo-Chinese (including Tibeto-Burman, Mon-Khmer, and Siamese-Chinese)
* Malayo-Polynesian
* Semitic
* Hamitic
* Unclassed groups, such as Gipsy

One significant Indo-Aryan language spoken in central and western India is Marathi. In 1901, there were 18,237,899 speakers of Marathi, a number comparable to the population of Spain. The language is spoken across an irregular triangular area of approximately 100,000 square miles. This region extends from Daman on the Gulf of Cambay in the north to Karwar on the Arabian Sea in the south, with its apex near the district of Balaghat in the Central Provinces. Marathi is the principal language of Portuguese India and the north-western portion of the dominions of His Highness the Nizam. It is also used in parts of British India, specifically the Central Provinces (including Berar) and Bombay. In the province of Bombay, where the standard form of speech is that of Poona, more than eight and a half million people use Marathi as their vernacular.

## Ancient Military Encounters
Historical accounts detail significant military conflicts involving Indian forces and foreign invaders. During the campaigns of Alexander, the Macedonians faced King Porus, who commanded a force consisting of two thousand cavalry and twenty thousand infantry. The difficulty of this victory made the Macedonian troops reluctant to advance further into India.

Alexander's desire to cross the river Ganges was met with violent opposition. The Ganges was described as being a hundred cubits deep and thirty-two furlongs wide. Reports indicated that the kings of the Præsiæ and Gandaritæ were prepared to attack with an army comprising six thousand elephants, eight thousand war chariots, two hundred thousand foot soldiers, and eighty thousand horsemen. Following these events, a king of the country named Androkottus subdued the entirety of India with an army of six hundred thousand men and presented five hundred elephants to Seleukus.

## The Mogul Dynasty and Baber
The Mogul dynasty was founded by Baber (also known as Babar), whose full name was Zahir ud-din-Mahomet. Born on February 14, 1483, Baber was a descendant of Timur and the son of Omar Sheik, the king of Ferghana in what is now Russian Turkestan. Baber ascended the throne at age twelve following his father's death in 1495.

Baber's early career was marked by territorial fluctuations. In 1497, he captured Samarkand, believing he had a hereditary right to it. However, he lost both Samarkand and his native kingdom due to a rebellion of his nobles and the desertion of his troops. After further reverses, he regained these territories, only to be defeated in 1501 by Shaibani Khan, the ruler of the Uzbegs. In 1504, Baber captured the city of Kabul after crossing the Hindu Kush, which re-established his fortunes.

Despite facing a revolt by leading Moguls that briefly forced him into flight, Baber regained his kingdom through personal courage. In 1510, following the death of Shaibani, he attempted to reclaim his native country with assistance from Shah Ismael of Persia, leading to a triumphal event in 1511.

## War Elephants in India
The use of elephants in Indian warfare has been a subject of historical debate. While some accounts suggested the Great Mogul possessed three or four thousand elephants, the traveler Tavernier discovered through inquiry that the Great Mogul actually had eighty or ninety elephants for war and five hundred for baggage. Other historical records question whether even the most powerful monarch of Hindostan ever fielded a battle line of seven hundred elephants. In contrast, the Greeks provided varying numbers regarding King Porus; Quintus Curtius recorded that Porus used eighty-five elephants of notable strength and size. Additionally, Antiochus once collected one hundred and fifty elephants through negotiations and wars with the princes of India, though he brought only fifty-four into battle against the Romans.

## Modern Military Contributions
In the context of global conflict, the Anglo-Indian Army was identified as a potential resource for allies of Russia. Lord Kitchener suggested that in times of grave need, six of the nine field divisions could be withdrawn from India. The proposed arrangement involved moving two native regiments out of India for every one British regiment.
