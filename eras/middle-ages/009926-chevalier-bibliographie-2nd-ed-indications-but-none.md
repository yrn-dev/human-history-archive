# Ulysse Chevalier

Ulysse Chevalier was a bibliographer whose works serve as extensive reference guides for historical, biographical, and geographical research. His publications are frequently cited as primary resources for locating monographs, articles, and source materials across a wide variety of subjects, ranging from medieval theology and hagiography to regional geography.

## Bibliographical Works and Scope

Chevalier produced several distinct bibliographical tools designed to categorize historical sources. One of his primary contributions is the *Répertoire des sources hist. Bio.-Bibl.* (also referred to as *Répertoire des sources hist. du moyen age*), which provides detailed bibliographies for specific historical figures. This work is utilized for research on a diverse array of individuals, including:

*   **Religious Figures:** The *Répertoire* contains entries for Saint Brigid (the patron saint of Ireland), Saint Bridget of Sweden, and the bishop Benno (referred to as "Bennon").
*   **Ecclesiastical Leaders:** It provides bio-bibliographical data for Pope Honorius I and the scholastic figure Durand de St Pourcain.

In addition to biographical records, Chevalier authored the *Topo-bibliographie* (published in Montbeliard, 1894-1899). This work focuses on geographical and regional studies, providing references for the history of specific locations, such as the town of Bamberg. It also serves as a guide for research on the island of Corsica, listing further works and references to articles in reviews.

## Application in Historical Research

The works of Ulysse Chevalier are employed by historians to navigate the vast literature of the Middle Ages and beyond. For instance, in the study of Boniface, Chevalier's *Bibliographie* (2nd edition) is cited as a source for indications regarding the many monographs written on the different phases of Boniface's life.

His guides are often used in conjunction with other major historical collections. For researchers studying the lives of saints, Chevalier's bibliographies complement the *Acta Sanctorum* of the Bollandists and specific critical editions of works, such as those published by the Swedish Historical Society.
