# Pope Urban V and the Order of St Saviour

## The Order of St Saviour
The Order of St Saviour, also known as the Bridgittines, was an order of Augustinian canonesses founded around 1350 by St Bridget of Sweden. The order was designed as a "double order," meaning that each convent included a small community of canons who served as chaplains, though the government of the convent remained under the authority of the abbess. 

The order's principal house was located at Vadstena, by Lake Vetter, and was richly endowed by the queen and King Magnus II. From its origins, the Bridgittines played a significant role in the promotion of literature and culture within Scandinavia, spreading widely across Norway and Sweden. The head house at Vadstena remained active until 1595. In total, the order established 80 houses across various lands.

## St Bridget of Sweden
St Bridget (also referred to as Brigitta or Bridgetta) was born around 1302 to Birger Persson, a provincial judge (_lagman_) and governor of Uppland, who was among the wealthiest landowners in the country. In 1316, she married Ulf Gudmarson, the lord of Nericia. The couple had eight children, including one who was later honored as St Catherine of Sweden.

Bridget became known for her charitable and saintly life, exerting a strong religious influence over her husband. Between 1341 and 1343, the couple went on a pilgrimage to St James of Compostella. Following the death of Ulf in 1344 at the Cistercian monastery of Alvastra in East Gothland, Bridget devoted herself entirely to religion. 

Having experienced visions since childhood, Bridget recorded "revelations" that became highly popular during the middle ages after being translated into Latin by her confessor, Peter (prior of Alvastra), and Matthias, a canon of Linköping. Around 1350, Bridget traveled to Rome to pursue a mission to elevate the moral tone of the era and to seek papal authorization for her new order. While in Rome, she became universally beloved for her good works and kindness. Aside from occasional pilgrimages, including a trip to Jerusalem in 1373, she resided in Rome until her death on July 23, 1373. She was canonized by Pope Boniface IX in 1391, and her feast is observed on October 9.

## Papal Confirmation and Legacy
Although Bridget sought authorization for the Bridgittines upon her arrival in Rome, it was not until 1370 that Pope Urban V confirmed the rule of her order. 

The order's influence extended to England, where Henry V founded and royally endowed the Bridgittine convent of Syon at Isleworth, Middlesex, in 1415. This institution became one of the most influential, fashionable, and richest nunneries in England. During the reign of Mary, Syon was one of the few religious houses restored, with nearly twenty members of the old community re-established there. Following the accession of Elizabeth, the community migrated to the Low Countries, then to Rouen, and finally to Lisbon in 1594. They continued to recruit members from England until 1861, when they returned to England. Today, Syon House is established at Chudleigh in Devon, representing the only English community with an unbroken conventual existence since pre-Reformation times. Currently, the order consists only of women, with some six other convents remaining on the Continent.
