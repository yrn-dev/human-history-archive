# Little Chapters

In the liturgical structure of the Roman Catholic Church, little chapters are a specific component of the daily service. They are defined as very short lessons that are read during the "hours" of the office, excluding Matins.

## The Liturgical Context
The daily service of the Roman Catholic Church is contained in a book known as the Breviary (from the Latin *breviarium*, meaning epitome or abridgment). The Breviary provides the offices for the canonical hours, which divide the day into specific liturgical segments. Each of these hours is composed of a consistent set of constituent elements, which include:
* Psalms (and canticles)
* Antiphons
* Responsories
* Hymns
* Lessons
* Little chapters
* Versicles
* Collects

While lessons are read at Matins—which is further subdivided into three nocturns—the little chapters serve as the shorter equivalent for the other hours of the office. Following the reading of a little chapter, short responsories known as versicles are used.

## Relationship to Lessons and the Breviary
Lessons are broader readings drawn from various sources, including the Bible, the Fathers of the Church, and the Acts of the Saints. In the primitive church, these readings sometimes included texts later excluded from the canon, such as the *Shepherd of Hermas* and the letters of Clement of Rome. Over time, monastic influence led to the addition of patristic commentary or exposition to biblical passages.

The Breviary serves as a compendium of these materials, including books of homilies compiled from the writings of figures such as Gregory the Great, Athanasius, Isidore, Hilary, and Augustine. The lessons and other elements are organized into different sections:
* **Proprium de Tempore**: The normal order of ferial offices.
* **Proprium Sanctorum**: Contains liturgical formularies, psalms, and lessons for saints' festivals. This section consists largely of hagiological biography and has encroached upon the *Proprium de Tempore* because approximately 90% of the days in the year are allotted to a saint.
* **Commune Sanctorum**: Comprises lessons, antiphons, and psalms for twelve different classes of feasts, such as confessors, virgins, martyrs, apostles, and the Blessed Virgin Mary. In this section, the lessons read at the third nocturn consist of patristic homilies on the Gospels, providing a rough summary of theological instruction.

## Evolution of the Office
The structure of the canonical hours evolved from early Christian worship, which initially followed Jewish custom and relied solely on the Bible for psalms and lessons. The first major change was the separation of the Psalter into a choir-book. By the 4th century, psalms began to be grouped together. This was further developed by monks who, to manage the recitation of all 150 psalms, spread the task over a week and divided each day into hours. In the 6th century, St. Benedict established an arrangement for this division, likely based on an older, less skillful Roman division. To assist those who were not skilled in metrical compositions or improvisation, additions such as collects, responses, antiphons, and the elements found in the Breviary were gradually added.
