# Translations of the Bible, Liturgical Works, and Editions

## The Value of Intermediate Editions
In the field of book-collecting, interest and price typically decline for editions produced after the first issue. While collecting numerous late editions of a work may sometimes serve only as an index of an author's popularity, certain categories of texts provide significant scholarly value through their intermediate editions. Specifically, in liturgical works, editions published during an author's lifetime, and translations of the Bible, a long sequence of intermediate editions can be of great aid in studying the development of the final text.

## History of Biblical Translations and Circulation
The translation and circulation of Holy Scriptures have been a priority for leaders of Christendom since early times. In A.D. 331, Emperor Constantine requested fifty copies of the Old and New Testaments from Eusebius, bishop of Caesarea, for use in the principal churches of Constantinople. Later, in 797, Charlemagne commissioned Alcuin to prepare an emended text of the Vulgate, which was then multiplied in writing-schools at Tours.

The Latin Bible was the first book printed in Europe; by the end of the 15th century, Copinger estimates that 124 editions of the Vulgate had been issued. Before the appearance of Luther's version, eighteen editions of the German Bible and a dozen editions of the Italian Bible had been printed. The Reformation further intensified interest in the Scriptures, leading to more systematic translation and circulation despite the adverse attitude of the Roman Church following the council of Trent.

## English Biblical Versions and Revisions
The English Bible underwent various stages of translation, revision, and legal status:

*   **Matthew's Bible:** Published in 1537 by Richard Grafton and Edward Whitchurch, this was a compilation of renderings by Tyndale and Coverdale. It was likely the work of John Rogers using the alias Thomas Matthew. It was "set forth with the kinges most gracyous lycence" and served as a foundation for the present Bible.
*   **Taverner's Revision:** In 1539, Richard Taverner, a Greek scholar, published a revised version of the Matthew Bible. Taverner is considered perhaps the first purist among Biblical translators, as he attempted to replace foreign expressions with words of native origin.
*   **The Great Bible:** This translation was the only one not interdicted during the proscription of 1546. Later, Archbishop Parker attempted to improve the Great Bible between 1563 and 1565 to challenge the popularity of the Genevan Bible by distributing portions of the text to learned men and bishops for collation and marginal notation.
*   **The Genevan Bible:** Planned by reformers including Coverdale in Geneva, this version produced a New Testament in June 1557. It was the first Bible to divide the text into sections and verses based on the best editions in other languages. In 1576, Lawrence Tomson revised the New Testament and added a commentary translated mainly from Beza.
*   **The Authorized Version:** This edition appeared in 1611 with the title *The Holy Bible, conteyning the Old Testament, and the New: Newly Translated out of the Originall tongues*. While millions of copies have been printed, modern copies show departures from the 1611 standard. Dr. Scrivener attributes some of these to negligence and others to deliberate, unauthorized changes.

## Liturgical Works and Authors
Liturgical and devotional writings have been produced by various theologians and scholars across different traditions.

**John Bona (1609-1674)**
An Italian cardinal and author whose writings focused on devotional and liturgical subjects. His works *Divina Psalmodia* and *De Libris Liturgicis* contain prayers, verses, and rubrical information. His devotional treatise *De Sacrificio Missae* is regarded as the classical work in its field.

**Jacob (or Dionysius) Bar-Salibi**
A 12th-century writer in the Syrian Jacobite church and bishop of Mar'ash and Mabbog. He produced exhaustive commentaries on the Old and New Testaments, summarizing interpretations from previous writers such as Cyril, Chrysostom, Ephrem, John of Dara, and Moses Bar-Kepha. His works also include homilies, epistles, liturgical treatises, and a polemic against the Mahommedans and the Israelites (Bani Israel). His *Exposition of the Liturgy* was edited and translated by H. Labourt in 1903.
