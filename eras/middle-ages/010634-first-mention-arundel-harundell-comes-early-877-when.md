# Arundel

Arundel is a market town and municipal borough located in the Chichester parliamentary division of Sussex, England. Situated on a hill slope above the river Arun—which is navigable for small vessels to Littlehampton—the town is approximately 58 miles south-south-west of London. As of 1901, the population was 2739.

## Early History and Development
The first mention of Arundel (then Harundell) occurs as early as 877, when King Alfred left it to his nephew Aethelm in his will. During the era of Edward the Confessor, the town appeared to consist of a fortification or earthwork and a mill. The earthwork was likely constructed by King Alfred as a defense against the Danes. By the time of the Norman Conquest, the settlement had grown in importance, and the Domesday Book records it as a thriving port and borough.

Following the Conquest, the town was granted to Roger de Montgomery, who built a castle on the site of the ancient earthwork. This castle served to guard the passage along the river through the hills. Although the castle existed in the time of King Alfred, it was rebuilt by Roger de Montgomery, though it was later taken from his son due to a rebellion against King Henry I.

## Governance and Commerce
Arundel has a long history of trade and local governance. Markets were held within the borough on Saturdays and Thursdays from very early times. In 1285, Richard Fitzalan, the earl of Arundel, secured a grant for two annual fairs, occurring on December 17 and May 14. Later, in 1586, Queen Elizabeth acknowledged the rights of the burgesses and mayor to hold four annual fairs, two weekly markets, and a court for pleas under forty shillings, as a body corporate. In 1688, James II confirmed a charter from two years prior, incorporating the borough with 12 burgesses, 4 aldermen, and a mayor.

Politically, the borough returned two members to parliament from 1302 until 1832, when the Reform Act reduced this to one member. The borough was completely disfranchised in 1868.

## Landmarks and Infrastructure
The town's architecture is dominated by Arundel Castle. While the stronghold was frequently assaulted and damaged during the 17th-century civil wars, it was restored by Charles, 11th duke of Norfolk (who died in 1815), becoming one of England's most splendid baronial mansions. Further reconstruction in the 13th-century style took place toward the end of the 19th century. The town hall, a castellated building, was presented to the corporation by the duke of Norfolk.

Religious sites include the church of St Nicholas, founded around 1375, which features Perpendicular architecture and a central low tower. The chancel of St Nicholas, which contains ancient monuments of the earls of Arundel, is the property of the duke of Norfolk; this ownership was the subject of a legal action and appeal in 1880. Additionally, the duke of Norfolk built the Roman Catholic church of St Philip Neri in 1873.

The town's infrastructure evolved over time; it was once a thriving seaport and was connected to London via canal in 1813. The town's name is linked to *hirondelle* (a swallow), according to the etymology on the corporation seal.

## Notable Figures and Events
The town and its associated titles have been linked to several prominent figures:
*   **Thomas Arundel (1353–1414):** The third son of Richard Fitzalan and Eleanor (daughter of Henry Plantagenet), he rose rapidly in the church to become archbishop of Canterbury. He served as lord chancellor twice and was archbishop of York from April 1388. In 1397, he was involved in a conspiracy at Arundel Castle to murder the lords of the council and dethrone Richard II. Following the arrest of Warwick, Gloucester, and Arundel, the archbishop was impeached and sentenced to banishment. As archbishop, he also presided over the court in St Paul's, London, that condemned the Lollard martyr John Badby to be burned at Smithfield.
*   **Henry Fitzalan:** The 12th earl of Arundel is credited by Stow with introducing coaches to England from Germany around 1580.
*   **The Arundell Family:** The barony continues to be held by the Arundell family, who have remained Roman Catholic.
