# Geschichte der Hohenstaufen and Ebert's Bibliographical Works

## Publication by F.A. Brockhaus
The work *Geschichte der Hohenstaufen*, authored by Raumer, was one of the large historical undertakings published by Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus. Brockhaus (1772–1823) was a German publisher born in Dortmund. After early education at a local gymnasium and a mercantile apprenticeship in Düsseldorf (1788–1793), he studied modern languages and literature in Leipzig for two years. 

Brockhaus initially operated an emporium for English goods in Dortmund, moving the business to Arnheim in 1801 and Amsterdam in 1802. He transitioned into publishing in 1805, though early government restrictions hindered two of his journals. Following complications in Holland, he returned to Germany and settled in Altenburg in 1811. 

Brockhaus is notably recognized for the *Konversations-Lexikon*; he purchased the copyright to the work (which had started in 1796) approximately three years before 1811 and completed the first edition between 1810 and 1811. He began a second edition under his own editorship in 1812. In 1818, he moved to Leipzig to establish a large printing-house. In addition to *Geschichte der Hohenstaufen*, his extensive literary output included the *Allgemeines bibliographisches Lexikon* by Ebert, as well as critical periodicals such as *Hermes*, *Zeitgenossen*, and the *Literarisches Konversationsblatt* (later renamed *Blätter für literarische Unterhaltung*).

## The Bibliographical Works of A. Ebert
A. Ebert is the author of several significant scholarly works concerning literature and history. One such work, the *Allgemeines bibliographisches Lexikon*, was published through the house of F.A. Brockhaus. 

Additionally, Ebert authored the *Allgemeine Geschichte der Litt. des Mittelalters im Abendlande* (also referred to as *Allgemeine Geschichte der Litteratur des Mittelalters*). This work is cited as a general authority on various historical figures and authors:
* **Ennodius:** Volume i of the work (1889) is cited for general consultation on Ennodius.
* **Dracontius:** Volume i (1874) is cited as a general source for information on Dracontius.
* **Fulgentius:** The *Allgemeine Geschichte der Litt. des Mittelalters* (vol. i) is listed as a reference for the works of Fulgentius.
* **Cassiodorus:** Volume i of the *Allgemeine Geschichte der Litteratur des Mittelalters* is cited as a general authority on Cassiodorus.

## Legacy of the Brockhaus Firm
Following the death of Friedrich Arnold Brockhaus on August 20, 1823, in Leipzig, the publishing business was continued by his sons. Friedrich Brockhaus (1800–1865) managed the business until his retirement in 1850. 

Heinrich Brockhaus (1804–1874) further extended the business. His contributions to science and literature were recognized by the university of Jena, which awarded him an honorary doctorate of philosophy in 1858. Between 1842 and 1848, Heinrich served as a representative for Leipzig in the Saxon second chamber. He was named an honorary citizen of Leipzig in 1872 and died in the city on November 15, 1874.
