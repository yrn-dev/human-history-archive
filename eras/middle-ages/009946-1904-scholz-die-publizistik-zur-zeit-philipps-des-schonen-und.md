# Bibliographic and Historical Authorities of the Early 20th Century

The scholarly landscape of the early 20th century was characterized by extensive bibliographic documentation across diverse fields, including ecclesiastical history, botany, mycology, and the study of European monarchs. The records from this period highlight a rigorous approach to primary sources and the publication of comprehensive historical analyses.

## Ecclesiastical and Papal Studies
Research into the papacy and early church history during this era relied on a variety of registers and critical editions. For the study of Boniface VIII, scholars utilized *Les Registres de Boniface VIII* (Paris, 1884 ff.) and the *Kirchenlexikon* (Freiburg, 1883). Key analyses of this period include H. Finke's *Aus den Tagen Bonifaz VIII.* (1902), described as epoch-making, and R. Scholz's 1903 work, *Die Publizistik zur Zeit Philipps des Schonen und Bonifaz VIII.* 

Specific theological and legal disputes were addressed in specialized literature. The *Unam Sanctum* was discussed exhaustively in the *Kirchenlexikon* (1901) and by C. Mirbt in *Quellen zur Geschichte des Papsttums* (1901). Similarly, *Clericis laicos* was documented by Gee and Hardy in *Documents Illustrative of English Church History* (1896). In the study of early church figures, the works of Athanasius were primarily accessed through the Maurine edition by Bernard de Montfaucon (1698), later enlarged by Giustiniani (1777). Modern biographical studies of Athanasius began to emerge through E. Schwartz's essays in the *Nachrichten der koniglichen Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Gottingen* (1904).

## Imperial and Royal History
The history of the Holy Roman Empire and the Bavarian monarchy was a subject of intense academic scrutiny. Regarding Ludwig IV (the Bavarian), authorities included F. von Weech's study of Kaiser Ludwig and King Johann of Bohemia (1860) and K. Müller's analysis of Ludwig's struggle with the Roman Curie (1879-1880). Other focused studies included W. Altmann's work on the *Römerzug* of Ludwig (1886) and Ueding's 1904 study on Ludwig and the cities of the lower Rhine.

The reign of King Ludwig I of Bavaria was documented through various lenses, including his relationship with the arts and his political history. Notable works include Sepp's *Ludwig I. Augustus, König von Bayern und das Zeitalter der Wiedergeburt der Künste* (1903) and L. Trose's collection of letters from Ludwig I to his son, King Otto of Greece (1891). The influence of Lola Montez was also a subject of study, appearing in works by P. Erdmann (1847) and E. Füchs (1904).

## Natural Sciences: Mycology and Bacteriology
In the biological sciences, the late 19th and early 20th centuries saw the systematization of fungi and bacteria. General authorities on plants and fungi included Engler and Prantl's *Die naturlichen Pflanzenfamilien* (1892 onwards) and Zopf's *Die Pilze* (1890). Specialized research focused on spore distribution, such as Falck's 1904 work on Basidiomycetes, and the morphology of fungi by De Bary and von Tafel.

Bacteriology was supported by texts such as Fischer's *The Structure and Functions of Bacteria* (1900) and Migula's *System der Bakterien* (1897). Research into the nucleus of bacteria was advanced by Vejdovsky in *Cent. f. Bakt.* (1904) and Mencl in *Archiv f. Protistenkunde* (1907). Additionally, the study of fossil bacteria was documented by Renault in 1896.

## Notable Historians and Authors
Ferdinand Gregorovius (1821-1891) stands as a prominent figure in German historical scholarship. Born in Neidenburg and educated at the university of Konigsberg, he spent over twenty years in Italy and became a citizen of Rome. His most significant contribution, *Geschichte der Stadt Rom im Mittelalter* (1859-1872), examines the relationship between the papacy and the Empire from A.D. 400 to 1534. Gregorovius also authored works on Emperor Hadrian, Corsica, and Lucrezia Borgia (translated into English in 1904).

Other contributors to the historical record include Hormayr, who produced the 20-volume *Osterreichischer Plutarch* (1807) and the *Archiv fur Gesch., Stat., Lit. und Kunst* (1809-1828). In the realm of musical history, Wilhelm Altenburg's 1904 work provided a technical and acoustic history of the clarinet.
