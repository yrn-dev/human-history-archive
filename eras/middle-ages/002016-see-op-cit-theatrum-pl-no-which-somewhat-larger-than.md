# Magdeburg Bag-pipe

The Magdeburg bag-pipe is a specific type of *sackpfeife* described by Praetorius. It is distinguished from the *schaeferpfeife* by being somewhat larger in size and pitched a third lower.

## Construction and Design
The instrument features two chaunters mounted within a single stock. Each of these chaunters is equipped with one thumb hole at the back and three holes in the front. The technical specifications of the instrument include drones tuned to G3 and D4, with the chaunter possessing a compass of D4 to D5.

## Musical Capabilities
The two chaunters are designed to produce different sets of notes:
*   **Right-hand chaunter:** Sounds the five notes D, E, F, G, and A.
*   **Left-hand chaunter:** Sounds the notes G, A, B, C, and D.

Because of this dual-chaunter configuration, the performer is capable of playing simple two-part melodies.

## Related Instruments
The Magdeburg bag-pipe exists within a broader category of bag-pipes mentioned by Praetorius and other sources:
*   **Musette:** A French bag-pipe similar in pitch to the *huemmelchen*, which is inflated using bellows.
*   **Calabrian bag-pipe:** An instrument inflated via a blow-pipe, featuring a goatskin bag with the hair left on. It consists of two drones and two chaunters fixed in one stock. Its chaunters have three or four finger-holes, and the right-hand pipe utilizes a key enclosed in a perforated box to cover the fourth hole. Both the drones and chaunters on this instrument use double reeds.
*   **Ancient Instruments:** The Roman *tibia utricularis* and the ancient Greek bag-pipe (*askaules*) belong to the class of mouth-inflated instruments, though it remains uncertain if they utilized drones.
