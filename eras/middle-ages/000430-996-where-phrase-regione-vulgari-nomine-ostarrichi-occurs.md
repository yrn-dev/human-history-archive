# Ostarrichi and the Early Austrian Mark

## Origins and Purpose
The foundation of the territory that would eventually become the Austro-Hungarian monarchy began with a small mark established around A.D. 800. This mark was situated along the south bank of the Danube, located to the east of the river Enns. Its primary purpose was to serve as a defensive barrier for the Frankish kingdom against the Slavs. Although the mark was relatively small, spanning only about 60 meters from east to west, it was associated in the popular consciousness with a vast and nearly unbroken expanse of land in eastern Europe.

## Etymology and the Charter of 996
The name *Oesterreich* (Austria) translates to "east empire" or "realm." This designation is attributed to the mark's geographical position relative to Germany in general and Bavaria specifically. The first recorded use of this term appears in a charter dated 996, which contains the specific phrase *in regione vulgari nomine Ostarrichi*.

## Territorial Development and Dynasties
The expansion of this small defensive mark into a major monarchy was a slow and gradual process. This development is categorized into two primary divisions based on the ruling dynasties:

*   **The House of Babenberg:** The efforts of this dynasty were focused primarily on strengthening the position of the mark and enlarging its immediate area.
*   **The House of Habsburg:** Following the Babenbergs, the house of Habsburg worked with success and perseverance to extend their rule over neighboring territories.

## Historical and Strategic Significance
For over a thousand years, Austria has maintained a consistent European position as a sentinel over the valley of the middle Danube. By barring the advance of the Slavs into Germany, the region—whether functioning as a mark, a duchy, or an empire—has served as a meeting-place for the Slav and the Teuton. This strategic role provides the region with unique importance in European history and links modern German ideas to those of Otto the Great and Charlemagne.
