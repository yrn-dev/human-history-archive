# The Envoys of the Great Kaan

The appointment of the two brothers, Nicolo and Maffeo Polo, as envoys for the Great Kaan was the result of a journey that began in 1260. Starting from Constantinople, the brothers engaged in a trading venture to the Crimea, which eventually led them north along the Volga and to the city of Bocara.

## Arrival at Bocara and the Journey to Court
Bocara, described as the best city in all Persia, was under the territory of a king named Barac. Upon arriving there, the two brothers found themselves unable to proceed forward or return, resulting in a three-year stay in the city. During this period, they encountered envoys from Alau, Lord of the Levant, who were traveling to the court of the Great Kaan, the Lord of all the Tartars in the world.

The envoys were amazed to see Latins in that region, as the Great Kaan had never seen any. They counseled the brothers to accompany them, promising that the Kaan would treat them with great liberality and honor, and that they would travel with perfect security. Following this advice, the brothers journeyed for a full year, traveling northward and north-eastward, witnessing many marvels before reaching the court of the Prince.

## Reception by the Great Kaan
Upon their arrival, the Great Kaan received the two brothers with hospitality and great honor. He expressed significant pleasure at their visit and questioned them extensively. His inquiries focused first on the nature of emperors, specifically how they administered justice, maintained their dignity, and conducted themselves in battle. He asked similar questions regarding other kings, princes, and potentates.

The Kaan further inquired about the customs of the Latins, the Church, and the activities occurring in Rome, with a particular interest in the Pope. Because the two brothers were proficient in the Tartar language, they were able to provide the Kaan with truthful and orderly accounts of these matters.

## Appointment as Envoys to the Pope
Following these exchanges, the Great Kaan sent the two brothers as his envoys to the Pope. To facilitate this mission and ensure their authority, the Kaan provided them with a tablet of gold which bore his orders on their behalf.

The brothers traveled from the court to the city of Acre and subsequently to Venice. Upon their return to Venice, they eventually departed again to present themselves before the new Pope, Gregory X. They were accompanied on this return journey by Mark, the son of Messer Nicolo, who had been born in 1254.
