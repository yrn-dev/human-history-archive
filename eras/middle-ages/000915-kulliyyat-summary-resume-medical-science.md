# Colliget (Kulliyyat)

The *Colliget*, also known as the *Kulliyyat* or summary, is a medical work authored by Averroes. It is described as a resume of medical science and exists among the Latin editions of his medical writings.

## Averroes and Medical Science
Averroes produced a diverse body of literary works spanning several disciplines, including astronomy, grammar, philosophy, medicine, and jurisprudence. Within the field of medicine, his Latin editions include not only the *Colliget* but also a commentary on a poem regarding medicine written by Avicenna. Despite these contributions, Averroes' renown in the medical field remained significantly lower than that of Avicenna.

## Context of Semitic Scientific Advancement
The work of scholars like Averroes occurred during a period of "astonishing renascence" in the Semitic world. During this era, the systematic accumulation and criticism of facts—originally begun by the Greeks—was resumed. This period saw the germination of the "seed of Aristotle" and the influence of the museum of Alexandria, leading to significant advances in physical, medical, and mathematical sciences. 

This scientific revival also introduced Arabic figures and the zero sign, replacing Roman numerals, and gave rise to the terms "algebra" and "chemistry." The influence of this philosophy eventually reanimated the Christian world and the medieval philosophy of Italy and France.

## Other Works of Averroes
Beyond his medical summaries, Averroes is noted for his extensive philosophical and legal writings. His works include:
* **Philosophical Treatises:** The *Commentaries on Aristotle*, *De Substantia Orbis*, *De Animae Beatitudine*, and the *Destructio Destructionis*, which was written against Ghazali.
* **Aristotelian Commentaries:** These are categorized into three types:
    * **Larger commentaries:** These quote a paragraph at length and expound upon its clauses individually. This format was an innovation of Averroes. Great commentaries of this type exist for *Metaphysics*, *De Anima*, *Physics*, *De Caelo*, and the *Posterior Analytics*.
    * **Medium commentaries:** These cite only the first words of a section.
    * **Paraphrases or analyses:** These serve as treatises on the subjects of the Aristotelian books.
* **Other Writings:** He wrote on jurisprudence, astronomy, special logical subjects, and prolegomena to philosophy, as well as criticisms of Alfarabius and Avicenna. Some of these remain in manuscript form in libraries such as the Escorial.

In his philosophical work, Averroes substituted Plato's *Republic* for the *Politics*, which was inaccessible at the time, and he did not produce a commentary on the *History of Animals*.

## Publication and Translation
A work by Averroes titled *Philosophy and Theology* was first published in Arabic by the Bavarian Academy in 1859, with a German translation following in 1873 by J. Mueller. This translation, along with a German version of an essay on the conjunction of the intellect with man, provided non-Semitic scholars with an adequate understanding of his work, as many of the Latin translations of his writings were considered obscure and barbarous. Between 1480 and 1580, approximately 100 Latin editions of his works were produced.
