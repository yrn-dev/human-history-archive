# Al-Bukhari

## Life and Early Education
Mahommed ibn Ismā'īl al-Bukhārī (810-872) was an Arabic author born in A.H. 194 (A.D. 810) in Bokhara to an Iranian family. He demonstrated an early aptitude for the learning of traditions by heart. At the age of sixteen, while accompanying his family on a pilgrimage to Mecca, he expanded his knowledge by gathering additions from authorities encountered along the route.

By the age of eighteen, al-Bukhārī had dedicated himself to the systematic collecting, testing, sifting, and arranging of traditions. To further this pursuit, he traveled extensively throughout the Moslem world, visiting regions from Egypt to Samarkand. According to accounts, he learned three hundred thousand traditions—both true and false—from more than a thousand men. His contemporaries regarded his speed and power of memory as miraculous, and he became the acknowledged authority on the subject of traditions.

## Theological and Legal Positions
Al-Bukhārī's theological position was characterized as anti-rationalistic and conservative. In this capacity, he enjoyed the respect and friendship of Aḥmad Ibn Ḥanbal. Regarding matters of law, he appears to have been a Shāfi'ite.

## The Sahih
After spending sixteen years away from his birthplace, al-Bukhārī returned to Bokhara to compile his *Sahih*. This work is a collection of 7,275 tested traditions. He arranged these traditions into chapters to provide the foundations for a complete system of jurisprudence that did not rely on speculative law. This was the first book of its kind.

The *Sahih* has achieved a status of quasi-canonicity within Islām and is treated almost like the Koran.

## Death and Legacy
Al-Bukhārī died in A.H. 256 while in banishment at Kartank, which is a suburb of Samarkand. His legacy persists through his writings and the veneration of his burial site, where solemn pilgrimages are made and prayers are believed to be heard.
