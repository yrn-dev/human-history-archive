# Eleanor, Daughter of Henry Plantagenet, Earl of Lancaster

Eleanor was the daughter of Henry Plantagenet, who held the titles of earl of Lancaster, Derby, Lincoln, and Leicester, and was created duke of Lancaster in 1351. Through her marriage to Richard Fitzalan, earl of Arundel and Warenne, she became part of an influential family.

## Family and Descendants
Eleanor was the second wife of Richard Fitzalan. From this union, she had a son, Thomas Arundel (1353–1414). Thomas entered the church and experienced a rapid ascent in preferment, becoming the archdeacon of Taunton in 1373 and being consecrated as the bishop of Ely in April 1374. He later served as the archbishop of York starting in April 1388 and was translated to the position of archbishop of Canterbury in September 1396.

## The House of Lancaster
Eleanor's father, Henry Plantagenet (c. 1299–1361), was the grandson of Edmund Crouchback, earl of Lancaster. Henry was created earl of Derby in 1337 and later became the second duke in England. The Lancaster family held significant estates and titles; for instance, Edmund Crouchback had held the manor of Belper (Beaurepaire), where he built a hunting seat and enclosed a park. This manor subsequently became part of the duchy of Lancaster and served as a residence for John of Gaunt.

The lineage of the earldom of Derby was complex. The title was originally held by Robert de Ferrers and his descendants until 1266, when the 6th earl was deprived of his lands for participating in a baronial rising against Henry III. These lands were granted to Henry III's son, Edmund, earl of Lancaster. Edmund's son, Thomas, earl of Lancaster, referred to himself as Earl Ferrers. Henry Plantagenet, Eleanor's father, eventually took the title of earl of Derby in 1337.

## Extended Kinship and Alliances
The family of Henry Plantagenet was connected to various high-ranking nobles and royalty. Henry's daughter, Blanche, married John of Gaunt, a son of Edward III. Their son, Henry, earl of Derby, eventually ascended the throne as King Henry IV in 1399.

Additionally, another daughter of Henry, earl of Lancaster, married William, the 3rd earl (1326–1333), who was the son of John de Burgh and Elizabeth, lady of Clare. William served as the lieutenant of Ireland in 1331 before his murder in his 21st year. Their daughter, the sole heiress to the de Burgh and Clare estates, married Lionel, a son of Edward III. Their direct representative, the duke of York, became King Edward IV in 1461.

## Political Associations of the Arundel Family
Eleanor's son, Thomas Arundel, was deeply involved in the political upheavals of the reign of King Richard II. He was associated with a party that included his own brother, Richard, earl of Arundel, as well as Thomas, duke of Gloucester, and Henry, earl of Derby (the future Henry IV). 

In 1386, Thomas Arundel and the duke of Gloucester were sent to Eltham to persuade King Richard II to return to parliament. Following the success of this mission, Arundel was appointed lord chancellor, replacing Michael de la Pole, duke of Suffolk. He served two tenures as chancellor; during the second, he attempted to move the courts of justice from London to York.

Arundel's political standing collapsed in 1397 when the king attacked the lords appellant. Following the arrest of Arundel, Warwick, and Gloucester, the archbishop was impeached by the Commons. He was charged with assisting in the procurement of a commission of regency that derogated royal authority. He was subsequently sentenced to banishment and given forty days to leave the realm.
