# BASSARAB

The Bassarab (or Bassaraba) dynasty was the ruling house of Walachia in Rumania, exercising power from the beginning of the region's history until 1658. The dynasty's first appearances as rulers—holding titles such as *voivod*, *ban*, or *knyaz*—occurred in the southern parts of Transylvania (specifically the old dukedoms of Almash and Fogarash, located on the right bank of the Olt/Aluta and extending south to Craiova and Severin) and in the western part of Rumania, an area originally known as Little Walachia.

## Origins and Etymology
The precise origin of the Bassarab family and name remains unexplained, though several conjectures exist. Some suggest a connection to the province of Bessarabia, a name used by oriental chroniclers in ancient times to describe the entirety of Walachia. Servian chroniclers have attempted to link the dynasty to their own nationality by identifying "Sarab" with "Serbia" or "Sorb."

The dynasty's heraldry reflects these various theories of origin. The Bassarab shield features three heads of negroes, which is believed to be a later western addition based on a popular etymology linking the latter part of the name "Bassarab" to Arabs, who were interpreted as Moors. Similarly, the inclusion of a star and a crescent in the heraldry is thought to be based on the supposition that the family had oriental origins.

## Establishment and Governance
Tradition holds that the foundation of the Walachian principality was established by a member of the Bassarab family who migrated from Transylvania, settling first in Tirgovishtea and Campulung. Throughout the dynasty's history, nearly every prince and voivod utilized a Slavonic surname. This naming convention is attributed to the influence of the Slavonic Church, to which the Rumanians belonged.

## Dynastic Factions and the "Drakul" Line
Beginning in the 13th Century, the Bassarabs divided into two rival factions. These were the descendants of two brothers, Dragul and Dan. The line descending from Dragul became known in history as "Drakul," a term meaning "devil." This was a nickname bestowed by the rival faction and became permanently associated with the family due to the extreme cruelties of Vlad Tsepesh (1456–1476) and Vlad Drakul (1433–1446), both of whom are depicted in popular legend as figures of fiendish cruelty.

## Prominent Members
Several members of the Bassarab dynasty are noted for their specific contributions or historical significance:

*   **Mircea (1386–1418):** A prominent ruler who accepted Turkish suzerainty.
*   **Neagoe:** The founder of the cathedral at Curtea de Argesh.
*   **Petru Cercel:** A scholar of profound learning who spoke twelve languages and maintained correspondence with Italian poets and scholars. He owned the oldest manuscripts of the Rumanian Gospels (which bear his autograph signature and were later published by Dr. M. Caster). He was drowned in Constantinople in 1590 following intrigues by Mihnea, his successor.
*   **Michael the Brave (1592–1601):** A prominent member of the family.
*   **Mateiu Bassarab (1633–1654):** He established the first printing-press in Rumania. Under his influence, the first code of laws was compiled and published in Bucharest in 1654.
*   **Matthias Bassarab:** A prince of Walachia who, in 1640, erected the cathedral in Buzeu on the site of an older church.

The Bassarab dynasty became extinct in 1658 with the death of Constantine Sherban.
