# Wenceslas II and the End of the Premyslide Dynasty

## Early Life and Regency
Wenceslas II ascended to the throne of Bohemia at the age of seven following the death of his father, Premysl Ottakar II. Due to his youth, the government of Bohemia was initially handled by Otto of Brandenburg, a nephew of Ottakar, who served as the guardian of the young sovereign. Otto's period of rule was marked by significant unpopularity, leading to an insurrection and a period of complete anarchy within the country.

Stability was eventually restored through the intervention of Rudolph of Habsburg. By the age of twelve, Wenceslas became the nominal ruler of Bohemia, although actual power remained with Zavis of Falkenstein. Zavis was a prominent Bohemian noble who had married Kunegunda, the mother of the king.

## Reign and Expansion
The influence of Zavis of Falkenstein eventually became invidious to Wenceslas. In 1290, the king ordered that Zavis be beheaded. At nineteen years of age, Wenceslas began to govern Bohemia himself. His reign is described as a period of great happiness for the country. 

Under his rule, Wenceslas expanded his influence beyond Bohemia; Poland accepted his rule, and he was offered the Hungarian crown. However, toward the end of his reign, Wenceslas became embroiled in a conflict with Albert, the archduke of Austria (who later became king of the Romans). Wenceslas died suddenly in 1305 while he was preparing to invade Austria.

## Succession and the Fall of the Premyslides
Wenceslas II was succeeded by his son, Wenceslas III, who was sixteen years old at the time of his father's death. Wenceslas III's rule over Bohemia lasted only one year. While he was planning a warlike expedition against Poland—a country over which the Bohemian sovereigns continued to maintain a claim—he was murdered by unknown assassins in 1306. This event marked the end of the Premyslide dynasty's rule over Bohemia.

## Subsequent Transitions of Power
Following the end of the Premyslide line, the Bohemians sought to maintain their right to elect their own sovereign despite claims by the Habsburg princes to a hereditary right to the throne. After a brief and unpopular reign by Henry, duke of Carinthia (who had married a daughter of Wenceslas II), the Bohemians elected John of Luxemburg, the son of Henry, king of the Romans. 

John of Luxemburg's connection to Bohemia was further strengthened by his marriage to Princess Elizabeth, a daughter of Wenceslas II. The Luxemburg dynasty subsequently ruled Bohemia until its extinction upon the death of Sigismund in 1437.
