# Abdulla Khan

Abdulla Khan was a ruler of Baluchistan and a descendant of Kambar, the leader of mountain shepherds who had originally deposed the last raja of the Hindu dynasty to assume government of the region. Abdulla Khan was the fourth in descent from Kambar.

## Military Ambitions and Governance
An intrepid and ambitious soldier, Abdulla Khan focused his efforts on the conquest of Kach Gandava. At the time of his campaigns, this area was held by various petty chiefs who operated under the authority of the nawabs of Sind.

During the period of Abdulla Khan's rule, Nadir Shah, the Turcoman ruler of Persia, advanced from Persia to invade Hindustan. While stationed at Kandahar, Nadir Shah established his authority in Baluchistan by dispatching several detachments into the province. Despite this Persian intervention, Nadir Shah issued orders that Abdulla Khan was to be continued in the government of the country.

## Death and Succession
Abdulla Khan's tenure ended when he was killed in a battle against the forces of the nawabs of Sind. Following his death, he was succeeded by his eldest son, Haji Mahommed Khan. The reign of Haji Mahommed Khan was characterized by oppressive taxation and a tyrannical and licentious lifestyle, which served to alienate his subjects.

## Legacy and the Rise of Nasir Khan
Abdulla Khan's second son, Nasir Khan, had accompanied the victorious Nadir Shah to Delhi. During this time, Nasir Khan acquired the confidence and favor of the Persian monarch. Due to the instability and tyranny caused by his brother's rule, Nasir Khan returned to Kalat, where the population hailed him as their deliverer.

After finding that attempts to reason with his brother were ineffective, Nasir Khan entered Haji Mahommed Khan's apartment and stabbed him to the heart. Upon the death of the tyrant, Nasir Khan ascended the *musnud* to the joy of his subjects. He immediately reported these events to Nadir Shah, who was encamped near Kandahar. Nadir Shah received the news with satisfaction and issued a *firman* in 1739 appointing Nasir Khan as the *beglar begi* (prince of princes) of all Baluchistan.
