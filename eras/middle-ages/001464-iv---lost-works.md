# LOST WORKS

The archive contains various records regarding the loss of intellectual, artistic, and literary contributions across different eras and cultures, as well as spiritual perspectives on the preservation of one's deeds.

## Classical and Byzantine Literary Losses
Significant portions of ancient and Byzantine scholarship have been lost over time. In the field of geography, the geographical work of Stephanus of Byzantium, dating from the time of Justinian, is lost. Similarly, the Byzantine writer Joannes Laurentius Lydus (born c. A.D. 490) was commissioned by Justinian to write a history of the emperor's successful campaign against Persia and a panegyric on the emperor; both of these works, along with several poetical compositions, are lost. However, Lydus's preserved works on the antiquities of Rome are considered valuable because he utilized the works of old Roman writers that are now lost.

In Greek rhetoric, the works of Lysias suffered extensive loss. While thirty-four speeches have survived (three of which are fragmentary), one hundred and twenty-seven other speeches are known only through titles or smaller fragments. Additionally, a work by Aristotle concerning the Grecian *Πολιτεῖαι* is cited by Athenæus but is itself lost.

## Musical and Artistic Losses
The archive notes several losses in the works of the composer Bach. While J. N. Forkel suggested that Bach wrote five *Jahrgange* of church cantatas—which would imply the loss of approximately 80 works—evidence from text-books and fragments suggests that no more than six or seven cantatas are actually lost. Regarding the Passions, the Passion according to St Mark is lost, though movements preserved in the *Trauer-Ode* indicate it was larger than the Passion according to St John. Furthermore, several lost secular works are partially preserved within portions of the Christmas oratorio. In the realm of instrumental music, one concerto for two violins and three violin concertos are known only through clavier versions. Additionally, portions of the first movement of the A major sonata for clavier and flute have been cut off.

## Personal and National Loss
The archive records instances where the death of an individual is characterized as a loss to a nation or community. The Portuguese novelist Camillo Castello Branco (1825-1890), who died by his own hand after losing his sight and suffering from chronic nervous disease, was recognized as the most national of Portugal's modern writers upon his death. In Japan, the death of Shotoku, who is regarded by historians as the father of the country's civilization and an incarnation of the Buddha, was met with widespread lamentation; the Chronicles state that the young felt as if they had lost a beloved parent and the old as if they had lost a dear child.

## Spiritual Perspectives on Loss
The sacred texts of the Qur'an present a vision of loss and preservation centered on the soul and the deeds of the individual.

The texts describe those who "lost their own souls" as people whose forged inventions or fancies failed them and left them in the lurch. Such individuals are described as those who could not bear to hear and did not see, and for them, the punishment will be doubled. These individuals are identified as the greatest losers in the Hereafter.

Conversely, the texts present a promise of preservation for the righteous. It is stated that Allah will not suffer the work of any worker—whether male or female—to be lost. This promise extends to those who believe, do good works, and humble themselves before their Lord, as well as those who fought, were slain, or suffered harm and were driven from their homes for His cause. These individuals are promised that their iniquities will be blotted out and they will be admitted into Gardens with rivers flowing beneath as a reward.

## Evolution of Form
The archive also notes the loss of specific characteristics in artistic mediums. In England, the cartoon has evolved from a weapon of "venomous attack" and "ferocity" into a humorous or sarcastic comment. It is noted that the cartoon has "almost wholly lost its rancour" and "totally lost its ferocity" as a result of peace and contentment.
