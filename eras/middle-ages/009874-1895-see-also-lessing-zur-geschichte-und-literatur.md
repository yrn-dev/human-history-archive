# Kulmbach

## Geography and Infrastructure
Kulmbach, also referred to as Culmbach, is a town located in Germany within the Bavarian province of Upper Franconia. It is situated on the Weisser Main and is positioned on the Munich-Bamberg-Hof railway. Geographically, the town is located 11 miles northwest of Bayreuth. As of 1900, the population of Kulmbach was 9,428.

## Civic and Religious Institutions
The town's infrastructure includes several schools and a museum. Its religious landscape consists of three Protestant churches and one Roman Catholic church.

## Economy and Industry
Kulmbach is known for its industrial activities, specifically in the textile sector, where it maintains several linen manufactories and a large cotton spinnery. However, the town is most famous for its extensive breweries. These breweries primarily produce a black beer that is similar to English porter, which is exported in large quantities. The brewing industry is supported by associated bottling works and malting operations.

## The Plassenburg Fortress
To the south-east of the town, situated on a rocky eminence reaching a height of 1,300 feet, is the former fortress of Plassenburg. During the 14th and 15th centuries, this fortress served as the residence for the margraves of Bayreuth, who were also known as the margraves of Brandenburg-Kulmbach. The fortress was dismantled in 1807 and currently serves as a prison.

## Historical Ownership
The history of Kulmbach and the Plassenburg is marked by a succession of owners. They originally belonged to the counts of Orlamunde and the dukes of Meran. In the 14th century, ownership passed to the Hohenzollerns, who were the burgraves of Nuremberg, and subsequently to the margraves of Bayreuth.
