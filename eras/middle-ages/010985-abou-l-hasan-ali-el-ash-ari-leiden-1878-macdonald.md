# Abu-l Hasan 'Ali ibn Isma'il ul-Ash'ari

## Biography
Abu-l Hasan 'Ali ibn Isma'il ul-Ash'ari (873-935) was an Arabian theologian. Born in Basra to a family of pure Arab stock and described as orthodox, he spent the majority of his life residing in Bagdad. 

For a significant portion of his early adulthood, Ash'ari was a pupil of al-Jubba'i, a prominent teacher of the Mu'tazalites. Ash'ari himself remained a Mu'tazalite until he reached his fortieth year. In 912, he departed from this school of thought and returned to the faith of his fathers.

## Theological Contributions
Following his return to his ancestral faith, Ash'ari became its most distinguished champion. In defending this faith, he employed the philosophical methods he had previously acquired during his time in the Mu'tazalite school, which is characterized in the archive as a school of heresy. 

His theological approach was noted for occupying a mediate position between extreme views on most points. This theology subsequently became the dominant view among the Shafi'ites. While it is reported that Ash'ari wrote over one hundred works, only four or five of these are known to be extant.

## Scholarly References
The life and work of Abu-l Hasan 'Ali el-Ash'ari have been the subject of several academic studies. These include *Zur Geschichte Abu 'l-Hasan al As'ari's* by W. Spitta (Leipzig, 1876) and *Expose de la reforme de l'Islamisme commencee par Abou. 'l-Hasan Ali el-Ash'ari* by A.F. Mehren (Leiden, 1878). Additionally, D.B. Macdonald's *Muslim Theology* (London, 1903) provides further detail, specifically regarding the creed of Ash'ari in its third appendix.
