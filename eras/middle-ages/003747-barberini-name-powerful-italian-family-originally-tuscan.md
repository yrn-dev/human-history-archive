# BARBERINI

The Barberini were a powerful Italian family of Tuscan extraction who established themselves in Florence during the early part of the 11th century. Over time, the family acquired significant influence and wealth, which is evidenced today by their magnificent library and palace located in Rome.

## Rise to Papal Power
The family reached a pinnacle of influence in 1623 when Maffeo Barberini was elevated to the papal throne, taking the name Urban VIII. During his papacy, Urban VIII utilized his position to further elevate his kin. He appointed his brother, Antonio—a distinguished soldier—and two of his nephews to the rank of cardinal. A third nephew, Taddeo, was granted the principality of Palestrina.

## Conflict and Decline
The rapid increase in the family's power generated great jealousy among neighboring princes. This tension led Odoardo Farnese, the duke of Parma, to wage war against Taddeo Barberini, resulting in the defeat of the papal troops.

Following the death of Urban VIII in 1644, the family's fortunes shifted. His successor, Innocent X, displayed hostility toward the Barberini. Consequently, Taddeo fled to Paris, where he died in 1647. With Taddeo's passing, the Barberini family became extinct in the male line.

## Succession of the Name
The Barberini name continued through female lines and marriage. In 1728, Cornelia, the daughter of Taddeo, married Prince Giulio Cesare Colonna di Sciarra, who incorporated her name into his own. Later, upon the death of Prince Enrico Barberini-Colonna, the name passed to his daughter and heiress, Donna Maria. Her husband, Marquis Luigi Sacchetti, was granted permission to bear the name of Barberini and received the title of prince of Palestrina.

## Legacy and Reputation
The Barberini are remembered for their wealth and the magnificence of their Roman estates. However, they are also associated with a reputation for ruthlessness regarding the adornment of their palaces. The family plundered ancient buildings to decorate their own residences, an action that gave rise to the saying, "Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini" (What the barbarians did not do, the Barberini did).
