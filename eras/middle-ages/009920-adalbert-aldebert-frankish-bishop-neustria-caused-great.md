# Adalbert of Neustria

Adalbert (also referred to as Aldebert) was a Frankish bishop of Neustria who became a figure of significant religious and social disturbance during the period of the Frankish church's reformation.

## Religious Activities and Claims
Adalbert gained a substantial following throughout the region of Neustria by claiming to possess supernatural abilities and divine connections. He performed miracles and asserted that he had received his relics directly from angels, distinguishing his claims from those of other church figures, such as Boniface, who received relics from Rome.

Adalbert's practices deviated from established ecclesiastical norms. He planted crosses in open fields, a practice that drew people away from traditional churches and toward his own movements.

## Context within the Frankish Church
The activities of Adalbert occurred during a time of broader ecclesiastical reorganization in the Frankish realms. During this era, Boniface had been charged by Pope Zacharias in 741 with the reformation of the entire Frankish church. This effort was supported by Pippin and Carloman, the mayors of the palace who had succeeded Charles Martel.

While Boniface found success in France and organized the east Frankish church into four bishoprics (Eichstadt, Buraburg, Wurzburg, and Erfurt), Adalbert's influence in Neustria represented a point of instability and disturbance within the religious landscape of the time.

## Regional Background: Neustria
Neustria served as the nucleus of what would eventually become France. It was one of the two primary divisions of the Frankish world, the other being Austrasia (the Rhineland). This division was characterized by a linguistic split; while the inhabitants of Austrasia retained their Low German speech, the Western Franks of Neustria occupied Romanized Gaul and spoke a Latinish speech that eventually evolved into the French language.
