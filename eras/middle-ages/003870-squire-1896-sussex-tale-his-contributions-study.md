# Broom Squire (1896)

*Broom Squire* is a novel published in 1896, characterized as a Sussex tale. It was written by Sabine Baring-Gould, an English novelist and clergyman.

## Author Biography
Sabine Baring-Gould (1834– ) was born in Exeter on January 28, 1834. He was educated at Clare College, Cambridge, and spent several years traveling before entering the clergy. His ecclesiastical career included serving as the curate of Horbury, Yorkshire, in 1864, the perpetual curate of Dalton, Yorkshire, in 1867, and the rector of East Mersea, Essex, in 1871. 

Following the death of his father in 1872, Baring-Gould inherited the Lew Trenchard estate in North Devon, where his family had been established for nearly three centuries. In 1881, he exchanged his Essex living for the rectory of Lew Trenchard.

## Literary and Scholarly Work
Baring-Gould began publishing a wide variety of works in 1854, spanning genres such as history, religion, mythology, travel, folk-lore, and fiction. His fictional works include:
* *Mehalah* (1880), set on the east coast of England.
* *John Herring* (1883), a tale of the west country.
* *Court Royal* (1886).
* *Red Spider* (1887).
* *The Pennycomequicks* (1889).
* *Cheap Jack Zita* (1893).
* *Broom Squire* (1896).

Beyond his novels, Baring-Gould was recognized for his serious research and learning in the fields of antiquities, topography, and folk-lore. Notable scholarly contributions include *Book of Were-wolves* (1865), *Curious Myths of the Middle Ages* (1866), and *Curious Survivals* (1892). He also wrote numerous volumes of popular theology and sermons, and served as the editor of *The Sacristy*, a quarterly review of ecclesiastical literature and art, from 1871 to 1873.
