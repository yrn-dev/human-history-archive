# Anna Comnena and the Alexiad

Anna Comnena was a historian and princess who "was born in the purple," although her father was a private, illustrious subject who ascended to the empire. She is the author of the *Alexiad*, a work that serves as a primary authority for the life and career of Bohemund.

## Historical Accounts in the Alexiad

The *Alexiad* provides detailed records of various military and political events. Anna Comnena relates the conquests of her father in Asia Minor, as well as the war of Epirus, which she describes with "tedious prolixity." Her writing also covers the Cilician war involving Bohemond and Tancred, and records the death of Bohemund.

Beyond these conflicts, Comnena provides accounts of religious and social movements. She records the transactions of her "apostolic father" with the Manichaeans, expressing a desire to refute what she termed their "abominable heresy." She also writes about Basil, a monk and the author of the Bogomiles, a Gnostic sect that eventually vanished. Additionally, she mentions the colony of Jacobites and Paulicians that John Zimisces transplanted from Armenia to Thrace in A.D. 970.

## Observations on Foreign Figures

Anna Comnena's work includes descriptions of "Barbarian" and Norman figures. She provides information on the birth, character, and first actions of Robert Guiscard. In her accounts of Robert's first expedition against the Greeks, she is cited alongside Jeffrey Malaterra and William Appulus as a contemporary and authentic source, though she was not an eye-witness to the war.

Comnena's writing occasionally reflects personal sentiment and curiosity. She "wantonly" praises and bewails a "handsome boy" who had been betrothed as her husband following the rupture of his "barbaric nuptials," describing his hawk's eyes and the red and white of his skin. In other instances, she records stories that are unknown to Latin writers, such as a tale involving a "Barbarian" who was shut up with a dead cock, an event she describes with wonder regarding how the individual could endure the putrefaction and confinement.

## Military and Numerical Records

The *Alexiad* is used to describe the scale of opposing forces in historical conflicts. Specifically, when describing the host of Kerboga, Anna Comnena uses vague expressions to describe the army as an "immensum agmen," "innumerae copiae," or "infinita multitudo." These descriptions are noted to correspond with the accounts provided by several Latin historians, including William of Tyre, Guibert, Baldric, Robert Monachus, and the author of the *Gesta*.
