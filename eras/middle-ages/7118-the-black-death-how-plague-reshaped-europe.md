# The Black Death: How Plague Reshaped Europe

## Overview
Between 1347 and 1351, the Bubonic Plague—caused by the bacterium *Yersinia pestis*—swept through Europe, claiming an estimated 30% to 60% of the total population. While the immediate effect was catastrophic loss of life and societal terror, the long-term consequences acted as a catalyst for the collapse of the medieval order. 

The plague fundamentally altered the economic relationship between the peasantry and the landed gentry. With a sudden, massive shortage of labor, surviving peasants found themselves in a position of unprecedented bargaining power. This shift accelerated the decline of serfdom, as laborers demanded higher wages and lower rents, effectively breaking the rigid bonds of the manorial system. Socially, the plague shook the foundations of the Catholic Church; the inability of clergy to stop the disease led some to question ecclesiastical authority, while others turned to extreme piety, such as the Flagellant movement.

## Key Facts
*   **Economic Transition:** The labor shortage led to the "Golden Age of the Peasantry," where real wages rose and diets improved as land became more available for livestock and diverse crops.
*   **Medical Limitations:** Medieval medicine, based on the "miasma theory" (bad air) and the four humors, was powerless against the bacteria. This failure eventually paved the way for more empirical observations in anatomy and public health.
*   **The Great Debate:** Historians continue to debate the exact mortality rate. While traditional estimates suggested a third of Europe died, more recent scholarship (such as that by Ole Benedictow) argues the toll may have been as high as 60%, depending on the region.
*   **Social Unrest:** The attempt by elites to freeze wages via laws (like England's Statute of Labourers 1351) created tensions that culminated in violent uprisings, most notably the Peasants' Revolt of 1381.

## Did You Know?
1. **Quarantine Origins:** The word "quarantine" comes from the Italian *quaranta giorni* (forty days), referring to the period ships were required to wait in isolation at the port of Venice.
2. **The Plague Doctor:** The iconic "beak mask" was not used during the initial 1347 outbreak; it was actually developed in the 17th century by Charles de Lorme.
3. **Environmental Impact:** The massive population drop led to the abandonment of thousands of villages (known as *Wüstungen* in Germany), allowing forests to reclaim previously farmed land.
