# CONCERNING THE RELIGION OF THE CATHAYANS

## General Beliefs and Practices
The Cathayans are described as idolaters. Their religious practice is divided between the worship of a celestial deity and a terrestrial deity. To honor the Most High and Heavenly God, individuals fix a tablet high upon the wall of their chambers inscribed with a name representing this deity. Daily worship consists of offering incense from a thurible, raising the hands aloft, and gnashing the teeth three times. In these prayers, the Cathayans ask only for health of mind and body.

In addition to the heavenly deity, the Cathayans worship a figure on the ground known as *Natigai*, who is identified as the god of things terrestrial. *Natigai* is attributed with having a wife and children. The manner of worship for *Natigai* mirrors that of the Heavenly God, involving the lifting of hands, the use of incense, and the gnashing of teeth. However, the petitions directed to *Natigai* differ; the Cathayans ask this deity for children, the fruits of the earth, and seasonable weather.

## Astrologers and Soothsayers
In the city of Cambaluc, there is a significant presence of astrologers and soothsayers, numbering some five thousand. This group includes not only Cathayans but also Saracens and Christians. These practitioners are provided with annual clothing and maintenance by the Great Kaan, similar to the support provided to the poor. They remain in the constant exercise of their art within the city.

## Social and Political Context
The religious and social landscape of Cathay was marked by tension under the rule of the Great Kaan. Because the Kaan held dominion by conquest rather than hereditary right, he lacked confidence in the native Cathayans. Consequently, he appointed governors who were foreigners—specifically Tartars, Saracens, or Christians—who were devoted to his service. The Cathayans detested this arrangement, as they felt they were treated like slaves by these foreign governors.

This resentment culminated in conspiracies among the Cathayan people. In one instance, leaders such as Vanchu, a commander of 10,000, and Chenchu, a commander of a thousand, plotted to massacre "all the men with beards." This specific target was chosen because Cathayans naturally have no beards, whereas beards were worn by Christians, Saracens, and Tartars.

## Influence and Charity
Archive notes indicate that there has been a Buddhist influence on Chinese charities. This is seen in the establishment of medical aid for animals and men, as well as hospitals for the destitute and diseased. Such charitable spirits are reflected in the distribution of food and medicine at "Houses of Beneficence" (*Punyasálás*).
