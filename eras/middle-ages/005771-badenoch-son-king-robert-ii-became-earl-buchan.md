# Alexander Stewart, the Wolf of Badenoch

Alexander Stewart (1343–1405), known as the "Wolf of Badenoch," was a son of King Robert II and a prominent Scottish nobleman who held the titles of earl of Buchan and lord of Badenoch. He is remembered for his ruffianly conduct in Elginshire, which earned him his descriptive moniker.

## Lordship of Badenoch
Badenoch is a district located in south-east Inverness-shire, Scotland. It is bounded by the Cairngorms and Braemar to the east, the Monadhliath mountains to the north, the Grampians and Atholl to the south, and Lochaber to the west. The region is characterized as a wild mountainous tract, with many hills exceeding 3,000 feet in height and forests in Feshie, Gaick, Drumochter, and Alder that provide some of the best deer country in the Highlands. The district is watered by the Spey and its tributaries, and its principal lakes are Loch Ericht and Loch Laggan.

The lordship of Badenoch was owned by the Comyns from 1229 to 1313. In 1371, King Robert II granted the lordship to his son, Alexander Stewart. The lordship later reverted to the crown and was bestowed upon the 1st earl of Huntly in 1452; the marquess of Huntly continues to hold the title of lord of Badenoch.

## Earldom of Buchan
The earldom of Buchan was originally combined with the earldom of Mar as one of the seven original Scottish earldoms before the two were separated. Early earls of Buchan included Henry Beaumont (d. 1340) and the constables of Scotland, John Comyn (d. c. 1313) and Alexander Comyn (d. 1289). John Comyn's wife, Isabel, the countess of Buchan, is noted for crowning Robert the Bruce as king at Scone in 1306.

Around 1382, Alexander Stewart became the earl of Buchan. Following his tenure, the Stewarts held the earldom for approximately a century and a half, though not in a direct line from Alexander. Notable Stewart earls included John, who was killed at the battle of Verneuil in 1424 and served as constable of France, and his father Robert, the duke of Albany and Scottish regent.

The earldom eventually passed to the Erskines in 1617 via James Erskine (d. 1640). James was the son of John Erskine, 2nd (or 7th) earl of Mar, and Mary, who had inherited the earldom from her father, James Douglas (d. 1601). Later Erskine earls included the eccentric David Steuart Erskine, 11th earl (1742–1829), who founded the Scottish Society of Antiquaries in 1780.

## Historical Associations and Remains
Alexander Stewart is associated with the ruins of old Balvenie Castle, a structure of Pictish origin largely built in the Scots Baronial style. The castle also has associations with the Duffs, the Stuarts, the Douglases, and the Comyns.

In August 1908, excavations conducted at Dunkeld uncovered remains believed to be those of Alexander Stewart.
