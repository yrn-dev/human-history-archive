# Willibrord

Willibrord was an Anglo-Saxon missionary and the first bishop of Utrecht who died in 738. He is recognized as one of the most famous heralds of the Catholic religion in Belgium and was part of a broader effort by English Benedictines to evangelize Holland and Friesland.

## Missionary Work and the Bishopric of Utrecht
Willibrord operated as a missionary during a period when large portions of Belgium, particularly the eastern regions, remained heathen. His efforts were part of a wider movement of Irish and Anglo-Saxon missionaries who worked on their own initiative to introduce the Christian religion to the region. 

As the first bishop of Utrecht, Willibrord provided direction for other missionaries. Notably, the Saxon monk Boniface (also known as Wynfrith) traveled to Frisia and preached successfully for three years under Willibrord's direction. This occurred after the death of Radbod, the king of the Frisians.

## Attempts to Convert the Danes
From his see at Utrecht, Willibrord extended his missionary efforts beyond the immediate region. He made an attempt to convert the "wild Danes," though this specific endeavor is recorded as having been unsuccessful.

## Ecclesiastical Context
The work initiated by missionaries like Willibrord was later completed by bishops who visited their extensive dioceses. During the eighth century, the region of Belgium was divided into several dioceses, including Utrecht, Liège, Cambrai, Arras, Noyon-Tournai, and Térouanne. The diocese of Utrecht, along with Liège, was subject to the metropolitan church of Cologne.
