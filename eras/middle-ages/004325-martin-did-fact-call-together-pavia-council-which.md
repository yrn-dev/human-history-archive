# Pavia

Pavia is a location associated with various historical events spanning from the early Middle Ages to the late 19th century, serving as a site for ecclesiastical councils, royal residences, and military political actions.

## Ecclesiastical Councils and Religious History
Pavia has been the site of multiple church councils and the seat of influential bishops. In 1022, Pope Benedict VIII held a council at Pavia where he worked to address the great decadence of ecclesiastical discipline at that time. 

In the 15th century, following a decree from the Council of Constance on October 9, 1417, the papacy was obliged to summon general councils periodically. Martin V, who became the sole Pope in 1417 after the healing of the Great Schism, called a council together at Pavia at the expiry of the first term fixed by that decree. However, due to an epidemic, the council had to be transferred almost immediately to Siena. This council was eventually dissolved in 1424, just as it had begun to discuss the subject of reform, though the circumstances of its dissolution are still imperfectly known.

Additionally, the city served as the see for Magnus Felix Ennodius (474–521), a Latin poet and rhetorician. Born in Arelate, Ennodius was raised in Pavia by an aunt and later ordained as a deacon there around 493 by Bishop Epiphanius. Ennodius eventually became the bishop of Pavia around 515. During his tenure, he was sent by Theodoric on two embassies to Constantinople (in 515 and 517) to attempt a reconciliation between the Western and Eastern churches. He died on July 17, 521, and his epitaph is located in the basilica of St Michael at Pavia.

## Gothic and Roman History
During the period of the Gothic wars, Pavia served as a center of power. After the palace of Pavia was purified from the Rugian usurper, a youth who preferred the Gothic throne over the service of Justinian reviewed a national force of five thousand soldiers there and undertook the restoration of the kingdom of Italy. 

Historical records from this era also mention the bishop of Pavia—specifically an ecclesiastic who desired the bishopric—who wrote to celebrate the physical attributes of his sovereign, including his eyes, hands, and complexion.

## 19th Century Political Events
In the late 19th century, Pavia is associated with General Pavia, who served as the captain-general of Madrid during the Spanish revolution. In 1873, General Pavia suggested to President Castelar that the armies in the field and the garrison of Madrid were disposed to support the president should a conflict arise with the majority of the federal Cortes. Castelar flatly refused this offer.

Following a vote of censure by the intransigent majority of the Cortes, Castelar resigned. At daybreak on January 3, 1874, General Pavia forcibly ejected the deputies and closed and dissolved the Cortes. Subsequently, he called upon Marshal Serrano to form a provisional government.
