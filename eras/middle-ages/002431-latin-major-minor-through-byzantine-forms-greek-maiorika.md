# Latin Major and Minor through Byzantine Forms

The terms "Major" and "Minor" in the context of the Baleares islands represent a linguistic evolution from Latin through Byzantine Greek forms. This transition reflects the broader historical interaction between Latin and Greek influences within the Mediterranean and the Byzantine Empire.

## Etymology of Majorca and Minorca
The modern names of the islands Majorca and Minorca are derived from the Latin words *Major* and *Minor*. These Latin roots were transmitted through the Byzantine Greek forms *Maiorika* and *Minorika* to arrive at their current designations. In contrast, other islands in the region have different origins; Iviza is derived from the older name Ebusus, which is likely of Carthaginian origin, while Formentera was known to the Greeks as Ophiusa and to the Romans as Colubraria.

## The Latin and Greek Influence in Byzantium
The transition of Latin terms into Greek forms is characteristic of the mixed nature of Byzantine culture. While the Byzantine Empire initially retained its Latin character even after the final division in 395, the dominant position of the Greek language in the Eastern empire eventually led to a process of Hellenization. 

Despite this shift, the Roman element remained an essential part of the civilization. The political character of the empire remained genuinely Roman, with legislation, administration, and military and naval traditions continuing as old Roman work. This influence was so profound that the Greeks eventually adopted the name "Romans" (*Rhomaioi*), a term that became a linguistic and ethnographical designation, superseding the name "Hellenes."

## Linguistic and Legal Transitions
The influence of Latin is particularly evident in the Greek language of the Byzantine and modern periods, which contains many Latin terms related to the imperial court, administration, and justice. However, in formal literature, these "barbarisms" were often avoided and replaced with Greek periphrases.

A primary example of the shift from Latin to Greek is found in jurisprudence. Latin was originally the exclusive language of Roman law. Under Justinian (527-565), the codification of law in the *Corpus juris* was the last great act of the Latin tradition. However, the Hellenizing of legal language began during his reign; the *Institutes* and the *Digest* were translated into Greek, and the *Novels* were composed partly in Greek. In later periods, the *Basilica* (*Ta basilika*)—a grand compilation of the *Digests*, *Codex*, and *Novels*—completely superseded the old Latin *Corpus juris* in the East.

## Distinctions in Music and Art
While the terms "major" and "minor" appear in modern musical scales, the archive notes that the ancient Greeks had no such categorical distinctions. The Greek musical system was based on the tetrachord (a series of four notes). While a modern observer might describe the Greek octave scale as "minor" based on descending forms, the Greek system operated on different logical principles and ratios, such as the 8:9 ratio for whole tones, which made the major third a dissonance.

In the realm of art, the Byzantine style followed the classical period and reached maturity under Justinian. This art, centered in Constantinople, had a significant influence on Europe and the East. It is characterized by the production of icons—a term derived from the Latinized form of the Greek *eikôn* (meaning portrait or image). These representations of sacred personages, often appearing as small metal plaques in archaic Byzantine style, were venerated by the Greek Church.

## Conflict Between Latin and Greek Entities
The relationship between the Latin and Greek worlds was often marked by hostility, particularly during the Crusades. The Crusaders served the Latin Church and frequently found themselves in conflict with both the Turks and the Byzantine Empire. In 1204, the Fourth Crusade stormed Constantinople, leading to a period where Latin emperors, such as Baldwin of Flanders, ruled as conquerors from 1204 to 1261. During this interval, the Latin and Greek Churches were declared reunited.
