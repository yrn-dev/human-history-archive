# The Byzantine Empire: The Eternal Continuation of Rome

## Overview
The Byzantine Empire is frequently characterized as a distinct medieval entity, yet to its own citizens, it was simply the *Imperium Romanum*. While the Western Roman Empire collapsed in 476 CE, the eastern half—centered in Constantinople—survived for another millennium. This state represented a complex synthesis of Roman political administration, Greek culture, and Orthodox Christian faith.

Historians have long debated the term "Byzantine," a label coined by 16th-century scholars long after the empire's fall. Some argue that the shift from Latin to Greek under Heraclius (r. 610–641) marked a fundamental break from Roman identity. However, most modern scholars emphasize continuity: the Byzantines maintained Roman law (most notably through the *Corpus Juris Civilis* of Justinian I), preserved the imperial bureaucracy, and viewed themselves as the legitimate heirs to Augustus and Constantine. The empire served as a vital bulwark for Europe, shielding the West from various eastern invasions while preserving the classical knowledge of antiquity.

## Key Facts
*   **The Capital:** Constantinople was strategically located on the Bosphorus, controlling the trade routes between the Black Sea and the Mediterranean, making it the wealthiest city in Christendom for centuries.
*   **Legal Legacy:** The *Codex Justinianus* streamlined centuries of contradictory Roman laws. This work became the foundation for most modern civil law systems in Europe.
*   **The Great Schism:** In 1054, theological and political tensions led to a formal split between the Eastern Orthodox Church in Constantinople and the Roman Catholic Church in Rome.
*   **Military Innovation:** To survive against superior numbers, the Byzantines developed "Greek Fire," a secret incendiary weapon that could burn on water, ensuring naval dominance for centuries.

## Did You Know?
1. **They never called themselves "Byzantines":** The inhabitants of the empire referred to themselves as *Rhomaioi* (Romans).
2. **The Walls of Theodosius:** The land walls of Constantinople were so advanced that they remained virtually impregnable for over a thousand years, until the advent of gunpowder cannons.
3. **Preservation of Plato:** Much of what we know about Greek philosophy today exists because Byzantine scribes meticulously copied and preserved ancient manuscripts during the Middle Ages.
