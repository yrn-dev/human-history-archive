# OF THE LIONS AND LEOPARDS AND WOLVES THAT THE KAAN KEEPS

The Great Kaan maintained a sophisticated system of hunting and game management, employing a vast array of trained carnivorous animals and birds of prey to assist in the chase. These animals were kept for both the practical purpose of catching game and for the Emperor's personal diversion.

## Trained Felines and the Chase
The Emperor kept numbers of leopards and a great many lynxes, both of which were trained for the chase to catch game. The Kaan utilized these animals for diversion; he would occasionally ride through his park with a leopard positioned behind him on the horse's croup. If an animal caught his fancy, he would slip the leopard at it. The game captured during these excursions was then used to feed the hawks kept in mew.

In addition to leopards and lynxes, the Kaan kept several great lions. These beasts were described as being larger than those of Babylonia, featuring skins striped with black, red, and white. These lions were trained to hunt fierce or great beasts, including wild cattle, bears, stags, wild asses, and boars. To employ these lions, they were transported in covered carts, accompanied by a small dog. This method was necessary to ensure the lions approached the game against the wind, preventing the prey from scenting the lions and escaping.

## Avian Hunters and Wolf-Catching
The Kaan's hunting establishment included a great number of eagles broken to catch wild goats, deer, foxes, and wolves. The eagles specifically trained for wolf-catching were noted for being particularly powerful and large, to the extent that no wolf was able to escape them.

Beyond the eagles, the Kaan maintained an extensive collection of hawks and gerfalcons. In a park enclosed by a wall with a compass of 16 miles—containing meadows, brooks, rivers, and fountains—the Emperor kept more than 200 gerfalcons alone, in addition to other hawks. To ensure a steady food supply for these birds, the Emperor procured and placed various wild animals within the park, excluding those of a ferocious nature.

## Hunting Infrastructure and Personnel
The scale of the Kaan's hunting operations was significant. Records indicate that Kubilai Khan maintained an army of 14,000 huntsmen distributed across other cities and Peking. The Kaan typically hunted in the Peking plain from the start of spring until his departure for Shang-tu. The Peking department provided ideal conditions for this activity due to its many low and marshy places that abounded in game.

The Kaan's hunting expeditions were highly organized, involving specific establishments for hawking and the use of great tents. The Emperor traveled in a litter and was supported by various officials, including those in charge of the hounds.

## Availability of Game
Game was exceptionally abundant, particularly near the capital. During the three winter months, large quantities of wildfowl and four-footed game were dispatched to the city. This game often arrived frozen and standing on its feet. The variety of available animals included:
*   **Mammals:** Various sorts of deer, stags, boars, elks, hares, rabbits, squirrels, wild-cats, rats, and three different kinds of bears.
*   **Birds:** Geese, ducks, and very fine jungle-fowl.
