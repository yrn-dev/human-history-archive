# Frankland-Russell-Astley, of Marq. of Ormonde, and Various

The terms "Frankland-Russell-Astley," "Marq. of Ormonde," and "Various Collections" appear within the archival records as specific designations for collections of manuscripts. These materials are cited as sources for the correspondence of Charles II and Scotland in 1650, and are listed among the Calendars of St. Pap. Dom. and the Hist. MSS. Comm. Series, alongside the manuscripts of the Duke of Buccleuch at Montagu House.

## The Marquessate of Ormonde

The Ormonde title is associated with a long line of the Butler family in Ireland. Sir Piers Butler, known as "red Piers," assumed the earldom of Ormonde in 1515. Although he was an Irishman born and allied to Irish chieftains through his mother, a daughter of the MacMorrogh Kavanagh, he served as the chief governor of Ireland as lord deputy in 1522. Following an agreement in February 1527/8, he was created earl of Ossory, and later, around February 1537/8, he received a patent for the Ormonde earldom. His son, James the Lame, was created Viscount Thurles in 1535/6 and later secured an act of parliament in 1543/4 that confirmed the earldom and restored the "pre-eminence" of the ancient earldom of 1328.

One of the most prominent figures in this lineage was James Butler, the grandson of Piers. He united the Ormonde titles and estates through his marriage to his cousin, Elizabeth Preston. A statesman and soldier, he proclaimed Charles II in 1649 but was forced to leave Ireland at the end of 1650 after his garrisons were broken by Cromwell following a defeat at Rathmines. Upon the Restoration, he was appointed lord-lieutenant and regained his estates, including the county palatine of Tipperary. His titles expanded over time: he was created a marquess in 1632, received the English earldom of Brecknock in 1660, an Irish dukedom of Ormonde in 1661, and an English dukedom in 1682.

The later history of the house saw James Butler, the second duke of Ormonde, become a leader of the London Jacobites. He was attainted in 1715, resulting in the forfeiture of his estates and honours. He died without surviving issue in 1745 after spending thirty years in exile, primarily in Avignon.

## Conflict with the Geraldines

The Butlers of Ormonde were hereditary enemies of the Geraldines. This rivalry was exemplified by the disputes between the earls of Ormonde and the earls of Desmond. The 9th earl of Ormonde had married Lady Joan Fitzgerald, the daughter and heiress-general of the 11th earl of Desmond, which led to claims by the Butlers over Desmond estates.

In the 16th century, Gerald Fitzgerald, 15th earl of Desmond, engaged in open war with Ormonde. Following a battle at Affane on the Blackwater, Desmond was defeated and taken prisoner. Later, during a rebellion led by James Fitzmaurice Fitzgerald, some Butlers—including Sir Edmund Butler, Piers Butler, and Edward Butler—joined the movement. However, the submission of the Butlers followed shortly after the appearance of Ormonde and Sidney in the south-west.

## Other Notable Figures Named Astley

The archives contain records of several individuals named Astley:

*   **Jacob Astley, Baron (1570-1652):** A royalist commander during the English Civil War from a Norfolk family. He served in the Netherlands under Counts Maurice and Henry of Orange and fought in the Thirty Years' War under Gustavus Adolphus and the elector palatine Frederick V. In England, he served Charles I as sergeant-major and major-general of the foot. He is remembered for his battle-prayer at Edgehill and his defense of Shaw House during the second battle of Newbury. He fought the final battle for the king at Stow-on-the-Wold in March 1646.
*   **Sir John Dugdale Astley, Bart. (1828-1894):** A descendant of Lord Astley and son of the 2nd baronet. He served as a lieutenant-colonel in the army during the Crimean War (1848-1859). After marrying an heiress in 1858, he became a well-known figure in pugilism and horse-racing, where he was known as "the Mate." He served as the Conservative M.P. for North Lincolnshire from 1874 to 1880 and published his reminiscences, *Fifty Years of my Life*, shortly before his death.

## Related Lineages and Branches

Other branches of the Butler family include the earls of Carrick (Ireland 1748) and Viscounts Ikerrin (Ireland 1629), who claim descent from a brother of the first Ormonde earl. The viscounts Mountgarret (Ireland 1550) descend from a younger son of the Red Earl of Ossory. Additionally, the barony of Caher (Ireland 1543) was held by an illegitimate branch of the Butlers until it became extinct in 1858. In the Holy Roman Empire, the count Buttler von Clonebough (Haimhausen) descends from the 3rd earl of Ormonde.
