# Letter of Peace (list mirny)

The "Letter of Peace," referred to in historical records as the *list mirny*, was a formal agreement reached between two rival factions of the nobility in Bohemia. This agreement served as a political compromise to resolve internal conflicts regarding the leadership and religious administration of the region.

## Political Context and Factions
The agreement emerged during a period of succession instability following the death of King Sigismund in 1437. Sigismund had been succeeded by his son-in-law, Albert of Austria, who reigned over Bohemia for less than two years before his own death. While it was known that Albert's widow, Elizabeth, was pregnant, the death of the king reignited disputes over whether the Bohemian crown was hereditary or elective.

This instability split the nobility into two primary divisions:
*   **The National Party:** Led by George of Podebrad.
*   **The Roman or Austrian Division:** Led by Ulrich of Rosenberg.

## Terms of the Agreement
The "Letter of Peace" was the final agreement reached between these two opposing parties. By signing the document, the participants pledged themselves to several specific conditions:

**Religious and Administrative Recognition**
Signatories pledged to recognize the Compacts. Additionally, they agreed to support John of Rokycan as the archbishop of Prague. John of Rokycan had been selected by the estates through an agreement made at the same time as the Compacts, although the Church of Rome refused to recognize his appointment.

**Succession and the Throne**
In exchange for the concessions made by the Roman or Austrian division, the national party agreed to abandon the candidature of Prince Casimir of Poland for the throne. This strategic move cleared the path for the eventual succession of the heir of Albert of Austria.

## Aftermath and Succession
Following the agreement, Queen Elizabeth gave birth to a son named Ladislas on February 22, 1440. The Bohemians formally acknowledged Ladislas as their king, though this occurred only after Albert, duke of Bavaria, had declined the crown. 

Despite the "Letter of Peace," Bohemia remained without a regular government for a period, as Ladislas stayed in Austria under the guardianship of his uncle, Frederick, duke of Styria (who later became Emperor Frederick III). Consequently, the region continued to experience constant conflicts between the rival parties of the nobility.

## Constitutional Developments
The political volatility of the era eventually led to a general meeting of the estates in 1446. This meeting included representatives from Bohemia, Moravia, Silesia, and Lusatia (the "lands of the Bohemian crown"). This assembly is noted for its exceptional importance to the constitutional history of Bohemia, as it decreed that members of the estates be divided into three bodies known as *curiae*.
