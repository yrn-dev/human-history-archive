# Boleslaus II and the First Bohemian War

## Accession and Objectives
Boleslaus II, known as "The Bold," served as the king of Poland from 1039 to 1081. The eldest son of Casimir I, he succeeded his father in 1058. While Casimir I had spent his reign painstakingly restoring tranquillity and domestic order to the kingdom, Poland had experienced a territorial shrinkage since the era of Boleslaus I. Consequently, the primary objective of Boleslaus II was to restore the importance and dignity of the Polish state.

## The First Bohemian War
The most immediate enemy to Poland during this period was Bohemia. Poland had recently been forced to pay tribute to the Bohemians for Silesia, which was Poland's oldest possession. In an effort to address this, Boleslaus II initiated a war against Bohemia. This first Bohemian war proved to be unsuccessful. The conflict was eventually terminated through a diplomatic arrangement: the marriage of Boleslaus's sister, Swatawa, to the Czech king Wratyslaus II.

## Subsequent Foreign Relations and Conflicts
Despite the failure of the first war with Bohemia, Boleslaus II found success in other regions. He provided assistance to the fugitive Magyar prince Bela, who succeeded in winning the crown of Hungary. In the East, Boleslaus achieved further success in 1069 by placing Izaslaus on the throne of Kiev. This action confirmed Poland's overlordship over Russia.

This new position of power allowed Boleslaus II to once again target his enemies, including Bohemia. By utilizing Russian auxiliaries, he was able to chastise the Bohemians. However, Wratyslaus of Bohemia appealed to the emperor for assistance. A full-scale war between the Empire and Poland was only avoided due to the sudden rupture between Henry IV and the Holy See, and the subsequent events that led to the emperor's humiliating surrender at Canossa.

## Later Reign and Exile
Boleslaus II maintained a positive relationship with Gregory VII, and there were discussions regarding the dispatch of papal legates to restore order within the Polish Church. On December 26, 1076, Boleslaus crowned himself with the royal diadem, an act indicating that Polish kings did not yet view their titles as entirely secure. His final recorded exploit was a second successful expedition to Kiev to reinstate his protege, Izaslaus.

By 1079, Boleslaus II had fallen from power and became an exile in Hungary, where he died around 1081. His sudden decline was caused by the cruel vengeance he took upon the *milites*, or the noble order. These nobles had been attempting to curb royal power, emulating the example of their counterparts in Bohemia.
